# Master Patient Index Identity Integrity API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A health system's Master Patient Index is the one place where a person is supposed to have exactly one identity. In practice it is the place where identity goes wrong: the same patient is registered twice at two different desks under two spellings of the same name, one patient's demographics are typed over another patient's chart, and a person presenting with a borrowed insurance card acquires a biometric profile that contradicts the one already on file. Every one of these defects has a clinical consequence — a result filed under the wrong identity, an allergy list that is only half the story — and every one of them has to be resolved by a human who can be held accountable for the decision.

This document specifies the API that the identity integrity team uses to do that work. The service scores pairs of records against configurable demographic weighting rules, places the ambiguous ones on a prioritised triage queue, captures the manual review evidence that justifies a verdict, and then executes the identity change — a merge, an unmerge, or an overlay correction — while fanning the change out to every downstream consumer of enterprise identity. Alongside the resolution workflow it carries two supporting concerns: identity fraud adjudication, for records whose conflicting biometrics or insurance point at deliberate misrepresentation rather than a clerical slip, and duplicate-rate measurement, which tells the organisation which registration desks are actually generating the duplicates.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **reversibility**: an identity change made in error must be undoable so exactly that no clinical element and no enterprise identity key is lost or invented in the round trip. The second is **accountability**: the analyst who recommends a merge is never the steward who executes it, every score is pinned to the exact rule set version that produced it, and every element moved between charts is counted on the way out and on the way back. The entire service is mounted under the `/mpi` path prefix, with a single test-driving clock endpoint mounted outside it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level behaviour that holds for every endpoint in the service: authentication, response shape, pagination, resource metadata, numeric precision, interval arithmetic, concurrency control, and the order in which failures are reported.

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Identity Scoping

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. The service recognises exactly five roles: `SiteRegistrar`, `IdentityAnalyst`, `IdentityStewardApprover`, `PrivacyOfficer`, and `MpiAdmin`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is any string other than those five, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are peers, not a hierarchy. `MpiAdmin` in particular is **not** a superset of the others: an administrator owns configuration and propagation but cannot create a patient record, cannot triage or recommend on a candidate pair, cannot approve a merge, cannot resolve a pair as distinct, cannot create, apply or complete an overlay correction, cannot remediate contamination, cannot adjudicate fraud, and cannot submit an external match. `SiteRegistrar` can search the index and create records at its own site and nothing else — it cannot create or rescore candidate pairs, attach evidence, recommend, approve, merge, unmerge, correct overlays, remediate contamination, open investigations, submit external matches, or run metrics. `IdentityAnalyst` owns the detection and manual-review half of the workflow but cannot approve a merge, unmerge, resolve a pair as distinct, return a recommendation to review, apply or complete an overlay, reassign or close a remediation, adjudicate fraud, or configure sites, rule sets and downstream systems. `IdentityStewardApprover` owns the approval half and correspondingly cannot triage, start review, attach evidence or recommend, cannot create or rescore pairs, cannot adjudicate fraud, cannot quarantine, reassign or close remediations, and cannot configure sites, rule sets or downstream systems. `PrivacyOfficer` owns fraud adjudication and clinical cross-contamination remediation and cannot create records or search attempts, cannot create, rescore, triage, review or recommend on pairs, cannot approve or reverse merges, cannot create, apply or complete overlay corrections, cannot submit external matches, and cannot configure sites, rule sets or downstream systems. All five roles may read every retrieval endpoint.

Role identity binds to persisted data differently per role, and the difference matters. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteRegistrar`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RegistrationSite` — the registrar desk is identified by the site it belongs to. Ownership checks therefore compare a resource's site reference to `X-User-ID` directly: when a `SiteRegistrar` records a registration search, the site named by the `{id}` path segment must equal `X-User-ID`, and when a `SiteRegistrar` creates a patient record, the body's `site_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch in either case is rejected with `ERR_SITE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `IdentityAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque analyst identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (as `submitted_by_user_id` on review evidence, as `recommended_by_user_id` on a candidate pair, and as `created_by_user_id` on an overlay correction) and compares it against other recorded operator identifiers for the duty-separation check, but never against any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `IdentityStewardApprover`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque approver identifier; the server records it as `approved_by_user_id` on a candidate pair and on a merge operation, and as `created_by_user_id` on an overlay correction, and compares it against the pair's `recommended_by_user_id`, but never against any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PrivacyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque privacy-officer identifier, recorded as `opened_by_user_id` on the investigations that officer files and not compared to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `MpiAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque administrator identifier that the service does not compare to any entity's `id` at all.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching record remains beyond the current window.

Unless a specific collection declares otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock hands many rows an identical `created_at`, any tie that a collection's declared ordering keys leave unresolved is broken by creation order — the sequence in which the rows were persisted — so a page is always deterministic. Three collections declare an ordering of their own because their consumers need a different reading order. The candidate-pair work queue is ordered by `priority_band` ascending, so that `P1` items are presented first, then by `effective_score` descending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `subject_record_mrn` ascending. The propagation-task collection is ordered by `created_at` ascending with `id` ascending applied only as a stabiliser for rows sharing an identical timestamp — the `id` tie-break exists to make the page deterministic and is never an input to a business decision. And a candidate pair's review-evidence collection is read back in exactly the order §3.4 uses to sum it: `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by `evidence_type` ascending and then by `outcome` ascending.

### 2.4. Resource Metadata, Immutable Event Records and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id`. Most resources additionally carry three audit fields: `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock on every mutation that increments `version`.

Three resource kinds are write-once event records and deliberately carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and no endpoint mutates them after creation. Those are the registration search attempt (`RegistrationSearchAttempt`), the review evidence entry (`ReviewEvidence`), and the site duplicate metric run (`SiteDuplicateMetric`). The absence of `version` on these three is why none of them can ever be the parent entity of a concurrency check.

Time in this service is a logical clock, never wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A test-driving capability, open to all five roles, sets the service-wide clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes it back; from that moment every `created_at`, every `updated_at`, every deadline computation and every temporal comparison in the service derives from the clock value, not from the host's real time.

### 2.5. Score Precision, Rounding Order and Interval Arithmetic

All scores in this service — per-attribute contributions, raw and penalised match scores, evidence adjustments, effective scores and fraud risk scores — are carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half up. Where a formula rounds more than once, the **order** of rounding is part of the specification and is stated explicitly in the workflow narrative that owns the formula; an implementation that rounds at a different point produces a different, wrong number even though every operand matches. Element counts, record counts and basis-point rates are integers and are never carried with a fractional part.

The service uses two deliberately different interval conventions, and confusing them is the most common source of wrong answers. Dispatch windows on downstream propagation are **half-open**: a window covers its start instant and excludes its end instant, so two windows that merely touch at an endpoint do not overlap and back-to-back dispatches are always permitted. Datetime comparisons for these windows are made at millisecond resolution against ISO 8601 UTC values with three fractional digits. Duplicate-rate measurement windows, by contrast, are **closed** date ranges: both the first and the last date belong to the window, so two windows that merely touch at a boundary date do collide, because that boundary date is counted inside both of them.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Supersede Operations

Two write operations in this service legitimately supersede earlier work of the same kind — a candidate pair may be re-scored any number of times while it remains unresolved, and a patient record may have a fresh overlay correction filed against it after an earlier one — so neither can be protected by a state precondition. Both are protected instead by an optimistic concurrency check against the parent resource's `version`.

Re-scoring a candidate pair requires `expected_pair_version` in the request body. This field is not optional. Its value must equal the target `DuplicateCandidatePair`'s current `version`. Triage, starting review, attaching evidence, recording a recommendation and the fraud-escalation cascade all advance that `version`, so a client working from a stale read is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PAIR_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than silently overwriting a score computed from a state it never observed.

Filing an overlay correction requires `expected_record_version` in the request body. This field is likewise not optional. Its value must equal the target `PatientRecord`'s current `version` as it stands **before** this request's own effects are applied. Every merge, remediation, fraud cascade and prior overlay filing touching that record has already advanced its `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECORD_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and no such endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.7. Nested Path Resolution

Several collections are addressed beneath a parent segment: registration search attempts and duplicate-metric runs live beneath a registration site, and review evidence lives beneath a candidate pair. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right. The parent named by the leading `{id}` segment must exist before anything to its right is considered, so a request naming a nonexistent site under which a metric run is requested fails as a missing site rather than as a malformed window, and a request naming a nonexistent pair under which evidence is listed fails as a missing pair. The single global ordering in §2.8 makes this concrete for every endpoint.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence: One Error, Chosen by Rank

A request may violate several rules at once. The service never merges failures and never reports the "most interesting" one: it evaluates the rules below strictly in ascending rank order and the **first** rule that fails determines the response's `error_id` and HTTP status. A later rule's condition is not even evaluated once an earlier one has failed. Reading the list top to bottom is therefore the only correct way to predict which error a bad request receives.

1. `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint.

Applies to exactly those two operations: `POST /mpi/registration-sites/{id}/search-attempts` and `POST /mpi/patient-records`.
4. The request body and query string are well formed — every required input field present, every type correct, every enum value inside its declared set, every date parseable as `YYYY-MM-DD`, every datetime parseable as ISO 8601 UTC, every numeric value inside its declared range, `attribute_weights` carrying exactly the seven declared attribute keys, `corrected_field_snapshot` carrying only allowed keys, `encounter_to_date` no earlier than `encounter_from_date`, `window_end_date` no earlier than `window_start_date`, `dispatch_window_end_at` strictly later than `dispatch_window_start_at`, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400. Several consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright, because they are easy to get backwards. A `SiteRegistrar` acting on the wrong site is told so (rank 3) before the body is validated at all (rank 4). Existence checks (ranks 5 through 15) precede every business rule, and among them the parent in the URL is resolved before any body-referenced entity of a different type. Both concurrency checks (ranks 19 and 20) fire **before** any fraud hold, remediation hold, site-suspension or state check on the same request, so a client holding a stale `version` learns about the stale version rather than about the record's condition. Conversely, a fraud hold (rank 21) outranks a remediation hold (rank 22), which outranks consent (rank 23), which outranks site suspension (rank 24), which outranks the identity-state check (rank 25) — a record that is simultaneously under a fraud hold and not `ACTIVE` reports the fraud hold. Duty separation (rank 34) is evaluated only after every state precondition has passed, so an approver who tries to resolve their own recommendation on a pair in the wrong state is told about the state first. And for record creation, the ordering runs missing-site, missing-search-attempt, missing-search-attempt-for-registrars, site-suspended, duplicate MRN, expired search attempt, and finally blocked creation — meaning a registrar who cites a stale search attempt that also blocked creation receives `ERR_SEARCH_ATTEMPT_EXPIRED`, not `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREATION_BLOCKED`.

### 2.9. The Two Independent Condition Dimensions of a Patient Record

A patient record carries **two** state dimensions on the same row, and they do not constrain each other. `identity_state` is the merge dimension: it is `ACTIVE` when the record stands for its own person, and `MERGED_SUBSUMED` when the record has been absorbed into another record's cluster. `integrity_state` is the trustworthiness dimension, with four values — `CLEAR`, `FRAUD_SUSPECTED`, `FRAUD_CONFIRMED` and `REMEDIATION_PENDING`. A record may be `ACTIVE` and `FRAUD_CONFIRMED` at once, or `MERGED_SUBSUMED` and `REMEDIATION_PENDING` at once; nothing in the merge dimension's transition set depends on the current value of the integrity dimension except where a validation rule says so explicitly, and nothing in the integrity dimension's transition set depends on the merge dimension at all.

`identity_state` is set directly by merge and unmerge. `integrity_state` is never set directly by any caller: it is **re-derived** from the current condition of the record's investigations, remediations and overlay corrections every time one of those is created or transitions, and on the unmerge cascade. The derivation reads all fraud investigations, all contamination remediations and all overlay corrections that reference the record, with no time window and no other filter, and applies strict precedence where the first matching rule wins. If any fraud investigation on the record is `SUBSTANTIATED` or `REFERRED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, the record is `FRAUD_CONFIRMED`. Otherwise, if any fraud investigation on the record is `OPEN`, the record is `FRAUD_SUSPECTED`. Otherwise, if any contamination remediation on the record is `OPEN`, `QUARANTINED` or `REASSIGNED`, or any overlay correction on the record is `DETECTED` or `UNDER_CORRECTION`, the record is `REMEDIATION_PENDING`. Otherwise the record is `CLEAR`. Because fraud outranks remediation, opening an investigation on a record that already has an open remediation moves the visible `integrity_state` to `FRAUD_SUSPECTED` while leaving the remediation itself untouched and still open — and when that investigation is later unsubstantiated, the still-open remediation pulls the record back down to `REMEDIATION_PENDING` rather than all the way to `CLEAR`. Every re-derivation that changes the value increments the record's `version` by 1.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counter Reconciliation

Identity changes in this domain are inherently multi-row: absorbing one record into another touches the survivor, the subsumed record, every record that previously pointed at the subsumed record, both sites' counters, the candidate pair that authorised it, and one propagation task per active downstream consumer. The rule is that **every** row the service mutates in the course of one request has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed — there is no notion of a "primary" row whose version alone advances. Where a cascade transitions many sibling rows (the fraud escalation that sweeps a record's candidate pairs, or the task cancellation sweep during a reversal), each affected row's `version` advances individually.

Five counters are derived and must be reconciled with reality after any request that could change them; none of them is ever supplied by a client. A registration site's `active_record_count` is the number of patient records registered at that site whose `identity_state` is `ACTIVE`. That same site's `flagged_record_count` is the number of patient records registered at it whose `identity_state` is `ACTIVE` **and** whose `integrity_state` is either `FRAUD_SUSPECTED` or `FRAUD_CONFIRMED` — this counter is a direct input to fraud risk scoring, so the order in which it is refreshed relative to a risk-score evaluation is specified in §3.8. A record's `subsumed_member_count` is the number of records pointing at it as their survivor whose `identity_state` is `MERGED_SUBSUMED`, which is the size of its merge cluster minus itself. A downstream system's `unacked_task_count` is the number of its propagation tasks whose `state` is `QUEUED` or `SENT`. A merge operation's `propagation_task_count` is fixed at merge time as the number of propagation tasks that merge created.

Reconciling one of these five counters restates its value; it is not itself a mutation of the row that publishes it. A counter refresh therefore never by itself advances that row's `version` or refreshes its `updated_at`: a registration site whose `active_record_count` or `flagged_record_count` moves because a record was created, merged, unmerged or flagged keeps the `version` and `updated_at` it already had, and the same holds for a downstream system's `unacked_task_count`, a patient record's `subsumed_member_count` and a merge operation's `propagation_task_count`. Where the same request mutates that row for some other reason — a survivor record whose `clinical_element_count` moves, a site amended by an administrator — the row's `version` still advances by 1 for that other mutation, exactly as the paragraph above requires.

### 2.11. Duty Separation Between Recommendation and Execution

The organisation's control over identity change is a two-person rule. Whichever operator identifier is recorded in a pair's `recommended_by_user_id` may not be the `X-User-ID` that executes the resolution of that pair — neither by approving a merge nor by resolving the pair as distinct. The comparison is a plain string comparison of operator identifiers, and it is skipped entirely when `recommended_by_user_id` is null. That null case is not a loophole but a deliberate exemption: a system-generated recommendation has no human author to be separated from, and a recommendation that an approver has already sent back for more evidence has had its author cleared. Where the rule does bite, the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Index: Sites, Rule Sets and Downstream Consumers

Before any identity work can happen, an `MpiAdmin` populates three pieces of configuration. Registering a front-end site records a `site_code` — a short, human-readable identifier of between 1 and 12 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, and unique across the whole enterprise — together with a display `name`, a `region_code` used to group sites for reporting, and a `registrar_headcount` giving the number of front-desk registrars staffed there, which must be zero or greater. A violation of any of those shapes is a malformed request. The new site opens with `status` set to `ACTIVE` and both derived counters, `active_record_count` and `flagged_record_count`, at 0. The `site_code` matters far beyond display: it is embedded verbatim into every enterprise identity key minted at that site, so it is effectively permanent.

An administrator may later amend a site by supplying any of `name`, `registrar_headcount` or `status`. Moving `status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` requires that the site currently be `ACTIVE`, refreshes the site's `updated_at` and advances its `version` by 1; the reverse move from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE` requires that the site currently be `SUSPENDED` and has the same metadata effect. Suspension is not retirement: the site's existing patient records are untouched and remain fully `ACTIVE`. What suspension does is close the front desk — while a site is `SUSPENDED`, neither a new registration search attempt at that site nor the creation of a new patient record naming that site is accepted, and both are rejected with `ERR_SITE_SUSPENDED`.

**Match rule sets** are the tunable half of duplicate detection. Defining one requires a `name`, an author-assigned `version_label` such as `v3.2`, an `attribute_weights` map, the two thresholds, and a penalty. The `attribute_weights` map must carry exactly seven keys — `last_name`, `first_name`, `dob`, `ssn_last4`, `sex`, `postal_code` and `phone` — with every value a number of 0.00 or more, and the seven values must sum to exactly 100.00; a sum that misses is rejected with `ERR_WEIGHTS_SUM_INVALID`. The `auto_link_threshold` is the score at or above which a pair is considered a confirmed match, and `review_threshold` is the lower score at or above which a pair is worth a human's attention; both are carried to two decimal places within the inclusive range 0.00 to 100.00, and `review_threshold` must not exceed `auto_link_threshold`, on pain of `ERR_THRESHOLD_ORDER_INVALID`. Finally `conflict_penalty_points`, also a two-decimal value in the inclusive range 0.00 to 100.00, is the number of points deducted for each demographic contradiction the scorer discovers. A newly defined rule set is minted with `status` of `DRAFT`.

Promoting a rule set requires that its `status` be `DRAFT`, and it moves that rule set `DRAFT → ACTIVE` while advancing its `version` by 1. In the same request, whichever rule set previously held `status` of `ACTIVE` — at most one ever does — is transitioned `ACTIVE → RETIRED` and its own `version` advances by 1 as well. `RETIRED` is terminal: a retired rule set is never promoted again. From that point onward, any scoring operation that does not name a rule set explicitly resolves to the newly promoted one. What activation deliberately does **not** do is re-score history: candidate pairs already on the queue keep the rule set they were scored under and are recomputed only when someone explicitly asks for a re-score. That is why a pair's score remains defensible years later, and why a scoring request is permitted to name a rule set in any status at all — `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `RETIRED`.

The third configuration object is the **downstream consumer** — an EHR, a lab system, a billing engine, an exchange gateway. Registering one records a `system_code` of between 1 and 16 characters, unique across the enterprise, a display `name`, a `propagation_mode` of either `SYNCHRONOUS` or `BATCH`, an `ack_required` boolean, and a `max_concurrent_dispatch` ceiling of at least 1. The system opens with `status` of `ACTIVE` and `unacked_task_count` at 0. `propagation_mode` selects how long the service has to deliver an identity change to that consumer: a `SYNCHRONOUS` consumer must be reached within 24 hours of a task being raised, a `BATCH` consumer within 72 hours. `ack_required` decides whether the consumer is a participant or a bystander in merge completion, and `max_concurrent_dispatch` caps how many overlapping dispatches the consumer will tolerate. An administrator may later amend `name`, `status` or `max_concurrent_dispatch`; each such amendment advances the system's `version` by 1. Lowering `max_concurrent_dispatch` is forward-looking only — it never retroactively invalidates a dispatch already in flight, it merely tightens the next occupancy test. Setting `status` to `PAUSED` has two effects: the paused consumer receives no tasks from any later merge, reversal or overlay correction, and no existing task of its own can be dispatched.

### 3.2. The Registration Desk: Duplicate-Prevention Search and Record Creation

Consider the moment a patient presents at a front desk. The registrar's first action is not to create anything — it is to search the index for the person already being there. Recording a registration search captures whatever the registrar typed: `searched_last_name` is required, while `searched_first_name`, `searched_dob`, `searched_sex`, `searched_ssn_last4`, `searched_postal_code` and `searched_phone` are each optional and are treated as absent when omitted. `searched_dob` is typed as `YYYY-MM-DD` and `searched_sex` must be one of `M`, `F`, `X` or `U`. The site under which the search is recorded is taken from the path, not the body, and is stored on the attempt as `site_id`. Both `SiteRegistrar` and `IdentityAnalyst` operators may record a search; for a `SiteRegistrar`, §2.1's scoping rule confines them to their own site.

The typed terms form a **virtual record**, and the service scores that virtual record against every candidate in the index. The candidate pool is enterprise-wide, not site-scoped — duplicate prevention that only looked at the local desk would miss precisely the cross-site duplicates that matter most — and it comprises every patient record whose `identity_state` is `ACTIVE`. Records already absorbed into a cluster are excluded. Every comparison uses the strict null handling described in §3.3 and the rule set whose `status` is currently `ACTIVE`. Four values are then written onto the attempt and returned to the registrar. `result_count` is the number of candidates scoring at or above the active rule set's `review_threshold`. `top_score` is the highest score achieved over the whole pool, or 0.00 when the pool is empty. `top_match_record_mrn` is the `mrn` of the single best candidate, ranked by score descending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `mrn` ascending, and is null exactly when `top_score` is 0.00. `blocked_creation` is true when `top_score` is at or above the active rule set's `auto_link_threshold`. Finally `rule_set_version_label` copies the `version_label` of the rule set that was `ACTIVE` at the moment the search ran — an audit snapshot of the configuration, held as a plain string rather than a reference, so that a later activation cannot rewrite the past. A search attempt is a write-once event record and is never amended.

Creating a patient record supplies the identifying core — `site_id`, `mrn`, `last_name`, `first_name`, `dob` and `sex` — plus a set of optional attributes: `ssn_last4`, `address_line`, `postal_code`, `phone`, `biometric_template_hash`, `insurance_member_id`, `clinical_element_count` and `origin_search_attempt_id`. `mrn` is the site's own medical record number, between 1 and 20 characters, and must be unique within its site: a second record with the same `site_id` and `mrn` combination is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN`. `dob` is an ISO date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and `sex` is one of `M`, `F`, `X` or `U`. `ssn_last4`, when supplied, is exactly four digits; when not collected it is null. `address_line` is captured for correspondence and is never scored. `biometric_template_hash` is an opaque hash of an enrolled biometric template and `insurance_member_id` is the payer's member identifier; both are the raw material for the identity-fraud signals of §3.9. `clinical_element_count` is the number of clinical elements — encounters, results, documents — filed under the record, and defaults to 0 when omitted; it may not be negative, and it is the quantity that every merge, reversal and reassignment in this service conserves.

The **search-attempt gate** is where duplicate-creation prevention becomes an enforced rule rather than a suggestion, and it applies asymmetrically by role. When the caller is a `SiteRegistrar`, `origin_search_attempt_id` is mandatory; omitting it is rejected with `ERR_SEARCH_ATTEMPT_REQUIRED`. When the caller is an `IdentityAnalyst` — the role that also performs bulk and historical loads — the field is optional, and an analyst-created record may legitimately have no originating search at all. Whenever the field **is** supplied, two conditions attach to it regardless of who supplied it. First, freshness: the cited attempt must be no older than 15 minutes, measured as the current logical clock being no later than the attempt's `created_at` plus that interval, because a search result the registrar looked at half an hour ago no longer describes the index. A stale citation is rejected with `ERR_SEARCH_ATTEMPT_EXPIRED`. Second, permission: the cited attempt's `blocked_creation` must be false. If the registrar's own search already found an existing record at or above the auto-link threshold, then creating a second chart for that person is exactly the defect this system exists to prevent, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREATION_BLOCKED`.

A record that survives all of that opens in a well-defined condition. Its `identity_state` is `ACTIVE`, its `integrity_state` derives to `CLEAR`, its `survivor_record_id` is null, and its `subsumed_member_count` is 0. Its `enterprise_id` — the key by which the whole enterprise refers to this person — is composed as the literal text `EID-`, followed by the registering site's `site_code`, followed by a hyphen, followed by the record's `mrn`. The registering site's `active_record_count` increases by 1. Records can be read individually and listed with filters on `site_id`, `survivor_record_id`, `origin_search_attempt_id`, `identity_state`, `integrity_state`, `enterprise_id` and `mrn`; the `survivor_record_id` filter is what lets an operator walk a merge cluster from its surviving member outward.

### 3.3. Detecting Duplicates: Pair Scoring, Conflict Flags and External Matching

Duplicate detection reduces to one reusable comparison: given two sets of demographics and a rule set, how much do they agree? That single comparison is what a candidate pair is scored with, what a registration search ranks its candidates with, and what an exchange partner's returned demographics are judged with. Exactly seven attributes participate, always evaluated in this fixed order: `last_name`, `first_name`, `dob`, `ssn_last4`, `sex`, `postal_code`, `phone`. `address_line` is never scored. String comparisons trim leading and trailing whitespace and then ignore letter case. Neither `identity_state` nor `integrity_state` restricts what the comparison may read — a record that has already been absorbed into a cluster can still be scored — the only place a pool restriction applies is the enterprise-wide registration search of §3.2, which considers `ACTIVE` records only.

**Step one is per-attribute agreement.** For an attribute where either side's value is null or absent, agreement is undefined and the null-handling variant below decides what happens. Where both sides have a value: `last_name` and `first_name` each score full agreement of 1 when the two values are equal, half agreement of 0.5 when their first three characters are equal but the full values differ, and no agreement of 0 otherwise. `postal_code` follows the same three-way rule — equal gives 1, an equal first three characters with differing full values gives 0.5, anything else gives 0. `ssn_last4`, `sex` and `phone` are all-or-nothing: 1 when equal, 0 otherwise, with no partial credit at all. `dob` has three ways to earn credit: full agreement of 1 when the two `YYYY-MM-DD` values are equal; half agreement of 0.5 when the year and month parts are equal but the day parts differ; and also half agreement of 0.5 when the two dates are a day-and-month transposition of one another, meaning the years are equal, the first date's month equals the second date's day, the first date's day equals the second date's month, and the first date's month and day are not themselves equal. Otherwise `dob` scores 0.

**Step two turns agreement into points, and the rounding order here is part of the contract.** Walking the seven attributes in their fixed order, each attribute's weight is read from the rule set's `attribute_weights` map under that attribute's key. Where agreement is defined, the attribute's contribution is the weight multiplied by the agreement value, rounded half up to two decimal places. Where agreement is undefined because a value was missing, the contribution depends on the pair's `score_variant`: under `STRICT` the contribution is exactly 0.00, and under `TOLERANT` the contribution is the weight multiplied by one quarter, rounded half up to two decimal places. The seven resulting values, keyed by attribute name, are published as `attribute_score_breakdown`. `raw_score` is then the sum of those **already-rounded** seven contributions, itself rounded half up to two decimal places. Rounding each contribution first and summing afterwards is the specified order; summing the exact products and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect computation.

The two variants exist because sparse data means two opposite things in two different contexts. `STRICT` — the default, and the only variant used by the registration search — never redistributes the weight of a missing attribute, so the denominator stays 100.00 and a thinly populated record scores low. That is the correct bias at a registration desk, where a low score means "let the registrar create the chart". `TOLERANT` gives a missing attribute a quarter of its weight, on the theory that absence is not disagreement, and is always used for external exchange matching where partner feeds are sparse by construction. The same two records can therefore land above `review_threshold` under `TOLERANT` while classifying as `NON_DUPLICATE` under `STRICT`, and that divergence is intended rather than a defect.

**Step three collects contradictions.** Three demographic contradictions are recognised, and `conflict_flags` lists whichever of them apply, always emitted in this fixed order: `SSN4_CONFLICT` when both sides have a non-null `ssn_last4` and the two values differ; `BIOMETRIC_CONFLICT` when both sides have a non-null `biometric_template_hash` and the two hashes differ; and `INSURANCE_CONFLICT` when both sides have a non-null `insurance_member_id` and the two identifiers differ. A contradiction requires both sides to be populated — one side being null is a gap, not a conflict.

**Step four applies the penalty and classifies.** `match_score` is `raw_score` reduced by the rule set's `conflict_penalty_points` multiplied by the number of entries in `conflict_flags`, floored at 0.00 so the score never goes negative, and rounded half up to two decimal places. `classification` is then `AUTO_LINK` when `match_score` is at or above the rule set's `auto_link_threshold`; otherwise `POTENTIAL_DUPLICATE` when `match_score` is at or above `review_threshold`; otherwise `NON_DUPLICATE`.

Placing a pair on the queue is available to an `IdentityAnalyst` and to an `MpiAdmin`. The request names `record_a_id` and `record_b_id` as the two unordered members, `rule_set_id` as the rule set to score under, and `score_variant`, which is `STRICT` unless `TOLERANT` is asked for. A record cannot be paired with itself: identical member identifiers are rejected with `ERR_MERGE_SELF_REFERENCE`. Neither can the same two records be queued twice while an earlier pair over them is still live — if any existing pair over the same unordered member set sits in a `queue_state` other than `RESOLVED_DISTINCT` or `RESOLVED_MERGED`, the request is rejected with `ERR_PAIR_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Once the pair is scored, one of its two members is designated the **subject** — the member with the later `created_at`, and on identical timestamps the member whose `mrn` is lexicographically greater. The subject's `mrn` is published as `subject_record_mrn` and the subject's own site is published as `detected_at_site_id`, because the site that created the redundant chart is the site the duplicate is charged to. The pair also computes its triage chain — `evidence_points`, which starts at 0, plus `base_band`, `evidence_adjustment`, `effective_score` and `priority_band` per §3.4.

The pair normally opens in `queue_state` of `NEW`, but two create-time branches override that, and the second outranks the first. If `conflict_flags` contains `BIOMETRIC_CONFLICT` or `INSURANCE_CONFLICT` **and** `raw_score` is at or above 70.00, the pair does not enter the queue at all: it opens directly in `ESCALATED_FRAUD` and the fraud machinery of §3.9 engages. Note that this test reads `raw_score`, before the conflict penalty, not `match_score`. Otherwise, if `classification` is `AUTO_LINK` and `conflict_flags` is empty, the pair opens in `RECOMMENDED_MERGE` — a system recommendation, so `recommendation` is set to `MERGE` and `recommended_by_user_id` is deliberately left null, which is precisely why §2.11's duty-separation check can never fail on a machine-recommended pair. A pair carrying any conflict flag is never auto-recommended, even at a perfect score, because `SSN4_CONFLICT` alone leaves the pair in `NEW` rather than escalating it, and the two conflict-bearing paths together exhaust every conflicted case.

Re-scoring an existing pair, available to an `IdentityAnalyst` or an `MpiAdmin`, submits a `rule_set_id`, a `score_variant` and the `expected_pair_version` of §2.6. Both scoring inputs are overwritten with the submitted values, and the entire chain — `raw_score`, `conflict_flags`, `match_score`, `classification`, `attribute_score_breakdown`, then `base_band`, `evidence_adjustment`, `effective_score` and `priority_band` — is recomputed. Existing review evidence is preserved, so `evidence_points` is recomputed from the evidence already on file rather than reset. Re-scoring is not a queue transition: `queue_state` is left exactly as it was, and the operation is permitted only from `NEW`, `TRIAGED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`. Asking to re-score a pair that has already been recommended, resolved or escalated is rejected with `ERR_PAIR_INVALID_STATE`. The pair's `version` advances by 1, which is what makes a second re-score based on the same stale read fail.

The same scorer serves **external identity matching for exchange participation**, recorded as an `ExternalMatchRequest`, which an `IdentityAnalyst` initiates. The request names the local `record_id`, the partner's `exchange_partner_code`, a `consent_on_file` boolean, and `external_demographics`: a partner-supplied map read with the same seven keys the scorer uses — `last_name`, `first_name`, `dob`, `sex`, `ssn_last4`, `postal_code` and `phone` — where any key the partner omitted counts as null. Three conditions must hold: the local record's `identity_state` is `ACTIVE`, its `integrity_state` is `CLEAR`, and `consent_on_file` is true. A missing authorisation is rejected with `ERR_EXCHANGE_CONSENT_MISSING`; a record whose `integrity_state` is `REMEDIATION_PENDING` is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_REMEDIATION_PENDING`, and one under a fraud hold with `ERR_RECORD_UNDER_FRAUD_HOLD`. The request then computes `external_score` — the local record against `external_demographics`, always under the `TOLERANT` variant, always against the rule set whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, carried to two decimal places. The row is minted with `state` of `SUBMITTED` and immediately takes exactly one of two outgoing branches inside the same request: `SUBMITTED → MATCHED` when `external_score` is at or above the active rule set's `auto_link_threshold`, and `SUBMITTED → NO_MATCH` when it is below. Both branches store `external_score` and advance the request's `version` by 1, and both destinations are terminal — there is no follow-up operation on an exchange match request, only retrieval, individually or filtered by `record_id`, `state` and `exchange_partner_code`.

### 3.4. The Triage Work Queue: Evidence, Bands and the Effective Score

A scored pair is a work item, and `queue_state` tracks where that work item stands. It takes one of eight values: `NEW`, `TRIAGED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RECOMMENDED_MERGE`, `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT`, `RESOLVED_MERGED`, `RESOLVED_DISTINCT` and `ESCALATED_FRAUD`. Only `RESOLVED_DISTINCT` is genuinely terminal; the queue is otherwise cyclic, and three back-edges make it so — a rejected recommendation returns to review, a reversed merge returns to review, and an unsubstantiated fraud escalation returns to review.

Ordering the queue is the point of the **band machinery**, and its evaluation order is pinned in four steps because the dependency chain looks circular at first glance: a band chooses a multiplier, the multiplier scales an adjustment, the adjustment shifts the score, and the score chooses a band. The resolution is that the multiplier always comes from the *pre-evidence* band and never from the final one. Step one derives `base_band` from `match_score` alone: `P1` when `match_score` is at or above 85.00, `P2` when it is at or above 70.00, and `P3` otherwise. Step two fixes the multiplier from that pre-evidence band — one and a half for `P1`, exactly one for `P2`, and one half for `P3` — and computes `evidence_points` as a signed sum over every review evidence entry attached to the pair, read in `created_at` ascending order with ties broken by `evidence_type` ascending and then `outcome` ascending: an entry whose `outcome` is `SUPPORTS_SAME_PERSON` adds its `weight_points`, an entry whose `outcome` is `SUPPORTS_DISTINCT` subtracts its `weight_points`, and an entry whose `outcome` is `INCONCLUSIVE` contributes 0.00. `evidence_adjustment` is then `evidence_points` multiplied by that pinned multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. Step three computes `effective_score` as `match_score` plus `evidence_adjustment`, clamped so that it is never below 0.00 and never above 100.00, rounded half up to two decimal places. Step four derives `priority_band` from `effective_score` using the identical cutoffs: `P1` at or above 85.00, `P2` at or above 70.00, `P3` otherwise. A pair whose `base_band` is `P1` therefore has its evidence counted at one and a half times face value even if the evidence drags `effective_score` down into `P2` — the leverage belongs to the pre-evidence band.

An `IdentityAnalyst` starts the human work by triaging a pair, which requires `queue_state` of `NEW`, supplies a `triage_note` capturing the analyst's first read, and moves the pair `NEW → TRIAGED`. Triage also recomputes the whole band chain — `evidence_points`, `base_band`, `evidence_adjustment`, `effective_score` and `priority_band` — and advances the pair's `version` by 1. Opening manual review takes no input at all and moves the pair `TRIAGED → UNDER_REVIEW`, again advancing `version` by 1. Both operations, along with attaching evidence and recording a recommendation, additionally require that **neither** member record is under a fraud hold: if either member's `integrity_state` is `FRAUD_SUSPECTED` or `FRAUD_CONFIRMED`, the operation is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_UNDER_FRAUD_HOLD`. A pair addressed in the wrong `queue_state` for the operation requested is rejected with `ERR_PAIR_INVALID_STATE`.

Evidence is what turns an analyst's opinion into a reviewable record. Each entry attaches to the pair named in the path — stored on the entry as `pair_id` — and carries an `evidence_type` drawn from `PHOTO_ID`, `INSURANCE_CARD`, `PHONE_VERIFICATION`, `CLINICAL_CHART_COMPARE` and `BIOMETRIC_COMPARE`; an `outcome` drawn from `SUPPORTS_SAME_PERSON`, `SUPPORTS_DISTINCT` and `INCONCLUSIVE`; a `weight_points` magnitude carried to two decimal places within the inclusive range 0.00 to 10.00; and an optional free-text `note`. The submitting analyst's operator identifier is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`. One subtlety deserves emphasis: `weight_points` is stored exactly as supplied and is always a non-negative magnitude — its **sign is not stored**. Direction lives entirely in `outcome`, and the sign is applied only when the band machinery sums the evidence. Attaching an entry is a write once and forget: the entry itself is immutable, carrying only `id` and `created_at` as metadata, while the parent pair's `evidence_points`, `evidence_adjustment`, `effective_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed and the parent pair's `version` advances by 1. A pair's evidence can be listed in its own right.

Recording a verdict requires `queue_state` of `UNDER_REVIEW`, at least **two** review evidence entries on the pair, and both members clear of fraud holds. A pair with fewer than two entries is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REVIEW_EVIDENCE` — a single photograph is not a defensible basis for collapsing two clinical histories into one. The submitted `recommendation` is either `MERGE`, which moves the pair `UNDER_REVIEW → RECOMMENDED_MERGE`, or `DISTINCT`, which moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT`. Both branches behave identically in every other respect: the analyst's operator identifier is written to `recommended_by_user_id`, `recommendation` records the submitted verdict, and the pair's `version` advances by 1. That recorded identifier is the anchor of §2.11's duty separation.

An `IdentityStewardApprover` who is not satisfied with a recommendation sends it back rather than overriding it. Returning a pair to review is permitted from either `RECOMMENDED_MERGE` or `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT`, accepts an optional `triage_note`, and lands the pair in `UNDER_REVIEW` in both cases — `RECOMMENDED_MERGE → UNDER_REVIEW` and `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT → UNDER_REVIEW` have exactly the same effect set. Both clear `recommendation` and `recommended_by_user_id` back to null, which forces a fresh analyst verdict and simultaneously releases the duty-separation lock, so the same analyst who recommended before may recommend again, and the returning approver is no longer barred from executing the eventual resolution. Both advance the pair's `version` by 1.

The approver's other option is to close the pair without a merge. Resolving a pair as distinct requires `queue_state` of `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT`, accepts an optional `resolution_note`, and requires that the approver's `X-User-ID` differ from the pair's `recommended_by_user_id`. It moves the pair `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT → RESOLVED_DISTINCT`, records the approver as `approved_by_user_id`, and advances `version` by 1. Neither member record's `identity_state` changes — the whole point of the verdict is that these are two people. What does change is future eligibility: because both `RESOLVED_DISTINCT` and `RESOLVED_MERGED` are excluded from the live-pair test of §3.3, closing a pair this way permits a new pair to be raised over the same two records later, should new information arrive. The queue itself can be listed and filtered on `record_a_id`, `record_b_id`, `rule_set_id`, `detected_at_site_id`, `queue_state`, `classification` and `priority_band`.

### 3.5. Approval, Merge and Cluster Compression

Executing a merge is the most consequential write in the service and belongs exclusively to the `IdentityStewardApprover`. The request names the `pair_id` whose recommendation authorises it, the `survivor_record_id` that will retain the identity, the `subsumed_record_id` that will be absorbed, and a written `rationale` retained permanently for merge audit. Six preconditions guard it, and §2.8 fixes the order in which they are reported. The pair must be in `queue_state` of `RECOMMENDED_MERGE`. Neither member record may be under a fraud hold, and the target records must not be in `REMEDIATION_PENDING`. Both records must have `identity_state` of `ACTIVE`; a record that is already `MERGED_SUBSUMED` cannot be merged again and is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_NOT_ACTIVE`. The approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the pair's `recommended_by_user_id`, unless that field is null. The survivor and subsumed identifiers must differ from each other, and together they must form exactly the same unordered set as the pair's `record_a_id` and `record_b_id` — naming a third record, or naming only one of the pair's members, is rejected with `ERR_PAIR_RECORD_MISMATCH`. Which of the pair's two members is chosen as survivor is entirely the approver's judgement; nothing in the pair prescribes it.

The merge then executes a precise, reversible transfer. Before anything is mutated, two snapshots are taken, and they are the only reason a reversal can ever be exact: `moved_clinical_element_count` records the subsumed record's `clinical_element_count` as it stood before the move, and `subsumed_prior_enterprise_id` records the subsumed record's `enterprise_id` as it stood before the overwrite. Then the subsumed record moves `ACTIVE → MERGED_SUBSUMED`; its `survivor_record_id` is pointed at the survivor; the snapshotted element count is added to the survivor's `clinical_element_count` and the subsumed record's own `clinical_element_count` is set to 0; and the subsumed record's `enterprise_id` is overwritten with the survivor's `enterprise_id`.

Because merge clusters are stars rather than chains, the merge also performs **path compression**. Every patient record that was pointing at the subsumed record as its own survivor, and whose `identity_state` is `MERGED_SUBSUMED`, is re-pointed at the new survivor and has its `enterprise_id` set to the survivor's `enterprise_id`. The `mrn` values of exactly those re-pointed records are captured in `recompressed_record_mrns`, ordered by their `created_at` ascending and then by `mrn` ascending — an ordered list, because a reversal re-points precisely those records and no others. Every row this touches advances its own `version` by 1: the subsumed record, the survivor, and each re-pointed record. The survivor's `subsumed_member_count` is then recomputed as the number of records pointing at it whose `identity_state` is `MERGED_SUBSUMED`, and both member sites' `active_record_count` values are recomputed, since the subsumed record has left the active population of its site. Meanwhile the authorising pair moves `RECOMMENDED_MERGE → RESOLVED_MERGED`, records the executing approver as its `approved_by_user_id`, and advances its own `version` by 1.

Two further consequences fire inside the same request. First, **downstream fan-out**: one propagation task is raised per downstream system whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, created in `system_code` ascending order, each carrying an `operation_kind` of `MERGE` and a `merge_operation_id` pointing back at this merge. A task for a system whose `ack_required` is true is minted in `state` of `QUEUED`; a task for a system whose `ack_required` is false is minted directly in `ACKED` and never occupies dispatch capacity at all. Systems whose `status` is `PAUSED` receive nothing. `propagation_task_count` records how many tasks were raised. Second, **contamination review**: when `moved_clinical_element_count` is greater than 25, the volume of clinical material that changed hands is large enough that some of it may belong to the wrong person, so a contamination remediation is opened against the survivor with `source_type` of `MERGE`, `state` of `OPEN`, its `record_id` set to the survivor, its `merge_operation_id` set to this merge, its `overlay_correction_id` left null, and its `element_count_subject` set to `moved_clinical_element_count`. The survivor's `integrity_state` consequently re-derives to `REMEDIATION_PENDING`. A merge of 25 elements or fewer opens no remediation.

The merge's own `state` resolves within this same request rather than later. It becomes `COMPLETED` immediately when `propagation_task_count` is 0 — no active downstream system exists — or when none of the tasks it created belongs to a system requiring acknowledgement, since in both situations there is nothing left to wait for. Otherwise it stays `PENDING_PROPAGATION`. Whenever the merge reaches `COMPLETED`, whether in this request or later, `completed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, `reversal_deadline_at` is computed as `completed_at` plus 90 days, and the merge's `version` advances by 1. While a merge remains `PENDING_PROPAGATION`, both `completed_at` and `reversal_deadline_at` are null. Merges are read back individually for historical audit — element counts, recompression list, reversal deadline and all — and listed with filters on `pair_id`, `survivor_record_id`, `subsumed_record_id` and `state`.

### 3.6. Downstream Propagation and Merge Completion

A propagation task is the service's promise to a downstream consumer that an identity change will reach it. Each task records which change it carries through `operation_kind` — `MERGE`, `UNMERGE` or `OVERLAY_CORRECTION` — together with exactly one upstream reference: `merge_operation_id` is populated and `overlay_correction_id` is null when the kind is `MERGE` or `UNMERGE`, and `overlay_correction_id` is populated with `merge_operation_id` null when the kind is `OVERLAY_CORRECTION`. `downstream_system_id` names the consumer to be notified. `state` moves through `QUEUED`, `SENT`, `ACKED`, `FAILED` and `CANCELLED`, with `ACKED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. `attempt_count` starts at 0 and increases by 1 on every entry into `SENT`. `propagation_deadline_at` is fixed at creation from the consumer's `propagation_mode`: the task's `created_at` plus 24 hours for a `SYNCHRONOUS` consumer, or the task's `created_at` plus 72 hours for a `BATCH` consumer.

Dispatching a task is open to an `MpiAdmin` and to an `IdentityAnalyst`, and requires the caller to claim an explicit half-open window by supplying `dispatch_window_start_at` and `dispatch_window_end_at`, the latter strictly later than the former. Four conditions are checked beyond the window's own shape. The task's `state` must be `QUEUED` or `FAILED`. The consumer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the dispatch is rejected with `ERR_DOWNSTREAM_SYSTEM_PAUSED`. The current logical clock must be no later than the task's `propagation_deadline_at`, or the dispatch is rejected with `ERR_PROPAGATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — a missed deadline is permanent, since nothing ever moves a deadline forward. And the consumer's dispatch capacity must not be exhausted: counting every **other** propagation task on the same consumer whose `state` is `SENT`, and treating each stored window as covering its start instant but not its end instant, the number of those windows that overlap the requested window must be strictly less than the consumer's `max_concurrent_dispatch`. Two windows overlap when the first starts before the second ends and the second starts before the first ends; touching endpoints therefore do not overlap, and back-to-back dispatches are always admissible. Tasks in `QUEUED`, `ACKED`, `FAILED` or `CANCELLED` hold no capacity whatsoever. When occupancy has already reached the ceiling, the dispatch is rejected with `ERR_DISPATCH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; capacity is an aggregate allowance, so a single overlap is perfectly legal as long as the count stays below the ceiling. A successful dispatch stores the window, moves the task `QUEUED → SENT` or `FAILED → SENT`, increases `attempt_count` by 1, advances the task's `version` by 1, and refreshes the consumer's `unacked_task_count`.

An `MpiAdmin` records the consumer's response. Acknowledgement requires `state` of `SENT`, moves the task `SENT → ACKED`, advances its `version` by 1, refreshes `unacked_task_count`, and then **bubbles up**: if the parent merge is still `PENDING_PROPAGATION` and every one of its tasks that belongs to an acknowledgement-requiring consumer is now `ACKED`, with cancelled tasks disregarded, the merge transitions `PENDING_PROPAGATION → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock, sets `reversal_deadline_at` to 90 days later, and advances the merge's `version` by 1. Stated as a condition on the merge, the completion test is that no task belonging to the merge remains in `QUEUED`, `SENT` or `FAILED`. A rejection is recorded instead by reporting a failure, which also requires `state` of `SENT`, supplies the consumer-reported `last_error_code`, moves the task `SENT → FAILED`, advances its `version` by 1, clears the stored dispatch window fields so the capacity slot is released for other tasks, and refreshes `unacked_task_count`. The parent merge stays `PENDING_PROPAGATION`. A failed task may be dispatched again — `SENT → FAILED → SENT` is a supported retry cycle, bounded only by the propagation deadline. Acknowledging or failing a task that is not `SENT`, or dispatching one that is neither `QUEUED` nor `FAILED`, is rejected with `ERR_TASK_INVALID_STATE`. Tasks are read individually and listed with filters on `merge_operation_id`, `overlay_correction_id`, `downstream_system_id`, `state` and `operation_kind`.

### 3.7. Reversal and the Conservation Guarantees

Merges get made in error, and the ability to undo one exactly is what makes stewards willing to make them at all. Reversal is shared between the `IdentityStewardApprover` and the `MpiAdmin`, and takes a single input: an `unmerge_reason` recorded permanently against the merge. Three preconditions apply. The merge's `state` must be `PENDING_PROPAGATION` or `COMPLETED`; `UNMERGED` is terminal, so a second reversal of the same merge is rejected with `ERR_MERGE_INVALID_STATE`. No propagation task belonging to the merge may be in `SENT` — a change already handed to a consumer cannot be silently retracted, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PROPAGATION_IN_FLIGHT`. And when the merge is `COMPLETED`, the current logical clock must be no later than its `reversal_deadline_at`; past that instant the merge is permanent and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REVERSAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. A merge still `PENDING_PROPAGATION` has no deadline to breach, since `reversal_deadline_at` is null until completion — the reversal window opens only once the change has landed everywhere.

The reversal is an exact mirror of §3.5, driven entirely off the snapshots taken there. The merge moves to `UNMERGED`, stamps `unmerged_at` from the logical clock, stores `unmerge_reason`, and advances its `version` by 1. The subsumed record moves `MERGED_SUBSUMED → ACTIVE`, has its `survivor_record_id` cleared to null, and has its `enterprise_id` restored from `subsumed_prior_enterprise_id` — byte for byte the key it carried before, not a recomputed one. Exactly `moved_clinical_element_count` elements move back: that quantity is subtracted from the survivor's `clinical_element_count` and added to the subsumed record's, restoring the snapshot value. Every record whose `mrn` appears in `recompressed_record_mrns` is re-pointed from the survivor back to the subsumed record, in the stored order, with `enterprise_id` set to the subsumed record's restored key. The subsumed record, the survivor and every re-pointed record each advance their own `version` by 1, and both sites' `active_record_count` values along with the affected `subsumed_member_count` values are recomputed.

Three cascades ride along. The authorising pair moves `RESOLVED_MERGED → UNDER_REVIEW`, with `recommended_by_user_id`, `recommendation` and `approved_by_user_id` all cleared to null and the pair's `version` advanced by 1 — the work item is genuinely re-opened, awaiting a fresh analyst verdict. Any contamination remediation that this merge opened and that is still in `OPEN`, `QUARANTINED` or `REASSIGNED` is closed: it moves to `CLOSED`, its `closure_note` is written by the system as the exact text `CLOSED_BY_UNMERGE`, its `version` advances by 1, and the survivor's `integrity_state` is re-derived, which returns it to `CLEAR` when nothing else holds it. And propagation is re-driven: every task belonging to this merge whose `state` is `QUEUED` or `FAILED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `version` advanced by 1 and the consumer's `unacked_task_count` reduced accordingly, while one fresh task with `operation_kind` of `UNMERGE` is raised per downstream system whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, following exactly the same acknowledgement rule as merge fan-out — `QUEUED` where the consumer requires acknowledgement, directly `ACKED` where it does not.

Two invariants make the round trip auditable, and both are stated over a merge followed by its reversal with no element reassignment in between. The first is **clinical element conservation**: summing `clinical_element_count` across the survivor, the subsumed record and every record in the merge's path-compression set yields the same integer immediately before the merge as it does immediately after the reversal. The merge only shifts the quantity between rows and the reversal shifts exactly `moved_clinical_element_count` of it back, so the total never drifts. Should a contamination reassignment occur between the two, elements deliberately leave the cluster and the invariant is not claimed for that span. The second is **enterprise identity conservation**: counting the distinct `enterprise_id` values across all patient records whose `identity_state` is `ACTIVE`, enterprise-wide, gives the same integer immediately before the merge as immediately after the reversal, and the restored record's `enterprise_id` equals `subsumed_prior_enterprise_id` exactly. A merge lowers that distinct count by precisely 1 as the subsumed record leaves the active population, and a reversal raises it by precisely 1 while restoring the original key — so no enterprise identity is ever lost, and none is ever invented.

### 3.8. Overlay Correction and Clinical Cross-Contamination Remediation

An **overlay** is a different defect from a duplicate and needs a different remedy. In an overlay, one patient's demographics have been typed over another patient's existing chart, so there is only one record involved and merging is exactly the wrong move; what is needed is a field-level restoration plus the extraction of whatever clinical material was filed under the wrong identity while the overlay was in force. Filing a correction is shared between the `IdentityAnalyst` and the `IdentityStewardApprover`. The request names the `record_id` of the damaged record, an `overlay_type` of either `DEMOGRAPHIC_OVERWRITE` or `CLINICAL_MISFILE`, a `corrected_field_snapshot` map of the corrected values, an `affected_clinical_element_count` of zero or more giving how many clinical elements were filed under the record during the overlay period, the `encounter_from_date` and `encounter_to_date` bounding that period as `YYYY-MM-DD` values with the later date no earlier than the first, and the `expected_record_version` of §2.6. The `corrected_field_snapshot` may carry only demographic keys: `last_name`, `first_name`, `dob`, `sex`, `ssn_last4`, `address_line`, `postal_code`, `phone`, `biometric_template_hash` and `insurance_member_id`. Any other key makes the request malformed. Notably `mrn` and `site_id` are **not** correctable this way, which is exactly why an overlay correction never disturbs a record's `enterprise_id`.

Filing is deliberately restrained about what it changes. The correction opens with `state` of `DETECTED` and records the filer's operator identifier as `created_by_user_id`. `prior_field_snapshot` captures the record's current values for precisely the keys present in `corrected_field_snapshot` — read before any correction is applied — and is the audit basis for reversal. No demographic field on the patient record is written yet; the only record-level effect of filing is that the new `DETECTED` correction pulls the record's `integrity_state` from `CLEAR` to `REMEDIATION_PENDING`, which advances the record's `version` by 1. The concurrency check on `expected_record_version` is evaluated against the record's `version` as it stood **before** that increment. The target record must have `identity_state` of `ACTIVE` and must not be under a fraud hold.

Applying the correction belongs exclusively to the `IdentityStewardApprover`, takes no input, requires `state` of `DETECTED`, and requires the target record not be under a fraud hold. It moves the correction `DETECTED → UNDER_CORRECTION` and advances its `version` by 1. Every key in `corrected_field_snapshot` is written onto the patient record and the record's `version` advances by 1. One propagation task with `operation_kind` of `OVERLAY_CORRECTION` is raised per downstream system whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, in `system_code` ascending order, under the same acknowledgement rule as merge fan-out. And when `affected_clinical_element_count` is greater than 25, a contamination remediation is opened with `source_type` of `OVERLAY`, `state` of `OPEN`, its `overlay_correction_id` pointing at this correction, its `merge_operation_id` null, its `record_id` set to the overlaid record, and its `element_count_subject` set to `affected_clinical_element_count`. Whether or not a remediation is opened, the record's `integrity_state` stays `REMEDIATION_PENDING` for the whole span from filing through to completion.

Completing a correction is likewise the approver's exclusive act. It requires `state` of `UNDER_CORRECTION` and requires that no contamination remediation belonging to this correction remains in `OPEN`, `QUARANTINED` or `REASSIGNED`. It supplies a `closure_note`, moves the correction `UNDER_CORRECTION → CORRECTED`, which is terminal, advances its `version` by 1, and re-derives the record's `integrity_state` — returning it to `CLEAR` once nothing else holds it. Corrections are read individually with both field snapshots visible, and listed with filters on `record_id`, `state` and `overlay_type`.

**Contamination remediation** is where cross-contaminated clinical material is actually untangled, and it is never created directly by a client: it arises only from the two auto-open branches above, which is what the `source_type` discriminator records. Each remediation carries three element counts. `element_count_subject` is the population under review, fixed at creation from the upstream defect. `element_count_quarantined` starts at 0 and records how many elements were pulled out of the chart for adjudication. `element_count_reassigned` also starts at 0 and records how many of those were refiled onto the correct patient. Quarantining is shared between the `IdentityAnalyst` and the `PrivacyOfficer`, requires `state` of `OPEN`, and submits `element_count_quarantined`, which must not exceed `element_count_subject` — a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_COUNT_INVALID`. It moves the remediation `OPEN → QUARANTINED` and advances its `version` by 1. Quarantining changes no patient record field at all: it records an intention. Elements only physically leave the subject chart on reassignment, so neither the subject record's `clinical_element_count` nor its `version` moves here.

Reassignment is the `PrivacyOfficer`'s exclusive act. It requires `state` of `QUARANTINED` and submits a `target_record_id` naming the patient the elements actually belong to, together with `element_count_reassigned`, which must not exceed `element_count_quarantined` on pain of `ERR_REMEDIATION_COUNT_INVALID`. The target must have `identity_state` of `ACTIVE`, must not be under a fraud hold, and — the crucial constraint — must carry an `enterprise_id` different from the remediated record's `enterprise_id`, because refiling elements onto another member of the same contaminated cluster does not remove the contamination. A target inside the same cluster is rejected with `ERR_REASSIGN_TARGET_INVALID`. On success the remediation moves `QUARANTINED → REASSIGNED`, advances its `version` by 1, and stores `reassigned_to_record_mrn` as an audit copy of the target's `mrn` rather than a live reference. The reassigned quantity is added to the target's `clinical_element_count` and subtracted from the remediated record's, and both records advance their `version` by 1. Whatever was quarantined but not reassigned — the quarantined figure less the reassigned figure — stays quarantined and remains accounted for by the conservation rule of §3.7.

Closing a remediation is also the `PrivacyOfficer`'s exclusive act. It is permitted from either `QUARANTINED` or `REASSIGNED`, supplies a `closure_note`, moves the remediation to `CLOSED`, which is terminal, advances its `version` by 1, and re-derives the subject record's `integrity_state`, returning it from `REMEDIATION_PENDING` to `CLEAR` when nothing else holds it. Notice the asymmetry with §3.7: an operator may close from `QUARANTINED` or `REASSIGNED` but never from `OPEN`, whereas the system's own reversal cascade closes a merge-sourced remediation from `OPEN`, `QUARANTINED` or `REASSIGNED` alike, writing `CLOSED_BY_UNMERGE` as the note. Remediations are read individually and listed with filters on `merge_operation_id`, `overlay_correction_id`, `record_id` and `state`.

### 3.9. Identity Fraud Adjudication and Site Duplicate-Rate Measurement

Not every demographic contradiction is a clerical slip. A record whose biometric template disagrees with the one already enrolled under the same name, or whose payer member identifier belongs to somebody else, may be evidence that a person is deliberately presenting as another — and that possibility must freeze the record rather than be merged away. A `FraudInvestigation` carries the `record_id` of the subject whose identity is in question, an optional `pair_id` naming the candidate pair whose contradictions provoked it, a `trigger_kind` drawn from `BIOMETRIC_CONFLICT`, `INSURANCE_CONFLICT`, `BOTH` and `MANUAL`, an `opened_by` discriminator of either `SYSTEM` or `OFFICER`, an `opened_by_user_id`, a computed `risk_score`, a `state`, an optional opening `note`, a `finding_note` recorded at adjudication, and a `referral_reference` recorded on escalation. Its `state` runs `OPEN`, `SUBSTANTIATED`, `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `REFERRED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, with the last two terminal.

Investigations arise two ways. A `PrivacyOfficer` files one manually, supplying `record_id`, an optional `pair_id`, a `trigger_kind` and an optional `note`; such an investigation opens with `state` of `OPEN`, `opened_by` of `OFFICER`, and `opened_by_user_id` set from the officer's `X-User-ID`. Alternatively the system opens one itself during pair scoring, on the branch described in §3.3: exactly **one** investigation is created, `opened_by` is `SYSTEM`, `opened_by_user_id` is null, `note` is null, `pair_id` is the pair being scored, `state` is `OPEN`, and the subject is the pair's subject record — the member with the later `created_at`, or on identical timestamps the member whose `mrn` is lexicographically greater. The other member is left completely untouched, its own `integrity_state` remaining whatever the derivation of §2.9 yields for it. On that automatic branch `trigger_kind` is derived from the pair's contradictions: `BOTH` when `conflict_flags` contains both `BIOMETRIC_CONFLICT` and `INSURANCE_CONFLICT`, `BIOMETRIC_CONFLICT` when it contains the biometric contradiction but not the insurance one, and `INSURANCE_CONFLICT` when it contains the insurance contradiction but not the biometric one. An `SSN4_CONFLICT` on its own never opens an investigation automatically.

Either way, opening an investigation moves the subject record's `integrity_state` to `FRAUD_SUSPECTED` — from `CLEAR` when nothing else held it, and equally from `REMEDIATION_PENDING`, since fraud outranks remediation in §2.9's precedence and the open remediation simply stops being the visible condition without itself changing. The record's `version` advances by 1 and the subject's site `flagged_record_count` is recomputed. The **order** of those two effects relative to scoring is load-bearing: `flagged_record_count` is refreshed *before* `risk_score` is evaluated, so the subject record being flagged right now is itself included in its site's count.

`risk_score` is the sum of four components, clamped to a maximum of 100.00 and rounded half up to two decimal places. The **conflict component** is 30.00 points for each entry in the referenced pair's `conflict_flags`, or 0.00 when no `pair_id` is referenced, as for a `MANUAL` investigation. The **score component** is how far the pair's `match_score` exceeds the `review_threshold` of the rule set that pair was scored under, computed as that difference rounded half up to two decimal places, floored at 0.00 so a pair below the threshold contributes nothing, and capped at 30.00; with no `pair_id` referenced it is 0.00. The **site component** is 2.00 points for every record currently counted in the subject's site `flagged_record_count`, capped at 20.00 — so a site with ten or more flagged records has saturated this component. The **insurance component** is a flat 25.00 points when a `pair_id` is referenced and that pair's `conflict_flags` contains `INSURANCE_CONFLICT`, and 0.00 otherwise; a payer-identity contradiction is weighted separately because it is the signal most often associated with deliberate misrepresentation, and it is therefore counted twice — once through the conflict component and again here.

Opening an investigation also sweeps the subject's work queue. Every candidate pair having the subject as a member and sitting in `NEW`, `TRIAGED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RECOMMENDED_MERGE` or `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT` transitions to `ESCALATED_FRAUD`, each advancing its own `version` by 1. On the two recommendation edges — `RECOMMENDED_MERGE → ESCALATED_FRAUD` and `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT → ESCALATED_FRAUD` — `recommended_by_user_id` and `recommendation` are additionally cleared to null, because a verdict reached before the fraud signal surfaced is no longer trustworthy. Pairs already in `RESOLVED_MERGED`, `RESOLVED_DISTINCT` or `ESCALATED_FRAUD` are left alone.

Adjudication is the `PrivacyOfficer`'s exclusive domain, and all three outcomes take the same shape: a note, a state move, a `version` increment, and a re-derivation. Substantiating requires `state` of `OPEN`, stores a `finding_note`, and moves the investigation `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED`. The subject's `integrity_state` hardens `FRAUD_SUSPECTED → FRAUD_CONFIRMED`, the site's `flagged_record_count` is recomputed, `risk_score` is recomputed, and the same queue sweep runs again over the subject's pairs in `NEW`, `TRIAGED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RECOMMENDED_MERGE` or `RECOMMENDED_DISTINCT`, with the two recommendation edges again clearing `recommended_by_user_id` and `recommendation`. Unsubstantiating also requires `state` of `OPEN`, stores a `finding_note`, and moves the investigation `OPEN → UNSUBSTANTIATED`, which is terminal. Here the subject's `integrity_state` is re-derived rather than assumed: it returns `FRAUD_SUSPECTED → CLEAR` only when no other investigation on that record remains `OPEN`, `SUBSTANTIATED` or `REFERRED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, no remediation on it remains `OPEN`, `QUARANTINED` or `REASSIGNED`, and no overlay correction on it remains `DETECTED` or `UNDER_CORRECTION`; if a remediation or overlay is still live the record lands on `REMEDIATION_PENDING` instead. When and only when the record does re-derive to `CLEAR`, the site's `flagged_record_count` is decremented and every pair having that record as a member and sitting in `ESCALATED_FRAUD` returns `ESCALATED_FRAUD → UNDER_REVIEW`, each advancing its `version` by 1 — the third and last of the queue's back-edges. Referring a case to law enforcement requires `state` of `SUBSTANTIATED`, stores a `referral_reference`, and moves the investigation `SUBSTANTIATED → REFERRED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, which is terminal; referral never releases the hold, and the subject stays `FRAUD_CONFIRMED`. Investigations are read individually with their `risk_score` visible, and listed with filters on `record_id`, `pair_id`, `state` and `trigger_kind`.

The final concern is measurement: which registration desks are actually producing duplicates. An `MpiAdmin` or an `IdentityAnalyst` runs a metric for a site over a closed date window, supplying `window_start_date`, `window_end_date` — no earlier than the start — and an `attribution_variant`. Before anything is computed, the requested window is checked for exclusivity against every existing metric run on the **same** site with the **same** `attribution_variant`: because both boundary dates belong to their window, two windows collide whenever the first's start date is no later than the second's end date and the second's start date is no later than the first's end date, so windows that merely abut collide too. There is no capacity dimension here — one collision is fatal, and the run is rejected with `ERR_METRIC_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Runs of the *other* variant are ignored entirely, so both attribution views may be computed over identical windows.

The run then counts two populations. `records_created` is the number of patient records registered at the site whose `created_at` date falls inside the closed window, compared date-only in UTC, counted with **no** `identity_state` filter at all — records later absorbed into a cluster still count, because the desk still created them. `confirmed_duplicates` is the number of merge operations whose `state` is `PENDING_PROPAGATION` or `COMPLETED`, excluding those reversed to `UNMERGED`, whose *attributed* record belongs to this site and whose attributed record's `created_at` date falls in the same closed window. Which record is attributed is what the variant selects: under `CREATION_SITE_ATTRIBUTION` the attributed record is the merge's **subsumed** record, charging the duplicate to whichever desk created the redundant chart, while under `SURVIVOR_SITE_ATTRIBUTION` it is the merge's **survivor**, charging it to the desk that owns the retained chart. For a cross-site merge the two variants therefore charge two different sites, and the same set of merges yields different rates per site — which is precisely why both views exist. `duplicate_rate_bps` expresses the result in basis points, that is hundredths of a percentage point: it is 0 when `records_created` is 0, and otherwise `confirmed_duplicates` multiplied by 10,000 and divided by `records_created`, with a **single** half-up rounding to an integer applied at the very end. No intermediate ratio is rounded and nothing is rounded per merge. Finally `sample_size_sufficient` is true when `records_created` is at least 30, warning readers off drawing conclusions from a desk that registered a handful of patients, and `rule_set_version_label` snapshots the `version_label` of whichever rule set was `ACTIVE` when the run executed, as a plain audit string. A metric run is a write-once event record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and is never amended. A site's runs can be listed and filtered by `attribution_variant`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The service follows one status-code convention throughout. An operation that mints a new resource — a site, a rule set, a search attempt, a patient record, a candidate pair, an evidence entry, a merge, an overlay correction, a downstream system, an investigation, an external match request or a metric run — responds with 201 Created. Every other successful operation, including all retrievals, all amendments, and every state-transition action such as triage, recommendation, approval, dispatch, acknowledgement, reversal and closure, responds with 200 OK. Error statuses are as catalogued in §5. Where the table below says "All roles", it means all five of `SiteRegistrar`, `IdentityAnalyst`, `IdentityStewardApprover`, `PrivacyOfficer` and `MpiAdmin`.
