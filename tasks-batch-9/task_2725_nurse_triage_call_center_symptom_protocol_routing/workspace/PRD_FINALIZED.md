# Nurse Triage Call Center API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A health system's nurse advice line sits between a worried caller and a closed medical office. Someone dials at 02:40 because a two-year-old will not stop crying, or at 21:15 because chest tightness has not eased, and within a few minutes a licensed nurse must decide whether that person should call 911, drive to an emergency department, wait for an urgent-care visit in the morning, book an office appointment, or safely manage the symptom at home. The advice line answers on behalf of dozens of contracted practices, each with its own office hours, its own billing rate, its own consent-recording policy, and its own tolerance for compliance variance. The service this document specifies is the system of record for that decision: who called, whose chart the call was bound to, which symptom protocol was scored, what advice was given, who authorised any departure from the recommendation, whether the caller was reached again afterwards, and whether what eventually happened to the patient agreed with what the nurse said.

Three pressures shape the design. The first is **clinical safety**: a nurse may only give advice in a state where they hold a live licence, a minor may not consent for themselves, an emergency recommendation may never be softened, and a caller routed to 911 may not have their call closed until a real dispatch centre has taken the handoff. The second is **auditability**: every disposition carries two independent lifecycles — the advice lifecycle, which records what was told to the caller and whether a supervisor revised it, and the audit lifecycle, which records what actually happened to the patient and whether that outcome agreed with the advice. The two advance separately, so an already-audited disposition can still be voided by a re-assessment, and a voided disposition still leaves its audit record behind. The third is **contractual accounting**: after-hours answering is a billed service, priced in sub-cent increments per billable minute, and both the minute ledger and the fee ledger must survive a supervisor reopening a call and re-closing it without drifting by a single cent.

Read this document as the complete behavioural contract. Section 2 fixes the parts that every endpoint shares — authentication, the response envelope, pagination, the logical clock, rounding, the version handshake, cascade discipline and the strict order in which requests are rejected. Section 3 tells the story of a call from the moment reference data is loaded to the moment a quality reviewer files an outcome, and it is where the business rules, the field names and the numeric thresholds live. Sections 4 and 5 are lookup tables only: the endpoint inventory and the error catalogue. Neither table repeats the rules, so a complete implementation requires reading Section 3 in full.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock control endpoint must present two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. When either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. When both headers are present but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The server trusts the headers; there is no token exchange, no signature, and no session.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `IntakeAgent`, `TriageNurse`, `ChargeNurse`, `ComplianceAuditor`, and `PracticeLiaison`.

`PlatformAdmin` is advice-line operations. It owns all reference data — practices, patient panels, nurses, licences, shifts and symptom protocols — and it can read everything. `IntakeAgent` is the non-clinical call taker: it opens calls, binds them to a chart, records consent, and relays the emergency dispatch centre's answer, but it never scores a protocol and never gives advice. `TriageNurse` is a licensed nurse on rotation, and is the only role that scores a protocol, determines a disposition, completes a call, opens or drives a warm transfer, or works a callback task. `ChargeNurse` is a shift supervisor who is also a licensed nurse; it owns the two supervisory compensating operations — overriding a determined disposition and reopening a closed call — and it may not perform any operation reserved for `TriageNurse`, so a charge nurse cannot score a protocol, cannot create a disposition, and cannot complete a call. `ComplianceAuditor` is a quality reviewer that files observed outcomes against advice already given and reads the concordance summary; it gives no advice and touches no call. `PracticeLiaison` is a contracted practice's own representative, whose reads are narrowed to that practice's own data.

Identity binding differs per role and determines how ownership is checked:

- When `X-User-Role` is `TriageNurse`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Nurse`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Nurse` compare the resource's nurse-reference field to `X-User-ID`. This is how licensure by the caller's state, concurrent-call capacity, the assigned-nurse check and segregation of duties all resolve.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ChargeNurse`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Nurse`, and the same ownership and licensure comparisons apply to supervisory operations.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Practice`; any practice-scoped path whose practice identifier is not equal to `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_PRACTICE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `auditor_user_id` on the `ComplianceAuditRecord` the auditor files, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two further access rules are worth stating up front because they cut across many endpoints. A `TriageNurse` may only act on a call whose assigned nurse is that nurse, and on a callback task whose assigned nurse is that nurse; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_NURSE` (HTTP 403). And the nurse who determined a disposition may neither override that disposition nor verify its callback; either attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many matching records are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — and that ordering is applied after all filters and before `limit` and `offset` are applied. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for the request, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters before paging, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records returned is still less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a single logical clock, and that clock is the sole source of "now" for the whole system. Nothing in this specification consults the host machine's wall clock. On a freshly started server the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it moves only when a caller sets it explicitly through the clock-control endpoint by supplying `to_iso8601`; the response reports the resulting instant as `current_utc`. That endpoint requires no authentication headers and ignores the caller's role, which makes it the one endpoint exempt from the `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rule.

The logical clock drives, at minimum: whether an inbound call is after-hours, whether a nurse licence is live, whether a nurse shift is running, when a callback falls due, whether a high-risk callback is verified in time, whether a completed call is still inside its 72-hour reopen window, the boundaries of a compliance audit period, and the timestamp stamped onto every consent, assignment, verification, handoff and closure. Because it moves in discrete jumps, a test can advance the clock past a licence expiry, a coverage window's end, or a callback deadline and observe the derived consequences immediately on the next read.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by the client, and a `created_at`, which is the logical-clock instant of creation rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T14:30:00.000Z`).

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the logical-clock instant of the most recent mutation and formatted identically, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. `Practice`, `Nurse`, `CoverageWindow`, `Patient`, `SymptomProtocol`, `TriageCall`, `ProtocolAssessment`, `Disposition`, `WarmTransfer` and `CallbackTask` all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

Three resources are immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all: `NurseLicense`, `NurseShift`, and `ComplianceAuditRecord`. A licence is never edited; a shift is never edited; an audit record is never edited. Where the status of a licence or a shift appears to change, that status is not stored at all — it is derived from the logical clock at read time, as Section 3 describes.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution

Several read paths are nested — a practice's patient panel, a practice's coverage windows, a nurse's licences, a nurse's shifts, a call's warm transfers, and a disposition's audit records. Nested paths are validated strictly left to right. The outer identifier is resolved first, and if no such record exists the request fails with that outer resource's not-found error before the inner segment is examined at all: an unknown practice identifier yields `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown nurse identifier yields `ERR_NURSE_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown call identifier yields `ERR_TRIAGE_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, and an unknown disposition identifier yields `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_FOUND`. Only once the outer record is known does the server filter the inner collection, and an inner collection that legitimately contains no rows is an empty successful list rather than an error.

Writes never rely on nesting for cross-entity linkage beyond the immediate parent named in the path. Where a body creates a link to another aggregate, the link travels as a field in the body.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Disposition Creation

Exactly one write in this system takes a version handshake. A `POST /dispositions` request, which creates a `Disposition` against a `TriageCall`, must carry `expected_call_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `TriageCall` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the call's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CALL_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

The handshake exists because a single call legitimately accepts more than one disposition over its life: the re-assessment loop voids the previous disposition and the next determination supersedes it, so a second create against an unchanged call is a valid product operation rather than an invalid-state request. The version check is therefore the only thing that distinguishes "I am writing the disposition I reasoned about" from "the call moved underneath me". Any write that bumps the call's `version` — binding a patient, recording consent, a successful nurse assignment, an assignment attempt that found no eligible nurse, a new protocol assessment, a supervisor override, a completion, a reopen or an abandonment — invalidates a version the client read earlier.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, overriding a disposition, verifying a callback, confirming a transfer location, completing a call and reopening a call all proceed without one.

### 2.8. Money, Minutes and Percentage Precision

All money in this system is carried in integers, never in floating-point currency. A practice's contracted rate `per_minute_rate_millicents` is expressed in millicents — thousandths of a cent — per billable minute, deliberately finer than a cent so that per-call rounding is observable. Every fee field that reaches a client is a whole number of cents: `advice_fee_cents` on a call, and `advice_subtotal_cents`, `escalation_gross_cents`, `coverage_credit_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` on the after-hours invoice readout.

Wherever this document says a cent amount is rounded, the rounding method is round half up: a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero, so 84.5 millicent-derived cents becomes 85 and never 84. This is not banker's rounding. Where a percentage is reported — `concordance_rate_strict_pct`, `concordance_rate_band_tolerant_pct` and `adequacy_ratio_pct` — the value is a number rounded half up to exactly one decimal place. `coverage_credit_pct` is an integer percentage taken from a fixed tier table and is never rounded.

Two ordering rules about rounding matter more than the rounding itself, and both are stated again in context in Section 3. Call billing rounds once per call and the practice ledgers then accumulate the already-rounded per-call cents; the raw sub-cent amounts across calls are never summed and rounded at the end. And the after-hours invoice's credit tier is selected from the advice subtotal alone, never from a running total that the credit itself would reduce.

### 2.9. Conserved Counters and Ledgers

Several stored integers are not free-form fields but conserved counters: at every observable moment their value must equal the count or sum they claim to represent, because every path that adds to them is matched by a path that removes exactly the same amount.

`Nurse.active_call_load` must at all times equal the number of `TriageCall` rows whose assigned nurse is that nurse and whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, `ASSESSING` or `DISPOSITIONED`. It is increased by one when a call is assigned to the nurse and increased by one when a closed call is reopened; it is decreased by one when a call is completed and decreased by one when a call is abandoned while it had an assigned nurse. An assign-then-abandon round trip therefore returns the counter to its starting value rather than leaving it at negative one or at one, a complete-then-reopen-then-complete round trip likewise returns it to where it started, and abandoning a call that was never assigned leaves every nurse counter untouched.

`Practice.advice_fee_accrued_cents` must equal the sum of `advice_fee_cents` over that practice's calls whose `status` is `CLOSED`, and `Practice.billed_advice_minutes_total` must equal the sum of `billable_minutes` over the same set. Completing a call credits exactly the two values frozen on that call; reopening it debits exactly those same stored values rather than recomputing them, so a rate change between completion and reopen cannot strand cents on the ledger. Completing an eighteen-minute call and then reopening it returns both ledgers to zero and also resets the call's own `billable_minutes` to 0 and `advice_fee_cents` to 0; completing, reopening, then completing again at thirteen minutes leaves the ledgers holding only the second completion's values, with no residue from the first.

`Practice.open_callback_count` must equal the number of `CallbackTask` rows belonging to that practice whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED`. Every path that creates such a task increments it by one and every path that moves such a task out of that set — verification, escalation, expiry or cancellation — decrements it by one, once per task.

`Practice.open_variance_count` is the number of that practice's dispositions whose `audit_status` is `VARIANCE_ESCALATED`. `Practice.escalated_callback_count` is a cumulative count that only ever grows: it counts the callback tasks for that practice that reached the terminal status `ESCALATED`, and it is the quantity the after-hours invoice charges against. `Nurse.pending_high_risk_count` is the number of calls assigned to that nurse that require a warm transfer and whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, `ASSESSING` or `DISPOSITIONED`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Many of this system's most important behaviours are chains: one write changes one row, that change causes a second row to change, the second change causes a third, and so on. Cascades are applied eagerly and completely within the request that triggered them — there is no deferred queue and no eventual convergence — and each hop bumps the `version` and refreshes the `updated_at` of the row it touches, exactly as a direct write would.

The deepest chain in the system runs four hops from a single write. Creating a second protocol assessment on a call supersedes the previous assessment; because that assessment became superseded, every disposition built on it is voided; because those dispositions were voided, their open callback tasks are cancelled; and because those tasks left the open set, the practice's open-callback counter is decremented once per cancelled task. A second four-hop chain runs from a compliance variance: a disposition enters variance escalation, the practice's open-variance counter rises, the practice's compliance posture flips to review, and every one of that practice's draft coverage windows is blocked.

Two independent lifecycles live on the same `Disposition` row and never interfere. `advice_status` tracks the advice itself and moves through `AUTHORIZED`, `OVERRIDDEN` and `VOIDED`. `audit_status` tracks the outcome review and moves through `AWAITING_OUTCOME`, `OUTCOME_RECORDED`, `CONCORDANT_CLOSED` and `VARIANCE_ESCALATED`. Voiding the advice does not touch the audit dimension, which is precisely why an already-audited disposition can still be voided; and an audit record already filed continues to exist afterwards even though it drops out of the concordance input set.

### 2.11. Licensure and Clinical Safety Gates

Licensure in this system is a filter, not a preference. The jurisdiction that matters is the **state of the caller** — `caller_state_code` on the call, the state the person on the line is physically in — and never the practice's state. A nurse may only be routed a call, may only determine its disposition, and may only override a disposition, while holding a `NurseLicense` for that state whose derived `status` is `ACTIVE`. A nurse without one is rejected with `ERR_NURSE_NOT_LICENSED_IN_STATE` (HTTP 409) on the clinical write, and is silently absent from the assignment selector's candidate pool on the routing path.

Segregation of duties is enforced on two operations. The nurse recorded as `determined_by_nurse_id` on a disposition may not override that disposition and may not verify its callback; both attempts return `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The same nurse is nonetheless the nurse who records callback attempts, because logging an unanswered call is not a clinical judgement. And de-escalation is structurally reserved: a triage nurse may give advice more severe than the recommendation, with documentation, but only a charge nurse may soften advice, and only against an already-created disposition.

### 2.12. The Order in Which Requests Are Rejected

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order of evaluation is part of the contract. Every candidate rejection is evaluated in strictly ascending priority order, and the **first** rule whose condition holds produces the response; no later rule is evaluated and no state is written. A request that presents a body with a bad enum value *and* targets a suspended practice therefore always answers with the malformed-request error, never with the suspension error, because the schema rule sits earlier in the order.

The full order, from first-evaluated to last-evaluated, is:

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` — the authentication headers are missing or empty. Guards every endpoint except the logical-clock control endpoint `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`.
2. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — the presented role is not among the endpoint's authorised roles. Guards every endpoint that has a role list.
3. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the request is malformed. Guards every write endpoint. It fires when a required input field is missing; when an enum field carries a value outside its declared set; when a numeric field falls outside its declared range, including `base_severity` outside 0 through 100, a negative `red_flag_count`, `match_confidence_pct` outside 0 through 100, `location_confidence_pct` outside 0 through 100, `handle_minutes` outside 1 through 240, a negative `per_minute_rate_millicents`, a `max_concurrent_calls` below 1, or a `variance_review_threshold` below 1; when an interval is inverted, meaning `end_utc` is at or before `start_utc`, or `office_close_utc_hhmm` is at or before `office_open_utc_hhmm`, or `max_age_years` is below `min_age_years`; or when a timestamp is not a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant. It also fires on the staffing readout when any of its three query parameters is absent.
4. `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_FOUND` — no practice with that identifier exists. Guards any endpoint carrying a practice identifier in its path or body.
5. `ERR_NURSE_NOT_FOUND` — no nurse with that identifier exists. Guards any endpoint carrying a nurse identifier in its path or body.
6. `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` — no patient with the supplied `patient_id` exists. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/match-patient`.
7. `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND` — no symptom protocol with that identifier exists. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/assessments` and `POST /symptom-protocols/{id}/retire`.
8. `ERR_TRIAGE_CALL_NOT_FOUND` — no triage call with that identifier exists. Guards any endpoint carrying a triage call identifier in its path or body.
9. `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` — no protocol assessment with that identifier exists. Guards `GET /protocol-assessments/{id}` and `POST /dispositions`.
10. `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_FOUND` — no disposition with that identifier exists. Guards any endpoint carrying a disposition identifier in its path or body.
11. `ERR_CALLBACK_TASK_NOT_FOUND` — no callback task with that identifier exists. Guards `GET /callback-tasks/{id}`, `POST /callback-tasks/{id}/attempts` and `POST /callback-tasks/{id}/verify`.
12. `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND` — no warm transfer with that identifier exists. Guards `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/confirm-location`, `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/psap-response` and `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/complete-handoff`.
13. `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND` — no coverage window with that identifier exists. Guards `POST /coverage-windows/{id}/activate`.
14. `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_NURSE` — the acting triage nurse is not the assigned nurse of the target call, or of the target callback task. Guards seven operations: `POST /triage-calls/{id}/assessments`, `POST /dispositions`, `POST /triage-calls/{id}/complete`, `POST /triage-calls/{id}/warm-transfers`, `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/confirm-location`, `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/complete-handoff` and `POST /callback-tasks/{id}/attempts`.
15. `ERR_PRACTICE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — the acting role is `PracticeLiaison` and `X-User-ID` is not the identifier of the practice named in the path. Guards five operations: `GET /practices/{id}/compliance-summary`, `GET /practices/{id}/after-hours-invoice`, `GET /practices/{id}/coverage-windows`, `GET /practices/{id}/patients` and `POST /coverage-windows/{id}/activate`.
16. `ERR_PRACTICE_SUSPENDED` — the practice's `status` is `SUSPENDED`. Guards three creates: `POST /triage-calls`, `POST /practices/{id}/patients` and `POST /coverage-windows`.
17. `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE` — the call's `status` is not in the from-set of the requested transition. Guards every triage-call action endpoint and `POST /dispositions`.
18. `ERR_ASSESSMENT_SUPERSEDED` — the referenced assessment's `status` is `SUPERSEDED`. Guards `POST /dispositions`.
19. `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE` — the disposition's `advice_status` or `audit_status` is not in the from-set of the requested transition, for example overriding a voided disposition or overriding one that is already overridden. Guards `POST /dispositions/{id}/override` and `POST /dispositions/{id}/audit-records`. One carve-out applies: on `POST /dispositions/{id}/audit-records`, when an audit record for that disposition already exists the answer is `ERR_OUTCOME_ALREADY_RECORDED` instead, so on that endpoint this rule fires only when `advice_status` is `VOIDED`.
20. `ERR_CALLBACK_INVALID_STATE` — the callback task's `status` is not in the from-set of the requested transition, such as recording an attempt on a verified task, verifying a task that is still pending, or touching a cancelled or expired task. Guards `POST /callback-tasks/{id}/attempts` and `POST /callback-tasks/{id}/verify`.
21. `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE` — the warm transfer's `status` is not in the from-set of the requested transition. Guards `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/confirm-location`, `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/psap-response` and `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/complete-handoff`.
22. `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE` — the coverage window's `status` is anything other than `DRAFT`, whether already `ACTIVE`, lazily `EXPIRED`, or `BLOCKED` by a compliance review. Guards `POST /coverage-windows/{id}/activate`.
23. `ERR_STALE_CALL_VERSION` — the supplied `expected_call_version` does not equal the call's current `version`. Guards `POST /dispositions`.
24. `ERR_NURSE_NOT_LICENSED_IN_STATE` — the acting nurse holds no licence for the call's `caller_state_code` whose derived status is `ACTIVE`. Guards `POST /dispositions` and `POST /dispositions/{id}/override`.
25. `ERR_MINOR_REQUIRES_GUARDIAN_CONSENT` — the patient is under eighteen years old as of the logical clock and the supplied `consent_type` is `PATIENT_SELF`. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/consent`.
26. `ERR_CONSENT_RECORDING_REQUIRED` — the practice sets `consent_recording_required` to true and the supplied `recording_reference` is absent or empty. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/consent`.
27. `ERR_OVERRIDE_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED` — the requested `final_level` differs from the applicable recommendation and either `override_reason_code` is absent or `override_narrative` is shorter than forty characters. Guards `POST /dispositions` and `POST /dispositions/{id}/override`.
28. `ERR_DEESCALATION_REQUIRES_CHARGE_NURSE` — on the create path, the requested `final_level` is less severe than the computed `recommended_level`. Guards `POST /dispositions`.
29. `ERR_EMERGENCY_DEESCALATION_FORBIDDEN` — the disposition's `recommended_level` is `EMERGENCY_911` and the requested `final_level` is anything else. Guards `POST /dispositions/{id}/override`.
30. `ERR_OVERRIDE_STEP_LIMIT` — the requested `final_level` is more than one severity step away from the disposition's current `final_level`. Guards `POST /dispositions/{id}/override`.
31. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — `X-User-ID` equals the disposition's `determined_by_nurse_id`. Guards `POST /callback-tasks/{id}/verify` and `POST /dispositions/{id}/override`.
32. `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_INCOMPLETE` — the call has `requires_warm_transfer` set to true and no warm transfer for that call has reached `HANDOFF_COMPLETE`. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/complete`.
33. `ERR_PATIENT_PRACTICE_MISMATCH` — the patient's practice is not the call's practice. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/match-patient`.
34. `ERR_ASSESSMENT_CALL_MISMATCH` — the referenced assessment's `call_id` is not the `call_id` in the body. Guards `POST /dispositions`.
35. `ERR_CALLBACK_NOT_RESOLVED` — the disposition has `requires_callback` set to true and its callback task's `status` is still `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED`. Guards `POST /dispositions/{id}/audit-records`.
36. `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_COVERAGE` — the call is after-hours and no active coverage window of that practice contains the logical clock. Guards `POST /triage-calls`.
37. `ERR_CALLBACK_PAST_DUE` — the callback task's `risk_class` is `HIGH_RISK` and the logical clock is strictly later than its `due_by_utc`. Guards `POST /callback-tasks/{id}/verify`.
38. `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — the logical clock is at or after seventy-two hours past the call's `closed_at`. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/reopen`.
39. `ERR_OUTCOME_PREDATES_DISPOSITION` — the supplied `outcome_observed_at` is strictly earlier than the disposition's `created_at`. Guards `POST /dispositions/{id}/audit-records`.
40. `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP` — the new shift interval overlaps an existing shift of the same nurse. Guards `POST /nurses/{id}/shifts`.
41. `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` — the new coverage window conflicts with an existing draft or active window of the same practice once a thirty-minute buffer is applied to both sides of the existing window. Guards `POST /coverage-windows`.
42. `ERR_PROTOCOL_INACTIVE` — the chosen symptom protocol's `status` is `RETIRED`. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/assessments`.
43. `ERR_PROTOCOL_AGE_INELIGIBLE` — the age the assessment actually scored on falls outside the protocol's eligibility band. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/assessments`.
44. `ERR_MATCH_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW` — the supplied `match_method` is not `MRN` and `match_confidence_pct` is below 80. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/match-patient`.
45. `ERR_INVALID_LOCATION` — `location_lat` falls outside minus ninety through ninety degrees, or `location_lng` falls outside minus one hundred eighty through one hundred eighty degrees, or `location_address_line` is empty. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/warm-transfers`.
46. `ERR_LOCATION_STATE_MISMATCH` — the supplied `location_state_code` is not equal to the call's `caller_state_code`. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/warm-transfers`.
47. `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_NOT_INDICATED` — the call's `requires_warm_transfer` is false. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/warm-transfers`.
48. `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_ALREADY_OPEN` — a warm transfer for that call already sits at `INITIATED`, `LOCATION_CONFIRMED`, `ACCEPTED_BY_PSAP` or `HANDOFF_COMPLETE`. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/warm-transfers`.
49. `ERR_OUTCOME_ALREADY_RECORDED` — an audit record for that disposition already exists. Guards `POST /dispositions/{id}/audit-records`.
50. `ERR_LOCATION_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW` — the supplied `location_confidence_pct` is below 75. Guards `POST /warm-transfers/{id}/confirm-location`.
51. `ERR_NURSE_AT_CAPACITY` — a `preferred_nurse_id` was supplied and that nurse's `active_call_load` is at or above their `max_concurrent_calls`. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/assign-nurse`.
52. `ERR_NO_LICENSED_NURSE_AVAILABLE` — the eligible-nurse candidate set for the call is empty. Guards `POST /triage-calls/{id}/assign-nurse`.
53. `ERR_PRACTICE_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` — the practice's `compliance_posture` is `UNDER_REVIEW`. Guards `POST /coverage-windows/{id}/activate`.

One consequence of this ordering deserves emphasis because it is easy to get backwards. On the disposition create path, the version handshake at position 23 is evaluated *before* the licensure gate at 24, which is itself before the documentation gate at 27, which is before the de-escalation gate at 28. A triage nurse who is unlicensed in the caller's state, submitting a stale version and softening the recommendation without a narrative, receives `ERR_STALE_CALL_VERSION` and nothing else. Equally, on the override path the disposition-state check at 19 precedes the licensure check at 24, which precedes documentation at 27, emergency protection at 29, the step limit at 30 and segregation of duties at 31 — so a charge nurse trying to override their own already-voided disposition receives `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And on the callback verification path the state check at 20 precedes segregation of duties at 31, which precedes the past-due check at 37.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Practices, Patient Panels and Symptom Protocols

Everything begins with a **practice** — a contracted medical group whose calls the advice line answers. Advice-line operations registers one by supplying its legal `name` of one to one hundred twenty characters, the two ends of its own office day as `office_open_utc_hhmm` and `office_close_utc_hhmm` (each a UTC hour and minute in twenty-four-hour form, with the close strictly later than the open), and its contracted `per_minute_rate_millicents`, which must be at least one. Three further inputs are optional and carry defaults: `weekend_is_after_hours`, which defaults to true and, when true, makes ISO weekday six (Saturday) and seven (Sunday) after-hours regardless of the time of day; `consent_recording_required`, which defaults to false; and `variance_review_threshold`, which defaults to 2 and must be at least one. A practice may also carry `audit_period_start_utc`, the inclusive lower bound of its current compliance audit period. A newly registered practice opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `compliance_posture` `NORMAL`, and all five of `open_callback_count`, `open_variance_count`, `escalated_callback_count`, `billed_advice_minutes_total` and `advice_fee_accrued_cents` at zero.

A practice's contract state is a two-value enumeration, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and operations can flip it either way by supplying a new `status`; the flip bumps the practice's `version`. Suspension is a hard commercial stop rather than a soft warning: while a practice is `SUSPENDED`, opening a call for it, adding a patient to its panel and drafting a coverage window for it are all rejected with `ERR_PRACTICE_SUSPENDED`. Reads of a suspended practice, including its counters and ledgers, continue to work.

Patients arrive as a panel attached to their practice. Adding one requires the patient's `full_name` (one to one hundred twenty characters) and `dob`, a calendar date in year-month-day form, which is the authoritative age source whenever a nurse chooses the confirmed scoring path. Optionally the panel record carries `sex`, one of `FEMALE`, `MALE` or `UNKNOWN`; `pregnancy_status`, one of `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `NOT_PREGNANT`, `PREGNANT` or `POSTPARTUM_6W`, defaulting to `NOT_APPLICABLE` and used only through the pregnancy modifier on the provisional scoring path, with `sex` gating whether a value other than `NOT_APPLICABLE` is meaningful at all; `phone`, the callback number on file, one to thirty-two characters, which is also the value an intake agent searches the panel by; and `mrn`, the practice's medical record number, which defaults to absent and, when supplied, is unique within that practice. The patient's `practice_id` is taken from the practice the panel belongs to and is not supplied in the body. A panel read may be narrowed by `phone` or by `mrn`.

**Symptom protocols** are the clinical content the advice line scores against. Each is published with a `code` matching three to twenty uppercase letters, digits or underscores and globally unique, a human-readable `title` of one to one hundred twenty characters, a `base_severity` between 0 and 100 that contributes the protocol's baseline acuity points before any modifier, and an eligibility band expressed as `min_age_years` and `max_age_years`, both between 0 and 120 inclusive on both ends, with the upper bound at or above the lower. Three behavioural switches follow. `pregnancy_sensitive` decides whether the pregnancy modifier can contribute anything at all — when it is false, the pregnancy contribution is zero points no matter what the pregnancy status says. `emergency_override` decides whether a single red flag forces the emergency band irrespective of the score. `min_disposition_level` is the protocol's own floor, one of `EMERGENCY_911`, `ED_NOW`, `URGENT_CARE_24H`, `OFFICE_APPT_72H` or `HOME_CARE`, below which the routing may never recommend. Finally `infant_modifier_points`, defaulting to 20 and constrained to 0 through 40, is the acuity contribution for a patient whose scored age is under one year.

A protocol is published with `status` `ACTIVE` and may later be retired, which sets `status` to `RETIRED` and bumps its `version`. Retirement is forward-only in effect: assessments already scored against the protocol keep their scores and their dispositions untouched, but a new assessment naming a retired protocol is rejected with `ERR_PROTOCOL_INACTIVE`. Protocol listings may be narrowed by `status` and by `code`.

### 3.2. Nurses, Licensure, Shifts and Coverage Authorisation

A **nurse** is registered with a `full_name` of one to one hundred twenty characters and a `seniority_level` between 1 and 5, where a higher number is more senior. Two capacity dials are optional: `max_concurrent_calls`, between 1 and 8, caps how many live calls the nurse may hold at once; `high_risk_cap`, between 1 and 5, is the number of pending warm-transfer calls at which the nurse is automatically pulled out of the routing pool. A fresh nurse starts with `active_call_load` at zero, `pending_high_risk_count` at zero, and `intake_eligibility` at `ON_ROTATION`. The eligibility flag is a two-value enumeration: it becomes `OFF_ROTATION` as soon as `pending_high_risk_count` reaches `high_risk_cap`, and it returns to `ON_ROTATION` as soon as the count falls back below the cap.

Licences and shifts are both immutable records hung off a nurse. A licence records the `state_code` it authorises advice in — exactly two uppercase letters — a board-issued `license_number` of one to forty characters that is unique for that nurse and state pairing, and `expires_on`, the inclusive last calendar date on which it is valid. The licence's `nurse_id` comes from the nurse the record is filed under. A licence's `status` is never stored: it is computed at every read and at every eligibility evaluation as `ACTIVE` when `expires_on` is on or after the calendar date of the logical clock, and `EXPIRED` otherwise. The expiry day itself is still active, so a licence with `expires_on` of 2026-08-03 is `ACTIVE` at 2026-08-03T23:59:00Z and `EXPIRED` at 2026-08-04T00:00:00Z. Licence listings may be narrowed by `state_code` and by `status`.

A shift records `start_utc` and `end_utc`, with the end strictly after the start, and its `nurse_id` comes from the nurse it is filed under. The interval is half-open — the shift includes its start instant and excludes its end instant — which means one shift may begin at the exact instant another ends. Two shifts for the same nurse conflict when the existing shift starts before the new one ends and the new one starts before the existing one ends; such a request is rejected with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`, while exact adjacency, where the new start equals an existing end, is accepted. `scheduled_minutes` is computed on creation as the whole number of minutes between the two instants, and it is the quantity the staffing readout sums. Like a licence, a shift's `status` is derived from the clock rather than stored: `SCHEDULED` while the clock is strictly before `start_utc`, `ACTIVE` while the clock is at or after `start_utc` and strictly before `end_utc`, and `ENDED` once the clock reaches `end_utc`. A shift is therefore never `ACTIVE` at its own end instant, and a nurse whose only shift has ended is not assignable. Shift listings may be narrowed by `status`.

**Coverage windows** are the contractual permission to answer for a practice while that practice's own office is closed. Drafting one requires the `practice_id` it authorises, the `start_utc` and `end_utc` of the interval (end strictly after start, and the interval half-open on the same convention as shifts), and a `coverage_kind` of `NIGHTS`, `WEEKEND`, `HOLIDAY` or `OVERFLOW`. Coverage windows carry a mandatory thirty-minute handoff buffer on **both** sides, so a new window for a practice is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` when the existing window's start minus thirty minutes falls before the new window's end and the new window's start falls before the existing window's end plus thirty minutes. Comparison is against every existing window of that practice whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`. A gap of exactly thirty minutes between two windows is acceptable; a gap of twenty-nine minutes is not. A drafted window is created with `status` `DRAFT` and `suspend_reason` absent.

Only an active window satisfies the after-hours coverage check, and activation is a deliberate second step that moves the window from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE` and bumps its `version`. Activation is refused in two ways that must not be confused. If the window is not in `DRAFT` — already `ACTIVE`, lazily `EXPIRED`, or `BLOCKED` — the answer is `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`. If the window is a genuine `DRAFT` but its practice's `compliance_posture` is `UNDER_REVIEW`, the answer is `ERR_PRACTICE_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`, because a practice under compliance review may not extend its coverage footprint. `DRAFT` windows can also be taken out of play without anyone asking: the final hop of the compliance variance cascade flips every `DRAFT` window of the affected practice to `BLOCKED` and stamps `suspend_reason` with the exact string `PRACTICE_UNDER_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`.

Expiry is lazy rather than scheduled. Whenever a coverage window is read individually, whenever a practice's windows are listed, and whenever an intake coverage check runs, any window whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `end_utc` the logical clock has reached is first flipped to `EXPIRED`, bumping its `version`, and only then is the read or the check computed. An expired window can consequently never satisfy a coverage check: a weekend window spanning 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z up to but not including 2026-08-08T00:00:00Z covers a call at 2026-08-01T14:30:00Z, and at 2026-08-08T00:00:00Z exactly it flips to `EXPIRED` and the same call is refused. Coverage listings may be narrowed by `status`.

### 3.3. Answering the Call: Intake, Identification and Consent

A call is opened by the intake agent who picks up the line. The body names the `practice_id` being answered for, the `caller_name` of the person actually on the line (one to one hundred twenty characters, and not necessarily the patient), the `caller_phone` they can be reached back on (one to thirty-two characters), and `caller_state_code`, two uppercase letters naming the state the caller is **physically located in**. That last field is not decorative: it is the licensure jurisdiction for every nurse who will touch this call, and it is compared against the state of the captured location if the call ends up being transferred to emergency services. Optionally the agent records `caller_relationship`, one of `SELF`, `PARENT_GUARDIAN`, `SPOUSE`, `CAREGIVER`, `HEALTHCARE_PROXY` or `OTHER` — of which only `PARENT_GUARDIAN` and `HEALTHCARE_PROXY` can supply guardian consent — and `reason_text`, the caller's stated reason verbatim, up to five hundred characters.

Two things happen before the row is written. First, `is_after_hours` is computed and then **frozen** for the life of the call; later clock advances never re-flip it. The computation reads the logical clock in UTC and derives a minute-of-day as the clock's hour multiplied by sixty plus its minute, then derives an opening minute and a closing minute by the same arithmetic from the practice's `office_open_utc_hhmm` and `office_close_utc_hhmm`. The call is after-hours when either of two conditions holds: the practice has `weekend_is_after_hours` set to true and the calendar date of the clock is ISO weekday six or seven; or the minute-of-day does not lie in the half-open window that begins at the opening minute inclusive and ends at the closing minute exclusive. Concretely, for a practice open 13:00 to 22:00, a Monday call at 14:30 has minute-of-day 870 inside the window running from 780 up to 1320 and is in-hours; the same practice hours on a Saturday give an after-hours call at 14:30 anyway when the weekend flag is set; and a Monday call at exactly 22:00 has minute-of-day 1320, which is not strictly below the closing minute 1320, so it too is after-hours. The closing boundary is exclusive and the opening boundary is inclusive.

Second, the coverage obligation is checked. An in-hours call needs no coverage authorization whatsoever and passes this check unconditionally — a practice with no coverage window at all can still be answered during its own office hours. An after-hours call, by contrast, is only permitted while that practice has a live authorization: after lazily expiring any of the practice's `ACTIVE` windows the clock has run past, the server looks for a window belonging to that practice whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose interval contains the logical clock, meaning the clock is at or after `start_utc` and strictly before `end_utc`. The window's `coverage_kind` is contractual metadata only and never narrows this check — any `ACTIVE` window of any kind satisfies it. If no such window exists, the call is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_COVERAGE` and no row is created.

A call that survives both checks is created at `status` `INTAKE`, with `requires_warm_transfer` false, `billable_minutes` at 0, `advice_fee_cents` at 0, `reopen_count` at 0, `deferred_assignment_count` at 0, and every identification, consent, assignment, transfer and closure field still absent. Call listings may be narrowed by `practice_id`, by `patient_id`, by `assigned_nurse_id` and by `status`.

The next step binds the call to a chart, moving `INTAKE → IDENTIFIED`. The intake agent supplies `patient_id`, a `match_method` of `DOB_AND_NAME`, `PHONE_AND_NAME` or `MRN`, and, for the two non-`MRN` methods, a `match_confidence_pct` between 0 and 100. A record-number match is trusted outright; any other method must carry a confidence of at least 80, and a lower one is refused with `ERR_MATCH_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW`. The named patient must exist, or the answer is `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, and the patient must belong to the same practice as the call, or the answer is `ERR_PATIENT_PRACTICE_MISMATCH` — a call may only ever be matched to a patient of the practice it was opened for. On success the call's `patient_id`, `match_method` and `match_confidence_pct` are set, `status` becomes `IDENTIFIED`, and the call's `version` increases by one with `updated_at` refreshed.

Consent is captured next, advancing `IDENTIFIED → CONSENTED`. The agent supplies `consent_type`, one of `PATIENT_SELF`, `GUARDIAN` or `HEALTHCARE_PROXY`, and `consent_given_by`, the name of the person granting it; `recording_reference` is supplied when a voice-recording locator is required. Two safety gates apply. If the matched patient is under eighteen years old — measured as the full years elapsed from the patient's `dob` to the calendar date of the logical clock — then a `consent_type` of `PATIENT_SELF` is refused with `ERR_MINOR_REQUIRES_GUARDIAN_CONSENT`; a minor's consent must come from a guardian or a healthcare proxy. If the practice sets `consent_recording_required` to true, then a `recording_reference` that is absent or empty is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_RECORDING_REQUIRED`. On success the call records `consent_type`, `consent_given_by` and `recording_reference`, stamps `consent_recorded_at` with the current logical-clock instant, moves to `CONSENTED`, and increases `version` by one.

At any point while a call is live — that is, while its `status` is `INTAKE`, `IDENTIFIED`, `CONSENTED`, `ASSIGNED`, `ASSESSING` or `DISPOSITIONED` — the caller may hang up or the call may otherwise have to be terminated. Recording an `abandon_reason` moves the call to the terminal state `ABANDONED` and increases `version` by one. Abandonment releases everything the call was holding, in order: if the call had an assigned nurse, that nurse's `active_call_load` is decreased by one, exactly compensating the increase the assignment made; then every disposition of that call whose `advice_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `OVERRIDDEN` is flipped to `VOIDED`; then, because those dispositions were voided, every callback task pointing at them whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED` flips to `CANCELLED` and the practice's `open_callback_count` is decreased by one for each such task; and if the call had `requires_warm_transfer` set, the assigned nurse's `pending_high_risk_count` is decreased by one and that nurse's `intake_eligibility` is re-evaluated against `high_risk_cap`. An abandoned call is final; nothing reopens it. Abandoning a call in any other state — one already `CLOSED` or already `ABANDONED` — is refused with `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.4. Routing the Call to a Licensed Nurse

A consented call has to reach a nurse who is legally allowed to advise the person on the line. Routing is a server-side selection, not a client choice: the request carries no nurse at all in the ordinary case, and the server picks. When the caller does want to steer the assignment, an optional `preferred_nurse_id` may be supplied, and that nurse must independently satisfy every eligibility clause below as well as having `active_call_load` strictly below `max_concurrent_calls`; a preferred nurse who is already at their concurrency cap is refused with `ERR_NURSE_AT_CAPACITY`, and a preferred nurse who fails any other clause falls through to the empty-pool outcome.

Eligibility is a conjunction of four clauses, all of which must hold for a nurse to enter the candidate pool:

- the nurse's `intake_eligibility` is `ON_ROTATION`;
- the nurse holds at least one `NurseLicense` whose `state_code` equals the call's `caller_state_code` and whose derived status is `ACTIVE`, meaning its `expires_on` is on or after the calendar date of the logical clock;
- the nurse has at least one `NurseShift` whose interval contains the logical clock, meaning the clock is at or after `start_utc` and strictly before `end_utc`;
- the nurse's `active_call_load` is strictly less than their `max_concurrent_calls`.

Ranking among the survivors is fully deterministic and consults no random value and no identifier ordering. Candidates are ordered first by `active_call_load` ascending, so the least-loaded nurse wins; ties are broken by `seniority_level` descending, so the more senior of two equally loaded nurses wins; remaining ties are broken by `created_at` ascending, so the nurse registered earlier wins. The first candidate under that ordering is the selection. Two on-rotation nurses licensed in the caller's state and both on a running shift with loads of zero and one resolve to the load-zero nurse; the same pair both at load zero with seniority levels four and two resolves to the level-four nurse; the same pair at load zero and seniority four resolves to whichever was created first. A nurse's `id` is never a tie-break.

On a successful selection the call moves `CONSENTED → ASSIGNED`: `assigned_nurse_id` is set to the winner, `assigned_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the winning nurse's `active_call_load` increases by one, and the call's `version` increases by one.

When the candidate pool is empty, something unusual happens that the rest of this specification never repeats: the request is refused with `ERR_NO_LICENSED_NURSE_AVAILABLE`, and yet the failed attempt is still recorded on the call. `deferred_assignment_count` increases by one and the call's `version` increases by one, while `status` stays at `CONSENTED`, `assigned_nurse_id` stays absent, and every nurse counter is left untouched. This is deliberate — the advice line must be able to prove afterwards how many times a caller sat waiting for a licensed nurse — and it has a real consequence downstream, because that `version` bump invalidates any call version a client had already read. A nurse who holds an active licence in a state other than the caller's produces an empty pool and this outcome; so does a nurse whose `active_call_load` has reached their `max_concurrent_calls`; so does a nurse whose only shift has already ended; so does a nurse who has been pulled `OFF_ROTATION` by the high-risk cascade.

### 3.5. Protocol Assessment and the Acuity Score

The assigned nurse scores a symptom protocol against the call, which moves an `ASSIGNED` call to `ASSESSING`. The body names the `protocol_id` chosen, a `confirmation_mode` of `PROVISIONAL` or `CONFIRMED`, and a `red_flag_count` between 0 and 10 counting the protocol red flags the caller answered yes to. Two further fields are conditionally required by the mode: `stated_age_years`, between 0 and 120, is required in `PROVISIONAL` mode and ignored entirely in `CONFIRMED` mode; `confirmed_pregnancy_status`, one of `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `NOT_PREGNANT`, `PREGNANT` or `POSTPARTUM_6W`, is required in `CONFIRMED` mode and ignored entirely in `PROVISIONAL` mode. Only the nurse recorded as the call's assigned nurse may score it; anyone else receives `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_NURSE`. The protocol must exist and its `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PROTOCOL_INACTIVE` respectively.

**The mode selects which facts the score is built from, and this is the single most consequential branch in the clinical path.** In `PROVISIONAL` mode the score reads the caller's own words: `age_years_used` is set to the supplied `stated_age_years`, and `pregnancy_status_used` is set to whatever `pregnancy_status` the practice recorded on the patient's chart. In `CONFIRMED` mode the score reads verified facts instead: `age_years_used` is recomputed as the full years elapsed from the patient's `dob` to the calendar date of the logical clock, ignoring `stated_age_years` completely, and `pregnancy_status_used` is set to the nurse-supplied `confirmed_pregnancy_status`, ignoring the chart. Both fields are persisted on the assessment so the inputs actually used are auditable after the fact. The two modes are not a formatting difference: a caller who says "sixty" but whose chart date of birth is 1948-09-10 scores 78 provisionally and 90 once confirmed, which is the difference between an emergency-department referral and a 911 transfer.

Whichever mode was chosen, the age that resulted must fall inside the protocol's eligibility band, at or above `min_age_years` and at or below `max_age_years`; an age outside that band is refused with `ERR_PROTOCOL_AGE_INELIGIBLE`. Note that the band is checked against the variant-selected age, so the same body can be eligible in one mode and ineligible in the other.

The acuity score is then assembled from four components and stored alongside them. `age_modifier_points` is the protocol's `infant_modifier_points` when the scored age is strictly under one year; otherwise 10 points when the scored age is at least one and strictly under twelve; otherwise 12 points when the scored age is at least sixty-five; otherwise zero, which is the case for any age from twelve up to but not including sixty-five. `pregnancy_modifier_points` is zero whenever the protocol's `pregnancy_sensitive` flag is false, regardless of status; on a pregnancy-sensitive protocol it is 15 points when `pregnancy_status_used` is `PREGNANT`, 8 points when it is `POSTPARTUM_6W`, and zero when it is `NOT_PREGNANT` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. `red_flag_points` is eight points per red flag, capped at twenty-four points, so it is the smaller of twenty-four and eight times `red_flag_count` — three red flags and four red flags both contribute twenty-four. `acuity_score` is the protocol's `base_severity` plus the three modifier contributions, then clamped to the range zero through one hundred: any sum above one hundred is reported as one hundred and any sum below zero as zero.

Worked through: a provisional assessment on a protocol with `base_severity` 62, a stated age of 36, a charted status of `NOT_PREGNANT` and one red flag scores no age modifier, no pregnancy modifier and eight red-flag points, for an `acuity_score` of 70. The same protocol scored provisionally at a stated age of 60 with two red flags still earns no age modifier and lands on 78. Scored in confirmed mode against a chart date of birth of 1948-09-10 with the clock at 2026-08-03, the scored age is 77, which earns the twelve-point geriatric modifier, and with two red flags the score is 90. On a pregnancy-sensitive protocol with `base_severity` 62, a confirmed age of 30, `confirmed_pregnancy_status` of `PREGNANT` and one red flag, the score is 85, while the identical assessment with `NOT_PREGNANT` is 70. An infant of scored age zero on a protocol with `base_severity` 40 and `infant_modifier_points` of 22 and no red flags scores 62. And a protocol with `base_severity` 61 and three red flags scores exactly 85, as does the same protocol with four red flags, because of the red-flag cap.

An assessment is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, and **only one assessment may be `ACTIVE` on a call at a time.** A nurse who re-scores a call — which is legitimate and common, because a caller who mentions a new symptom two minutes in deserves a fresh protocol — drives the re-assessment loop `DISPOSITIONED → ASSESSING`, and that single write sets off the deepest cascade in the system. In the first hop, the new assessment is created `ACTIVE` and the call's previously `ACTIVE` assessment flips to `SUPERSEDED`. In the second hop, because that assessment became `SUPERSEDED`, every disposition whose `assessment_id` points at it and whose `advice_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `OVERRIDDEN` flips its `advice_status` to `VOIDED`; and where such a disposition had set the call's `requires_warm_transfer`, that flag is cleared, the determining nurse's `pending_high_risk_count` is decreased by one, and that nurse's `intake_eligibility` returns to `ON_ROTATION` once the count falls below their `high_risk_cap`. In the third hop, because those dispositions became `VOIDED`, every callback task whose `disposition_id` points at one of them and whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED` flips to `CANCELLED`. In the fourth hop, because those tasks left the open set, the practice's `open_callback_count` is decreased by one for each cancelled task. Finally the call's `status` becomes `ASSESSING` and its `version` increases by one. Each of the four hops is caused by the hop before it rather than by the original request, and every row touched has its own `version` bumped.

Assessments taken from either the `ASSIGNED` or the `DISPOSITIONED` state are otherwise identical in their input requirements, their eligibility gates and their scoring; the only difference is the cascade, which has nothing to void on the first assessment of a call. Requesting an assessment while the call is in any other state is refused with `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`. Assessment listings may be narrowed by `call_id`, by `protocol_id` and by `status`.

### 3.6. Determining, Escalating and Overriding the Disposition

The five disposition levels form a strict severity scale, and almost every rule in this section compares levels on it rather than alphabetically. From most severe to least severe the levels and their severity ordinals are `EMERGENCY_911` at 5, `ED_NOW` at 4, `URGENT_CARE_24H` at 3, `OFFICE_APPT_72H` at 2, and `HOME_CARE` at 1. "More severe" always means a higher ordinal.

The assigned nurse determines the disposition for the call's `ACTIVE` assessment, moving the call `ASSESSING → DISPOSITIONED`. The body carries `call_id`, `assessment_id`, the `final_level` the nurse actually intends to give the caller, and `expected_call_version` for the version handshake described in Section 2.7; `override_reason_code` and `override_narrative` accompany it whenever the nurse departs from the recommendation. The named assessment must exist, must have `status` `ACTIVE` — a superseded one is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_SUPERSEDED` — and must belong to the call named in the body, or the answer is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_CALL_MISMATCH`. The acting nurse must be the call's assigned nurse and must hold a licence for the call's `caller_state_code` whose derived status is `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_NURSE_NOT_LICENSED_IN_STATE`.

`recommended_level` is computed by the server from the active assessment and is never editable by anyone. It is produced by banding the acuity score and then applying four override steps **in a fixed order**, each step reading the level the previous step produced:

- **Step one — band the score.** The level is `EMERGENCY_911` when `acuity_score` is at least 85; `ED_NOW` when it is at least 70 and at most 84; `URGENT_CARE_24H` when it is at least 50 and at most 69; `OFFICE_APPT_72H` when it is at least 30 and at most 49; and `HOME_CARE` when it is below 30. A score of exactly 85 bands to `EMERGENCY_911` and a score of 84 bands to `ED_NOW`.
- **Step two — the hard protocol override.** If the protocol's `emergency_override` flag is true and the assessment's `red_flag_count` is at least one, the level becomes `EMERGENCY_911` regardless of which band step one produced.
- **Step three — the infant floor.** If the assessment's `age_years_used` is strictly under one year and the level standing after step two is `OFFICE_APPT_72H` or `HOME_CARE`, the level becomes `URGENT_CARE_24H`. Children under one are never sent home and never told to wait three days.
- **Step four — the after-hours floor.** If the call's `is_after_hours` is true and the level standing after step three is `OFFICE_APPT_72H`, the level becomes `URGENT_CARE_24H`. No office appointment can be booked while the practice's own office is closed.
- **Step five — the protocol floor.** Finally, the level is compared with the protocol's `min_disposition_level` on the severity scale. If the level's ordinal is at least the floor's ordinal it stands; otherwise `recommended_level` is the protocol's `min_disposition_level`.

The order is load-bearing. Steps three and four both consume the level that step two produced, and step five consumes the level that steps three and four produced. Applying the protocol floor before the hard protocol override would let a floor of `HOME_CARE` erase the 911 jump; applying the infant floor before the banding would have nothing to read. Worked through: an acuity of 70 on a non-override protocol at age 36, in-hours, with a floor of `HOME_CARE` bands to `ED_NOW`, leaves steps two through four inert, and survives step five as `ED_NOW`. An acuity of 62 — which bands to `URGENT_CARE_24H` — on an `emergency_override` protocol with one red flag is promoted by step two to `EMERGENCY_911`. An acuity of 40 bands to `OFFICE_APPT_72H`, and a scored age of zero raises it by step three to `URGENT_CARE_24H`; the same acuity of 40 at age 36 on an after-hours call is raised to `URGENT_CARE_24H` by step four instead. An acuity of 20 bands to `HOME_CARE`, and a protocol floor of `OFFICE_APPT_72H` raises it by step five from ordinal one to ordinal two.

What the nurse may do with that recommendation is asymmetric by design. A `TriageNurse` may set `final_level` equal to `recommended_level`, or **more severe** than it, but never less severe. A `final_level` whose ordinal is below the computed `recommended_level` on the create path is refused with `ERR_DEESCALATION_REQUIRES_CHARGE_NURSE`, because softening advice is a supervisory act performed against an already-created disposition. Any `final_level` that differs from `recommended_level` in either direction is an override and demands documentation: `override_reason_code` must be present, chosen from `CALLER_REFUSED_ED`, `TRANSPORT_UNAVAILABLE`, `CLINICIAN_DIRECTED`, `PROTOCOL_MISMATCH` and `LANGUAGE_BARRIER`, and `override_narrative` must be at least forty characters long. A missing code or a shorter narrative is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`. The flag `is_override` is set true whenever `final_level` differs from `recommended_level` at any point in the disposition's life.

A successful determination creates the disposition with `advice_status` `AUTHORIZED`, `audit_status` `AWAITING_OUTCOME`, `determined_by_nurse_id` set to the acting nurse's identifier, `overridden_by_nurse_id` absent, `callback_verified_at` absent, and `requires_callback` computed as described below. The call's `status` becomes `DISPOSITIONED` and its `version` increases by one. Two further consequences follow.

The first is the callback obligation. `requires_callback` is true unless **both** of these hold: the `final_level` is `HOME_CARE` **and** the call's `is_after_hours` is false. In other words only in-hours home-care advice is exempt from a verification callback; after-hours home-care advice given on behalf of a closed practice always owes one, because there is no office to fall back on in the morning. When a callback is owed, exactly one callback task is created at `status` `PENDING`, with its `practice_id` copied from the call's practice and its `assigned_nurse_id` copied from the disposition's `determined_by_nurse_id`, and the practice's `open_callback_count` increases by one. The task's `risk_class` is `HIGH_RISK` when the `final_level` ordinal is at least four — that is, `ED_NOW` or `EMERGENCY_911`; otherwise it is `NEXT_DAY` when the call is after-hours and the ordinal is at most two — that is, `OFFICE_APPT_72H` or `HOME_CARE`; otherwise it is `STANDARD`. `due_by_utc` follows from the class and the disposition's own `created_at`: two hours after `created_at` for a `HIGH_RISK` task, twelve hours after `created_at` for a `STANDARD` task, and for a `NEXT_DAY` task the instant formed by taking the calendar date of `created_at`, adding one day, and applying the practice's `office_open_utc_hhmm` as the time of day — which is exactly why these tasks are called the practice's next-day callback list. `max_attempts` is 3 for a `HIGH_RISK` task and 2 for every other class. An `EMERGENCY_911` disposition created at 2026-08-01T14:30:00Z therefore owes a `HIGH_RISK` callback due 2026-08-01T16:30:00Z with three permitted attempts; an `URGENT_CARE_24H` disposition created at the same instant owes a `STANDARD` callback due 2026-08-02T02:30:00Z with two; a `HOME_CARE` disposition on an after-hours call created at that instant for a practice that opens at 13:00 owes a `NEXT_DAY` callback due 2026-08-02T13:00:00Z; and a `HOME_CARE` disposition on an in-hours call owes nothing and no task is created at all.

The second is the warm-transfer hold, which is a three-hop chain. When the `final_level` is `EMERGENCY_911`, the first hop sets the call's `requires_warm_transfer` to true; the second hop, caused by that flag turning on, increases the determining nurse's `pending_high_risk_count` by one; and the third hop, caused by that count reaching the nurse's `high_risk_cap`, sets that nurse's `intake_eligibility` to `OFF_ROTATION`, which immediately removes them from the routing pool for every subsequent call.

Overriding a determined disposition is the charge nurse's compensating operation, and it moves the advice dimension `AUTHORIZED → OVERRIDDEN`. The body carries a new `final_level`, a mandatory `override_reason_code` and a mandatory `override_narrative` of at least forty characters. Five gates apply. The disposition's `advice_status` must be `AUTHORIZED` — a `VOIDED` disposition or one already `OVERRIDDEN` is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE` — and the call's `status` must be `DISPOSITIONED`. The acting charge nurse must hold an `ACTIVE` licence in the call's `caller_state_code`. If the disposition's `recommended_level` is `EMERGENCY_911`, then the only permitted `final_level` is `EMERGENCY_911` itself: de-escalating away from an emergency recommendation is forbidden outright and answers `ERR_EMERGENCY_DEESCALATION_FORBIDDEN`. The requested `final_level` must be within one severity step of the disposition's current `final_level` in either direction — a difference of two or more ordinals is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_STEP_LIMIT`. And the acting nurse must not be the disposition's `determined_by_nurse_id`, or the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A successful override sets `final_level` to the requested level, sets `is_override` true, records `override_reason_code`, `override_narrative` and `overridden_by_nurse_id` as the acting nurse's identifier, moves `advice_status` to `OVERRIDDEN`, and increases both the disposition's `version` and the call's `version` by one each. It then recomputes the callback obligation from scratch against the new `final_level`, using exactly the rules stated above. If the new level is less severe and the existing callback task's `status` is `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED`, that task flips to `CANCELLED` and the practice's `open_callback_count` decreases by one; and if the recomputed `requires_callback` is still true, a replacement task is created carrying the `risk_class`, `due_by_utc` and `max_attempts` of the **new** level, and `open_callback_count` increases again. An `ED_NOW` disposition whose `HIGH_RISK` task is de-escalated to `OFFICE_APPT_72H` in-hours therefore ends with the high-risk task `CANCELLED` and a fresh `STANDARD` task due twelve hours after the new task's creation. Separately, if the disposition's `final_level` had been `EMERGENCY_911` and the new level is not, the call's `requires_warm_transfer` is set back to false, the `determined_by_nurse_id` nurse's `pending_high_risk_count` decreases by one, and that nurse's `intake_eligibility` is restored once the count falls below their `high_risk_cap`.

The advice dimension has one further move, driven entirely by the system rather than by any request. A disposition whose `advice_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `OVERRIDDEN` becomes `VOIDED` when the assessment it was built on is superseded by a later assessment, or when its call is abandoned. Voiding sets `advice_status` to `VOIDED`, cancels that disposition's callback tasks that were `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED` while decreasing the practice's `open_callback_count` once per cancelled task, clears the call's `requires_warm_transfer` and decreases the determining nurse's `pending_high_risk_count` by one if this disposition was the one that set that flag, and increases the disposition's `version` by one. `VOIDED` is terminal on the advice dimension. Crucially, voiding does **not** touch `audit_status` — the two dimensions on the row are independent, which is why a disposition that has already been audited can still be voided, and why its audit record survives even as it drops out of the practice's concordance input set. Disposition listings may be narrowed by `call_id`, by `assessment_id`, by `determined_by_nurse_id`, by `overridden_by_nurse_id`, by `advice_status` and by `audit_status`, and a single read exposes both state dimensions side by side.

### 3.7. Warm Transfer to Emergency Services

When a disposition lands on `EMERGENCY_911`, the caller is not simply told to hang up and dial 911 — the nurse stays on the line and hands the call over to a public-safety answering point, with the caller's physical location captured and read back. Opening a transfer is reserved to the call's assigned nurse and is only permitted while the call's `requires_warm_transfer` is true; a call that does not need one is refused with `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_NOT_INDICATED`. Only one transfer may be in play per call at a time: a request is refused with `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_ALREADY_OPEN` when a transfer for that call already sits at `INITIATED`, `LOCATION_CONFIRMED`, `ACCEPTED_BY_PSAP` or `HANDOFF_COMPLETE`. Only a transfer that ended at `REJECTED_BY_PSAP` frees the slot for a second attempt.

The body captures the destination and the location together: `psap_center_code`, three to twenty characters of uppercase letters, digits or hyphens, identifying the answering point; `location_source`, one of `CALLER_STATED`, `DEVICE_GPS` or `PRACTICE_RECORD`, recording where the location came from; `location_address_line`, the street address for dispatch, one to two hundred characters; `location_state_code`, two uppercase letters; `location_lat` and `location_lng` as decimal degrees; and `callback_number`, one to thirty-two characters, the number dispatch can call back on. A latitude outside minus ninety through ninety degrees, a longitude outside minus one hundred eighty through one hundred eighty degrees, or an empty address line is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOCATION`. The `location_state_code` must equal the call's `caller_state_code`, because dispatch is routed on the caller's state of record; a mismatch is a data error rather than a routing choice and is refused with `ERR_LOCATION_STATE_MISMATCH`. A created transfer starts at `status` `INITIATED` with `location_verified_by`, `location_confidence_pct` and `psap_incident_number` all absent. A call's transfers may be listed, narrowed by `status`.

The assigned nurse then reads the location back to the caller and attests to it, moving the transfer `INITIATED → LOCATION_CONFIRMED`. The only input is `location_confidence_pct`, between 0 and 100, and it must be at least 75; anything lower is refused with `ERR_LOCATION_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW`, so a transfer can never be offered to a dispatch centre on a guess. On success the transfer records `location_verified_by` as the acting nurse's identifier along with the supplied `location_confidence_pct`, moves to `LOCATION_CONFIRMED`, and increases its `version` by one.

The dispatch centre's answer is relayed by the intake agent and branches. The body carries `accepted`, a boolean, and `psap_incident_number` when the answer is positive. On acceptance the transfer records `psap_incident_number` and moves `LOCATION_CONFIRMED → ACCEPTED_BY_PSAP`; the incident number is required on this branch. On refusal the transfer moves `LOCATION_CONFIRMED → REJECTED_BY_PSAP`, `psap_incident_number` stays absent, and — this is the part that matters clinically — the call's `requires_warm_transfer` stays true, so the call still cannot be completed until a fresh transfer reaches `HANDOFF_COMPLETE`. Rejection is terminal for that transfer but re-opens the one-transfer-per-call slot. Either branch increases the transfer's `version` by one.

The assigned nurse closes out an accepted transfer, moving `ACCEPTED_BY_PSAP → HANDOFF_COMPLETE` with no body at all. Completion sets the transfer's `status` to `HANDOFF_COMPLETE` and increases its `version` by one, and it also stamps `warm_transfer_completed_at` on the call with the current logical-clock instant and increases the call's `version` by one. That terminal state is the precondition for closing the call itself. Requesting any of the three transfer moves from a state that does not permit it is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`, and naming a transfer that does not exist is refused with `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.8. Callback Verification and Escalation

A callback task is never created by hand; it exists because a disposition owed one, and it carries `disposition_id` pointing back at that disposition, `practice_id` denormalised from the disposition's call so that a practice's callback list is a single filtered read, and `assigned_nurse_id` copied from the disposition's `determined_by_nurse_id`. That copy is deliberate and slightly counter-intuitive: the nurse who gave the advice is the nurse who **works** the task — who dials, redials and logs whether anyone picked up — but is barred from **verifying** its clinical outcome. Logging an unanswered ring is clerical; asking a caller whether they got worse is a clinical judgement, and it must come from a second pair of eyes.

Recording an attempt is reserved to the task's `assigned_nurse_id`; any other nurse receives `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_NURSE`. The body carries a single `outcome` of `NO_ANSWER`, `BUSY` or `REACHED`, and it is stored on the task as `last_attempt_outcome`. The three outcomes branch differently, and the branch on a failed attempt depends on how many attempts have already been logged.

A failed attempt — `NO_ANSWER` or `BUSY` — where `attempt_count` plus one is still strictly below `max_attempts` increases `attempt_count` by one, records `last_attempt_outcome`, increases the task's `version` by one, and leaves the task at `ATTEMPTED`. This is a genuine self-loop from `PENDING` on the first such attempt and from `ATTEMPTED` on later ones: the second failed attempt on a `HIGH_RISK` task, which permits three, leaves the status reading `ATTEMPTED` while the counter advances from one to two.

A failed attempt where `attempt_count` plus one reaches or exceeds `max_attempts` instead exhausts the task. It increases `attempt_count` by one, records `last_attempt_outcome`, moves the status to the terminal `EXPIRED`, decreases the practice's `open_callback_count` by one, and increases the task's `version` by one. And when that expired task's `risk_class` is `HIGH_RISK`, it also drives the parent disposition's audit dimension `AWAITING_OUTCOME → VARIANCE_ESCALATED` together with the full practice variance cascade described below — an unreachable high-risk caller is itself a compliance variance, not merely an administrative loose end. An expired task of any other risk class escalates nothing.

An `outcome` of `REACHED` moves the task from `PENDING` or `ATTEMPTED` to `REACHED`, records `last_attempt_outcome` as `REACHED`, increases the task's `version` by one, and pointedly leaves `attempt_count` **unchanged** — a call that was answered is not a failed attempt, so a task reached on the second dial still shows one prior failed attempt rather than two. Recording an attempt against a task that is `REACHED`, `VERIFIED`, `ESCALATED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_CALLBACK_INVALID_STATE`.

Verification is only possible from `REACHED`, and it is where segregation of duties bites: the acting nurse must not be the disposition's `determined_by_nurse_id`, and an attempt by that nurse is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. For a `HIGH_RISK` task there is also a hard deadline — the logical clock must be at or before the task's `due_by_utc`, and a clock strictly past that instant is refused with `ERR_CALLBACK_PAST_DUE`, because a late high-risk verification cannot be back-dated into compliance. Tasks of other risk classes carry no such deadline on verification. The body supplies `patient_condition`, one of `IMPROVED`, `UNCHANGED` or `WORSENED`; `advice_followed`, a boolean recording whether the caller actually did what they were told; and `verifier_attestation`, the verifying nurse's attestation text. All three are recorded, along with `verified_by_nurse_id` as the acting nurse's identifier and `verified_at` as the current logical-clock instant, and the task's `version` increases by one.

The clinical answer then branches. When `patient_condition` is `IMPROVED` or `UNCHANGED`, the task moves `REACHED → VERIFIED`, the practice's `open_callback_count` decreases by one, and the parent disposition has `callback_verified_at` stamped with the current logical-clock instant and its `version` increased by one. When `patient_condition` is `WORSENED`, the task moves `REACHED → ESCALATED` instead, the practice's `open_callback_count` decreases by one, the practice's cumulative `escalated_callback_count` increases by one, and a four-hop cascade runs: the parent disposition's `audit_status` moves `AWAITING_OUTCOME → VARIANCE_ESCALATED`; because that disposition entered variance escalation, the practice's `open_variance_count` increases by one; because `open_variance_count` reached the practice's `variance_review_threshold`, the practice's `compliance_posture` becomes `UNDER_REVIEW`; and because the posture became `UNDER_REVIEW`, every coverage window of that practice whose `status` is `DRAFT` flips to `BLOCKED` with `suspend_reason` set to `PRACTICE_UNDER_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`. The two verification branches are symmetric in what they record and asymmetric only in their consequences, and both are terminal for the task.

Cancellation is the fourth way a task can end, and it is never requested directly. A task whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED` flips to `CANCELLED` when the parent disposition's `advice_status` becomes `VOIDED`, or when a supervisor's override softens the disposition to a level whose callback obligation is served by a replacement task. Cancellation decreases the practice's `open_callback_count` by one and increases the task's `version` by one.

Callback listings are the operational workhorse of the advice line. They may be narrowed by `practice_id`, by `disposition_id`, by `assigned_nurse_id`, by `verified_by_nurse_id`, by `status`, by `risk_class`, and by `due_before_utc`, which restricts results to tasks falling due before a supplied instant. A practice liaison filtering to their own practice with a `due_before_utc` of the next morning is reading exactly the next-day callback list the after-hours contract promises them.

### 3.9. Closing, Billing and Reopening a Call

Only the assigned nurse closes a call, and only from `DISPOSITIONED`, moving it to `CLOSED`. The single input is `handle_minutes`, the nurse-attested wall time spent on the call, which must be at least 1 and at most 240. One gate stands in front of closure: if the call's `requires_warm_transfer` is true, then some warm transfer for that call must have reached `HANDOFF_COMPLETE`, and otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_WARM_TRANSFER_INCOMPLETE`. A caller who was told to call 911 is never allowed to fall off the end of the workflow without a dispatch centre having taken the handoff.

Billing happens at closure and is a two-stage computation with the rounding pinned to a single point. First, `billable_minutes` is `handle_minutes` rounded **up** to the next whole multiple of five: thirteen minutes bills as fifteen, eighteen as twenty, twenty-eight as thirty, and exactly twenty bills as twenty with no inflation to twenty-five. Second, `advice_fee_cents` is the product of `billable_minutes` and the practice's `per_minute_rate_millicents`, divided by one thousand to convert millicents to cents, and rounded half up to a whole cent. With a rate of 4225 millicents per minute, thirteen handle-minutes give fifteen billable minutes and 63.375 cents, which rounds to 63; eighteen handle-minutes give twenty billable minutes and exactly 84.5 cents, which rounds **up** to 85 rather than down to 84; twenty-eight handle-minutes give thirty billable minutes and 126.75 cents, which rounds to 127.

The practice ledgers then accumulate the already-rounded per-call figures: `billed_advice_minutes_total` is credited with this call's `billable_minutes` and `advice_fee_accrued_cents` is credited with this call's `advice_fee_cents`. Rounding happens once per call and never once per practice. Two completed eighteen-minute calls on a practice at that rate leave `advice_fee_accrued_cents` at 170 and `billed_advice_minutes_total` at 40 — not the 169 that summing forty raw minutes of millicents and rounding at the end would produce. That one-cent gap is the observable signature of getting the order wrong.

Closure also stamps `closed_at` with the current logical-clock instant, decreases the assigned nurse's `active_call_load` by one, and — when the call had `requires_warm_transfer` set — decreases that nurse's `pending_high_risk_count` by one and restores their `intake_eligibility` to `ON_ROTATION` once the count falls below their `high_risk_cap`. The call's `version` increases by one.

Reopening is the charge nurse's compensating operation and the one cycle back out of a terminal-looking state, moving the call `CLOSED → DISPOSITIONED`. It is available only while the logical clock is strictly earlier than seventy-two hours after the call's `closed_at`; at or beyond that instant the request is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. An optional `reopen_reason` may accompany the request. A reopen debits the practice's `billed_advice_minutes_total` by exactly this call's stored `billable_minutes` and its `advice_fee_accrued_cents` by exactly this call's stored `advice_fee_cents`, leaving both ledgers at their pre-completion values; because the debit reuses the stored figures rather than recomputing them, a rate change between closure and reopen cannot strand cents on the ledger. The call's own `handle_minutes` returns to absent, `billable_minutes` to 0 and `advice_fee_cents` to 0; `reopen_count` increases by one; the assigned nurse's `active_call_load` increases by one because the call is live again; and where `requires_warm_transfer` is true, the nurse's `pending_high_risk_count` increases by one and their `intake_eligibility` is re-evaluated against `high_risk_cap`. The call's `version` increases by one. `closed_at` is deliberately **retained** rather than cleared, so the seventy-two-hour window stays anchored to the original completion and a call cannot be reopened indefinitely by reopening and re-closing it.

### 3.10. Outcome Audit, Concordance and Management Readouts

Weeks after the advice was given, the outcome feed reports what actually happened, and a quality reviewer files it against the disposition. Filing an outcome carries `reported_outcome`, one of `ED_ADMITTED`, `ED_TREATED_RELEASED`, `URGENT_CARE_VISIT`, `OFFICE_VISIT` or `SELF_CARE_RESOLVED`; `outcome_source`, one of `EHR_CLAIM`, `PATIENT_REPORT` or `ED_FEED`, recording the provenance; and `outcome_observed_at`, the instant the outcome was observed, which must be at or after the disposition's `created_at` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_OUTCOME_PREDATES_DISPOSITION`. Three further preconditions apply: the disposition's `advice_status` must be `AUTHORIZED` or `OVERRIDDEN`, so a `VOIDED` disposition cannot be audited at all; its `audit_status` must be `AWAITING_OUTCOME`, and a disposition that already carries an audit record is refused with `ERR_OUTCOME_ALREADY_RECORDED` because records are immutable and one-per-disposition; and where the disposition's `requires_callback` is true, the callback task for it must have reached one of `VERIFIED`, `ESCALATED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED`, with a task still `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `REACHED` refused as `ERR_CALLBACK_NOT_RESOLVED`. The outcome cannot be judged while the advice line is still trying to reach the patient.

The record itself carries the two ordinals it compares. `outcome_ordinal` is the severity of `reported_outcome` on a shared five-point scale, where `ED_ADMITTED` is 5, `ED_TREATED_RELEASED` is 4, `URGENT_CARE_VISIT` is 3, `OFFICE_VISIT` is 2 and `SELF_CARE_RESOLVED` is 1. `disposition_level_ordinal` is the severity of the disposition's `final_level` — never its `recommended_level` — captured at the moment the record was filed, on the same five-point scale defined in Section 3.6. The comparison therefore follows a charge nurse's override: the same reported outcome yields a different verdict once the advice has been revised. `variance_class` is `CONCORDANT` when the two ordinals are equal, `UNDERTRIAGE` when the outcome ordinal exceeds the disposition ordinal by at least one — the patient turned out sicker than the advice implied — and `OVERTRIAGE` when the outcome ordinal falls below the disposition ordinal by at least one. `variance_severity_points` weights the gap asymmetrically, because under-triage is the dangerous direction: it is zero for a concordant record, seven points per ordinal step of divergence for an under-triage record, and three points per ordinal step for an over-triage record. Advice of `ED_NOW` against an outcome of `ED_ADMITTED` is a one-step under-triage worth 7 points; advice of `ED_NOW` against `ED_TREATED_RELEASED` is concordant and worth zero; advice of `EMERGENCY_911` against `SELF_CARE_RESOLVED` is a four-step over-triage worth 12 points.

Filing a record always moves the audit dimension `AWAITING_OUTCOME → OUTCOME_RECORDED` and increases the disposition's `version` by one. Within the very same request the classification then decides whether the audit dimension advances again. A `CONCORDANT` record advances it `OUTCOME_RECORDED → CONCORDANT_CLOSED`, which is terminal and quiet. An `UNDERTRIAGE` record advances it `OUTCOME_RECORDED → VARIANCE_ESCALATED` and sets off the same four-hop cascade the worsened callback does: the practice's `open_variance_count` increases by one; because that count reached the practice's `variance_review_threshold`, `compliance_posture` becomes `UNDER_REVIEW`; and because the posture flipped, every `DRAFT` coverage window of that practice becomes `BLOCKED` with `suspend_reason` `PRACTICE_UNDER_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`. An `OVERTRIAGE` record does neither: it leaves the audit dimension resting at `OUTCOME_RECORDED`, escalates nothing, and touches no practice counter. Only under-triage escalates.

The practice concordance readout answers a question two different constituencies ask in two incompatible ways, and it returns both answers in the same response. Its input set is the audit records whose `created_at` falls at or after the practice's `audit_period_start_utc` and strictly before the logical clock, whose disposition's `advice_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `OVERRIDDEN` — voided advice is excluded — and whose disposition's call belongs to that practice. `audited_count` is the size of that set. The regulator's strict reading counts a record as concordant only when its `outcome_ordinal` and `disposition_level_ordinal` are exactly equal, and reports that count as `concordant_count_strict`. The clinical committee's band-tolerant reading counts a record as concordant when the two ordinals differ by at most one step in either direction, and reports that count as `concordant_count_band_tolerant`. Each count becomes a rate the same way: when `audited_count` is zero the rate is 0.0 and never a division error; otherwise the rate is one hundred multiplied by the concordant count, divided by `audited_count`, rounded half up to one decimal place. The two rates are reported as `concordance_rate_strict_pct` and `concordance_rate_band_tolerant_pct`. A single record with an ordinal gap of exactly one drives them to opposite extremes: 0.0 strict against 100.0 band-tolerant. Three records with gaps of zero, plus one and minus three give one of three strictly, or 33.3, and two of three band-tolerantly, or 66.7. With no records in the period both rates are 0.0. And a record whose disposition was later voided still exists but drops out of the input set, lowering `audited_count` by one and shifting both rates.

The same readout reports the raw material behind the cascade: `undertriage_count` and `overtriage_count` as the number of records in that input set whose `variance_class` is `UNDERTRIAGE` and `OVERTRIAGE` respectively, `variance_severity_points_total` as the sum of `variance_severity_points` over the whole input set, and the practice's live `open_variance_count` and `compliance_posture` as they stand. Those ten fields — `audited_count`, `concordant_count_strict`, `concordant_count_band_tolerant`, `concordance_rate_strict_pct`, `concordance_rate_band_tolerant_pct`, `undertriage_count`, `overtriage_count`, `variance_severity_points_total`, `open_variance_count` and `compliance_posture` — are the whole of this readout's `data` object: unlike the staffing readout it echoes nothing of its request back, so it carries no `practice_id`, and being a computed figure rather than a stored row it carries no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` either.

The after-hours invoice readout prices the coverage contract, and its five steps must be evaluated in order because step three reads the output of step one rather than a running total. Step one sets `advice_subtotal_cents` to the practice's `advice_fee_accrued_cents`, and the readout also surfaces `billed_advice_minutes_total` and `escalated_callback_count` unchanged so the arithmetic can be checked. Step two sets `escalation_gross_cents` to `escalated_callback_count` multiplied by twenty-five dollars per escalation, expressed as 2500 cents each. Step three selects `coverage_credit_pct` from a three-tier table keyed on `advice_subtotal_cents` **alone**: 0 percent when the subtotal is at most 99 cents, 5 percent when it is at least 100 and at most 999 cents, and 10 percent when it is at least 1000 cents. Step four sets `coverage_credit_cents` to `escalation_gross_cents` multiplied by `coverage_credit_pct`, divided by one hundred and rounded half up to a whole cent; this is the only rounding in the whole computation. Step five sets `invoice_total_cents` to `advice_subtotal_cents` plus `escalation_gross_cents` minus `coverage_credit_cents`.

Keying the tier on the running total instead would be circular — the credit would depend on a figure the credit itself reduces — and it silently changes the answer whenever the escalation charges push that running total past 1000 cents. With accrued advice fees of 127 cents and one escalated callback, the gross is 2500, the tier is read off 127 and is therefore 5 percent, the credit is 125, and `invoice_total_cents` is 2502; the circular misreading would read the tier off 2627, take 10 percent, credit 250, and arrive at the wrong answer of 2377. With accrued fees of 99 cents and two escalations the gross is 5000, the tier is 0 percent, the credit is zero, and the total is 5099; with accrued fees of 100 cents and the same two escalations the tier becomes 5 percent, the credit is 250, and the total is 4850 — one extra cent of subtotal removes 250 cents of charge. With no escalated callbacks at all the gross and the credit are both zero and the total equals the accrued advice fees exactly.

The staffing-adequacy readout compares supply against demand for one state over one window, and all three of its query parameters — `state_code`, `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` — are mandatory at request time; a missing one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Demand is derived from volume: `offered_call_count` is the number of calls whose `caller_state_code` equals the queried `state_code` and whose `created_at` falls at or after `window_start_utc` and strictly before `window_end_utc`. `required_nurse_minutes` is `offered_call_count` multiplied by an average handle time of fourteen minutes, then divided by a target occupancy of eighty percent — that is, multiplied by one hundred and divided by eighty — with the result rounded up to a whole minute. Supply is derived from the roster: `scheduled_nurse_minutes` is the sum, over every nurse shift, of the whole minutes by which the shift's half-open interval overlaps the queried half-open window, counting only shifts whose nurse holds a licence whose `state_code` equals the queried `state_code` and whose `expires_on` is on or after the calendar date of `window_start_utc`. `licensed_nurse_count` reports how many distinct nurses satisfied that licence condition and so contributed to the supply side.

`adequacy_ratio_pct` is 100.0 whenever `required_nurse_minutes` is zero — the zero-demand case is always fully adequate and never a division error — and otherwise one hundred multiplied by `scheduled_nurse_minutes`, divided by `required_nurse_minutes`, rounded half up to one decimal place. `staffing_posture` is `ADEQUATE` when the ratio is at least 100.0, `STRAINED` when it is at least 80.0 and strictly below 100.0, and `CRITICAL` when it is below 80.0; the 80.0 boundary belongs to `STRAINED`. Two offered calls demand exactly thirty-five required minutes: two multiplied by fourteen multiplied by one hundred is 2800, and 2800 divided by 80 is 35 with nothing left to round up. Against those thirty-five required minutes, twenty-eight scheduled minutes give a ratio of 80.0 and a posture of `STRAINED`; twenty-seven scheduled minutes give 77.142857, reported as 77.1, and a posture of `CRITICAL`; thirty-five scheduled minutes give 100.0 and `ADEQUATE`. With no offered calls the required minutes are zero, the ratio is 100.0 and the posture is `ADEQUATE`. A shift whose nurse is licensed only in another state contributes zero scheduled minutes and does not appear in `licensed_nurse_count`, so adding that nurse to the roster cannot move the posture — which is precisely what makes licensure a staffing constraint rather than a routing detail. The readout echoes `state_code`, `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` back alongside the computed figures.
