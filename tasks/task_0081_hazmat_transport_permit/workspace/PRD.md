# Hazmat Transport Permit Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

This document specifies the externally observable behaviour of the dangerous-goods transportation permit management service, a regulator-and-carrier-facing REST API whose domain name is *hazmat-transport-permit* and whose URL prefix is `/v1` on every endpoint. The originating scenario, recorded verbatim in the project's scaffold, reads: "Dangerous goods transportation permit management: transportation company qualifications, driver qualifications, vehicle inspection, transportation route approval, GPS monitoring, penalties for violations, and annual permit renewal.  difficulty set to Advanced, not be too hard". The accompanying build-constraints document `INSTRUCTION.md` (sitting beside this file in the workspace) names the stack and the Dockerfile shape the reference implementation must conform to; the present document is the *behavioural* specification — what the system shall do — while `INSTRUCTION.md` is the *operational-shell* specification — how the system is packaged and exposed to the test harness.

The system models seven core business resources and two append-only event logs. A **TransportCompany** is the regulated carrier that holds a hazmat licence and carries a prepaid wallet measured in cents. A **Driver** belongs to one company and carries a hazmat endorsement attested by the regulator; a **Vehicle** likewise belongs to one company and carries a periodic-inspection history. The **VehicleInspection** records are the append-only certificates the regulator writes against a vehicle, each carrying an outcome of `PASSED`, `FAILED`, or `CONDITIONAL`. A **TransportRoute** is a polyline-defined geographic corridor the carrier applies to have approved for hazmat transit. A **Permit** is the central aggregate — it names one company, one route, a non-empty subset of the company's drivers, and a non-empty subset of the company's vehicles, and its `lifecycle_status` is a posture-driven aggregate derived from the validity of those five grounding inputs plus the wall-clock time window. A **Trip** is a single transit run authorized under an active permit, binding one of the permit's authorized drivers to one of its authorized vehicles within a scheduled time window. **GPSReport** rows are append-only telemetry samples attributed to an in-progress trip, and **Penalty** rows are the append-only disciplinary ledger keyed by a polymorphic `(subject_type, subject_id)` pair drawn from the values `DRIVER`, `COMPANY`, and `VEHICLE`.

Four user roles interact with the system: a `CompanyOperator` acts on behalf of a single TransportCompany and drives the carrier-side workflow; a `RegulatorOfficer` represents the government regulator and performs every approval, attestation, inspection, issuance, verification, and appeal-adjudication action; a `Driver` is an individual driver whose API access is limited to self-reading their own row, their own trips, and their own disciplinary summary; and a `TestHarness` role exists only in test deployments to drive the two internal endpoints that advance the logical clock and run the daily compliance sweep on demand. The benchmark's testability-completeness principle requires that every time-gated automatic transition be reachable through the API surface, and the test-harness pair of endpoints is the load-bearing mechanism that closes the loop on the wall-clock-driven `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` permit transition, the endorsement-validity-expiry `VALID → INVALID` driver transition, and the rolling-window trailing-edge fall-off on the driver's penalty-point total.

The system's hard structural commitments are five: the response envelope is a fixed JSON shape with a `meta` object holding the request id and timestamp and a `data` field holding either the resource payload or the `error_id`-and-`message` error body (see §2.2); pagination follows the standard `limit`/`offset`/`total_count`/`has_more` quartet (see §2.3); mutable resources carry a quartet of common system fields `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version` while the three append-only event entities `VehicleInspection`, `GPSReport`, and `Penalty` carry only `id` and `created_at` because the rows are never mutated in place (see §2.4); validation errors arrive in a strict precedence order across the seven tiers of authentication, authorization, existence, state precondition, business safety, business referential and fiscal, and idempotency-or-OCC checks (see §2.7); and the conservation laws on the company wallet's currency and on the driver's rolling-window disciplinary-points total are exact integer identities the test layer can audit against arbitrary action sequences (see §2.11). The remainder of this document is organized into the global cross-cutting principles of §2, the per-aggregate business-logic narratives of §3, the endpoint reference table of §4, and the error catalogue of §5.

The domain's complexity is concentrated in two dual-variant calculations on the Permit aggregate and on the Driver's disciplinary standing. The permit's validity is the boolean conjunction of five legs evaluated against either the strict on-disk snapshot last written by a regulator's verify-compliance call or the live cross-entity recompute performed at read time, and the difference between the two answers reveals whether a grounding input has flipped since the most-recent verification. The driver's penalty-point total is likewise read both as a frozen snapshot stamped onto the permit at issue time (the audit-forensic answer "was the driver lawful at the moment the permit was issued?") and as the live rolling-window-sum recomputed on every penalty creation, every appeal-uphold, and every daily sweep tick (the operational answer "is the driver above the suspension threshold right now?"). These two dual-variant axes — Permit posture and Driver standing — are the conceptual core of the regulatory domain. Three cascading effects connect them: a GPS-report whose observed speed exceeds the hazmat speed limit triggers an automatic speed-violation Penalty against the trip's driver, which on threshold-crossing flips the driver's endorsement to `INVALID`, which on the next verify-compliance fires the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` transition on every permit listing that driver; a regulator's revocation of a company's qualification or a regulator's rejection of an inspection on a vehicle propagates by the same deferred-cascade mechanism to every permit that names the affected entity; and a wall-clock advance past a permit's `valid_until` boundary fires the periodic-sweep's `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` transition with the synchronous fan-out abort of every still-open child trip with `abort_reason = PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE`.

## 2. Global Specifications and System-Wide Principles

The following subsections describe rules that span the whole API surface — the authentication and authorization contract, the envelope and the pagination shape, the system-managed fields that appear on every mutable resource, the deterministic-clock and the periodic-sweep test affordances, the cross-tenant isolation rule, the precedence ordering among the validation tiers, the shared enumerations the entities draw their categorical values from, the half-open and the closed-on-prior-edge interval semantics, the optimistic-concurrency and idempotency mechanisms, and the two independent conservation invariants on the wallet and on the disciplinary-point ledger. The per-aggregate workflows in §3 reference these subsections by name when a workflow-level rule is the realization of a system-level invariant.

### 2.1. Roles, the Authentication Headers, and the Identity Model

Every request carries two mandatory headers identifying the acting principal: `X-User-ID` is the principal's opaque-or-entity-backed identifier and `X-User-Role` is the role-name string. The four canonical role names recognized by the system are `CompanyOperator`, `RegulatorOfficer`, `Driver`, and `TestHarness`. A request that omits either header, that supplies an empty string in either field, or that supplies a role name outside the four canonical strings is rejected at the top of the validation pipeline with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` returned at HTTP status 401. A request whose headers parse as a canonical role but whose role lacks the privilege bit for the targeted endpoint per the RBAC table is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` at HTTP status 403 — the default-deny outcome that fires whenever the requesting role simply is not in the endpoint's allow-set.

The four roles partition the API surface as follows. A **CompanyOperator** drives the carrier-side workflow: filing the initial company application via the company-create endpoint, submitting the company application for regulator review, registering drivers and vehicles under the company, submitting route applications, filing permit applications, scheduling trips under issued permits, advancing trips through depart-arrive-and-abort, pushing GPS-telemetry samples up the gps-report ingest path, and renewing permits annually. The identity associated with a `CompanyOperator` request is *entity-backed* on the TransportCompany table: the `X-User-ID` of an operator's request is the `id` of the TransportCompany the operator is acting for, and a syntactically valid `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to a TransportCompany row is treated as an invalid credential and returns 401 `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` per the auth-tier resolution rule. The lone exception is the very first company-create request, where the operator has not yet been issued a company id: in that bootstrap call the server mints the row's `id` server-side and the client switches its `X-User-ID` to the returned value for every subsequent call. Cross-tenant access by an operator (the operator's `X-User-ID` does not equal the targeted row's effective owning-company id) is denied with a *distinct* error id `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` at HTTP status 403, separating the "your role does not in general have access to this kind of endpoint" cause from the "your role does have access in general but the specific row you addressed belongs to another tenant" cause.

A **RegulatorOfficer** performs every regulatory-mandate action: approving and revoking carrier qualifications, attesting and revoking driver hazmat endorsements through the refresh-endorsement endpoint, appending inspection certificates to vehicles, approving and rejecting route applications, issuing permits and running compliance verifications on them, voiding a freshly issued permit within its grace window, issuing manual disciplinary penalties, and adjudicating appeals via the uphold action. The regulator's identity is *opaque*: the `X-User-ID` is an officer-identifier string not tied to any persisted entity, and the system stores its value verbatim on the audit-trail fields of the records the regulator's actions touch — `inspector_id` on a `VehicleInspection`, `approved_by` on a `TransportRoute`, `issued_by` on a `Permit`, and `upheld_by` on a `Penalty`. Because the regulator's id does not correspond to a TransportCompany row, the regulator is exempt from the cross-tenant ownership predicate and reads every row in the system regardless of the row's owning tenant, in line with the regulatory-mandate cross-tenant exemption.

A **Driver** is an individual driver whose API access is read-only and is restricted to the driver's own row. The role's identity is *entity-backed* on the Driver table: a `Driver` request's `X-User-ID` must equal the `id` of an existing Driver row, and a non-resolving id is rejected at the auth tier the same way an operator's non-resolving company id is. The Driver-role's allow-set is exactly three endpoints — reading the driver's own record by id, reading a Trip when the trip's `driver_id` equals the request's `X-User-ID`, and reading the driver's own disciplinary summary at the penalty-summary endpoint — and the self-only predicate is enforced as a distinct rejection cause: a Driver attempting to read another Driver's row, another driver's trip, or another driver's summary receives `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 (the same error id the cross-tenant rule on the operator side uses, with the meaning generalized to "the addressed row's identity does not match the requester's identity"). The Driver role has no write endpoint at all — driver-side observations such as GPS samples are uploaded by the operator's fleet-tracking back-end on the driver's behalf, not by the driver's role credentials.

A **TestHarness** principal exists exclusively to drive the wall-clock-and-sweep test affordances and has no business-logic access. The role's identity is opaque (a test-runner string the deployment may choose freely), and the role's allow-set is exactly the two test-mode endpoints `POST /v1/_clock/advance` and `POST /v1/_sweep/run` covered in §2.5. In a production deployment with the test-harness role disabled at the authentication middleware, every request bearing `X-User-Role: TestHarness` is rejected at the 401 tier, which is how the production-vs-test deployment distinction is implemented without changing the URL space.

The shared cross-cutting authentication contract is that the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers are checked at the top of every endpoint handler before any other validation. The two ordered auth checks are (a) the role-name-is-canonical-and-headers-are-present check, which fires `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` 401 on any failure, and (b) for the two entity-backed roles, the entity-id-resolves check, which fires the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` 401 because a credential naming a non-existent backing entity is itself an invalid credential rather than a "not found" reference. Once the auth tier passes, the RBAC table's cell for the (endpoint, role) pair determines whether the request is generally permitted (the default-allow cells), generally denied (the default-deny cells returning `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403), or conditionally permitted subject to a per-row ownership predicate (the deviation cells where the cell's outcome is `ALLOW` if the ownership predicate holds and `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 otherwise). The distinction between the two 403 error ids matters for the test suite, which asserts on the specific id rather than the bare status code.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

In addition to the 4xx error statuses, the system uses HTTP status 401 for missing-or-invalid authentication headers (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), HTTP status 402 "Payment Required" exclusively for the wallet-shortfall condition `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_BALANCE`, HTTP status 403 for both the generic role-deny (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`) and the cross-tenant ownership-deny (`ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS`), HTTP status 404 for the seven entity-specific not-found errors of the form `ERR_<ENTITY>_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP status 409 for state-conflict and business-rule-conflict conditions including the optimistic-concurrency loser, and HTTP status 422 for body-shape and enum-value violations packaged under the generic `ERR_VALIDATION` plus the dedicated `ERR_PERMIT_VEHICLE_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` cardinality error. Successful creates return 201 (the `POST` endpoints that mint a new row); successful mutating state transitions and successful reads return 200. The full mapping from each error id to its HTTP status code is in the catalogue of §5; the trigger conditions for each id are scattered through the workflows of §3.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two standard query parameters. `limit` is an integer specifying the maximum number of items in the response page; when omitted it defaults to 10, the minimum accepted value is 1, and the maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100 is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` at HTTP status 422. `offset` is a non-negative integer specifying the number of items to skip before the page starts; when omitted it defaults to 0. A negative `offset` is also rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Every list response includes the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the response envelope's `meta` object, alongside the standard `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` fields. The `total_count` is the count of rows that satisfy the query's filters across the entire underlying collection (not just the current page), and `has_more` is the boolean indicating whether further pages exist beyond the current `offset + limit` boundary.

The default sort order is ascending by `created_at`, the standard system-managed insertion-timestamp field discussed in §2.4. Specific list endpoints may pin a different default — the inspection-list endpoint sorts inspections by `inspected_at` descending then by `id` descending so the most recent inspection appears first, the GPS-report list endpoint sorts by `reported_at` ascending then by `id` ascending so the stream reads chronologically, and the permit-list endpoint sorts by `created_at` descending then by `id` descending. Where a sort order other than the global default applies, the §3 narrative naming the relevant list endpoint states the deviation in the workflow text adjacent to that endpoint's discussion. Filters specific to a particular list — such as the `qualification_status` filter on the company list, the `outcome` filter on the inspection list, the `lifecycle_status` filter on the permit list, the `company_id` filter on the permit list (regulator-only useful, since an operator's effective view is always restricted to their own company), and the `violation_only` boolean filter on the GPS-report list — are documented inside §3's discussion of the owning entity.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Append-Only Event Records

The six mutable entity kinds — `TransportCompany`, `Driver`, `Vehicle`, `TransportRoute`, `Permit`, and `Trip` — share a quartet of system-managed fields. The `id` is a server-minted UUID without any type prefix, generated at insert and immutable thereafter. The `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits, fixed at the row's insert moment and never refreshed. The `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, set equal to `created_at` at insert and refreshed to the current logical-clock value on every mutation, including the cascade-driven side-effect mutations of §3.10's reconciliation paths and the sweep-driven automatic transitions of §2.5. The `version` is a non-negative integer counter, initialized to 1 at insert and incremented by 1 on every successful mutation; clients may include the expected current `version` in the body of a mutating request to engage the optimistic-concurrency check (see §2.10), and an absent `version` in the request body means the client opts out of the OCC check for that request.

The three append-only event-log entity kinds — `VehicleInspection`, `GPSReport`, and `Penalty` — carry only the `id` and `created_at` fields from the system-managed set. They are append-only in the strict sense that the row's content is never edited after the insert: a correction to an erroneous inspection is a fresh inspection record with a later `inspected_at` that supersedes the earlier one in the parent vehicle's "latest inspection" derived pointer, and a correction to an erroneous penalty is the appeal-uphold compensating action that flips the penalty's `void_status` to `UPHELD` (the one exception to strict append-only, narrated in §3.8 as the regulator's adjudication act rather than as an in-place mutation). Because there is no in-place mutation on the three event kinds, they have no `updated_at` and no `version`: the absence is the observable indicator that the row is a ledger entry rather than a stateful resource.

The Permit entity carries, in addition to its standard system fields, a family of derived strict-snapshot fields that capture the five-leg validity verdict at the moment of the last successful verify-compliance call. These are `last_verified_at` (the timestamp of the last verification), the boolean `snapshot_company_qualified` and `snapshot_route_approved` and `snapshot_within_window`, and the two map-valued fields `snapshot_driver_endorsements` (a mapping from each authorized driver id to a boolean) and `snapshot_vehicle_inspections` (a mapping from each authorized vehicle id to a boolean). These snapshot fields are populated by the issue and verify-compliance handlers and are otherwise inert — the read endpoint reports their on-disk values verbatim and also computes a live-mode `live_per_leg` map for the same five legs which the GET response embeds alongside the snapshot for the dual-variant axis discussed in §3.5. The Vehicle entity similarly carries three derived "latest inspection" pointers — `latest_inspection_id`, `latest_inspection_outcome`, and `latest_inspection_valid_until` — atomically refreshed whenever an inspection with a strictly larger `inspected_at` is appended, with the tie-break for equal-timestamp inspections being the lexicographically larger `id`. The Driver entity carries a cached `current_rolling_points` integer that mirrors the live rolling-window output of the `driver_penalty_point_rolling_total` calculation discussed in §2.11 and §3.2, and the Trip entity carries the derived `latest_gps_at` cursor that the GPS-stream monotonicity check reads.

A handful of business fields carry default values on insert. A new TransportCompany row's `wallet_balance_cents` is initialized to zero, with regulator-side top-ups happening out of band; a new Driver row's `hazmat_endorsement_status` is `NOT_ATTESTED` with `endorsement_valid_until` null and `endorsement_classes` the empty array and `current_rolling_points` zero; a new Vehicle row's three latest-inspection pointers are null; a new TransportRoute row's `approval_status` is `DRAFT` with `approved_at`, `approved_by`, and `rejection_reason` all null; a new Permit row is in `lifecycle_status = DRAFT` with `valid_until` null and the snapshot fields all null until the issue step writes them and with `renewal_history` the empty array and `voided_at` and `void_refunded_cents` null; a new Trip row is in `trip_status = PLANNED` with `actual_departure_at`, `actual_arrival_at`, `aborted_at`, and `abort_reason` all null and `latest_gps_at` null and `gps_violation_count` zero; a new GPSReport row carries the server-computed `off_route_distance_m` and the `received_at` server-receive timestamp alongside the client-supplied position fields and the optional `idempotency_key` lifted from the header; a new Penalty row carries the server-set `source` (which is `MANUAL_REGULATOR` for the regulator-issued path and one of `GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED` or `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE` for the auto-creation path) along with the optional `source_gps_report_id` and `trip_id` context fields, and the initial `void_status` of `ACTIVE` with the three adjudication fields `upheld_at` and `upheld_by` null. The `reason_note` field on a Penalty is the operator-or-system-supplied free-text cause and, after an appeal-uphold, is prepended on the server side with a canonical "upheld-on-appeal" marker carrying the regulator-supplied `uphold_reason` from the uphold request's body, so the on-disk `reason_note` after a successful uphold encodes both the original and the appeal-resolution text.

Cross-entity references are by foreign-key id values on the row. The Driver's `company_id` and the Vehicle's `company_id` and the TransportRoute's `company_id` and the Permit's `company_id` and `route_id` and the Trip's `permit_id` and `driver_id` and `vehicle_id` and the GPSReport's `trip_id` and the Penalty's `subject_id` and `trip_id` are all references that the existence-tier check (see §2.7) resolves at request time. The Permit's `authorized_driver_ids` and `authorized_vehicle_ids` are non-empty arrays of ids covering the drivers and vehicles the operator's permit application binds to the permit; the existence check walks each id in left-to-right order on the body's array, and the cardinality bound on `authorized_vehicle_ids` is the inclusive integer interval from 1 to the `MAX_VEHICLES_PER_PERMIT` constant, with a violation rejected by the dedicated `ERR_PERMIT_VEHICLE_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` 422 error. The corresponding cardinality bound on the driver list is the inclusive interval from 1 to 50, an explicit constant set in the entity's field documentation; a violation there falls under the generic `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than the dedicated vehicle-count error because the driver-count is not a load-bearing fee input and so does not warrant its own error id. The Permit's `hazard_classes` field — the array of UN hazard-class codes the permit authorizes — must be a non-empty subset of the intersection of the company's `hazard_classes_authorized` and the route's `hazard_classes_supported` at both the application-time check and the issue-time recheck. The Driver's `endorsement_classes` field — the regulator-attested hazard-class subset the driver may carry — is initialized empty at registration and is overwritten on each regulator's refresh-endorsement call to the regulator's verified set; it must be a subset of the parent TransportCompany's `hazard_classes_authorized` at the moment the regulator attests it. The Vehicle's `hazard_classes_compatible` field is the construction-certified hazard-class set; the cargo-vs-vehicle compatibility on a trip is the check that the trip's single `cargo_hazard_class` is in the parent vehicle's `hazard_classes_compatible` set, with a violation under the generic `ERR_VALIDATION` and the field-path message naming the offending class.

The free-text fields are bounded in length to keep the API's payload sizes predictable. Free-text limits across the entities: a company's `legal_name` is 1 to 200 characters, its `address_line` is 0 to 500 characters; a driver's `full_name` is 1 to 120 characters; a vehicle's `plate_number` is 4 to 16 characters and the `vehicle_type` is the enum from §2.8's vehicle-class set with the value strings `TANKER_LIQUID`, `TANKER_GAS`, `BOX_DRY_BULK`, `FLATBED_PACKAGED`, and `OTHER`; an inspection's `findings_note` is 0 to 2000 characters; a route's `origin_label` and `destination_label` are each 1 to 200 characters and the polyline is a 2-to-200-waypoint list of `{lat, lon}` pairs with `lat` in the inclusive range from -90 to 90 and `lon` in the inclusive range from -180 to 180; a permit-issue or void-issue or route-reject body's `reason_note` and `rejection_reason` and `approval_note` and the renewal's `renew_note` and the depart-and-arrive-and-abort notes are all 0-or-1-to-500-character strings depending on whether the field is required or optional on the specific endpoint (the required ones — `reason_note` on the revoke and the void-issue and the manual-penalty and the trip-abort, `rejection_reason` on the route-reject, `uphold_reason` on the penalty-uphold — start at the lower-bound 1, the optional notes start at 0); the trip's `cargo_description` is 1 to 500 characters and the auto-set canonical reason notes on auto-Penalty rows are the fixed prefix strings `"Auto: speed exceeded the hazmat speed limit"` and `"Auto: off-route beyond the permitted corridor"` for the speed and off-route violation types respectively; the manual penalty's `points_value` is an integer in the inclusive range from 1 to 50 and the `subject_type` is the enum `{DRIVER, COMPANY, VEHICLE}` from §2.8; the company registration number `registration_number` is a string matching the regular expression of uppercase letters and digits in the length range 8 to 32 inclusive, with the exact pattern being eight-to-thirty-two characters drawn from the alphabet of capital A-through-Z and the digits zero-through-nine. The driver's `license_number` is 6 to 32 characters with no embedded whitespace, and its uniqueness scope is per-company (two different companies may both employ a driver named on a same-string license, but no two drivers in one company may share a license number). The vehicle's `plate_number` uniqueness scope, by contrast, is system-wide because a single physical license plate identifies the vehicle nationally. Both uniqueness collisions raise the shared error id `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` at HTTP status 409, with the response message field naming which natural-key collided so a caller can disambiguate among the three uniqueness scopes (the company's `registration_number` is the third system-wide unique key, alongside the vehicle plate; the driver license number is the only per-company-scoped uniqueness key in the scaffold).

### 2.5. The Server's Logical Clock and the Daily Compliance Sweep

All timestamps in the system are read off a single server-side logical clock that advances strictly monotonically and reports the current value as an ISO 8601 UTC string with three fractional-second digits. In a production deployment the logical clock is backed by the operating-system clock and ticks once per real-world second; in test deployments the logical clock is decoupled from the OS clock so the test harness can drive time deterministically. The convention "the current logical-clock value" is the canonical name for what regulatory prose calls "now" — the value of the system's notion of present time at the instant a handler is evaluating a rule.

Two endpoints expose the clock-and-sweep infrastructure to the `TestHarness` role. The first is `POST /v1/_clock/advance`, an admin-only test-driving capability whose request body carries an `advance_seconds` field (a non-negative integer-or-float number of seconds to add to the logical-clock variable) and a boolean `run_sweep_after_advance` flag (defaulting to false). The response carries the pre-advance `prior_now`, the post-advance `new_now`, the `advance_seconds_applied` echo of the request, and a `sweep_summary` sub-object that is null when `run_sweep_after_advance` is false and otherwise carries the same payload the sweep endpoint described below returns. A negative `advance_seconds` is a body-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` 422, and the same generic error fires for a non-boolean `run_sweep_after_advance` or for an `advance_seconds` exceeding the deployment's maximum-single-advance bound (the bound is large — on the order of a century of seconds — to admit the year-long permit-term-expiry tests without admitting unbounded forward jumps that could overflow the year-9999 datetime ceiling). A zero-duration advance is a permitted no-op for the harness to synchronize its local clock idea with the server.

The clock advance itself does not synchronously fire any time-gated automatic transition: the post-advance world is the world in which the wall-clock has moved forward, but the rows whose state would have flipped at the new time remain at their prior labels until something reads them. The synchronous trigger for the time-driven transitions is the second test-mode endpoint, `POST /v1/_sweep/run`, which accepts a body `scope` field with the three allowed values `ALL`, `PERMITS_ONLY`, and `DRIVERS_ONLY` (the default is `ALL`) and runs the daily compliance sweep over the named subset of the row tables. The sweep performs the following canonical sequence in order. First, for every Permit whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `valid_until` is at or before the current logical-clock value, the sweep fires the wall-clock-expiry transition to `EXPIRED` (the half-open right-exclusive convention discussed in §2.9 means the moment `now()` equals `valid_until` is the expired moment, not the last-active moment), and as a synchronous side-effect every still-open child Trip on each newly-expired permit — those in `PLANNED` or `IN_TRANSIT` — is transitioned to `ABORTED` with the `abort_reason` field set to the discriminator value `PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE`. The wallet is not credited on expiry: the permit term elapsed and the fee was earned. Second, the still-in-term ACTIVE permits each have their per-permit verify-compliance routine run internally so the deferred cascades from any upstream company-revoke, driver-endorsement-flip, vehicle-inspection-lapse, or route-status-change observed since the prior tick are flushed through the standard `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` transitions documented in §3.5. Third, for every Driver row, the cached `current_rolling_points` field is recomputed against the trailing-edge of the rolling penalty window — penalties whose `observed_at` has just slipped out of the window are excluded from the new total — and the wall-clock-expiry of a driver's `endorsement_valid_until` (when its value is at or before the new now and the current status is `VALID`) fires the `VALID → INVALID` automatic transition on the Driver's hazmat-endorsement state machine. The upward direction `INVALID → VALID` is deliberately not fired by the sweep: a driver whose rolling total has just decayed below the suspension threshold remains in `INVALID` until a regulator explicitly re-attests the endorsement via the refresh-endorsement endpoint, per the keep-the-regulator-in-the-loop convention discussed in §3.2.

The sweep's response payload is a structured summary of the work performed. The top-level fields are `as_of_now` (the logical-clock value at sweep start), `scope` (the body's echoed value), `permit_transitions` (an array of objects each carrying the affected permit's `permit_id`, the `pre_status` and `post_status`, the `cause` discriminator drawn from the values `WALL_CLOCK_EXPIRY` / `GROUNDING_INVALIDATION` / `GROUNDING_REVALIDATION` for the three flavours of the sweep's per-permit work, and the `cascade_trip_aborts` array of trip ids the per-permit work cascade-aborted), `driver_recomputes` (an array of objects each carrying the driver's id, the pre and post rolling-points integer, and a `status_flip` discriminator drawn from `VALID_TO_INVALID` or null), the `n_permits_inspected` and `n_drivers_inspected` integer summaries, and a `wallclock_advanced` boolean that is always false on the sweep-run path (the sweep does not advance the clock — that is the clock-advance endpoint's job; the verb-asymmetry between the two test-mode endpoints reflects the production-deployment-equivalent split between the OS clock tick and the periodic-cron sweep). The two endpoints' separability lets a test advance the clock, observe the permit row's status hasn't yet flipped (the "the wall clock has moved but the system has not yet noticed" state), and then run the sweep to flush the deferred transitions and observe the post-flush snapshot — the canonical pattern for the time-boundary tests on the permit's `valid_until` edge and the driver's `endorsement_valid_until` edge and the penalty's rolling-window trailing edge.

The conservation-of-time guarantee is that the logical clock advances strictly monotonically: any sequence of `POST /v1/_clock/advance` calls leaves the new `now` strictly greater than the prior `now` (except for the zero-duration no-op which leaves the value unchanged), and the server rejects any attempt to roll the clock backwards. This is the consistency model that lets the rolling-window calculation (whose interval is the open-on-the-left, closed-on-the-right interval ending at the current logical-clock value) be evaluated deterministically: at any reproducible clock state the rolling total has a single well-defined value. The same monotonicity, paired with the GPS-stream's strictly-greater-than monotonicity discussed in §2.9, gives the test harness deterministic control over the four time-windows the scaffold tracks — the permit's annual validity, the appeal's grace window measured from a penalty's `observed_at`, the void-issue's short grace window measured from the permit's `issued_at`, and the renewal's open-window-and-late-grace-pair measured backward from the permit's `valid_until`.

### 2.6. Cross-Tenant Isolation and the Ownership Predicate

Cross-tenant isolation is the rule that an operator on one company sees, reads, and may modify only the rows belonging to that one company. The operator's effective tenant identity is the `X-User-ID` value of every operator-role request — by the entity-backed-identity contract of §2.1, that string equals the `id` of the requesting TransportCompany. Each business resource carries a single effective owning-tenant id derived as follows: a TransportCompany's owning tenant is its own `id`; a Driver's owning tenant is the driver's `company_id`; a Vehicle's owning tenant is the vehicle's `company_id`; a VehicleInspection's owning tenant is the parent Vehicle's owning tenant (a transitive lookup over the inspection's `vehicle_id` foreign key); a TransportRoute's owning tenant is the route's `company_id` because routes are filed by the carrier even when the geographic corridor is shared between two carriers in the real world; a Permit's owning tenant is the permit's `company_id`; a Trip's owning tenant is the parent Permit's owning tenant (transitively over the trip's `permit_id`); a GPSReport's owning tenant is the parent Trip's parent Permit's owning company (a two-hop transitive lookup); and a Penalty's owning tenant is the subject's owning company — the driver's `company_id` for a `DRIVER` subject, the vehicle's `company_id` for a `VEHICLE` subject, and the subject itself for a `COMPANY` subject. The cross-tenant predicate fires when the resolved owning tenant id is non-null and does not equal the requesting operator's `X-User-ID`, and the failure code is `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` at HTTP status 403.

The cross-tenant check is at the *business referential* tier — priority 530 in the validation precedence of §2.7 — which is strictly below the *existence* tier at priority 300. This precedence is load-bearing for the rule-5 priority-conflict-B scenario where a wrong-tenant operator addresses a non-existent resource id: the existence check fires first and the response is 404 with the appropriate `ERR_<ENTITY>_NOT_FOUND` (one of `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_PENALTY_NOT_FOUND`), not the 403 cross-tenant error. The convention preserves the privacy property that an operator on company A cannot, by probing the API, distinguish between "this id belongs to company B and I cannot see it" and "this id does not exist at all" — both produce the same 404 response. The complementary rule for the cross-aggregate body-references on the permit-application endpoint is symmetric: a body that lists a driver id from another tenant produces `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` rather than a cross-tenant 403, because from the operator's perspective the other tenant's drivers are not part of the operator's universe and therefore are "not found" in the operator's view.

For the read endpoints on the company list and the permit list, the cross-tenant rule has a special-case interaction with the optional `company_id` query parameter. The regulator's list call accepts the `company_id` filter as a narrowing parameter — present and matching one tenant's id, the list is that tenant's rows; absent, the list is the global cross-tenant set. The operator's list call enforces a stricter convention: an absent `company_id` is the implicit-self default and the response is the operator's own (always exactly one-or-zero rows on the company list, since each operator owns exactly one company), while a present `company_id` that does not equal the operator's `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 rather than being silently rewritten to the operator's-own filter. The explicit-rejection convention forces consistent client behaviour — a misconfigured client gets an immediate error rather than a surprising data view.

The Driver role has its own self-only variant of the predicate (priority 531 in the validation ordering): a Driver-role request whose `X-User-ID` does not match the addressed Driver row's `id`, the addressed Trip row's `driver_id`, or the addressed penalty-summary endpoint's path id is the same `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403, with the meaning generalized from "wrong company" to "wrong individual identity". The RegulatorOfficer and TestHarness roles are exempt from both flavours of the predicate — the regulator's cross-tenant exemption is the regulatory-mandate convention and the test-harness's exemption is moot because its allow-set is only the two non-tenanted clock-and-sweep endpoints.

A final subtlety is the bootstrap exemption on the company-create endpoint. The operator's very first request — `POST /v1/companies` to file the initial qualification application — necessarily carries an `X-User-ID` that does not yet correspond to a TransportCompany row because the row is what the request is asking the server to create. The handler treats this single case specially: the request's `X-User-ID` is accepted as the would-be operator identifier, the server mints a fresh UUID for the new row's `id` regardless of the header's value, and the response carries the minted id which the client uses as its `X-User-ID` for every subsequent call. The cross-tenant predicate consequently does not apply to the company-create endpoint at all (the endpoint's RBAC cell for the `CompanyOperator` role is a default-allow); no other endpoint has this bootstrap shape. The authentication tier's "the entity-backed id must resolve" check is the rule the company-create call is exempt from, and the special-casing is the realization of the principle that an operator must be able to onboard the system without a chicken-and-egg credential prerequisite.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence and the Error-Category Tiers

When more than one rule would reject a given request, the system returns the error of the *lowest-priority-number* failing rule, where the priority numbers are integers assigned to the rules of the validation pipeline in seven canonical tiers. The tiers and the priorities of the rules within them are stable across endpoints, which fixes the answer to the question "which error does the caller see when two of its preconditions fail simultaneously". The seven tiers in increasing priority-number order — that is, the order in which they are checked, with the *earliest* failing tier producing the response — are: (Tier 1) Authentication at priorities 100 and 110; (Tier 2) Authorization, the RBAC cell lookup, at priority 200; (Tier 3) Existence, the URL-path-id and body-id lookups, at priority 300; (Tier 4) State precondition, the same-row and parent-row state checks the state-machine guards require, at priority 400; (Tier 5) Business rules, split into five sub-tiers — safety (5a), constraint (5b/c), referential (5b/c), fiscal (5d), idempotency-and-OCC (5e and 5f) — covering the priority range 500 to 590; and effectively a (Tier 6) catch-all `ERR_VALIDATION` for body-shape and enum-membership and numeric-range violations of the request payload, sitting at the bottom of Tier 5 at priority 590 so that any more-specific business-rule error wins over the catch-all.

The tier-1 authentication checks are described in §2.1: a missing or unrecognized `X-User-Role`, or a missing `X-User-ID`, fires `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` at HTTP 401 at priority 100; a syntactically valid `X-User-ID` on an `entity_backed:<EntityName>` role that does not resolve to a row in the named entity's table fires the same error id at priority 110, on the principle that a credential naming a non-existent entity is itself invalid. The tier-2 authorization check is the RBAC cell lookup described in §2.6 — the cell is either an allow or a default-deny that fires `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` at HTTP 403 at priority 200, or the conditionally-allow cell whose negative outcome is the distinct `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 at the business-referential priority 530.

The tier-3 existence check walks the path-parameters of the URL left to right. For a path like `/v1/companies/{company_id}/drivers` the company id is the only path parameter, and a non-resolving value returns `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND` 404. For a hypothetical multi-level path like the inspection-create's `/v1/vehicles/{vehicle_id}/inspections`, the single path-parameter is the vehicle id and the failure is `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND` 404. The left-to-right walk pins which not-found error wins when two ids are unresolvable: the leftmost-failing id. The existence check is extended to the body-supplied foreign-key references on the trip-creation, the permit-application, the permit-renewal's optional fresh-driver-and-vehicle lists, and the manual-penalty's subject-id and the optional trip-id field — these body-supplied id existence failures still produce the appropriate `ERR_<ENTITY>_NOT_FOUND` 404 rather than being demoted to a body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION`, on the principle that a foreign-key body field whose value does not resolve is logically the same kind of failure as a path parameter that does not resolve. The existence-tier-beats-cross-tenant precedence (300 versus 530) is the rule-5 priority-conflict-B already discussed in §2.6.

The tier-4 state precondition check is the same-row "is the current state in the guard's allowed pre-set" test and the cross-aggregate "is the parent's state in the required value" test. The same-row failure mode is the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 across every state-action endpoint — the company submit/approve/revoke triplet, the route submit/approve/reject triplet, the driver refresh-endorsement action, the permit issue and verify-compliance and renew and void-issue actions, the trip depart and arrive and abort actions, and the penalty uphold action. The cross-aggregate parent-state failure on the trip's depart-and-gps-ingest endpoints is the dedicated `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409, distinct from the generic state-transition error because the cause is on the parent permit's `lifecycle_status` rather than the addressed trip's own `trip_status`. The two state-tier error ids cannot fire on the same request — the dedicated parent-state error fires on the endpoints where the parent-permit-active gate is part of the guard's conjunction, and the generic same-row state error fires everywhere the guard's conjunction includes a same-row state predicate. The cross-aggregate state check at the trip-creation endpoint `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/trips` likewise fires `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409 when the named parent permit is not in `ACTIVE`; the state-check on the parent's row precedes the business-tier checks on the body's referenced grounding entities, so a trip-creation against a non-active parent permit whose body also names a route whose `approval_status` is non-approved returns the parent-state error rather than the route-approval error — the rule-5 priority-conflict-A pin that state at Tier 4 beats business-referential at Tier 5b.

The tier-5 business-rule tier is subdivided. The safety sub-tier (5a, priorities 509 through 515) carries the GPS-ingest's in-window check at 509 producing `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_WINDOW` 409, the driver-endorsement-invalid check at 510 producing `ERR_DRIVER_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID` 409, the vehicle-inspection-lapsed check at 511 producing `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409, the GPS-stream monotonicity check at 512 producing `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_ORDER` 409, the appeal-window check at 513 producing `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409, the void-issue window check at 514 producing `ERR_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409, and the GPS-ingest's parent-permit-active gate (which lives in the safety sub-tier at 515 because the cause of a permit's suspension is regulatory-safety) producing `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409. The relative ordering within sub-tier 5a is fixed by the integer priorities: the in-window check at 509 fires before the monotonicity check at 512, which is the rule-5 priority-conflict-C pin — a GPS report that is both outside the trip's in-flight window and out-of-order against the trip's `latest_gps_at` cursor returns the window error, not the order error. The same in-window-first convention pushes the parent-permit-active gate at 515 to fire after both the window and order checks: a request to push GPS samples to a trip whose parent permit has expired and whose `reported_at` is also outside the trip's window returns the window error, because the trip's cascade-abort on the parent-permit-expiry's side-effect-list has already pulled the trip out of `IN_TRANSIT` so the in-window gate naturally fails first.

The business-constraint and business-referential sub-tier (5b/c, priorities 520 through 531) carries the natural-key uniqueness check at 520 producing `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` 409 (used by the company's `registration_number`, the driver's per-company `license_number`, and the vehicle's system-wide `plate_number`), the vehicle-double-booking interval-overlap check at 521 producing `ERR_VEHICLE_DOUBLE_BOOKED` 409, the trip-vs-permit window-membership check at 522 producing `ERR_OUTSIDE_VALIDITY_WINDOW` 409, the permit's vehicle-list cardinality check at 523 producing the dedicated `ERR_PERMIT_VEHICLE_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` 422 (the only 422 in the business-rule tier — its 422 status reflects the body-shape-as-business-constraint flavour of cardinality bounds), the hazard-class-subset-of-intersection check at 524 producing `ERR_VALIDATION` 422, the route-approval-status check at 525 producing `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_APPROVED` 409, the company-qualification-status check at 526 producing `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409, the cross-tenant ownership predicate at 530 producing `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403, and the driver-self-only predicate at 531 producing the same `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 with the meaning specialized to driver-identity rather than company-identity. Within sub-tier 5b's temporal-window family, the vehicle-double-booked check at 521 fires before the outside-validity-window check at 522, which is the rule-5 bonus priority-conflict-E pin (a trip whose schedule both collides with another trip on the same vehicle and falls outside the parent permit's term receives the double-booking error). The rule-5 priority-conflict-A pin between the trip's parent-permit-state at Tier 4 (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` at 400, the cross-aggregate parent-state guard) and the route's approval state at 525 (`ERR_ROUTE_NOT_APPROVED` 409 on the permit-application's route-leg) is that the Tier-4 state-error wins because the lower priority-number fires first.

The fiscal sub-tier (5d, priority 540) carries the wallet-shortfall check `company.wallet_balance_cents` is at least the formula's computed fee at the issue and the renew endpoints, producing `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_BALANCE` at the unique HTTP 402 "Payment Required" status. The fiscal tier is the highest priority number within Tier 5's business-rule tier — meaning it fires *after* the safety and the referential checks — so a permit-issue request on a permit whose route is unapproved and whose company wallet is also short returns the route-approval error, not the wallet error, on the principle that regulatory and safety failures dominate fiscal failures. Conversely, the fiscal-vs-state conflict between an `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_BALANCE` and an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` on an already-issued permit's retry-issue attempt resolves to the state error winning (Tier 4's 400 priority beats Tier 5d's 540 priority) — the rule-5 bonus priority-conflict-D pin.

The renewal-window edges occupy priorities 541 and 542 — the early edge fires `ERR_RENEWAL_TOO_EARLY` 409 when the renew request lands strictly before `valid_until - RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS`, and the late edge fires `ERR_RENEWAL_AFTER_EXPIRY` 409 when the wall clock has already reached `valid_until` (the renewal handler observes that the permit is or is about to be in `EXPIRED` and returns the specific expiry-flavoured error rather than the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`). The idempotency-collision check on the GPS-ingest endpoint is at priority 550 producing `ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` 409 when the request's `Idempotency-Key` header matches an existing per-trip slot but the request body's bytes do not match the slot's stored body hash, and the matching-body-hash case is the success path that replays the original 201 response with a `replayed: true` flag in the response meta. The optimistic-concurrency check across every mutating endpoint that accepts an optional `version` field in the body is at priority 560 producing `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` 409 on a mismatch — see §2.10 for the full OCC contract.

At the bottom of Tier 5 sits the generic body-shape catch-all at priority 590, producing `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 for any malformed JSON, missing required field, type mismatch, numeric out of declared range, enum value outside the allowed set, string longer than the per-field maximum, array cardinality outside its declared bounds, regex mismatch on a format-constrained string like the company's `registration_number`, or a list-of-enum value that does not draw from the relevant taxonomy of §2.8. The error response's `message` field carries the field-path-and-cause string for human debugging while the test layer asserts on the `error_id` and on a substring of `message` to disambiguate among the many causes funnelled into the single error id. The system does not introduce per-field-shape error ids — the principle is that the generic body-shape error id catches everything that the more-specific business-rule errors did not first catch, and the test layer reads the `message` substring for the cause discrimination.

A handful of the dedicated body-shape errors do exist outside the generic catch-all. The vehicle-count-out-of-range error at priority 523 is one (the cardinality is treated as a business-constraint rather than a generic shape violation, hence its dedicated id and the same 422 status). The hazard-class-not-in-the-intersection error at priority 524 reuses the generic `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 id because the hazard-class-subset check is a body-shape predicate on the body's `hazard_classes` array, with the response message naming the offending hazard-class string. The body fields that have specific format constraints — the registration number's regex, the email's RFC 5322 syntactic check, the ISO 8601 datetime fields' format-and-range constraints, the lat/lon range checks on a polyline waypoint or a GPS sample, the speed-non-negativity check, the polyline length-in-waypoints bounds of two-to-two-hundred — all funnel into the generic `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 with the cause string in `message`. The convention "the catalogue has one body-shape error and the message field disambiguates the cause" keeps §5's table compact at the cost of forcing the response-message convention. The §5 catalogue's twenty-five error ids are the complete set; no other error id appears in any response. Where a single underlying cause has two named ids that arise in different contexts — the cross-aggregate driver-foreign-key-not-found case on the permit application versus the path-parameter driver-id-not-found case on the driver-read endpoint, both `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` — the two contexts share the id and the response message discriminates.

The validation order on a single endpoint handler is: parse the request body and headers (which alone can produce the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 on the most catastrophic shape failures like non-JSON content), then check the authentication tier in priority-100-and-110 order, then check the authorization tier at priority 200, then walk the existence tier at priority 300 in the left-to-right URL-path order (and after the path walk, the body-foreign-key existence checks in the body-field-declaration order), then check the state-precondition tier at priority 400 (with the cross-aggregate parent-state check at priority 400 on the relevant cross-aggregate endpoints firing before the same-row state check at the same tier on the same handler — the §1.2 state-machine guard's conjunction is left-to-right, with the parent-state predicate written first as a sibling of the same-row pre-state predicate), then the business-rule sub-tiers in their integer-priority order, ending at the generic body-shape catch-all at priority 590. The OCC version check at priority 560 fires on a body that has the optional `version` field — the field's absence opts the request out of the check entirely, and the field's presence engages the equality test against the row's current `version`. The convention "the lowest priority number wins" means the implementation walks the checks in the listed order and returns the first failure's error id, never aggregating multiple errors into a single response and never silently masking a higher-priority error with a lower-priority one. The single response carries exactly one `error_id`, which is the implementation invariant the test suite asserts against.

### 2.8. The Hazard-Class Taxonomy, Inspection Outcomes, and Penalty Sources

Several entity fields draw their categorical values from shared enumerations. The **UN hazard-class taxonomy** is the union of the standard United Nations hazardous-materials hazard-class codes — the string values `"1.1"`, `"1.2"`, `"1.3"`, `"1.4"`, `"1.5"`, `"1.6"` covering the six explosives subclasses; `"2.1"`, `"2.2"`, `"2.3"` for flammable gas and non-flammable non-toxic gas and toxic gas respectively; `"3"` for flammable liquid; `"4.1"`, `"4.2"`, `"4.3"` for flammable solid and spontaneously-combustible and dangerous-when-wet substances; `"5.1"` for oxidizers and `"5.2"` for organic peroxides; `"6.1"` for toxic substances and `"6.2"` for infectious substances; `"7"` for radioactive material; `"8"` for corrosives; and `"9"` for the catch-all "miscellaneous dangerous goods" class. A hazard-class string outside this set, wherever it appears in a request body, is a body-shape violation under the generic `ERR_VALIDATION`. The class strings flow through four entity fields: the TransportCompany's `hazard_classes_authorized` array (the set the company is licensed to handle), the Driver's `endorsement_classes` array (the regulator-attested per-driver subset), the Vehicle's `hazard_classes_compatible` array (the construction-certified per-vehicle subset), the TransportRoute's `hazard_classes_supported` array (the per-route approved subset), the Permit's `hazard_classes` array (the per-permit-issuance subset), and the Trip's single-class `cargo_hazard_class` field. The membership-and-subset relationships among these six fields are the regulatory-fit chain narrated in §3 — a permit's authorized hazard-class set must lie inside the company-authorized and the route-supported intersection, the driver's attested set must lie inside the parent company's authorized set, the vehicle's compatibility set is fixed by the vehicle's construction at registration, and the trip's single cargo class must lie inside the permit's authorized set and (for the vehicle compatibility check at the depart-arrive precondition tier) also inside the assigned vehicle's compatibility set.

The **inspection-outcome taxonomy** for a `VehicleInspection`'s `outcome` field has three values: `PASSED` (the inspection certifies the vehicle for hazmat transit for the inspection's validity window), `FAILED` (the inspection found a disqualifying condition and the inspection's `valid_until` is forced equal to the `inspected_at` so the certificate carries zero forward validity), and `CONDITIONAL` (the inspector flagged a remediable issue and the inspection's `valid_until` is the standard `inspected_at + INSPECTION_VALIDITY_DAYS`, which the scaffold pins at 180 days, but the inspection does not count as `PASSED` for the permit-validity AND-chain — only `PASSED` counts as compliant on the vehicle leg). The vehicle's "latest inspection" is the one with the largest `inspected_at` timestamp, ties broken by the lexicographically larger `id`. The permit-validity check requires the latest inspection's `outcome` to equal `PASSED` and the inspection's `valid_until` to be at or after the permit's `valid_until` — the inspection must cover the full permit term. A `CONDITIONAL` outcome reported as the latest inspection therefore fails the vehicle-leg of the permit-validity conjunction, the same as a `FAILED` outcome, even though the `valid_until` field carries a non-zero value. This subtlety is the regulatory convention that conditional inspections do not licence hazmat transit until the remediation has been re-inspected and a fresh `PASSED` certificate appended.

The **vehicle-type taxonomy** has five values for the Vehicle's `vehicle_type` field: `TANKER_LIQUID` for a liquid-cargo tank truck, `TANKER_GAS` for a gas-cargo tank truck, `BOX_DRY_BULK` for a sealed-box dry-bulk carrier, `FLATBED_PACKAGED` for a flatbed carrying packaged containers, and `OTHER` as the catch-all for vehicle classes the scaffold does not enumerate explicitly. The type is purely descriptive; it does not gate any compliance rule (the regulatory checks read the vehicle's `hazard_classes_compatible` set and its inspection-certificate validity, not its type-string), but it appears in the entity's read response and is filterable in audit views the regulator side maintains out of band.

The **penalty-subject-type taxonomy** distinguishes the subject of a disciplinary entry. The three values are `DRIVER`, `COMPANY`, and `VEHICLE`. A penalty with `subject_type = DRIVER` is the only one that drives the automatic suspension cascade documented in §3.8 and §3.10: the driver's `current_rolling_points` is the sum over the points-values of the non-upheld penalties on this subject within the 365-day rolling window, and the boundary at `current_rolling_points >= THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS` is the inclusive-equality boundary at which the driver's hazmat endorsement flips from `VALID` to `INVALID`. A penalty with `subject_type = COMPANY` or `VEHICLE` is recorded in the same Penalty table for the audit trail but does not drive any automatic state transition on its subject's home aggregate — the scaffold reserves the company-and-vehicle subject paths for future expansion and the current rule set leaves them inert. The corresponding existence-check error ids on a manual-penalty endpoint with a subject id that does not resolve are `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404 and discriminated by the `subject_type` value of the request body.

The **penalty-source taxonomy** has three values labelling the origin of a penalty record. `MANUAL_REGULATOR` is the value the server sets on a penalty created through the manual-penalty endpoint by a `RegulatorOfficer`, and the source-related fields `source_gps_report_id` and `trip_id` are null unless the manual penalty's body explicitly supplies the optional `trip_id` context field for a trip-contextual offline observation. `GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED` is the value the server sets on the auto-Penalty row written as a side effect of a GPS-report ingest whose `speed_kph` strictly exceeded the hazmat speed limit `MAX_HAZMAT_SPEED_KPH`; the auto-Penalty's `points_value` is the constant `GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED_POINTS`, its `source_gps_report_id` points back to the offending sample, its `trip_id` is the parent trip, and its `subject_type` is `DRIVER` with `subject_id` the trip's driver. `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE` is the symmetric auto-source for the off-route violation: the same source-traceability fields, the points value of `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE_POINTS`, the `observed_at` field equal to the triggering report's `reported_at`. The two auto-violation flavours are independent — a single GPS sample may simultaneously fire both, producing two auto-Penalty rows in the same transaction, which the parent trip's `gps_violation_count` increments by two and the parent GPS report's `auto_penalty_ids` array records as a two-element list. A clean GPS sample's `auto_penalty_ids` is the empty array, the trip's `gps_violation_count` is unchanged, and no Penalty rows are inserted by the gps-report side-effects.

The **abort-reason taxonomy** is the Trip's enum for the cause of a non-completed-but-terminal trip. The values are `OPERATOR_ABORT` (the operator's explicit abort via the abort endpoint, supplying a reason note for the audit trail), `PERMIT_SUSPENDED_CASCADE` (the discriminator-by-enum-economy convention for cascade-aborts arising from the permit's `ACTIVE → VOIDED` transition's trip-cascade side effect — the scaffold reuses this value rather than introducing a fourth `PERMIT_VOIDED_CASCADE` value to keep the enum compact, with the audit trail's out-of-band log distinguishing the void-cause from a hypothetical operator-suspension cause), `PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE` (the cascade-abort from the sweep-driven wall-clock-expiry of the parent permit), and the reserved `GPS_VIOLATION_LIMIT` value which is declared in the enum for forward compatibility with a hypothetical "this trip accumulated too many GPS violations and is auto-aborted" rule but is not actually written by any transition in the current rule set. Implementations populate this field on every terminal-ABORTED trip; the null value is the value for trips that are not terminal-aborted (the still-open trips and the trips that completed successfully).

The **GPS-stream contractual constants** are the deployment-configured numeric values associated with the named symbols `MAX_HAZMAT_SPEED_KPH` (the strict upper bound on the speed-violation classifier: a sample whose `speed_kph` is strictly greater than this constant fires the speed-violation auto-Penalty, with the boundary `speed_kph == MAX_HAZMAT_SPEED_KPH` being non-violating), `OFF_ROUTE_RADIUS_M` (the strict upper bound on the perpendicular distance in meters from the sample's `(lat, lon)` to the nearest segment of the parent permit's route polyline: a sample whose computed `off_route_distance_m` is strictly greater than this constant fires the off-route auto-Penalty, again with the boundary value not a violation), the two points-value constants `GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED_POINTS` and `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE_POINTS` for the two auto-Penalty kinds, and the geodesic-computation constant `EARTH_RADIUS_M` whose pinned value is the WGS-84 mean Earth radius of 6,371,000.0 meters fed into the haversine-segment distance formula used to evaluate the off-route distance against the polyline's piecewise great-circle segments. Tests pin the input lat/lon, the polyline waypoints, and the speed values so the computed distance and the boolean outcome of the comparison against the radius are a known literal pair, with a millimeter-level tolerance of 1e-3 meters absorbing any floating-point variation in the haversine formula's intermediate arithmetic. The convention "the comparison is strict greater than" means a sample exactly on the boundary circle of the polyline corridor or exactly at the speed limit is admitted as a clean sample, not flagged as a violation.

The **time-window and threshold constants** that drive the validity, appeal, void, renewal, suspension-threshold, and rolling-window behaviour are the named symbols whose deployment values are configured at startup time. The threshold constant `THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS` is the integer rolling-points value at which a driver's hazmat endorsement automatically flips to `INVALID`; the boundary is inclusive on the side of the threshold — a rolling total of exactly this many points is the just-suspended state, and a rolling total of one less is the last-clean state. The rolling-window length constant `PENALTY_WINDOW_DAYS` is the trailing-edge offset in days for the rolling window's left edge — the window is the half-open-left, closed-on-the-right interval ending at the current logical-clock value and extending backwards by this many days, so a penalty whose `observed_at` is more than this many days in the past is excluded from the rolling total and a penalty whose `observed_at` is exactly the trailing-edge value is excluded as well (the half-open-left convention is what makes the rolling window's behavior at the day-boundary moment deterministic). The scaffold's prose names this as "the 12-month rolling-window" in the entity description of the Driver's `current_rolling_points` and in the taxonomy note for penalty-subject types, fixing the symbolic constant's intended scale at 365 days; the exact integer the deployment uses is configured rather than hard-coded so the test layer can pin shorter windows for boundary tests on the trailing-edge fall-off without having to wait a full calendar year.

The **appeal-window constant** `APPEAL_WINDOW_DAYS` is the duration after a penalty's `observed_at` during which an appeal-uphold action is permitted; the scaffold pins the intended value at 30 days, with the convention that the boundary `now() - observed_at == APPEAL_WINDOW_DAYS` is the still-in-window case (closed-right on the duration), and any strictly larger time-difference is the out-of-window case rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The **void-issue-window constant** `VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS` is the corresponding duration after a permit's `issued_at` during which a clerical-error void is permitted; the scaffold pins the intended value at 24 hours, also with the closed-right boundary convention, and a violation is `ERR_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The **inspection-validity constant** `INSPECTION_VALIDITY_DAYS` is the forward-validity window of a `PASSED` inspection certificate measured from the `inspected_at` of the certificate; the scaffold pins the intended value at 180 days. The **renewal-window constants** are a pair: the outer-window constant `RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS` is the days-before-`valid_until` at which the renewal endpoint begins accepting requests (the scaffold pins the value at 60 days), and the inner late-grace-window constant `LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` is the smaller days-before-`valid_until` interval that, when reached, makes the renewal fee carry the late-renewal surcharge `LATE_RENEWAL_PENALTY_CENTS` on top of the base-plus-per-vehicle fee. The outer-window's left edge is closed-left ("at or after `valid_until` minus `RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS`" is the moment renewals open) and the right edge is open-right ("strictly before `valid_until`" is the moment the renewal becomes too late), so a request whose arrival is strictly before the outer-window's left edge returns `ERR_RENEWAL_TOO_EARLY` and a request whose arrival is at or after the right edge returns `ERR_RENEWAL_AFTER_EXPIRY` because the permit has either just entered or is about to enter the `EXPIRED` state at the wall-clock-crossing of `valid_until`. The **permit-term-length constant** `PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` is the term length the issue step writes into the new permit's `valid_until` as `valid_from + PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS`, and the renew step extends `valid_until` by the same constant amount measured forward from the prior `valid_until` value rather than from the current logical-clock value — so a chain of on-time renewals lands the term-end exactly one term-length after the previous term-end with no clock drift, while a chain of late renewals lands the term-end at the same one-term-length-forward stride from the previous term-end but each carrying the late surcharge in the fee paid. The **fee-formula constants** are the four cents-denominated integers `BASE_RENEWAL_FEE_CENTS` (the flat base of the issue-and-renewal fee), `PER_VEHICLE_SURCHARGE_CENTS` (the additive surcharge per vehicle on the permit's authorized-vehicle list), `LATE_RENEWAL_PENALTY_CENTS` (the conditional add-on triggered by the late-grace window), and the saturating ceiling `MAX_RENEWAL_FEE_CENTS` (the hard cap the formula applies after the conditional add). The **vehicle-list-cardinality constant** `MAX_VEHICLES_PER_PERMIT` is the inclusive upper bound on the permit's `authorized_vehicle_ids` array length; the lower bound is 1 and the same constant gates the renewal endpoint's optional fresh-vehicle-list. The **endorsement-renewal-grace constant** `ENDORSEMENT_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` is the post-`endorsement_valid_until` grace window before the driver's auto-flip to `INVALID` fires; the scaffold pins this at zero days (hard cutoff at the exact `endorsement_valid_until` moment), contrasting deliberately with the permit's 60-day-renewal-open-window-and-the-grace-of-the-late-fee-window pair so the boundary-precision tests on the two analogous time-windows have different grace-widths and the test suite can assert each independently. The **clock-advance bound** is the maximum-single-advance for the test-mode clock-advance endpoint, a deployment configuration on the order of 100 years of seconds, which exceeds any sane test scenario's needed forward jump while bounding accidental overflows into the year-9999 datetime ceiling.

A handful of length-bound and range-bound constants on individual fields fill out the request-validation surface. The polyline waypoint count is bounded inclusively in the integer range from 2 to 200. The pagination `limit` is in the inclusive integer range from 1 to 100 with a default of 10. The pagination `offset` is the non-negative integers with a default of 0. The optional `Idempotency-Key` header's string-length bound is the inclusive integer range from 8 to 128 characters; an out-of-range header value is itself an `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 because the header-format itself is body-shape-adjacent. The manual-penalty's `points_value` is the inclusive integer range from 1 to 50. The free-text fields' character bounds, the email's RFC 5322 syntactic check, the company-registration-number's regex of eight-to-thirty-two characters of uppercase letters and digits, the driver's six-to-thirty-two-character no-whitespace license number, the four-to-sixteen-character vehicle plate, the inclusive degree ranges on `lat` (-90 to 90), `lon` (-180 to 180), and `heading_deg` (0 to 360 with the convention that 360 and 0 mean the same compass north), and the non-negativity of `speed_kph` and `cargo_weight_kg` and `max_payload_kg` are the body-shape rules whose individual violations are catch-all `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 responses.

### 2.9. Wall-Clock Windows, Interval Semantics, and Boundary Conventions

The system tracks five distinct time-windows and one strictly-monotone event stream, and the interval-semantics conventions differ subtly across them in ways the test layer asserts against. The conventions are uniform across the entities that use them and are pinned here once so the §3 workflow narrative can refer back to "the half-open right-exclusive convention" or "the closed-right convention on the duration" without redefining the terms each time.

The **permit-validity window** runs from `valid_from` inclusive to `valid_until` exclusive on the wall-clock axis, the half-open right-exclusive `[valid_from, valid_until)` interval. The expiry moment in the wall-clock-driven `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and `SUSPENDED → EXPIRED` transitions fired by the sweep (and by the embedded wall-clock check inside the verify-compliance handler) is the moment `now()` is at or past `valid_until` — at exactly the moment `now() == valid_until` the permit has crossed into `EXPIRED`, not the last instant of `ACTIVE`. The within-window leg of the permit-validity AND-chain in the live recompute is `valid_from <= now() < valid_until`, and the corresponding strict-snapshot leg `snapshot_within_window` is the value of that boolean as of `last_verified_at`. The trip-vs-permit window-membership check on a new trip's `[scheduled_departure_at, scheduled_arrival_at]` interval requires the trip's left edge to be at or after the permit's `valid_from` and the trip's right edge to be at or before the permit's `valid_until` — the convention is closed-on-the-arrival-edge against the permit's `valid_until` (a trip scheduled to arrive at the exact `valid_until` moment is admissible at scheduling time even though by the half-open-permit-window convention the permit will be `EXPIRED` at that exact moment), and the actual-arrival-time recorded later by the arrive endpoint is whatever the logical clock reads at that moment; the asymmetry between "the scheduled arrival may touch the permit's expiry edge" and "the live wall-clock-vs-`valid_until` comparison is the strict-less-than" is the deliberate scaffold-internal interval-semantics asymmetry that the test layer pins on a trip scheduled to arrive exactly at the permit's expiry.

The **vehicle-calendar** uses the half-open-right-exclusive `[scheduled_departure_at, scheduled_arrival_at)` interval semantics for the cross-trip double-booking check: a new trip's interval collides with an existing trip's interval (on the same `vehicle_id`, across every permit because a vehicle is a physical asset and cannot be in two places at once) when the two open intervals intersect non-emptily. The half-open convention means two trips whose boundary timestamps touch (the existing trip's `scheduled_arrival_at` exactly equal to the new trip's `scheduled_departure_at`) do not collide and the new trip is admitted. The error on a collision is `ERR_VEHICLE_DOUBLE_BOOKED` 409, and the principle of the cross-permit scope of the check is that the conflict-check ranges over every Trip row in the system whose `vehicle_id` matches the new trip's `vehicle_id`, ignoring the trip's parent-permit identity.

The **GPS event stream's monotonicity rule** uses the closed-on-the-prior-edge convention — the new GPS report's `reported_at` must be strictly greater than the parent Trip's `latest_gps_at` cursor. An equal `reported_at` (the inclusive-equality boundary on the strict-greater-than predicate) is the rejection case, returning `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_ORDER`. The contrast with the vehicle-calendar's half-open-and-touching-boundary-admitted convention is the scaffold's "different interval semantics on different resources" pin: the vehicle calendar treats touching boundaries as non-overlapping (the half-open interval's right edge does not include the boundary point), and the GPS stream treats touching timestamps as the prior cursor still in force (the strict-greater-than predicate excludes the boundary point on the new-arrival side). The GPS-stream's in-window check on the trip's in-flight interval is the closed-closed interval `[actual_departure_at, min(actual_arrival_at, aborted_at, the current logical-clock value)]` with the right edge being whichever of the trip's terminal-recorded times exists or the current logical-clock value if the trip is still `IN_TRANSIT`. A report whose `reported_at` is strictly before `actual_departure_at` or strictly after the effective right edge is the out-of-window case `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_WINDOW`, which fires at priority 509 — earlier than the strict-monotonicity check at priority 512 — so a report that is both out-of-window and out-of-order returns the window error.

The **appeal window** is the closed-right interval on the duration `now() - observed_at`. An appeal-uphold action at the exact moment `now() - observed_at == APPEAL_WINDOW_DAYS` is in-window and admissible; the moment one tick beyond that boundary is out-of-window and rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The duration is measured from the penalty's `observed_at`, not from the penalty row's `created_at` — the convention matters for auto-Penalty rows whose `observed_at` (equal to the triggering GPS report's `reported_at` field) may be earlier than the row's `created_at` if the GPS unit's onboard clock was running ahead of the server's logical clock at the moment of upload. The void-issue window is the closed-right interval on the duration `now() - issued_at` against the constant `VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS`, with the same convention that the boundary moment is the last in-window moment and a strictly-larger duration is out-of-window with `ERR_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The renewal's outer-window left edge is closed-left ("at or after `valid_until` minus the outer-window constant" is the renewals-are-open moment, the moment one tick earlier is too-early), and the renewal's outer-window right edge is open-right, the same convention as the permit's `valid_until` itself ("strictly before `valid_until`" is in-window, the moment `now() == valid_until` is the just-expired moment that the wall-clock-vs-valid_until comparison's strict-less-than predicate excludes). The late-grace inner-window — over which the late-renewal-penalty surcharge applies on the fee — is the closed-left, open-right interval `[valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS, valid_until)`, sharing the outer-window's right edge. The error ids at the two outer-window edges are `ERR_RENEWAL_TOO_EARLY` for the left-edge violation and `ERR_RENEWAL_AFTER_EXPIRY` for the right-edge violation, both at HTTP 409.

The **rolling penalty-points window** is the open-on-the-left, closed-on-the-right duration interval anchored at the moment of evaluation. The interval is the set of timestamps `t` satisfying `(asof - PENALTY_WINDOW_DAYS) < t <= asof`, where `asof` is the current logical-clock value at the moment the rolling-total calculation runs. A penalty with `observed_at` exactly at the lower edge `asof - PENALTY_WINDOW_DAYS` has just fallen out of the window — the strict-greater-than on the left edge is the fall-off boundary's value-not-in. A penalty with `observed_at` exactly equal to `asof` is in (the closed-on-the-right edge admits the endpoint). The choice of "open on the left, closed on the right" matches the calendar-day trailing-window convention regulators use in practice: a violation on the same calendar instant as the inquiry counts; a violation exactly the window-length earlier just stopped counting. The trailing-edge fall-off is the rolling-window's value-changing event for a driver whose rolling total has been at-or-above the threshold and is about to slip below it as the trailing edge advances past an in-window penalty's `observed_at`.

The **endorsement-validity window** on the Driver's `endorsement_valid_until` field is the closed-left, open-right interval `[ now-relative anchor, endorsement_valid_until)` in the sense that the endorsement is in force at every `now()` strictly less than `endorsement_valid_until`. At `now() >= endorsement_valid_until` the endorsement has expired and the sweep's `VALID → INVALID` automatic transition fires (or any time-of-read which observes the comparison flips the live verdict). The grace-window constant `ENDORSEMENT_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` is the days-after-`endorsement_valid_until` that the system tolerates without flipping to `INVALID`; the scaffold pins this at zero so the flip is the exact-moment of crossing — this is the contrast with the permit's 60-day-renewal-open and 30-day-late-grace pair where the late-grace cushion *is* the conceptual analog for the endorsement and the scaffold deliberately gives it zero width.

The **two-time-point hazard-class subset re-check** on the route's submit-and-approve pair is a non-temporal interval check whose subtlety lies in the time-of-check-versus-time-of-use gap rather than a boundary. The operator submits a route whose `hazard_classes_supported` is a subset of the parent company's `hazard_classes_authorized` at the moment of the operator's submit call; the regulator's approve call later re-evaluates the same subset relation against the company's `hazard_classes_authorized` as it stands at the approval moment, which may be a smaller set if the company's authorized list has narrowed between the two timestamps (because, for example, the regulator revoked one of the company's hazard classes through an out-of-band attestation in the gap). The re-check at approval time is the rule: the approve handler reads the company's current authorized list and the route's body-supplied (or stored, since the body's content is fixed at the submit moment) supported list, and the failing case is the supported list having any element no longer in the now-narrower authorized list, with the failure being `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 because the cause is a body-shape-style "this list contains a no-longer-valid value" violation. The same submit-time-and-approve-time consistency pin appears in the route's submit endpoint when it walks the operator's request body's hazard-class list against the company's then-current authorized list, so the operator gets early feedback at submit time if a class is missing from the company's set even though the canonical-correct check is on the approve side. The principle is "the hazard-class fitness is checked at every transition that pivots on it, so a transient narrowing of the company's set during the route's draft-to-approval cycle is caught either at submit (if the narrowing happened before the operator's submit) or at approve (if it happened in the submit-to-approve gap)".

The **scheduled-departure-not-in-the-past** rule on a new trip's `scheduled_departure_at` is the strict-less-than the current logical-clock value at the moment of the trip-create call: `scheduled_departure_at > now()` is the in-window case, the equal case is the boundary that the scaffold pins as not-permitted (the rule's wording is "strictly greater than now"), and the strictly-less case is the rejection `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 for back-dated departures. The arrive-is-strictly-after-depart rule on the scheduled times is the strict-less-than `scheduled_departure_at < scheduled_arrival_at` so a zero-duration trip is not admissible. The same strict-less-than ordering on the actuals — `actual_departure_at < actual_arrival_at` — is implied by the trip-state-machine progression with the actual-departure and actual-arrival each being equal to the logical-clock value at the moment of the depart and arrive endpoint calls respectively, and the strict monotonicity of the clock guaranteeing the strict-less-than relation.

The **inspection-date-not-in-the-future** rule on a new `VehicleInspection`'s `inspected_at` is the inclusive-less-than-or-equal `inspected_at <= now()` at the moment of the inspection-insert call, with the boundary-equal case admissible (an inspection logged at the instant of its physical occurrence is valid). The future-dated case is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` 422. The same convention applies to the manual-penalty's `observed_at` field — `observed_at <= now()` at the create moment.

The **observed-at of a GPS-auto-Penalty** is the parent GPSReport's `reported_at` value (the wall-clock moment of the original observation, not the server-receive moment), so the appeal window on a speed-violation auto-penalty is measured from when the GPS unit said the observation happened, which is the regulatorily-correct point — a sample uploaded hours late after a network outage still has its appeal window anchored on the original observation. The penalty row's `created_at` reflects the server-side insert moment and is the standard system-managed field with the standard ISO 8601 UTC millisecond format.

The conventions across the six axes are summarized: the **permit validity** and the **vehicle calendar** and the **renewal outer-and-late-grace pair** are half-open-right-exclusive (the boundary belongs to the after-state, not the before-state, on the right edge); the **GPS-stream monotonicity** is closed-on-the-prior-edge (the prior cursor's value is not surpassable by an equal new arrival, so the boundary belongs to the prior state); the **appeal duration** and the **void-issue duration** are closed-right (the boundary moment of the duration is the last admissible moment, the strictly-larger moment is the rejection); the **rolling-points window** is open-left-and-closed-right; the **endorsement-validity** is closed-left-and-open-right with the grace-window of width zero; and the **trip-vs-permit window-membership** check on the trip's scheduled arrival is the closed-right exception, where the scheduled arrival is allowed to touch the permit's `valid_until` exactly because the scheduled arrival is itself a planned-but-not-yet-occurred moment and the test layer's existing interval-arithmetic on the permit's validity is separately enforced at the depart-and-gps-ingest time-of-action checks.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency, Idempotent Replays, and Race Resolution

The mutable entities — TransportCompany, Driver, Vehicle, TransportRoute, Permit, and Trip — each carry the `version` integer counter described in §2.4. The optimistic-concurrency-control contract is uniform across every endpoint that mutates one of these rows: the request body may optionally include a `version` integer field equal to the client's expectation of the row's current `version`, and on a mismatch with the row's actual `version` at the moment the handler reads the row, the request is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` at HTTP status 409 (priority 560 in the validation precedence of §2.7). A request body without the `version` field engages no OCC check at all — the handler proceeds with a read-then-write that is subject to the standard last-writer-wins semantics of concurrent same-row mutations, which is the conventional opt-out for callers who do not care about the lost-update race. On a successful mutation the row's `version` is incremented by exactly one and the `updated_at` is set to the current logical-clock value; the response carries the post-mutation values of both fields.

The OCC check applies on the following mutating endpoints, each on its owning entity's row. The company-side endpoints `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/submit`, `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/approve`, and `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/revoke` bump the TransportCompany row's `version`; the driver endorsement attestation `POST /v1/drivers/{driver_id}/refresh-endorsement` bumps the Driver row's `version`; the route's three actions `POST /v1/routes/{route_id}/submit`, `POST /v1/routes/{route_id}/approve`, and `POST /v1/routes/{route_id}/reject` bump the TransportRoute row's `version`; the permit's four mutating actions `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/issue`, `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/verify-compliance`, `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/renew`, and `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/void-issue` bump the Permit row's `version`; and the trip's three state actions `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/depart`, `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/arrive`, and `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/abort` bump the Trip row's `version`. The create-endpoints — `POST /v1/companies`, `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/drivers`, `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/vehicles`, `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/routes`, `POST /v1/permits`, and `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/trips` — are pure inserts that start a fresh row at `version = 1` and have no OCC mechanism, because the row whose version one would check does not yet exist. The append-only event-log inserts — `POST /v1/vehicles/{vehicle_id}/inspections`, the GPS-ingest `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/gps-reports`, and the manual-penalty `POST /v1/penalties` — also do not carry a `version` field because their target entities are common-fields-false and have no `version` to check.

The Vehicle entity has no direct mutating endpoint of its own (vehicles are read-only after creation, save for the indirect refresh of the three derived `latest_inspection_*` pointer fields that the inspection-create endpoint atomically updates as a side-effect of its own append). The atomic side-effect on the parent Vehicle row from a successful inspection-create bumps the parent Vehicle's `version` by one — the inspection's append is a logical mutation on the Vehicle's derived state even though the inspection's own row is itself append-only and has no version of its own. The same idea applies to the Penalty-creation's effect on the subject Driver's cached `current_rolling_points` field: a successful manual-penalty insert or a successful GPS-auto-penalty insert bumps the parent Driver row's `version` because the cached field on the Driver is a logical mutation as the rolling-window's sum changes. The OCC check on the Driver's `version` is exposed on the `refresh-endorsement` endpoint, the only client-facing mutating action on the Driver row, and the implicit-mutation side-effects from penalties do not require an OCC interaction because the client does not directly drive them — the penalty-issuer's OCC happens at the Penalty-insert level (a manual-penalty's body does not have a `version` field at all, in keeping with the append-only nature of the entity; the body has only the cause-fields).

The GPS-ingest endpoint has the **idempotency-key contract** described in §1.0 of the scaffold's response-envelope-and-manifest header. The optional request header `Idempotency-Key` is a deduplication slot key scoped per parent trip: two requests on the same trip carrying the same `Idempotency-Key` value with byte-identical request bodies receive the same 201 response (the original GPSReport row's id and the original auto-penalty ids and an explicit `replayed: true` flag in the response meta), and the second request causes no fresh GPS-row insertion and no fresh auto-Penalty inserts and leaves the parent trip's `latest_gps_at` cursor and `gps_violation_count` exactly as the first request set them. Two requests on the same trip with the same `Idempotency-Key` value but byte-different bodies — the second request's body hash does not match the first's — return the rejection `ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` at HTTP 409, the first request's recorded effect being the canonical one and the second request's effect being suppressed. The same `Idempotency-Key` string used on two different parent trips is two independent slots; the scope is `(trip_id, idempotency_key)` pair-keyed and not global. The slot's lifetime is the trip's lifetime — the scaffold does not pin a garbage-collection window because no test exercises a stale-slot revival.

Five **race-condition contracts** govern the non-idempotent mutating endpoints' concurrent-arrival behaviour. The first race is two trip-creation requests for the same `vehicle_id` arriving with overlapping scheduled-time intervals: the first-writer-wins on the vehicle's calendar table, and the loser's interval is compared against the now-committed winner's interval under the half-open semantics of §2.9; a non-empty intersection causes the loser to be rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_DOUBLE_BOOKED` 409 and exactly-touching boundaries are not an intersection so the second-arrival is admitted in that exact-touch case. The second race is two GPS-ingest requests for the same trip with the same `reported_at` value arriving simultaneously and carrying different `Idempotency-Key` headers (or no headers at all): the strict-greater-than monotonicity check serializes on the parent Trip's `latest_gps_at` cursor — the first-arrival's insert sets the cursor to its own `reported_at`, and the second-arrival's re-read of the cursor sees its own `reported_at` is now equal to (not strictly greater than) the cursor, and the second arrival is rejected with `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_ORDER` 409. The third race is the idempotency-key-with-different-body race described above, which serializes on the `(trip_id, idempotency_key)` slot and rejects the body-hash-mismatched arrival with `ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` 409 while replaying the original 201 on the body-hash-matched arrival. The fourth race is two verify-compliance requests on the same permit arriving concurrently: the OCC mechanism on the Permit row's `version` field serializes them — the first-writer's bump of the `version` is the canonical winner, and the second-arrival's body-version (which it read before the first-writer's commit) does not match the row's now-bumped value, so the second arrival is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` 409. The client's standard pattern on the OCC loser is to re-read the row, observe the post-winner snapshot, and decide whether a fresh verify-compliance is still needed; the response's body and meta on the OCC-loser case carry the row's current `version` so the client can retry with the correct value. The fifth race is a simultaneous renew and void-issue on a freshly-issued permit whose 24-hour void window and 60-day renewal-open window both happen to be open at the same instant (which is the knife-edge near the start of a permit's term where the void-window has not yet closed but the renewal-window — counted backwards from `valid_until`, which is a year away — is also not yet open; in practice this race fires when the deployment's `RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS` equals or exceeds the `PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` minus the `VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS` converted to days, a configuration the test layer pins explicitly). The serialization is again the Permit row's `version` OCC — both endpoints accept an optional `version` and the first-writer-wins, with the loser receiving `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` 409 and observing the winner's effect (either the renewal's appended `renewal_history` entry and the extended `valid_until`, or the void's `lifecycle_status = VOIDED` and the refunded `void_refunded_cents`); if the loser was the void and the winner was the renewal, the loser's retry will see the row in `ACTIVE` with the extended `valid_until` and the renewal-history entry, and the void can still succeed if the wall-clock has not advanced past the 24-hour boundary measured from the unchanged `issued_at` (the `issued_at` is set at the original issue and is not touched by the renewal); if the wall-clock has advanced past that boundary by the time the loser retries, the void's retry fails with the dedicated `ERR_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409 instead.

The OCC contract's interaction with the existence-and-state tiers of §2.7 is the standard "lower priority number wins" — a request whose addressed row has been deleted between the client's last-read and the request's arrival (a hypothetical scenario in this scaffold, which has no delete endpoints, but worth pinning for completeness) would face the existence tier's 404 at priority 300 long before the OCC check at priority 560 fires, and a request whose addressed row's state has transitioned away from the action's required-pre-state by the same race (a second-arrival on a permit-issue whose first-arrival just flipped the state from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`) faces the state tier's `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 at priority 400, again ahead of the OCC at priority 560. The principle is "OCC is the last line of the race-resolution stack, fired only when the row exists and is in the right state and the only thing that's wrong is the client's view of the row's `version` is stale". The body's `version` field is optional on every mutating endpoint that owns the field at the per-entity level documented above, and a body without it is the opt-out the convention permits — a client that wants the OCC guarantee includes the field, a client that just wants the action to land sends the body without it.

The single-row OCC scope is intentional. A request whose successful outcome would mutate two rows (the verify-compliance handler's snapshot-write on the Permit row and the no-op on the grounding-entity rows that the live recompute reads but does not write; the issue handler's wallet-debit on the parent TransportCompany row and the state-flip on the Permit row in the same atomic transaction) holds the row-level lock on the canonical primary row — the Permit row in both cases — and the body's `version` field is the Permit row's version, not a multi-row composite key. The TransportCompany row's wallet-balance updates carried by the issue-and-renew-and-void-issue endpoints' atomic transactions are observable through the company row's bumped `version` after the action commits, but a body-`version` on the action's endpoint refers to the Permit row's version and a stale wallet-side view of the company's balance is not policed by the body's `version` field — the wallet-balance check (the fiscal-tier-540 `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_BALANCE` 402) is the consistency mechanism for the wallet, evaluated at the moment the action's transaction begins, on the company row's value read inside the transaction's read-set. The transactional model is per-action, with the action's mutating writes (the Permit row, the parent Company row's wallet on the fiscal-touching actions, the synchronous trip-cascade side-effects on the Permit's `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and `ACTIVE → VOIDED` transitions) committed together; the sweep's multi-row work fans out as a sequence of per-row transactions rather than one giant transaction so a sweep that hits a transient infrastructure error on its fifty-third permit can leave the first fifty-two transitioned without rolling everything back.

### 2.11. The Wallet Ledger and the Disciplinary-Points Ledger — Two Conservation Invariants

The system carries two independent conservation invariants the test suite can audit across any sequence of actions, one on the currency axis and the other on the disciplinary-points axis. The two invariants govern different aggregates and have no interaction at the data level — voiding a permit refunds the wallet but does not touch the driver's disciplinary record, and upholding a penalty zeroes the upheld points' contribution to the rolling total but does not touch the company's wallet — and the test layer asserts both invariants over disjoint axes.

The **currency conservation invariant** is that the sum of every TransportCompany's `wallet_balance_cents` plus the conceptual regulator-ledger field (which is the running total of every cent the regulator has collected from the carriers, modelled as an implicit out-of-band quantity since the scaffold does not surface it as an entity) is invariant across the issue-renewal-void lifecycle of any permit. Concretely: a permit's issue-step debits the company's wallet by the formula-computed `issue_fee_cents`, which is the value of the calculation `annual_renewal_fee` at the moment of the issue with the `is_late` flag false; each renewal debits the wallet by the renewal-time value of the same formula, with the `is_late` flag set true when the wall-clock arrival is in the late-grace inner-window `[valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS, valid_until)` and false in the outer-but-not-late open window `[valid_until - RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS, valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS)`; the renewal-history list records each renewal as an entry `{renewed_at, fee_cents, late_penalty_applied, prior_valid_until, new_valid_until}` so the audit trail can sum the fee_cents column to obtain the total currency that has flowed from the wallet across the permit's renewals. The void-issue's compensating action computes the refund as the literal sum `issue_fee_cents + sum of fee_cents over the renewal_history`, credits the wallet by exactly that amount, sets the on-row `void_refunded_cents` field to the same value for the audit trail, and transitions the permit's `lifecycle_status` to the terminal `VOIDED`. The wallet ledger's net change across a complete (issue, zero-or-more renewals, void-issue) sequence is therefore exactly zero, which is the conservation identity the test layer asserts over an arbitrary action sequence. The wall-clock-driven expiry of a permit at the `valid_until` boundary is *not* a wallet-touching event — the permit's term has elapsed in the ordinary course and the fees collected over the term are earned, so the `EXPIRED` transition has no compensating wallet credit. The void-issue's 24-hour `VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS` window from the original `issued_at` is the regulatory boundary that distinguishes the clerical-error reversal (which the conservation invariant applies to) from the natural-end-of-term expiry (which it does not). The principle is "the wallet's net change is exactly zero across the issue-plus-renewals-plus-void-issue cycle, and is the sum of the formula's fee outputs across the issue-plus-renewals sequence on the issue-and-renewals-then-expiry path".

The `annual_renewal_fee` formula that drives the wallet debits is the staged combinatorial computation taking four inputs — the current logical-clock value `now`, the integer vehicle count from the permit's `authorized_vehicle_ids` length, the permit's `valid_until` (or null at the issue step, signalling the is-late flag is forced false), and the seven-constant table of fee parameters. The first stage is the subtotal in cents equal to `BASE_RENEWAL_FEE_CENTS` plus the product of the vehicle count and `PER_VEHICLE_SURCHARGE_CENTS`, an integer arithmetic that does not round. The second stage is the conditional add of `LATE_RENEWAL_PENALTY_CENTS` if and only if `valid_until` is non-null and the arrival time `now` is in the closed-left, open-right late-grace inner-window `[valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS, valid_until)`, with the boundary at exactly `now == valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` being the first late moment (the late penalty fires at the exact boundary) and the boundary at exactly `now == valid_until` being the renewal-after-expiry moment (which is the renewal-window's outer-right edge and is rejected by the outer-window check `ERR_RENEWAL_AFTER_EXPIRY` 409 at the validation tier before the formula evaluates). The third stage is the saturating ceiling: the post-late-add subtotal is compared against the cap `MAX_RENEWAL_FEE_CENTS` and the final fee is the minimum of the two. The cap-after-the-late-add ordering matters — the cap saturates the late-surcharge contribution rather than being applied to the base-plus-vehicles subtotal alone, so a near-cap vehicle count with the late flag set may see the late surcharge swallowed by the cap. The fourth stage is the rounding rule, pinned even though the integer-cents arithmetic of the three earlier stages does not introduce any fractional cents in practice: any future multiplicative step that produces a fractional cent is rounded half-up to the nearest whole cent at the moment of the multiplication, the additions happen on the rounded operands, and the cap is applied to the rounded-and-summed value (the "round then cap" rule, fixing the ambiguity that the equivalent "cap then round" would resolve differently at a boundary where the un-rounded subtotal is between the cap and the next-cent-above-the-cap). The formula's output is the integer cents the wallet is debited and which the on-row `issue_fee_cents` and the appended `renewal_history` entry's `fee_cents` field record verbatim, so the void-issue's refund computation re-uses the stored integers rather than re-running the formula — the conservation is bit-exact because the addition of the stored debits and the subtraction of the stored credits cancel exactly.

The **disciplinary-points conservation invariant** is the parallel structure on the points axis. The function `driver_penalty_point_rolling_total` takes a driver id and a `asof_timestamp` and returns the integer sum of the `points_value` field across the set of Penalty rows that satisfy three filters: the row's `subject_type` equals `DRIVER` and its `subject_id` equals the driver id; the row's `void_status` equals `ACTIVE` (the `UPHELD` rows are excluded — an upheld penalty does not count); and the row's `observed_at` lies in the open-on-the-left, closed-on-the-right window `(asof_timestamp - PENALTY_WINDOW_DAYS, asof_timestamp]`. The threshold predicate driving the driver's hazmat-endorsement state is the comparison of this integer total against the constant `THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS` with the inclusive-greater-than-or-equal predicate: at exactly the threshold the driver is in the suspended state, at one below the threshold the driver is in the clean state. The cached `current_rolling_points` field on the Driver row mirrors the function's value at the current logical-clock value `now`, with the cache refreshed whenever a Penalty is created (the new row's points contribute to the live total), whenever a Penalty is upheld (the upheld row's points are subtracted from the live total because the function's filter excludes upheld rows), whenever the sweep fires (the trailing-edge of the rolling window advances past any in-window penalty's `observed_at`, removing those rows' contribution), and whenever a `verify-compliance` runs on a permit that lists the driver (the live-recompute leg of §3.5's permit-validity check reads the Driver's cached field). The cache's freshness invariant is that the cached value equals the function evaluated at the current logical-clock value with the driver's id as input — the same value the dedicated penalty-summary endpoint of §3.8 computes at read time to populate its `current_rolling_points` response field.

The point-axis conservation identity is that a create-then-uphold pair on the same penalty within the rolling window leaves the rolling total at the pre-create value. The create-side bumps the cache by the points-value; the uphold-side flips the row's `void_status` from `ACTIVE` to `UPHELD` and the function's filter on `void_status == ACTIVE` excludes the now-upheld row, so the post-uphold cache value equals the pre-create cache value, modulo any intervening penalty-create-or-uphold events on the same driver in the gap. The audit-vs-operational dual variant on this same axis is the contrast between the as-of-issue snapshot stored on each Permit row's `snapshot_driver_endorsements` map (the per-driver boolean recording "was this driver's flag true at the moment this permit's issue or last verify-compliance happened?", a forensic-audit answer to the question "was the issue lawful given the driver's standing at that moment?") and the current-rolling variant (the rolling-window sum at the current logical-clock value, the operational answer to "is the driver currently above the threshold?"). The two variants are the two flavours of the same quantity evaluated at two different reference timestamps, and the dual-variant axis on the Driver-domain is the disagreement between them when a penalty has been created or upheld in the gap between the snapshot's `last_verified_at` and the present-moment evaluation. The penalty-summary endpoint of §3.8 surfaces both variants in its `permits_currently_listing_this_driver` array — each entry's `snapshot_endorsement_for_this_driver` is the strict-snapshot value and the parallel `live_endorsement_for_this_driver` is the live-mode value — and the difference between the two is the observable signal that a regulator's next verify-compliance call on that permit would change the permit's lifecycle status.

The two conservation invariants are deliberately independent. A penalty-uphold flips the penalty's `void_status` and recomputes the subject driver's `current_rolling_points` but does not touch the wallet ledger — the principle is that the regulator's disciplinary adjudication adjusts the disciplinary record without refunding any monetary effect, and conversely the regulator's clerical-error void of a permit's issue refunds the wallet without touching any of the disciplinary penalties earned during the (now voided) permit's term. The penalties earned across a voided permit's term remain on the disciplinary ledger with their `void_status` unchanged at `ACTIVE` (unless the regulator separately upholds the appeal on each), and the wallet's refund does not interact with the rolling-points axis. The two axes' separation is the test layer's "no cross-axis side-effect" pin and is the reason the scaffold's two conservation rules can be asserted independently with no need for an aggregated multi-axis quantity.

The wallet's individual top-up actions — by which the regulator-side bookkeeping increments a company's `wallet_balance_cents` to reflect a payment received out-of-band through the regulator's banking system — are conceptually additive constants from the system's perspective and are not modelled as an in-API operation in this scaffold. A company that needs funds for a forthcoming issue or renewal acquires the credit through a regulator-side procedure outside the API, and the on-row `wallet_balance_cents` field reflects the post-top-up value on the company's row read after the top-up. The currency-conservation identity applies to the *API-side* deltas — the issue/renew debits and the void-issue credit — and is an identity modulo the out-of-band top-up constant, which is a non-API-side input the conservation statement absorbs as a fixed offset. The standard test-suite pattern is to top up the wallet to a known integer at test setup time (the test fixtures' simulated regulator-side action) and then assert that the in-API deltas across the issue-and-renewals-and-void-issue sequence net to zero against that starting balance.

The full named-constant catalogue used by the two conservation invariants and the four time-window-and-threshold rules is, with the scaffold's pinned where-given values where they appear in the scaffold prose: the **suspension threshold** `THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS` (an integer; the deployment configures the exact value); the **rolling-window length** `PENALTY_WINDOW_DAYS` (the scaffold's prose names "the 12-month rolling window" and "the 365-day rolling window" interchangeably as the intended scale, 365 days the nominal value the deployment is expected to use); the **appeal-window length** `APPEAL_WINDOW_DAYS` (the scaffold's intended value is 30 days, the closed-right boundary on the appeal-uphold's `now() - observed_at` comparison); the **void-issue window length** `VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS` (the scaffold's intended value is 24 hours, the closed-right boundary on the void's `now() - issued_at` comparison); the **inspection validity length** `INSPECTION_VALIDITY_DAYS` (the scaffold pins 180 days, with a `FAILED` outcome's `valid_until` overridden to the same instant as the `inspected_at` for a zero-validity certificate); the **permit term length** `PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` (the scaffold's "annual" naming convention pins the nominal value at one year, and the renewal's term-extension is by the same constant amount measured from the prior `valid_until` rather than from `now()` so the term-end stride is exact); the **renewal-open-window length** `RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS` (the scaffold's prose names "the 60-day renew-open window" as the intended scale, 60 days the nominal value); the **late-grace-window length** `LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` (the inner-window-width inside the outer renewal-open window beyond which the late surcharge applies; the deployment configures the integer); the **vehicle-list cap** `MAX_VEHICLES_PER_PERMIT` (the deployment-configured upper bound on the permit's `authorized_vehicle_ids` cardinality; the lower bound is the constant 1); the **GPS speed limit** `MAX_HAZMAT_SPEED_KPH` (the strict upper bound on a sample's `speed_kph` above which the speed-violation classifier fires); the **off-route radius** `OFF_ROUTE_RADIUS_M` (the strict upper bound in meters on the perpendicular distance to the polyline above which the off-route classifier fires); the **fee-formula's four cents constants** `BASE_RENEWAL_FEE_CENTS` and `PER_VEHICLE_SURCHARGE_CENTS` and `LATE_RENEWAL_PENALTY_CENTS` and `MAX_RENEWAL_FEE_CENTS`; the **two GPS-auto-penalty points-values** `GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED_POINTS` and `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE_POINTS`; the **Earth-radius geodesic constant** `EARTH_RADIUS_M` whose pinned value is 6,371,000.0 meters for the haversine-segment perpendicular-distance computation; and the **endorsement-renewal grace constant** `ENDORSEMENT_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` whose pinned value is zero days for the hard-cutoff convention on the driver's endorsement-expiry. The deployment loads these symbols from configuration at startup; the implementation surface they touch is the calculation modules that compute the fee and classify the GPS violations and evaluate the rolling-points threshold and the various time-window guards, each module reading the constants by symbol name through the configuration loader.

## 3. Business Logic and Workflows

The eight subsections that follow walk the system's domain logic in the order the entities themselves are introduced into a hypothetical fresh carrier's operating sequence: the carrier files its initial qualification application and is approved by the regulator (§3.1), then registers its drivers and the regulator attests their hazmat endorsements (§3.2), then registers its vehicles and the regulator appends the inspection certificates (§3.3), then files a route application that the regulator approves (§3.4), at which point the carrier can file a permit application that the regulator issues — the central act after which day-to-day operations begin (§3.5). The renewal-and-void path on the issued permit (§3.6), the per-trip scheduling and lifecycle (§3.7), and the GPS-telemetry stream with its automatic-penalty cross-aggregate cascade (§3.8) round out the operational core. Each subsection names the state-machine transitions in upper-case state-name strings exactly as the enums declare them, identifies the actor role for each transition, names the error ids the rejection paths produce, and ties each rule's wording back to the cross-cutting principle in §2 it is the realization of.

### 3.1. The Carrier Qualification Lifecycle

The **TransportCompany** is the carrier-side aggregate root. Its lifecycle is governed by the state-machine state-field `qualification_status` whose four states are `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, and `REVOKED`, with no terminal-absorbing state because a carrier is a long-lived legal entity that can cycle through revocation-and-reinstatement repeatedly. Every state transition is fired by an endpoint named in the §4 endpoint reference, with the actor role drawn from the §1.7 RBAC matrix.

A carrier comes into being when a CompanyOperator files the initial application through the company-create endpoint, carrying the body fields `legal_name` (a free-text 1-to-200-character string), `registration_number` (the format-constrained government-issued identifier matching the regex of eight-to-thirty-two uppercase letters and digits and system-wide unique — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` 409 at the business-constraint priority 520), `primary_contact_email` (an RFC 5322 syntactically valid email address for the responsible safety officer; the syntactic check is the body-shape-validation tier's catch-all `ERR_VALIDATION` on a syntactic miss), `address_line` (a 0-to-500-character free-text headquarters address, not load-bearing for any rule), and the non-empty array `hazard_classes_authorized` enumerating the UN hazard-class subset the carrier is applying to handle (drawn from the UN-hazard-class taxonomy of §2.8; an empty array or an entry outside the taxonomy is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422). The response is the 201 row at `qualification_status = DRAFT`, with the server-minted `id`, the four standard system-managed fields, the bootstrap-time `wallet_balance_cents = 0` (the regulator-side top-up funds the wallet out-of-band as discussed in §2.11), and the body-fields echoed including the operator's-stated hazard-class authorization list. The bootstrap-identity special case discussed in §2.6 applies on this endpoint: the request's `X-User-ID` is not yet a TransportCompany id because the row is the one being created, and the server mints the fresh UUID server-side and the operator switches to it on every subsequent call.

The `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` transition is the operator's act of submitting the completed application to the regulator's review queue, fired by the submit endpoint at the path `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/submit`. The guard's conjunction requires the acting operator's `X-User-ID` to equal the path-parameter `company_id` (the cross-tenant predicate at priority 530 throwing `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 on a wrong-tenant submit), the company's current `qualification_status` to be in the set `{DRAFT, REVOKED}` (the standard `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 on a wrong-state submit), the field `hazard_classes_authorized` to be non-empty, and the fields `primary_contact_email` and `legal_name` to be non-empty (the body-pre-submit-required-fields-non-empty check at the catch-all body-shape priority 590 throwing the generic `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 on a missing field). The handler atomically writes `submitted_at` to the current logical-clock value, bumps the row's `version` by one (subject to the standard OCC contract on the optional body `version` field as described in §2.10, with a mismatch producing `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` 409 at priority 560), and refreshes the row's `updated_at`. The two-source-state `{DRAFT, REVOKED}` allowance is the **revocation revision-loop cycle**: a carrier whose qualification was revoked for a serious safety incident may reapply by going through the same submit endpoint, with the handler internally walking the state in the order `REVOKED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED` as a single atomic step (the externally observable trace is the `qualification_status` value flipping from `REVOKED` before the call to `SUBMITTED` after, with the `submitted_at` refreshed to the new submission's timestamp and the previous `revoked_at` and `approved_at` fields left as the audit trail of the prior cycle). The internal `REVOKED → DRAFT` arc has its own guard requiring the source state be `REVOKED` and the acting operator be the company's owner; the externally observable behavior is the same single submit endpoint accepting the call regardless of whether the source state was `DRAFT` (the first-time-submission case) or `REVOKED` (the reapply-after-revocation case). The presence of this cycle is what makes the company's state machine non-linear in the sense the scaffold's complexity-profile prose identifies — a graph with a back-edge to the initial-or-near-initial state, in contrast with a strictly linear lifecycle that has no back-edges.

The `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` transition is the regulator's act of attesting the qualification, fired by the approve endpoint at `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/approve`. The actor role is `RegulatorOfficer` per the RBAC cell for this endpoint, and a non-regulator hitting this endpoint gets the default-deny `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403 from the authorization tier of §2.7. The body is optional, carrying just the `version` field for OCC and the `approval_note` free-text 0-to-500-character note that the audit trail records out-of-band but does not store on the row. The guard's same-row pre-state must be `SUBMITTED`; a state mismatch is the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 from the state tier. The handler writes `approved_at` to the current clock value (overwriting any prior value from a previous approve-revoke-reapply cycle, with the convention that the `approved_at` documents the most recent approval, not the original first one), bumps `version` and refreshes `updated_at`. The wallet is not touched by this transition — the wallet is only touched by the permit-side fiscal actions of §3.6.

The `APPROVED → REVOKED` transition is the regulator's stripping of the qualification, fired by `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/revoke`. The actor is again the regulator, and the body carries the required `reason_note` 1-to-500-character free-text cause (a missing or empty reason note is the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422), plus the optional OCC `version`. The handler writes `revoked_at` to the current logical-clock value, bumps `version` and refreshes `updated_at`. The cross-aggregate cascade onto the company's already-issued ACTIVE permits is intentionally **deferred** rather than synchronous — the revoke action does not walk the Permit table inside its own transaction. Instead, every subsequent verify-compliance call on each affected permit observes that the live recompute of the company-leg of the validity AND-chain (the predicate `company.qualification_status == "APPROVED"`) is now false even though the permit's strict-snapshot's `snapshot_company_qualified` was true at the last verification, and the verify-compliance handler fires the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` automatic transition on the permit per the rules of §3.5; the daily sweep's per-ACTIVE-permit-verify-compliance fan-out (the canonical flush mechanism described in §2.5) provides the determinism guarantee for the cascade's eventual landing without requiring the regulator to walk each permit by hand. This deferred-cascade pattern is the **eventual-consistency seam** that the test harness exercises by sequencing a revoke call, a clock-advance, and a sweep-run — the post-sweep snapshot shows every previously-ACTIVE permit on the now-revoked company in `SUSPENDED`, and the audit trail's per-transition `cause` discriminator in the sweep's response carries the value `GROUNDING_INVALIDATION` for these cascade-driven transitions. The downward arm of the **rule-4 cascade-A chain** has its starting hop in this revoke action's deferred cascade, with the chain continuing to the per-permit verify-compliance's status flip and (when the suspension is mid-flight on a trip) the gated-GPS-stream behaviour described in §3.7.

The reverse cycle's upward arm — the `REVOKED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED` reapply path described above, followed by the regulator's fresh approval to `APPROVED` — produces the **rule-4 cascade upward**, the symmetric reinstatement: every previously-`SUSPENDED` permit on this company that's still within its `valid_from`/`valid_until` window observes the now-restored company-leg on its next verify-compliance and transitions `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` (the sweep's `GROUNDING_REVALIDATION` cause), provided all four other validity legs (every driver's endorsement, every vehicle's inspection, the route's approval, and the time-window) also hold. The upward arm has the same eventual-consistency property as the downward — the company's approve action does not synchronously touch the permits — and the same sweep-or-explicit-verify-compliance flush mechanism brings the cascade to completion.

The list endpoint `GET /v1/companies` is the cross-tenant-filterable list of companies. The optional `qualification_status` query filter is a comma-separated subset of the enum values `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, and `REVOKED` and narrows the response to companies in any of the listed statuses. The optional `company_id` query filter narrows further to a single company — the regulator may pass any company's id (the cross-tenant-allow column for the regulator), while the operator may pass only their own (the cross-tenant predicate fires `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 on an operator's request whose `company_id` query parameter does not equal their `X-User-ID`, the special-case convention described in §2.6). The single-company read endpoint `GET /v1/companies/{company_id}` returns the named row's current state with the same cross-tenant predicate: a regulator reads any row, an operator reads only the row whose `id` equals the operator's `X-User-ID`, and the cross-tenant 403's distinct error id `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` separates this rejection cause from the generic "the role is not allowed on this endpoint at all" cause `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The not-found case at the existence tier is `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND` 404 and fires before the cross-tenant check per the rule-5 priority-conflict-B convention.

The carrier's qualification status is the gating precondition for every downstream act: a not-yet-`APPROVED` company cannot register drivers (the children-creation endpoints `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/drivers`, `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/vehicles`, and `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/routes` each fail at the `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409 business-referential precondition tier when the parent company's `qualification_status` is anything other than `APPROVED`); a not-yet-`APPROVED` company cannot have a permit issued against it (the issue endpoint's company-leg check fires `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409 at the same tier, and the application endpoint enforces the same predicate on the company filing the application, as discussed in §3.5); and a previously-`APPROVED` company that flips to `REVOKED` mid-permit fires the deferred-cascade-into-`SUSPENDED` on every existing ACTIVE permit, as the rule-4-cascade-A upward-narration above documents. The wallet field `wallet_balance_cents` is the company-side fiscal anchor of the system — it is initialized at zero on the company-create, topped up out-of-band by the regulator's-side banking system to the value needed for the next fee-paying action, and debited atomically inside the issue and renew handlers of §3.6 by the formula-computed fee. A zero or insufficient balance at the moment of the issue or renew handler's fiscal check is the unique HTTP 402 `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_BALANCE` rejection of §2.7's fiscal tier 540 — the only endpoint family in the system that returns HTTP 402.

### 3.2. Drivers, the Endorsement Attestation, and the Rolling Points Standing

Once a company is in `qualification_status = APPROVED`, the operator may register drivers under it through the children-create endpoint `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/drivers`. A **Driver** is a person who works for one specific TransportCompany, with the foreign-key `company_id` matching the parent's id and immutable thereafter — a driver who moves to a different company is registered fresh under that new company rather than re-keyed on the existing row. The acting principal is the parent company's operator (the cross-tenant rule at priority 530 requires `X-User-ID == {company_id}` and the parent-must-be-`APPROVED` rule at the business-referential tier produces `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409 on the not-yet-approved case). The body carries the driver's `full_name` (a 1-to-120-character free-text legal name as it appears on the licence document), the government-issued `license_number` (a no-whitespace 6-to-32-character string unique within the company — the per-company uniqueness scope, contrasted with the system-wide scope of the vehicle's plate number, means two companies' drivers can carry the same string and the natural-key collision-error `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` 409 at priority 520 fires only on a same-company collision), the `license_class` short-code string (an example would be `"A2"` or `"B1"`; the value is free-string-formatted because no business rule reads it), and an `endorsement_classes_requested` array of UN hazard-class strings the operator attests the driver is qualified to handle (the array's entries must each be in the UN-hazard-class taxonomy of §2.8 with no duplicates, but the operator-asserted set is conceptually a wish-list and the on-disk `endorsement_classes` field is initialized to the empty array — the regulator-verified set is what gets stored, after the refresh-endorsement attestation below).

The new driver row's `hazmat_endorsement_status` is the initial value `NOT_ATTESTED`, the field's initial state in the three-value state machine on `{NOT_ATTESTED, VALID, INVALID}`. In this state the driver carries no regulator attestation and cannot appear in any permit's `authorized_driver_ids` array — an attempt to file a permit application listing a NOT_ATTESTED driver gets `ERR_DRIVER_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID` 409 at the safety business-rule tier (priority 510), with the same error id covering the NOT_ATTESTED-cause and the regulator-revoked-cause and the points-threshold-crossed-cause and the wall-clock-endorsement-expired-cause through the unified "is the endorsement currently in the green-light state `VALID`?" predicate. The decoupling of the cause from the error id is the scaffold's "keep the error catalogue at twenty-five entries" choice; the four sub-causes are observable on the row through the `hazmat_endorsement_status` field's value (which tells `NOT_ATTESTED` from `INVALID`) and the `endorsement_valid_until` field (which distinguishes the still-future-but-revoked case from the wall-clock-expired case by carrying either the future timestamp the regulator wrote at the last attestation or the now-elapsed timestamp the attestation has aged past) and the `current_rolling_points` field (which distinguishes the over-threshold case from the under-threshold-but-otherwise-revoked case). The newly registered driver's other system-set fields are `endorsement_valid_until = null`, `endorsement_classes = []`, and `current_rolling_points = 0`, the standard common-fields quartet, and the response is the 201 with the body and the system fields echoed.

The regulator's endorsement-attestation endpoint is `POST /v1/drivers/{driver_id}/refresh-endorsement`. Its actor is the `RegulatorOfficer` and its allow-set excludes every other role (the operator cannot self-attest its own drivers — the canonical regulatory-attestation gate — and the driver cannot self-attest either, on the principle that the endorsement is the regulator's grant and not the driver's self-declaration; both are `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403 at priority 200). The request body has two mutually exclusive shapes. The **attestation shape** carries `endorsement_valid_until` (an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp strictly later than the current logical-clock value — a past-or-equal value is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422) and `endorsement_classes` (a non-empty subset of the parent company's `hazard_classes_authorized` at the moment of the call; an element outside this intersection is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422). The **revoke shape** carries the `revoke: true` boolean and the `revoke_reason` 1-to-500-character free-text, with the attestation-shape fields absent. A body containing both shapes' fields or neither's is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422. Both shapes accept the optional OCC `version` integer per §2.10.

The attestation shape drives the `NOT_ATTESTED → VALID` and the `INVALID → VALID` transitions in the Driver state machine, and the guard's preconditions are: the source state is in the set `{NOT_ATTESTED, INVALID}` (a `VALID` source under an attestation-shape body is the standard `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 — the regulator cannot re-attest an already-valid driver without first flipping it via a revoke); the body's `endorsement_valid_until` is strictly greater than the current logical-clock value; the body's `endorsement_classes` is non-empty and is a subset of the parent company's authorized hazard-class list at the call moment; and the driver's `current_rolling_points` is strictly less than the suspension threshold `THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS` at the moment of the call. The points-precondition is a regulatory-attestation interlock: the regulator may not attest a driver whose current rolling total is at or above the threshold, because doing so would put the driver in a contradictory state (`VALID` while the points-rule alone would say `INVALID`). The path to bring an over-threshold driver back to `VALID` is the rolling-window's natural trailing-edge fall-off (waiting until enough penalties' `observed_at` slips past the window's lower edge so the rolling total drops below the threshold) or the appeal-uphold compensating action of §3.8 (which retroactively zeroes the upheld penalty's contribution); after either has reduced the rolling total below the threshold the regulator's attestation may then fire the transition to `VALID`. The handler on a successful attestation writes the body's `endorsement_valid_until` and `endorsement_classes` onto the row, sets `hazmat_endorsement_status = VALID`, bumps `version` and refreshes `updated_at`. The status flips to `VALID` regardless of whether the prior state was `NOT_ATTESTED` (the first-ever attestation) or `INVALID` (the re-attestation after the cause of the prior invalidation has cleared); the on-disk audit trail is the bumped `version` and the refreshed `endorsement_valid_until`.

The revoke shape drives the manual `VALID → INVALID` transition, with the handler writing `endorsement_valid_until` equal to the current logical-clock value (so the natural wall-clock-expiry predicate is also retroactively true and the wall-clock-expiry path's observability is the same), flipping `hazmat_endorsement_status` to `INVALID`, recording the `revoke_reason` to the audit log (the scaffold does not store the revoke reason on the row, sending it to an out-of-band audit stream), and bumping `version` and `updated_at`. A revoke-shape body on a non-`VALID` source state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 (the regulator cannot manually revoke a driver who is already `NOT_ATTESTED` or `INVALID`, on the principle that the manual-revoke action's effect is conceptually a "withdraw the standing attestation" and a driver with no standing attestation has nothing to withdraw).

There are two **automatic** transitions on the Driver state machine that fire without a direct API call. The first is the wall-clock-driven `VALID → INVALID` at the moment `now() >= endorsement_valid_until`: the daily sweep of §2.5 walks the driver table and flips every `VALID` driver whose stored `endorsement_valid_until` is at or past the current logical-clock value. The grace-window constant `ENDORSEMENT_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` is the days-after-the-stored-validity-end that the system tolerates before firing the flip; the scaffold's intended value of zero days makes the flip happen at the exact moment of crossing, in deliberate contrast with the permit's late-renewal grace that has a non-zero width. The second automatic flip is the rolling-points-driven `VALID → INVALID` fired as a synchronous side-effect of a Penalty-insert when the post-insert recompute of the subject driver's `current_rolling_points` is at or above the threshold; this side-effect happens inside the same atomic transaction that wrote the Penalty row, so the post-penalty-create observation of the Driver's row shows the new points value and the flipped status together. The third automatic mode — the body-revoke-shape flag's effect on the refresh-endorsement endpoint — is a hybrid: it is fired by an API call but takes the system-actor identity from the perspective of the state machine's diagram, sharing the effect with the wall-clock-and-points automatic paths. The upward `INVALID → VALID` direction is the manual attestation only; the **keep-the-regulator-in-the-loop** convention forbids an automatic upward flip even when the rolling-points fall-off or the appeal-uphold has brought the total back below the threshold — the regulator must explicitly run the attestation endpoint after the disqualifying cause has cleared. This rule asymmetry between the downward-auto and the upward-manual directions is the regulatory-audit invariant the scaffold's complexity-profile prose flags.

The Driver's three deviation-cell read endpoints from the §1.7 RBAC matrix are: `GET /v1/drivers/{driver_id}` accessible by the parent company's operator (with the cross-tenant predicate at priority 530 requiring the operator's `X-User-ID` to equal the driver's `company_id`), the regulator (cross-tenant exempt), and the driver themselves (with the self-only predicate at priority 531 requiring the Driver-role's `X-User-ID` to equal the path's `{driver_id}`), all on the not-found-first-then-cross-tenant ordering of the validation tiers. The `GET /v1/drivers/{driver_id}/penalty-summary` endpoint surfaces the disciplinary view of the driver — discussed in detail in §3.8 — and has the same three-role access pattern. There is no GET-list of drivers in the scaffold's endpoint surface; the audit of all drivers across a company is done by the operator's iteration over the GET-by-id endpoint, and the regulator's audit of a specific driver is the same endpoint with the regulator's cross-tenant exemption letting it read any driver. The Driver entity's cached `current_rolling_points` is the canonical observable for the disciplinary-points conservation invariant of §2.11 — the test layer reads the field before and after a sequence of penalty-create-and-uphold actions to confirm the conservation identity holds across the algebra of the rolling window.

The cross-permit cascade from a Driver's endorsement-flip is the **rule-4 cascade-A** chain's third hop — the chain starts with a GPS-report whose `speed_kph` strictly exceeds `MAX_HAZMAT_SPEED_KPH` (the speed-violation classifier of §2.8) inserting an auto-Penalty row of `source = GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED` and `points_value = GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED_POINTS` against the trip's driver, the auto-Penalty's atomic side-effect bumping the driver's `current_rolling_points` to a value possibly at or above the threshold, the threshold-crossing flipping the `hazmat_endorsement_status` to `INVALID` in the same transaction, and the deferred-cascade-onto-permits firing on the next verify-compliance call on each permit whose `authorized_driver_ids` contains the just-flipped driver. The depth-from-the-GPS-report-trigger is four hops (the GPS-report-insert, the Penalty-insert side-effect, the Driver-endorsement-flip side-effect, the Permit-suspend on the next verify-compliance), comfortably exceeding the cascade-depth-three lower bound the scaffold's rule-4 prose pins. The corresponding upward arm — the appeal-uphold of §3.8 reducing the rolling total below the threshold, followed by a regulator's explicit refresh-endorsement attestation flipping the status back to `VALID`, followed on each affected permit by a verify-compliance observing the now-valid drivers-leg and firing `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` — is the same depth-four chain in reverse and is the standard "reinstate after appeal" workflow the audit trail records on a regulator's discretionary action.

A subtlety on the upward arm: the appeal-uphold of a single penalty alone does not flip the driver's endorsement back to `VALID`. The uphold side-effect zeroes the upheld row's contribution to the rolling total (the points-conservation invariant of §2.11), which may bring the total back below the threshold, but the driver remains in `INVALID` until the regulator separately runs the refresh-endorsement attestation. This two-step pattern is the regulatory record-keeping convention that the regulator's authority is the act of attestation, not the automatic consequence of a points fall-off; the test layer asserts this by issuing an uphold and observing the Driver's `current_rolling_points` decremented while `hazmat_endorsement_status` is unchanged, then issuing a refresh-endorsement and observing the status flip. The sweep's per-driver `status_flip` discriminator value `VALID_TO_INVALID` covers only the downward direction precisely because the upward direction is not automatic.

The Driver's `company_id` foreign key is set at insert and immutable thereafter — the scaffold provides no driver-transfer or driver-delete endpoint, so a driver's binding to a company is permanent in the system's view, and a real-world driver who changes employers is registered as a fresh driver row under the new company. The operator's outgoing-employee bookkeeping is conceptually an out-of-band administrative action with no API surface; the on-API observation is simply that the old driver row remains in the system in whatever last-attested state it was in, with no further mutation absent regulator action. The `endorsement_classes` field on the driver — the regulator-attested per-driver hazard-class subset — is the constraint the permit-application's per-driver check uses: a permit's `hazard_classes` field must be a non-empty subset of the intersection of the company's `hazard_classes_authorized` and the route's `hazard_classes_supported`, and a stricter version of the same compatibility check at trip-creation time considers the specific driver and vehicle named on the trip and the trip's single `cargo_hazard_class`. The driver-side hazard-class set is the regulatory-jurisdiction limit on the per-driver authorization (the driver is certified by the regulator for some subset of hazard classes the company is in general allowed to carry, and the regulator's training-and-certification database tracks the driver's individual standing), and the array's body-shape constraints — non-empty, no duplicates, every entry in the UN-hazard-class taxonomy of §2.8, every entry also in the parent company's `hazard_classes_authorized` at the moment the regulator's attestation writes it — are the regulator-side rule the refresh-endorsement endpoint's handler enforces.

The Driver role's three read-allowed endpoints — the by-id driver read, the by-id trip read on a trip whose `driver_id` matches the role's `X-User-ID`, and the by-id penalty-summary read on the matching driver-id path — exhaust the role's API surface. The role has no write privileges, which is the data-minimization principle for the driver-individual: the driver observes the consequences of the regulator-and-operator's actions on the driver's standing (the rolling-points total, the endorsement status, the list of permits the driver is currently authorized on, the trip the driver is currently assigned to) but does not initiate any state change of their own through the API. The operational driving activity — the GPS samples observed by the vehicle's on-board GPS unit during a trip — is pushed up the gps-report ingest path by the company's fleet-tracking back-end under the operator's role credentials, not the driver's, on the same operator-mediates-the-driver convention as the trip depart and arrive endpoints.

### 3.3. Vehicles and the Append-Only Inspection Ledger

A **Vehicle** is a physical hazmat-rated truck or tanker owned and operated by one TransportCompany, with the same parent-must-be-`APPROVED` precondition and the same cross-tenant ownership predicate as the Driver. The operator registers a vehicle through `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/vehicles`, with the body carrying `plate_number` (a 4-to-16-character licence-plate string whose uniqueness scope is system-wide — a collision against any vehicle in any company is `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` 409 at the natural-key-uniqueness priority 520; the contrast with the driver's per-company-scoped licence-number uniqueness is one of the scaffold's keep-the-error-id-on-the-cause-rather-than-the-scope choices), the `vehicle_type` enum value drawn from the §2.8 vehicle-class taxonomy of `TANKER_LIQUID`, `TANKER_GAS`, `BOX_DRY_BULK`, `FLATBED_PACKAGED`, and `OTHER`, the integer `max_payload_kg` of the manufacturer-rated cargo capacity in kilograms (strictly positive), and the non-empty no-duplicates `hazard_classes_compatible` array drawn from the UN-hazard-class taxonomy. The system-set fields on the new row are the three derived "latest inspection" pointers `latest_inspection_id`, `latest_inspection_outcome`, and `latest_inspection_valid_until` — all null on a freshly registered vehicle because no inspection has yet been appended — and the standard four-field common-set with `version` starting at 1. The response is the 201 row.

The vehicle row is **read-only** after registration in the sense that no PATCH or update endpoint exists on it — the vehicle's static attributes (the plate, the type, the payload, the compatibility list) are fixed at registration time and a "change of plate" or a "re-certification of the compatibility list" is conceptually a fresh-vehicle registration. The dynamic state of a vehicle — whether it has a current valid inspection — is the **VehicleInspection** event log keyed by `vehicle_id`, and the parent Vehicle row's three derived `latest_inspection_*` fields are kept in sync with the head of the inspection log atomically as each inspection is appended. The Vehicle row's `version` field bumps on each inspection-append's atomic side-effect on the parent — the inspection's own row is the append-only ledger entry with no `version` (per the common-fields-false convention of §2.4), and the Vehicle's derived-state mutation is the visible "this aggregate has been touched" signal the OCC of any concurrent vehicle-mutating action would observe.

The **VehicleInspection** entity is the append-only certificate of a regulator's physical inspection of a vehicle, recorded through `POST /v1/vehicles/{vehicle_id}/inspections`. The actor role is the `RegulatorOfficer`; the operator cannot self-inspect its own vehicles because the audit-trail integrity requires the inspector identity to be a regulator's officer rather than the operator's identity. The body carries `inspected_at` (an ISO 8601 UTC wall-clock timestamp of the physical inspection's occurrence; the value must be at or before the current logical-clock value because future-dated inspections are not permitted, with the rejection being the body-shape catch-all `ERR_VALIDATION` 422), the `outcome` enum value drawn from the §2.8 inspection-outcomes taxonomy of `PASSED`, `FAILED`, and `CONDITIONAL`, the optional `findings_note` 0-to-2000-character free-text inspector note, and an optional `inspector_id` field that overrides the audit-recorded inspector identity (defaulting to the request's `X-User-ID` when the field is absent — the regulator's opaque officer string discussed in §2.1). The server-computed system fields on the new inspection row are `valid_until`, computed as `inspected_at + INSPECTION_VALIDITY_DAYS` for a `PASSED` outcome (the standard inspection-validity window length the scaffold pins at 180 days) and forced equal to the `inspected_at` itself for a `FAILED` outcome (the zero-validity case, so the certificate carries no forward licensing weight). A `CONDITIONAL` outcome's `valid_until` is the same `inspected_at + INSPECTION_VALIDITY_DAYS` as the `PASSED` case but the outcome's interpretation in the permit-validity AND-chain is "non-compliant" — only the `PASSED` outcome counts as compliant for the vehicle-leg of §3.5's validity check, while `CONDITIONAL` and `FAILED` are both treated as a failed vehicle-leg. The on-disk `outcome` and `valid_until` fields together encode the distinction between "the most recent inspection was a clean pass with this validity-end-date", "the most recent inspection was conditional with this nominal validity-end-date but the vehicle does not licence hazmat transit until the next clean pass", and "the most recent inspection was a fail with no forward validity at all". The free-text `findings_note` is the inspector's prose record of what was observed; it is not load-bearing for any state-machine guard but is captured on the row for the audit trail.

The inspection-append's atomic side-effect on the parent Vehicle row is the **"latest inspection" pointer refresh**: if the newly-inserted inspection's `inspected_at` is the largest across the vehicle's complete inspection history, the parent Vehicle's `latest_inspection_id` is set to the new inspection's id, `latest_inspection_outcome` to the new outcome, and `latest_inspection_valid_until` to the new inspection's `valid_until` field. The tie-break for two inspections sharing the exact same `inspected_at` timestamp is the lexicographically larger inspection `id` — the same UUID-string-lex comparison the GPS-stream's tie-break uses in §3.8 — and the convention "the lex-larger id wins" lets the test layer pin a specific ordering on contrived equal-timestamp inserts by knowing the UUIDs in advance, though in practice UUIDs from a single generator are lex-uncorrelated and the equal-timestamp case is exceedingly rare. An inspection whose `inspected_at` is not the largest across the history is appended verbatim but does not change the parent's pointer fields — the historical-correction case (a regulator backfilling a missed inspection from an earlier date) does not retroactively change which inspection is "the latest" because the latest-by-timestamp invariant is on the timestamp axis, not the insertion-order axis.

The inspection-list endpoint `GET /v1/vehicles/{vehicle_id}/inspections` returns the named vehicle's complete inspection history, sorted by `inspected_at` descending and ties broken by `id` descending (the same tie-break direction as the latest-pointer's, so the head of the list is exactly the inspection the parent Vehicle's `latest_inspection_*` fields point at). Pagination is the standard `limit` and `offset` quartet of §2.3 with the default limit of 10 and the standard maximum of 100. The optional `outcome` query filter narrows the response to inspections whose `outcome` is the named enum value. The cross-tenant predicate on the operator's read is on the parent vehicle's `company_id` matching the operator's `X-User-ID`, the standard transitive-ownership rule for child-of-vehicle reads. The regulator reads any vehicle's inspection history. The Driver and TestHarness roles have no access.

The vehicle-inspection's role in the **permit-validity AND-chain** is the vehicle-leg of the five-leg conjunction in §3.5. For each vehicle in a permit's `authorized_vehicle_ids` list, the vehicle's effective compliance flag is the boolean conjunction `latest_inspection_outcome == "PASSED" AND latest_inspection_valid_until >= permit.valid_until` — meaning the head-of-history inspection must be a clean pass and the inspection's forward-validity end must extend through the permit's term-end. A vehicle that fails either sub-check at the moment of a permit's issue is the rejection cause `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409 at the safety-tier priority 511; the same predicate at the verify-compliance time is the live-recompute leg of the vehicle conjunction in the validity check of §2.11 and triggers the permit's `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` automatic transition on a previously-clean permit whose vehicle's inspection has aged out or been replaced with a non-`PASSED` successor. The trip-creation endpoint's per-vehicle live re-check (the trip's `vehicle_id` must have a `PASSED` latest inspection whose `valid_until` is at or after the trip's `scheduled_arrival_at`) fires the same error id `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409 when the regulator's revocation of a vehicle's inspection (through a fresh `FAILED` append) has happened between the permit's issue and the trip-creation moment. The cross-aggregate cascade trajectory — a fresh `FAILED` inspection's append flipping the parent vehicle's `latest_inspection_outcome` to `FAILED`, the next verify-compliance on every permit listing the vehicle observing the vehicle-leg of the validity AND-chain flipped to false, and the per-permit `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` transition firing — is the cascade from the regulator's bad-inspection-finding side of the rule-4 cascade-A family, parallel to the GPS-violation-and-points-threshold-cascading-to-the-driver-flip-cascading-to-the-permit-suspend chain, with the difference being the trigger (a regulator's inspection append in this case versus a GPS-report's automatic violation in the other case) and the depth (three hops here — inspection-append, vehicle's-pointer-refresh side-effect, next-verify-compliance's permit-suspension — versus four hops on the GPS path through the intermediate Penalty-insert and Driver-flip side-effects).

The Vehicle's read endpoint `GET /v1/vehicles/{vehicle_id}` returns the row's current state including the three derived latest-inspection pointer fields, with the standard cross-tenant predicate (operator must own the parent company, regulator is cross-tenant exempt, the driver and test-harness roles have no access). The not-found case is `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND` 404 at the existence tier, and an existing-but-wrong-tenant vehicle returns `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 after the existence check passes per the existence-beats-cross-tenant convention of §2.6. The system has no vehicle-update endpoint (the row is read-only post-registration) and no vehicle-delete endpoint (the natural end-of-life of a vehicle is the regulator's failed inspection of it, after which the vehicle's parent permits suspend and the operator either retires the vehicle in the company's own fleet-management system or files a fresh registration after physical decommissioning and replacement). There is no vehicle-list endpoint either — the audit of all vehicles in a company is the operator's iteration over the parent-company's permit listings and the per-permit `authorized_vehicle_ids` reads, or the regulator's same iteration cross-tenant. This is the same minimalist endpoint surface the route and the permit aggregates carry on the read side — only the specific-id reads exist for the day-to-day operations, with the dedicated list endpoints reserved for the company-, the inspection-history, the permit-, the trip-GPS, and the driver-penalty-summary collections where listing is the canonical access pattern.

The Vehicle's wallet-or-fiscal interaction is **none** — the vehicle does not carry money and the regulator's inspection action is fee-free (the only fee-paying actions in the scaffold are the permit-issue and the permit-renew, both on the Permit aggregate per §3.6). The vehicle's hazard-class compatibility list `hazard_classes_compatible` is the construction-certified set of classes the vehicle's physical build can carry: a tanker certified for flammable liquids carries `"3"` and possibly `"6.1"` for toxics; a flatbed certified for radioactive containers carries `"7"`. The list is set at registration and is not editable, again on the "physical-asset's certification is fixed by build" convention. The cargo-vs-vehicle compatibility check at trip-creation time requires the trip's single `cargo_hazard_class` to be a member of the vehicle's `hazard_classes_compatible` array; a mismatch is the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 with the message naming the offending class against the vehicle's set. The aggregate of the relationships across the four hazard-class-bearing entities — the company's `hazard_classes_authorized` (the regulatory licence's scope), the route's `hazard_classes_supported` (the approved corridor's scope), the driver's `endorsement_classes` (the regulator-attested per-individual scope), and the vehicle's `hazard_classes_compatible` (the construction-certified per-vehicle scope), with the per-trip-shipment's single class drawn from the intersection — is the "class fit" predicate's full expansion, and the permit-issue handler's check is the conjunction over the route-and-company intersection-subset on the permit's array, the trip-creation handler's per-driver hazmat-endorsement check the driver-side filter, and the same handler's per-vehicle compatibility check the vehicle-side filter. The trip's body field `cargo_hazard_class` is the runtime selection of one specific class out of the permit's authorized set, and the body-shape rule "the cargo class must be in the permit's authorized array" is the catch-all `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 cause that the trip-create endpoint's response message disambiguates.

The vehicle's `gps_violation_count` field is not on the Vehicle aggregate — it lives on the Trip aggregate per §3.7 because the GPS-stream is per-trip rather than per-vehicle. A vehicle that crosses multiple permits or multiple trips accumulates its GPS-violation history across the per-trip counts; the audit of a vehicle's lifetime GPS-violation count is a regulator-side aggregation over the Penalty table filtered on a vehicle's id appearing in the trip-context chain. There is no per-vehicle disciplinary-counter analogous to the driver's `current_rolling_points` because the scaffold's penalty model is keyed at the driver-as-principal level on the GPS-violation auto-paths, with the company-subject and the vehicle-subject manual-penalty paths recorded for the audit trail only (the §2.8 taxonomy note that the `COMPANY` and `VEHICLE` subject types' totals do not drive any automatic state transition). The vehicle is the physical anchor of the GPS stream — every GPS report carries a `lat`-`lon`-`speed_kph`-`heading_deg` quartet attributed to the trip's `vehicle_id` implicitly through the trip's foreign-key — but the per-vehicle aggregate of those samples is not exposed by the API as a top-level resource. The Vehicle's static-attribute view through `GET /v1/vehicles/{vehicle_id}` and its inspection-history view through `GET /v1/vehicles/{vehicle_id}/inspections` are the two reads the API surface offers on the vehicle aggregate.

### 3.4. Route Applications, the Polyline, and the Two-Time-Point Hazard-Class Check

A **TransportRoute** is a regulator-approved geographic corridor along which a specific TransportCompany may transport hazardous cargo. The route is keyed by an `id` and an owning `company_id` foreign key; routes are not shared across companies — each company files its own route application even when two carriers want approval on the same physical highway, on the principle that the regulatory approval is per-carrier-per-corridor rather than a global facility. The route's central payload is the **polyline**, an ordered list of latitude-longitude waypoint objects in WGS-84 (each waypoint a JSON object of shape `{"lat": float, "lon": float}` with the latitude in the inclusive interval from -90 to 90 and the longitude in the inclusive interval from -180 to 180), with the array's length bounded inclusively in the integer interval from 2 (a degenerate one-segment polyline from origin to destination) to 200 (the per-route upper bound on the corridor's curvature granularity). The two human-readable string fields `origin_label` and `destination_label` are the 1-to-200-character city-or-facility names the operator supplies for the corridor's endpoints; they are display labels and do not enter any compliance rule, so a non-matching origin-and-coordinate-pair pair is not a validation error.

The route's `approval_status` state machine has four states `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, and `REJECTED`, the same four-state shape as the company's qualification machine and with the same revision-loop cycle structure. The operator files a new route via `POST /v1/companies/{company_id}/routes` (with the parent-must-be-`APPROVED` precondition on the company per the children-creation rule of §3.1, producing `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409 if the company is not yet qualified, and the standard cross-tenant predicate on the `{company_id}` path matching the operator's `X-User-ID` producing `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 on a wrong-tenant filing), with the body carrying the `origin_label`, the `destination_label`, the polyline array, and the non-empty no-duplicate `hazard_classes_supported` array of UN hazard-class strings the operator is asking the regulator to approve for this corridor. The hazard-class array must be a subset of the parent company's `hazard_classes_authorized` at the moment of the submit — a hazard class that the company is not yet licensed for cannot be on a route the company is filing, which the submit-time check enforces with `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 on a non-subset entry (the body-shape-style violation funnelling through the catch-all error id). The response is the 201 row in `approval_status = DRAFT`, with the body-fields echoed and the standard four common-fields quartet, and the three system-managed approval-trail fields `approved_at`, `approved_by`, and `rejection_reason` all null.

The scaffold does not expose a PATCH endpoint for editing a route in `DRAFT`: the operator's path to refine a route's polyline-or-supported-classes-or-labels is to file a fresh route, on the keep-the-endpoint-surface-small convention. The on-disk route row is immutable from the operator side except for the state transitions of the four-state machine, which are: `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` (the operator's submit of the application to the regulator's queue, fired by `POST /v1/routes/{route_id}/submit`), `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` (the regulator's approval, fired by `POST /v1/routes/{route_id}/approve`), `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` (the regulator's rejection, fired by `POST /v1/routes/{route_id}/reject` with a required `rejection_reason` 1-to-500-character body field that is stored on the row's `rejection_reason` field for the operator to read), and the cycle-closing internal `REJECTED → DRAFT` arc that the same submit endpoint walks internally when the source state observed at the handler's entry is `REJECTED` (the operator's reapply after a rejection — the externally observable trace is the row's `approval_status` flipping from `REJECTED` to `SUBMITTED` in one call, the previous `rejection_reason` being cleared to null, and the audit trail in the row's `version`-bump-and-`updated_at` refresh and the regulator-side log capturing the rejection-and-reapply pair).

The submit endpoint's guard's conjunction is: the acting operator's `X-User-ID` equals the route's owning `company_id` (the cross-tenant predicate on a transitive-parent ownership, with the failure being `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403); the route's current `approval_status` is in the two-element set `{DRAFT, REJECTED}` (the standard `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 on a wrong-state submit on a `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` route); the polyline's length is at least 2 (the minimum-waypoint check); and the **two-time-point hazard-class subset check** holds against the parent company's current `hazard_classes_authorized`. The two-time-point name comes from the fact that the same subset check fires on the approve endpoint against the company's authorized list as it stands at the approval moment, which may have narrowed in the gap if the regulator revoked one of the company's hazard classes through an out-of-band attestation refresh. The submit-time evaluation is the early-warning check that gives the operator immediate feedback if the route's body lists a class the company is not authorized for; the approve-time evaluation is the regulatory-correctness check that ensures the now-approved route does not authorize a hazard class the parent company has lost authorization for between submission and approval. Both check evaluations report the same failure-cause error id `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 with the response message disambiguating which class is the offender and at which of the two time points the check fired.

The approve endpoint's guard is the source-state-is-`SUBMITTED` predicate, the two-time-point subset re-check, and the polyline's geometric well-formedness (latitudes and longitudes each in the WGS-84 valid ranges of §2.8, and no `NaN` values, and the implied arc length of the polyline being non-negative across the consecutive-pair segments). The handler on a successful approval writes `approved_at` to the current logical-clock value, writes `approved_by` to the regulator's `X-User-ID`, clears any stale `rejection_reason` from a prior cycle, bumps the `version`, and refreshes `updated_at`. The reject endpoint's guard is the same source-state-is-`SUBMITTED` predicate plus the body's required non-empty `rejection_reason` in the 1-to-500-character range (the missing-or-empty-reason case is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 at the body-shape catch-all). The reject handler writes the body's reason onto the row's `rejection_reason` field, leaves `approved_at` and `approved_by` at their prior values for the audit history of the prior cycle (the canonical-only-source-of-the-current-standing field is `approval_status` itself; the history fields document prior states without being authoritative on the present), bumps `version`, and refreshes `updated_at`. The reapply-after-reject's internal `REJECTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED` step on a fresh submit clears the `rejection_reason` (set to null) and refreshes the `updated_at` to mark the new submission, while leaving the `approved_at` and `approved_by` fields untouched (those reflect the prior approval if there was one in an even-earlier cycle, the empty-history case being the obvious one).

The route read endpoint `GET /v1/routes/{route_id}` returns the row, with the standard cross-tenant predicate on the operator (transitive-parent ownership via the route's `company_id`) and the regulator's cross-tenant exemption and the no-access for the Driver and TestHarness roles. The not-found case is `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND` 404, the wrong-tenant-but-existing case is `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 after the existence check passes, the role-not-allowed case is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403, and the auth-tier failure is `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` 401. The scaffold does not have a route-list endpoint at all — the audit of all routes across the system is the regulator's iteration through the relevant company's permit listings, which transitively name the routes, or the regulator's direct DB read in the out-of-band administrative tooling. The omitted list-endpoint is consistent with the lean-endpoint-surface theme.

The route's `hazard_classes_supported` field is the regulatory-corridor's hazard-class scope. The intersection of the supported set with the parent company's authorized set is the upper bound on what a Permit's `hazard_classes` field — the per-permit-authorization array — can be a subset of. The permit-application step (§3.5) computes this intersection at application-filing time and rejects a permit-application whose `hazard_classes` is not a non-empty subset of it; the permit-issue step recomputes the same intersection-vs-subset relation at issue time (the time-of-check-vs-time-of-use gap admits a narrowing of the company's authorized list in the application-to-issue gap, the same as the route's submit-to-approve narrowing case), with the failure producing `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 on a body-shape-flavoured violation or `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409 if the gap-narrowing collapsed the company's authorized list entirely (the latter dominant failure on a wholesale qualification revocation), or `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_APPROVED` 409 if the route's `approval_status` has flipped away from `APPROVED` between the two time points. The triangle of consistency among the company, the route, and the permit's hazard-class arrays is the regulator's-side guarantee that a permit always names a hazard-class subset of what both the carrier is licensed to handle and the corridor is approved for.

The route's polyline is the geometric input to the **GPS-monitoring off-route check** of §3.8 and §2.8. The off-route classifier on a GPS report's `lat`-`lon` pair computes the perpendicular great-circle distance in meters from the sample point to the nearest polyline segment using the haversine-on-segments formula with the Earth-radius constant `EARTH_RADIUS_M` fixed at 6,371,000.0 meters (the WGS-84 mean Earth radius), and a distance strictly greater than the corridor-radius constant `OFF_ROUTE_RADIUS_M` is the off-route violation. The on-the-boundary case (the computed distance exactly equals the radius) is not a violation by the strict-greater-than convention. The distance is computed at the gps-report-ingest moment and stored on the new GPSReport row's `off_route_distance_m` field for the audit trail (so the regulator's later analysis of an off-route violation does not need to re-evaluate the haversine formula against the polyline; the historical distance is on the report row). The polyline's segments are the consecutive-pair-of-waypoint great-circle arcs, and the standard cross-track-distance formula on the WGS-84 sphere is the geometric mechanism; tests pin the input lat/lon and the polyline waypoints so the distance is a known literal value, with a millimeter-level tolerance of 1e-3 meters absorbing the haversine intermediate arithmetic's floating-point variability. The scaffold's notes on the polyline geometry are that the polyline is an open curve (the first and last waypoints are the corridor's endpoints, not a closed loop), and the distance-from-a-point-to-the-polyline is the minimum across the per-segment cross-track distances clamped to the along-track range so the corridor's endpoints are treated as terminal points rather than as part of an infinite line.

The corridor's hazard-class consistency is the third leg in the per-trip cargo-vs-everything-else check at trip-creation: the trip's body's single `cargo_hazard_class` must be a member of the permit's `hazard_classes` (the permit-level authorization the regulator already vetted at issue time against the company-route intersection), the trip's assigned driver's `endorsement_classes` (the per-driver attested set), and the trip's assigned vehicle's `hazard_classes_compatible` (the per-vehicle construction-certified set). The bottom-line "is this specific load on this specific driver-vehicle pair under this specific permit and this specific route compatible?" reduces to a membership-and-intersection chain across five hazard-class arrays — the four entity-level scope arrays of company, route, driver, and vehicle, plus the permit's authorized array which is itself the subset of the company-route intersection. The trip's single class is the runtime selection that must lie in the intersection of the driver's and vehicle's sets (which themselves lie in their respective parent scopes), and a violation at any link in the chain is the rejection — generally the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 with the message naming which scope's set the cargo class is missing from, or the dedicated `ERR_DRIVER_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID` 409 if the failing leg is the driver-endorsement-vs-the-cargo-class because the driver's standing as a whole is the wrong-value failure (which the unified driver-endorsement error covers from both the "the driver's endorsement is not in the green-light state" cause and the more-specific "the driver's endorsed class set doesn't include this cargo's class" sub-cause), or the `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409 if the failing piece on the vehicle leg is the inspection-currency rather than the construction-compatibility (which folds into `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 on the body-shape side).

The route's lifecycle's "first-time approval" path is the linear sequence `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, where the operator files the application, the regulator reviews and approves, and the route is now nameable as the `route_id` of a permit application. The cycle's "rejection-and-reapply" path is the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REJECTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED` sequence (the internal `REJECTED → DRAFT` arc inside the second submit's handler), which closes the back-edge cycle that makes the route's state machine non-linear in the same sense as the company's qualification machine. The two non-linear-cycle machines on the company and the route, plus the cycle on the driver's endorsement (the `VALID → INVALID → VALID` round-trip discussed in §3.2), the cycle on the permit's posture (the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` round-trip discussed in §3.5), and the fork-without-cycle on the trip (the `IN_TRANSIT → {COMPLETED, ABORTED}` two-branch terminal-split discussed in §3.7) make the overall state-machine landscape non-linear on five aggregates, comfortably exceeding the scaffold's complexity-profile minimum of two non-linear-shape machines. The Penalty's `void_status` two-state `{ACTIVE, UPHELD}` machine is the only strictly linear machine in the system, and it carries the UPHELD-terminal flag that excludes upheld penalties from the rolling-points sum of §2.11's points-axis conservation.

A subtlety on the regulator's-approval-after-prior-rejection case: the audit history of the row carries the `rejection_reason` from the most-recent rejection and the `approved_at` from the most-recent approval (which may be from an earlier cycle if the row's history is `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED → some-out-of-band-revocation → SUBMITTED → REJECTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, though the scaffold's actual edges do not include an `APPROVED → SUBMITTED` arc — once approved, a route stays approved until an out-of-band administrative action would unset it, which is not in the API surface, so in practice the `APPROVED` state is effectively terminal in the forward direction). The scaffold's prose on the route's state machine notes the `APPROVED` state has no outgoing transition in the modeled API surface; the regulator's revocation-of-an-approved-route is an out-of-band action whose effect on the on-disk row is conceptually a flip back to a non-`APPROVED` state with the audit log capturing the cause, but the scaffold does not expose the corresponding API endpoint. The downstream effect on permits naming the route — which is what the verify-compliance's route-leg of the permit-validity AND-chain reads — is the same as the company-revoke's deferred cascade in §3.1, with the next-verify-compliance observing the live route-leg as false against the snapshot's value of true and firing the permit's `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. The error id on the issue-time route-leg failure is the dedicated `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_APPROVED` 409 at the business-referential priority 525, distinct from the company-leg's `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409 at 526.

### 3.5. The Permit Aggregate — Application, Issuance, Verification, and the Snapshot Posture

The **Permit** is the central aggregate of the system. A permit is a regulator-issued authorization, scoped to one carrier and one corridor, that lists the specific drivers and vehicles the carrier is authorized to dispatch on hazmat transit runs within a wall-clock validity window, naming the hazard-class subset that the carrier may carry over this corridor with this driver-and-vehicle roster. The Permit's `lifecycle_status` is the five-value state machine on the set `{DRAFT, ACTIVE, SUSPENDED, EXPIRED, VOIDED}`, with `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` being terminal-absorbing states (no transition out of either), `DRAFT` being the initial state from the operator's application, `ACTIVE` being the post-issue licensed state, and `SUSPENDED` being the cyclically-reachable intermediate state representing a temporary loss of one of the five validity legs while the wall-clock has not yet crossed `valid_until`.

The operator files a permit application via `POST /v1/permits`, with the body carrying the references and parameters that will pin the permit's binding: the `route_id` foreign key (which must resolve to a `TransportRoute` whose `company_id` equals the operator's `X-User-ID` and whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED` at the moment of the call); the `authorized_driver_ids` array of one-to-fifty driver-ids (each id must resolve to a `Driver` whose `company_id` equals the operator's `X-User-ID` and whose `hazmat_endorsement_status` is `VALID` and whose `endorsement_valid_until` is greater than or equal to the would-be permit's `valid_from + PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` — that is, the endorsement must cover the full term of the would-be permit; a violation is the dedicated safety-tier error `ERR_DRIVER_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID` 409 at priority 510); the `authorized_vehicle_ids` array of one-to-`MAX_VEHICLES_PER_PERMIT` vehicle-ids (each vehicle's `company_id` equals the operator's, each vehicle's `latest_inspection_outcome` is `PASSED`, and each vehicle's `latest_inspection_valid_until` is at or after the would-be `valid_until`; a length violation is the dedicated `ERR_PERMIT_VEHICLE_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` 422 at priority 523, and the per-vehicle inspection-coverage failure is `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409 at priority 511); the non-empty no-duplicate `hazard_classes` array (each entry a UN hazard-class string from §2.8, and the array must be a non-empty subset of the intersection of the parent company's `hazard_classes_authorized` and the named route's `hazard_classes_supported` at the moment of the call; a violation of the subset-of-intersection rule is the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422); and the `valid_from` ISO 8601 UTC datetime which must be at or after the current logical-clock value (a back-dated `valid_from` is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422).

The application-step's response is the 201 row in `lifecycle_status = DRAFT` with the body-fields echoed, the standard four common-fields quartet at `version = 1`, the post-issue fields (`valid_until`, `issued_at`, `issued_by`, `issue_fee_cents`, `last_verified_at`, the five snapshot fields, and the `renewal_history` array initially empty, the `voided_at` and `void_refunded_cents` both null) all null or empty, and an additional transient field `application_preview` carrying the five validity-leg booleans computed at application time against the current grounding-entity state (the `live_company_qualified`, the map `live_driver_endorsements`, the map `live_vehicle_inspections`, the boolean `live_route_approved`, and the boolean `live_within_window`, plus the AND-conjunction `live_overall`). The preview is a non-stored response-only convenience field — it gives the operator immediate visibility into "do all the grounding entities currently report green?", whereas the on-disk snapshot fields stay null until the regulator's issue step writes them. The preview's five-leg booleans use the same calculation as the live recompute on the verify-compliance endpoint, namely `permit_validity_check` in its live-mode variant of §2.11, computed at the application moment.

The cross-entity precondition fan-out on the application is the existence-and-ownership check on the route, the driver list, and the vehicle list (each id resolves to a row in its entity's table and the resolved row's `company_id` equals the operator's tenant, on the convention from §2.6 that a cross-aggregate body id pointing to another tenant's row is reported as the existence-tier not-found rather than as the cross-tenant 403, because the other tenant's rows are outside the operator's universe), the duplicate-id check within each id list (a duplicated driver-id or vehicle-id in the array is the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422), the cardinality bounds (the vehicle-list's `[1, MAX_VEHICLES_PER_PERMIT]` and the driver-list's `[1, 50]` inclusive intervals), the hazard-class-subset-of-intersection, the company's own qualification status being `APPROVED` (the `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409 at priority 526), the route's `approval_status` being `APPROVED` (the `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_APPROVED` 409 at priority 525), the per-driver endorsement-and-coverage check (the `ERR_DRIVER_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID`), the per-vehicle inspection-and-coverage check (the `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED`), and the `valid_from` not-in-the-past check (the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422). The validation order follows the priority-tier convention of §2.7: authentication first, then the RBAC role-allow at priority 200 (the application endpoint is `CompanyOperator`-exclusive, so a non-operator role gets the generic `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403 at this tier), then the existence walk on the URL path (there is no path id on this endpoint — `POST /v1/permits` is a flat creation), then the body-foreign-key existence checks on the route and the driver-list and the vehicle-list in their body-declaration order with the leftmost-unresolvable winning, then the cross-tenant ownership predicate on the bootstrap-side operator's own company (the operator's `X-User-ID` must be the `id` of an existing TransportCompany whose qualification is `APPROVED`), then the state-tier check that the cross-aggregate parent entities are in their appropriate states (the route's approval, the company's qualification), then the safety-tier checks on the per-driver-endorsement and per-vehicle-inspection arrays, then the cardinality and hazard-class-subset business constraints at priorities 523 and 524, then the validation catch-all at priority 590 for the assorted body-shape issues (the `valid_from`-in-the-past, the duplicate id, the cargo-class outside the taxonomy on a related body-shape miss). The first failing rule's error id is the response, by the rule-5 precedence convention.

The `DRAFT → ACTIVE` transition is the **regulator's issue** action, fired by `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/issue` with the actor role `RegulatorOfficer`. The body is optional and carries just the `version` field for OCC and an optional `issue_note` 0-to-500-character note for the audit trail. The guard's conjunction is the source-state-is-`DRAFT` predicate at the state tier (the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 on a wrong-source — note that an already-issued permit's retry-issue attempt sees the state as `ACTIVE` and gets this generic error rather than a more specific "this permit is already issued" id, which is the keep-the-catalogue-compact convention), the **time-of-check-vs-time-of-use recheck** of every cross-entity precondition that was checked at application time (the company qualification, every driver's endorsement-and-coverage with the suspension-points-below-threshold sub-check, every vehicle's inspection-pass-and-validity-coverage, the route's approval-status, the `valid_from`'s not-in-the-past predicate against the current clock, the hazard-class subset of the intersection of the company's and the route's authorized lists at the moment of issue), and the fiscal-tier check that the parent company's `wallet_balance_cents` is at least the issue fee computed by the `annual_renewal_fee` formula of §2.11 with the current vehicle count and `is_late = false` (a fee that exceeds the wallet is the `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_BALANCE` 402 at the fiscal tier's priority 540). Each gap-narrowing in the cross-entity preconditions (a driver whose endorsement was attested at application time but has since flipped to `INVALID` because of a manual revocation or a wall-clock-expiry or a points-threshold-crossing in the application-to-issue interval; a vehicle whose latest inspection was passing at application time but has since been superseded by a fresh `FAILED` inspection; a route whose `approval_status` was `APPROVED` at application time but has since been administratively revoked; a company whose `qualification_status` was `APPROVED` at application time but has since been revoked) produces the appropriate dedicated error id on the issue's response. The 5b/c business-referential tier's ordering — driver-endorsement at 510, vehicle-inspection at 511, GPS-monotonicity and GPS-window are not relevant here, the appeal-and-void-windows are not relevant here, the natural-key uniqueness at 520 is not relevant on the issue (the permit's natural identifier is its own UUID, system-wide unique by construction), the hazard-subset at 524 producing the generic `ERR_VALIDATION`, the route-approved at 525 producing `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_APPROVED`, the company-qualified at 526 producing `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` — fires in the integer-priority order, so the first failing leg in this priority sequence is the response's error id.

The issue handler's successful side-effects are the atomic write-and-debit transaction: compute the fee `issue_fee_cents = annual_renewal_fee(now, vehicle_count = len(authorized_vehicle_ids), valid_until = null on the is-not-yet-issued case so is_late is forced false, the deployment-configured fee constants)`; debit the parent company's `wallet_balance_cents` by that amount in the same transaction (the wallet-and-permit-row pair is the two-row atomic update under a row-level lock on each); write the permit's `valid_until = valid_from + PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` field with the standard term-length constant; write the per-leg strict-snapshot fields with the all-passing values (every entry in the `snapshot_driver_endorsements` map keyed by a driver-id and each value `true` because the per-driver check just passed; every entry in the `snapshot_vehicle_inspections` map keyed by a vehicle-id and each value `true`; `snapshot_company_qualified = true`, `snapshot_route_approved = true`, and `snapshot_within_window` equal to the comparison `valid_from <= now() < valid_until`, which is true if and only if the operator's `valid_from` was at or before the current clock and the term has not yet elapsed in the typical case of `valid_from = now() + small_offset`); set `last_verified_at = now()`, `issued_at = now()`, `issued_by = X-User-ID` of the issuing regulator; flip `lifecycle_status` to `ACTIVE`; bump the row's `version` by one and refresh its `updated_at`. The response embeds the updated row plus a sub-object summarizing the wallet-debit's effect (`{wallet_debited_cents: <issue_fee_cents>, new_wallet_balance_cents: <post-debit balance>}`) for the test layer's atomicity assertion. The `renewal_history` array stays empty (the issue is the first fee-paying event and lives on the dedicated `issue_fee_cents` field rather than as a renewal-history entry, on the convention that the issue and the renewals are accounted separately even though the void-refund sums across both).

The **verify-compliance action** at `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/verify-compliance` is the regulator's periodic compliance audit on a previously-issued permit. The actor is the `RegulatorOfficer` (the test harness's sweep endpoint runs the per-permit verify-compliance internally without exposing a separate test-harness role on the per-permit endpoint, on the convention from §2.5 that the test harness's surface is the bulk-sweep rather than per-row attestations). The endpoint accepts the optional OCC `version` field and an optional `note` text. The guard requires the source state to be in the set `{ACTIVE, SUSPENDED}` — a `DRAFT` permit cannot be verified (the issue is the first verification by construction), and the two terminal states `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` cannot be verified because the permit has run its course or been administratively reversed and the verification result would be moot. A wrong source-state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409. The handler reads the wall-clock-vs-`valid_until` comparison first: if the current logical-clock value is at or past the permit's `valid_until`, the transition target is the terminal `EXPIRED` and the same trip-cascade-on-expiry side-effect fires as the sweep's wall-clock-expiry path; the `cause` discriminator on this transition is `WALL_CLOCK_EXPIRY`. Otherwise the wall-clock is still within the term and the handler runs the five-leg live recompute of `permit_validity_check` in its live-mode variant — the function's evaluation order is pinned per §2.11 as company-first, then drivers in ascending-by-id order, then vehicles in ascending-by-id order, then route, then time-window — and the boolean conjunction of the five legs is the **live verdict**.

The verify-compliance handler's transition decision based on the live verdict's comparison against the on-disk snapshot's conjunction (the AND of the five snapshot fields) is the **snapshot-versus-live dual-variant** axis. If the snapshot's conjunction was true at the prior verification (`last_verified_at`) and the live verdict is false at the current moment because some grounding entity has flipped its relevant state in the gap, the transition is `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with the cause discriminator `GROUNDING_INVALIDATION`. The specific leg-or-legs that flipped are observable on the response's `live_per_leg` sub-object — the five-element boolean breakdown — which the test layer asserts against to pin which entity caused the suspension. The snapshot is rewritten by the handler to the live values (the now-false legs are recorded as false in the snapshot map), `last_verified_at` is set to the current clock, the `version` bumps, and `updated_at` refreshes. Symmetrically, if the snapshot was non-all-true and the live verdict is now all-true (the grounding entities have all returned to their green states), the transition is `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with the cause discriminator `GROUNDING_REVALIDATION`, and the snapshot is rewritten with all-true. If the snapshot's conjunction matches the live verdict and neither the wall-clock nor any leg has flipped, the verify-compliance call is a **no-op state transition** that still refreshes the snapshot (idempotent at the value level by the convention "two successive verify-compliance calls with no gap activity produce structurally identical post-states modulo the refreshed `last_verified_at` timestamp"), bumps the version, and returns the response with `pre_status == post_status` and the `transition_fired` discriminator value `NO_CHANGE`. The response shape carries `permit` (the full updated row), `pre_status`, `post_status`, the `transition_fired` discriminator from the set `{ACTIVE_TO_SUSPENDED, SUSPENDED_TO_ACTIVE, ACTIVE_TO_EXPIRED, SUSPENDED_TO_EXPIRED, NO_CHANGE}`, the five-element `live_per_leg` boolean map in the pinned evaluation order with the field names `live_company_qualified`, `live_drivers_ok`, `live_vehicles_ok`, `live_route_approved`, and `live_within_window`, and the parallel `snapshot_per_leg` map showing the pre-write snapshot's values for the test's comparison.

The **read endpoint** `GET /v1/permits/{permit_id}` returns the row's current snapshot fields verbatim (as they stand on disk from the last successful verify-compliance write) and additionally computes a live-mode result on the fly without writing it back. The response field `live_verdict` is the boolean AND of the five live legs at the read moment, `live_per_leg` is the per-leg breakdown, and `snapshot_overall` is the boolean AND of the on-disk snapshot's five fields. The principle is "the read never writes — only the explicit `verify-compliance` POST writes the snapshot — so the read endpoint is a side-effect-free observation that distinguishes the audit-forensic "what was the snapshot at last verification?" answer from the operational "what is the verdict right now?" answer". The two-variant dual axis of §2.11's calculation-1 is the difference between these two booleans across a time-gap during which a grounding entity has changed state without a verify-compliance having run.

The **list endpoint** `GET /v1/permits` is paginated with the standard `limit` and `offset`. The sort order is `created_at` descending then `id` descending (the most-recently-applied permits at the head, contrasted with the global default of `created_at` ascending — the permit list's reverse-chronological convention reflects the regulator-and-operator audit pattern of "show me the most recent applications first"). The optional `company_id` query parameter is the regulator's filter to one tenant's permits (the regulator may pass any tenant; the operator may pass only their own with the explicit-mismatch returning `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 per §2.6, and the absent-parameter case for the operator's request is the implicit-self filter). The optional `lifecycle_status` query parameter is a comma-separated subset of the five-value enum and narrows the response to permits in any of the named states. The response is the standard envelope with the array of Permit rows in the page and the standard pagination meta-fields.

The **automatic time-driven expiry transitions** — `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and `SUSPENDED → EXPIRED` — are fired by the sweep endpoint of §2.5 or by the embedded wall-clock-check inside the verify-compliance handler when `now() >= valid_until`. The boundary is half-open right-exclusive on the validity window `[valid_from, valid_until)`, so at the exact moment `now() == valid_until` the transition fires and the permit is in `EXPIRED` (not the last instant of the prior state — the moment of equality belongs to the new state). The side-effect of the expiry transition is the synchronous fan-out cascade onto every still-open child Trip of the expired permit: every `PLANNED` and every `IN_TRANSIT` trip is transitioned to `ABORTED` with `aborted_at` set to the current logical-clock value and `abort_reason` set to the discriminator value `PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE`. The cascade is the rule-4 cascade-B chain's depth-2 instance (sweep observes the expiry, the per-permit handler flips the permit's status and walks the child trips), originating from the wall-clock-tick trigger rather than the GPS-violation trigger of the cascade-A family. The wallet is not refunded on expiry (the term elapsed, the fee was earned, the standard regulatory convention), so the conservation invariant on the wallet axis for an issued-and-expired permit's lifetime accounts for the issue fee (debited) and the renewal fees if any (also debited) summing to the regulator-collected total, with no refund.

The renewal-and-void path on an ACTIVE permit is the subject of §3.6. The trip-and-GPS path on an ACTIVE permit is the subject of §3.7 and §3.8. The cascade interactions across the aggregates — the cross-aggregate deferred cascades from a grounding-entity's state flip into the permit's suspension-or-revalidation transitions — are the subject of §3.5's verify-compliance discussion above and are summarized in §3.8's narrative on the GPS-violation-cascade family.

The Permit's append-only-history field `renewal_history` is the audit ledger of the permit's renewals: each successful renewal appends one entry of the shape `{renewed_at, fee_cents, late_penalty_applied, prior_valid_until, new_valid_until}` to the array. The initial value of the array is the empty list, set at the application-step's create. The void-issue's refund computation sums the `fee_cents` column of the array and adds the on-row `issue_fee_cents` to obtain the refund total — the canonical-correctness invariant on the conservation axis is that the void's refund cancels exactly the sum of the wallet debits the (issue, renewals) sequence took out of the company's wallet, no matter how many renewals occurred or whether they were on-time or late. The `late_penalty_applied` boolean on each renewal-history entry is the discriminator that the late-grace inner window of the renewal-window fired the late-renewal surcharge for that specific renewal (the field is `true` when the surcharge was applied to the fee and `false` otherwise), so the audit trail records which renewals incurred the surcharge.

The on-row "snapshot per leg" maps `snapshot_driver_endorsements` and `snapshot_vehicle_inspections` are objects keyed by the corresponding entity's id with boolean values. The per-driver boolean encodes the conjunction "the driver's `hazmat_endorsement_status` was `VALID` at `last_verified_at` AND the driver's `endorsement_valid_until` is at or after the permit's `valid_until` AND the driver's `current_rolling_points` was strictly below the threshold at `last_verified_at`". The per-vehicle boolean encodes "the vehicle's `latest_inspection_outcome` was `PASSED` at `last_verified_at` AND the vehicle's `latest_inspection_valid_until` is at or after the permit's `valid_until`". The conjunction across the per-driver map's values and the conjunction across the per-vehicle map's values are the drivers-leg and the vehicles-leg of the validity AND-chain respectively. The two scalar boolean fields `snapshot_company_qualified` and `snapshot_route_approved` are the company-leg and the route-leg, and the scalar `snapshot_within_window` is the time-leg. The five-element conjunction is the value of `permit_validity_check` in the snapshot-mode of §2.11, and the same conjunction in live-mode is the live-verdict at the current logical-clock moment with the per-entity field reads chasing the live foreign-key references.

The Permit's read endpoint also surfaces the disagreement-axis between snapshot and live as the response's separately-computed `live_verdict` and `live_per_leg` fields, computed at the read moment by the same live-mode evaluation that the verify-compliance handler runs (but without writing the result back). The principle is "the disagreement between snapshot and live, at any given clock moment, is the observable signal of a pending state-machine transition the next verify-compliance will fire". The test layer's standard pattern for the snapshot-versus-live dual-variant assertions is to issue an action that flips one grounding entity (e.g., a regulator's revoke on the parent company), then a GET on each affected permit observing the snapshot is unchanged from before the revoke and the live-verdict has flipped to false on the company-leg, then a verify-compliance on each permit observing the transition fires and the snapshot is rewritten to the new values, then a GET observing the snapshot now equals the live-verdict.

The `ACTIVE ↔ SUSPENDED` cycle on the Permit aggregate is the third non-linear-shape state machine the scaffold carries (the company's `DRAFT-SUBMITTED-APPROVED-REVOKED-DRAFT` revocation-revision cycle of §3.1 and the route's `DRAFT-SUBMITTED-REJECTED-DRAFT` rejection-revision cycle of §3.4 being the first two on different entities, and the driver's `VALID-INVALID-VALID` endorsement-revocation-reattest cycle of §3.2 being a fourth, and the trip's `IN_TRANSIT → {COMPLETED, ABORTED}` non-cycle fork-out of §3.7 being a fifth non-linear shape but a fork rather than a cycle). The Permit's cycle is a posture-driven cycle in the sense that no API endpoint fires the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` or `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` transitions directly — both are the side-effect of the verify-compliance handler's internal comparison of the live verdict to the prior snapshot, which is the regulator's compliance-audit action. The wall-clock-expiry transition `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and the corresponding `SUSPENDED → EXPIRED` from a still-not-cleared suspension reaching the term-end are the time-driven absorbing-state transitions fired by the sweep or by the wall-clock check embedded in verify-compliance. The void-issue transition `ACTIVE → VOIDED` is the regulator's clerical-error reversal of §3.6.

The regulator's authority on the permit aggregate is the issue-and-verify-and-void triple (`POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/issue`, the verify-compliance endpoint just described, and the void endpoint of §3.6). The operator's authority is the application-create at `POST /v1/permits` and the renewal endpoint of §3.6 and the trip-creation endpoint `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/trips` of §3.7. The driver and test-harness roles have no permit-touching endpoints in the RBAC matrix.

The cross-aggregate **wallet ledger** is the canonical observable for the rule-9-invariant-A conservation: the parent company's `wallet_balance_cents` is the carrier-side currency anchor; the issue-step debits it by the formula-computed fee; each renewal debits it by the renewal-time fee with the late-surcharge add-on when the call arrives in the inner late-grace window; and the void-issue refunds it by the integer sum of the on-row `issue_fee_cents` and the sum of the `fee_cents` field across the `renewal_history` array, with the on-row `void_refunded_cents` field carrying the refund amount for the audit trail. The implementation invariant is the integer-arithmetic identity that the wallet's net change across an (issue, zero-or-more renewals, void-issue) sequence is exactly zero, and the wallet's net change across an (issue, zero-or-more renewals, natural expiry) sequence is the sum of the wallet's debits across the issue and the renewals (no refund on expiry). The conservation is auditable by reading the company's `wallet_balance_cents` before and after the sequence and observing the deltas match the sum of the recorded `issue_fee_cents` and `renewal_history[].fee_cents` integers; the test layer's assertions for the conservation invariant rely on the integer-arithmetic exactness of the cents-denominated arithmetic, which is why the fee formula's stages are all integer arithmetic with no fractional cents introduced.

### 3.6. The Annual Renewal, the Late-Grace Surcharge, and the Void-Issue Reversal

An ACTIVE permit's term ends naturally at the wall-clock crossing of its `valid_until`, at which the sweep's wall-clock-expiry path transitions the permit to the terminal `EXPIRED` state. Before that crossing, the operator may extend the term through the **renewal action** `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/renew`, fired by the parent company's operator (the cross-tenant predicate at priority 530 requires the operator's `X-User-ID` to equal the permit's `company_id`, with the wrong-tenant case being `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 after the existence check passes), with the renewal-window's two outer edges gating the call.

The renewal-window is the half-open interval `[valid_until - RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS, valid_until)` on the wall-clock axis. A renewal request whose arrival time `now()` is strictly before the lower edge `valid_until - RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS` is the **too-early** case, rejected with `ERR_RENEWAL_TOO_EARLY` 409 at priority 541. A renewal request whose arrival time is at or after the upper edge `valid_until` is the **after-expiry** case, rejected with `ERR_RENEWAL_AFTER_EXPIRY` 409 at priority 542 (the renewal handler observes that the permit has either just entered the terminal `EXPIRED` state or is about to on the immediately-following sweep tick, and returns the specific expiry error rather than the generic state-transition error). The lower edge is closed on the left — a request landing exactly at the moment `now() == valid_until - RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS` is in-window and is the boundary-precision pin for the test layer's "the renewal window opens at the exact moment". The upper edge is open on the right (the standard half-open right-exclusive convention on the validity window from §2.9), so the moment `now() == valid_until` is the just-expired moment and the renewal is out-of-window. The scaffold's intended `RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS` value is 60 days, naming the conventional carrier-sided regulatory window for renewal applications.

Inside the renewal-window the **late-grace inner-window** governs the fee. The inner window is the half-open interval `[valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS, valid_until)` and is a sub-interval of the outer renewal window. A renewal whose arrival time is inside the inner window is the **late renewal** case: the fee formula's `is_late` flag is true, the conditional add-on of `LATE_RENEWAL_PENALTY_CENTS` is applied (subject to the post-add cap of `MAX_RENEWAL_FEE_CENTS`), and the resulting `renewal_history` entry carries the boolean `late_penalty_applied` as `true`. A renewal in the outer-but-not-inner window — that is, the half-open interval `[valid_until - RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS, valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS)` — is the on-time case with `is_late = false`, no late surcharge, and the audit-entry's `late_penalty_applied` is `false`. The boundary at the exact moment `now() == valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` is the first late moment by the closed-left convention on the inner window, a boundary precision point the test layer asserts on at the exact-equal scenario. The fee formula's full evaluation order is the staged sequence described in §2.11 — subtotal of base plus per-vehicle surcharge in cents, conditional late-add, saturating cap at the maximum-fee constant, the rounding rule pinned at "round-half-up at any multiplicative step that introduces a fractional cent, sum integer-cents inputs without rounding, apply the cap after the rounded-and-summed value" even though the integer-cents arithmetic in the current rule set does not actually exercise the rounding step.

The renewal endpoint's request body carries five optional fields. The OCC `version` integer engages the optimistic-concurrency check on the Permit row's `version` (a mismatch is `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` 409 at priority 560). The optional `authorized_vehicle_ids` array, when present, replaces the permit's vehicle list atomically with the new one — the field's atomicity is total-replacement-not-merge (partial-replacement is not supported and the new list is the full new authorized vehicle set), and the new list must satisfy the same cardinality bound `[1, MAX_VEHICLES_PER_PERMIT]` as the issue-time check (a violation is `ERR_PERMIT_VEHICLE_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` 422 at priority 523), with each entry resolving to a Vehicle of the same parent company and the same per-vehicle inspection-coverage check applying against the new would-be `valid_until` of `prior_valid_until + PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` (failure is `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409 at priority 511). The optional `authorized_driver_ids` array, when present, replaces the driver list with the same total-replacement semantics and the same per-driver endorsement-and-coverage check against the new term-end. The optional `hazard_classes` array, when present, replaces the hazard-class authorization with the same subset-of-the-company-and-route-intersection constraint, with a violation being the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422. The optional `renew_note` is a 0-to-500-character free-text audit note.

The handler's guard's conjunction is: the source state is `ACTIVE` (a `SUSPENDED` permit cannot be renewed — the operator must wait for the suspension cause to clear and the verify-compliance reinstate to fire before renewing, and a `DRAFT`-or-`EXPIRED`-or-`VOIDED` permit is also non-renewable, with the corresponding error ids being the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 for the `DRAFT`-and-`SUSPENDED`-and-`VOIDED` cases and the specific `ERR_RENEWAL_AFTER_EXPIRY` 409 for the `EXPIRED` case as the dedicated cause discriminator); the wall-clock-vs-the-two-renewal-window-edges check passing; the body's full set of fields if any are present satisfying their per-field constraints; each cross-entity reference in the optional new driver-or-vehicle-or-hazard-class lists existing and being on the same tenant and being in the appropriate state at the renewal moment; the parent company's qualification still being `APPROVED` (the gap-narrowing case as on issue, with `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_QUALIFIED` 409); the route's `approval_status` still being `APPROVED` (with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_APPROVED` 409, the route-leg's gap-narrowing case); and the fiscal-tier wallet-balance check that the company's `wallet_balance_cents` is at least the formula-computed fee for this renewal (with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_BALANCE` 402 at the fiscal-tier priority 540, the same dedicated 402 status as the issue-time wallet check).

The renewal handler's successful side-effects are the atomic update transaction: extend the permit's `valid_until` by exactly the `PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` constant measured forward from the prior `valid_until` value (not from the current logical-clock value, on the convention that a chain of on-time renewals lands the term-end at exact one-term-length strides from the previous term-end with no clock drift, and a chain of late renewals lands at the same fixed stride from the prior term-end while paying the late surcharge as the only price of the lateness — the spec's "the term clock is anchored to the previous term-end, not the renewal-call moment" rule); recompute and store the new fee on the appended `renewal_history` entry's `fee_cents` field; debit the parent company's wallet by that fee in the same transaction; if the body supplied the optional fresh driver-or-vehicle-or-hazard-class lists, atomically replace the on-row corresponding arrays with the new values; refresh the strict-snapshot fields based on the now-current grounding-entity values, the same write the verify-compliance handler does — the renewal step embeds a verify-compliance pass so the snapshot reflects the post-renewal world (with the new fleet roster and the extended `valid_until` against which the per-driver-endorsement-coverage and the per-vehicle-inspection-coverage sub-predicates are evaluated); the `lifecycle_status` stays at `ACTIVE` (the renewal does not change the state-machine state because the permit was and remains active — only the term's right edge moves and the fee meter ticks); bump the `version` and refresh the `updated_at`. The response carries the updated permit row including the appended `renewal_history` entry and the wallet-debit sub-object `{wallet_debited_cents, new_wallet_balance_cents}` for the test layer's wallet-conservation assertions. The new `renewal_history` entry's shape, as discussed in §3.5, is `{renewed_at: <now()>, fee_cents: <the formula's output>, late_penalty_applied: <the is-late boolean>, prior_valid_until: <the old valid_until>, new_valid_until: <the post-extension valid_until>}`, so the audit trail is structurally explicit about which window the renewal landed in and what the term-extension's start-and-end-points were.

The **void-issue action** is the regulator's compensating-action reversal of a freshly-issued permit, fired by `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/void-issue` and gated to the **24-hour window from the original `issued_at`**. The actor is the `RegulatorOfficer`, and the request body carries the required `reason_note` 1-to-500-character free-text cause (the standard cause-required convention on the regulator's intervention actions) and the optional `version` for OCC. The guard's conjunction is: the source state is `ACTIVE` (a `SUSPENDED` permit cannot be voided in the scaffold's pin, with the regulator's option being to wait for the suspension to clear via verify-compliance and then void within whatever window of `issued_at` remains; the convention is to keep the state machine smaller by not adding a `SUSPENDED → VOIDED` arc, and a `DRAFT`-and-`EXPIRED`-and-`VOIDED` source state is the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409); the duration `now() - issued_at` is less than or equal to the `VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS` constant (the closed-right convention on the duration discussed in §2.9, with the boundary moment at exactly the 24-hour duration still being in-window per the scaffold's intended 24-hour value; a duration strictly larger is `ERR_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409 at priority 514); and the body's `reason_note` field's presence and length-bound conformance.

The void-issue handler's successful side-effects are the compensating refund and the trip-cascade. The refund amount is computed as the literal integer sum `void_refunded_cents = issue_fee_cents + the sum of fee_cents across the renewal_history array`, which is — by the conservation identity of §2.11 — the exact integer the wallet has been debited across the issue and the renewals so far. The wallet is credited by this amount atomically inside the same transaction that flips the permit's `lifecycle_status` to the terminal `VOIDED` and writes the on-row `voided_at = now()` and `void_refunded_cents` fields and the on-row audit `void_reason_note` field (the body's `reason_note` value stored for the audit trail — the scaffold does not name an explicit on-row field for this in the entity table but the audit log captures the cause, and the response surfaces it). The `issued_at` field is **left intact** on the on-disk row for the audit trail of the original issuance moment — the void does not erase the issuance history, it just records the reversal. The on-row `renewal_history` array is likewise left intact, because the array is the record of the wallet-debit history that the refund sum is computed against; clearing the array would lose the audit trail of which renewals existed and were subsequently void-refunded.

The trip-cascade on the void's `ACTIVE → VOIDED` transition is the synchronous fan-out abort over every `PLANNED`-or-`IN_TRANSIT` child trip of the now-voided permit. Each affected trip's `trip_status` flips to the terminal `ABORTED`, its `aborted_at` is set to the current logical-clock value, and the `abort_reason` discriminator is set to the value `PERMIT_SUSPENDED_CASCADE` — the scaffold's enum-economy convention reusing the suspended-cascade enum value for the void-cascade case to avoid introducing a fourth discriminator value `PERMIT_VOIDED_CASCADE`, with the audit log distinguishing the two causes out-of-band by the parent permit's transition history. The four canonical `abort_reason` enum values are therefore `OPERATOR_ABORT` (for the operator's explicit abort), `PERMIT_SUSPENDED_CASCADE` (the on-disk discriminator for the void-cascade case despite the misleading name, since the suspension itself does not auto-abort trips per the §3.7 narrative), `PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE` (for the sweep-driven wall-clock-expiry cascade), and the reserved-but-unused `GPS_VIOLATION_LIMIT` declared in the enum for forward compatibility. The void's response embeds the updated permit row plus the wallet-credit sub-object `{wallet_credited_cents, new_wallet_balance_cents}` and a `cascade_summary` sub-object listing the ids of the cascade-aborted trips and their post-cascade `abort_reason` and `aborted_at` values for the test layer's atomicity assertions.

The void's interaction with the disciplinary-points side is **none** — the penalties earned by drivers during the now-voided permit's term remain on the disciplinary ledger with their `void_status` unchanged at `ACTIVE`, and the rolling-points sum of §2.11 is unaffected by the wallet-side reversal. The two conservation invariants — currency on the wallet axis, disciplinary-points on the driver-ledger axis — are kept independent on the principle that the regulatory ledger of an individual driver's offenses is not erased when the company's permit issuance is administratively undone. The regulator's path to also void specific penalties earned during the voided-permit's term is the separate appeal-uphold action of §3.8, and the audit trail is the conjunction of the permit's void-record and any subsequent penalty-uphold records keyed on the affected drivers.

The wallet's atomicity guarantee on both the debit-side (issue, renew) and the credit-side (void-issue) is the single-transaction "lock the company row's wallet field and the permit row's status field together, write both, release the locks at commit". An OCC race between two concurrent renewal requests on the same permit, two concurrent renewal-and-void requests, or two concurrent void-and-the-natural-expiry-sweep paths is resolved by the `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` 409 on the permit row's `version` for the renewal-vs-renewal and renewal-vs-void cases (the concurrency-rule entries of §2.10 name this race explicitly), and by the wall-clock-monotonicity guarantee that the sweep's wall-clock check runs after the void's commit in the unusual case where both fire near the 24-hour-void-window-and-the-permit's-natural-expiry-end coincide (which can only happen if `valid_until - issued_at <= VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS`, an unusual short-term-permit configuration the scaffold does not pin against but which the test harness could exercise by setting a very short `PERMIT_DEFAULT_VALIDITY_DAYS` in test mode). The principle is "the row's `version` field is the canonical serializer for the two non-idempotent fee-touching actions on the same permit, the OCC contract handling the race".

The **renewal-window's outer-and-inner-edges** are the four boundary-precision points the test layer asserts on. The four are: the outer-left at exactly `now() == valid_until - RENEWAL_OPEN_WINDOW_DAYS` (the just-opened renewal-window moment, in-window per the closed-left convention and the rejection `ERR_RENEWAL_TOO_EARLY` 409 firing one instant earlier); the inner-left at exactly `now() == valid_until - LATE_RENEWAL_GRACE_DAYS` (the just-late moment, where the surcharge first applies per the closed-left convention on the late-grace inner-window, the in-the-non-late-zone being one instant earlier and carrying no surcharge); the outer-right at exactly `now() == valid_until` (the permit-expiry moment by the half-open right-exclusive convention of §2.9, where the renewal is rejected with `ERR_RENEWAL_AFTER_EXPIRY` 409 and the permit has flipped to `EXPIRED` by the sweep-or-embedded-wall-clock-check); and the symmetric **void-window's** closed-right edge at exactly `now() - issued_at == VOID_ISSUE_WINDOW_HOURS` (the last in-window moment for the void per the closed-right convention on the duration, the one instant past being the rejection `ERR_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409). The four edges are independent — they share the wall-clock axis but anchor on different reference timestamps (the renewal-window's edges anchor on `valid_until`, the void-window's edge anchors on `issued_at` — and the appeal-window's edge of §3.8 anchors on the penalty's `observed_at`, the endorsement's expiry-edge anchors on `endorsement_valid_until`, the inspection's expiry on the inspection's `valid_until`, and the rolling-window's trailing edge on the calculation's `asof` argument). The unifying convention on all of them is the boundary-precision-pin documented in §2.9: the boundary moment is whichever side the "the boundary belongs to" convention assigns it (the after-state for the half-open right-exclusive windows, the prior-state for the closed-right durations).

The void-issue endpoint's RBAC cell is `RegulatorOfficer`-only — the operator cannot self-void its own permit, on the principle that the void is the regulator's clerical-error reversal authority specifically, while the operator's recourse against an undesired permit is to let the term elapse to natural expiry or to file a fresh application after the term ends. The regulator's actual decision-procedure for issuing a void — what counts as a "clerical error" warranting a within-24-hour reversal — is the regulatory-policy convention not modeled in the API surface; from the system's perspective the void endpoint is the bare mechanism, with the audit log's stored `reason_note` capturing the regulator's stated cause in free text. The test harness exercises the boundary cases on the 24-hour window's closed-right edge with a clock-advance to exactly 24 hours minus an epsilon (in-window — the void succeeds) and a clock-advance to exactly 24 hours plus an epsilon (out-of-window — the void is rejected with `ERR_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED`), and the closed-right boundary's exact-equality case at exactly 24 hours which is in-window per the convention. The conservation invariant's audit is the post-void wallet balance comparison against the pre-issue wallet balance: the net change across a fresh-issue-and-immediate-void pair is zero, with no intervening renewals contributing because there have been none in the 24-hour window in the typical test scenario.

### 3.7. Trips, the Vehicle-Calendar, and the Depart-Arrive-Abort Triplet

A **Trip** is a single transit run authorized under a specific permit, binding one of the permit's `authorized_driver_ids` to one of its `authorized_vehicle_ids` for a scheduled time window. The trip's lifecycle is the four-state machine on `{PLANNED, IN_TRANSIT, COMPLETED, ABORTED}`, with `PLANNED` as the initial state from the scheduling-create endpoint, `IN_TRANSIT` as the in-flight state between depart and arrive, `COMPLETED` as the terminal state of a successful arrival, and `ABORTED` as the terminal state of an operator-initiated cancellation or a cascade-from-the-parent-permit's-transition. The state machine's non-linear shape is the **fork from IN_TRANSIT** to either `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` (the two-out-degree branching at the non-initial state, the fifth non-linear-shape machine across the scaffold's six state machines, after the cycle-bearing four on the company, the route, the driver, and the permit), and the side-effect lists on the two branches differ (the arrive sets `actual_arrival_at` and the abort sets `aborted_at` and the `abort_reason` discriminator).

The trip-scheduling endpoint is `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/trips`, fired by the parent company's operator with the cross-tenant predicate on the permit's `company_id` matching the operator's `X-User-ID`. The body carries the **per-trip binding**: the `driver_id` foreign key (which must be a member of the parent permit's `authorized_driver_ids` array and the named Driver must have `hazmat_endorsement_status = VALID` at the moment of the trip-create call — the live-not-snapshot check on the endorsement, distinguishable from the permit-application-time snapshot because a driver whose endorsement was valid when the permit was issued but has since flipped to `INVALID` cannot be the driver of a fresh trip even if the permit's `authorized_driver_ids` array still contains the driver-id, with the failure being `ERR_DRIVER_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID` 409 at the safety-tier priority 510); the `vehicle_id` foreign key (a member of the parent permit's `authorized_vehicle_ids` array, with the live-not-snapshot inspection-coverage check that the vehicle's `latest_inspection_outcome` is `PASSED` and the `latest_inspection_valid_until` is at or after the trip's scheduled arrival, the failure being `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409 at priority 511); the `cargo_description` 1-to-500-character free-text description of the hazardous cargo; the single `cargo_hazard_class` UN-hazard-class string drawn from the permit's `hazard_classes` array (a class outside the permit's authorized set is the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422); the `cargo_weight_kg` integer in the inclusive range from zero to the assigned vehicle's `max_payload_kg` (a negative weight or an over-capacity weight is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422); the `scheduled_departure_at` ISO 8601 UTC datetime strictly greater than the current logical-clock value (a back-dated or now-equal departure is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 — the strict-greater-than convention on the planned time); and the `scheduled_arrival_at` ISO 8601 UTC datetime strictly greater than the departure and at or before the parent permit's `valid_until` (the closed-on-the-arrival-edge convention against the permit's right edge from §2.9, contrasting with the half-open-window convention the wall-clock-vs-`valid_until` comparison uses elsewhere; the trip's `scheduled_arrival_at == permit.valid_until` exactly is admissible at scheduling time as a deliberate scaffold-internal asymmetry).

The trip-create handler's full guard's conjunction is the priority-tier ordered chain of §2.7: authentication and authorization first, then the existence check on the URL's `{permit_id}` (the failure being `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND` 404), then the cross-tenant predicate on the parent permit's company matching the operator (`ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 after the existence check passes), then the **parent-permit-active gate** at the state tier with priority 400 (the parent permit's `lifecycle_status` must be `ACTIVE`; the SUSPENDED case is `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409 because the suspension implies one of the validity legs is currently false and trips cannot start under a non-fully-valid permit, the EXPIRED case is the same error id because the term has elapsed, the VOIDED case is the same error id because the permit has been administratively reversed, and the DRAFT case is structurally impossible by the existence-check's ordering since a DRAFT permit has not yet been issued and trip-creation on a non-issued permit is the standard generic state error). The state-tier check on the parent's state at priority 400 fires **before** the business-referential tier's checks on the body's referenced grounding entities at priorities 510 and 511 and 525 and 526, which is the **rule-5 priority-conflict-A** pin: a trip-creation request whose parent permit is SUSPENDED and whose body-named route or driver or vehicle simultaneously has a non-green state returns `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` for the parent-state cause, not the more-specific grounding-entity error.

After the parent-state check the handler walks the body's existence and validity checks: the driver-id and the vehicle-id existence at the existence-tier (the failures `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` 404 and `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND` 404 in the body-declaration order), the membership-in-the-permit-array body-shape rule (the driver-id-is-in-`authorized_driver_ids` and the vehicle-id-is-in-`authorized_vehicle_ids` are body-shape catch-all `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 with the response message naming which array the value is missing from), the live-endorsement check on the driver at priority 510 (the `ERR_DRIVER_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID` 409, which subsumes the cause "the driver-id was in the permit's array but the driver's current `hazmat_endorsement_status` is not `VALID`" because the permit's `authorized_driver_ids` is a snapshot from the issue moment and the driver's standing may have changed since), the live-inspection check on the vehicle at priority 511 (the `ERR_VEHICLE_INSPECTION_LAPSED` 409 for the symmetric "the vehicle-id was authorized but its latest inspection is no longer `PASSED` or no longer covers the trip's scheduled arrival" case), the **vehicle-double-booking interval-arithmetic check** at the business-temporal-constraint tier with priority 521 (the new trip's half-open scheduled interval `[scheduled_departure_at, scheduled_arrival_at)` must have no non-empty intersection with the same-half-open interval of any other Trip row sharing the same `vehicle_id`, across all permits — the cross-permit scope is the principle that a vehicle is a physical asset that cannot be in two places at once; the failure is `ERR_VEHICLE_DOUBLE_BOOKED` 409, with the half-open convention meaning the exact-touch case where one trip's `scheduled_arrival_at` equals another's `scheduled_departure_at` is permitted as "one trip ends, the next starts immediately on the same vehicle"), the trip-vs-permit window-membership check at priority 522 (the new trip's left edge at or after `permit.valid_from` and the right edge at or before `permit.valid_until`, with the failure being `ERR_OUTSIDE_VALIDITY_WINDOW` 409 — note the closed-right convention on the arrival-edge against the permit's right edge here, in contrast with the half-open right-exclusive convention on the wall-clock-vs-`valid_until` comparison elsewhere; the asymmetry is the scaffold's deliberate "scheduled times may touch the permit's expiry edge but the live wall-clock comparison is strict" pin), and the cross-aggregate state-checks on the parent permit's company-and-route legs which the issue-step pinned and the trip-creation does not re-check at the grounding-entity-state level (the permit's `lifecycle_status == ACTIVE` covers the bundled "all five legs were green at the most recent verify-compliance" guarantee).

The rule-5 conflict on the priority-521-versus-522 pair is the bonus **conflict-E** of §2.7: a new trip whose scheduled window both collides with another trip on the same vehicle and falls outside the permit's term-window returns the double-booking error (the lower priority number 521 winning over 522). The rule-5 priority-conflict-A on the parent-permit-state-versus-the-grounding-entities, as noted above, is the Tier-4-versus-Tier-5b pair, with the Tier-4 state error winning. The handler on a successful guard-pass writes the new Trip row in `trip_status = PLANNED` with the standard four common-fields quartet at `version = 1`, the body's binding fields echoed, the system-set fields `actual_departure_at` and `actual_arrival_at` and `aborted_at` and `abort_reason` all null, the derived field `latest_gps_at` null, and the derived field `gps_violation_count` at zero. The response is the 201 row.

The **depart action** `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/depart` fires the `PLANNED → IN_TRANSIT` transition. The actor is the parent company's operator — the depart is the company's fleet-dispatch system's act of starting the trip, not a driver-self-action on the convention that the driver's individual role-credentials are read-only and the operator's fleet back-end is the practical-uploading-actor for the trip's lifecycle. The body is optional, carrying just the OCC `version` integer and an optional `depart_note` 0-to-500-character note. The guard requires `trip_status == PLANNED` (the standard `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 on a wrong source) and the **parent-permit-still-ACTIVE** predicate (the dedicated `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409, the same cross-aggregate state-error id as the trip-create's parent-state check, fired here because the permit may have transitioned to `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` or `VOIDED` between the trip's `PLANNED`-creation and its attempted depart). The successful side-effect is `actual_departure_at = the current logical-clock value`, `trip_status = IN_TRANSIT`, the `version` bumped, and the `updated_at` refreshed. The scheduled-vs-actual-departure slack is not policed by the API — the operator may dispatch a trip ahead of or behind its `scheduled_departure_at` and the on-disk `actual_departure_at` is whatever the clock reads at the depart endpoint's call moment; the regulator's audit of late departures is an out-of-band activity on the audit log.

The **arrive action** `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/arrive` fires the `IN_TRANSIT → COMPLETED` transition. The body is optional, carrying the OCC `version` and an optional `arrive_note` 0-to-500-character note and an optional `delivery_attestation` 0-to-1000-character free-text confirmation. The guard's `trip_status == IN_TRANSIT` is the standard same-row state check (the wrong-source case being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 — the typical cause is an arrive-without-depart attempt on a still-`PLANNED` trip, or an idempotent retry on an already-`COMPLETED`-or-`ABORTED` trip). The cross-aggregate parent-state predicate on the arrive is **more permissive** than on the depart: a `SUSPENDED` parent permit's in-flight trip is allowed to arrive (the suspension gates the GPS-stream writes on the in-flight trip per §3.8 but allows the operational wind-down attestation through, on the regulatory-practice principle that a transient mid-trip suspension caused by a fixable cause should not prevent the operator from confirming the cargo's arrival once it is delivered). The `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` parent-state cases never reach the arrive's same-row state check because those two terminal transitions on the parent permit cascade-abort the child trip to `ABORTED` synchronously per §3.5 and §3.6, so an arrive call on a now-cascaded-aborted trip sees the trip's own state as `ABORTED` and fails the same-row state check with the generic state-transition error. The arrive handler's successful side-effects are `actual_arrival_at = the current logical-clock value`, `trip_status = COMPLETED`, the `version` bumped, the `updated_at` refreshed, and as a consequence the trip's in-flight-window's right edge is now-finite for the gps-ingest's in-window check of §3.8: any subsequent GPS-report on the now-terminal trip is rejected with `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_WINDOW` 409 because the trip's interval `[actual_departure_at, actual_arrival_at]` is closed and the report's `reported_at` falling outside the closed interval is out-of-window.

The **operator-abort action** `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/abort` fires the `PLANNED → ABORTED` or the `IN_TRANSIT → ABORTED` transitions in the operator-initiated branch of the fork — the abort works on both pre-departure and in-flight trips, on the operator's discretion that the cargo was not ready or the trip is being cancelled mid-flight for an operational reason. The body carries the required `reason_note` 1-to-500-character free-text cause (a missing or empty cause is the body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422) and the optional OCC `version`. The guard requires the source state to be in the set `{PLANNED, IN_TRANSIT}` — the terminal `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` cases reject with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 as the standard idempotent-retry-on-terminal failure. The successful side-effect is `aborted_at = the current logical-clock value`, `abort_reason = OPERATOR_ABORT` (the enum discriminator value for the operator-initiated branch, distinct from the two cascade-branch discriminators `PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE` and `PERMIT_SUSPENDED_CASCADE` that the parent-permit-transition's side-effect fan-out sets), `trip_status = ABORTED`, `actual_departure_at` and `actual_arrival_at` left as they were (so a never-departed trip carries null on both, an in-flight aborted trip carries the prior `actual_departure_at` value and null on `actual_arrival_at`, and a completed trip cannot be aborted because the state-machine has no `COMPLETED → ABORTED` edge), the `version` bumped, and the `updated_at` refreshed. The GPS-stream's right-edge after the abort is `min(aborted_at, the current logical-clock value)`, and subsequent gps-reports on the now-terminal-aborted trip fail the in-window check with `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_WINDOW`.

The **cascade-from-permit-transition aborts** are the two server-internal arcs into the trip's terminal `ABORTED` state. The first is the `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` or `SUSPENDED → EXPIRED` transition on the parent permit's wall-clock-expiry (fired by the sweep or by the embedded check in the verify-compliance handler of §3.5), whose side-effect-list walks every still-open (i.e., `PLANNED` or `IN_TRANSIT`) child trip of the now-expired permit and atomically transitions each to `ABORTED` with `aborted_at = the current logical-clock value` and `abort_reason = PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE`. The cascade is synchronous inside the per-permit transaction the sweep's per-permit handler runs, so a single sweep tick that observes N expiring permits each having M open trips fires N × M trip-aborts in a sequence of per-permit transactions. The second cascade-arc is the `ACTIVE → VOIDED` transition on the void-issue action of §3.6, whose side-effect is the same fan-out abort with the discriminator value `PERMIT_SUSPENDED_CASCADE` (the on-disk enum-economy reuse for the void's cause, with the audit log distinguishing void-from-suspension cause out-of-band). The third logical cascade — the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` transition's effect on child trips — does **not** abort the trips: the suspension is the temporary-failure-of-a-grounding-leg state, intended to be reversible by the next verify-compliance observing the legs all green again, and an in-flight trip whose parent permit transiently suspends is not torn down; the GPS-stream into the trip is the gated quantity rather than the trip's existence (see §3.8 for the `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409 gate on the gps-ingest endpoint during the parent's suspension). The depart-action's parent-must-be-ACTIVE precondition means a still-`PLANNED` trip whose parent is suspended cannot depart until the suspension clears via verify-compliance, but the trip itself is not cascade-aborted on the suspension. The arrive-action's permissive parent-state predicate from above allows an in-flight trip that was already in `IN_TRANSIT` when the suspension fired to wind down to `COMPLETED` once it physically arrives, with the GPS-stream having been gated off during the suspended interval. The asymmetry between the suspension (no trip-cascade) and the expiry-or-void (full trip-cascade-to-ABORTED) is the scaffold's "transient versus terminal" treatment of the parent-state transitions.

The **vehicle-double-booking** check on the trip-creation deserves a more careful narrative because it is the cross-permit interval-arithmetic rule that pins the rule-8 overlap-A interval-semantics in the scaffold. The check fires on the body's vehicle-id-and-scheduled-times tuple. The handler reads every existing Trip row in the system whose `vehicle_id` equals the new trip's `vehicle_id` (across all permits — the cross-permit scope is the physical-asset principle that a single vehicle cannot be on two trips at the same wall-clock instant regardless of which carriers' permits authorize them, and the carrier-side ownership of vehicles is per-vehicle-one-company so the same-vehicle-different-permit case is the same-company's two permits each naming the vehicle, which the regulator allows because two non-overlapping trips on the same vehicle is the basic fleet-utilization pattern). For each existing trip in the same-vehicle set, the handler computes the half-open intersection of the existing trip's `[scheduled_departure_at, scheduled_arrival_at)` and the new trip's same-half-open interval; an intersection that is non-empty is the double-booking conflict and produces `ERR_VEHICLE_DOUBLE_BOOKED` 409. The half-open convention means two trips whose boundary timestamps touch exactly — one's `scheduled_arrival_at` is exactly the other's `scheduled_departure_at` — do not intersect under the standard interval-arithmetic on half-open intervals, so the back-to-back-on-the-same-vehicle dispatch pattern is the admissible boundary case. The check does **not** consider the trip's `actual_departure_at` or `actual_arrival_at` — only the scheduled times — because the scheduling collision is the per-application-time rule and the wall-clock-actuals' slack is not policed at scheduling time. A trip that departs late and runs into the next scheduled trip's scheduled window is an operator-side scheduling problem the API does not flag, on the principle that the scheduling-time check is the front-line gate and the after-the-fact reality-check is an out-of-band audit concern.

The **trip read endpoint** `GET /v1/trips/{trip_id}` returns the row, with the standard cross-tenant predicate on the trip's parent permit's `company_id` for the operator side, the regulator's cross-tenant exemption, the **driver-self-read deviation cell** allowing the Driver-role principal whose `X-User-ID` equals the trip's `driver_id` field to read their own trips (the same self-only principle as the driver's row-read of §3.2), and no access for the test-harness role. The not-found case is `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` 404 at the existence tier, the wrong-tenant-or-wrong-driver case is `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 from the business-referential cross-tenant predicate at priority 530-or-531, and the role-not-allowed case is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403 at priority 200. The response includes the trip's derived runtime fields `latest_gps_at` (the maximum `reported_at` across the trip's GPS-stream, or null if no reports have arrived yet) and `gps_violation_count` (the integer count of auto-Penalty rows linked to this trip across the trip's GPS-stream-driven cascade chain, exposed for audit but not driving any automatic abort).

The **trip-vs-the-permit-the-vehicle-the-driver dependency-chain** at trip-creation is the trip's "is everything that should be in the right state actually in the right state for this trip to begin?" check, and it is the **rule-7 entity-pair-B** cross-entity-state dependency: the trip's existence and its initial scheduled-time-window are pinned by the parent permit's identity (the trip is under the named permit, the trip's window must lie inside the permit's term-window, the trip's driver must be on the permit's authorized list, the trip's vehicle must be on the permit's authorized list, the trip's cargo-class must be in the permit's authorized hazard-class array). The depart action's cross-entity dependency on the parent permit's `lifecycle_status == ACTIVE` is the runtime "is the permit still in force at the moment we are firing the depart?" check that may catch a permit-side change between the trip's creation and the depart attempt. The arrive action's permissive treatment of the suspended-parent case is the runtime exception: an in-flight trip's arrival attestation is allowed through a transient suspension, while a depart-from-a-suspended-permit is gated.

The complete trip endpoint surface is therefore: the create at `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/trips`, the read at `GET /v1/trips/{trip_id}`, the three action endpoints `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/depart` and `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/arrive` and `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/abort`, the gps-ingest at `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/gps-reports` and the gps-list at `GET /v1/trips/{trip_id}/gps-reports` of §3.8 — six endpoints in the trip-and-gps cluster. There is no trip-list endpoint at the top level (the per-permit's trip listing is implicit through the parent permit's id and the standard "the operator iterates over its own permits to enumerate the trips" pattern), the gps-and-penalty side has its own endpoints discussed in §3.8 and §3.8. The trip's state-machine's reachability from each of the five state-values, with the canonical edges narrated above, is the test-layer's coverage matrix on the trip lifecycle: every state is reachable, every transition is exercisable by an explicit endpoint call or by a cascade from a parent-permit transition that the test harness can fire via the sweep-or-verify-compliance path. The terminal states are `COMPLETED` (reached only through the arrive endpoint on an `IN_TRANSIT` source) and `ABORTED` (reached through the operator-abort endpoint on either a `PLANNED` or an `IN_TRANSIT` source, or through the cascade-abort fan-out from the parent permit's terminal-or-void transition).

The trip's `cargo_weight_kg` field is the load-weight figure that, combined with the vehicle's `max_payload_kg` field, is the body-shape constraint on the load: `cargo_weight_kg` is in the integer interval from zero (an empty-cargo trip is permitted, on the convention that a deadhead positioning move counts as a trip even though no cargo is moved — the trip's role is to record the vehicle-driver-route combination's wall-clock window in the regulator-facing audit log) to the vehicle's `max_payload_kg` inclusive. A negative weight or an over-capacity weight is the body-shape catch-all `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 with the message naming whether the lower or upper bound was crossed. The cargo-description-and-hazard-class-and-weight triple records the regulator-facing cargo-manifest snapshot at the trip's scheduling moment; the operator's-internal manifest-tracking is the carrier's-own system and the regulator's interest is the hazard-class compliance and the audit trail.

The trip-creation race-condition contract is the **rule-8 overlap-A interval-arithmetic** scenario: two concurrent trip-create requests for the same vehicle with overlapping scheduled windows race on the vehicle-calendar table, the first-writer wins and writes its scheduled-window pair into the vehicle's effective calendar, and the second-arrival's window-overlap check against the now-committed first window returns the conflict (the `ERR_VEHICLE_DOUBLE_BOOKED` 409). The serialization is on the vehicle-id-keyed calendar index (each vehicle's set of scheduled-time-intervals is the index's value), and the implementation guarantees the atomic check-and-insert under a row-level lock on the vehicle id. The exact-touch boundary case where the second-arrival's `scheduled_departure_at` equals the first-arrival's `scheduled_arrival_at` is admitted by the half-open semantics, per §2.9's interval-conventions table.

The **gps-stream's strict-monotonicity** rule on the trip's `latest_gps_at` cursor is the rule-8 overlap-B interval-semantics counterpart of the vehicle-calendar's half-open overlap-A. The convention "the new GPS report's `reported_at` must be strictly greater than the trip's current `latest_gps_at`" gives the **closed-on-prior-edge** boundary on the GPS-event-stream's monotonicity (a sample with the equal-timestamp is rejected, the prior cursor's value belongs-to-the-prior-arrival's-state), in contrast with the vehicle-calendar's "the boundary timestamp does not belong to either interval and the touching-boundary case is admitted as non-overlapping" half-open right-exclusive convention. The two interval-semantics on the two different physical resources — the vehicle-calendar's scheduling intervals and the GPS-stream's temporal-monotonicity check — are the canonical "different interval semantics on different resources" pair the scaffold's complexity-profile prose names as the rule-8 differentiation clause. The full discussion of the GPS-stream's boundary conventions, the in-flight-window check, the idempotency-key dedup, and the auto-penalty cascade chain is in §3.8.

A small note on the **driver-side observability** of the trip lifecycle: the Driver role's self-read on `GET /v1/trips/{trip_id}` for a trip whose `driver_id` equals the Driver's `X-User-ID` returns the trip-row's current state including the four state-machine state values and the timestamps, so the driver can observe the trip the operator has scheduled them onto and the trip's current status as the operator fires the depart, arrive, and abort actions. There is no driver-side write endpoint on a trip — the trip's lifecycle is operator-driven and the driver is the named principal on the trip-row's `driver_id` foreign key without being an actor on the trip's state transitions, on the operator-mediates-the-driver convention discussed in §2.1 and §3.2. The corresponding driver-side observability of the driver's disciplinary record is the `GET /v1/drivers/{driver_id}/penalty-summary` self-read of §3.8, which surfaces the rolling-points-and-endorsement-standing view and the list of permits the driver is currently named on.

### 3.8. The GPS Telemetry Stream, Automatic Penalties, and the Cross-Aggregate Cascade

The **GPSReport** entity is the append-only stream of position-and-speed samples attributed to an in-flight Trip. A sample is pushed into the stream through `POST /v1/trips/{trip_id}/gps-reports` by the parent company's operator's fleet-tracking back-end (the role's cell in the RBAC matrix is `CompanyOperator`-allow with the transitive-tenant predicate on the trip's parent permit's `company_id` being the operator's `X-User-ID`; the regulator's read access is on the GET-list of the trip's GPS-stream, not on the ingest path, since the regulator audits the stream rather than producing it; the driver's role has no gps-write or gps-read access on the principle that the driver observes the consequences of the GPS-tracked behavior through the penalty-summary digest of §3.8 rather than the raw stream). The request body carries the position-and-time-and-speed quartet of fields: `reported_at` (the on-board GPS unit's wall-clock-from-the-satellite timestamp at which the position fix was taken, in ISO 8601 UTC with millisecond precision), `lat` (latitude in the inclusive decimal-degree range from -90 to 90), `lon` (longitude in the inclusive decimal-degree range from -180 to 180), `speed_kph` (a non-negative float of the instantaneous speed in km/h at the sample's moment), and `heading_deg` (a float in the inclusive degree range from 0 to 360 inclusive, with the convention that 360 and 0 mean the same compass north — the field is diagnostic and does not drive any compliance rule). The optional `Idempotency-Key` HTTP header in the inclusive string-length range from 8 to 128 characters is the per-trip dedup-slot key discussed in §2.10.

The handler's validation order on the gps-ingest is the priority-tier sequence of §2.7 with two notable internal sub-orderings on the safety tier. First the authentication-and-authorization tier with the cross-tenant predicate on the parent permit's company; then the existence check on the URL's `{trip_id}` with the failure `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` 404; then the body-shape catch-all for the structural well-formedness of the four position-and-time fields (the lat-and-lon range checks, the speed-non-negativity check, the millisecond-precision-ISO-8601 format on `reported_at`, the optional `Idempotency-Key` string-length bound — each failure being `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 with the response message naming which field is malformed); then the **idempotency-slot lookup** at priority 550 — if the request carries the optional `Idempotency-Key` header and the per-trip slot for that key already holds a stored body-hash, the handler compares the request body's byte-hash to the stored hash, returning the original 201 response unchanged (with a `replayed: true` flag in the response's `meta` object) on a match and rejecting the second-arrival with `ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` 409 on a mismatch, both outcomes leaving the on-disk GPSReport table and the parent trip's derived fields exactly as the first-arrival left them; the replay-on-match case is the standard idempotent-retry success scenario.

If the idempotency lookup says the request is fresh (the key is absent from the header or the slot for the present-key is empty), the handler runs the **in-window check** at the safety-tier priority 509 — the `reported_at` value must lie in the trip's in-flight interval `[actual_departure_at, min(actual_arrival_at, aborted_at, the current logical-clock value)]` with the convention that on a still-`IN_TRANSIT` trip the right edge is the current logical-clock value and on a terminal-`COMPLETED`-or-`ABORTED` trip the right edge is the trip's recorded `actual_arrival_at` or `aborted_at` respectively (whichever is the trip's terminal-time stamp). A trip in still-`PLANNED` has `actual_departure_at` null, so the interval is degenerate (the empty interval) and any incoming GPS sample is out-of-window. A trip that has already terminated has its right-edge frozen at the terminal moment, so any sample whose `reported_at` is past the terminal moment is out-of-window. The error id on a window-failure is `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_WINDOW` 409. The check is at priority 509, which is lower (and therefore earlier-firing) than the strict-monotonicity check at priority 512 — so a sample that is both out-of-window and out-of-order returns the window error, which is the **rule-5 priority-conflict-C** intra-tier disambiguation pin.

Next the **strict-monotonicity check** at priority 512 runs: the new sample's `reported_at` must be strictly greater than the parent trip's derived `latest_gps_at` cursor (which is the maximum `reported_at` across the existing GPS-stream for the trip, or null when the stream is empty in which case the strict-greater-than is trivially satisfied against the conceptual minus-infinity initial cursor). The strict-greater-than predicate's boundary case — `reported_at == latest_gps_at` exactly — is the rejection case `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_ORDER` 409, on the "closed-on-prior-edge" interval-semantics of the GPS-monotonicity rule which contrasts with the vehicle-calendar's half-open-right-exclusive semantics that admits a touching-boundary as non-overlapping. The rule-8 differentiation between these two interval semantics on the two different resources (the vehicle-calendar and the GPS-event-stream) is the scaffold's deliberate "two different rules on two different physical-resource axes" pin discussed in §2.9 and §3.7.

Then the **parent-permit-active gate** at the safety-tier priority 515 fires: the parent Trip's parent Permit's `lifecycle_status` must be `ACTIVE` at the moment of the GPS-ingest call. If the permit is in `SUSPENDED` (the suspension is the regulator's signal that one of the five validity legs has flipped to false in the gap since the last verify-compliance, and the GPS-stream is the live operational data that the suspended-permit cannot accept new entries on), the response is `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409. If the permit is in `EXPIRED` or `VOIDED`, the cascade-side-effect-list of the parent's terminal-or-void transition has already cascade-aborted the trip and the trip's `trip_status` is now `ABORTED`, which the in-window check at priority 509 catches first as "the trip's terminal-time is now-finite and any new sample's `reported_at` is either before-the-departure or after-the-terminal-time depending on the specific case" — so the canonical observable on a GPS-ingest-after-the-parent-permit-has-terminated is the `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_WINDOW` 409 error id rather than the parent-permit-not-active id, on the priority-509-fires-before-priority-515 ordering. The `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` error on the gps-ingest path is the live-suspension case — the permit is in the still-within-the-term-but-currently-suspended state and the trip's own `trip_status` is still `IN_TRANSIT` (the suspension does not cascade-abort the trip per §3.5's principle), so the trip's in-flight window is non-empty and the in-window check passes, and then the parent-permit-active gate fails. The two ordering pins — in-window at 509, monotonicity at 512, parent-permit-active at 515 — are the **rule-5 priority-conflict-C** within-Tier-5a integer-priority disambiguation that the test layer asserts the three-way simultaneous-failure precedence of.

The gps-ingest handler on a passing guard chain reads the parent Trip's parent Permit's route's polyline and runs the **gps_violation_classification calculation** of §2.8 and the scaffold's §1.3 to compute the two boolean violation flags. The **speed-violation flag** is the strict-greater-than predicate on `gps_report.speed_kph > MAX_HAZMAT_SPEED_KPH`: a sample whose speed is strictly larger than the configured maximum hazmat speed is the speed-violation, and a sample at exactly the boundary speed-limit is not a violation by the strict-greater-than convention. The **off-route flag** is the strict-greater-than predicate on the haversine-segment perpendicular distance from the sample's `(lat, lon)` pair to the nearest of the polyline's piecewise great-circle segments using the constant `EARTH_RADIUS_M` of 6,371,000.0 meters: a sample whose computed `off_route_distance_m` is strictly greater than the configured corridor radius `OFF_ROUTE_RADIUS_M` is the off-route violation. The boundary at exactly the radius is not a violation. The two flags are independent and a single sample may simultaneously fire both.

The handler then atomically writes the new GPSReport row and the zero, one, or two auto-Penalty rows (one per fired violation flag) in the same transaction. The new GPSReport row carries the body's input fields, the server-set `received_at` equal to the current logical-clock value (which may differ from the on-board-clock's `reported_at` by network-and-processing latency — the audit observes both timestamps), the server-computed `off_route_distance_m` field, the optional `idempotency_key` lifted from the header (or null if absent), and the system-set `auto_penalty_ids` array listing the ids of the auto-Penalty rows the same transaction has just inserted (the array is the empty list for a clean sample, a one-element list for a single-flavour violation, or a two-element list for the simultaneous-both case). The auto-Penalty rows each carry `subject_type = DRIVER` and `subject_id = the trip's driver_id` (the driver is the disciplinary subject for both GPS-violation kinds, per the §2.8 penalty-source taxonomy's assignment), the appropriate `source` value (`GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED` for the speed flag, `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE` for the off-route flag), the corresponding `points_value` (`GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED_POINTS` for the speed kind, `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE_POINTS` for the off-route kind), the `observed_at` equal to the parent GPSReport's `reported_at` (the on-board-clock-time-of-observation, the timestamp the appeal-window of §3.8 is measured against), the `source_gps_report_id` pointing back to the new GPSReport row's id, the `trip_id` set to the parent trip's id, the auto-set `reason_note` of a canonical string naming the violation cause and the threshold constant (the prefix `"Auto: "` followed by a description of the violation cause), the initial `void_status = ACTIVE`, the `upheld_at` and `upheld_by` fields null, and the standard append-only `id` and `created_at` system fields.

The parent Trip's two derived fields `latest_gps_at` and `gps_violation_count` are atomically refreshed in the same transaction: `latest_gps_at` is set to the new GPSReport's `reported_at` (the strict-monotonicity guarantee from the priority-512 check means the new value is always strictly larger than the prior cursor, so the cursor advance is a strict-increase), and `gps_violation_count` is incremented by the integer length of the auto_penalty_ids array (zero for clean samples, one for single-flavour violations, two for simultaneous-both samples). The on-disk Trip row's `version` and `updated_at` are also bumped by the side-effect, on the convention that the derived-field refresh is a logical mutation of the trip aggregate.

The **disciplinary-points cascade** chain from a GPS-violation insert is the **rule-4 cascade-A** depth-four chain that the scaffold's complexity-profile prose's cross-aggregate-cascade clause names as the load-bearing instance. Hop one is the gps-ingest's new GPSReport-row insertion (the trigger). Hop two is the auto-Penalty-row insertion in the same transaction, which is the first cross-entity hop because the Penalty is a separate aggregate keyed on the subject driver. Hop three is the synchronous side-effect of the Penalty insert on the subject Driver's cached `current_rolling_points` field — the cached value is recomputed as the sum-over-the-non-upheld-penalties-in-the-rolling-window per the formula of §2.11, and if the post-increment value is at-or-above the threshold `THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS` (the inclusive-greater-than-or-equal boundary discussed in §2.8 as the suspension threshold's exact-equality firing rule), the Driver's state-machine fires the automatic `VALID → INVALID` transition on the `hazmat_endorsement_status` field in the same atomic transaction, writing the bumped Driver `version` and refreshing the Driver's `updated_at`. Hop four is the **deferred cross-aggregate cascade** onto every Permit whose `authorized_driver_ids` array contains the just-flipped driver: the cascade is **not synchronous** with the gps-ingest's transaction — the gps-ingest's transaction touches only the GPSReport, the zero-to-two Penalty rows, and the Driver's cached fields and status — and the permit-side reconciliation happens on the next verify-compliance call on each affected permit (or on the next sweep tick that runs verify-compliance internally on the affected permit per §2.5), at which the live-recompute of the drivers-leg of the permit-validity AND-chain in calculation 1 of §2.11 observes the driver's `hazmat_endorsement_status` is now `INVALID` (or the per-driver-flag in the snapshot map disagrees with the live value of the per-driver-conjunction-of-endorsement-in-force-and-coverage-and-points-below-threshold from the live-mode evaluation), and the permit transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` per the verify-compliance handler's snapshot-vs-live disagreement logic of §3.5, with the cause discriminator value `GROUNDING_INVALIDATION` on the sweep's response.

The depth-four cascade chain's reverse — the **upward arm** from a regulator's appeal-uphold action of §3.8 that voids a contributing penalty (recomputing the driver's rolling total downward), followed by the regulator's explicit refresh-endorsement attestation of §3.2 that flips the driver's status back to `VALID` (the keep-the-regulator-in-the-loop convention preventing an automatic upward flip from the rolling-total fall-off alone), followed on each affected permit by a verify-compliance observation of the now-valid drivers-leg and the SUSPENDED-to-ACTIVE reinstatement transition — is the symmetric reinstatement that brings the cascade back to the all-green steady state. The reinstate-side's depth is also four and the trigger is the regulator's discretionary appeal-and-attest sequence rather than the automatic gps-violation.

The gps-stream's **wall-clock-of-the-parent-permit-expiry interaction** is the other end of the cascade family: a permit's natural-end-of-term `valid_until` boundary fires the wall-clock-expiry `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` (or `SUSPENDED → EXPIRED`) on the sweep or the verify-compliance's embedded check, with the synchronous fan-out abort on every still-open child trip carrying `abort_reason = PERMIT_EXPIRED_CASCADE` per §3.6's discussion. After the cascade, any subsequent gps-ingest on the aborted trip fails the priority-509 in-window check because the trip's right edge is now the `aborted_at` instant, and a sample whose `reported_at` is past that edge is out-of-window. The **rule-4 cascade-B** depth-two instance (sweep tick fires permit's wall-clock-expiry, which synchronously fans out to abort the trips) is the other half of the cascade-pair that the scaffold's complexity-profile prose names: cascade-A is the four-hop disciplinary-points chain from the gps-side trigger, cascade-B is the two-hop wall-clock-driven chain from the time-side trigger. The two cascades have different originating triggers — the operator's gps-report submission versus the system's sweep-tick — and different depths — four versus two — and originate on different aggregates — the GPSReport append versus the wall-clock comparison on each permit — satisfying the cross-aggregate-cascade clause's "two cascades, different originating events, at least one of depth at least three" requirement.

The **gps-list endpoint** `GET /v1/trips/{trip_id}/gps-reports` returns the trip's GPS-stream as a paginated list sorted by `reported_at` ascending and ties broken by `id` ascending (the chronological reading-order). The optional `violation_only` boolean query parameter (defaulting to false) filters the response to only those GPS-reports whose `auto_penalty_ids` array is non-empty — the violation-only audit view, useful for the regulator's post-trip analysis of where on the route the violations occurred and what their distances and speeds were. The standard `limit` and `offset` pagination apply, with the standard `total_count` and `has_more` meta-fields. The cross-tenant predicate is the transitive-parent-permit-company-id-equals-operator's-X-User-ID rule, the regulator is cross-tenant-exempt, the driver and the test-harness have no access. The not-found case is `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` 404, the wrong-tenant case is `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 after the existence check, the role-not-allowed case (driver-or-test-harness) is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403, and the malformed-query-parameters case is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422.

The **manual-penalty endpoint** `POST /v1/penalties` is the regulator's path to write a disciplinary penalty against a driver, a company, or a vehicle for an out-of-band-observed offense (a checkpoint stop, a paperwork irregularity, a regulator's-personal-observation that is not in the GPS-stream's data set). The actor is the `RegulatorOfficer`-only — the operator cannot self-penalize its own drivers (the penalty-issuance authority is the regulator's prerogative on the regulatory-domain principle) and the driver cannot self-penalize either; both are the default-deny `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403 at the authorization tier. The auto-Penalty rows on the gps-ingest path are server-side side-effects of the operator's gps-report submission rather than separate manual-penalty calls, so the auto-rows bypass this endpoint and carry their `source` discriminator set to `GPS_VIOLATION_SPEED` or `GPS_VIOLATION_OFF_ROUTE` rather than the manual endpoint's `MANUAL_REGULATOR`.

The manual-penalty body carries the subject's `subject_type` enum value (one of the three values `DRIVER`, `COMPANY`, `VEHICLE` from the §2.8 penalty-subject-types taxonomy), the `subject_id` UUID of the subject entity (which must resolve to a row in the corresponding entity's table — the existence-tier check fires the appropriate `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` 404 or `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND` 404 or `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND` 404 on a non-resolving id, with the discrimination by the `subject_type` field), the `points_value` integer in the inclusive range 1 to 50 (a value outside the range is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422), the `observed_at` ISO 8601 UTC timestamp at or before the current logical-clock value (a future-dated `observed_at` is `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 — the regulator cannot record a penalty for an event that has not yet occurred), the required `reason_note` 1-to-500-character free-text cause (the regulator's prose justification for the penalty, missing-or-empty being `ERR_VALIDATION` 422), and the optional `trip_id` UUID for the trip-contextual variant (a manual penalty for an offense observed during a specific trip but not arising from the GPS-stream — the trip-id must resolve to a Trip row if present, the failure being `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` 404, and the `trip_id` field is null on the on-disk row if the body's optional field is absent).

The handler's successful side-effect for a `DRIVER`-subject manual penalty is the same as for an auto-Penalty insert: the new row's `points_value` is added to the subject Driver's `current_rolling_points` cache by the standard rolling-window-sum recomputation, and if the post-increment value is at-or-above the threshold the Driver's `hazmat_endorsement_status` flips from `VALID` to `INVALID` atomically inside the same transaction, with the Driver's `version` and `updated_at` bumped. The cross-aggregate downstream cascade onto every ACTIVE permit listing the affected driver is the **deferred cascade-A-prime** — the same shape as the gps-violation cascade discussed above but with the originating trigger being the manual-penalty insert rather than the gps-report insert, the chain depth from the manual-penalty trigger being three (penalty-insert, driver-flip side-effect, deferred permit-suspension on the next verify-compliance), one shorter than the four-deep chain from the gps-side because there is no upstream gps-report-event in the manual chain. The two chains are the rule-4 cascade-A's two-instance set with the same downstream targets (the driver's status and the affected permits' lifecycle status) but the different upstream triggers (the operator's gps-report submission versus the regulator's manual-penalty creation).

For `COMPANY`-subject and `VEHICLE`-subject manual penalties, the new row is appended to the Penalty table for the regulatory audit trail but **drives no automatic state transition** — the scaffold's current rule set assigns no consequence to a non-driver-subject penalty's points-total accumulation, on the §2.8 taxonomy note that the company-and-vehicle subject totals are tracked-but-unused. A future expansion of the rule set could add the symmetric "the company's rolling-points total at or above a threshold flips the company's qualification to revoked" or "the vehicle's rolling-points total at or above a threshold flips the vehicle's inspection-required-flag", but the scaffold does not currently expose any such automatic. The audit-only nature of the non-driver-subject penalties is the regulator-side recordkeeping convention.

The manual-penalty response is the 201 row with the body fields echoed and the server-set fields `id`, `created_at`, `source = MANUAL_REGULATOR`, `source_gps_report_id = null` (the manual-penalty has no upstream GPS-report linkage), `void_status = ACTIVE`, `upheld_at = null`, `upheld_by = null`, plus a `cascade_summary` sub-object reporting the post-insert state of the subject driver's rolling-points-total (the new `current_rolling_points` integer) and the resulting `hazmat_endorsement_status` (which is the post-cascade value — flipped to `INVALID` if the just-inserted penalty's contribution crossed the threshold, unchanged otherwise) and the list of the permit-ids of every ACTIVE permit listing the affected driver (the "to-be-cascaded" identifiers the next verify-compliance call on each permit will pick up). The cascade-summary is a transient response-field that lets the test harness assert on the depth-three chain's reach without having to GET each permit and observe the snapshot-vs-live disagreement separately.

The **appeal-uphold endpoint** `POST /v1/penalties/{penalty_id}/uphold` is the regulator's compensating action on a previously-issued Penalty whose appeal has been adjudicated in the appellant's favor — the regulator's act of "upholding the appeal" in the standard regulatory parlance, where the appeal succeeded and the penalty is rendered void. The actor is the `RegulatorOfficer`-only. The body carries the required `uphold_reason` 1-to-500-character free-text justification (missing-or-empty being `ERR_VALIDATION` 422), and the system-set fields on the row's mutation are `void_status = UPHELD`, `upheld_at = the current logical-clock value`, `upheld_by = the regulator's opaque X-User-ID`, and the `reason_note` field is server-side prefixed with a canonical "upheld-on-appeal" header followed by a delimiter and the original reason note's text — the on-disk `reason_note` after a successful uphold encodes both the appeal-outcome's regulator-stated reason and the original cause, with the audit-trail observable on the `void_status`, `upheld_at`, and `upheld_by` triple (which is the canonical observable the test layer asserts the uphold's effect on; the prepended-reason-note's exact format is the implementation-detail of the merged-text encoding and is not load-bearing as long as both the appeal-reason and the original-reason are conserved in the field). The Penalty's row has no `version` field because the entity is common-fields-false in the standard append-only sense; the in-place mutation of the three flip-fields and the reason-note is the one exception to the strict-append-only contract, and the scaffold pins it explicitly as a single-transition state machine with the two states `ACTIVE` and `UPHELD` and the unique edge `ACTIVE → UPHELD`. The terminal `UPHELD` state has no out-going edge: an upheld appeal cannot be re-opened through the API. The standard naming-confusion-warning on the verb "uphold": in regulatory parlance, "to uphold an appeal" means the appeal succeeds and the underlying penalty is voided, so the on-disk discriminator value `UPHELD` semantically means "the penalty is voided because the appeal was upheld in the appellant's favor". The function `driver_penalty_point_rolling_total` of §2.11 specifically filters out `UPHELD` rows from the rolling sum, on the principle that a successfully-appealed penalty does not count toward the disciplinary total.

The uphold's guard's conjunction is the source-state-`void_status == ACTIVE` predicate (an already-`UPHELD` penalty's idempotent-retry attempt is the standard `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409), the appeal-window check at the safety-tier priority 513 (the duration `now() - observed_at` is at most `APPEAL_WINDOW_DAYS` — the scaffold's intended 30 days, with the closed-right convention on the duration meaning the boundary moment at exactly 30 days is the last in-window moment and any strictly-larger duration is out-of-window with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409; the window's anchor on the `observed_at` rather than on the row's `created_at` ensures the appeal-window's clock starts at the moment of the underlying violation rather than the server's-side processing-arrival moment, which matters for auto-Penalty rows whose `observed_at` is the on-board-GPS-clock's value at the moment the violation was observed and may precede the server's receive-and-insert moment by minutes or hours on a delayed-network upload). The handler's successful side-effects on a passing guard are the four-field mutation described above (the `void_status` flip, the `upheld_at` and `upheld_by` writes, and the `reason_note` prepend), plus the **points-conservation recomputation** of the subject driver's `current_rolling_points` cache: the new cache value is the rolling-sum-over-`ACTIVE`-penalties-in-the-window, which by the algebraic identity equals the prior cache value minus the upheld penalty's `points_value` field (when the cache was consistent before the uphold and the upheld penalty's `observed_at` was in the current rolling window; outside-window penalties' points were never in the prior sum so the uphold of an outside-window penalty is a no-op on the cache, with the `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409 actually preventing that exact case because the appeal-window's 30-day-from-`observed_at` upper bound is much shorter than the rolling-window's 365-day-from-`now` lower bound for any reasonable configuration). The Driver's `hazmat_endorsement_status` is **not automatically flipped back** from `INVALID` to `VALID` by the uphold's side-effect even when the post-recompute rolling total has fallen below the threshold — the keep-the-regulator-in-the-loop convention of §3.2 means the regulator must explicitly run the refresh-endorsement attestation to flip the driver back to `VALID`, with the uphold-then-attest pair being the standard reinstatement-after-successful-appeal workflow. The cross-aggregate cascade on the affected permits is the deferred mirror-image of the downward cascade: the next verify-compliance on each `SUSPENDED` permit whose drivers-leg flipped to false because of the now-upheld penalty observes the live drivers-leg as true again (the upheld penalty is excluded from the rolling sum, the rolling total is back below the threshold, the regulator has separately re-attested the endorsement, and the driver's `hazmat_endorsement_status` is again `VALID`), and the verify-compliance transitions the permit `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with the cause discriminator `GROUNDING_REVALIDATION` per §3.5's logic. The full chain depth on the reinstatement arm is the same as the downward depth — four hops from the uphold's trigger through the driver's cache recomputation and the regulator's separate attestation to the per-permit's verify-compliance state-flip. The sweep's run-all-verify-compliance step on the test-harness path is the deterministic flush mechanism that makes the reinstatement observable at the API boundary without the test having to call verify-compliance individually on each permit.

The uphold's response is the 200 with the updated Penalty row, the cascade-summary sub-object reporting the subject driver's post-recompute `current_rolling_points` and the unchanged `hazmat_endorsement_status` (unchanged because the keep-regulator-in-the-loop principle means the uphold does not auto-flip the driver back to `VALID`), and the list of the permit-ids whose state is now eligible for the SUSPENDED-to-ACTIVE reinstate on the next verify-compliance call (the deferred-cascade-target set).

The **driver penalty summary endpoint** `GET /v1/drivers/{driver_id}/penalty-summary` is the driver-side aggregate observable that surfaces the rolling-points-and-endorsement-standing in a structured form. The actor roles on this endpoint are: `CompanyOperator` with the transitive-tenant predicate on the driver's `company_id` matching the operator's `X-User-ID` (the operator's fleet-compliance dashboard view of its own drivers, with a wrong-tenant case being the `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 at the priority-530 ownership check after the existence check passes), `RegulatorOfficer` with the cross-tenant-exempt all-driver view, the `Driver` role itself with the self-only check at the priority-531 self-driver-id predicate (a Driver-role request whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the path's `{driver_id}` is the same `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 with the cause specialized to the wrong-individual identity), and the test-harness role with no access. The not-found case at the existence tier is `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` 404.

The response payload is the structured digest of the driver's disciplinary standing at the moment of the read: the path's `driver_id` echo, the `as_of` ISO 8601 timestamp of the read moment (the current logical-clock value used as the `asof_timestamp` argument of the rolling-window calculation), the driver's `current_rolling_points` integer total (the cached value on the Driver row, which the implementation guarantees is in sync with the live formula at the current clock, with the cache-refresh hooks on every penalty-create-or-uphold and the daily sweep providing the freshness invariant), the `threshold` integer exposing the configured `THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS` so the client can compute the buffer of "points below the suspension threshold" without needing the constant out of band, the driver's current `hazmat_endorsement_status` enum value from the three-value set `{NOT_ATTESTED, VALID, INVALID}`, the driver's `endorsement_valid_until` ISO 8601 timestamp (or null if the driver has never been attested), the driver's `endorsement_classes` array (the regulator-attested hazard-class subset, or the empty array on a never-attested driver), the **active-penalties-in-window** array of penalty digests for the rolling-window's currently-counted entries (each entry is an object with the penalty's `id`, `points_value`, `observed_at`, `source` enum value, `trip_id` linkage (null for non-trip-contextual manual penalties), and a truncated `reason_note_first_120_chars` string for the summary view; the array is sorted descending by `observed_at` and ties broken by `id` descending; only ACTIVE-status penalties whose `observed_at` is in the rolling window are included, and the algebraic sum of the array's `points_value` field equals the response's `current_rolling_points` integer by the conservation identity), the **upheld-penalties-in-window** array of penalty digests for entries whose `observed_at` is still in the rolling window but whose `void_status` has flipped to `UPHELD` (the audit-transparency entries — these don't contribute to the rolling total but are surfaced so the client can see the regulator's appeal-uphold history without having to issue a separate per-penalty read), and the **permits-currently-listing-this-driver** array of permit-link summaries (each entry carrying the `permit_id`, the permit's current `lifecycle_status`, the per-driver flag in the snapshot map's value `snapshot_endorsement_for_this_driver` (the strict-snapshot's boolean for this driver on this permit, from the permit's `snapshot_driver_endorsements` map's lookup keyed by the driver's id), and the parallel live-mode evaluation `live_endorsement_for_this_driver` at the read moment). The permits-array's entries are sorted in `created_at` descending order with a cardinality cap (the spec's intended cap is on the order of 50 entries for the per-permit-list scope, which exceeds the realistic per-driver permit-membership count by a wide margin so the cap is a sanity guard rather than a load-bearing limit).

The penalty-summary endpoint surfaces the **snapshot-versus-live dual-variant** axis on the driver-side: each permit-entry's pair of booleans (`snapshot_endorsement_for_this_driver` versus `live_endorsement_for_this_driver`) is the disagreement signal for the dual-variant calculation 2 of §2.11 (the as-of-issue snapshot of the driver's endorsement standing stamped onto the permit's `snapshot_driver_endorsements` map at issue time and refreshed at each verify-compliance, versus the live evaluation of the same conjunction `(hazmat_endorsement_status == VALID) AND (endorsement_valid_until >= permit.valid_until) AND (current_rolling_points < THRESHOLD_DRIVER_SUSPENSION_POINTS)` at the read moment). The dual-variant's observable diff — a permit whose snapshot says the driver was green at the last verification but whose live evaluation says the driver is now red, or vice versa — is the signal that a verify-compliance on the permit would flip the permit's status. The endpoint is the canonical regulator-side audit view that surfaces this disagreement without forcing the regulator to compose the cross-aggregate join by hand. The test layer asserts the dual-variant on this endpoint by sequencing a Penalty insert that flips the driver's standing, then a GET on the penalty-summary observing the live-flag is now false and the snapshot-flag is still true (because no verify-compliance has yet fired on the affected permits), then a verify-compliance on each affected permit observing the snapshot-and-live now agreeing on false and the permit transitioning to SUSPENDED, then a fresh GET observing the snapshot-flag and the live-flag now both false and the permit's lifecycle_status now SUSPENDED. The reverse sequence — an uphold that lowers the rolling total below the threshold, the regulator's explicit refresh-endorsement attestation flipping the status back to VALID, the verify-compliance on each affected permit observing the now-valid live drivers-leg and flipping the permit back to ACTIVE — is the symmetric reinstatement assertion-pattern on the upward direction.

The penalty-summary endpoint's pagination is not engaged on the active-and-upheld penalty arrays themselves (the windows are bounded by the rolling window's 365-day extent and the per-driver realistic penalty count is on the order of tens at most, so the summary returns the full arrays without a `limit/offset` cursor), and the permits-array is similarly bounded by the per-driver permit-membership count. The endpoint is therefore a non-paginated dedicated digest view rather than a generic list with the standard envelope's pagination meta-fields — the standard `total_count` and `has_more` fields are not present on this response's `meta` block because the response is structurally a single-object summary rather than a paged collection. The implementation-side guarantee is that the response is computed in one query-walk over the relevant Penalty rows (filtered by `subject_type == DRIVER` and `subject_id == the path's {driver_id}`) and one query-walk over the relevant Permit rows (filtered by membership of the driver-id in the per-permit `authorized_driver_ids` array), with the two walks combined into the response payload in the order described above. The freshness invariant is the same cache-and-recompute discipline that the Driver row's `current_rolling_points` field is maintained under, with the read's `as_of` field reflecting the logical-clock value at which the rolling-window's right edge was anchored for the read's computations.

The full set of error ids the gps-ingest and the penalty endpoints draw from is therefore: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` 401 at the auth tier; `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` 403 at the authorization tier for the wrong-role case; `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` 403 at the priority-530-or-531 tenant-or-self-only predicate; `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` 404 at the existence tier on the gps-ingest's URL `{trip_id}` or the manual-penalty's optional body `trip_id`; `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` / `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND` / `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND` 404 on the manual-penalty's body `subject_id` discriminated by the `subject_type` enum value; `ERR_PENALTY_NOT_FOUND` 404 on the uphold endpoint's URL `{penalty_id}`; `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 at the body-shape catch-all for the various field-format-or-range violations including the wrong-cargo-hazard-class-for-the-permit case on the trip-creation (which is the trip's analog, not the gps-side, but the body-shape error id is the same generic catch-all); `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` 409 on the uphold endpoint's source-state-not-ACTIVE case; `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_WINDOW` 409 at the gps-ingest's priority-509 in-window check; `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_ORDER` 409 at the gps-ingest's priority-512 strict-monotonicity check; `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` 409 at the gps-ingest's priority-515 parent-permit-active gate; `ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` 409 at the gps-ingest's priority-550 idempotency-slot-body-mismatch case; and `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409 at the uphold endpoint's priority-513 closed-right-on-the-duration appeal-window check. The full catalogue is in §5, with the response-envelope's standard error shape from §2.2 carrying the `error_id` and the human-readable `message` field with the cause discriminator string for the specific failure within an error id's broad cause-class.

A final note on the **scope of the auto-penalty's appeal**: the auto-Penalty row carries the same `void_status` two-state machine and the same uphold endpoint as the manual-Penalty row. The driver-or-the-company on whose behalf the appeal is filed is responsible for the out-of-band filing of the appeal with the regulator's office, the regulator's adjudication is out-of-band, and the uphold endpoint is the regulator's act of recording the appeal's successful outcome in the system. The uphold endpoint does not distinguish between a manual penalty and an auto-penalty at the schema level — the `source` field on the Penalty row remembers the original cause but the uphold's effect is uniform across the two flavours, and the appeal-window's anchor on `observed_at` (the on-board-GPS-clock-time for an auto-penalty's case, the regulator's-written `observed_at` for a manual case) is uniform too. The auto-penalty's `source_gps_report_id` field gives the audit trail's link back to the offending GPS sample even after the appeal-uphold has zeroed the penalty's contribution to the rolling total, and the parent GPSReport row's `auto_penalty_ids` array continues to point at the now-upheld Penalty row for the symmetric backward traceability. The wallet ledger of the parent company is **not** touched by either the manual penalty's creation or the GPS-auto-penalty's creation or any subsequent uphold: the disciplinary-points axis is an integer-points accounting on the driver's individual record, independent of the wallet's currency-cents accounting on the company's record, and the two conservation invariants of §2.11 are independent in the axis-of-quantity sense the scaffold's complexity-profile prose's "two conservation invariants on different quantity types" clause pins.

## 4. Endpoint Reference

The HTTP status convention is uniform across the API. A successful creation that mints a new row — every `POST` whose URL pattern is a flat create or a child-create-under-a-parent — returns the standard 201 with the new row in the response envelope's `data` field. A successful mutation of an existing row — every `POST` whose URL pattern names an existing-row's id-and-action pair — returns the standard 200 with the updated row in the response envelope's `data` field. A successful read — every `GET` — returns 200 with the row (object response type) or the array of rows (array response type with the pagination meta-fields). The five **race-condition contracts** of §2.10 each name one of the 409 conflict-tier ids as the canonical loser-error: the vehicle-calendar overlap-race on the trip-creation produces `ERR_VEHICLE_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, the GPS-stream-strict-monotonicity race on the gps-ingest-from-two-equal-timestamp-arrivals produces `ERR_GPS_OUT_OF_ORDER`, the idempotency-key-with-different-bodies race on the gps-ingest produces `ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`, the simultaneous-verify-compliance race on a permit produces the OCC `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT`, and the simultaneous-renew-and-void-issue race on a freshly-issued permit produces the same OCC `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` on the row's `version` field. The four error ids the concurrency rules name are all in the catalogue above, and the cross-reference between the §2.10 concurrency-rule block and the §5 error catalogue is the canonical-completeness check the scaffold's prose's "every concurrency-rule error id is in the catalogue" invariant ensures.

The two **conservation invariants** of §2.11 — the currency-conservation on the wallet ledger across the (issue, renewals, void-issue) cycle and the points-conservation on the rolling penalty-points window across the (create, uphold) pair — are not error-ids in the catalogue because they are exact-equality identities rather than rejection-causes: an action that would violate one of the invariants is impossible by the action-set's construction (every fee-touching action records its delta on the row's audit field — the on-row `issue_fee_cents` and the appended `renewal_history` entries' `fee_cents` field and the void's `void_refunded_cents` — and the refund's value is the literal sum of the stored deltas so the round-trip is bit-exact; every penalty-touching action records the row's points-value and the on-row `void_status` flag so the rolling sum's value is the deterministic function of the table's contents). The conservation properties are observables for the test layer to assert against arbitrary action sequences — read the company's `wallet_balance_cents` before and after, read the driver's `current_rolling_points` before and after, check the deltas match the action-sequence's recorded fee-and-points changes — and the implementation's "do it right" obligation is to maintain the audit-field-equals-the-delta-applied invariant on every fee-and-points-touching transaction. The standard test-layer assertion-pattern is the sequence "snapshot, action sequence, snapshot, compare the deltas to the recorded audit fields, assert the round-trip is zero on the matching reverse-sequence".

The two **dual-variant axes** of §2.11 — the permit's snapshot-vs-live posture and the driver's as-of-issue-vs-current-rolling standing — are likewise not error-ids; they are the calculations' two-mode read paths that produce two booleans whose disagreement is the observable signal of a pending state transition. The verify-compliance handler's "transition target on the disagreement direction" logic of §3.5 and the penalty-summary endpoint's per-permit `snapshot_endorsement_for_this_driver` versus `live_endorsement_for_this_driver` pair of §3.8 are the canonical surfaces on which the dual-variant disagreements are observable; the relevant `transition_fired` discriminator values on the verify-compliance response (`ACTIVE_TO_SUSPENDED` and `SUSPENDED_TO_ACTIVE` and the two `ACTIVE_TO_EXPIRED` and `SUSPENDED_TO_EXPIRED` variants and the `NO_CHANGE` no-op) capture the state-machine's observable on the dual-variant disagreement's resolution.

The catalogue's compactness — twenty-five ids covering the system's full failure-cause space — is achieved through the convention that the generic body-shape `ERR_VALIDATION` 422 absorbs the dozens of per-field format-and-range violations without introducing per-field error ids (the response's `message` field carries the specific cause for the human-debuggability of which field-and-bound was violated, but the error id is uniform on the catch-all), the convention that the natural-key uniqueness violations on the three uniqueness-bearing fields (`registration_number`, `license_number`, `plate_number`) all share the single `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` id with the response message naming which natural-key collided, the convention that the cross-tenant ownership predicate's failure on the operator's transitive-tenant rules and on the driver's self-only rule share the single `ERR_CROSS_TENANT_ACCESS` id at the same 403 status, and the convention that the seven entity-types' existence-failures each have their own dedicated `ERR_<ENTITY>_NOT_FOUND` 404 id discriminated by which entity's table the lookup failed in — a fixed seven-ids-for-the-seven-entities count that the §2.7 existence-tier's left-to-right path-walk-and-body-foreign-key-walk produces. The catalogue is the closed set the response envelope's `data.error_id` field draws from, and every test-assertion in the benchmark's test suite picks its expected error-id from this set.
