# Airline Distribution Offers API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An airline no longer sells one price list through one pipe. The same seat reaches the market through a direct website, through modern retailing connections operated by aggregators, and through decades-old legacy distribution links, and each of those routes carries a different cost, a different content entitlement, and a different regulatory display obligation. Commercial teams have responded by differentiating content per channel — richer bundles here, a restricted catalogue there, a negotiated corporate fare that must never surface in public shopping — and the airline's own systems have not kept up. Today the fare filing lives in one place, the agency incentive agreement in a spreadsheet, the surcharge structure in a legacy table, and nobody can answer the one question the CFO keeps asking: what does a booking actually cost us on each channel?

This document specifies the Airline Distribution Offers service, a single system of record for distribution content and the offers built from it. The service holds the channel configuration (technology, content tier, markup, cost model, servicing capability, disclosure obligations), the filed fare inventory with its sale and travel windows and its revision chain, the ancillary and bundle catalogue, the commercial agreements that govern how partners are compensated, and the corporate contracts that unlock negotiated private fares under strict traceability. On top of that content it constructs channel-specific offers, prices every line deterministically, publishes them subject to regulatory acknowledgement, converts them into orders, and services those orders through changes, refunds and voids with capability parity checked per channel. Alongside the commercial flow it runs the technology-partner certification programme: connectivity test slots on shared certification environments, weighted readiness scoring, and the airline's certification decision — which, when it goes the wrong way, must cascade through agreements and offers rather than leave stale content in the market.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **determinism**: every monetary figure, every readiness score, every cost-per-booking number must be reproducible from stored inputs in a pinned evaluation order, because these figures are audited and negotiated over. Second, **traceable causality**: when content is pulled, suspended or revoked, the effect must propagate immediately and visibly to every offer built on it, and the audit rows that recorded the old prices must survive untouched. A regulator asking "which disclosure codes did this agency acknowledge when it booked?" and an aggregator asking "why did my published offer disappear?" must both get an answer from stored state alone.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Distribution Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role it acts in. If either header is absent, empty, or contains only whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a whitespace-only value is treated exactly as an absent one. A value in `X-User-Role` that is not one of the six role names declared below is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. When the role name is recognised but that role is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- **`PlatformAdmin`** — the airline's distribution platform operator. Registers channels and their surcharge structures, registers distribution partners, records certification decisions, and drives the service's logical clock.
- **`PricingAnalyst`** — files and re-files fares, publishes ancillary products, and defines bundles. May suspend or withdraw filings but has no authority over commercial agreements.
- **`DistributionManager`** — owns commercial distribution: per-channel content policy, agency incentive agreements, corporate contracts, and servicing decisions on orders.
- **`ComplianceAuditor`** — a strictly read-only regulatory reviewer. Inspects disclosure acknowledgements, negotiated-fare traceability and distribution cost per booking, and may never perform a mutating operation.
- **`Agency`** — a travel agency or corporate booking channel acting under its own incentive agreement. Counters negotiations, constructs and publishes offers, creates orders and opens servicing requests.
- **`TechnologyPartner`** — an aggregator or technology provider driving connectivity certification. Opens certifications, schedules connectivity tests and reports their outcomes; it can never take the airline's certification decision.

Four of the six roles are **operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow calls for provenance — for example as `filed_by_user_id` on a fare filing, `proposed_by_user_id` or `signed_by_user_id` on an agency agreement, `submitted_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on a servicing request, or `tested_by_user_id` on a certification — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `PricingAnalyst`, for `DistributionManager`, and for `ComplianceAuditor`: each supplies an opaque operator identifier, which the server may store as provenance but never resolves against a stored resource.

The remaining two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Agency`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DistributionPartner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DistributionPartner` compare the resource's `partner_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `TechnologyPartner`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `DistributionPartner`, and the same `partner_id` comparison governs every certification and connectivity-test resource it touches. The negotiation workflow makes this concrete: an `Agency` recording a counter-offer on an agreement must be the very partner the agreement names, and if the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the agreement's `partner_id` the call fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Authorization is not the same as segregation of duties. A single operation in this service additionally requires that two *different* human operators be involved: accepting an agency agreement. That rule is specified with the negotiation workflow and carries its own error code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422); no other operation is guarded by it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the range from 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two listings override that default because their consumers depend on evaluation order rather than insertion order: a channel's surcharge rules are returned in ascending `threshold_cents` order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-breaker — the exact order in which the surcharge calculation evaluates them — and an offer's priced lines are returned in ascending `sequence` order.

For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size applied), `offset` (the effective starting position applied), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Audit Trail

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client. Alongside the identifier, resources carry a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, set when the row is written and never rewritten afterwards.

Most resources are mutable and additionally carry `updated_at` — same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation that the service accepts. `version` is the currency of the concurrency rules in §2.7 and of the content-traceability stamp in §3.5; clients read it, hold it, and hand it back.

Two resource kinds are deliberately immutable once written and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: a channel's surcharge rules, and an offer's priced lines. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on those two resource kinds — they are absent from the response payload entirely, not present-but-null. A surcharge rule is never edited; a new rule is added instead, and existing offers keep the lines they were priced with. An offer's priced lines are never repriced; when the content behind an offer is pulled, the offer's own state changes while its lines are retained verbatim for audit.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Window Arithmetic

The service never reads real wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a **logical clock** whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Every deadline evaluation in this specification — a fare's sale window, a fare's travel window, a corporate contract's validity window, an offer's expiry, a corporate contract's derived validity state — compares against the logical clock and never against the host's actual time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. Moving the clock does not rewrite any stored instant: values such as an offer's `expires_at` or a connectivity test's `scheduled_end` stay exactly as computed and are simply re-compared against the new clock.

Window inclusivity is specified per window and is never guessed:

- A fare filing's **sale window** is half-open: the instant `sale_from` is inside the window and the instant `sale_to` is outside it. An offer may be constructed when the logical clock is at or after `sale_from` and strictly before `sale_to`.
- A fare filing's **travel window** is closed: a `departure_date` equal to `travel_from` is permitted, and a `departure_date` equal to `travel_to` is also permitted.
- A corporate contract's **validity window** is half-open on `effective_from` and `effective_to`, with the same inclusive-start, exclusive-end reading.
- A connectivity test's **environment slot** is a closed interval running from `scheduled_start` to `scheduled_end` inclusive, which means two slots that merely touch at a single instant do share that instant.

Timestamps supplied by clients in request bodies are ISO 8601 UTC instants. A window whose bounds are inverted or degenerate is a malformed request: `sale_from` at or after `sale_to`, `travel_from` after `travel_to`, or `effective_from` at or after `effective_to` all yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this service are integers denominated in **whole cents**, and every field name that holds money ends in `_cents`. There are no fractional-cent amounts anywhere in stored state. Wherever a computation produces a non-integral value, the service rounds **half up** — a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude — and it does so at the exact point the relevant calculation nominates, never earlier and never later. Rounding order is a first-class part of each formula in §3, not an implementation detail: per-line pricing rounds each line before any summation, the surcharge calculation rounds the rate multiplication before applying its clamps, the incentive accrual rounds exactly once after the cap, and the cost-per-booking variants round only their final division.

Percentage-bearing fields — `content_markup_pct`, `rate_pct`, `bundle_discount_pct`, `proposed_incentive_pct`, `agreed_incentive_pct` — are decimal fractions, not whole-number percentages: a five percent markup is expressed as 0.05. Each carries its own range and its own maximum decimal precision, stated where the field is introduced. A percentage field whose supplied value falls outside the interval from 0 through 1 inclusive is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), as is any negative money or count field.

Readiness scores are the one non-monetary computed numeric family: they are integers in the range 0 through 100 inclusive, produced by rounding a weighted mean half up.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Distribution Content

Two write operations in this service destroy content that other clients may be relying on, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the *parent* resource's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required** input, not an optional one; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

**Opening an agency agreement.** A `POST /agency-agreements` request must carry `expected_partner_version` holding the `version` the client last observed on the target `DistributionPartner`. A new agreement legitimately supersedes that partner's prior agreement on the same channel, so the writer must prove its view of the partner is current before that supersede is allowed to land. If `expected_partner_version` does not equal the partner's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

**Filing a fare revision.** A `POST /fare-filings/{id}/revisions` request must carry `expected_fare_filing_version` holding the `version` the client last observed on the base filing named in the path. A revision supersedes that base filing and withdraws every offer built on it, so a stale view of the base must not be allowed to supersede a revision the writer never saw. If `expected_fare_filing_version` does not equal the base filing's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FARE_FILING_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and neither the revision nor the supersede takes effect.

No other endpoint in this service accepts an expected-version field. Operations that are not listed above — publishing an offer, capturing a payment, advancing fulfilment, reviewing or applying a servicing request, recording a certification decision, reporting a test outcome, revising channel content policy — take no version input at all and must not be rejected for a missing one.

### 2.8. Content Access Tiers

Content differentiation across channels is enforced by a single numeric comparison. Every channel carries a `content_tier`, one of `FULL`, `STANDARD` or `RESTRICTED`, defaulting to `STANDARD` when the channel is registered without one. From that enum the service derives `content_tier_rank`, an integer: `FULL` maps to 3, `STANDARD` maps to 2, and `RESTRICTED` maps to 1. `content_tier_rank` is computed by the service and is never supplied by a client; it is recomputed whenever the channel's content policy is revised.

Four kinds of sellable content carry a minimum entitlement of their own, each in the field `min_content_tier_rank`, an integer between 1 and 3 inclusive: a fare filing (default 1), an ancillary product (default 1), a bundle definition (default 1), and a corporate contract (default 2 — negotiated content is by policy withheld from restricted channels unless deliberately opened up). A channel may sell a piece of content only when its own `content_tier_rank` is greater than or equal to that content's `min_content_tier_rank`. The comparison is applied to every component of an offer at once and a single failure is reported as `ERR_CONTENT_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation and Hierarchical Consistency

The service uses immediate consistency: by the time a mutating call returns, every downstream effect it triggers is already visible to a subsequent read. There is no deferred queue and no eventual reconciliation.

Cascades in this domain run in one direction — from content and commercial standing down to offers — and they are always *destructive downward, never resurrecting upward*. Pulling a fare filing withdraws the offers built on it; restoring the filing does not bring those offers back. Suspending a partner's commercial standing suspends its agreement and withdraws that agreement's live offers; reinstating the partner returns the agreement to signed but leaves the withdrawn offers withdrawn. Every hop of every cascade refreshes the touched resource's `updated_at` and increases its `version` by 1, so a client holding a version stamp can always detect that a cascade passed through. Orders are never cascaded away: an order already created is a contract with a customer and survives the withdrawal of the offer it came from.

Ledger figures behave the same way. When a cascade removes revenue from an agreement, the incentive accrual is recomputed on the reduced revenue base and the channel's distribution-cost figures are recomputed after it, in that order — the cost recomputation must see the new accrual, not the old one.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence and Path Resolution

Requests are validated in a strict, globally fixed precedence order, and the **first** failing check determines the response. This matters whenever a single request is wrong in more than one way: a caller with a missing header and a nonexistent resource id gets the authentication error, not the not-found error. The order is:

1. **Authentication** — both context headers present and non-empty (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401).
2. **Authorization** — the role is one of the six, the role is listed for the endpoint, and for the entity-backed roles the `X-User-ID` ownership comparison of §2.1 holds (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403).
3. **Request shape** — every required field present, every enum value inside its declared domain, every numeric and string constraint from the field definitions honoured, `limit` within 1 to 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400).
4. **Path resolution** — every identifier in the URL path resolves, validated strictly left to right, so an outer segment's not-found error always wins over an inner segment's. Depending on which segment failed, the error is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FARE_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONNECTIVITY_TEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.
5. **Body reference resolution** — every foreign key and catalogue code carried in the request body resolves: `partner_id`, `channel_id`, `fare_filing_id`, `bundle_id`, `corporate_contract_id`, `offer_id`, `negotiated_fare_filing_id`, `corporate_partner_id`, and every entry of `ancillary_codes`. The reported error is `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FARE_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUNDLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ANCILLARY_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate, each HTTP 404. This step applies to `POST /agency-agreements`, `POST /corporate-contracts`, `POST /offers`, `POST /orders`, `POST /bundle-definitions` and `POST /partner-certifications`.
6. **Optimistic concurrency** — the submitted expected-version field equals the parent's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_FARE_FILING_VERSION`, 412), on `POST /agency-agreements` and `POST /fare-filings/{id}/revisions` respectively.
7. **Offer expiry** — the targeted offer has not reached `expires_at`. The lazy expiry transition is applied first and the operation is then rejected with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /offers/{id}/publish` and `POST /orders`.
8. **Fare filing still sellable** — the referenced filing's `status` is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FARE_FILING_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /offers` and `POST /offers/{id}/publish`.
9. **Offer published** — the referenced offer's `status` is `PUBLISHED`, else `ERR_OFFER_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409). Applies to `POST /orders`.
10. **Transition legality** — the targeted resource's current state has an outgoing transition for the requested trigger, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). This covers the fare-filing `to_state` request, the agreement negotiation `action`, the order `payment` and `fulfillment` actions, opening a servicing request, the servicing `review` and `apply` actions, the certification `decision`, scheduling a connectivity test, the connectivity-test `outcome`, and the still-scheduled-tests gate on a certify decision. Applies to `POST /fare-filings/{id}/state`, `POST /agency-agreements/{id}/negotiation-rounds`, `POST /orders/{id}/payment`, `POST /orders/{id}/fulfillment`, `POST /orders/{id}/service-requests`, `POST /service-requests/{id}/review`, `POST /service-requests/{id}/apply`, `POST /partner-certifications/{id}/decision`, `POST /partner-certifications/{id}/connectivity-tests` and `POST /connectivity-tests/{id}/outcome`.
11. **Payment-state preconditions** — the order's `payment_status` admits the operation: `PAID` is required to ticket, to open a refund servicing request, and to apply one; `AUTHORIZED` is required to open a void servicing request. Otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID` (409). Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/fulfillment`, `POST /orders/{id}/service-requests` and `POST /service-requests/{id}/apply`.
12. **Signed agreement present** — when the ordering partner's `partner_type` is `TRAVEL_AGENCY`, a signed agreement exists for the offer's partner-and-channel pair, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SIGNED` (409). Applies to `POST /orders`.
13. **No open certification** — no other certification for the same partner-and-channel pair is in `TESTING`, `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_ALREADY_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /partner-certifications`.
14. **Segregation of duties** — the acceptor differs from the proposer (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 422), on `POST /agency-agreements/{id}/negotiation-rounds`.
15. **Regulatory disclosure** — the submitted acknowledgement covers the channel's required codes (`ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`, 422), on `POST /offers/{id}/publish` and `POST /orders`.
16. **Servicing capability parity** — the channel supports the servicing action being applied (`ERR_SERVICING_NOT_SUPPORTED_ON_CHANNEL`, 422), on `POST /service-requests/{id}/apply`.
17. **Private-fare content linkage** — `ERR_PRIVATE_FARE_REQUIRES_CONTRACT` (422), on `POST /offers`.
18. **Traceability reference present** — `ERR_CORPORATE_TRACKING_REF_MISSING` (422), on `POST /orders`.
19. **Traceability reference format** — `ERR_CORPORATE_TRACKING_REF_INVALID` (422), on `POST /orders`.
20. **Partner type eligibility** — `ERR_PARTNER_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422), on `POST /agency-agreements`, `POST /corporate-contracts` and `POST /partner-certifications`.
21. **Contract in effect** — `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_IN_EFFECT` (409), on `POST /offers`.
22. **Sale window open** — `ERR_FARE_SALE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), on `POST /offers`.
23. **Travel date inside window** — `ERR_TRAVEL_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW` (422), on `POST /offers`.
24. **No overlapping active filing** — `ERR_FARE_SALE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), when a filing is driven to `ACTIVE` through `POST /fare-filings/{id}/state`.
25. **Same-test teardown buffer** — `ERR_TEST_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409), on `POST /partner-certifications/{id}/connectivity-tests`.
26. **Environment concurrency capacity** — `ERR_TEST_ENV_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on `POST /partner-certifications/{id}/connectivity-tests`.
27. **Content access tier** — `ERR_CONTENT_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on `POST /offers`.
28. **Channel code uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_CODE` (409), on `POST /channels`.
29. **Negotiation round limit** — `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` (422), on `POST /agency-agreements/{id}/negotiation-rounds`.
30. **Readiness minimum** — `ERR_READINESS_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422), when a certify decision is recorded through `POST /partner-certifications/{id}/decision`.
31. **Corporate fiscal cap** — `ERR_CONTRACT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422), on `POST /offers`.
32. **Payment amount match** — `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422), on `POST /orders/{id}/payment`.

Note the consequences of this ordering on the offer-construction path in particular: an offer request that names a suspended filing *and* an out-of-tier channel *and* an over-cap total reports the filing problem first (step 8), then the tier problem (step 27), and only reaches the cap (step 31) once the earlier checks pass. Similarly, a contract that is not in effect (step 21) is reported before the departure date is examined (step 23).

### 2.11. Regulatory Display and Disclosure Acknowledgement

Each channel declares `required_disclosure_codes`, an array of regulatory display and disclosure code strings that may legitimately be empty. Two operations demand acknowledgement of those codes: publishing an offer into the channel, and creating an order in the channel. Each of those requests may carry `disclosure_ack_codes`, an array of strings; when the field is omitted the service treats it as an empty array.

Completeness is an order-insensitive set comparison. The service computes the set of required codes not present among the acknowledged codes; acknowledgement is complete precisely when that set is empty. Extra codes are harmless — acknowledging codes the channel did not require never causes a failure. When acknowledgement is incomplete, the request is rejected with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422).

On a successful publish the offer records `acknowledged_disclosure_codes` as the codes submitted on that call, verbatim and including any codes the channel did not require, and sets `disclosure_complete` to true. On order creation the check is a gate only: no acknowledgement field is written onto the order beyond the `disclosure_ack_codes` the caller supplied, and no field on the offer is touched. Crucially, **acknowledgement does not carry forward**: codes acknowledged at publish time are not credited to the order, so an order request must resubmit them.

The empty-required-set boundary is deliberate and must be honoured: when a channel's `required_disclosure_codes` is empty, the missing set is empty for any acknowledgement whatsoever — including an entirely omitted `disclosure_ack_codes` — so both publish and order creation succeed, and the published offer records `disclosure_complete` as true with `acknowledged_disclosure_codes` as an empty array.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Channel Configuration, Content Policy and the Surcharge Structure

Everything in this service hangs off a channel. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying a `channel_code` — a human-readable identifier of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across all channels, compared case-sensitively, with a reuse rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_CODE` — together with a `channel_kind` naming the distribution technology behind it: `DIRECT` for the airline's own storefront, `NDC_AGGREGATOR` for a modern retailing connection, or `GDS_LEGACY` for a traditional distribution link. The remaining registration inputs are all optional and each has a defined default, because most channels are registered with a template and tuned afterwards.

The commercial dials are set at registration. `content_tier` grants the channel its content entitlement and defaults to `STANDARD`. `content_markup_pct` is the per-line distribution markup applied to every offer line priced through the channel; it defaults to 0, must lie between 0 and 0.25 inclusive, and is accepted with at most four decimal places. `incentive_multiplier` scales the marginal agency incentive earned on the channel; it defaults to 1.0, must lie between 1.0 and 2.0 inclusive, and is accepted with at most two decimal places. Three cost inputs feed the two distribution-cost views described in §3.9: `monthly_platform_fee_cents`, the fixed monthly platform fee the accounting view spreads across bookings; `per_message_cost_cents`, the cost of one distribution message; and `per_segment_fee_cents`, the fee per booked segment. All three default to 0 and none may be negative.

Two boolean flags declare the channel's **servicing capability parity** — the extent to which the channel can service what it sold. `servicing_parity_change` defaults to true and states that change requests can be applied through the channel; `servicing_parity_refund` also defaults to true and states the same for refunds. A channel that sells but cannot refund is a real and permitted configuration; the consequence surfaces in §3.7. Finally `required_disclosure_codes` lists the regulatory display codes that must be acknowledged in this channel, defaulting to an empty array.

On registration the service derives `content_tier_rank` from `content_tier` using the fixed mapping of §2.8, and initialises the channel's four ledger fields — `booking_count`, `activity_cost_cents`, `cost_per_booking_flat_cents` and `cost_per_booking_activity_cents` — to 0. Those four are computed fields; a client never writes them.

Commercial policy drifts, and a `DistributionManager` revises it. The content-policy revision accepts any subset of `content_tier`, `servicing_parity_change`, `servicing_parity_refund` and `required_disclosure_codes`, recomputes `content_tier_rank` from the new tier, refreshes `updated_at` and increases the channel's `version` by 1. What it deliberately does **not** do is reprice or withdraw anything: offers already constructed keep the prices they were built with and keep their recorded `priced_channel_version`, and no offer changes state because the policy moved. Tightening a channel's tier therefore affects future offers only.

A channel's surcharge structure is built from immutable rules, each added by a `PlatformAdmin` and permanently bound to one channel through its `channel_id`. A rule declares a `surcharge_code` of 1 to 32 characters, which is stamped onto the surcharge line the rule generates; an `item_scope` of `FARE`, `ANCILLARY` or `ALL`, which selects the base the rule is measured against; a `rate_pct` strictly greater than 0 and at most 0.5, accepted with at most four decimal places; a `threshold_cents` at or above which the rule fires, defaulting to 0; a `min_cents` lower clamp, defaulting to 0; and a `max_cents` upper clamp, which must be greater than or equal to `min_cents`. Because rules are never edited, the set of rules a given offer was priced against is fixed at the moment of construction. Adding a rule increases the owning channel's `version` by 1, and from that point on every new offer on the channel emits a surcharge line whenever the rule's scope base reaches `threshold_cents`.

Reading a channel returns its live distribution-cost ledger — both cost-per-booking variants side by side — which is why the read is open to every role including `ComplianceAuditor`, `Agency` and `TechnologyPartner`.

### 3.2. Fare Content: Filing, Activation, Revision and the Catalogue

A `PricingAnalyst` files a fare into `DRAFT`. The filing declares its market and product — a `fare_basis_code` of 1 to 16 characters, an `origin` and a `destination` each of exactly three uppercase letters, and a `cabin` of `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM` or `BUSINESS` — plus a `base_amount_cents` strictly greater than 0, which is the filed base fare before any channel markup or surcharge. Four instants define its two windows: `sale_from` and `sale_to` bound the half-open sale window, and `travel_from` and `travel_to` bound the closed travel window, with `sale_to` required to be strictly after `sale_from` and `travel_to` required to be at or after `travel_from`. Three policy flags complete the filing: `min_content_tier_rank`, an integer from 1 to 3 defaulting to 1; `refundable`, defaulting to true, which determines whether the fare line this filing produces enters the refundable base at settlement time; and `is_private`, defaulting to false, which marks the filing as a negotiated fare sellable only under a corporate contract. On creation the service records `filed_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, sets `revision_number` to 0, leaves `base_filing_id` null, and sets `status` to `DRAFT`.

The filing lifecycle is driven by submitting a target state in `to_state`, optionally with a free-text `reason`. A filing moves `DRAFT → FILED` once the service re-verifies that `sale_to` is strictly after `sale_from` and that `travel_to` is at or after `travel_from`; a filing whose windows are inverted at this point is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The move refreshes `updated_at` and increases the filing's `version` by 1. `FILED → ACTIVE` is the moment the fare becomes sellable, and it is gated by the sale-window exclusivity rule below; once a filing is `ACTIVE`, offer construction against it stops failing with `ERR_FARE_FILING_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the transition increases `version` by 1.

**Sale-window exclusivity.** Two filings that share the same `origin`, the same `destination`, the same `cabin` and the same `fare_basis_code` may not both be `ACTIVE` with overlapping sale windows. Before a filing is allowed into `ACTIVE`, the service compares its half-open sale window against the half-open sale window of every other filing already in `ACTIVE` on that same four-part market key. Overlap is strict: two windows overlap when they share at least one instant of positive extent, which means windows that merely touch at their boundary — one filing's `sale_to` equal to another filing's `sale_from` — do **not** overlap and are both permitted. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_FARE_SALE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, and the same check is applied again on the revert edge described next.

Pulling a fare out of the market is where cascades begin. An `ACTIVE` filing may be driven to `SUSPENDED` by either a `PricingAnalyst` or a `DistributionManager` — this is the one filing transition the commercial side shares — with no precondition beyond transition legality. Suspension increases the filing's `version` by 1 and, in the same operation, sets every offer that references this filing and sits in `CONSTRUCTED` or `PUBLISHED` to `WITHDRAWN`, each such offer's `version` increasing by 1. A suspended filing may return to sale, `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, but only a `PricingAnalyst` may reactivate it, the sale-window exclusivity check is re-applied in full, and — this is the asymmetry that matters commercially — the offers withdrawn during the suspension are **not** resurrected. Reactivation increases `version` by 1 and nothing else.

Withdrawal is terminal. A filing may be withdrawn from `ACTIVE`, from `FILED`, or from `SUSPENDED`; in every case the transition increases `version` by 1. The three withdrawal edges differ in exactly one respect: withdrawing from `ACTIVE` also sets every referencing offer in `CONSTRUCTED` or `PUBLISHED` to `WITHDRAWN`, because those offers were live in the market, whereas withdrawing from `FILED` and withdrawing from `SUSPENDED` carry no offer cascade — a `FILED` filing was never sellable, and a `SUSPENDED` filing's offers were already withdrawn at suspension time.

**Re-filing a fare.** Rather than editing a live filing, a `PricingAnalyst` files a revision against it. The revision request carries a fresh `base_amount_cents`, a fresh `sale_from` and `sale_to`, and the `expected_fare_filing_version` demanded by §2.7. Revision is permitted against a base filing in `ACTIVE` or in `FILED`, and both edges behave identically in structure: the base filing moves to `SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increases by 1, and a brand-new filing is created in `DRAFT` that copies the base's `fare_basis_code`, `origin`, `destination`, `cabin`, `travel_from`, `travel_to`, `min_content_tier_rank`, `refundable` and `is_private`, records `base_filing_id` as the base filing's identifier, and sets `revision_number` to the base's `revision_number` plus 1. The two edges differ only in the cascade: superseding a base filing that was `ACTIVE` also sets every offer referencing it in `CONSTRUCTED` or `PUBLISHED` to `WITHDRAWN`, while superseding a base that was still `FILED` withdraws nothing, since no sellable offer could exist. A stale `expected_fare_filing_version` aborts the whole operation with `ERR_STALE_FARE_FILING_VERSION` — no revision is created and the base is not superseded. Both `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal: any further lifecycle trigger against a filing in either state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Fare filings are discoverable by `status`, by `origin`, by `destination`, by `cabin` and by `base_filing_id` — the last of these being how a caller walks a revision chain, since listing by a base filing's identifier returns the revisions filed against it.

Alongside fares, a `PricingAnalyst` maintains the ancillary catalogue. An ancillary product declares an `ancillary_code` of 1 to 16 characters that must be unique across all ancillary products, a display `name` of 1 to 80 characters, a `list_amount_cents` strictly greater than 0, a `min_content_tier_rank` from 1 to 3 defaulting to 1, and a `refundable` flag that — note the inversion relative to fares — defaults to **false**. Ancillaries are optimistically assumed non-refundable unless the analyst says otherwise.

Bundles group those ancillaries at a discount. A bundle definition declares a `bundle_code` of 1 to 16 characters unique across all bundle definitions, an `ancillary_codes` array holding between 1 and 6 entries where every entry must resolve to an existing ancillary product (an unresolvable entry is `ERR_ANCILLARY_NOT_FOUND`), a `bundle_discount_pct` between 0 and 0.5 inclusive accepted with at most four decimal places, and a `min_content_tier_rank` from 1 to 3 defaulting to 1. The discount is applied to each member ancillary's `list_amount_cents` before the channel markup, in the pinned order specified in §3.5.

### 3.3. Distribution Partners, Incentive Agreements and the Negotiation Cycle

Before any commercial relationship exists there is a partner. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one with a `partner_name` of 1 to 120 characters holding the legal name, a `partner_type` declaring which distribution relationship the partner holds — `TRAVEL_AGENCY`, `TECH_PROVIDER` or `CORPORATE` — an `iata_code` of 1 to 16 characters carrying the IATA or account identifier, and a `home_country` of exactly two uppercase letters in ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 form. A freshly registered partner's `certification_status` starts at `UNCERTIFIED`; that field is computed from the partner's certification records and moves only through the workflow of §3.8, never by direct write. `partner_type` is the polymorphic hinge of the whole domain: the same partner registry serves agencies that sell, technology providers that connect, and corporate accounts that consume negotiated fares, and each operation admits only the types it is meant for.

A `DistributionManager` opens an incentive agreement with a travel agency on one channel. The agreement names its counterparty through `partner_id` and its scope through `channel_id`, and it may be opened only when that partner's `partner_type` is `TRAVEL_AGENCY` — a technology provider or a corporate account is rejected with `ERR_PARTNER_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The commercial terms are `proposed_incentive_pct`, the incentive percentage currently on the table, which must be strictly greater than 0 and at most 0.15 and is accepted with at most four decimal places; `incentive_cap_cents`, a hard cap on how much incentive the agreement can ever accrue, strictly greater than 0; and `target_net_revenue_cents`, strictly greater than 0, which is recorded purely for reporting and never participates in any calculation. The request also carries the `expected_partner_version` required by §2.7.

Opening an agreement is a supersede operation, which is exactly why it is version-guarded. Every existing agreement for the same partner-and-channel pair that sits in `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED` is set to `SUPERSEDED`. The new agreement starts in `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `negotiation_round` at 0, `cumulative_net_revenue_cents` at 0, `incentive_accrued_cents` at 0, `incentive_tier` at `TIER_1`, `tier_downgrade_count` at 0, and both `agreed_incentive_pct` and `signed_by_user_id` null. In the same operation the `DistributionPartner`'s `version` increases by 1 — which means a second client holding the old partner version can no longer open a competing agreement without re-reading.

Negotiation is a genuine cycle, driven by an `action` on the agreement together with an optional replacement `proposed_incentive_pct` and an optional free-text `note`. A `COUNTER` is the agency's move and advances `PROPOSED → COUNTERED`: it may be performed only by the `Agency` whose `X-User-ID` equals the agreement's `partner_id`, it increases `negotiation_round` by 1, and it replaces `proposed_incentive_pct` with the countered value. A `PROPOSE` is the airline's move and closes the cycle, `COUNTERED → PROPOSED`: a `DistributionManager` replaces `proposed_incentive_pct` with the airline's new value and `proposed_by_user_id` is reset to the acting `X-User-ID`. Because `COUNTERED → PROPOSED` returns the agreement to the state a `COUNTER` starts from, the pair can be traversed repeatedly — that is the negotiation.

The cycle is bounded. The service allows at most three counter rounds on any one agreement: a `COUNTER` is permitted only while the agreement's `negotiation_round` is strictly below 3, so the fourth counter is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. The airline's `PROPOSE` is bounded by the same figure but read inclusively — it is permitted while `negotiation_round` is at most 3 — so the airline may always answer the third counter, and only a further counter is refused. Both moves increase the agreement's `version` by 1.

`ACCEPT` signs the deal, `PROPOSED → SIGNED`, and it is the single operation in this service under segregation of duties: the `DistributionManager` performing it must have an `X-User-ID` different from the value stored in `proposed_by_user_id`, and an attempt by the proposer to sign its own proposal is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On signing, `agreed_incentive_pct` is frozen to whatever `proposed_incentive_pct` currently holds — the frozen figure, not the mutable one, is what the accrual calculation uses forever after — `signed_by_user_id` records the accepting operator, and `version` increases by 1. A `WITHDRAW` is available to a `DistributionManager` from either `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`, drives the agreement to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state, and increases `version` by 1. Both `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal; any negotiation action against an agreement in either state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

A signed agreement can also be suspended and reinstated, but never by a negotiation action — those two edges, `SIGNED → SUSPENDED` and `SUSPENDED → SIGNED`, are system cascades driven by the certification decisions of §3.8. Suspension pulls the agency's live content: every offer whose partner-and-channel pair matches the agreement and which sits in `CONSTRUCTED` or `PUBLISHED` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, and the agreement's `version` increases by 1. Reinstatement is deliberately narrow in scope: `agreed_incentive_pct`, `cumulative_net_revenue_cents` and `incentive_accrued_cents` are all left exactly as the suspension found them, the agreement's `version` increases by 1, and the offers withdrawn during suspension stay withdrawn.

**The revenue ledger.** An agreement accumulates `cumulative_net_revenue_cents`, defined as the sum of `net_revenue_cents` over the orders that name this agreement and whose `payment_status` is `PAID`. Orders sitting in `AUTHORIZED`, `VOIDED` or `REFUNDED` contribute nothing, and an order's `fulfillment_status` is irrelevant to the ledger entirely — a cancelled-but-paid order still counts. The ledger moves in exactly two situations: it increases by an order's `net_revenue_cents` when that order's payment is captured, and it decreases by the same figure when a refund is applied to a previously paid order.

**Incentive accrual.** Whenever `cumulative_net_revenue_cents` moves, the service recomputes the agreement's `incentive_accrued_cents` and `incentive_tier` from the *new* ledger balance, in the following pinned steps. Call the new balance the qualifying revenue.

1. **Walk the marginal bands.** The incentive is marginal, not an average rate applied to the whole balance. The first band is the lesser of the qualifying revenue and 5,000,000 cents, and earns 0.010. The second band is the qualifying revenue less 5,000,000 cents, floored at zero and then capped at 15,000,000 cents — the width between the two ceilings — and earns 0.025. The third band is the qualifying revenue less 20,000,000 cents, floored at zero, and earns 0.040. The marginal amount is the sum of each band's size multiplied by that band's rate, and it is carried forward at full precision with no rounding at this step.
2. **Scale it.** The marginal amount is multiplied by the channel's `incentive_multiplier`, and then multiplied by the agreement's frozen `agreed_incentive_pct` divided by 0.025 — the middle band's rate, which acts as the reference rate the negotiated percentage is expressed relative to. An agreement signed at exactly 0.025 therefore scales by one and earns the raw marginal figure times the channel multiplier. This step, too, stays exact.
3. **Cap, then round once.** The scaled figure is compared against `incentive_cap_cents` and the lesser of the two is taken. That capped value is then rounded half up to whole cents, and the result is stored in `incentive_accrued_cents`. The rounding happens exactly once, here, after the cap — never on the band walk and never on the multiplier.
4. **Label the tier.** `incentive_tier` is derived from the same qualifying revenue: `TIER_3` when the revenue is at or above 20,000,000 cents, otherwise `TIER_2` when it is at or above 5,000,000 cents, otherwise `TIER_1`. Both boundaries are inclusive on entry, so a balance of exactly 5,000,000 cents already labels the agreement `TIER_2` while placing zero cents in the second band.
5. **Count downgrades.** If the newly computed tier ranks strictly below the tier previously stored on the agreement, `tier_downgrade_count` increases by 1. In practice this can only happen on the refund path, since capturing a payment only ever raises the balance; when the recomputation is triggered by a capture the tier may rise but `tier_downgrade_count` is left untouched.

### 3.4. Corporate Contracts and Negotiated-Fare Traceability

Corporate accounts buy on negotiated terms, and every one of those terms is written into a corporate contract by a `DistributionManager`. The contract identifies the account holder through `corporate_partner_id`, which must name a partner whose `partner_type` is `CORPORATE` — anything else is `ERR_PARTNER_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` — and the piece of content it unlocks through `negotiated_fare_filing_id`, the private filing that may otherwise not be sold at all. An `account_code` of 1 to 24 characters is printed onto the offers and orders produced under the contract.

Three policy dials govern how the contract behaves. `max_offer_total_cents`, strictly greater than 0, is a fiscal cap: an offer priced under this contract may not exceed that total, and one that does is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. `traceability_required` defaults to true and demands that every order placed under the contract carry a tracking reference. `tracking_ref_prefix` defaults to the literal `CORP-`, must be 1 to 12 characters, and pins the required leading characters of that reference. Alongside them, `min_content_tier_rank` defaults to 2 rather than 1 — negotiated content is withheld from `RESTRICTED` channels unless the contract deliberately lowers the bar — and is otherwise an integer from 1 to 3 like every other content entitlement.

Contract validity is a half-open interval bounded by `effective_from` inclusive and `effective_to` exclusive, with `effective_to` required to be strictly after `effective_from`. From that interval and the logical clock the service derives `validity_state`, a computed field with three values: `PENDING` while the clock is strictly before `effective_from`, `IN_EFFECT` while the clock is at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`, and `LAPSED` once the clock reaches `effective_to`. Because the derivation reads the clock at request time rather than at write time, the same contract reports different states as the clock advances, with no mutation and no version bump in between.

Offers may be priced under a contract only while it reports `IN_EFFECT`; a contract that is still `PENDING` or has already `LAPSED` fails offer construction with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_IN_EFFECT`. Traceability is enforced one step later, at order time: when the offer's contract has `traceability_required` set to true, the order request must supply `corporate_tracking_ref`, and omitting it is `ERR_CORPORATE_TRACKING_REF_MISSING`; when it is supplied it must begin with the contract's `tracking_ref_prefix`, and a reference with the wrong prefix is `ERR_CORPORATE_TRACKING_REF_INVALID`. The two failures are distinct codes on purpose, because corporate travel teams debug them differently: one is a missing integration field, the other a misconfigured one.

Contracts are discoverable by `corporate_partner_id`, by `negotiated_fare_filing_id` and by `validity_state`, the last of which is evaluated against the clock at the moment the list is served.

### 3.5. Constructing and Publishing a Channel Offer

Offer construction is where content, channel and commercial terms meet, and it is the most heavily gated operation in the service. Either an `Agency` or a `DistributionManager` constructs an offer, naming the `channel_id` the offer is built for, the `partner_id` requesting it, and the `fare_filing_id` that will produce the fare line. Three optional inputs enrich it: `bundle_id`, naming a bundle whose members become bundled ancillary lines and defaulting to null; `corporate_contract_id`, naming the contract the offer is priced under and defaulting to null for public content; and `ancillary_codes`, an array of ancillary codes sold à la carte outside any bundle, defaulting to an empty array. The trip itself is described by `trip_origin` and `trip_destination`, each exactly three uppercase letters, a `departure_date` instant, and a requested `cabin` of `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM` or `BUSINESS`.

Seven conditions must hold before a single line is priced. The filing's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FARE_FILING_NOT_ACTIVE`. The logical clock must sit inside the filing's half-open sale window — at or after `sale_from` and strictly before `sale_to` — else `ERR_FARE_SALE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The requested `departure_date` must sit inside the filing's closed travel window, at or after `travel_from` and at or before `travel_to`, else `ERR_TRAVEL_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. The channel's `content_tier_rank` must be greater than or equal to the `min_content_tier_rank` of *every* referenced component — the filing, each à la carte ancillary, the bundle if one is attached, and the corporate contract if one is named — with a single shortfall anywhere reported as `ERR_CONTENT_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`. If the filing has `is_private` set to true, a `corporate_contract_id` must be present **and** that contract's `negotiated_fare_filing_id` must be the very filing being sold; a private fare offered with no contract, or under a contract that unlocks a different filing, is `ERR_PRIVATE_FARE_REQUIRES_CONTRACT`. If a contract is named at all, its `validity_state` must be `IN_EFFECT`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_IN_EFFECT`. And once the total has been computed, an offer priced under a contract must not exceed that contract's `max_offer_total_cents`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_CAP_EXCEEDED` — the cap is evaluated against the finished figure, so it is necessarily the last of the seven to be decided.

**Per-line pricing.** A successful construction generates one immutable priced line per component, and each line is priced independently in three steps. First the line's `list_amount_cents` is established: for the fare line it is the filing's `base_amount_cents`; for a bundled ancillary line and for an à la carte ancillary line alike it is that ancillary product's `list_amount_cents`. Second, the bundle discount is applied — and *only* to bundled ancillary lines: the discounted value is the line's list amount multiplied by one minus the bundle's `bundle_discount_pct`, carried at full precision with no rounding. Fare lines and à la carte ancillary lines skip this step entirely; their discounted value is simply their list amount. Third, the channel markup is applied and the line is rounded: the line's `net_amount_cents` is the discounted value multiplied by one plus the channel's `content_markup_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents.

The order of rounding and summation is pinned and is not an implementation choice. Each line is rounded to whole cents at the third step, and the offer's `subtotal_cents` is then the sum of those **already rounded** `net_amount_cents` values across the fare line, the bundled ancillary lines and the à la carte ancillary lines. Summing the unrounded line values and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule, and the two approaches can differ by as much as one cent per line beyond the first. Surcharge lines are produced afterwards and never enter `subtotal_cents`.

**Surcharge structure.** With every non-surcharge line priced, the service walks the channel's surcharge rules in ascending `threshold_cents` order, breaking ties by ascending `created_at`. Each rule computes its own scope base from its `item_scope`: a rule scoped to `FARE` measures the `net_amount_cents` of the fare line alone; a rule scoped to `ANCILLARY` measures the sum of `net_amount_cents` across the à la carte ancillary lines and the bundled ancillary lines together; a rule scoped to `ALL` measures the offer's `subtotal_cents`. The rule fires only when its scope base is greater than or equal to its `threshold_cents` — the boundary is inclusive, so a scope base exactly equal to the threshold does fire. When the scope base is strictly below the threshold the rule emits nothing at all, which is why `item_count` varies between two otherwise identical offers on the same channel.

A firing rule produces one surcharge line. The raw amount is the scope base multiplied by the rule's `rate_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents; that rounding happens **before** the clamps, not after. The clamped amount is then the raw amount raised to at least the rule's `min_cents` and lowered to at most the rule's `max_cents`. The emitted line records the raw amount in its `list_amount_cents` and the clamped amount in its `net_amount_cents`, so a clamped surcharge remains auditable against what the rate alone would have produced. The offer's `surcharge_cents` is the sum of `net_amount_cents` over the emitted surcharge lines, and `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `surcharge_cents`.

Every line carries an `item_type` of `FARE`, `ANCILLARY`, `BUNDLE_ANCILLARY` or `SURCHARGE`, an `item_code` holding the filing's `fare_basis_code`, the ancillary's `ancillary_code` or the rule's `surcharge_code` according to that type, an `offer_id` naming its owning offer, and a `refundable` flag copied from the source filing's `refundable` for a fare line and from the source ancillary product's `refundable` for either kind of ancillary line — surcharge lines always carry `refundable` false, without exception. Lines are numbered by a 1-based `sequence` in a fixed presentation order: the fare line first, then the bundled ancillary lines ordered by `item_code` ascending, then the à la carte ancillary lines ordered by `item_code` ascending, then the surcharge lines in the same ascending `threshold_cents` and `created_at` order in which they were evaluated. `item_count` records how many lines the offer ended up with.

Construction also stamps two traceability values. `expires_at` is set to the offer's `created_at` plus thirty minutes — the offer time-to-live is half an hour, and it is not configurable per channel. `priced_channel_version` records the channel's `version` as observed while pricing, so an auditor can later tell whether the channel's content policy has moved since the offer was quoted. A newly constructed offer sits in `CONSTRUCTED` with `acknowledged_disclosure_codes` empty and `disclosure_complete` false.

**Lazy expiry.** An offer is never expired by a background sweep. Instead, at the very start of any operation that targets an offer — reading it, publishing it, or creating an order from it — the service checks whether the logical clock has reached the offer's `expires_at`. If it has and the offer sits in `CONSTRUCTED` or `PUBLISHED`, the offer's `status` becomes `EXPIRED` and its `version` increases by 1 *before* the targeting operation is evaluated. A read therefore returns the offer already showing `EXPIRED`, while a publish or an order attempt is then rejected with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. The comparison is at-or-after: an operation arriving at exactly `expires_at` is too late.

**Publication.** An `Agency` or a `DistributionManager` publishes a constructed offer into its channel, advancing `CONSTRUCTED → PUBLISHED`. Three conditions guard it: the logical clock must be strictly before `expires_at`; the offer's fare filing must *still* be `ACTIVE`, because a filing that left `ACTIVE` between construction and publication makes the quote unsellable and yields `ERR_FARE_FILING_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the submitted `disclosure_ack_codes` must cover every entry of the channel's `required_disclosure_codes` under the set semantics of §2.11, failing with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`. On success the offer's `acknowledged_disclosure_codes` is set to the submitted codes verbatim, `disclosure_complete` becomes true, and `version` increases by 1. Publication is one-shot: a second publish attempt against an offer already in `PUBLISHED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

An offer's three terminal states are `ORDERED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED`. Whichever way it terminates, the offer's priced lines are immutable and are retained verbatim for audit — they are the only record of what the market was quoted — and they are observable only by listing an offer's lines, since they are never embedded in the offer payload itself. Offers are discoverable by `channel_id`, `partner_id`, `fare_filing_id`, `bundle_id`, `corporate_contract_id` and `status`.

### 3.6. Orders: Payment and Fulfilment as Independent Dimensions

An order is created from a published offer by an `Agency` or a `DistributionManager` and needs only the `offer_id`, plus whatever the channel and the contract demand. The offer's `status` must be `PUBLISHED` — a constructed-but-unpublished offer, a withdrawn one or an already-ordered one all yield `ERR_OFFER_NOT_PUBLISHED` — and the offer must not have reached `expires_at`. The channel's disclosure codes must be acknowledged again through `disclosure_ack_codes` on this very request; acknowledgement recorded at publish time does not carry over, and an order that fails to repeat them is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`. When the offer was priced under a contract requiring traceability, `corporate_tracking_ref` must be present and correctly prefixed as specified in §3.4. And when the ordering partner's `partner_type` is `TRAVEL_AGENCY`, an agreement in `SIGNED` status must exist for the offer's partner-and-channel pair; without one the order is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SIGNED`, which is the mechanism by which a suspended agency stops selling.

Two optional cost inputs ride along on the order because they are the raw material of activity-based costing: `message_count`, the number of distribution messages exchanged to create this order, defaulting to 1 and required to be at least 1; and `segment_count`, the number of booked segments, likewise defaulting to 1 and at least 1.

On creation the service copies `channel_id` and `partner_id` from the source offer — a client never supplies them — and resolves `agency_agreement_id` to the identifier of the `SIGNED` agreement for that partner-and-channel pair, leaving it null when the ordering partner is not a travel agency. `net_revenue_cents` is frozen to the offer's `total_cents` at this instant and never moves again; it is the quantity the agreement revenue ledger accumulates, and freezing it means a later policy or catalogue change cannot retroactively rewrite reported revenue. `paid_amount_cents` and `refunded_amount_cents` both start at 0. The order opens in `payment_status` `AUTHORIZED` and `fulfillment_status` `RESERVED`, and the source offer advances `PUBLISHED → ORDERED` with its `version` increasing by 1.

The two status dimensions are genuinely independent: they are advanced by separate operations and neither is inferred from the other. **The payment dimension** accepts an `action` of `CAPTURE` or `VOID`, with `payment_amount_cents` supplied on a capture. A capture advances `AUTHORIZED → PAID` and requires that `payment_amount_cents` equal the source offer's `total_cents` — the same figure the order froze into `net_revenue_cents` — with any other amount rejected as `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. On success `paid_amount_cents` becomes the captured amount, the channel's `booking_count` increases by 1, and then, in this order: if the order names an agreement, that agreement's `cumulative_net_revenue_cents` increases by the order's `net_revenue_cents` and the incentive accrual of §3.3 re-runs and rewrites `incentive_accrued_cents` and `incentive_tier` (a capture can only raise the balance, so the tier may rise and `tier_downgrade_count` is left untouched); and finally the channel's distribution-cost figures re-run, rewriting `activity_cost_cents`, `cost_per_booking_flat_cents` and `cost_per_booking_activity_cents`. The order's `version` increases by 1. A void advances `AUTHORIZED → VOIDED` with no precondition; it forces `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED`, leaves the channel's `booking_count` untouched precisely because the order never reached `PAID`, and increases `version` by 1. The remaining payment edge, `PAID → REFUNDED`, is never requested directly — it arrives only as a cascade from an applied refund, described in §3.7.

**The fulfilment dimension** accepts an `action` of `TICKET`, `FLY` or `CANCEL` and never changes `payment_status`. Ticketing advances `RESERVED → TICKETED` and is the one fulfilment move that reads the other dimension: it requires `payment_status` to be `PAID`, else `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID`. Flying advances `TICKETED → FLOWN`, has no precondition, and is reserved to a `DistributionManager` — an agency cannot declare its own passenger flown. Cancelling advances either `RESERVED → CANCELLED` or `TICKETED → CANCELLED` with no precondition, and deliberately leaves `payment_status` alone: a cancelled-but-paid order keeps its revenue in the agreement ledger until a refund servicing request is actually applied. Every fulfilment move increases the order's `version` by 1 and touches no money field. The remaining fulfilment edge, `TICKETED → CHANGED`, is likewise cascade-only.

Each dimension has its own terminal set. Payment terminates in `VOIDED` or `REFUNDED`; fulfilment terminates in `FLOWN` or `CANCELLED`. A trigger aimed at a dimension already in one of its terminal states is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Orders are discoverable by `channel_id`, `partner_id`, `offer_id`, `agency_agreement_id`, and by either `payment_status` or `fulfillment_status` independently.

### 3.7. Servicing Parity: Change, Refund and Void

Servicing capability parity across channels is a commitment the airline makes and a constraint the service enforces. Any `Agency` or `DistributionManager` may open a servicing request against an order, declaring a `request_type` of `CHANGE`, `REFUND` or `VOID` and optionally a `requested_change_note` of up to 240 characters describing what is wanted; the note defaults to null. Which types are admissible depends on where the order stands. A refund may be opened only against an order whose `payment_status` is `PAID`, and a void only against one whose `payment_status` is `AUTHORIZED` — both mismatches report `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID`. A change may be opened only against an order whose `fulfillment_status` is `TICKETED`, and a change requested against a merely reserved, flown or cancelled order is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A newly opened request sits in `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`, `order_id` bound by the service to the order it was opened against — never supplied by the client — and all four settlement figures — `refundable_base_cents`, `cancellation_fee_cents`, `refund_total_cents` and `incentive_clawback_cents` — at 0. Servicing requests are discoverable per order and filterable by `status` and by `request_type`.

Review is a `DistributionManager` responsibility and proceeds through an `action`, optionally carrying a `reason`. `START_REVIEW` advances `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and records `reviewed_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the request forks: `REJECT` advances it to the terminal `REJECTED` state and changes nothing on the order whatsoever, while `APPROVE` advances it to `APPROVED`. Each of the three review actions increases the request's `version` by 1.

Approval is where a refund is *quantified* but not yet *executed*. When the request's `request_type` is `REFUND`, approval runs the settlement calculation below and writes all four figures onto the request while leaving the order completely untouched — the money has been computed, not moved. When the `request_type` is `CHANGE` or `VOID`, approval writes nothing: all four settlement figures stay at 0, because neither branch has a refund amount to compute.

**Refund settlement.** The four figures look circularly defined — the fee depends on the base, the total depends on the fee, the clawback depends on the total, and the clawback concerns an incentive the refund itself is about to change — so the evaluation order is pinned and no other order is legal.

1. **The refundable base.** `refundable_base_cents` is the sum of `net_amount_cents` over the priced lines of the order's offer whose `refundable` flag is true. Surcharge lines always carry `refundable` false and are therefore never refunded; the fare line is refundable exactly when the source filing's `refundable` was true; and à la carte and bundled ancillary lines are refundable exactly when their source ancillary product's `refundable` was true.
2. **The cancellation fee, keyed on step 1.** When the refundable base is strictly below 50,000 cents, `cancellation_fee_cents` is a flat 2,500 cents. When the base is at or above 50,000 cents and strictly below 200,000 cents, the fee is a flat 7,500 cents. When the base is at or above 200,000 cents, the fee is the base multiplied by 0.05, rounded half up to whole cents. The two floors are inclusive on entry, so a base of exactly 50,000 cents draws the middle flat fee and a base of exactly 200,000 cents draws the proportional fee.
3. **The refund total, keyed on steps 1 and 2.** `refund_total_cents` is the refundable base less the cancellation fee, floored at zero — a base smaller than its own flat fee refunds nothing rather than going negative.
4. **The audit clawback, keyed on step 3 and the tier as stored at approval time.** `incentive_clawback_cents` is the refund total multiplied by the rate of the tier the agreement carries *at this moment*, before the refund cascade has moved anything: 0.010 when the agreement's `incentive_tier` is `TIER_1`, 0.025 when it is `TIER_2`, and 0.040 when it is `TIER_3`, with the product rounded half up to whole cents. When the order names no agreement at all, the clawback is 0.

The clawback figure is recorded for audit and **is never subtracted from** the agreement's `incentive_accrued_cents`. The apply step below re-runs the incentive accrual from scratch on the decremented revenue base, and subtracting the clawback as well would double-count the same reversal.

**Applying the request.** A `DistributionManager` applies an approved request, advancing `APPROVED → APPLIED`. Channel parity is verified first: applying a refund requires the channel's `servicing_parity_refund` to be true and applying a change requires its `servicing_parity_change` to be true, with either shortfall reported as `ERR_SERVICING_NOT_SUPPORTED_ON_CHANNEL`. Applying a refund additionally re-checks that the order's `payment_status` is *still* `PAID`, since the order may have moved between approval and apply, and reports `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID` if it has not. Applying is one-shot: a second apply against a request already in the terminal `APPLIED` state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and `REJECTED` is equally terminal.

The three branches diverge sharply in what they cascade. The **refund** branch runs four hops in a fixed causal order. Hop one moves the order itself: `payment_status` becomes `REFUNDED`, `refunded_amount_cents` becomes the request's `refund_total_cents`, and `fulfillment_status` is forced to `CANCELLED`. Hop two follows because the order has left the paid revenue base: the channel's `booking_count` decreases by 1 and the agreement's `cumulative_net_revenue_cents` decreases by the order's `net_revenue_cents`. Hop three follows from that revenue decrement: the incentive accrual re-runs on the new balance, rewriting `incentive_accrued_cents` and `incentive_tier`, and if the recomputed tier ranks strictly below the tier that was stored, `tier_downgrade_count` increases by 1 — this is the only path on which that counter ever moves. Hop four follows from the accrual change: the channel's distribution-cost figures re-run, rewriting `activity_cost_cents`, `cost_per_booking_flat_cents` and `cost_per_booking_activity_cents`, and because it runs after hop three it necessarily sees the new accrual rather than the old one.

The **change** branch is far narrower: the order's `fulfillment_status` becomes `CHANGED`, its `message_count` increases by 1 to account for the extra distribution traffic the change generated, and the channel's distribution-cost figures then re-run. The order's `payment_status` is untouched — the two dimensions advance independently even under a cascade — and the agreement's revenue ledger is untouched entirely, since a change moves no money. The **void** branch sets the order's `payment_status` to `VOIDED` and its `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED`, and performs no ledger movement and no cost recomputation at all, because an order that never reached `PAID` never contributed to either. In all three branches the servicing request's `version` increases by 1.

### 3.8. Connectivity Certification and the Shared Test Environments

Before an aggregator or an agency may rely on a channel, its connectivity is certified. A `TechnologyPartner` or a `PlatformAdmin` opens a certification naming the `partner_id` under test, the `channel_id` it is certifying against, and the `env_code` of the shared certification environment its tests will occupy — either `CERT_ENV_A` or `CERT_ENV_B`. Only partners whose `partner_type` is `TECH_PROVIDER` or `TRAVEL_AGENCY` may be certified; a `CORPORATE` partner is rejected with `ERR_PARTNER_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Nor may two certifications run in parallel for the same partner-and-channel pair: if any existing certification for that pair sits in `TESTING`, `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`, the request is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_ALREADY_OPEN`. A new certification opens in `TESTING` with `tested_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`, `reserved_test_minutes` at 0, `readiness_score_strict` and `readiness_score_provisional` at 0, `mandatory_failed_count` at 0, and `certified_variant` null. Opening it also moves the partner's `certification_status` to `IN_CERTIFICATION` and increases the `DistributionPartner`'s `version` by 1.

A `TechnologyPartner` then schedules connectivity test slots against the certification, which must be in `TESTING` for scheduling to be legal. Each test declares a `test_code` of 1 to 32 characters identifying the test case, an optional `is_mandatory` flag defaulting to false, a `scheduled_start` instant, and a `duration_minutes` between 1 and 240 inclusive. The service computes `scheduled_end` as `scheduled_start` advanced by `duration_minutes`, and the slot the test occupies is the **closed** interval running from `scheduled_start` through `scheduled_end` inclusive. Scheduling increases the certification's `reserved_test_minutes` by `duration_minutes` and increases the certification's `version` by 1; the test's `score` starts null and its `status` starts at `SCHEDULED`, with `certification_id` bound to the owning certification.

Two independent interval rules govern the shared environment, and only tests currently in `SCHEDULED` participate in either — tests that have passed, failed or been cancelled release their slot and their minutes immediately and are invisible to both rules.

**The teardown-buffer rule** is pairwise and narrow: it compares the requested slot only against scheduled tests that share both the same `env_code` and the same `test_code`, because re-running the same test case needs the harness torn down and rebuilt in between. Each existing slot is widened by fifteen minutes on **both** sides — its start pulled fifteen minutes earlier and its end pushed fifteen minutes later — and a conflict exists when the requested closed interval shares at least one instant with any widened interval. Since both intervals are closed, a gap of exactly fifteen minutes between two slots is a conflict, while a gap strictly greater than fifteen minutes is not. A conflict is reported as `ERR_TEST_SLOT_CONFLICT`.

**The environment capacity rule** is aggregate and wide: it counts every scheduled test on the same `env_code` regardless of `test_code`, and applies **no** buffer — raw closed intervals only. The service counts how many existing scheduled slots on that environment share at least one instant with the requested slot; if that count plus the requested test itself would exceed two, the request is refused with `ERR_TEST_ENV_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Closed intervals that touch at a single instant do share that instant and therefore do count. Because the buffer rule is scoped to a code pair while the capacity rule is scoped to the whole environment, neither can mask the other and both must be evaluated.

Reporting an outcome is also the `TechnologyPartner`'s job and likewise requires the parent certification to still be in `TESTING`. The `outcome` is `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `CANCELLED`; a `score` between 0 and 100 inclusive must be present on a pass and on a failure, and its absence there is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every outcome decreases the certification's `reserved_test_minutes` by that test's `duration_minutes` and increases the certification's `version` by 1. A pass and a failure both trigger a recomputation of **both** readiness variants; a failure on a test whose `is_mandatory` is true additionally increases `mandatory_failed_count` by 1. A cancellation is the deliberate exception: it releases the reserved minutes — restoring exactly the value they held before the test was scheduled — leaves `score` null, and leaves both readiness figures untouched, so a cancelled slot leaves no trace in the score.

**Readiness scoring, two ways.** Only tests in `PASSED` or `FAILED` are considered; scheduled tests, which still hold reserved minutes, and cancelled tests never enter either variant's numerator or denominator. Each considered test carries a weight of 3 when its `is_mandatory` is true and 1 when it is false. The `STRICT` variant, stored in `readiness_score_strict`, is 0 whenever `mandatory_failed_count` is greater than 0, and also 0 when no test has been considered at all; otherwise it is the sum over considered tests of that test's `score` multiplied by its weight, divided by the sum of those same weights, rounded half up to an integer. The `PROVISIONAL` variant, stored in `readiness_score_provisional`, first drops every considered test that is both mandatory and failed from the population — removing it from the numerator **and** from the denominator — and is 0 when the remaining population is empty; otherwise it is the same weighted mean over the remaining tests, rounded half up. The consequence is the point of having two variants: a single failed mandatory test zeroes `STRICT` outright, making certification on that variant impossible until the test is re-run under a new certification, while the very same test population can clear the bar under `PROVISIONAL`.

**The airline's decision.** Only a `PlatformAdmin` records a `decision` on a certification, optionally naming a `readiness_variant`. A `CERTIFY` decision advances `TESTING → CERTIFIED` and is doubly gated: no test of the certification may still be in `SCHEDULED` — an outstanding slot makes the decision premature and yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and the readiness score of the variant named by `readiness_variant` must be at or above 80, with anything below reported as `ERR_READINESS_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Since `STRICT` is forced to 0 while `mandatory_failed_count` is above 0, naming `STRICT` in that situation always fails this gate. On success `certified_variant` records which variant was used, the partner's `certification_status` becomes `CERTIFIED` with the `DistributionPartner`'s `version` increasing by 1, and every agreement of that partner-and-channel pair sitting in `SUSPENDED` returns to `SIGNED`.

A `SUSPEND` decision advances `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with no precondition, and it is the destructive direction. The partner's `certification_status` falls back to `IN_CERTIFICATION` and the partner's `version` increases by 1; every agreement of that partner-and-channel pair in `SIGNED` becomes `SUSPENDED`; and each such agreement's offers sitting in `CONSTRUCTED` or `PUBLISHED` become `WITHDRAWN`. A `REINSTATE` decision advances `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED`, restores the partner's `certification_status` to `CERTIFIED` with a `version` increase of 1, and returns every `SUSPENDED` agreement of the pair to `SIGNED` — without resurrecting the offers that were withdrawn.

A `REVOKE` decision drives the certification to the terminal `REVOKED` state and is legal from `TESTING`, from `CERTIFIED` and from `SUSPENDED`. All three edges cancel the certification's remaining scheduled tests: every test in `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED` and `reserved_test_minutes` becomes 0. All three also set the partner's `certification_status` to `UNCERTIFIED` and increase the `DistributionPartner`'s `version` by 1. What differs is the commercial fallout. Revoking from `TESTING` stops there — the partner was never certified, so there is no signed content standing on the certification. Revoking from `CERTIFIED` runs the full four-hop cascade: hop one revokes the certification and cancels its scheduled slots; hop two, caused by hop one, sets the partner's `certification_status` to `UNCERTIFIED` and bumps the partner's `version`; hop three, caused by hop two — an uncertified partner loses content access — sets every agreement of that partner-and-channel pair in `SIGNED` to `SUSPENDED`; and hop four, caused by hop three, sets every offer of that partner-and-channel pair sitting in `CONSTRUCTED` or `PUBLISHED` to `WITHDRAWN`. Revoking from `SUSPENDED` performs the partner downgrade but touches no agreement, because agreements already suspended simply stay `SUSPENDED`.

### 3.9. Distribution Cost per Booking, Two Ways

The question that started this project — what does a booking cost on each channel — has two defensible answers, and the service publishes both side by side rather than choosing. Both are recomputed on exactly the same occasions: when an order's payment is captured, as the fourth cascade hop of an applied refund, and on the change branch of an applied servicing request. Both read the same order population — the orders on this channel whose `payment_status` is `PAID`, which is precisely the population counted by the channel's `booking_count` — and the same clock-independent channel configuration. Call that count the paid-booking count.

The **`CHANNEL_AVERAGE`** variant is the accounting view: it spreads the fixed monthly platform fee across whatever bookings actually happened. Its result, stored in `cost_per_booking_flat_cents`, is 0 when the paid-booking count is 0, and otherwise the channel's `monthly_platform_fee_cents` divided by the paid-booking count, rounded half up to whole cents. This variant ignores `message_count`, `segment_count` and incentive accrual entirely, which is why a channel with a single booking carries the whole monthly fee on that one booking.

The **`ACTIVITY_BASED`** variant is the traced view used for channel-shift decisions, and it is built in two parts. First the channel's `activity_cost_cents` is assembled: for every order on the channel whose `payment_status` is `PAID`, the service adds the channel's `per_message_cost_cents` multiplied by that order's `message_count` plus the channel's `per_segment_fee_cents` multiplied by that order's `segment_count`; to that transaction total it adds the sum of `incentive_accrued_cents` over every agency agreement on this channel whose `status` is `SIGNED`. Agreements in any other status contribute nothing — a `SUSPENDED` or `SUPERSEDED` agreement's accrual is excluded outright. Then the per-booking figure, stored in `cost_per_booking_activity_cents`, is 0 when the paid-booking count is 0, and otherwise `activity_cost_cents` divided by the paid-booking count, rounded half up to whole cents. In both variants the rounding is applied only to that final division; the sums that feed it are exact integers.

This variant ignores `monthly_platform_fee_cents` entirely and charges instead per message, per segment, and for the incentive the airline has committed to on that channel — so the same set of bookings routinely produces a different per-booking figure under `ACTIVITY_BASED` than under `CHANNEL_AVERAGE`. Reading a channel returns both figures together with the underlying `activity_cost_cents` and `booking_count`, which is the report a `ComplianceAuditor` or a `DistributionManager` uses to compare a legacy distribution link against a direct or aggregator connection.
