# Hotel Housekeeping Labor & Workload API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Housekeeping is the largest variable-labor line in a hotel's operating statement and the department most tightly bound by local ordinance. Every morning a housekeeping director has to convert a forecast — how many rooms will be occupied tonight, how many guests arrive, how many depart — into a headcount, and then convert that headcount into a room-by-room allocation that no attendant can reasonably contest. The instrument the industry uses for that conversion is the **workload credit**: a normalised unit of cleaning effort that lets a departure clean of a suite, a stayover touch-up of a standard king, and a rotational deep clean all be compared, summed, and capped against the same ordinance ceiling.

This API is the system of record for that conversion and for everything downstream of it. It holds the property's ordinance regime and credit-minute standard, the room-type credit tables, the daily forecast and its published staffing plan, the per-attendant shift roster with its two independent state dimensions (a lifecycle dimension and an ordinance-compliance dimension), the individual room assignments and their credit valuations, the quality inspections that sample those cleans and trigger re-cleans when they fail, the linen par levels and laundry cycles that gate deep-clean work, the guest-facing opt-out and every-other-day service programmes with their incentive-point accruals, and the custody chain for property guests leave behind. Productivity is reported in minutes per room, in two deliberately divergent variants, and always subject to a quality guardrail that outranks the minutes comparison.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, **conservation**: credits allocated to rosters must always reconcile back to the forecast day, and linen sets must always reconcile back to the number of sets the property owns — the system recomputes these conserved totals on every mutation that could move them rather than trusting an incremental delta. Second, **segregation of duties**: nobody inspects their own work, and nobody approves the ordinance waiver they themselves requested. Both commitments are enforced server-side, deterministically, with named error codes, because they are the parts of the process a labour auditor will ask to see.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API surface. Where §3 describes a workflow, the rules below are still in force and are not restated at every call site.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Six roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `HousekeepingDirector`, `Supervisor`, `RoomAttendant`, `LaundryLead`, `FrontDeskAgent`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. An `X-User-Role` value that is not one of those six strings is also treated as `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` — an unrecognised role is an authentication failure, never a permission failure. Only once the role has been recognised does the system ask whether that role is permitted on the target operation; if it is not, the answer is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the six roles are backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Supervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Attendant`; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks compare that value against the relevant attendant foreign key on the resource. When `X-User-Role` is `RoomAttendant`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Attendant`, and ownership checks on any roster or assignment compare the roster's `attendant_id` to `X-User-ID`. For both of these roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an `Attendant` row yields `ERR_ATTENDANT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) on the operations where the identity must be resolved.

The other four roles are opaque operator identifiers. When `X-User-Role` is `HousekeepingDirector`, `LaundryLead`, `FrontDeskAgent`, or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `waiver_requested_by_user_id` or `waiver_decided_by_user_id` on a `ShiftRoster`, where the relevant transitions write it — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The division of labour behind those roles is worth stating in business terms. A `HousekeepingDirector` is property leadership: they configure the property, its room types, its rooms and its attendants, they publish and close forecast days, and they own the logical clock. A `Supervisor` is a working floor supervisor — an `Attendant` row whose `can_inspect` flag is true — who builds room assignments, records inspections, files ordinance workload waivers, decides lost-and-found claims and closes shifts. A `RoomAttendant` clocks in on their own roster, starts and completes their own rooms, and logs recovered guest property. A `LaundryLead` opens linen inventory lines and dispatches, receives and voids laundry cycles. A `FrontDeskAgent` maintains room occupancy posture, enrols guests in service programmes and files lost-and-found claims. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the only role that may decide an ordinance workload waiver, and is never the role that files one. All six roles may read every collection and every single-resource read endpoint.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets the number of items to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

All list endpoints in this API also accept the optional filter parameters named in their §4 description. Filters are combined with logical conjunction — a request naming two filters returns only rows matching both — and are applied before pagination, so `total_count` reflects the filtered set rather than the whole collection.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T06:15:00.000Z`).

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that resource. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation for this purpose: when a room assignment completion re-derives `ShiftRoster.assigned_credits`, the roster's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though no client sent a request naming the roster.

There is one deliberate exception. An `Inspection` is immutable once filed: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither appears in its response payload. Every other resource — `Property`, `RoomType`, `Room`, `Attendant`, `ForecastDay`, `ShiftRoster`, `RoomAssignment`, `LinenItem`, `LaundryCycle`, `LostFoundItem` and `ServiceEnrollment` — carries all four.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical UTC clock** that drives every timestamp default and every retention or turnaround comparison. Before any call to the clock-setting capability, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must never fall back on real wall-clock time. A `HousekeepingDirector` may advance or rewind the logical clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The logical clock supplies `LostFoundItem.logged_at` and `stored_at`, every retention-window comparison, the default for `RoomAssignment.started_at` and `completed_at` when the body omits them, and the default for `LaundryCycle.returned_at` when the body omits it.

Date-typed fields are distinct from timestamps. `ForecastDay.business_date`, `Room.last_serviced_business_date`, `Room.last_deep_cleaned_business_date` and `Attendant.seniority_date` are plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component.

### 2.5. Flat Addressing and the Order in Which References Are Resolved

The URL surface is deliberately flat. Every collection is mounted directly under `/housekeeping`, and no resource is addressed through a parent path segment. A room assignment is not reached through its roster, and a laundry cycle is not reached through its linen line. Relationships are therefore discovered in one of two ways: by reading a foreign-key field off a resource you already hold, or by calling the relevant collection with a filter — for example, listing room assignments filtered by `roster_id`, or listing rooms filtered by `property_id`. This is the only discovery channel for system-generated re-clean assignments, which no client ever creates directly; they are found by listing room assignments filtered by `supersedes_assignment_id`.

Where a path does carry an identifier, it is validated before the body is examined, and when more than one identifier appears in a path they are validated strictly left to right. The first identifier that does not resolve determines the 404: `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTENDANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORECAST_DAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROSTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINEN_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAUNDRY_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOST_FOUND_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate to the entity that failed to resolve.

Foreign keys inside a request body are resolved after the path and after the body's shape has been checked, and they are resolved in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields. The same 404 catalogue applies. Where an operation requires the acting user's own `X-User-ID` to resolve to an `Attendant` row — that is, for the `Supervisor` and `RoomAttendant` roles — the failure to resolve is reported at this same stage as `ERR_ATTENDANT_NOT_FOUND`.

Once every reference resolves, one further referential rule fires before any state, capacity or occupancy rule is consulted: **property scope coherence**. All entities named by a single request must belong to the same `Property`. A room whose `property_id` differs from the property of the roster's forecast day, a linen line belonging to another property, an attendant being rostered onto another property's forecast day, or a lost-and-found item whose room sits under a different property are all rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). This rule is enforced on exactly four write operations: room-assignment creation, shift-roster creation, lost-and-found item logging, and laundry-cycle dispatch.

### 2.6. The Workload Credit Standard, Precision and Rounding

A **workload credit** is the property's normalised unit of cleaning effort. `Property.credit_minute_value` declares how many standard minutes of work one credit represents; it defaults to 30 minutes and must be between 5 and 120 minutes inclusive. Every credit figure the system stores or compares is a decimal number carried to two decimal places, and every rounding in this specification uses the round-half-up rule: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Where the specification says a number is rounded to two decimals, that means round half up to two decimals — never truncation, never banker's rounding.

Individual room assignments are additionally **snapped to quarter credits**. The unit is 0.25 credits, no assignment may ever carry less than 0.25 credits, and no single room may ever carry more than 3.00 credits regardless of how expensive its room type is. §3.4 gives the full derivation.

Other precision commitments, stated once here and relied on throughout: square footage, minute counts, set counts, day counts, headcounts and money in cents are integers; `Inspection.score_pct` is carried to one decimal place; `LinenItem.coverage_ratio` is carried to four decimal places; `ForecastDay.credit_variance_pct` is a signed value carried to two decimal places. Duration derivations from two timestamps — `RoomAssignment.actual_minutes` and `LaundryCycle.turnaround_minutes` — are whole minutes truncated toward zero, so a window of 89 seconds is one minute, not two.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Roster and Linen Writes

Two write operations in this API grow a shared aggregate rather than transitioning a single resource through a state machine, and for those two the server cannot fall back on a state precondition to protect the caller from acting on a stale read. Both therefore require the client to declare the version of the parent resource it last observed.

A `POST /housekeeping/room-assignments` request must carry `expected_roster_version`, a non-optional integer field whose value is the `version` of the target `ShiftRoster` as the client last observed it. Adding a further room to a roster is entirely legitimate product behaviour, so there is no state that could reject the second create; but the ordinance ceilings are evaluated against the roster's credit and square-foot totals, and a caller holding a stale view would size its request against superseded totals. If `expected_roster_version` does not equal the roster's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROSTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). To let a client chain a sequence of assignment writes without re-reading the roster between each one, every response that mutates a roster's totals reports `roster_version_after` on the assignment payload: the roster's `version` immediately after the mutation that produced that response.

A `POST /housekeeping/laundry-cycles` request must carry `expected_linen_item_version`, a non-optional integer field whose value is the `version` of the target `LinenItem` as the client last observed it. Dispatching a second batch out of the same soiled queue is legitimate, and every room completion also debits the same linen ledger, so a caller's view of `soiled_queue`, `in_laundry` and the property's aggregate laundry capacity may have been superseded between read and write. If `expected_linen_item_version` does not equal the linen item's current `version`, the dispatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LINEN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every laundry-cycle response that mutates the linen ledger reports `linen_item_version_after`, the linen item's `version` immediately after that mutation, so a client can chain dispatches, returns and voids without re-reading.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Omitting `expected_roster_version` from an assignment create, or `expected_linen_item_version` from a laundry dispatch, is a request-shape failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than a stale-version rejection.

### 2.8. Ordinance Compliance as an Independent State Dimension

A `ShiftRoster` carries three enumerated state dimensions that advance on unrelated triggers and must not be conflated. The lifecycle dimension `status` moves `PLANNED` → `CLOCKED_IN` → `CLOSED`. The ordinance dimension `compliance_state` takes the values `COMPLIANT`, `AT_LIMIT`, `BREACH_BLOCKED` and `WAIVER_GRANTED`, and is recomputed by the server rather than commanded by a client. The waiver dimension `waiver_state` takes the values `NONE`, `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED` and `DENIED`. Clocking in advances `status` and leaves `compliance_state` and `waiver_state` untouched; filing a waiver advances `waiver_state` and leaves `status` and `compliance_state` untouched.

The ordinance dimension is derived from two ceilings and two running totals. `effective_credit_cap` is `Property.max_daily_credits` reduced by the restricted-duty factor when the rostered attendant's `restricted_duty` flag is true — that is, three quarters of the property ceiling for a restricted-duty attendant and the full ceiling otherwise — with the product rounded half up to two decimals. `effective_sqft_cap` is `Property.max_daily_square_feet` when the property's `ordinance_profile` is `SQFT_CAP_2200`, and is null under the `NONE` and `CREDIT_CAP_STRICT` profiles, in which case no square-foot ceiling applies at all. Against those ceilings the server holds `assigned_credits`, the sum of the credits of the roster's counted assignments rounded half up to two decimals, and `assigned_square_feet`, the integer sum of the square-foot snapshots of the same counted set.

The **counted set** is the roster's assignments whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `INSPECTED_PASS` or `RECLEAN_REQUIRED`. Assignments in `CANCELLED` or `DEFERRED` are excluded from both sums; every other status is counted, including `RECLEAN_REQUIRED` rows that have already been superseded by a re-clean, so a failed clean continues to consume ordinance headroom alongside its replacement.

The resolution rule is evaluated in this fixed order. If `waiver_state` is `GRANTED` and `assigned_credits` is less than or equal to `waiver_requested_credits` and the square-foot total does not exceed `effective_sqft_cap`, then `compliance_state` is `WAIVER_GRANTED`. Otherwise, if `assigned_credits` is strictly greater than `effective_credit_cap`, or a square-foot ceiling applies and `assigned_square_feet` is strictly greater than it, then `compliance_state` is `BREACH_BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if `assigned_credits` exactly equals `effective_credit_cap`, or a square-foot ceiling applies and `assigned_square_feet` exactly equals it, then `compliance_state` is `AT_LIMIT`. In every remaining case `compliance_state` is `COMPLIANT`. Both comparisons are exact: equality lands on `AT_LIMIT` and is a permitted, workable condition; only strictly-greater lands on `BREACH_BLOCKED`.

A roster in `BREACH_BLOCKED` accepts no further work of any kind. It cannot be clocked in, no assignment on it may be started, no assignment on it may be completed, and no further assignment may be created against it; all four refusals carry `ERR_ROSTER_WORKLOAD_BREACH` (HTTP 409). The condition is escapable in exactly two ways: a `ComplianceOfficer` grants a waiver that covers the assigned figure, or work is cancelled or deferred off the roster until the totals fall back below the ceilings. `WAIVER_GRANTED` re-opens both clock-in and assignment starts.

Because a client can never push a roster over its ceiling through ordinary assignment creation — the pre-create ordinance checks of §3.4 reject that request outright — there are only two paths into `BREACH_BLOCKED`. The first is a `HousekeepingDirector` setting an attendant's `restricted_duty` flag to true, which shrinks `effective_credit_cap` underneath work already allocated. The second is the insertion of a system-generated re-clean assignment after a failed inspection, which is exempt from the pre-create ordinance checks precisely so that a quality failure is never silently dropped. Both paths recompute `compliance_state` from `COMPLIANT` or from `AT_LIMIT`, and both may land it on `BREACH_BLOCKED` — but only while `waiver_state` is not `GRANTED`. The reverse edges are symmetrical: cancelling or deferring an over-cap assignment recomputes the totals and returns the roster from `BREACH_BLOCKED` to `COMPLIANT`, or from `AT_LIMIT` to `COMPLIANT`, whenever `assigned_credits` is strictly below `effective_credit_cap` and either no square-foot ceiling applies or `assigned_square_feet` is strictly below it.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and the Conserved Day Quantity

Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource, and the secondary effects are as much part of the contract as the primary one. The governing convention is that derived aggregates are **recomputed from their definition** on every event that could move them, never adjusted by an incremental delta. There are three aggregates that behave this way and that a reader should keep in mind everywhere below.

`ShiftRoster.assigned_credits` and `assigned_square_feet` are recomputed from the roster's counted set, and `compliance_state` is recomputed immediately afterwards, on every event that changes any assignment's `status` or `credits`, on any change to the rostered attendant's `restricted_duty` flag, and on any waiver decision.

`ForecastDay.allocated_credits_total` is the conserved day quantity: the sum of `ShiftRoster.assigned_credits` across every roster whose `forecast_day_id` names that day. It is recomputed whenever any of those rosters' `assigned_credits` changes. Because it is recomputed rather than incremented, cancelling an assignment restores it exactly to its pre-create value.

`LinenItem.par_required_sets`, `coverage_ratio` and `shortage_status` are recomputed on linen-item creation, on a manual soiling, on the linen debit that accompanies an assignment completion, on room-type creation, on room creation, and on every laundry-cycle transition.

Each such recomputation independently mutates the resource it lands on: `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` is incremented on the roster, the forecast day, the room, or the linen item, exactly as if a client had written to it. A single room-assignment completion can therefore advance four versions — the assignment's, the room's, the roster's and the linen item's.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Validated

The system evaluates a request through a fixed precedence ladder, and the first failing rung determines the response. The ordering matters because a request can violate several rules at once, and the benchmark for correctness is which error the caller sees, not merely that an error occurred.

The ladder runs as follows. **First**, header presence: both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must be present and non-empty, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. **Second**, role recognition: `X-User-Role` must name one of the six declared roles, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` again. **Third**, role permission: the recognised role must appear in the target operation's authorised list, or `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. **Fourth**, request-body shape on every write operation: every required input field present, every JSON type correct, every enum value inside its declared set, counts and minute totals non-negative, inspection point fields inside 0 through 10, and the `expected_roster_version` or `expected_linen_item_version` field present on the two operations that declare it — any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). **Fifth**, path identifier resolution, left to right, per §2.5. **Sixth**, body foreign-key resolution in the endpoint's declared field order, per §2.5. **Seventh**, ownership: when the acting role is `RoomAttendant`, the targeted roster — or the roster owning the targeted assignment — must have an `attendant_id` equal to `X-User-ID`, or `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). That ownership rule guards exactly three operations: starting an assignment, completing an assignment, and clocking in on a roster. **Eighth**, property scope coherence per §2.5. **Ninth**, the optimistic concurrency check of §2.7. **Tenth**, the target-state precondition on the entity being acted upon. **Eleventh**, cross-entity state gates. **Twelfth**, uniqueness constraints. **Thirteenth**, segregation of duties. **Fourteenth**, inspector qualification. **Fifteenth onward**, the domain safety, capacity, temporal and fiscal rules described in §3, in the order in which §3 introduces them for each operation.

Two consequences of that ordering deserve emphasis. Because scope coherence sits above every state and capacity rule, a cross-property request is rejected as a scope mismatch even when it would also have breached a credit ceiling. And because the optimistic concurrency check sits above the state preconditions, a stale `expected_roster_version` surfaces as `ERR_STALE_ROSTER_VERSION` even on a roster that is also in the wrong `status`.

Within the tenth rung — target-state preconditions, all HTTP 409 — the applicable rule depends on the operation, and each has its own code. A roster must be `CLOCKED_IN` before a room on it may be started or completed and before an inspection may be recorded against it, or `ERR_ROSTER_NOT_CLOCKED_IN`; any mutation targeting a roster already in `CLOSED` yields `ERR_ROSTER_ALREADY_CLOSED`. An assignment must be `ASSIGNED` to be started (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ASSIGNED`), `IN_PROGRESS` to be completed (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`), `COMPLETED` to be inspected (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_COMPLETED`), and `ASSIGNED` to be cancelled — a cancel request against an assignment in any other status, including `INSPECTED_PASS`, `CANCELLED` and `DEFERRED`, is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_TERMINAL`. A room already `IN_SERVICE` for another assignment cannot receive a second start, which is `ERR_ROOM_ALREADY_IN_SERVICE`. A lost-and-found item must be `LOGGED` to be stored (`ERR_ITEM_NOT_LOGGED`), `STORED` to be claimed or disposed of (`ERR_ITEM_NOT_STORED`), and `CLAIM_PENDING` for a claim decision (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDING`). A laundry cycle must be `QUEUED` to be returned or voided (`ERR_CYCLE_NOT_QUEUED`). A service enrolment must be `ACTIVE` to accrue or be voided (`ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`). A waiver must be in `REQUESTED` for a decision to be recorded — a decision filed while `waiver_state` is still `NONE` is `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_REQUESTED`, and a decision filed while `waiver_state` is already `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, or a second waiver request filed on a roster whose `waiver_state` has left `NONE`, is `ERR_WAIVER_ALREADY_DECIDED`. A forecast day must be `DRAFT` to be published (`ERR_FORECAST_NOT_DRAFT`), and any mutation targeting a forecast day already in `CLOSED` yields `ERR_FORECAST_ALREADY_CLOSED` — the `CLOSED` code pre-empts the publish and close status preconditions where a request would satisfy both, as §3.2 spells out.

Within the twelfth rung — uniqueness, all HTTP 409 — seven constraints apply. At most one `ForecastDay` may exist per property and business date (`ERR_FORECAST_DAY_DUPLICATE`); at most one `ShiftRoster` per forecast day and attendant (`ERR_ROSTER_DUPLICATE`); `room_number` is unique within a property (`ERR_ROOM_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`); a room type's `code` is unique within a property (`ERR_ROOM_TYPE_CODE_DUPLICATE`); an attendant's `employee_code` is unique within a property (`ERR_EMPLOYEE_CODE_DUPLICATE`); at most one `LinenItem` per property and `linen_type` (`ERR_LINEN_TYPE_DUPLICATE`); and at most one non-terminal `RoomAssignment` per roster, room and `service_type` combination (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_DUPLICATE`).

### 2.11. The Linen Ledger Identity

Linen is tracked as a closed ledger, and the identity below must hold after every single mutation of a `LinenItem`, without exception:

> the sets on the clean shelf, plus the sets held by the laundry, plus the sets waiting in the soiled queue, equals the sets the property owns.

In field terms, `on_hand_clean` plus `in_laundry` plus `soiled_queue` always equals `total_owned_sets`. Every movement in the system is therefore a transfer between the three buckets, never a creation or destruction of stock — with one carefully bounded exception. When a laundry batch comes back short, the missing sets are written off against `total_owned_sets` at the same time as they leave `in_laundry`, which reduces the owned figure and leaves the identity intact rather than breaking it.

No transfer may drive its source bucket below zero. Completing an assignment debits sets out of `on_hand_clean`, and dispatching a laundry batch moves sets out of `soiled_queue`; if either movement would make its source negative the whole operation is refused with `ERR_LINEN_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409). Those are the only two operations that carry this code.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Property, Its Inventory and Its People

Everything begins with a **Property**, registered by a `HousekeepingDirector`. The registration carries the hotel's `name` (between 1 and 120 characters) and, more consequentially, the entire parameter set that governs how labour will be measured and capped there. `ordinance_profile` selects the local regime and is one of three values: `NONE` means no per-attendant square-foot ceiling applies and an ordinance waiver may ask for up to a quarter more than the credit cap; `SQFT_CAP_2200` means the per-attendant square-foot ceiling drawn from `max_daily_square_feet` is enforced, again with waiver headroom of one quarter above the credit cap; and `CREDIT_CAP_STRICT` means no square-foot ceiling applies but a waiver may never ask for more than the credit cap itself. When the field is omitted, `ordinance_profile` defaults to `NONE`.

The remaining property parameters are the arithmetic backbone of the whole system, and each has a default and a bounded range. `credit_minute_value` is the standard minutes of work one workload credit represents — default 30, permitted range 5 to 120. `standard_shift_minutes` is the paid minutes in a standard shift before overtime accrues — default 480 (eight hours), permitted range 60 to 720. `pre_post_shift_minutes` is the non-cleaning time per shift consumed by line-up, cart set-up and close-out, which is removed before a shift's length is converted into assignable credits — default 45, permitted range 0 to 240. `min_daily_paid_minutes` is the ordinance minimum daily paid time guaranteed to any attendant who clocked in — default 240 (four hours), permitted range 0 to 480. `max_daily_credits` is the ordinance ceiling on workload credits assignable to one attendant per business date before any restricted-duty reduction — default 16.00 credits, permitted range 0.25 to 40.00 credits at two decimals. `max_daily_square_feet` is the ordinance ceiling on cleaned square feet per attendant per business date — default 2200, permitted range 100 to 20000 — and it bites only under the `SQFT_CAP_2200` profile. `laundry_sla_minutes` is the maximum turnaround the laundry contract allows before a returned batch is booked as a breach — default 240 (four hours), permitted range 30 to 1440. `laundry_capacity_sets` is the ceiling on linen sets that may be in flight across overlapping laundry cycles at that property — default 300, permitted range 1 to 10000. `reclean_threshold_score` is the inspection score at or above which an inspection passes — default 85.0, expressed on a 0.0 to 100.0 scale at one decimal. `inspection_sample_rate` is the baseline fraction of an attendant's completed cleans that must be inspected before their roster may be settled — default 0.20, that is one clean in five, permitted range 0.00 to 1.00 at two decimals. `probation_sample_rate` is the sampling fraction that replaces the baseline once an attendant is on quality probation — default 0.50, permitted range 0.00 to 1.00 at two decimals. And `rest_buffer_minutes` is the injury-prevention rest time that must separate two cleaning windows worked by the same attendant — default 10 minutes, permitted range 0 to 120.

Two property figures are computed rather than supplied. `total_rooms` is the count of rooms belonging to the property, counted regardless of their cleanliness or occupancy posture, and starts at 0. `outstanding_incentive_points` is the sum of `points_accrued` across every service enrolment whose room belongs to the property and whose `status` is still `ACTIVE`, and likewise starts at 0.

With the property registered, the director defines its **room types**. A room type belongs to a property, carries a `code` (1 to 24 uppercase characters, unique within the property — a collision is `ERR_ROOM_TYPE_CODE_DUPLICATE`), a `square_feet` figure for one room of that type between 50 and 5000, and the three credit columns that make the credit standard concrete: `checkout_credits` for a departure clean, `stayover_credits` for an occupied-room service, and `deep_clean_credits` for a rotational deep clean. Each of those three is between 0.25 and 10.00 credits at two decimals. `linen_sets_per_room` declares how many linen sets are needed to make up one room of the type — default 2, permitted range 0 to 40 — and it drives both the property's linen par level and the linen debit taken when a room of that type is finished. A computed `room_count` tracks how many rooms of the type exist, starting at 0. Defining a room type immediately recomputes `par_required_sets` and `shortage_status` on every linen line of the property, because the par level is a function of the inventory mix.

**Rooms** are added next. A room names its `property_id` and its `room_type_id` — the room type must belong to the same property — plus a guest-facing `room_number` of 1 to 12 characters that is unique within the property (`ERR_ROOM_NUMBER_DUPLICATE` on collision). The `floor` field defaults to 1 and exists for reporting only; it is never a decision input anywhere in this specification. Three enumerated postures may be seeded at creation and otherwise take their defaults. `service_program` is the guest service programme in force and is one of `DAILY` (serviced every day), `EVERY_OTHER_DAY` (stayover service permitted only when the room was last serviced two or more business dates ago) or `OPT_OUT` (stayover service declined by the guest), defaulting to `DAILY`. `occupancy_state` is the front-desk posture for the current business date and is one of `VACANT` (empty and ready), `STAYOVER` (occupied by a continuing guest), `DUE_OUT` (departure expected today) or `ARRIVAL` (held for an inbound guest and not serviceable), defaulting to `VACANT`. `cleanliness_status` is the housekeeping condition and is one of `CLEAN`, `DIRTY`, `IN_SERVICE`, `INSPECTED_PASS`, `RECLEAN_REQUIRED` or `OUT_OF_ORDER`; it starts at `DIRTY` on creation and is thereafter driven by the system rather than commanded, except through the explicit operator correction described below. Finally, `last_serviced_business_date` and `last_deep_cleaned_business_date` are calendar dates recording the most recent completed service and the most recent completed deep clean respectively; both may be seeded at creation and both default to null. Adding a room increments the room type's `room_count` and the property's `total_rooms`, and recomputes `par_required_sets`, `coverage_ratio` and `shortage_status` on every linen line of the property.

A `HousekeepingDirector` or a `FrontDeskAgent` may subsequently update a room's posture, supplying any subset of `occupancy_state`, `service_program`, `cleanliness_status`, `last_serviced_business_date` and `last_deep_cleaned_business_date`. The update increments the room's `version` by one. Two of those edits carry state-machine weight. Setting `occupancy_state` to `DUE_OUT` returns a serviced room to the head of the cleaning loop: a room whose condition was `CLEAN` moves `CLEAN → DIRTY`, and a room whose condition was `INSPECTED_PASS` moves `INSPECTED_PASS → DIRTY`, ready for the next business date. Setting `cleanliness_status` to `OUT_OF_ORDER` is the operator correction that takes a room off the floor entirely, moving `DIRTY → OUT_OF_ORDER`; it is refused with `ERR_ROOM_ALREADY_IN_SERVICE` when the room's condition is `IN_SERVICE`, because a room being cleaned right now cannot be pulled out from under the attendant. `OUT_OF_ORDER` is a terminal condition for the room-condition machine.

**Attendants** are hired last. An attendant belongs to a property and carries an `employee_code` of 1 to 24 characters, unique within the property (`ERR_EMPLOYEE_CODE_DUPLICATE`), a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `seniority_date` — the bargaining-unit seniority date, which is the pinned tie-break key whenever a balancing decision needs one — and an `hourly_rate_cents` base wage between 100 and 20000 cents per hour that prices the shift at settlement. Three flags shape what the attendant may do. `can_inspect` marks a credentialed working supervisor who may file inspections and defaults to false. `restricted_duty` is the injury-prevention flag and defaults to false; when it is true the attendant's effective credit ceiling becomes three quarters of the property ceiling and deep-clean work is barred outright. `status` is the employment posture — one of `ACTIVE`, `ON_LEAVE` or `TERMINATED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE` — and only an `ACTIVE` attendant may be rostered. Two computed fields track quality history: `quality_probation`, a boolean set true by the first failing inspection on any of the attendant's assignments and initialised false, and `lifetime_fail_count`, the number of failing inspections recorded against assignments on the attendant's rosters, initialised to 0.

A director may later revise an attendant's `restricted_duty`, `can_inspect` or `status`. This is not a bookkeeping edit: flipping `restricted_duty` re-derives `effective_credit_cap` and `compliance_state` on every one of that attendant's rosters whose `status` is not `CLOSED`, and re-derives `required_inspection_samples` on those same rosters. Turning restricted duty on therefore shrinks the ceiling underneath work already allocated, and is one of the two routes by which a roster can land in `BREACH_BLOCKED`.

### 3.2. Forecasting the Day and Sizing the Team

Each operating day at a property is represented by a **ForecastDay**, created by a `HousekeepingDirector` in `DRAFT`. It names its `property_id` and its `business_date`, which must be unique for that property — a second forecast day for the same date is `ERR_FORECAST_DAY_DUPLICATE` — and must not be earlier than the property's own creation date; an earlier date is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. Three counters describe the night ahead: `forecast_occupied_rooms` (rooms forecast to be occupied tonight, 0 to 5000), `forecast_arrivals` (rooms forecast to check in today, 0 to 5000, and never more than `forecast_occupied_rooms`), and `forecast_departures` (rooms forecast to check out today, 0 to 5000). A fourth, `planned_deep_clean_rooms`, records how many rooms the planner intends to deep clean today; it defaults to 0 and ranges to 5000.

From those counters the system derives the day's staffing demand immediately, at creation, writing `projected_required_credits`. The derivation reads the room-type inventory mix and nothing else — no room assignment ever influences it. It proceeds as follows. The number of **checkout rooms** is taken to be `forecast_departures`. The number of **stayover rooms** is `forecast_occupied_rooms` less `forecast_arrivals` — the arriving rooms are held for inbound guests and are not serviced as stayovers. The number of **deep-clean rooms** is `planned_deep_clean_rooms`. Each room type of the property then takes a share of all three figures equal to that type's `room_count` divided by the property's `total_rooms`; this share is an exact rational and is never rounded. For one room type, the raw credit demand is the checkout-room count multiplied by that share and then by the type's `checkout_credits`, plus the stayover-room count multiplied by the share and then by the type's `stayover_credits`, plus the deep-clean-room count multiplied by the share and then by the type's `deep_clean_credits`.

The evaluation and rounding order here is pinned and is not a matter of implementation taste. Iterate the property's room types in ascending order of `code`; round **each** room type's raw credit demand half up to two decimal places; and only then add the rounded per-type figures together, rounding that sum half up to two decimals one final time. The competing reading — adding the raw per-type figures first and rounding once at the end — produces a different answer whenever two types each carry a half-cent tail, and it is rejected. Should the property have no rooms at all, so that `total_rooms` is zero, `projected_required_credits` is 0.00 and the per-type loop contributes nothing; a room type with no rooms of its own likewise contributes nothing.

The same request also computes `required_attendants`, the headcount the projection implies. First, the credits one shift can absorb: take `standard_shift_minutes`, subtract `pre_post_shift_minutes` to remove the non-cleaning time, and divide the remainder by `credit_minute_value`. Under the defaults that is 480 minus 45, which is 435 minutes, divided by 30 minutes per credit — 14.5 credits per shift. Second, the assignable ceiling: the smaller of that per-shift figure and `max_daily_credits`, because a shift long enough to absorb more credits than the ordinance permits is still bounded by the ordinance. Third, the headcount itself: zero when `projected_required_credits` is zero, and otherwise the projected credits divided by the assignable ceiling and rounded **up** to the next whole attendant. The rounding up is applied to the exact quotient, never to a quotient that has already been rounded to two decimals.

A third figure, `allocated_credits_total`, is initialised to 0.00 at creation. It is the conserved day quantity of §2.9 — the sum of `assigned_credits` across every roster of the day — and it is recomputed, never incremented, whenever any of those rosters' credit totals move.

Publishing the day is what releases the staffing plan, and it is reserved to a `HousekeepingDirector`. The day must be in `DRAFT`, and the property must have at least one room type defined. Publishing re-runs the projection against the **current** room inventory, which matters: rooms and room types added after the day was drafted are included in the republished figure, so `projected_required_credits` and `required_attendants` can both move on publish. The day then advances `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, its `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes. Rosters may only be built against a `PUBLISHED` day.

Closing the day is the reconciliation, again reserved to a `HousekeepingDirector`, and requires the day to be `PUBLISHED`. It is blocked while any roster of the day is still `PLANNED` or `CLOCKED_IN`: every roster of the day must have reached `CLOSED` first, or the close is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_STILL_OPEN`. When it does proceed, the close writes `actual_reconciled_credits` — the second, chronological variant of the same demand quantity, and a deliberately different reading of the day. Where the projection derived demand from the inventory mix, the reconciliation derives it from the work actually performed: it sums the `credits` of every room assignment sitting on a roster of this day whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, `INSPECTED_PASS` or `RECLEAN_REQUIRED`, and rounds that sum half up to two decimals. Assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `CANCELLED` or `DEFERRED` are excluded, so deferred deep cleans and cancelled rooms never enter the reconciliation, and the same day therefore yields a different number from the projection whenever the mix assumption or the deferral pattern differed from plan.

The close finally writes `credit_variance_pct`, the signed percentage by which reality diverged from plan: `actual_reconciled_credits` minus `projected_required_credits`, divided by `projected_required_credits`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimals. When `projected_required_credits` is zero the variance is null rather than an error, because there is no base to divide by. Both `actual_reconciled_credits` and `credit_variance_pct` are null until the day reaches `CLOSED`; the day then advances `PUBLISHED → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and its `version` increments. Any subsequent mutation aimed at a `CLOSED` day is refused with `ERR_FORECAST_ALREADY_CLOSED`, and a publish attempt on a day that is not `DRAFT` is `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_DRAFT`. Where a request satisfies both readings at once, the `CLOSED` refusal wins: on each of the two operations that target the day itself, the `CLOSED` test is evaluated before the operation's own status precondition, so a publish aimed at a `CLOSED` day answers `ERR_FORECAST_ALREADY_CLOSED` rather than `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_DRAFT`, and a close aimed at a `CLOSED` day answers `ERR_FORECAST_ALREADY_CLOSED` rather than `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Both codes are HTTP 409.

### 3.3. Rostering the Shift, Deriving the Ordinance Ceilings, and Workload Waivers

A **ShiftRoster** is one attendant's day. It is built by a `HousekeepingDirector` or a `Supervisor` against a published forecast day, naming the `forecast_day_id`, the `attendant_id` and an optional `planned_start_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits that defaults to null and exists for reporting only; nothing in this specification reads it as a decision input. Two cross-entity gates guard the create: the forecast day's `status` must be `PUBLISHED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_PUBLISHED`, and the attendant's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ATTENDANT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Only one roster may exist per forecast day and attendant; a duplicate is `ERR_ROSTER_DUPLICATE`. The attendant must belong to the same property as the forecast day, which is the scope-coherence rule of §2.5 and fires before either state gate.

The create derives the ordinance ceilings that will govern the shift. `effective_credit_cap` and `effective_sqft_cap` are computed exactly as §2.8 describes, and `compliance_state` is set to `COMPLIANT` because the roster carries no work yet. `status` starts at `PLANNED`, `waiver_state` at `NONE`, `productivity_flag` at `PENDING`, `assigned_credits` at 0.00 and `assigned_square_feet` at 0. All the settlement figures — `worked_minutes`, `paid_minutes`, `overtime_minutes`, `minutes_per_room_gross`, `minutes_per_room_net`, `target_minutes_per_room`, `base_wage_cents`, `ordinance_premium_cents` and `total_labor_cost_cents` — are null until the shift is settled, while `break_minutes` starts at 0 and the counters `rooms_completed_gross`, `rooms_credited_net`, `required_inspection_samples` and `inspections_completed` all start at 0.

Clocking in is what makes the roster workable, and it advances the lifecycle dimension `PLANNED → CLOCKED_IN` without touching either the compliance or the waiver dimension. Either the `Supervisor` or the `RoomAttendant` whose roster it is may perform it, and a `RoomAttendant` may only clock in on their own roster — an `X-User-ID` that differs from the roster's `attendant_id` is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER`. Four conditions must hold: the roster must still be `PLANNED`; the parent forecast day must still be `PUBLISHED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the attendant's employment `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_ATTENDANT_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the roster's `compliance_state` must not be `BREACH_BLOCKED`, or `ERR_ROSTER_WORKLOAD_BREACH`. On success the roster's `version` increments. Assignments may be created against a roster while it is still `PLANNED`, but no assignment may be **started** until the roster is `CLOCKED_IN`.

When a roster's allocated work sits above its ordinance ceiling, the only lawful way forward is an **ordinance workload waiver**, and the process is deliberately split between two people. A `Supervisor` files the request, naming `waiver_requested_credits` — the credit ceiling the request asks for. The roster's `waiver_state` must still be `NONE`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_ALREADY_DECIDED`, and the roster's `status` must be either `PLANNED` or `CLOCKED_IN`. The requested figure is then tested against the ordinance headroom: it must not exceed `effective_credit_cap` multiplied by the property's headroom factor, which is 1.25 under the `NONE` and `SQFT_CAP_2200` profiles and exactly 1.00 — no headroom whatsoever — under `CREDIT_CAP_STRICT`. A request above that product is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_EXCEEDS_ORDINANCE_MAX` (HTTP 422). A successful filing stamps `waiver_requested_credits`, records `waiver_requested_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, advances `waiver_state` from `NONE` to `REQUESTED`, and increments the roster's `version`; `status` and `compliance_state` are both left untouched.

Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may decide the request, supplying a `decision` of either `GRANT` or `DENY`. The roster's `waiver_state` must be `REQUESTED` — a decision filed while it is still `NONE` is `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_REQUESTED`, and a decision filed after the waiver has already been decided is `ERR_WAIVER_ALREADY_DECIDED`. Segregation of duties is absolute here: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the roster's `waiver_requested_by_user_id`, and an operator deciding their own request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Either outcome stamps `waiver_decided_by_user_id` and increments the roster's `version`.

The two outcomes diverge in their effect on the compliance dimension, and both branches matter. A `GRANT` advances `waiver_state` from `REQUESTED` to `GRANTED` and re-runs the compliance derivation, which yields `WAIVER_GRANTED` when `assigned_credits` is at or below `waiver_requested_credits` — so a roster sitting in `BREACH_BLOCKED` moves `BREACH_BLOCKED → WAIVER_GRANTED` and both clock-in and assignment starts re-open. A `DENY` advances `waiver_state` from `REQUESTED` to `DENIED` and re-runs the same derivation while **ignoring** the waiver entirely, so a roster over its ceiling stays in `BREACH_BLOCKED` and remains frozen. Both `GRANTED` and `DENIED` are terminal for the waiver dimension: no second request may be filed on that roster.

### 3.4. Allocating Rooms: Credit Valuation and the Eligibility Gates

A **RoomAssignment** is a single piece of cleaning work handed to a rostered attendant. A `HousekeepingDirector` or a `Supervisor` creates one by naming the `roster_id`, the `room_id`, a `service_type`, and the `expected_roster_version` demanded by §2.7. The `service_type` is one of four values: `CHECKOUT` for a departure clean, `STAYOVER` for occupied-room service, `DEEP_CLEAN` for rotational deep cleaning, and `LINEN_REFRESH` for a linen-only touch on a room whose guest has opted out of service. At most one non-terminal assignment may exist for a given roster, room and service type together; a second is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_DUPLICATE`.

Once the request's references resolve, its scope coheres and its `expected_roster_version` matches, the assignment passes through eight domain gates that are evaluated in a strictly pinned order. The order is a policy statement: statutory ordinance limits come before contractual ones, both come before the injury-prevention freeze, and guest-facing programme rules come last.

1. **The square-footage ordinance.** Under `ordinance_profile` `SQFT_CAP_2200` only, the roster's `assigned_square_feet` including the incoming room must not exceed `effective_sqft_cap`. A breach is `ERR_ORDINANCE_SQFT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). This is evaluated first because the square-foot ceiling is statutory while the credit ceiling is contractual.
2. **The credit ceiling.** The roster's `assigned_credits` including the incoming room must not exceed `effective_credit_cap`, unless a `GRANTED` waiver already covers the resulting figure. A breach is `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Landing exactly on the cap is permitted and moves the roster's compliance dimension `COMPLIANT → AT_LIMIT`; only exceeding it is refused. Because of these first two gates, ordinary operator-driven creation can never leave a roster in `BREACH_BLOCKED`.
3. **The injury-prevention freeze.** A roster whose `compliance_state` is already `BREACH_BLOCKED` accepts no additional assignment at all, whatever the headroom arithmetic says, and the refusal is `ERR_ROSTER_WORKLOAD_BREACH`.
4. **Restricted-duty eligibility.** An attendant whose `restricted_duty` flag is true may not be given a `DEEP_CLEAN` room under any circumstances, regardless of remaining credit headroom, and the refusal is `ERR_RESTRICTED_DUTY_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).
5. **The linen shortage block.** A `DEEP_CLEAN` assignment may not be created while any linen line of the property has a `shortage_status` of `SHORTAGE` or `ESCALATED`; the refusal is `ERR_LINEN_SHORTAGE_BLOCK` (HTTP 409). `CHECKOUT`, `STAYOVER` and `LINEN_REFRESH` work is unaffected — only rotational deep cleaning is deferred until the linen supply recovers.
6. **Opt-out respected.** A room whose `service_program` is `OPT_OUT` accepts neither a `CHECKOUT` nor a `STAYOVER` assignment; the refusal is `ERR_SERVICE_OPTED_OUT` (HTTP 422). `DEEP_CLEAN` and `LINEN_REFRESH` remain permitted on an opted-out room because they are property-driven rather than guest-driven.
7. **Every-other-day cadence.** A room whose `service_program` is `EVERY_OTHER_DAY` accepts a `STAYOVER` assignment only when its `last_serviced_business_date` is null, or is at least two calendar days before the forecast day's `business_date`. A gap of exactly two days is permitted; a gap of one day is not. A premature request is `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 422).
8. **Occupancy coherence.** The `service_type` must match the room's `occupancy_state` under a pinned pairing: `CHECKOUT` requires `DUE_OUT`, `STAYOVER` requires `STAYOVER`, and `DEEP_CLEAN` requires `VACANT`. Any other pairing among those three service types — most obviously any of them against an `ARRIVAL` room, which is held for an inbound guest and is not serviceable — is rejected with `ERR_OCCUPANCY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). `LINEN_REFRESH` carries no occupancy requirement and is accepted whatever the room's `occupancy_state`.

Surviving all eight gates, the system values the work. The **base credit** comes from the room's type and the service type: `checkout_credits` for `CHECKOUT`, `stayover_credits` for `STAYOVER`, `deep_clean_credits` for `DEEP_CLEAN`, and a flat one quarter of a credit for `LINEN_REFRESH`, which is the same for every room type because a linen-only touch does not scale with the room. Two multiplicative factors then apply. The **programme factor** is one half when the room's `service_program` is `EVERY_OTHER_DAY` **and** the service type is `STAYOVER`, and is one otherwise — alternating-day service earns half credit for the stayover touch, while `CHECKOUT` and `DEEP_CLEAN` work is never discounted. The **re-clean factor** is 0.60 when the assignment's `reclean_generation` is 1 or more, and is one for an originally planned clean; a re-clean is worth three fifths of a first clean because the room has already been substantially serviced.

The raw value is the base multiplied by the programme factor and then by the re-clean factor. That raw value is then **snapped to quarter credits**: divide it by 0.25, round the resulting number of quarters half up to a whole number, and multiply back by 0.25. Finally two clamps apply, floor before ceiling: the result is raised to 0.25 credits if it fell below that, and then reduced to 3.00 credits if it rose above that. A room type whose deep-clean value is 3.50 credits therefore snaps to 3.50 and is clamped down to 3.00. A `CHECKOUT` base of 1.00 credit at re-clean generation 1 gives a raw value of 0.60; dividing by 0.25 gives 2.4 quarters, which rounds half up to 2 quarters, so the assignment carries 0.50 credits. A `CHECKOUT` base of 2.00 credits at generation 1 gives a raw 1.20; dividing by 0.25 gives 4.8 quarters, which rounds half up to 5 quarters, so the assignment carries 1.25 credits.

Creation also snapshots the room type's `square_feet` into the assignment's own `square_feet_snapshot`. That snapshot is what all subsequent ordinance arithmetic reads, so a later edit to the room type's square footage never retroactively moves an existing assignment's contribution. The assignment's `status` starts at `ASSIGNED`, its `reclean_generation` at 0, its `supersedes_assignment_id` at null, its `linen_sets_debited` at 0, and `started_at`, `completed_at` and `actual_minutes` all at null. The create then cascades: the roster's `assigned_credits`, `assigned_square_feet` and `compliance_state` are recomputed, the roster's `required_inspection_samples` is recomputed, the forecast day's `allocated_credits_total` is recomputed, the roster's `version` increments, and the resulting roster version is reported back on the assignment as `roster_version_after` so the caller can chain its next write without re-reading the roster.

Cancelling an assignment is the compensating move, available to a `HousekeepingDirector` or a `Supervisor` while the assignment is still `ASSIGNED`. It advances `ASSIGNED → CANCELLED`, which removes the assignment from the counted set of §2.8 and therefore releases both its credits and its square feet. The roster's `assigned_credits` and `assigned_square_feet` are recomputed and its `compliance_state` re-derived — which is how a roster escapes `BREACH_BLOCKED` by shedding work, moving `BREACH_BLOCKED → COMPLIANT` or `AT_LIMIT → COMPLIANT` once the totals fall strictly below both ceilings. The forecast day's `allocated_credits_total` is recomputed, restoring the conserved day quantity exactly to its pre-create value, and both the assignment's and the roster's `version` increment, with the new roster version reported as `roster_version_after`.

### 3.5. Cleaning the Room: Starting, Finishing, and the Window Rules

Work begins when the attendant starts the room, which advances the assignment `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS`. Either the `RoomAttendant` who owns the roster or a `Supervisor` may perform it; a `RoomAttendant` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the roster's `attendant_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER`. Five conditions must hold. The assignment must be in `ASSIGNED`, or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The parent roster must be `CLOCKED_IN`, or `ERR_ROSTER_NOT_CLOCKED_IN` — this is the cross-entity gate that stops work being recorded against a shift nobody has started. The roster's `compliance_state` must not be `BREACH_BLOCKED`, or `ERR_ROSTER_WORKLOAD_BREACH`. No other assignment on the same room may be `IN_PROGRESS`, or `ERR_ROOM_ALREADY_IN_SERVICE`. And the room's own condition must be one of `DIRTY`, `RECLEAN_REQUIRED`, `CLEAN` or `INSPECTED_PASS` — a room already `IN_SERVICE` cannot receive a second start.

The start stamps `started_at`, taking it from the request body when supplied and from the logical clock when the body omits it, sets the assignment to `IN_PROGRESS`, and drives the room's condition to `IN_SERVICE`. Both the assignment's and the room's `version` increment. For an originally planned clean this is the room-condition edge `DIRTY → IN_SERVICE`; for a system-generated re-clean — an assignment whose `reclean_generation` is 1 or more — it is the edge `RECLEAN_REQUIRED → IN_SERVICE`, which is what closes the service loop `RECLEAN_REQUIRED → IN_SERVICE → CLEAN` and makes the room-condition machine genuinely cyclic. A room may traverse that loop at most twice, because re-clean generations are capped at 2.

Finishing the room advances the assignment `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and is subject to the same ownership rule and the same actors. The assignment must be `IN_PROGRESS`, or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, and the roster must not be in `BREACH_BLOCKED`. Then three temporal rules apply, and they are the sharpest edges in the system.

The first is simple sanity: `completed_at` — supplied in the body, or defaulted from the logical clock — must be **strictly after** `started_at`, or the completion is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). The second is room-level exclusivity: the interval from `started_at` up to but not including `completed_at` must not overlap the corresponding interval of any other assignment on the **same room** whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, `INSPECTED_PASS` or `RECLEAN_REQUIRED`. These room intervals are half-open, so one attendant finishing a room at 10:30 and another starting the same room at exactly 10:30 is permitted — the windows touch but do not overlap. A genuine overlap is `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The third is the attendant rest buffer: the intervals of one attendant's own cleaning windows — that is, the windows of other assignments on the **same roster** whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, `INSPECTED_PASS` or `RECLEAN_REQUIRED` — are treated as **closed** intervals, and must additionally be separated from the new window by at least `Property.rest_buffer_minutes`. Expanding the new window by the rest buffer on both sides must leave it clear of every other window on the roster; if it touches one, the completion is refused with `ERR_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Back-to-back rooms with no gap therefore violate the rest buffer even though the room-level half-open rule would have allowed them, and under the default buffer of 10 minutes an attendant who finishes one room at 10:30 cannot start their next one before 10:40.

A successful completion does a great deal at once. It stamps `completed_at` and computes `actual_minutes` as the whole minutes from `started_at` to `completed_at`, truncated toward zero. It sets the assignment to `COMPLETED` and drives the room's condition `IN_SERVICE → CLEAN`, stamping the room's `last_serviced_business_date` from the forecast day's `business_date`; when the service type was `DEEP_CLEAN` it additionally stamps the room's `last_deep_cleaned_business_date` from the same date.

It then takes the **linen debit**, and this cascade is conditional in a way that matters. When a linen line with `linen_type` `SHEET_SET` exists for the room's property **and** the assignment's `service_type` is anything other than `STAYOVER`, the number of sets given by the room type's `linen_sets_per_room` is moved out of that linen line's `on_hand_clean` and into its `soiled_queue`, and the moved quantity is recorded on the assignment as `linen_sets_debited`. Should that movement drive `on_hand_clean` below zero, the entire completion is refused with `ERR_LINEN_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`. When no `SHEET_SET` line exists for the property, or when the service type **is** `STAYOVER`, the debit is skipped entirely and `linen_sets_debited` stays 0 — a stayover touch-up reuses the guest's linen and a property with no sheet inventory line has nothing to debit. Where a debit did occur, the linen line's `coverage_ratio` and `shortage_status` are recomputed and its `version` increments.

Finally, the completion re-derives the roster's productivity inputs `rooms_completed_gross` and `rooms_credited_net`, recomputes `required_inspection_samples`, increments the roster's `version`, and reports the resulting roster version on the assignment as `roster_version_after`. A single completion can therefore advance four versions — the assignment's, the room's, the roster's and, when a debit occurred, the linen line's.

The two productivity counters are defined over the roster's **performed** assignments, meaning those whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, `INSPECTED_PASS` or `RECLEAN_REQUIRED`. `rooms_completed_gross` counts them one for one, so each physical clean is counted and a re-cleaned room is counted twice. `rooms_credited_net` counts the distinct rooms among them, so a re-cleaned room is counted once. That divergence is deliberate and is the whole point of §3.7's two minutes-per-room readings.

### 3.6. Inspection Sampling, Quality Scoring, and the Re-Clean Loop

Completed rooms are sampled rather than universally inspected, and the sampling obligation is a hard precondition of settlement rather than an afterthought. The roster's `required_inspection_samples` is derived from the count of its performed assignments — those in `COMPLETED`, `INSPECTED_PASS` or `RECLEAN_REQUIRED` — and from a sampling rate that depends on the attendant's quality history. The rate is `Property.probation_sample_rate` when the attendant's `quality_probation` flag is true, and `Property.inspection_sample_rate` otherwise. When the performed count is zero the obligation is zero. Otherwise multiply the performed count by the rate, round **up** to a whole number, raise the result to at least one — any completed work at all owes at least one inspection — and then cap it at the performed count, since one cannot inspect more rooms than were cleaned. The rounding up is applied to the exact product, so five completed rooms at the baseline rate of 0.20 owe exactly one sample, not two. `inspections_completed` counts the inspections filed against assignments of the roster, and the outstanding obligation is the required figure less the completed figure, floored at zero.

A read-only capability reports that plan and names the rooms to sample. Any role may call it against a roster, and it returns `roster_id`, `completed_count`, `sample_rate`, `required_samples`, `inspections_completed`, `remaining_samples` and `selected_assignment_ids`. The selection is fully pinned and contains no random input. The eligible pool is the roster's assignments whose `status` is exactly `COMPLETED` — an already-inspected assignment has left that status and drops out of the pool by construction, while `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `CANCELLED` and `DEFERRED` rows never enter it. The pool is ranked by `credits` descending, then by the room's `room_number` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending; the `created_at` comparison is the final tie-break and the `id` field is never consulted. `selected_assignment_ids` is the first `remaining_samples` identifiers of that ranked list. When the pool is empty the list comes back empty with no error, and `required_samples` is still reported so the caller can see the outstanding obligation.

Filing an inspection is exclusive to a `Supervisor` and names the `assignment_id` together with five item scores, each an integer from 0 to 10: `bathroom_points`, `linen_points`, `dust_points`, `amenity_points` and `floor_points`. The `inspector_attendant_id` is taken from the filing supervisor's `X-User-ID` and is never accepted from the body. Three access rules guard the filing. The assignment must be in `COMPLETED`, or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_COMPLETED`, and its roster must be `CLOCKED_IN`, or `ERR_ROSTER_NOT_CLOCKED_IN`. Segregation of duties then applies: the inspecting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `attendant_id` of the roster that owns the inspected assignment, because nobody inspects their own work, and a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Last, inspector qualification: the `Attendant` identified by `X-User-ID` must have `can_inspect` true and employment `status` `ACTIVE`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_NOT_INSPECTION_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 403).

The score is a weighted roll-up of the five items, consulting nothing but the numbers on this one request — no history is read. Weight the bathroom score at 0.30, the linen score at 0.25, the dusting score at 0.20, the amenity score at 0.15 and the floor score at 0.10, and add the five products; the result lands somewhere between 0.00 and 10.00. Multiply that by ten and round half up to one decimal place to obtain `score_pct`, a figure on a 0 to 100 scale. Separately, `critical_fail` is true when either the bathroom score or the linen score is 6 or lower — those two items are the ones a guest notices first, and a poor mark on either condemns the clean whatever the total says. The `result` is then `FAIL` when `critical_fail` is true, or when `score_pct` is strictly below the property's `reclean_threshold_score`, and `PASS` otherwise; the pass comparison is inclusive, so a score exactly equal to the threshold passes. Under the default threshold of 85.0, item scores of 9, 8, 8, 8 and 10 give a score of 85.0 and pass at the exact boundary; the same set with 9 instead of 10 on the floor item gives 84.0 and fails; and 6, 10, 10, 10, 10 gives 88.0, comfortably above the threshold, yet fails anyway on the critical-item rule.

An `Inspection` records `score_pct`, `critical_fail`, `result` and `generated_reclean_assignment_id`, and, being immutable, carries no `updated_at` and no `version`. Every filing increments the roster's `inspections_completed`.

The two outcomes fork sharply, and both branches are specified in full.

**On `PASS`**, the inspected assignment advances `COMPLETED → INSPECTED_PASS` and the room's condition advances `CLEAN → INSPECTED_PASS`. The roster's `inspections_completed` is recomputed and the affected versions increment. That is all: a pass generates no re-clean, changes no credit total, and touches neither `quality_probation` nor `lifetime_fail_count`. `generated_reclean_assignment_id` stays null.

**On `FAIL`**, a chain of five effects fires. First, the inspected assignment advances `COMPLETED → RECLEAN_REQUIRED`. Second, and only while that assignment's `reclean_generation` is below 2, the system inserts a brand-new room assignment on the same roster and the same room, with `supersedes_assignment_id` pointing back at the failed assignment, with `reclean_generation` one higher than the failed assignment's, with `status` `ASSIGNED`, and with `credits` valued by the ordinary credit derivation of §3.4 with the re-clean factor applied; the room's condition advances `CLEAN → RECLEAN_REQUIRED`. The new assignment's `id` is recorded on the inspection as `generated_reclean_assignment_id`. Third — the alternative to that second step — when the failed assignment's `reclean_generation` is already 2, **no** re-clean row is inserted, `generated_reclean_assignment_id` stays null, and the room's condition advances `RECLEAN_REQUIRED → OUT_OF_ORDER` instead: two failed re-cleans mean the room comes off the floor rather than being cleaned a fourth time. Fourth, where a re-clean row was inserted, the roster's `assigned_credits` and `assigned_square_feet` both grow — the superseded `RECLEAN_REQUIRED` row stays in the counted set alongside its replacement — and `compliance_state` is re-derived. The system-generated re-clean is deliberately **exempt** from the pre-create ordinance gates of §3.4, so this is one of only two routes by which `compliance_state` may land on `BREACH_BLOCKED`; a quality failure is never silently dropped merely because the roster is full. Fifth, because the roster's `assigned_credits` changed, the forecast day's `allocated_credits_total` is recomputed.

Branching off the first of those effects, and independent of whether a re-clean row was inserted, the failure also marks the attendant: `quality_probation` is set true and `lifetime_fail_count` increments by one. Because the sampling rate is keyed on `quality_probation`, that single flag raises `required_inspection_samples` on the roster immediately — under the defaults, from one clean in five to one in two — and on every later roster of that attendant. The `lifetime_fail_count` figure is the running total of failing inspections whose assignment sat on a roster of that attendant.

### 3.7. Closing the Shift: Settlement, Productivity and Labour Cost

Settling a shift is the single densest operation in the system. A `HousekeepingDirector` or a `Supervisor` closes a roster, supplying `worked_minutes` — the clock minutes actually reported — and `break_minutes`, the unproductive break time inside them. The roster must be `CLOCKED_IN`; a close aimed at a roster already `CLOSED` is `ERR_ROSTER_ALREADY_CLOSED`. Four further conditions are then checked in order. No assignment on the roster may still be `IN_PROGRESS`, or the close is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409) — a room being cleaned right now cannot be settled around. The sampling obligation must be discharged: `inspections_completed` must be at least `required_inspection_samples`, or the close is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_SAMPLE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). `worked_minutes` must lie between 0 and 720 inclusive at both ends, or `ERR_WORKED_MINUTES_IMPLAUSIBLE` (HTTP 422) — twelve hours is the plausibility ceiling for a single housekeeping shift. And `break_minutes` must not exceed `worked_minutes`, or `ERR_BREAK_EXCEEDS_WORKED` (HTTP 422), so that productive time can never go negative.

The close then runs a cascade of three hops before it prices anything. **Hop one**: every assignment on the roster still sitting in `ASSIGNED` advances `ASSIGNED → DEFERRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Rooms that were planned but never started are pushed off the day rather than left dangling. **Hop two**: because those rows have left the counted set of §2.8, `assigned_credits` and `assigned_square_feet` are recomputed and `compliance_state` is re-derived — a roster that ended the day in `AT_LIMIT` or `BREACH_BLOCKED` may therefore settle as `COMPLIANT`. **Hop three**: because `assigned_credits` changed, the parent forecast day's `allocated_credits_total` is recomputed.

Next comes **wage settlement**, and its steps must be evaluated strictly in the numbered order below. The guarantee gap looks circular because it is defined from the minimum it then helps satisfy, so the order is not negotiable. Step one: take `worked_minutes` from the request body. Step two: the guarantee gap is the property's `min_daily_paid_minutes` less `worked_minutes`, floored at zero — an attendant who worked longer than the daily minimum has no gap. Step three: `paid_minutes` is `worked_minutes` plus that gap, which is how the ordinance minimum daily guarantee is honoured; under the default guarantee of 240 minutes, an attendant who clocked only 150 minutes is still paid 240. Step four: `overtime_minutes` is `paid_minutes` less the property's `standard_shift_minutes`, floored at zero. Step five: productive minutes are `worked_minutes` less `break_minutes` — and this is derived from `worked_minutes`, **never** from `paid_minutes`. The competing reading, subtracting breaks from paid minutes, would inflate the productivity of any short shift that had been lifted by the daily minimum, and it is rejected. All four of these are whole minutes.

**Productivity** is then measured, in minutes per room, in two deliberately divergent variants over the same productive minutes. The gross reading, `minutes_per_room_gross`, divides productive minutes by `rooms_completed_gross` — the number of physical cleans — and is the operational figure used for labour standards. Because a re-cleaned room is counted twice there, a shift with re-cleans looks better on this reading than it really was. The net reading, `minutes_per_room_net`, divides the same productive minutes by `rooms_credited_net` — the number of distinct rooms — and is the payroll figure that drives the productivity flag. Because a re-cleaned room is counted once there, the same minutes yield a strictly larger figure than the gross reading whenever any re-clean exists. Both are rounded half up to two decimals, and each is null when its own divisor is zero.

Alongside them, `target_minutes_per_room` states the standard the allocation implied: multiply the roster's `assigned_credits` by the property's `credit_minute_value` to convert credits into standard minutes, divide by `rooms_credited_net`, and round half up to two decimals. It is null when `rooms_credited_net` is zero.

The comparison of net against target yields `productivity_flag`, and the quality guardrail **outranks** the minutes comparison — this ordering is the whole reason the flag exists. Evaluate in this order. If any assignment on the roster whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` has a `reclean_generation` of 1 or more, the flag is `QUALITY_HOLD`, whatever the minutes say: a shift that produced a re-clean is not celebrated for being fast. Otherwise, if `rooms_credited_net` is zero, the flag stays `PENDING`. Otherwise, if `minutes_per_room_net` is less than or equal to `target_minutes_per_room` the flag is `ON_TARGET` — the comparison is inclusive at equality — and if it exceeds the target the flag is `OVER_TARGET`.

**Labour cost** is priced last, in whole cents, and its tier selection carries the same kind of pinned ordering as the settlement steps. `base_wage_cents` is `paid_minutes` multiplied by the attendant's `hourly_rate_cents` and divided by 60, rounded half up to a whole cent. The ordinance premium is a percentage of that base wage, and the percentage is chosen by a tier — but the tier is keyed on `base_wage_cents` and **not** on `total_labor_cost_cents`. The competing reading, keying the tier on the total that already contains the premium, is rejected: it would lift a base wage of 29800 cents into the higher band and produce 31886 cents instead of the correct 30992.

The premium applies only when the shift earned it: either `overtime_minutes` is greater than zero, or the roster's `compliance_state` as recomputed by this very close is `WAIVER_GRANTED`. Overtime and waived overwork are the two conditions the ordinance premium exists to compensate. When the premium does not apply, `ordinance_premium_cents` is 0. When it does, the percentage is zero for a base wage strictly below 20000 cents, four percent for a base wage from 20000 cents up to but not including 30000 cents, and seven percent at or above 30000 cents; multiply the base wage by that percentage and round half up to a whole cent. `total_labor_cost_cents` is simply `base_wage_cents` plus `ordinance_premium_cents`. The tier boundaries themselves are exact integer comparisons.

With all of that written, the roster stamps `worked_minutes` and `break_minutes`, advances `CLOCKED_IN → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and increments its `version`. Only once every roster of a forecast day has reached `CLOSED` may the day itself be reconciled and closed.

### 3.8. Linen Par Levels, Laundry Turnaround and Shortage Escalation

Linen inventory is held as a **LinenItem**: one line per property and linen class, opened by a `HousekeepingDirector` or a `LaundryLead`. The line names its `property_id` and its `linen_type`, one of `SHEET_SET`, `TOWEL_SET` or `DUVET_COVER`, unique within the property (`ERR_LINEN_TYPE_DUPLICATE` on a second line of the same class). Only `SHEET_SET` stock is debited by room completions and only `SHEET_SET` shortage gates deep-clean creation; the other two classes are tracked but do not drive workload decisions. `total_owned_sets` declares how many sets the property owns, from 0 to 100000, and it is the conserved quantity of the ledger identity in §2.11. An optional `initial_soiled_sets` seeds the line with stock already in the soiled queue and defaults to 0; creation therefore sets `on_hand_clean` to `total_owned_sets` less `initial_soiled_sets`, `soiled_queue` to `initial_soiled_sets`, and `in_laundry` to 0, so the identity holds from the very first moment.

The line's health is expressed by three derived figures. `par_required_sets` is the property's linen requirement: for each room type of the property, multiply that type's `room_count` by its `linen_sets_per_room`, and add the products across all room types regardless of whether a given type currently has any rooms. `coverage_ratio` is `on_hand_clean` divided by `par_required_sets`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is null when the par requirement is zero. `shortage_status` is then a four-rung ladder read off that ratio, with each comparison inclusive at its floor: a ratio at or above 1.0000 is `OK`; below that but at or above 0.8500 is `WATCH`; below that but at or above 0.6000 is `SHORTAGE`; and anything below 0.6000 is `ESCALATED`. A null ratio — a property with no linen requirement at all — reports `OK`.

One condition overrides the whole ladder. While **any** laundry cycle of this linen line sits in `LATE_ESCALATED`, `shortage_status` is forced to `ESCALATED` regardless of what `coverage_ratio` says. The override is sticky: a full clean shelf does not clear it while a late cycle exists, because the operational problem is turnaround reliability rather than raw stock. Every laundry cycle of the line is consulted for this purpose regardless of age.

Outside a room completion, a `Supervisor` or a `LaundryLead` may soil stock directly — after a guest complaint strip-down, for instance — by naming the number of `sets` to move. The movement takes sets out of `on_hand_clean` and into `soiled_queue`, preserving the ledger identity exactly, re-derives `coverage_ratio` and `shortage_status`, and increments the line's `version`.

Dispatching soiled linen to the plant creates a **LaundryCycle**, and this is exclusive to a `LaundryLead`. The dispatch names the `linen_item_id`, the `batch_sets` being sent (1 to 100000), a `dispatched_at` hand-off instant, an `expected_return_at` contracted return instant, and the `expected_linen_item_version` demanded by §2.7. Both instants are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits and both are required; `expected_return_at` must be **strictly after** `dispatched_at`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

Before the batch is accepted, the plant's aggregate capacity is checked — and this is a collective rule, not a per-batch one. Consider every laundry cycle of the same property whose `status` is `QUEUED` or `LATE_ESCALATED` and whose occupation window overlaps the new cycle's window. Occupation windows run from `dispatched_at` up to but not including `expected_return_at`, so two windows that merely touch at an instant do not overlap. Add the `batch_sets` of every such overlapping cycle to the new `batch_sets`; if that sum exceeds the property's `laundry_capacity_sets`, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_LAUNDRY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Each individual batch may be perfectly acceptable on its own; only the collective overlap breaches. A dispatch that would drive `soiled_queue` below zero is separately refused with `ERR_LINEN_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`.

An accepted dispatch moves `batch_sets` out of `soiled_queue` and into `in_laundry`, sets the cycle's `status` to `QUEUED`, leaves `returned_at`, `returned_sets`, `turnaround_minutes` null and `sla_breach` false, re-derives the line's `coverage_ratio` and `shortage_status`, increments the line's `version`, and reports that version back on the cycle as `linen_item_version_after`.

Receiving the batch is where the SLA bites. A `LaundryLead` returns a `QUEUED` cycle — a return aimed at any other status is `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_QUEUED` — optionally supplying `returned_at`, which defaults to the logical clock, and `returned_sets`, which defaults to `batch_sets` and may not exceed it. The `returned_at` instant must be at or after `dispatched_at`, or `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. `turnaround_minutes` is then the whole minutes from `dispatched_at` to `returned_at`, truncated toward zero, and `sla_breach` is true when that figure is **strictly greater** than the property's `laundry_sla_minutes`.

Both return branches move the linen: `returned_sets` go from `in_laundry` back into `on_hand_clean`, and any shortfall — `batch_sets` less `returned_sets` — is written off against both `in_laundry` and `total_owned_sets`, which keeps the ledger identity of §2.11 intact rather than breaking it. Both branches re-derive `coverage_ratio` and `shortage_status` and increment both the cycle's and the line's `version`, reporting the new line version as `linen_item_version_after`.

Where the branches diverge is everything downstream. A cycle inside the SLA sets `sla_breach` false and advances `QUEUED → RETURNED`, and that is the end of it. A cycle beyond the SLA sets `sla_breach` true and advances `QUEUED → LATE_ESCALATED`, which sets off a five-hop cascade. Hop one computes the turnaround, sets the breach flag and moves the cycle to `LATE_ESCALATED`. Hop two returns the sets to `on_hand_clean` and, because a late cycle now exists on this line, forces the line's `shortage_status` to `ESCALATED` regardless of `coverage_ratio`. Hop three, because the line is now `ESCALATED`, sweeps deep cleans off the floor: every room assignment whose `service_type` is `DEEP_CLEAN` and whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, sitting on a roster whose forecast day belongs to the same property and is still `PUBLISHED`, advances `ASSIGNED → DEFERRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Hop four, because those assignments have left the counted set, recomputes `assigned_credits`, `assigned_square_feet` and `compliance_state` on every affected roster. Hop five, because those rosters' credit totals moved, recomputes `allocated_credits_total` on every affected forecast day. A late laundry batch therefore reaches all the way through to the day's conserved credit total, which is precisely the reconciliation gap §3.2's close is designed to surface.

A dispatched batch that never actually left the property may instead be voided by a `LaundryLead` while it is still `QUEUED`. The void advances `QUEUED → VOIDED` and moves `batch_sets` out of `in_laundry` and back into `soiled_queue` — the exact compensation of the dispatch, leaving the ledger where it began. The line's `coverage_ratio` and `shortage_status` are re-derived and both versions increment, with the new line version reported as `linen_item_version_after`. `RETURNED`, `LATE_ESCALATED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal for a cycle.

### 3.9. Guest-Facing Programmes and the Custody of Guest Property

Guests who decline housekeeping are compensated with loyalty points, and the arrangement is recorded as a **ServiceEnrollment**. A `FrontDeskAgent` creates one against a `room_id`, naming the `reservation_code` the incentive accrues to (1 to 32 characters), the `program`, and the `nights` of the stay the enrolment covers (1 to 60). The `program` is one of two values: `OPT_OUT`, where the guest declines stayover service outright, and `EVERY_OTHER_DAY`, where the guest accepts service on alternating business dates. A room may not hold two `ACTIVE` enrolments at once; a second is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ACTIVE_EXISTS` (HTTP 409).

Creating the enrolment rewrites the room: the room's `service_program` is set to `OPT_OUT` or `EVERY_OTHER_DAY` to match the programme chosen, which is exactly what activates the opt-out and cadence gates of §3.4. The enrolment's `points_per_skip` is derived from the programme — 500 points per skipped service under `OPT_OUT`, and 250 under `EVERY_OTHER_DAY`, since an alternating-day guest is only forgoing half as much service. `status` starts `ACTIVE`, with `skips_accrued` and `points_accrued` both 0.

Each skipped service is recorded by a `Supervisor` or a `FrontDeskAgent` as an accrual. The enrolment must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The accrual is also bounded by a **skip budget**, which is not the same as the night count for both programmes: the budget equals `nights` under `OPT_OUT`, but equals `nights` divided by two and rounded **up** to a whole number under `EVERY_OTHER_DAY` — an every-other-day guest cannot, by definition, skip every night of their stay. Once `skips_accrued` has reached that budget, a further accrual is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_SKIPS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 422).

A permitted accrual increments `skips_accrued` by one and recomputes `points_accrued` as `skips_accrued` multiplied by `points_per_skip`; the property's `outstanding_incentive_points` is then recomputed across its `ACTIVE` enrolments, and the enrolment's `version` increments. The enrolment advances `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` on the accrual that makes `skips_accrued` equal `nights`, and otherwise remains `ACTIVE` — an accrual that leaves the count short is a self-edge on `ACTIVE` with every other effect unchanged. The two programmes therefore end differently, and both endings are intended. An `OPT_OUT` enrolment reaches `COMPLETED` on its last budgeted accrual, because its budget and its night count coincide. An `EVERY_OTHER_DAY` enrolment exhausts its budget while still `ACTIVE`, because its budget is only half its night count, and its next accrual is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_SKIPS_EXHAUSTED`. A five-night every-other-day stay, for instance, has a budget of three skips and never reaches `COMPLETED`.

A `FrontDeskAgent` may void an `ACTIVE` enrolment, which reverses it completely. The void advances `ACTIVE → VOIDED`, zeroes both `skips_accrued` and `points_accrued`, restores the room's `service_program` to `DAILY` — the exact compensation of the creation side effect, so the room becomes eligible for ordinary checkout and stayover work again — and recomputes the property's `outstanding_incentive_points` back to its pre-enrolment value. Both the enrolment's and the room's `version` increment. `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED` are both terminal.

Property left behind by guests is tracked as a **LostFoundItem**, logged by a `Supervisor` or a `RoomAttendant`. The log names the `property_id`, the `room_id` the item was recovered from, the `found_by_attendant_id` of the attendant who recovered it, a free-text `description` of 1 to 240 characters used later as description-match claim proof, and a `category`. The category is the retention class and is one of `HIGH_VALUE`, `ID_DOCUMENT`, `STANDARD` or `PERISHABLE`. The item's `logged_at` is stamped from the logical clock, its `status` starts `LOGGED`, and `claim_attempt_count` starts at 0.

Binning the item is what starts the retention clock. A `Supervisor` or a `FrontDeskAgent` stores a `LOGGED` item — any other status is `ERR_ITEM_NOT_LOGGED` — naming the secure `storage_bin` label. The store stamps `stored_at` from the logical clock, advances `LOGGED → STORED`, and computes the retention window from the category alone: `retention_days` is 90 for `HIGH_VALUE`, 60 for `ID_DOCUMENT`, 30 for `STANDARD` and 0 for `PERISHABLE`, and `retention_expires_at` is `stored_at` advanced by that many whole days. The item's `version` increments.

The retention window is half-open, and the boundary instant belongs to the disposal side. A claim is accepted while the logical clock is **strictly before** `retention_expires_at`; at or after that instant a claim is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). Disposal is accepted while the logical clock is **at or after** `retention_expires_at`; before it, disposal is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). A consequence worth stating plainly: a `PERISHABLE` item has a retention window of zero days, so `retention_expires_at` equals `stored_at`, its claim window is empty, and disposal is permitted the instant it is stored.

A `FrontDeskAgent` files a claim against a `STORED` item — any other status is `ERR_ITEM_NOT_STORED` — supplying the `claimant_name`, a `claimant_contact` string, and the `proof_kind` offered, which is one of `PHOTO_ID`, `DESCRIPTION_MATCH` or `RECEIPT`. Proof sufficiency is graded by category: a `HIGH_VALUE` or `ID_DOCUMENT` item requires either `PHOTO_ID` or `RECEIPT`, and `DESCRIPTION_MATCH` alone is insufficient for those two categories, which is refused with `ERR_PROOF_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). A `STANDARD` or `PERISHABLE` item accepts any of the three proof kinds. A valid claim stamps `claimant_name`, `claimant_contact` and `proof_kind`, increments `claim_attempt_count` by one, advances `STORED → CLAIM_PENDING`, and increments `version`.

A `Supervisor` decides the pending claim, and the item must be in `CLAIM_PENDING` or the decision is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDING`. The `decision` field is `APPROVE` or `DENY`. An approval must name the `disposition` explicitly, either `RELEASED` for a hand-over at the desk or `SHIPPED` for a consignment to the claimant; an approval with no disposition named is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422), and a `SHIPPED` disposition additionally requires a non-empty `claimant_contact` on the item, failing which the same code applies. For compatibility, the combined decision values `APPROVE_PICKUP` and `APPROVE_SHIP` are accepted and mean approval with the `RELEASED` and `SHIPPED` dispositions respectively.

The three decision outcomes have deliberately divergent effects, and all three are specified in full. An approved pickup advances `CLAIM_PENDING → RELEASED`, clears `storage_bin` because the item has left the property, and leaves `tracking_code` null. An approved shipment advances `CLAIM_PENDING → SHIPPED`, requires a `tracking_code` in the body — its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — stamps that carrier `tracking_code` on the item, and likewise clears `storage_bin`. A denial advances `CLAIM_PENDING → STORED`, requires a `denial_reason` in the body — its absence is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — stamps that `denial_reason`, clears `claimant_name` and `claimant_contact`, and leaves `storage_bin`, `stored_at` and `retention_expires_at` completely untouched. That last point is the reason denial returns the item to `STORED` rather than to a terminal state: the retention clock keeps running, so a second claimant may file inside the same window, and `claim_attempt_count` records every attempt including the denied ones.

Unclaimed items are eventually disposed of by a `Supervisor`, and the item must be `STORED` with the retention window closed. The `disposition` field is `DISPOSED` or `DONATED`. Donation is only permitted for items whose `category` is `STANDARD` or `PERISHABLE`; an `ID_DOCUMENT` or `HIGH_VALUE` item may never be donated and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_PERMITTED`. A permitted disposal advances `STORED → DISPOSED` or `STORED → DONATED`, clears `storage_bin` and increments `version`. `RELEASED`, `SHIPPED`, `DISPOSED` and `DONATED` are all terminal.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions
