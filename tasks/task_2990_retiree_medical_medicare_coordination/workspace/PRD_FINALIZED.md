# Retiree Medical & Medicare Coordination API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employers that promised medical coverage to their retirees now administer three overlapping obligations at once: a benefit promise whose eligibility rules were rewritten twice but never applied retroactively, a coordination duty with Medicare that decides who pays first for every covered claim, and an accounting liability that must be measured and disclosed every year. Today these obligations live in three unconnected places — a spreadsheet of grandfathered hire dates, a third-party administrator's claims portal, and an actuary's valuation model — and they disagree with each other constantly. A retiree whose appeal reverses an ineligibility finding stays out of the valuation cohort for a full quarter. A retiree who enrols in Medicare Part B mid-year keeps drawing a pre-Medicare health reimbursement credit that the plan document does not fund. An option whose prescription-drug benefit fails actuarial equivalence testing keeps accepting elections because nobody wired the test result back to the enrollment gate.

This document specifies a single system of record for the retiree medical programme, mounted under `/retiree-benefits`, that closes those gaps by making every derived value a stored consequence of a named rule rather than a manual entry. The programme is organized by plan year. Within a plan year the sponsor publishes plan options across three design families — pre-Medicare group coverage, a Medicare group wrap, and an individual marketplace exchange funded through a health reimbursement arrangement — and each option carries its own retiree drug subsidy vehicle whose actuarial equivalence must be attested by an actuary and independently certified by a compliance function. Retirees are tested against the eligibility rule set that their hire date grandfathered them into, elect coverage inside a strictly bounded annual window or a qualifying life-event window, are billed monthly with a service-weighted employer subsidy, and have their Medicare secondary-payer order recomputed every time a fact that could change it moves.

Two properties drive nearly every design decision that follows. The first is **conservation**: the health reimbursement arrangement is a closed ledger whose funded total is never destroyed, only moved between buckets, and a plan option's enrolled headcount must always equal the number of rows that justify it. The second is **segregation of duties**: the party who prepares a record may never be the party who approves it, and the system enforces that separation on the acting user identifier rather than trusting process discipline. Because eligibility windows, delinquency ageing, forfeiture and valuation all depend on the passage of time, the server keeps a logical clock that test harnesses and operators drive explicitly, so that every temporal rule in this specification is reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `BenefitsAdmin`, `EligibilityAnalyst`, `EnrollmentCounselor`, `HraAdministrator`, `BillingClerk`, `Actuary`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `Retiree`.

`BenefitsAdmin` is the plan sponsor's administrator: it owns the plan-year and plan-option catalogue, the retiree roster, the logical clock and the nightly administration sweep. `EligibilityAnalyst` runs the hire-date, age and service rules and creates the resulting determinations. `EnrollmentCounselor` is enrollment support: it submits and adjudicates elections on a retiree's behalf, opens appeals, and helps retirees file reimbursement claims. `HraAdministrator` is the third-party reimbursement-arrangement administrator: it funds accounts, adjudicates substantiation, and closes accounts at year end. `BillingClerk` owns premium billing and collections, settles approved claims, and reinstates lapsed coverage. `Actuary` attests prescription-drug actuarial values and runs the accounting liability valuation. `ComplianceOfficer` is an independent control function that certifies subsidy elections and decides eligibility appeals. `Retiree` is the covered individual.

Seven of the eight roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `BenefitsAdmin`, `EligibilityAnalyst`, `EnrollmentCounselor`, `HraAdministrator`, `BillingClerk`, `Actuary` or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule says so — as `created_by_user_id`, `certified_by_user_id`, `attested_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id` or `appeal_decided_by_user_id` on the record being acted upon — and compares it against other recorded operator identifiers for segregation-of-duties purposes, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The eighth role is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Retiree`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Retiree` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Retiree` compare the resource's retiree foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Retiree` opening an appeal must be the subject of the determination being appealed, and a `Retiree` attaching substantiating documentation to a claim must own the reimbursement account that claim draws against. An ownership mismatch is an authorization failure and is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer in the inclusive range 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer of at least 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. The pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` are returned in the `meta` object of every list response: `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in the programme — `PlanYear`, `Retiree`, `PlanOption`, `EligibilityDetermination`, `ElectionEvent`, `Enrollment`, `HraAccount`, `ReimbursementClaim` and `PremiumInvoice` — carries the same four housekeeping properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations reached through a cascade rather than through a direct request against the row itself. `updated_at` is refreshed on the same mutations that advance `version`. No entity in this programme is exempt: all nine carry `updated_at` and `version`.

Time is not read from the host. The server maintains a **logical UTC clock** that every deadline, window, proration, ageing and sweep rule in this document consults. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to wall-clock time. A `BenefitsAdmin` may reposition it by calling `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` with a `to_iso8601` instant; the response body is an object carrying a single `current_utc` field holding the new clock reading. Setting the clock mutates no entity — it changes only what every subsequent request sees as "now".

Path parameters are validated from left to right. Where a URL nests an identifier — `{plan_year_id}`, `{retiree_id}`, `{plan_option_id}`, `{determination_id}`, `{election_event_id}`, `{enrollment_id}`, `{hra_account_id}`, `{claim_id}` or `{invoice_id}` — the leftmost segment is resolved first and its "not found" error is reported before any later segment or body field is examined.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations mutate a parent record as a side effect of creating a child, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on that parent.

A `POST /retiree-benefits/eligibility-determinations` request must carry `expected_retiree_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Retiree`. If that value does not equal the retiree's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RETIREE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard matters precisely because a new determination legitimately supersedes the retiree's earlier determination for the same plan year, so the create would otherwise succeed on its own terms; requiring the version forces the writer to re-read the retiree — whose `eligibility_status`, `credited_service_months` or `determination_sequence` another writer may already have advanced — before superseding anything.

A `POST /retiree-benefits/reimbursement-claims` request must carry `expected_hra_account_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `HraAccount`. If that value does not equal the account's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HRA_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A claim draws down the account's available balance at the moment of submission, so a claim admitted on a stale view of that balance would be held at a provisional amount computed from a balance that no longer exists.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints other than these two must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Order

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is a non-negative integer in requests. Rates are expressed in basis points in fields whose names end in `_bps`, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent; these are likewise non-negative integers. The single exception to integer arithmetic is `carryover_cap_pct`, which is a decimal fraction that must lie in the inclusive range 0 through 1.

There is no half-up rounding anywhere in this programme. Every division and every rate application truncates toward zero — that is, it takes the floor of a non-negative quotient — and the truncation happens at the exact step the rule names, never later. Because floor-then-multiply and multiply-then-floor differ, the order is pinned explicitly in each calculation and an implementation that reorders the steps will produce answers that differ by a few cents. Three orders are load-bearing and are called out where they appear: the reimbursement credit is floored to whole cents per month before being multiplied by the number of covered months; the administrative fee is a percentage of the base premium only and not of a total that already contains the fee; and the liability valuation floors each retiree's contribution before summing the cohort rather than summing first and flooring once.

Date-only fields — `birth_date`, `hire_date`, `retirement_date`, `medicare_part_b_effective_date`, `esrd_entitlement_date`, `event_date` and `service_date` — are `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates and are compared as calendar dates. Instant fields are compared at millisecond resolution.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties Across the Control Chain

The programme's control model forbids a user from approving their own work. Whenever an approval, certification, appeal decision or settlement is requested, the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the operator identifier already recorded on the target record, and a match is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Six pairings are enforced, and they are the complete set:

- Certifying an eligibility determination is compared against that determination's `created_by_user_id`.
- Deciding an appeal on a determination is compared against that determination's `certified_by_user_id`.
- Certifying or rejecting a plan option's subsidy election is compared against that option's `attested_by_user_id`.
- Deciding an election event is compared against that event's `created_by_user_id`.
- Reviewing a reimbursement claim is compared against that claim's `created_by_user_id`.
- Settling a reimbursement claim is compared against that claim's `reviewed_by_user_id`.

Role separation reinforces the same principle structurally: an `Actuary` attests actuarial equivalence but may never certify it, and a `ComplianceOfficer` decides appeals and certifies subsidies but never creates the records it reviews.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

The ordering of validation checks is **total**. When a single request violates more than one rule, the rule that appears earliest in the following ladder produces the response, and no later rule is evaluated or reported. Implementations must therefore evaluate in this order rather than in whatever order is convenient.

1. Authentication — both headers present and non-empty and `X-User-Role` one of the eight declared roles → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. Authorization — the caller's role appears in the target endpoint's role list → `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. A `plan_year_id` in the path or body resolves to an existing plan year → `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_NOT_FOUND` (404), on plan-year reads and lifecycle and valuation writes, and on plan-option, eligibility-determination, election-event and reimbursement-account creation.
4. A `retiree_id` in the path or body resolves to an existing retiree → `ERR_RETIREE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on retiree read and update, and on eligibility-determination, election-event and reimbursement-account creation.
5. A `plan_option_id` in the path, or `requested_plan_option_id` in the body, resolves to an existing plan option → `ERR_PLAN_OPTION_NOT_FOUND` (404), on plan-option reads and writes and on election-event creation.
6. A `determination_id` in the path resolves to an existing eligibility determination → `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every determination read and write.
7. An `election_event_id` in the path resolves to an existing election event → `ERR_ELECTION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every election-event read and write.
8. An `enrollment_id` in the path or body resolves to an existing enrollment → `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every enrollment read and write and on premium-invoice creation.
9. An `hra_account_id` in the path or body, and a supplied `prior_year_account_id`, resolve to existing reimbursement accounts → `ERR_HRA_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` (404), on account reads and writes, account creation and claim creation.
10. A `claim_id` in the path resolves to an existing reimbursement claim → `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every claim read and write.
11. An `invoice_id` in the path resolves to an existing premium invoice → `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every invoice read and write.
12. Request shape — every required body field is present, every field has its declared JSON type, every enum field carries a declared member, every `_cents` and `_bps` field is a non-negative integer, every date and instant field parses as ISO 8601, `carryover_cap_pct` lies in the inclusive range 0 through 1, and every interval field pair satisfies a start strictly earlier than its end → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint.
13. Pagination — `limit` is an integer in the inclusive range 1 through 100 and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 → `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400), on every list endpoint.
14. `expected_retiree_version` equals the retiree's current `version` → `ERR_STALE_RETIREE_VERSION` (412), on eligibility-determination creation.
15. `expected_hra_account_version` equals the account's current `version` → `ERR_STALE_HRA_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (412), on reimbursement-claim creation.
16. The requested plan-year status change is one of the three declared successors → `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_YEAR_TRANSITION` (409), on the plan-year lifecycle endpoint.
17. The governing plan year's `status` is `OPEN` → `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_NOT_OPEN` (409), on eligibility-determination, election-event, reimbursement-account and premium-invoice creation.
18. The target plan option's `status` is `ACTIVE` → `ERR_PLAN_OPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on plan-change-notice issuance and on election-event creation.
19. The determination's `status` permits the requested move — certification requires `DRAFT`, opening an appeal requires `CERTIFIED`, and deciding an appeal requires `UNDER_APPEAL` → `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE` (409).
20. The election event's `status` permits the requested move — taking it into review requires `SUBMITTED` and deciding it requires `UNDER_REVIEW` → `ERR_INVALID_ELECTION_STATE` (409).
21. The enrollment's state permits the requested move — termination requires a `coverage_status` of `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and reinstatement requires a `coverage_status` of `TERMINATED` together with a `billing_status` of `LAPSED` → `ERR_INVALID_ENROLLMENT_STATE` (409).
22. The reimbursement account's `status` is `OPEN` → `ERR_HRA_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN` (409), on claim creation and on year-end close.
23. The claim's `status` permits the requested move — attaching documentation requires `PENDING_SUBSTANTIATION`, review requires `SUBMITTED` and an approval additionally requires a non-null `documentation_reference`, settlement by payment requires `APPROVED`, and settlement by reversal requires `PAID` → `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE` (409).
24. The invoice's `status` is `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAST_DUE`; a `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` invoice accepts no further payment → `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_SETTLED` (409).
25. Segregation of duties, in all six pairings listed in §2.7 → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on determination certification and appeal decision, subsidy certification, election decision, claim review and claim settlement.
26. The attested gross prescription-drug value is at least the standard benchmark → `ERR_ACTUARIAL_GROSS_TEST_FAILED` (422), on attestation.
27. For an employer-group-waiver option, the attested net prescription-drug value is at least the standard net benchmark, evaluated only after the gross test has passed → `ERR_ACTUARIAL_NET_TEST_FAILED` (422), on attestation.
28. When the requested option's `subsidy_vehicle` is `RDS` or `EGWP`, its `subsidy_certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` → `ERR_SUBSIDY_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409), on the election decision.
29. The retiree holds a determination for the same plan year whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` → `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409), on election-event creation, on an approving election decision, and on reimbursement-account creation.
30. That certified determination's `outcome` is `ELIGIBLE` rather than `INELIGIBLE` → `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_RETIREE_MEDICAL` (422), on election-event creation, on an approving election decision, and on reimbursement-account creation.
31. For an `ANNUAL_OPEN` election the logical clock lies inside the annual window → `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422), on election-event creation.
32. For a qualifying life event the logical clock lies inside the life-event window → `ERR_LIFE_EVENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422), on election-event creation.
33. The requested option's `coverage_class` matches the retiree's `medicare_status` → `ERR_PLAN_CLASS_MISMATCH` (422), on election-event creation.
34. When the option's `requires_medicare_part_b` is true, the retiree's `medicare_part_b_effective_date` is non-null and not later than the election's `coverage_effective_date` → `ERR_MEDICARE_PART_B_REQUIRED` (422), on election-event creation.
35. The retiree's `grandfathered_group` appears in the option's `allowed_grandfather_groups` → `ERR_GRANDFATHER_GROUP_NOT_PERMITTED` (422), on election-event creation.
36. The new coverage interval does not collide with an existing coverage interval of the same retiree → `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT_PERIOD` (409), on the election decision.
37. The new billing interval does not collide with an existing non-written-off invoice interval of the same enrollment → `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on premium-invoice creation.
38. The whole-day separation between the logical clock and the requested `effective_date` is at least the plan year's `notice_lead_days` → `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on plan-change-notice issuance.
39. The logical clock is not later than the enrollment's `reinstatement_deadline` → `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422), on reinstatement.
40. The claim's `service_date` falls inside the plan-year coverage window of the account's plan year → `ERR_EXPENSE_OUTSIDE_PLAN_YEAR` (422), on claim creation.
41. No other plan year already carries the requested `year` → `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_YEAR` (409), on plan-year creation.
42. No other plan option in the same plan year already carries the requested `plan_code` → `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_CODE` (409), on plan-option creation.
43. No reimbursement account already exists for the same retiree and plan year in any status, and any supplied `prior_year_account_id` belongs to the same retiree and references an earlier plan year → `ERR_DUPLICATE_HRA_ACCOUNT` (409), on reimbursement-account creation.
44. A supplied `prior_year_account_id` references an account whose `status` is `CLOSED` → `ERR_PRIOR_HRA_ACCOUNT_NOT_CLOSED` (409), on reimbursement-account creation.
45. The retiree carries no past-due invoice with a non-zero outstanding balance on any enrollment other than the one being reinstated → `ERR_OUTSTANDING_PREMIUM_BALANCE` (409), on reinstatement.
46. The account's `available_balance_cents` is strictly greater than zero, so the account can fund at least part of the submitted claim → `ERR_HRA_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on claim creation.
47. `amount_cents` does not exceed the invoice's remaining balance → `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (422), on payment posting.

Read as bands, the ladder runs authentication and authorization first, then existence, then request shape and pagination, then optimistic concurrency, then state-machine legality, then regulatory and segregation-of-duties safety, then temporal windows, then referential and interval integrity, then uniqueness and reinstatement constraints, and finally money availability. Money is deliberately checked last: a request that is unauthorized, malformed or aimed at the wrong state never reaches a balance test.

### 2.9. Interval and Window Semantics

Six temporal rules in this programme compare intervals, and they do not all use the same endpoint convention. The differences are deliberate and each is load-bearing.

The **annual election window** of a plan year is half-open: it runs from `open_enrollment_start` inclusive to `open_enrollment_end` exclusive, so a clock reading exactly equal to `open_enrollment_end` is outside the window. `open_enrollment_end` must be strictly later than `open_enrollment_start`.

A **life-event window** is closed on both ends: it runs from the event date at midnight UTC through that instant plus 31 days, inclusive of both endpoints.

**Coverage intervals** are half-open. An enrollment covers from its `coverage_effective_date` inclusive up to its `coverage_end_date` exclusive, and a null `coverage_end_date` means the interval is open-ended. Two coverage intervals collide when the new interval's start is strictly earlier than the existing interval's end and the existing interval's start is strictly earlier than the new interval's end. The practical consequence is that an existing interval whose `coverage_end_date` equals the new `coverage_effective_date` is merely adjacent, not overlapping, and is admitted.

**Billing periods** are closed on both ends. An invoice bills from `billing_period_start` inclusive through `billing_period_end` inclusive, and `billing_period_end` must be strictly later than `billing_period_start`. Two billing periods collide when the new period's start is not later than the existing period's end and the existing period's start is not later than the new period's end. The practical consequence is the opposite of the coverage rule: a new period whose start equals a prior period's end **is** a collision, so contiguous monthly billing must begin strictly after the prior period ends.

**Plan change notice lead time** is a buffer between two instants, not an overlap between two intervals. The available lead time is the whole number of days obtained by taking the difference between the requested effective instant and the logical clock and truncating it to whole days of 86400 seconds each. A lead time exactly equal to the plan year's required minimum is admitted.

The **reinstatement window** is closed at its upper end: reinstatement is permitted while the logical clock is not later than `reinstatement_deadline`.

### 2.10. Ledger and Headcount Conservation

The reimbursement arrangement is a conserved ledger. For every `HraAccount`, at every observable moment and after every mutation that touches the account or any claim drawing on it, `funded_cents` equals the sum of five buckets: `available_balance_cents`, `pending_hold_cents`, `substantiated_paid_cents`, `premium_offset_cents` and `forfeited_cents`. `funded_cents` is fixed at account creation and never changes afterwards; every subsequent event — a claim hold, a denial releasing a hold, an approval revising a hold, a payment, a payment reversal, a premium offset, a year-end forfeiture — only moves cents between those five buckets. The equality is exact integer equality with zero tolerance for drift. A consequence worth stating on its own: paying a claim and then reversing that payment must return `available_balance_cents` to precisely the value it held before the payment, assuming no other activity intervened.

Enrolled headcount is conserved in the same spirit. For every `PlanOption`, `enrolled_count` equals the number of enrollments referencing that option whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; and summed across all options of a plan year, those counts equal the number of enrollments of that plan year in those same three coverage states. An approved election is the forward move that adds one; voluntary or administrative termination, a premium-lapse cascade and a plan-termination cascade each remove one; reinstatement adds one back. No path may leave the stored count and the underlying row set disagreeing.

### 2.11. Parallel State Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

Three entities carry two independent state dimensions that must never be collapsed into one. A `Retiree` carries `eligibility_status` alongside `medicare_status`: certifying or reversing a determination moves only the former, and updating Medicare facts moves only the latter. A `PlanOption` carries `status` alongside `subsidy_certification_status`: activating an option or closing it to new entrants leaves the certification dimension untouched, and attesting or certifying actuarial equivalence leaves availability untouched. An `Enrollment` carries `coverage_status` alongside `billing_status`, and the delinquency chain moves them in separate, individually specified steps. Any implementation that treats these as a single status column will fail, because legal combinations such as `SUSPENDED` coverage with `DELINQUENT` billing, or a `CLOSED_TO_NEW` option that is nonetheless `CERTIFIED`, must be representable.

Cascades follow three rules without exception. First, **fan-out is ordered**: whenever one operation touches a set of child rows, those rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Second, **every touched row is a mutation**: each row a cascade rewrites has its own `version` incremented by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, including the parent that triggered the cascade. Third, **causation is chained rather than flat**: a cascade hop that is caused by an earlier hop fires only when that earlier hop actually fired. Closing a plan year denies an election only because the option that election requested left `ACTIVE`; freezing a reimbursement account happens only because coverage terminated on lapse; denying the account's open claims happens only because the account froze. Where the chain matters, the individual workflow sections below name the hop order explicitly.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Plan Year and Its Option Catalogue

Everything in the programme hangs off a plan year. A `BenefitsAdmin` registers one by supplying the calendar `year` it covers, which must be an integer of at least 2024 and at most 2099 and which no other plan year may already carry — a second registration of the same `year` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_YEAR`. The same request establishes the annual election calendar through `open_enrollment_start` and `open_enrollment_end`, and the instant `coverage_start` at which coverage elected during the annual window becomes effective. It also sets the reimbursement credit schedule for the year: `hra_credit_pre_medicare_cents` is the full-year employer credit for a retiree who is not yet enrolled in Medicare, and `hra_credit_medicare_cents` is the full-year credit for one who is; `carryover_cap_pct` is the fraction of the year's credit that is allowed to survive the year-end close. Finally, the actuarial assumptions are captured up front: `discount_rate_bps` must be an integer between 0 and 5000 inclusive, `assumed_annual_subsidy_cents` is the assumed annual employer subsidy per covered retiree, and `assumed_payment_years` is the number of years that subsidy is assumed to be paid and must be at least 1.

Three calendar parameters on the plan year are optional and carry defaults chosen to match common plan documents. `premium_grace_period_days` — the number of days added to an invoice's due date before that invoice ages into past-due status — defaults to 31 days, roughly a full billing month of forbearance. `reinstatement_window_days`, the number of days after a lapse during which coverage can still be bought back, defaults to 60 days. `notice_lead_days`, the minimum whole days that must separate a plan change communication from the date the change takes effect, defaults to 60 days as well. All three must be at least 0.

A newly registered plan year opens in `DRAFT` with no valuation attached: `liability_method` and `liability_valued_at` are null, and `measured_retiree_count`, `epbo_total_cents`, `apbo_total_cents` and `service_cost_total_cents` all start at 0. Its lifecycle is a strict single-successor chain, driven by a `BenefitsAdmin` supplying a `to_status`: `DRAFT → OPEN`, then `OPEN → CLOSED`, then `CLOSED → ARCHIVED`. Any requested `to_status` that is not the one legal successor of the current `status` — including a repeat of the current status, a skip, or a move backwards — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_YEAR_TRANSITION`. Reaching `ARCHIVED` additionally requires that no reimbursement account of that plan year is still `OPEN`; `ARCHIVED` is terminal and cascades nothing further.

The `OPEN → CLOSED` move is where a plan year sheds its in-flight work, and it does so in two chained hops. Hop one closes the catalogue: every plan option of that year whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, in `created_at` ascending order, each such option's `version` incrementing. Hop two is caused by hop one rather than by the plan-year change itself: for each option hop one just moved to `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, every election event whose `requested_plan_option_id` names that option and whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason_code` set to `PLAN_CLOSED`, again in `created_at` ascending order, each such event's `version` incrementing. An election is denied only because the option it requested left `ACTIVE` — an election requesting an option that was already `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` before the request is untouched. The plan year's own `version` also increments.

Plan options are defined under a plan year by a `BenefitsAdmin`. Each carries a `plan_code` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique within its `plan_year_id`, a duplicate being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_CODE`. The `coverage_class` names one of three design families — `PRE_MEDICARE_GROUP`, `MEDICARE_GROUP_WRAP` or `INDIVIDUAL_EXCHANGE_HRA` — and it is what decides which Medicare postures may elect the option. `monthly_premium_cents` is the base premium before administrative fee, employer subsidy and reimbursement offset. `requires_medicare_part_b` marks an option that only a retiree carrying Part B may elect. `allowed_grandfather_groups` is a non-empty array drawn from `GRANDFATHERED_A`, `TRANSITION_B` and `STANDARD_C`, naming the grandfather cohorts permitted to elect it. `subsidy_vehicle` records the drug-subsidy route chosen for the option and is one of `RDS`, `EGWP` or `NONE`. `rx_gross_value_bps` and `rx_net_value_bps` record the option's drug benefit value in basis points of the standard Part D benefit, gross and net of the supplemental premium offset. A new option starts with `status` `DRAFT`, `subsidy_certification_status` `NOT_STARTED` and `enrolled_count` 0.

Before an option can be elected it must be activated. Activation moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE` and is permitted only while the owning plan year is in `DRAFT` or `OPEN`. Note the asymmetry that follows from the parallel dimensions: activation says nothing about actuarial equivalence, and an option may be `ACTIVE` while its `subsidy_certification_status` is still `NOT_STARTED` — the certification gate bites later, at the moment an election is approved.

Plan changes are communicated in advance. A `BenefitsAdmin` issues a change notice against an `ACTIVE` option, supplying a `change_type` of either `PREMIUM_INCREASE` or `PLAN_TERMINATION` and the `effective_date` instant at which it takes hold; `new_monthly_premium_cents` must accompany a `PREMIUM_INCREASE` and is absent for a `PLAN_TERMINATION`. A `PREMIUM_INCREASE` notice with no `new_monthly_premium_cents` is malformed and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Issuance is gated on lead time: taking the difference between `effective_date` and the logical clock and truncating it to whole 86400-second days must yield at least the plan year's `notice_lead_days`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`; a separation exactly equal to `notice_lead_days` is accepted. On admission the option records `change_notice_issued_at` at the logical clock, `pending_change_type`, `pending_change_effective_date` and `pending_new_monthly_premium_cents`, and nothing else changes yet — the announced change is applied later, by the nightly sweep, when the clock reaches the effective instant.

### 3.2. Actuarial Equivalence and the Subsidy Certification Chain

An employer that keeps its retirees in group drug coverage must prove that coverage is at least as good as standard Part D, and the proof runs through two hands. The `Actuary` attests; the `ComplianceOfficer` certifies. Neither may do the other's job, and the same human may not do both.

Attestation is only meaningful for an option that actually claims a subsidy. Attesting an option whose `subsidy_vehicle` is `NONE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and this check runs before either equivalence test. The option's availability dimension must be `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED_TO_NEW`; a `RETIRED` option is beyond attestation. The `Actuary` supplies the attested `rx_gross_value_bps` and `rx_net_value_bps`, which overwrite whatever the option carried before.

Two tests then run against fixed benchmarks. The gross test compares the attested gross value against the standard Part D gross value of 10000 basis points — that is, 100 percent of the standard benefit — and `gross_test_passed` is true when the attested gross value is at least that benchmark. The net test compares the attested net value against the standard Part D net value of 7450 basis points, and `net_test_passed` is true when the attested net value is at least that benchmark. `equivalence_margin_bps` records the attested net value minus the 7450-basis-point benchmark, and may therefore be negative. Both thresholds are inclusive: an attested net value of exactly 7450 basis points passes and yields an `equivalence_margin_bps` of exactly 0.

Gating is asymmetric between the two subsidy vehicles, and the gates are evaluated in a pinned order. First, an option with `subsidy_vehicle` `NONE` is rejected as described above. Second, if the gross test does not pass, the attestation is refused with `ERR_ACTUARIAL_GROSS_TEST_FAILED`. Third — and only after the gross test has passed — an option whose `subsidy_vehicle` is `EGWP` whose net test does not pass is refused with `ERR_ACTUARIAL_NET_TEST_FAILED`. A retiree-drug-subsidy option, `subsidy_vehicle` `RDS`, is gated on the gross test alone: it may be attested with a failing net test, and its `net_test_passed` will simply be recorded as false. Historical attestations are never consulted; each attestation is judged solely on the values supplied with it plus the option's `subsidy_vehicle`.

A successful attestation writes `gross_test_passed`, `net_test_passed` and `equivalence_margin_bps`, records `attested_by_user_id` as the acting `Actuary`'s `X-User-ID`, and moves `subsidy_certification_status` to `ATTESTED`. The permitted entry states are `NOT_STARTED` and `REJECTED`, which means a rejected option can be re-attested with better values and travel `REJECTED → ATTESTED` again as often as needed. Throughout, the option's availability `status` is untouched.

Certification is the independent second look. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a `decision` of either `CERTIFY` or `REJECT` against an option whose `subsidy_certification_status` is `ATTESTED`. Both branches record `certified_by_user_id` as the acting officer's `X-User-ID` and increment the option's `version`; `CERTIFY` moves `subsidy_certification_status` from `ATTESTED` to `CERTIFIED`, and `REJECT` moves it from `ATTESTED` to `REJECTED`. Neither branch touches the option's availability `status`, its premium, or its attested values. The acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the option's `attested_by_user_id`; if the actuary who attested the values comes back wearing the compliance hat, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Certification's consequence surfaces at election time: approving an election onto an option whose `subsidy_vehicle` is `RDS` or `EGWP` requires `subsidy_certification_status` to be `CERTIFIED`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_SUBSIDY_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

### 3.3. Retiree Records, Grandfather Cohorts and the Eligibility Rule Sets

A `BenefitsAdmin` or an `EligibilityAnalyst` enrols a retiree record with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `birth_date`, a `hire_date`, a `retirement_date` marking benefit commencement, and `credited_service_months`, the whole months of credited service at retirement, which must be at least 0. Seven further attributes are optional at creation and describe the retiree's Medicare and other-coverage posture: `medicare_status` defaults to `PRE_MEDICARE`; `medicare_part_b_effective_date` defaults to null, meaning Part B is not in force; `other_group_coverage_employer_size` defaults to 0, where 0 means the retiree has no other group health coverage at all; `active_employment_flag` defaults to false and is true when that other coverage arises from current employment of the retiree or a spouse; `esrd_flag` defaults to false and is true when Medicare entitlement rests on end-stage renal disease; `esrd_entitlement_date` defaults to null and records the first day of that entitlement; and `disability_entitlement_flag` defaults to false and is true when entitlement rests on disability rather than age. A new retiree record starts with `eligibility_status` `UNDETERMINED` and `determination_sequence` 0.

Grandfathering is decided once, at creation, from `hire_date` alone, and `grandfathered_group` never changes afterwards. A retiree hired strictly before 1 January 1993 is a `GRANDFATHERED_A`. Otherwise, a retiree hired strictly before 1 January 2005 is a `TRANSITION_B`. Everyone else is a `STANDARD_C`. Both boundaries are half-open on the low side, so a `hire_date` of exactly 1993-01-01 lands in `TRANSITION_B` and a `hire_date` of exactly 2005-01-01 lands in `STANDARD_C`. The comparison is a calendar-date comparison, not an instant comparison.

Two derived quantities follow the retiree everywhere. **Completed age at retirement** is the whole number of completed years between `birth_date` and `retirement_date`: take the difference of the calendar years, then subtract one when the month-and-day of `retirement_date` falls strictly before the month-and-day of `birth_date`. **Service years** is `credited_service_months` divided by 12 and truncated down to a whole number, so 143 months is 11 service years and 144 months is 12. The retiree's own `eligibility_points` is the sum of completed age at retirement and service years; it is computed when the retiree record is created, rewritten whenever `credited_service_months` changes, and rewritten again whenever a new determination is run for that retiree.

Which eligibility rule set a retiree faces is a direct function of the cohort. A `GRANDFATHERED_A` retiree is measured under `RULE_OF_75_GRANDFATHERED`, a `TRANSITION_B` retiree under `AGE_60_SERVICE_15`, and a `STANDARD_C` retiree under `AGE_65_SERVICE_10`. Each rule set produces an `outcome` of either `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE`, and every threshold in every rule set is inclusive.

- Under `RULE_OF_75_GRANDFATHERED`, the retiree is `ELIGIBLE` when three conditions all hold: the sum of completed age at retirement and service years is at least 75 points, completed age at retirement is at least 55 years, and service years is at least 10. Exactly 75 points is `ELIGIBLE`. Failing any one of the three yields `INELIGIBLE`.
- Under `AGE_60_SERVICE_15`, the retiree is `ELIGIBLE` when completed age at retirement is at least 60 years and service years is at least 15; otherwise `INELIGIBLE`.
- Under `AGE_65_SERVICE_10`, the retiree is `ELIGIBLE` when completed age at retirement is at least 65 years and service years is at least 10; otherwise `INELIGIBLE`.

No other retiree's data and no plan-year data participate in the rule evaluation — it consults one retiree row plus its frozen `grandfathered_group` and nothing else.

Retiree records stay editable in exactly one respect: the facts that drive Medicare coordination. A `BenefitsAdmin` or `EligibilityAnalyst` may update `medicare_status`, `medicare_part_b_effective_date`, `other_group_coverage_employer_size`, `active_employment_flag`, `esrd_flag`, `esrd_entitlement_date`, `disability_entitlement_flag` and `credited_service_months`. `hire_date` and the `grandfathered_group` derived from it are immutable, and `eligibility_status` is never written by an update — that dimension belongs exclusively to determination certification and appeal decisions.

The Medicare dimension advances through three declared moves, each leaving `eligibility_status` untouched. From `PRE_MEDICARE` a retiree may move to `MEDICARE_ELIGIBLE_NOT_ENROLLED`. From `MEDICARE_ELIGIBLE_NOT_ENROLLED` a retiree may move to `MEDICARE_WAIVED`. And from either `MEDICARE_ELIGIBLE_NOT_ENROLLED` or `MEDICARE_WAIVED` a retiree may move to `MEDICARE_ENROLLED`, which additionally requires that a non-null `medicare_part_b_effective_date` either accompanies the request or is already recorded on the retiree. Whichever move fires, the Medicare secondary-payer determination is re-run for every enrollment of that retiree whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, in `created_at` ascending order, and each such enrollment's `version` increments alongside the retiree's own.

### 3.4. Determination, Certification, Appeal and Supersession

An `EligibilityAnalyst` runs the rules for one retiree and one plan year by supplying `retiree_id`, `plan_year_id` and `expected_retiree_version`. The governing plan year must be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_NOT_OPEN`. The resulting determination freezes everything the decision rested on so that it stays auditable when the retiree record moves on: `grandfathered_group` is copied from the retiree, `rule_set_code` records the rule set the cohort selected, `age_years_at_retirement` and `service_years` record the two derived quantities as of the determination, `points` records their sum, and `outcome` records the verdict. The determination is created in `DRAFT` and records `created_by_user_id` as the acting analyst's `X-User-ID`.

Creating a determination is also a write against the retiree, which is why it is the operation guarded by optimistic concurrency. `determination_sequence` — the running count of determinations ever created for that retiree — increments, `eligibility_points` is rewritten, and the retiree's `version` therefore increments. In the same breath the retiree's earlier determinations for that same retiree-and-plan-year pair are retired: every one of them whose `status` is not already `SUPERSEDED` — that is, any determination sitting in `DRAFT`, `CERTIFIED` or `UNDER_APPEAL` — moves to `SUPERSEDED`, each with its own `version` increment. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, and a superseded determination accepts no certification, no appeal and no appeal decision; attempting any of those is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE`.

Certification is what makes a determination operative. It moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `CERTIFIED`, requires the governing plan year to be in `DRAFT` or `OPEN` — a `CLOSED` or `ARCHIVED` plan year is refused with `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_NOT_OPEN` — and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the determination's `created_by_user_id`, a match being refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the determination records `certified_by_user_id` as the certifying user's `X-User-ID` and `certified_at` at the logical clock; the subject retiree's `eligibility_status` is set to the determination's `outcome`, so `ELIGIBLE` and `INELIGIBLE` propagate directly onto the retiree, and the retiree's `version` increments. Because certification changes who belongs to the plan year's measured valuation cohort, the plan year's `version` also increments. The determination's own `version` increments as well.

A retiree who disagrees, or an `EnrollmentCounselor` acting for that retiree, may open an appeal against a `CERTIFIED` determination, supplying an `appeal_reason` in free text. When the caller acts as a `Retiree`, `X-User-ID` must equal the determination's `retiree_id`. Opening the appeal moves `status` from `CERTIFIED` to `UNDER_APPEAL`, stores the `appeal_reason`, and — because the retiree's eligibility is no longer settled — returns the subject retiree's `eligibility_status` to `UNDETERMINED`, incrementing the retiree's `version` as well as the determination's.

Appeals are decided by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying a `decision` of either `UPHOLD` or `REVERSE`, and the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the determination's `certified_by_user_id`; a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both decisions return `status` from `UNDER_APPEAL` to `CERTIFIED`, record `appeal_decision`, record `appeal_decided_by_user_id` as the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, refresh `certified_at` to the logical clock, resynchronise the subject retiree's `eligibility_status` from the determination's resulting `outcome`, and increment both the retiree's `version` and the determination's `version`. The two branches differ in exactly one respect, and the difference is the whole point of the mechanism: `UPHOLD` leaves `outcome` as it stands, so the retiree returns to the eligibility they had before the appeal, while `REVERSE` flips `outcome` — an `ELIGIBLE` determination becomes `INELIGIBLE` and an `INELIGIBLE` determination becomes `ELIGIBLE` — so the retiree's `eligibility_status` is resynchronised to the flipped verdict. The `CERTIFIED → UNDER_APPEAL → CERTIFIED` cycle is repeatable without bound, and successive reversals can legitimately walk a retiree `ELIGIBLE → INELIGIBLE → ELIGIBLE`.

### 3.5. Election Windows, Adjudication and the Birth of an Enrollment

Elections are submitted either by the `Retiree` personally or by an `EnrollmentCounselor` on their behalf, naming `retiree_id`, `plan_year_id`, `requested_plan_option_id`, an `event_type` and an `event_date`. The `event_type` distinguishes the annual window from four qualifying life events: `ANNUAL_OPEN`, `MEDICARE_ENTITLEMENT`, `LOSS_OF_OTHER_COVERAGE`, `MARRIAGE` and `DEPENDENT_DEATH`. `event_date` is the calendar date of the qualifying event; for an `ANNUAL_OPEN` election it is simply the submission date.

Submission is gated hard, because an election admitted outside its window is a compliance failure rather than an inconvenience. The plan year must be `OPEN` and the requested option must be `ACTIVE`, refused with `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_NOT_OPEN` and `ERR_PLAN_OPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` respectively. The retiree must already hold a `CERTIFIED` determination for that plan year, otherwise `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and that determination's `outcome` must be `ELIGIBLE`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_RETIREE_MEDICAL`.

The window itself is computed differently for the two kinds of event, and both computations write `window_deadline` and `coverage_effective_date` onto the election. For an `ANNUAL_OPEN` election, `window_deadline` is the plan year's `open_enrollment_end`, and the election is admitted only while the logical clock is at or after `open_enrollment_start` and strictly before `open_enrollment_end`; a clock outside that half-open window is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Its `coverage_effective_date` is the plan year's `coverage_start`. For any of the four qualifying life events, `window_deadline` is the `event_date` taken at midnight UTC plus 31 days, and the election is admitted only while the logical clock is at or after the `event_date` at midnight UTC and at or before that `window_deadline` — closed at both ends — a clock outside it being refused with `ERR_LIFE_EVENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Its `coverage_effective_date` is the first day of the calendar month **after** the month containing `event_date`, at midnight UTC.

Three referential checks then confirm the retiree may actually wear the option they picked, and they fire in a fixed order. First, `coverage_class` must match the retiree's Medicare posture: a retiree whose `medicare_status` is `MEDICARE_ENROLLED` may elect only a `MEDICARE_GROUP_WRAP` or an `INDIVIDUAL_EXCHANGE_HRA` option, while a retiree whose `medicare_status` is `PRE_MEDICARE`, `MEDICARE_ELIGIBLE_NOT_ENROLLED` or `MEDICARE_WAIVED` may elect only a `PRE_MEDICARE_GROUP` or an `INDIVIDUAL_EXCHANGE_HRA` option; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PLAN_CLASS_MISMATCH`. Second, when the option's `requires_medicare_part_b` is true, the retiree's `medicare_part_b_effective_date` must be non-null and must not fall after the election's computed `coverage_effective_date`; otherwise `ERR_MEDICARE_PART_B_REQUIRED`. Third, the retiree's `grandfathered_group` must appear in the option's `allowed_grandfather_groups`, or the election is refused with `ERR_GRANDFATHER_GROUP_NOT_PERMITTED`.

An admitted election is created with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` set to the logical clock, `created_by_user_id` set to the submitting user's `X-User-ID`, `denial_reason_code` null and `resulting_enrollment_id` null.

Adjudication happens in two steps. An `EnrollmentCounselor` or a `BenefitsAdmin` first takes a `SUBMITTED` election into review, which requires the plan year to still be `OPEN`, moves `status` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and records `reviewed_by_user_id` as the acting user's `X-User-ID`. The same roles then supply a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` against an election that is `UNDER_REVIEW`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the election's `created_by_user_id` — a counselor may not approve the election they themselves submitted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The two branches of the decision fork are deliberately lopsided, because one of them creates a resource and the other does not. `DENY` moves `status` from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DENIED`, sets `denial_reason_code` to the value supplied in the request when one is present and to `COUNSELOR_DENIAL` otherwise, creates no enrollment so that `resulting_enrollment_id` stays null, leaves `enrolled_count` on the requested option untouched, and increments the election's `version`. No eligibility, subsidy-certification, plan-class or interval check applies to a denial, precisely because nothing is being brought into existence.

`APPROVE` carries the full gauntlet. The requested option's `status` must still be `ACTIVE`. The retiree must still hold a `CERTIFIED` determination for the plan year — re-checked here rather than trusted from submission time, because an appeal opened after submission can move a determination out of `CERTIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`; and that determination's `outcome` must still be `ELIGIBLE`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_RETIREE_MEDICAL`. When the requested option's `subsidy_vehicle` is `RDS` or `EGWP`, its `subsidy_certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_SUBSIDY_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Finally the new coverage interval is tested against the retiree's history.

That overlap test is broader than intuition suggests, and getting its scope wrong is the most common way to admit a double-covered retiree. The candidate set is **every** enrollment of the electing retiree, across every plan year and regardless of `coverage_status` — `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` enrollments all count — examined in `created_at` ascending order. The interval being proposed runs from the election's `coverage_effective_date` and is open-ended. An existing enrollment's interval runs from its `coverage_effective_date` up to its `coverage_end_date`, or is open-ended when that is null. The two collide when the proposed start is strictly earlier than the existing end and the existing start is strictly earlier than the proposed end; on the first collision the approval is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT_PERIOD`. Because the semantics are half-open, an existing enrollment whose `coverage_end_date` equals the proposed `coverage_effective_date` is adjacent, not overlapping, and the approval proceeds.

A surviving approval moves `status` from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `APPROVED` and materialises exactly one enrollment. That enrollment carries `retiree_id` and `plan_year_id` from the election, `plan_option_id` copied from the election's `requested_plan_option_id`, and `election_event_id` pointing back at the election that produced it. Its `coverage_effective_date` is copied from the election. Its `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE` when the logical clock has already reached that `coverage_effective_date` and `PENDING` when it has not, its `billing_status` is `CURRENT`, and its `coverage_end_date` and `termination_reason` are null. The Medicare secondary-payer determination runs immediately to write `payer_order` and `msp_basis_code`. The premium waterfall runs through its first six steps, with no reimbursement offset applied, to write `monthly_retiree_premium_cents`. The requested option's `enrolled_count` increments by 1 and that option's `version` increments. The election records `resulting_enrollment_id` and increments its own `version`. Counters that record delinquency start clean: `consecutive_unpaid_periods` is 0, and `delinquency_first_unpaid_period_start`, `lapse_date`, `reinstatement_deadline` and `reinstatement_count` start at null, null, null and 0 respectively.

Coverage can also be ended by hand. A `BenefitsAdmin` or `EnrollmentCounselor` terminates an enrollment whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, supplying a `termination_reason` in free text; an already-`TERMINATED` enrollment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENROLLMENT_STATE`. Termination sets `coverage_status` to `TERMINATED`, closes the coverage interval by setting `coverage_end_date` to the logical clock, stores the supplied `termination_reason`, decrements the option's `enrolled_count` by 1 and increments that option's `version` — the exact compensating move for the increment an approval made, so that headcount conservation is restored — moves every `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoice of that enrollment to `WRITTEN_OFF`, and increments the enrollment's `version`.

### 3.6. Coordination of Benefits with Medicare

Whether the group plan or Medicare pays first is not a stored preference; it is a derived conclusion, recomputed from the retiree's current facts and the logical clock every time one of them could have moved. It is written when an approved election materialises an enrollment, re-derived whenever a retiree's Medicare or other-coverage attributes are updated, refreshed when a lapsed enrollment is reinstated, and swept during the nightly administration run so that end-stage-renal-disease coordination periods expire on time. Only enrollments whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` are ever recomputed; a `TERMINATED` enrollment keeps whatever it last held.

The determination writes two fields: `payer_order`, which is one of `GROUP_PRIMARY`, `GROUP_PRIMARY_WORKING_AGED` or `MEDICARE_PRIMARY`, and `msp_basis_code`, which records **which** branch produced that answer. Eight branches are evaluated strictly top to bottom against the enrollment's retiree, and the first branch that matches wins; the order is pinned and no later branch is consulted once an earlier one matches.

1. If the retiree's `medicare_status` is anything other than `MEDICARE_ENROLLED`, then `payer_order` is `GROUP_PRIMARY` with `msp_basis_code` `NO_OTHER_COVERAGE`. Medicare cannot be secondary to a plan it has not begun paying under.
2. Otherwise, if `esrd_flag` is true and `esrd_entitlement_date` is non-null and the whole months elapsed from that entitlement date to the logical clock's date are fewer than 30, then `payer_order` is `GROUP_PRIMARY` with `msp_basis_code` `ESRD_WITHIN_COORDINATION`. Thirty months is the coordination period during which the group plan stays primary for renal-disease entitlement.
3. Otherwise, if `esrd_flag` is true and `esrd_entitlement_date` is non-null — meaning the 30-month coordination period has run its course — then `payer_order` is `MEDICARE_PRIMARY` with `msp_basis_code` `ESRD_AFTER_COORDINATION`.
4. Otherwise, if `active_employment_flag` is true and `other_group_coverage_employer_size` is at least 20, then `payer_order` is `GROUP_PRIMARY_WORKING_AGED` with `msp_basis_code` `WORKING_AGED_LARGE_EMPLOYER`. An employer of 20 or more employees stays primary for a working-aged beneficiary.
5. Otherwise, if `active_employment_flag` is true and `other_group_coverage_employer_size` is greater than 0 — a small employer, fewer than 20 employees — then `payer_order` is `MEDICARE_PRIMARY` with `msp_basis_code` `WORKING_AGED_SMALL_EMPLOYER`.
6. Otherwise, if `disability_entitlement_flag` is true and `other_group_coverage_employer_size` is at least 100, then `payer_order` is `GROUP_PRIMARY` with `msp_basis_code` `DISABILITY_LARGE_EMPLOYER`. The disability threshold is five times the working-aged one: 100 employees rather than 20.
7. Otherwise, if `disability_entitlement_flag` is true and `other_group_coverage_employer_size` is greater than 0, then `payer_order` is `MEDICARE_PRIMARY` with `msp_basis_code` `DISABILITY_SMALL_EMPLOYER`.
8. Otherwise, `payer_order` is `MEDICARE_PRIMARY` with `msp_basis_code` `MEDICARE_PRIMARY_RETIREE_COVERAGE` — the ordinary case of a Medicare-enrolled retiree whose only other coverage is this retiree plan.

Both employer-size thresholds are inclusive, so exactly 20 employees selects the working-aged large-employer branch and exactly 100 employees selects the disability large-employer branch. The elapsed-months arithmetic used by the renal-disease branches is exact and integer: multiply the difference in years between the two dates by 12, add the difference in their month numbers, and then subtract one when the day-of-month of the later date is strictly less than the day-of-month of the earlier date. A retiree exactly 30 whole months past entitlement has left the coordination period and falls to the third branch, because the second branch requires strictly fewer than 30 months.

### 3.7. Reimbursement Arrangement Funding, Claims and the Year-End Close

An `HraAdministrator` opens a reimbursement account for one retiree and one plan year, naming `retiree_id`, `plan_year_id`, `covered_months` — the whole months of retiree-medical coverage the retiree holds this plan year, which must be between 0 and 12 inclusive — and optionally `prior_year_account_id` pointing at the closed prior-year account whose surviving balance is to carry over. The plan year must be `OPEN`, and the retiree must hold a `CERTIFIED` determination for that plan year with `outcome` `ELIGIBLE`, refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_CERTIFIED` and `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_RETIREE_MEDICAL` respectively. Only one account may exist per retiree and plan year in any status, and a supplied `prior_year_account_id` must belong to the same retiree and reference an earlier plan year; a violation of any of those three is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_HRA_ACCOUNT`. A supplied `prior_year_account_id` must additionally reference an account whose `status` is `CLOSED`, since an account that has not been closed has no final unused balance to inherit; anything else is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_HRA_ACCOUNT_NOT_CLOSED`.

Funding at creation follows a pinned order that matters to the cent. The full-year credit is chosen by Medicare posture: a retiree whose `medicare_status` is `MEDICARE_ENROLLED` draws the plan year's `hra_credit_medicare_cents`, and every other posture draws `hra_credit_pre_medicare_cents`. That full-year figure is then divided by 12 and truncated down to whole cents to give a monthly credit, and only then multiplied by `covered_months` to give `annual_credit_cents`. Flooring per month before multiplying is the rule; dividing the year's credit by 12 after multiplying by the covered months is not, and the two differ — a full-year credit of 209500 cents over 7 covered months yields 122206 cents under the pinned order and 122208 cents under the rejected one. `carryover_in_cents` is the `available_balance_cents` of the account named by `prior_year_account_id`, or 0 when no prior account was supplied. `funded_cents` is `annual_credit_cents` plus `carryover_in_cents`, and it is this figure that the ledger invariant conserves forever after. `available_balance_cents` opens equal to `funded_cents`, and the four spent buckets — `pending_hold_cents`, `substantiated_paid_cents`, `premium_offset_cents` and `forfeited_cents` — all open at 0. The account's `status` opens `OPEN` and `closed_at` is null.

Claims are filed by the `Retiree` or by an `EnrollmentCounselor` acting for them, naming `hra_account_id`, an `expense_category`, a `service_date`, `submitted_cents` — the amount claimed, which must be strictly greater than 0 — and `expected_hra_account_version`; `documentation_reference` may accompany the claim or be omitted, in which case it is null. The `expense_category` is one of `MEDICARE_PART_B_PREMIUM`, `MEDIGAP_PREMIUM`, `PART_D_PREMIUM`, `EXCHANGE_PREMIUM`, `DENTAL_VISION_PREMIUM` or `QUALIFIED_MEDICAL`. The account must be `OPEN`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_HRA_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN`. The `service_date` must fall inside the account's plan-year expense window, which runs from the plan year's `coverage_start` through one year later minus one day, inclusive at both ends; an expense outside it is refused with `ERR_EXPENSE_OUTSIDE_PLAN_YEAR`. And the account's `available_balance_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, so that at least part of the expense can be held; an account already drawn to zero refuses the claim with `ERR_HRA_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`.

The eligible amount of a claim is computed twice, under two variants that deliberately disagree. At submission the **provisional** variant runs: `provisional_eligible_cents` is the lesser of `submitted_cents` and the account's `available_balance_cents`, `eligible_cents` is set to that same figure, and the money is immediately moved — `pending_hold_cents` increases by the provisional amount and `available_balance_cents` decreases by it, so the account's `version` increments. The provisional variant caps only against the balance; it ignores the expense category's reimbursement percentage and the per-claim cap entirely. The claim opens with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `substantiation_resubmit_count` 0, `denial_reason_code` null, `paid_at` null, and `created_by_user_id` recorded from the submitting user's `X-User-ID`.

Adjudication is the `HraAdministrator`'s exclusive job, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `created_by_user_id`, a match being refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The administrator supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `REQUEST_SUBSTANTIATION` against a claim whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, and the account must still be `OPEN`. All three branches record `reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and increment the claim's `version`, and all three leave the ledger invariant holding.

- **`REQUEST_SUBSTANTIATION`** moves `status` from `SUBMITTED` to `PENDING_SUBSTANTIATION` and leaves the held amount exactly where it is, still counted in `pending_hold_cents`. Nothing is released, because the claim has not been rejected — it is merely waiting for paperwork.
- **`DENY`** moves `status` from `SUBMITTED` to `DENIED`, releases the whole held amount by decreasing `pending_hold_cents` by the claim's `eligible_cents` and increasing `available_balance_cents` by that same amount, then sets `eligible_cents` to 0, and stores `denial_reason_code` from the value supplied in the request, defaulting to `REVIEWER_DENIAL` when none is supplied. The declared reason codes are `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, `INELIGIBLE_CATEGORY`, `ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN` and `REVIEWER_DENIAL`. The account's `version` increments.
- **`APPROVE`** requires a non-null `documentation_reference` on the claim; approving an unsubstantiated claim is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. It recomputes the eligible amount under the **final** variant, rewrites `eligible_cents`, adjusts the hold accordingly, moves `status` from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`, and increments the account's `version`.

The final variant applies the category rule first and the balance rule second — the reverse of the provisional variant's single step — which is why identical claim data can yield a strictly different `eligible_cents` on approval than it did at submission. A `DENTAL_VISION_PREMIUM` expense is reimbursed at 5000 basis points, that is half of `submitted_cents`, truncated down to whole cents. A `QUALIFIED_MEDICAL` expense is allowed up to the lesser of `submitted_cents` and a per-claim cap of 50000 cents, five hundred dollars. Every other category is allowed at the full `submitted_cents`. Whichever of those three produced the category-allowed figure, the final `eligible_cents` is the lesser of that figure and the sum of the account's current `available_balance_cents` and the claim's own `provisional_eligible_cents` — the addition matters because the provisional amount is already sitting in `pending_hold_cents` and would otherwise be double-counted as unavailable. The difference between `provisional_eligible_cents` and the final `eligible_cents` is then unwound: `pending_hold_cents` decreases by that difference and `available_balance_cents` increases by it, so a final amount smaller than the provisional one returns the surplus to the balance and a final amount equal to it moves nothing.

A claim sent back for paperwork is completed by the `Retiree` or an `EnrollmentCounselor` supplying a `documentation_reference`; when the caller acts as a `Retiree`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `retiree_id` of the account the claim draws against. The account must still be `OPEN`. This moves `status` from `PENDING_SUBSTANTIATION` back to `SUBMITTED`, stores the supplied `documentation_reference`, and increments `substantiation_resubmit_count` by 1. The `SUBMITTED → PENDING_SUBSTANTIATION → SUBMITTED` documentation cycle is repeatable without bound, and the hold stays untouched throughout it.

Settlement is the `BillingClerk`'s job, driven by an `action` of `PAY` or `REVERSE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `reviewed_by_user_id`; a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The account must be `OPEN` for either action. `PAY` requires `status` `APPROVED` and moves it to `PAID`, sets `paid_at` to the logical clock, and moves the claim's `eligible_cents` out of `pending_hold_cents` and into `substantiated_paid_cents`, leaving `available_balance_cents` untouched. `REVERSE` requires `status` `PAID` and moves it to `REVERSED`, clears `paid_at`, and moves the claim's `eligible_cents` out of `substantiated_paid_cents` and back into `available_balance_cents`. Reversal is the exact inverse of payment: a `PAY` followed by its `REVERSE` with nothing else intervening must leave `available_balance_cents` at precisely its pre-payment value, and both actions leave the five-bucket sum equal to `funded_cents`. Both increment the account's `version` and the claim's `version`.

Year-end close is what makes unused balance disappear, and it happens in a strict order. An `HraAdministrator` closes an account whose `status` is `OPEN` — a `FROZEN` or already-`CLOSED` account is refused with `ERR_HRA_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN` — and the governing plan year must be `OPEN` or `CLOSED`. First the account sheds its unfinished work: every claim on the account whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `PENDING_SUBSTANTIATION` or `APPROVED` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason_code` `ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN`, its held `eligible_cents` returning from `pending_hold_cents` to `available_balance_cents`, processed in `created_at` ascending order with each claim's `version` incrementing. Because that cascade runs first, `pending_hold_cents` is 0 by the time forfeiture is computed. Then forfeiture runs: the carryover cap is `annual_credit_cents` multiplied by the plan year's `carryover_cap_pct`, truncated down to whole cents; `forfeited_cents` is the excess of `available_balance_cents` over that cap, or 0 when the balance does not exceed the cap; and `available_balance_cents` is reduced by exactly `forfeited_cents`. Note carefully that the cap is a fraction of **this** plan year's `annual_credit_cents` alone — inherited `carryover_in_cents` does not raise it, so a retiree carrying a large balance forward will forfeit most of it a second time. Finally `status` moves from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`, `closed_at` is set to the logical clock, and the account's `version` increments. `CLOSED` is terminal, and the five buckets must still sum to `funded_cents` afterwards.

Coverage lapse can also take an account out of service temporarily rather than permanently. A lapsing enrollment freezes the `OPEN` account belonging to the same retiree and plan year, moving `status` from `OPEN` to `FROZEN`; reinstating that enrollment moves the account from `FROZEN` back to `OPEN`. The `OPEN → FROZEN → OPEN` cycle is legitimate and repeatable, and each hop increments the account's `version`. Freezing carries the same claim-denial cascade as closing does, for the same reason: an account that cannot pay must not hold money against claims it will never adjudicate.

### 3.8. Premium Billing, Payment and the Delinquency Chain

A `BillingClerk` issues one invoice per billing period per enrollment, naming `enrollment_id`, `billing_period_start` and `billing_period_end`. The plan year must be `OPEN`. The proposed period is tested against every non-`WRITTEN_OFF` invoice already on that enrollment, in `created_at` ascending order, under closed-interval semantics: the two collide when the proposed start is not later than the existing end and the existing start is not later than the proposed end, and the first collision refuses the request with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because both endpoints are inclusive, a new period beginning on the same instant a prior period ended is a collision — contiguous monthly billing must start strictly after the prior period's end. Written-off invoices are excluded from the candidate set entirely, which is what lets a reinstated enrollment be re-billed for a period whose earlier invoice was written off at lapse.

Assembling the invoice walks ten pinned steps, and the order is the specification — a rearrangement produces different cents.

1. `base_premium_cents` is the plan option's `monthly_premium_cents` as it stands at invoice creation.
2. `admin_fee_cents` is 350 basis points — three and a half percent — of `base_premium_cents`, truncated down to whole cents. The fee is deliberately **fee-exclusive**: it is a percentage of the base premium only, never of a total that already contains the fee. The circular fixed-point reading is not used, and the two differ visibly: a `base_premium_cents` of 74500 yields an `admin_fee_cents` of 2607 under the pinned rule, where the rejected fixed point would yield 2702.
3. `gross_premium_cents` is `base_premium_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`.
4. `employer_subsidy_bps` starts from the retiree's grandfather cohort: 8000 basis points, that is eighty percent, for a `GRANDFATHERED_A` retiree; 6000 basis points for a `TRANSITION_B` retiree; and 4000 basis points for a `STANDARD_C` retiree. A long-service bump then applies: when the retiree's service years — `credited_service_months` divided by 12 and truncated down — is at least 25, the rate rises by 1000 basis points, capped so that it never exceeds 10000 basis points. A `GRANDFATHERED_A` retiree with 25 or more service years therefore lands on 9000 basis points, and the cap only ever binds if a cohort rate were already at or above 9000.
5. `employer_subsidy_cents` is `gross_premium_cents` multiplied by `employer_subsidy_bps`, divided by 10000, and truncated down to whole cents.
6. `retiree_share_cents` is `gross_premium_cents` minus `employer_subsidy_cents`.
7. The available reimbursement balance is the `available_balance_cents` of the `OPEN` account belonging to the invoice's retiree and plan year, and it is treated as 0 when no such account exists. `hra_applied_cents` is the lesser of that available balance and `retiree_share_cents`.
8. `net_due_cents` is `retiree_share_cents` minus `hra_applied_cents`, and it is the amount the retiree must actually remit.
9. `due_date` is `billing_period_start` plus 30 days. `past_due_at` is `due_date` plus the plan year's `premium_grace_period_days`, and it is the instant at which the nightly sweep will age the invoice.
10. The invoice's `status` is `PAID` when `net_due_cents` is 0 — a fully subsidised and fully offset period requires no remittance — and `ISSUED` otherwise. `paid_cents` opens at 0.

When `hra_applied_cents` is greater than zero the invoice also moves money on the account: `available_balance_cents` decreases by `hra_applied_cents`, `premium_offset_cents` increases by the same amount, and the account's `version` increments. The five buckets still sum to `funded_cents`, because the offset is a transfer and not a spend.

The same waterfall serves a second purpose. When an approved election materialises an enrollment, steps 1 through 6 alone are run — with the available reimbursement balance forced to zero, so no offset is ever applied — and the resulting share is stored as the enrollment's `monthly_retiree_premium_cents`. That figure is the retiree's standing premium before any reimbursement dollars, which is why it does not move when the account's balance does.

Payments are posted by the `Retiree` or a `BillingClerk` supplying `amount_cents` against an invoice whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAST_DUE`; a `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` invoice accepts nothing further and refuses with `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_SETTLED`. The payment may not exceed the remaining balance, that is `net_due_cents` minus `paid_cents`, and an overpayment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. An accepted payment increases `paid_cents` by `amount_cents`; the invoice moves to `PARTIALLY_PAID` while `paid_cents` remains below `net_due_cents` and to `PAID` once the two are equal, and its `version` increments.

Full payment of an invoice that had aged into `PAST_DUE` unwinds delinquency. The owning enrollment's `consecutive_unpaid_periods` decrements by 1, and when it reaches 0 — equivalently, when no invoice on that enrollment is left in `PAST_DUE` — `billing_status` moves from `DELINQUENT` back to `CURRENT`, `delinquency_first_unpaid_period_start` is cleared to null, `coverage_status` moves from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, and the enrollment's `version` increments. This closes the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` coverage cycle.

Reinstatement is the heavier remedy, reserved for coverage that already lapsed, and only a `BillingClerk` may perform it. The enrollment must sit in `coverage_status` `TERMINATED` with `billing_status` `LAPSED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENROLLMENT_STATE`. The logical clock must not be later than the enrollment's `reinstatement_deadline`, or the request is refused with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. And the retiree must not be carrying a `PAST_DUE` invoice with a non-zero outstanding balance on any *other* enrollment, since buying back one coverage while another is in arrears would let a retiree cherry-pick which debt to service; such a request is refused with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_PREMIUM_BALANCE`. On success, `billing_status` moves from `LAPSED` to `REINSTATED`, `consecutive_unpaid_periods` resets to 0, `delinquency_first_unpaid_period_start` and `lapse_date` are cleared to null, `reinstatement_count` increments by 1, `coverage_status` returns to `ACTIVE` with `coverage_end_date` and `termination_reason` cleared to null, the plan option's `enrolled_count` increments by 1 with that option's `version` incrementing, the `FROZEN` account for the same retiree and plan year returns to `OPEN` with its own `version` incrementing, the Medicare secondary-payer determination is re-run to refresh `payer_order` and `msp_basis_code`, and the enrollment's `version` increments. Because `REINSTATED` is a distinct billing state rather than a return to `CURRENT`, a reinstated enrollment re-enters `DELINQUENT` on its next past-due invoice, and the `CURRENT → DELINQUENT → LAPSED → REINSTATED → DELINQUENT` cycle is fully reachable.

### 3.9. The Nightly Administration Sweep and the Accounting Liability Valuation

A `BenefitsAdmin` drives the scheduled daily benefits administration sweep, an operator capability that ages the whole programme forward against the current logical clock. It runs across every invoice, enrollment, plan option, reimbursement account and claim in the system with no plan-year filter, and it reports six counters in its response body: `processed_invoice_count`, `activated_enrollment_count`, `suspended_enrollment_count`, `lapsed_enrollment_count`, `applied_plan_change_count` and `recomputed_msp_count`. Its four phases run in a pinned order, each running to completion before the next begins.

**Phase 1 — activation.** Every enrollment whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING` and whose `coverage_effective_date` is at or before the clock moves to `ACTIVE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Each such enrollment is counted in `activated_enrollment_count`. Its `billing_status` is untouched.

**Phase 2 — the past-due sweep and the delinquency chain.** Every invoice whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and whose `past_due_at` is at or before the clock is processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each is counted in `processed_invoice_count`. Hop one moves the invoice itself to `PAST_DUE`. Hop two, caused by hop one, touches the owning enrollment: `consecutive_unpaid_periods` increments by 1, `delinquency_first_unpaid_period_start` is set to that invoice's `billing_period_start` if it was previously null and left alone otherwise, and `billing_status` moves from `CURRENT` or `REINSTATED` to `DELINQUENT`. Hop three, caused by hop two, moves `coverage_status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, and each such enrollment is counted in `suspended_enrollment_count`.

Hop four fires only when `consecutive_unpaid_periods` has reached 3 — three consecutive unpaid billing periods, roughly a quarter of arrears. Then `billing_status` moves from `DELINQUENT` to `LAPSED`, `lapse_date` is set to the logical clock, and `reinstatement_deadline` is set to the clock plus the plan year's `reinstatement_window_days`. In the same hop `coverage_status` becomes `TERMINATED` with `coverage_end_date` at the clock and `termination_reason` `PREMIUM_LAPSE`, the plan option's `enrolled_count` decrements by 1 and that option's `version` increments, and every `PAST_DUE` invoice on the enrollment moves to `WRITTEN_OFF` in `created_at` ascending order. Hop five is caused by hop four rather than by the invoice ageing: the `OPEN` reimbursement account for that retiree and plan year moves to `FROZEN`, its `version` incrementing. Hop six is caused by hop five: for that now-frozen account, every claim whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `PENDING_SUBSTANTIATION` or `APPROVED` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason_code` `ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN`, its held `eligible_cents` released from `pending_hold_cents` back into `available_balance_cents`, processed in `created_at` ascending order with each claim's `version` incrementing. Each lapsing enrollment is counted in `lapsed_enrollment_count`.

**Phase 3 — pending plan changes.** Every plan option whose `change_notice_issued_at` is non-null and whose `pending_change_effective_date` is at or before the clock is processed in `created_at` ascending order and counted in `applied_plan_change_count`. A `pending_change_type` of `PREMIUM_INCREASE` rewrites `monthly_premium_cents` to `pending_new_monthly_premium_cents`. A `pending_change_type` of `PLAN_TERMINATION` moves the option's `status` to `RETIRED` and terminates its book of business: every enrollment on that option whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED` with `coverage_end_date` at the clock and `termination_reason` `PLAN_TERMINATION`, in `created_at` ascending order, and `enrolled_count` is set to 0. Either way `pending_change_type`, `pending_change_effective_date` and `pending_new_monthly_premium_cents` are all cleared to null and the option's `version` increments. `RETIRED` is terminal for the availability dimension.

**Phase 4 — Medicare secondary-payer refresh.** Every enrollment whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` is re-evaluated against the eight-branch determination, and any enrollment whose freshly computed result differs from its stored `payer_order` or `msp_basis_code` has both fields rewritten, in `created_at` ascending order, and is counted in `recomputed_msp_count`. An enrollment whose result is unchanged is not rewritten and not counted. This phase is what makes an end-stage-renal-disease coordination period expire on schedule without anyone touching the retiree record.

Separately from the sweep, an `Actuary` runs the accounting liability valuation for a plan year, supplying a `method` of either `PROJECTED_UNIT_CREDIT` or `ATTRIBUTION_TO_FULL_ELIGIBILITY`. The valuation cohort is every retiree who holds an eligibility determination for that plan year whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `outcome` is `ELIGIBLE`, iterated in the retirees' `created_at` ascending order. `DRAFT`, `UNDER_APPEAL` and `SUPERSEDED` determinations are excluded, and so are `CERTIFIED` determinations whose `outcome` is `INELIGIBLE`. Enrollment state is irrelevant to the cohort — a certified-eligible retiree who never elected coverage is still measured.

For each retiree in the cohort, the expected postretirement benefit obligation is the plan year's `assumed_annual_subsidy_cents` multiplied by its `assumed_payment_years`, multiplied by 10000, divided by the sum of 10000 and the plan year's `discount_rate_bps`, and truncated down to whole cents — a single discount applied through the basis-point rate. Two service horizons then frame the attribution. The full-eligibility service horizon comes from the retiree's cohort: 15 years for a `GRANDFATHERED_A`, 20 years for a `TRANSITION_B`, and 25 years for a `STANDARD_C`. The assumed-retirement horizon is that full-eligibility horizon plus 5 further years of post-eligibility service. The retiree's own service years are `credited_service_months` divided by 12 and truncated down.

Under `PROJECTED_UNIT_CREDIT` the retiree's accumulated obligation is the expected obligation multiplied by the lesser of the retiree's service years and the assumed-retirement horizon, divided by the assumed-retirement horizon, truncated down — the obligation is attributed over service to the assumed retirement date, so the denominator is the full-eligibility horizon plus 5. Under `ATTRIBUTION_TO_FULL_ELIGIBILITY` it is the expected obligation multiplied by the lesser of the retiree's service years and the full-eligibility horizon, divided by the full-eligibility horizon, truncated down — the obligation is attributed to the full-eligibility date instead, so the denominator is smaller and the numerator saturates five years earlier. The two methods therefore report materially different obligations from identical inputs. The retiree's annual service cost is the expected obligation divided by the assumed-retirement horizon and truncated down when the retiree's service years are strictly below that horizon, and exactly 0 once they are at or above it.

Rounding order is pinned here too: each retiree's expected obligation, accumulated obligation and service cost are truncated individually, and only then summed across the cohort. Summing unrounded per-retiree values and truncating once at the end is not used. The plan year then records `measured_retiree_count` as the size of the cohort, `epbo_total_cents` as the sum of the per-retiree expected obligations, `apbo_total_cents` as the sum of the per-retiree accumulated obligations, `service_cost_total_cents` as the sum of the per-retiree service costs, `liability_method` as the requested method, and `liability_valued_at` as the logical clock at which the run executed. Each run overwrites the previous result, and the plan year's `version` increments.
