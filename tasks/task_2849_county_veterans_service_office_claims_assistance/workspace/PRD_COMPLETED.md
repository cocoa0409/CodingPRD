# County Veterans Service Office API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A County Veterans Service Office sits between a veteran and every benefit the veteran has earned. The office takes an intake, verifies separation paperwork, screens the household against a lattice of federal and state eligibility tests, prepares and files claims, develops the medical evidence those claims depend on, counsels appeal options once a decision letter arrives, administers a set of state programs funded from finite annual pools, runs outreach to veterans the ordinary channels never reach, and then reports its own outcomes upward to the state department. Today that work is spread over paper case files, spreadsheets and an accreditation binder, which means the office cannot answer two questions it is routinely asked: *how complete is the evidence on this claim right now*, and *which of our representatives has capacity to take the next case*.

This document specifies the service that replaces those artifacts. It is a compliance-and-audit system first and a workflow orchestrator second: nearly every write is gated on somebody's standing to perform it, on a document having been verified, on a deadline not having passed, or on a pool of money not having been over-committed. Three quantities in the system are conserved — a program's committed allocation, an examination site's booked slot count, and a representative's active designation count — and every forward operation that moves one of them has a compensating operation that moves it back by exactly the same amount. Segregation of duties is structural rather than advisory: the accredited representative who prepares a claim can never be the one who peer-reviews it or marks it ready to file, and determination on a state benefit application belongs to a different role entirely.

The API is mounted under `/cvso`. All time-dependent behaviour — appeal deadlines, evidence timeliness windows, examination buffers, quarter-close rules, accreditation expiry — is evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that the office's rules can be exercised deterministically. Fourteen resource families, eleven lifecycles and sixteen named calculations are described below; the endpoint surface in §4 and the error catalogue in §5 are the mechanical summary of the narrative in §2 and §3, not a substitute for it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent, empty, or composed entirely of whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined.

Exactly five roles exist, and `X-User-Role` must spell one of them exactly: `SystemAdmin`, `IntakeClerk`, `CountyOfficer`, `StateAuditor`, `VeteranClient`. Any other value — including a misspelling or a differently-cased variant — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401). A syntactically valid role that does not appear in the authorized-role list of the addressed endpoint (see the table in §4) is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles divide the office's work as follows. A `SystemAdmin` is the office system administrator: they own the accreditation registry, the examination-site and state-program configuration, the disbursement ledger, the appeal-expiry sweep, report reopening, and the logical clock. A `SystemAdmin` never prepares or reviews a claim. An `IntakeClerk` is the front-desk intake worker: they open veteran records, upload and resubmit discharge documents, offer power-of-attorney designations, assign representatives and file state benefit applications, but cannot verify a document, prepare a claim or adjudicate anything. A `CountyOfficer` is an accredited county veterans service officer: they verify documents, screen veterans, prepare and file claims, develop evidence, book examinations, counsel appeal options and prepare the quarterly report. A `StateAuditor` is a state department reviewer: they adjudicate state benefit applications against program pools and accept or return the county's quarterly outcome report, and they write nothing in the claim workflow. A `VeteranClient` is the veteran themselves: they may accept or revoke their own power-of-attorney designation and withdraw their own decision review, and they may read their own veteran, designation, claim, application and review rows.

Two of the five roles are **entity-backed**, which is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `CountyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Representative`; ownership and authorship checks on any resource that references a `Representative` compare that resource's representative-bearing field to `X-User-ID` — this is how `prepared_by_representative_id`, `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id`, `verified_by_representative_id`, `counseled_by_representative_id` and `certified_by_representative_id` are populated and later compared. When `X-User-Role` is `VeteranClient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Veteran`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Veteran` compare the resource's `veteran_id` to `X-User-ID`. For either of these two roles, if `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing row of its backing entity — a `CountyOfficer` id that is not a `Representative` `id`, or a `VeteranClient` id that is not a `Veteran` `id` — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (HTTP 403). This check applies to every endpoint whose authorized-role list includes `CountyOfficer` or `VeteranClient`.

The remaining three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may accept and log it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `IntakeClerk`, and again when `X-User-Role` is `StateAuditor`: in all three cases `X-User-ID` need only be present and non-empty, and no existence check is performed against it.

Ownership is enforced narrowly and precisely for the veteran. A `VeteranClient` may only act on rows belonging to the veteran their `X-User-ID` identifies: on a power-of-attorney acceptance or revocation, the addressed designation's `veteran_id` must equal `X-User-ID`; on a decision-review status action, the `veteran_id` of the claim the review hangs off must equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch on any of those three operations is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When the addressed designation or review does not exist at all the owning veteran cannot be resolved, so the ownership comparison is skipped rather than failed and the request falls through to the band-3 404 of §2.11.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with 24 lowercase hexadecimal characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, a non-integer `limit`, a negative `offset` or a non-integer `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422); this rule applies to every GET endpoint in §4, single-resource reads included — a single-resource read validates the two parameters and then ignores them — while a POST or PATCH ignores `limit` and `offset` entirely and never rejects them.

Unless a subsection below states otherwise, list results are returned in ascending `created_at` order, so the oldest row of the filtered set appears first and the ordering is stable across pages; because the logical clock of §2.4 does not advance on its own, many rows share an identical `created_at`, and such ties are broken by the order in which the server created the rows, which makes the effective ordering plain ascending creation order in every case. A filter query parameter whose value cannot be read as the type of the field it narrows — a token other than `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` for a boolean filter, or a non-integer token for `fiscal_year` — matches no rows at all rather than being rejected; every other filter compares the parameter's raw string for exact equality against the stored value, and a query parameter the endpoint does not list is ignored.

The four pagination fields are `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective starting position actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is strictly less than `total_count`). They are reported in `meta` as shown in §2.2, and for list endpoints they are additionally mirrored in a sibling top-level `pagination` object carrying those same four keys with the same values. Single-resource reads carry neither the pagination fields in `meta` nor a `pagination` object.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

The server holds a single logical clock, and every time-dependent rule in this specification is evaluated against it: appeal deadlines, evidence timeliness, examination buffers and past-dating, accreditation expiry, quarter-close, minted case-number years, and every date or instant the server itself stamps onto a row. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It does not advance on its own between requests, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time.

A `SystemAdmin`-only test-driving capability moves the clock to any supplied instant, passed as `current_utc`; the response's `data` carries `current_utc` and nothing else, echoing the new value canonically formatted with exactly three fractional-second digits even when the supplied instant was finer-grained (the finer precision is retained internally for comparisons, only the echo is truncated). The clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards. Moving the clock has no retroactive effect on stored values, but it does change every quantity that is re-derived at read time — most visibly `days_remaining` on a decision review, which is recomputed against the clock on every read of the row.

Two representations are used throughout. A **date** field is an ISO 8601 calendar date formatted `YYYY-MM-DD`. A **datetime** field is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits, ending in `Z`. Every datetime the server itself mints — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `verified_at`, `accepted_at`, `submitted_at` and the clock echo — carries exactly three fractional-second digits, while the only two caller-supplied instant fields, an examination appointment's and an outreach event's `starts_at` and `ends_at`, are stored and echoed back exactly as the caller wrote them and are never re-rendered; on input the fractional-second part may be omitted entirely or carry any number of digits, of which only the first six are significant, and the `T` and `Z` separators are accepted in either letter case. A date field that does not match `YYYY-MM-DD` exactly, or a datetime field that is not an ISO 8601 UTC instant ending in `Z` — including one carrying a numeric UTC offset in place of `Z`, or a value that is not a string at all — is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

### 2.5. Record Identity, Versioning and Audit Fields

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, assigned at creation and never reused. Clients never supply an `id`.

Thirteen of the fourteen resource families additionally carry three system-managed audit fields. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the most recent mutation of the row; at creation it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the row's owner did not initiate, such as a recomputed derived figure, a state change driven by a cascade from another entity, or a counter adjustment made to keep one of the conserved ledgers in §2.8 balanced. Whenever `version` increments, `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant. A `PATCH` correction endpoint counts as a mutation unconditionally: it increments `version` and refreshes `updated_at` even when the body omits every correctable field, or supplies values identical to the ones already stored.

The one exception is the eligibility screen. Screen rows are append-only historical records: once written they are never mutated, so a screen carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither field should appear in a screen payload. Every other resource family — representatives, veterans, discharge documents, powers of attorney, claims, evidence items, examination sites, examination appointments, decision reviews, state programs, state benefit applications, outreach events and outcome reports — carries all four.

No client may write a field the server owns. Any request body containing a field whose value the server computes or assigns — every derived total, every counter, every lifecycle status, every server-stamped timestamp or date — is rejected with `ERR_READONLY_FIELD` (HTTP 422) rather than being silently ignored. This applies to every creating and amending endpoint. The set of server-owned names is fixed system-wide rather than per-endpoint, with one exception: where the addressed endpoint itself declares such a field among its own inputs, that field is accepted there and refused everywhere else. A body key that is neither one of the addressed endpoint's declared inputs nor a server-owned name is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.6. Standing to Act: Accreditation as a Precondition

Because a `CountyOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` is a `Representative` `id`, the office can and does check that representative's professional standing on every single officer action. Two independent checks apply to every endpoint whose authorized-role list includes `CountyOfficer`.

First, the acting representative's `accreditation_status` must read `ACTIVE`. A representative whose accreditation is `PENDING` (registered but never activated), `LAPSED` (activation expired without renewal) or `REVOKED` (terminated) cannot act at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_ACCREDITED` (HTTP 403). Second, and independently, the acting representative's `accreditation_expires_on` date must not be earlier than the logical-clock date. An expiry date that has already passed refuses the action with `ERR_ACCREDITATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 403) *even when* `accreditation_status` still reads `ACTIVE` — nothing sweeps the status field automatically, so a stale `ACTIVE` row with a past expiry is exactly the case this second check exists to catch.

Deliberately, letting an accreditation lapse is cascade-free: a `LAPSED` representative keeps every designation and every claim they already hold, and the only observable consequence is that each of their attempted writes is refused. Revocation is the opposite: it detonates a five-hop cascade described in §3.1.

### 2.7. Amounts, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

Money is carried in **integer cents** everywhere in this system, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. There is no decimal money type and no currency string; a household income of two hundred dollars is the integer 20000. Because every monetary constant in the specification is itself an integer number of cents, ordinary cents arithmetic requires no rounding at all — the retroactive-award computation, for example, multiplies a whole number of months by a whole number of cents.

Where rounding is nonetheless required, the method and the number of places are fixed per quantity and must not be generalised:

- A claim's `evidence_completeness_pct` is carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up, and the rounding is applied **twice** — once to each contributing evidence item's `weight_points`, and once to the resulting percentage. The order is pinned; see §3.5.
- An evidence item's `weight_points` is carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up.
- An outcome report's `performance_score` is carried to exactly 1 decimal place, rounded half up, and the rounding is applied exactly once, to the final percentage.
- A veteran's `combined_rating_pct` is combined at full precision and rounded exactly once at the end, half up, to the nearest multiple of 10. It is always a multiple of 10 between 0 and 100 inclusive.
- On a state benefit approval, the property-exemption product is rounded **half up** to whole cents while the administration fee is rounded **up** (ceiling) to whole cents. The two rounding modes are deliberately different and must not be unified.
- A representative's `caseload_weight` is exact integer arithmetic reported in a numeric field: its value is always an exact integer even though the field's type is numeric.
- Wartime service days, examination-site slot counts and active designation counts are whole numbers compared for exact equality, with no tolerance. Interval and buffer arithmetic is in whole minutes.

Every rounded percentage and score named above travels as a JSON **number**, never as a quoted string, and it is rendered in the shortest form that reproduces the rounded value — so a `weight_points` of 20.00 appears on the wire as `20.0`, an `evidence_completeness_pct` of 0.00 as `0.0`, and a `performance_score` of 100.0 as `100.0`. The decimal places fixed above govern the rounding, not the count of digits printed. Every threshold comparison in this document states its own inclusivity explicitly, and those inclusivities are load-bearing. Where a rule says "at least", the boundary value passes; where it says "strictly less than", the boundary value fails.

### 2.8. Three Conserved Quantities

The system maintains three running aggregates that are not caches but invariants: after every operation, each must equal the aggregate recomputed from scratch over its defining row set. Every forward move has an exactly-equal compensating move, so a forward-plus-compensating pair leaves the aggregate at its original value with zero drift.

**The program pool.** A state program's `allocated_cents` equals the sum of `benefit_amount_cents` over every state benefit application referencing that program whose `disbursement_status` is either `PENDING` or `ISSUED`. Applications whose disbursement dimension reads `NOT_APPLICABLE` — never approved, or denied — and applications that have been `CLAWED_BACK` are excluded. Approving an application raises `allocated_cents` by exactly that application's `benefit_amount_cents`; every claw-back, whether operator-driven or triggered by a rating cascade, lowers it by exactly the same figure. Comparison is exact integer cents.

**The examination slot count.** An examination site's `booked_slot_count` equals the count of appointments at that site whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`. Appointments that are `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` are excluded. Booking adds exactly one slot; every terminal transition — completion, no-show, operator cancellation, or a cancellation arriving through the representation cascade — releases exactly one. Comparison is exact whole slots.

**The active designation count.** A representative's `active_poa_count` equals the count of powers of attorney whose `representative_id` is that representative and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Designations in `OFFERED`, `REVOKED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded. Office-wide, the sum of `active_poa_count` across all representatives equals the total number of `ACTIVE` designations in the system. Comparison is exact whole designations.

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation and Hierarchical Consistency

Two operations in this system are transitive cascades, and both are specified hop by hop in §3 rather than being left to implementation judgement: revoking an accreditation propagates five hops (§3.1), and recording an adjudicated decision propagates four (§3.7). Several narrower propagations also exist — a veteran's death withdraws their in-preparation claims and appends a survivor screen, a designation revocation reverts the claims it supported, a claim withdrawal cancels its examinations and waives its outstanding evidence requests.

Three rules govern all of them. First, cascades are applied immediately and synchronously within the triggering request; there is no deferred settlement and no eventual consistency. Second, every row a cascade touches has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been written directly — a client can therefore detect that a row it holds is stale purely from the version having moved. Third, a cascade only touches rows that its stated from-state filter admits: a hop that says it reverts claims in `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW` or `READY_TO_FILE` leaves a `FILED`, `DECIDED` or `WITHDRAWN` claim of the same veteran alone, and changes the status of no `DRAFT` claim either.

Derived values are refreshed as part of the same request that invalidates them. When a claim changes status, the preparing representative's `caseload_weight` is recomputed before the response is written. When an evidence item changes status, the parent claim's `evidence_completeness_pct` is recomputed. When a decision is recorded, the veteran's `combined_rating_pct` and `priority_group` are recomputed. A client never has to poke a resource to make its derived fields correct.

### 2.10. Stale-Version Guards on Two Writes

Two writes in this system carry an optimistic-concurrency guard, because in both cases a second sequential write would otherwise succeed while reasoning from a parent row the client has not actually observed.

Accepting a power-of-attorney designation is an optimistic-concurrency write against the `Veteran` row. The request must carry `expected_veteran_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the veteran the designation names. If that value does not equal the veteran's current `version` at the moment of the write, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VETERAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing at all is changed. The reason this guard is the only deterministic protection available is that acceptance is legitimately a create-with-supersede operation: a new designation is *supposed* to displace the prior one, so there is no "already active" state to refuse. The stale-version check is what stops a client from superseding a designation it never saw.

Creating a state benefit application is an optimistic-concurrency write against the `StateProgram` row. The request must carry `expected_program_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the program being applied against. If that value does not equal the program's current `version` at the moment of the write, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created. A program legitimately accepts many applications against the same annual pool, so nothing about a second create is intrinsically wrong — what is wrong is filing it while reasoning from an `allocated_cents` figure that an intervening determination has already moved. Retuning a program's configuration increments its `version` and therefore invalidates any `expected_program_version` a client captured earlier.

On both of these two writes, and only on these two, the version field must be present and must be an integer of at least 1; a missing or malformed version field is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422) before the value is compared. No other endpoint in the system accepts or requires a version field.

### 2.11. Rejection Precedence

Many requests fail more than one rule at once, and the code the client receives must be deterministic. The server therefore evaluates rules in a fixed global order and returns the **first** failure it encounters, stopping immediately. The order is by band, and within each band by the sequence given here.

**Band 1 — authentication and authorization.** (1) Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401), on every endpoint. (2) `X-User-Role` is exactly one of the five role names, else `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (401), on every endpoint. (3) The supplied role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorized-role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), on every endpoint. (4) For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing row of its backing entity, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (403), on every endpoint whose role list includes `CountyOfficer` or `VeteranClient`. (5) A `VeteranClient` acts only on their own rows, else `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_OWNER` (403), on power-of-attorney acceptance, power-of-attorney revocation and decision-review status actions.

**Band 2 — request shape and read-only discipline.** The body is parsed before any rule in this band runs, and it is parsed regardless of the request's `Content-Type`: an absent or empty body is treated as an empty JSON object, while a body that is not valid JSON, or that parses to anything other than a JSON object, is `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). (10) Every field the addressed endpoint declares without a default is present and non-null, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (422), on every POST and PATCH endpoint. (11) Every enum-typed field carries one of the values declared for it, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (422), on every POST and PATCH endpoint. (12) The `action` field carries one of the values the addressed dispatch endpoint declares, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTION` (422); this applies to the eleven dispatch endpoints — `POST /cvso/representatives/{id}/accreditation`, `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/status`, `POST /cvso/discharge-documents/{id}/review`, `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/transitions`, `POST /cvso/evidence-items/{id}/status`, `POST /cvso/exam-appointments/{id}/status`, `POST /cvso/decision-reviews/{id}/status`, `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications/{id}/determination`, `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications/{id}/disbursement`, `POST /cvso/outreach-events/{id}/status` and `POST /cvso/outcome-reports/{id}/review`. An absent or null `action` is itself an unrecognised value and reports `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTION`, not a missing-field `ERR_VALIDATION`. Two further checks are evaluated at this same point, immediately after the action value has been recognised and therefore ahead of rules 13 through 22: where a dispatch endpoint restricts an individual `action` to a subset of its own authorized roles, that per-action restriction is reported as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) here rather than in band 1; and any field the endpoint declares as required only for the chosen action must be present and non-null, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). (13) Date-typed fields match `YYYY-MM-DD` and datetime-typed fields are ISO 8601 UTC instants ending in `Z`, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (422), on every POST and PATCH endpoint. (14) Integer fields respect their declared ranges, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (422), on every POST and PATCH endpoint: `household_income_cents`, `net_worth_cents`, `annual_pool_cents`, `max_award_cents` and `assessed_property_value_cents` are at least 0; `dependent_count` is from 0 through 12; `tuition_credits_requested` is from 0 through 30; `concurrent_capacity` is at least 1; `buffer_minutes` is from 0 through 120; `fiscal_year` is from 2020 through 2100; `min_rating_pct` and `awarded_rating_pct` are multiples of 10 from 0 through 100; and `ce_credits`, `veterans_contacted` and `veterans_enrolled` are at least 0. An integer field must carry a JSON integer, so a boolean, a fractional number or a string of digits fails here whatever its magnitude. This same rule number also carries the declared string and boolean shapes: `requires_wartime` and `underserved_flag` must be JSON booleans; `full_name` and a site's `name` hold 1 to 120 characters, `accreditation_number` and `program_code` 1 to 40, `county_code` 2 to 5, and `location_name`, `program_name` and `provider_name` 1 to 160, with the lower bound applied to the value once surrounding whitespace is stripped and the upper bound to the value exactly as supplied; and every field whose name ends in `_id`, together with `reason`, `rework_note` and `fiscal_quarter`, must be a non-blank string — so a `fiscal_quarter` that is not a string at all fails here as `ERR_VALIDATION` before rule 19 ever examines its shape. (15) `limit` is an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` is an integer of at least 0, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422), on every GET endpoint, single-resource reads included. This one rule is the sole exception to the band ordering that follows: on a GET whose path carries an `{id}`, the band-3 existence resolution of that identifier runs *before* rule 15, so a request pairing an unusable `limit` or `offset` with an unknown id reports that entity's 404 and not `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Against every band from 4 onward — including the two accreditation gates numbered 42 and 54 — rule 15 keeps its band-2 precedence normally. (16) `claimed_conditions` is an array of at least 1 and at most 8 non-blank entries, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (422), on `POST /cvso/claims` and `PATCH /cvso/claims/{id}`. (17) `ends_at` is strictly greater than `starts_at`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422), on `POST /cvso/exam-appointments` and `POST /cvso/outreach-events`. (18) A service span does not end before it starts — `service_end_date` is not earlier than `service_start_date` on a veteran, and `claimed_service_end` is not earlier than `claimed_service_start` on a discharge document — else `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_DATES` (422), on `POST /cvso/veterans`, `PATCH /cvso/veterans/{id}`, `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/discharge-documents` and `POST /cvso/discharge-documents/{id}/resubmit`. (19) `fiscal_quarter` is four digits followed by the letter Q followed by a single digit from 1 through 4, else `ERR_INVALID_FISCAL_QUARTER` (422), on `POST /cvso/outcome-reports`. (20) `veterans_enrolled` is less than or equal to `veterans_contacted`, else `ERR_ENROLLED_EXCEEDS_CONTACTED` (422), on an outreach `HOLD`. (21) No server-owned field appears in a write body unless the addressed endpoint itself declares that name among its own inputs (see §2.5), else `ERR_READONLY_FIELD` (422), on every POST and PATCH endpoint. (22) On the two optimistic-concurrency writes, the expected-version field is present and is an integer of at least 1, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (422), on `POST /cvso/powers-of-attorney/{id}/accept` and `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`. Finally, any further shape rule a §3 workflow states for one particular endpoint — a supplied date bounded above by the logical-clock date, a minimum continuing-education credit count, an awarded rating that must agree with the decision outcome — is evaluated at the very end of this band, after rule 22 and still ahead of band 3, and every such rule reports `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).

**Band 3 — existence, resolved left to right.** Every `{id}` path segment is resolved in left-to-right path order, and each identifier reference in the request body is resolved after the path. The first unresolvable reference decides the code, and the code is the entity-specific one wherever the addressed entity has one. (30) The generic fallback `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (404) covers a path id on an entity that has no more specific code and any body identifier reference not covered by a rule below — notably `reviewer_representative_id` on `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/transitions`, which the representative-specific rule does not list. (31) A `veteran_id` in the path or body resolves to an existing veteran, else `ERR_VETERAN_NOT_FOUND` (404), on `GET /cvso/veterans/{id}`, `PATCH /cvso/veterans/{id}`, `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/screen`, `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/discharge-documents`, `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/assign-representative`, `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/status`, `GET /cvso/veterans/{id}/eligibility-screens`, `POST /cvso/powers-of-attorney`, `POST /cvso/claims` and `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`. (32) A `representative_id` in the path or body resolves to an existing representative, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on `GET /cvso/representatives/{id}`, `PATCH /cvso/representatives/{id}`, `POST /cvso/representatives/{id}/accreditation`, `GET /cvso/representatives/{id}/outreach-events`, `GET /cvso/representatives/{id}/caseload`, `POST /cvso/powers-of-attorney`, `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/assign-representative` and `POST /cvso/outreach-events`. (33) A `claim_id` in the path or body resolves to an existing claim, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` (404), on `GET /cvso/claims/{id}`, `PATCH /cvso/claims/{id}`, `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/transitions`, `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/record-decision`, `GET /cvso/claims/{id}/evidence-items`, `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/evidence-items`, `POST /cvso/exam-appointments` and `POST /cvso/decision-reviews`. (34) A `poa_id` in the body resolves to an existing designation, else `ERR_POA_NOT_FOUND` (404), on `POST /cvso/claims` and on the designation read and its accept and revoke actions. (35) An `exam_site_id` in the body resolves to an existing site, else `ERR_EXAM_SITE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on `POST /cvso/exam-appointments`, `GET /cvso/exam-sites/{id}` and `PATCH /cvso/exam-sites/{id}`. (36) A `program_id` in the body resolves to an existing program, else `ERR_STATE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (404), on `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`, `GET /cvso/state-programs/{id}` and `PATCH /cvso/state-programs/{id}`. The remaining entity-specific 404 codes cover an unresolvable path id on their own entity: `ERR_DISCHARGE_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a discharge document, `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` for an evidence item, `ERR_EXAM_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an examination appointment, `ERR_DECISION_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for a decision review, `ERR_BENEFIT_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` for a state benefit application, `ERR_OUTREACH_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` for an outreach event and `ERR_OUTCOME_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` for an outcome report — each also HTTP 404.

**Band 4 — state preconditions, including the two stale-version guards.** (40) The addressed row is in a state from which the requested action is declared; any other from-state, and any action applied to a terminal state, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409), on every action endpoint that drives a lifecycle. (41) The veteran the write hangs off is not in `intake_status` `DECEASED`, else `ERR_VETERAN_DECEASED` (409), on `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/discharge-documents`, `POST /cvso/powers-of-attorney`, `POST /cvso/powers-of-attorney/{id}/accept`, `POST /cvso/claims`, `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/transitions` and `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/assign-representative`. (42) The acting representative's `accreditation_status` is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_ACCREDITED` (403), on every endpoint whose role list includes `CountyOfficer`. (43) The veteran's `discharge_doc_status` is `VERIFIED`, else `ERR_DISCHARGE_NOT_VERIFIED` (409), on `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/screen` and `POST /cvso/claims`. (44) The designation named by `poa_id` has `status` `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_POA_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on `POST /cvso/claims` and `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/transitions`. (45) `expected_veteran_version` equals the veteran's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_VETERAN_VERSION` (412), on `POST /cvso/powers-of-attorney/{id}/accept`. (46) `expected_program_version` equals the referenced program's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (412), on `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`. (47) The veteran has at least one eligibility screen whose `basic_eligibility` is true, else `ERR_NOT_SCREENED` (409), on `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/assign-representative`. (48) The outcome report has been computed at least once, meaning its `weighting_basis` is not null, else `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED` (409), on `POST /cvso/outcome-reports/{id}/submit`. (49) The veteran's `intake_status` matches the program type applied for — `SCREENED` or `ACTIVE` for a `PROPERTY_TAX_EXEMPTION` or `TUITION_WAIVER` application, and `DECEASED` for a `BURIAL_BENEFIT` application — else `ERR_VETERAN_STATUS_INELIGIBLE` (409), on `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`.

**Band 5 — safety, compliance and segregation of duties.** (50) `reviewer_representative_id` differs from the claim's `prepared_by_representative_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409), on a claim `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW`. (51) No evidence item of the claim is left in an open status — none in `REQUESTED` when the claim is submitted for peer review, and none in `INSUFFICIENT` when the claim is filed — else `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_OUTSTANDING` (409), on a claim `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW` and on a claim `FILE`. (52) `prep_session_completed` is true, else `ERR_EXAM_PREP_INCOMPLETE` (409), on an appointment `COMPLETE`. (53) A veteran whose `homeless_status` is `SHELTERED` or `UNSHELTERED` may not be deactivated, else `ERR_HOMELESS_VETERAN_PROTECTED` (409), on a veteran `DEACTIVATE`. (54) The acting representative's `accreditation_expires_on` is not earlier than the logical-clock date, else `ERR_ACCREDITATION_EXPIRED` (403), on every endpoint whose role list includes `CountyOfficer`.

**Band 6 — temporal rules.** (55) The decision review's `days_remaining` is not negative, else `ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED` (409), on a review `ELECT` and on a review `FILE`. (56) `starts_at` is at or after the logical-clock instant, else `ERR_APPOINTMENT_IN_PAST` (409), on `POST /cvso/exam-appointments`. (57) `decided_on` is not earlier than the claim's `filed_on` and not later than the logical-clock date, else `ERR_DECISION_DATE_INVALID` (409), on `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/record-decision`. (58) `intent_to_file_date` is not later than the logical-clock date and not earlier than the veteran's `service_end_date`, else `ERR_INTENT_TO_FILE_DATE_INVALID` (409), on `POST /cvso/claims` and `PATCH /cvso/claims/{id}`. (59) The logical-clock date is later than the last calendar day of the report's `fiscal_quarter`, else `ERR_QUARTER_NOT_CLOSED` (409), on `POST /cvso/outcome-reports/{id}/compute`.

**Band 7 — referential agreement between two rows that already exist.** (60) The designation named by `poa_id` belongs to the same veteran as the claim, else `ERR_POA_VETERAN_MISMATCH` (409), on `POST /cvso/claims`. (61) The application's `fiscal_year` equals the referenced program's `fiscal_year`, else `ERR_FISCAL_YEAR_MISMATCH` (409), on `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`. (62) `reviewer_representative_id` is not the representative designated on the claim's power of attorney, else `ERR_REVIEWER_IS_DESIGNATED_REPRESENTATIVE` (409), on a claim `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW`. (63) The money-bearing input matches the program type, else `ERR_PROGRAM_INPUT_MISMATCH` (422), on `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`. (64) `nexus_opinion_strength` agrees with `evidence_type`, else `ERR_NEXUS_STRENGTH_MISMATCH` (422), on `POST /cvso/claims/{id}/evidence-items`.

**Band 8 — constraint satisfaction.** (65) `evidence_completeness_pct` is at least the ready-to-file threshold, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (409), on a claim `MARK_READY`. (66) The overlapping scheduled-block count at the site is strictly less than `concurrent_capacity`, else `ERR_EXAM_SITE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on `POST /cvso/exam-appointments`. (67) No planned or held outreach event of the same representative has a travel-buffered block overlapping the requested one, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_TRAVEL_CONFLICT` (409), on `POST /cvso/outreach-events`. (68) When `representative_id` is omitted, the automatic selector finds at least one candidate, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REPRESENTATIVE` (409), on `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/assign-representative`. (69) The representative's `active_poa_count` is strictly less than the per-representative designation ceiling, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_CASELOAD_FULL` (409), on `POST /cvso/powers-of-attorney/{id}/accept` and `POST /cvso/veterans/{id}/assign-representative`. (70) `accreditation_number` is not already held by another representative, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCREDITATION_NUMBER` (409), on `POST /cvso/representatives` and `PATCH /cvso/representatives/{id}`. (71) `program_code` is not already held by another program, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE` (409), on `POST /cvso/state-programs` only — `program_code` is not a retunable input, so the rule has nothing to test on `PATCH /cvso/state-programs/{id}` (§3.10). (72) `fiscal_quarter` is not already held by another outcome report, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_FISCAL_QUARTER` (409), on `POST /cvso/outcome-reports`. (73) No other state benefit application exists with the same `veteran_id`, `program_id` and `fiscal_year` whose `determination_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_BENEFIT_APPLICATION` (409), on `POST /cvso/state-benefit-applications`.

**Band 9 — fiscal capacity, evaluated last.** (74) The veteran's `combined_rating_pct` is at least the program's `min_rating_pct`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_MIN_RATING_NOT_MET` (409), on a state benefit application `APPROVE`. (75) When the program's `requires_wartime` is true, the veteran's `wartime_service_days` meets the wartime minimum, else `ERR_PROGRAM_WARTIME_REQUIRED` (409), on a state benefit application `APPROVE`. (76) The program's `allocated_cents` plus the newly computed `benefit_amount_cents` is at most its `annual_pool_cents`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (409), on a state benefit application `APPROVE`.

The practical consequence of this ordering is worth stating plainly. Authorization always beats shape, shape always beats existence (the single exception being pagination validation on an id-bearing GET, noted at rule 15), existence always beats state, state always beats safety, and money is checked last — so an unaccredited officer submitting a malformed body sees `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_ACCREDITED` only after the body has cleared band 2, while an approval that both misses the rating floor and would exhaust the pool always reports `ERR_PROGRAM_MIN_RATING_NOT_MET` rather than `ERR_PROGRAM_POOL_EXHAUSTED`.

### 2.12. Nested Reads and the Three Reverse Lookups

Most write endpoints in this system address their own top-level collection and carry their foreign keys in the request body; only three read paths are nested under a parent. A veteran's append-only screen history is read under the veteran, a claim's evidence file is read under the claim, and a representative's outreach calendar is read under the representative. In all nested paths, identifiers are validated strictly left to right: a request for evidence items under a claim id that does not exist reports `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and never looks at the query parameters.

Two of those three nested reads are the *only* discovery channel for rows the system synthesizes on its own. The survivor eligibility screen appended when a veteran's death is recorded is visible only through the veteran's screen history. The examination-derived evidence item created when an appointment is completed is visible only through the claim's evidence file or through the cross-claim evidence list. Any client that needs to observe those rows must read them there.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Accredited Representative Roster

Nobody can act on a veteran's behalf in this system without an entry in the accreditation registry, and the registry is owned exclusively by the `SystemAdmin`. Registering a representative captures four inputs: the person's legal `full_name`, between 1 and 120 characters; their state accreditation registry number `accreditation_number`, between 1 and 40 characters and unique across the whole roster; the `accreditation_type` naming the channel under which they may represent claimants, one of `COUNTY_CVSO`, `VSO_ORG`, `ATTORNEY` or `CLAIMS_AGENT`; and `accreditation_expires_on`, the calendar date on which the accreditation lapses. A fresh row lands in `accreditation_status` `PENDING` with `ce_credits_earned` at 0, `active_poa_count` at 0, `caseload_weight` at 0, and `revocation_reason` present in the payload but null. Reusing an `accreditation_number` another representative already holds — whatever that representative's `accreditation_status`, `REVOKED` included — is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCREDITATION_NUMBER`; the same uniqueness check runs again when registry details are corrected, and a correction may touch `full_name`, `accreditation_number`, `accreditation_type` or `accreditation_expires_on` but never a lifecycle field.

The accreditation lifecycle runs `PENDING → ACTIVE`, cycles `ACTIVE → LAPSED → ACTIVE` as often as renewals occur, and terminates at `REVOKED`, which is a terminal state from which nothing further is possible. Activation, named by the `action` value `ACTIVATE`, is available only from `PENDING`; it sets `accreditation_status` to `ACTIVE` and recomputes `caseload_weight`, which on a freshly activated row is 0 because the representative holds nothing yet. Lapsing, named `LAPSE`, is available only from `ACTIVE` and is deliberately cascade-free — it touches no designation and no claim, so the *only* observable effect is that every subsequent write attempt by that representative is refused, which is precisely how the accreditation gate in §2.6 can be observed in isolation. Renewal, named `RENEW`, returns a representative from `LAPSED` to `ACTIVE`, and it is the one accreditation action with substantive inputs of its own: the caller supplies `ce_credits`, which must be at least 12 to satisfy the biennial continuing-education requirement, and a fresh `accreditation_expires_on`, which must fall strictly after the logical-clock date. Both of those two breaches are reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422), and both are request-shape rules evaluated in band 2 — so they are decided before the addressed representative id is resolved and before the from-state check of §2.11 rule 40, meaning a `RENEW` carrying fewer than 12 credits against an unknown representative id reports `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_FOUND`. Renewal overwrites the stored `accreditation_expires_on` and copies the supplied credit count into `ce_credits_earned`, replacing any earlier total rather than adding to it. A representative may traverse the lapse-and-renew cycle any number of times.

Revocation, named `REVOKE`, is the roster's demolition charge. It is available from either `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`, it requires a `reason` which is stored as `revocation_reason`, and it propagates through five hops in a single synchronous request. **Hop one** sets `accreditation_status` to `REVOKED`, stores the reason, and — as a direct fan-out rather than part of the transitive chain — clears `assigned_representative_id` on every veteran whose case was assigned to this representative. **Hop two** follows from the accreditation now being `REVOKED`: every power of attorney naming this representative whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` set to the literal string `REPRESENTATIVE_ACCREDITATION_REVOKED`, and as a consequence this representative's `active_poa_count` falls to 0. **Hop three** follows from those designations having been revoked: every claim citing one of them whose `status` is `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW` or `READY_TO_FILE` falls back to `DRAFT` with both `poa_id` and `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id` set to null, while a claim already sitting in `DRAFT` has only its `poa_id` cleared and does not change status. **Hop four** follows from those claims having fallen back to `DRAFT`: every examination appointment belonging to one of them whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to the literal string `REPRESENTATION_CASCADE`. **Hop five** follows from those cancellations: each affected examination site has its `booked_slot_count` decremented by the number of appointments cancelled at that site, restoring the slot invariant of §2.8 exactly. Finally, `caseload_weight` is recomputed for the revoked representative and for every other representative whose claim set the cascade changed, and `version` is incremented by 1 on the representative and on every veteran, designation, claim, appointment and examination site the cascade touched.

A representative's `caseload_weight` is the ranking key the office uses to decide who takes the next case, and it is a pure function of what they currently hold. Each of the representative's `ACTIVE` designations contributes 1 point. Each claim hanging off one of those designations contributes points according to the stage it is in: 3 points while in `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, 5 points while in `PEER_REVIEW`, 2 points while in `READY_TO_FILE`, and 4 points while in `FILED`. Each outreach event assigned to the representative that is still in `PLANNED` contributes 2 points. Nothing else counts: a claim in `DRAFT`, `DECIDED` or `WITHDRAWN` contributes nothing, a claim whose `poa_id` is null contributes nothing even if the representative prepared it, and an outreach event that has been `HELD` or `CANCELLED` contributes nothing. The sum is a whole number of points computed with exact integer arithmetic, and it is refreshed on every designation acceptance, supersession or revocation, on every claim status change that enters or leaves the four open stages, and on every outreach event creation, hold or cancellation.

The workload behind that single number is also readable directly. A computed workload decomposition exposes exactly the terms the sum is built from: `representative_id`, `active_poa_count`, `open_claim_count` (how many claims under the representative's active designations sit in one of the four open stages), `planned_outreach_count` (how many of their outreach events are still `PLANNED`), the resulting `caseload_weight`, and `assignment_eligible` — a boolean stating whether this representative currently satisfies every filter the automatic assignment selector applies. The roster itself can be listed and narrowed by `accreditation_status` and by `accreditation_type`, and a representative's outreach calendar can be read back by `status`, both alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Intake and the Discharge Document File

Opening a case is front-desk work, available to an `IntakeClerk` and to a `CountyOfficer` alike. Intake captures the veteran's `full_name`, `date_of_birth`, the `service_start_date` and `service_end_date` as stated at the desk, the means-test pair `household_income_cents` and `net_worth_cents` (countable annual household income and countable net worth, both in integer cents and both at least 0), the `dependent_count` claimed for compensation and income-limit purposes (from 0 through 12, defaulting to 0), the `homeless_status` recording housing posture (`NOT_HOMELESS`, `AT_RISK`, `SHELTERED` or `UNSHELTERED`, defaulting to `NOT_HOMELESS`), and the `referral_source` recording how the veteran reached the office (`WALK_IN`, `OUTREACH_EVENT`, `JAIL_INREACH`, `SHELTER_REFERRAL` or `STATE_HOTLINE`, defaulting to `WALK_IN`). A separation date earlier than the corresponding entry date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_DATES`. No other relationship among the three captured dates is examined: `date_of_birth` is checked for calendar shape only, and none of the three is bounded by the logical-clock date, so a birth date or a service span lying in the clock's future is accepted.

The server mints the office case number itself. `county_case_number` takes the form `CVSO-` followed by the four-digit year, another hyphen, and a six-digit zero-padded sequence — for example `CVSO-2026-000001`. The year is the logical-clock year at the moment of creation. The sequence is the 1-based creation rank of this veteran among *all* veteran rows ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the very first veteran the office ever opens is number 1 regardless of year, the second is 2, and so on. Case numbers are unique and are never reused.

A brand-new intake record starts with `intake_status` at `INTAKE_STARTED`, `discharge_doc_status` at `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `character_of_service` at the placeholder `UNCHARACTERIZED` (meaning no verified document has yet established it), `wartime_service_days` at 0, `combined_rating_pct` at 0, `priority_group` at `P4`, `assigned_representative_id` unset and `date_of_death` unset. That `P4` is a literal starting band rather than a value derived from the housing posture and income just captured, so an intake taken for an `UNSHELTERED` veteran still reads `P4` until one of the re-derivation events listed in §3.3 occurs. Means-test and housing inputs can be corrected later — a correction may supply any of `full_name`, `date_of_birth`, `service_start_date`, `service_end_date`, `household_income_cents`, `net_worth_cents`, `dependent_count`, `homeless_status` and `referral_source` — and any correction immediately re-derives `priority_group` and bumps the veteran's `version`, which is what makes a fresh screen meaningful afterwards. When a correction supplies only one of `service_start_date` and `service_end_date`, the supplied value is compared against the stored counterpart and an inverted span is still `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_DATES`; that merged comparison is made only after the addressed veteran has been resolved, so a correction carrying it against an unknown veteran id reports `ERR_VETERAN_NOT_FOUND` instead. The veteran roll can be narrowed by `intake_status`, `priority_group`, `homeless_status`, `assigned_representative_id` and `discharge_doc_status`.

Separation paperwork is submitted against the veteran, and `veteran_id` is taken from the path rather than the body. A submission declares its `document_type` (`DD214`, `NGB22`, `DD215` or `STATEMENT_OF_SERVICE`), its `source` — the provenance of the copy, one of `VETERAN_PROVIDED`, `NPRC_RESPONSE` or `STATE_ARCHIVE` — and the three facts printed on the paper: `claimed_service_start`, `claimed_service_end` and `claimed_character_of_service`, the last being one of `HONORABLE`, `GENERAL`, `OTHER_THAN_HONORABLE`, `BAD_CONDUCT`, `DISHONORABLE` or `UNCHARACTERIZED`. The row lands in `verification_status` `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with `resubmission_count` at 0 and `discrepancy_reason` unset, and submitting it also pushes the veteran's `discharge_doc_status` to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and increments the veteran's `version`. That mirror field always reflects the `verification_status` of the veteran's document with the greatest `created_at` — ties on `created_at`, which are the norm because the logical clock does not advance on its own, being broken in favour of the document created last — and reads `NOT_SUBMITTED` while the veteran has no document at all. Neither claimed date is bounded by the logical-clock date either.

Adjudicating a document is `CountyOfficer` work and drives one of three outcomes, selected by the `action` value `VERIFY`, `HOLD_DISCREPANCY` or `REJECT`. **Verifying** a document (`VERIFY`) is available only from `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and additionally requires the acting representative's own accreditation to read `ACTIVE`. It sets `verification_status` to `VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at` with the logical-clock instant and `verified_by_representative_id` with the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, copies `claimed_character_of_service` onto the veteran's `character_of_service`, refreshes the veteran's `discharge_doc_status` under the newest-document rule stated above — which reads `VERIFIED` whenever the document just verified is itself the veteran's newest, and otherwise reports the newer document's own status — recomputes the veteran's `wartime_service_days` from the governing document described at the end of this subsection, re-derives the veteran's `priority_group`, and increments both the document's and the veteran's `version`. **Holding a discrepancy** (`HOLD_DISCREPANCY`) is also available only from `PENDING_VERIFICATION`; it requires a `reason`, which is stored as `discrepancy_reason`, moves the document to `DISCREPANCY_HELD`, refreshes the veteran's `discharge_doc_status` under that same newest-document rule, and bumps both versions. **Rejecting** a document (`REJECT`) is available from either `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `DISCREPANCY_HELD`, requires a `reason` stored as `discrepancy_reason`, moves the document to `REJECTED`, refreshes the veteran's mirror the same way, and — importantly — establishes no character of service at all, leaving the veteran's `character_of_service` at its pre-verification placeholder. Both `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

A document held for discrepancy can be corrected and put back in the queue, which is front-desk or officer work. Resubmission is available only from `DISCREPANCY_HELD`; it overwrites whichever of `claimed_service_start`, `claimed_service_end` and `claimed_character_of_service` the caller supplies, clears `discrepancy_reason`, increments `resubmission_count` by 1, returns the document to `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, refreshes the veteran's `discharge_doc_status` under the newest-document rule, and bumps both versions. Here too, when only one of the two claimed dates is supplied it is compared against the stored counterpart, and that merged comparison is made after the addressed document has been resolved. The hold-and-resubmit loop may run any number of times, and `resubmission_count` is the count of completed passes through it. The document file can be listed across the office and narrowed by `veteran_id`, `verified_by_representative_id`, `verification_status` and `document_type`.

Wartime service is counted from exactly one document — the veteran's document whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `created_at` is greatest, again breaking a `created_at` tie in favour of the document created last. Documents in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `DISCREPANCY_HELD` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing at all, so a veteran with no verified document has `wartime_service_days` of 0. The count is taken against three fixed statutory wartime windows, evaluated in this order: 28 February 1961 through 7 May 1975, 2 August 1990 through 11 April 1991, and 11 September 2001 through 30 August 2021. For each window in turn, take the later of the document's `claimed_service_start` and the window's opening date, and the earlier of the document's `claimed_service_end` and the window's closing date. If that later start is not after that earlier end, the overlap contributes the number of days from the start to the end plus one — the plus one making both ends inclusive — and otherwise contributes nothing. `wartime_service_days` is the sum of those contributions across all three windows. Because the three windows never overlap one another, no single day of service can be counted twice, and no rounding is ever involved: the result is a whole number of days. The figure is recomputed whenever a document is verified and is re-read by every eligibility screen.

### 3.3. Screening, Priority Banding and the Case Lifecycle

An eligibility screen is a `CountyOfficer` action that takes no request body at all and produces a new, permanently immutable screen record. The first screen drives the veteran from `INTAKE_STARTED` to `SCREENED` and requires both that the veteran is still in `INTAKE_STARTED` and that their `discharge_doc_status` reads `VERIFIED`; without the verified document the attempt is refused with `ERR_DISCHARGE_NOT_VERIFIED`. Screening can also be repeated: a re-screen is a `SCREENED → SCREENED` self-loop available while the veteran's `intake_status` is either `SCREENED` or `ACTIVE`, again requiring a `VERIFIED` document, and it exists so that a corrected income, net worth, dependent count or housing posture can be re-evaluated. A re-screen appends another record and leaves `intake_status` unchanged; the first screen additionally sets it to `SCREENED`. Either way the veteran's `priority_group` is re-derived from the fresh inputs and the veteran's `version` is incremented.

Each screen record carries the `veteran_id` it belongs to and a `screen_sequence`, the 1-based ordinal of this screen among that veteran's screens ordered by `created_at` ascending — so the first is 1 and every later screen is the previous maximum plus 1, with the value never below 1. Screens are append-only and are never mutated once written, which is why (per §2.5) they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`. Alongside the sequence, a screen freezes four derived values and one structured result. `basic_eligibility` is true exactly when the veteran's `character_of_service` is `HONORABLE` or `GENERAL`, and false for any other value. `income_limit_applied_cents` is the countable-income ceiling in force at screen time: eighteen thousand eight hundred forty dollars — 1884000 cents — plus four thousand eight hundred thirty dollars, or 483000 cents, for each dependent claimed. `wartime_service_days_at_screen` is the veteran's `wartime_service_days` frozen at that instant. `priority_group_assigned` is the band the screen assigned, one of `P1`, `P2`, `P3` or `P4`.

The structured result is `category_results`, a map from benefit category to an object with two keys, `eligible` (a boolean) and `reason` (a string, and the empty string on any category that came out true). It always carries exactly six keys, spelled `DISABILITY_COMPENSATION`, `PENSION`, `DEPENDENCY_INDEMNITY`, `STATE_PROPERTY_TAX_EXEMPTION`, `STATE_TUITION_WAIVER` and `BURIAL_BENEFIT`. Basic eligibility short-circuits the whole screen: when `basic_eligibility` is false, every one of the six categories is false. Otherwise each category is decided independently against the veteran's inputs and computed fields as of the screen instant, and no other entity is read:

- `DISABILITY_COMPENSATION` is eligible exactly when basic eligibility holds.
- `PENSION` is eligible when basic eligibility holds **and** the veteran's `wartime_service_days` is at least 90 days **and** `household_income_cents` is at most the income ceiling described above **and** `net_worth_cents` is at most one hundred fifty-five thousand sixty dollars, that is 15506000 cents.
- `DEPENDENCY_INDEMNITY` is eligible when basic eligibility holds **and** the veteran's `intake_status` is `DECEASED`.
- `STATE_PROPERTY_TAX_EXEMPTION` is eligible when basic eligibility holds **and** the veteran's `combined_rating_pct` is at least 30.
- `STATE_TUITION_WAIVER` is eligible when basic eligibility holds **and** `dependent_count` is at least 1.
- `BURIAL_BENEFIT` is eligible when basic eligibility holds **and** the veteran's `intake_status` is `DECEASED`.

For every category that comes out false, the `reason` string names the leftmost condition in that category's list that failed, spelled exactly as the field that conjunct tests — one of `basic_eligibility`, `wartime_service_days`, `household_income_cents`, `net_worth_cents`, `intake_status`, `combined_rating_pct` or `dependent_count` — so a living veteran with honourable service gets `intake_status` named as the reason on `BURIAL_BENEFIT`, while a veteran with an other-than-honourable discharge gets the basic-eligibility conjunct named on all six. `eligible_category_count` is simply how many of the six keys came out true, a value from 0 through 6.

Priority banding is evaluated strictly top to bottom, and the first branch that matches wins; the branches are not independent tests. A veteran lands in `P1` when their `homeless_status` is `UNSHELTERED` or `SHELTERED`, or when their `combined_rating_pct` is at least 50. Failing that, they land in `P2` when their `combined_rating_pct` is at least 30, or when their `homeless_status` is `AT_RISK`. Failing that, they land in `P3` when `household_income_cents` is at most the income ceiling for their dependent count. Failing all three, they land in `P4`, which is also the value a brand-new intake starts at. Both rating comparisons are inclusive, so a combined rating of exactly 50 yields `P1` and exactly 30 yields `P2`. The band is re-derived on every screen, on every correction of the veteran whatever fields that correction actually touches, on the verification of a discharge document, and on every recomputation of `combined_rating_pct`.

Beyond screening, a veteran's case moves through three further lifecycle actions, available to the front desk and to an officer. The three `action` values are `DEACTIVATE`, `REACTIVATE` and `RECORD_DEATH`. **Deactivation** (`DEACTIVATE`) takes an `ACTIVE` case to `INACTIVE`. It is blocked outright when the veteran's `homeless_status` is `SHELTERED` or `UNSHELTERED` — the office must keep the case open while housing is unresolved — and that refusal is `ERR_HOMELESS_VETERAN_PROTECTED`. **Reactivation** (`REACTIVATE`) takes an `INACTIVE` case back to `ACTIVE`, closing a cycle a case may traverse any number of times. **Recording a death** (`RECORD_DEATH`) is available from either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, requires a `date_of_death` that is not later than the logical-clock date, and takes the case to `DECEASED`, which is terminal. Recording a death does three things beyond setting `intake_status` and `date_of_death`: every claim of that veteran whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW` or `READY_TO_FILE` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` set to the literal string `CLAIMANT_DECEASED`; a fresh immutable screen record is appended for the veteran with `screen_sequence` equal to the previous maximum plus 1, recomputed under the rules above — and because the veteran's `intake_status` is now `DECEASED`, this is the screen on which `BURIAL_BENEFIT` and `DEPENDENCY_INDEMNITY` first come out true; and `version` is incremented on the veteran and on every withdrawn claim. The `caseload_weight` of each withdrawn claim's `prepared_by_representative_id` is recomputed as well, and that representative's `version` moves only where the recomputation actually changes one of their caseload terms. Nothing else is cascaded: the appointment-cancelling and evidence-waiving cleanup that a caller-driven withdrawal performs in §3.5 is **not** run here, so a `SCHEDULED` examination of a death-withdrawn claim stays `SCHEDULED` and keeps its slot, and an outstanding evidence request stays `REQUESTED`. That survivor screen is discoverable only by reading the veteran's screen history, which accepts nothing but `limit` and `offset`. The status action also accepts an optional `reason` for the office's own audit narrative.

Once a veteran is `DECEASED`, the case is closed to new work. Submitting a discharge document, offering or accepting a designation, opening a claim, driving a claim transition, or assigning a representative against a deceased veteran are all refused with `ERR_VETERAN_DECEASED`.

### 3.4. Designating Representation and Assigning a Case Officer

Two different things attach a representative to a veteran and they must not be confused. A **power-of-attorney designation** is the legal instrument that lets a representative prosecute claims; it is offered by the office and accepted by the veteran. An **assignment** is the office's internal allocation of the case to a case officer; it is made by the front desk and recorded on the veteran as `assigned_representative_id`.

Offering a designation is `IntakeClerk` or `CountyOfficer` work and captures `veteran_id`, `representative_id`, the `form_type` the veteran executed (`VA_21_22` for a service organisation or county officer, `VA_21_22A` for an individual), the `scope` of the designation (`ALL_BENEFITS` or `SPECIFIC_CLAIM`), and `executed_on`, the date the veteran signed. The row starts in `status` `OFFERED` with `accepted_at` and `revoked_reason` unset. `executed_on` is validated for calendar shape only and is not bounded by the logical-clock date in either direction. An offer commits nothing: it does not touch `active_poa_count` and it does not supersede anything, it does not examine the designated representative's `accreditation_status`, `accreditation_type` or designation ceiling — all of those are tested only at acceptance — and it does not care whether the veteran already holds an `ACTIVE` designation. Of its two body references the `veteran_id` is resolved first, so an offer naming an unknown veteran and an unknown representative reports `ERR_VETERAN_NOT_FOUND`.

Only the veteran can accept, and acceptance is the more heavily guarded of the two stale-version writes in the system. The acting `VeteranClient`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the designation's `veteran_id`. The designation must be in `OFFERED`. The request must carry `expected_veteran_version`, an integer of at least 1, which must equal the veteran's current `version` at the moment of the write — if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_VETERAN_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and nothing whatsoever changes, no designation is activated and no counter moves. The designated representative's `accreditation_status` must read `ACTIVE` — a designation naming a `PENDING`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED` representative is refused with `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_ACCREDITED` (HTTP 403), even though the offending representative is not the caller — and their `active_poa_count` must be strictly less than the 40-designation ceiling. Those two are tested in that order and both after the stale-version guard, so a request that is stale *and* names an unaccredited representative already at the ceiling reports `ERR_STALE_VETERAN_VERSION`, while one that merely names an unaccredited representative at the ceiling reports `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_ACCREDITED` rather than `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_CASELOAD_FULL`. Acceptance then sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, stamps `accepted_at` with the logical-clock instant, increments the designated representative's `active_poa_count` by 1, and increments the veteran's `version` by 1 — which is exactly why a client holding a stale version number loses the race. As part of the same write, **every other designation of the same veteran whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`**, and each superseded designation's representative has their `active_poa_count` decremented by 1. Supersession is therefore the system-driven mirror of acceptance: a veteran can have at most one `ACTIVE` designation at a time, the aggregate designation count stays balanced, and `caseload_weight` is recomputed both for the newly designated representative and for every superseded one. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

Revocation of a designation has two distinct shapes that must not be conflated, and which shape runs is decided by the designation's own `status` rather than by the acting role — either authorized role may drive either shape. Revoking a designation that is `ACTIVE` triggers the full representation cascade: `status` becomes `REVOKED` with the supplied `reason` stored as `revoked_reason`, the representative's `active_poa_count` is decremented by 1, every claim citing this designation whose `status` is `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW` or `READY_TO_FILE` reverts to `DRAFT` with `poa_id` and `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id` both nulled, a claim already sitting in `DRAFT` that cites the designation has only its `poa_id` cleared and keeps its status, every `SCHEDULED` examination appointment of the reverted claims moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `REPRESENTATION_CASCADE`, each affected site's `booked_slot_count` is decremented by 1 per cancelled appointment, `caseload_weight` is recomputed, and `version` is incremented by 1 on the designation and on every representative, claim, appointment and site the cascade touched. Revoking a designation still in `OFFERED` does far less: `status` becomes `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason`, `version` is incremented by 1, and `active_poa_count` is **left completely untouched**, because an `OFFERED` designation was never counted in the first place; no claim, appointment, site or representative row is read or written at all. Neither shape touches the veteran's own row, so `assigned_representative_id` — an internal allocation rather than a legal instrument — survives a revocation unchanged, and revocation is available whatever the veteran's `intake_status`, a `DECEASED` veteran included. Both paths end in `REVOKED`, which is terminal. Attempting to revoke a designation that is neither `OFFERED` nor `ACTIVE` is an invalid transition.

Assignment is `IntakeClerk` work and drives the veteran from `SCREENED` to `ACTIVE`. A veteran in `DECEASED` is refused with `ERR_VETERAN_DECEASED` rather than with the from-state code, that test being taken ahead of the `SCREENED` from-state check; every other `intake_status` gives `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. It requires that the veteran already have at least one eligibility screen whose `basic_eligibility` came out true; a veteran who has never been screened, or whose only screens all failed basic eligibility, is refused with `ERR_NOT_SCREENED`. The caller may supply `representative_id` to assign a specific person; a nominated representative need only exist and sit strictly below the 40-designation ceiling, because neither their `accreditation_status` nor their `accreditation_type` is examined on that path — the three filters below constrain the automatic selector alone. Omitting it invokes the automatic selector, which builds its candidate set from the roster by three filters applied together: the representative's `accreditation_status` must be `ACTIVE`; their `accreditation_type` must be `COUNTY_CVSO` or `VSO_ORG`, so an `ATTORNEY` or a `CLAIMS_AGENT` is never auto-assigned however light their load; and their `active_poa_count` must be strictly less than 40. Candidates are then ordered by `caseload_weight` ascending, and ties are broken by `created_at` ascending — the longest-registered representative wins a tie, and the ordering is fully deterministic because `created_at` is unique per row. The first candidate is chosen. If the candidate set is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REPRESENTATIVE` at HTTP 409 and no assignment is made. Either way, a successful assignment sets `assigned_representative_id` and `intake_status` to `ACTIVE` and increments the veteran's `version` by 1. The 40-designation ceiling is enforced on assignment as well as on acceptance, and breaching it is `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_CASELOAD_FULL`.

Designations can be listed and narrowed by `veteran_id`, `representative_id`, `status` and `form_type`.

### 3.5. Claim Preparation, Peer Review and Segregation of Duties

A claim is opened by a `CountyOfficer` against an already-`ACTIVE` designation. Creation captures `veteran_id`, `poa_id`, the `claim_type` (`DISABILITY_COMPENSATION`, `PENSION`, `DEPENDENCY_INDEMNITY` or `SURVIVOR_PENSION`), `claimed_conditions` — between 1 and 8 elements, each an object with a `condition_code` string and a `claimed_rating_pct` integer that is a multiple of 10 from 0 through 100 — and optionally `intent_to_file_date`. The server sets `status` to `DRAFT`, `prepared_by_representative_id` to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, `rework_count` and `reopen_count` to 0, `evidence_completeness_pct` to 0.00, and `awarded_rating_pct` and `retro_award_cents` to 0, while `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id`, `filed_on`, `effective_date`, `effective_date_basis`, `decided_on`, `decision_outcome` and `withdrawal_reason` are all present in the payload but null. Of the two body references, `veteran_id` is resolved before `poa_id`, so a create naming two unknown ids reports `ERR_VETERAN_NOT_FOUND`. Three referential preconditions apply at creation: the veteran's `discharge_doc_status` must be `VERIFIED`, else `ERR_DISCHARGE_NOT_VERIFIED`; the cited designation's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_POA_NOT_ACTIVE`; and that designation's own `veteran_id` must equal the claim's `veteran_id`, else `ERR_POA_VETERAN_MISMATCH` at HTTP 409 — a veteran may not be represented under somebody else's instrument. When `intent_to_file_date` is supplied it must be no later than the logical-clock date and no earlier than the veteran's `service_end_date`, else `ERR_INTENT_TO_FILE_DATE_INVALID`. A claim may be amended while it is still in `DRAFT` or `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, supplying `claimed_conditions` or `intent_to_file_date`; amending the condition list changes the required-points denominator and therefore immediately re-scores `evidence_completeness_pct`. Claims can be listed and narrowed by `veteran_id`, `poa_id`, `prepared_by_representative_id`, `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id`, `status` and `claim_type`.

The preparation workflow runs `DRAFT → EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT → PEER_REVIEW → READY_TO_FILE → FILED → DECIDED`, with a rework cycle between the second and third stages and a reopen cycle between the last two. Each step is driven by naming an `action` — one of `OPEN_EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW`, `RETURN_FOR_REWORK`, `MARK_READY`, `FILE` and `WITHDRAW` — and each has its own preconditions. Every one of the identity requirements below is enforced as a state precondition rather than as an authorization one: when the acting officer's `X-User-ID` is not the identity the chosen action demands, the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), and it is evaluated immediately after the from-state test of §2.11 rule 40 — therefore ahead of the deceased-veteran gate, ahead of the acting officer's own accreditation gates of §2.6, and ahead of every verified-document, designation, reviewer, segregation, outstanding-evidence and completeness check. An officer who is neither the claim's preparer nor accredited sees the invalid-transition code, not `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_ACCREDITED`.

**Opening evidence development** (`OPEN_EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`) is available only from `DRAFT`. The acting officer's `X-User-ID` must equal `prepared_by_representative_id` — only the preparer moves their own claim forward — and their accreditation must read `ACTIVE`. The veteran's `discharge_doc_status` must be `VERIFIED` and their `intake_status` must be `SCREENED` or `ACTIVE` — an `INTAKE_STARTED` or `INACTIVE` veteran is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, tested between the verified-document check of rule 43 and the designation check of rule 44, while a `DECEASED` one is already caught by rule 41's `ERR_VETERAN_DECEASED`. The claim's `poa_id` must be non-null and that designation must still be `ACTIVE`. The step sets `status` to `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, recomputes `evidence_completeness_pct` (which is 0.00 while nothing has been received), recomputes the preparer's `caseload_weight`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1 — and the preparer's `version` too whenever that recomputation actually moves one of their caseload terms, which it does whenever the claim rests on a designation the preparer themselves holds.

**Submitting for peer review** (`SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW`) is available only from `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT` and is where segregation of duties bites. The acting officer must be the preparer and must be `ACTIVE`. The body must supply `reviewer_representative_id`, naming a representative who exists — an unknown `reviewer_representative_id` is the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND` of §2.11 rule 30 — and whose `accreditation_status` is `ACTIVE`, a reviewer in any other accreditation state being refused with `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_ACCREDITED` (HTTP 403) ahead of both segregation checks below. That reviewer must **not** be `prepared_by_representative_id` — a preparer may never peer-review their own claim, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 409. The reviewer must also **not** be the representative designated on the claim's power of attorney, because the designee holds a representational interest and cannot supply an independent review; that attempt is refused with the distinct code `ERR_REVIEWER_IS_DESIGNATED_REPRESENTATIVE`, also at HTTP 409. Finally, no evidence item of this claim may still be sitting in `REQUESTED`; an outstanding request must be received, re-requested or waived first, and the refusal is `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_OUTSTANDING`. The step sets `status` to `PEER_REVIEW` and `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id` to the nominated reviewer, recomputes `evidence_completeness_pct` and the preparer's `caseload_weight`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1.

**Returning for rework** (`RETURN_FOR_REWORK`) is available only from `PEER_REVIEW` and only to the reviewer: the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must equal `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id`. The body must supply `rework_note`. The step sets `status` back to `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, increments `rework_count` by 1, and deliberately **leaves `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id` in place**, so the same reviewer is re-used on the next submission unless a different `reviewer_representative_id` is supplied. The rework loop may run any number of times, and `rework_count` counts the completed passes.

**Marking a claim ready to file** (`MARK_READY`) is available only from `PEER_REVIEW`, only to the reviewer, and only while their accreditation is `ACTIVE`. It additionally requires `evidence_completeness_pct` to be at least the ready-to-file threshold of 80.00 percent; the comparison is inclusive, so an exact 80.00 passes and 79.99 does not, and the refusal is `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` at HTTP 409. The step sets `status` to `READY_TO_FILE`, recomputes `evidence_completeness_pct` and the preparer's `caseload_weight`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1.

**Filing** (`FILE`) is available only from `READY_TO_FILE` and returns to the preparer: `X-User-ID` must equal `prepared_by_representative_id` and their accreditation must be `ACTIVE`. The body must supply `filed_on`, a date no later than the logical-clock date. No evidence item of the claim may be sitting in `INSUFFICIENT` at filing time — that is the second half of the outstanding-item rule and is likewise `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_OUTSTANDING`. Filing sets `status` to `FILED` and `filed_on`, selects `effective_date_basis` and `effective_date` under the retroactive-award rules of §3.8, recomputes the preparer's `caseload_weight`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1.

**Withdrawing** (`WITHDRAW`) is available from `DRAFT`, `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW` or `READY_TO_FILE`, is restricted to the preparer, and requires a `reason`. It sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN` — a terminal state — and stores the reason as `withdrawal_reason`. It also cleans up the claim's dependents: every `SCHEDULED` examination appointment of the claim moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to the literal string `CLAIM_WITHDRAWN` and each corresponding site's `booked_slot_count` is decremented by 1, and every evidence item still in `REQUESTED` moves to `WAIVED` with `status_reason` set to the literal string `CLAIM_WITHDRAWN` — each such item's `weight_points` being recomputed at the half credit a waiver earns, which raises the withdrawn claim's `evidence_completeness_pct` accordingly, while an item already sitting in `INSUFFICIENT` is left exactly where it is. The preparer's `caseload_weight` is recomputed and `version` is incremented by 1 on the claim and on every touched evidence item, appointment and site.

There is one further way into `DRAFT`, and it is never caller-driven: when the designation a claim rests on is revoked — either directly by the veteran or by the accreditation-revocation cascade of §3.1 — a claim in `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW` or `READY_TO_FILE` falls back to `DRAFT` with `poa_id` and `peer_reviewed_by_representative_id` nulled, its `SCHEDULED` appointments cancelled and each site's `booked_slot_count` decremented. The reopen cycle is likewise not driven from this endpoint: its action list holds exactly the six values above, and a `DECIDED` claim returns to `FILED` — with `reopen_count` incremented by 1 — only through the decision-review filing of §3.9. Naming an unrecognised action is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTION` at HTTP 422; naming a recognised action whose from-state does not match the claim's current `status` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` at HTTP 409.

### 3.6. Evidence Development and the Completeness Score

Evidence is requested against a claim, and `claim_id` comes from the path rather than the body. A request supplies the `evidence_type` — one of `SERVICE_TREATMENT_RECORD`, `PRIVATE_MEDICAL`, `VA_MEDICAL`, `LAY_STATEMENT`, `NEXUS_LETTER` or `DBQ` — the `provider_name` identifying the custodian or author it was requested from, holding 1 to 160 characters, and optionally `nexus_opinion_strength`. That last field must agree with the type: a `NEXUS_LETTER` item must carry a strength other than `NONE`, and every other evidence type must carry `NONE` or omit the field entirely; a disagreement is refused with `ERR_NEXUS_STRENGTH_MISMATCH` at HTTP 422. The row starts in `status` `REQUESTED` with `requested_on` set to the logical-clock date, `nexus_opinion_strength` stored as `NONE` whenever the caller omitted it, `weight_points` at 0.00, `received_on` and `status_reason` null and `auto_generated` false, and creating it recomputes the claim's `evidence_completeness_pct` and increments the claim's `version` by 1. Raising a request is not itself gated on the parent claim's `status`: the open-for-development gate of the next paragraph governs the four lifecycle actions only, so a request may be raised against a claim in `DRAFT`, `READY_TO_FILE`, `FILED`, `DECIDED` or `WITHDRAWN` just as readily as against one in development. Items can be listed across the office and narrowed by `claim_id`, `status`, `evidence_type` and `auto_generated`; the claim's own evidence file accepts `status` and `evidence_type` but not `auto_generated`, alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

Every evidence action requires the parent claim's `status` to be `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT` or `PEER_REVIEW` — evidence is developed only while the claim is open for development, never on a `DRAFT`, `READY_TO_FILE`, `FILED`, `DECIDED` or `WITHDRAWN` claim, and a parent claim in any of those states refuses the action with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — tested after the item's own from-state, so an action that is invalid for both the item and its parent reports the item's own invalid transition. The four `action` values are `RECEIVE`, `MARK_INSUFFICIENT`, `RE_REQUEST` and `WAIVE`. **Receiving** an item (`RECEIVE`) is available from `REQUESTED` or from `INSUFFICIENT`; the logical-clock date, which is what `received_on` is set to, must not be earlier than `requested_on`, and a clock standing earlier than that is a third `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, evaluated after both the item's from-state and the parent-claim gate. It sets `status` to `RECEIVED` and `received_on`, then recomputes this row's `weight_points` and the claim's `evidence_completeness_pct`. **Marking an item insufficient** (`MARK_INSUFFICIENT`) is available only from `RECEIVED`, requires a `reason` stored as `status_reason`, sets `status` to `INSUFFICIENT`, clears `received_on`, and recomputes `weight_points` to 0.00 along with the claim's score. **Re-requesting** (`RE_REQUEST`) is available only from `INSUFFICIENT`; it returns `status` to `REQUESTED` and **overwrites `requested_on` with the logical-clock date**, which restarts the timeliness window from scratch and resets the timeliness multiplier baseline — this is the mechanism by which a stale item can be brought back inside the punctuality window. The `RECEIVED → INSUFFICIENT → REQUESTED → RECEIVED` cycle may run any number of times, and neither `RE_REQUEST` nor `RECEIVE` clears the `status_reason` the insufficiency wrote — only a further insufficiency or a waiver, the cascade-driven waiver of §3.5 included, ever writes that field again. **Waiving** an item (`WAIVE`) is available from either `REQUESTED` or `INSUFFICIENT`, requires a `reason` stored as `status_reason`, and sets `status` to the terminal `WAIVED` with `weight_points` recomputed at half credit. Every one of these four actions increments both the item's and the claim's `version` by 1.

A claim's `evidence_completeness_pct` is a weighted coverage percentage, and the order of its rounding steps is fixed and observable. Only items in `RECEIVED` or `WAIVED` contribute; an item in `REQUESTED` or `INSUFFICIENT` contributes exactly 0.00 points. For each contributing item, start from the base weight of its type: a `SERVICE_TREATMENT_RECORD` is worth 20.0 points, a `PRIVATE_MEDICAL` record 12.5, a `VA_MEDICAL` record 12.5, a `LAY_STATEMENT` 6.0, a `NEXUS_LETTER` 22.5, and a `DBQ` 17.5. Then apply two multipliers.

The first is the medical-opinion multiplier, and it is read **only** when the item's `evidence_type` is `NEXUS_LETTER`; for every other type the multiplier is exactly 1.00 no matter what `nexus_opinion_strength` says. For a nexus letter, a strength of `NONE` or `POSSIBLE` gives 1.00, `AT_LEAST_AS_LIKELY_AS_NOT` gives 1.15, and `HIGHLY_PROBABLE` gives 1.30.

The second multiplier depends on the item's status. A `WAIVED` item is credited at half weight — a factor of exactly 0.5 — and its timeliness multiplier is fixed at 1.00, so a waiver is never additionally penalised for lateness. A `RECEIVED` item is credited at full weight, but with a punctuality factor: if `received_on` is more than 30 days after `requested_on` the factor is 0.85, and otherwise it is 1.00. The comparison is strict, so an item received on exactly the thirtieth day after request is on time at 1.00 and one received on the thirty-first day is late at 0.85.

The item's `weight_points` is the product of base weight, opinion multiplier and status-and-timeliness multiplier, **rounded half-up to two decimal places at the item level**. Those already-rounded per-item values are then summed to give the claim's earned points. The denominator is the required-points figure for this claim: a base of 40 points plus 15 points for each element of `claimed_conditions`. The percentage is the earned points divided by the required points, multiplied by 100, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and then capped at 100.00 — so a claim can be over-evidenced without the score exceeding 100.00. The rounding order is not negotiable: round each item, sum the rounded items, then round the percentage. Rounding at the end instead, or truncating instead of rounding half-up, produces a different score and a different answer to whether the 80.00 ready-to-file threshold has been met.

### 3.7. Examination Sites and Booking a Compensation-and-Pension Examination

Contract examination facilities are configured by the `SystemAdmin`. A site carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `county_code` of two to five characters identifying the county it serves, a `concurrent_capacity` of at least 1 stating how many `SCHEDULED` appointments may have intersecting occupied intervals, and a `buffer_minutes` turnaround from 0 through 120 that is appended to the **end** of every appointment when intersection is tested. Where the office does not state them, a site is understood to run a 15-minute turnaround buffer and a concurrent capacity of 2. `booked_slot_count` starts at 0 and is thereafter maintained by the system, never by a caller. A site's `name`, `county_code`, `concurrent_capacity` and `buffer_minutes` can all be retuned later, and a retune writes every one of those four keys the body actually carries — a key carrying an explicit `null` is written as null rather than skipped, while a key the body omits is left untouched; existing appointments are left exactly where they are, and the new values apply only to subsequent overlap evaluations. Sites can be listed and narrowed by `county_code`.

Booking an examination is `CountyOfficer` work and supplies `claim_id`, `exam_site_id`, `starts_at` and `ends_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants exactly as §2.4 defines them — the fractional-second part is optional on input, and the value is stored and echoed back as the caller wrote it rather than re-rendered — with `ends_at` strictly after `starts_at`; a zero-length or inverted interval is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` at HTTP 422. The `starts_at` instant must be at or after the logical-clock instant: an examination may not be booked into the past, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_IN_PAST` at HTTP 409. The referenced claim may be in **any** status: booking imposes no claim-state precondition, so an examination can be attached to a `DRAFT` claim as readily as to a `FILED` one, and nothing requires the acting officer to be that claim's preparer or its designated representative. A successful booking sets `status` to `SCHEDULED` and `prep_session_completed` to false, increments the site's `booked_slot_count` by 1 and the site's `version` by 1. Appointments can be listed and narrowed by `claim_id`, `exam_site_id`, `status` and `prep_session_completed`.

Site capacity is tested with **half-open** interval semantics and an **asymmetric, trailing-only** buffer. The block the requested appointment would occupy runs from its `starts_at` inclusive up to — but not including — its `ends_at` plus the site's `buffer_minutes`. Each existing appointment at the same site whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` occupies a block computed the same way, from its own `starts_at` up to but not including its own `ends_at` plus the same `buffer_minutes`. Two blocks overlap when the requested block's start is strictly before the existing block's end **and** the existing block's start is strictly before the requested block's end. Because both comparisons are strict, a new appointment beginning at exactly the instant a previous block ends — that is, exactly at the earlier appointment's `ends_at` plus the buffer — does **not** overlap it. The buffer is trailing only: nothing at all is reserved before an appointment's `starts_at`, so an appointment ending precisely when another begins still leaves the trailing buffer of the first one in conflict with the second. Count the overlapping `SCHEDULED` appointments; if that count is greater than or equal to the site's `concurrent_capacity` the booking is refused with `ERR_EXAM_SITE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 409. A capacity of 2 therefore admits a second concurrent examination and refuses the third. Appointments in `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` free their block immediately and are never considered.

The appointment lifecycle has one self-loop and three terminal exits, all driven by a `CountyOfficer`. The four `action` values are `COMPLETE_PREP`, `COMPLETE`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCEL`. **Completing the preparation session** (`COMPLETE_PREP`) is a `SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED` self-loop available only while `prep_session_completed` is false, a second `COMPLETE_PREP` being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` at HTTP 409 by the same band-4 from-state check that guards the other three actions; it sets that flag to true, leaves `status` at `SCHEDULED`, and increments the appointment's `version` by 1. It exists because examination-preparation coaching must be logged before an examination can be closed out. **Completing** the appointment (`COMPLETE`) is available only from `SCHEDULED`, requires `prep_session_completed` to already be true — otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_EXAM_PREP_INCOMPLETE` at HTTP 409 — and requires a `completed_on` date no later than the logical-clock date. Completion sets `status` to `COMPLETED` and `completed_on`, decrements the site's `booked_slot_count` by 1, and **synthesizes a new evidence item on the linked claim**: `evidence_type` `DBQ`, `status` `RECEIVED`, `requested_on` stamped with the logical-clock date, `received_on` equal to the appointment's `completed_on`, `provider_name` and `status_reason` both null, `nexus_opinion_strength` `NONE`, and `auto_generated` true. Because `completed_on` can never be later than the logical-clock date, a synthesized item is always inside the timeliness window and always earns the full on-time `DBQ` weight of 17.5 points. Because that item lands already received, the claim's `evidence_completeness_pct` is recomputed on the spot, and `version` is incremented by 1 on the appointment, the site and the claim. That synthesized item is discoverable only by reading the claim's evidence file. **Recording a no-show** (`NO_SHOW`) is available only from `SCHEDULED` and only once the logical-clock instant is at or after the appointment's `ends_at` — an attempt made before that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` at HTTP 409, because this temporal condition is folded into the band-4 from-state check rather than carrying a temporal code of its own; it sets `status` to `NO_SHOW` and decrements the site's `booked_slot_count` by 1. **Cancelling** (`CANCEL`) is available only from `SCHEDULED`, requires a `reason` stored as `cancellation_reason`, sets `status` to `CANCELLED` and likewise decrements `booked_slot_count` by 1, restoring the site's ledger to its pre-booking value. All three exits are terminal, and every one of them releases exactly one slot, which is why the slot invariant of §2.8 holds no matter which path an appointment takes — including the cascade-driven cancellations of §3.1 and §3.5, which set `cancellation_reason` to a literal rather than to caller text.

### 3.8. Recording a Decision: Retroactive Award, Combined Rating and the Rating Cascade

Recording a decision is the single most consequential write in the system. It is `CountyOfficer` work, is available only from `FILED`, is open to **any** accredited `CountyOfficer` — unlike the preparation transitions of §3.5 it does not require the caller to be the claim's preparer or its reviewer — and supplies `decision_outcome` (`GRANTED`, `PARTIAL_GRANT` or `DENIED`), `awarded_rating_pct` and `decided_on`. The rating must be 0 when the outcome is `DENIED`, and otherwise a multiple of 10 from 10 through 100. The `decided_on` date must be no earlier than the claim's `filed_on` and no later than the logical-clock date; a violation of either bound is `ERR_DECISION_DATE_INVALID` at HTTP 409.

The **retroactive award** is settled in whole cents with no rounding anywhere, because every constant involved is an integer number of cents. Its anchor date is chosen at filing time, not at decision time, and the choice is recorded in `effective_date_basis`. If the claim carries an `intent_to_file_date` and `filed_on` falls no more than 365 days after it, the basis is `INTENT_TO_FILE_ANCHORED` and `effective_date` is the intent-to-file date; the comparison is inclusive, so a gap of exactly 365 days still anchors on the intent to file and a gap of 366 days does not. The test is on the signed gap `filed_on` minus `intent_to_file_date`, so an intent-to-file date that is *later* than `filed_on` — which nothing in the system forbids, since both are merely bounded above by the logical clock — is inside the window too and anchors the claim on that later date. Otherwise — no intent to file at all, or a filing more than 365 days after it — the basis is `FILED_DATE_ANCHORED` and `effective_date` is `filed_on`. The distinction is material: anchoring on the intent to file makes the retroactive period span the whole intent-to-file window and yields a materially larger award, while anchoring on the filing date confines it to decision-processing time, so identical ratings, dependents and decision dates produce a strictly smaller award.

From that anchor, count **whole calendar months** up to the decision. Take the difference in years between `decided_on` and `effective_date`, multiply by twelve, add the difference in their month numbers, and then subtract 1 if the day-of-month of `decided_on` is strictly less than the day-of-month of `effective_date` — that final subtraction is what makes the count whole months elapsed rather than a difference of month labels. If the result is negative, treat it as 0. Next compute the dependent add-on: when `awarded_rating_pct` is at least 30 the add-on is 8200 cents — eighty-two dollars — for each dependent counted, where the count is the veteran's `dependent_count` capped at 3, read from the veteran as it stands at the moment the decision is recorded rather than as it stood at filing, so a veteran with five dependents is credited for three; when the rating is below 30 the add-on is exactly 0 regardless of `dependent_count`. The base monthly rate is read from the statutory schedule by the awarded rating: a rating of 0 pays nothing, 10 pays 17500 cents, 20 pays 34600, 30 pays 53700, 40 pays 77400, 50 pays 110200, 60 pays 139500, 70 pays 175900, 80 pays 204400, 90 pays 229800, and 100 pays 383100 cents per month. `retro_award_cents` is the number of whole retroactive months multiplied by the sum of the base monthly rate and the dependent add-on. A `DENIED` decision carries a rating of 0, so its monthly rate is 0 and its `retro_award_cents` is 0.

The decision then propagates through four hops. **Hop one** sets the claim's `status` to `DECIDED` along with `decision_outcome`, `awarded_rating_pct`, `decided_on` and the newly computed `retro_award_cents`. **Hop two** follows from the claim now being `DECIDED`: the veteran's `combined_rating_pct` is recomputed and `priority_group` is re-derived from it. **Hop three** follows from the rating having changed: every state benefit application of that veteran whose `determination_status` is `APPROVED` and whose program's `min_rating_pct` now exceeds the new `combined_rating_pct` has its `determination_status` set to `DENIED` with `determination_reason` set to the literal string `RATING_REDUCED_BELOW_PROGRAM_MINIMUM`, and its `disbursement_status` set to `CLAWED_BACK`. **Hop four** follows from those claw-backs: each affected program's `allocated_cents` is decremented by that application's `benefit_amount_cents`, exactly reversing the commitment made at approval and restoring the pool invariant. The preparer's `caseload_weight` is recomputed, since a `DECIDED` claim leaves the open-workload set, and `version` is incremented by 1 on the claim, the veteran and every touched application and program.

The **combined rating** is not a sum. Gather `awarded_rating_pct` from every claim of this veteran whose `status` is `DECIDED`, whose `decision_outcome` is `GRANTED` or `PARTIAL_GRANT`, and whose `awarded_rating_pct` is strictly greater than 0. Claims that were denied, are still in preparation, are merely `FILED`, or have been withdrawn are all excluded — and a claim that has been reopened back to `FILED` drops out of the set until it is decided again, which is precisely how a reduction becomes visible. Order the collected ratings by `awarded_rating_pct` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending; the order is pinned because the arithmetic is not commutative in its rounding behaviour. Begin with a residual capacity of 100 and a running combined value of 0. For each rating in that order, add to the combined value the residual multiplied by the rating and divided by 100, then set the residual to 100 minus the new combined value. Carry that arithmetic at full precision through every iteration. Only at the very end round the combined value half-up to the nearest multiple of 10: a combined 75 becomes 80, and a combined 74 becomes 70. Two 50-percent ratings therefore combine to 75 before rounding and 80 after, never 100.

### 3.9. Appeal Counselling, Deadlines and the Expiry Sweep

Once a claim is `DECIDED` the office counsels the veteran on their review options, and that counselling session is recorded by a `CountyOfficer` as a decision-review row. Creation supplies `claim_id` and the `review_lane` counselled — `HIGHER_LEVEL_REVIEW`, `SUPPLEMENTAL_CLAIM`, `BOARD_APPEAL_DIRECT`, `BOARD_APPEAL_EVIDENCE` or `BOARD_APPEAL_HEARING`. The row starts in `status` `COUNSELED` with `counseled_by_representative_id` set from the acting officer's `X-User-ID`. The server copies `decision_notice_date` from the claim's `decided_on` **once, at creation**, and never refreshes it afterwards; a claim that has been reopened to `FILED` has no current decision and cannot anchor a new review until it is decided again, and counselling any claim that is not `DECIDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` at HTTP 409. Any accredited `CountyOfficer` may record the session — the claim's preparer holds no privileged position here — and nothing limits a claim to one review row: several reviews may hang off the same decision, each carrying its own copy of `decision_notice_date` and its own deadline clock. Reviews can be listed and narrowed by `claim_id`, `counseled_by_representative_id`, `status` and `review_lane`.

The appeal clock is derived, not supplied. `deadline_on` is `decision_notice_date` plus 365 days. `days_remaining` is `deadline_on` minus the logical-clock date in whole days, and it is **re-derived on every read**, single-resource reads and list reads alike, so moving the logical clock changes what a read reports without any write having occurred. The value may be negative once the window has closed, and it keeps sliding with the clock even for rows that have already reached the terminal `FILED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` states: nothing freezes `days_remaining` at the value it held when the row closed, and it is never clamped at 0. There is no grace period: `days_remaining` of exactly 0 means the deadline is today and the lane may still be elected or filed, while −1 is the first day on which it may not.

Three caller-driven `action` values move a review: `ELECT`, `FILE` and `WITHDRAW`. The endpoint's role list admits both `CountyOfficer` and `VeteranClient`, but each individual action is reserved to exactly one of them: `ELECT` and `FILE` are `CountyOfficer` work and `WITHDRAW` is `VeteranClient` work. A caller who holds the endpoint but not the action they asked for is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` at HTTP 403, reported inside band 2 at rule (12) as soon as the action value has been recognised — so it outranks that action's own required-field check and also outranks the 404 for an unknown review id. This is a different mechanism from the actor-identity restrictions of §3.5, which report `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` from band 4; the two must not be collapsed into one rule. For a `VeteranClient` the band-1 ownership rule is evaluated earlier still and without reference to the action at all, so a veteran addressing a review that hangs off another veteran's claim sees `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_OWNER` whichever of the three actions they sent. **Electing** a lane (`ELECT`) is `CountyOfficer` work available only from `COUNSELED`; it requires `days_remaining` to be at least 0 and an `elected_on` date no later than the logical-clock date. It sets `status` to `ELECTED` and `elected_on`, and when `review_lane` is supplied with a non-null value it overwrites the counselled lane with the elected one, while an omitted or explicitly null `review_lane` leaves the counselled lane exactly as it was — a veteran counselled on one lane may elect another. **Filing** the review (`FILE`) is `CountyOfficer` work available only from `ELECTED`; it too requires `days_remaining` to be at least 0 and a `filed_on` no later than the logical-clock date. It sets `status` to `FILED` and `filed_on`, and it reopens the underlying claim **provided that claim is still `DECIDED`**: the claim moves from `DECIDED` back to `FILED` and its `reopen_count` is incremented by 1, while `decision_outcome`, `awarded_rating_pct`, `decided_on` and `retro_award_cents` are all retained until the next recorded decision overwrites them. The preparer's `caseload_weight` is recomputed because the claim re-enters the open-workload set. Where the claim has already left `DECIDED` — because a sibling review hanging off the same decision was filed first — the review itself still moves to `FILED`, but the claim, its `reopen_count` and the preparer's `caseload_weight` are all left exactly as they stand. This is what closes the `DECIDED → FILED → DECIDED` cycle, and it is the only way a revised — possibly lower — rating can be recorded, which in turn is the only way the rating-reduction cascade of §3.8 becomes observable. Either election or filing attempted once `days_remaining` has gone negative is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED` at HTTP 409. **Withdrawing** (`WITHDRAW`) is the veteran's own action: a `VeteranClient` whose `X-User-ID` matches the `veteran_id` of the claim the review hangs off may withdraw from either `COUNSELED` or `ELECTED`, supplying a `reason` that is stored as `closure_reason`. Withdrawal sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and the behaviour is identical from both source states.

The fourth exit is not caller-driven at the row level. A `SystemAdmin` runs an expiry sweep across the whole office; every review whose `status` is `COUNSELED` or `ELECTED` and whose `days_remaining` is strictly less than 0 at the current logical-clock position moves to `EXPIRED` with `closure_reason` set to the literal string `DEADLINE_SWEEP`, and each swept row's `version` is incremented by 1. The sweep reports how many rows it expired as `expired_count` and lists their identifiers as `expired_review_ids`, in ascending creation order of the reviews themselves rather than in any deadline order. It is idempotent: `EXPIRED` rows are no longer in the candidate set, so a second call at an unchanged clock position expires nothing and reports zero. `FILED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

### 3.10. State Benefit Programs, Applications and the Two Independent Dimensions

A state program is a `SystemAdmin` configuration for exactly one fiscal year. It carries a `program_code` of 1 to 40 characters unique across all programs, a human-readable `program_name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `program_type` naming which benefit it administers (`PROPERTY_TAX_EXEMPTION`, `TUITION_WAIVER` or `BURIAL_BENEFIT`), the `fiscal_year` from 2020 through 2100 the pool belongs to, the `annual_pool_cents` total the program may allocate that year, the `min_rating_pct` floor — a multiple of 10 from 0 through 100 — a veteran must meet to have an application **approved** and to retain that approval, a `requires_wartime` flag defaulting to false where the configuration omits it, and a `max_award_cents` per-application ceiling on the gross award. The floor is not examined at filing time: an application from a veteran far below it is accepted and refused only at adjudication. `allocated_cents` starts at 0 and is thereafter maintained only by approvals and claw-backs. A duplicate `program_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE` at configuration time. Retuning may change `program_name`, `annual_pool_cents`, `min_rating_pct`, `requires_wartime` or `max_award_cents` and nothing else, writing each of those five keys the body actually carries — a key carrying an explicit `null` is written as null rather than skipped, while a key the body omits is left untouched. `program_code`, `program_type` and `fiscal_year` are fixed once configured, and a retune body naming one of those three is ignored rather than refused (§2.5), so the uniqueness check has nothing to test on a retune and can never fire there. Already-approved applications are never re-adjudicated, but the write increments the program's `version` by 1, which invalidates any `expected_program_version` a client captured earlier. Programs can be listed and narrowed by `program_type` and `fiscal_year`.

Filing an application is `IntakeClerk` or `CountyOfficer` work and is the second stale-version write. It supplies `veteran_id`, `program_id`, the `fiscal_year` the application draws from, `expected_program_version`, and whichever money-bearing input the program type calls for: `assessed_property_value_cents`, the assessor's value of at least 0, or `tuition_credits_requested`, a semester credit count from 0 through 30. Six checks apply. `expected_program_version` must be present and an integer of at least 1, else `ERR_VALIDATION`; it must equal the program's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` at HTTP 412 with nothing changed. The application's `fiscal_year` must equal the referenced program's `fiscal_year`, else `ERR_FISCAL_YEAR_MISMATCH` at HTTP 409. The money-bearing input must match the program type — a `PROPERTY_TAX_EXEMPTION` application carries `assessed_property_value_cents` greater than 0, a `TUITION_WAIVER` application carries `tuition_credits_requested` greater than 0, and a `BURIAL_BENEFIT` application carries neither — and a mismatch is `ERR_PROGRAM_INPUT_MISMATCH` at HTTP 422. The veteran must be in an eligible case state for the program type: `intake_status` of `SCREENED` or `ACTIVE` for a property-tax exemption or a tuition waiver, and `DECEASED` for a burial benefit, with `ERR_VETERAN_STATUS_INELIGIBLE` at HTTP 409 otherwise. And no other application may already exist with the same `veteran_id`, `program_id` and `fiscal_year` whose `determination_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_BENEFIT_APPLICATION` at HTTP 409 — a denied or clawed-back prior attempt does not block a refile. A successful filing sets `determination_status` to `SUBMITTED`, `disbursement_status` to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and `gross_award_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` and `benefit_amount_cents` all to 0, and it does **not** move the program's `allocated_cents`: money is committed only at approval. Applications can be listed and narrowed by `veteran_id`, `program_id`, `fiscal_year`, `determination_status` and `disbursement_status`.

An application carries **two independent state dimensions**, and they must be implemented as two fields that evolve on their own schedules. The determination dimension runs `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` or `SUBMITTED → DENIED`. The disbursement dimension runs `NOT_APPLICABLE → PENDING → ISSUED`, with `CLAWED_BACK` reachable from either `PENDING` or `ISSUED`. `DENIED` and `CLAWED_BACK` are terminal on their respective dimensions.

Adjudication belongs to the `StateAuditor` alone. The two determination `action` values are `APPROVE` and `DENY`. **Approving** (`APPROVE`) requires `determination_status` to be `SUBMITTED` and imposes three substantive gates, evaluated after every cheaper check: the veteran's `combined_rating_pct` must be at least the program's `min_rating_pct`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_MIN_RATING_NOT_MET`; when the program's `requires_wartime` is true, the veteran's `wartime_service_days` must be at least 90, else `ERR_PROGRAM_WARTIME_REQUIRED`; and the program's `allocated_cents` plus the newly computed `benefit_amount_cents` must be at most `annual_pool_cents`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_POOL_EXHAUSTED` — the comparison is inclusive, so an allocation that exactly fills the pool is accepted and the next cent is not. Approval sets `determination_status` to `APPROVED`, freezes `rating_at_approval_pct` at the veteran's `combined_rating_pct` as of that instant, moves the disbursement dimension from `NOT_APPLICABLE` to `PENDING` as a coupled initialisation, and increases the program's `allocated_cents` by `benefit_amount_cents`. **Denying** (`DENY`) requires `determination_status` to be `SUBMITTED` and a `reason` stored as `determination_reason`; it leaves `disbursement_status` at `NOT_APPLICABLE`, leaves `gross_award_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `benefit_amount_cents` and `rating_at_approval_pct` all at 0 because no award arithmetic is run for a denial, and leaves the program's `allocated_cents` entirely unchanged.

The award arithmetic is a waterfall whose evaluation order is fixed. **First**, read the exemption rate from the veteran's `combined_rating_pct`, taking the first band that matches from the top: a rating of 100 or more gives 1.00, at least 70 gives 0.60, at least 50 gives 0.40, at least 30 gives 0.25, and anything lower gives 0.00. **Second**, compute the gross award by program type. For a `PROPERTY_TAX_EXEMPTION`, multiply `assessed_property_value_cents` by that rate, round the product half-up to whole cents, and take the lesser of that and the program's `max_award_cents`. For a `TUITION_WAIVER`, multiply `tuition_credits_requested` by the unit credit cost of 45000 cents — four hundred fifty dollars per semester credit — and take the lesser of that and `max_award_cents`. For a `BURIAL_BENEFIT`, take the lesser of the flat allowance of 90000 cents, that is nine hundred dollars, and `max_award_cents`. **Third**, select the administration fee band **on the gross award and never on the net**: a gross of at least 5000000 cents attracts 0.02, at least 2000000 cents attracts 0.01, and anything lower attracts 0.00. Keying the band on the net figure is a circular definition — the net depends on the fee and the fee band would depend on the net — and it does not converge. **Fourth**, compute `admin_fee_cents` as the gross multiplied by the fee rate, rounded **up** to whole cents. The two rounding modes are deliberately different: the exemption product rounds half-up, the administration fee always rounds up, and they must not be unified. **Fifth**, `benefit_amount_cents` is `gross_award_cents` minus `admin_fee_cents`, and it is that net figure — not the gross — that is charged against the pool. **Sixth**, apply the pool guard described above.

The disbursement dimension is moved only by the `SystemAdmin`. The two disbursement `action` values are `ISSUE` and `CLAW_BACK`. **Issuing** (`ISSUE`) requires `disbursement_status` to be `PENDING` and `determination_status` to be `APPROVED`, and an `issued_on` date no later than the logical-clock date. It sets `disbursement_status` to `ISSUED` and `issued_on`, leaves `determination_status` untouched, and leaves the program's `allocated_cents` unchanged — because both `PENDING` and `ISSUED` are inside the ledger's inclusion set, the money was already committed at approval. This is the transition that demonstrates the two dimensions are genuinely independent: it moves one without touching the other. **Clawing back** (`CLAW_BACK`) is available from either `PENDING` or `ISSUED`, requires a non-blank `reason` that is validated but is not retained anywhere on the row — `determination_reason` keeps whatever it held, and an `issued_on` already stamped is kept too — sets `disbursement_status` to `CLAWED_BACK`, and decreases the program's `allocated_cents` by `benefit_amount_cents`, exactly reversing the approval commitment. Crucially, an operator claw-back **leaves `determination_status` at `APPROVED`**, because an operator recovery is not a re-adjudication. That is the observable difference from the system-driven claw-back of §3.8, which arrives from a rating reduction and also drives `determination_status` to `DENIED` with `determination_reason` set to `RATING_REDUCED_BELOW_PROGRAM_MINIMUM`. Both forms decrement the pool by the same amount; only the determination dimension distinguishes them.

### 3.11. Outreach to Underserved Veterans

Outreach events are planned by a `CountyOfficer` or a `SystemAdmin` on a single representative's travel calendar. An event carries the `assigned_representative_id` whose calendar it occupies — that representative must exist, but their own accreditation is never examined here, so an event may be planned onto a `PENDING`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED` representative's calendar, and the revocation cascade of §3.1 leaves their planned events standing and still costing 2 caseload points each — the `event_type` modality (`HOMELESS_STANDDOWN`, `RURAL_CLINIC`, `TRIBAL_OUTREACH` or `JAIL_INREACH`), the `location_name` of the venue holding 1 to 160 characters, `starts_at` and `ends_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants exactly as §2.4 defines them — stored and echoed back as the caller wrote them — with `ends_at` strictly after `starts_at`, and the `underserved_flag` recording whether the venue sits on the state underserved-area list, defaulting to false. The row starts in `status` `PLANNED` with `veterans_contacted` and `veterans_enrolled` both 0, and planning it recomputes the assigned representative's `caseload_weight` — a planned event costs 2 points — and increments that representative's `version` by 1. Events can be listed and narrowed by `assigned_representative_id`, `status`, `event_type` and `underserved_flag`.

A representative's travel calendar uses deliberately different overlap semantics from an examination site. Intervals are **closed** on both ends, the travel buffer is **symmetric** — 30 minutes reserved before `starts_at` and 30 minutes after `ends_at` — and the capacity is exactly one: a representative may be in one place at a time. The block a proposed event would occupy therefore runs from 30 minutes before its `starts_at` to 30 minutes after its `ends_at`. Each existing event of the **same** representative whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `HELD` occupies a block computed the same way; `CANCELLED` events release their block entirely. Two blocks overlap when the proposed block's start is strictly before the existing block's end and the existing block's start is strictly before the proposed block's end. Any overlap at all refuses the plan with `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_TRAVEL_CONFLICT` at HTTP 409. Because the comparison is strict while each side reserves 30 minutes, two events separated by a gap of exactly 60 minutes have blocks that meet without crossing and are both accepted, whereas a 59-minute gap is refused. The same conflict test is applied when an event is held, not only when it is planned — with the event being held excluded from its own comparison, so re-testing an otherwise unchanged calendar never refuses it.

The two `action` values are `HOLD` and `CANCEL`, and both are `CountyOfficer` work alone: a `SystemAdmin` may plan an event but may not hold or cancel one, and that attempt is the ordinary endpoint-level `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) of band 1 rather than a per-action restriction. **Holding** an event (`HOLD`) is available only from `PLANNED`, and only once the logical-clock instant has reached or passed the event's `starts_at`; an attempt made before that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` at HTTP 409, folded into the band-4 from-state check rather than carrying a temporal code. It requires both `veterans_contacted` and `veterans_enrolled`, with `veterans_enrolled` less than or equal to `veterans_contacted`; a headcount that claims more enrolments than contacts is refused with `ERR_ENROLLED_EXCEEDS_CONTACTED` at HTTP 422. Holding sets `status` to `HELD` and records both counts, which then feed the quarterly report's contact total, recomputes the assigned representative's `caseload_weight` because a held event leaves the planned-outreach set, and increments both the event's and the representative's `version` by 1. **Cancelling** (`CANCEL`) is likewise available only from `PLANNED` and requires a `reason` stored as `cancellation_reason`; it sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, recomputes `caseload_weight`, frees the representative's travel window so a later event may occupy it, and increments both versions by 1. `HELD` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

### 3.12. Quarterly Outcome Reporting to the State Department

The office owes the state department one report per calendar quarter. A `CountyOfficer` opens it with a single input, `fiscal_quarter`, a label of the form four digits followed by the letter Q and a digit from 1 through 4 — a label that does not match that shape is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FISCAL_QUARTER` at HTTP 422, and a label another report already holds is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FISCAL_QUARTER` at HTTP 409. The row starts in `status` `OPEN` with `weighting_basis` unset and `filed_claim_count`, `favorable_claim_count`, `performance_score`, `retro_dollars_cents` and `veterans_contacted_total` all at their empty values. Reports can be listed and narrowed by `status`, `fiscal_quarter`, `weighting_basis` and `certified_by_representative_id`.

Computing the quarter is a `CountyOfficer` action carrying `weighting_basis`, and it may only run once the quarter has closed: the logical-clock date must be strictly later than the last calendar day of the labelled quarter, else `ERR_QUARTER_NOT_CLOSED` at HTTP 409. The label expands to a closed calendar-date interval: a first quarter spans 1 January through 31 March of the labelled year, a second 1 April through 30 June, a third 1 July through 30 September, and a fourth 1 October through 31 December. Both endpoints are inside the window. The computation is re-runnable while the report is `OPEN` or `RETURNED`, so the same rows can be scored both ways and the second run overwrites the first; a computation run on a `RETURNED` report leaves its `status` at `RETURNED` and its `return_reason` intact, so computing alone never puts a returned report back into the submittable state.

The claim set for the window is every claim whose `filed_on` falls inside that closed interval. Claims in `DRAFT`, `EVIDENCE_DEVELOPMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW` or `READY_TO_FILE` have no `filed_on` and are excluded, as is a claim withdrawn before it was ever filed; a claim filed inside the window but still awaiting a decision counts in the denominator only. `filed_claim_count` is the size of that set. The favourable subset is those members whose `status` is `DECIDED` and whose `decision_outcome` is `GRANTED` or `PARTIAL_GRANT`, and `favorable_claim_count` is its size. `retro_dollars_cents` is the sum of `retro_award_cents` over the favourable subset. `veterans_contacted_total` is the sum of `veterans_contacted` over every outreach event whose `status` is `HELD` and whose `starts_at` date falls inside the same interval — note that it is the event's start date that decides membership, and that `PLANNED` and `CANCELLED` events contribute nothing.

`performance_score` is a percentage carried to exactly one decimal place, rounded half-up exactly once at the end. When `filed_claim_count` is 0 the score is 0.0: an empty reporting window is a scored outcome, not an error. Otherwise the two weighting bases differ in what they count. Under `CLAIM_WEIGHTED`, the score is `favorable_claim_count` divided by `filed_claim_count`, multiplied by 100 — every claim counts once, so a veteran with several claims in the window moves the score several times. Under `VETERAN_WEIGHTED`, count the distinct `veteran_id` values appearing in the favourable subset, divide by the number of distinct `veteran_id` values appearing in the whole window set, and multiply by 100 — each veteran counts once however many claims they filed. The same rows can therefore yield very different scores: a veteran with one granted and one denied claim scores 50.0 under claim weighting and 100.0 under veteran weighting.

The report then moves through a review cycle. **Submitting** is `CountyOfficer` work available only from `OPEN`; it requires `weighting_basis` to be set, meaning the report has been computed at least once, else `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED` at HTTP 409, and it requires the acting officer's accreditation to read `ACTIVE`. Submission recomputes the quarter's numbers under the stored `weighting_basis`, sets `status` to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` with the logical-clock instant and `certified_by_representative_id` with the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, and increments `version` by 1. The review step carries an `action` of `ACCEPT` or `RETURN`. **Accepting** (`ACCEPT`) is `StateAuditor` work available only from `SUBMITTED` and sets `status` to the terminal `ACCEPTED`. **Returning** (`RETURN`) is also `StateAuditor` work from `SUBMITTED`, requires a `reason` stored as `return_reason`, and sets `status` to `RETURNED`. **Reopening** is `SystemAdmin` work available only from `RETURNED`; it sets `status` back to `OPEN`, clears `certified_by_representative_id` and `submitted_at`, retains `return_reason` as the audit trail, and leaves the previously computed figures — and the stored `weighting_basis` — in place until the next computation overwrites them. Because the basis survives the reopen, a reopened report may be submitted again without being recomputed, and `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED` can never fire twice on the same row. That closes the `OPEN → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → OPEN` cycle, which may run any number of times, while `ACCEPTED` ends it for good.
