# State Assessment Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every spring, a state education agency runs a summative assessment program that touches every public school inside its borders, and almost none of the risk in that program is academic. The risk is administrative: a testing window that opens before secure booklets have shipped, a proctor whose security training lapsed two days before test day, a room double-booked between two grade bands, a shipment of booklets whose paperwork says forty-seven and whose custody trail says fifty, a score released to families while a breach investigation is still open. Each of these failures is individually small and collectively catastrophic — a single unaccounted booklet can invalidate an entire subject's results for a district, and a single premature release can force a statewide re-administration.

This document specifies the State Assessment Administration API, the system of record that state, district, school, and room-level staff use to run a testing window end to end. It governs the scheduling of test windows and sessions, the registration of students and the coding and review of their testing accommodations, the receipt, distribution, return, and reconciliation of secure materials under an unbroken chain of custody, proctor training and test-security affidavits, the reporting and adjudication of irregularities and security breaches, make-up testing and participation accounting, score release and the data appeal window, and the final distribution of results to families.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the staff member who codes an accommodation may never be the staff member who approves it, the proctor who files an irregularity report may never be the officer who adjudicates it, and a student who missed the initial administration may never be re-seated with the proctor who ran the session they missed. The second is **auditability by reconstruction**: material custody, participation percentages, and district charge ledgers are all derived by replaying recorded facts in a pinned order rather than by trusting a running total, so that any number the API reports can be defended in an audit years later. Where a rule below fixes an evaluation order, that order is part of the contract and not an implementation convenience.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide rules, formats, and cross-cutting principles that apply to every endpoint in the API. All resource collections are mounted at the root of the URL space; there is no shared prefix segment.

### 2.1. Principals, Roles, and Operational Scope

Every request to the API, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names something outside the recognized set, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.

The system recognizes exactly four roles, spelled as follows: `StateAssessmentOfficer`, `DistrictTestCoordinator`, `SchoolTestCoordinator`, and `Proctor`.

A `StateAssessmentOfficer` is the state-level program owner. This role builds the organizational skeleton and the testing calendar, ships secure materials, records scores, reviews and adjudicates irregularity reports, decides score appeals, and releases results. It never touches a session floor and never codes an accommodation. When `X-User-Role` is `StateAssessmentOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example, in the `actor_user_id` of a custody entry, were an officer ever to record one) but it never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check is applied to this role.

A `DistrictTestCoordinator` is the district's materials and compliance authority: it receives and destroys secure booklets, reconciles returned lots, and rules on the accommodation packages that school staff coded. When `X-User-Role` is `DistrictTestCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting District; ownership checks on any resource that references a District compare the resource's district-bearing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a lot write requires `X-User-ID` to equal the target lot's `district_id`, and an accommodation decision requires `X-User-ID` to equal the `district_id` of the School that owns the Session that owns the registration.

A `SchoolTestCoordinator` is the school-level administrator: it builds the proctor roster and its training records, schedules sessions and rooms, registers students, codes accommodations, and moves booklets between the district and the school. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolTestCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting School; ownership checks on any resource that references a School compare the resource's school-bearing field to `X-User-ID`. A session or proctor may only be created with a `school_id` equal to `X-User-ID`; session and registration writes require `X-User-ID` to equal the owning Session's `school_id`; and for a custody movement, the acting School's `district_id` must equal the lot's `district_id`.

A `Proctor` is the room-level test administrator: it signs the security affidavit, checks booklets in and out of the room, runs the session, records attendance, and files irregularity reports. When `X-User-Role` is `Proctor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Proctor; ownership checks compare the resource's proctor-bearing field to `X-User-ID`. A session transition, an attendance record, a session-scoped custody movement, or the filing of an irregularity report requires `X-User-ID` to equal the target Session's `proctor_id` — for a filing, the Session named in the request body — and a Session whose `proctor_id` is still null therefore matches no principal at all; signing an affidavit requires `X-User-ID` to equal the `proctor_id` named in the body; and moving a report through submission or withdrawal requires `X-User-ID` to equal the report's `reporter_proctor_id`. A violation of any of these ownership rules is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Role authorization has two layers. The first layer asks whether the caller's role is listed for the endpoint at all. The second layer applies to the three endpoints that multiplex several distinct operations behind one path, and asks whether the role owns the specific sub-operation requested. On session transitions, a `SchoolTestCoordinator` may request only `SCHEDULED` or `CANCELLED`, and a `Proctor` may request only `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED`. On irregularity-report transitions, a `Proctor` may request only `SUBMITTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and a `StateAssessmentOfficer` may request only `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, or `ADJUDICATED`. On custody events, a `DistrictTestCoordinator` may record only `RECEIPT` or `DESTRUCTION`, a `SchoolTestCoordinator` only `TRANSFER_TO_SCHOOL` or `RETURN_TO_DISTRICT`, and a `Proctor` only `SESSION_CHECKOUT` or `SESSION_CHECKIN`. A failure at either layer produces `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Every read endpoint in the API is open to all four roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many items come back; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter says how many items to skip before collecting the page and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by their creation timestamp in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Beyond pagination, list endpoints accept filter parameters that perform reverse lookups along the foreign keys of the domain. Schools may be narrowed by `district_id`. Test windows may be narrowed by `subject`, `grade_band`, and `status`. Material lots may be narrowed by `window_id`, `district_id`, and `status`. Custody events may be narrowed by `material_lot_id`, `session_id`, `previous_event_id`, and `event_type`, and are returned in ascending custody sequence within a lot. Proctors may be narrowed by `school_id` and `certification_status`. Affidavits may be narrowed by `proctor_id`, `window_id`, and `status`. Sessions may be narrowed by `window_id`, `school_id`, `proctor_id`, `status`, and `session_type`. Registrations may be narrowed by `session_id`, `student_state_id`, `makeup_of_registration_id`, `attendance_status`, and `score_status`. Irregularity reports may be narrowed by `session_id`, `registration_id`, `reporter_proctor_id`, `material_lot_id`, `status`, and `category` — and listing reports is the only way to discover the report that a discrepant lot reconciliation raises on the system's own initiative. Score appeals may be narrowed by `registration_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Custody Log Exception

Districts, schools, test windows, proctors, security affidavits, sessions, registrations, material lots, irregularity reports, and score appeals all carry the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted unique identifier formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix of any kind.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the resource was first created.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp recording when the resource was last modified. Any change to any property of the resource refreshes it, including changes the server makes on its own initiative as a downstream effect of some other request.
- **`version`** (integer): a server-managed counter that increases by exactly one on every mutation of the resource, including recomputations of derived fields such as a session's seat pressure or a district's charge ledger.

Custody entries are the single exception. A custody entry is an append-only fact about a physical movement of booklets and is never edited after it is written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither an `updated_at` nor a `version` property, and requests must not expect either to be present.

### 2.5. The Logical Assessment Clock

Testing programs are governed by calendars, and this system's calendar is a logical clock rather than the machine's wall clock. Every timestamp the server stamps onto a resource — the `created_at` and `updated_at` of §2.4 on every resource that carries them, `signed_at` on an affidavit, `occurred_at` on a custody entry, `opened_at` and `closed_at` on a session, `released_at` on a window, `training_completed_at` and `revoked_at` on a proctor, `filed_at`, `decided_at`, `adjudicated_at`, `reconciled_at`, and `report_delivered_at` — is read from that logical clock, and every time-gated precondition compares against the same clock. Before any request has moved it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. The envelope's `api_request_timestamp` of §2.2 is the sole exception: it is not a resource timestamp and is read from real wall-clock time.

A `StateAssessmentOfficer` may set the clock to a supplied instant by naming that instant in the `to_iso8601` field. The response reports the new clock as `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and triggers no cascade; it changes only what "now" means for subsequent requests.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Not-Found Semantics

Requests whose path names one or more resources are resolved strictly from left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the rejection. Each identifier has its own error: `district_id` produces `ERR_DISTRICT_NOT_FOUND`, `school_id` produces `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `window_id` produces `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `proctor_id` produces `ERR_PROCTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `security_affidavit_id` produces `ERR_SECURITY_AFFIDAVIT_NOT_FOUND`, `session_id` produces `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `registration_id` produces `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `material_lot_id` produces `ERR_MATERIAL_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `custody_event_id` produces `ERR_CUSTODY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `irregularity_report_id` produces `ERR_IRREGULARITY_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, and `score_appeal_id` produces `ERR_SCORE_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404.

The same catalogue governs identifiers supplied inside a request body rather than in the path. A `district_id` on a school, a `window_id` or `school_id` on a session, a `proctor_id` on an assignment or an affidavit, the `window_id` on an affidavit, a `session_id` or `makeup_of_registration_id` on a registration, a `window_id` or `district_id` on a material lot, a `session_id` on a custody entry, a `session_id` or `registration_id` on an irregularity report, and a `registration_id` on a score appeal all resolve against the same error vocabulary and the same 404 status.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Screened

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which the server checks them is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in strictly ascending order and **the first check that fails determines both the `error_id` and the HTTP status**; no later check may mask an earlier one. The full order is:

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming one of the four roles. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization**, including sub-operation ownership on the three multiplexing endpoints described in §2.1. Failure: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Path resolution**, left to right, per §2.6. Failure: the matching not-found error (404).
4. **Ownership scope** — the acting principal's identity covers the target, per §2.1. Failure: `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
5. **Body well-formedness** — every required field present, every enum value a declared member, every number inside its declared range, every string matching its declared pattern, every datetime a parseable ISO 8601 UTC instant, and every cross-field arithmetic rule satisfied. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Body reference resolution** — every identifier carried in the body resolves, per §2.6. Failure: the matching not-found error (404).
7. **Version pinning** — the supplied expected version matches the parent's current `version`, per §2.8. Failure: `ERR_STALE_PROCTOR_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION` (412).
8. **The target resource's own status** permits the operation. Failure: `ERR_SESSION_NOT_ACCEPTING_REGISTRATIONS`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_AWAITING_RECONCILIATION`, `ERR_SCORE_NOT_RELEASED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, each with HTTP 409.
9. **The governing test window's phase** permits the operation. Failure: `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID` (409).
10. **Proctor certification.** Failure: `ERR_PROCTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422).
11. **Affidavit presence.** Failure: `ERR_AFFIDAVIT_MISSING` (422).
12. **Chain-of-custody continuity.** Failure: `ERR_CUSTODY_CHAIN_BROKEN` (422).
13. **Booklet availability.** Failure: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MATERIALS` (422).
14. **Make-up segregation of duties.** Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422).
15. **Make-up linkage eligibility.** Failure: `ERR_MAKEUP_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
16. **Session interval containment inside its phase.** Failure: `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (422).
17. **Room occupancy conflict.** Failure: `ERR_ROOM_TIME_CONFLICT` (409).
18. **Proctor availability conflict.** Failure: `ERR_PROCTOR_TIME_CONFLICT` (409).
19. **Logical-clock gates on transitions.** Failure: `ERR_TIMING_PRECONDITION_NOT_MET` (422).
20. **Appeal window still open.** Failure: `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).
21. **Code uniqueness.** Failure: `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409).
22. **Registration uniqueness.** Failure: `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION` (409).
23. **Seat capacity after applying the request.** Failure: `ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
24. **No accommodation still awaiting district review at activation.** Failure: `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_NOT_APPROVED` (422).
25. **Return-for-information cycle limit.** Failure: `ERR_RETURN_CYCLE_LIMIT` (409).
26. **Participation floor at release.** Failure: `ERR_PARTICIPATION_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422).
27. **Security hold at release.** Failure: `ERR_SECURITY_HOLD_ACTIVE` (422).

Two of these orderings are load-bearing and worth stating twice. A room conflict (check 17) is screened before a proctor conflict is possible at all, because the two live on different endpoints; but session-interval containment (check 16) is screened **before** room occupancy (check 17), so a session proposed entirely outside its window's dates reports `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` even when it also collides with an existing booking. Likewise, a session's own status gate (check 8) precedes the window-phase gate (check 9), so attempting a transition that the session's current state does not offer reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`, even when the window is in the wrong phase as well.

Ownership scope (check 4) applies to every write endpoint in the system: school creation, proctor creation, training completion, affidavit signing, session creation, proctor assignment, session transitions, registration creation, accommodation coding, accommodation decisions, attendance recording, custody events, lot reconciliation, irregularity report creation, irregularity report transitions, and score appeal filing.

The window-phase gate (check 9) applies to six operations: session creation, session transitions, registration creation, score recording, appeal filing, and the report distribution run.

### 2.8. Version Pinning on Proctor and Session Writes

Two writes in this system legitimately supersede an earlier write of the same kind, which means no state precondition can distinguish "I am deliberately replacing what is there" from "I never saw what is there". Both therefore require the client to pin the version it last observed.

Signing a security affidavit — the write served by `POST /security-affidavits` — requires `expected_proctor_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the Proctor as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the proctor's current `version` — because another affidavit or a training completion has advanced it since — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROCTOR_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

Assigning a proctor to a session — the write served by `POST /sessions/{session_id}/proctor-assignments` — requires `expected_session_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the Session as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the session's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION` and HTTP 412; this prevents a coordinator from silently displacing an assignment it never observed.

Both fields are mandatory on their endpoints, not optional hints. No other endpoint in the API accepts or requires a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.9. Counting, Rounding, and Charge Precision

Participation percentages are stored and reported to exactly one decimal place, rounded half up. They are computed by aggregating first and rounding once: the window-level percentage is derived from window-level counts, and is never the average of per-school percentages, which are never materialized at all. Whenever a participation denominator is zero, the resulting percentage is exactly 0.0 rather than an error or a null.

Quiz scores are compared at one decimal place, and comparisons against the passing threshold are inclusive — a score exactly equal to the threshold passes.

Money is handled entirely in integer cents. Charges are rounded half up to whole cents **once per material lot**, and only the already-rounded per-lot amounts are summed into a district total. Summing unrounded products and rounding the total once at the end is a different and incorrect answer, and the API's stored totals must reflect the round-then-sum order.

Booklet counts, seat counts, and custody sequence numbers are integers throughout and are never rounded. Where a formula divides before rounding up, the rounding-up step is applied to the exact rational quotient rather than to a decimal approximation of it.

### 2.10. Cascades, Recomputation, and Hierarchical Consistency

Many operations in this system change more than one resource. Cancelling a session withdraws its pending registrations; reverting a window to planning cancels its sessions, which in turn withdraws their pending registrations, which in turn forces those sessions to recompute their seat pressure; confirming a security breach revokes an affidavit, which decertifies a proctor, which cancels that proctor's scheduled sessions, which withdraws those sessions' pending registrations. Every resource touched anywhere in such a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been modified by a direct request — the cascade is not exempt from the common-property rules of §2.4.

Derived fields are recomputed rather than incrementally patched. A material lot's four custody buckets are rebuilt by replaying its entire custody chain in sequence order after every append; a district's charge ledger is rebuilt from the full set of its lots whenever any lot's charge or reversal status changes; a window's open breach count and security hold are rebuilt from the full set of its reports whenever any report's status or disposition changes; a proctor's certification standing is re-derived from its training record and the logical clock on every read and on every precondition that consults it. A client that reads a resource always sees the derived values that the current stored facts imply.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties as a Standing Principle

Four separations are structural and appear repeatedly in the workflows of §3. A `SchoolTestCoordinator` codes accommodations but may never decide them. A `DistrictTestCoordinator` decides accommodations but may never code them, and may never record a score. A `Proctor` files irregularity reports but may never adjudicate them, and may never record a score. And a make-up seat may never be proctored by the proctor who ran the session the student originally missed. The first three are enforced by role authorization; the fourth is enforced as a business rule at registration time and is described in §3.4.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Program Skeleton: Districts, Schools, and the Proctor Corps

Nothing else in the system can exist until the organizational skeleton does, and only a `StateAssessmentOfficer` may lay it down. A district is registered with a human-readable `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `district_code` that must be two uppercase letters, a hyphen, and four digits, and a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the state the district reports under. District codes are unique across the entire program; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A newly registered district starts with a clean ledger: its `replacement_charge_total_cents`, its `security_assessment_cents`, and its `open_discrepancy_count` are all 0.

Schools hang off districts. A school is created with the `district_id` of its parent — immutable thereafter — a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `school_code` of two uppercase letters, a hyphen, and two digits, and a `max_concurrent_seats` value between 1 and 500 inclusive that declares the hard ceiling on how many test seats the school can staff at one time. School codes need only be unique inside their own district, but a collision inside that district is still `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

The proctor roster is the `SchoolTestCoordinator`'s responsibility. Adding a proctor requires the `school_id` of the roster being built, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and an `email` address containing exactly one at-sign, used for training notices. A brand-new proctor is untrained and uncertified: `training_status` is `NOT_TRAINED`, `certification_status` is `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `quiz_score_pct` and `training_completed_at` and `revoked_at` and `latest_affidavit_id` are all null, and `strike_count` is 0.

Test-security training is recorded against a proctor by the same coordinator, supplying the `module_version` of the training package that was taken and the `quiz_score_pct` the proctor achieved. A score of at least 80.0 percent sets `training_status` to `PASSED`; anything below sets it to `FAILED`. The boundary itself passes: a score of exactly 80.0 is a pass. The submitted score always overwrites `quiz_score_pct`, whatever the outcome, but `training_completed_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant **only** when the attempt passes — a later failed attempt flips `training_status` to `FAILED` while leaving the earlier passing timestamp untouched. Recording a completion re-derives the proctor's certification standing and increments the proctor's `version`.

**Certification standing is never stored as an independent decision; it is derived, in this order, every time a proctor is read and every time a rule consults it.** If `revoked_at` is not null, `certification_status` is `REVOKED`. Otherwise, if `training_status` is anything other than `PASSED`, it is `NOT_CERTIFIED`. Otherwise, if the logical clock has reached or passed the instant 365 days after `training_completed_at`, it is `EXPIRED`. Otherwise it is `CERTIFIED`. Certification therefore decays on its own: a proctor who was certified when assigned to a session can be uncertified by the time that session is scheduled, which is precisely why the check is repeated at both moments.

Before running any session, a proctor must personally sign the test-security affidavit for the window in question — the one operation in the system that a proctor performs on the proctor's own record. Signing names the `proctor_id` being attested (which must equal the signer's own identity), the `window_id` the attestation covers, the `affidavit_version` of the text that was signed as four digits, a period, and one or more further digits, and the `expected_proctor_version` described in §2.8. The server stamps `signed_at` from the logical clock and records the signer's `X-User-ID` as `signer_user_id`; the new affidavit is created with `status` `ACTIVE` and a null `revoked_reason`. Re-signing is legitimate and expected: when a new affidavit lands for a proctor and window pair that already has an `ACTIVE` one, the older affidavit moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and takes a `version` increment of its own, the proctor's `latest_affidavit_id` is repointed at the newer affidavit, and the proctor's `version` increments as well. Because affidavits are addressable in their own right, `latest_affidavit_id` is a convenience pointer rather than a complete history; the full set for a proctor is discoverable by listing affidavits filtered by that proctor.

### 3.2. Laying Out a Test Window

A test window is the temporal container for everything else, and a `StateAssessmentOfficer` creates one by declaring a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `subject` drawn from `ELA`, `MATH`, `SCIENCE`, and `SOCIAL_STUDIES`, a `grade_band` drawn from `G3_5`, `G6_8`, and `G9_12`, and four instants: `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the initial testing period, and `makeup_start` and `makeup_end` bounding the make-up period. The arithmetic between them is checked at creation: `window_end` must fall strictly after `window_start`, `makeup_start` must fall at or after `window_end`, and `makeup_end` must fall strictly after `makeup_start`. Two further inputs are optional. The `appeal_window_days` value, between 1 and 45 inclusive, sets how long families and staff have to appeal a released score and defaults to 10. The `min_participation_pct` value, between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive, sets the participation floor the window must clear before results may be released and defaults to 95.0.

Fresh windows begin in `PLANNED` with `security_hold` false, `open_breach_count` 0, `released_at` and `appeal_window_closes_at` null, both participation percentages null, and `registered_distinct_count` and `present_distinct_count` at 0. From there, the officer drives the window through its lifecycle by naming a target `to_status`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `reason`. Seven target statuses are reachable in total — `OPEN`, `PLANNED`, `MAKEUP`, `SCORING`, `RELEASED`, `FINALIZED`, and `CANCELLED` — but only along the edges described below. Any other pairing of current status and requested `to_status`, any repeat of a transition that already executed, and any request against the terminal `FINALIZED` or `CANCELLED` statuses is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The same error is the fallback for an edge that *is* declared but whose preconditions are unmet, except where a more specific error is named for that precondition below.

**`PLANNED → OPEN`** opens the initial testing period. It requires that the logical clock still be strictly before `window_end` — a window whose initial period has already elapsed can never be opened, and a late attempt fails with `ERR_TIMING_PRECONDITION_NOT_MET` — and that at least one material lot has already been shipped for this window, so that no school can be told to test with no booklets in the state's possession. The window's `status` becomes `OPEN` and its `version` increments.

**`OPEN → PLANNED`** is the escape hatch for an administrative mistake, and it is destructive. It is permitted only while no session in the window has reached `ACTIVE`, `CLOSED`, or `INVALIDATED` — once a single room has opened, the mistake can no longer be quietly unwound. When it is permitted, the revert propagates in three hops: every session in the window sitting in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`; every registration on those newly cancelled sessions whose `attendance_status` is still `PENDING` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; and each affected session then recomputes its `seats_consumed`, its `separate_setting_count`, and its `effective_seat_capacity` against the reduced roster. The window's `status` returns to `PLANNED` and its `version` increments.

**`OPEN → MAKEUP`** closes the initial period and opens the make-up period. It requires that the logical clock have reached or passed `window_end`, failing with `ERR_TIMING_PRECONDITION_NOT_MET` otherwise, and that no session in the window is still `ACTIVE`. The window's `status` becomes `MAKEUP` and its `version` increments.

**`MAKEUP → SCORING`** closes testing altogether. It requires that the logical clock have reached or passed `makeup_end`, that every session in the window has landed in `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED`, or `INVALIDATED`, and that every material lot for the window has landed in `RECONCILED` or `DISCREPANT` — no window may be scored while a single booklet lot is unaccounted for. Reaching `SCORING` runs the provisional participation calculation described in §3.8, writing `participation_rate_provisional_pct` along with the `registered_distinct_count` and `present_distinct_count` that produced it, and increments the window's `version`.

**`PLANNED → CANCELLED`** abandons a window before it ever opens, and is permitted only while no session exists in the window at all. The `status` becomes `CANCELLED` and the `version` increments. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

The remaining three edges — the `SCORING → MAKEUP` back-edge, `SCORING → RELEASED`, and `RELEASED → FINALIZED` — belong to the participation and results narratives and are specified in §3.8 and §3.9.

### 3.3. Scheduling Sessions and Assigning Proctors

A test session is a single room, on a single date range, run by a single proctor, and only a `SchoolTestCoordinator` may create one. Creation names the `window_id` the session tests under and the `school_id` hosting it — both immutable afterwards — the `room_code` as one uppercase letter followed by three digits, the planned `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` instants (the end strictly after the start), a `seat_capacity` between 1 and 400 inclusive declaring the room's physical seat count, and optionally a `session_type` of `INITIAL` or `MAKEUP`, defaulting to `INITIAL`. The new session is born in `DRAFT` with a null `proctor_id`, null `opened_at` and `closed_at`, `seats_consumed` at 0, `separate_setting_count` at 0, `materials_checked_out` and `materials_checked_in` at 0, and an `effective_seat_capacity` already computed by the seat-pressure rule of §3.4.

The window's phase governs which kind of session may be built at all: an `INITIAL` session may only be created while its window is `OPEN`, and a `MAKEUP` session only while its window is `MAKEUP`. A mismatch is `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`. The session's interval must also sit entirely inside the matching phase's own dates — an `INITIAL` session requires `window_start` to be at or before `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` to be at or before `window_end`, and a `MAKEUP` session requires `makeup_start` to be at or before `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` to be at or before `makeup_end`. A session that pokes outside its bounds at either end is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`.

Rooms are a shared physical resource, and the system will not book one twice. Occupancy is checked on the pairing of `school_id` and `room_code` against every existing session at that school and room whose status is `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE`, or `CLOSED`, and the check builds in a mandatory 15-minute turnover buffer for clearing and resetting the room. The proposed session conflicts with an existing one exactly when the proposed start falls strictly before the existing end plus 15 minutes **and** the existing start falls strictly before the proposed end plus 15 minutes; if either comparison is not strict, there is no conflict. A proposed session that starts exactly 15 minutes after an existing session ends therefore fits, while one that starts fourteen minutes after does not. A conflict is reported as `ERR_ROOM_TIME_CONFLICT`.

Assigning the proctor is a separate act, performed by the same coordinator on a session that is still `DRAFT`, and it is the second of the two version-pinned writes: the body carries the `proctor_id` being assigned and the `expected_session_version` described in §2.8. Three substantive checks then run. The proctor's derived `certification_status` must be exactly `CERTIFIED`; the values `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `EXPIRED`, and `REVOKED` all fail with `ERR_PROCTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. An affidavit with `status` `ACTIVE` must exist for the pairing of that proctor and the session's window, failing with `ERR_AFFIDAVIT_MISSING`. And the proctor must be free: availability is checked against every session already assigned to that proctor whose status is `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, or `ACTIVE`, **across every school and every window in the program**, using closed intervals and no buffer at all. The assignment conflicts exactly when the proposed start is at or before the existing end and the existing start is at or before the proposed end — so two sessions that merely touch, where one begins at the very instant the other ends, do conflict. This is deliberately stricter than the room rule: a room can be turned over in a quarter of an hour, but a person cannot be in two places at the same instant. A collision is `ERR_PROCTOR_TIME_CONFLICT`. A successful assignment writes `proctor_id`, replacing any previous assignment outright, and increments the session's `version`.

**`DRAFT → SCHEDULED`** is requested by the `SchoolTestCoordinator` once the room and the proctor are settled. Four preconditions apply. The session's `proctor_id` must not be null — attempting to schedule an unstaffed session is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The assigned proctor's `certification_status` must, at this moment and not merely at assignment time, still be `CERTIFIED`, failing with `ERR_PROCTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. An `ACTIVE` affidavit must, at this moment, still exist for that proctor and this session's window, failing with `ERR_AFFIDAVIT_MISSING`. And the window must be in the phase that matches the session's type: `OPEN` for an `INITIAL` session, `MAKEUP` for a `MAKEUP` session, failing with `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`. Both cross-entity guards are re-evaluated here rather than trusted from assignment time, because certification expires on a clock and affidavits can be revoked in between.

### 3.4. Registering Students, Coding Accommodations, and Seat Pressure

Students are seated by the `SchoolTestCoordinator`, one registration per seat. A registration names the `session_id` it belongs to — immutable afterwards — the `student_state_id` as the letter S followed by exactly eight digits, the enrolled `grade` as an integer between 3 and 12 inclusive, optionally a `guardian_email` to which the family score report will later be delivered, and optionally a `makeup_of_registration_id` naming the earlier registration that this seat replaces. A new registration starts with `attendance_status` `PENDING`, `accommodation_status` `NONE`, `score_status` `NOT_SCORED`, `report_delivery_status` `NOT_QUEUED`, an empty `accommodation_codes` list, `seats_required` 1, and null values for `accommodation_decision_note`, `raw_score`, `report_suppressed_reason`, and `report_delivered_at`. Creating it re-runs the owning session's seat pressure and increments that session's `version`.

Registrations may only be added to a session whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`; a session that has already opened, closed, been cancelled, or been invalidated rejects them with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_ACCEPTING_REGISTRATIONS`. The governing window must also be in the phase matching the session's type, or the request fails with `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`. Duplicate seating is prevented on two axes at once: a student may hold at most one initial registration per window — counting only registrations whose `makeup_of_registration_id` is null and whose `attendance_status` is not `WITHDRAWN` — and any given original registration may be replaced by at most one make-up seat. Either collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`.

When `makeup_of_registration_id` is supplied, two further gates apply and their order matters. The segregation-of-duties gate is evaluated first: if both the make-up session and the session that held the original registration have a `proctor_id`, those two proctors must differ, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Only then is linkage eligibility tested: the original registration must carry the same `student_state_id`, must sit inside the same test window, must either have an `attendance_status` of `ABSENT` or `EXCUSED` or have a `score_status` of `INVALIDATED`, and the target session must have `session_type` `MAKEUP`. Any failure among those is `ERR_MAKEUP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Because the segregation check runs first, a make-up attempt that both re-seats the student with the original proctor *and* names an ineligible original reports the segregation error.

Accommodations are coded onto an existing registration by the school coordinator, who submits `accommodation_codes` as a list that **replaces** the registration's current list wholesale rather than adding to it. The list may hold at most five entries, may not repeat an entry, and may only draw on the seven catalogued codes; a violation of any of those is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Coding is only accepted while the owning session is `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, and otherwise fails with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_ACCEPTING_REGISTRATIONS`.

The catalogue has two attributes per code: a review tier, and whether the code requires the student to be tested in a separate setting. `EXTENDED_TIME` is a standard-tier code that does not require a separate setting. `SMALL_GROUP` is a standard-tier code that does require one. `ASSISTIVE_TECH` is a standard-tier code that does not require one. `SEPARATE_ROOM` is a specialized-tier code that does require one. `READ_ALOUD` and `SCRIBE` are specialized-tier codes that do not require one. `BRAILLE_FORM` is a specialized-tier code that does require one.

Review routing follows directly from the tiers present in the submitted list. If the submitted list is empty, the registration's `accommodation_status` becomes `NONE`. If any submitted code is specialized-tier, the registration moves to `REQUESTED` and waits for the district — the school that codes may never approve. If every submitted code is standard-tier, the registration moves straight to `APPROVED` with no district involvement. This routing applies uniformly from any starting point, which makes the accommodation dimension cyclic: `NONE → APPROVED`, `NONE → REQUESTED`, `REJECTED → REQUESTED`, `REJECTED → APPROVED`, and `APPROVED → REQUESTED` are all reachable simply by re-coding, and a rejected package can be revised and resubmitted as often as the coordinator needs. Every coding call rewrites `accommodation_codes`, recomputes the registration's `seats_required`, re-runs the owning session's seat pressure, and increments the registration's `version`.

The district's ruling is the counterpart act, performed by a `DistrictTestCoordinator` on a registration whose `accommodation_status` is `REQUESTED`. The body carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `note`. Approval moves `REQUESTED → APPROVED`; rejection moves `REQUESTED → REJECTED`. Either way the supplied text is stored in `accommodation_decision_note` and the registration's `version` increments. The acting district must be the district that owns the school that owns the session, or the ruling is `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Seat pressure is the arithmetic that keeps rooms staffable, and it is re-evaluated on registration creation, on every accommodation coding, and whenever a registration's `attendance_status` moves into or out of `WITHDRAWN`. It has a demand side and a supply side.

On the demand side, a registration is said to need a separate setting when any code currently on it is one of the three catalogued codes that require one. Such a registration has `seats_required` of 3 — one seat for the student plus 2 additional seats charged for the separate setting — and every other registration has `seats_required` of 1. Only *live* registrations count, meaning those whose `attendance_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`. The session's `separate_setting_count` is the number of live registrations needing a separate setting, and its `seats_consumed` is the sum of `seats_required` across all live registrations.

On the supply side, the session begins from the smaller of its own `seat_capacity` and the owning school's `max_concurrent_seats`, then subtracts a proctor-span penalty for supervising scattered settings. The penalty is computed by taking the session's `separate_setting_count`, subtracting the two separate settings a single proctor can absorb for free, dividing the remainder by 3, rounding that quotient up to the next whole number, treating any negative or zero result as zero, and multiplying by 4 seats. The session's `effective_seat_capacity` is the smaller-of-the-two ceiling minus that penalty, floored at 0 so it never goes negative.

After applying the request, `seats_consumed` may not exceed `effective_seat_capacity`; if it would, the whole request is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and nothing is written. The interaction is deliberately non-linear and coordinators should expect it: adding one more separate-setting student raises demand by 3 seats and, when it happens to cross a span boundary, simultaneously lowers supply by 4 more, so a session with room to spare on paper can fail on the very next registration.

### 3.5. Secure Materials and the Unbroken Chain of Custody

Secure booklets are the physical asset the entire program is built to protect, and the system tracks them as an append-only chain of movements rather than as a mutable inventory count. A `StateAssessmentOfficer` ships a lot by declaring the `window_id` it serves and the `district_id` it ships to — both immutable afterwards — a `lot_code` formed as the literal prefix `LOT-` followed by between two and eight characters, each an uppercase letter or a digit, the `quantity_shipped` as a count between 1 and 5000 inclusive, and the sealed serial range as `seal_range_start` (at least 1) and `seal_range_end`. The range and the count must agree exactly: subtracting `seal_range_start` from `seal_range_end` and adding one must equal `quantity_shipped`, and a range that does not tally is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Lot codes are unique across the whole program, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A shipped lot begins in `SHIPPED` with all four of its custody buckets — `quantity_at_district`, `quantity_at_school`, `quantity_in_sessions`, and `quantity_destroyed` — at 0, `chain_accounted_count` at 0, `missing_count` and `seal_broken_count` and `replacement_charge_cents` at 0, `charge_reversed` false, and `declared_accounted_count`, `chain_vs_declared_delta`, and `reconciled_at` all null.

Every movement of booklets is recorded as one entry appended to the lot's chain. The recorder supplies an `event_type`, the `quantity` of booklets moved as an integer of at least 1, the `sealed_count` of those booklets that were still sealed (at least 0 and never more than `quantity`), an optional free-text `note`, and — for the two session-scoped movements only — the `session_id` involved. Supplying `session_id` on a movement that is not session-scoped, or omitting it on one that is, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Everything else on the entry is server-derived and never client-supplied: the `sequence_no` as a one-based position within this lot's chain, the `previous_event_id` pointing at the immediately preceding entry on the same lot (null for the lot's first entry), the `from_holder_type` and `to_holder_type` naming which bucket the booklets left and entered, the `actor_user_id` and `actor_role` taken from the request headers, the `occurred_at` instant taken from the logical clock, and the `chain_balance_after` described below. Custody entries are readable individually and as a list, and the list is the audit trail; they are never amended.

Six movement types exist, and each is owned by exactly one role and legal in exactly one lot state. A `RECEIPT`, recorded by the district that owns the lot, moves booklets from the `STATE` holder to the `DISTRICT` holder and drives `SHIPPED → RECEIVED`; it must be the lot's very first entry and its `quantity` must equal `quantity_shipped` exactly, because a district either takes delivery of the whole lot or does not take delivery at all. A `TRANSFER_TO_SCHOOL`, recorded by a school coordinator whose school belongs to the lot's district, moves booklets from `DISTRICT` to `SCHOOL` and drives `RECEIVED → IN_DISTRIBUTION`. A `SESSION_CHECKOUT` and a `SESSION_CHECKIN`, both recorded by the assigned proctor, shuttle booklets between the `SCHOOL` and `SESSION` holders; they leave the lot's status where it was, a self-loop written `IN_DISTRIBUTION → IN_DISTRIBUTION`, because the interesting movement lives in the bucket counters rather than in the status field. A `RETURN_TO_DISTRICT`, recorded by a school coordinator, moves booklets from `SCHOOL` back to `DISTRICT` and drives `IN_DISTRIBUTION → RETURNED_PENDING`, but only under three conditions: `quantity_in_sessions` must already be 0, the `quantity` returned may not exceed `quantity_at_school`, and the return must empty the school bucket completely — a partial return that would leave any booklets at the school is refused, so the status only advances when the school is genuinely clear. A `DESTRUCTION`, recorded by the district, moves booklets from `DISTRICT` to the `DESTROYED` holder while the lot stays put in a second self-loop, `RETURNED_PENDING → RETURNED_PENDING`, and its `quantity` may not exceed `quantity_at_district`.

Whenever an entry lands, the server rebuilds the lot's position by replaying the entire chain from the beginning in ascending `sequence_no` order — never in identifier order, and never by patching the previous totals. All four buckets start at zero for the replay. A `RECEIPT` adds its quantity to the district bucket. A `TRANSFER_TO_SCHOOL` subtracts from the district bucket and adds to the school bucket. A `SESSION_CHECKOUT` subtracts from the school bucket and adds to the sessions bucket. A `SESSION_CHECKIN` subtracts from the sessions bucket and adds back to the school bucket. A `RETURN_TO_DISTRICT` subtracts from the school bucket and adds to the district bucket. A `DESTRUCTION` subtracts from the district bucket and adds to the destroyed bucket. After each entry is applied, that entry's `chain_balance_after` is stamped with the sum of all four buckets at that moment. Because every movement only relocates booklets, that sum is a conserved quantity: it equals the lot's `quantity_shipped` after every entry, forward or compensating, and any implementation in which it drifts has a defect. When the replay finishes, the four bucket fields are written from the replay's final state, and `chain_accounted_count` is set to the district bucket plus the destroyed bucket — the booklets the paper trail says are back in responsible hands.

Two distinct failures guard these appends, and the chain-continuity check is evaluated before the availability check. Chain continuity asks whether the submitted `event_type` is legal for the lot's current status and whether its required predecessor exists: a transfer to a school before any receipt, a second receipt on a lot that already has one, a checkout against a session that is not `SCHEDULED`, a check-in against a session with no open checkout, a return while booklets are still out in sessions or one that would leave booklets at the school, and a destruction before the lot reached `RETURNED_PENDING` all fail here with `ERR_CUSTODY_CHAIN_BROKEN`. Availability asks the narrower arithmetic question of whether the source bucket actually holds at least the requested `quantity`, and fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MATERIALS`. Separately from either, the acting principal must own the lot: a district-recorded movement requires `X-User-ID` to equal the lot's `district_id`, a school-recorded movement requires the acting school's district to equal the lot's `district_id`, and a session-scoped movement requires `X-User-ID` to equal the named session's `proctor_id`, on pain of `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Reconciliation closes a lot out and is the moment the paper trail is compared with the district's own count. Only the owning `DistrictTestCoordinator` may run it, and only against a lot whose status is `RETURNED_PENDING`; any other status is `ERR_LOT_NOT_AWAITING_RECONCILIATION`. The district declares three numbers: `declared_returned_count`, `declared_destroyed_count`, and `broken_seal_count`. From them the system writes `declared_accounted_count` as the sum of the returned and destroyed declarations, `chain_vs_declared_delta` as `chain_accounted_count` minus `declared_accounted_count`, `missing_count` as `quantity_shipped` minus `declared_accounted_count` with any negative result treated as 0, and `seal_broken_count` as the declared broken-seal figure, and it stamps `reconciled_at` from the logical clock. The two answers deliberately come from different sources — the chain answers "where does the paper trail say the booklets are", ordered and cumulative; the declaration answers "how many does the district say it actually has", order-free — and the difference between them is exactly `chain_vs_declared_delta`.

The outcome is binary. If `missing_count` is 0 **and** `chain_vs_declared_delta` is 0 **and** the declared broken-seal count is 0, the lot moves `RETURNED_PENDING → RECONCILED`, its `replacement_charge_cents` is set to 0, and the matter is closed. If any one of those three is non-zero, the lot moves `RETURNED_PENDING → DISCREPANT` and four things follow in order. First, the lot's `replacement_charge_cents` is computed. Second, the owning district's `replacement_charge_total_cents`, `security_assessment_cents`, and `open_discrepancy_count` are recomputed. Third, the system files an irregularity report of its own accord — `system_generated` true, `material_lot_id` set to this lot, `session_id` null, `category` `SECURITY_BREACH`, `severity` `HIGH`, and `status` `SUBMITTED`, skipping the draft stage entirely. Fourth, because that report is an open security breach, the governing window's `open_breach_count` is recomputed and its `security_hold` is raised. In both outcomes the lot's `version` increments, and `RECONCILED` and `DISCREPANT` are both terminal for the lot.

The replacement charge itself is priced per lot and rounded per lot. Each missing booklet costs 1250 cents — twelve dollars and fifty cents — and that unit cost is multiplied by a security factor that depends on whether seals were disturbed: a factor of 1.25 when the lot's `seal_broken_count` is 0, and a factor of 1.75 when it is greater than 0, since a broken seal means the state must assume the content itself was exposed. The lot's `replacement_charge_cents` is therefore `missing_count` multiplied by 1250 and then by the applicable factor, rounded half up to whole cents at that point. Only after each lot has been rounded to whole cents are the lots summed: a district's `replacement_charge_total_cents` is the sum of `replacement_charge_cents` across every one of its lots whose status is `DISCREPANT` and whose `charge_reversed` is false, and its `open_discrepancy_count` is how many lots are in that same filtered set. Rounding order is part of the contract, not a detail — summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end produces a different total and is wrong.

The district's `security_assessment_cents` is derived immediately afterwards, from the roll-up and from nothing else. The gross is `replacement_charge_total_cents`, and the waiver band is selected **from that gross**, never from the post-waiver net: a gross below 5000 cents earns no waiver, a gross of at least 5000 cents but below 20000 cents earns a waiver of 10 percent, and a gross of 20000 cents or more earns a waiver of 20 percent. The waiver amount is the gross multiplied by the applicable percentage, rounded half up to whole cents once, and `security_assessment_cents` is the gross minus that waiver amount, with the subtraction exact. Reading the band off the net would push a district that sits just above a band edge down into the lower band and change the answer; that reading is explicitly rejected.

### 3.6. Test Day: Opening a Room, Recording Attendance, and Closing Out

Booklets reach the room before the room opens. While a session is still `SCHEDULED`, its assigned proctor checks booklets out of the school's holding by recording a `SESSION_CHECKOUT` naming that session, which raises the session's `materials_checked_out` by the quantity moved and may not draw more than the lot's `quantity_at_school` currently holds. Once the room is running, the proctor records `SESSION_CHECKIN` movements to return booklets, which raise `materials_checked_in`; a check-in is only accepted against an `ACTIVE` session and may never exceed the booklets still outstanding, that is, `materials_checked_out` minus `materials_checked_in`.

Session transitions, like window transitions, are requested by naming a target `to_status` and may carry an optional free-text `reason`.

**`SCHEDULED → ACTIVE`** is the proctor's own act and the most heavily guarded transition in the system. The caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the session's `proctor_id`. The logical clock must have reached the moment 30 minutes before `scheduled_start` and must not have passed `scheduled_end` — a room may be opened up to half an hour early to seat students, but never after the scheduled end has gone by, and either failure is `ERR_TIMING_PRECONDITION_NOT_MET`. The governing window must still be `OPEN` for an `INITIAL` session or `MAKEUP` for a `MAKEUP` session, failing with `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`. No registration on the session may still have an `accommodation_status` of `REQUESTED`, because a student whose accommodation package is still awaiting the district cannot be tested — that failure is `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_NOT_APPROVED`. And the room must be materially ready: the count of booklets currently out to the session, meaning `materials_checked_out` minus `materials_checked_in`, must be at least the number of registrations on the session whose `attendance_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`, failing with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MATERIALS`. On success the `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `opened_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the session's `version` increments.

Attendance is recorded one registration at a time by the proctor running the room, and only while that room is `ACTIVE`; the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the owning session's `proctor_id`. The submitted `attendance_status` is one of `PRESENT`, `ABSENT`, or `EXCUSED`, moving the registration from `PENDING` along `PENDING → PRESENT`, `PENDING → ABSENT`, or `PENDING → EXCUSED` and incrementing its `version`. Attendance is a one-way street: `PENDING` is the only status from which a proctor may move, and the three outcomes are settled once recorded. Recording `ABSENT` or `EXCUSED` has a second consequence that surfaces much later — it makes the registration eligible to be named as the original of a make-up seat, which is exactly what §3.4's linkage rule looks for.

**`ACTIVE → CLOSED`** is likewise the proctor's act and requires that the floor be genuinely clear. No registration on the session may still be sitting at `PENDING` attendance, and the session's `materials_checked_in` must equal its `materials_checked_out` — every booklet that left the school for this room must have come back to it. The `status` becomes `CLOSED`, `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the `version` increments.

Cancellation is the coordinator's escape hatch and is available from either `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, in both cases only while the session holds no outstanding booklets, meaning `materials_checked_out` equals `materials_checked_in`. Both `DRAFT → CANCELLED` and `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` behave identically: the `status` becomes `CANCELLED`, every registration on the session whose `attendance_status` is still `PENDING` moves `PENDING → WITHDRAWN`, the session then recomputes `seats_consumed`, `separate_setting_count`, and `effective_seat_capacity` over the surviving roster, and the session's `version` increments. Withdrawn registrations stop contributing their `seats_required` to the session's demand.

Two further session transitions are never requested by a client at all. The system itself moves a `SCHEDULED` session to `CANCELLED` when the assigned proctor's `certification_status` has become `REVOKED` by a confirmed breach, withdrawing that session's `PENDING` registrations in the same motion. And the system moves a `CLOSED` session to `INVALIDATED` when an irregularity report naming it is adjudicated as an invalidated session; that transition also drives every registration on the session whose `score_status` is `NOT_SCORED`, `SCORED`, `RELEASED`, `UNDER_APPEAL`, or `CORRECTED` onward to `INVALIDATED`. Both bump the session's `version`, and both are described from the adjudicator's side in §3.7. `CANCELLED` and `INVALIDATED` are terminal for a session.

### 3.7. Irregularities, Breaches, and Adjudication

An irregularity is anything that happened in a room that should not have. A `Proctor` files one against the session being run, naming the `session_id`, optionally a `registration_id` when the irregularity concerns one specific student, a `category` drawn from `ADMIN_ERROR`, `SECURITY_BREACH`, `TECH_FAILURE`, and `STUDENT_MISCONDUCT`, a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, or `HIGH` as the filer judges it, and a `narrative` of between 1 and 2000 characters describing what happened. The filed report begins in `DRAFT` with `system_generated` false, `return_count` 0, `material_lot_id` and `disposition` and `adjudicated_at` null, and `reporter_proctor_id` taken from the filer's `X-User-ID`. Reports raised by a discrepant lot reconciliation are the exception described in §3.5: they are created directly in `SUBMITTED`, skipping `DRAFT` altogether, with `system_generated` true and `reporter_proctor_id` null.

The report then moves by named `to_status`, optionally with a `note`, and each edge belongs to exactly one role. The filing proctor — and only the proctor whose `X-User-ID` equals the report's `reporter_proctor_id` — may move `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` to put it in front of the state, or `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN` to abandon it. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. A `StateAssessmentOfficer` moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` with no further preconditions, and from review may either return the report for more information or adjudicate it.

`UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_INFO` sends the report back to its filer and increases `return_count` by one. It is available only for reports the system did not generate itself — a `system_generated` report has no human filer to return it to — and only while `return_count` is still below 2. A report that has already been returned twice cannot be returned a third time; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETURN_CYCLE_LIMIT` and the report stays in `UNDER_REVIEW`. The filing proctor closes the loop with `RETURNED_FOR_INFO → SUBMITTED`, and review may begin again.

Adjudication moves the report `UNDER_REVIEW → ADJUDICATED` and requires a `disposition`, which is mandatory whenever `to_status` is `ADJUDICATED`. The four dispositions have disjoint consequences.

A disposition of `DISMISSED` carries no preconditions and finds no fault. It stamps `adjudicated_at` from the logical clock. If the report names a `material_lot_id` — that is, if it was the system's own report about a discrepant lot — the lot's `charge_reversed` flag is set to true, which drops that lot out of the district's ledger entirely, and the owning district's `replacement_charge_total_cents`, `security_assessment_cents`, and `open_discrepancy_count` are all recomputed on the reduced set.

A disposition of `INVALIDATE_REGISTRATION` requires that the report actually name a `registration_id`, and that the window it is scoped to has not already reached `FINALIZED`. It drives that one registration's `score_status` to `INVALIDATED` from whichever of `SCORED`, `RELEASED`, `UNDER_APPEAL`, or `CORRECTED` it currently holds — that is, `SCORED → INVALIDATED`, `RELEASED → INVALIDATED`, `UNDER_APPEAL → INVALIDATED`, or `CORRECTED → INVALIDATED`. A registration that was never scored is not reachable by this disposition; only a session-wide invalidation can drive `NOT_SCORED → INVALIDATED`.

A disposition of `INVALIDATE_SESSION` requires that the report name a `session_id`, that the named session's `status` be exactly `CLOSED`, and that the window not already be `FINALIZED`. It drives the session `CLOSED → INVALIDATED` and, with it, every registration on that session whose `score_status` is `NOT_SCORED`, `SCORED`, `RELEASED`, `UNDER_APPEAL`, or `CORRECTED` to `INVALIDATED` — `NOT_SCORED → INVALIDATED`, `SCORED → INVALIDATED`, `RELEASED → INVALIDATED`, `UNDER_APPEAL → INVALIDATED`, and `CORRECTED → INVALIDATED` respectively. A registration that was under appeal when this happens keeps its open appeal: the appeal stays `FILED` and must still be decided before the window can be finalized.

A disposition of `BREACH_CONFIRMED` requires that the report name a `session_id` and that the named session have a non-null `proctor_id`, and it produces the deepest cascade in the system, five hops long. The report is adjudicated. The `ACTIVE` affidavit for that session's proctor and that session's window moves `ACTIVE → REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` set to `BREACH_CONFIRMED`. The proctor then takes `revoked_at` stamped from the logical clock, has `strike_count` increased by one, and has `certification_status` re-derived to `REVOKED`. Every session anywhere in the program assigned to that proctor and still sitting in `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`. And every registration on those newly cancelled sessions whose `attendance_status` is `PENDING` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Each entity touched at each hop takes its own `version` increment.

All four dispositions end by recomputing the governing window's breach accounting, as does every submission and withdrawal of a report. A window's `open_breach_count` is the number of reports scoped to that window — reached either through the session that owns them or through the material lot that raised them — whose `category` is `SECURITY_BREACH` and whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`. The window's `security_hold` is true exactly when that count is greater than zero, and false otherwise. This is the mechanism by which one unresolved breach anywhere in a window blocks the release of every score in it, and by which dismissing or adjudicating the last open breach clears the block.

### 3.8. Make-up Testing, Participation, and Scoring

Participation is the number the state is judged on, and the system computes it twice from the same stored rows with deliberately different rules.

The **provisional** rate is computed when a window moves `MAKEUP → SCORING`. Its denominator is the count of distinct `student_state_id` values across all registrations reachable from the window through their session, restricted to registrations whose session `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE`, or `CLOSED` and whose `attendance_status` is `PENDING`, `PRESENT`, `ABSENT`, or `EXCUSED`. Its numerator is the count of distinct `student_state_id` values within that very same restricted set whose `attendance_status` is `PRESENT`. The rate is the numerator divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to one decimal place; if the denominator is 0, the rate is exactly 0.0. The result is written to `participation_rate_provisional_pct`, and the denominator and numerator are recorded as `registered_distinct_count` and `present_distinct_count` respectively. Counting distinct student identifiers rather than rows is what keeps a make-up seat from inflating either side: a student who is registered twice, once for the original session and once for the make-up, is one student in both counts.

When the provisional rate falls short, the window can go back. **`SCORING → MAKEUP`** is permitted exactly when `participation_rate_provisional_pct` is strictly below `min_participation_pct`. It returns the `status` to `MAKEUP`, extends `makeup_end` in place by 7 days — without that extension the reopened phase would already lie in the past and no make-up session could be scheduled inside it — clears `participation_rate_provisional_pct` back to null, and increments the window's `version`. Schools then seat the students they missed, using make-up sessions and the `makeup_of_registration_id` linkage of §3.4, and the window is driven back to `SCORING` when the extended make-up period ends, recomputing the provisional rate over the enlarged set of present students.

Scoring itself is the `StateAssessmentOfficer`'s work. Recording a score supplies a `raw_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive and drives a registration `NOT_SCORED → SCORED`. Three conditions must hold: the governing window's `status` must be `SCORING`, or the request fails with `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`; the registration's `attendance_status` must be `PRESENT`, since a student who never sat the test has nothing to score; and the owning session's `status` must be `CLOSED`. The `raw_score` is written, `score_status` becomes `SCORED`, and the registration's `version` increments.

### 3.9. Release, Appeals, Finalization, and Getting Results to Families

**`SCORING → RELEASED`** is the gate between the state's internal view of a window and the public one, and three conditions guard it. The window's provisional participation must be at least `min_participation_pct` — the comparison is inclusive, so a window that lands exactly on its floor passes — and a shortfall is `ERR_PARTICIPATION_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The window's `security_hold` must be false, and an unresolved breach is `ERR_SECURITY_HOLD_ACTIVE`. And every registration in the window whose `attendance_status` is `PRESENT` must have a `score_status` of either `SCORED` or `INVALIDATED`; a present student with no outcome at all blocks the release. Because the participation check is screened before the security check, a window that is simultaneously short on participation and under a security hold reports the participation error.

On release the window's `status` becomes `RELEASED`, its `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `appeal_window_closes_at` is set to `released_at` plus `appeal_window_days` days, counted as that many periods of twenty-four hours. Every registration in the window whose `score_status` is `SCORED` moves `SCORED → RELEASED` in the same operation, and both the window and each touched registration take a `version` increment.

Appeals are the safety valve on a released score, and either a `SchoolTestCoordinator` or a `DistrictTestCoordinator` may file one. An appeal names the `registration_id` under dispute, a `reason_code` drawn from `SCORING_ERROR`, `ROSTER_ERROR`, `ACCOMMODATION_NOT_PROVIDED`, and `IRREGULARITY_IMPACT`, and a supporting `narrative` of between 1 and 2000 characters. The server records `filed_by_role` and `filed_by_user_id` from the request headers and `filed_at` from the logical clock, and creates the appeal in `FILED` with `corrected_raw_score` and `decided_at` null. Three gates apply: the governing window must be `RELEASED`, or the filing is `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`; the registration's `score_status` must be exactly `RELEASED`, or the filing is `ERR_SCORE_NOT_RELEASED`; and the logical clock must be strictly before `appeal_window_closes_at`, since an appeal filed at the very instant the window closes is too late and earns `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing drives the registration `RELEASED → UNDER_APPEAL` and increments its `version`.

Only a `StateAssessmentOfficer` decides an appeal, supplying a `decision` of `UPHELD` or `DENIED` and an optional `note`. The appeal must still be `FILED` — a second decision on an already-decided appeal is `ERR_SCORE_NOT_RELEASED` — and the registration must correspondingly still be `UNDER_APPEAL`. An `UPHELD` decision requires a `corrected_raw_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Upholding moves the appeal `FILED → UPHELD`, writes `corrected_raw_score` onto the appeal and `decided_at` from the logical clock, overwrites the registration's `raw_score` with the corrected value, and drives the registration `UNDER_APPEAL → CORRECTED`. Denying moves the appeal `FILED → DENIED`, stamps `decided_at`, and returns the registration `UNDER_APPEAL → RELEASED`, exactly where it was before the appeal, closing the loop. Both outcomes increment the appeal's `version`, and both are terminal for the appeal.

**`RELEASED → FINALIZED`** closes the window for good, and it waits for three things. The logical clock must have reached or passed `appeal_window_closes_at`, failing with `ERR_TIMING_PRECONDITION_NOT_MET`. No score appeal on any registration in the window may still be sitting in `FILED` — including one left open on a registration whose score was invalidated mid-appeal, which is why that case is called out in §3.7. And no irregularity report scoped to the window may still be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`. The order of the finalization side effects is pinned: **first**, every registration in the window whose `score_status` is `RELEASED` or `CORRECTED` moves to `FINAL`, along `RELEASED → FINAL` and `CORRECTED → FINAL` respectively; **then** the final participation rate is computed over the resulting state; **then** the window's `status` becomes `FINALIZED` and its `version` increments. Computing participation before the flip would count nothing at all, so the sequence is not interchangeable.

The **final** participation rate is computed over a deliberately wider population than the provisional one. Its denominator is the count of distinct `student_state_id` values across registrations whose session `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE`, `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED`, or `INVALIDATED` and whose `attendance_status` is `PENDING`, `PRESENT`, `ABSENT`, `EXCUSED`, or `WITHDRAWN` — every seat the provisional variant dropped is restored here. Its numerator is the count of distinct `student_state_id` values within that same set whose `score_status` is `FINAL`. As before, the quotient is multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to one decimal place, a zero denominator yields exactly 0.0, and the result is written to `participation_rate_final_pct` with the denominator and numerator overwriting `registered_distinct_count` and `present_distinct_count`. The two variants ask genuinely different questions — the provisional one asks who showed up, the final one asks who produced a usable score against every seat the program ever created — so for identical stored data they return different percentages, and that difference is expected rather than a defect. `FINALIZED` is terminal for a window.

Family score reports are distributed by a `StateAssessmentOfficer` running a distribution pass over a window, which is only permitted while the window's `status` is `RELEASED`; any other phase is `ERR_WINDOW_PHASE_INVALID`. The pass walks every registration reachable from the window through its session, in ascending `created_at` order — never in identifier order — and classifies each one by the first matching rule in this pinned precedence. A registration whose `report_delivery_status` is anything other than `NOT_QUEUED` was already handled by an earlier pass and is counted as skipped, untouched. Otherwise, a registration whose `score_status` is `INVALIDATED` is marked `SUPPRESSED` with `report_suppressed_reason` of `INVALIDATED`. Otherwise, a registration whose `score_status` is `UNDER_APPEAL` is marked `SUPPRESSED` with `report_suppressed_reason` of `UNDER_APPEAL`. Otherwise, a registration whose `score_status` is `RELEASED` or `CORRECTED` is deliverable, and is marked `SUPPRESSED` with `report_suppressed_reason` of `MISSING_GUARDIAN_EMAIL` when its `guardian_email` is null or empty, or `DELIVERED` with `report_delivered_at` stamped from the logical clock when a `guardian_email` is present. Everything else — `NOT_SCORED`, `SCORED`, and `FINAL` — is counted as skipped.

The pass reports what it did as `distributed_count`, `suppressed_count`, and `skipped_count`, alongside the `window_id` it ran against, and it increments the `version` of every registration it actually touched. Because already-handled rows are skipped rather than reprocessed, running the pass a second time is safe: it will neither re-deliver a report nor overturn a suppression. A run that finds no candidates at all is not an error — it returns three zeros with HTTP 200.
