# Rental Fleet Planning & Remarketing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A rental car fleet department lives or dies on two decisions it must make years apart: how many cars of which kind to buy into which city, and when and through which channel to sell them again. Between those two decisions sits everything else — registration and licensing paperwork before a unit may earn its first dollar, prep bays that can only host so many cars a day, seasonal demand that empties one airport while starving another, odometer readings that quietly erode residual value, hailstorms that write off a car in an afternoon, and manufacturer repurchase programs that guarantee a floor price only if the unit is returned inside a mileage and holding-day envelope. Today those decisions are made in spreadsheets that disagree with each other, and the department discovers the cost of a bad holding-period call only when the auction hammer falls.

This document specifies the Rental Fleet Planning & Remarketing API, the system of record for that entire arc. The API is mounted under `/fleet`. It carries fleet-sizing plans by market, season and segment; purchase allocations split across manufacturer program vehicles and risk vehicles; the in-fleeting pipeline that turns a purchase order into a rentable unit; inter-city redistribution with an explicit transport-cost payback test; a depreciation and residual-value engine with two deliberately divergent valuation variants; damage recovery and total-loss adjudication; and remarketing across auction, retail lots, dealer-direct sales and manufacturer repurchase, with reconditioning decisions in between.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. The first is that **every dollar the fleet spends or recovers on a unit lands as an immutable line in a per-vehicle cost ledger** — nothing is netted silently, and compensating actions post reversing lines rather than editing history. The second is **segregation of duties**: the planner who proposes a transfer may not authorize it, the manager who consigns a car may not settle its sale, and the controller who drafts a depreciation policy may not activate it. These commitments are not advisory. They are enforced as request-time validations with dedicated error codes, and they shape the state machines described in section 3.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the API: authentication, response shape, pagination, the logical clock, resource identity, rounding discipline, concurrency control and the fixed order in which validations fire.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting operator, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that operator is acting. If either header is absent, or is present but empty or consisting only of whitespace, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries any value other than one of the five role names below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (also HTTP 401). A recognized role that is not on the target operation's authorized list yields `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The system recognizes exactly five roles, spelled as follows:

- `PlatformAdmin` — owns the reference configuration of the platform: markets, vehicle segments, manufacturer programs, remarketing channels and the logical clock. This role touches no vehicle, no plan and no money.
- `FleetPlanner` — sizes the fleet. Drafts plans, allocates purchases across program and risk vehicles, mints vehicles against allocations, and proposes inter-city redistributions. It cannot lock its own plan and cannot authorize its own transfer.
- `FleetController` — the control function. Locks plans, owns depreciation policy, authorizes and rejects redistributions, waives in-fleeting tasks, and adjudicates damage write-offs and total losses.
- `RemarketingManager` — runs disposal. Consigns vehicles to channels, authorizes and books reconditioning, lists, records sales, settles and withdraws, and works damage-recovery claims.
- `MarketOperator` — the branch on the ground. In-fleets vehicles, schedules and completes registration, licensing and preparation work, posts rental usage, reports and revises damage, and dispatches and receives transfers.

Four of the five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `FleetPlanner`, `FleetController` or `RemarketingManager`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is, however, recorded where a transition demands an audit subject: as `proposed_by_user_id` and `authorized_by_user_id` on a redistribution order, as `created_by_user_id` and `settled_by_user_id` on a consignment, and as `created_by_user_id` and `activated_by_user_id` on a depreciation policy. Those recorded values are what the segregation-of-duties checks in §2.8 compare against.

`MarketOperator` is different: its identity is **entity-backed by a Market**. When `X-User-Role` is `MarketOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Market` row, and ownership checks compare the target resource's market foreign key to `X-User-ID` directly. That comparison is a plain string equality test and nothing else: the server never verifies that the quoted identifier names a `Market` row that actually exists, so an operator acting under an unknown identifier simply fails the ownership comparison rather than receiving a 404. The comparison field depends on the operation, and there are eight operations under this scope: in-fleeting a vehicle, posting usage on a vehicle, scheduling an in-fleeting task for a vehicle and reporting damage on a vehicle all compare against that vehicle's `current_market_id`; completing an in-fleeting task compares against the task's `market_id`; revising a damage estimate compares against the damage event's `market_id`; dispatching a redistribution order compares against the order's `origin_market_id`; and receiving a redistribution order compares against the order's `destination_market_id`, because the receiving branch is the one that signs for the haul. A mismatch is `ERR_MARKET_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This check runs only after the path identifiers have been resolved, since the owning market cannot be determined before the target row is loaded.

All five roles may perform every read operation in this API, with two exceptions: the plan-performance computed read and the vehicle channel-recommendation computed read are closed to `MarketOperator` and available to the other four roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from real time, so every response reports the clock's current value and repeated calls carry an identical timestamp until the clock is repositioned.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. A resource object always carries every field its entity declares, whatever the row's state: a field that has not yet been assigned a value is present and null rather than omitted.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted the server uses 10, and an explicitly supplied value must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 200. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins; when omitted it is 0, and a supplied value must be an integer of at least 0. A `limit` outside the accepted range of 1 through 200, or a negative `offset`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422), and so is either parameter supplied as anything that is not a base-ten integer. A parameter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, and an `offset` that lands past the end of the matching set yields an empty page rather than an error.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. Because the logical clock of §2.4 stands still between repositions, many rows legitimately share an identical `created_at`, so every ordering in this API carries the server's own insertion sequence as its final tie-break: rows written under the same instant come back in the order they were created. The two ledger listings are the documented exceptions to the sort key itself: they order by `posted_at` ascending, break ties by `created_at` ascending, and then fall back to that same insertion sequence.

Both paginated and unpaginated list responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the current window. Every filter a list endpoint declares is an exact-match comparison against the stored field, and filtering happens before the ordering and before the page window. A filter value that matches nothing simply produces an empty page rather than a validation error — including a boolean filter spelled as anything other than `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`, and an integer filter such as `program_year` spelled as anything that is not a base-ten integer — and a query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored.

### 2.4. The Logical Fleet Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. The server maintains a single **logical UTC clock**, and every holding-period computation, every prep-bay window comparison, every capacity calendar date, every season evaluation and every stamped instant reads that logical clock. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must continue to read that value until it is explicitly repositioned; falling back to real time is a defect. A `PlatformAdmin` may reposition the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and any role may read the clock's current value. Both the reposition and the read answer with a single-field object carrying `current_utc`, whose value is the instant exactly as the most recent reposition spelled it — echoed back verbatim rather than normalised to three fractional digits — and is the literal `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until the first reposition.

Repositioning the clock is not retroactive. No stored entity is recomputed when the clock moves; already-stamped instants such as `in_service_at` or `listed_at` keep the values they were given, and only computations performed after the move see the new instant. Moving the clock backwards is therefore permitted and is the ordinary way to arrange a scenario, but it never rewrites history.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID. The identifier bears no type prefix and no domain-encoded structure; clients must never parse it or synthesize one. Each resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits recorded from the logical clock at the moment the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each subsequent mutation. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a child vehicle enters service and its parent allocation's `in_fleeted_count` is recomputed, that allocation's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, exactly as if a client had written to it. This holds for `Market`, `VehicleSegment`, `ManufacturerProgram`, `RemarketingChannel`, `DepreciationPolicy`, `FleetPlan`, `PurchaseAllocation`, `Vehicle`, `InFleetingTask`, `RedistributionOrder`, `DamageEvent` and `Consignment`.

Ledger rows are the single exception. A `LedgerEntry` is append-only and immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field at all, and neither should appear in its representation. There is deliberately no create, update or delete operation for ledger rows — every line is written as a side effect of some other transition, and the only way to reach one is to read it.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in **whole cents**, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is such an integer. Wherever a monetary result is derived from a multiplication involving a fractional rate or factor, the result is rounded to the nearest whole cent using **round half up** — a residue of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero, upward in magnitude. No monetary field is ever returned as a decimal string or as a floating-point number with sub-cent precision.

Percentage and factor fields — `target_utilization_pct`, `utilization_target_pct`, `repurchase_guarantee_pct`, `fee_pct`, `expected_proceeds_multiplier`, `monthly_depreciation_pct` and `floor_residual_pct` — are stored as fractions of one, carried to four decimal places, and are echoed back at the precision they were supplied with. A value carrying more than four decimal places is neither rejected nor rounded on the way in: it is stored, echoed and used in every calculation at exactly the precision the client gave. Utilization figures produced by the plan-performance calculation are likewise fractions of one rounded half up to four decimal places.

Two rounding conventions matter enough to state as principles, because getting either wrong changes published numbers by cents:

- **Per-line rounding before summation.** When a total is assembled from several cost lines that each involve a rate, each line is rounded to whole cents on its own and only then are the rounded lines added. Summing the unrounded lines and rounding the total once is a different and forbidden order.
- **Floor, not round, for per-unit revenue.** Revenue-per-unit figures are integer divisions truncated toward zero — the fractional cent is discarded, never rounded up. Day counts and month counts derived from elapsed time are likewise integer floors.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Rows

Two write operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against their parent row's `version`. In both cases the version field is a **required** input, not an optional hint, and a request that omits it is a schema violation.

Adding a purchase allocation to a fleet plan requires `expected_plan_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on that `FleetPlan`. If it does not equal the plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A plan legitimately accepts many allocations, so a writer holding a fresh version always succeeds; the rejection exists to catch the case where an earlier allocation — or an allocation cancellation — has already moved `allocated_unit_count` and therefore the plan's `posture`, invalidating the sizing arithmetic on which the current writer based its `unit_count`.

Creating a consignment requires `expected_vehicle_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on that `Vehicle`. If it does not equal the vehicle's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VEHICLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new consignment legitimately supersedes a withdrawn one on the same vehicle, so a writer holding a fresh version always succeeds; the rejection catches the case where a usage posting, a damage report or a withdrawal has already moved the vehicle's odometer, condition or hold baseline, invalidating the valuation on which the channel choice was made.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Operations not named above must not be given one and must not require one; supplying an unexpected field is ignored rather than rejected, per §2.9.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties in Two-Man Controls

Three controls in this system are deliberately split across two people, and in all three the second actor's `X-User-ID` must differ from the first actor's recorded identifier. Authorizing a redistribution order compares the acting `X-User-ID` against that order's stored `proposed_by_user_id`. Settling a consignment compares the acting `X-User-ID` against that consignment's stored `created_by_user_id`. Activating a depreciation policy compares the acting `X-User-ID` against that policy's stored `created_by_user_id`. Whenever the two identifiers are equal the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), regardless of how correct the rest of the request is.

Note the asymmetry that follows from re-proposal: because `proposed_by_user_id` is rewritten every time a redistribution order is proposed, the operator who is locked out of authorizing is the one who submitted the *most recent* proposal, not whoever submitted the first one.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

When a request path carries more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve determines the response. Each entity has its own dedicated 404 code: `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLEET_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DAMAGE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. A request naming a vehicle under an allocation that does not exist reports the allocation's code, not the vehicle's.

Foreign keys carried in a request **body** are resolved after the path, in the order the operation's input list declares them, and they use the same per-entity 404 codes. Five write operations resolve body foreign keys this way: drafting a depreciation policy (its `segment_id`, then its optional `supersedes_policy_id`), drafting a fleet plan (its `market_id`), adding an allocation to a plan (its `program_id`, then its `segment_id`), creating a consignment (its `vehicle_id`, then its `channel_id`), and drafting a redistribution order (its `vehicle_id`, then its `destination_market_id`). A body foreign key that is present but is not a non-empty string — a null, a number, or a blank string — is not treated as an unresolvable reference at this rank; it falls through to the schema rank below, where a required key reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and an optional one such as `supersedes_policy_id` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted when it is null, while a blank string or a non-string value there reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` just as a required key would.

Because many rules can be violated by a single request, the order in which checks fire is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following sequence, and the **first** violation encountered is the one reported:

1. Headers present and non-empty — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).
2. The declared role is one of the five recognized names — `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
3. The declared role appears on the target operation's authorized list — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. Market scope for `MarketOperator`, evaluated after existence so the owning market can be resolved — `ERR_MARKET_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
5. Every path identifier resolves, left to right — the per-entity 404 codes above.
6. Every body foreign key resolves, in declared field order — the same per-entity 404 codes.
7. Request schema: all required body fields present, enum values inside their declared sets and matched case-sensitively, integer fields supplied as JSON integers and non-negative where declared — a whole-valued float, a numeric string or a boolean is refused even though its value would be in range, while the percentage and factor fields accept any JSON number — and instants parseable as ISO-8601 UTC, which here means anywhere from zero to nine fractional digits with either a `Z` suffix or an explicit `±hh:mm` offset that is normalised to UTC for every comparison yet stored and echoed back verbatim in the spelling the client supplied — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). A field explicitly set to null counts as absent, so a required one is reported missing and an optional one takes its documented default. A request body that is absent or unparseable is treated as an empty object and its required fields are reported missing, while a body that parses to something other than a JSON object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unknown body fields, and attempts to write system-managed or computed fields, are silently ignored rather than rejected. Pagination bounds on list operations fire at this same rank as `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422), as does the allocation-specific requirement that `unit_count` be an integer of at least 1, reported as `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_COUNT` (422).
8. Optimistic concurrency — `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_VEHICLE_VERSION` (412).
9. Uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_MARKET_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEGMENT_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_FLEET_PLAN` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_TASK_TYPE` (all 409).
10. State preconditions on the target row and its ancestors — `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_LOCKED`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_FLEET_STATE`, `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE`, `ERR_TASK_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DAMAGE_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED` and `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (all 409).
11. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). It ranks *below* state checks, so a settle attempt by the creating operator on a consignment that is not in the right state reports the state error first.
12. Total-loss routing safety — `ERR_TOTAL_LOSS_DETERMINATION_REQUIRED` and `ERR_TOTAL_LOSS_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422).
13. Open damage blocking disposal — `ERR_OPEN_DAMAGE_BLOCKS_LISTING` (422).
14. Temporal sequencing and window shape — `ERR_PREP_BEFORE_REGISTRATION`, `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` and `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_INVALID` (422), then the minimum-holding gate `ERR_MIN_HOLDING_PERIOD_NOT_MET` (422), then the interval-collision checks `ERR_VEHICLE_TRANSFER_OVERLAP` and `ERR_PREP_BAY_CONFLICT` (409). Within this band the three shape checks outrank the holding gate, and the holding gate outranks the two collision checks.
15. Referential and configuration checks, in this internal order: `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` and `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_MISMATCH`; then `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED` and `ERR_SAME_MARKET_TRANSFER`; then `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_DEPRECIATION_POLICY` and `ERR_MILEAGE_BAND_EXCLUDES_CHANNEL` (all 422).
16. Capacity and constraint checks, in this internal order: `ERR_ALLOCATION_UNIT_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_PREP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_ODOMETER_REGRESSION`; then `ERR_RECONDITION_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_RECONDITION_OVERRUN` and `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_CAP`; then `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CHANNEL` (all 422).
17. Fiscal checks last, in this internal order: `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`; then `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN` and `ERR_TRANSPORT_COST_EXCEEDS_BENEFIT`; then `ERR_PROCEEDS_BELOW_FLOOR` (all 422).

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. A consignment request that names an ineligible channel *and* whose vehicle is over the segment's hard mileage limit reports `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` only when the ineligibility comes from something other than mileage; the retail-lot mileage exclusion has its own dedicated code and is reported as `ERR_MILEAGE_BAND_EXCLUDES_CHANNEL`. And a stale-version create is rejected before any uniqueness or state check runs, so an allocation carrying a stale `expected_plan_version` against a plan that is also frozen reports the stale version, not the posture.

### 2.10. The Per-Vehicle Fleet Cost Ledger

Every unit in the fleet owns an append-only stream of ledger rows, and that stream is the authoritative record of the unit's cost basis. A row names the `vehicle_id` it is booked against, an `entry_type` classifying the line, a signed `amount_cents`, the `posted_at` instant taken from the logical clock, and a human-readable `memo` describing the line's provenance — for a recovery settlement, for example, the memo spells out the three-way split, in the shape `recovery waterfall recondition 250000 / depreciation 350000 / income 100000`. The sign convention is fixed and uniform: a **positive** `amount_cents` is a cost debited to the unit's fleet cost basis, and a **negative** `amount_cents` is a credit, a recovery or sale proceeds.

`entry_type` is drawn from exactly ten values: `CAPITALIZATION`, `PREP_COST`, `TRANSPORT_COST`, `RECONDITION_COST`, `RECONDITION_REVERSAL`, `CHANNEL_FEE`, `DAMAGE_RECOVERY`, `SALVAGE_PROCEEDS`, `DISPOSAL_PROCEEDS` and `DISPOSAL_GAIN_LOSS`.

Because a ledger row can be emitted by any of several different transitions, its origin is recorded polymorphically rather than as a conventional foreign key: `source_entity_type` names the kind of row whose transition emitted the line and is one of `Vehicle`, `InFleetingTask`, `RedistributionOrder`, `DamageEvent` or `Consignment`, while `source_entity_id` carries that row's identifier. The pair is deliberately not a single-target foreign key because the target type varies from line to line.

Corrections are never edits. When a cost has to be undone — the reconditioning spend on a withdrawn consignment is the canonical case — the system posts a new, oppositely signed line rather than deleting or amending the original, so that the arithmetic sum of the stream always equals the unit's current cost basis.

### 2.11. Parallel Lifecycle Dimensions and Cascade Propagation

A `Vehicle` carries **two independent status dimensions on the same row**, and they must be evaluated and mutated separately. `fleet_status` tracks where the unit sits in the fleet's economic lifecycle and takes one of `ON_ORDER`, `IN_FLEETING`, `IN_SERVICE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `HELD_FOR_SALE`, `SOLD` or `TOTAL_LOSS`. `condition_status` tracks the physical state of the car and takes one of `SOUND`, `DAMAGE_REPORTED`, `RECONDITIONING`, `RECONDITIONED` or `TOTALED`. Reporting damage moves `condition_status` and leaves `fleet_status` untouched; dispatching a transfer moves `fleet_status` and leaves `condition_status` untouched. An illegal move on the first dimension is `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_FLEET_STATE` (HTTP 409); an illegal move on the second is `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE` (HTTP 409). Both dimensions are cyclic: a unit may leave and re-enter `IN_SERVICE` through `IN_TRANSIT` and through `HELD_FOR_SALE`, and a car may return to `SOUND` after reconditioning and then be damaged again.

Some transitions in this system are never requested by a client at all. They are **bubble-up** transitions the server performs because a child's change satisfied a parent's criteria, and they mutate the parent exactly as a client write would: refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version`. The in-fleeting chain is the longest of these and runs four hops: resolving an in-fleeting task promotes the vehicle when it is the last of the three required task types; the vehicle's promotion recomputes its allocation's counters and may advance the allocation's status; the allocation's advance to a fully in-fleeted state triggers the plan's closure evaluation; and the plan closes when its in-fleeted count has reached its planned count. The total-loss chain runs three: declaring a total loss on a damage event retires the vehicle on both dimensions, and the vehicle's retirement cancels every still-open redistribution order and withdraws every still-open consignment for that unit.

Cascades that reverse an operation are held to the same standard as the forward path. Cancelling an allocation returns its units to the plan's headroom and recomputes the plan's posture; cancelling an in-transit order restores the origin market's census to exactly its pre-dispatch value; withdrawing a consignment restores the vehicle's census contribution, clears its frozen hold baseline, and posts a reversing reconditioning line so that the unit's reconditioning total returns to zero.

### 2.12. Derived Census and Roll-up Counters

Several fields in this system are computed, never written by clients, and are recomputed whenever their inputs change.

A `Market` exposes `in_service_unit_count`, the number of vehicles whose `current_market_id` equals that market and whose `fleet_status` is exactly `IN_SERVICE`. A unit in `IN_TRANSIT` is counted by neither its origin nor its destination — it deliberately falls out of both censuses for the duration of the haul, and reappears at whichever market ends up holding it. The census is derived from the vehicle rows themselves every time a market is read rather than kept as a stored counter, and a change in it is **not** a mutation of the market row: a `Market`'s `updated_at` and `version` move only when the market is suspended.

A `FleetPlan` exposes three derived numbers. `allocated_unit_count` is the sum of `unit_count` across the plan's allocations whose status is `ORDERED`, `PARTIALLY_INFLEETED` or `INFLEETED`; allocations in `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. `in_fleeted_unit_count` is the count of vehicles belonging to any allocation of the plan whose `fleet_status` is one of `IN_SERVICE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `HELD_FOR_SALE` or `SOLD` — units still `ON_ORDER` or `IN_FLEETING` have not yet been in-fleeted, and a unit in `TOTAL_LOSS` is no longer counted. `posture` is the plan's derived gate over child allocation writes and is described where it bites, in §3.3.

A `PurchaseAllocation` exposes two. `in_fleeted_count` counts its own vehicles whose `fleet_status` is one of `IN_SERVICE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `HELD_FOR_SALE` or `SOLD`. `created_vehicle_count` counts **all** of its vehicles regardless of `fleet_status`, and is capped at the allocation's `unit_count`.

A `Vehicle` exposes `total_recondition_cents`, the signed sum of that unit's ledger rows whose `entry_type` is `RECONDITION_COST` or `RECONDITION_REVERSAL`. Because reversals are negative, a spend that is later reversed brings the total back to 0. It also exposes `net_book_value_cents`, the output of the residual valuation described in §3.5 under the variant that the unit's manufacturer program pins. Unlike the four operations named in §3.2, an ordinary vehicle read never fails for want of a policy: when the unit sits on a `RISK` program and its segment has no `ACTIVE` depreciation policy, the read reports `net_book_value_cents` as null, while a unit on a `PROGRAM` program reports its guaranteed value whether a policy exists or not.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Reference Configuration a Fleet Is Planned Against

Before a single car can be planned, four kinds of reference data must exist, and all four are owned exclusively by `PlatformAdmin`.

A **Market** is a rental city or airport. It is registered with a `code` of three to five uppercase letters that must be globally unique — a second market claiming an existing code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MARKET_CODE` (HTTP 409) — a human-readable `name`, and a `region` that is one of `EAST`, `CENTRAL` or `WEST`. The rest of a market's fields are economics the rest of the system reads. `transport_hub_distance_km` is the distance in kilometres from the market to its remarketing hub and must be zero or greater; it drives the transport line on every consignment sold out of this market. `transfer_cost_per_km_cents` is the line-haul rate in cents per kilometre charged when this market is the **origin** of a transfer and must be greater than zero. `storage_cost_per_day_cents` is the daily yard cost in cents for a unit held at this market and must be zero or greater. `prep_capacity_per_day` is the ceiling on how many preparation jobs this market's bays can start on a single UTC calendar date and must be at least 1. `registration_lead_days` is the market's advertised title-and-registration lead time in days, must be zero or greater, and is purely informational — no rule reads it. A newly registered market opens in status `ACTIVE` with an `in_service_unit_count` of 0.

A market can be taken out of play. Suspension moves `status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, is permitted only while the market is `ACTIVE` — suspending a market that is already `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422) — and increments the market's `version`. For the purposes of this system suspension is one-way: there is no un-suspend capability. A `SUSPENDED` market can no longer host new fleet plans, cannot be the market a vehicle is in-fleeted at, and cannot be the destination of a transfer, each of those refusals carrying `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422). Markets are discoverable by `region`, by `status` and by `code`.

A **VehicleSegment** is a class of car — `ECONOMY`, `MIDSIZE`, `SUV`, `LUXURY`, `VAN` and the like. Its `code` is uppercase and globally unique, with a collision reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEGMENT_CODE` (HTTP 409), and it carries a `name` alongside five policy numbers. `baseline_daily_rate_cents` is the segment's reference daily rental rate in cents, must be greater than zero, and is the basis of the transfer payback test. `target_utilization_pct` is the segment's utilization target expressed as a fraction of one carried to four decimal places, greater than zero and at most 1. `target_revenue_per_unit_month_cents` is the target monthly revenue per in-fleet unit in cents and must be greater than zero; like the utilization target it is a planning reference the API stores and returns rather than enforces. `mileage_band_soft_limit_km` is the odometer reading in kilometres above which a unit's mileage band becomes elevated and must be greater than zero; `mileage_band_hard_limit_km` is the reading above which the retail-lot channel is closed to the unit entirely and must be strictly greater than the soft limit. `recondition_cap_cents` is the most that may be authorized as reconditioning spend on one consignment for a car in this segment and must be zero or greater. Segments are discoverable by `code`.

A **ManufacturerProgram** captures the purchase terms behind a lot of cars. `manufacturer_name` and `program_year` identify the offer, the latter an integer greater than zero and otherwise unconstrained; `program_type` is either `PROGRAM`, meaning the manufacturer guarantees a repurchase, or `RISK`, meaning the fleet bears the whole residual exposure. `repurchase_guarantee_pct` is the fraction of capitalized cost guaranteed at repurchase, between 0 and 1 inclusive at four decimal places, and it must be exactly 0 when `program_type` is `RISK`. `min_holding_days` is the minimum number of days a unit must sit in service before it may be pulled for sale and must be zero or greater; `max_holding_days` is the number of days beyond which manufacturer repurchase stops being an eligible channel and must be strictly greater than `min_holding_days`. `max_eligible_mileage_km` is the odometer ceiling in kilometres past which excess-mileage charges begin and repurchase eligibility ends, and must be greater than zero; `excess_mileage_charge_cents_per_km` is the cents deducted from the guarantee for each kilometre above that ceiling and must be zero or greater; `damage_deduction_cap_cents` is the ceiling in cents on the aggregate open-damage deduction against the guarantee and must be zero or greater. A program opens `ACTIVE`.

Closing a program moves it from `ACTIVE` to `CLOSED`, is permitted only from `ACTIVE` — closing one that is already `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED` (HTTP 409) — and increments its `version`. From that point no new purchase allocation may name it, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED` (HTTP 409); allocations and vehicles already bought under the program keep their terms untouched. Programs are discoverable by `program_type`, by `program_year` and by `status`.

A **RemarketingChannel** is a route to a buyer. Its `code` is one of exactly four values — `AUCTION`, `RETAIL_LOT`, `DEALER_DIRECT` or `PROGRAM_REPURCHASE` — and is unique, so a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_CODE` (HTTP 409). Alongside a `name`, a channel declares `fee_pct`, the selling commission as a fraction of gross proceeds at four decimal places, at least 0 and strictly less than 1; `fixed_fee_cents`, a flat listing or entry fee in cents of zero or more; `expected_days_to_sell`, the expected days to liquidation, greater than zero, which is also the ascending tie-break key of the channel selector; `expected_proceeds_multiplier`, the projected gross proceeds as a multiple of the unit's net book value at four decimal places and greater than zero; and `recondition_required`, a boolean that, when true, forces every consignment on this channel through a reconditioning stage before it may be published. Channels open `ACTIVE` and are discoverable by `code`, by `status` and by `recondition_required`.

Suspending a channel moves its `status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, is permitted only from `ACTIVE` — and because the catalogue carries no dedicated channel-state code, suspending one that is already `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) — increments its `version`, and removes it from every vehicle's candidate pool for channel selection. Only `ACTIVE` channels are ever considered.

### 3.2. Depreciation Policy: Draft, Two-Man Activation, Supersession

Residual value in this system is not a constant — it is the output of a per-segment depreciation curve that the fleet's control function owns and revises over time. A `FleetController` drafts a **DepreciationPolicy** against a `segment_id`, giving it a `name` such as `MID-2026-H1` and three curve parameters: `monthly_depreciation_pct`, the fraction of capitalized cost written off per whole completed holding month, strictly between 0 and 1 at four decimal places; `mileage_depreciation_cents_per_km`, the cents of depreciation charged per odometer kilometre, zero or greater; and `floor_residual_pct`, the fraction of capitalized cost below which the schedule's book value may not fall, again strictly between 0 and 1 at four decimal places. An `effective_from` instant, ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, records the earliest moment the policy may legally be switched on.

A draft may name the policy it intends to replace through `supersedes_policy_id`. When supplied, that identifier must name a policy on the **same** `segment_id` whose status is `ACTIVE`; naming a policy from another segment, or one that is still `DRAFT` or already `SUPERSEDED`, is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). For the very first policy on a segment the field is left out entirely and defaults to null. The draft is stored in status `DRAFT`, and the drafting operator's `X-User-ID` is recorded on it as `created_by_user_id`.

Activation is the two-man half of the control, and it is also a `FleetController` action. Five things must hold. The policy's `status` must still be `DRAFT`; an already `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` policy is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). The policy's `effective_from` must be at or before the current logical clock — a policy whose effective instant is still in the future is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). If some policy for the same segment is currently `ACTIVE`, this draft's `supersedes_policy_id` must be exactly that policy's `id`; and if no policy for the segment is currently `ACTIVE`, this draft's `supersedes_policy_id` must be null. A violation of either of those last two conditions is reported as `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

On success the activating operator's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `activated_by_user_id`, the policy moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, and the policy it superseded — if there was one — moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. Both rows are mutations: the activated policy and the superseded policy each have their `version` incremented by 1. The `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` move is never requested directly; it happens only because another policy for the same segment named this one and was activated. The invariant this machinery protects is simple and absolute: **at most one `ACTIVE` policy exists per segment at any instant**, and valuation reads only ever consult that one — `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` curves are never read by any calculation. When no `ACTIVE` policy exists for a segment, every operation that needs a valuation for a car in that segment fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_DEPRECIATION_POLICY` (HTTP 422); those operations are the vehicle valuation read, the channel-recommendation read, consignment creation and damage reporting. Policies are discoverable by `segment_id`, by `supersedes_policy_id` and by `status`.

### 3.3. Sizing the Fleet: Plans, Postures and Purchase Allocations

A `FleetPlanner` opens the planning cycle by drafting a **FleetPlan** for one market, one season and one calendar year. The payload names the `market_id` the plan covers, a `season` of `PEAK`, `SHOULDER` or `OFF_PEAK`, a `plan_year` of 2020 or later, a `planned_unit_count` greater than zero, a `revenue_per_unit_target_cents` greater than zero, and a `utilization_target_pct` greater than 0 and at most 1 carried to four decimal places. The combination of `market_id`, `season` and `plan_year` is unique across all plans, so a second plan for the same triple is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FLEET_PLAN` (HTTP 409), and the named market must be `ACTIVE` at draft time or the request fails with `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422). A new plan is stored in status `DRAFT`, with `allocated_unit_count` 0, `in_fleeted_unit_count` 0 and `posture` `OPEN`. Plans are discoverable by `market_id`, by `season`, by `plan_year` and by `status`.

A `DRAFT` plan buys nothing. Locking it is the `FleetController`'s act of committing to the sizing, and it moves the plan `DRAFT → LOCKED`. Two conditions are checked: the plan's `status` must still be `DRAFT`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409); and the plan's market must still be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED`. Locking recomputes the plan's `posture` from its `allocated_unit_count` — which for a plan that has taken no allocations means a fill ratio of zero and therefore `OPEN` — and increments the plan's `version` by 1.

**Posture** is the derived gate that governs every allocation write against the plan, and it is worth spelling out precisely because two of the system's fiscal refusals hang off it. The plan's fill ratio is expressed in basis points: multiply `allocated_unit_count` by ten thousand, divide by `planned_unit_count`, and take the integer floor of the quotient. When that figure is below 9000 — that is, when the plan is less than ninety percent allocated — `posture` is `OPEN`. When it is 9000 or above but still below 10000 — from ninety percent up to, but not reaching, full allocation — `posture` is `RESTRICTED`. When it is 10000 or above — the plan is allocated to its planned size or beyond — `posture` is `FROZEN`.

Adding a **PurchaseAllocation** to a `LOCKED` plan is how the planned units get split across manufacturer programs and segments. The payload names a `program_id`, a `segment_id`, a `unit_count`, a `capitalized_cost_per_unit_cents` greater than zero, and the optimistic-concurrency field `expected_plan_version` described in §2.7. Allocations may only be added to a plan whose `status` is `LOCKED`: a plan still in `DRAFT` has committed to nothing, and a `CLOSED` plan is finished, so either case is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). `unit_count` must be an integer of at least 1, and a non-integer or smaller value — as well as one omitted altogether or supplied as null — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_COUNT` (HTTP 422) rather than with the generic schema code. The named program must be `ACTIVE`, not `CLOSED`. And the plan's posture gates the write: while the posture is `RESTRICTED`, an allocation is accepted only if adding its `unit_count` leaves `allocated_unit_count` at or below `planned_unit_count`, and an allocation that would overshoot is refused with `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422); while the posture is `FROZEN`, no allocation is accepted at any size whatsoever, and the refusal is `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 422). The posture that gates the write is the one derived from the plan's fill ratio **before** this allocation is applied, and an `OPEN` posture is not size-gated at all: a single allocation against a plan still under ninety percent allocated is accepted however far past `planned_unit_count` it carries the plan, which is exactly how a plan arrives at `FROZEN` in one step. Because the restricted gate is checked before the frozen gate, an oversized allocation against a plan sitting at ninety-five percent reports the restricted code.

A successful allocation is stored in status `ORDERED`. It also mutates its parent: the plan's `allocated_unit_count` rises by the allocation's `unit_count`, the plan's `posture` is recomputed from the new fill ratio, and the plan's `version` is incremented by 1. That last effect is exactly why the concurrency field exists — the very next allocation a client submits against the same plan must carry the new version.

Allocation cancellation is the compensating action, and it belongs to the `FleetPlanner`. It moves the allocation `ORDERED → CANCELLED` and is permitted only when the allocation's `status` is `ORDERED` **and** its `created_vehicle_count` is exactly 0; an allocation that is `PARTIALLY_INFLEETED`, `INFLEETED` or already `CANCELLED`, or one that has already minted even a single car, is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (HTTP 409). The plan the allocation belongs to must be `LOCKED`, or the refusal is `ERR_PLAN_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409); the allocation's own cancellability is tested first, so an already-`CANCELLED` allocation hanging beneath a `CLOSED` plan reports `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_CANCELLABLE` and not the plan's state. Cancellation conserves the plan's arithmetic exactly: the plan's `allocated_unit_count` is reduced by the cancelled allocation's `unit_count`, the plan's `posture` is recomputed from the reduced figure, the plan's `version` is incremented by 1, and the allocation's own `version` is incremented by 1. A plan pushed to `FROZEN` by an over-eager allocation therefore returns to `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN` when that allocation is cancelled.

Beyond `CANCELLED`, an allocation's status is **compound** — it is derived from what its child vehicles have done, never set by an operator. Three system transitions are declared. From `ORDERED` an allocation moves to `PARTIALLY_INFLEETED` when a child vehicle enters service and the recomputed `in_fleeted_count` is greater than 0 but strictly less than the allocation's `unit_count`; the move recomputes `in_fleeted_count` and increments the allocation's `version` by 1. From `ORDERED` an allocation moves straight to `INFLEETED` when a child vehicle enters service and the recomputed `in_fleeted_count` equals `unit_count`; this is the path a single-unit allocation always takes, skipping `PARTIALLY_INFLEETED` entirely. From `PARTIALLY_INFLEETED` an allocation moves to `INFLEETED` on the same condition. Both of the transitions that land in `INFLEETED` recompute `in_fleeted_count`, increment the allocation's `version` by 1, and then hand control to the plan's closure evaluation. `INFLEETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal for an allocation. Allocations are discoverable by `plan_id`, by `program_id`, by `segment_id` and by `status`.

The plan's own terminal transition closes the loop. A plan moves `LOCKED → CLOSED` when an allocation beneath it has just reached `INFLEETED` and the plan's recomputed `in_fleeted_unit_count` is greater than or equal to its `planned_unit_count`. The transition requires the plan to be in `LOCKED` at the moment of evaluation, recomputes both `in_fleeted_unit_count` and `posture`, and increments the plan's `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal, and it is reachable **only** by this bubble-up — no client request can close a plan directly.

### 3.4. From Purchase Order to First Rent

Minting a physical car against an allocation is a `FleetPlanner` action. The payload carries only two things: a `vin` of exactly sixteen characters that is globally unique across the whole fleet — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN` (HTTP 409) — and a `current_market_id` naming the market that will physically hold the unit. That `current_market_id` is not one of the five body foreign keys enumerated in §2.9, so it is resolved only after the `vin` schema check, and an identifier naming no market reports `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); the named market's `status` is deliberately **not** examined at minting, so a car may be minted into a `SUSPENDED` market and is stopped later, when in-fleeting is attempted. Everything economic about the car is inherited and cannot be supplied by the client: `segment_id` and `program_id` are copied from the allocation's `segment_id` and `program_id` and are immutable for the life of the unit, and `capitalized_cost_cents` is copied from the allocation's `capitalized_cost_per_unit_cents`. The allocation must be in `ORDERED` or `PARTIALLY_INFLEETED` for minting to be allowed, and an allocation in any other status is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (HTTP 409). The allocation's unit count is a hard ceiling: minting a car that would push the count of **every** vehicle ever minted against the allocation — whatever its `fleet_status`, a unit written off as a `TOTAL_LOSS` very much included — past its `unit_count` is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_UNIT_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422), so a destroyed car never frees its slot up again.

A newly minted car appears with `fleet_status` `ON_ORDER`, `condition_status` `SOUND`, an `odometer_km` of 0, and zeros across `rented_days_total`, `available_days_total` and `revenue_to_date_cents`. Its milestone instants — `registered_at`, `licensed_at`, `prep_completed_at`, `first_rent_ready_at`, `in_service_at`, `held_for_sale_at` — are all null, as are `net_book_value_at_hold_cents`, and `total_recondition_cents` starts at 0. Creation also books the purchase: a ledger line of `entry_type` `CAPITALIZATION` is posted for the unit with an `amount_cents` equal to its `capitalized_cost_cents` as a positive debit, with `source_entity_type` `Vehicle` and `source_entity_id` the new vehicle's own identifier. The allocation's `created_vehicle_count` is recomputed — being derived from the vehicle rows at read time it needs no write, so minting neither refreshes the allocation's `updated_at` nor increments its `version`, and it leaves the grandparent plan untouched as well. Vehicles are discoverable by `allocation_id`, by `segment_id`, by `program_id`, by `current_market_id`, by `fleet_status`, by `condition_status` and by `vin`.

In-fleeting begins when the branch takes delivery. A `MarketOperator` moves the unit `ON_ORDER → IN_FLEETING`, and four things are checked beyond the operator's market scope: the unit's `fleet_status` must be `ON_ORDER`; its allocation must be in `ORDERED` or `PARTIALLY_INFLEETED`; the **grandparent** plan — the plan that owns the allocation that produced this car — must be in `LOCKED`, which is the cross-generation guard that makes `ERR_PLAN_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409) reachable from a vehicle operation; and the unit's current market must be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED`. The transition increments the vehicle's `version` by 1 and nothing else. Note carefully that the gate is the plan's state, not the allocation's.

Three pieces of paperwork stand between an in-fleeting car and its first rent, and each is an **InFleetingTask** scheduled by a `MarketOperator` against a vehicle that is in `IN_FLEETING`. The task declares a `task_type` of `REGISTRATION`, `LICENSING` or `PREPARATION` and a `cost_cents` of zero or more. Its `market_id` is copied from the vehicle's `current_market_id` at creation and is the scope against which prep-bay capacity is measured. Three further fields belong exclusively to preparation work: `bay_code`, naming the prep bay at that market, and `window_start` and `window_end`, the inclusive bounds of the bay occupation as ISO 8601 UTC instants. All three are **required** when `task_type` is `PREPARATION` and must be **omitted** when `task_type` is `REGISTRATION` or `LICENSING`; supplying them for the wrong type, or omitting them for preparation, is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). That rule governs the request only: a task's representation always carries all three keys, null on a `REGISTRATION` or `LICENSING` task. Tasks are stored in status `PENDING` with a null `completed_at`, and are discoverable by `vehicle_id`, by `market_id`, by `task_type` and by `status`.

Four rules police task scheduling, and they fire in the order §2.9 fixes. A vehicle may hold at most one non-waived task per `task_type`, so scheduling a second `REGISTRATION` while the first is `PENDING` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TASK_TYPE` (HTTP 409); a task that was `WAIVED` does not block a fresh one. Preparation cannot jump the queue: a `PREPARATION` task may only be created once the vehicle's `REGISTRATION` **and** `LICENSING` tasks are both resolved, meaning each is either `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED`, and an early attempt is refused with `ERR_PREP_BEFORE_REGISTRATION` (HTTP 422). The window must be well formed: `window_start` must fall strictly before `window_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). And the bay must actually be free.

Bay collision and daily capacity are two different constraints, and a request can pass one while failing the other. **Bay collision** works on the closed interval from the new task's `window_start` to its `window_end`, expanded outward by sixty minutes at each end to allow for turnaround; the buffer is applied once, to that interval alone, and every already-stored task is compared on its own **unexpanded** closed `window_start`-to-`window_end` interval. If the expanded interval shares any instant with the interval of any other `PENDING` or `COMPLETED` `PREPARATION` task on the same `market_id` and the same `bay_code`, the request is refused with `ERR_PREP_BAY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Adjacency is a conflict here: two intervals that merely touch at an endpoint do intersect and are rejected. Because the buffer is applied once rather than to both sides, the effective separation the rule demands is sixty minutes: a new window whose nearer edge sits exactly sixty minutes away from a stored occupation on that bay still collides, while a gap strictly greater than sixty minutes clears the check. **Daily capacity** works on calendar dates instead: the count of `PENDING` plus `COMPLETED` `PREPARATION` tasks at the same market whose `window_start` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the new task's `window_start` may not exceed that market's `prep_capacity_per_day`, and a breach is `ERR_PREP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Because bays are checked individually and dates are checked in aggregate, a market with several free bays can still refuse the day's fifth car.

Resolving a task is where money and milestones move. A `MarketOperator` completes a task, moving it `PENDING → COMPLETED`; the task must still be `PENDING`, since a task that has already been completed or waived is refused with `ERR_TASK_ALREADY_RESOLVED` (HTTP 409), and the parent vehicle must still be in `IN_FLEETING`. Completion stamps the task's `completed_at` from the logical clock, posts a ledger line of `entry_type` `PREP_COST` for a positive `amount_cents` equal to the task's `cost_cents` with `source_entity_type` `InFleetingTask`, stamps the matching milestone on the vehicle — `registered_at` for `REGISTRATION`, `licensed_at` for `LICENSING`, `prep_completed_at` for `PREPARATION` — increments the task's `version` by 1, and then hands off to the vehicle's promotion evaluation. Stamping the milestone refreshes the vehicle's `updated_at` but does **not** increment the vehicle's `version`: on the vehicle row it is the promotion below, not the milestone stamp, that counts as the version-incrementing mutation, so a resolved task that leaves the unit short of promotion moves the task's version and not the car's.

Waiving is the `FleetController`'s parallel action and is treated with deliberately equal weight. It moves the task `PENDING → WAIVED` under the same two preconditions — the task must be `PENDING`, or the refusal is `ERR_TASK_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, and the vehicle must be in `IN_FLEETING` — stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock, stamps the same vehicle milestone according to `task_type`, increments the task's `version` by 1, and hands off to the same promotion evaluation. The single difference is fiscal: a waived task posts **no** ledger line at all, so its `cost_cents` never enters the unit's cost basis. Both `COMPLETED` and `WAIVED` are terminal, and both count as resolved for the promotion gate.

Promotion itself is a system transition no client can request. When the third distinct `task_type` for a vehicle reaches a resolved state — that is, when the vehicle holds one task in `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` for **each** of `REGISTRATION`, `LICENSING` and `PREPARATION` — a vehicle sitting in `IN_FLEETING` moves to `IN_SERVICE`. The move sets both `first_rent_ready_at` and `in_service_at` to the current logical clock, so the two instants are equal; increments the `in_service_unit_count` of the market named by the vehicle's `current_market_id` by 1; increments the vehicle's `version` by 1; and triggers the recomputation of the parent allocation's `in_fleeted_count`, which is where the four-hop cascade of §2.11 continues upward. `in_service_at` is written once and once only: later round trips through `IN_TRANSIT` and back never rewrite it, because it is the start of the holding-period clock.

### 3.5. Life in Service: Usage Posting and Residual Valuation

An in-service unit earns, accumulates kilometres, and loses value; the branch reports the first two and the system computes the third. A `MarketOperator` posts a usage record against a vehicle in `fleet_status` `IN_SERVICE`, and the payload deliberately mixes one absolute reading with three increments: `odometer_km` is the **cumulative** dashboard reading and **replaces** the stored value outright, while `rented_days_delta`, `available_days_delta` and `revenue_cents_delta` are **increments** that are added to the stored `rented_days_total`, `available_days_total` and `revenue_to_date_cents` respectively. All four are required JSON integers of zero or more: the three deltas are strictly additive, so a negative one is refused as a schema violation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) rather than subtracting from a total, while a delta of zero is accepted and simply changes nothing. Because the odometer is cumulative it can only move forward: a submitted `odometer_km` lower than the stored `odometer_km` is refused with `ERR_ODOMETER_REGRESSION` (HTTP 422), and an equal reading — a day with no driving — is accepted. The posting increments the vehicle's `version` by 1. The same anti-regression rule governs the odometer reading captured when a redistribution is received, so a haul cannot wind the clock back either.

Valuation is a pure read that never mutates anything. It resolves the vehicle's segment, its manufacturer program and the single `ACTIVE` depreciation policy for its segment, and it reports **both** valuation variants side by side along with the inputs it used: `holding_days`, `holding_months`, `odometer_km`, `capitalized_cost_cents`, `policy_net_book_value_cents`, `program_guaranteed_value_cents`, `net_book_value_cents`, `accumulated_depreciation_cents`, `applied_variant`, `mileage_band` and `open_damage_cents`. The same computation runs, invisibly, in four other places: when a unit is consigned, to freeze `net_book_value_at_hold_cents`; when damage is reported, to freeze `net_book_value_at_report_cents`; as the input to the channel recommendation; and wherever `net_book_value_cents` is exposed on a vehicle read.

The **holding clock** is the spine of the calculation. If the vehicle's `in_service_at` is null — it has never been promoted — then `holding_days` is 0. Otherwise the end of the measured interval is the vehicle's `held_for_sale_at` when that instant is set, and the current logical clock otherwise; so consigning a unit freezes its holding clock, and withdrawing the consignment (which clears `held_for_sale_at`) starts it running again. `holding_days` is the integer floor of the elapsed interval divided by eighty-six thousand four hundred seconds and is floored at zero — an interval that comes out zero or negative, which is what a clock repositioned back before `in_service_at` produces, reports `holding_days` 0 rather than a negative count — and `holding_months` is the integer floor of `holding_days` divided by thirty. A unit fifty-nine days into service has therefore accrued one depreciation month, not two.

The **`POLICY_SCHEDULE`** variant depreciates from the purchase price. Time decay is the capitalized cost multiplied by the policy's `monthly_depreciation_pct` multiplied by `holding_months`, rounded half up to whole cents. Mileage decay is the vehicle's `odometer_km` multiplied by the policy's `mileage_depreciation_cents_per_km` — an exact integer product, since both operands are integers. The floor value is the capitalized cost multiplied by the policy's `floor_residual_pct`, rounded half up. `policy_net_book_value_cents` is then the larger of two figures: the capitalized cost minus the sum of the time and mileage decay, and the floor value. The clamp is what keeps a very high-mileage unit from depreciating below its salvage worth, and a unit driven hard enough sits exactly on the floor with the excess decay simply discarded.

The **`PROGRAM_GUARANTEE`** variant ignores elapsed time entirely and works backwards from the manufacturer's repurchase promise. The guarantee base is the capitalized cost multiplied by the program's `repurchase_guarantee_pct`, rounded half up. Excess kilometres are the vehicle's `odometer_km` minus the program's `max_eligible_mileage_km`, floored at zero, and the excess charge is that figure multiplied by the program's `excess_mileage_charge_cents_per_km`. The open-damage figure, `open_damage_cents`, is the sum of `estimate_cents` across every damage event on the vehicle whose `status` is `REPORTED` or `RECOVERY_PENDING` — events in `RECOVERED`, `WRITTEN_OFF` or `TOTAL_LOSS_DECLARED` are excluded, so resolving a claim raises the guaranteed value again — and the damage deduction is the smaller of that sum and the program's `damage_deduction_cap_cents`. `program_guaranteed_value_cents` is the guarantee base minus the excess charge minus the damage deduction, floored at zero. `holding_months` never enters this variant, which is precisely why the two variants disagree on identical inputs.

Variant selection is **pinned to the program, not chosen as a preference**: when the program's `program_type` is `PROGRAM` the reported `net_book_value_cents` is `program_guaranteed_value_cents` and `applied_variant` is `PROGRAM_GUARANTEE`; when it is `RISK` the reported `net_book_value_cents` is `policy_net_book_value_cents` and `applied_variant` is `POLICY_SCHEDULE`. **Both** variant figures are always reported regardless of which one was applied. `accumulated_depreciation_cents` is the capitalized cost minus the applied `net_book_value_cents`, so it too follows the pinned choice. Because the policy variant is always computed, the single `ACTIVE` policy for the segment is required even for a car on a repurchase program, and its absence is the `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_DEPRECIATION_POLICY` refusal of §3.2.

The `mileage_band` is a separate, three-valued classification of the same odometer against the vehicle's segment: it is `OVER_HARD` when `odometer_km` is strictly greater than the segment's `mileage_band_hard_limit_km`; failing that, `OVER_SOFT` when `odometer_km` is strictly greater than the segment's `mileage_band_soft_limit_km`; and `WITHIN_SOFT` otherwise. A reading exactly equal to a limit is therefore still inside that band. The band has no effect on money — it gates which disposal channels a unit may be sent to.

### 3.6. Inter-City Redistribution

Fleet is never in the right city. A `FleetPlanner` drafts a **RedistributionOrder** naming a `vehicle_id`, a `destination_market_id`, a `distance_km` greater than zero, and a planned window of `planned_depart_at` and `planned_receive_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants. The order's `origin_market_id` is copied from the vehicle's `current_market_id` at creation and never changes afterwards, even though the vehicle itself will be rehomed. The order is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0, a null `rejection_reason`, null `proposed_by_user_id` and `authorized_by_user_id`, null `depart_at` and `receive_at`, and a null `actual_transport_cost_cents`. Orders are discoverable by `vehicle_id`, by `origin_market_id`, by `destination_market_id` and by `status`.

Four checks stand between a draft request and a stored order, and they fire in the priority order of §2.9 rather than in the order they are narrated here: the fleet-state gate first, then the window-shape check, then the transfer-overlap check, then the same-market check, and last of all the payback gate described two paragraphs below. The vehicle must be in `fleet_status` `IN_SERVICE`: units on order, in the in-fleeting bay, already in transit, consigned, sold or written off are not repositionable. The `destination_market_id` must differ from the vehicle's `current_market_id`, since a transfer to the city the car is already in is meaningless, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SAME_MARKET_TRANSFER` (HTTP 422). `planned_depart_at` must fall strictly before `planned_receive_at`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). And the proposed window must not collide with another haul for the same car: taking the interval from `planned_depart_at` inclusive to `planned_receive_at` **exclusive**, it must not overlap the corresponding interval of any existing non-terminal order for the same vehicle, and an overlap is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_TRANSFER_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Because that interval is half-open, back-to-back hauls are legal: one order's `planned_receive_at` may be exactly the next order's `planned_depart_at`. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the prep-bay rule of §3.4, where a closed interval plus a buffer makes touching endpoints a conflict. Neither market's `status` is examined at draft time: a suspended origin, and a suspended destination, both leave the draft itself acceptable — the destination-suspension refusal of §3.1 first bites at authorization and again at dispatch.

Creation also freezes the money. `estimated_transport_cost_cents` is computed once, at draft time, and never recomputed. The base haul is the order's `distance_km` multiplied by the **origin** market's `transfer_cost_per_km_cents`; the destination market's rate is never read, so hauling from a cheap city to an expensive one costs the cheap city's rate. The fuel surcharge percentage is then selected **from the base haul alone, before anything is added to it**: strictly below fifty thousand cents of base haul the rate is 0.12; from fifty thousand cents up to but not including one hundred fifty thousand cents it is 0.08; at one hundred fifty thousand cents or above it is 0.05. The tier boundaries are exclusive at the ceiling and inclusive at the floor, so a base haul of exactly fifty thousand cents takes 0.08 and a base haul of exactly one hundred fifty thousand cents takes 0.05. The surcharge itself is the base haul multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half up; the base haul and the flat handling charge of seven thousand five hundred cents are exact integers and are never rounded. `estimated_transport_cost_cents` is the sum of the base haul, the surcharge and the handling flat. Selecting the tier from that final total instead — the circular reading that a surcharge tier "depends on the total cost" invites — is a different and forbidden computation, because a haul whose base sits just under a ceiling can be pushed over it by its own surcharge and handling.

The payback gate is applied once the cost is known. The ceiling is the vehicle segment's `baseline_daily_rate_cents` multiplied by forty-five days, rounded half up: a transfer must be recoverable inside about a season of rental income. If `estimated_transport_cost_cents` strictly exceeds that ceiling the order is refused with `ERR_TRANSPORT_COST_EXCEEDS_BENEFIT` (HTTP 422), and a cost exactly equal to the ceiling is accepted.

The order's lifecycle is the system's only **cyclic** workflow. A `FleetPlanner` proposes a `DRAFT` order, moving it `DRAFT → PROPOSED`; the order must be in `DRAFT` and the vehicle must still be `IN_SERVICE`, and the transition writes `proposed_by_user_id` from the acting operator and increments `version` by 1. A `FleetController` may then reject it, moving it `PROPOSED → REJECTED` with a `rejection_reason` taken from the request body — a **required** non-empty string, so an omitted, null, non-string or whitespace-only value is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) before the order's own state is even examined — and `version` incremented by 1. A rejected order may be re-proposed by a `FleetPlanner`, moving it `REJECTED → PROPOSED`: the order must be in `REJECTED`, its `revision_count` must be strictly less than three, and the vehicle must still be `IN_SERVICE`. Re-proposal increments `revision_count` by 1, **rewrites** `proposed_by_user_id` to the acting operator, clears `rejection_reason` back to null, and increments `version` by 1. The loop is therefore bounded: an order may be rejected and re-proposed three times, and the fourth re-proposal attempt — made while `revision_count` already reads 3 — is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). Because `proposed_by_user_id` is rewritten each time, the segregation-of-duties subject for authorization is always the **latest** proposer.

Authorization is the two-man control. A `FleetController` moves the order `PROPOSED → AUTHORIZED` provided the order is in `PROPOSED`, the acting operator's identifier differs from `proposed_by_user_id`, and the destination market's `status` is `ACTIVE`. It writes `authorized_by_user_id` from the acting operator and increments `version` by 1. A self-authorization is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) and a suspended destination with `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422).

Dispatch belongs to the origin branch. A `MarketOperator` whose identifier equals the order's `origin_market_id` moves the order `AUTHORIZED → IN_TRANSIT`, provided the order is `AUTHORIZED`, the vehicle is `IN_SERVICE`, and the destination market is still `ACTIVE` — a market suspended between authorization and dispatch stops the haul. Departure stamps `depart_at` from the logical clock, drives the vehicle `IN_SERVICE → IN_TRANSIT`, **decrements** the origin market's `in_service_unit_count` by 1, and increments both the order's and the vehicle's `version` by 1. Between departure and arrival the unit belongs to no market's rentable census, which is exactly the census hole §2.12 describes.

Receipt belongs to the destination branch, and it is the receiving market that signs for the haul: a `MarketOperator` whose identifier equals the order's `destination_market_id` moves the order `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED`, submitting the invoiced `actual_transport_cost_cents` and the arrival `odometer_km`, both **required** JSON integers of zero or more. The order must be in `IN_TRANSIT` and the vehicle must be in `IN_TRANSIT`, and the odometer's anti-regression check is evaluated after both of those state gates. Receipt stamps `receive_at` from the logical clock, stores `actual_transport_cost_cents`, rewrites the vehicle's `current_market_id` to the order's `destination_market_id`, replaces the vehicle's `odometer_km` with the submitted cumulative reading under the anti-regression rule, drives the vehicle `IN_TRANSIT → IN_SERVICE`, **increments** the destination market's `in_service_unit_count` by 1, posts a ledger line of `entry_type` `TRANSPORT_COST` for a positive `amount_cents` equal to `actual_transport_cost_cents` with `source_entity_type` `RedistributionOrder`, and increments both the order's and the vehicle's `version` by 1. The invoiced figure is what hits the ledger — the estimate frozen at draft time is a gate, not a booking. `RECEIVED` is terminal.

Cancellation is available to a `FleetController` from every non-terminal status. From `DRAFT`, from `PROPOSED`, from `REJECTED` and from `AUTHORIZED` it simply moves the order to `CANCELLED` and increments its `version` by 1, with no effect on the vehicle. From `IN_TRANSIT` it is a genuine **compensating action**: the order must be `IN_TRANSIT` and the vehicle must be `IN_TRANSIT`, and the cancellation drives the vehicle `IN_TRANSIT → IN_SERVICE` while **leaving `current_market_id` unchanged at the origin**, increments the origin market's `in_service_unit_count` by 1 so the census returns to exactly its pre-departure value, posts **no** `TRANSPORT_COST` ledger line at all — the haul was recalled, so no invoice exists — and increments both versions by 1. `CANCELLED` is terminal. A request to move an order from a state the target transition does not declare is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); this is the code returned for proposing an `AUTHORIZED` order, rejecting a `DRAFT` one, authorizing a `REJECTED` one, departing a `PROPOSED` one, receiving an `AUTHORIZED` one, or cancelling a `RECEIVED` or already-`CANCELLED` one.

Finally, an order can be cancelled by the system rather than by an operator. When the vehicle it moves is declared a total loss, **every** order for that vehicle whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED`, `REJECTED`, `AUTHORIZED` or `IN_TRANSIT` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `version` incremented by 1. This is the third hop of the total-loss cascade of §3.7, and it applies uniformly from all five non-terminal statuses.

### 3.7. Damage, Recovery and Total Loss

Damage is reported by the branch holding the car. A `MarketOperator` files a **DamageEvent** against a vehicle with a `damage_type` of `COLLISION`, `HAIL`, `VANDALISM` or `MECHANICAL` and an `estimate_cents` greater than zero. The event's `market_id` is copied from the vehicle's `current_market_id` at report time and is the scope the reporting operator is checked against. Reporting requires the vehicle's `condition_status` to be `SOUND` or `RECONDITIONED` — a car already carrying an open damage report cannot carry a second one, and one in `RECONDITIONING` or `TOTALED` cannot be reported on at all — and requires the vehicle's `fleet_status` to be anything **other than** `SOLD` or `TOTAL_LOSS`, so a unit still on order or in the in-fleeting bay may be damaged in the compound just as a rentable one may. The fleet-status gate is evaluated **before** the condition gate, so a `SOLD` car that also sits in `RECONDITIONING` reports `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_FLEET_STATE` and not `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE`, and both outrank the missing-policy refusal that freezing the baseline can raise. The event is created in `status` `REPORTED` with a null `recovery_source`, a null `recovered_cents`, null recovery buckets, a null `salvage_proceeds_cents` and a null `total_loss_gain_loss_cents`, and the vehicle moves `SOUND → DAMAGE_REPORTED` or `RECONDITIONED → DAMAGE_REPORTED` on its condition dimension with its `version` incremented by 1 — the vehicle's `fleet_status` is left completely untouched. Damage events are discoverable by `vehicle_id`, by `market_id`, by `damage_type` and by `status`.

Two computed fields are frozen at report time. `net_book_value_at_report_cents` captures the vehicle's `net_book_value_cents` at that instant, which is why the report requires an `ACTIVE` depreciation policy for the segment. `total_loss_required` is then decided by the **ratio test**, and the test is deliberately pure integer cross-multiplication with no floating point and therefore no rounding at the boundary: the event requires a total-loss determination when `estimate_cents` multiplied by ten thousand is **greater than or equal to** `net_book_value_at_report_cents` multiplied by seven thousand. In plain terms, a repair estimate reaching seventy percent of the frozen book value is a total loss, and the exact seventy-percent point counts as one.

The estimate can be revised. A `MarketOperator` submits a new `estimate_cents` for an event whose `status` is `REPORTED` or `RECOVERY_PENDING`, provided the vehicle's `fleet_status` is not `SOLD` or `TOTAL_LOSS`. The revision replaces `estimate_cents`, recomputes `total_loss_required` **against the unchanged `net_book_value_at_report_cents` baseline**, leaves `status` exactly where it was — this is a self-loop, not a transition — and increments the event's `version` by 1. The baseline is emphatically **not** re-frozen: months of depreciation may have passed since the report, and re-freezing would let a stale claim become a total loss purely through the passage of time. The corollary is that a revision can flip `total_loss_required` from false to true while the event is sitting in `RECOVERY_PENDING`, which is the only way that status becomes a total-loss origin.

From `REPORTED` the event forks. **Branch A** opens a recovery: a `RemarketingManager` moves it `REPORTED → RECOVERY_PENDING`, supplying a `recovery_source` of `CUSTOMER`, `THIRD_PARTY_INSURER` or `OWN_INSURANCE`. The event must be in `REPORTED` and its `total_loss_required` must be false; the vehicle's `condition_status` is untouched, since the two dimensions move independently, and the event's `version` is incremented by 1. **Branch B** declares the total loss and is described below. The two branches are mutually exclusive by construction: an event whose `total_loss_required` is true may **not** be routed through the ordinary recovery path at all, and both an attempt to open recovery on it and an attempt to write it off are refused with `ERR_TOTAL_LOSS_DETERMINATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Symmetrically, an attempt to declare a total loss on an event whose `total_loss_required` is false is refused with `ERR_TOTAL_LOSS_NOT_APPLICABLE` (HTTP 422).

Settling a recovery runs the **waterfall**. A `RemarketingManager` moves the event `RECOVERY_PENDING → RECOVERED`, supplying `recovered_cents` as a **required** JSON integer of zero or more — a settlement that recovered nothing is expressed as an explicit 0 and runs the waterfall over a zero amount, while a negative figure is a schema violation. The recovery is capped first: the ceiling is `estimate_cents` multiplied by 1.25, rounded half up, and a `recovered_cents` strictly above it is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 422) — an insurer may pay a modest premium over the estimate but not an unbounded one. The recovered money is then split across three **ordered buckets** with no remainder ambiguity whatsoever. Bucket one, `recovery_alloc_recondition_cents`, is the smaller of `recovered_cents` and the vehicle's `total_recondition_cents`: reconditioning money already spent on the car is reimbursed first. Bucket two, `recovery_alloc_depreciation_cents`, is the smaller of what remains after bucket one and the quantity `estimate_cents` minus the vehicle's `total_recondition_cents` floored at zero: the unreimbursed portion of the repair estimate absorbs the next slice. Bucket three, `recovery_alloc_income_cents`, is everything still left over — `recovered_cents` minus the first two buckets — and it falls to income. The three buckets sum to `recovered_cents` exactly and always. The vehicle's `total_recondition_cents` is read **live** at settlement time, so a reconditioning spend posted after the damage was reported does enter bucket one; and bucket two's ceiling uses the **current** `estimate_cents`, meaning the value after any revision. Settlement posts a single ledger line of `entry_type` `DAMAGE_RECOVERY` for a **negative** `amount_cents` equal to `recovered_cents` — money coming back is a credit against the unit's cost basis — with `source_entity_type` `DamageEvent` and a `memo` recording the three-bucket split, increments the event's `version` by 1, and triggers the conditional condition cascade described below.

Writing off is the alternative resolution and reaches the same terminal shape without any money. A `FleetController` moves the event to `WRITTEN_OFF` either from `RECOVERY_PENDING` — a claim pursued and abandoned — or directly from `REPORTED`, and in **both** cases the event's `total_loss_required` must be false, exactly as it must be for the recovery path. Either way the write-off sets `recovered_cents` to 0 and all three allocation buckets to 0, posts **no** ledger line whatsoever, increments the event's `version` by 1, and triggers the same conditional cascade. The repair cost simply stays in the unit's basis, unrecovered.

That cascade is **conditional**, which is what makes it interesting. When an event reaches `RECOVERED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, the system counts the vehicle's remaining damage events whose `status` is `REPORTED` or `RECOVERY_PENDING`; if that count is zero **after** the triggering transition and the vehicle's `condition_status` is still `DAMAGE_REPORTED`, the vehicle moves `DAMAGE_REPORTED → SOUND` with its `version` incremented by 1. If any other event on the car is still open the cascade is suppressed and the car stays in `DAMAGE_REPORTED`; and if the condition has meanwhile moved on to `RECONDITIONING` or `RECONDITIONED` under a consignment, the cascade is likewise suppressed, because the condition dimension is no longer that vehicle's to reclaim. `RECOVERED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are both terminal.

Declaring a total loss retires the car. A `FleetController` moves the event to `TOTAL_LOSS_DECLARED` from `REPORTED` or from `RECOVERY_PENDING`, in each case requiring `total_loss_required` to be true, and supplies the `salvage_proceeds_cents` realised on the wreck as a **required** JSON integer of zero or more. The three gates fire in this order: the event's own status first, then the vehicle's `fleet_status` — a `SOLD` or already `TOTAL_LOSS` unit is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_FLEET_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and only then the ratio-test requirement. The declaration computes `total_loss_gain_loss_cents` as `salvage_proceeds_cents` plus `recovered_cents` — treated as zero when null, which is the ordinary case for an event declared straight from `REPORTED` — minus `net_book_value_at_report_cents`. A car whose salvage and insurance together exceed its frozen book value produces a positive figure, a gain; the usual outcome is negative. Two ledger lines are posted, both with `source_entity_type` `DamageEvent`: one of `entry_type` `SALVAGE_PROCEEDS` for a **negative** `amount_cents` equal to `salvage_proceeds_cents`, and one of `entry_type` `DISPOSAL_GAIN_LOSS` for an `amount_cents` equal to `total_loss_gain_loss_cents`, carrying whatever sign that figure has. The event's `version` is incremented by 1. `TOTAL_LOSS_DECLARED` is terminal, and a request to move an event from a status its target transition does not declare is refused with `ERR_DAMAGE_EVENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — revising a settled event, opening recovery on one already in `RECOVERY_PENDING`, settling one still in `REPORTED`, writing off a `RECOVERED` one, or declaring a total loss on a `WRITTEN_OFF` one.

The declaration then drives the vehicle to `TOTAL_LOSS`, and this is where the fleet dimension matters, because a car can be destroyed at any point in its life and each origin has different bookkeeping. All five branches share the same two preconditions — the event's `total_loss_required` is true and its `status` is `REPORTED` or `RECOVERY_PENDING` — and all five set the vehicle's `condition_status` to `TOTALED` and increment the vehicle's `version` by 1. From **`ON_ORDER`** nothing else happens: the car was never in a census, never in the bay, and has no downstream paperwork, so the write-off is purely a book entry. From **`IN_FLEETING`** the abandoned paperwork is cleaned up: every `InFleetingTask` for the vehicle whose `status` is `PENDING` is set to `WAIVED` with its `completed_at` stamped at the current logical clock and **no** `PREP_COST` posted, while tasks already `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` are left exactly as they are — a conditional cascade that touches only unresolved work, and one that deliberately does not promote the car to service even though it may thereby hold three resolved tasks. This origin stops there: the downstream order-and-consignment cascade described below is reached from `IN_SERVICE`, `IN_TRANSIT` and `HELD_FOR_SALE` only, never from `IN_FLEETING` and never from `ON_ORDER`. From **`IN_SERVICE`** the census is corrected — the market named by the vehicle's `current_market_id` has its `in_service_unit_count` decremented by 1 — and the downstream cascade runs. From **`IN_TRANSIT`** the census is *not* touched, because departure already removed the unit from the origin's count and nothing was ever added at the destination, and the downstream cascade runs. From **`HELD_FOR_SALE`** the census is likewise not touched, because consignment already removed the unit, and the downstream cascade runs.

That downstream cascade — the third hop — is identical wherever it fires: **every** `RedistributionOrder` for the vehicle whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED`, `REJECTED`, `AUTHORIZED` or `IN_TRANSIT` moves to `CANCELLED`, and **every** `Consignment` for the vehicle whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `RECONDITION_PENDING` or `LISTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, each with its own `version` incremented by 1. A vehicle in `SOLD` or already in `TOTAL_LOSS` cannot be declared a total loss at all, and `TOTAL_LOSS` is terminal on the fleet dimension exactly as `TOTALED` is terminal on the condition dimension. One asymmetry deserves emphasis, because it is a genuine accounting decision rather than an oversight: a consignment withdrawn by an operator posts a `RECONDITION_REVERSAL` to hand back the reconditioning spend, whereas a consignment withdrawn by the total-loss cascade posts **none** — the money sunk into fixing up a car that was then destroyed is retained in the book value the total-loss gain or loss is measured against.

### 3.8. Remarketing: Channel Choice, Reconditioning and Disposal

Before a car is consigned, the system will tell you where it should go. The channel recommendation is a computed read that ranks every disposal channel for one specific vehicle and reports the winner as `recommended_channel_id` and `recommended_channel_code`, its `projected_net_proceeds_cents`, the `eligible_channel_count`, the full ranking as `ranked_channel_codes`, and the rejects as `excluded_channel_codes`. It reads the vehicle's **live** valuation — the `net_book_value_cents` and `holding_days` computed at read time by the calculation of §3.5, never the frozen hold value — so it requires an `ACTIVE` depreciation policy for the segment.

The **eligibility filter** admits a channel only if its `status` is `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` channel never enters the pool at all. Two code-specific exclusions then apply. A channel whose `code` is `RETAIL_LOT` is excluded when the vehicle's `odometer_km` strictly exceeds its segment's `mileage_band_hard_limit_km` — retail buyers will not take a worn-out car. A channel whose `code` is `PROGRAM_REPURCHASE` is excluded unless **all five** of the following hold: the vehicle's program has `program_type` `PROGRAM`; the vehicle's `holding_days` is at least the program's `min_holding_days`; its `holding_days` is at most the program's `max_holding_days`; its `odometer_km` is at most the program's `max_eligible_mileage_km`; and its `condition_status` is `SOUND` or `RECONDITIONED`. The manufacturer's repurchase promise is a contract with windows on both ends, and a car returned too early, too late, too far driven, or damaged falls outside it. Channels bearing any other `code` face neither exclusion and are eligible whenever they are `ACTIVE`.

Each surviving candidate is then **scored**, per line and in whole cents. Projected gross is the vehicle's `net_book_value_cents` multiplied by the channel's `expected_proceeds_multiplier`, rounded half up. Projected fee is that projected gross multiplied by the channel's `fee_pct`, rounded half up, **plus** the channel's `fixed_fee_cents` — the fixed component is added after the rounding and is never itself rounded. Projected transport is the vehicle's current market's `transport_hub_distance_km` multiplied by that market's `transfer_cost_per_km_cents` multiplied by 1.15, rounded half up; the factor loads the ordinary per-kilometre rate for the awkwardness of a one-way haul to an auction house. Projected reconditioning is zero when the channel's `recondition_required` is false, and otherwise the smaller of the segment's `recondition_cap_cents` and the vehicle's `capitalized_cost_cents` multiplied by 0.02 rounded half up — two percent of the purchase price is the planning estimate, and the segment cap binds it. `projected_net_proceeds_cents` is the projected gross minus the fee, minus the transport and minus the reconditioning, and the subtraction is exact integer arithmetic.

Candidates are ranked by `projected_net_proceeds_cents` **descending**. Ties are broken first by ascending `expected_days_to_sell` — of two channels netting the same money, the faster one wins — and then by ascending `created_at`, so the channel registered earlier wins. The channel's own `id` is **never** consulted as a tie-break at any point. `ranked_channel_codes` lists the eligible channels in exactly that ranked order, while `excluded_channel_codes` is not ranked at all: the rejects come back in registration order, ascending `created_at` with the insertion sequence of §2.3 as the final tie-break. If the candidate pool is empty the read does not return an empty ranking; it fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CHANNEL` (HTTP 422).

Consigning the car is a `RemarketingManager` action that names a `vehicle_id`, a `channel_id` and the concurrency field `expected_vehicle_version` described in §2.7. Five substantive gates apply beyond that, and they are evaluated in one fixed order: fleet state, then open damage, then condition, then the minimum-holding gate, then general channel eligibility, then the segment's `ACTIVE`-policy requirement, and last the mileage-band exclusion. That order departs from the general ladder of §2.9 in exactly one place, and the departure is deliberate: the open-damage check runs **before** the condition guard, so a car whose `condition_status` reads `DAMAGE_REPORTED` while a claim against it is still open reports `ERR_OPEN_DAMAGE_BLOCKS_LISTING` and not `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE` — the open claim is the more informative diagnosis, since the condition flag is merely its shadow. The vehicle's `fleet_status` must be `IN_SERVICE`. Its `condition_status` must be `SOUND` or `RECONDITIONED`. No damage event for the vehicle may be in `status` `REPORTED` or `RECOVERY_PENDING`, and an open claim is refused with `ERR_OPEN_DAMAGE_BLOCKS_LISTING` (HTTP 422). The named channel must be in the vehicle's eligible pool as computed by the filter above, with one deliberate carve-out: the `RETAIL_LOT` mileage exclusion is reported by its own dedicated code, `ERR_MILEAGE_BAND_EXCLUDES_CHANNEL` (HTTP 422), while every other exclusion — a `PROGRAM_REPURCHASE` request for a car on a `RISK` program, or past `max_holding_days`, or over `max_eligible_mileage_km`, or not in condition `SOUND` or `RECONDITIONED`, and any request naming a `SUSPENDED` channel of any code — is reported as `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). And for a `PROGRAM_REPURCHASE` channel specifically the vehicle's `holding_days` must be at least the program's `min_holding_days`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MIN_HOLDING_PERIOD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422); this minimum-holding check carries its own code and, ranking above `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` in the ladder of §2.9, is reported ahead of the general eligibility failure that the same shortfall would also produce. It is reached only when the named channel is itself `ACTIVE` **and** the vehicle's program carries `program_type` `PROGRAM`: a `SUSPENDED` `PROGRAM_REPURCHASE` channel, or one named for a car bought on a `RISK` program, is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` even when the holding shortfall also exists. The distinct codes for the mileage exclusion and the general ineligibility are what make it observable *why* a channel was refused.

A successful consignment is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `created_by_user_id` set from the acting operator, and it freezes the reserve: `floor_price_cents` is the vehicle's `net_book_value_at_hold_cents` multiplied by 0.75, rounded half up. Because it is frozen at creation it never moves afterwards, even if reconditioning is later spent on the car or a redistribution changes its market. The same request drives the vehicle `IN_SERVICE → HELD_FOR_SALE`, stamping `held_for_sale_at` from the logical clock, setting `net_book_value_at_hold_cents` to the vehicle's `net_book_value_cents` evaluated at that instant, decrementing the current market's `in_service_unit_count` by 1 — the unit leaves the rentable census the moment it is consigned, not when it sells — and incrementing the vehicle's `version` by 1. Consignments are discoverable by `vehicle_id`, by `channel_id` and by `status`.

From `DRAFT` the consignment **forks**, and the two branches converge on `LISTED` with materially different books. **Branch one** goes through the body shop. A `RemarketingManager` authorizes a budget, moving the consignment `DRAFT → RECONDITION_PENDING` with a `recondition_authorized_cents` — a **required** JSON integer of zero or more — that may not exceed the vehicle segment's `recondition_cap_cents`; a breach is refused with `ERR_RECONDITION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The condition dimension is gated before that cap and after the consignment's own `DRAFT` requirement: the vehicle must sit in `SOUND` or `DAMAGE_REPORTED`, and any other condition — `RECONDITIONING`, `RECONDITIONED` or `TOTALED` — is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE` (HTTP 409), so a unit already reconditioned under an earlier consignment cannot be sent back through the shop. Authorization also drives the vehicle's condition dimension to `RECONDITIONING`, from `SOUND` or from `DAMAGE_REPORTED` — a car reported damaged may be repaired under a consignment even though it may not be *listed* while the claim is open — and increments both the vehicle's and the consignment's `version` by 1. The actual spend is then booked: the manager submits a `recondition_actual_cents` — again a **required** JSON integer of zero or more — that may not exceed the previously authorized `recondition_authorized_cents`, and an overrun is refused with `ERR_RECONDITION_OVERRUN` (HTTP 422). Two gates outrank that overrun test: the consignment must be in `RECONDITION_PENDING`, and the vehicle must still read condition `RECONDITIONING`, or the refusal is `ERR_VEHICLE_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE` (HTTP 409). Together they make the booking single-shot, since the first booking drives the condition to `RECONDITIONED` and a second attempt therefore fails on the condition gate rather than overwriting the spend. Booking the spend computes `recondition_loaded_cents` as `recondition_actual_cents` multiplied by 1.075, rounded half up — a 7.5 percent overhead loading for shop administration — posts a ledger line of `entry_type` `RECONDITION_COST` for a positive `amount_cents` equal to `recondition_loaded_cents` with `source_entity_type` `Consignment`, recomputes the vehicle's `total_recondition_cents`, drives the vehicle's condition `RECONDITIONING → RECONDITIONED`, and increments both versions by 1. Note that booking the spend **does not** move the consignment: it stays in `RECONDITION_PENDING`, and a separate publish call is what lifts it to `LISTED`, stamping `listed_at` from the logical clock. Publishing off this branch is gated by the open-damage rule alone — that rule guards **both** publish branches, so a claim still in `REPORTED` or `RECOVERY_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_OPEN_DAMAGE_BLOCKS_LISTING` (HTTP 422) — and by no condition gate of its own: a `RECONDITION_PENDING` consignment whose authorized spend was never booked may be published while the vehicle still reads `RECONDITIONING`, and that is the one way a `LISTED` unit is not in `SOUND` or `RECONDITIONED`.

**Branch two** skips the shop entirely: a `RemarketingManager` publishes straight from `DRAFT` to `LISTED`. This is permitted only when the channel's `recondition_required` is false — a channel that insists on a reconditioned car cannot be sent an unreconditioned one, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and only when no damage event for the vehicle is in `REPORTED` or `RECOVERY_PENDING` and the vehicle's `condition_status` is `SOUND` or `RECONDITIONED` — checked in that order, so a `DAMAGE_REPORTED` car with a claim still open again reports `ERR_OPEN_DAMAGE_BLOCKS_LISTING` rather than the condition code. It stamps `listed_at` and posts **no** `RECONDITION_COST` line at all. The join is therefore lossy in a useful way: two consignments both sitting in `LISTED` can be distinguished by whether a `RECONDITION_COST` row exists and by whether the vehicle's `condition_status` reads `RECONDITIONED` or `SOUND`.

Recording the sale runs the **net-proceeds calculation**. A `RemarketingManager` moves the consignment `LISTED → SOLD_PENDING_SETTLEMENT`, submitting the `gross_proceeds_cents` actually achieved as a **required** JSON integer of zero or more. The reserve binds: a `gross_proceeds_cents` below the frozen `floor_price_cents` is refused with `ERR_PROCEEDS_BELOW_FLOOR` (HTTP 422), and a figure exactly equal to the floor is accepted. Three cost lines are then each computed and **rounded independently to whole cents before any of them are added together**; summing the unrounded lines and rounding once is a different and forbidden order that can differ by a cent or more. Line one, `channel_fee_cents`, is `gross_proceeds_cents` multiplied by the channel's `fee_pct`, rounded half up, plus the channel's `fixed_fee_cents`. Line two, `transport_to_channel_cents`, is the **vehicle's current market's** `transport_hub_distance_km` multiplied by that market's `transfer_cost_per_km_cents` multiplied by 1.15, rounded half up; the market is read at sale time, so a redistribution completed before the car was held for sale genuinely changes this line. Line three is the loaded reconditioning: zero when `recondition_actual_cents` is null, and otherwise `recondition_actual_cents` multiplied by 1.075 rounded half up, which is the same `recondition_loaded_cents` already booked to the ledger — a consignment that never went through the shop keeps that field null rather than writing a zero. `net_proceeds_cents` is `gross_proceeds_cents` minus the sum of those three rounded lines, and `gain_loss_cents` is `net_proceeds_cents` minus the vehicle's frozen `net_book_value_at_hold_cents`. Recording the sale posts **no** ledger lines whatsoever — the four disposal rows post at settlement — and increments the consignment's `version` by 1.

Settlement is the second two-man control. A `RemarketingManager` moves the consignment `SOLD_PENDING_SETTLEMENT → SETTLED`, and the acting operator's identifier must differ from the consignment's `created_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): whoever chose the channel does not get to close the money. Settlement sets `settled_by_user_id` from the acting operator, stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock, and posts **four** ledger lines, all with `source_entity_type` `Consignment`: one of `entry_type` `DISPOSAL_PROCEEDS` for a **negative** `amount_cents` equal to `gross_proceeds_cents`, since sale proceeds are a credit against the unit's basis; one of `entry_type` `CHANNEL_FEE` for a positive `amount_cents` equal to `channel_fee_cents`; one of `entry_type` `TRANSPORT_COST` for a positive `amount_cents` equal to `transport_to_channel_cents`; and one of `entry_type` `DISPOSAL_GAIN_LOSS` for an `amount_cents` equal to `gain_loss_cents`, carrying whatever sign that figure has. It then drives the vehicle `HELD_FOR_SALE → SOLD` and increments both the vehicle's and the consignment's `version` by 1. `SETTLED` is terminal for the consignment and `SOLD` is terminal for the vehicle's fleet dimension.

Withdrawal is the compensating action, available to a `RemarketingManager` from `DRAFT`, from `RECONDITION_PENDING` and from `LISTED`; a consignment in `SOLD_PENDING_SETTLEMENT` or `SETTLED` may **not** be withdrawn, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Withdrawal drives the vehicle `HELD_FOR_SALE → IN_SERVICE`, clearing both `held_for_sale_at` and `net_book_value_at_hold_cents` back to null — which restarts the holding clock of §3.5 — and increments the vehicle's current market's `in_service_unit_count` by 1, exactly undoing the decrement the consignment applied. When the consignment's `recondition_loaded_cents` is not null, a compensating ledger line of `entry_type` `RECONDITION_REVERSAL` is posted for a **negative** `amount_cents` equal to `recondition_loaded_cents` with `source_entity_type` `Consignment`, so the vehicle's recomputed `total_recondition_cents` returns to its pre-consignment value of zero; withdrawing from `DRAFT`, where no spend was ever booked, posts nothing. And when the vehicle's `condition_status` is `RECONDITIONING` or `RECONDITIONED`, it is set back to `SOUND`; a car withdrawn mid-repair is treated as sound again, since the consignment that authorized the work no longer exists. Both the vehicle's and the consignment's `version` are incremented by 1, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. A request to move a consignment from a status its target transition does not declare — authorizing reconditioning on a `LISTED` one, booking a spend on a `DRAFT` one, publishing a `SETTLED` one, recording a sale on a `RECONDITION_PENDING` one, or settling a `LISTED` one — is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

### 3.9. Plan Performance Reporting

The planning cycle closes where it opened. A plan's performance read reports `planned_unit_count`, `allocated_unit_count`, `in_fleeted_unit_count`, `posture` and `season_days` alongside **both** variants of the utilization and revenue-per-unit calculation: `utilization_pct_planned`, `revenue_per_unit_cents_planned`, `utilization_pct_actual`, `revenue_per_unit_cents_actual` and `rpu_variance_cents`. It is the one read `MarketOperator` cannot reach, since a plan spans decisions above a single branch.

The season length is fixed by the plan's `season`: 92 days for `PEAK`, 122 days for `SHOULDER` and 151 days for `OFF_PEAK`. The candidate population is every vehicle whose `allocation_id` belongs to a purchase allocation under this plan whose `status` is **not** `CANCELLED`; units bought under an allocation that was later cancelled are excluded from every figure. Two inclusion sets are drawn from those candidates. Set A holds candidates whose `fleet_status` is `ON_ORDER`, `IN_FLEETING`, `IN_SERVICE` or `IN_TRANSIT` — the whole pipeline, including units that have never earned a cent. Set B holds candidates whose `fleet_status` is exactly `IN_SERVICE`. Vehicles in `HELD_FOR_SALE`, `SOLD` or `TOTAL_LOSS` are excluded from **both** sets.

The **`FORWARD_LOOKING_PLANNED`** variant measures against the plan. `utilization_pct_planned` is the sum of `rented_days_total` over set A divided by the product of the plan's `planned_unit_count` and the season length, rounded half up to four decimal places. `revenue_per_unit_cents_planned` is the sum of `revenue_to_date_cents` over set A divided by `planned_unit_count`, taken as an integer floor. Both denominators come from the plan rather than from observation, and the inclusion set is the whole pipeline, so both metrics are diluted by every car still waiting for paperwork. Since `planned_unit_count` is required to be greater than zero and the season length is a positive constant, neither denominator can be zero.

The **`CHRONOLOGICAL_ACTUAL`** variant measures against what actually happened. Its utilization denominator is the sum of `available_days_total` over set B; when that sum is zero, `utilization_pct_actual` is reported as 0.0 rather than failing, and otherwise it is the sum of `rented_days_total` over set B divided by that sum, rounded half up to four decimal places. Its revenue denominator is the **count** of set B; when set B is empty, `revenue_per_unit_cents_actual` is reported as 0, and otherwise it is the sum of `revenue_to_date_cents` over set B divided by that count, taken as an integer floor. `rpu_variance_cents` is `revenue_per_unit_cents_actual` minus the plan's `revenue_per_unit_target_cents`, and it is measured against **this** variant only — a negative figure means the in-service fleet is under-earning against the target that justified buying it.

The two variants answer different questions and will disagree on identical data whenever the pipeline is not entirely in service, which is the normal condition of a fleet mid-cycle. Reporting both side by side is deliberate: the planned figures tell a controller whether the capital deployed is earning, and the actual figures tell an operator whether the cars on the road are busy.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every error response carries the `error_id` shown below in its `data` object, alongside a human-readable `message`, and uses the HTTP status shown. Where several rules could fire on one request, the precedence ladder of §2.9 decides which of them is reported. Three further codes sit outside the catalogue because they answer requests that never reach a domain rule at all: a path matching no endpoint is `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a path matched with an HTTP method it does not declare is `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405), and an unexpected server fault is `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500).
