# Interchange Qualification & Merchant Category Assignment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every card transaction that clears through the network is priced by an interchange rate, and the rate that applies is not a matter of negotiation — it is the arithmetic consequence of two things: the merchant category the merchant has been assigned, and the quality of the data the acquirer actually delivered in the clearing message. When either is wrong, money moves wrongly. A merchant miscategorised as a utility biller pays utility rates on retail volume; an acquirer that omits a terminal identifier from its clearing record loses the preferential program it was entitled to and silently absorbs the standard rate. Today those two failure modes are reconciled weeks later, by hand, across spreadsheets that nobody trusts.

This document specifies the service that closes that gap. It owns the merchant category directory and the rules by which a business type resolves to a four-digit merchant category code; it accepts category challenges from acquirers and routes them through a two-person review cycle; it evaluates every clearing record twice — once provisionally at intake against the authorization data, and once definitively at finalization against the clearing data — and it records exactly why a record was downgraded when it was. On top of that pricing core sit three governance functions: quarterly rate-change publication with per-acquirer implementation windows and a compliance posture that blocks clearing for acquirers who miss them; volume-commitment incentive programs with a finite capacity pool, a waitlist and periodic performance scoring; and a misrepresentation audit function that can freeze a merchant's category, restate it, and assess a penalty against the volume booked under the false category.

Two properties are non-negotiable throughout. First, **money is conserved**: the assessed interchange recorded on a batch and on a merchant must at all times equal the sum over the records that actually survive, and a reversal must roll the exact contribution back out rather than decrement a cached counter. Second, **the reasons are recorded, not inferred**: when a record is downgraded, the specific data-quality failure that caused it is stored on the record; when an audit rewrites a category, the prior posture is snapshotted so a cleared finding restores it verbatim. Implementers should read the workflow narratives in Section 3 as the authoritative description of behaviour, and Sections 4 and 5 as the transport-level index into it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request — reads included — must carry both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the five role names below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This is the very first check the service performs; nothing else about the request is examined until it passes.

The five roles are `NetworkAdmin`, `AcquirerOps`, `CategoryAnalyst`, `CategoryApprover` and `ComplianceAuditor`. Once the caller is authenticated, the acting role must appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being invoked (Section 4 carries that list per endpoint); a role that is absent from it is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

`NetworkAdmin` is network operations. It owns the reference data — processing platforms, acquirers, the merchant category directory, rate programs, quarterly rate versions, currency-conversion rates and incentive programs — finalizes clearing batches, scores incentive performance, and drives the logical clock. It deliberately cannot verify, review, decide or audit merchant categories; those powers sit with the category and compliance roles. `AcquirerOps` is acquirer-side operations: it boards merchants, opens and submits clearing batches, files category challenges, schedules implementation windows and enrols merchants into incentive programs. `CategoryAnalyst` verifies documentation-gated categories and works the review side of the reclassification cycle — taking a request under review and returning it for more information — but may never decide one. `CategoryApprover` has exactly one write capability, the reclassification decision. `ComplianceAuditor` owns the audit lifecycle end to end: opening an audit, performing fieldwork, sustaining or clearing findings, and closing.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership checks depend on them. When `X-User-Role` is `AcquirerOps`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Acquirer`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Acquirer` compare the resource's acquirer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `AcquirerOps` caller may act only where `Merchant.acquirer_id`, `SettlementBatch.acquirer_id` or `AcquirerImplementationWindow.acquirer_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`; for a reclassification request or an incentive enrollment the check follows the row through to its merchant and compares that merchant's sponsoring acquirer; and where the request body itself carries an `acquirer_id`, that value must equal `X-User-ID`. A violation is refused with `ERR_ACQUIRER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkAdmin`, `CategoryAnalyst`, `CategoryApprover` or `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow says so — as `verified_by_user_id` on a `Merchant`, as `reviewer_user_id` or `decider_user_id` on a `ReclassificationRequest`, as `auditor_user_id` or `closed_by_user_id` on a `MerchantCategoryAudit` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two segregation-of-duties rules are enforced against those recorded identifiers, and both report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403. The `CategoryApprover` deciding a reclassification request must present an `X-User-ID` different from the `reviewer_user_id` stored on that request. The `ComplianceAuditor` closing an audit must present an `X-User-ID` different from the `auditor_user_id` stored on that audit. In both cases the intent is the same: the person who worked the case may not be the person who ratifies it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as optional query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and is a zero-based row skip. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so a client paging through a collection sees rows in the order the network recorded them. Three collections publish a different order because their business meaning demands it. The merchant category directory listing is ordered by `high_risk` ascending — non-high-risk definitions first — and then by `mcc_code` ascending, which is precisely the ranking the category assignment rule applies when it picks a code for a newly boarded merchant. The currency-conversion rate listing is ordered by `effective_from` descending and then `created_at` descending, which is the order the assessment pipeline uses to pick the applicable rate row, so the first row of the first page is the one that would be applied right now. The incentive enrollment listing is ordered by `created_at` ascending, which is the head-of-line order used for waitlist promotion.

For list responses, `response_type` is `"array"` and the `meta` object additionally carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective skip), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Filters, where an endpoint offers them, narrow the matched set before `total_count` is computed.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Version Counter

Every resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID string carrying no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id` on creation. Foreign-key fields hold the plain UUID of the referenced row; the one deliberate exception to that convention is the merchant category code, which is carried by value as a four-digit string rather than by reference — `Merchant.assigned_mcc`, `ReclassificationRequest.requested_mcc`, `ReclassificationRequest.decided_mcc`, `MerchantCategoryAudit.correct_mcc`, `MerchantCategoryAudit.mcc_at_audit_open`, `TransactionRecord.mcc_at_qualification`, `RateProgram.eligible_mcc_codes` and `IncentiveProgram.eligible_mcc_codes` all hold or contain codes that are matched against `MccDefinition.mcc_code` by string equality, never against a definition's `id`.

Every resource in this service also carries `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Any operation that changes a resource — whether the client addressed that resource directly or the change arrived as a cascade from somewhere else — refreshes the row's `updated_at` and advances its `version`. This applies uniformly: no entity in this domain is version-free or timestamp-free.

Because `version` is the currency of the concurrency mechanism described in Section 2.8, it is worth being explicit about which cascades move it. Verifying a merchant's category, approving a reclassification, opening an audit, sustaining findings and clearing findings each increment `Merchant.version` by 1. Adding a clearing record, submitting a batch and finalizing a batch each increment `SettlementBatch.version` by 1. Creating an enrollment, parking one on the waitlist, withdrawing one, and any capacity release that flows from a sustained audit each increment `IncentiveProgram.version` by 1.

### 2.5. Reference Resolution and Not-Found Reporting

Identifiers embedded in a request URL are resolved strictly left to right, in the order they appear in the path. The first identifier that does not resolve to a stored row terminates the request with HTTP 404 and the not-found code belonging to that identifier's entity type, so a request against a record inside a batch that does not exist reports the missing batch rather than the missing record. Identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved after the path and after the request-shape check, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields; again the first unresolvable reference wins. Body-reference resolution applies to acquirer creation (its platform reference), implementation window creation (its acquirer, rate program version and platform references), merchant boarding (its acquirer reference), reclassification request creation (its merchant reference), batch opening (its acquirer reference), clearing record addition (its merchant reference), enrollment creation (its program and merchant references) and audit creation (its merchant reference).

The fourteen not-found codes are `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACQUIRER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MCC_DEFINITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECLASS_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCENTIVE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FX_RATE_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404 and each naming the entity type whose identifier failed to resolve.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Its Three-Step Sweep

Nothing in this service reads the host's wall clock. All time-dependent behaviour — publication instants, window starts, authorization ageing, quarter attribution — is evaluated against a **logical UTC clock** that the service holds in memory. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it must never fall back to wall-clock time, either before or after the first time it is set. A `NetworkAdmin` moves it by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`; the clock may be moved forward or backward, and the sweep below runs for any new value.

Setting the clock runs a sweep in exactly three steps, in the order given. Within each step, matching rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order; the row `id` is never consulted for ordering. **Step one** supersedes rate versions: every `RateProgramVersion` in status `PUBLISHED` whose `effective_to` is at or before the new clock value moves to `SUPERSEDED`. There is no cascade — implementation windows already pointing at that version keep whatever status they hold. **Step two** marks missed implementation windows: every `AcquirerImplementationWindow` in status `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` whose `window_end` is strictly earlier than the new clock value moves to `MISSED`; then, for every acquirer touched by that step, `missed_window_count` is recomputed and the acquirer's `compliance_status` moves from `COMPLIANT` to `NON_COMPLIANT` whenever the recomputed count is greater than zero. **Step three** expires stale authorizations: every `TransactionRecord` whose `authorization_status` is `AUTH_APPROVED` and whose `qualification_status` is `PENDING` and whose age measured from `auth_timestamp` to the new clock value is strictly greater than 30 days — the authorization validity horizon is 720 hours — moves to `AUTH_EXPIRED`.

Note the deliberate asymmetry in the three comparisons: step one uses "at or before" against `effective_to`, step two uses strictly-earlier against `window_end`, and step three uses strictly-greater against the 720-hour horizon. No step ever undoes the effect of a previous sweep, so rewinding the clock does not resurrect a superseded version, an un-miss a window, or restore an expired authorization.

### 2.7. Interchange Arithmetic: Basis Points, Whole Cents and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this service are integers in the minor unit of their currency — cents — and no field anywhere holds a fractional cent. Rates are expressed in basis points, one basis point being one ten-thousandth, so applying a rate of `base_rate_bps` to an amount means multiplying the amount by that basis-point figure and dividing by ten thousand.

Wherever a division or a rate application would produce a fraction, the result is rounded **half up** — a remainder of exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole unit. That single rule governs the ad-valorem interchange component, the cross-border surcharge, the currency-conversion markup, the amortisation of a per-item fee into comparable basis points, the misrepresentation penalty and the incentive credit; every one of those produces a whole number of cents. Two quantities are rounded half up to two decimal places rather than to whole units: `IncentiveEnrollment.performance_score_pct` and its comparison partner `IncentiveProgram.performance_threshold_pct`.

One rounding-order rule is load-bearing and is stated here because getting it wrong silently changes settlement totals. When a batch total is computed, each record's assessment is rounded to whole cents first and the already-rounded per-record amounts are then added together. It is forbidden to accumulate unrounded ad-valorem products across records and round once at the end. The two orders genuinely differ: two records of 1200 cents each on a program of 254 basis points with a 10-cent per-item fee yield 30 plus 10 plus 30 plus 10, a total of 80 cents under round-then-sum, whereas summing first and rounding once yields 81.

One derived integer is not rounded but truncated. `TransactionRecord.clearing_delay_hours` is the whole number of complete hours between `auth_timestamp` and `clearing_timestamp`: take the elapsed seconds, divide by 3600, and discard any remainder toward zero. A delay of 23 hours 59 minutes 59 seconds is therefore 23, not 24. This value is computed once, at record creation, and is never recomputed afterwards even if the record is later reassessed.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Versions

Three write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency against a parent resource's `version`. In each case the guard exists because the operation is perfectly legitimate to repeat — a second record in the same open batch, a second enrollment against the same program — so no state precondition could distinguish a well-informed caller from one acting on a stale read. The only thing that can distinguish them is the parent version the caller carries.

Adding a clearing record to a settlement batch requires `expected_batch_version` in the request body. It is not optional. Its value must equal the current `version` of the `SettlementBatch` named in the path; if some other writer has advanced that batch's `version` since the client last read it, the addition is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Because every accepted record recomputes `record_count` and advances `SettlementBatch.version`, a client adding several records in sequence must re-read the batch between additions to obtain the new version.

Creating a reclassification request requires `expected_merchant_version` in the request body, matching the current `version` of the target `Merchant`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This is the guard that catches the client whose view of the merchant predates a newer request having already superseded the prior one, or predates a category verification or an audit cascade having rewritten the merchant's category posture.

Creating an incentive enrollment requires `expected_incentive_program_version` in the request body, matching the current `version` of the target `IncentiveProgram`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Every accepted enrollment — and every waitlist parking — redraws `uncommitted_capacity_cents`, `enrolled_count` and `enrollment_state`, and advances `IncentiveProgram.version`, so the version a client read before the previous enrollment landed is no longer usable.

No other endpoint in this service accepts an expected-version field. Endpoints not named above take no version input and are never rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

Many operations in this service are guarded by several checks at once, and a request can fail more than one of them. The service reports exactly one error, and which one it reports is fixed: checks are evaluated in the strict priority order below, from 1 downward, and the first check that fails ends the request. Lower numbers therefore mask higher ones. This ordering is part of the contract — an implementation that reports a capacity problem where the ladder demands a stale-version problem is wrong even though both conditions hold.

1. Both authentication headers are present and `X-User-Role` names one of the five roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every identifier embedded in the request path resolves to a stored row, checked left to right in path order — otherwise the matching 404 not-found code. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
4. Every identifier supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields — otherwise the matching 404 not-found code. Applies to `POST /acquirers`, `POST /implementation-windows`, `POST /merchants`, `POST /reclassification-requests`, `POST /settlement-batches`, `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records`, `POST /incentive-enrollments` and `POST /merchant-category-audits`.
5. The request body is well formed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every POST endpoint. Well formed means: every required field is present and not null; every enum-valued field holds a value inside its declared domain (which is why `DOWNGRADE_THRESHOLD` supplied as `trigger_reason` on a manual audit creation is refused here); integer money and basis-point fields are not negative; `amount_cents` is at least 1; `committed_volume_cents` is at least 1; timestamps parse as ISO 8601; currency codes are exactly three uppercase letters; a supplied merchant category code is exactly four digits; `window_start` is strictly earlier than `window_end`; `effective_from` is strictly earlier than `effective_to`; `fieldwork_start` is at or before `fieldwork_end`; `data_elements` is a list of unique uppercase tokens drawn from the declared element vocabulary; and `decision` is either `APPROVE` or `DENY`.
6. When the acting role is `AcquirerOps`, `X-User-ID` equals the acquirer that owns the target resource — otherwise `ERR_ACQUIRER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorized-role list includes `AcquirerOps`.
7. The target resource's own status permits the requested transition, and an implementation window being scheduled references a `RateProgramVersion` in status `PUBLISHED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This is the catch-all for state preconditions that have no dedicated code of their own, and it applies to `POST /rate-program-versions/{id}/publish`, `POST /implementation-windows`, `POST /implementation-windows/{id}/start`, `POST /implementation-windows/{id}/complete`, `POST /merchants/{id}/verify-category`, `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/review`, `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/request-info`, `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/resubmit`, `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/decide`, `POST /transaction-records/{id}/reverse`, `POST /incentive-enrollments/{id}/evaluate-performance`, `POST /incentive-enrollments/{id}/withdraw`, `POST /merchant-category-audits/{id}/start-fieldwork`, `POST /merchant-category-audits/{id}/sustain-findings`, `POST /merchant-category-audits/{id}/clear-findings`, `POST /merchant-category-audits/{id}/close`, `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/submit` and `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/finalize`. It also covers audit creation against a merchant whose category posture does not permit an audit to open.
8. The settlement batch receiving a clearing record is in status `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records`.
9. The incentive program is in `enrollment_state` `OPEN`; when it is `CLOSED_FULL` the enrollment is admissible only if the request sets `waitlist_requested` to true — otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_CLOSED` (409). Applies to `POST /incentive-enrollments`.
10. `expected_batch_version` equals the stored `SettlementBatch.version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records`.
11. `expected_merchant_version` equals the stored `Merchant.version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /reclassification-requests`.
12. `expected_incentive_program_version` equals the stored `IncentiveProgram.version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /incentive-enrollments`.
13. The referenced merchant's `mcc_status` is not `MISREPRESENTED` — otherwise `ERR_MERCHANT_MISREPRESENTED` (409). A merchant proven to have misrepresented its category is frozen out of every downstream money or category operation, so this guard covers four operations: `POST /reclassification-requests`, `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records`, `POST /incentive-enrollments` and `POST /merchants/{id}/verify-category`.
14. The referenced merchant's `mcc_status` is not `UNDER_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_MERCHANT_UNDER_CATEGORY_REVIEW` (409). While a category audit is open the merchant's category may not be re-litigated and it may not take on new incentive commitments, so this guard covers three operations: `POST /reclassification-requests`, `POST /incentive-enrollments` and `POST /merchants/{id}/verify-category`.
15. The referenced merchant's `mcc_status` is `VERIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_VERIFIED` (409). Applies to `POST /incentive-enrollments`.
16. The owning acquirer's `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` — otherwise `ERR_ACQUIRER_NON_COMPLIANT` (409). An acquirer that has missed a rate-change implementation window may not book interchange at the new rates. Applies to `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/finalize`.
17. The deciding approver's `X-User-ID` differs from the request's `reviewer_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/decide`.
18. The closing auditor's `X-User-ID` differs from the audit's `auditor_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /merchant-category-audits/{id}/close`.
19. `clearing_timestamp` is at or after `auth_timestamp` — otherwise `ERR_CLEARING_BEFORE_AUTH` (422). Equality is legal and yields a clearing delay of zero hours. Applies to `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records`.
20. The implementation window lies inside its rate version's publication-to-deadline envelope: `window_start` is at or after the version's `published_at` and `window_end` is at or before the version's `implementation_deadline`, both bounds inclusive — otherwise `ERR_IMPLEMENTATION_WINDOW_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (422). Applies to `POST /implementation-windows`.
21. Publishing a rate version does not overlap another `PUBLISHED` version of the same rate program under half-open interval semantics — otherwise `ERR_RATE_VERSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /rate-program-versions/{id}/publish`.
22. The number of `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` windows on the same platform whose blackout-buffered closed interval touches the candidate window is below that platform's `max_concurrent_windows` — otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /implementation-windows`. Note that this check sits below the envelope check at priority 20, so a window that is both outside its envelope and platform-conflicting reports `ERR_IMPLEMENTATION_WINDOW_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`.
23. Natural-key uniqueness holds — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409). The natural keys are: `MccDefinition.mcc_code` globally unique; `RateProgram.program_code` globally unique; `Acquirer.bin_prefix` globally unique; a rate version unique on the combination of its `rate_program_id` and `publication_quarter`; a settlement batch unique on the combination of `acquirer_id`, `batch_date` and `currency_code`; a currency-conversion row unique on the combination of `base_currency`, `quote_currency` and `effective_from`; and `TransactionRecord.auth_code` unique within a single settlement batch. Applies to `POST /mcc-definitions`, `POST /rate-programs`, `POST /acquirers`, `POST /rate-programs/{id}/versions`, `POST /settlement-batches`, `POST /fx-rates` and `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records`.
24. `ReclassificationRequest.review_round` is strictly below the review-round ceiling of 3 — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/request-info` and to `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/resubmit`.
25. A supplied `requested_mcc` or `correct_mcc` names an existing `MccDefinition.mcc_code` — otherwise `ERR_MCC_NOT_DEFINED` (422). Applies to `POST /reclassification-requests` and `POST /merchant-category-audits/{id}/sustain-findings`.
26. At least one `MccDefinition` exists whose `business_type_key` equals the merchant's declared `business_type_key`, so that a category can be resolved — otherwise `ERR_MCC_NOT_DEFINED` (422). Applies to `POST /merchants`.
27. The merchant's `assigned_mcc` appears in the incentive program's `eligible_mcc_codes` — otherwise `ERR_MCC_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_PROGRAM` (422). Applies to `POST /incentive-enrollments`.
28. At least one rate program is selectable for the record under the qualification rules, which in practice means the mandatory `STANDARD` fallback program exists — otherwise `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_RATE_PROGRAM` (422). Applies to `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records` and `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/finalize`.
29. When `transaction_currency` differs from the batch's `currency_code`, a currency-conversion row exists for that pair and is already effective — otherwise `ERR_FX_RATE_UNAVAILABLE` (422). Applies to `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/transaction-records` and `POST /settlement-batches/{id}/finalize`.
30. When the target `MccDefinition` has `requires_documentation` set to true, a non-empty documentation reference accompanies the request — `documentation_ref` on category verification, `supporting_doc_ref` on a resubmission — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /merchants/{id}/verify-category` and `POST /reclassification-requests/{id}/resubmit`.
31. The merchant holds no enrollment in status `PENDING`, `ENROLLED`, `WAITLISTED` or `PROBATION` on the same incentive program — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` (409). Applies to `POST /incentive-enrollments`.
32. `committed_volume_cents` is at least the program's `min_commitment_cents` — otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422). Applies to `POST /incentive-enrollments`.
33. `committed_volume_cents` is at most the program's `uncommitted_capacity_cents` — otherwise `ERR_INCENTIVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). This check is skipped entirely when the enrollment is being admitted to the waitlist rather than enrolled. Applies to `POST /incentive-enrollments`.

Read the enrollment path against that ladder and the consequences are concrete: a request against a `CLOSED_FULL` program without `waitlist_requested` reports `ERR_ENROLLMENT_CLOSED` even if its `expected_incentive_program_version` is also stale and its commitment is also below the minimum, because priority 9 sits above priorities 12 and 32. Equally, a stale version masks every merchant-eligibility and fiscal check below it.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities and Cascade Discipline

Two quantities in this service are conserved, and both must hold with zero drift after every operation that touches them. Neither may be maintained by incrementing or decrementing a cached counter; both are re-derived from the rows that currently exist.

The first is the **assessment ledger**, which is monetary. At all times `SettlementBatch.total_assessed_cents` must equal the sum of `assessed_interchange_cents` over the transaction records belonging to that batch whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `DOWNGRADED`; records in `PENDING` or `REVERSED` contribute nothing. In parallel, `Merchant.lifetime_net_assessed_cents` must equal the sum of `assessed_interchange_cents` over that merchant's records whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `DOWNGRADED`, across all batches. Finalizing a batch is the forward action that posts amounts into both sums; a clearing reversal is the compensating action that zeroes the record's amount and moves it to `REVERSED`, after which both sums must again equal the sum over the surviving qualified and downgraded rows exactly. An implementation that zeroes the record but forgets to re-derive the two totals, or that re-derives the totals but forgets to zero the record, breaks the invariant.

The second is the **incentive capacity pool**, which is a reservation of committable volume rather than money. At all times an incentive program's `total_capacity_cents` must equal its `uncommitted_capacity_cents` plus the sum of `committed_volume_cents` over its enrollments in status `ENROLLED` or `PROBATION`. Enrollments in `WAITLISTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `TERMINATED_SHORTFALL` or `TERMINATED_INELIGIBLE` sit outside the committed set and contribute nothing. Admitting an enrollment and promoting one off the waitlist are the debits; withdrawal, shortfall termination and ineligibility termination are the credits. Withdrawing a waitlisted row is a no-op against the pool — it never debited — and the equality must still hold afterwards with no drift. Note that `PROBATION` is inside the committed set: an enrollment moved onto probation keeps its capacity reserved.

Cascades in this domain are described as ordered hops, and the ordering is meaningful because later hops read what earlier hops wrote. Where a cascade touches a set of rows, that set is always processed in ascending `created_at` order, never by `id`. Where a cascade changes a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances exactly as if the change had been requested directly. And where a cascade snapshots a value so it can be restored later — a merchant's category posture before an audit, a reclassification request's status before suspension — the restore writes the snapshot back verbatim and then clears the snapshot field to null.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Network Reference Data: Platforms, Acquirers and the Category Directory

Before any merchant can be boarded or any transaction priced, network operations must lay down the reference layer. It begins with **processing platforms** — the acquirer-side systems on which rate changes are physically deployed. Registering a platform records a human label as `name`, unique across platforms and between 1 and 80 characters; the network region it serves as `region_code`, which must be one of `NA`, `EU`, `AP`, `LAC` or `CEMEA`; and two capacity parameters. The first, `max_concurrent_windows`, is at least 1 and states how many implementation windows in status `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` may occupy overlapping buffered intervals on this platform at once. The second, `blackout_buffer_days`, is zero or more whole days and is the symmetric change-freeze pad the platform demands around each of its already-booked windows; how it is applied is described in Section 3.2.

An **acquirer** is registered onto exactly one platform. Its `name` runs from 1 to 120 characters; its `bin_prefix` is exactly six digits and must be unique across all acquirers; `platform_id` names the platform on which it will schedule its rate-change implementation windows; `country_code` is the acquirer's ISO-3166 alpha-2 country in exactly two uppercase letters; and `settlement_currency` is the ISO-4217 code it settles in, exactly three uppercase letters. Every newly registered acquirer starts life with `compliance_status` set to `COMPLIANT` and `missed_window_count` set to 0. The `country_code` is not decoration: it is the value against which a merchant's home country is compared to decide whether a transaction is cross-border, and it is read from the acquirer that owns the batch, not from the merchant's own acquirer reference.

The **merchant category directory** is the network's published catalogue of four-digit codes. Each definition records `mcc_code`, exactly four digits and globally unique; `description`, the category text as published in the directory, between 1 and 200 characters; and `business_type_key`, an uppercase key of A–Z characters and underscores, 1 to 40 characters long, which is how a declared business type is mapped onto a category. That key is deliberately **not** unique — several definitions may share one key, and the choice between them is made by the ranking rule in Section 3.3. Three more attributes decide how the code behaves downstream. `program_eligibility` is a duplicate-free set drawn from `SMALL_TICKET`, `UTILITY`, `GOVERNMENT` and `CHARITY`, and it is the gate that decides which special interchange programs this category may ever reach; it defaults to the empty set. `requires_documentation`, when true, means a merchant assigned this code starts unverified and needs an explicit category verification, and a reclassification *into* this code may only be approved once a supporting document reference is on file. `high_risk` is the flag that sorts a definition to the back of the queue when the assignment rule has a choice.

Currency-conversion reference data lives in the same layer. Publishing a conversion row records `base_currency` — the ISO-4217 code the cardholder was billed in — and `quote_currency`, the settlement code, each exactly three uppercase letters; `rate`, the mid-market conversion factor, strictly greater than zero and carried to at most six decimal places; `markup_bps`, the currency-conversion assessment in basis points from 0 through 500; and `effective_from`, the inclusive instant from which the row becomes selectable. The combination of `base_currency`, `quote_currency` and `effective_from` is a natural key, so republishing the same pair at the same instant is a duplicate. The `rate` itself is published for downstream reconciliation and reporting; the amount this service assesses is driven by `markup_bps`, as Section 3.6 sets out.

### 3.2. Rate Programs, Quarterly Rate Publication and Implementation Windows

An **interchange rate program** is a priced tier. Defining one records `program_code`, 1 to 40 characters and globally unique; `name`, the published program name of 1 to 120 characters; and `category`, which is one of `CPS_RETAIL`, `SMALL_TICKET`, `UTILITY`, `GOVERNMENT`, `CHARITY` or `STANDARD`. Exactly one program with category `STANDARD` must exist, and it acts as the unconditional fallback tier — the destination of every record the network cannot place anywhere better. The program's coverage is `eligible_mcc_codes`, a non-empty duplicate-free array of four-digit code strings compared by value against a merchant's assigned category. Its price has two components: `base_rate_bps`, an ad-valorem rate from 0 through 1000 basis points, and `per_item_fee_cents`, a flat per-transaction fee from 0 through 500 cents added after the ad-valorem component. Its data-quality demands are `requires_data_elements`, a duplicate-free subset of `AUTH_CODE`, `TERMINAL_ID`, `TRANSACTION_DATE`, `CARDHOLDER_ZIP`, `CUSTOMER_ACCOUNT_REF` and `TAX_AMOUNT` that must **all** be present in a clearing record for the program to remain a candidate; it defaults to the empty set. Finally `max_clearing_delay_hours`, from 0 through 8760, is the largest clearing delay the program tolerates — a constraint that, importantly, is only enforced at finalization and not at record intake.

Rates are restated quarterly. A **rate program version** is drafted under a program and records `rate_program_id`, the program it restates; `publication_quarter`, a label matching a four-digit year beginning `20` followed by `Q` and a digit 1 through 4, so `2026Q3` is well formed; `effective_from`, the inclusive start of the version's effective interval; and `effective_to`, which is the **exclusive** end. The interval is half-open, which means adjacency is legal: a version whose `effective_from` equals a predecessor's `effective_to` hands over cleanly with no gap and no collision. A version also restates `base_rate_bps` and `per_item_fee_cents` for the quarter, and declares `implementation_deadline`, the last instant at which any acquirer's implementation window for this version may end. A drafted version is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `published_at` null, and the same program may not carry two versions for the same `publication_quarter`.

Publication is where the interval arithmetic bites. Publishing moves a version `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and stamps `published_at` with the current logical-clock instant. Before it does, the service compares the candidate's half-open interval against every other version of the **same** rate program that is currently in status `PUBLISHED`; versions of other programs are irrelevant, and rivals in `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` are ignored entirely. Two half-open intervals collide when the candidate's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the rival's `effective_to` **and** the rival's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the candidate's `effective_to`; both comparisons are strict, and the capacity is one, so a single colliding rival is enough to reject the publication with `ERR_RATE_VERSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The consequence worth internalising is that a shared endpoint is safe while a one-millisecond intrusion is not: a candidate starting exactly at a rival's `effective_to` publishes, and a candidate starting one millisecond earlier than that does not. A published version leaves `PUBLISHED` only by ageing out — the logical-clock sweep moves it to `SUPERSEDED` once its `effective_to` is at or before the clock — and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

Once a version is published, its acquirers must implement it, and each does so inside a declared **implementation window**. Scheduling one records `acquirer_id`, the acquirer that must implement; `rate_program_version_id`, the published version being implemented, which must be in status `PUBLISHED` or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; `platform_id`, the platform whose capacity the window occupies; and `window_start` and `window_end`, which are both **inclusive** — this interval is closed, the opposite convention to the rate version interval above. The window is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `started_at` and `completion_note` null.

Two independent checks guard scheduling, and their order is fixed. First the **envelope check**: `window_start` must be at or after the referenced version's `published_at`, and `window_end` must be at or before that version's `implementation_deadline`. Both bounds are inclusive, so starting exactly at `published_at` is legal and ending exactly at `implementation_deadline` is legal; anything outside is refused with `ERR_IMPLEMENTATION_WINDOW_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`. Only then comes the **platform capacity check**, which uses deliberately different arithmetic from the rate-version check. The service gathers the windows already on the same platform in status `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — windows in `COMPLETED`, `COMPLETED_LATE` or `MISSED` are excluded, windows on other platforms are excluded, and windows belonging to *other acquirers* are very much included, because a platform is a shared resource. Each such rival's closed interval is then inflated symmetrically on both ends by the platform's `blackout_buffer_days` converted to whole days of seconds. The candidate is said to collide with an inflated rival when the candidate's `window_start` is at or before the rival's inflated end **and** the rival's inflated start is at or before the candidate's `window_end`; both comparisons are non-strict, so merely touching endpoints do collide here. The colliding rivals are counted, and if that count reaches the platform's `max_concurrent_windows`, the schedule is refused with `ERR_PLATFORM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Two windows that look geometrically disjoint on a calendar can therefore still collide once the buffer is applied.

The window lifecycle itself is short. Starting a window advances it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `started_at` with the current logical-clock instant; it requires the referenced rate version still to be `PUBLISHED`. Reporting completion from `IN_PROGRESS` advances the window to `COMPLETED`, stores the supplied `completion_note`, and recomputes the acquirer's `missed_window_count`. If instead the logical-clock sweep found the window still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` after its `window_end` had passed, the window is already sitting in `MISSED`; reporting completion from there advances it to `COMPLETED_LATE`, stores `completion_note`, and again recomputes `missed_window_count`. Both `COMPLETED` and `COMPLETED_LATE` are terminal.

An acquirer's compliance posture is a pure function of that count. `missed_window_count` is the number of implementation windows belonging to the acquirer that currently sit in `MISSED`, and it is recomputed on every window status change. Whenever a recomputation leaves the count greater than zero and the acquirer is `COMPLIANT`, the acquirer moves `COMPLIANT → NON_COMPLIANT`; while it is `NON_COMPLIANT`, finalization of *any* of its settlement batches is refused with `ERR_ACQUIRER_NON_COMPLIANT`. Whenever a recomputation brings the count back to zero and the acquirer is `NON_COMPLIANT`, it moves `NON_COMPLIANT → COMPLIANT` and finalization is unblocked. Late completion is therefore a genuine remedy, not merely a bookkeeping note.

### 3.3. Boarding a Merchant and Verifying Its Category

Boarding a merchant is the moment a category is assigned, and the assignment is derived, never dictated. The acquirer supplies `legal_name` (1 to 120 characters), `business_type_key` (the declared business type, an uppercase A–Z and underscore key of 1 to 40 characters), `acquirer_id` (the sponsoring acquirer, which for an `AcquirerOps` caller must be its own identity) and `home_country_code` (the outlet's ISO-3166 alpha-2 country in exactly two uppercase letters). The merchant does not supply a category code.

Instead the service resolves one. It gathers every category definition whose `business_type_key` equals the merchant's declared `business_type_key` — the whole directory is in scope, since category definitions have no lifecycle and none is ever excluded. If that candidate set is empty, no merchant row is created at all and the request is refused with `ERR_MCC_NOT_DEFINED`. Otherwise the candidates are ranked by three keys applied in order: `high_risk` ascending, so definitions that are not flagged high-risk are preferred over those that are; then `mcc_code` ascending as a lexicographic comparison over the four-digit string; and finally, only if both of those tie, `created_at` ascending. The row `id` is never used as a tie-break. The first candidate under that ordering wins, and its `mcc_code` is written to the merchant's `assigned_mcc` as a value — the merchant stores the code, not a reference to the definition.

The chosen definition then decides the merchant's opening posture. `mcc_status` is one of `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `MISREPRESENTED`. When the chosen definition has `requires_documentation` false, the merchant is auto-verified: `mcc_status` is set to `VERIFIED` inside the very request that creates the row, so `PENDING_VERIFICATION` is never observable for such a merchant. When the chosen definition has `requires_documentation` true, the row is returned in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and an explicit verification step is required before the merchant can be enrolled in anything. Either way the merchant's two rolling counters open at zero: `downgrade_count_current_quarter` is 0 and `lifetime_net_assessed_cents` is 0. Three further fields open null and stay null until the workflows that own them run: `mcc_status_before_review`, the snapshot slot an audit uses; `documentation_ref`; and `verified_by_user_id`.

Verifying a category is a `CategoryAnalyst` action and it advances `mcc_status` `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED`. It carries a single input, `documentation_ref`, which must be a non-empty string; where the assigned definition has `requires_documentation` true and the reference is empty or absent, the request is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`. On success the supplied value is stored as `documentation_ref`, the acting operator's identity is recorded as `verified_by_user_id`, and `Merchant.version` advances by 1. Verification is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` unless the merchant is actually in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_UNDER_CATEGORY_REVIEW` while a category audit holds the merchant in `UNDER_REVIEW`, and refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_MISREPRESENTED` once the merchant has reached `MISREPRESENTED`.

`MISREPRESENTED` is terminal for a merchant. No transition leaves it, and while it holds, the merchant may not have clearing records added for it, may not be the subject of a new reclassification request, may not be enrolled into an incentive program, and may not be verified. Listing merchants supports narrowing by sponsoring `acquirer_id`, by `mcc_status`, by `assigned_mcc` and by `business_type_key`, which together are the only discovery channel for merchants the network's own cascades have moved.

### 3.4. Challenging a Category: The Reclassification Review Cycle

An acquirer that believes a merchant has been mis-categorised files a **reclassification request**. The submission carries `merchant_id`, the merchant whose category is challenged; `requested_mcc`, the four-digit code being asked for, which must name an existing definition or the request is refused with `ERR_MCC_NOT_DEFINED`; `justification`, free text of 1 to 1000 characters; and `expected_merchant_version`, the merchant version the client last observed. The request is created in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `review_round` at 1 and every other system-owned field null: `supporting_doc_ref`, `reviewer_user_id`, `info_request_note`, `decider_user_id`, `decision_note`, `decided_mcc` and `prior_status_before_suspension`. Creating it advances `Merchant.version` by 1, which is why a client that files two requests in a row must re-read the merchant in between.

Filing a request is also a supersession event. Any earlier request on the same merchant that is still live — meaning its `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` — is moved to `SUPERSEDED` by the system as the new one is created, and the new request opens in `SUBMITTED` with `review_round` 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, alongside `APPROVED` and `DENIED`.

The review cycle proper is a loop. A `CategoryAnalyst` takes a `SUBMITTED` request under review, advancing it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and recording their own identity as `reviewer_user_id`; if the request has been round-tripped before, the new reviewer's identity **overwrites** whatever was there. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the analyst may return the request for more information, advancing it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and storing the supplied `info_request_note`. The acquirer then answers, supplying `supporting_doc_ref`; this advances the request `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, increments `review_round` by 1, stores the supplied document reference and clears `info_request_note` back to null. Answering is additionally blocked while the merchant's own `mcc_status` is `UNDER_REVIEW` or `MISREPRESENTED`.

The loop is bounded at three rounds. `review_round` counts how many times the request has entered `SUBMITTED`, starting at 1, and it must be strictly below 3 for the cycle to turn again — both for a further information request and for a further answer. Once `review_round` has reached 3, a further attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and the request stays exactly where it was, in `INFO_REQUESTED`; where documentation is demanded on the answer and none is supplied, the refusal is `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`.

Deciding is a `CategoryApprover` action taken from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and it carries `decision` — either `APPROVE` or `DENY` — plus `decision_note`. The approver's identity is recorded as `decider_user_id`, and it must differ from the stored `reviewer_user_id`; where it does not, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On `APPROVE` the request advances `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, `decided_mcc` is set to the `requested_mcc`, and the approval must also clear one further hurdle: where the requested category's definition has `requires_documentation` true, a non-null `supporting_doc_ref` must already be on file. Approval then cascades. **Hop one** rewrites the merchant: `assigned_mcc` becomes the requested code, and where the requested definition has `requires_documentation` true the merchant's `mcc_status` is reset to `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, while where it is false the `mcc_status` is left exactly as it was. **Hop two**, caused by hop one rather than by the decision itself, walks the merchant's incentive enrollments in `ENROLLED` or `PROBATION` in ascending `created_at` order and moves every one whose program's `eligible_mcc_codes` does not contain the newly assigned code to `TERMINATED_INELIGIBLE`, releasing its committed capacity as described in Section 3.8. `Merchant.version` advances by 1.

On `DENY` the picture is deliberately narrower: the request advances `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, `decider_user_id` and `decision_note` are recorded, `decided_mcc` stays null, and both `Merchant.assigned_mcc` and `Merchant.mcc_status` are left entirely untouched. No enrollment is affected and no capacity moves. The segregation-of-duties requirement applies identically to a denial and to an approval.

A live request can also be frozen rather than decided. When a category audit opens on the merchant — whether opened by a compliance auditor or created automatically by the downgrade cascade in Section 3.6 — every request on that merchant in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` snapshots its current status into `prior_status_before_suspension` and moves to `SUSPENDED_PENDING_AUDIT`, with the affected rows processed in ascending `created_at` order. A `SUBMITTED` request snapshots `SUBMITTED`; an `UNDER_REVIEW` request snapshots `UNDER_REVIEW`; an `INFO_REQUESTED` request snapshots `INFO_REQUESTED`. What happens next depends on how the audit ends. If the audit clears the merchant, each suspended request is restored verbatim to the status held in `prior_status_before_suspension` — back to `SUBMITTED`, back to `UNDER_REVIEW` or back to `INFO_REQUESTED` respectively — and `prior_status_before_suspension` is then cleared to null, again processing rows in ascending `created_at` order. If instead the audit sustains its findings, each suspended request moves to `DENIED` with `decision_note` set to the exact string `auto-denied by sustained category audit`, and `decider_user_id` stays null because no approver acted.

Reclassification requests can be listed and narrowed by `merchant_id`, by `status` and by `requested_mcc`.

### 3.5. Clearing Batches, Record Intake and Provisional Qualification

Clearing is presented in batches. Opening a **settlement batch** records `acquirer_id`, the acquirer presenting the clearing; `batch_date`, the calendar date of the batch as a four-digit year, two-digit month and two-digit day separated by hyphens; and `currency_code`, the ISO-4217 settlement currency of the batch in exactly three uppercase letters. The combination of those three values is a natural key, so an acquirer cannot open two batches for the same date in the same currency. The batch opens in `status` `OPEN` with `record_count` 0, `total_assessed_cents` 0 and `finalized_at` null. Its lifecycle is linear: `OPEN → SUBMITTED → FINALIZED`, and `FINALIZED` is terminal.

Records may only be added while the batch is `OPEN`; an addition against a batch in any other status is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_OPEN`. Each addition supplies `merchant_id`, the merchant that acquired the transaction; `amount_cents`, strictly greater than zero and at most 100000000000, expressed in the minor unit of the transaction's own currency; `transaction_currency`, the ISO-4217 code the cardholder was billed in; `auth_code`, the six-character authorization approval code from the authorization message, which must be unique within the batch or the addition is a duplicate; `auth_timestamp`, the instant the authorization was approved; `clearing_timestamp`, the instant the clearing record was presented, which must be at or after `auth_timestamp`; `data_elements`, the duplicate-free set of clearing data elements actually present in the message, drawn from `AUTH_CODE`, `TERMINAL_ID`, `TRANSACTION_DATE`, `CARDHOLDER_ZIP`, `CUSTOMER_ACCOUNT_REF` and `TAX_AMOUNT` and defaulting to the empty set; and `expected_batch_version`, the batch version the client last observed. A `clearing_timestamp` strictly earlier than `auth_timestamp` is refused with `ERR_CLEARING_BEFORE_AUTH`; equality is legal and simply means the record cleared in the same instant it was authorized.

A clearing record carries **two state dimensions that evolve independently on the same row**, and confusing them is the single most common way to get this domain wrong. `authorization_status` is the first dimension and holds `AUTH_APPROVED`, `AUTH_REVERSED` or `AUTH_EXPIRED`; it opens at `AUTH_APPROVED`, moves only on an authorization reversal or on the logical-clock expiry sweep, and is **never** moved by batch finalization. `qualification_status` is the second dimension and holds `PENDING`, `QUALIFIED`, `DOWNGRADED` or `REVERSED`; it opens at `PENDING`, moves only on batch finalization or on a clearing reversal, and is **never** moved by an authorization reversal or by authorization expiry.

Everything the definitive pass will later write opens empty: `qualified_rate_program_id`, `mcc_at_qualification`, `downgrade_reason`, `reversal_type` and `reversal_reason` are all null on a freshly accepted record, while `assessed_interchange_cents`, `cross_border_surcharge_cents` and `fx_markup_cents` are all 0. The two provisional fields are the exception — they are filled in at intake, as described below.

Two values are derived once at intake and never recomputed. `clearing_delay_hours` is the whole number of complete hours between `auth_timestamp` and `clearing_timestamp`, truncated as described in Section 2.7. `cross_border` is true exactly when the merchant's `home_country_code` differs from the `country_code` of the acquirer that owns this record's batch — note the comparison reaches through the batch to its acquirer, and it is frozen at intake even if either country is later restated.

Intake also runs a **provisional qualification** — a forward-looking price estimate under the authorization data alone. The service reads the merchant's `assigned_mcc` as it stands at that instant and reads that category definition's `program_eligibility` set. It then filters the rate programs, keeping only those that satisfy every one of the following conditions. First, the merchant's category appears in the program's `eligible_mcc_codes`. Second, the program's `category` is either `STANDARD`, or `CPS_RETAIL`, or a category that appears in the merchant's category definition's `program_eligibility` set — so a `UTILITY` program is only reachable by a merchant whose category definition lists `UTILITY`. Third, every element listed in the program's `requires_data_elements` is present in the record's `data_elements`; a program demanding an element the message did not carry is out. Fourth, either the program's `category` is not `SMALL_TICKET`, or the record's `amount_cents` is at most the small-ticket ceiling of 1500 cents — fifteen dollars — so a large ticket cannot reach a small-ticket program. Fifth, either the program's `category` is `STANDARD`, or the record's `authorization_status` is `AUTH_APPROVED`; the fallback tier accepts a record whose authorization has been reversed or has expired, while every preferential tier does not. At intake, and only at intake, the program's clearing-delay tolerance is **not** consulted at all: clearing timing is not yet treated as an acquirer commitment, so a program remains a candidate no matter how large the record's `clearing_delay_hours` is.

If that filter leaves no candidate at all, the addition is refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_RATE_PROGRAM`; in practice this can only happen if the mandatory `STANDARD` fallback program is absent from the reference data. Otherwise the survivors are ranked by the cheapest total cost to the merchant *at this ticket size*. For each candidate the service computes a comparable cost in basis points: take the program's `per_item_fee_cents`, multiply by ten thousand, divide by the record's `amount_cents`, round that quotient half up to a whole basis point, and add the result to the program's `base_rate_bps`. Amortising the flat fee before comparison — and rounding the amortisation — is what makes the ranking fully integer-deterministic. Candidates are then ordered by that amortised cost ascending, then by `base_rate_bps` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending; the row `id` is never consulted. The first candidate wins.

The winner of the provisional pass is stored as `provisional_rate_program_id`, and the assessment computed under it is stored as `provisional_assessed_cents`. Neither figure is ever posted to any ledger: `qualification_status` stays `PENDING`, `assessed_interchange_cents` stays at its default of 0, and neither the batch total nor the merchant's lifetime figure moves. The provisional numbers exist so an acquirer can see what a record is likely to cost before the batch closes. Accepting the record advances `record_count` by 1 and advances `SettlementBatch.version` by 1; because the response body is the record rather than the batch, a client adding a second record must re-read the batch to learn its new version.

Submitting the batch advances it `OPEN → SUBMITTED`, after which no further records may be added, and advances `SettlementBatch.version` by 1. A batch whose `record_count` is 0 may be submitted — the empty batch is a legal boundary case, not an error. Batches can be listed and narrowed by `acquirer_id`, `status` and `batch_date`; records can be listed and narrowed by `settlement_batch_id`, `merchant_id`, `provisional_rate_program_id`, `qualified_rate_program_id`, `qualification_status` and `authorization_status`.

### 3.6. Finalization: Definitive Qualification, Downgrade Detection and Assessment

Finalization is where money is actually booked, and it is a `NetworkAdmin` operation on a batch in `SUBMITTED`. Before anything is computed, the owning acquirer's `compliance_status` must be `COMPLIANT`; an acquirer sitting in `NON_COMPLIANT` because of a missed implementation window cannot have its clearing finalized, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ACQUIRER_NON_COMPLIANT`. On success the batch advances `SUBMITTED → FINALIZED`, `finalized_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and `SettlementBatch.version` advances by 1.

Between those two facts sits the work. Every member record whose `qualification_status` is `PENDING` is evaluated, in ascending `created_at` order, under the **definitive** qualification pass. That pass repeats the intake filter of Section 3.5 with two differences. First, the merchant's category is **re-read at finalization time** rather than reused from intake, and the value read is snapshotted onto the record as `mcc_at_qualification` — which may legitimately differ from the category that was in force when the record was created, because a reclassification or an audit may have intervened. Second, the clearing-timing condition that intake omitted is now enforced: a program remains a candidate only if its `category` is `STANDARD`, or the record's `clearing_delay_hours` is at most that program's `max_clearing_delay_hours`. All five intake conditions still apply unchanged, the ranking is the same amortised-cost ordering, and an empty candidate set is again refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_RATE_PROGRAM`. For identical stored inputs the two passes therefore select **different** programs, and produce different cent amounts, whenever the clearing delay exceeds the cheapest candidate's tolerance or the merchant's category changed in the interim — which is precisely why `provisional_assessed_cents` is an estimate and `assessed_interchange_cents` is the truth.

The outcome mapping is binary. If the selected program's `category` is anything other than `STANDARD`, the record advances `PENDING → QUALIFIED`, `qualified_rate_program_id` and `assessed_interchange_cents` and `mcc_at_qualification` are written, `downgrade_reason` stays null, and `authorization_status` is not touched. If the selected program is the `STANDARD` fallback, the record advances `PENDING → DOWNGRADED` instead, the same fields are written with the `STANDARD` program as `qualified_rate_program_id`, a `downgrade_reason` is resolved, and the record contributes one to the merchant's quarterly downgrade count.

**Resolving the downgrade reason** answers a specific question: what did this record fail to earn? The service first builds a reference program — the cheapest non-fallback tier the merchant's category could *ever* reach, ignoring this record's data quality entirely. Candidates for that reference are the rate programs whose `eligible_mcc_codes` contain the merchant's category, whose `category` is not `STANDARD`, and whose `category` is either `CPS_RETAIL` or appears in the category definition's `program_eligibility` set. If no such program exists, the reason is `MCC_INELIGIBLE` and resolution stops there. Otherwise the reference is the candidate with the lowest `base_rate_bps`, and on a tie the one with the earliest `created_at`. Against that reference the service then applies five checks **in this fixed order**, stopping at the first one that fires: if any element of the reference's `requires_data_elements` is missing from the record's `data_elements`, the reason is `MISSING_DATA_ELEMENTS`; otherwise if the record's `clearing_delay_hours` is greater than the reference's `max_clearing_delay_hours`, the reason is `LATE_CLEARING`; otherwise if the reference's `category` is `SMALL_TICKET` and the record's `amount_cents` exceeds the small-ticket ceiling of 1500 cents, the reason is `TICKET_SIZE_INELIGIBLE`; otherwise if the record's `authorization_status` is anything other than `AUTH_APPROVED`, the reason is `AUTH_MISSING`; otherwise the reason is `MCC_INELIGIBLE`. Order matters here: a record that is both missing a required element and cleared late reports `MISSING_DATA_ELEMENTS`, never `LATE_CLEARING`. A `downgrade_reason` is written only for records the definitive pass routes to `STANDARD`; on a qualified record it stays null.

**Computing the assessment** is a four-step pipeline, and each step is run against the selected program — the provisional program at intake, the definitive program at finalization. Step one is the ad-valorem component: multiply the record's `amount_cents` by the program's `base_rate_bps`, divide by ten thousand, round half up to whole cents, and then add the program's `per_item_fee_cents`. The rounding happens *before* the flat fee is added, not after. Step two is the cross-border component: when the record's `cross_border` is true, `cross_border_surcharge_cents` is the larger of a fifteen-cent floor and the record's `amount_cents` multiplied by the cross-border rate of 40 basis points, divided by ten thousand and rounded half up; when `cross_border` is false it is 0. Step three is the currency-conversion component: when the record's `transaction_currency` differs from the batch's `currency_code`, the service gathers the conversion rows whose `base_currency` equals the record's `transaction_currency`, whose `quote_currency` equals the batch's `currency_code`, and whose `effective_from` is at or before the current logical-clock instant at which the evaluation runs. If that pool is empty the whole operation is refused with `ERR_FX_RATE_UNAVAILABLE`. Otherwise the applicable row is the one with the latest `effective_from`, and on a tie the one with the latest `created_at`; `fx_markup_cents` is then the record's `amount_cents` multiplied by that row's `markup_bps`, divided by ten thousand and rounded half up. When the two currencies match, `fx_markup_cents` is 0 and no conversion row is consulted at all.

Step four is a **nested clamp whose order is pinned: cap first, then floor.** Add the step-one figure, the cross-border surcharge and the conversion markup together to obtain a raw amount. Cap that raw amount at the record's `amount_cents` multiplied by the assessment ceiling of 350 basis points, divided by ten thousand and rounded half up — meaning the assessment takes the smaller of the raw amount and that ceiling. Then floor the capped result at 8 cents, meaning take the larger of the capped result and 8. Swapping the two clamps changes the answer whenever the computed cap falls below the eight-cent floor, so the sequence is not negotiable. The result is the record's `assessed_interchange_cents` at finalization, or its `provisional_assessed_cents` at intake, and both `cross_border_surcharge_cents` and `fx_markup_cents` are retained on the record as components *inside* that total rather than as additions on top of it.

Once every member record has been assessed, the batch's `total_assessed_cents` is re-derived as the sum of `assessed_interchange_cents` over the batch's records whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `DOWNGRADED`, traversed in ascending `created_at` order; records still in `PENDING` and records in `REVERSED` are excluded. Each addend is already a whole-cent figure rounded by the assessment pipeline — this is the round-then-sum discipline of Section 2.7, and accumulating unrounded products instead is forbidden.

Finalization then rolls up into the merchants. For every merchant touched by the batch, `lifetime_net_assessed_cents` is re-derived as the sum of `assessed_interchange_cents` over that merchant's records in `QUALIFIED` or `DOWNGRADED` across all batches, and `downgrade_count_current_quarter` is re-derived as the count of that merchant's records whose `qualification_status` is `DOWNGRADED`, whose batch is in `FINALIZED`, and whose batch's `batch_date` falls in the same calendar quarter as the current logical clock. Quarters are the conventional UTC calendar quarters — the first runs 1 January through 31 March, the second 1 April through 30 June, the third 1 July through 30 September and the fourth 1 October through 31 December — and the window is inclusive at both ends, from the first day of the quarter at midnight through the last day at one millisecond before midnight.

That count is a tripwire. Where a merchant's recomputed `downgrade_count_current_quarter` reaches three or more — the comparison is at-or-above three, so exactly three downgrades is enough — and that merchant's `mcc_status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `VERIFIED`, and that merchant has no category audit already sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `FIELDWORK`, the system opens one automatically. The audit is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `trigger_reason` `DOWNGRADE_THRESHOLD`, `auditor_user_id` null, `penalty_assessed_cents` 0, and `mcc_at_audit_open` snapshotting the merchant's current `assigned_mcc`. Simultaneously the merchant's current `mcc_status` is snapshotted into `mcc_status_before_review` and the merchant moves to `UNDER_REVIEW`. Affected merchants are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Finally, each such merchant's live reclassification requests — those in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` — snapshot their status into `prior_status_before_suspension` and move to `SUSPENDED_PENDING_AUDIT`, exactly as Section 3.4 describes. These four hops are causally chained: assessment produces downgrades, downgrades produce the count, the count produces the audit, and the audit produces the suspensions. The automatically created audit is discoverable only by listing audits and narrowing on `merchant_id`, `status` or `trigger_reason`.

### 3.7. Reversals Across Two Independent Dimensions

A reversal names which of a record's two dimensions it acts on. The request carries `reversal_type`, either `AUTH_REVERSAL` or `CLEARING_REVERSAL`, and `reversal_reason` as free text; both are stored on the record.

An **authorization reversal** requires the record's `authorization_status` to be `AUTH_APPROVED` and advances it `AUTH_APPROVED → AUTH_REVERSED`. Nothing monetary moves. `qualification_status`, `assessed_interchange_cents`, the batch's `total_assessed_cents` and the merchant's `lifetime_net_assessed_cents` are all left untouched — the dimensions are isolated, and an implementation that reaches into the ledger here is wrong. A record whose authorization has been reversed remains a member of its batch and remains eligible for finalization, where it will fail the authorization condition of every preferential tier and land on `STANDARD` with reason `AUTH_MISSING`.

The same is true of expiry. When the logical-clock sweep finds a record still in `AUTH_APPROVED` and still in `PENDING` whose age past `auth_timestamp` exceeds 720 hours, it moves the record `AUTH_APPROVED → AUTH_EXPIRED`. Again no ledger moves; the record stays in its batch, and at finalization it takes the same `AUTH_MISSING` path.

A **clearing reversal** is the compensating action against the assessment ledger. It requires the record's `qualification_status` to be `QUALIFIED` or `DOWNGRADED`, and it advances the record to `REVERSED` from either. Three hops follow, in order. First, the record's own money is zeroed: `assessed_interchange_cents`, `cross_border_surcharge_cents` and `fx_markup_cents` all become 0. Second, caused by that zeroing, the owning batch's `total_assessed_cents` is re-derived over its surviving `QUALIFIED` and `DOWNGRADED` records. Third, also caused by the zeroing, the merchant's `lifetime_net_assessed_cents` and `downgrade_count_current_quarter` are both re-derived. Where the reversed record had been `DOWNGRADED`, that third hop necessarily lowers the merchant's quarterly downgrade count by one, because the row no longer counts as downgraded. Throughout, `authorization_status` is not touched: a record can perfectly well sit in `AUTH_APPROVED` and `REVERSED` at the same time.

### 3.8. Incentive Programs: A Finite Capacity Pool, a Waitlist and Performance Scoring

An **incentive program** offers merchants a rate credit in exchange for a volume commitment, and the volume it can promise is finite. Publishing one records `program_name`, 1 to 120 characters and unique across programs; `eligible_mcc_codes`, a non-empty duplicate-free array of four-digit category codes matched by value against a merchant's `assigned_mcc` both at enrollment and again whenever that merchant's category later changes; `total_capacity_cents`, strictly positive and immutable thereafter — this is the pool; `min_commitment_cents`, strictly positive and no greater than the total capacity, which serves double duty as the floor on any single enrollment's commitment *and* as the threshold at which the program closes; `reopen_threshold_cents`, which must be at least the minimum commitment and is the level of free capacity at which a closed program reopens; and `performance_threshold_pct`, a percentage from 0.00 through 1000.00 carried to two decimal places, which is the score an enrollment must reach to stay out of probation. A published program opens with `enrollment_state` `OPEN`, `uncommitted_capacity_cents` equal to `total_capacity_cents`, and `enrolled_count` 0. Programs can be listed and narrowed by `enrollment_state`.

Enrolling is an acquirer action, and the request carries `incentive_program_id`, `merchant_id`, `committed_volume_cents` (strictly positive), the optional `waitlist_requested` flag which defaults to false, and `expected_incentive_program_version`. The merchant must be `VERIFIED` — a merchant still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` is refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_VERIFIED`, one held `UNDER_REVIEW` by an audit is refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_UNDER_CATEGORY_REVIEW`, and one in `MISREPRESENTED` is refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_MISREPRESENTED` — and the merchant's `assigned_mcc` must appear in the program's `eligible_mcc_codes`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_MCC_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_PROGRAM`. The merchant must not already hold an enrollment in `PENDING`, `ENROLLED`, `WAITLISTED` or `PROBATION` on the same program, or the request is a duplicate. And `committed_volume_cents` must be at least the program's `min_commitment_cents`, or the refusal is `ERR_COMMITMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`.

An enrollment's `status` is one of `PENDING`, `ENROLLED`, `WAITLISTED`, `PROBATION`, `TERMINATED_SHORTFALL`, `TERMINATED_INELIGIBLE` or `WITHDRAWN`. `PENDING` exists only inside the creating request and is never observable: the create call resolves it to `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED` before responding. It resolves to `ENROLLED` when the program's `enrollment_state` is `OPEN` and the requested `committed_volume_cents` is at most the program's `uncommitted_capacity_cents`; `enrolled_at` is then stamped with the current logical-clock instant, `uncommitted_capacity_cents` is debited by the committed volume, `enrolled_count` rises by 1, and `IncentiveProgram.version` advances by 1. It resolves to `WAITLISTED` instead when `waitlist_requested` is true and the program either sits in `CLOSED_FULL` or cannot fit the commitment inside its remaining free capacity; a waitlisted row takes **no** capacity debit, leaves `enrolled_at` null, and still advances `IncentiveProgram.version` by 1. Where the program is `CLOSED_FULL` and `waitlist_requested` is not true, the refusal is `ERR_ENROLLMENT_CLOSED`; where the program is `OPEN` but the commitment exceeds the free capacity and `waitlist_requested` is not true, the refusal is `ERR_INCENTIVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. That capacity comparison is skipped entirely when the row is being parked on the waitlist. Either way the scoring fields open at rest: `achieved_volume_cents`, `gross_assessed_volume_cents`, `incentive_credit_cents`, `net_assessed_volume_cents` and `probation_count` are all 0, `performance_score_pct` is 0.00, and `last_evaluated_basis`, `tier_code` and `withdrawal_reason` are all null.

The program's own state follows the pool mechanically. After any capacity debit — a fresh enrollment or a waitlist promotion — the program moves `OPEN → CLOSED_FULL` if the remaining `uncommitted_capacity_cents` has fallen strictly below `min_commitment_cents`, on the reasoning that a pool too small to admit even a minimum commitment is functionally full. After any capacity release — a withdrawal, a shortfall termination or an ineligibility termination — a program sitting in `CLOSED_FULL` moves back to `OPEN` as soon as `uncommitted_capacity_cents` has reached or exceeded `reopen_threshold_cents`. That reopening carries a cascade: the service examines the **single** waitlisted enrollment of the program with the smallest `created_at` and promotes it `WAITLISTED → ENROLLED` if and only if its `committed_volume_cents` fits inside the current `uncommitted_capacity_cents`, stamping `enrolled_at`, debiting the pool and raising `enrolled_count`. This is strict head-of-line service, not best-fit: if the earliest waitlisted row does not fit, **no** promotion happens at all and no later row is considered, even if a later row would have fitted. Only one row is ever promoted per release.

Scoring an enrollment is a `NetworkAdmin` operation, and the request carries a single input, `basis`, which is either `TRAILING` or `PROJECTED`; the value used is recorded on the enrollment as `last_evaluated_basis`. Both variants share the same measurement base: the merchant's transaction records whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `DOWNGRADED`, whose settlement batch is in `FINALIZED`, and whose batch's `batch_date` falls from the calendar date of the enrollment's `enrolled_at` through the calendar date of the current logical clock, inclusive at both ends. Records still `PENDING`, records that have been `REVERSED`, and records sitting in batches that are still `OPEN` or merely `SUBMITTED` are all excluded. Those rows are then partitioned by calendar quarter relative to the quarter containing the current logical clock: the **prior** aggregate is the sum of `amount_cents` over rows whose batch quarter falls strictly before the current quarter, and the **current-quarter** aggregate is the sum of `amount_cents` over rows whose batch quarter is the current one.

Under the `TRAILING` basis, `achieved_volume_cents` is the prior aggregate alone — volume booked in the current, still-incomplete quarter is discarded entirely, so only completed quarters count. Under the `PROJECTED` basis, the current partial quarter is scaled up to a whole quarter and added: take the number of calendar days in the current quarter, which is 90, 91 or 92 depending on which quarter it is; take the number of whole days elapsed from the first day of that quarter through the current logical clock's date, counting both ends, so that the first day of a quarter yields one elapsed day rather than zero; multiply the current-quarter aggregate by the days in the quarter, divide by the elapsed days, round half up to whole cents, and add that projection to the prior aggregate. For identical stored rows the two bases therefore produce different `achieved_volume_cents` and different scores, and can send the same enrollment to probation under `TRAILING` while leaving it untouched under `PROJECTED`.

`performance_score_pct` is then the achieved volume multiplied by one hundred, divided by the enrollment's `committed_volume_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Routing against the program's `performance_threshold_pct` uses at-or-above as the passing comparison, so equality passes. An `ENROLLED` enrollment whose score is strictly below the threshold moves `ENROLLED → PROBATION` and its `probation_count` rises by 1; its capacity stays debited, because probation sits inside the committed set and `uncommitted_capacity_cents` does not change. An `ENROLLED` enrollment whose score reaches the threshold stays `ENROLLED` with no transition. A `PROBATION` enrollment whose score reaches the threshold recovers `PROBATION → ENROLLED`, and `probation_count` is **not** decremented — it is a lifetime counter of how many times the enrollment has fallen short. A `PROBATION` enrollment whose score is again strictly below the threshold is terminated: it moves `PROBATION → TERMINATED_SHORTFALL`, its `committed_volume_cents` is credited back to `uncommitted_capacity_cents`, `enrolled_count` falls by 1, and the reopen-and-promote cascade above may follow. An enrollment may oscillate between `ENROLLED` and `PROBATION` any number of times; only a shortfall while already on probation ends it.

The same scoring request also resolves the enrollment's volume tier and credit, and the evaluation order here is pinned because the relationship looks circular — the tier depends on assessed volume, and the discount the tier grants reduces assessed volume. First, `gross_assessed_volume_cents` is the sum of `assessed_interchange_cents` over the merchant's `QUALIFIED` and `DOWNGRADED` records in `FINALIZED` batches whose `batch_date` falls from the date of `enrolled_at` through the date of the current logical clock inclusive. Note that this gross figure spans the **full** measurement window with no quarter partitioning and no dependence on `basis` — unlike the score above, it is the same under both bases. Second, `tier_code` is resolved from that gross figure and from nothing else: `TIER_3` when the gross reaches 50000000 cents, otherwise `TIER_2` when it reaches 20000000 cents, otherwise `TIER_1` when it reaches 5000000 cents, otherwise `TIER_0`. The breakpoints are inclusive lower bounds. Third, `incentive_credit_cents` is the gross multiplied by the tier's discount in basis points — 0 for `TIER_0`, 25 for `TIER_1`, 60 for `TIER_2` and 100 for `TIER_3` — divided by ten thousand and rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, `net_assessed_volume_cents` is the gross minus the credit, as exact integer subtraction. There is no second pass and no fixed-point iteration: the tier is **never** recomputed from the net figure. Recomputing it would produce a different and wrong credit whenever the discount pushes the net below the tier's lower breakpoint — a gross of 20000005 resolves to `TIER_2` and a credit of 120000, leaving a net of 19880005, and a second pass over that net would fall back to `TIER_1` and a credit of 50000.

Withdrawal is voluntary and is available from `ENROLLED`, `PROBATION` or `WAITLISTED`, each moving to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. The supplied `withdrawal_reason` is stored. Where the enrollment held capacity — that is, from `ENROLLED` or `PROBATION` — its `committed_volume_cents` is credited back to `uncommitted_capacity_cents`, `enrolled_count` falls by 1, and the reopen-and-promote cascade may follow. Withdrawing from `WAITLISTED` credits nothing, because a waitlisted row never debited the pool in the first place, and the capacity invariant of Section 2.10 must still hold exactly afterwards. Enrollments are listed and narrowed by `incentive_program_id`, `merchant_id` and `status`.

There is one further way an enrollment ends, and the merchant does not choose it. Whenever a merchant's `assigned_mcc` changes — by a sustained audit finding or by an approved reclassification — every one of that merchant's enrollments in `ENROLLED` or `PROBATION` whose program's `eligible_mcc_codes` no longer contains the new code moves to `TERMINATED_INELIGIBLE`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Each such termination credits `committed_volume_cents` back to `uncommitted_capacity_cents` and lowers `enrolled_count` by 1; the program then moves `CLOSED_FULL → OPEN` if the credited capacity reaches `reopen_threshold_cents`; and the head-of-line waitlisted enrollment is promoted if it fits. `TERMINATED_INELIGIBLE`, `TERMINATED_SHORTFALL` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

### 3.9. Misrepresentation Investigation and Merchant Category Audits

A **merchant category audit** is the network's instrument for testing whether a merchant is trading under the category it claims. Opening one is a `ComplianceAuditor` action and carries `merchant_id` plus `trigger_reason`. The reason vocabulary is `DOWNGRADE_THRESHOLD`, `RECLASS_SUSPICION`, `PERIODIC` and `COMPLAINT`, but only the latter three are manually admissible: `DOWNGRADE_THRESHOLD` is reserved for the audit the finalization cascade of Section 3.6 opens by itself, and supplying it on a manual creation is an out-of-domain enum value refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A merchant may only be put under audit while its `mcc_status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `VERIFIED`; a merchant already `UNDER_REVIEW` or sitting in the terminal `MISREPRESENTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Opening the audit does four things at once. The audit row is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `auditor_user_id`, `fieldwork_start`, `fieldwork_end`, `correct_mcc`, `findings_note` and `closed_by_user_id` all null and `penalty_assessed_cents` 0, and `mcc_at_audit_open` snapshots the merchant's `assigned_mcc` as it stands at that moment. The merchant's current `mcc_status` is snapshotted into `mcc_status_before_review` — the field holds `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED` or null and nothing else — and the merchant then moves to `UNDER_REVIEW`, from either `VERIFIED` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`. Every live reclassification request on that merchant is frozen into `SUSPENDED_PENDING_AUDIT` with its prior status snapshotted, as Section 3.4 describes, with the affected requests processed in ascending `created_at` order. And `Merchant.version` advances by 1.

Fieldwork is the fork point. Beginning fieldwork advances the audit `SCHEDULED → FIELDWORK`, records the acting operator as `auditor_user_id`, and stores the declared `fieldwork_start` and `fieldwork_end`. The two instants must satisfy the request-shape rule that the start is at or before the end, and they must additionally satisfy the stricter transition precondition that `fieldwork_start` is **strictly** earlier than `fieldwork_end` — so two identical instants pass the shape check and are then refused by the transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. From `FIELDWORK` exactly one of two mutually exclusive branches may be taken, and both are reserved to the operator recorded as `auditor_user_id`; another auditor attempting either branch is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Branch A — sustaining the findings.** The auditor supplies `correct_mcc`, the category they determined to be the true one, and `findings_note`. The supplied code must name an existing category definition, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_MCC_NOT_DEFINED`. The audit advances `FIELDWORK → FINDINGS_SUSTAINED` and a penalty is assessed: `penalty_assessed_cents` is the larger of a fifty-dollar minimum of 5000 cents and the merchant's `lifetime_net_assessed_cents` multiplied by the misrepresentation penalty rate of 2500 basis points — a quarter of the ledger — divided by ten thousand and rounded half up to whole cents. Crucially the merchant's ledger is read **before** any part of the cascade runs, so the penalty reflects the volume actually booked under the misrepresented category rather than anything the restatement produces. Then the cascade fires. Hop one rewrites the merchant: `assigned_mcc` becomes the audited `correct_mcc` and `mcc_status` advances `UNDER_REVIEW → MISREPRESENTED`, which is terminal. Hop two, caused by hop one rather than by the audit call itself, terminates the merchant's now-ineligible incentive enrollments exactly as Section 3.8 describes — releasing their committed capacity, which can reopen a `CLOSED_FULL` program, which can promote that program's head-of-line waitlisted enrollment. Separately, every reclassification request on the merchant sitting in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_AUDIT` moves to `DENIED` with `decision_note` set to `auto-denied by sustained category audit`. `Merchant.version` advances by 1, and so does the `version` of any incentive program whose capacity moved.

**Branch B — clearing the findings.** The auditor supplies only `findings_note`. The audit advances `FIELDWORK → FINDINGS_CLEARED`, `penalty_assessed_cents` stays at 0 and `correct_mcc` stays null. The merchant's `mcc_status` is restored verbatim from `mcc_status_before_review` and that snapshot field is then set back to null: a merchant whose snapshot held `VERIFIED` returns `UNDER_REVIEW → VERIFIED`, and a merchant whose snapshot held `PENDING_VERIFICATION` returns `UNDER_REVIEW → PENDING_VERIFICATION`. The merchant's `assigned_mcc` is left completely untouched, no money moves, and **no** incentive enrollment is touched — the asymmetry with branch A is deliberate and total. The merchant's suspended reclassification requests are each restored verbatim to the status held in `prior_status_before_suspension`, which is then cleared to null. `Merchant.version` advances by 1.

Closing is the join point where the two branches meet, and it may be taken from either `FINDINGS_SUSTAINED` or `FINDINGS_CLEARED`, in both cases advancing the audit to the terminal `CLOSED`. The acting operator is recorded as `closed_by_user_id` and must be a different `X-User-ID` from the `auditor_user_id` who reached the finding; the auditor who worked the case may not ratify their own conclusion, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Closing freezes `penalty_assessed_cents` at whatever the sustained branch assessed, and preserves it unchanged thereafter. Closing an audit that came out of the cleared branch also makes the merchant auditable again — with no audit of that merchant remaining in `SCHEDULED` or `FIELDWORK`, a fresh audit may be opened, whether by an auditor or by the downgrade tripwire.
