# Annual Bonus Pool & Profit Sharing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every year the corporate incentive plan team has to answer one deceptively simple question: how much money does the company owe its people for the year that just closed, and who gets which share of it? Answering it well requires a chain of decisions that no single team owns. Plan designers fix the performance window and the weighting between company, business-unit and individual performance. Business unit leaders score their own units and later spend their unit's share award by award. A compensation committee certifies the audited performance figures, votes the money into existence, and rules on every discretionary exception. HR records the transfers, leaves and terminations that change what an individual is owed. Finance accrues a best guess against the profit-and-loss statement month by month and trues it up in cash at settlement. Today this work is done in spreadsheets that disagree with each other, and the disagreements are only discovered when an employee asks why their number changed.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. Its central design commitment is that the bonus pool is a **fixed quantity of money, minted once**. A funding formula over certified metrics produces a single multiplier; that multiplier is applied to each business unit's eligible target to produce a frozen per-unit pool; managers then distribute inside that frozen pool under published guidelines, and the only way to exceed it is a separately budgeted discretionary authority that the committee must approve case by case. Everything downstream — calibration review, the plan lock, payout, accrual and the year-end true-up — reads from that minted number rather than recomputing it.

The second commitment is auditability. Money is held in integer cents, every rounding order is pinned rather than left to the implementation, compensating actions restore the exact figures they reverse, and every governance verdict records the acting user so that segregation-of-duties rules can be enforced mechanically. Where two defensible readings of a business rule exist — round each unit's pool separately or round the company total once; treat a retiree who left in November as employed on the payment date or not — this specification names the reading that applies and, where the divergence is financially interesting, requires the system to record the size of the divergence for the auditors.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint in the system. Where a rule below is qualified by domain narrative, the narrative in §3 supplies the qualification.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the six roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six recognized roles are `PlanAdmin`, `CompensationCommittee`, `BusinessUnitManager`, `HRBusinessPartner`, `FinanceController` and `Employee`.

- `PlanAdmin` — the corporate incentive plan team. Designs plans, the metric set, the job-level target grid, the participant population and the unit pools; runs target assignment; opens and adjudicates calibration; may file discretionary adjustments. When `X-User-Role` is `PlanAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on a discretionary adjustment the operator files) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `CompensationCommittee` — the governance body. Certifies metrics and unit scorecards, funds the pool, rules on discretionary adjustments and locks the plan. When `X-User-Role` is `CompensationCommittee`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `certified_by_user_id`, `locked_by_user_id`, `scorecard_certified_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` as the relevant transition dictates, and compares it only against other recorded operator identifiers for the segregation-of-duties rules below.
- `BusinessUnitManager` — a business unit leader. When `X-User-Role` is `BusinessUnitManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BusinessUnit`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `BusinessUnit` compare the resource's own business-unit reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, scorecard and calibration submissions require `X-User-ID` to equal the pool's `business_unit_id`, and allocation writes require `X-User-ID` to equal the award's `business_unit_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNERSHIP` (HTTP 403).
- `HRBusinessPartner` — HR operations. Records eligibility events, may file discretionary adjustments, and reviews and resolves disputes. When `X-User-Role` is `HRBusinessPartner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `submitted_by_user_id`, `assigned_by_user_id` or `resolved_by_user_id` where a transition writes it, and does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- `FinanceController` — accounting. Opens, posts and reverses accrual periods, executes the payout and books the settlement true-up. When `X-User-Role` is `FinanceController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`.
- `Employee` — a plan participant. When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant`; ownership checks compare the resource's participant reference to `X-User-ID`. Filing a dispute requires `X-User-ID` to equal the disputed award's `participant_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNERSHIP`; the `participant_id` supplied in the request body is not part of the ownership check but must agree with the award's own `participant_id`, and a body value that disagrees is reported later in the ladder as `ERR_PLAN_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

Ownership is checked only on the writes named above. The collection and detail reads that authorize `BusinessUnitManager` or `Employee` in §4 are not ownership-filtered: any such caller may read any plan, participant, pool, award or dispute, and list responses are never narrowed to the caller's own unit or participant record.

Two same-user prohibitions sit on top of the role split, both reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A committee member may not decide a discretionary adjustment they themselves submitted — the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the adjustment's `submitted_by_user_id`. And a committee member may not lock a plan they certified — the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the plan's `certified_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty randomly drawn lowercase letters and digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request to an unrecognised route, or with a method the route does not offer, or with a body that cannot be parsed at all, is answered in this same envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and the transport's own status — 404, 405 and 400 respectively.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1 and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0; a negative value is invalid. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned is still short of `total_count`.

Unless a collection says otherwise, records are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two collections deviate: accrual periods are returned in ascending `period_start` order, and eligibility events are returned in ascending `effective_date` order with `created_at` ascending breaking ties. Because `created_at` is only millisecond-precise, records that share a timestamp are ordered by the sequence in which the server stored them, in every collection and in every ordering rule that falls back to `created_at`.

Collections also accept optional equality filters, and any combination of the filters offered by a collection may be supplied together. Bonus plans filter on `plan_year` and `status`. Company metrics filter on `plan_id` and `status`. Job-level targets filter on `plan_id` and `job_level`. Business units filter on `unit_code`. Participants filter on `plan_id`, `business_unit_id`, `current_business_unit_id`, `employment_status` and `job_level`. Eligibility events filter on `participant_id`, `new_business_unit_id`, `supersedes_event_id`, `event_type` and `superseded`. Business-unit pools filter on `plan_id`, `business_unit_id`, `calibration_status` and `distribution_status`. Participant awards filter on `plan_id`, `participant_id`, `business_unit_id`, `bu_pool_id`, `allocation_status`, `payment_status` and `eligibility_status`. Discretionary adjustments filter on `plan_id`, `award_id` and `status`. Accrual periods filter on `plan_id` and `status`. Payout runs filter on `plan_id` and `status`. Disputes filter on `plan_id`, `award_id`, `participant_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when it was first stored. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every subsequent write, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every write that changes any stored field — including writes that only change derived roll-up figures such as a pool's `allocated_cents` or a plan's `governance_posture`.

`EligibilityEvent` is the one exception. Eligibility events are an append-only record: an event is never edited, and a mistake is corrected by appending a new event that names the mistaken one. Consequently an eligibility event carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Every other resource in the system — bonus plans, company metrics, business units, job-level targets, participants, business-unit pools, participant awards, discretionary adjustments, accrual periods, payout runs and disputes — carries all four fields.

Every date comparison in this domain reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time: whether the payment date has been reached, which day an accrual period is being posted on, and the date a payout run stamps into `payment_date_used`. Before anything sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. A test-driving capability, restricted to `PlanAdmin` and `FinanceController`, sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, after which every subsequent request reads the new value; `current_utc` is any ISO 8601 timestamp (a value carrying no zone offset is read as UTC), and the response carries an empty `data` object rather than any resource.

### 2.5. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All money is held and returned as an integer number of cents; no monetary field is ever a decimal. Wherever a monetary result must be rounded, the rounding is to whole cents using the round-half-up convention: a fractional remainder of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Intermediate terms inside a single formula are kept at full precision and only the stated result is rounded, unless the formula explicitly pins an earlier rounding step (§2.6).

Percentage and ratio fields have fixed precision. Achievement percentages, weights and burden rates — `company_achievement_pct`, `bu_weighted_achievement_pct`, `blended_achievement_pct`, `achievement_pct`, `bu_metric_achievement_pct`, `forecast_company_achievement_pct`, `company_weight_pct`, `business_unit_weight_pct`, `individual_weight_pct`, `weight_pct`, `discretionary_budget_pct`, `target_bonus_pct`, `burden_rate_pct` and `utilization_pct` — carry two decimal places and are rounded half up. Two fields carry four decimal places, also rounded half up: a plan's `funding_multiplier` and an award's `allocation_ratio`. Guideline ratio bounds `guideline_min_ratio` and `guideline_max_ratio` carry two decimal places. A percentage or ratio supplied in a request body with more precision than its field carries is not refused: it is rounded half up to the field's precision on the way in, and every range, relationship and uniqueness check then reads that already-rounded value. All of these fields are serialized as JSON numbers rather than strings, so a two-decimal value that happens to be whole may be rendered without its trailing zeros.

`utilization_pct` is display-only. Every gate in this system that decides whether a pool is over-allocated or under-utilized compares integer cents, never the rounded percentage.

### 2.6. Two Pinned Rounding Orders

Two places in the funding chain admit two arithmetically defensible readings, and both are pinned.

The first is **proration**: a participant's year is cut into segments, each with its own salary and job level, and each segment's target is rounded to whole cents *before* the segments are summed. Summing un-rounded segment amounts and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule; the two readings can differ by as much as one cent less than the number of segments.

The second is **pool funding**: each business unit's pool is rounded to whole cents separately, and the plan's authoritative `funded_pool_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-pool figures. Multiplying the plan's whole `total_target_cents` by the multiplier and rounding once is *not* the authoritative total — but because auditors ask, the system computes that competing figure anyway and stores the signed difference in `aggregate_rounding_delta_cents`: the once-rounded product of `total_target_cents` and `funding_multiplier`, minus `funded_pool_cents`. The delta is non-zero whenever the per-pool remainders fail to cancel, and it is an audit record only — no gate reads it.

### 2.7. The Roll-Up Chain: Award, Pool, Plan

The money model is three-tiered, and consistency is maintained immediately rather than by a later sweep. An individual award holds a target and an allocation; the business-unit pool aggregates the awards assigned to it; the plan aggregates the pools. Whenever an award's target or money changes — because targets were assigned, because an eligibility event re-prorated it, because a manager allocated or retracted, or because a discretionary adjustment was approved or reversed — the owning pool's `eligible_target_cents`, `eligible_headcount`, `max_top_tier_count`, `allocated_cents`, `available_cents`, `utilization_pct`, `top_tier_award_count` and `distribution_status` are recomputed in the same request, and the plan's `over_allocated_pool_count`, `under_utilized_pool_count` and `governance_posture` are recomputed from the resulting pool states. Both parent writes refresh `updated_at` and increment `version` on the entity they touch — but only when the recomputation actually moved one of that entity's stored fields; a roll-up that lands back on the figures already held leaves the parent's `version` and `updated_at` untouched. The same rule governs the cascaded children: a re-prorated award, a re-derived participant and a recomputed pool each move their own `version` only if a stored field changed. Three writes bump unconditionally: a successful allocation and a successful retraction always increment both the award's and the owning pool's `version`, and a target-assignment run always increments the plan's.

The cascade runs in the other direction too. Committee and plan-admin verdicts that change a plan's state push that change down into every pool of the plan and, through the pools, into every award — a returned certification decertifies scorecards, a funding vote funds pools, a lock freezes pools and awards. Where a child transition invalidates a parent's state, the parent moves back on its own: returning a single pool to reallocation while the plan sits in calibration review bubbles the plan back to `ALLOCATION_OPEN` without any separate request against the plan. §3 states each cascade at the point in the workflow where it fires.

### 2.8. Version Preconditions on Pool and Plan Writes

Three write operations act against a balance the client has already read, and each requires the client to present the `version` of the parent resource that balance was computed from. These are not optional inputs and there is no "unversioned" form of the request.

Allocating money to an award (`POST /participant-awards/{awardId}/propose-allocation`) requires `expected_pool_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the award's `BusinessUnitPool`. If it does not match the pool's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rejected request would typically have been perfectly legal on its own terms — a different award, an amount inside the guidelines — so a state precondition cannot express the conflict; what the check prevents is a manager spending against a remaining balance that no longer exists because another allocation has already been recorded against the same pool.

Retracting an allocation (`POST /participant-awards/{awardId}/retract-allocation`) likewise requires `expected_pool_version` carrying the client's last observed pool `version`, and a mismatch with the pool's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A retraction computed against a superseded pool balance is refused rather than silently re-derived.

Creating a discretionary adjustment (`POST /discretionary-adjustments`) requires `expected_plan_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `BonusPlan` whose `discretionary_budget_cents` and `discretionary_used_cents` pair the amount was sized from. If it does not match the plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — once any other adjustment has been approved, the remaining-budget arithmetic the new draw was sized from is stale.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.9. Identifier Resolution Order

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names nothing decides the error. The identifiers in use are `{planId}` for a bonus plan (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`), `{metricId}` for a company metric (`ERR_METRIC_NOT_FOUND`), `{unitId}` for a business unit (`ERR_BUSINESS_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`), `{targetId}` for a job-level target row (`ERR_JOB_LEVEL_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`), `{participantId}` for a participant (`ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`), `{eventId}` for an eligibility event (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`), `{poolId}` for a business-unit pool (`ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`), `{awardId}` for a participant award (`ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`), `{adjustmentId}` for a discretionary adjustment (`ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_FOUND`), `{periodId}` for an accrual period (`ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`), `{payoutRunId}` for a payout run (`ERR_PAYOUT_RUN_NOT_FOUND`) and `{disputeId}` for a dispute (`ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`). All are HTTP 404.

References supplied in a request body are resolved after the body has been checked for shape, and they are resolved in the order the fields are declared on the resource being written: `plan_id` first, then `participant_id`, then `business_unit_id` or `new_business_unit_id`, then `bu_pool_id`, then `award_id`, then `supersedes_event_id`. Each unresolved reference raises its own not-found error — `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUSINESS_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` respectively.

### 2.10. The Validation Ladder

Requests routinely violate several rules at once. The ladder below is total: every check has a distinct position, and a request that fails more than one check returns the error of the highest-positioned (earliest) check it fails. An incentive plan must refuse an unauthorized or out-of-sequence action before it ever reports a budget number, so identity comes first and money comes last.

1. **Headers.** Both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming one of the six roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Path identifiers**, resolved left to right as described in §2.9.
4. **Request shape.** Every required input field present, every enum value inside its declared set, every date a valid ISO 8601 `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, every cents field an integer, pagination inside its band, and every conditionally-required field present: `leave_end_date` for a `LEAVE` event, `termination_reason` for a `TERMINATION` event, `new_job_level` together with `new_base_salary_cents` and `new_business_unit_id` for a `TRANSFER` event, and `adjusted_amount_cents` for a dispute resolution of `ADJUST`. Any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Body references**, resolved in declaration order as described in §2.9.
6. **Field-relationship and uniqueness rules** on creation, in this order: the three plan weights summing to exactly 100.00 (`ERR_PLAN_WEIGHTS_INVALID`), the plan period and payment date (`ERR_PLAN_PERIOD_INVALID`), the strictly increasing metric band (`ERR_METRIC_BAND_INVALID`), the guideline ratio band (`ERR_GUIDELINE_BAND_INVALID`), and only then the uniqueness constraints (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`).
7. **Resource ownership** for the two entity-backed roles — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNERSHIP`.
8. **Lifecycle state**, in this order: the plan being in the state its own requested transition needs (`ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`), setup-phase writes needing a plan in `DRAFT` or `TARGETS_SET` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_SETUP`), allocation-phase writes needing the allocation states (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_ALLOCATION`), a metric actual not already filed (`ERR_METRIC_ACTUAL_ALREADY_FILED`), the pool being in the state its requested transition needs (`ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`), the pool being open for money movement (`ERR_POOL_NOT_ALLOCATING`), the award's allocation state permitting the operation (`ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`), the adjustment's state permitting a decision or a reversal (`ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_PENDING`), the accrual period's state permitting a post or a reverse (`ERR_ACCRUAL_ALREADY_POSTED`), the plan not having reached settlement when accruing (`ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_SETTLED`), the payout run's state permitting execution or settlement and only one run existing per plan (`ERR_PAYOUT_ALREADY_EXECUTED`), the plan being locked to execute and paid out to settle (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_LOCKED`), the dispute's state permitting assignment or resolution (`ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`), and a dispute being filed only in the permitted plan states (`ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_ALLOWED_YET`).
9. **Version preconditions** — `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`, as specified in §2.8.
10. **Governance safety**, in this order: segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), the documentation requirement on large adjustments (`ERR_ADJUSTMENT_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`), and the governance posture gate on the lock (`ERR_GOVERNANCE_POSTURE_BREACHED`).
11. **Temporal integrity**, in this order: an event's `effective_date` inside the plan period (`ERR_EVENT_DATE_OUTSIDE_PLAN_PERIOD`), an event not preceding the hire date and a leave not ending before it starts (`ERR_EVENT_DATE_BEFORE_HIRE`), leave intervals not overlapping (`ERR_LEAVE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`), no event after a live termination (`ERR_PARTICIPANT_ALREADY_TERMINATED`), accrual periods not overlapping (`ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), accrual periods tiling contiguously and earlier periods being resolved before a later one posts (`ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_GAP`), accrual reversal being last-in-first-out (`ERR_ACCRUAL_REVERSAL_OUT_OF_ORDER`), and the payment date having been reached (`ERR_PAYMENT_DATE_NOT_REACHED`).
12. **Referential integrity**, in this order: plan-scope agreement among linked resources (`ERR_PLAN_SCOPE_MISMATCH`), a job-level target existing for every job level held (`ERR_JOB_LEVEL_TARGET_MISSING`), a pool existing for every resolved business unit (`ERR_POOL_MISSING_FOR_UNIT`), a valid supersession target (`ERR_SUPERSEDES_EVENT_INVALID`), complete metric weights (`ERR_METRIC_WEIGHTS_INVALID`), all metric actuals filed (`ERR_METRICS_NOT_ALL_ACTUALIZED`), all scorecards certified (`ERR_SCORECARDS_NOT_ALL_CERTIFIED`), a positive plan target (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS`), pool calibration completeness (`ERR_POOL_CALIBRATION_PENDING`) and no deferred awards at settlement (`ERR_DEFERRED_AWARDS_PENDING`).
13. **Distribution constraints**, in this order: a positive funding multiplier (`ERR_FUNDING_MULTIPLIER_ZERO`), award eligibility (`ERR_AWARD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`), the guideline ratio band (`ERR_GUIDELINE_RATIO_BREACH`), the top-tier concentration cap (`ERR_TOP_TIER_CONCENTRATION`), the pool being inside its distribution guidelines (`ERR_POOL_NOT_WITHIN_GUIDELINES`), retraction being blocked by an approved adjustment (`ERR_AWARD_HAS_APPROVED_ADJUSTMENT`), an award not being driven negative (`ERR_NEGATIVE_AWARD_NOT_ALLOWED`) and the dispute adjustment ceiling (`ERR_DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`).
14. **Fiscal checks**, last, in this order: the frozen per-award adjustment cap (`ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_PER_AWARD_LIMIT`), the pool's remaining money (`ERR_POOL_FUNDS_EXCEEDED`) and the plan's discretionary budget (`ERR_DISCRETIONARY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`).

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities and Compensating Actions

Three quantities are conserved exactly — with integer equality and no tolerance — at every observable moment, and the system's three compensating actions are specified as exact inverses rather than as approximate reversals.

For every pool, `funded_cents` always equals `allocated_cents` plus `available_cents`. After a manager allocates to an award and then retracts that same allocation, the pool's `allocated_cents` and `available_cents` are back at the values they held before the allocation.

For every plan, `accrued_base_cents` always equals the sum of `period_accrual_cents` over the plan's accrual periods that are currently `POSTED`, and `accrued_total_cents` always equals the sum of `total_period_cents` over the same set. Reversing a posted period subtracts back exactly what posting added.

For every plan, `discretionary_used_cents` always equals the sum of the absolute values of `adjustment_cents` over the plan's discretionary adjustments that are currently `APPROVED`. After an adjustment is approved and then reversed, the award's `adjustment_total_cents` and `final_award_cents`, the pool's `allocated_cents`, `available_cents` and `distribution_status`, and the plan's `governance_posture` are all back at their pre-approval values.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Plan Design: The Performance Window, the Funding Weights and the Target Grid

A plan year begins with a `PlanAdmin` creating a **bonus plan**, which is the root of everything that follows and which starts life in `DRAFT`. The design payload names the year with `plan_year` (a calendar year between 1900 and 2999) and labels the plan with `plan_name`; the pair must be unique across the system, and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The performance window is given as `plan_period_start`, the first day of the period inclusive, and `plan_period_end`, which is **exclusive** — the window is half-open, and `plan_period_end` must fall exactly 365 days after `plan_period_start`. `payment_date` is the scheduled payout date and must be the same day as `plan_period_end` or later. A window of the wrong length, or a payment date before the end of the window, is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_PERIOD_INVALID`.

The funding formula's shape is fixed at design time by three weights: `company_weight_pct`, `business_unit_weight_pct` and `individual_weight_pct`, each a two-decimal percentage that is not individually range-checked — only their total is validated, so a weight outside the 0-to-100 band is accepted when the other two compensate for it. They must sum to exactly 100.00 — not 99.99, not 100.01 — and any other total is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_WEIGHTS_INVALID`. The individual component is deliberately not a variable at funding time: it always funds at exactly 100.00 achievement, because individual differentiation is what managers do later inside the pool. Two further design choices may be supplied and both have defaults. `discretionary_budget_pct`, the share of the funded pool held back for discretionary exceptions, lies between 0 and 25 with two decimals and defaults to 10.00. `termination_treatment_policy` selects which payout variant runs at execution and is either `STRICT_PAYMENT_DATE` or `GOOD_LEAVER_PRORATION`, defaulting to `STRICT_PAYMENT_DATE`. On creation, `status` is `DRAFT`, `governance_posture` is `COMPLIANT`, and every achievement, multiplier and money field is initialised to zero: `company_achievement_pct`, `bu_weighted_achievement_pct` and `blended_achievement_pct` at 0.00, `funding_multiplier` at 0.0000, and `total_target_cents`, `funded_pool_cents`, `aggregate_rounding_delta_cents`, `discretionary_budget_cents`, `discretionary_used_cents`, `accrued_base_cents`, `accrued_total_cents`, `locked_total_cents`, `paid_total_cents`, `forfeited_total_cents`, `dispute_adjustment_cents`, `settlement_burden_cents`, `true_up_cents`, `over_allocated_pool_count` and `under_utilized_pool_count` all at 0. `certified_by_user_id` and `locked_by_user_id` are null until the committee acts.

**Business units** are registered independently of any plan, each with a globally unique `unit_code` and a display `name`; a duplicate code is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. A business unit's `id` is not merely a foreign key — it is the identity a `BusinessUnitManager` acts as, which is why unit registration is a `PlanAdmin` responsibility.

The **job-level target grid** translates job levels into money. Each row names a `job_level` code such as L3 or L5, unique within its `plan_id`, and carries `target_bonus_pct`, the target bonus as a percentage of annual base salary at that level, between 0.01 and 200 with two decimals. Each row also publishes the distribution guideline band that constrains managers later: `guideline_min_ratio` is the smallest allowed ratio of allocation to prorated target for a non-zero allocation at that level, and `guideline_max_ratio` is the largest. Both lie between 0 and 5 with two decimals, `guideline_max_ratio` must be strictly greater than `guideline_min_ratio`, and a band that fails either condition is rejected with `ERR_GUIDELINE_BAND_INVALID`.

**Company metrics** define the company component. Each metric carries a `metric_code` unique within its `plan_id` and a `weight_pct` between 0.01 and 100 inclusive with two decimals, expressing its share inside the company component. Its scoring band is three numbers: `threshold_value`, the value that scores exactly 50.00 achievement; `target_value`, the value that scores exactly 100.00; and `max_value`, the value at which achievement is capped at 150.00. The band must be strictly increasing — `threshold_value` below `target_value` below `max_value` — and anything else is `ERR_METRIC_BAND_INVALID`. A new metric is stored with `status` `DRAFT` and `achievement_pct` 0.00; `actual_value` and `source_note` are both null until the audited actual is filed, and a filing that omits `source_note` leaves it null rather than blanking it. Metric definition, target-grid definition, participant enrolment and pool creation are all setup-phase writes: they require the plan to be in `DRAFT` or `TARGETS_SET`, and are otherwise rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_SETUP`.

Each business unit that will spend money in a plan needs a **business-unit pool**, created by a `PlanAdmin` with the pool's `plan_id` and `business_unit_id`; at most one pool may exist per plan-and-unit pair, and a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The creation payload may pre-seed `bu_metric_achievement_pct` (the unit's scorecard achievement, between 0 and 200 with two decimals) and `scorecard_narrative`, though the manager normally supplies both later; omitted, they default to 0.00 and null respectively. A new pool starts with `calibration_status` `DRAFT` and `distribution_status` `NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS`, with `eligible_target_cents`, `eligible_headcount`, `funded_cents`, `allocated_cents`, `available_cents`, `min_utilization_cents`, `top_tier_award_count`, `max_top_tier_count` and `calibration_returned_count` at 0, `utilization_pct` at 0.00, and `scorecard_certified_by_user_id` null.

### 3.2. The Participant Population and the Append-Only Eligibility Record

A `PlanAdmin` enrols each eligible employee as a **participant** of a plan, supplying `plan_id`, the employee's home `business_unit_id`, an `employee_code` unique within the plan, the `full_name` used in award communication, the `job_level` held at plan entry, `base_salary_cents` (the annual base salary in cents, at least 1) and `hire_date`, which is the first day the employee can be credited. Neither the `job_level` nor the home business unit is validated against the plan's target grid or its pools at enrolment: a level with no job-level target row and a unit with no pool in the plan are both accepted here, and the gap surfaces only when target assignment runs, as `ERR_JOB_LEVEL_TARGET_MISSING` or `ERR_POOL_MISSING_FOR_UNIT`. A new participant is `ACTIVE`: `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`, `termination_date` and `termination_reason` are null, and the derived trio `current_job_level`, `current_base_salary_cents` and `current_business_unit_id` is seeded from the supplied `job_level`, `base_salary_cents` and `business_unit_id`.

Everything that changes an employee's entitlement during the year is recorded by an `HRBusinessPartner` as an **eligibility event** against a `participant_id`. Events are append-only and come in three kinds, named by `event_type`. A `TRANSFER` carries `new_job_level`, `new_base_salary_cents` (at least 1) and `new_business_unit_id`, all of which take effect from its `effective_date` onward. A `LEAVE` carries `leave_end_date`, the last day of leave **inclusive** — so the occupied interval runs from `effective_date` through `leave_end_date` — plus the `paid_leave` flag, which defaults to false. A `TERMINATION` carries `termination_reason`, one of `VOLUNTARY_RESIGNATION`, `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE`, `REDUNDANCY`, `RETIREMENT`, `DEATH` or `DISABILITY`, and its `effective_date` is the last day worked. Any event may carry a free-text `note`. Recording an event is not gated on the plan's own status: an event may be appended while the plan sits anywhere from `DRAFT` through `SETTLED`.

For a `TRANSFER` the event's `effective_date` is the first day at the new level, salary and unit; for a `LEAVE` it is the first day of leave; for a `TERMINATION` it is the last day worked. Four temporal rules guard the record, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.10. The `effective_date` must lie inside the half-open plan window — on or after `plan_period_start` and strictly before `plan_period_end` — or the event is refused with `ERR_EVENT_DATE_OUTSIDE_PLAN_PERIOD`. It must also fall on or after the participant's `hire_date`, and a `LEAVE` must not end before it starts, or the event is refused with `ERR_EVENT_DATE_BEFORE_HIRE`. A new leave interval, running from `effective_date` through the day after `leave_end_date` exclusively, must not overlap any existing non-superseded leave interval of the same participant; because the interval is half-open, a leave that starts exactly on the day after another's `leave_end_date` is adjacent, not overlapping, and is allowed, while a genuine overlap is `ERR_LEAVE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Both this scan and the termination scan below skip the event named in `supersedes_event_id`, so a correction may overlap or replace exactly the event it supersedes even though that event is still live at the moment the checks run. And once a participant carries a live termination, no event may be recorded with an `effective_date` strictly after that termination's `effective_date`, and a second termination is refused unless it supersedes the first — both cases report `ERR_PARTICIPANT_ALREADY_TERMINATED`.

Corrections are filed, never edited. A new event may name a previous one in `supersedes_event_id`; the named event's `superseded` flag becomes true and that event is thereafter excluded entirely from every proration input set, including all of its leave days. The named event must belong to the *same* participant and must not already be superseded, or the correction is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDES_EVENT_INVALID`. Every event's `superseded` flag defaults to false.

Recording an event immediately refreshes the participant's derived fields. A `TERMINATION` sets `employment_status` to `TERMINATED` and writes `termination_date` (the last day worked) and `termination_reason`; a `LEAVE` sets `employment_status` to `ON_LEAVE`; a `TRANSFER` leaves `employment_status` as it stands and rewrites `current_job_level`, `current_base_salary_cents` and `current_business_unit_id`. The derived values always reflect the participant's non-superseded events read in ascending `effective_date` order with `created_at` ascending breaking ties, so superseding the only termination returns the participant to `ACTIVE` with a null `termination_date` and `termination_reason`, and a participant with no events at all is `ACTIVE`. The derived trio always describes the state in force on the last credited day of the participation window.

Once awards exist, an event does more than update a personnel record: it re-prorates the affected award, re-resolves which pool the award draws from, recomputes the distribution state of both the old and the new pool, and rolls the result up to the plan — the full chain of §2.7 in one request. Because an event is never refused for a downstream gap, this re-proration degrades instead of rejecting: a segment whose job level has no target row in the plan contributes 0 cents rather than raising `ERR_JOB_LEVEL_TARGET_MISSING`, and a resolved unit with no pool in the plan leaves the award's `business_unit_id` and `bu_pool_id` on their previous values rather than raising `ERR_POOL_MISSING_FOR_UNIT`. One cascade is worth calling out here because it moves money: a termination whose recomputed classification is `BAD_LEAVER` zeroes a `PROPOSED` allocation, returning the award's `allocation_status` from `PROPOSED` to `UNALLOCATED` with `proposed_allocation_cents` set to 0, `allocation_ratio` to 0.0000 and `final_award_cents` to 0.

### 3.3. Target Assignment: Proration, Segmentation and Pool Placement

Target assignment is the `PlanAdmin` operation that turns the participant population into **participant awards**, and it drives the plan from `DRAFT` to `TARGETS_SET`. It takes no payload — everything it needs is already stored. It has no separate precondition on the plan's contents: a run against a plan with no participants creates no award and still drives the plan to `TARGETS_SET`, and a missing target row or missing pool is reported only for a participant that actually needs one, as `ERR_JOB_LEVEL_TARGET_MISSING` or `ERR_POOL_MISSING_FOR_UNIT`. It may also be re-run while the plan is already in `TARGETS_SET`, as a self-transition from `TARGETS_SET` to `TARGETS_SET`; that is the only way to refresh proration in bulk after transfer or leave corrections, and it updates existing award rows in place, preserving each award's `id` and incrementing its `version` only where the refresh actually moved a stored field. A re-run rewrites the proration and placement fields alone — `business_unit_id`, `bu_pool_id`, `target_bonus_pct`, `credited_days`, `excluded_leave_days`, `segment_count`, `prorated_target_cents` and `eligibility_status`, plus the derived `allocation_ratio` and `final_award_cents` — and leaves both status dimensions, `proposed_allocation_cents`, `retraction_count` and `allocation_rationale` as they stand; in particular the `BAD_LEAVER` release of §3.2 fires only on the eligibility-event cascade and never on a re-run. The plan's own `version` increments on every run, including one that changes nothing else. Awards whose participant no longer exists are left untouched. Outside `DRAFT` and `TARGETS_SET` the operation is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`.

The run creates or refreshes exactly one award per participant and prorates it. **The participation window** is half-open: it starts on the later of the plan's `plan_period_start` and the participant's `hire_date`, and it ends, exclusively, at the plan's `plan_period_end` — except that for a participant whose `employment_status` is `TERMINATED`, the window ends at the earlier of `plan_period_end` and the day after `termination_date`. The day immediately before the window's exclusive end is the **last credited day**.

**Segmentation.** The window is cut at every non-superseded `TRANSFER` event whose `effective_date` falls strictly after the window's start and strictly before its end, taken in ascending `effective_date` order with `created_at` ascending breaking ties between transfers sharing a date. Each resulting segment carries the job level, base salary and business unit in force on its first day, and `segment_count` records how many segments there are. A window that contains no days at all — the case of a participant whose `hire_date` falls on or after `plan_period_end` — is cut into no segments, so `segment_count` and `credited_days` are 0 and `target_bonus_pct` is 0.00.

**Unpaid leave and the credit cap.** Paid leave days are always fully credited and never consume any allowance. For unpaid leave, walk the participant's non-superseded `LEAVE` events whose `paid_leave` is false in ascending `effective_date` order with `created_at` ascending breaking ties, and collect every calendar date from each event's `effective_date` through its `leave_end_date` inclusive that also lies inside the participation window. Taking that collected set of dates in ascending date order, the first 30 dates are credited anyway; every date beyond the thirtieth is excluded from proration and the count of excluded dates is written to `excluded_leave_days`. In other words a participant may take a full calendar month of unpaid leave with no reduction, and only the thirty-first unpaid day onward costs them anything.

**Per-segment targets.** For each segment, the segment's credited days are its length in days minus the number of excluded unpaid-leave dates falling inside it. The segment's target is the product of its credited days, its `base_salary_cents` and the `target_bonus_pct` of the job-level target row matching the segment's job level, divided by 100 to convert the percentage and then divided by a fixed 365-day plan-year basis, and that amount is rounded half up to whole cents. `prorated_target_cents` is the sum of those per-segment rounded amounts — the pinned rounding order of §2.6. The award's `credited_days` is the sum of the segments' credited days, and its `target_bonus_pct` copies the percentage of the **last** segment, which is the display figure an employee sees.

**The minimum-service gate and leaver classification** produce `eligibility_status`. A participant credited with fewer than 90 days is `INELIGIBLE_MIN_SERVICE`, and their `prorated_target_cents` is forced to 0; exactly 90 credited days is eligible, and 89 is not. Otherwise, a participant whose `employment_status` is `TERMINATED` is classified `GOOD_LEAVER` when `termination_reason` is `REDUNDANCY`, `RETIREMENT`, `DEATH` or `DISABILITY`, and `BAD_LEAVER` when it is `VOLUNTARY_RESIGNATION` or `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE`. Everyone else is `ELIGIBLE`. If a participant holds a job level on any segment for which the plan has no job-level target row, the whole run is refused with `ERR_JOB_LEVEL_TARGET_MISSING`.

**Pool placement.** Each award's `business_unit_id` is the unit in force on the last credited day, and only transfers move a participant between pools — leaves and terminations never do. Consider the participant's non-superseded `TRANSFER` events that carry a `new_business_unit_id` and whose `effective_date` is on or before the last credited day. If there are none, the award's unit is the participant's home `business_unit_id`. Otherwise it is the `new_business_unit_id` of the transfer with the latest `effective_date`, and where two such transfers share that date the first one recorded — the earliest `created_at` — wins. The award's `bu_pool_id` is then the business-unit pool of this plan whose `business_unit_id` equals the resolved unit; if no such pool exists the entire target-assignment run is refused with `ERR_POOL_MISSING_FOR_UNIT`.

A newly created award starts with `allocation_status` `UNALLOCATED` and `payment_status` `PENDING`, with `proposed_allocation_cents` 0, `allocation_ratio` 0.0000, `adjustment_total_cents` 0, `final_award_cents` 0, `payout_amount_cents` 0, `retraction_count` 0 and an empty `allocation_rationale`. These two status fields are **parallel dimensions on one row** and evolve independently: the allocation dimension runs `UNALLOCATED` to `PROPOSED` to `CALIBRATED` to `LOCKED` and is driven by managers and the calibration workflow, while the payment dimension runs `PENDING` to `DEFERRED`, `PAID` or `FORFEITED` and is driven exclusively by payout execution and dispute resolution. `eligibility_status` is neither — it is a recomputed classification.

Because the awards changed, each pool immediately recomputes `eligible_target_cents` as the sum of `prorated_target_cents` over its awards whose `eligibility_status` is anything other than `INELIGIBLE_MIN_SERVICE`, and `eligible_headcount` as the count of those same awards, along with `max_top_tier_count`, `allocated_cents`, `available_cents` and `distribution_status`. The plan's `total_target_cents` is then the sum of `eligible_target_cents` across the pools of the plan.

### 3.4. Certifying Performance and Minting the Fixed Pool

The audited outcome of a company metric arrives as a single filing by a `PlanAdmin`, which carries `actual_value` and an optional `source_note` recording provenance and moves the metric from `DRAFT` to `ACTUAL_ENTERED`. It may be filed exactly once — a second filing against a metric already in `ACTUAL_ENTERED` is `ERR_METRIC_ACTUAL_ALREADY_FILED` — and only while the plan is still in setup.

Filing the actual scores the metric onto `achievement_pct` along a four-part curve, using the metric's own band. An actual below `threshold_value` scores 0.00. An actual that is at least `threshold_value` but below `target_value` scores 50.00 plus half of the fraction of the way it has travelled from `threshold_value` to `target_value`, expressed in percentage points — that is, 50 plus the difference between `actual_value` and `threshold_value`, divided by the difference between `target_value` and `threshold_value`, multiplied by 50. An actual that is at least `target_value` but below `max_value` scores 100.00 plus the difference between `actual_value` and `target_value`, divided by the difference between `max_value` and `target_value`, multiplied by 50. An actual at or above `max_value` scores exactly 150.00, the hard cap. The result is rounded half up to two decimals. The boundaries are inclusive at the bottom of each band: an actual exactly equal to `threshold_value` scores exactly 50.00, one exactly equal to `target_value` scores exactly 100.00, and one exactly equal to `max_value` scores exactly 150.00.

While the plan admin is filing actuals, each `BusinessUnitManager` submits their unit's scorecard, moving their pool's `calibration_status` from `DRAFT` to `SCORECARD_SUBMITTED`. The submission carries `bu_metric_achievement_pct` and `scorecard_narrative`, both of which overwrite whatever the pool held. Submission requires the plan to still be in setup (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_SETUP`), the pool to be in `DRAFT` (`ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`), and the acting `X-User-ID` to be the pool's own `business_unit_id` (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNERSHIP`). The committee then rules on the submitted scorecard with a `decision` of either `CERTIFY` or `RETURN`, optionally attaching a `decision_note`. `CERTIFY` moves the pool from `SCORECARD_SUBMITTED` to `SCORECARD_CERTIFIED` and records the acting `X-User-ID` in `scorecard_certified_by_user_id`. `RETURN` moves the pool from `SCORECARD_SUBMITTED` back to `DRAFT` and clears `scorecard_certified_by_user_id`, so the unit revises and resubmits; a pool may travel `DRAFT` to `SCORECARD_SUBMITTED` to `DRAFT` as often as the committee insists. Either decision requires the plan to still be in `DRAFT` or `TARGETS_SET` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_SETUP`) and the pool to be in `SCORECARD_SUBMITTED` (`ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`).

Submitting the plan for certification moves it from `TARGETS_SET` to `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` and is where the setup phase is audited as a whole. Four conditions must hold, and they are checked in the ladder order of §2.10: the plan's company-metric weights must sum to exactly 100.00 and at least one metric must exist (`ERR_METRIC_WEIGHTS_INVALID`); every company metric of the plan must be in `ACTUAL_ENTERED` (`ERR_METRICS_NOT_ALL_ACTUALIZED`); every business-unit pool of the plan must be in `SCORECARD_CERTIFIED` (`ERR_SCORECARDS_NOT_ALL_CERTIFIED`); and `total_target_cents` must be greater than 0, meaning at least one award survived the ninety-day minimum-service gate (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS`). Submission moves no money — it only increments the plan's `version`.

The **certification decision** belongs to the `CompensationCommittee` and takes a `decision` of `CERTIFY` or `RETURN` with an optional `decision_note`. The plan must be in `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`.

`CERTIFY` advances the plan from `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` to `CERTIFIED` and runs the funding chain in a pinned order. First, `company_achievement_pct` is the sum, over every company metric of the plan in `ACTUAL_ENTERED`, of that metric's `weight_pct` divided by 100 and multiplied by its `achievement_pct`; each term is kept at full precision and only the total is rounded half up to two decimals. Because submission already guaranteed complete weights, the weights are used exactly as declared, with no re-normalisation. Second, `bu_weighted_achievement_pct` is a target-weighted mean of the unit scorecards: for each pool of the plan that is in `SCORECARD_CERTIFIED` and whose `eligible_target_cents` is greater than 0, multiply its `eligible_target_cents` by the lesser of 150.00 and its `bu_metric_achievement_pct`, sum those products, divide the sum once by the plan's `total_target_cents`, and round half up to two decimals. Pools with no eligible target contribute neither a product nor weight; if the filtered set is empty the result is 0.00. Note that the 150.00 cap bites here and only here — a unit may store a scorecard up to 200.00, but it enters the funding formula capped. Third, `blended_achievement_pct` is the sum of three weighted terms, rounded half up to two decimals on the total only: `company_weight_pct` divided by 100 times `company_achievement_pct`, plus `business_unit_weight_pct` divided by 100 times `bu_weighted_achievement_pct`, plus `individual_weight_pct` divided by 100 times a fixed 100.00. Fourth, the blended figure is pushed through the **funding curve** to produce `funding_multiplier`, rounded half up to four decimals: a blended achievement below 80.00 yields 0.0000; from 80.00 up to but not including 100.00 the multiplier is 0.50 plus the difference between the blended figure and 80, divided by 20, multiplied by 0.50; from 100.00 up to but not including 120.00 it is 1.00 plus the difference between the blended figure and 100, divided by 20, multiplied by 0.50; and at 120.00 or above it is the 1.5000 cap. The curve is inclusive at the bottom of each band: exactly 80.00 funds at 0.5000 rather than 0.0000, exactly 100.00 funds at 1.0000, and exactly 120.00 funds at the 1.5000 cap. Certification finally records the acting `X-User-ID` in `certified_by_user_id`.

`RETURN` is the revision cycle, and it is deliberately expensive. The plan goes from `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` back to `TARGETS_SET`; every pool of the plan that stood in `SCORECARD_CERTIFIED` is pushed back to `DRAFT` with its `scorecard_certified_by_user_id` cleared; and because the certified-pool input set is now empty, `bu_weighted_achievement_pct` and `blended_achievement_pct` reset to 0.00 and `funding_multiplier` resets to 0.0000. `certified_by_user_id` is cleared as well. Every scorecard must then be resubmitted and re-certified before the plan can be submitted again. This is the only route by which a pool moves from `SCORECARD_CERTIFIED` to `DRAFT`; the scorecard decision itself can never do it.

**Funding the pool** is the committee act that mints the money and moves the plan from `CERTIFIED` to `ALLOCATION_OPEN`. It requires a `funding_multiplier` strictly greater than 0.0000 — a plan whose blended achievement missed the 80.00 floor cannot open allocation at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_MULTIPLIER_ZERO`. Processing the pools of the plan in ascending `created_at` order (an audit ordering that affects no figure), each pool's `funded_cents` becomes its `eligible_target_cents` multiplied by the plan's `funding_multiplier`, rounded half up to whole cents; its `min_utilization_cents` becomes 99.50 percent of `funded_cents` truncated downward to a whole cent — that is, `funded_cents` multiplied by 9950, divided by 10000, with any fraction discarded; and its `available_cents` becomes `funded_cents` minus `allocated_cents`. Each pool moves from `SCORECARD_CERTIFIED` to `FUNDED`. Pools with no eligible target are included: they are funded with 0 cents and keep `distribution_status` `NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS`. The plan's `funded_pool_cents` is the sum of the per-pool figures just written, and `aggregate_rounding_delta_cents` records the divergence described in §2.6. `discretionary_budget_cents` is then `funded_pool_cents` multiplied by `discretionary_budget_pct`, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents — on a pool of 10,000,000 cents at the default reserve of one tenth, that is 1,000,000 cents of discretionary authority. Once funded, each pool has money but nothing allocated, so its `distribution_status` becomes `UNDER_UTILIZED` (pools with an `eligible_headcount` of 0 stay `NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS`, and a pool that funds to 0 cents while still holding eligible awards lands in `WITHIN_GUIDELINES`, because its `min_utilization_cents` is 0 too and the under-utilization test is strict), the plan's `under_utilized_pool_count` is recomputed accordingly, and `governance_posture` becomes `FLAGGED` for as long as any pool remains under-utilized. `funded_cents` is frozen at this moment and is never re-derived, no matter what happens to targets afterwards.

A `PlanAdmin`, `CompensationCommittee` or `FinanceController` may also request a plan's funding summary, a computed read whose payload is exactly the plan's own representation — the identical field set the plan detail read returns, with no summary-only field added and none omitted — so that the whole chain arrives in one payload: company, business-unit and blended achievement, the funding multiplier, the summed per-pool funded total, the aggregate rounding delta, the discretionary budget and the amount used, the accrual ledger and the governance posture.

### 3.5. Allocating Inside a Fixed Pool

With the plan in `ALLOCATION_OPEN`, each `BusinessUnitManager` distributes their unit's frozen pool award by award. Proposing an allocation writes `proposed_allocation_cents` and an `allocation_rationale` onto one award and moves its `allocation_status` from `UNALLOCATED` to `PROPOSED`; both are required inputs and `allocation_rationale` must be non-empty. The same operation may be repeated on an award already in `PROPOSED` — a self-transition from `PROPOSED` to `PROPOSED` that simply replaces the amount, which is how a manager rebalances inside a fixed pool. Each call also carries `expected_pool_version` as specified in §2.8.

The checks are the ladder of §2.10 applied to this operation, in this order: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the award's `business_unit_id` (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNERSHIP`); the plan must be in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_ALLOCATION`); the pool must be in `FUNDED` or `ALLOCATING` (`ERR_POOL_NOT_ALLOCATING`); the award's `allocation_status` must be `UNALLOCATED` or `PROPOSED` (`ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`); `expected_pool_version` must match the pool's `version` (`ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION`); the award's `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE` or `GOOD_LEAVER`, since an `INELIGIBLE_MIN_SERVICE` or `BAD_LEAVER` award may receive nothing (`ERR_AWARD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); the guideline band must be respected; and finally the pool must still have the money.

The **guideline band** works on the ratio of `proposed_allocation_cents` to the award's `prorated_target_cents`. For a non-zero amount, that ratio — taken at full precision, not as the stored four-decimal `allocation_ratio` — must be at least the `guideline_min_ratio` and at most the `guideline_max_ratio` of the job-level target row for the award's level, meaning the row for the level of the award's last segment, the same row that supplied its `target_bonus_pct`; a ratio outside the band is `ERR_GUIDELINE_RATIO_BREACH`. A non-zero amount against an award whose `prorated_target_cents` is 0, or whose level has no target row in the plan at all, has no computable ratio and is refused with that same `ERR_GUIDELINE_RATIO_BREACH`. An amount of zero is always permitted — a manager may decide an individual earns nothing — and bypasses the band entirely. The stored `allocation_ratio` is `proposed_allocation_cents` divided by `prorated_target_cents`, rounded half up to four decimals, and is 0.0000 whenever `prorated_target_cents` is 0.

The **pool money check** is the last gate to run. The pool's `allocated_cents`, evaluated after this award's previous proposed amount has been removed and the new one added, must not exceed the pool's frozen `funded_cents`; if it would, the request is refused with `ERR_POOL_FUNDS_EXCEEDED`. This is what makes the pool genuinely fixed: a manager who wants to pay one person more must first take it from someone else.

A successful allocation writes `proposed_allocation_cents`, `allocation_rationale`, `allocation_ratio` and `final_award_cents` (which at this stage equals `proposed_allocation_cents` plus `adjustment_total_cents`), then recomputes the pool and the plan. A pool sitting in `FUNDED` moves to `ALLOCATING` on the first successful allocation against any of its awards. The pool's `allocated_cents` becomes the sum, over its awards whose `allocation_status` is `PROPOSED`, `CALIBRATED` or `LOCKED`, of `proposed_allocation_cents` plus `adjustment_total_cents`; unallocated awards contribute to `eligible_target_cents` but never to `allocated_cents`. `available_cents` becomes `funded_cents` minus `allocated_cents` and may go negative when discretionary adjustments overshoot. `utilization_pct` is `allocated_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `funded_cents`, rounded half up to a whole number and then divided by 100, and is 0.00 when `funded_cents` is 0. `top_tier_award_count` is the number of the pool's awards in `PROPOSED`, `CALIBRATED` or `LOCKED` whose `allocation_ratio` is at least 1.50 — a ratio of exactly 1.50 does count as top tier — and `max_top_tier_count` is the greater of 1 and a quarter of `eligible_headcount` truncated downward, so a unit of seven eligible people may nominate at most one top-tier award while a unit of eight may nominate two; a pool with an `eligible_headcount` of 0 has a `max_top_tier_count` of 0 rather than 1. Finally the pool's `distribution_status` is decided in this order: `NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` when `eligible_headcount` is 0; otherwise `OVER_ALLOCATED` when `allocated_cents` is strictly greater than `funded_cents`; otherwise `UNDER_UTILIZED` when `allocated_cents` is strictly less than `min_utilization_cents`; otherwise `WITHIN_GUIDELINES`. Allocating exactly `min_utilization_cents` is therefore within guidelines, since the under-utilization test is strict.

The plan then re-derives its governance view: `over_allocated_pool_count` is the number of its pools in `OVER_ALLOCATED`, `under_utilized_pool_count` the number in `UNDER_UTILIZED`, and `governance_posture` is `BREACHED` when two or more pools are over-allocated, otherwise `FLAGGED` when exactly one pool is over-allocated or at least one pool is under-utilized, otherwise `COMPLIANT`. Every successful allocation increments the pool's `version`, which is precisely why the next allocation must present a fresh `expected_pool_version`.

Retracting an allocation is the compensating action, moving the award from `PROPOSED` back to `UNALLOCATED`. It requires the acting manager to own the award's business unit, the plan to be in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_ALLOCATION`), the pool to be in `FUNDED` or `ALLOCATING` (`ERR_POOL_NOT_ALLOCATING`), the award to be in `PROPOSED` (`ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`), and a matching `expected_pool_version`. It also requires the award's `adjustment_total_cents` to be exactly 0 — an approved discretionary adjustment must be reversed first, and retracting an award that still carries one is refused with `ERR_AWARD_HAS_APPROVED_ADJUSTMENT`. A retraction may carry a `retraction_reason`, which is stored as the award's new `allocation_rationale`; when it is omitted the `allocation_rationale` is cleared to the empty string rather than left as the manager wrote it. It sets `proposed_allocation_cents` to 0, `allocation_ratio` to 0.0000 and `final_award_cents` to 0, increments `retraction_count`, and then runs the same pool recomputation and plan governance roll-up, incrementing the pool's `version`. Allocate then retract the same award and the pool's `allocated_cents` and `available_cents` are back exactly where they started.

### 3.6. Discretionary Adjustment Authority and Its Governance Limits

Some outcomes the guidelines cannot reach: a retention case, a mid-year role change the grid does not capture, an error discovered too late to reopen a pool. For these, a `PlanAdmin` or an `HRBusinessPartner` files a **discretionary adjustment** against one award, drawn not on the unit's pool but on the plan-level discretionary budget reserved at funding time. This separation is deliberate and it has a visible consequence: an approved adjustment can push a pool's `allocated_cents` above its `funded_cents`, turning it `OVER_ALLOCATED`.

The filing carries `plan_id`, `award_id`, a signed non-zero `adjustment_cents`, a mandatory `justification` of at least one character, an optional `documentation_ref` and the `expected_plan_version` of §2.8. `documentation_ref` is not always optional: when the absolute value of `adjustment_cents` is strictly greater than 100000 cents — a thousand dollars — a non-empty `documentation_ref` pointing at the filed governance paperwork is required, and its absence — including a `documentation_ref` that is supplied but blank or whitespace-only — is `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`. An adjustment of exactly 100000 cents needs no documentation. Filing also imposes, in this order, that the plan be in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` or `CALIBRATION_REVIEW` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_ALLOCATION`), that the award's pool be in `FUNDED` or `ALLOCATING` (`ERR_POOL_NOT_ALLOCATING`), that the award's `allocation_status` be `PROPOSED` or `CALIBRATED` (`ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`), that `expected_plan_version` match, that the `documentation_ref` rule just given hold, that the award belong to the plan named in the filing — the only scope comparison made (`ERR_PLAN_SCOPE_MISMATCH`) — and last that the award's `eligibility_status` be `ELIGIBLE` or `GOOD_LEAVER` (`ERR_AWARD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`).

Creation freezes the per-award ceiling. `per_award_cap_cents` is the greater of 50000 cents and a quarter of the award's `prorated_target_cents` — the latter computed as `prorated_target_cents` multiplied by 25, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents — read at creation time and never moved afterwards, so a later re-proration that changes `prorated_target_cents` leaves an existing adjustment's frozen cap exactly as it was. The new row stores `submitted_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, sets `status` to `PENDING_APPROVAL`, leaves `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` null, and moves no money at all.

The `CompensationCommittee` then rules, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` with an optional `decision_note`. Either verdict requires the plan to be in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` or `CALIBRATION_REVIEW` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_ALLOCATION`), the adjustment to be in `PENDING_APPROVAL` (`ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_PENDING`) and the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the adjustment's `submitted_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). The two branches then diverge completely.

`APPROVE` moves the adjustment from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED` and must clear three further gates in ladder order. First, the award's `final_award_cents` plus the signed `adjustment_cents` must be at least 0, since no employee may be given a negative award (`ERR_NEGATIVE_AWARD_NOT_ALLOWED`). Then the absolute value of `adjustment_cents` must not exceed the frozen `per_award_cap_cents` — exactly equal to the cap is allowed — or the decision is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_PER_AWARD_LIMIT`. And last, the plan's `discretionary_used_cents` plus the absolute value of `adjustment_cents` must not exceed `discretionary_budget_cents`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_DISCRETIONARY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Approval then fires four money effects: `decided_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID` and the plan's `discretionary_used_cents` increases by the absolute value of `adjustment_cents`; the award's `adjustment_total_cents` increases by the signed `adjustment_cents` and its `final_award_cents` becomes `proposed_allocation_cents` plus `adjustment_total_cents`; the pool's `allocated_cents`, `available_cents`, `utilization_pct` and `distribution_status` are recomputed, which is where a pool may tip into `OVER_ALLOCATED`; and the plan's `over_allocated_pool_count` and `governance_posture` are recomputed from the resulting pool states. Note that the plan's discretionary usage is tracked with absolute values: a negative adjustment consumes budget exactly as a positive one does, because both are exercises of the same governance authority.

`REJECT` moves the adjustment from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `REJECTED`, records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note`, and deliberately fires none of the four money effects — no field on the award, the pool or the plan changes. `REJECTED` is terminal: a rejected adjustment cannot be revived, and a fresh filing is required instead. The per-award cap, the discretionary budget and the negative-award gates are approval-only; a rejection is never blocked by them.

An approved adjustment may be **reversed** by the committee, which supplies a mandatory `decision_note` — overwriting whatever note the approval left, while `decided_by_user_id` continues to name the approver rather than the reversing member — and moves the adjustment from `APPROVED` to `REVERSED`, also terminal. Reversal requires the adjustment to be in `APPROVED` (`ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_PENDING`) and the plan to be in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` or `CALIBRATION_REVIEW` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_ALLOCATION`). It is an exact inverse of approval: the plan's `discretionary_used_cents` decreases by the absolute value of `adjustment_cents`, the award's `adjustment_total_cents` decreases by the signed `adjustment_cents` and its `final_award_cents` is recomputed, the pool's `allocated_cents`, `available_cents`, `utilization_pct` and `distribution_status` are recomputed, and the plan's `over_allocated_pool_count` and `governance_posture` follow — landing every one of those figures back on its pre-approval value, as §2.11 requires.

### 3.7. Calibration Review and the Committee Lock

When a manager considers their unit's distribution finished, they submit it for calibration, moving the pool's `calibration_status` from `ALLOCATING` to `CALIBRATION_SUBMITTED`; the submission may carry a revised `scorecard_narrative`. Submission requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be the pool's `business_unit_id` (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNERSHIP`), checked before anything else, and then the plan to be in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_ALLOCATION`) and the pool to be in `FUNDED` or `ALLOCATING` (`ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`), after which it enforces two distribution gates in this order. The pool's `top_tier_award_count` must not exceed its `max_top_tier_count`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_TOP_TIER_CONCENTRATION`. And the pool's `distribution_status` must be `WITHIN_GUIDELINES` or `NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` — an `UNDER_UTILIZED` pool that has left money on the table and an `OVER_ALLOCATED` pool that has overspent are both refused with `ERR_POOL_NOT_WITHIN_GUIDELINES`.

There is one short circuit. A unit with no eligible participants at all never allocates anything, so it never leaves `FUNDED`; such a pool submits for calibration directly from `FUNDED` to `CALIBRATION_SUBMITTED`, subject to the plan being in `ALLOCATION_OPEN`, to the manager owning the unit, and to the same two distribution gates as a submission out of `ALLOCATING`, so that its `distribution_status` may be `NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` or `WITHIN_GUIDELINES` — anything else is `ERR_POOL_NOT_WITHIN_GUIDELINES`. The submission changes no award, since the pool has none to change, and only increments the pool's `version`. Submitting from any state other than `FUNDED` or `ALLOCATING` is `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`.

The `PlanAdmin` adjudicates each submitted pool with a `decision` of `CERTIFY` or `RETURN` and an optional `decision_note`, while the plan is in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` or `CALIBRATION_REVIEW` — a state checked before either branch's own conditions. The `decision_note` is accepted for the record but is not retained on the pool, which carries no field for it. `CERTIFY` requires the pool to be in `CALIBRATION_SUBMITTED` and its `distribution_status` still to be `WITHIN_GUIDELINES` or `NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` (`ERR_POOL_NOT_WITHIN_GUIDELINES`); it moves the pool from `CALIBRATION_SUBMITTED` to `CALIBRATED` and promotes every award of the pool from `PROPOSED` to `CALIBRATED`, touching no money field. `RETURN` reopens the pool for reallocation and can be applied both to a pool in `CALIBRATION_SUBMITTED` and to one already in `CALIBRATED`, in either case landing it back in `ALLOCATING`: it increments `calibration_returned_count`, demotes every award of the pool from `CALIBRATED` back to `PROPOSED`, and — because the pool has left the state that let the plan advance — sends a plan sitting in `CALIBRATION_REVIEW` back to `ALLOCATION_OPEN` without any separate request against the plan. Returning a pool that is in `CALIBRATED` additionally requires the plan to be in `ALLOCATION_OPEN` or `CALIBRATION_REVIEW`. A pool may cycle through `ALLOCATING`, `CALIBRATION_SUBMITTED` and back to `ALLOCATING` any number of times, and `calibration_returned_count` counts how often it happened.

Once every pool has been submitted or calibrated, the `PlanAdmin` closes manager allocation by opening the calibration review, which moves the plan from `ALLOCATION_OPEN` to `CALIBRATION_REVIEW`. Every pool of the plan must be in `CALIBRATION_SUBMITTED` or `CALIBRATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_POOL_CALIBRATION_PENDING`. No money field changes; the plan's `version` increments. Because a single calibration return bubbles the plan back down, the plan can traverse `ALLOCATION_OPEN` to `CALIBRATION_REVIEW` and back to `ALLOCATION_OPEN` any number of times before the numbers hold still.

The **lock** is the committee's final act on the allocation side and moves the plan from `CALIBRATION_REVIEW` to `LOCKED`. Three conditions guard it, in ladder order: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the plan's `certified_by_user_id`, so that the member who certified the performance figures is not the member who freezes the money (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); the plan's `governance_posture` must be `COMPLIANT`, which means no pool may be left over-allocated or under-utilized (`ERR_GOVERNANCE_POSTURE_BREACHED`); and every pool of the plan must be in `CALIBRATED`, not merely submitted (`ERR_POOL_CALIBRATION_PENDING`). Locking moves every pool of the plan from `CALIBRATED` to `LOCKED` — a terminal state for a pool — and, through them, every award in `CALIBRATED` to `LOCKED`. The plan's `locked_total_cents` is the sum of `final_award_cents` over the awards now in `LOCKED`, and `locked_by_user_id` records the acting member.

### 3.8. Accrual Accounting Through the Year

The company cannot wait for certification to recognise the expense, so a `FinanceController` accrues the pool month by month against a *forecast* of the very same funding formula. Accrual periods are closed-inclusive intervals: an **accrual period** carries `plan_id`, `period_start` (its first day) and `period_end` (its last day, inclusive), plus `forecast_company_achievement_pct`, the forecast company achievement between 0 and 200 with two decimals that stands in for the audited figure. The periods must tile the plan year exactly. A new period must not overlap any existing non-`REVERSED` period of the plan (`ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), and its `period_start` must equal the plan's `plan_period_start` if it is the plan's first period and otherwise the day after the latest existing `period_end` — the latest taken over every period the plan has, `REVERSED` ones included, even though the overlap test just described passes over them, so that reversing a period does not free its dates for a fresh one. Unlike leave intervals, accrual periods may not be adjacent-with-a-gap, and a one-day hole is refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_GAP`. `period_end` must be on or after `period_start`, an ordering checked as plain field validation (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`) before the plan is resolved; it is not tested against the last day of the plan period, so a period may run past the plan year's end without objection. A period may not be opened or posted once the plan has reached `PAID_OUT` or `SETTLED`, and may not be reversed once the plan has reached `SETTLED` — reversal is still permitted while the plan is `PAID_OUT` — with either refusal reported as `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_SETTLED`. A new period starts with `status` `OPEN`, `provisional_target_basis`, `posting_note` and `reversal_reason` null, `burden_rate_pct` 0.00, and `provisional_pool_cents`, `elapsed_days`, `cumulative_accrual_target_cents`, `period_accrual_cents`, `burden_cents` and `total_period_cents` all 0 until the posting fills them.

**Posting** a period moves it from `OPEN` to `POSTED` and is where the forecast is valued. It requires the period to be `OPEN` (`ERR_ACCRUAL_ALREADY_POSTED`), the plan not to have reached `PAID_OUT` or `SETTLED`, and every earlier period of the plan — every period whose `period_end` precedes this period's `period_start` — to be `POSTED` or `REVERSED` already, so that periods are posted in ascending order (`ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_GAP`). The posting may carry a `posting_note`.

Posting first values the pool under the **provisional funding variant**, which differs from the certified variant in three ways: the company component comes from the period's own `forecast_company_achievement_pct` rather than from audited actuals, the business-unit component defaults to full target achievement until scorecards are certified, and the target basis may be un-prorated. Concretely, a forecast blended achievement is computed exactly like the certified blend but with forecast inputs: `company_weight_pct` divided by 100 times `forecast_company_achievement_pct`, plus `business_unit_weight_pct` divided by 100 times a forecast business-unit figure, plus `individual_weight_pct` divided by 100 times 100.00, rounded half up to two decimals. The forecast business-unit figure is 100.00 when no pool of the plan is in `SCORECARD_CERTIFIED`, and otherwise is the same target-weighted, 150.00-capped mean over the certified pools that certification itself would compute. That forecast blend is pushed through the identical four-decimal funding curve to get a provisional multiplier.

The target basis then depends on how far the plan has got. If the plan has at least one participant award, `provisional_target_basis` is `AWARD_PRORATED` and the basis is the sum of `prorated_target_cents` over the plan's awards whose `eligibility_status` is anything other than `INELIGIBLE_MIN_SERVICE` — note that `BAD_LEAVER` awards are *included*, because their target remains an accounting obligation until the payout variant forfeits it. If the plan has no awards yet, `provisional_target_basis` is `PARTICIPANT_UNPRORATED` and the basis is the sum, over the plan's participants whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`, of each one's `current_base_salary_cents` multiplied by the `target_bonus_pct` of the job-level target row for their `current_job_level`, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents — whole-year targets, ignoring proration entirely, and excluding terminated participants. `provisional_pool_cents` is then the basis multiplied by the provisional multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents.

Four further steps then run **in this pinned order**. First, `elapsed_days` is the number of days from the plan's `plan_period_start` to this period's `period_end` counting both endpoints. Second, `cumulative_accrual_target_cents` is `provisional_pool_cents` multiplied by `elapsed_days`, divided by 365, rounded half up to whole cents. Third, `period_accrual_cents` is `cumulative_accrual_target_cents` minus the plan's `accrued_base_cents` as it stands before this posting; it may be negative, which is how a downgraded forecast claws back a previous over-accrual. Fourth, employer burden is loaded. The burden rate is selected from `cumulative_accrual_target_cents` — the pre-burden cumulative figure of step two, not from the loaded amount and not from the plan's `accrued_total_cents`: `burden_rate_pct` is 7.65 when the cumulative figure is below 5,000,000 cents, 9.25 when it is below 20,000,000 cents, and 10.50 at or above that. Only then is `burden_cents` computed as `period_accrual_cents` multiplied by `burden_rate_pct`, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents, and `total_period_cents` is `period_accrual_cents` plus `burden_cents`. The dependency looks circular and is deliberately broken in one direction: a loaded accrual can sit in a higher burden band than the cumulative figure it came from, and the band is nevertheless chosen from the cumulative figure alone. Posting finally adds `period_accrual_cents` to the plan's `accrued_base_cents` and `total_period_cents` to its `accrued_total_cents`.

A posted period may be **reversed**, moving it from `POSTED` to the terminal `REVERSED` with a mandatory `reversal_reason`. Reversal is last-in-first-out: no later period of the plan may still be `POSTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_REVERSAL_OUT_OF_ORDER`, and the plan must not have reached `SETTLED`. It subtracts `period_accrual_cents` from the plan's `accrued_base_cents` and `total_period_cents` from its `accrued_total_cents` — exact compensation — while the period keeps every computed amount it holds for audit. Reversing a period that is not `POSTED` is `ERR_ACCRUAL_ALREADY_POSTED`.

### 3.9. Payout, Payment-Date Eligibility, Disputes and Settlement

A `FinanceController` opens a **payout run** with `plan_id` and an optional `run_note`. Opening it is not gated on the plan's status: the run may be drafted against a plan in any state, and the `LOCKED` requirement bites only when the run is executed. At most one run may exist per plan; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_PAYOUT_ALREADY_EXECUTED`, as is executing a run that is not in `DRAFT` or settling one that is not in `EXECUTED`. Because a run reaches only the plan it names, none of its three writes ever raises `ERR_PLAN_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. A new run starts in `DRAFT` with `paid_award_count`, `deferred_award_count`, `forfeited_award_count`, `total_paid_cents`, `total_forfeited_cents`, `settlement_burden_cents` and `true_up_cents` at 0, and `payment_date_used` and `settlement_note` null.

**Executing** the run moves it from `DRAFT` to `EXECUTED` and, as a cascade, the plan from `LOCKED` to `PAID_OUT`. It requires the plan to be `LOCKED` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_LOCKED`) and the logical clock date to have reached the plan's `payment_date` — on the payment date itself is permitted, before it is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. The run stamps the logical clock date into `payment_date_used`.

Execution then computes a verdict for every award of the plan whose `allocation_status` is `LOCKED`, and only those. The first question is whether the participant counts as **employed on the payment date**: they do when their `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`, and they also do when they have a `termination_date` that falls on or after the plan's `payment_date` — a participant whose last day worked is exactly the payment date counts as employed. The verdict then depends on the plan's `termination_treatment_policy`, and the two variants are genuinely different policies rather than degrees of the same one. Under `STRICT_PAYMENT_DATE`, an award pays its `final_award_cents` if the participant was employed on the payment date and pays 0 otherwise: any termination before the payment date forfeits the whole award regardless of reason, and the retiree and the resigner are treated identically. Under `GOOD_LEAVER_PRORATION`, an award pays its `final_award_cents` if the participant was employed on the payment date, and also pays the full already-prorated `final_award_cents` if the participant's `eligibility_status` is `GOOD_LEAVER`; only a `BAD_LEAVER` forfeits. For a single retired participant the two policies differ by exactly that award's `final_award_cents`.

The verdict amount is then filtered by the dispute state, which is what actually sets the payment dimension. If any dispute of the award is in `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, the award moves from `payment_status` `PENDING` to `DEFERRED`: its `payout_amount_cents` is written to the verdict amount but the cash is held, and the award counts only toward `deferred_award_count`. Otherwise, a verdict amount greater than 0 moves the award from `PENDING` to `PAID` with `payout_amount_cents` set to that amount, contributing to `paid_award_count` and `total_paid_cents`; and a verdict amount of 0 moves the award from `PENDING` to `FORFEITED` with `payout_amount_cents` set to 0, contributing its `final_award_cents` to `forfeited_award_count` and `total_forfeited_cents`. From the per-award verdicts the plan's `paid_total_cents` (the sum of `payout_amount_cents` over awards in `PAID`) and `forfeited_total_cents` (the sum of `final_award_cents` over awards in `FORFEITED`) are written. Before executing, any authorized reader may ask for a single award's payout preview, a computed read that reports what the verdict would be under the plan's active policy while changing nothing, and which is likewise open in any plan state. Its payload is the award's own representation with the projected verdict written over the `payment_status` and `payout_amount_cents` it shows, plus four fields carried only by the preview: `previewed_payment_status` and `previewed_payout_amount_cents` repeating that verdict, the plan's `termination_treatment_policy`, and the boolean `employed_on_payment_date`.

**Disputes** are how an employee contests their number. An `Employee` files a dispute against their own award with `plan_id`, `award_id`, `participant_id`, a `dispute_reason` stating the case and an optional `claimed_amount_cents` (0 or greater) naming what they believe is owed, left null when omitted. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the award's `participant_id` (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNERSHIP`); that single comparison is the whole ownership test and it is made first. The plan must then be in `LOCKED` or `PAID_OUT` — an award must exist and be final before it can be contested, and filing outside that window is `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_ALLOWED_YET`. Last, the award must belong to the plan named in the filing and its `participant_id` must equal the filing's `participant_id` (`ERR_PLAN_SCOPE_MISMATCH`), so a body `participant_id` naming anyone other than the award's participant is reported as a scope failure rather than an ownership one. A new dispute is `OPEN` with `reviewer_note`, `resolution`, `adjusted_amount_cents`, `resolution_note`, `assigned_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` all null.

An `HRBusinessPartner` takes an `OPEN` dispute under review, moving it to `UNDER_REVIEW`, optionally recording a `reviewer_note` and storing the acting `X-User-ID` in `assigned_by_user_id`. Taking a dispute that is not `OPEN` under review, or resolving one that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. Resolution carries a `resolution` of `UPHOLD` or `ADJUST`, an optional `resolution_note`, and records `resolved_by_user_id`.

`UPHOLD` moves the dispute from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `RESOLVED_UPHELD` and keeps the award as computed. If the award was left `DEFERRED` by the run, upholding releases it: an award whose `payout_amount_cents` is greater than 0 moves from `DEFERRED` to `PAID`, and an award whose `payout_amount_cents` is 0 — the run's verdict was forfeiture — moves from `DEFERRED` to `FORFEITED`, with its `final_award_cents` added to the plan's `forfeited_total_cents`. Either way the plan's `paid_total_cents` or `forfeited_total_cents` is recomputed, because the award has left the deferred set.

`ADJUST` moves the dispute from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `RESOLVED_ADJUSTED` and overrides the payout. It requires `adjusted_amount_cents` to be supplied and to be 0 or greater — its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it may not exceed twice the award's `final_award_cents`, a request beyond that ceiling being refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`. On success the award's `payout_amount_cents` becomes `adjusted_amount_cents` and its `payment_status` becomes `PAID`, the plan's `dispute_adjustment_cents` accumulates the signed difference between `adjusted_amount_cents` and the award's `final_award_cents`, and the plan's `paid_total_cents` is recomputed.

**Settlement** closes the year. The `FinanceController` books the true-up, moving the run from `EXECUTED` to the terminal `SETTLED` and the plan from `PAID_OUT` to the terminal `SETTLED`, optionally recording a `settlement_note`. The plan must be in `PAID_OUT` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_LOCKED`) and no award of the plan may still be in `payment_status` `DEFERRED` — every live dispute must be resolved first, and an attempt while one remains is refused with `ERR_DEFERRED_AWARDS_PENDING`. Settlement takes the plan's `paid_total_cents` — the cash actually paid, including awards that were deferred and later paid through dispute resolution, with forfeited awards contributing 0 — and computes `settlement_burden_cents` as `paid_total_cents` multiplied by the burden rate for that amount, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents, using the same three-band table the accrual posting uses: 7.65 percent below 5,000,000 cents, 9.25 percent below 20,000,000 cents and 10.50 percent at or above that, keyed on `paid_total_cents`. `true_up_cents` is then `paid_total_cents` plus `settlement_burden_cents`, minus the plan's `accrued_total_cents`, and it is signed — positive when the year was under-accrued, negative when it was over-accrued. Both figures are written onto the run and mirrored onto the plan's own `settlement_burden_cents` and `true_up_cents`. The closure holds exactly, for any sequence of posted and reversed accrual periods: the plan's `accrued_total_cents` plus its `true_up_cents` equals its `paid_total_cents` plus its `settlement_burden_cents`.
