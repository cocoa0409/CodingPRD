# Rail Demurrage Billing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Demurrage and detention are the charges a Class I railroad assesses when a rail car sits at a customer's facility longer than the free time the published tariff grants. In practice almost every dollar of that revenue is contested, and it is contested for a small number of recurring reasons: the customer says the car was never properly noticed, or that the railroad delivered six cars on a Tuesday when the plant can absorb two a day, or that the clock should have started at actual placement rather than at constructive placement, or that the tariff rate itself is unreasonable. Today those arguments are settled in spreadsheets and email threads, which means the same fact pattern produces two different answers depending on who runs the numbers. This document specifies the service that replaces that process: a single system of record for the placement clock, the charge ladder, the invoice, the dispute, and the regulatory escalation that follows when the railroad's own adjudication is exhausted.

The service is mounted under `/demurrage` and is built around one central idea — **every money figure is derived, never asserted**. A charge line is born when a car is constructively placed, is recomputed on every subsequent event that moves the clock, is frozen when the car is released, is rolled into an invoice, and can then be credited, un-credited by an appeal, and credited again by a second-round decision. At no point does an operator type in an amount. The consequence for the implementer is that the derived-field recomputation rules in this document are not conveniences; they are the specification. A cascade that is skipped will surface as an invoice whose totals no longer reconcile.

Five constituencies use the service, and their authority is deliberately kept apart. Railroad operating staff move cars and send notices. Consignees acknowledge notices, refuse spots, release cars, and file claims and disputes. A back-office billing function creates and issues invoices, runs average-agreement reconciliation, and voids bad cycles. An adjudication function reviews and decides disputes and analyses bunching claims — and is never permitted to also raise or void the charges it is deciding. A regulator publishes tariffs and decides complaints against them. The separation is not cosmetic: several of the rules below exist purely to make sure no single actor can both create a charge and forgive it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role Authority

Every request to the service — without exception, including list and read operations — must carry two headers. `X-User-Role` names the operational capacity the caller is acting in, and `X-User-ID` identifies the individual actor. The service recognises exactly five roles, spelled: `Customer`, `RailroadAgent`, `DemurrageAdjudicator`, `RegulatoryOfficer`, and `BillingAdmin`. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is absent, present but an empty string, or present but carrying any value other than those five is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before any path parameter is resolved, so an unauthenticated request aimed at an identifier that does not exist returns 401 and never 404 — the caller learns nothing about which identifiers are real.

`X-User-ID` is required on every endpoint whose acting role is `Customer` or `DemurrageAdjudicator`, and its absence or emptiness on such an endpoint is also `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) rather than a schema error. The reason is that both of those roles need the user identifier to make an authorization decision: consignee identity resolution for the former, segregation of duties for the latter.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` differs by role, and the difference matters. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting consignee record; ownership checks on any resource that references a consignee compare that resource's consignee reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `RailroadAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operating-employee identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the railroad side of the service is not tenant-scoped, so the identifier never restricts which cars or facilities the agent may touch, and it is not stored. When `X-User-Role` is `DemurrageAdjudicator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque adjudicator identifier, and this is the **only** role whose user identifier is persisted: beginning a review stamps it into the dispute's `first_level_adjudicator_id` or `appeal_adjudicator_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `RegulatoryOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque regulator identifier that the service may record in its logs but never compares to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `BillingAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque back-office identifier that is not persisted and not compared to any entity's `id`.

Authority is granted per operation. A caller whose role does not appear in the authorized-role list for the operation it invoked is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403), and that rejection happens before the service checks whether the identifiers in the request exist — so a `RailroadAgent` attempting to create an invoice against a fabricated consignee identifier receives 403 and learns nothing about the identifier. §4 carries the authoritative per-operation role list.

Consignee-scoped access is enforced on top of role authority. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer` and the request addresses a specific resource, `X-User-ID` must resolve to the consignee that owns it, or the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership resolves along a declared chain: a placement cycle is owned by the consignee it names; a car notification is owned through its cycle; a demurrage charge is owned through its cycle; an invoice, a dispute, a bunching claim and an average agreement are each owned by the consignee they name directly; a piece of dispute evidence is owned through its dispute; and a regulatory complaint is owned through the dispute it escalates. List operations behave differently and more quietly: a `Customer`-role caller is silently narrowed to its own rows rather than being refused, so a consignee listing placement cycles, charges, invoices, disputes or complaints simply never sees another shipper's data — and supplying a `customer_id` filter naming a different consignee yields an empty result rather than an error.

Finally, the adjudication ladder carries its own segregation-of-duties bar. The adjudicator identified by a dispute's `first_level_adjudicator_id` may not begin the review of, nor decide, that same dispute's second round; either attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The bar attaches to the stored first-level identifier and therefore follows the dispute rather than the session — a third adjudicator who never touched round one is free to take the appeal, and the round-one adjudicator remains free to work every other dispute in the system. Two role families are kept structurally disjoint for the same reason: the money ladder (creating, issuing and closing invoices, creating and reconciling average agreements, applying credit holds, voiding cycles, and driving the logical clock) belongs entirely to `BillingAdmin`, while the adjudication ladder (analysing bunching claims, beginning review, requesting information, and deciding disputes) belongs entirely to `DemurrageAdjudicator`. No role appears on both.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it must be an integer no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100, and when it is omitted the service applies 10. `offset` controls the starting position: it must be an integer no smaller than 0, and when it is omitted the service applies 0. A `limit` of 0, a `limit` of 101 and an `offset` of −1 are each rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); omitting both parameters entirely is always valid.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object alongside the array in `data`. `total_count` reports the size of the full filtered result set, not the size of the returned page, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Unless a subsection below states otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending, with `id` ascending as the tie-break, so that offset-based paging is stable across requests even when rows are created at the same logical instant.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Version Counters and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and a `version` integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation that the narrative below declares. Two resources are deliberately lighter: a car notification and a piece of dispute evidence carry only `id` and `created_at` — neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version`, because neither is ever mutated in place except for the single acknowledgement transition described in §3.3.

Timestamps supplied by clients follow the same discipline. Every instant accepted in a request body must be ISO 8601 with an explicit `Z` UTC designator; an instant without the `Z` suffix is a schema violation and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Instants the service computes are always rendered with three fractional digits, and every deadline or expiry instant the service computes lands on the last representable millisecond of its date, namely `23:59:59.999Z`.

Because free time, dispute windows and adjudication deadlines are all measured against "now", the service does not read the host clock. It maintains a **logical clock**, and an admin-only test-driving capability sets it from a supplied `to_iso8601` instant, returning the newly effective value as `current_utc`. On a cold start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it moves only through that capability. Moving the clock never recomputes anything retroactively: figures already frozen stay frozen, and only requests arriving after the move see the new "now".

### 2.5. Three Day Calendars, and Why They Must Not Be Conflated

Three distinct notions of "a day" coexist in this domain, and mixing them is the single most common source of an off-by-one charge.

The first is the **eligible date**, which is tariff-dependent. When the governing tariff's `weekend_excluded` flag is false, every calendar date is eligible. When it is true, UTC Saturdays and Sundays are not eligible: they are neither consumed as free days nor charged as demurrage days. Wherever this document says "the *n*th eligible date after a given date", the rule is: for *n* equal to zero the answer is the given date itself; otherwise walk forward one calendar day at a time from the given date, counting only eligible dates, and stop on the *n*th one — so for any *n* of at least one, the answer is strictly later than the starting date.

The second is the **business date**, which is always Monday through Friday in UTC and is never affected by any tariff's `weekend_excluded` setting. Adjudication deadlines and regulatory response deadlines are measured in business dates using the same forward walk: the *n*th business date after a given date is found by stepping forward one calendar day at a time and counting only Mondays through Fridays.

The third is the plain **calendar date**, used where neither weekends nor tariffs matter — most importantly for the invoice dispute window, which is a run of consecutive calendar days regardless of what falls inside it. A facility's own `operates_weekends` flag is a fourth, narrower switch that governs storage days only, and it is independent of the tariff's `weekend_excluded` flag; a yard may operate weekends under a tariff that excludes them, and the reverse is equally legal.

### 2.6. Money Discipline: Integer Cents and the Invoice Money Invariant

All monetary amounts in this service are **integer counts of cents**. There are no decimal amounts anywhere in a request or a response body, and there is therefore no currency formatting concern; what there is instead is a rounding concern, because several rules multiply a cents amount by a fraction. Three different roundings appear, and each is stated at the point of use: per-line dispute credit is rounded **half up** to a whole cent; the average-agreement credit ceiling is **floored** to a whole cent; and the rolling bunching baseline, the halved evidence weight, and the storage hourly day count each use their own explicitly named rounding. Where a fraction is multiplied against a per-line amount, the rounding is applied **per line and then summed** — never applied once to the summed total. The two orders of operation do not agree, and the discrepancy is real money: two lines whose gross amounts are 47550 and 12550 cents, credited at a fraction of 0.65, yield 39066 cents when each line is rounded and then added, but 39065 cents if the sum is rounded once.

One invariant must hold on every invoice after every transition described in §3: `total_net_cents` plus `total_credit_cents` equals `total_gross_cents`. A related invariant holds per charge line: `credit_applied_cents` never exceeds `gross_amount_cents`, and `net_amount_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` less `credit_applied_cents`. Both are consequences of the derived-field discipline in §2.9 rather than independent rules, but an implementation that satisfies the cascades and violates the invariants has a bug in the cascades.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency: Presenting the Parent's Version

Two create operations mutate a parent resource as a side effect and therefore require the caller to prove it has seen that parent's current state. Both use optimistic concurrency control against the parent's `version` counter, and there is no locking capability and no retry token anywhere in this service — presenting the version is the only sanctioned mechanism.

`POST /demurrage/placement-cycles` requires a non-optional `expected_rail_car_version` field carrying the `version` the client last observed on the rail car it is ordering. If that value does not equal the car's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_RAIL_CAR_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments the car's `version` by 1 and re-derives its `active_cycle_count`, so a second create replaying the first request's `expected_rail_car_version` necessarily fails — which is exactly how two agents ordering the same car serialise into one cycle rather than two.

`POST /demurrage/disputes` requires a non-optional `expected_invoice_version` field carrying the `version` the client last observed on the invoice it is disputing. If that value does not equal the invoice's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments the invoice's `version` by 1 and re-derives its `dispute_count`, so a replayed `expected_invoice_version` fails and the client cannot silently overwrite a dispute roster it has not observed.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field of any kind. Where a mutation is an action on an already-identified resource, the state machine itself provides the serialisation.

### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder: How Competing Failures Are Ordered

A single malformed request frequently violates several rules at once, and callers depend on getting the *same* answer every time. Every endpoint therefore evaluates its applicable checks in one fixed global order, and **the first check whose violation condition holds wins and short-circuits the rest** — no response ever reports two violations. The order, from first evaluated to last, is:

1. **Role-header authentication.** The `X-User-Role` header must be present, non-empty and one of the five recognised names, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. This applies to every endpoint.
2. **User-identifier authentication.** On every endpoint whose acting role is `Customer` or `DemurrageAdjudicator`, `X-User-ID` must be present and non-empty, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. **Role authorization.** The caller's role must appear in the endpoint's authorized-role list, or `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN`. This applies to every endpoint.
4. **Consignee ownership.** On every endpoint reachable by the `Customer` role that addresses a specific resource, the resolved owner must equal `X-User-ID`, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
5. **Segregation of duties.** On beginning the review of, and on deciding, a dispute whose current round is the second, the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `first_level_adjudicator_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. These are the only two operations this check guards.
6. **Path existence.** Every identifier appearing in the URL path must resolve to a live row, resolved left to right along the path, or the addressed entity's own not-found code at HTTP 404. There is exactly one such code per addressable entity: `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TARIFF_RULE_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RAIL_CAR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEMURRAGE_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUNCHING_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEMURRAGE_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AVERAGE_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REGULATORY_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`. Arrival notices and dispute exhibits have no not-found code of their own because neither is individually addressable.
7. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign reference in the request body must resolve, or the referenced entity's own not-found code at HTTP 404. This guards the six create operations that take references: creating a placement cycle, an invoice, an average agreement, a bunching claim, a dispute, and a regulatory complaint. Body references are resolved in the order they are declared for that operation, so a request carrying two bad identifiers reports the first-declared one.
8. **Request schema.** Field types, enum membership, string lengths and character sets, numeric ranges, and the required-field set must all be satisfied, or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This applies to every endpoint that takes a request body, and it covers unrecognised enum members, a non-integer where cents are expected, an instant lacking its `Z` designator, an `agreement_month` that does not match the pattern `^[0-9]{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])$`, and a `credit_cap_ratio` outside the range greater than 0 and up to and including 1. Note that a `customer_code` containing hyphens is perfectly valid while one containing lower-case letters is not.
9. **Pagination schema.** On every list endpoint, `limit` and `offset` must satisfy §2.3, or `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
10. **State.** On every action endpoint, the addressed resource must currently be in a state from which the requested transition exists, or the addressed entity's own invalid-state code at HTTP 409 — `ERR_INVALID_MOVEMENT_STATE` for a movement action on a placement cycle, and `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTABLE`, `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CHARGE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_TARIFF_INVALID_STATE` for the others. This band sits above the temporal and fiscal bands, so an invoice that is both closed and past its dispute deadline reports the state failure and not the deadline failure.
11. **Optimistic concurrency.** Where §2.7 requires a parent version, the presented value must match, or the rule's 412 code. Placing this band below existence and state but above every business rule is deliberate: a stale reader is told its read is stale before it is told about a conflict it could not possibly have seen.
12. **Physical safety.** A facility's `access_mode` must not be `EMBARGOED` for constructive placement or for release, or `ERR_FACILITY_EMBARGOED`; and it must be exactly `OPEN` for actual placement, or `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_OPEN`. Physical safety outranks money in this ladder: an embargoed track blocks a release even when the consignee is simultaneously on credit hold.
13. **Tariff referential integrity.** For creating a placement cycle and for constructive placement, the pinned tariff must not be withdrawn, its ownership scope must cover the car, and it must be in force — checked in exactly that order, yielding `ERR_TARIFF_WITHDRAWN`, then `ERR_TARIFF_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_TARIFF_NOT_PUBLISHED`, all at HTTP 422.
14. **Reference pairing.** Optional references must agree with the discriminator that governs them, or `ERR_DISPUTE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, and failing that the disputed line must belong to the disputed invoice, or `ERR_CHARGE_INVOICE_MISMATCH` — both HTTP 422.
15. **Car occupation exclusivity.** A new placement cycle must not overlap an existing cycle's occupation of the same car, or `ERR_CAR_CYCLE_OVERLAP`.
16. **Facility capacity and switching.** For actual placement, spare spot capacity is checked first — `ERR_FACILITY_SPOT_UNAVAILABLE` — and only then the switch buffer, `ERR_FACILITY_SWITCH_BUFFER`. Capacity comes first because a full track makes the buffer moot.
17. **Attempt counters.** Refusals, information requests and appeals each carry a per-resource attempt ceiling, yielding `ERR_REFUSAL_LIMIT`, `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT` and `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT` respectively at HTTP 409.
18. **Dispute window.** Submitting a dispute requires the logical clock to be at or before the invoice's dispute deadline, or `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
19. **Event chronology.** Recorded instants must be consistent with the instants already on the cycle, or `ERR_NON_CHRONOLOGICAL_EVENT`. This guards five operations: constructive placement, sending a notice, acknowledging a notice, actual placement, and release.
20. **Window and period ordering.** A bunching claim's window and an invoice's billing period must each be non-inverted, and a claim window may span at most 14 calendar dates, or `ERR_INVALID_CLUSTER_WINDOW`.
21. **Claim window exclusivity.** A new bunching claim's window must not overlap one already granted in whole or in part for the same consignee, or `ERR_CLUSTER_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
22. **Agreement month exclusivity.** A new average agreement must not duplicate an open agreement for the same month, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_MONTH`.
23. **Fiscal posture.** Release requires the consignee's `billing_posture` to be `CURRENT`, or `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 402). Being the lowest band in the ladder, it surfaces only when the facility is releasable and every other check has already passed.
24. **Non-empty prerequisite sets.** Issuing an invoice requires at least one live attached charge, or `ERR_INVOICE_NO_CHARGES`; submitting or resubmitting a dispute requires a non-empty evidence set, or `ERR_DISPUTE_NO_EVIDENCE`.
25. **Claim ceilings.** `claimed_credit_cents` must not exceed the gross of the dispute's resolved target set, or `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_CHARGE`; and `ordered_credit_cents` on a regulatory decision must not exceed the escalated dispute's `claimed_credit_cents`, or `ERR_ORDERED_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`.
26. **Decision consistency and grounds prerequisites.** `allowance_pct` must agree with `decision`, or `ERR_ALLOWANCE_PCT_MISMATCH`; and a dispute whose `grounds` is `BUNCHING` must rest on a claim that was granted, or `ERR_BUNCHING_CLAIM_NOT_GRANTED`.
27. **Exhaustion and analysability.** A regulatory complaint requires an exhausted dispute, or `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_EXHAUSTED`; analysing a bunching claim requires at least one delivery in the window, or `ERR_CLAIM_NO_DELIVERIES`.
28. **Notice availability.** Acknowledging a notice requires an acknowledgeable notice to exist, or `ERR_NO_PENDING_NOTIFICATION`; and releasing a car from an industry track requires that a notice was in fact sent, or `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_SENT`. Both are HTTP 422 and both sit at the very bottom of the ladder.

Two rules of thumb follow from the ordering and are worth stating outright. First, authorization always precedes existence, so a caller who is not permitted to perform an operation never learns whether the identifiers it supplied were real. Second, the state band precedes every temporal and fiscal band, so an operation that is impossible in the resource's current state always reports the state failure even when a deadline has also lapsed or a balance is also unpaid.

### 2.9. Derived Fields, Cascades and Version Semantics

A large fraction of the fields in this service are **read-only derived values**. A derived field is never patched incrementally; it is always recomputed from scratch from its source rows at every moment the narrative says it is re-derived. This distinction is load-bearing rather than stylistic: a voided charge line disappears from an aggregate purely because it stops matching the aggregate's filter, and an implementation that maintains running counters instead will drift the moment a void or an appeal reverses something.

Whenever an operation mutates a resource, that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock and its `version` increases by exactly 1. When an operation cascades into a second resource — an invoice whose totals are rewritten, a consignee whose balance is re-derived, a rail car whose cycle count changes, a facility whose occupancy changes — the narrative in §3 states explicitly whether that second resource's `version` also increments. Where §3 says it does, the increment is part of the contract, because it is what makes the optimistic-concurrency rules of §2.7 bite; where §3 is silent, no increment occurs on that secondary resource. Cascade order does not matter to the caller, but completeness does: every cascade named in §3 must have happened by the time the response is written, because the service is immediately consistent and offers no eventual-consistency window.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right. Where a resource is addressed beneath a parent — a cycle's notifications, a dispute's evidence, a consignee's average agreements, a consignee's bunching claims — the parent identifier is resolved and reported as missing before the service looks at anything nested beneath it, and a nested collection is always scoped to the parent named in the path rather than being filtered afterwards.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Consignees, Facilities, Rail Cars and Published Tariffs

Nothing can be billed until the four reference collections are populated, and each is owned by a different part of the organisation.

A `BillingAdmin` registers a consignee. The payload carries a `customer_code` — the shipper's billing code, between 1 and 16 characters, restricted to upper-case letters, digits and the hyphen, and unique across all consignees, so a code containing a hyphen is acceptable while one containing a lower-case letter is not — together with the consignee's legal `name` and the `serving_station_code`, between 1 and 8 characters, of the railroad station that serves it. Two operational parameters may be supplied and both have defaults. `notification_method` names the channel the railroad is contractually obliged to use for car-arrival notices and is one of `EDI`, `EMAIL` or `FAX`, defaulting to `EDI`; the choice has a sharp consequence explained in §3.3, because a `FAX` consignee's clock does not start until it positively acknowledges the notice. `normal_daily_placement_capacity` records how many cars the plant can absorb per UTC day, must be zero or greater, and defaults to 2; it is the baseline one of the two bunching methods uses. A newly registered consignee has `status` `ACTIVE`, `billing_posture` `CURRENT`, `credit_hold_reason` null, `credit_balance_cents` zero and `dispute_posture` `CLEAR`. The `status` field also admits the value `SUSPENDED`, but no operation in this service ever writes it — the member is reserved.

Three consignee fields are read-only and are re-derived rather than assigned. `credit_balance_cents` is the sum of `credit_applied_cents` across every charge line whose placement cycle belongs to this consignee and whose own status is either `FINAL` or `ADJUSTED`. `dispute_posture` is evaluated in a strict order: it is `ESCALATED` if any of this consignee's disputes is in status `ESCALATED`; otherwise it is `UNDER_REVIEW` if any of them is in status `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `APPEALED`; otherwise it is `CLEAR`. And `billing_posture` is written by exactly one capability: a `BillingAdmin` applies or lifts a credit hold by supplying a boolean `on_hold` together with a `reason`. A true `on_hold` sets `billing_posture` to `CREDIT_HOLD` and records the `reason` in `credit_hold_reason`; a false `on_hold` returns `billing_posture` to `CURRENT` and clears `credit_hold_reason` back to null. Either way the consignee's `version` increments by 1. The hold is strictly prospective: it blocks future releases, as §3.5 describes, and does not disturb any cycle that has already been released.

A `BillingAdmin` also registers facilities, and the facility's `kind` is the single most consequential field in the whole reference layer because it decides which clock bills the car. An `INDUSTRY_TRACK` produces demurrage or detention from the placement clock; a `RAIL_YARD` or an `INTERMODAL_TERMINAL` produces storage from the dwell clock. The payload carries a `facility_code` of 1 to 16 characters, unique across facilities, and then a run of optional parameters: `kind` itself, defaulting to `INDUSTRY_TRACK`; `storage_clock_mode`, either `CALENDAR_DAY_MIDNIGHT` (the default) or `HOURLY_FROM_ARRIVAL`, and entirely ignored when the facility is an industry track; `gate_open_hour` and `gate_close_hour`, each a UTC hour from 0 to 23 and defaulting to 7 and 16 respectively; `car_spot_capacity`, at least 1 and defaulting to 3; `switch_buffer_minutes`, zero or greater and defaulting to 120 — that is, two hours; `storage_free_hours`, a multiple of 24 between 0 and 240 and defaulting to 48, which is two free days; `storage_daily_rate_cents`, zero or greater and defaulting to 9000 cents per storage day; and `operates_weekends`, a boolean defaulting to false. A new facility has `access_mode` `OPEN`, `access_mode_reason` null, `occupied_spot_count` zero and `last_switch_at` null.

The two derived facility fields describe live occupancy. `occupied_spot_count` is the number of placement cycles whose facility is this one and whose `movement_status` is currently `ACTUALLY_PLACED`. `last_switch_at` is the latest instant the facility saw switching activity: across every placement cycle at this facility, take the greatest of the non-null values among that cycle's `actual_placement_at` and `released_at`, and then the greatest such value across all of those cycles; it is null while the facility has no cycles at all.

Access is controlled by the railroad, not by billing. A `RailroadAgent` changes a facility's posture by supplying a new `access_mode` — `OPEN`, `GATE_CONTROLLED` or `EMBARGOED` — together with a `reason` that is recorded in `access_mode_reason`, and the facility's `version` increments by 1. The three postures are not a simple ladder: `OPEN` permits both actual placement and release; `GATE_CONTROLLED` blocks a new spot but still permits a release, which is why the release check is strictly wider than the placement check; and `EMBARGOED` blocks both. Cars already sitting at the facility are unaffected by any of the three — an embargo prevents new movements, it does not evict what is already spotted.

Rail cars belong to the railroad and are registered by a `RailroadAgent`. Each carries a `car_mark`, the reporting mark plus number in 1 to 16 characters and unique across cars, and an `ownership` value that is mandatory and is one of `RAILROAD_OWNED`, `PRIVATE` or `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED`. Two descriptive fields are optional: `car_type`, defaulting to `BOXCAR`, and `capacity_tons`, a nominal load limit between 1 and 200 defaulting to 100. A new car begins with `active_cycle_count` zero and `version` 1. The count is derived, not stored incrementally: it is the number of placement cycles on this car whose `movement_status` is neither `RELEASED` nor `VOIDED`. `ownership` deserves a warning that §3.6 makes concrete — it selects both the free-time allowance and the rate ladder, but it selects them through **two different predicates**. `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` takes the railroad free-time allowance while riding the private rate ladder, so the two decisions must not be collapsed into a single branch.

A `RegulatoryOfficer` authors and publishes tariffs. A new rule set carries a `tariff_code` of 1 to 16 characters — the publication code, such as 6004-C — and may specify any of fifteen further parameters, each of which takes a default when omitted. `car_ownership_scope` restricts which cars the rule set may govern and is `RAILROAD_OWNED`, `PRIVATE` or `ANY`, defaulting to `ANY`; note that a scope of `PRIVATE` covers both `PRIVATE` and `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` cars, while `ANY` covers all three ownerships. `free_time_hours_railroad` is the allowance granted to `RAILROAD_OWNED` and `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` cars and defaults to 48 hours, which is two free days; `free_time_hours_private` is the allowance granted to `PRIVATE` cars and defaults to 24 hours, a single free day. Both must be multiples of 24 in the range 0 to 240, and the free-day count actually used is the hours divided by 24 with any remainder discarded — a division that is exact only because of the multiple-of-24 constraint. The rate ladder is expressed as three tiers: `tier1_day_count` days at the tier-one rate, defaulting to 2 days; `tier2_day_count` days at the tier-two rate immediately after, defaulting to 3 days; and every further day at the tier-three rate. Each tier carries two per-diem rates, one for each ladder: `tier1_rate_cents_railroad` defaults to 12550 cents and `tier1_rate_cents_private` to 8150; `tier2_rate_cents_railroad` defaults to 22450 and `tier2_rate_cents_private` to 14950; `tier3_rate_cents_railroad` defaults to 35050 and `tier3_rate_cents_private` to 24050. Every rate and both day counts must be zero or greater. Three further parameters complete the rule set: `weekend_excluded`, a boolean defaulting to false, which when true makes UTC Saturdays and Sundays ineligible in the sense of §2.5; `notification_grace_hours`, from 0 to 48 and defaulting to 4, the hours that must elapse after a non-fax notice is sent before the constructive clock may run; and `adjudication_days_base`, from 5 to 90 and defaulting to 20, the base business-day allowance for adjudicating a dispute filed under this tariff.

A rule set is born `DRAFT` with `challenge_count` zero and is not usable until it is published. Publication moves it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and increments its `version` by 1; publication of a rule set that is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_INVALID_STATE`. Publication has one cascade: any sibling rule set sharing the same `tariff_code` and currently `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and its own `version` increments by 1. A superseded rule set can no longer back a *new* placement cycle, but placement cycles already pinned to it keep using it and are never re-rated — a point the billing organisation relies on, since a mid-month tariff revision must not retroactively change last week's invoice. Two further transitions exist and are driven from elsewhere: a rule-reasonableness complaint takes a rule set `PUBLISHED → CHALLENGED`, and the resolution of that complaint takes it either `CHALLENGED → PUBLISHED` or `CHALLENGED → WITHDRAWN`. Both are described in §3.9. A `CHALLENGED` rule set remains fully in force; being challenged is not being suspended.

### 3.2. Ordering a Car and Placing It Constructively

The **placement cycle** is the central record of this service: one car's occupation interval, from the moment the railroad orders it for a consignee to the moment it is released and billed. A `RailroadAgent` opens a cycle by supplying the `customer_id` of the consignee to be billed, the `rail_car_id` of the car whose occupation interval the cycle owns, the `facility_id` of the track, yard or terminal the car is destined for, the `tariff_rule_set_id` of the rule set that will govern it, a `waybill_number` of 1 to 24 characters referencing the movement, and the `expected_rail_car_version` that §2.7 requires. The tariff is **pinned** at this moment and is never re-read from a newer publication, which is why a superseded rule set continues to rate cycles that were created while it was in force.

A cycle deliberately carries two independent state dimensions, and confusing them is the fastest way to build this service incorrectly. `movement_status` is the physical dimension and begins at `ORDERED`; `billing_status` is the money dimension and begins at `UNBILLED`. The two advance on their own schedules — a cycle can be `INVOICED` while its car is still physically `RELEASED`, and a `RELEASED` cycle can be voided while its billing dimension already reads `INVOICED`. A freshly created cycle also has `clock_basis` `NONE`, `refusal_count` zero, `free_days` zero, `chargeable_days` zero, `early_release_days` zero, and null in each of `constructive_placement_at`, `constructive_reason_code`, `notification_sent_at`, `notification_acknowledged_at`, `actual_placement_at`, `released_at`, `clock_start_at` and `free_time_expires_at`. No charge line exists yet: a charge is born at constructive placement, not at ordering.

Creating a cycle is a write against the car, so it cascades there: the car's `version` increments by 1 and its `active_cycle_count` is re-derived. It is also the point at which the service enforces that a car hosts only one live cycle at a time. A cycle's **occupation interval** runs from its `created_at`, which is the logical clock at the moment of creation, up to but not including its `released_at` once it has been released, and from its `created_at` to positive infinity while it is any other non-voided state; a voided cycle occupies no interval at all. Because the interval is half-open at its upper end, creating a new cycle for the same car with the logical clock set to *exactly* the prior cycle's `released_at` succeeds, while setting the clock one millisecond earlier fails with `ERR_CAR_CYCLE_OVERLAP`. Note the ordering consequence spelled out in §2.8: the stale-version check on the car is evaluated *before* the overlap check, so a client working from a stale read is told its read is stale rather than being told about an overlap it had no way of seeing.

Two tariff conditions are also enforced at creation, and again at constructive placement. A rule set whose status is `WITHDRAWN` can never back a cycle and yields `ERR_TARIFF_WITHDRAWN`. A rule set whose `car_ownership_scope` does not cover the car's `ownership` — a `RAILROAD_OWNED`-scoped tariff cited for a `PRIVATE` car, for instance — yields `ERR_TARIFF_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. And a rule set that is `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` is simply not in force, yielding `ERR_TARIFF_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Only `PUBLISHED` and `CHALLENGED` rule sets pass; as §3.1 noted, a challenge does not suspend a tariff.

**Constructive placement** is the event that starts the billing story. Physically it means the railroad is holding the car — at a yard, on a hold track, outside a closed gate — because it cannot spot it at the consignee's own facility. A `RailroadAgent` records it by supplying the `constructive_placement_at` instant and a `reason_code`, which is stored as the cycle's `constructive_reason_code` and is one of `TRACK_FULL`, `GATE_CLOSED`, `CUSTOMER_HOLD`, `EMBARGO` or `YARD_ARRIVAL`. The cycle must currently be `ORDERED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MOVEMENT_STATE`; the facility's `access_mode` must not be `EMBARGOED`, or `ERR_FACILITY_EMBARGOED`; and the pinned tariff must satisfy the two conditions above. The transition is `ORDERED → CONSTRUCTIVELY_PLACED`, and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

Constructive placement creates the cycle's single **charge line**, always in status `PROVISIONAL`, always referencing its owning cycle through `placement_cycle_id`, and always with `invoice_id` still null, `bunching_exempt_days` zero, `billable_days` zero, `chargeable_days` zero, `gross_amount_cents` zero, `credit_applied_cents` zero and `net_amount_cents` zero. The line carries no tariff reference of its own: it is not projected with a `tariff_rule_set_id`, and every rating or re-rating of the line reaches the governing rule set through the `tariff_rule_set_id` pinned on its owning cycle. What differs is `charge_kind`, and it is decided by the facility's kind crossed with the car's ownership: a `RAILROAD_OWNED` car on an `INDUSTRY_TRACK` produces a `DEMURRAGE` line; a `PRIVATE` or `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` car on an `INDUSTRY_TRACK` produces a `DETENTION` line; and a car at a `RAIL_YARD` or an `INTERMODAL_TERMINAL` produces a `STORAGE` line regardless of who owns it. The line also carries a `computation_basis` recording which clock produced its day count — `CONSTRUCTIVE` or `ACTUAL` for demurrage and detention, and `STORAGE_CALENDAR` or `STORAGE_HOURLY` for storage — which is frozen when the line is finalised.

Recording constructive placement also re-runs the clock-basis election of §3.3, but on its own it cannot start a clock: with no arrival notice yet on file, `clock_basis` remains `NONE`. Charges are read through their own retrieval capabilities and are never created or edited directly; a charge can be fetched individually, or listed with optional filters on `placement_cycle_id`, `invoice_id`, `charge_kind` and `status`, ordered by `created_at` ascending then `id` ascending, with a `Customer`-role caller silently narrowed to charges whose cycle belongs to it. Cycles themselves can likewise be fetched individually or listed with optional filters on `customer_id`, `rail_car_id`, `facility_id`, `tariff_rule_set_id`, `movement_status` and `billing_status`, ordered the same way so that offset paging is stable.

Finally, every instant a caller records on a cycle is checked for chronological consistency against the instants already there, and a violation is `ERR_NON_CHRONOLOGICAL_EVENT`. These comparisons are always between *recorded* instants and never against the logical clock, so back-dating an entire cycle is perfectly legal as long as the recorded instants remain mutually consistent.

### 3.3. Arrival Notice, Acknowledgement and the Election of the Clock Basis

A constructive placement without a notice bills nothing. The railroad's obligation is to tell the consignee the car is waiting, and the service records each attempt as a **car notification**. A `RailroadAgent` sends one by supplying the `channel` the notice actually went out on — `EDI`, `EMAIL` or `FAX` — and the `sent_at` instant it left the railroad. The cycle must be in `movement_status` `CONSTRUCTIVELY_PLACED` or `ACTUALLY_PLACED`, and `sent_at` must be at or after the cycle's `constructive_placement_at`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_NON_CHRONOLOGICAL_EVENT`. Sending a notice while the car is constructively placed is a self-transition — the cycle stays `CONSTRUCTIVELY_PLACED` — but it is a mutation nonetheless, and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

Each notification records the cycle it belongs to through `placement_cycle_id`, its `channel`, its `sent_at`, an `acknowledged_at` that is null until acknowledged, a `sequence_number`, and a derived `delivery_status`. The `sequence_number` is this notice's 1-based position among the cycle's notifications ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `delivery_status` is decided in a fixed order: if the `channel` used differs from the consignee's contractual `notification_method`, the notice is `FAILED`; otherwise, if the channel is `EDI`, the notice is `DELIVERED`; otherwise it is `SENT`. In other words a matching `EDI` notice is deemed delivered on transmission, while a matching `EMAIL` or `FAX` notice is merely sent. A cycle's notifications can be listed with an optional `delivery_status` filter.

The cycle exposes two derived instants that summarise its notice history. `notification_sent_at` is the `sent_at` of the **first** notification on the cycle whose `delivery_status` is `SENT`, `DELIVERED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, taking notifications in order of `sent_at` ascending and breaking ties by `created_at` ascending; it is null when no such notification exists. Because a `FAILED` notice never qualifies, a notice transmitted on the wrong channel contributes nothing at all — the consignee's clock simply never starts, and any later attempt to release the car from an industry track is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_SENT`. `notification_acknowledged_at` is the `acknowledged_at` of whichever notification was most recently moved to `ACKNOWLEDGED` on this cycle, and is null when none has been.

Acknowledgement is the consignee's lever. A `Customer` acknowledges by supplying an `ack_at` instant. The service selects the notification to acknowledge rather than accepting one from the caller: it takes the notification whose `delivery_status` is `SENT` or `DELIVERED` and whose `sent_at` is greatest, breaking a tie by the greatest `created_at`. If the cycle has no such notification — because its only notice `FAILED` on the wrong channel, or because every eligible notice is already `ACKNOWLEDGED` — the request is refused with `ERR_NO_PENDING_NOTIFICATION`. The supplied `ack_at` must be at or after the selected notification's `sent_at`, or `ERR_NON_CHRONOLOGICAL_EVENT`. On success the selected notification moves to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, its `acknowledged_at` is set to `ack_at`, the cycle's `notification_acknowledged_at` is re-derived, the clock-basis election below is re-run, and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

**Electing the clock basis** is the calculation that decides what the consignee is actually billed for, and it is re-run after every one of five events: constructive placement, sending a notice, acknowledging a notice, actual placement, and refusal. It proceeds in five steps and writes three fields — `clock_basis`, `clock_start_at` and `free_time_expires_at` — alongside `free_days`.

The first step establishes when the notice became **legally effective**. For a consignee whose `notification_method` is `FAX`, the effective instant is the cycle's `notification_acknowledged_at`, full stop: a fax is effective only on positive acknowledgement, and until the consignee acknowledges, there is no effective notice however many faxes were sent. For a consignee on any other method, the effective instant is null when `notification_sent_at` is null, and otherwise is `notification_sent_at` advanced by the pinned tariff's `notification_grace_hours` — with the default of 4, four hours after the notice went out.

The second step establishes the **constructive start**: it is null whenever the effective notice instant is null, and otherwise it is the later of the cycle's `constructive_placement_at` and the effective notice instant. The clock cannot start before the car was held, and it cannot start before the notice was effective; whichever of those two happened last is the start.

The third step computes `free_days`. When the car's `ownership` is `PRIVATE`, it is the tariff's `free_time_hours_private` divided by 24 with any remainder discarded; for `RAILROAD_OWNED` and for `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` alike, it is `free_time_hours_railroad` divided by 24 the same way. This is the predicate that gives `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` the railroad allowance, and it is a *different* predicate from the one §3.6 uses to pick the rate ladder.

The fourth step defines the expiry of any candidate start instant: take the calendar date of that instant, walk forward to the `free_days`-th eligible date after it in the sense of §2.5 — which is that same date itself when `free_days` is zero — and take the last millisecond of that date, `23:59:59.999Z`. So a Friday start with two free days and a tariff that excludes weekends expires at the end of the following Tuesday.

The fifth step elects the basis. If the cycle has no `actual_placement_at` yet, then a null constructive start means no clock can run at all — `clock_basis` is `NONE`, and both `clock_start_at` and `free_time_expires_at` are null — while a non-null constructive start yields `clock_basis` `CONSTRUCTIVE`, `clock_start_at` equal to the constructive start, and `free_time_expires_at` equal to that start's expiry. If the cycle *does* have an `actual_placement_at` but the constructive start is null, the basis is `ACTUAL` with `clock_start_at` equal to `actual_placement_at` and the expiry computed from it. Otherwise both candidates exist, and the tie is broken by comparing the actual placement against the constructive expiry: when `actual_placement_at` is at or before the constructive expiry, the railroad got the car spotted inside the constructive free time and the consignee is entitled to a fresh clock, so the basis is `ACTUAL` with `clock_start_at` at `actual_placement_at` and its own expiry; when `actual_placement_at` is after the constructive expiry, the railroad blew through the free time, the constructive clock stands, and the late spot buys the consignee nothing — the basis is `CONSTRUCTIVE` with the constructive start and the constructive expiry.

Three boundaries in that fifth step are worth calling out because they are one millisecond apart. An `actual_placement_at` exactly equal to the constructive expiry elects `ACTUAL`; one millisecond later elects `CONSTRUCTIVE`. And a refusal, described next, sets `actual_placement_at` back to null, which flips an `ACTUAL` basis back to `CONSTRUCTIVE` and moves `free_time_expires_at` *backwards* — the only situation in this service where a derived expiry retreats.

### 3.4. Spotting the Car, Refusal, and the Physical Limits of a Track

Once the car is held and noticed, the railroad's next move is to spot it. A `RailroadAgent` records **actual placement** by supplying the `actual_placement_at` instant, taking the cycle `CONSTRUCTIVELY_PLACED → ACTUALLY_PLACED`. A cycle in any other `movement_status` — an `ORDERED` cycle that was never constructively placed, for example — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MOVEMENT_STATE`.

Four physical conditions guard the spot, and they are evaluated in the order §2.8 fixes. The facility's `access_mode` must be exactly `OPEN`; a `GATE_CONTROLLED` or `EMBARGOED` facility yields `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_OPEN`. The facility must have a spare spot, meaning its `occupied_spot_count` is strictly less than its `car_spot_capacity`, or the request yields `ERR_FACILITY_SPOT_UNAVAILABLE`; this is an aggregate condition, so several cars can each be individually placeable while the collective attempt exceeds the track's capacity. The facility's switch buffer must have elapsed, meaning either `last_switch_at` is null or the supplied `actual_placement_at` is at or after `last_switch_at` advanced by `switch_buffer_minutes` minutes, or the request yields `ERR_FACILITY_SWITCH_BUFFER`. Capacity is checked before the buffer because a full track makes the buffer moot. And `actual_placement_at` must be at or after the cycle's `constructive_placement_at`, or `ERR_NON_CHRONOLOGICAL_EVENT`.

Spotting the car cascades in four directions. The cycle's `actual_placement_at` is written and its `version` increments by 1. The clock-basis election of §3.3 is re-run, which is where `clock_basis` may flip from `CONSTRUCTIVE` to `ACTUAL` and `free_time_expires_at` may move forward. The facility's `occupied_spot_count` and `last_switch_at` are both re-derived — the latter now taking the new placement instant into account, so a second car cannot follow within the buffer. And the cycle's still-provisional charge line is recomputed.

A consignee that cannot take the car after all has a lever of its own: **refusal**. A `Customer` refuses by supplying a `refusal_reason`, which moves the cycle back `ACTUALLY_PLACED → CONSTRUCTIVELY_PLACED`. Refusal is capped: the cycle's `refusal_count` must currently be less than 2, so the first and second refusals succeed and the third is rejected with `ERR_REFUSAL_LIMIT`. A refusal on a cycle that is not `ACTUALLY_PLACED` is `ERR_INVALID_MOVEMENT_STATE`. On success the cycle's `actual_placement_at` is set back to null, `refusal_count` increments by 1, the facility's `occupied_spot_count` is re-derived downward — freeing the spot for another car — the clock-basis election is re-run and reverts `clock_basis` to `CONSTRUCTIVE` with `clock_start_at` back at the constructive instant, the provisional charge is recomputed, and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

While a cycle remains unreleased, its charge line stays in status `PROVISIONAL` and is recomputed on each of the three events that can move the clock — sending a notice, actual placement, and refusal — as a self-transition `PROVISIONAL → PROVISIONAL`. The recomputation is a full one: `chargeable_days` is recalculated under the provisional variant described in §3.5, and then `billable_days`, `gross_amount_cents` and `net_amount_cents` are recalculated from it. This is why a consignee watching its charge line can see the amount go both up and down as events land: a spot inside the free window pushes the expiry forward and the amount down, while a refusal pulls the expiry back and the amount up.

### 3.5. Release, Free-Time Settlement and the Two Storage Clocks

Release is the consignee's act, not the railroad's, and it is the moment the money stops moving. A `Customer` releases the car by supplying the `released_at` instant. Two source states are permitted and their preconditions differ. From `ACTUALLY_PLACED → RELEASED` is the ordinary industry-track path. From `CONSTRUCTIVELY_PLACED → RELEASED` is permitted **only** when the facility's `kind` is `RAIL_YARD` or `INTERMODAL_TERMINAL`; a car destined for an `INDUSTRY_TRACK` must be actually placed before it can be released, and attempting otherwise is `ERR_INVALID_MOVEMENT_STATE`.

Both paths share three guards. The facility's `access_mode` must be `OPEN` or `GATE_CONTROLLED` — a gate-controlled facility blocks new spots but never blocks a release — and an `EMBARGOED` facility yields `ERR_FACILITY_EMBARGOED`. The consignee's `billing_posture` must be `CURRENT`, or the release is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 402); this is the sole operation the credit hold of §3.1 actually blocks. And the supplied `released_at` must be chronologically sound: at or after `actual_placement_at` on the industry-track path, and at or after `constructive_placement_at` on the yard path, or `ERR_NON_CHRONOLOGICAL_EVENT`. The industry-track path carries one further guard: the cycle's `notification_sent_at` must not be null, or the release is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_SENT` — which is precisely what happens when the only notice on the cycle `FAILED` because it went out on the wrong channel.

A successful release writes `released_at`, finalises the cycle's `chargeable_days` and `early_release_days`, moves the charge line `PROVISIONAL → FINAL` with its amounts recomputed from `released_at` and its `computation_basis` frozen to the cycle's elected `clock_basis` or the facility's storage clock as appropriate, re-derives the facility's `occupied_spot_count` and `last_switch_at`, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. It also reaches into the consignee's average agreement: if the consignee has an agreement in status `OPEN` whose month matches the release month, that agreement's `credit_days_earned`, `debit_days_incurred` and `net_days` are re-derived and its `version` increments by 1.

**Counting chargeable days** for a demurrage or detention line works from the cycle's `free_time_expires_at` and a reference instant, and it has two variants. In the **final** variant, used once the cycle is `RELEASED`, the reference instant is `released_at`, and the release date itself counts as a chargeable day exactly when the time-of-day component of `released_at` is at or after the facility's `gate_close_hour` on the hour — that is, at or after `gate_close_hour` hours, 0 minutes, 0 seconds and 0 milliseconds. In the **provisional** variant, used while the cycle is still `CONSTRUCTIVELY_PLACED` or `ACTUALLY_PLACED` and recomputed on every notice, spot and refusal, the reference instant is the current logical clock and the reference date is *never* charged, because today is still running. That asymmetry means a provisional charge is always at least one day lighter than a same-instant final charge would be at a late gate hour.

Given the expiry instant and the reference instant, the count is built as follows. If either the expiry or the reference instant is null, `chargeable_days` is zero. Otherwise, start the count at zero and walk each calendar date from the day after the expiry date through the reference date inclusive; skip any date that is not eligible in the sense of §2.5; for a date strictly earlier than the reference date add one; and for the reference date itself add one only if the release date is chargeable under the variant in force. The mirror-image figure, `early_release_days`, is the number of eligible dates strictly after the reference date and up to and including the expiry date — the free days the consignee handed back by releasing early. At most one of `chargeable_days` and `early_release_days` can be non-zero for any cycle.

Two boundaries deserve a sentence of their own. A release one millisecond before the gate closes does not charge the release date, while a release at exactly the gate-close hour does; and because the count is over **dates** rather than 24-hour blocks, a car released thirty minutes after midnight on the day following expiry accrues one full chargeable day. A release on or before the expiry date yields `chargeable_days` of zero and a positive `early_release_days`.

Storage at a yard or an intermodal terminal is billed on an entirely different clock, and the arrival instant it works from is the cycle's `constructive_placement_at` rather than its `clock_start_at` — notification timing is simply irrelevant at a yard. Which clock runs depends on the facility's `storage_clock_mode`. Under `CALENDAR_DAY_MIDNIGHT`, first find the first chargeable date by taking the calendar date of `constructive_placement_at` and advancing it by `storage_free_hours` divided by 24 calendar days — two days at the default of 48 free hours. The storage day count is then the number of calendar dates from that first chargeable date through the date of `released_at` inclusive, counting a date only when the facility's `operates_weekends` is true or the date is a Monday through Friday. Because the free period is measured in whole dates from the arrival date, an arrival at 23:59Z burns exactly the same free date as an arrival at 00:01Z the same day. Under `HOURLY_FROM_ARRIVAL`, take the whole-millisecond dwell from `constructive_placement_at` to `released_at`, subtract `storage_free_hours` converted to milliseconds by multiplying by 3,600,000, floor the result at zero, then divide by 86,400,000 — the milliseconds in a day — and round **up** to the next whole number. A dwell of exactly `storage_free_hours` therefore yields zero storage days while one millisecond more yields one. The hourly clock ignores `operates_weekends` entirely; it never stops. The two modes genuinely disagree: the same dwell interval yields different day counts whenever the free period ends mid-week and the interval spans a weekend, because the calendar clock skips that weekend when the facility does not operate weekends and the hourly clock does not.

Whichever storage mode applies, the resulting day count is written to the charge line's `chargeable_days`, `billable_days` becomes that count less any `bunching_exempt_days` with the result floored at zero, and `gross_amount_cents` is `billable_days` multiplied by the facility's flat `storage_daily_rate_cents`. The line's `computation_basis` is frozen to `STORAGE_CALENDAR` or `STORAGE_HOURLY` accordingly. Storage charges are flat-rate: the tariff's tiered ladder of §3.6 is never applied to them.

### 3.6. Rating the Charge: the Tier Ladder, Bunching Claims and Re-Laddering

A demurrage or detention line is priced by walking the tariff's three-tier ladder, and the amount is recomputed from scratch every time either `chargeable_days` or `bunching_exempt_days` moves. The first step is always the same: `billable_days` is `chargeable_days` less `bunching_exempt_days`, floored at zero, so an exemption larger than the accrual produces zero billable days and never a negative amount.

The ladder to walk is chosen by the car's `ownership`, and this is the second of the two predicates §3.1 warned about. A `RAILROAD_OWNED` car uses `tier1_rate_cents_railroad`, `tier2_rate_cents_railroad` and `tier3_rate_cents_railroad`. Every other ownership — `PRIVATE` **and** `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` alike — uses `tier1_rate_cents_private`, `tier2_rate_cents_private` and `tier3_rate_cents_private`. A `PRIVATE_ASSIGNED` car therefore receives the railroad free-time allowance while paying the private rates, and an implementation that decides both questions with one branch will misprice it.

With the ladder chosen, the days are apportioned. The number of tier-one days is the lesser of `billable_days` and `tier1_day_count`. The number of tier-two days is `billable_days` less `tier1_day_count`, floored at zero, and then capped at `tier2_day_count`. The number of tier-three days is `billable_days` less `tier1_day_count` less `tier2_day_count`, floored at zero. `gross_amount_cents` is the tier-one day count multiplied by the tier-one rate, plus the tier-two day count multiplied by the tier-two rate, plus the tier-three day count multiplied by the tier-three rate. `net_amount_cents` is then `gross_amount_cents` less whatever `credit_applied_cents` currently stands at. At the tariff defaults of 2 tier-one days and 3 tier-two days: exactly 2 billable days bills entirely at the tier-one rate; 3 billable days straddle into tier two for exactly one day; exactly 5 billable days bill nothing at the tier-three rate; and a sixth day adds exactly one tier-three day. The ladder is steep by design — at the railroad defaults the tier-three rate of 35050 cents per day against the tier-one rate of 12550 cents per day means an average rate per day is never a usable shortcut for any of the arithmetic below.

The apportionment also implies a **per-day rate vector**, which §3.7 needs. For each day position from 1 through `billable_days`, that day's rate is the tier-one rate when the position is at most `tier1_day_count`, the tier-two rate when the position is greater than `tier1_day_count` and at most `tier1_day_count` plus `tier2_day_count`, and the tier-three rate otherwise.

**Bunching** is the consignee's structural defence. Cars that arrive in a cluster the plant could not possibly absorb are, by tariff convention, not the consignee's fault, and the remedy is to forgive days off the front of the accrual. A `Customer` opens a claim by supplying its own `customer_id`, a `cluster_window_start` and `cluster_window_end` bounding the alleged cluster, and a `method` electing which attribution algorithm the adjudicator must run — either `ORDERED_VS_CAPACITY` or `ROLLING_AVERAGE_BASELINE`. The window must not be inverted and must span at most 14 calendar dates, or the claim is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLUSTER_WINDOW`. The consignee must also have no other claim in status `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED` whose window overlaps this one, or `ERR_CLUSTER_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; windows are **closed** intervals, so two windows sharing even a single endpoint date overlap. A claim that was `DENIED`, or one still sitting at `SUBMITTED`, blocks nothing — a consignee is free to re-file after a denial. A new claim is born `SUBMITTED` with `baseline_per_day`, `delivered_car_count`, `exempt_days_granted` and `cluster_chargeable_days` all zero and `exempted_cycle_ids` empty. Claims can be listed per consignee with an optional `status` filter.

Analysis is the adjudicator's act and is a single request that both analyses and disposes of the claim. A `DemurrageAdjudicator` triggers it against a claim that must currently be `SUBMITTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. The claim moves from `SUBMITTED` to `ANALYZED` and then, in the same request, forks to one of `GRANTED`, `PARTIALLY_GRANTED` or `DENIED`; `ANALYZED` is therefore a transient state that is computed and left behind within one request and can never be observed by a reader. The claim's `version` increments by 1.

The analysis begins by resolving the **cluster set**: every placement cycle belonging to this consignee whose `movement_status` is not `VOIDED` and whose `constructive_placement_at` falls inside the closed window from `cluster_window_start` through `cluster_window_end`. `delivered_car_count` is the size of that set, and if it is zero the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NO_DELIVERIES`. `cluster_chargeable_days` is the sum of `chargeable_days` across the charge lines of every cycle in the set.

Next the baseline. Under `ORDERED_VS_CAPACITY`, `baseline_per_day` is simply the consignee's `normal_daily_placement_capacity`. Under `ROLLING_AVERAGE_BASELINE`, count the consignee's placement cycles whose `movement_status` is not `VOIDED` and whose `constructive_placement_at` falls on one of the seven calendar dates immediately preceding the date of `cluster_window_start`, then divide that count by 7 and discard any remainder. A consignee with no history therefore gets a baseline of zero, under which *every* car in the window is excess — which is why the two methods diverge most sharply on a brand-new consignee.

The exemption is then attributed date by date. Walk each UTC date from the date of `cluster_window_start` through the date of `cluster_window_end` in ascending order. For each date, take the cluster cycles whose `constructive_placement_at` falls on that date, ordered by `constructive_placement_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. The excess for that date is the number of those arrivals less `baseline_per_day`, floored at zero. The consignee is presumed able to absorb the *first* `baseline_per_day` arrivals of the day, so the cars exempted are the **last** ones — take that many entries from the end of the day's ordered arrival list and append them to the running exemption list. When the walk finishes, `exempted_cycle_ids` is the list of those cycles' identifiers in exactly that order: dates ascending, and within a date by `constructive_placement_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. The ordering inside a date is load-bearing, because it decides which car is forgiven.

Forgiveness then grows with position in the cluster. Taking the exemption list in order and numbering it from 1, the *k*-th exempted cycle has its charge line's `bunching_exempt_days` set to the lesser of *k* and that line's own `chargeable_days`, the line's tiered amount is recomputed, and `exempt_days_granted` accumulates the value actually written. The cap against what the line accrued is why `exempt_days_granted` can come out below the sum 1 + 2 + … + *n* that a full cluster would otherwise imply.

The disposition follows mechanically from the total. When `exempt_days_granted` is zero the claim is `DENIED`, and no charge line is touched at all: `exempted_cycle_ids` stays empty and every `gross_amount_cents` in the cluster is left exactly where it was. When `exempt_days_granted` equals `cluster_chargeable_days` the claim is `GRANTED`, and every exempted cycle's line carries its `bunching_exempt_days` with `billable_days` and `gross_amount_cents` recomputed. When `exempt_days_granted` is greater than zero but less than `cluster_chargeable_days` the claim is `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`, and only the exempted cycles' lines are rewritten while the non-exempted cycles keep their full gross. Two worked outcomes make the two methods concrete: three cars arriving on a single date against a `normal_daily_placement_capacity` of exactly 3 produce an excess of zero, an empty exemption list and a `DENIED` claim — the claim is analysed and refused, and nothing is re-rated; the same three cars analysed under `ROLLING_AVERAGE_BASELINE` for a consignee with no prior history produce a baseline of zero, all three cars exempted, and 1 plus 2 plus 3, or 6, exempt days granted.

The most important consequence of the exemption is that **the ladder is re-walked from tier one**, not clipped at the top. Exempting the first *k* days does not leave the surviving days on the tiers they originally occupied; the survivors slide down the ladder. Forgiving one day off a fourteen-day charge therefore removes a tier-three day from the bill rather than a tier-one day, and the money removed per exempt day shrinks as more days are exempted.

### 3.7. Invoicing, Average Agreements, Closing and Voiding

Billing runs on periods. A `BillingAdmin` opens an invoice by supplying the `customer_id` to be billed and a `billing_period_start` and `billing_period_end` bounding the period inclusively. An inverted period — a start later than the end — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLUSTER_WINDOW`, the same code the bunching window uses, because both are the same ordering rule applied to two different intervals. The invoice is born `DRAFT` with `total_gross_cents`, `total_credit_cents`, `total_net_cents`, `agreement_credit_cents` and `dispute_count` all zero and `dispute_deadline_at` null.

Creation is also an attachment operation. Every charge line whose status is `FINAL`, whose `invoice_id` is still null, whose cycle belongs to this consignee, and whose cycle's `released_at` falls inside the closed billing period is attached — its `invoice_id` is set to the new invoice — and each attached cycle's `billing_status` moves `UNBILLED → INVOICED`. Note that the movement dimension is untouched by this: the cycle stays `RELEASED`. The invoice's `attached_charge_count` is derived from the attachment, but the money totals deliberately stay at zero until the invoice is issued, because the credit calculation is part of issuing rather than part of assembling.

Issuing is a one-way act. A `BillingAdmin` issues an invoice that must currently be `DRAFT`, taking it `DRAFT → ISSUED`; issuing anything else is `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE`. The invoice must have at least one live attached charge, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_NO_CHARGES`. Issuing runs the rollup described below, then the average-agreement credit, writes `total_gross_cents`, `total_credit_cents`, `total_net_cents` and `agreement_credit_cents`, and sets `dispute_deadline_at` to the last millisecond — `23:59:59.999Z` — of the date 30 **calendar** days after the logical-clock date at the moment of issue. Thirty calendar days is neither thirty business days nor thirty eligible days; this is the third of the three calendars §2.5 distinguishes. Because the deadline is derived from the logical clock at issue time rather than from `billing_period_end`, re-issuing at a different clock would move it — which is precisely why issuing is one-way. The invoice's `version` increments by 1.

**Average agreements** are the credit programme that makes early releases worth something. A `BillingAdmin` enrols a consignee by supplying its `customer_id`, an `agreement_month` in `YYYY-MM` form matching the pattern `^[0-9]{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])$`, and optionally a `credit_cap_ratio` — greater than 0, at most 1, carrying at most two decimal places, and defaulting to 0.30 — which bounds the fraction of the month's invoice gross that agreement credit may absorb. A consignee may hold at most one `OPEN` agreement per month, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_MONTH`; an agreement already `RECONCILED` or `FORFEITED` for that month does not block a new one. A new agreement is `OPEN` with `credit_days_earned`, `debit_days_incurred`, `net_days` and `agreement_credit_cents` all zero. Agreements can be listed per consignee with an optional `status` filter.

The credit itself is computed at invoice issue, in eight steps. First, the month's gross is the sum of `gross_amount_cents` over the invoice's attached charges whose status is not `VOIDED`. Second, find the agreement: the consignee's agreement in status `OPEN` whose `agreement_month` equals the year-and-month of the invoice's `billing_period_start`; if there is none, `agreement_credit_cents` stays zero and nothing further happens. Third, rebuild the day ledger from scratch — never by increment, so that a voided cycle silently disappears from both sides: `credit_days_earned` is the sum of `early_release_days` over the consignee's cycles released inside the month, `debit_days_incurred` is the sum of `chargeable_days` over the consignee's charge lines whose status is `FINAL` or `ADJUSTED` and whose cycle was released inside the month, and `net_days` is `credit_days_earned` less `debit_days_incurred`. Note that the debit side reads `chargeable_days` and not `billable_days`, so a bunching exemption reduces the money owed without reducing the debit days incurred. Fourth, the spendable credit days are the lesser of `credit_days_earned` and `debit_days_incurred` — a consignee can only spend credit days against days it actually owes, so one that earned 5 credit days while owing only 3 debit days spends 3 and keeps a positive `net_days` of 2.

Fifth, spend those days on the **most expensive days first**. Build one entry for every billable day across every attached charge line, tagging each entry with that day's rate taken from the per-day rate vector of §3.6, then sort the entries by rate descending, breaking ties by the owning charge line's `created_at` ascending and then by the day position within the line ascending, and take as many entries from the front as there are spendable credit days. The uncapped credit is the sum of the rates of the taken entries. Sorting descending by rate rather than ascending by day position matters: it makes the credit larger than a naive first-day-first waterfall would, and a first-day-first implementation will under-credit every multi-tier month. Sixth, apply the contractual ceiling, which uses **floor** and not rounding: the cap is the month's gross multiplied by `credit_cap_ratio`, truncated to a whole cent. A gross of 47550 cents at the default ratio gives exactly 14265 cents, while a gross of 47551 cents also gives 14265 because the fractional cent is discarded rather than rounded up.

Seventh, `agreement_credit_cents` on the agreement is the lesser of the uncapped credit and the cap, and the same figure is written to the invoice's own `agreement_credit_cents`. The credit is landed on the lines by walking the taken day-entries in that same descending-rate order and adding each entry's rate to the `credit_applied_cents` of the charge line the entry belongs to, stopping as soon as the total landed reaches `agreement_credit_cents`, so the per-line credits always sum to exactly that figure. Storing the figure in both places is deliberate: a forfeited reconciliation has to reverse exactly what was applied. Eighth, roll up: the invoice's `total_gross_cents` is the month's gross, `total_credit_cents` is the sum of `credit_applied_cents` over the attached charges, and `total_net_cents` is the difference. Dispute credit is always zero at issue time, since no charge can be `DISPUTED` before an invoice exists to dispute.

**The rollup and the compound status.** Every event that can change what an invoice owes re-derives the whole invoice from its live charge lines rather than patching a total. A line is live when its status is not `VOIDED`; voided lines are invisible to every aggregate. The rollup sets `attached_charge_count` to the number of live lines, `total_gross_cents` to the sum of their `gross_amount_cents`, `total_credit_cents` to the sum of their `credit_applied_cents`, `total_net_cents` to the gross less the credit, and `dispute_count` to the number of disputes filed against this invoice. The money invariant of §2.6 holds by construction.

The status is then derived, and the order of the tests is part of the contract. A `DRAFT` invoice stays `DRAFT`. A `CLOSED` invoice stays `CLOSED`. Otherwise, if any live line is `DISPUTED` the invoice is `PARTIALLY_DISPUTED`; otherwise, if any live line has `credit_applied_cents` greater than zero the invoice is `ADJUSTED`; otherwise the invoice is `ISSUED`. Four consequences follow from taking those tests in that sequence and not another. A dispute denied on a line that carries no credit walks the invoice all the way back from `PARTIALLY_DISPUTED` to `ISSUED`, because nothing else is true of it any more. An appeal filed against a line that was already credited walks the invoice from `ADJUSTED` back to `PARTIALLY_DISPUTED`, because the `DISPUTED` test is asked first and wins. Voiding the only credited line on an invoice re-derives `ISSUED` out of `ADJUSTED`, because the void removes the line from the live set. And `total_gross_cents` is an invariant of the entire dispute lifecycle: granting a dispute moves money into `total_credit_cents` and out of `total_net_cents` and never touches the gross, which is why the invariant is a genuine test of the implementation rather than an accounting decoration.

**Reconciliation** settles the month. A `BillingAdmin` reconciles an agreement that must currently be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Reconciliation re-derives `credit_days_earned`, `debit_days_incurred` and `net_days` from scratch, using the same definitions the credit calculation uses, and then forks on the sign. When `net_days` is zero or positive the agreement moves `OPEN → RECONCILED` and its `agreement_credit_cents` is frozen at the value landed at issue time — the credit is honoured. When `net_days` is negative the agreement moves `OPEN → FORFEITED`: `agreement_credit_cents` is zeroed, the credit already landed on the month's charge lines is reversed, every invoice touched by that reversal is re-derived — totals and compound status alike — and has its `version` incremented, and the consignee's `credit_balance_cents` is re-derived downward. Forfeiture is a full unwind, not a flag, and an implementation that merely sets the status will leave money on invoices that no longer have a reason to carry it. Either way the agreement's `version` increments by 1.

**Closing** finishes an invoice. A `BillingAdmin` closes an invoice whose status is `ISSUED` or `ADJUSTED`, taking it to `CLOSED`. A `DRAFT` invoice cannot be closed, a `PARTIALLY_DISPUTED` invoice cannot be closed, and an invoice carrying any `DISPUTED` charge line cannot be closed; all three refusals are `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE`. Closing cascades: every attached cycle's `billing_status` moves to `SETTLED`, whether it was sitting at `INVOICED` or at `ADJUSTED`, and every dispute on the invoice whose status is `DECIDED` moves to `CLOSED`, after which the consignee's `dispute_posture` is re-derived and falls back to `CLEAR` once no dispute of theirs is open any more. The invoice's `version` increments by 1. An `ESCALATED` dispute is the one asymmetry worth stating plainly: it does not block closing — only `DISPUTED` does — but it is not swept to `CLOSED` either, because only a `DECIDED` dispute is. A closed invoice can therefore still carry an open regulatory escalation.

**Voiding a cycle** is the reversal path, and it is the widest cascade in the system. A `BillingAdmin` voids a cycle by supplying a `void_reason`, which is required and must be a non-empty string. The reason is a request-schema obligation only: unlike the `constructive_reason_code` of §3.2, it is not retained on the cycle and does not appear anywhere in the cycle's projection, so a voided cycle reads back with exactly the same field inventory it had before. Only a cycle whose `movement_status` is `ORDERED` or `RELEASED` may be voided — anything in flight between those two is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MOVEMENT_STATE` — and a cycle whose charge line sits on an invoice that is already `CLOSED` may not be voided at all, which is `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE`. Closing is thus the point of no return for the money.

Voiding a `RELEASED` cycle unwinds everything downstream of it. The charge line moves to `VOIDED` from whatever status it held — `FINAL`, `ADJUSTED` or `PROVISIONAL` — and thereby leaves every aggregate it participated in. The invoice it was attached to has its `attached_charge_count`, its three totals and its derived status re-derived, and its `version` incremented. The consignee's `OPEN` agreement has `credit_days_earned`, `debit_days_incurred` and `net_days` re-derived, which moves them downward, and `credit_balance_cents` is re-derived with them. The rail car's `active_cycle_count` is re-derived, which is what frees the car for its next cycle. The cycle's own `version` increments by 1. When the voided line was `ADJUSTED`, both its gross and its credit leave the invoice totals together, so `total_net_cents` still equals `total_gross_cents` less `total_credit_cents` afterwards; reversing only the gross would break the money invariant and is the characteristic bug of this operation.

Voiding an `ORDERED` cycle is the degenerate case of the same operation. No charge line exists yet, no invoice can be attached, and no agreement day count can have been affected, so nothing cascades except the rail car's `active_cycle_count`, which is re-derived to free the car, and the cycle's own `version`.

Invoices are readable individually and as a list filtered by optional `customer_id` and `status`. As everywhere else, a `Customer` reading the list is silently scoped to their own invoices, and a `Customer` reading one invoice that is not theirs is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 3.8. Disputes: Filing, Review, Decision, Appeal and Supersession

A dispute is the consignee's formal objection to an invoice, and it is a two-round adjudication with an evidence file, a computed deadline per round, and a strict bar on the same adjudicator hearing both rounds.

A `Customer` opens a dispute by naming the `invoice_id` under dispute, a `scope` of either `SINGLE_CHARGE` or `WHOLE_INVOICE`, `grounds` drawn from `RAILROAD_CAUSED_DELAY`, `BUNCHING`, `NOTIFICATION_FAILURE`, `RATE_UNREASONABLE` or `CLOCK_BASIS_ERROR`, a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters, a `claimed_credit_cents` of at least 1, and the `expected_invoice_version` demanded by §2.7. Three optional references may accompany it: `demurrage_charge_id`, the single line targeted; `bunching_claim_id`, the granted claim relied on as evidence; and `supersedes_dispute_id`, the earlier decided dispute on the same invoice that this filing replaces. The dispute is born `DRAFT` with `decision` at `PENDING`, `round_number` 1, `info_request_count` 0, `target_charge_count` 0, `evidence_weight` 0, `allowed_credit_cents` 0, and `allowance_pct`, `decision_rationale`, `submitted_at`, `adjudication_due_at`, `first_level_adjudicator_id` and `appeal_adjudicator_id` all null. The `customer_id` is copied from the disputed invoice's own `customer_id` and any value the caller supplies for it is ignored. Creation increments the invoice's `version` by 1 and re-derives its `dispute_count`, but **no charge changes status yet**: filing a dispute does nothing to the money, and everything financial waits for submission.

Three referential rules police the filing. The `scope` and the `demurrage_charge_id` must agree — `SINGLE_CHARGE` requires the charge reference and `WHOLE_INVOICE` requires its absence — or the filing is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`; the same pairing discipline governs the `BUNCHING` grounds against `bunching_claim_id`. If they agree but the named charge is not attached to the named invoice, the refusal is `ERR_CHARGE_INVOICE_MISMATCH`. A `supersedes_dispute_id` pointing at a dispute on a different invoice, or at one whose status is not `DECIDED`, is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`. Finally the claim ceiling is checked: `claimed_credit_cents` may not exceed the gross of the target set, which for `SINGLE_CHARGE` is that one line's `gross_amount_cents` and for `WHOLE_INVOICE` is the invoice's `total_gross_cents`, and a claim above it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_CHARGE`.

**Evidence.** A `Customer` files exhibits against a dispute, each with an `evidence_type`, an `observed_at` instant the exhibit documents, and a `description` of 1 to 500 characters. Each exhibit's `weight_points` is fixed by its class and derived server-side, with any caller-supplied value ignored: a `SWITCH_LIST` is worth 3, a `GATE_LOG` 3, an `EMAIL_NOTICE` 1, a `TRACK_EMBARGO_NOTICE` 4, and a `WEIGHT_CERTIFICATE` 2. Every exhibit re-derives the dispute's `evidence_weight` as the sum of the `weight_points` of its exhibits. Exhibits may be filed only while the dispute is `DRAFT` or `INFO_REQUESTED` — the two states in which the file is open — and an attempt in any other state is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE`. As noted in §2.4, an exhibit carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is an immutable record of something that was observed. Exhibits are listable per dispute.

**Submission** is where the money moves. A `Customer` submits a dispute that must be `DRAFT`, taking it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Four conditions gate it beyond the state test. The dispute must carry at least one exhibit, or `ERR_DISPUTE_NO_EVIDENCE`. The invoice's status must be `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_DISPUTED` or `ADJUSTED` — a `DRAFT` invoice has no settled figures to dispute and a `CLOSED` one is past argument — or `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTABLE`, which is a 409 conflict and not a 422. The logical clock must be at or before the invoice's `dispute_deadline_at`, or `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Every target charge must be `FINAL`, or `ERR_CHARGE_INVALID_STATE`. And when the grounds are `BUNCHING`, the referenced claim's status must be `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`, or `ERR_BUNCHING_CLAIM_NOT_GRANTED` — a condition tested at submission and deliberately not at filing, so a consignee may file a dispute while its bunching claim is still under analysis and submit once the claim lands. Because the state band of §2.8 sits above the temporal band, a `CLOSED` invoice whose dispute window has *also* expired reports `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTABLE` and not `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Submission sets `submitted_at` to the logical clock, freezes `target_charge_count` and `evidence_weight` for the round, computes `adjudication_due_at`, moves every target charge `FINAL → DISPUTED` and every target cycle's `billing_status` to `DISPUTED`, re-derives the invoice's `dispute_count` and status — which becomes `PARTIALLY_DISPUTED` — re-derives the consignee's `dispute_posture` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and increments the dispute's `version`, the invoice's `version` and the consignee's `version` each by 1.

**The adjudication deadline.** Each round's `adjudication_due_at` is computed from the base allowance the tariff publishes, the legal theory pleaded, and — on the first round only — the strength of the evidence file. The base is the `adjudication_days_base` of the tariff rule set pinned to the disputed charges. The grounds multiplier is 1.00 for `RAILROAD_CAUSED_DELAY`, 1.25 for `BUNCHING`, 0.75 for `NOTIFICATION_FAILURE`, 1.50 for `RATE_UNREASONABLE` and 1.00 for `CLOCK_BASIS_ERROR`. On the first round, take the base times the multiplier and round it **up** to a whole day, then subtract the evidence credit, which is the evidence weight halved and rounded **down** and then capped at 5 days; the allowance is the larger of that result and a floor of 5 days. Note the direction: a better-evidenced dispute is decided *faster*, because the weight is subtracted rather than added. On the second round the allowance ignores evidence entirely and is the larger of 5 days and the base times 0.50 rounded up. The deadline itself is the last millisecond of the allowance-th **business date** — Monday through Friday, unconditionally, and never the tariff's `weekend_excluded` calendar — counted forward from the date of `submitted_at`, at `23:59:59.999Z`.

Four consequences of that arithmetic are worth stating outright. An evidence weight of 10 halves to exactly 5 and hits the cap precisely; a weight of 12 halves to 6 but is still capped at 5, so evidence past that point buys nothing. A weight of 1 — a single `EMAIL_NOTICE` — halves down to 0 and buys no time at all. `NOTIFICATION_FAILURE` on a base of 20 gives 20 times 0.75, which is 15.0, whose ceiling is 15, *before* any evidence credit is subtracted: the multiplier is applied before the subtraction and never after. And the 5-day floor is not decoration — a base of 5 with an evidence weight of 10 would otherwise produce an allowance of 0 days.

**Taking up the review.** A `DemurrageAdjudicator` begins a review, which serves both rounds: it takes a `SUBMITTED` dispute to `UNDER_REVIEW` and equally takes an `APPEALED` one to `UNDER_REVIEW`. On the round-1 entry it records the caller's `X-User-ID` in `first_level_adjudicator_id`, but only when `round_number` is 1 and the field is still null. On the round-2 entry it records the caller in `appeal_adjudicator_id`, and the round-2 entry is barred to whoever holds `first_level_adjudicator_id` with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Crucially, `first_level_adjudicator_id` is left untouched by the round change, so the bar survives it — an implementation that overwrites the first-level identity with the appeal identity destroys the very record the bar depends on. The bar is on the first-level adjudicator alone: a third adjudicator who never touched round 1 may take the appeal, and the round-1 adjudicator remains free to work any other dispute. The dispute's `version` increments by 1.

**Requesting more information** pauses the round. A `DemurrageAdjudicator` requests information by supplying `requested_items`, taking the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`. The `info_request_count` must be below 2, so the third request is refused with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT`, and a successful request increments the counter by 1. The request **replaces** `adjudication_due_at` outright with the last millisecond of the 10th business date strictly after the current logical-clock date, rather than extending the deadline it found there; the replacement can therefore move the deadline either later or earlier depending on when the request is made. The target charges stay `DISPUTED` and no money is touched.

**Resubmitting** hands the round back. A `Customer` resubmits an `INFO_REQUESTED` dispute, taking it `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. At least one exhibit must have been filed *after* the information request — not merely one exhibit overall — or the resubmission is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_NO_EVIDENCE`. Resubmission refreshes `submitted_at` to the logical clock and re-derives `evidence_weight` over the enlarged file, but it leaves `adjudication_due_at` exactly where the information request put it: the extension the adjudicator granted stands, and recomputing the deadline from the fresh `submitted_at` would silently hand the customer a second extension.

**Deciding** the round is where credit is granted. A `DemurrageAdjudicator` decides an `UNDER_REVIEW` dispute by supplying a `decision`, an `allowance_pct` and a `decision_rationale`, taking it `UNDER_REVIEW → DECIDED`. On a round-2 decision the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `first_level_adjudicator_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `allowance_pct` must agree with the `decision`: `ALLOWED` demands exactly 1, `DENIED` demands exactly 0, and `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED` demands a value strictly between 0 and 1; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_PCT_MISMATCH`.

The credit is then allocated in four steps. First, resolve the target set in a deterministic order: under `SINGLE_CHARGE` it is the single line named by `demurrage_charge_id`; under `WHOLE_INVOICE` it is every charge on the disputed invoice whose status is `DISPUTED`, ordered by its cycle's `released_at` ascending and then by the charge's own `created_at` ascending. Second, compute the raw credit **per line and then sum**, which as §2.6 warns is not the same number as rounding the sum: each line's credit is its `gross_amount_cents` times `allowance_pct` rounded half-up to a whole cent, then reduced to its own gross if it somehow exceeded it, and the total is the sum of those per-line figures. With grosses of 47550 and 12550 cents at an allowance of 0.65 the correct total is 39066 cents while the naive round-the-sum answer is 39065. Third, enforce the consignee's own `claimed_credit_cents` ceiling with a **backwards** waterfall: if the total exceeds the claim, take the excess off the last line in the resolved order first, then the second-to-last, and so on until the excess is exhausted, so the earliest-released line keeps its credit intact and the total lands exactly on the claim. The resulting total is stored in `allowed_credit_cents`. Fourth, land the credits: each line's `credit_applied_cents` becomes its allocated figure, its `net_amount_cents` becomes its gross less that figure, and its status becomes `ADJUSTED` when the figure is positive or `FINAL` when it is 0.

Four properties of that allocation deserve emphasis. An `ALLOWED` decision credits each line its full gross and the claim ceiling may *still* bind, so the waterfall is not dead code on a full allowance. A `DENIED` decision credits nothing and returns every line to `FINAL` rather than leaving it `ADJUSTED`. Because the waterfall runs backwards, which line loses money depends on release order and not on creation order, so creating the same two charges in the opposite sequence must produce the same allocation. And a single-charge dispute still runs the per-line path, which means the per-line and round-the-sum answers coincide at one line only by coincidence and never by design.

Deciding cascades outward. Each target cycle's `billing_status` moves to `ADJUSTED`, or back to `INVOICED` when its line's credit is 0. The invoice's `total_credit_cents`, `total_net_cents` and derived status are rewritten and its `version` increments by 1. The consignee's `credit_balance_cents` — the sum of `credit_applied_cents` over every charge on that consignee's cycles whose status is `FINAL` or `ADJUSTED` — and `dispute_posture` are re-derived and its `version` increments by 1. The invoice's `total_gross_cents` does not move.

**Appealing** unwinds the round completely. A `Customer` appeals a `DECIDED` dispute by supplying an `appeal_narrative`, taking it `DECIDED → APPEALED`. The `round_number` must be below 2, so a second appeal is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT`. The appeal sets `round_number` to 2, returns `decision` to `PENDING`, `allowance_pct` to null and `allowed_credit_cents` to 0, and **fully reverses** the first-level credit: every target charge returns to `DISPUTED` with its `credit_applied_cents` restored to the pre-decision value it held, the invoice's totals and derived status return to their pre-decision values with its `version` incremented by 1, and the consignee's `credit_balance_cents` returns to its pre-decision value while `dispute_posture` re-derives to `UNDER_REVIEW`. The `adjudication_due_at` is recomputed with the appeal multiplier, ignoring evidence weight, counted from the appeal date rather than the original submission date — the appeal refreshes `submitted_at`, and the second round's clock therefore starts when the appeal is filed. As §3.7 notes, an invoice that had reached `ADJUSTED` walks back to `PARTIALLY_DISPUTED` here, because the derivation asks about `DISPUTED` lines before it asks about credited ones.

Disputes are readable individually by every role, and listable with optional `customer_id`, `invoice_id`, `demurrage_charge_id`, `bunching_claim_id`, `supersedes_dispute_id` and `status` filters, with a `Customer` caller silently scoped to their own disputes.

### 3.9. Regulatory Escalation and Challenges to the Tariff Itself

When the railroad's own two rounds are exhausted, the consignee's remaining remedy is external. A `Customer` files a regulatory complaint by naming the `demurrage_dispute_id` being escalated, a `complaint_type` of either `RULE_REASONABLENESS` or `BILLING_PRACTICE`, a `docket_narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters, and — when and only when the type is `RULE_REASONABLENESS` — the `tariff_rule_set_id` of the tariff being attacked. The two complaint types differ in their target: `BILLING_PRACTICE` challenges the railroad's conduct on this bill, while `RULE_REASONABLENESS` challenges the published tariff itself and therefore has to cite one. Supplying a tariff reference on a `BILLING_PRACTICE` complaint, or omitting it on a `RULE_REASONABLENESS` one, is the same pairing failure the dispute scope rule polices and is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

Escalation requires exhaustion. The cited dispute must be `DECIDED` **with `round_number` at least 2**, so a consignee who never appealed cannot go to the regulator; that refusal is `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_EXHAUSTED`. The two failure modes must not be confused: a dispute that is not `DECIDED` at all fails the state band first and reports `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE`, and `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_EXHAUSTED` is reserved for the narrower case of a `DECIDED` dispute that never went to appeal.

A filed complaint is born `FILED` with `answer_narrative` null and `ordered_credit_cents` 0. Its `filed_at` is the logical clock at filing. Its `docket_number` is deterministic and public: the literal `D-` followed by the zero-padded five-digit, one-based position of this complaint among all regulatory complaints ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the first complaint ever filed is `D-00001` and the two-hundredth is `D-00200`. Its `response_due_at` is the last millisecond of the 15th business date strictly **after** the filing date, at `23:59:59.999Z` — business dates again meaning Monday through Friday and never the tariff calendar, and *strictly after* meaning that a complaint filed on a Monday does not count that Monday.

Filing cascades in two directions. The escalated dispute moves `DECIDED → ESCALATED` and the consignee's `dispute_posture` re-derives to `ESCALATED`, which outranks `UNDER_REVIEW` in that field's own precedence. And when the type is `RULE_REASONABLENESS`, the cited tariff rule set moves `PUBLISHED → CHALLENGED` and its `challenge_count` increments by 1. A challenged tariff **stays in force**: as §3.2 states, a `CHALLENGED` rule set may still back a new placement cycle, because a pending challenge is an allegation and not a suspension.

**The railroad's answer.** A `RailroadAgent` answers a complaint that must be `FILED`, supplying an `answer_narrative`, which takes it `FILED → RAILROAD_ANSWERED` and increments its `version` by 1. Answering anything that is not `FILED` is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE`, as is deciding anything that is not `RAILROAD_ANSWERED`: the regulator cannot rule before the railroad has been heard.

**The regulator's decision.** A `RegulatoryOfficer` decides a `RAILROAD_ANSWERED` complaint by supplying an `outcome` and an `ordered_credit_cents`, which takes it to `DECIDED_FOR_CUSTOMER`, `DECIDED_FOR_RAILROAD` or `DISMISSED`. Two money rules bind the order. The `ordered_credit_cents` must be 0 unless the outcome is `DECIDED_FOR_CUSTOMER`, and on any outcome it may never exceed the escalated dispute's `claimed_credit_cents`; both violations are refused with `ERR_ORDERED_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`. The complaint's `version` increments by 1.

The decision's most consequential side effect is on the tariff, and it closes the tariff's own loop. On a `RULE_REASONABLENESS` complaint the cited rule set moves `CHALLENGED → WITHDRAWN` when the outcome is `DECIDED_FOR_CUSTOMER`, and `CHALLENGED → PUBLISHED` on either of the other two outcomes, with its `version` incremented either way. The return to `PUBLISHED` happens only once no other complaint still cites that rule set, and it preserves `challenge_count` rather than resetting it: the tariff carries the scar of every challenge it has survived. A `WITHDRAWN` rule set is terminal and can no longer back a new placement cycle, which is where `ERR_TARIFF_WITHDRAWN` comes from, but the withdrawal is not retroactive — cycles already pinned to it keep the rates they were rated at, exactly as a superseded rule set's cycles do.

The regulator's order is **recorded, not posted**. The escalated dispute stays `ESCALATED`: the regulator's order does not reopen the railroad's own adjudication, no charge line is rewritten, no invoice total moves, and `ordered_credit_cents` lives on the complaint alone. An implementation that helpfully credits the consignee's charges when the regulator rules for them has invented a money movement this specification does not authorise.

Complaints are readable individually and listable with optional `demurrage_dispute_id`, `tariff_rule_set_id` and `status` filters, with a `Customer` caller silently scoped to complaints on their own disputes.

**The test-driving clock.** Finally, one administrative capability exists purely so that the timelines above can be exercised. A `BillingAdmin` sets the server's logical clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response carries the resulting `current_utc`. Every subsequent request reads that instant as "now". The clock starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` on a cold start, moves only through this capability, and never recomputes anything retroactively: moving it forward past a deadline does not decide a dispute, and moving it backwards does not un-decide one.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/demurrage` prefix. Creation endpoints return HTTP 201 and every other successful call returns HTTP 200; there is no 204 anywhere, because every mutation returns the resource it changed. Every single-resource path parameter is literally `{id}`. There is no `PATCH` and no `DELETE` anywhere in this API: every mutation is a named action endpoint, and reversals are explicit actions of their own — a cycle is voided, an invoice is closed, a dispute is appealed. Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` and returns the four pagination fields of §2.3 alongside its array. Authorization is evaluated exactly as §2.1 describes: role membership first, then existence, then ownership.
