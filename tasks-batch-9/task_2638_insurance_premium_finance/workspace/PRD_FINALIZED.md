# Insurance Premium Finance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Commercial insurance is rarely paid for in one cheque. A trucking company that owes ninety thousand dollars of annual premium across four policies pays a fraction of it at binding and finances the rest, and the entity that advances that money is a licensed premium finance company. This document specifies the servicing platform for such a company: the system of record for the note the insured signs, the two-party verification of what the underlying policies actually cost, the monthly installment schedule, the late fees and statutory notices that follow a missed payment, the power of attorney that lets the finance company cancel the policies when the insured stops paying, and the settlement that follows once each carrier returns whatever premium it had not yet earned.

Three characteristics make the domain unusually demanding for an implementation. First, almost every rule is statutory rather than commercial: the maximum rate, the minimum down payment, the maximum term, the size of a late fee, the number of days of notice that must elapse before coverage may be pulled, and the method used to compute the unearned premium are all dictated by the state whose regime governs the note, and the platform must apply them per-state rather than globally. Second, money moves in cascades. A single carrier posting a return can credit a policy, credit the note, drive the note into a deficiency, mint a chargeback against the producing agency, and suspend that agency from writing new business — five effects from one request. Third, the same account carries two lifecycles at once: a financing lifecycle that runs from draft to paid-in-full, and a collection lifecycle that runs from current to cancelled, each advancing on its own triggers and neither derivable from the other.

The platform is therefore designed as a strict ledger. Every amount is an integer number of cents, every derived balance is recomputed rather than remembered, every date-sensitive rule reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, and two named conservation identities — one for payment posting and reversal, one for final settlement of a cancelled note — must hold exactly after every operation. Where the platform cannot be certain of a figure (the premium the carrier will confirm, the return the carrier will actually send), it records an estimate, records the actual, and records the variance between them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Five roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `FinanceAnalyst`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ProducerAgent`, `Insurer`, and `Insured`. A header value outside that set, or a role that is not listed among the authorized roles of the operation being called, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

A `FinanceAnalyst` originates notes, attaches the policies being financed, submits and activates agreements, posts and reverses payments, issues notices of intent to cancel, exercises the power of attorney, finances endorsements and restructures accounts. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns the statutory surface — state rate regimes, licenses, agency onboarding and suspension, carrier registration, rate exceptions, late-fee waivers, the servicing run and chargeback offsets — and may never originate or activate a note. A `ProducerAgent` is the producing agency; it attests premiums on the notes it produced, files endorsements and reads its own chargeback exposure. An `Insurer` is the carrier; it attests the premium and effective date on its own policies and posts the unearned premium it returns. An `Insured` is the borrower; it pays the note and reads its own schedule, payments, late fees, notices and payoff quote.

Two of these roles are operator identities and three are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `FinanceAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a rule demands it — as `originated_by_user_id` on an agreement at creation, as `issued_by_user_id` on a notice of intent to cancel, and as `requested_by_user_id` on a carrier cancellation request — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; it is recorded as `rate_exception_approved_by_user_id` when a rate exception is granted and as `waived_by_user_id` when a late fee is waived, and it is never matched against an entity `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ProducerAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ProducerAgent`, and ownership checks on any resource that references a producing agency compare the resource's agency foreign key — `producer_agent_id` on the agreement, directly or through the policy in the path — to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Insurer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Insurer`, and ownership checks compare the addressed policy's `insurer_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Insured`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Insured`, and ownership checks compare the addressed agreement's `insured_id` to `X-User-ID`.

An entity-backed caller that addresses a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership gate guards four operations specifically: reading a single agreement, posting a payment against an agreement, filing an endorsement on a policy, and posting a carrier return against a carrier cancellation request. An `Insured` may read and pay only its own agreement; a `ProducerAgent` may read only an agreement that names it and may endorse only a policy hanging off such an agreement; an `Insurer` may post a return only on a request whose policy names it. Attestation of premium carries its own, stricter standing rule with its own code, `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), described where verification is discussed.

Because the company is a lender rather than a broker, two decisions are subject to mandatory two-person control. The analyst who originated an agreement may not be the compliance officer who approves a priced exception on it — an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SOD_ORIGINATOR_CANNOT_APPROVE` (HTTP 403) — and the analyst who issued a notice of intent to cancel may not be the analyst who exercises the power of attorney that executes it, which is rejected with `ERR_SOD_NOTICE_ISSUER_CANNOT_EXECUTE` (HTTP 403). Both comparisons are made on `X-User-ID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list operation accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the closed range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a list operation states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest row appears first and paging through the collection is stable. List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: the `limit` and `offset` actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before paging, and `has_more`, which is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters on individual list operations are documented with the workflows that produce the rows being filtered.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Row Versions

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at the moment the row is written. Nearly every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and a `version` integer. `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row; `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical-clock instant on the same mutation. Every other instant-valued field the platform emits — an agreement's `activated_at`, an endorsement's `financed_at`, a payment's `reversed_at`, and the `current_utc` the clock control echoes — is serialized, whenever it is not null, in exactly that ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits; fields that carry a calendar date rather than an instant stay in plain `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Both `updated_at` and `version` are present on `StateRateRule`, `License`, `Insured`, `ProducerAgent`, `Insurer`, `PremiumFinanceAgreement`, `FinancedPolicy`, `Installment`, `Payment`, `LateFeeAssessment`, `CancellationNotice`, `CarrierCancellationRequest`, `PremiumEndorsement` and `AgentChargeback`.

One resource is deliberately different. A `PremiumVerification` — the attestation a carrier or an agency files against a policy — is an immutable audit record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither appears in its response payload. When a disputed policy is reopened, existing attestations are not edited; they are marked as retired and new ones are filed alongside them.

A single request frequently mutates several rows, and each mutated row bumps its own `version` independently. When a payment settles a late fee and closes out an installment, the `Payment` row, the `LateFeeAssessment` row, the `Installment` row and the `PremiumFinanceAgreement` row all advance. The agreement's `version` is the busiest counter in the system, because every derived balance on the note lives on the agreement row, and every operation described in section 3 that touches those balances advances it.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this platform reads the host's wall clock. Instead the server maintains a logical clock which every date-sensitive rule consults: grace periods, notice timelines, license effectiveness, earned days, payment receipt validation and every `created_at` stamp. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and an implementation must never fall back to real time. Throughout this document, *clock date* means the calendar date part of the logical clock in UTC.

A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `FinanceAnalyst` may move the clock forward by supplying `to_iso8601`; the operation echoes the new value as `current_utc`. The clock is monotonic: a supplied instant earlier than the current value is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422), while an instant equal to the current value is accepted. Advancing the clock has one derived consequence worth naming here — a license whose window has closed stops being effective, so the next read of that license reports it as no longer effective, and origination in that state stops without anybody touching the license row.

### 2.6. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; there are no floating-point money fields anywhere in the API. All rates, commissions, penalties and tolerances are integers denominated in basis points, where one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so 10000 basis points is exactly 100 percent. Wherever a computation divides, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent — a fractional remainder of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero.

Rounding *order* is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, and section 3 pins it for each calculation. Three rules recur and are stated once here. When interest accrues period by period on a declining balance, each period's interest is rounded to whole cents before it is added to the running total; summing exact fractional products and rounding the single total at the end produces a different figure and is incorrect. When a total must be divided across a set of installments, the quotient is floored (or rounded, where the calculation says so) for every installment except the last, and the last installment receives the exact arithmetic remainder, so that the parts sum to the total with no drift. When a per-item amount is aggregated across policies, each policy's amount is rounded first and the rounded amounts are then summed.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Referential Reachability

Identifiers in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost segment that fails to resolve decides the error. A request naming a state rate regime that does not exist fails with `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `license_id` yields `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `agent_id` yields `ERR_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `agreement_id` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `policy_id` yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `payment_id` yields `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `late_fee_id` yields `ERR_LATE_FEE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `request_id` yields `ERR_CANCEL_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `endorsement_id` yields `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; and an unknown `chargeback_id` yields `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND`. Every one of these is an HTTP 404. Because resolution is left to right, a licence read under a non-existent state regime reports the state regime as missing rather than the licence.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved in the order in which they are declared for that operation, which for agreement creation and policy attachment means `insured_id`, then `producer_agent_id`, then `state_rule_id`, then `insurer_id`. An unresolvable borrower yields `ERR_INSURED_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable agency `ERR_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable state regime `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND` and an unresolvable carrier `ERR_INSURER_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. Every foreign key in the model is reverse-reachable: an agreement can be listed by borrower, agency, state regime, restructure predecessor, financing status or collection stage; policies by agreement, carrier or status; carrier cancellation requests by agreement, policy or status; chargebacks by agency, agreement or status.

Coherence between two identifiers that name each other is enforced rather than silently ignored. When a policy is named alongside an agreement it does not belong to, the request is refused with `ERR_POLICY_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). This applies when an attestation is filed against a policy whose agreement is not the one whose verification cycle is open, when a carrier return names an `agreement_id` that is not the `agreement_id` of the request's policy, and — deliberately — when a listing of carrier cancellation requests supplies `agreement_id` and `policy_id` together as an incoherent pair. The ledger refuses an incoherent query rather than answering it with an empty page.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Notes and Chargebacks

Two write operations are legitimately repeatable and therefore cannot be protected by a state precondition: folding endorsements into a note (each new endorsement is financed by its own call) and offsetting a chargeback against an agency's reserve (partial offsets may recur until the remainder is settled). Both are protected instead by optimistic concurrency on the parent row's `version`.

Financing the pending endorsements of an agreement requires `expected_agreement_version` as a mandatory input field, carrying the `version` of the `PremiumFinanceAgreement` the client last observed. Folding endorsements in rebuilds the remaining installment schedule from the agreement's current money fields, so if the stored `version` has moved on since that read, the schedule the request intends to supersede is not the schedule the server holds; the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Offsetting a chargeback requires `expected_chargeback_version` as a mandatory input field, carrying the `version` of the `AgentChargeback` the client last observed. A partial offset is computed against the remainder the client saw, and an offset already recorded has moved both that remainder and the agency's reserve, so a mismatch against the chargeback's stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CHARGEBACK_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other operation takes a version field. Submitting, activating, voiding, paying, reversing, noticing, executing or restructuring a note carries no expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.9. Statutory Rate Regimes and Licensing

Every note is written under exactly one **state rate regime**, a `StateRateRule` row that encodes the statutes of one state and is fixed on the agreement at creation. The regime is identified by `state_code`, which is exactly two uppercase letters and must be unique across all regimes — one rate regime per state. `finance_charge_method` is either `ADD_ON` or `SIMPLE_INTEREST` and decides how the finance charge and the installment amounts are computed. `max_annual_rate_bps` is the statutory ceiling on the annual finance-charge rate in basis points, at least 1 and at most 3000, and it binds absolutely: nothing in this platform, not even an approved exception, may price above it. `max_service_fee_cents` is the ceiling on the one-time service fee that may be financed and may not be negative. `min_down_payment_bps` expresses the statutory minimum down payment as basis points of the total premium and lies between 0 and 10000 inclusive. `max_term_months` is the largest permitted number of monthly installments, at least 2 and at most 36.

Four parameters govern delinquency. `late_fee_bps` is the late fee expressed as basis points of the past-due installment amount, between 0 and 2000 inclusive; `late_fee_min_cents` is the statutory floor applied to a computed fee and may not be negative; `late_fee_max_cents` is the statutory ceiling and may not be smaller than `late_fee_min_cents`; and `max_late_fees_per_agreement` caps how many fees may ever be assessed over the life of one note, at least 1 and at most 12. `grace_period_days` is the number of days after a due date that must pass before a fee may be assessed at all, between 0 and 30 inclusive. `noic_notice_days` is the statutory number of days that must elapse between issuing a notice of intent to cancel and exercising the power of attorney, at least 5 and at most 30.

Three further parameters govern the money that flows back after a cancellation. `unearned_premium_method` is either `PRO_RATA` or `SHORT_RATE` and selects how the platform estimates a carrier's return; `short_rate_penalty_bps`, between 0 and 5000 inclusive, is the fraction withheld from the pro-rata figure under the short-rate method; and `rebate_method` is either `RULE_OF_78` or `ACTUARIAL` and selects how the unearned finance charge is rebated on payoff, cancellation or restructure. Finally, `license_required` is a boolean that defaults to true and states whether an effective premium finance license is needed to originate in that state, and `active_agreement_count` is a derived counter, starting at 0, holding the number of agreements referencing this regime whose financing status is either `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `ACTIVE`.

A `License` records a regulator-issued authority to write premium finance business in one state. It names the regime it satisfies through `state_rule_id`, carries a non-empty `license_number` that must be unique among the licenses of that regime, and defines a window through `effective_date` and `expiration_date`, both ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, with the expiration strictly after the effective date. Its `status` is `ACTIVE` on creation and can become `SUSPENDED`. The derived flag `is_effective` is true when, and only when, `status` is `ACTIVE` and the clock date falls inside the window with both bounds inclusive — that is, the clock date is on or after `effective_date` and on or before `expiration_date`. Suspension sets `is_effective` to false immediately.

Licensing is enforced at four points in a note's life: when an agreement is created, when it is submitted, when it is activated, and when it is restructured. At each, if the governing regime has `license_required` set to true and no license of that regime is effective at the clock date, the operation fails with `ERR_STATE_NOT_LICENSED` (HTTP 422). When `license_required` is false the check is skipped entirely, and a regime with an expired or suspended licence still permits origination.

### 2.10. Order of Rejection

Any single request can violate several rules at once, and the platform must always report the same one. Checks are therefore strictly ordered, and the first violated check decides the response. The ordering rationale is that authentication precedes existence, existence precedes ownership, ownership precedes optimistic concurrency, concurrency precedes state preconditions, and inside the business band the statutory and licensing checks — which can void the transaction for the finance company altogether — run before referential, temporal and finally fiscal checks. A request that is both unlicensed and mispriced reports the licensing failure, because pricing a deal the company may not legally write is moot.

The full precedence, highest priority first:

1. Both authentication headers present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The role is one of the five defined roles and is authorized for the operation — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`.
3. Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right — otherwise the corresponding not-found code from section 2.7.
4. Every foreign key in the body resolves, in declared order — otherwise the corresponding not-found code.
5. An entity-backed role owns the addressed resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This guards reading one agreement, posting a payment, filing an endorsement and posting a carrier return.
6. An attestation is filed by a party with standing on the policy — otherwise `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.
7. The body conforms to the operation's input schema: required fields present, types correct, enum values in range, cents fields non-negative integers, dates well-formed ISO 8601 calendar dates, a payment's `received_date` no later than the clock date, `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` not negative — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A payment tendered with a future `received_date` therefore reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even on a note whose financing status would fail the gate at priority 11.
8. The compliance officer approving a rate exception is not the analyst who originated the agreement — otherwise `ERR_SOD_ORIGINATOR_CANNOT_APPROVE`.
9. The analyst exercising the power of attorney is not the analyst who issued the notice being executed — otherwise `ERR_SOD_NOTICE_ISSUER_CANNOT_EXECUTE`.
10. The supplied expected version matches the parent row's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` on endorsement financing or `ERR_STALE_CHARGEBACK_VERSION` on a chargeback offset.
11. The agreement's financing `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. `DRAFT` accepts policy attachment, submission, voiding and rate-exception approval; `PENDING_VERIFICATION` accepts activation, voiding and rate-exception approval; `ACTIVE` accepts payments, notices, cancellation execution, endorsement financing and restructuring; `PAID_IN_FULL` accepts nothing except payment reversal; and `VOID`, `RESTRUCTURED`, `CANCELLED_DEFICIENT` and `CLOSED_ZERO` are terminal and accept nothing. This gate guards policy attachment, submission, activation, voiding, payment posting, notice issuance, cancellation execution, endorsement financing, restructuring and rate-exception approval.
12. The agreement's `delinquency_stage` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_DELINQUENCY_STATE_INVALID`. Issuing a notice requires `LATE` or `NOIC_ISSUED`; a stage that already carries an open notice clears this gate and is refused instead by the more specific notice-window rule below, so the caller learns the actual reason. Executing a cancellation requires `NOIC_ISSUED`. The `CURRENT`, `CANCELLATION_REQUESTED` and `CANCELLED` stages refuse both operations.
13. The policy's `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_POLICY_STATE_INVALID`. Filing an attestation requires `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, reopening requires `DISPUTED`, and filing an endorsement requires `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `VERIFIED`.
14. The notice selected for execution has status `ISSUED` — otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`; `CURED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `EXECUTED` notices cannot be executed again.
15. The carrier cancellation request has status `REQUESTED` when a return is posted — otherwise `ERR_CANCEL_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.
16. The chargeback has status `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_OFFSET` — otherwise `ERR_CHARGEBACK_STATE_INVALID`; a chargeback already `OFFSET` accepts no further offsets.
17. The endorsement has status `PENDING` when it is declined — otherwise `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.
18. The payment has status `POSTED` when it is reversed — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_REVERSED`.
19. The late fee has status `OUTSTANDING` when it is waived — otherwise `ERR_LATE_FEE_NOT_OUTSTANDING`; `PAID`, `WAIVED` and `REVERSED` fees are not waivable.
20. A policy named together with an agreement belongs to that agreement — otherwise `ERR_POLICY_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH`, on attestation filing, on carrier return posting, and on an incoherent agreement-and-policy filter pair when listing carrier cancellation requests.
21. The attesting party has no live attestation on this policy already — otherwise `ERR_VERIFICATION_ALREADY_RECORDED`.
22. The state regime is licensed at the clock date when it requires a license — otherwise `ERR_STATE_NOT_LICENSED`, on agreement creation, submission, activation and restructuring.
23. The named producing agency has status `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_AGENT_SUSPENDED`, on agreement creation, submission and restructuring.
24. The `state_code` of a new rate regime is not already registered — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_RULE`.
25. `installment_count` does not exceed the regime's `max_term_months` — otherwise `ERR_TERM_EXCEEDS_STATE_MAXIMUM`, on agreement creation and restructuring.
26. The down payment meets the statutory minimum — otherwise `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`, on submission and restructuring.
27. `service_fee_cents` is within the statutory or approved ceiling — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_FEE_ABOVE_CAP`, on agreement creation and on rate-exception approval.
28. An approved rate exception does not exceed the statutory ceiling — otherwise `ERR_RATE_ABOVE_STATE_MAXIMUM`, on rate-exception approval.
29. The agreement finances at least one policy — otherwise `ERR_NO_FINANCED_POLICIES`, on submission.
30. Every policy of the agreement is verified — otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_VERIFIED`, on activation.
31. The policy term is well formed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_TERM`, on policy attachment.
32. The policy term does not overlap an existing term for the same carrier and policy number — otherwise `ERR_POLICY_TERM_OVERLAP`, on policy attachment.
33. `first_installment_due_date` lies in the permitted window — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_FIRST_DUE_DATE`, on agreement creation, submission and restructuring.
34. No notice of intent to cancel on this agreement is already `ISSUED` — otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_OPEN`, on notice issuance.
35. No cure blackout is running — otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_BLACKOUT_ACTIVE`, on notice issuance.
36. The statutory notice period has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, on cancellation execution.
37. The logical clock moves forward — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND`.
38. A corrected premium stays within the variance tolerance — otherwise `ERR_PREMIUM_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`, on attestation filing.
39. A payment does not overshoot the net payoff beyond the permitted overpayment — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PAYOFF`, on payment posting.
40. An offset fits within the agency's reserve and the chargeback's unsettled remainder — otherwise `ERR_OFFSET_EXCEEDS_RESERVE`, on a chargeback offset.
41. The agreement is eligible for restructuring — otherwise `ERR_RESTRUCTURE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
42. At least one pending endorsement exists to finance — otherwise `ERR_NO_PENDING_ENDORSEMENTS`, on endorsement financing.

The thresholds and business meaning behind rules 22 through 42 are given in section 3 alongside the workflows they guard.

### 2.11. Two Conservation Identities

The ledger is validated by two named identities that an implementation can assert against itself. The first, **total receivable**, is defined as the agreement's `principal_balance_cents` plus `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` plus `late_fee_balance_cents`, minus `unapplied_credit_cents`. Posting an ordinary payment must reduce total receivable by exactly the amount tendered, and reversing a payment must restore total receivable to exactly the value it had before that payment was posted, together with the `paid_cents` and `status` of every installment the payment touched and the status of every late fee it settled. One counter is deliberately excluded from this identity: `late_fee_count` is a lifetime counter that never moves backwards, so neither a waiver nor a reversal restores it.

The second, **note settlement residual**, must be exactly zero once an agreement has reached `CANCELLED_DEFICIENT` or `CLOSED_ZERO`. It is defined as the agreement's `total_of_payments_cents`, plus the sum of the amounts of every late fee ever assessed on the note other than those that were waived or reversed, minus the sum of the amounts of every payment on the note still in `POSTED` status, minus the total rebate of unearned finance charge ever granted on the note, minus `unearned_premium_received_cents`, minus the residual deficiency computed before the cancellation fee is added, minus `unapplied_credit_cents`. In words: every dollar of the note is either paid by the insured, rebated as unearned finance charge, returned by a carrier, or still owed as a deficiency. A cascade that credits the ledger twice, or that forgets the rebate, shows up here as a non-zero residual even when every individual balance looks plausible.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing the Statutory Regime and the Counterparties

Nothing can be financed until a `ComplianceOfficer` has registered the statutory regime of the state in question. Registering a regime supplies its `state_code`, its `finance_charge_method`, and the whole statutory parameter set described in section 2.9 — `max_annual_rate_bps`, `max_service_fee_cents`, `min_down_payment_bps`, `max_term_months`, `late_fee_bps`, `late_fee_min_cents`, `late_fee_max_cents`, `grace_period_days`, `noic_notice_days`, `unearned_premium_method`, `short_rate_penalty_bps`, `rebate_method` and `max_late_fees_per_agreement` — with `license_required` optional and defaulting to true. The new regime starts with `active_agreement_count` at zero. A second attempt to register the same `state_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_RULE` (HTTP 409), because a state has exactly one rate regime. Reading a regime back returns the live counter, so a compliance officer can see how many notes are currently exposed to that statute.

Licenses hang off the regime they satisfy. A compliance officer records one by supplying its `license_number`, `effective_date` and `expiration_date`; the platform stamps `status` as `ACTIVE` and derives `is_effective` immediately from that status, the recorded window and the clock date. Licenses of one regime can be listed and filtered by `status`. When a regulator acts against the company, a compliance officer suspends the license: `status` moves `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED`, `is_effective` becomes false, the license's `version` advances, and origination in that state stops from that instant, because the licensing gate re-evaluates on every affected operation rather than caching a decision.

Borrowers are registered by a `FinanceAnalyst` with a non-empty `legal_name`, a `mailing_state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters used for addressing statutory notices, and an `email` that must contain an at-sign. Carriers are registered by a compliance officer with a non-empty `carrier_name`, a `naic_code` that must be unique across all carriers, and an optional `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` that defaults to `ACTIVE`; a carrier's status is fixed at creation and records whether the carrier is admitted for new financing, and there is no operation that changes it afterwards.

Producing agencies are onboarded by a compliance officer, and this is where the platform's chargeback exposure is configured. Onboarding supplies `agency_name`, the `state_code` in which the agency is appointed, `agent_commission_bps` — the agency's commission as basis points of financed premium, at least 0 and at most 3000 — the `chargeback_basis` that decides which chargeback variant applies to the agency's uncollected deficiencies, a non-negative `chargeback_liability_cap_cents` that is the lifetime ceiling on the agency's outstanding chargeback exposure, and a non-negative `initial_reserve_balance_cents` that seeds the agency's reserve. On creation, `chargeback_reserve_balance_cents` is set to the supplied initial reserve, `outstanding_chargeback_cents` is set to 0, and `status` is set to `ACTIVE`. The `chargeback_basis` is either `COMMISSION_CAPPED`, under which the agency absorbs a deficiency only up to the commission it earned, or `RESERVE_CAPPED`, under which it absorbs a deficiency only up to its remaining liability headroom.

An agency's `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED_FOR_CHARGEBACKS` or `SUSPENDED_BY_COMPLIANCE`. The first suspension is produced automatically by the chargeback cascade of section 3.8; the second is produced when a compliance officer suspends the agency directly, optionally recording a `reason`, which moves `status` `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED_BY_COMPLIANCE` and advances the agency's `version`. Both suspensions block new origination identically: any attempt to create, submit or restructure an agreement naming a suspended agency is refused with `ERR_AGENT_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422). A compliance suspension is never lifted by settling chargebacks; only the chargeback suspension is released that way. An agency may read its own record, including `outstanding_chargeback_cents` and `chargeback_reserve_balance_cents`, and so may analysts and compliance officers.

### 3.2. Originating a Note: Draft, Policies, Priced Exceptions and Submission

A `FinanceAnalyst` opens a note by naming the borrower through `insured_id`, the producing agency through `producer_agent_id` and the governing regime through `state_rule_id`, and by supplying the commercial terms: `down_payment_cents` collected in cash before financing, a `service_fee_cents` added to the amount financed, an `installment_count` of at least 2, and the `first_installment_due_date` on which installment number 1 falls. The new note is stamped `DRAFT` in its financing `status` and `CURRENT` in its `delinquency_stage`, its `originated_by_user_id` is frozen from `X-User-ID` for the later two-person control, and every balance, derived money field and counter is zeroed — including `total_premium_cents`, `amount_financed_cents`, `effective_annual_rate_bps`, `finance_charge_cents`, `total_of_payments_cents`, `principal_balance_cents`, `unpaid_finance_charge_cents`, `late_fee_balance_cents`, `late_fee_count`, `unapplied_credit_cents`, `unearned_premium_received_cents`, `deficiency_balance_cents`, `refund_due_cents`, `agent_chargeback_cents`, `policy_count`, `verified_policy_count` and `carried_over_balance_cents` — while `quote_basis`, `activated_at`, `restructured_from_agreement_id`, `approved_service_fee_cap_cents` and `approved_annual_rate_bps` are all null and `rate_exception_approved` is false. The state regime's `active_agreement_count` is deliberately *not* touched at creation; submission owns that increment.

Four statutory gates already apply at creation. The regime must be licensed if it demands a license; the named agency must be `ACTIVE`; `installment_count` must not exceed the regime's `max_term_months`, or the request fails with `ERR_TERM_EXCEEDS_STATE_MAXIMUM` (HTTP 422); and `service_fee_cents` must not exceed the regime's `max_service_fee_cents`, or the request fails with `ERR_SERVICE_FEE_ABOVE_CAP` (HTTP 422). The first installment must also fall in a narrow window: the clock date must be strictly earlier than `first_installment_due_date`, and that date must be no more than 60 days after the clock date. A date on the clock date itself, or 61 days out, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIRST_DUE_DATE` (HTTP 422); a date exactly 60 days out is accepted. Because the clock moves while a draft sits unfunded, the same window is re-checked at submission against the clock in force then, and it is checked again when a successor note is minted by a restructure.

Policies are attached to the draft one at a time. Each attachment names the issuing carrier through `insurer_id` and supplies the carrier's `policy_number`, the agency-quoted `gross_premium_cents` (which must be strictly positive), the quoted `effective_date` and `expiration_date`, an optional `min_earned_days` defaulting to 0, and an optional `fully_earned` flag defaulting to false. `fully_earned` marks a premium as non-refundable, so no unearned premium is ever returned for that policy; `min_earned_days` is the carrier's minimum earned window, meaning a cancellation inside that window still earns the carrier premium for exactly that many days. The coverage interval is half-open — the effective date is inside the term and the expiration date is the exclusive upper bound — the expiration date must be strictly after the effective date and no more than 730 days after it, and `min_earned_days` must not exceed the term length in days; a violation of the term shape yields `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_TERM` (HTTP 422). A new policy may not overlap an existing one: if any other policy with the same carrier and the same `policy_number` whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` has a half-open term that overlaps the new term, the attachment is refused with `ERR_POLICY_TERM_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409); terms that merely touch, where the earlier expiration date equals the new effective date, are accepted. A successful attachment stamps the policy `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, sets `carried_over` to false, provisionally sets `financed_premium_cents` equal to `gross_premium_cents`, increments the agreement's `policy_count` and advances the agreement's `version`.

Where a deal genuinely needs to price above the ordinary schedule, a `ComplianceOfficer` may grant a priced exception on a note that is still `DRAFT` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`. The approval carries a mandatory `justification` and either or both of `override_service_fee_cents` and `override_annual_rate_bps`. It sets `rate_exception_approved` to true, records the approver as `rate_exception_approved_by_user_id`, copies `override_service_fee_cents` into `approved_service_fee_cap_cents` and `override_annual_rate_bps` into `approved_annual_rate_bps` when each is supplied, and advances the note's `version`. Two limits bind the exception. An `override_annual_rate_bps` above the regime's `max_annual_rate_bps` is refused with `ERR_RATE_ABOVE_STATE_MAXIMUM` (HTTP 422) — the statutory ceiling is never negotiable. And the note's existing `service_fee_cents` must fit inside the granted `override_service_fee_cents`; if it does not, the approval is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_FEE_ABOVE_CAP`. The approver may not be the analyst recorded in `originated_by_user_id`; that comparison is the two-person control on pricing and yields `ERR_SOD_ORIGINATOR_CANNOT_APPROVE`.

Submitting the draft advances the financing status `DRAFT` → `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and prices the note provisionally. Eight preconditions must hold, and each maps to a specific rejection: at least one non-cancelled policy must be attached, or `ERR_NO_FINANCED_POLICIES` (HTTP 422); every policy of the note must still be `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, or the policy-state gate refuses the submission; the regime must be licensed, or `ERR_STATE_NOT_LICENSED`; the producing agency must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_AGENT_SUSPENDED`; the down payment must meet the statutory minimum, or `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422); `service_fee_cents` must sit within the regime's `max_service_fee_cents`, or within `approved_service_fee_cap_cents` when a rate exception has been approved, or `ERR_SERVICE_FEE_ABOVE_CAP`; `installment_count` must be at least 2 and no greater than `max_term_months`, or `ERR_TERM_EXCEEDS_STATE_MAXIMUM`; and the first-due-date window must still hold against the current clock date, or `ERR_INVALID_FIRST_DUE_DATE`. The minimum-down-payment test is a basis-point comparison and equality passes it: the down payment multiplied by 10000 must be greater than or equal to the premium basis multiplied by the regime's `min_down_payment_bps`, where the premium basis at submission is the sum of the agency-quoted `gross_premium_cents` across the note's policies. A regime demanding 2000 basis points — one fifth of the premium — therefore accepts a down payment of exactly one fifth and refuses one cent less.

A successful submission runs the pricing engine in its **provisional** variant, stamps `quote_basis` as `PROVISIONAL`, increments the regime's `active_agreement_count` by 1 and advances the note's `version`. The pricing engine works in five steps. First it establishes the premium basis: the provisional variant sums `gross_premium_cents` over the note's policies whose `status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and whose `carried_over` flag is false, and writes the result to `total_premium_cents`; disputed and cancelled policies contribute nothing, and carried-over policies never enter the basis because their unpaid balance arrives separately through `carried_over_balance_cents`. Second, `amount_financed_cents` is the premium basis minus `down_payment_cents`, plus `service_fee_cents`, plus `carried_over_balance_cents`. Third, a volume tier is selected from the amount financed: an amount financed of 5,000 dollars or less takes a tier rate of 1800 basis points, an amount above 5,000 dollars but not above 20,000 dollars takes 1500 basis points, and anything above 20,000 dollars takes 1200 basis points — the larger the loan, the cheaper the money. Fourth, `effective_annual_rate_bps` is fixed: when the note carries an approved exception with a non-null `approved_annual_rate_bps`, the effective rate is the smaller of that approved rate and the regime's `max_annual_rate_bps`; otherwise it is the smaller of the tier rate and the regime's `max_annual_rate_bps`. The statutory ceiling always wins, in both branches.

The fifth step branches on the regime's `finance_charge_method`, and the two branches are genuinely different arithmetic. Under `ADD_ON`, the whole finance charge is computed once: multiply `amount_financed_cents` by `effective_annual_rate_bps`, multiply by `installment_count`, divide by 10000 to convert basis points and by 12 to convert an annual rate to monthly, and round the single result half up to whole cents — that figure becomes `finance_charge_cents`. Under `SIMPLE_INTEREST`, interest accrues on a declining balance over equal principal slices: let the base principal slice be `amount_financed_cents` divided by `installment_count` and truncated down to whole cents, and let the outstanding balance start at `amount_financed_cents`; then, for each installment position from the first to the last, the principal retired is the base slice for every position except the last, where it is the entire remaining outstanding balance, while that period's interest is the outstanding balance at the start of the period multiplied by `effective_annual_rate_bps`, divided by 10000 and by 12, and rounded half up to whole cents. Each period's rounded interest is added to `finance_charge_cents` and the period's principal is then subtracted from the outstanding balance. Every period is rounded before it is summed; summing exact products and rounding once at the end is a different and forbidden result. Finally, `total_of_payments_cents` is `amount_financed_cents` plus `finance_charge_cents`.

A note that will never fund is voided by an analyst, optionally recording a `reason`. Voiding is permitted only from `DRAFT` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, and only while no `Payment` row exists against the note; the financing status moves to `VOID`, and every policy of the note moves to `CANCELLED` with its `cancellation_effective_date` left null, because coverage was never pulled — it was never funded. Whether the regime's `active_agreement_count` is decremented depends on where the void came from: a void out of `PENDING_VERIFICATION` decrements it by 1, while a void out of `DRAFT` leaves it untouched, since a draft was never counted in the first place. The note's `version` advances either way.

### 3.3. Two-Party Verification of Premium and Effective Date

The agency quotes a premium; the carrier knows the premium. Between submission and activation, the platform requires both sides to attest independently to what the policy actually costs and when coverage actually starts, and it funds nothing until they agree. An attestation is filed against a single policy by an `Insurer` or a `ProducerAgent`, and standing is checked before anything else about the filing: a caller acting as `Insurer` must present an `X-User-ID` equal to the policy's `insurer_id`, and a caller acting as `ProducerAgent` must present an `X-User-ID` equal to the `producer_agent_id` of the policy's agreement. A caller without standing is refused with `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and a policy addressed alongside an agreement whose verification cycle it does not belong to is refused with `ERR_POLICY_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH`. The policy itself must be `PENDING_VERIFICATION`; a `VERIFIED`, `DISPUTED` or `CANCELLED` policy refuses the filing with `ERR_POLICY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Each side gets one live voice per policy: if a non-superseded attestation from the same party already exists on that policy, the filing is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_ALREADY_RECORDED` (HTTP 409).

The filing itself carries a `decision`, which is `CONFIRMED`, `CORRECTED` or `REJECTED`, and an optional free-text `note` retained for audit. `CONFIRMED` means the party accepts the quoted figures as they stand. `CORRECTED` means the party asserts different figures, and it requires both `corrected_premium_cents` — strictly positive — and `corrected_effective_date`; both fields are forbidden on a `CONFIRMED` or `REJECTED` filing, and supplying or omitting them contrary to the decision is a request-shape failure. `REJECTED` means the party repudiates the policy altogether. The platform derives the rest of the record: `party` becomes `INSURER` when the caller acts as `Insurer` and `PRODUCER_AGENT` when the caller acts as `ProducerAgent`; `party_user_id` is taken from `X-User-ID`, so it equals the policy's `insurer_id` for a carrier filing and the agreement's `producer_agent_id` for an agency filing; `superseded` is false. Two derived comparison fields are then stamped: on `CONFIRMED`, `effective_premium_cents` takes the policy's quoted `gross_premium_cents` and `effective_date_asserted` takes the policy's quoted `effective_date`; on `CORRECTED`, they take `corrected_premium_cents` and `corrected_effective_date`; on `REJECTED`, both remain null.

A correction may not rewrite the deal. A `corrected_premium_cents` must stay within 2500 basis points — a quarter — of the agency-quoted `gross_premium_cents`: the absolute difference between the corrected premium and the quoted premium, multiplied by 10000, must be less than or equal to the quoted premium multiplied by 2500. Equality is accepted, so a correction of exactly one quarter above or below the quote passes and one cent further out is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A carrier that disagrees by more than a quarter is telling the finance company that the deal it priced does not exist, and the correct remedy is repudiation rather than a correction.

Nothing happens to the policy while only one side has spoken; the policy stays `PENDING_VERIFICATION` until both parties hold a non-superseded record. Once the second record lands, the pair is evaluated and the policy forks. It becomes `VERIFIED` when both records carry a decision of `CONFIRMED` or `CORRECTED` and their asserted premiums are equal and their asserted effective dates are equal. In that case the agreed figures are stamped as `verified_premium_cents` and `verified_effective_date`, `financed_premium_cents` is recomputed as `verified_premium_cents` plus `endorsed_premium_cents`, the agreement's `verified_policy_count` increases by 1, and the policy's `version` advances.

It becomes `DISPUTED` in the complementary case — either record is `REJECTED`, or the two asserted premiums differ, or the two asserted effective dates differ. Then `dispute_reason` is stamped by a strict precedence: `PARTY_REJECTED` when either record is a repudiation; otherwise `PREMIUM_MISMATCH` when the asserted premiums differ; otherwise `EFFECTIVE_DATE_MISMATCH`. Both `verified_premium_cents` and `verified_effective_date` are left null, and the policy's `version` advances. A dispute also reaches up into the note: if the parent agreement is sitting in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, it falls back to `DRAFT`, its provisional money fields — `total_premium_cents`, `amount_financed_cents`, `effective_annual_rate_bps`, `finance_charge_cents` and `total_of_payments_cents` — are all reset to 0, its `quote_basis` returns to null, the regime's `active_agreement_count` is decremented by 1, and the agreement's `version` advances. A single carrier saying "that is not the premium" therefore un-prices the whole note.

The way out of a dispute is a re-attestation cycle driven by a `FinanceAnalyst`. Reopening verification on a `DISPUTED` policy — permitted only while the parent agreement is in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION` — sets `superseded` to true on every existing attestation of that policy, clears `dispute_reason`, moves the policy `DISPUTED` → `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and advances its `version`. Both parties may then file fresh attestations, since the retired records no longer count as live voices, and the cycle may repeat without limit until the two sides agree. Superseded records are never deleted: listing the attestations of a policy returns every record ever filed, superseded ones included, and the list may be narrowed with a `party` filter.

### 3.4. Activation, the Final Quote and the Installment Schedule

Activation is the moment the note becomes a receivable. A `FinanceAnalyst` activates a `PENDING_VERIFICATION` note, and four preconditions are re-tested at that instant rather than trusted from submission. At least one policy must be attached and every attached policy must be `VERIFIED` — formally, `verified_policy_count` must equal `policy_count` with `policy_count` at least 1 — and any policy still `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or sitting in `DISPUTED` blocks activation with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 422). The regime must still be licensed at the current clock date, or `ERR_STATE_NOT_LICENSED`. The minimum-down-payment test is re-run, this time against the *attested* premium basis: `down_payment_cents` multiplied by 10000 must be at least the sum of `financed_premium_cents` over the note's policies multiplied by `min_down_payment_bps`, and a failure is `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. A down payment that was sufficient against an agency quote can be insufficient against a corrected, higher attested premium — that is the intended consequence. And `service_fee_cents` must still fit inside the regime's `max_service_fee_cents`, or inside `approved_service_fee_cap_cents` when a rate exception is on file, or `ERR_SERVICE_FEE_ABOVE_CAP`.

The activation then reprices the note in the pricing engine's **final** variant. The five steps are exactly those described in section 3.2 — premium basis, amount financed, volume tier, statutory capping, method branch — with one difference in step one: the final variant sums `financed_premium_cents` (the attested premium plus any financed endorsements) over the note's policies whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `carried_over` flag is false, rather than the agency-quoted gross premiums over pending policies. Because every corrected premium moves `total_premium_cents`, it can also move `amount_financed_cents` across a tier boundary and therefore change `effective_annual_rate_bps`; a note quoted provisionally at 1800 basis points can activate at 1500. All five money fields — `total_premium_cents`, `amount_financed_cents`, `effective_annual_rate_bps`, `finance_charge_cents` and `total_of_payments_cents` — are rewritten, and `quote_basis` is stamped `FINAL`.

With the final quote in hand the platform cuts the schedule, inserting exactly `installment_count` installment rows in `SCHEDULED` status, each carrying a 1-based `installment_number`, a `due_date`, an `amount_cents`, its split into `principal_component_cents` and `finance_charge_component_cents`, and a `paid_cents` of 0. The note's `principal_balance_cents` is set to `amount_financed_cents`, its `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` to `finance_charge_cents`, its `activated_at` to the current logical-clock instant, its financing status to `ACTIVE`, and its `version` advances.

How the installment amounts are derived depends again on `finance_charge_method`. Under `ADD_ON` the note pays level installments: the base amount is `total_of_payments_cents` divided by `installment_count` and truncated down to whole cents, every installment except the last takes that base amount, and the last installment takes `total_of_payments_cents` minus the base amount multiplied by one less than the installment count. The finance charge is spread straight-line by the same discipline but with rounding rather than truncation: the base finance-charge component is `finance_charge_cents` divided by `installment_count`, rounded half up, every installment except the last takes that base component, and the last installment takes `finance_charge_cents` minus that base component multiplied by one less than the installment count. Each installment's `principal_component_cents` is then its `amount_cents` minus its `finance_charge_component_cents`. The arithmetic remainder always lands on the final installment so that the installment amounts sum to `total_of_payments_cents` exactly and the finance-charge components sum to `finance_charge_cents` exactly; rounding each installment independently would leave a one-cent drift and is wrong.

Under `SIMPLE_INTEREST` the installments decline instead. The base principal slice is `amount_financed_cents` divided by `installment_count`, truncated down to whole cents, and the outstanding balance starts at `amount_financed_cents`. Walking the positions in order, each installment's `principal_component_cents` is the base slice, except the last, which takes the entire remaining outstanding balance; its `finance_charge_component_cents` is the outstanding balance at the start of that period multiplied by `effective_annual_rate_bps`, divided by 10000 and by 12, and rounded half up; its `amount_cents` is the sum of the two components; and the outstanding balance is then reduced by that installment's principal component before the next position is computed.

Due dates walk forward by whole calendar months from `first_installment_due_date`: installment number 1 falls on that date, and installment *k* falls one month later than installment *k* minus one. The day of the month is preserved wherever the target month has such a day, and where it does not, the last day of the target month is used — a first due date of 31 January 2026 gives a second due date of 28 February 2026, and the third returns to 31 March 2026. The schedule of a note may be listed by an analyst, a compliance officer or the borrower, optionally filtered by installment `status`, and installment `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `PAST_DUE`, `PAID` or `VOIDED`.

### 3.5. Servicing, Late Fees and Waivers

Delinquency is discovered by a nightly servicing pass, not by a background timer. A `ComplianceOfficer` runs the pass, and it evaluates the whole book against the current logical clock in a pinned order: agreements are processed by `created_at` ascending, and within one agreement its installments are processed by `installment_number` ascending. Only notes whose financing status is `ACTIVE` are considered, and only those whose `delinquency_stage` is `CURRENT`, `LATE` or `NOIC_ISSUED`; drafts, pending notes and terminal notes are skipped entirely, as are installments already `PAID` or `VOIDED`. The pass reports three counts: `past_due_marked`, `late_fees_assessed` and `agreements_advanced_to_late`.

Two things can happen to an installment during a pass. If it is `SCHEDULED`, its `due_date` is strictly earlier than the clock date and its `paid_cents` is less than its `amount_cents`, it moves `SCHEDULED` → `PAST_DUE` and its `version` advances. Then, if an installment is `PAST_DUE` and the clock date is *strictly after* its `due_date` advanced by the regime's `grace_period_days`, a late fee may be assessed against it. The strictness of that comparison is deliberate and is a frequent source of off-by-one errors: with a grace period of five days and a due date of 10 August, a pass run on 15 August assesses nothing, and a pass run on 16 August assesses the fee.

Two further conditions guard the assessment. The note's lifetime `late_fee_count` must be strictly less than the regime's `max_late_fees_per_agreement`, so a regime capping fees at three refuses a fourth for the rest of the note's life. And no late fee may already exist for that agreement and that `installment_number` unless the existing one is `REVERSED`, so a single installment is charged once no matter how many passes run over it, while a fee reversed by a restructure does not block a fresh assessment on the successor note. When all conditions hold, the fee amount is computed by taking the installment's `amount_cents` multiplied by the regime's `late_fee_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up, and then clamping that raw figure into the statutory band — first raising it to `late_fee_min_cents` if it falls below the floor, then lowering it to `late_fee_max_cents` if it exceeds the ceiling. Rounding happens before the clamp; clamping first and rounding second can differ by a cent. The new assessment is written with that `amount_cents`, a `status` of `OUTSTANDING`, an `assessed_date` equal to the clock date, and the `installment_number` of the installment that triggered it — a plain integer identifying the position in the schedule, not a reference to the installment row. The note's `late_fee_count` increases by 1, its `late_fee_balance_cents` increases by the fee, and its `version` advances. An agreement that acquires a newly past-due installment while `CURRENT` advances `CURRENT` → `LATE` in the same pass.

Fees can be listed per note and filtered by `status`, which is one of `OUTSTANDING`, `PAID`, `WAIVED` or `REVERSED`. A compliance officer may waive an `OUTSTANDING` fee as a concession, supplying a mandatory `justification`; a fee that is `PAID`, already `WAIVED` or `REVERSED` cannot be waived and yields `ERR_LATE_FEE_NOT_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409). The waiver moves the fee `OUTSTANDING` → `WAIVED`, records the acting officer in `waived_by_user_id`, decrements the note's `late_fee_balance_cents` by the fee amount, and advances both rows' `version`. What a waiver does *not* do is refresh the statutory cap: `late_fee_count` is never decremented, so waiving two fees under a three-fee cap still leaves room for only one more. And a waiver can cure a delinquency — if, after the waiver, the note carries no `OUTSTANDING` fee and no `PAST_DUE` installment, its `delinquency_stage` advances to `CURRENT` and any notice of intent to cancel still in `ISSUED` moves to `CURED`, exactly as a payment would have cured it.

### 3.6. Payment Posting, Payoff Quotes and Returned Items

Before paying anything, an analyst, a compliance officer or the borrower may ask what it would take to close the note out. This computed read accepts an optional `as_of_date`, defaulting to the clock date, and returns that `as_of_date`, the `gross_balance_cents` (the note's `principal_balance_cents` plus its `unpaid_finance_charge_cents`), the `unearned_finance_charge_rebate_cents` the borrower would be granted, the `outstanding_late_fee_cents` still owed, the `unapplied_credit_cents` sitting in suspense, the resulting `net_payoff_cents`, and the `rebate_method` under which the figure was computed.

The rebate exists because a borrower who pays early should not pay interest for months they never used. Let *r* be the number of installments not yet retired: `installment_count` minus the count of the note's installments whose `status` is `PAID`. If *r* is zero or negative, the rebate is zero. Under the `RULE_OF_78` method, the rebate is `finance_charge_cents` multiplied by *r*, multiplied by *r* plus one, divided by the product of `installment_count` and `installment_count` plus one, rounded half up — the sum-of-digits curve, which front-loads finance charge and therefore rebates *less* than a straight-line schedule actually allocated to the unpaid tail. Under the `ACTUARIAL` method, the rebate is instead the exact sum of `finance_charge_component_cents` over the note's installments whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PAST_DUE`; no rounding occurs, because the components being summed are already whole cents. Installments that are `VOIDED` count towards neither figure. Whichever method applies, the rebate is finally capped at the note's `unpaid_finance_charge_cents`, so a rebate can never create finance charge that was never owed. The net payoff is then `principal_balance_cents` plus `unpaid_finance_charge_cents`, minus the rebate, plus `late_fee_balance_cents`, minus `unapplied_credit_cents`. For an identical `ADD_ON` schedule the actuarial figure is strictly larger than the sum-of-digits figure, so the same account genuinely has two different payoff numbers depending on which state wrote its statute.

A payment is posted by a `FinanceAnalyst` or by the borrower itself, and carries a strictly positive `amount_cents`, a `received_date` that may not be later than the clock date, and a `method` of `ACH`, `CHECK` or `CARD`. It lands in `POSTED` status. The platform tolerates a modest overpayment but not an arbitrary one: an `amount_cents` exceeding the net payoff by more than 50000 cents — five hundred dollars — is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PAYOFF` (HTTP 422), where the net payoff is computed as of the payment's `received_date`. A payment on a note that is not `ACTIVE` is refused by the agreement-state gate.

Allocation begins by choosing a variant, and the choice is made *before* any money moves: the payment runs as the `PAYOFF` variant when the tendered `amount_cents` is greater than or equal to the net payoff computed as of `received_date`, and as the `STANDARD` variant otherwise. The chosen variant is recorded in `allocation_variant`. The consequence is that the same tendered amount retires a strictly larger obligation under the payoff variant than under the standard one, because only the payoff variant grants the rebate.

Under the `PAYOFF` variant the rebate is granted first: `rebate_applied_cents` is stamped with the rebate figure and the note's `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` is reduced by it. The payment then absorbs everything left: `applied_late_fee_cents` equals the whole `late_fee_balance_cents`, and every `OUTSTANDING` fee on the note moves to `PAID`; `applied_finance_charge_cents` equals the post-rebate `unpaid_finance_charge_cents`; `applied_principal_cents` equals the whole `principal_balance_cents`; and `applied_unapplied_cents` is whatever remains of the tendered amount after those three have been taken. All three balances become zero, and every installment still `SCHEDULED` or `PAST_DUE` moves to `PAID`.

Under the `STANDARD` variant, `rebate_applied_cents` stays 0 and the money flows through four buckets in a fixed order. Bucket one settles `OUTSTANDING` late fees, ordered by `assessed_date` ascending, then `installment_number` ascending, then `created_at` ascending; a fee is settled only if the unallocated remainder covers its `amount_cents` whole, and a remainder smaller than the fee leaves that fee `OUTSTANDING` and flows on rather than part-paying it. Bucket two pays `PAST_DUE` installments, ordered by `due_date` ascending then `installment_number` ascending, and inside a single installment the `finance_charge_component_cents` is retired before the `principal_component_cents`; partial application is allowed here and leaves the installment `PAST_DUE`. Bucket three does the same for `SCHEDULED` installments while any money remains, always taking the lowest `installment_number` first and retiring finance charge before principal within each. Bucket four parks whatever is left in the note's `unapplied_credit_cents`, and the same figure is recorded on the payment as `applied_unapplied_cents`. Fees, installments and rows in `PAID`, `WAIVED`, `REVERSED` or `VOIDED` status are never touched. As the buckets fill, each installment's `paid_cents` accumulates and an installment whose `paid_cents` reaches its `amount_cents` moves to `PAID`; the note's `principal_balance_cents`, `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` and `late_fee_balance_cents` are decremented by exactly the amounts absorbed in buckets one to three; and `applied_late_fee_cents`, `applied_finance_charge_cents` and `applied_principal_cents` record the three totals.

Two cascades can fire off a posting. If the allocation leaves no `PAST_DUE` installment and a `late_fee_balance_cents` of zero, the delinquency dimension is cured: `LATE` → `CURRENT`, or `NOIC_ISSUED` → `CURRENT`, and any notice of intent to cancel still in `ISSUED` moves to `CURED` with its `cured_date` set to the clock date — which opens the re-notice blackout window described in section 3.7. If the allocation drives `principal_balance_cents`, `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` and `late_fee_balance_cents` all to zero, the financing dimension completes: the note moves `ACTIVE` → `PAID_IN_FULL`, every installment still `SCHEDULED` or `PAST_DUE` moves to `PAID`, and `refund_due_cents` is set to the surviving `unapplied_credit_cents`, which is the surplus the company owes back to the borrower. The note's `version` advances on the posting, and so does the `version` of every installment and fee the allocation touched.

Cheques bounce and card payments are charged back, so a `FinanceAnalyst` may reverse a `POSTED` payment on a note that is `ACTIVE` or `PAID_IN_FULL`, supplying a `reversal_reason` of `NSF` or `BANK_ERROR`. Reversing a payment already `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_REVERSED` (HTTP 409). The reversal moves the payment `POSTED` → `REVERSED`, stamps `reversed_at` from the logical clock, and unwinds the allocation exactly: `principal_balance_cents` increases by `applied_principal_cents`; `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` increases by `applied_finance_charge_cents` *plus* `rebate_applied_cents`, because a rebate granted on a payoff that never cleared must be taken back too; `unapplied_credit_cents` decreases by `applied_unapplied_cents`; every fee the payment settled returns from `PAID` to `OUTSTANDING` and its amount is added back to `late_fee_balance_cents`; and every installment the payment touched has its `paid_cents` reduced by that payment's contribution, returning to `PAST_DUE` when its `due_date` is strictly earlier than the clock date and to `SCHEDULED` otherwise. `refund_due_cents` is set back to 0.

The reversal also unwinds the state cascades. A note in `PAID_IN_FULL` returns to `ACTIVE`; the total-receivable identity of section 2.11 must land exactly on its pre-payment value. If the reversal leaves a `PAST_DUE` installment or a positive `late_fee_balance_cents` behind on an `ACTIVE` note, the delinquency dimension re-delinquences `CURRENT` → `LATE` — a second, differently triggered entry into `LATE` than the servicing pass. A notice of intent to cancel that had already reached `CURED` stays `CURED`, because that state is terminal, and its blackout window keeps running regardless of the returned item. `late_fee_count` is not decremented, so the statutory fee cap still counts every fee the reversed payment had settled. Payments of a note can be listed and filtered by `status`, and reversed payments remain visible in that list.

### 3.7. The Notice of Intent to Cancel and the Power of Attorney

A premium finance agreement grants the finance company a power of attorney over the policies it funded, but no state lets that power be exercised without warning. The statutory instrument is a **notice of intent to cancel**, and it is issued by a `FinanceAnalyst` on a note whose financing status is `ACTIVE` and whose `delinquency_stage` is `LATE`. Issuing also requires that at least one late fee on the note still be `OUTSTANDING`; a note whose fees have all been paid or waived no longer justifies a notice and the issuance is refused with `ERR_DELINQUENCY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), the same code that refuses a note sitting in `CURRENT`, `CANCELLATION_REQUESTED` or `CANCELLED`.

Two timing gates then apply, and they are separate rules with separate codes because they describe different situations. A note may carry only one open notice at a time: if any notice of the note is already `ISSUED`, the request is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_OPEN` (HTTP 409) — that occupied window is also why a note already in `NOIC_ISSUED` clears the stage gate and is refused here instead, so the caller learns the real reason. And a borrower who cures a delinquency earns a breathing space: for the most recently `CURED` notice of the note, the closed window running from its `cured_date` through its `cured_date` advanced by the regime's `noic_notice_days` blocks a fresh notice. Concretely, the issuance is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_BLACKOUT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) for as long as `cured_date` advanced by `noic_notice_days` is greater than or equal to the clock date, and is accepted only once the clock date is strictly beyond that. A notice that ended as `SUPERSEDED` rather than `CURED` opens no blackout at all.

A successful issuance writes a notice in `ISSUED` status with `issued_date` set to the clock date, `issued_by_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, `notice_days` snapshotted from the regime's `noic_notice_days` at that moment, and `effective_cancel_date` set to `issued_date` advanced by `notice_days` calendar days. It also snapshots `past_due_amount_cents`: the sum, over the note's installments whose `status` is `PAST_DUE`, of each installment's `amount_cents` minus its `paid_cents`, plus the note's `late_fee_balance_cents`. That figure is a frozen statement of what the borrower must pay, not a live balance, and it is not recomputed afterwards. The note's `delinquency_stage` advances `LATE` → `NOIC_ISSUED` and its `version` advances. Notices of a note can be listed and filtered by `status`, and because the regime's parameter may change later, the snapshotted `notice_days` on the notice — not the regime's current value — governs that notice's timeline.

From `ISSUED`, a notice forks three ways with disjoint consequences. It reaches `CURED`, with `cured_date` stamped at the clock date, when a payment or a late-fee waiver clears every past-due obligation — no `PAST_DUE` installment and a `late_fee_balance_cents` of zero. It reaches `EXECUTED`, with `executed_date` stamped at the clock date, when the power of attorney is exercised. It reaches `SUPERSEDED` when the note is restructured. All three are terminal: a `CURED`, `EXECUTED` or `SUPERSEDED` notice can never be executed, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Exercising the power of attorney is the most consequential single operation in the platform, and it is guarded accordingly. It requires an `ACTIVE` note whose `delinquency_stage` is `NOIC_ISSUED` and a notice in `ISSUED` status; the clock date must be on or after that notice's `effective_cancel_date`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (HTTP 422), with the boundary day itself permitted; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the notice's `issued_by_user_id`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SOD_NOTICE_ISSUER_CANNOT_EXECUTE`; and at least one policy of the note must still be `VERIFIED`, since there is otherwise nothing to cancel, in which case the attempt is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 422).

The execution then does six things at once. The notice moves `ISSUED` → `EXECUTED` with `executed_date` set to the clock date. Every policy of the note whose `status` is `VERIFIED` moves to `CANCELLED`, its `cancellation_effective_date` set to the notice's `effective_cancel_date` — the statutory date, not the date the analyst happened to act — and its `earned_days` and `unearned_premium_estimate_cents` are computed. One carrier cancellation request is created per cancelled policy, in `REQUESTED` status, carrying the `agreement_id` and `policy_id` it concerns, a `requested_date` of the clock date, the same `cancellation_effective_date`, the acting analyst as `requested_by_user_id`, the estimate as `computed_unearned_premium_cents`, and the regime's `unearned_premium_method` snapshotted as `estimate_method`. The unearned finance charge rebate is granted by the rule of section 3.6 and `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` is reduced by it. Every installment still `SCHEDULED` with a `paid_cents` of zero moves to `VOIDED`, while `PAST_DUE` installments stay past due and are settled at the balance level rather than the schedule level. The `delinquency_stage` advances `NOIC_ISSUED` → `CANCELLATION_REQUESTED`, and the note's `version` advances. The operation reports `requests_created`, the aggregate `estimated_unearned_premium_cents`, the `rebate_applied_cents` it granted, and the `gross_balance_cents` of the note — its `principal_balance_cents` plus its post-rebate `unpaid_finance_charge_cents`.

Estimating what a carrier will return is the arithmetic heart of this step, and it runs once per cancelled policy. The term length in days is the number of days from the policy's `verified_effective_date` to its `expiration_date`, treating the interval as half-open so the expiration date is not itself a covered day. The elapsed length is the number of days from `verified_effective_date` to the `cancellation_effective_date`. The policy's `earned_days` is then the elapsed length raised to the carrier's `min_earned_days` floor if it falls short, and capped at the full term length if it overshoots — that is, the smaller of the term length and the larger of `min_earned_days` and the elapsed length. The minimum earned window is inclusive on the earned side: a cancellation whose elapsed length equals `min_earned_days` earns exactly `min_earned_days`, and one a day earlier still earns `min_earned_days`. Unearned days are the term length minus `earned_days`.

If the policy is flagged `fully_earned`, its `unearned_premium_estimate_cents` is zero and nothing further is computed — a non-refundable premium returns nothing however early coverage is pulled. Otherwise the pro-rata figure is the policy's `financed_premium_cents` multiplied by the unearned days, divided by the term length in days, rounded half up. Under a `PRO_RATA` regime that pro-rata figure *is* the estimate. Under a `SHORT_RATE` regime the carrier keeps a penalty on top: the estimate is the already-rounded pro-rata figure multiplied by 10000 minus the regime's `short_rate_penalty_bps`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up again. The penalty is applied to the rounded pro-rata figure, not to an exact quotient. Because the same policy cancelled on the same date yields a strictly smaller return under `SHORT_RATE`, it also yields a strictly larger deficiency downstream. The aggregate the operation reports is the sum of the per-policy estimates, each rounded before the summation.

### 3.8. Carrier Returns, Deficiency Settlement and Agency Chargebacks

The carrier now owes money back, and only the carrier can say how much. An `Insurer` posts the return against a specific carrier cancellation request, supplying the `carrier_returned_cents` it is actually sending — which may be zero but never negative — and the `posted_date` on which it did so. The request must be in `REQUESTED` status, or the posting is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the `insurer_id` of the request's policy, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; and if the body names an `agreement_id`, it must be the `agreement_id` of that policy, or `ERR_POLICY_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH`. Requests may be listed and read by analysts, compliance officers and carriers, filtered by note, policy or `status`.

The posting forks on the amount. A strictly positive return moves the request `REQUESTED` → `RETURN_POSTED`: `variance_cents` is stamped as `carrier_returned_cents` minus `computed_unearned_premium_cents`, which is negative whenever the carrier returned less than the platform estimated; the policy's `unearned_premium_returned_cents` is set to the posted amount; the note's `unearned_premium_received_cents` increases by the posted amount; and the credit is applied to the note in a pinned order — first reducing `principal_balance_cents`, then `unpaid_finance_charge_cents`, then `late_fee_balance_cents`, with anything left over increasing `unapplied_credit_cents`. A zero return moves the request `REQUESTED` → `CLOSED_NO_RETURN` instead: `variance_cents` becomes zero minus `computed_unearned_premium_cents`, the policy's `unearned_premium_returned_cents` stays at 0, and nothing at all is credited to the note. In both branches the request's `version` advances, and in neither branch is any installment row touched — once coverage has been pulled, the schedule has stopped being the accounting of record and the note's balances are.

Settlement waits for the last carrier. While any request of the note is still `REQUESTED`, the note stays `ACTIVE` in its financing dimension and `CANCELLATION_REQUESTED` in its collection dimension. When the posting that closes the final outstanding request lands — that is, when every request of the note has reached either `RETURN_POSTED` or `CLOSED_NO_RETURN` — the note settles. The residual before any cancellation fee is the note's `principal_balance_cents` plus `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` plus `late_fee_balance_cents`, minus `unapplied_credit_cents`. That figure, and only that figure, keys the cancellation fee: a residual of zero or less draws no fee; a positive residual of 250 dollars or less draws a fee of 25 dollars; a residual above 250 dollars draws a fee of 50 dollars. The evaluation order is pinned precisely because the fee is itself part of the final deficiency — the fee never enters its own tier input.

The two outcomes are symmetric branches of the same trigger with opposite signs and disjoint effects. When the residual is zero or negative, the carriers returned at least as much as the note was owed: the financing status moves `ACTIVE` → `CLOSED_ZERO`, `deficiency_balance_cents` is set to 0, `agent_chargeback_cents` is set to 0, `refund_due_cents` is set to the magnitude of the residual (its sign inverted, so a residual of negative four thousand cents becomes a refund of four thousand cents), and no chargeback row whatsoever is created. When the residual is strictly positive, the financing status moves `ACTIVE` → `CANCELLED_DEFICIENT` and `deficiency_balance_cents` is set to the residual plus the cancellation fee. Either way the collection dimension advances `CANCELLATION_REQUESTED` → `CANCELLED`, the note's `version` advances, and the note-settlement-residual identity of section 2.11 must be exactly zero afterwards.

On the deficient branch, the producing agency is made to answer for part of what the borrower did not pay, and how much depends on the `chargeback_basis` snapshotted from the agency. Under `COMMISSION_CAPPED`, the commission the agency earned is the note's `total_premium_cents` multiplied by the agency's `agent_commission_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up, and `agent_chargeback_cents` is the smaller of `deficiency_balance_cents` and that commission — a generous commission means the whole deficiency comes back. Under `RESERVE_CAPPED`, the agency's remaining headroom is its `chargeback_liability_cap_cents` minus its current `outstanding_chargeback_cents`, floored at zero so a fully exposed agency has no headroom at all, and `agent_chargeback_cents` is the smaller of `deficiency_balance_cents` and that headroom — an identical deficiency on an identical note therefore produces a smaller chargeback for a reserve-capped agency, and can push it onto its cap.

One chargeback row is then created in `PENDING` status against the agency, carrying the `producer_agent_id` and `agreement_id` it arises from, an `amount_cents` equal to the note's `agent_chargeback_cents`, the `basis` snapshotted from the agency's configuration, an `offset_applied_cents` of 0, and a `reason` of `CANCELLATION_DEFICIENCY`, which is the only origin in this release. The agency's `outstanding_chargeback_cents` is the sum, over its chargebacks whose `status` is `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_OFFSET`, of each row's `amount_cents` minus its `offset_applied_cents`; if counting the new row brings that total to or above the agency's `chargeback_liability_cap_cents`, the agency moves `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED_FOR_CHARGEBACKS` and its `version` advances, which immediately blocks any further origination naming it. That is the fifth and last hop of a cascade that began with a single carrier posting a cheque: request settled, note credited, deficiency computed, chargeback minted, agency suspended.

Chargebacks are worked down by a `ComplianceOfficer` against the agency's reserve. An offset supplies `offset_amount_cents` and the mandatory `expected_chargeback_version` described in section 2.8. The amount must be strictly positive, must not exceed the chargeback's unsettled remainder (`amount_cents` minus `offset_applied_cents`), and must not exceed the agency's `chargeback_reserve_balance_cents`; a violation of either ceiling is refused with `ERR_OFFSET_EXCEEDS_RESERVE` (HTTP 422). An offset strictly smaller than the remainder moves the chargeback `PENDING` → `PARTIALLY_OFFSET`, or leaves an already partially offset chargeback in `PARTIALLY_OFFSET` — a legitimate self-loop that may repeat as often as the reserve allows, which is exactly why this write is protected by the expected-version check rather than by a state precondition. An offset that exactly equals the remainder settles the row and moves it to `OFFSET`, from either `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_OFFSET`, with `offset_applied_cents` ending equal to `amount_cents`. A chargeback already in `OFFSET` accepts nothing further and yields `ERR_CHARGEBACK_STATE_INVALID`.

Every offset, partial or final, adds `offset_amount_cents` to the chargeback's `offset_applied_cents`, subtracts the same amount from the agency's `chargeback_reserve_balance_cents`, recomputes `outstanding_chargeback_cents`, and advances the chargeback's `version`. When the recomputed exposure falls strictly below the agency's `chargeback_liability_cap_cents` and the agency is sitting in `SUSPENDED_FOR_CHARGEBACKS`, the agency returns to `ACTIVE` and its `version` advances — the suspension is released by settlement, not by time. A suspension recorded as `SUSPENDED_BY_COMPLIANCE` is never released this way. Chargebacks can be listed by agency, note or `status` and read individually, and an agency may inspect its own exposure through both surfaces.

### 3.9. Mid-Term Endorsements and Account Restructuring

Coverage changes mid-term, and when it does the financed amount has to change with it. An endorsement is filed against a single policy by the producing agency or by a `FinanceAnalyst`, and it records an `endorsement_type` of either `ADDITIONAL_PREMIUM` or `RETURN_PREMIUM`, a strictly positive `amount_cents`, and an `effective_date` that must fall on or after the policy's `verified_effective_date`. The policy must be `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `VERIFIED`; a `DISPUTED` or `CANCELLED` policy refuses the filing with `ERR_POLICY_STATE_INVALID`. An agency filing on a policy whose note does not name it is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The endorsement lands in `PENDING` status with a derived `signed_amount_cents` that carries the direction of the change: the amount itself for an `ADDITIONAL_PREMIUM`, and the amount negated for a `RETURN_PREMIUM`. Endorsements of a policy may be listed and filtered by `status`. An analyst may kill one before it ever touches the ledger: declining a `PENDING` endorsement moves it `PENDING` → `DECLINED`, optionally recording a `reason`, and advances its `version`; an endorsement that is not `PENDING` refuses the decline with `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. A `DECLINED` endorsement never enters the financed premium.

Folding the pending endorsements into the note is a separate, deliberate act by a `FinanceAnalyst`, permitted only while the note's financing status is `ACTIVE` and its `delinquency_stage` is `CURRENT` — a delinquent account is not re-financed, it is restructured. The request must carry `expected_agreement_version` matching the note's current `version`, or it is refused with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and at least one `PENDING` endorsement must exist on a `VERIFIED` policy of that note, or it is refused with `ERR_NO_PENDING_ENDORSEMENTS` (HTTP 422). Every qualifying endorsement then moves `PENDING` → `FINANCED` and has its `financed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Each affected policy's `endorsed_premium_cents` becomes the sum of `signed_amount_cents` over its `FINANCED` endorsements — so a return premium reduces it — and its `financed_premium_cents` is re-derived as `verified_premium_cents` plus `endorsed_premium_cents`.

The note is then repriced in the pricing engine's final variant over that new basis, which can move `total_premium_cents`, `amount_financed_cents`, the volume tier, `effective_annual_rate_bps`, `finance_charge_cents` and `total_of_payments_cents`, and the schedule is rebuilt over the unpaid tail only. Installments whose `status` is `PAID` or `VOIDED` are frozen and are not recomputed; the new `total_of_payments_cents` less the amounts already paid is redistributed across the installments whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PAST_DUE`, taken in ascending `installment_number` order, applying the same discipline as a fresh schedule to that tail — truncate the per-installment share for every installment of the tail except the last one, and give the last installment of the tail the exact arithmetic remainder, so the tail sums to the redistributed total with no drift. The note's `principal_balance_cents` and `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` are adjusted by the deltas the repricing produced, and its `version` advances.

Restructuring exists for the account that cannot pay the schedule it signed. A `FinanceAnalyst` restructures an `ACTIVE` note whose `delinquency_stage` is `CURRENT`, `LATE` or `NOIC_ISSUED`, which still has at least one `VERIFIED` policy and a strictly positive `principal_balance_cents`; a note whose stage is `CANCELLATION_REQUESTED` or `CANCELLED` is past saving, and any of these eligibility failures is reported as `ERR_RESTRUCTURE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). The request supplies the fresh commercial terms: an `additional_down_payment_cents` the borrower is putting up to earn the restructure, a new `installment_count`, and a new `first_installment_due_date`. The statutory gates of origination all apply again — the regime must be licensed, the agency must be `ACTIVE`, the new `installment_count` must not exceed `max_term_months`, the new first due date must sit strictly after the clock date and no more than 60 days beyond it, and the minimum-down-payment rule is re-tested with `additional_down_payment_cents` in the role of the down payment against the successor's premium basis. No expected-version field is required here, because the successor is minted from a snapshot of the predecessor rather than from a schedule the client has read.

On the predecessor, the restructure first grants the unearned finance charge rebate under the regime's `rebate_method` and reduces `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` by it, exactly as a payoff would. Its financing status then moves `ACTIVE` → `RESTRUCTURED`; its `principal_balance_cents`, `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` and `late_fee_balance_cents` are set to 0; its `delinquency_stage` is set back to `CURRENT`, since it no longer carries any delinquent obligation; every installment still `SCHEDULED` or `PAST_DUE` moves to `VOIDED`, with the unpaid remainder of each past-due installment folded forward; every late fee still `OUTSTANDING` moves to `REVERSED`, with its `amount_cents` likewise folded forward; any notice of intent to cancel still `ISSUED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which — unlike a cure — opens no re-notice blackout; and the predecessor's `version` advances. `RESTRUCTURED` is terminal: the predecessor accepts nothing further.

The successor is a new agreement row created in the same act and returned by the operation. It copies the predecessor's `insured_id`, `producer_agent_id` and `state_rule_id`, records the acting analyst as its own `originated_by_user_id`, and sets `restructured_from_agreement_id` to the predecessor's `id`, which is also the field a caller filters on to find it later. Its `carried_over_balance_cents` is the predecessor's post-rebate net balance — principal plus unpaid finance charge after the rebate, plus the folded-in past-due remainders and reversed late fees, less any unapplied credit — minus `additional_down_payment_cents`; because the additional down payment is netted into the carried-over balance in this way, the successor's own `down_payment_cents` is 0 and it is not deducted a second time, and its `service_fee_cents` is 0 as well. Every non-cancelled policy of the predecessor is re-pointed to the successor by rewriting its `agreement_id`, and each re-pointed policy has `carried_over` set to true, which keeps its premium out of the successor's premium basis — the money it represents is already arriving through `carried_over_balance_cents`, and counting both would double-finance the same premium.

The successor is then priced and scheduled immediately rather than passing through verification again: it runs the pricing engine's final variant, where its premium basis is zero because every attached policy is carried over, so its `amount_financed_cents` is simply its `carried_over_balance_cents`, and its `effective_annual_rate_bps` is selected from that figure by the same volume tiers and capped by the same statutory ceiling. A fresh schedule of `installment_count` installments is cut from the new `first_installment_due_date` by the rules of section 3.4, `principal_balance_cents` is set to `amount_financed_cents`, `unpaid_finance_charge_cents` to `finance_charge_cents`, `quote_basis` to `FINAL`, `activated_at` to the current clock instant, `status` to `ACTIVE` and `delinquency_stage` to `CURRENT`. The successor carries no rate exception of its own: its `rate_exception_approved` is false and its `approved_service_fee_cap_cents`, `approved_annual_rate_bps` and `rate_exception_approved_by_user_id` are null until a compliance officer grants one.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new stand-alone resource into existence — a state rate regime, a license, a borrower, an agency, a carrier, an agreement, a financed policy, an attestation, a payment, a notice of intent to cancel, an endorsement, or the successor agreement minted by a restructure — return `201 Created`. Every other operation, including every read, every state transition on an existing resource, the servicing run and the clock control, returns `200 OK`. Error responses carry the status code listed for their error identifier in section 5.
