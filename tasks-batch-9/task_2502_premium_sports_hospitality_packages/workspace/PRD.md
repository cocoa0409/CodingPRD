# Premium Sports Hospitality Packages API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A premium sports hospitality provider does not own the thing it sells. Every seat, every suite and every pit-lane walk begins life as a contractual promise from a team, a promoter or a federation, and that promise is honoured on the rights holder's own timetable rather than the provider's. The commercial consequence is uncomfortable: the provider signs guaranteed minimums it must pay for whether or not inventory ever arrives, sells packages to individual buyers, corporate clients and travel partners months before the inventory is confirmed, and then discovers — sometimes days before kick-off — that the release fell short. This API exists to make that exposure visible and arithmetically honest at every hop.

The system tracks a single finite pool of inventory through four successive narrowings. Rights are acquired against a fixture and carry a contracted quantity plus a guaranteed minimum. The rights holder then confirms release in tranches, each of which may arrive in full, arrive short, or lapse entirely. Whatever is actually released is waterfall-allocated across three sales channels according to a commercial priority order. Only then can a client booking commit units, be credentialed, and be delivered. Every one of those hops can under-deliver, and each under-delivery must propagate transitively back through the chain — reducing channel allocations, reverting already-sold bookings, re-dating payment schedules, voiding host rosters and re-scoring fulfillment risk — without ever leaking a unit or a cent.

Around that spine sit the operational realities of the business: package construction from ticket, hotel, transport, experience and catering lines at four pricing tiers with a margin floor that a second pair of eyes must sign off; venue hospitality build-outs with catering and staffing that occupy a venue zone for a service window; payment schedules with credit control; postponement handling with a bounded rebooking window and two entirely different cancellation settlement regimes; client servicing with itineraries, credentials and on-site hosts; and margin analysis per fixture that carries unsold inventory as an explicit exposure line. The requirements below specify all of it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts, conventions and cross-cutting principles that hold across every endpoint in the system.

### 2.1. Operating Roles and Request Authentication

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the declared role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authorisation per endpoint is tabulated in §4.

The seven roles are `RightsManager`, `CommercialManager`, `CommercialDirector`, `ClientServiceAgent`, `OperationsManager`, `FinanceController` and `ClientPortalUser`. They are drawn deliberately along segregation-of-duties lines: rights acquisition, commercial product construction, price approval, client servicing, venue operations, money movement and client self-service are six separate hands plus the buyer itself.

- A `RightsManager` acquires inventory rights from teams, promoters and federations. It owns the fixture lifecycle, deal commitment, the recording and confirmation of promised releases, and inventory recall.
- A `CommercialManager` builds the sellable product: channel allocation blocks with their waterfall priority, package skeletons, package component lines, and the pricing pass. It may never approve its own price.
- A `CommercialDirector` approves the reviewed margin and releases a package for sale. Publication is exclusive to this role, and is additionally blocked when the acting user is the same user who priced the package.
- A `ClientServiceAgent` sells and services: it registers clients, takes bookings, commits allocation to them, mints credentials and itineraries, rebooks after a postponement, and rosters on-site hosts.
- An `OperationsManager` runs the venue and the clock: it plans hospitality build-outs, declares them dressed and staffed, registers hosts, and drives the daily rollover sweep.
- A `FinanceController` controls money and cost recognition: it approves build, catering and staffing spend, settles rights deals against the holder, records installment payments, and reads the event margin.
- A `ClientPortalUser` is the buying client acting for itself through the self-service portal.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the client role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `RightsManager`, `CommercialManager`, `CommercialDirector`, `ClientServiceAgent`, `OperationsManager` or `FinanceController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for an audit stamp — as `committed_by_user_id` on a `RightsDeal`, as `priced_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a `PackageProduct`, and as `approved_by_user_id` on a `HospitalityBuild` — and it compares two such stamps to each other for the segregation-of-duties rule, but it never compares an operator's `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `ClientPortalUser`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`. Ownership checks therefore compare the resource's client linkage to `X-User-ID` directly: the header value must match an existing `Client` record, and it must equal the `client_id` of every `Booking`, `PaymentInstallment` (reached through the installment's owning booking) or `Client` resource the request touches. A violation is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope check guards reading a client record, reading a booking, reading an installment, paying an installment and cancelling a booking.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` outside that inclusive range is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every record matching the filter before the page window is applied.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, list endpoints expose filters that make every stored relationship reverse-reachable — the deal list filters on `event_id`, `deal_status` and `inventory_type`; the tranche list on `rights_deal_id` and `tranche_state`; the allocation-block list on `rights_deal_id`, `channel` and `block_state`; the package list on `event_id`, `package_status` and `tier`; the component list on `package_product_id` and `component_type`; the booking list on `client_id`, `package_product_id`, `allocation_block_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `payment_status`; the installment list on `booking_id`, `installment_state` and `installment_kind`; the build list on `event_id`, `build_state` and `zone_code`; and the host-assignment list on `on_site_host_id`, `booking_id`, `hospitality_build_id` and `assignment_state`. Every filter is optional and filters combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system — fixtures, rights deals, release tranches, allocation blocks, packages, package components, clients, bookings, payment installments, hospitality builds, on-site hosts and host assignments alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits (for example, `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`), stamped when the record is first created and never changed afterwards.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record — including mutations the client did not request directly, such as a derived aggregate being recomputed by a cascade.
- `version` (integer): a monotonically increasing counter that begins at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the record. It is the value clients pin when a write must be serialised against other writers (§2.6).

No entity in this system is exempt from these four; there is no create-only resource that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. All temporal reasoning — whether a tranche has lapsed, whether an installment is overdue, how far away a fixture is, whether the rebooking window has closed — reads a server-held **logical clock**. An `OperationsManager` may move that clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying `current_utc`, and the endpoint echoes back the clock's new value as `current_utc`. Setting the clock recomputes nothing on its own; it merely changes what every subsequent rule observes. Before the clock has ever been set, its value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and every rule that reads the clock sees that default rather than the real wall clock. Wherever a field below is described as being stamped with the current instant — `confirmed_at`, `lapsed_at`, `recalled_at`, `settled_at`, `priced_at`, `published_at`, `paid_at`, `released_at`, `run_at` — the value written is the logical clock's value at the moment of the write.

Elapsed whole days between two instants are computed by taking the difference between the two instants in seconds, dividing by 86,400, and discarding any fractional remainder. This is arithmetic on exact instants, never on calendar dates, and it is the definition used by every "days to event", "days overdue" and scheduling threshold in this document.

### 2.5. Money, Units and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. No monetary value is ever stored as a floating-point number, and no monetary field ever carries a fractional part. Where a money amount is produced by multiplying by a percentage, the result is rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties resolved away from zero — the "round half up" convention — and this rounding happens exactly once, at the step where the amount is created. An amount that has already been rounded is never re-rounded downstream; it is carried forward as the integer it became.

Two fields are fractions rather than cent amounts: `margin_pct` on a package and on the margin report, and `volume_discount_pct` on a booking. Both are rounded half-up to exactly four decimal places. `markup_pct` on a package component and `min_margin_pct` on a package are client-supplied fractions likewise expressed to four decimal places.

Inventory quantities — contracted units, promised units, confirmed units, target units, allocated units, committed units, released-back units, package units, tickets per package, space units, catering covers and staff counts — are integers throughout, and no rounding of any kind is applied to them. The fulfillment risk score is likewise an integer, produced by rounding a weighted sum half-up and then clamping it so that it can never exceed 100.

Where an amount is split across several lines and the parts must reconstitute the whole, the reconciling line is computed by subtraction rather than by rounding, so that residual cents land deterministically on that line and the parts sum to the whole with zero drift. Where a per-unit cost must be derived from a percentage and then multiplied by a quantity, the per-unit cost is rounded first and the rounded value is then multiplied — never the reverse.

Finally, nothing in this specification orders, selects or breaks a tie using `id`. Wherever an ordering or a tie-break is needed and no domain key supplies one, the key is `created_at`.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Contested Writes

Three writes in this system are contested: several legitimate actors may target the same parent record at once, and no state precondition distinguishes the writer who is working from a stale view from the writer who is not. Those three writes therefore require the client to pin the parent's `version`, and each has its own rejection code.

Allocating a booking against a channel block requires `expected_allocation_block_version` in the request body. It is a required field, not an optional hint. Its value must equal the target `AllocationBlock`'s current `version`. If the block's `version` has advanced since the client last read it — because another agent allocated a different booking against the same block, or because a waterfall pass or a recall touched it — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Two different bookings may each legitimately claim free units on the same block, so nothing else in the ladder would catch the writer holding the out-of-date view.

Confirming a release tranche requires `expected_rights_deal_version` in the request body, a required field whose value must equal the current `version` of the tranche's parent `RightsDeal`. A deal legitimately accepts many tranche confirmations, and each confirmation re-runs the channel waterfall, so a client holding a superseded view of `released_units` would otherwise allocate against inventory arithmetic that no longer holds. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Publishing a package requires `expected_package_product_version` in the request body, a required field whose value must equal the package's current `version`. Re-pricing a package is a legitimate repeat operation that rewrites `list_price_cents` and `margin_pct` and bumps the package's `version`, so the director must publish exactly the version whose margin was reviewed. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — most commonly because a re-price intervened and cleared the previous approval.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Booking creation, tranche creation, block creation, recall, commit, settle, credential issuance, rebooking, cancellation, installment payment, build approval, build readiness, host rostering and host release all take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the fixed sequence below and the first failing check produces the response. This matters constantly in practice: a caller who both lacks permission and references a missing fixture always sees the permission error, and a caller who both holds a stale version and targets a record in the wrong state always sees the stale-version error, because version pinning is evaluated ahead of every state precondition.

1. **Authentication.** Headers present and the role recognised — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation.** The role appears in the called endpoint's authorised list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Client scope.** For a `ClientPortalUser`, every touched client, booking or installment traces back to `X-User-ID` — `ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. **Request shape.** Body parses, every required field is present, every value matches its declared type, every enumerated value is a declared member, every integer bound holds, `sequence_no` is unique within its parent package, a build's `service_window_end` is strictly after its `service_window_start`, a shift's `shift_end` is strictly after its `shift_start`, `hospitality_zone_codes` is a non-empty array of unique strings, and any supplied `limit` lies between 1 and 100 inclusive — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Existence**, resolved for path identifiers left to right and then for body foreign keys, in this order: fixture (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`), rights deal (`ERR_RIGHTS_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`), release tranche (`ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_FOUND`), allocation block (`ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`), package (`ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`), package component (`ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`), client (`ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`), booking (`ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`), payment installment (`ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND`), hospitality build (`ERR_BUILD_NOT_FOUND`), on-site host (`ERR_HOST_NOT_FOUND`), host assignment (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`).
6. **Version pinning**, in the order block, deal, package: `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION`.
7. **State preconditions**, in the order fixture, deal, tranche, block, package, booking, installment, build, assignment: `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`, `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`, `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_PAYABLE`, `ERR_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.
8. **Operational safety**, in the order: hospitality readiness for credentialing (`ERR_BUILD_NOT_READY`), segregation of duties at publication (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), critical fulfillment risk at booking creation (`ERR_EVENT_RISK_CRITICAL`).
9. **Temporal rules**, in the order: venue zone conflict on build planning (`ERR_BUILD_ZONE_CONFLICT`), then the rebooking window (`ERR_REBOOK_WINDOW_CLOSED`).
10. **Referential rules**, in the order: booking not yet allocated (`ERR_BOOKING_NOT_ALLOCATED`), package with no component lines (`ERR_PACKAGE_HAS_NO_COMPONENTS`), host-assignment scope mismatch (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`), zone not at the venue (`ERR_ZONE_NOT_AT_VENUE`).
11. **Quantity and duty constraints**, in the order: guaranteed minimum above contract (`ERR_MINIMUM_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT`), duplicate live channel block (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_BLOCK`), promised units above contract (`ERR_TRANCHE_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT`), confirmed units out of range (`ERR_CONFIRMED_UNITS_OUT_OF_RANGE`), host capacity (`ERR_HOST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), installment amount mismatch (`ERR_INSTALLMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`), margin below floor (`ERR_MARGIN_BELOW_FLOOR`), channel or deal mismatch on allocation (`ERR_CHANNEL_OR_DEAL_MISMATCH`).
12. **Fiscal gates**, evaluated last, in the order: client credit standing (`ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`), block units unavailable (`ERR_ALLOCATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE`), credit limit (`ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`).

Two consequences of this ordering are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. On build planning, the zone-overlap conflict is evaluated **before** the check that the zone belongs to the fixture's venue, so a build that both overlaps an existing occupancy and names an unknown zone returns `ERR_BUILD_ZONE_CONFLICT`. On booking creation, the critical-risk acknowledgement gate is evaluated **before** the client's credit standing and **before** the credit limit, so a booking placed on a critically risky fixture for a blocked client with no headroom returns `ERR_EVENT_RISK_CRITICAL`.

### 2.8. Flat Topology and Foreign-Key Resolution

Every collection in this API sits at the root of the URL space, and no relationship is expressed by nesting one resource inside another's path. A release tranche is created by posting to the tranche collection with a `rights_deal_id` in the body, not by posting under a deal; an allocation block carries `rights_deal_id`; a package carries `event_id`; a component carries `package_product_id`; a booking carries `client_id` and `package_product_id`; a build carries `event_id`; a host assignment carries `on_site_host_id`, `booking_id` and `hospitality_build_id`. Relationships travel in request bodies as explicit identifiers.

Where a path does carry an identifier, that identifier is resolved before any body foreign key is resolved, and the corresponding 404 is returned for the first identifier that fails to resolve. A request naming both a missing record in the path and a missing foreign key in the body therefore always reports the path identifier's absence. Some foreign keys are never client-supplied: a booking's `allocation_block_id` is written by the server when inventory is committed to that booking and cleared back to null when the booking is reverted, rebooked or cancelled, and an installment's `booking_id` and `sequence_no` are written by the server because installments are only ever created as a consequence of booking creation, rebooking or cancellation settlement — never by a direct client call.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Maintenance

Many operations in this system mutate more than the record the caller named. A postponement moves bookings; moved bookings move their installments and their host rosters; moved host rosters move host counters; moved installments move client exposure. The rule throughout is that each hop is caused by the previous hop rather than by the original call, and that every record touched by any hop is a genuine mutation of that record: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as though it had been written directly. Recomputing a derived aggregate counts as a mutation for this purpose.

Derived fields are never stale. `released_units`, `shortfall_units`, `unallocated_units` and `minimum_liability_cents` on a rights deal, `allocated_units`, `committed_units` and `released_back_units` on an allocation block, `base_cost_cents`, `component_price_subtotal_cents`, `list_price_cents` and `margin_pct` on a package, `line_cost_cents` and `line_price_cents` on a component, `outstanding_cents` and `overdue_installment_count` on a client, the whole price breakdown and `paid_cents` on a booking, `days_overdue` on an installment, `total_cost_cents` on a build, `active_assignment_count` on a host, and `fulfillment_risk_score` with its derived `risk_posture` on a fixture are all recomputed at the moment their inputs change, within the same request.

One invariant deserves emphasis because it is routinely mis-modelled: an allocation block's `block_state` is a pure function of allocation against target, never of sales. It reads `OPEN` while `allocated_units` is below `target_units` and `EXHAUSTED` once `allocated_units` equals `target_units`; `RECALLED` is reached only by pulling the block. Committing a booking against a block, or releasing one back, moves `committed_units` and `released_back_units` but never touches `block_state`. Only the waterfall — or a recall — can change it.

### 2.10. Interval Semantics for Venue Zones and Host Shifts

Two different interval conventions coexist in this system, and confusing them produces wrong answers at the boundary.

A hospitality build's occupancy of a venue zone is a **closed** interval: both `service_window_start` and `service_window_end` are instants at which the zone is considered occupied. For conflict testing, each already-recorded window is additionally inflated by ninety minutes — an hour and a half of teardown and re-dress — on **both** ends. Two builds on the same fixture and the same zone therefore conflict when the new window's start is at or before the existing window's end plus ninety minutes **and** the new window's end is at or after the existing window's start minus ninety minutes. Windows that merely touch conflict; a gap of exactly ninety minutes conflicts; a gap of ninety-one minutes does not.

A host's shift, by contrast, is a **half-open** interval covering `shift_start` and every instant up to but not including `shift_end`, with no buffer at all. A shift that ends at precisely the instant another begins does not overlap it, and adjacency is therefore permitted. The host capacity rule is an **aggregate** test rather than a pairwise one: for every instant covered by the proposed shift, the number of that host's existing `ASSIGNED` assignments whose half-open shift contains that instant, plus one for the proposed shift itself, must be at most the host's `max_concurrent_bookings`. A host with a capacity of two may hold two overlapping assignments but not three, even where no two of the three overlap each other everywhere.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Fixture and Acquiring Rights Against It

Everything begins with a fixture. A `RightsManager` registers one by supplying its public `name`, its `sport` — one of `FOOTBALL`, `BASKETBALL`, `MOTORSPORT`, `TENNIS` or `RUGBY` — the `venue_name` hosting it, the `hospitality_zone_codes` inside that venue that may host a build-out, and the scheduled kick-off instant as `event_date`. The zone codes arrive as a non-empty array of unique strings and are the definitive list of places the operations team may later dress. A newly registered fixture opens in `SCHEDULED` with `postponement_count` at 0, `fulfillment_risk_score` at 0, `risk_posture` at `NORMAL`, and both `original_event_date` and `rebook_deadline_at` unset. The fixture's `status` moves only through `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED`, `RESCHEDULED`, `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED`, of which the last two are terminal.

Against a `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED` fixture — and never against one that has been cancelled or played, which draws `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE` — the same manager acquires inventory. A rights acquisition names the fixture through `event_id`, the `rights_holder_name` of the team, promoter or federation selling it, and the `inventory_type` being bought: `TICKET`, `HOSPITALITY_SPACE` or `ACCESS_EXPERIENCE`. It records `contracted_units`, at least 1, as the quantity the holder has undertaken to deliver, and `guaranteed_minimum_units`, at least 0, as the quantity the provider must pay for whether or not it is ever released or sold. A guaranteed minimum larger than the contracted quantity is commercially incoherent and is rejected with `ERR_MINIMUM_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT`. `unit_cost_cents`, at least 1, is the acquisition cost of one released unit.

A fresh acquisition opens in `DRAFT` with `released_units`, `shortfall_units`, `unallocated_units` and `minimum_liability_cents` all at 0 and with `committed_by_user_id` and `settled_at` unset. `DRAFT` is a working state: nothing may be promised against the deal and no channel may reserve from it. Committing the deal — permitted only from `DRAFT`, and only while the fixture is still `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED` — advances `deal_status` to `COMMITTED`, stamps `committed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and is the moment the provider takes on the guaranteed-minimum liability. Committing a deal that is anything other than `DRAFT` yields `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`; because the fixture's state is checked ahead of the deal's, a commit attempted on a cancelled fixture reports the fixture error rather than the deal error. Both the deal's minimum liability and the fixture's fulfillment risk are recomputed as part of the commit.

**Minimum liability** is the provider's exposure to the rights holder and is recomputed on deal commitment, on the recording of a new promised release, on every tranche confirmation, whenever the daily rollover lapses a tranche, and at settlement. It is derived in three steps. First, `released_units` is the total of `confirmed_units` across the deal's tranches whose `tranche_state` is `CONFIRMED` or `SHORT_CONFIRMED`; tranches that are still `PENDING` and tranches that have gone `LAPSED` contribute nothing to it. Second, `shortfall_units` is 0 whenever `released_units` is greater than or equal to `guaranteed_minimum_units` — a release that lands exactly on the guarantee leaves no shortfall — and is otherwise the guarantee minus the released quantity. Third, the shortfall unit cost is obtained by taking sixty percent of `unit_cost_cents` and rounding that single per-unit figure half-up to a whole cent, and `minimum_liability_cents` is then `released_units` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`, plus `shortfall_units` multiplied by that already-rounded shortfall unit cost. Rounding happens once, on the per-unit figure, before the multiplication by the shortfall quantity — not after it.

The deal's own `unallocated_units` measures released inventory that no channel currently holds: it is `released_units`, less the total of `allocated_units` across the deal's blocks whose `block_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED`, less the total of the `allocated_units` frozen on the deal's `RECALLED` blocks at the moment each was recalled.

A `FinanceController`, not the rights manager, closes the deal out against the holder. Settlement is permitted only when `deal_status` is `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, `FULLY_RELEASED` or `SHORTFALL_DECLARED`, the fixture has reached `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, and no tranche of the deal is still `PENDING`; any of those failing gives `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. Settlement moves `deal_status` to `SETTLED`, stamps `settled_at`, freezes `minimum_liability_cents` at its final recomputed value, moves every one of the deal's blocks whose `block_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` to `RECALLED` with `recalled_at` stamped, and recomputes the fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture`. `SETTLED` and `TERMINATED` are the two terminal deal states; the latter is reached only when the fixture itself is cancelled.

### 3.2. Promised Releases, Confirmation, Short Delivery and Shortfall

Rights holders release inventory on their own timetable, and the system models each promise explicitly. A `RightsManager` records one by naming the deal through `rights_deal_id`, the quantity as `promised_units` (at least 1), and `promised_release_at` as the instant by which the holder has undertaken to confirm it. A promise may only be recorded against a deal that is `COMMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` or `SHORTFALL_DECLARED`. The provider may not promise more than it bought: the total of `promised_units` across the deal's tranches whose `tranche_state` is `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `SHORT_CONFIRMED`, plus the new promise, must not exceed `contracted_units`, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT`. A new tranche opens in `PENDING` with `confirmed_units` at 0 and `confirmed_at` and `lapsed_at` unset.

Recording a promise on a deal that had already given up hope re-opens it. Where `deal_status` is `SHORTFALL_DECLARED` and the new promise brings `released_units` plus the total `promised_units` across all `PENDING` tranches (the new one included) back to at least `guaranteed_minimum_units`, the deal moves out of `SHORTFALL_DECLARED` — to `COMMITTED` when `released_units` is still 0, and to `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` when `released_units` is already above 0. Both branches recompute `minimum_liability_cents` and the fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture`. Crucially, re-entering `COMMITTED` does **not** zero `shortfall_units`: that figure remains the guarantee less the units actually released, because only genuine confirmed releases reduce it, and a promise is not a release.

Confirmation is where the promise meets reality. The manager confirms a tranche by supplying `confirmed_units` together with `expected_rights_deal_version` (§2.6). The tranche must still be `PENDING`, or the call fails with `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_PENDING`; its deal must be `COMMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` or `SHORTFALL_DECLARED`; and `confirmed_units` must lie between 1 and the tranche's `promised_units` inclusive, with anything outside that range rejected as `ERR_CONFIRMED_UNITS_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Two branches follow, and they are not symmetric in consequence. When `confirmed_units` equals `promised_units` the tranche becomes `CONFIRMED`; when `confirmed_units` is at least 1 and strictly below `promised_units` it becomes `SHORT_CONFIRMED`. Either way `confirmed_units` is written, `confirmed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the parent deal's `version` increases by one, and the deal's `released_units`, `shortfall_units`, `unallocated_units`, `minimum_liability_cents` and `deal_status` are all recomputed. Either way the channel waterfall then runs over the deal's blocks. What distinguishes the short branch is that it can push the deal into shortfall, which in turn shrinks nothing already allocated but does re-price the provider's liability; the full branch never can.

The deal's status is not set by hand on confirmation — it is re-derived by a single status function that runs after every tranche state change, whether that change is a confirmation, a short confirmation or a lapse. Let the pending quantity be the total of `promised_units` across the deal's tranches still in `PENDING`. Then: if `released_units` is greater than or equal to `contracted_units`, the deal is `FULLY_RELEASED`; otherwise, if `released_units` plus the pending quantity is strictly below `guaranteed_minimum_units`, the deal is `SHORTFALL_DECLARED`; otherwise, if `released_units` is above 0, the deal is `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`; otherwise the deal is `COMMITTED`. Reaching `FULLY_RELEASED` additionally forces `shortfall_units` to 0. This function is what produces the deal's whole middle life: `COMMITTED → PARTIALLY_RELEASED` on the first partial release, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED → PARTIALLY_RELEASED` as a self-loop on each further release that still leaves the contract unfilled, and `PARTIALLY_RELEASED → FULLY_RELEASED` or `COMMITTED → FULLY_RELEASED` once the contracted quantity is met or beaten.

When a tranche reaches a terminal state and the remaining promises can no longer cover the guarantee, the deal declares shortfall — `COMMITTED → SHORTFALL_DECLARED` or `PARTIALLY_RELEASED → SHORTFALL_DECLARED` — and a four-hop chain follows. The first hop sets `deal_status` to `SHORTFALL_DECLARED` and `shortfall_units` to `guaranteed_minimum_units` minus `released_units`. Because `shortfall_units` became non-zero, the second hop recomputes `minimum_liability_cents`, so the provider now owes `released_units` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents` **plus** `shortfall_units` multiplied by the rounded sixty-percent shortfall unit cost, for units it will never receive. Because the deal's `released_units` and `minimum_liability_cents` changed, the third hop re-runs the fixture's fulfillment risk score and re-bands `risk_posture` — which is what later gates new bookings on that fixture. And the fourth hop re-runs the channel waterfall over the deal's `OPEN` blocks to distribute whatever *was* released; that pass never reduces an existing allocation, because `released_units` only ever grows.

A promise that is never honoured lapses. During the operations rollover sweep, every tranche that is still `PENDING` whose `promised_release_at` is strictly earlier than the logical clock, and whose deal is neither `SETTLED` nor `TERMINATED`, moves `PENDING → LAPSED`: `lapsed_at` is stamped, `confirmed_units` stays at 0, the parent deal's `version` increases by one, the deal status function re-runs (and may declare shortfall exactly as above), and the fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture` are recomputed. Lapsed tranches are processed in ascending `promised_release_at` order, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. `CONFIRMED`, `SHORT_CONFIRMED` and `LAPSED` are all terminal tranche states.

### 3.3. Reserving Inventory for Channels: The Allocation Waterfall

Released inventory does not sit in one undifferentiated pool. A `CommercialManager` carves it up by sales channel, creating an allocation block that names the deal through `rights_deal_id`, the `channel` it serves — `INDIVIDUAL`, `CORPORATE` or `TRAVEL_PARTNER` — the `target_units` that channel wants (at least 1), and a `priority_rank` (at least 1) that decides who gets filled first when releases fall short. Rank 1 fills first and larger numbers fill later. Blocks may only be created against a deal that is `COMMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` or `SHORTFALL_DECLARED`, and a deal may hold at most one live block per channel: if another block on the same deal already serves the same `channel` with `block_state` of `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED`, the create is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_BLOCK`. A recalled block does not block a replacement. A new block opens `OPEN` with `allocated_units`, `committed_units` and `released_back_units` all at 0 and `recalled_at` unset.

The **waterfall** is the single mechanism that decides how many units each block holds, and it runs in two passes. Its candidate set is every block of the deal whose `block_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED`, ordered by `priority_rank` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; `RECALLED` blocks are excluded and keep their frozen `allocated_units` untouched. The quantity available for distribution is the deal's `released_units` less the total `allocated_units` frozen on that deal's `RECALLED` blocks. The distribution pass then walks the candidates in order and gives each block the lesser of its own `target_units` and the quantity still remaining, writes that figure to `allocated_units`, sets `block_state` to `EXHAUSTED` when the figure equals `target_units` and to `OPEN` otherwise, and subtracts the figure from the remaining quantity. Whatever remains after the last candidate is recorded as the deal's `unallocated_units`. Where the candidate set is empty — a deal with released units and no live blocks — the entire distributable quantity is simply recorded in `unallocated_units` and no error is raised.

The distribution pass is monotonic in every case but one. Confirming a tranche, lapsing a tranche and recalling a block can only ever raise a block's `allocated_units`, because `released_units` never decreases. The one input that lowers an incumbent's allocation is the creation of a **new** block whose `priority_rank` sorts ahead of it: the newcomer takes units the incumbent already held, so the incumbent's `allocated_units` falls, and a block sitting at `EXHAUSTED` returns `EXHAUSTED → OPEN` as it gives units back to the higher-priority channel.

That is what the second pass exists for. Where the distribution pass has left a block holding fewer allocated units than it has already sold, the revert pass unwinds sales until the block is no longer over-committed. Walking the candidates again in the same `priority_rank` ascending, `created_at` ascending order, the pass computes the block's over-commitment as `committed_units` minus `allocated_units` and skips any block for which that figure is not positive. For an over-committed block it walks that block's bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED`, ordered by `created_at` **descending** so that the most recently committed sale is undone first, and for each such booking it takes the consumed quantity to be the booking's `units` multiplied by that booking's package's `tickets_per_package`, moves the booking `ALLOCATED → RESERVED`, clears its `allocation_block_id` to null, subtracts the consumed quantity from the block's `committed_units`, adds the same quantity to the block's `released_back_units`, and reduces the outstanding over-commitment by it. The walk stops as soon as the over-commitment is no longer positive. Bookings that are `CREDENTIALED` or `DELIVERED` are never reverted; only `ALLOCATED` ones are. Afterwards the block's `block_state` is set once more purely from allocation against target — `OPEN` when `allocated_units` is below `target_units`, `EXHAUSTED` when it equals it — and never from `committed_units`. This revert pass is the only mechanism in the entire system by which an `ALLOCATED` booking returns to `RESERVED`.

The waterfall is invoked on the recording-of-release side by every tranche confirmation, on both the `CONFIRMED` and the `SHORT_CONFIRMED` branch; by every tranche lapse during the operations rollover; by the creation of a block, which is the only reducing case; and by a recall.

A `RightsManager` may pull a channel's allocation back to the rights holder, but only while nothing has been sold from it: the block's `block_state` must be `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` **and** its `committed_units` must be exactly 0, and a violation of either gives `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_OPEN`. A recall moves the block to `RECALLED`, stamps `recalled_at`, freezes `allocated_units` at its current figure, increases the parent deal's `version` by one, recomputes the deal's `unallocated_units`, re-runs the waterfall across the deal's remaining `OPEN` blocks so the freed units flow to the next channels in priority order, and recomputes the fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture`. `RECALLED` is terminal for a block, and it is reached in exactly three ways: an explicit recall, deal settlement, and fixture cancellation.

### 3.4. Building, Pricing and Publishing a Hospitality Package

A package is the commercial object the client actually buys, and it is assembled independently of the inventory chain. Either a `CommercialManager` or a `CommercialDirector` may create the skeleton, naming the fixture through `event_id`, giving the package a commercial `name`, selecting a pricing `tier` of `BRONZE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `PLATINUM`, and declaring `tickets_per_package` — the number of rights-holder ticket units that one unit of this package consumes, at least 1. Three inputs are optional: `hotel_nights`, the hotel nights bundled per package unit, which defaults to 0; `transport_included`, a flag for bundled ground transportation, which defaults to false; and `min_margin_pct`, the floor the package's computed margin must clear before it may go on sale, which defaults to 0.25. Skeletons may only be created while the fixture is `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED`. A new package opens in `DRAFT` with `base_cost_cents`, `component_price_subtotal_cents`, `list_price_cents` and `margin_pct` all at 0 and with `priced_by_user_id`, `priced_at`, `published_by_user_id` and `published_at` unset. Its `package_status` moves only through `DRAFT`, `PRICED`, `PUBLISHED` and the terminal `CLOSED`.

Component lines are added by a `CommercialManager` only. Each line names its package through `package_product_id`, declares a `component_type` of `TICKET`, `HOTEL`, `TRANSPORT`, `EXPERIENCE` or `CATERING`, carries a human-readable `descriptor`, a `quantity` of at least 1 units of that component per package unit, a `unit_cost_cents` of at least 0, a `markup_pct` between 0 and 5 inclusive expressed as a fraction to four decimal places, and a `sequence_no` of at least 1 that must be unique within the package. That ordinal is not cosmetic: it pins the order in which the pricing pass rounds the lines. Lines may only be added while the package is `DRAFT` or `PRICED`; adding to a `PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED` package is `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_STATE`. When a line is created its own `line_cost_cents` and `line_price_cents` are computed there and then, but the parent package's cost, subtotal, list price and margin are deliberately *not* refreshed — those fields only ever move when the pricing pass runs.

Pricing is available from `DRAFT` and, as a self-loop, from `PRICED`, and may be performed by a `CommercialManager` or a `CommercialDirector`. A package with no component lines at all cannot be priced and is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_HAS_NO_COMPONENTS`. The pass first re-derives every line, walking the package's components in ascending `sequence_no` order — an order that is total, since the ordinal is unique per package, so no tie-break is ever needed. For each line, `line_cost_cents` is that line's `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by its `quantity`, and `line_price_cents` is that line cost multiplied by one plus the line's `markup_pct`, rounded half-up to a whole cent **at the line**. The package's `base_cost_cents` is then the total of `line_cost_cents` across all its lines, and its `component_price_subtotal_cents` is the total of the already-rounded `line_price_cents` across all its lines. The rounding order is pinned and consequential: each line is rounded first and the rounded lines are then summed. Summing unrounded line prices and rounding the total once is explicitly *not* the rule and yields a different cent figure.

With the subtotal in hand the pass applies the tier. A `BRONZE` package is multiplied by 1.00, a `SILVER` package by 1.10, a `GOLD` package by 1.25 and a `PLATINUM` package by 1.45; `list_price_cents` is `component_price_subtotal_cents` multiplied by that tier multiplier and rounded half-up to a whole cent. The package's `margin_pct` is then the list price less the base cost, divided by the list price, rounded half-up to four decimal places — except that when `list_price_cents` comes out as 0 the margin is fixed at 0 rather than divided by zero. Finally the pass sets `package_status` to `PRICED`, stamps `priced_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `priced_at` with the logical clock, clears both `published_by_user_id` and `published_at` back to null, and increases the package's `version` by one. Clearing the approval stamps is deliberate: an already-reviewed price cannot be published after it has been changed underneath the reviewer, and the version bump is exactly what the publication version pin observes.

Publication belongs to the `CommercialDirector` alone and is the compliance spine of the commercial process. The package must be `PRICED`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_STATE`. The director must supply `expected_package_product_version` matching the package's current `version`, or the request is `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION` — which is precisely what happens when someone re-priced the package after the director read it. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the package's `priced_by_user_id`: the operator who set a price may never be the operator who approves it, and an attempt to do both is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The computed `margin_pct` must be greater than or equal to `min_margin_pct`, with a shortfall rejected as `ERR_MARGIN_BELOW_FLOOR`. And publication is refused outright where the package's fixture carries no rights deal at all for `TICKET` inventory whose `deal_status` is `COMMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` or `FULLY_RELEASED` — a package must not go on sale with no ticket supply standing behind it. When all of that holds, `package_status` becomes `PUBLISHED`, `published_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting user, `published_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the package's `version` increases by one again.

Packages do not close by hand. Whenever the fixture is completed or cancelled, every one of its packages whose `package_status` is not already `CLOSED` moves to `CLOSED` — `PUBLISHED → CLOSED`, `PRICED → CLOSED` and `DRAFT → CLOSED` alike.

### 3.5. Selling: Clients, Bookings and the Price Build-Up

A `ClientServiceAgent` registers the buyer, supplying a `name`, a `client_type` of `INDIVIDUAL`, `CORPORATE` or `TRAVEL_PARTNER`, and optionally a `credit_limit_cents` that defaults to 0 and caps the outstanding exposure the provider will carry. A new client opens with `client_status` at `ACTIVE`, `outstanding_cents` at 0 and `overdue_installment_count` at 0.

Taking a booking is the busiest gate in the system. The agent supplies `client_id`, `package_product_id`, the `channel` the sale belongs to — `INDIVIDUAL`, `CORPORATE` or `TRAVEL_PARTNER` — the number of package `units` bought (at least 1), and optionally `risk_override_ack`, a boolean acknowledgement that defaults to false. The target package must be `PUBLISHED`, or the sale is `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_STATE`, and its fixture must still be `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED`. Where the fixture's `risk_posture` currently reads `CRITICAL`, the agent must knowingly accept the fulfillment risk by sending `risk_override_ack` as true; without it the sale is refused with `ERR_EVENT_RISK_CRITICAL` (HTTP 422). Only then does credit control speak: the client's `client_status` must be `ACTIVE`, with an `ON_HOLD` or `BLOCKED` client refused as `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the client's `outstanding_cents` plus this booking's freshly computed `net_price_cents` must be at most `credit_limit_cents`. The comparison is strict on the far side: landing exactly on the limit is permitted, and only strictly exceeding it is refused, with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

A booking that survives all of that opens with `fulfillment_status` at `RESERVED` on its delivery dimension and `payment_status` at `UNPAID` on its money dimension, with `allocation_block_id`, `settlement_variant`, `credential_serial_prefix` and `itinerary` all null, and `credentials_issued_count` and `rebook_count` at 0. The two status dimensions are genuinely parallel: they live on the same record and evolve independently, and an operation that drives one leaves the other untouched.

**How a booking is priced.** The evaluation order below is pinned, and it matters because the naive reading — that the partner commission and the guarantee surcharge are percentages of the net booking value — is circular, since the net value is itself defined from both of them. The order resolves that circularity by keying every percentage on the discounted subtotal.

1. `gross_price_cents` is the package's `list_price_cents` multiplied by the booking's `units`.
2. `volume_discount_pct` is chosen from the unit count alone, never from any money amount: 0.1200 when `units` is at least 20, otherwise 0.0800 when `units` is at least 10, otherwise 0.0400 when `units` is at least 5, and otherwise 0.0000.
3. The volume discount in cents is the gross price multiplied by that fraction, rounded half-up to a whole cent, and `discounted_subtotal_cents` is the gross price less that rounded discount.
4. `partner_commission_cents` is the discounted subtotal multiplied by the commission rate for the booking's `channel` — 0.0000 for `INDIVIDUAL`, 0.0300 for `CORPORATE` and 0.1000 for `TRAVEL_PARTNER` — rounded half-up to a whole cent.
5. `guarantee_surcharge_cents` is keyed on the discounted subtotal as well, never on the net price, and it is charged only where the fixture is actually exposed. Exposure means the package's fixture has at least one rights deal for `TICKET` inventory whose `deal_status` is `COMMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` or `SHORTFALL_DECLARED` and whose `released_units` is strictly below its `contracted_units`. Where no such deal exists, the surcharge is 0. Where one does, the surcharge rate runs *inversely* to size, because larger bookings carry a smaller guarantee load per cent: 0.0200 when `discounted_subtotal_cents` is at least 5,000,000 cents, otherwise 0.0350 when it is at least 1,000,000 cents, otherwise 0.0500. The surcharge is the discounted subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half-up to a whole cent.
6. `net_price_cents` is the discounted subtotal, less the partner commission, plus the guarantee surcharge.

Each intermediate cent figure is rounded once at the step that creates it, and no already-rounded figure is rounded again downstream. The same pricing routine runs again, unchanged, whenever a booking is rebooked onto a different package.

**Committing inventory to a booking.** A `RESERVED` booking holds a price but no inventory. The agent commits inventory by naming an `allocation_block_id` and supplying `expected_allocation_block_version` matching that block's current `version` (§2.6). The booking must be `RESERVED`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The block's `block_state` must be `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` — allocating from a `RECALLED` block is `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_OPEN`. The block's `channel` must equal the booking's `channel`, and the block's rights deal must have `deal_status` of `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` or `FULLY_RELEASED`; either failing gives `ERR_CHANNEL_OR_DEAL_MISMATCH`. Finally the block must actually have the units free: its `allocated_units` less its `committed_units` must be at least the booking's `units` multiplied by the package's `tickets_per_package`, and a shortfall is `ERR_ALLOCATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE`. On success the booking moves `RESERVED → ALLOCATED`, its `allocation_block_id` is set, the block's `committed_units` increases by the booking's `units` multiplied by `tickets_per_package`, the block's `version` increases by one, and the fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture` are recomputed. The booking's `payment_status` is untouched.

### 3.6. Payment Schedules, Collection and Credit Standing

Every booking is sold with a payment schedule, and the schedule is generated by the server as a consequence of the sale rather than by any direct client call. Installment rows are discoverable only through the installment list filtered by `booking_id`; each row carries its parent `booking_id`, a 1-based `sequence_no` unique within that booking, an `amount_cents`, a `due_at`, an `installment_kind` and an `installment_state`, plus `paid_amount_cents` at 0, `paid_at` unset and `days_overdue` at 0.

**Schedule shape.** Let the payable amount be the booking's `net_price_cents` less its `paid_cents`, floored at 0 — on a fresh sale `paid_cents` is 0, so the payable amount is simply the net price. Let the distance to the fixture be the whole days between the logical clock and the fixture's `event_date`. When that distance is at least 30 days — exactly 30 days out still qualifies — the schedule has three lines. The first line's `amount_cents` is thirty percent of the payable amount rounded half-up; the second line's is forty percent of the payable amount rounded half-up; and the third line's is the payable amount less the first two lines, computed by subtraction so that residual cents land on the last line and the three lines sum exactly to the payable amount with no drift. The first line falls due three days after the logical clock. The second falls due ninety days before the fixture, or on the first line's due date if that is later. The third falls due thirty days before the fixture, or on the second line's due date if that is later. When the fixture is fewer than 30 days away, the schedule collapses to a single line for the whole payable amount, due three days after the logical clock. Lines whose `amount_cents` works out to 0 are still created, so that sequence numbers stay stable and the conservation identity always holds: the total `amount_cents` across a booking's `SCHEDULE` installments that are `SCHEDULED`, `OVERDUE` or `PAID`, plus whatever had already been paid, equals the booking's `net_price_cents`.

Two kinds of installment exist. `SCHEDULE` is the purchase schedule described above and is the default. `SETTLEMENT` is the single line raised when a cancellation leaves the client owing the provider money (§3.8). An installment's `installment_state` runs `SCHEDULED`, `PAID`, `OVERDUE` or `VOIDED`, with `PAID` and `VOIDED` terminal.

**Collection.** A `FinanceController`, or the `ClientPortalUser` paying for itself, settles one installment by supplying `amount_cents_paid`. Payment must be exact: `amount_cents_paid` has to equal the installment's `amount_cents`, and both partial payments and overpayments are rejected with `ERR_INSTALLMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — the well-formed-but-wrong counterpart to a malformed body. The installment's `installment_state` must be `SCHEDULED` or `OVERDUE`, and where its `installment_kind` is `SCHEDULE` its parent booking must not be `CANCELLED`; a `PAID` or `VOIDED` installment, or a `SCHEDULE` line on a cancelled booking, gives `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_PAYABLE`. A `SETTLEMENT` line remains payable on a cancelled booking, which is the whole point of it. On success the installment moves `SCHEDULED → PAID` or `OVERDUE → PAID`, its `paid_amount_cents` is set to its `amount_cents`, `paid_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `days_overdue` is reset to 0. The parent booking's money dimension then advances, its `paid_cents` is recomputed as the total of `paid_amount_cents` across its installments less its `refund_cents`, and the owning client's `outstanding_cents` and `overdue_installment_count` are recomputed.

The money dimension itself reads as follows. From `UNPAID`, a payment that still leaves at least one `SCHEDULE` installment of the booking in `SCHEDULED` or `OVERDUE` moves `payment_status` to `DEPOSIT_PAID`. From `DEPOSIT_PAID`, a payment after which no `SCHEDULE` installment of the booking remains in `SCHEDULED` or `OVERDUE` moves it to `PAID_IN_FULL`. A single-line schedule therefore goes `UNPAID → PAID_IN_FULL` directly, since the one payment leaves nothing outstanding. Separately, where a cancellation has left the booking in `SETTLEMENT_DUE`, paying the `SETTLEMENT` installment moves `SETTLEMENT_DUE → SETTLED` and zeroes `balance_due_cents`. `REFUNDED` is the sixth value of the dimension and is reached only through cancellation. Each of these payment-side transitions leaves `fulfillment_status` untouched.

**Client credit exposure.** A client's `outstanding_cents` is the sum of two parts. The first is, across the client's bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `RESERVED`, `ALLOCATED`, `CREDENTIALED`, `DELIVERED` or `REBOOK_PENDING`, the booking's `net_price_cents` less its `paid_cents`, floored at 0 for each booking individually. The second is, across the client's bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `CANCELLED` **and** whose `payment_status` is `SETTLEMENT_DUE`, that booking's `balance_due_cents`; a cancelled booking that has already settled or been refunded contributes nothing. The client's `overdue_installment_count` is simply the number of installments across all of that client's bookings whose `installment_state` is `OVERDUE`. Both figures are recomputed on booking creation, on cancellation, on rebooking, on installment payment, on fixture postponement, on fixture cancellation, and during the operations rollover.

**The operations rollover.** An `OperationsManager` drives a daily sweep that performs four things in order and reports what it did. First it lapses promises: every `PENDING` tranche whose `promised_release_at` is now behind the logical clock moves to `LAPSED` as described in §3.2, in ascending `promised_release_at` order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, bumping each affected deal's `version` and re-running the minimum-liability calculation and the deal status function. Second it ages money: every `SCHEDULED` installment whose `due_at` is now behind the logical clock moves `SCHEDULED → OVERDUE` and has its `days_overdue` recomputed as the whole days between its `due_at` and the logical clock; an installment that is not `OVERDUE` reports `days_overdue` as 0. Third it re-evaluates credit standing for every affected client. Fourth it re-runs the deal status function, the channel waterfall and the fixture risk score for everything it touched.

Credit standing is a pure function of the overdue count after the sweep's ageing pass, and it moves in both directions. A client with an `overdue_installment_count` of at least 1 and at most 2 lands on `ON_HOLD` — whether it arrives there from `ACTIVE` or back up from `BLOCKED`. A client with an `overdue_installment_count` of 3 or more lands on `BLOCKED`, whether from `ON_HOLD` or straight from `ACTIVE`. A client with an `overdue_installment_count` of exactly 0 returns to `ACTIVE`, whether from `ON_HOLD` or from `BLOCKED`. No credit-standing state is terminal, and only the rollover moves `client_status`.

The sweep answers with `run_at`, the logical instant it ran; `lapsed_tranche_count`, how many tranches it lapsed; `overdue_installment_count`, how many installments it aged into `OVERDUE`; `shortfall_deal_count`, how many deals it pushed into `SHORTFALL_DECLARED`; and `client_status_change_count`, how many clients changed credit standing.

### 3.7. Venue Build-Out, On-Site Hosts and Credentialing

Selling a package is not the same as being able to deliver it, and delivery runs through the venue. An `OperationsManager` plans a hospitality build-out against a fixture that is still `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED`, naming the fixture through `event_id` and the venue zone it occupies through `zone_code`. That code must be one of the fixture's own `hospitality_zone_codes`; a zone that is not at the venue is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_AT_VENUE` (HTTP 422). The occupancy runs from `service_window_start` to `service_window_end`, the latter strictly after the former, and it may not collide with another live occupancy of the same zone on the same fixture. Concretely, no other build on the same `event_id` and the same `zone_code` whose `build_state` is `PLANNED`, `APPROVED` or `READY` may overlap once the existing window has been inflated by the ninety-minute teardown buffer on both sides, as spelled out in §2.10; a collision is `ERR_BUILD_ZONE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Note the ordering: the collision test runs ahead of the zone-membership test, so a build that both collides and names an unknown zone reports the collision.

The plan also records `space_units`, the hospitality-space units drawn for the build (at least 1); `catering_covers`, the number of catering covers ordered (at least 0); `staff_count`, the catering and hosting staff rostered (at least 0); and the three cost lines `build_cost_cents`, `catering_cost_cents` and `staffing_cost_cents`, each at least 0. The build's `total_cost_cents` is simply the sum of those three cost lines and is computed at once. A new build opens in `PLANNED` with `approved_by_user_id` unset, and — importantly — a `PLANNED` build does not yet enter the fixture's margin cost base.

Finance sign-off is what pulls the spend into the margin. A `FinanceController` approves a build that is `PLANNED`, on a fixture that is `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED`; approving a build in any other `build_state` is `ERR_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`, and because the fixture's state is checked ahead of the build's, an approval on a cancelled fixture reports `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. Approval moves `PLANNED → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and from that moment the build's `total_cost_cents` counts against the fixture's margin. Declaring the build-out dressed, catered and staffed is the `OperationsManager`'s call and requires the build to be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`; it also requires the build to be genuinely resourced, meaning `staff_count` of at least 1 and `catering_covers` of at least 1, and a build that is neither staffed nor catered cannot be declared ready. `APPROVED → READY` is the safety gate that makes credential issuance possible on the fixture. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal build states and are reached only when the fixture is completed or cancelled.

Hosts are registered independently of any fixture. An `OperationsManager` supplies the host's `full_name` as it will be printed on client itineraries, a `languages` array of language codes the host services clients in, and optionally `max_concurrent_bookings`, the number of assignments that may overlap on that host at any single instant, which defaults to 1 and must be at least 1. A new host opens with `active_assignment_count` at 0; that counter is always the number of that host's assignments whose `assignment_state` is `ASSIGNED`.

Rostering a host is shared between the `ClientServiceAgent` and the `OperationsManager`. An assignment names the host through `on_site_host_id`, the client booking through `booking_id` and the build-out the host works inside through `hospitality_build_id`, and it runs from `shift_start` to `shift_end`, the latter strictly after the former. The booking must already hold inventory: its `fulfillment_status` must be `ALLOCATED` or `CREDENTIALED`, and rostering a host onto a booking that is not yet allocated is `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The build must be `APPROVED` or `READY`, since a `PLANNED` build has no confirmed hospitality footprint to staff and a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` build can no longer be staffed; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`. The build named by `hospitality_build_id` must belong to the same fixture as the booking named by `booking_id`, and the shift must lie inside that build's service window; either failing is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Finally the aggregate capacity test of §2.10 must pass, or the roster is refused with `ERR_HOST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A new assignment opens `ASSIGNED` with `released_at` unset, and it immediately triggers a recompute of the host's `active_assignment_count` and of the host list on the booking's itinerary.

An assignment leaves `ASSIGNED` in two quite different ways. Released deliberately — by an `OperationsManager` or a `ClientServiceAgent`, and also automatically for every still-`ASSIGNED` roster when the fixture is completed — it moves `ASSIGNED → RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped at the logical clock, and the host's `active_assignment_count` and the booking's itinerary host list are recomputed. Releasing an assignment that is already `RELEASED` or `VOIDED` is `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`. Voided instead — which happens automatically whenever the parent booking leaves `CREDENTIALED` through a booking cancellation, a fixture postponement or a fixture cancellation — it moves `ASSIGNED → VOIDED`, also with `released_at` stamped, and also followed by a recompute of the host's `active_assignment_count`. Both `RELEASED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

**Credentialing** is the last servicing step before the fixture. A `ClientServiceAgent` mints credentials for a booking whose `fulfillment_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, `DELIVERED`, `REBOOK_PENDING` or `CREDENTIALED` — those four give `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`, which in practice means credentials are minted from `ALLOCATED`. The fixture must have at least one hospitality build in `build_state` `READY`, because clients are never credentialed into a venue whose hospitality space has not been signed off; the absence of one is `ERR_BUILD_NOT_READY` (HTTP 422). And the booking's `allocation_block_id` must be non-null, because credentials can only be minted against inventory actually committed to this booking; a null gives `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_ALLOCATED` (HTTP 422). The readiness check precedes the allocation check, so a booking that is neither allocated nor served by a ready build reports `ERR_BUILD_NOT_READY`.

Issuance moves the booking `ALLOCATED → CREDENTIALED`, sets `credentials_issued_count` to the booking's `units` multiplied by one more than the package's `tickets_per_package` — one credential per ticket plus one hospitality credential per package unit — and stamps a `credential_serial_prefix` on the booking. It also freezes the servicing `itinerary`, an object with a pinned set of keys: `event_name` and `event_date` and `venue_name` taken from the fixture, `hotel_nights` and `transport_included` taken from the package, `credential_count` matching the credentials just minted, and `host_names`, which lists the `full_name` of each host on this booking's assignments whose `assignment_state` is `ASSIGNED`, ordered by those assignments' `created_at` ascending. That host list is refreshed whenever a roster is added or an assignment is released.

### 3.8. Disruption: Postponement, Rebooking and Cancellation Settlement

Fixtures move, and when they do the whole sold book has to move with them. Only a `RightsManager` postpones a fixture, and the same operation behaves in two entirely different ways depending on where the fixture currently stands. The single optional input is `new_event_date`.

From `SCHEDULED`, postponement freezes the book. A supplied `new_event_date`, if present, must be strictly after the logical clock. The fixture stamps `original_event_date` with its current `event_date` — but only when `original_event_date` is still unset, so the first-ever scheduled kick-off is preserved and never overwritten by a later postponement. Where `new_event_date` was supplied, `event_date` is replaced by it; where it was omitted, `event_date` is left alone. `postponement_count` increases by one, `status` becomes `POSTPONED`, and `rebook_deadline_at` is set to twenty-one days after the logical clock — the rebooking window is three weeks wide and is re-opened from scratch on every fresh postponement.

That status change then propagates in four hops, each caused by the hop before it rather than by the postponement call itself.

- **Hop two.** Every booking on a package of this fixture whose `fulfillment_status` is `RESERVED`, `ALLOCATED` or `CREDENTIALED` moves to `REBOOK_PENDING`, processed in ascending `created_at` order.
- **Hop two-b.** Because each of those bookings was previously `ALLOCATED` or `CREDENTIALED`, its allocation block gives the units back: the block's `committed_units` decreases by the booking's `units` multiplied by the package's `tickets_per_package`, the block's `released_back_units` increases by the same figure, and the booking's `allocation_block_id` is cleared to null. The block's `block_state` is deliberately not touched — it tracks allocation against target, not sales. A booking that was `CREDENTIALED` additionally has its `credentials_issued_count` reset to 0.
- **Hop three.** Because each booking entered `REBOOK_PENDING`, every one of its installments whose `installment_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `OVERDUE` has its `due_at` rewritten to the fixture's new `rebook_deadline_at` and its `installment_state` set to `SCHEDULED` — an overdue line is un-aged, because the client is no longer late for a fixture that is no longer happening.
- **Hop three-b.** Because each booking left `CREDENTIALED`, every one of its host assignments whose `assignment_state` is `ASSIGNED` moves to `VOIDED` with `released_at` stamped at the logical clock.
- **Hop four.** Because those assignments left `ASSIGNED`, each affected host's `active_assignment_count` is recomputed; and because those installments changed state and due date, each affected client's `outstanding_cents` and `overdue_installment_count` are recomputed.

The fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture` are recomputed at the end.

From `POSTPONED`, the same operation does something much narrower: it confirms a replacement date. Here `new_event_date` is mandatory and must be strictly after the logical clock — a postponement call on an already-`POSTPONED` fixture without a replacement date is `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. The fixture moves `POSTPONED → RESCHEDULED`, `event_date` becomes the supplied replacement, and `rebook_deadline_at` is left exactly as it was. Bookings already sitting in `REBOOK_PENDING` stay in `REBOOK_PENDING`: naming a replacement date does not rebook anybody. Only the risk score is recomputed, and it typically falls, because the postponed-status weight drops out of it.

And from `RESCHEDULED`, the cycle closes: a replacement date that itself collapses sends the fixture `RESCHEDULED → POSTPONED`, increasing `postponement_count` by one again, setting `rebook_deadline_at` afresh to twenty-one days after the logical clock, replacing `event_date` where a `new_event_date` was supplied, and running the whole hop-two-through-hop-four cascade again over whatever bookings are still `RESERVED`, `ALLOCATED` or `CREDENTIALED`. Postponement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE` on a fixture that is already `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED`.

**Rebooking** is how a frozen booking is rescued, and it belongs to the `ClientServiceAgent`. The agent supplies `target_package_product_id`. The booking must be `REBOOK_PENDING`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The logical clock must be at or before the fixture's `rebook_deadline_at`, inclusive — landing exactly on the deadline instant is still inside the window, and passing it is `ERR_REBOOK_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). The target package must have `package_status` of `PUBLISHED`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_STATE`, and must belong to a fixture whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED`.

A successful rebooking replaces the booking's `package_product_id` with the target, increases `rebook_count` by one, and moves the booking `REBOOK_PENDING → RESERVED`. The pricing routine of §3.5 re-runs in full against the target package, rewriting `gross_price_cents`, `volume_discount_pct`, `discounted_subtotal_cents`, `partner_commission_cents`, `guarantee_surcharge_cents` and `net_price_cents`. Every installment of the booking whose `installment_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `OVERDUE` is moved to `VOIDED`, and the schedule-generation routine of §3.6 then issues a fresh `SCHEDULE`; because that routine's payable amount is the new `net_price_cents` less the booking's existing `paid_cents` floored at 0, the new schedule's total plus what the client has already paid comes to exactly the new `net_price_cents`. Lines that had already reached `PAID` stay `PAID` and are represented by that already-paid figure. The client's `outstanding_cents` and `overdue_installment_count` are recomputed, and the fulfillment risk score is recomputed for **both** the fixture the booking left and the fixture it arrived at.

**Cancellation** may be performed by a `ClientServiceAgent` or by the `ClientPortalUser` that owns the booking, in which case `X-User-ID` must equal the booking's `client_id`. The booking's `fulfillment_status` must not already be `CANCELLED` or `DELIVERED`; either is `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. Cancellation is available from `RESERVED`, from `ALLOCATED`, from `CREDENTIALED` and from `REBOOK_PENDING`.

The settlement arithmetic has **two variants**, and which one applies is decided purely by the fixture's `status` at the moment of cancellation. Both variants begin from the same two quantities: the whole days between the logical clock and the fixture's `event_date`, and the booking's `paid_cents` as it stood immediately *before* this call.

The **`TIER_SCHEDULE`** variant applies when the fixture's `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `RESCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. The fee percentage is read off a days-to-event ladder in which every comparison is inclusive: 0.10 when the fixture is at least 90 days away, otherwise 0.25 when it is at least 60 days away, otherwise 0.50 when it is at least 30 days away, otherwise 0.80 when it is at least 7 days away, otherwise 1.00. `cancellation_fee_cents` is the booking's `net_price_cents` multiplied by that percentage, rounded half-up to a whole cent.

The **`COST_RECOVERY`** variant applies when the fixture's `status` is `POSTPONED` — the client is walking away rather than rebooking, so the schedule is replaced by what the provider genuinely cannot recover. Two cost components are added. The ticket component is 0 where the booking's `allocation_block_id` is null, because a booking that was never allocated committed no rights-holder inventory; otherwise it is the booking's `units` multiplied by the package's `tickets_per_package` multiplied by the `unit_cost_cents` of the rights deal reached through that allocation block. The non-ticket component is the package's `base_cost_cents` multiplied by the booking's `units` multiplied by twenty percent — the irrecoverable share of hotel, transport, experience and catering commitments — rounded half-up to a whole cent. `cancellation_fee_cents` is then the lesser of the booking's `net_price_cents` and the sum of those two components, so the fee can never exceed what the client agreed to pay. Identical booking data therefore yields a different fee under `COST_RECOVERY` than under `TIER_SCHEDULE`, because the fee is driven by committed acquisition cost rather than by how far away the fixture is.

Whichever variant ran, its name is recorded on the booking as `settlement_variant` — null until cancellation, then either `TIER_SCHEDULE` or `COST_RECOVERY`. From the fee, `refund_cents` is the pre-cancellation paid amount less the fee, floored at 0, and `balance_due_cents` is the fee less the pre-cancellation paid amount, also floored at 0. Exactly one of the two can be positive, and the conserved identity always holds afterwards: the fee equals the pre-cancellation paid amount, less the refund, plus the balance due.

The cancellation then applies a common set of effects. `fulfillment_status` becomes `CANCELLED` and `allocation_block_id` is cleared to null. `payment_status` becomes `REFUNDED` when `balance_due_cents` is 0, and `SETTLEMENT_DUE` otherwise. Every installment of the booking whose `installment_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `OVERDUE` moves to `VOIDED` with `days_overdue` reset to 0, and where `balance_due_cents` is above 0 exactly one further installment is created with `installment_kind` of `SETTLEMENT` for that amount, falling due seven days after the logical clock. The booking's `paid_cents` is reduced by `refund_cents`. The client's `outstanding_cents` and `overdue_installment_count` are recomputed, and so are the fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture`.

Three of the four originating states add effects on top of that common set, and they nest. Cancelling from `ALLOCATED` additionally returns the units: the block's `committed_units` decreases by the booking's `units` multiplied by `tickets_per_package` and its `released_back_units` increases by the same figure, with `block_state` left untouched. Cancelling from `CREDENTIALED` does everything the `ALLOCATED` case does and additionally resets `credentials_issued_count` to 0 and moves every one of the booking's host assignments whose `assignment_state` is `ASSIGNED` to `VOIDED`, after which each affected host's `active_assignment_count` is recomputed. Cancelling from `RESERVED` or from `REBOOK_PENDING` applies the common set only, since neither state holds a block.

### 3.9. Fulfillment Risk, Fixture Closure and Margin Analysis

**Fulfillment risk** is the number the commercial team watches, and it is recomputed on every mutation that touches a fixture, its rights deals, its release tranches, its allocation blocks or its bookings — concretely: deal commitment, the recording of a promised release, tranche confirmation, block recall, deal settlement, booking creation, allocation, booking cancellation, rebooking, fixture postponement, fixture cancellation, fixture completion, and the operations rollover. Four independent inputs feed it, all scoped to the fixture:

- the promised quantity, being the total of `contracted_units` across the fixture's rights deals whose `deal_status` is anything other than `TERMINATED`;
- the released quantity, being the total of `released_units` across those same non-terminated deals;
- the lapsed quantity, being the total of `promised_units` across the tranches of those deals whose `tranche_state` is `LAPSED`;
- the committed quantity, being the total, across the fixture's bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `RESERVED`, `ALLOCATED`, `CREDENTIALED` or `REBOOK_PENDING`, of each booking's `units` multiplied by its package's `tickets_per_package`. Bookings that are `CANCELLED` or `DELIVERED` never contribute.

The score is the sum of four weighted components. The **gap component** is 40 multiplied by the following fraction, itself capped at 1: the committed quantity less the released quantity, floored at 0, divided by the committed quantity or by 1 where the committed quantity is 0. The **lapse component** is 30 multiplied by the lapsed quantity divided by the promised quantity, or by 1 where the promised quantity is 0, again capped at 1. The **horizon component** is 20 when the fixture is at most 14 whole days away, 10 when it is at most 30 whole days away, and 0 otherwise. The **status component** is 10 when the fixture's `status` is `POSTPONED` and 0 for every other status. `fulfillment_risk_score` is the sum of those four components rounded half-up to a whole number and then capped so that it can never exceed 100.

`risk_posture` is a band over that score, and both boundaries are inclusive: `CRITICAL` when `fulfillment_risk_score` is 70 or more, `ELEVATED` when it is 40 or more, and `NORMAL` otherwise. A `CRITICAL` posture is what forces a selling agent to acknowledge the risk explicitly on a new booking (§3.5).

**Cancelling a fixture** is a `RightsManager` action available from `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED`, and it is the widest cascade in the system. The fixture's `status` becomes `CANCELLED`. Every rights deal of the fixture whose `deal_status` is neither `SETTLED` nor `TERMINATED` moves to `TERMINATED`; because each of those deals terminated, its `PENDING` tranches move to `LAPSED` with `lapsed_at` stamped and its blocks whose `block_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` move to `RECALLED` with `recalled_at` stamped. Every package of the fixture whose `package_status` is not already `CLOSED` moves to `CLOSED`. Every booking of the fixture whose `fulfillment_status` is neither `CANCELLED` nor `DELIVERED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and because the provider caused the cancellation the fee is waived entirely: `settlement_variant` is recorded as `TIER_SCHEDULE`, `cancellation_fee_cents` is 0, `refund_cents` equals whatever the booking had paid, `balance_due_cents` is 0, `payment_status` becomes `REFUNDED`, and `allocation_block_id` is cleared to null. Because those bookings were cancelled, every one of their installments whose `installment_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `OVERDUE` moves to `VOIDED`, and because those installments were voided each affected client's `outstanding_cents` and `overdue_installment_count` are recomputed. Every hospitality build of the fixture whose `build_state` is `PLANNED` or `APPROVED` moves to `CANCELLED` while every one that is `READY` moves to `CLOSED`; because those builds left the active set, every host assignment of the fixture's bookings whose `assignment_state` is `ASSIGNED` moves to `VOIDED` and each affected host's `active_assignment_count` is recomputed. Finally the fixture's `fulfillment_risk_score` and `risk_posture` are recomputed.

**Completing a fixture** is likewise a `RightsManager` action, available from `SCHEDULED` and from `RESCHEDULED` only. It requires the fixture's `event_date` to be at or before the logical clock — completing a fixture that has not yet kicked off is `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE` — and it requires that no booking of the fixture is still `RESERVED` or `REBOOK_PENDING`, since every sold place must have been either delivered or disposed of before the books close. Completion sets `status` to `COMPLETED`; moves every booking whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED` or `CREDENTIALED` to `DELIVERED`; moves every package whose `package_status` is not already `CLOSED` to `CLOSED`; moves every hospitality build that is `READY` to `CLOSED` and every build that is `PLANNED` or `APPROVED` to `CANCELLED`; and moves every host assignment of the fixture's bookings whose `assignment_state` is `ASSIGNED` to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped, recomputing each affected host's `active_assignment_count`. `CANCELLED` and `DELIVERED` are the terminal fulfillment states of a booking.

**Margin analysis** is a computed read available to a `FinanceController` or a `CommercialDirector`. It has two variants and an optional `variant` input that selects between them. While the fixture's `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `POSTPONED` or `RESCHEDULED` the report defaults to `PROVISIONAL`; once the fixture's `status` is `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` it defaults to `FINAL`. Either variant may be requested explicitly for the same data by supplying `variant` as `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, so a live fixture can be read on a realised basis and a finished one on a forward-looking basis.

The cost base and the unsold position are shared between the variants. `rights_cost_cents` is the total of `minimum_liability_cents` across the fixture's rights deals whose `deal_status` is anything other than `TERMINATED`. `build_cost_cents` is the total of `total_cost_cents` across the fixture's hospitality builds whose `build_state` is `APPROVED`, `READY` or `CLOSED`; builds that are still `PLANNED` and builds that were `CANCELLED` never enter the cost base. `cost_cents` is those two added together. The unsold position is then accumulated deal by deal, walking the fixture's non-terminated deals in ascending `created_at` order: for each deal, the sold quantity is the total of `committed_units` across that deal's blocks whose `block_state` is `OPEN`, `EXHAUSTED` **or** `RECALLED` — a recalled block's frozen committed units still count as sold — and that deal's unsold quantity is its `released_units` less that sold quantity, floored at 0. `unsold_units` is the total of those per-deal unsold quantities, and the full-cost exposure is accumulated as the sum over deals of each deal's unsold quantity multiplied by its own `unit_cost_cents`.

The variants then differ on exactly two lines. Under `PROVISIONAL`, `revenue_cents` is the total of `net_price_cents` across the fixture's bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `RESERVED`, `ALLOCATED`, `CREDENTIALED`, `DELIVERED` or `REBOOK_PENDING` — every live booking is assumed to convert — and `unsold_exposure_cents` is the full-cost exposure carried at one hundred percent of acquisition cost. Under `FINAL`, `revenue_cents` is the total of `net_price_cents` across only the bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `DELIVERED`, plus the total of `cancellation_fee_cents` across the bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `CANCELLED` — realised delivery revenue plus retained cancellation fees — and `unsold_exposure_cents` is the full-cost exposure written down by the salvage recovery, that is, the full-cost exposure multiplied by one less thirty-five percent, rounded half-up to a whole cent.

From there both variants close the same way. `margin_cents` is `revenue_cents` less `cost_cents`. `net_margin_cents` is `margin_cents` less `unsold_exposure_cents`. And `margin_pct` is `net_margin_cents` divided by `revenue_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, except that where `revenue_cents` is 0 the reported margin percentage is 0. The report answers with `variant`, `revenue_cents`, `rights_cost_cents`, `build_cost_cents`, `cost_cents`, `unsold_units`, `unsold_exposure_cents`, `margin_cents`, `net_margin_cents` and `margin_pct`.
