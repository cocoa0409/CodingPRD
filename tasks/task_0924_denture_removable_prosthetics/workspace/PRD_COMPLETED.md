# Denture & Removable-Prosthetics Clinic API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A denture and removable-prosthetics clinic is not one workflow but several that run at different speeds and settle against each other only at the end. A clinician takes an impression or an intraoral scan, judges its quality, books a wax or framework try-in, ships the work to an external laboratory, receives it back, inspects it, delivers the finished appliance, and then lives with that appliance for years — adjusting it, relining it, repairing it, recalling the patient, and occasionally remaking it under warranty. In parallel, and on an entirely different calendar, the front office asks the carrier for a predetermination, submits a claim to a primary plan and then a secondary plan, strikes an installment plan for whatever the patient still owes, and chases that plan when it goes unpaid. The two calendars touch in exactly the places that hurt: a laboratory's workmanship failure becomes a credit against a patient's installment schedule; an unpaid balance freezes new fabrication but must never freeze the payment that would cure it; a remake restarts the clinical warranty while deliberately refusing to restart the insurance replacement clock.

This document specifies the REST API that runs that clinic. Every resource is mounted under `/prosthetics`. The system is deliberately opinionated about ordering: the order in which requests are refused, the order in which money is computed, the order in which cascading side effects fire, and the order in which installments absorb a credit are all pinned here, because in this domain two implementations that differ only in ordering will produce different numbers and different error codes for the same request.

Three structural commitments shape the rest of the document. First, a **per-arch** model: a case may cover the maxillary arch, the mandibular arch, or both, and each arch advances on its own clock, so one arch can be sitting in a remake while its sibling is already delivered. Second, a **parallel-axis** model on the delivered appliance: warranty and recall are two independent state axes carried on one row, and a movement on one never implies a movement on the other. Third, a **fiscal gate**: a patient whose installment plan has gone delinquent or defaulted is frozen out of new clinical starts, but never out of reading their record, never out of emergency repair, and never out of paying.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Clinic Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, if `X-User-ID` is empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the six declared roles, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This is the first check the server performs on any request, ahead of everything else including resource lookup. When the headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called (§4 carries that list per endpoint), the request is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403.

The six roles are `Prosthodontist`, `ClinicalDirector`, `LabTechnician`, `FrontOffice`, `Patient`, and `PlatformAdmin`, spelled exactly so.

- The `Prosthodontist` is the treating clinician and owns the clinical surface: exam close-out, arch planning, impressions, try-in decisions, clinic-side QC, delivery, post-delivery services, recall visits, and remake *requests*.
- The `ClinicalDirector` is the authorising clinician. It is the only role that may adjudicate a warranty fault or decide a remake request, and — by design — it is never permitted to decide a remake it itself requested.
- The `LabTechnician` is the external laboratory acting as itself.
- The `FrontOffice` owns billing and insurance: coverages, predeterminations, claims, payment plans, laboratory dispatch logistics, and payment reversals.
- The `Patient` acts on their own record only.
- The `PlatformAdmin` is an operator role. It owns laboratory registration, the delinquency sweep, and the logical clock, and performs no clinical work.

Two of these roles are entity-backed, meaning `X-User-ID` is the identifier of a persisted row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `LabTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DentalLab`; ownership checks on any resource that references a laboratory compare the resource's `dental_lab_id` to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a laboratory may only receive a lab case that was dispatched to it.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient`; ownership checks compare the patient reachable from the resource to `X-User-ID`. The ownership map resolves a patient row to its own `id`, a payment plan to its `patient_id`, an installment to its parent plan's `patient_id`, and a case to its `patient_id`.

The remaining four roles — `Prosthodontist`, `ClinicalDirector`, `FrontOffice`, and `PlatformAdmin` — are opaque. For each of them, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says to (as `decided_by_user_id` on a try-in milestone, and as `requested_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a remake request) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Ownership enforcement is a distinct, third check that runs after authentication and role authorisation and before any resource-existence check. It is enforced on exactly five endpoints:

- `GET /prosthetics/patients/{patient_id}`
- `GET /prosthetics/payment-plans/{payment_plan_id}`
- `GET /prosthetics/installments/{installment_id}`
- `POST /prosthetics/installments/{installment_id}/pay`
- `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/receive`

On the first four, a `Patient` caller whose `X-User-ID` is not the owning patient is refused. On the fifth, a `LabTechnician` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the lab case's `dental_lab_id` is refused. In every case the refusal is `ERR_NOT_OWNER` with HTTP 403. Callers in other roles are not subject to this check on these endpoints; their access is decided by the role list alone.

Those five split into two shapes, and the split decides what a caller sees when the path names **nothing at all**. On `GET /prosthetics/patients/{patient_id}` the owner is the path segment itself, so the comparison needs no stored row: a `Patient` caller naming any identifier other than their own is refused `ERR_NOT_OWNER` and HTTP 403 whether or not that patient exists. On the other four the owner has to be *read out of* the target row — a plan's `patient_id`, an installment's plan's `patient_id`, a lab case's `dental_lab_id` — so when the path names no row there is nothing to compare against; the ownership check is skipped and rung 4 speaks, giving HTTP 404. A `Patient` probing for a payment plan that does not exist is therefore told 404, while the same caller probing for a patient that does not exist is told 403.

Segregation of duties is enforced on identity, not on role. Two different people may both hold `ClinicalDirector`; what the system forbids is the same `X-User-ID` both requesting and deciding the same remake. That check is described in §2.8 at its ladder position and again in §3.7 where it belongs to the workflow.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits; it is freshly random on every response and is never reused or correlated with anything.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the **logical clock of §2.6, not from the host machine's wall clock**, so every response issued while the clock stands at one instant carries that same instant verbatim, and the value only moves when a `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. It is advisory prose only; no client behaviour depends on its wording, and the same `error_id` may carry different messages at different call sites.
- Two transport-level failures fall outside the business ladder of §2.10 and outside the catalogue's declared status pairing. A path matching no declared route answers `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and a declared path called with an undeclared method answers `ERR_VALIDATION` with **HTTP 405** — the one place `ERR_VALIDATION` does not carry 400.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and has a minimum of 0; there is no upper bound on it. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-schema failure and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400. A `limit` or `offset` that is present but not parseable as an integer is the same failure; a parameter supplied as the empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and takes its default.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because two rows minted inside the same cascade can carry the same `created_at`, ties are broken by `id` ascending so that pagination is stable across pages. Identifiers are minted so that this tie-break is also **creation order**: although each `id` is a syntactically ordinary bare UUID, its leading digits are drawn from a monotonically increasing counter, so within one `created_at` the row minted first sorts first. That is what makes a cascade's own output — a superseding lab case after the draft it displaced, an installment schedule in `sequence_number` order — readable in the order it was written.

The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters *before* the limit and offset window is applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is strictly less than `total_count`.

Filter query parameters, where an endpoint declares them, are combined with logical AND, and an omitted filter is simply not applied; a filter supplied as the empty string counts as omitted. A query parameter an endpoint does not declare is ignored outright rather than refused. The two kinds of declared filter behave differently on a value that matches nothing: an **enum-valued** filter carrying a value outside its member set is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400 (never `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` — see rung 8), while an **identifier-valued** filter naming a row that does not exist is not validated at all and simply returns an empty page with `total_count` 0. The filters each list endpoint accepts are named in §3 alongside the workflow that needs them, and the full endpoint inventory is in §4.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and Response Projection

Every resource type in this system — `Patient`, `Coverage`, `DentalLab`, `ProsthesisCase`, `CaseArch`, `ImpressionRecord`, `TryInMilestone`, `LabCase`, `Prosthesis`, `PostDeliveryService`, `WarrantyClaim`, `RemakeRequest`, `Predetermination`, `Claim`, `PaymentPlan`, and `Installment` — carries the same four common properties, without exception:

- `id`: a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix; it is a bare UUID string. Clients never supply it.
- `created_at`: the instant the row was created.
- `updated_at`: the instant the row was last mutated. At creation it equals `created_at`.
- `version`: an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

The `version` field is the backbone of the concurrency rules in §2.8 and of the cascade accounting in §2.13, so its granularity matters. One request may mutate several rows; each mutated row's `version` advances by exactly 1 for that request, regardless of how many individual fields on it changed. A row that a request merely reads does not advance. A row that a request writes twice inside one cascade still advances by 1 in total, not 2. That per-request cap is absolute and overrides every per-transition statement in §3: where §3 says a transition "increments the row's `version` by 1" and a single request drives two such transitions on the same row — a prosthesis whose warranty band and recall band both move on one read, an arch reached by two legs of one cascade — the row still ends the request exactly 1 higher. The **only** exception is a leg §3 explicitly calls "a further `version` increment", which is the automatic move of a claim on to `CLOSED` in §3.8; that leg deliberately bumps a second time.

A row created by a request is born at `version` 1, and that 1 already accounts for every write the same request later makes to it: a payment plan whose `principal_amount` is struck after the row is minted, a lab case the system mints and then routes, are all returned at `version` 1, not 2.

A further consequence, and the one that decides most of the ambiguous cases: **a `version` belongs to the transition that caused the write, not to every field that moved.** Three families of change therefore leave `version` and `updated_at` alone.

- **Derived fields.** A number of response fields are not stored at all but computed afresh at projection time from other rows: `write_gate_active`, `delivered_prosthesis_count` and `last_frequency_anchor_at` on a patient; `benefit_remaining` on a coverage; `completed_milestones`, `next_required_milestone`, `remake_count` and `remake_request_count` on an arch; `retake_required` on an impression record; `adjustments_remaining` on a prosthesis; `paid_amount`, `outstanding_amount` and `missed_count` on a payment plan. When one of these changes because some *other* row was written, the row carrying the field is untouched — its `version` and `updated_at` do not move, and no request has to be issued against it for the new value to be visible on the next read.
- **Money recomputation on a case.** Re-running the nine-step balance of §3.9 over a `ProsthesisCase` is a pure recomputation of `billed_amount`, `insurance_paid_amount`, `prompt_pay_discount_amount` and `patient_responsibility_amount`; on its own it never advances the case's `version` or `updated_at`. The case advances only when the same request also drives a declared case transition — the exam close-out, the delivery bubble-up, the payment-plan create, the remake re-entry, closure.
- **Bookkeeping fields written as a by-product.** Where §3 names a field a cascade sets on a row whose own state axis is not moving, the section says so at that point; unless it does, the by-product write carries no `version` increment.

Response projection is one shape per entity: the body of a create, the body of a mutating call, the body of a single read and each row of a list page all carry exactly the same field set, in the same rendering. No field is added on create and dropped on read, and no list row is a reduced summary of the detail body.

Response projection is post-commit. The body a mutating call returns is the state of the resource *after* the write has been applied, including the already-incremented `version` and the already-refreshed `updated_at`. A client that reads `version` out of a create or update response and then uses it as an expected-version input on a subsequent request must find it accepted.

Fields declared nullable are always present in the response body; when they have no value they are rendered as JSON `null` rather than omitted. Computed collection fields — `required_milestones` and `completed_milestones` on an arch — are always present as JSON arrays, empty rather than null when they hold nothing.

### 2.5. Timestamps and the Rendering of Instants

There is exactly one rendering convention for instants in this API, and it applies uniformly. **Every instant — server-written stamp, caller-supplied instant echoed back, or derived/computed instant — is rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`, in the form `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.sssZ`.** There is no second convention and no field exempt from it.

That covers, without exception:

- Server-written stamps: `created_at`, `updated_at`, `reviewed_at`, `decided_at`, `dispatched_at`, `received_at`, `expired_at`, `completed_at`, `voided_at`, `adjudicated_at`, `determined_at`, `submitted_at`, `reversed_at`, `paid_at`, `delivered_at`, and `fabrication_started_at`.
- Caller-supplied instants echoed back: `effective_date` on a coverage and `first_due_date` on a payment plan are normalised into the canonical form on the way in, so a caller who submits `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` reads back `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. The same normalisation applies to the clock-setting input. On the way in an instant must carry a date **and** a time-of-day; a bare calendar date such as `2026-05-01` is not an instant and is a rung 7 schema failure. Seconds, fractional seconds and the zone designator are each optional, a value with no zone designator is read as already being UTC, and a value carrying a numeric offset is converted to UTC before it is stored — so what is echoed back may differ from what was sent by exactly that offset.
- Derived and computed instants: `promised_due_at`, `shelf_life_expires_at`, `warranty_expires_at`, `frequency_anchor_at`, `replacement_eligible_date`, `recall_due_at`, `adjustment_window_ends_at`, `mandatory_reline_due_at`, `expires_at`, `due_date`, and `last_frequency_anchor_at`.

**Deadlines are instants, never end-of-day markers.** When a rule adds a whole number of calendar days or months to an anchor instant, the time-of-day component of the anchor is preserved exactly. A shelf life of 45 days computed from a dispatch at `2026-05-01T09:15:00.000Z` expires at `2026-06-15T09:15:00.000Z`, not at the end of that calendar day. Nothing in this specification snaps a deadline to midnight or to 23:59:59.

Calendar-month arithmetic (used by the replacement-frequency clock and the service-frequency limits) keeps the day-of-month and clamps to the last day of the target month when the target month is shorter: 60 months after `2026-03-31` lands on `2031-03-31`, and 1 month after `2026-01-31` would land on `2026-02-28`.

One field is deliberately not an instant: `date_of_birth` on a patient is a plain calendar date string in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and is stored and echoed exactly as supplied.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Every window, ceiling, expiry, grace period, due date, overlap interval, and frequency limit described in this document is evaluated against a **logical clock** that the server holds.

Before any caller has set it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. It never falls back to real time, never drifts, and never advances on its own. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`; the endpoint answers with the resulting clock in a `current_utc` field and HTTP 200.

Moving the clock has consequences on the next read of every clock-derived band, because three families of transition are evaluated lazily whenever the affected row is read or handled rather than being driven by any caller action: the warranty band on a delivered appliance, the recall band on that same appliance, and the expiry of an approved or alternate-benefit predetermination. Those three are described where they belong, in §3.6 and §3.8.

### 2.7. Money, Precision and Rounding

All monetary amounts are decimal numbers carried to **two decimal places**, and every rounding operation in this specification is **round half up** (a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, so 0.125 becomes 0.13). Money is rendered in responses as a JSON number with two decimal places.

Two ordering rules govern how rounding interacts with the rest of the arithmetic, and both are load-bearing:

1. **Round each declared step, then combine.** Where a calculation in §3 is written as numbered steps, the rounding happens at each numbered step, not once at the end. Where a cap or floor is applied to a rounded product — for instance the annual-maximum cap on an insurance payment — the product is rounded first and the cap is applied to the rounded value afterwards.
2. **Round the unit, then place the residual.** Where a principal is divided into equal installments, the per-installment base is rounded first and the whole residual is placed on the final installment. Summing first and rounding once is specifically wrong here and will produce a different schedule.

Counts, scores, ceilings, and day/month intervals are integers and are compared exactly. Where a boundary is inclusive or exclusive, this document says so at that boundary; no boundary is left to inference.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Supersede-Style Creates

Three create operations in this system legitimately *supersede* something that already exists. Because a supersede write is not guarded by any state precondition — the older row is in a perfectly valid state and is simply displaced — the only protection against a client acting on a view it has not refreshed is an explicit expected-version check. All three are guarded, and no other endpoint in this API takes a version field.

- `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases` requires `expected_case_arch_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `CaseArch` named by `case_arch_id`. If that value does not equal the arch's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The rule exists because a newer draft lab case supersedes an older draft lab case on the same arch; without the check a second create working from a stale view of the arch would silently discard the first dispatch plan.
- `POST /prosthetics/services` requires `expected_prosthesis_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Prosthesis` named by `prosthesis_id`. A mismatch with the prosthesis's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A newer service of the same `service_type` voids an older un-completed one, so a client working from a stale view of `adjustments_remaining`, `in_patient_possession`, or `warranty_status` would supersede work it never saw.
- `POST /prosthetics/payment-plans` requires `expected_prosthesis_case_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ProsthesisCase` named by `case_id`. A mismatch with the case's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A newer plan supersedes the case's current active or delinquent plan and strikes its principal from the case's patient responsibility, so a stale view of that amount would mint a schedule for the wrong balance.

In all three the field is a required input, not an optional one: omitting it is a request-schema failure refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400, and that schema check sits *below* the version check on the ladder, so a request that omits the field never reaches the version comparison.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Nested Reads

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that names nothing decides the error. The path parameters used across this API are `patient_id`, `coverage_id`, `lab_id`, `case_id`, `case_arch_id`, `impression_id`, `try_in_id`, `lab_case_id`, `prosthesis_id`, `service_id`, `warranty_claim_id`, `remake_request_id`, `predetermination_id`, `claim_id`, `payment_plan_id`, and `installment_id`.

Five entity types carry their own dedicated not-found code, and everything else shares one:

- a `patient_id` that names no patient yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`;
- a `case_id` that names no case yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`;
- a `case_arch_id` that names no arch yields `ERR_ARCH_NOT_FOUND`;
- a `lab_case_id` that names no lab case yields `ERR_LAB_CASE_NOT_FOUND`;
- a `prosthesis_id` that names no prosthesis yields `ERR_PROSTHESIS_NOT_FOUND`;
- a path segment naming a `Coverage`, `DentalLab`, `ImpressionRecord`, `TryInMilestone`, `PostDeliveryService`, `WarrantyClaim`, `RemakeRequest`, `Predetermination`, `Claim`, `PaymentPlan`, or `Installment` that resolves to no row yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

All six carry HTTP 404.

Foreign keys arriving in a **request body** are a separate matter and are checked one rung lower, after the write gate. A body field naming a row that does not exist — `patient_id`, `case_id`, `case_arch_id`, `dental_lab_id`, `prosthesis_id`, `coverage_id`, `warranty_claim_id`, `post_delivery_service_id`, `attributed_lab_case_id`, `superseded_prosthesis_id`, or `payment_plan_id` — is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404, which is a different code from the path-segment codes above.

### 2.10. The Order of Refusal

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error comes back, and which one is not a matter of implementation taste. The server evaluates its checks as a strict ladder of 31 rungs in the order below, stops at the first rung that fails, and returns that rung's code. A rung that a request does not reach never runs and never writes anything (with the two deliberate exceptions called out in §2.12). The ladder is listed here once, in full, in evaluation order.

1. **Rung 1 — authentication.** `X-User-Role` is present and names one of the six roles, and `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Scope: every endpoint.
2. **Rung 2 — role authorisation.** The role named by `X-User-Role` appears in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called. Failure: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, HTTP 403. Scope: every endpoint.
3. **Rung 3 — entity-backed ownership.** As described in §2.1. Failure: `ERR_NOT_OWNER`, HTTP 403. Scope: `GET /prosthetics/patients/{patient_id}`, `GET /prosthetics/payment-plans/{payment_plan_id}`, `GET /prosthetics/installments/{installment_id}`, `POST /prosthetics/installments/{installment_id}/pay`, and `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/receive` — five endpoints and no others.
4. **Rung 4 — path existence.** Every resource named by a path segment exists, validated left to right, with the per-entity codes of §2.9. Failure: `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAB_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROSTHESIS_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; all HTTP 404. Scope: every endpoint that has a path parameter. Note that this rung sits *below* ownership: a `Patient` caller asking for someone else's **existing** plan is told it is not the owner, not that the plan does not exist. Where ownership can only be established by reading the target row, however, a path naming no row leaves rung 3 with nothing to compare and this rung answers instead — see the two shapes described in §2.1.
5. **Rung 5 — the global write gate.** Detailed in §2.11. Failure: `ERR_ACCOUNT_DELINQUENT`, HTTP 403. Scope: the gated family enumerated in §2.11. This rung sits above every schema, enum, concurrency, and state check, and therefore pre-empts 26 of the 31 rungs below it.
6. **Rung 6 — body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body names an existing row of its declared parent entity, as listed in §2.9. Failure: `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Scope: every create that accepts a foreign key in its body.
7. **Rung 7 — request schema and ranges.** Every required input field of the endpoint is present, types match, numeric fields lie inside their declared ranges, pagination parameters are in bounds, and declared uniqueness pairs are free. The declared ranges are: `quality_score` from 0 to 100 inclusive; `unit_count` from 1 to 32 inclusive; `bench_line` from 1 to the target laboratory's `bench_line_count` inclusive; `installment_count` from 2 to 12 inclusive; `down_payment_amount` at least 0.20 times the case's *pre-discount* patient responsibility (the value observed before any prompt-pay discount is struck, as described in §3.9); `amount_paid` strictly greater than zero and equal to the installment's `amount` to the cent; `limit` from 1 to 100; `offset` at least 0. **Exactly one** uniqueness pair is enforced at this rung: `case_id` together with `arch_position` on a new arch. (The other candidate pair, `patient_id` together with `rank` on a new coverage, is *not* enforced here — it belongs to rung 27 and its dedicated code; see the note at the end of this subsection.) Failure: `ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400.
8. **Rung 8 — enum membership, exhaustively routed.** A value outside the declared member set of any of the following fields routes to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` with HTTP 422, **and only these fields do**:
   - `prosthesis_type` on `POST /prosthetics/cases`
   - `arch_position` on `POST /prosthetics/case-arches`
   - `capture_method` on `POST /prosthetics/impressions` and on `POST /prosthetics/impressions/{impression_id}/request-retake`
   - `milestone_kind` on `POST /prosthetics/try-ins`
   - `decision` on `POST /prosthetics/try-ins/{try_in_id}/decide`
   - `turnaround_class` on `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases`
   - `purpose` on `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases`
   - `qc_result` on `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/qc`
   - `recall_outcome` on `POST /prosthetics/prostheses/{prosthesis_id}/recall-complete`
   - `service_type` on `POST /prosthetics/services`
   - `fault_verdict` on `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims/{warranty_claim_id}/adjudicate`
   - `resolved_verdict` on `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims/{warranty_claim_id}/clinical-review`
   - `reason_code` on `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests`
   - `rank` on `POST /prosthetics/coverages`
   - `cob_convention` on `POST /prosthetics/coverages`
   - `determination` on `POST /prosthetics/predeterminations/{predetermination_id}/determine`
   - `adjudication_outcome` on `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/post-payment`
   - `frequency` on `POST /prosthetics/payment-plans`

   **No other field routes to this error; all other enum-membership failures route to `ERR_VALIDATION`.** That includes every enum-valued *query* filter on a list endpoint and every enum-valued field the server computes rather than accepts.
9. **Rung 9 — optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_case_arch_version`, `expected_prosthesis_version`, or `expected_prosthesis_case_version` equals the current `version` of the named parent row, as specified in §2.8. Failure: `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases`, `POST /prosthetics/services`, `POST /prosthetics/payment-plans`.
10. **Rung 10 — terminal state.** The target row is not already in a terminal state of the state axis the operation drives. This rung is **axis-scoped**: an operation that drives a prosthesis's recall axis is gated by the recall terminal set only, and an operation that drives its warranty axis by the warranty terminal set only, so a warranty-voided appliance is not thereby blocked from a recall operation and vice versa. Failure: `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, HTTP 409. Scope: every state-changing endpoint. This rung sits directly above rung 11 and rung 12, which matters for the pre-emption described in §2.12.
11. **Rung 11 — remake eligibility.** The arch a remake is requested for has `remake_eligibility` of `ELIGIBLE`. Failure: `ERR_REMAKE_FINAL_DENIED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests`. This rung sits above rung 22 and pre-empts it on every attempt after the first; see §2.12.
12. **Rung 12 — from-state.** The target row's current state is a declared origin state of the transition the endpoint drives. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, HTTP 409. Scope: every state-changing endpoint.
13. **Rung 13 — cross-entity state.** A precondition checked against a *different* row than the target. There are exactly eight, carried by seven endpoints:
    - `POST /prosthetics/case-arches/{case_arch_id}/start-fabrication` — the parent case's `case_status` must be `EXAM_COMPLETE` when the arch is leaving `PLANNED`, and `IN_FABRICATION` when it is leaving `REMAKE_PENDING`.
    - `POST /prosthetics/prostheses` — the named arch's `arch_status` must be `READY_FOR_DELIVERY`.
    - `POST /prosthetics/services/{service_id}/complete` on a row that is `AT_LAB` — the linked lab case's `lab_status` must be `QC_PASSED`; a row sitting in `AT_LAB` with no `lab_case_id` at all, or one whose linked lab case has since been cancelled or expired, fails here too.
    - `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests/{remake_request_id}/approve` — when the request cites a `warranty_claim_id`, that claim's `claim_status` must be `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT`.
    - `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests/{remake_request_id}/approve` — **and, as a second and separately evaluated check on the same endpoint, the parent arch's `arch_status` must be `DELIVERED` or `MILESTONE_PENDING`.** The two are evaluated in the order printed: the cited claim's status first, the arch's status second, so a request citing an unadjudicated claim on an arch that is also in the wrong state reports the claim. Both carry the same code, so the pair is distinguishable only by which condition the caller repairs. The denial leg, `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests/{remake_request_id}/deny`, runs **neither** of them: a remake may always be denied, whatever the arch is doing and whatever the cited claim says.
    - `POST /prosthetics/try-ins` — the parent arch's `arch_status` must be `MILESTONE_PENDING`.
    - `POST /prosthetics/impressions` — the parent arch's `arch_status` must be `IMPRESSION_PENDING`.
    - `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases` — the parent arch's `arch_status` must be `LAB_FABRICATION`.

    Failure: `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_STATE`, HTTP 409. A lab case that the system mints for itself as part of a post-delivery service is not a caller create and is therefore not subject to this rung. This list is exhaustive in the other direction too: no endpoint outside it consults any other row's state, so in particular `POST /prosthetics/try-ins/{try_in_id}/decide` never inspects the parent arch, and `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims` never inspects the appliance it is opened against.
14. **Rung 14 — segregation of duties.** The `X-User-ID` approving or denying a remake request differs from that request's `requested_by_user_id`. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests/{remake_request_id}/approve` and `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests/{remake_request_id}/deny`.
15. **Rung 15 — laboratory shelf life (mutate-then-reject).** On `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/receive`, when the logical clock is strictly after the lab case's `shelf_life_expires_at`, the row is first written to `EXPIRED` and only then does the call raise. Failure: `ERR_LAB_CASE_EXPIRED`, HTTP 409. Full semantics in §2.12.
16. **Rung 16 — milestone sequence.** A try-in may only be created with a `milestone_kind` equal to the parent arch's `next_required_milestone`, and an approving decision is only accepted on the row whose `milestone_kind` equals `next_required_milestone`. Creating a try-in at all on an arch whose `required_milestones` list is empty always fails here. Failure: `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/try-ins` and `POST /prosthetics/try-ins/{try_in_id}/decide`.
17. **Rung 17 — impression retake ceiling.** The parent arch's `retake_count` is strictly below 3. Failure: `ERR_RETAKE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/impressions/{impression_id}/request-retake`.
18. **Rung 18 — try-in adjust ceiling.** The milestone's `adjust_count` is strictly below 2 when the decision is an adjustment. Failure: `ERR_TRYIN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/try-ins/{try_in_id}/decide`.
19. **Rung 19 — QC resend ceiling.** The lab case's `resend_count` is strictly below 2. Failure: `ERR_QC_RESEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/resend`.
20. **Rung 20 — arch possession.** The arch holds no other lab case whose `lab_status` is `DISPATCHED`, `RETURNED`, `QC_FAILED`, or `RESENT`, and — when the arch's `last_lab_received_at` is not null — the logical clock is **strictly after** `last_lab_received_at` plus 24 hours. A dispatch at exactly `last_lab_received_at` plus 24 hours is therefore a conflict, not a pass. Failure: `ERR_ARCH_OCCUPIED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/dispatch`.
21. **Rung 21 — bench-line overlap.** Detailed in §3.5. Failure: `ERR_LAB_SLOT_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/dispatch`. Rung 20 is evaluated before rung 21, so an arch that is itself still occupied reports arch occupancy even when the target bench line is also busy.
22. **Rung 22 — remake ceiling (mutate-then-reject).** On `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests`, when the target arch's `remake_request_count` is already at or above 2, the request row is first created and moved to `FINAL_DENIED`, the arch's `remake_eligibility` is first flipped to `FINAL_DENIED`, and only then does the call raise. Failure: `ERR_REMAKE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409. Full semantics in §2.12.
23. **Rung 23 — warranty already spent.** The prosthesis a warranty claim is adjudicated against does not have `warranty_status` of `WARRANTY_CONSUMED` or `WARRANTY_VOIDED`. An appliance whose warranty is already consumed or voided cannot fund a second no-charge remedy. `WARRANTY_EXPIRED` deliberately **passes** this rung, so that rung 24 stays reachable. Failure: `ERR_WARRANTY_CONSUMED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims/{warranty_claim_id}/adjudicate` and `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims/{warranty_claim_id}/clinical-review`.
24. **Rung 24 — warranty window.** The logical clock is at or before the prosthesis's `warranty_expires_at` **when the verdict is `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT`**. This rung is deliberately not applied when the verdict is `PATIENT_FAULT` or `INDETERMINATE`, because a chargeable verdict does not depend on the warranty window. Failure: `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED`, HTTP 422. Scope: the same two adjudication endpoints as rung 23.
25. **Rung 25 — service-type eligibility.** A clasp addition is only accepted when the prosthesis type is `CAST_PARTIAL` or `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL`; a rebase is refused when the prosthesis type is `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL`. Failure: `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/services`.
26. **Rung 26 — service frequency limit.** Detailed in §3.7. Failure: `ERR_SERVICE_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/services`. Rung 25 is evaluated before rung 26, so an ineligible service type reports ineligibility even when it would also be inside a frequency window.
27. **Rung 27 — coordination-of-benefits rank uniqueness.** A patient may hold at most one coverage at `PRIMARY` and at most one at `SECONDARY`. Failure: `ERR_COB_RANK_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/coverages`.
28. **Rung 28 — waiting period.** The logical clock is at or after the targeted coverage's `effective_date` plus its `waiting_period_days` days. The check runs against the primary coverage when the claim moves from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED_PRIMARY`, and against the secondary coverage when it moves from `PRIMARY_PAID` to `SUBMITTED_SECONDARY`. Failure: `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/submit`.
29. **Rung 29 — replacement frequency.** Detailed in §3.8. Failure: `ERR_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/submit`.
30. **Rung 30 — predetermination gate.** When the target case's `predetermination_required` is true, a predetermination for that case must exist whose `determination_status` is `APPROVED` or `ALTERNATE_BENEFIT` and whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock. A `DENIED`, `SUBMITTED`, or `EXPIRED` determination — or no determination at all — fails here. Failure: `ERR_PREDETERMINATION_REQUIRED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/case-arches/{case_arch_id}/start-fabrication` and `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/submit`.
31. **Rung 31 — annual maximum.** The coverage the current submission targets has `benefit_remaining` strictly greater than 0.00. Failure: `ERR_ANNUAL_MAX_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/submit`, **first stage only**. A **first-stage** submission — the one moving `DRAFT → SUBMITTED_PRIMARY` — therefore runs rungs 28, 29, 30, and 31 in that order against the primary coverage, and a claim that fails several of them reports the lowest-numbered failure. The **second-stage** submission — the one moving `PRIMARY_PAID → SUBMITTED_SECONDARY` — runs rung 28 alone, re-evaluated against the secondary coverage; rungs 29, 30 and 31 are deliberately not re-run there, so a secondary submission proceeds even against a coverage whose `benefit_remaining` is already 0.00 (the cap is then applied to the payment itself, in §3.8) and even on a case whose predetermination has meanwhile expired.

**Coverage rank uniqueness belongs to rung 27, not to rung 7.** The rule that a patient holds at most one coverage per rank could be read as either a generic uniqueness-pair check at rung 7 (`ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400) or as the dedicated coordination-of-benefits check at rung 27 (`ERR_COB_RANK_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409). It is **the latter**: a second coverage at a rank the patient already holds is refused with `ERR_COB_RANK_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409, because that is what the dedicated code exists for. Rung 7 does not claim the pair, so the dedicated rung stays reachable, and a request that is *both* a rank collision and, say, an out-of-member `cob_convention` still reports the enum failure first — rung 8 precedes rung 27. The other uniqueness pair sits at the other rung and carries no such ambiguity: a duplicate `case_id`-and-`arch_position` belongs to rung 7 and `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 400 alone, and no `ERR_COB_RANK_CONFLICT`-style dedicated code exists for it.

### 2.11. The Global Write Gate

A patient who owes the clinic money on a broken installment plan does not get new laboratory work started on their behalf. That policy is implemented as a single gate at rung 5: **the patient reached from the target resource must not own any `PaymentPlan` whose `plan_status` is `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULTED`.** When such a plan exists, the call is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_DELINQUENT` and HTTP 403 *before* any body-reference, schema, enum, concurrency, or state check runs. The mirror of the gate is carried on the patient row as `write_gate_active`, a computed boolean that is true exactly when the patient owns at least one delinquent or defaulted plan.

The patient is reached from whichever resource the call targets: from `patient_id` on a create that names one, from the case on an arch operation, from the case behind the arch on an impression, try-in, lab case, or delivery, and from the arch behind the prosthesis on a service. Because this rung sits above rung 6, the walk starts from a body foreign key that has not yet been validated; when that key names nothing, no patient is reachable, the gate cannot be evaluated and is simply not applied — the request falls through to rung 6 and is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. A gated patient is therefore never the reason a create naming a non-existent parent is refused.

**Gated family — every endpoint below is frozen while the gate is up:**

- `POST /prosthetics/cases`
- `POST /prosthetics/case-arches`
- `POST /prosthetics/case-arches/{case_arch_id}/start-fabrication`
- `POST /prosthetics/impressions`
- `POST /prosthetics/impressions/{impression_id}/request-retake`
- `POST /prosthetics/try-ins`
- `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases`
- `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/dispatch`
- `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/resend`
- `POST /prosthetics/prostheses`
- `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests`
- `POST /prosthetics/services`, except when it is an emergency repair (see the carve-out below)

**Outside the gate — and the reason each one is excluded:**

- **Every `GET` endpoint.** Reading is never frozen; the patient and the front office must be able to see why the account is gated and what is owed.
- `POST /prosthetics/patients` and `POST /prosthetics/coverages`. Registration and insurance enrolment precede any balance and cannot be the thing a balance blocks.
- `POST /prosthetics/labs`. Laboratory configuration is clinic-wide, not patient-scoped, so no patient gate can apply to it.
- `POST /prosthetics/payment-plans`. Establishing — or replacing — a plan is the remedy for the gate; gating it would make the gate permanent.
- `POST /prosthetics/installments/{installment_id}/pay`. Paying is the cure; gating the cure makes the gate unrecoverable.
- `POST /prosthetics/installments/{installment_id}/reverse-payment`. The front office must be able to undo a mis-posted payment even on a gated account.
- `POST /prosthetics/payment-plans/{payment_plan_id}/assess-delinquency`. The sweep is what raises and lowers the gate; it cannot be subject to it.
- `POST /prosthetics/services` when `is_emergency_repair` is true **and** `service_type` is `REPAIR`. A patient whose denture has fractured cannot eat; emergency repair is a clinical-safety carve-out. Both conditions are required: an emergency flag on any other service type does not lift the gate, and a repair without the flag does not lift it either. Because the gate is rung 5, the carve-out is decided on the **raw** body before any schema, enum, reference or concurrency check has run: `service_type` must be the exact string `REPAIR` and `is_emergency_repair` must be the JSON boolean `true` (a string `"true"` or the number 1 does not lift it). The carve-out lifts the gate only; every rung below still applies in full, so an emergency repair on a gated account can still be refused for a stale `expected_prosthesis_version` or a frequency limit.
- `POST /prosthetics/services/{service_id}/complete` and `POST /prosthetics/services/{service_id}/void`. Finishing or undoing work already started is not a new fabrication start, and the appliance must come back to the patient.
- `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/receive` and `POST /prosthetics/lab-cases/{lab_case_id}/qc`. Work already at the laboratory must be received and inspected regardless of the balance; only *new* dispatches are frozen.
- `POST /prosthetics/impressions/{impression_id}/review` and `POST /prosthetics/try-ins/{try_in_id}/decide`. Judging an appointment that already happened is not a new start.
- `POST /prosthetics/prostheses/{prosthesis_id}/recall-complete`. Recall is a safety follow-up on an appliance the patient already has.
- `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims`, `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims/{warranty_claim_id}/adjudicate`, and `POST /prosthetics/warranty-claims/{warranty_claim_id}/clinical-review`. Fault adjudication decides who owes the money; letting the clinic's own receivable block it would let the clinic profit from its own delay.
- `POST /prosthetics/predeterminations`, `POST /prosthetics/predeterminations/{predetermination_id}/determine`, `POST /prosthetics/claims`, `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/submit`, `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/post-payment`, and `POST /prosthetics/claims/{claim_id}/reverse`. Insurance work reduces the balance and is part of the remedy path.
- `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests/{remake_request_id}/approve` and `POST /prosthetics/remake-requests/{remake_request_id}/deny`. The request creation was already gated; deciding an existing request is not a new start.
- `POST /prosthetics/cases/{case_id}/complete-exam` and `POST /prosthetics/cases/{case_id}/cancel`. Closing out or cancelling an existing case reduces exposure rather than adding to it.
- `POST /prosthetics/_clock/set_current_utc`. Test-driving infrastructure, outside the business surface entirely.

The gate rises and falls on its own, driven by the plan lifecycle described in §3.9; there is no endpoint that sets `write_gate_active` directly.

### 2.12. Mutate-Then-Reject, and What the Second Attempt Sees

Two rungs on the ladder break the usual "a failing rung writes nothing" rule. Each of them performs a bookkeeping write, **commits it**, and only then raises. The write survives the error; it is not rolled back. And in both cases the *second* attempt on the same row does not reach the same rung at all, because the write the first attempt committed trips a higher rung — which means the second attempt reports a different error.

**Rung 15 — receiving a lab case past its shelf life.** When a laboratory tries to return a case and the logical clock is strictly after that case's `shelf_life_expires_at`, the server first sets `lab_status` to `EXPIRED`, stamps `expired_at` with the logical clock, releases both the laboratory bench-line occupation and the arch occupation the case was holding, and increments the lab case's `version` by 1. That write is committed. Only then does the call fail with `ERR_LAB_CASE_EXPIRED` and HTTP 409. If the parent arch was in `LAB_FABRICATION` it stays in `LAB_FABRICATION`, so a fresh lab case can be drafted and dispatched for it.

*On a subsequent receive attempt against that same lab case*, the row is now sitting in `EXPIRED`, which is a terminal state of the lab-case axis. Rung 10 therefore fires first, the call returns **`ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` with HTTP 409 — not `ERR_LAB_CASE_EXPIRED`** — and no further write of any kind is performed.

**Rung 22 — requesting a remake past the ceiling.** When a remake is requested on an arch whose `remake_request_count` is already at or above 2, the server first creates the remake request row in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, immediately moves it to `FINAL_DENIED` with `decided_at` stamped, flips the arch's `remake_eligibility` to `FINAL_DENIED`, and increments the arch's `version` by 1. That write is committed — the denied request row is a real, persisted row and is discoverable by listing remake requests filtered on `case_arch_id`. Only then does the call fail with `ERR_REMAKE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409.

*On a subsequent remake request against that same arch*, the arch's `remake_eligibility` is now `FINAL_DENIED`, so rung 11 fires first, the call returns **`ERR_REMAKE_FINAL_DENIED` with HTTP 409 — not `ERR_REMAKE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`** — and **no second `FINAL_DENIED` request row is minted.** The arch accumulates exactly one final-denied row no matter how many times the request is repeated.

### 2.13. Cascades, Bubble-Ups and Conserved Quantities

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. Where they do, the full chain is spelled out in the §3 subsection that owns the operation, and each link's effect on `version` and `updated_at` is stated there. Two chains are large enough to have names used throughout this document: the **delivery cascade**, which runs when an appliance is delivered, and the **remake cascade**, which runs when a remake is authorised. Both are described in §3.6 and §3.7 respectively.

Parent state is often derived by bubble-up rather than by a direct call: a case moves to `IN_FABRICATION` because one of its arches left `PLANNED`, and to `DELIVERED` because its last outstanding arch was delivered. Every such bubble-up increments the parent's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`, exactly as a direct mutation would. A *state* bubble-up does; a merely derived one does not — where a child write changes only a computed field on the parent (a patient's `delivered_prosthesis_count`, an arch's `remake_request_count`, a plan's `outstanding_amount`) the parent's `version` and `updated_at` stay exactly where they were, as §2.4 sets out.

Four quantities are **conserved** — they must hold at every instant an observer could read them, both after the forward action and after the compensating action that undoes it:

1. **Adjustment allowance.** On any prosthesis, `adjustments_used` plus `adjustments_remaining` always equals 4. Completing an included adjustment moves one unit from remaining to used; voiding that completed row moves it back. A *chargeable* adjustment — one past the count or past the window — must move neither counter, so the sum is untouched on that path too.
2. **Payment plan balance.** On any payment plan, `paid_amount` plus `outstanding_amount` always equals `principal_amount`. Paying an installment moves the installment's `amount` from outstanding to paid; reversing that payment moves it back.
3. **Laboratory credit.** On any laboratory, `credit_issued_total` minus `credit_applied_total` always equals `credit_available_balance` — `credit_available_balance` is in fact re-derived from that difference on each move rather than adjusted independently. Adjudicating a lab-fault warranty claim issues credit; applying that credit during a remake raises `credit_applied_total` by exactly the amount `credit_available_balance` falls by, leaving `credit_issued_total` untouched. Neither move is a transition of the `DentalLab` row's own: a laboratory's ledger changes without its `version` or `updated_at` moving at all, on both the issue and the apply leg.
4. **Coverage benefit.** On any coverage, `benefit_used_ytd` plus `benefit_remaining` always equals `annual_maximum_amount`; `benefit_remaining` is the derived side of that identity, computed as `annual_maximum_amount` minus `benefit_used_ytd` and floored at 0.00. Posting a payment consumes benefit; reversing the claim returns it. As with a laboratory, the `Coverage` row is not itself the subject of a transition on either leg: its `benefit_used_ytd` moves without its `version` or `updated_at` moving.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registration: Patients, Coverages and Laboratories

Everything begins with a patient record, which a `FrontOffice` or `PlatformAdmin` operator registers with three pieces of information: `full_name`, `date_of_birth`, and `phone`. The record is minted with three derived fields already settled — `write_gate_active` false, `delivered_prosthesis_count` zero, and `last_frequency_anchor_at` null — and the server maintains all three from then on. `delivered_prosthesis_count` counts the appliances delivered to this patient that are *not* remakes: every prosthesis whose arch belongs to one of this patient's cases and whose `is_remake` is false. `last_frequency_anchor_at` is the greatest `frequency_anchor_at` across all of this patient's prostheses, or null while the patient has none; it is the anchor of the insurance replacement clock and is discussed again in §3.8.

Insurance enrolment attaches a coverage to a patient. The enrolling operator supplies `patient_id`, `carrier_name`, `rank`, and `effective_date`, and may supply six further values, each of which has a default the server applies when it is omitted: `annual_maximum_amount` defaults to 1500.00, `coinsurance_major_rate` defaults to 0.50, `waiting_period_days` defaults to 180, `replacement_frequency_months` defaults to 60, `cob_convention` defaults to `STANDARD`, and `alternate_benefit_enabled` defaults to true. `rank` is either `PRIMARY` or `SECONDARY` and nothing else; `cob_convention` is either `STANDARD` or `NON_DUPLICATION` and nothing else. A patient may hold one coverage at each rank; a second coverage at a rank the patient already holds collides with `ERR_COB_RANK_CONFLICT` as described at rung 27. Beyond their types none of the six optional values carries a declared range: `annual_maximum_amount` and `coinsurance_major_rate` need only be numbers, `waiting_period_days` and `replacement_frequency_months` only integers, and no endpoint refuses a coverage for an implausible one. `annual_maximum_amount` is rounded half up to two decimal places on the way in; `coinsurance_major_rate` is stored and echoed exactly as supplied. One of the six is inert: **`replacement_frequency_months` is stored and echoed but consulted by nothing.** The replacement clock of §3.6 runs on the flat 60-month interval declared there, and the replacement-frequency rung of §3.8 reads `replacement_eligible_date` off the appliances, never this field, so setting it to some other number changes no behaviour anywhere in the system.

A newly enrolled coverage starts with `benefit_used_ytd` at 0.00 and `benefit_remaining` equal to `annual_maximum_amount`, which is what establishes the coverage-benefit conservation described in §2.13. Thereafter `benefit_used_ytd` is the sum of `plan_paid_amount` over the claims that cite this coverage and whose `adjudication_status` is `PRIMARY_PAID`, `SECONDARY_PAID`, or `CLOSED`, and `benefit_remaining` is `annual_maximum_amount` minus `benefit_used_ytd`, floored at 0.00 so that it never goes negative. Coverages can be listed and filtered by `patient_id` and by `rank`.

External laboratories are registered by a `PlatformAdmin` alone, because a laboratory is clinic-wide configuration rather than anything belonging to a patient. Registration takes `lab_name` and three optional operating parameters: `bench_line_count`, which defaults to 3 and must be at least 1 and at most 10; `standard_turnaround_days`, which defaults to 10; and `rush_turnaround_days`, which defaults to 4. Only `bench_line_count` carries that declared range; the two turnaround figures need only be integers, and nothing requires the rush figure to be the shorter of the two. The identifier that comes back from registration is the value a laboratory sends as `X-User-ID` when it acts as a `LabTechnician`. A new laboratory's credit ledger — `credit_issued_total`, `credit_applied_total`, and `credit_available_balance` — starts at 0.00 on all three counters.

### 3.2. Case Intake, Arch Planning and the Exam Close-Out

A prosthesis case is opened for one patient at one `prosthesis_type`, and the type chosen at intake parameterises almost everything that follows: the fee, the required try-in sequence, the recall interval, and which post-delivery services are even permitted. The six types are `COMPLETE_DENTURE`, `IMMEDIATE_DENTURE`, `CAST_PARTIAL`, `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL`, `OVERDENTURE`, and `IMPLANT_RETAINED_OVERDENTURE`. A `Prosthodontist` or a `FrontOffice` operator opens the case by supplying `patient_id` and `prosthesis_type` — an act inside the global write gate, so a patient with a broken payment plan gets no new case opened; the case is born in `INTAKE` with `fee_schedule_total` at 0.00 and `predetermination_required` false, and with `fabrication_started_at` and `delivered_at` both null. Cases can be listed and filtered by `patient_id` and by `case_status`.

Each case then acquires one or two **arch units**. A `Prosthodontist` adds an arch by naming the `case_id` and the `arch_position`, which is `MAXILLARY` or `MANDIBULAR`; adding an arch is likewise inside the global write gate, at most one arch row may exist per case per position, and a duplicate pair is a rung 7 schema failure carrying `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400. The case's own `case_status` is not consulted, so an arch may be added at any point in the case's life — but because the exam close-out below runs from `INTAKE` only and is the sole thing that prices an arch, an arch added after the close-out keeps `fee_schedule_amount` 0.00 for good and is never folded into the case's `fee_schedule_total`. It counts everywhere else an arch counts: in the delivery bubble-up's "every arch settled" test, and in the arch multiplier of the alternate-benefit calculation. A new arch is born in `PLANNED` with `retake_count`, `remake_count`, and `remake_request_count` at zero, `remake_eligibility` at `ELIGIBLE`, `fee_schedule_amount` at 0.00, and `last_lab_received_at` null. At creation the server also computes the arch's `required_milestones` from the parent case's `prosthesis_type`, and that list is what the try-in stage will walk:

- A `COMPLETE_DENTURE` requires a wax try-in and then a finish try-in, in that order.
- An `IMMEDIATE_DENTURE` requires **no** try-in milestones at all — its `required_milestones` is the empty list.
- A `CAST_PARTIAL` requires a framework try-in, then a wax try-in, then a finish try-in, in that order.
- A `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL` requires a wax try-in only.
- An `OVERDENTURE` requires a wax try-in and then a finish try-in.
- An `IMPLANT_RETAINED_OVERDENTURE` requires a framework try-in and then a finish try-in.

Alongside `required_milestones` the arch carries `completed_milestones`, the ordered list of `milestone_kind` values of this arch's try-in rows that reached approval, sorted by their `created_at` ascending, and `next_required_milestone`, which is the first entry of `required_milestones` not yet present in `completed_milestones`, or null once every required milestone is complete. Arches can be listed and filtered by `case_id` and by `arch_status`.

Closing the exam is what prices the case. A `Prosthodontist` closes it, moving `case_status` from `INTAKE` to `EXAM_COMPLETE`; the call requires that at least one arch already exists on the case, and a case with no arches is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Closing the exam sets each non-abandoned arch's `fee_schedule_amount` to the fee for the case's `prosthesis_type` — 1800.00 for a complete denture, 2100.00 for an immediate denture, 1650.00 for a cast partial, 1200.00 for a flexible partial, 2400.00 for an overdenture, and 3600.00 for an implant-retained overdenture — then sets the case's `fee_schedule_total` to the sum of `fee_schedule_amount` over every arch of the case whose `arch_status` is not `ARCH_ABANDONED`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because the sum is order-independent no tie-break is needed. Finally it sets `predetermination_required` to true exactly when `fee_schedule_total` is at or above 1500.00, and increments the case's `version` by 1. A single-arch complete denture at 1800.00 therefore requires a predetermination; a single-arch flexible partial at 1200.00 does not; a two-arch flexible partial at 2400.00 does. Three details of the close-out are worth pinning. The arch-existence check is a rung 7 schema failure and therefore sits *above* the terminal and from-state rungs, so a close-out attempted on an already-cancelled case that never had an arch reports `ERR_VALIDATION`, not `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. The repricing of the arches is a by-product write, not a transition of theirs: each non-abandoned arch's `fee_schedule_amount` changes with **no `version` increment and no `updated_at` refresh on the arch**, and the case's single increment is the only one the whole call produces. And the close-out is one of the triggers of the nine-step balance of §3.9 — it re-runs the whole computation, so a freshly priced case reads back with `billed_amount` and `patient_responsibility_amount` already equal to `fee_schedule_total` rather than still at 0.00.

Fabrication starts per arch, not per case. When a `Prosthodontist` starts fabrication on an arch sitting in `PLANNED`, three preconditions apply beyond the ladder's generic checks: the parent case must be in `EXAM_COMPLETE`, the patient must not be behind the write gate, and — when the case's `predetermination_required` is true — a live approved or alternate-benefit predetermination must exist for the case, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_PREDETERMINATION_REQUIRED`. The arch moves `PLANNED → IMPRESSION_PENDING`, its `retake_count` is reset to zero, its `required_milestones` is recomputed from the case's `prosthesis_type`, and its `version` is incremented by 1. That same action bubbles the parent case from `EXAM_COMPLETE` to `IN_FABRICATION`, stamps the case's `fabrication_started_at` with the logical clock, and increments the case's `version` by 1 — a bubble-up that happens only once, on the first arch of the case to leave `PLANNED`.

The same action serves the remake loop. An arch sitting in `REMAKE_PENDING` restarts with `REMAKE_PENDING → IMPRESSION_PENDING`; here the parent case must already be in `IN_FABRICATION` and the patient must still be clear of the write gate, `retake_count` is again reset to zero, the arch's `version` is incremented by 1, and no case-level bubble-up occurs because the case is already where it needs to be. Two gates that the `PLANNED` leg runs are deliberately **not** re-run on this leg. The predetermination gate of rung 30 is one: it is declared on the `PLANNED` leg alone, so a remake restarts even on a case whose `predetermination_required` is true and whose only determination has meanwhile lapsed to `EXPIRED`. The other is the recomputation of `required_milestones`: the remake leg leaves that list exactly as it stands, and leaves the arch's approved try-in rows in place, so the arch re-enters `IMPRESSION_PENDING` still carrying every milestone it completed in the round before — which is what makes the impression review of §3.3 send it straight past the try-in stage.

Cancellation is available to a `Prosthodontist` from `INTAKE`, `EXAM_COMPLETE`, or `IN_FABRICATION`, optionally carrying a `cancellation_reason` — which, when supplied, must be a string, but is otherwise **not persisted and appears on no response**; the case row carries no field for it. Cancellation cascades in three directions at once. The case moves to `CANCELLED`. Every arch of the case whose `arch_status` is not already `ARCH_CLOSED` or `ARCH_ABANDONED` moves to `ARCH_ABANDONED` — and that includes an arch already sitting in `DELIVERED`, so a two-arch case can be cancelled after one arch was delivered and the delivered arch is abandoned along with its sibling. Every lab case belonging to those arches whose `lab_status` is not `QC_PASSED`, `EXPIRED`, or `CANCELLED` moves to `CANCELLED`, releasing whatever bench-line occupation it held. Every try-in milestone of those arches sitting in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `ADJUST_REQUESTED` moves to `CANCELLED`. Each touched row's `version` increments by 1. What cancellation deliberately does **not** do is re-price or re-balance: the case keeps the `fee_schedule_total` it was given at close-out even though every arch has just become `ARCH_ABANDONED`, the nine-step balance of §3.9 is not re-run, and `billed_amount`, `insurance_paid_amount`, `prompt_pay_discount_amount` and `patient_responsibility_amount` are all left exactly as they stood. Nor does it touch the case's delivered appliances, its predeterminations, its claims or its payment plans — those rows survive the cancellation untouched, at their existing statuses and versions.

At the far end of the lifecycle sits closure, which the system performs for itself rather than any caller invoking it. A case in `DELIVERED` moves to `CLOSED` once every claim of the case has an `adjudication_status` of `CLOSED`, `DENIED`, or `REVERSED`, **and** every payment plan of the case has a `plan_status` of `PAID_OFF` or `SUPERSEDED` — or, where the case never needed a plan at all, once the case has no payment plan and its `patient_responsibility_amount` is 0.00. Closure sets every arch of the case still sitting in `DELIVERED` to `ARCH_CLOSED`, incrementing **each such arch's own `version` by 1**, and increments the case's `version` by 1. Because no caller invokes it, closure is only ever *evaluated* at the four moments a request could have satisfied its last condition: when a claim auto-closes after a primary payment with no secondary coverage behind it, when a claim auto-closes after a secondary payment, when a closed claim is reversed, and when an installment payment is posted. It is deliberately not evaluated anywhere else — not when a claim is denied, not when a service is completed or voided, not when a payment is reversed — so a case that becomes closeable through one of those other routes stays in `DELIVERED` until one of the four moments next occurs. The conditions are re-checked from scratch each time, so nothing is lost by the delay. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are the two terminal states of the case axis; `ARCH_CLOSED` and `ARCH_ABANDONED` are the two terminal states of the arch axis.

### 3.3. Impression Capture and the Three-Band Quality Review

With the arch in `IMPRESSION_PENDING`, a `Prosthodontist` records a capture by naming the `case_arch_id` and the `capture_method`, which is either `PHYSICAL_IMPRESSION` or `DIGITAL_SCAN`. The parent arch must be in `IMPRESSION_PENDING` for the capture to be accepted at all; an arch anywhere else yields `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_STATE`. Creating the record is inside the write gate. The record is born in `PENDING_REVIEW` with `attempt_number` equal to the arch's current `retake_count` plus one — so the first capture after fabrication starts is attempt 1 — and with `quality_score` null, `retake_required` false, `reviewed_at` null, and `superseded_by_impression_id` null.

Review is a separate act, and it deliberately sits **outside** the write gate: the appointment already happened, and refusing to judge it would help nobody. The reviewing clinician supplies an integer `quality_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive; a score outside that range is a rung 7 schema failure. The score decides the band, and the boundaries are inclusive on the lower edge in both cases:

- A score of **85 or more** puts the record in `ACCEPTED`. `retake_required` stays false.
- A score of **at least 70 but below 85** puts the record in `PROVISIONAL_ACCEPTED` and sets `retake_required` to true.
- A score **below 70** puts the record in `REJECTED` and likewise sets `retake_required` to true.

In all three bands the review stamps `reviewed_at` with the logical clock, persists the supplied `quality_score`, and increments the record's `version` by 1. `retake_required` is not a stored flag but a reading of the band: it is true exactly while `quality_status` is `PROVISIONAL_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, and false in every other status. It therefore flips **back** to false, with no write of its own, the moment a provisional or rejected record is superseded by a retake.

Where the bands differ is what happens to the arch. On `ACCEPTED`, the arch advances out of `IMPRESSION_PENDING` — to `MILESTONE_PENDING` when its `required_milestones` list is non-empty, with `next_required_milestone` set to the first entry of that list, or straight to `LAB_FABRICATION` when the list is empty, which holds only for an `IMMEDIATE_DENTURE`. There is a third path, and it is the one the remake loop takes. The declared `MILESTONE_PENDING → LAB_FABRICATION` transition of §3.4 is not tied to an approval: it fires as soon as its precondition holds, which is that `next_required_milestone` is null. On a first pass through a type with required milestones that precondition is false and the arch settles in `MILESTONE_PENDING`. On a **remake restart** it is already true — the arch kept its `required_milestones` and its approved try-in rows through the remake, so every required milestone is still in `completed_milestones` — and the arch therefore moves `IMPRESSION_PENDING → MILESTONE_PENDING → LAB_FABRICATION` inside this one call and is projected in `LAB_FABRICATION`, never observably resting in `MILESTONE_PENDING`. In all of these the arch's `version` increments by exactly 1, however many of the steps ran. On `PROVISIONAL_ACCEPTED` and on `REJECTED`, the arch is left exactly where it was, in `IMPRESSION_PENDING`, with no write and therefore **no `version` increment of its own**, and the clinician's next move is a retake.

Requesting a retake is inside the write gate and is only available on a record sitting in `PROVISIONAL_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`. It carries one input, the `capture_method` of the replacement capture, which need not match the method of the record being replaced. Before anything happens the arch's `retake_count` must be **strictly below 3**; at three retakes the arch is out of attempts and the request is refused with `ERR_RETAKE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. When it is allowed, four things happen together: the arch's `retake_count` increases by one and its `version` increments by 1; a successor record is created on the same arch in `PENDING_REVIEW` with `attempt_number` one higher than the superseded record's and the `capture_method` supplied on the request; the superseded record's `quality_status` becomes `SUPERSEDED` and its `superseded_by_impression_id` is set to the successor's identifier; and the superseded record's `version` increments by 1. **The response body is the superseded record, not the successor** — the successor is reachable by following `superseded_by_impression_id`, or by listing impression records filtered on `case_arch_id`, on `quality_status`, or on `superseded_by_impression_id`. Because the body is the row named in the path rather than the row just minted, this call answers **HTTP 200, not 201**, notwithstanding that it does create a resource. The superseded record projected in that body reads `retake_required` **false**, since the flag follows `quality_status` and that is now `SUPERSEDED`; its `quality_score` and `reviewed_at` are left exactly as the review left them, and nothing about the retake is recorded on it beyond the new status and the successor's identifier. The successor is born at `version` 1 and, coming from a create, its `attempt_number` is the only thing that distinguishes it from a first capture.

`ACCEPTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the two terminal states of the impression axis. `PROVISIONAL_ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` are not terminal, precisely because the retake leg has to remain reachable from both of them.

### 3.4. Try-In Milestones and the Type-Parameterised Sequence

A try-in milestone is booked on an arch that is in `MILESTONE_PENDING`, by a `Prosthodontist`, naming the `case_arch_id` and the `milestone_kind` — `WAX`, `FRAMEWORK`, or `FINISH`. Booking is inside the write gate. The arch must be in `MILESTONE_PENDING`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_STATE`; and the `milestone_kind` must equal the arch's `next_required_milestone`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE`. Because an `IMMEDIATE_DENTURE` has an empty `required_milestones` list, and therefore a null `next_required_milestone` from the very start, **any** attempt to book a try-in on an immediate denture fails with `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE`. A framework try-in is only ever the next required milestone on a `CAST_PARTIAL` or an `IMPLANT_RETAINED_OVERDENTURE`; on the other types it can never be booked. The row is created in `PENDING_APPROVAL` with `adjust_count` zero and `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` null.

Deciding a milestone is outside the write gate — judging an appointment that already happened is not a new start — and the decision carries a single `decision` input taking one of four values: `APPROVE`, `ADJUST`, `RESUBMIT`, or `REJECT`. Every one of the four stamps `decided_at` with the logical clock, records the calling `X-User-ID` in `decided_by_user_id`, and increments the milestone's `version` by 1. Beyond that they diverge sharply, and each branch carries its own preconditions:

**Approval.** Permitted from `PENDING_APPROVAL` only, and only while this row's `milestone_kind` equals the arch's `next_required_milestone`; approving an out-of-sequence row is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE`. That sequence check is the **only** precondition beyond the from-state: unlike the booking of a try-in, the decision endpoint carries no cross-entity check on the arch and appears nowhere in the rung 13 list, so it never refuses on the arch's `arch_status`. The arch's state is consulted only afterwards, to decide whether to move it. Approval moves the row to `APPROVED`, appends its `milestone_kind` to the arch's `completed_milestones`, and recomputes `next_required_milestone`. When that recomputation yields null — meaning the last required milestone has just been approved — the arch advances `MILESTONE_PENDING → LAB_FABRICATION` and its `version` increments by 1; when the arch is not in `MILESTONE_PENDING` at that moment the approval still succeeds and the arch is simply left alone, unmoved and unversioned. A cast partial therefore needs three approvals before its arch reaches the laboratory stage; a flexible partial needs one.

**Adjustment.** Permitted from `PENDING_APPROVAL`, and only while the row's `adjust_count` is **strictly below 2**; a third adjustment on the same row is refused with `ERR_TRYIN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. It moves the row `PENDING_APPROVAL → ADJUST_REQUESTED`, increases `adjust_count` by one, and leaves the parent arch untouched in `MILESTONE_PENDING`.

**Resubmission.** Permitted from `ADJUST_REQUESTED` only, and it moves the row back `ADJUST_REQUESTED → PENDING_APPROVAL`, closing the adjust-and-re-try-in cycle on the same row. `adjust_count` is deliberately **not** reset, so the ceiling of two is cumulative across the life of the row: adjust, resubmit, adjust, resubmit leaves the row at two adjustments and a third adjustment is refused.

**Rejection.** Permitted from `PENDING_APPROVAL`, and — like approval — subject to no check on the parent arch's state. It moves the row to `REJECTED_FOR_REMAKE` and, as a side effect, auto-creates a remake request on the parent arch in `PENDING_APPROVAL` with `reason_code` of `TRYIN_REJECTED`, `requested_by_user_id` set to the deciding `X-User-ID`, `warranty_claim_id` null, `charge_basis` `CHARGEABLE` (no claim is cited, so the no-charge branch cannot apply), and `attempt_number` one above the arch's current `remake_request_count`. That auto-created request is what later drives the arch into `REMAKE_PENDING`, described in §3.7. Being system-minted rather than requested, it is subject to **neither** the remake-eligibility rung 11 **nor** the remake-ceiling rung 22: a rejected try-in mints its request even on an arch already at the ceiling or already flipped to `FINAL_DENIED`, and never mints a `FINAL_DENIED` row of its own. The arch itself is not written — its `remake_request_count` rises because the count is derived, so the arch's `version` and `updated_at` do not move.

Milestones sitting in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `ADJUST_REQUESTED` when their case is cancelled move to `CANCELLED` with a `version` increment, as covered in §3.2. `APPROVED`, `REJECTED_FOR_REMAKE`, and `CANCELLED` are the three terminal states of the milestone axis. Try-in milestones can be listed and filtered by `case_arch_id` and by `approval_status`.

### 3.5. Laboratory Coordination, Bench Lines and the Shelf-Life Clock

Once an arch reaches `LAB_FABRICATION`, the work has to go out to a laboratory, and a **lab case** is the paperwork that travels with it. A `Prosthodontist` or a `FrontOffice` operator drafts one by naming the `case_arch_id` it belongs to, the `dental_lab_id` it is going to, the `bench_line` it will occupy, the `turnaround_class` it is booked at, the `unit_count` of billable units on it, the `purpose` it exists for, and the `expected_case_arch_version` that guards the write. `lab_invoice_amount` is optional and defaults to 450.00; it is the amount the laboratory invoices the clinic and, later, the base of any lab-fault credit. `turnaround_class` is `STANDARD` or `RUSH`. `purpose` is one of `FABRICATION`, `RELINE`, `REBASE`, `REPAIR`, `ADDITION`, or `REMAKE`. `unit_count` must be at least 1 and at most 32, and `bench_line` must be at least 1 and at most the target laboratory's `bench_line_count` — a laboratory left on the default of 3 bench lines therefore accepts bench lines 1, 2, and 3 only.

Drafting a lab case is inside the write gate and requires the parent arch to be in `LAB_FABRICATION`, failing which the create is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_STATE`. It also **supersedes**: any lab case already sitting in `DRAFT` on the same arch is moved to `CANCELLED`, releasing whatever bench-line reservation it held, with a `version` increment on each displaced row. That supersede is exactly why the create is guarded by `expected_case_arch_version`, as §2.8 explains. **The parent arch's `version` is incremented by 1 on every successful lab-case create.** That is not a consequence of the supersede and does not depend on one having happened: the very first lab case drafted on an arch, with no prior `DRAFT` to displace, still advances the arch by exactly 1, and so does each subsequent create. The rule is the same shape as the one governing a payment-plan create in §3.9, where the case advances whether or not a prior plan was superseded, and it is what makes `expected_case_arch_version` a moving target — a client drafting two lab cases in a row must re-read the arch between them. The new row is born in `DRAFT`, with `rush_fee_amount` at 0.00, `resend_count` at zero, and `dispatched_at`, `promised_due_at`, `shelf_life_expires_at`, `received_at`, `qc_result`, and `expired_at` all null.

**Dispatch** is what starts the clocks, and it is inside the write gate. Before anything is written, two overlap checks run in order. The first, at rung 20, is about the arch: the arch must hold no other lab case whose `lab_status` is `DISPATCHED`, `RETURNED`, `QC_FAILED`, or `RESENT`, and — when the arch's `last_lab_received_at` is not null — the logical clock must be strictly after `last_lab_received_at` plus 24 hours. That buffer is a closed interval on the wrong side: a dispatch at exactly 24 hours after the last return is still a conflict and yields `ERR_ARCH_OCCUPIED`; only 24 hours and one millisecond later does it pass. The second check, at rung 21, is about the laboratory's capacity: the interval running from the dispatch instant up to but **not including** the new case's `promised_due_at` must not intersect the same half-open interval of any other lab case sharing the same `dental_lab_id` and `bench_line` whose `lab_status` is `DISPATCHED`, `RETURNED`, `QC_FAILED`, or `RESENT`. Because the intervals are half-open, touching is permitted: a dispatch landing exactly on another case's `promised_due_at` passes, while a dispatch one millisecond earlier collides with `ERR_LAB_SLOT_CONFLICT`.

Dispatch then stamps `dispatched_at` with the logical clock and derives three values from it. `promised_due_at` is `dispatched_at` plus the laboratory's `standard_turnaround_days` when `turnaround_class` is `STANDARD`, or plus its `rush_turnaround_days` when it is `RUSH`; on a laboratory left at its defaults that is ten calendar days for a standard case and four for a rush case, with the time-of-day of the dispatch preserved. `shelf_life_expires_at` is `dispatched_at` plus 45 calendar days, and it is computed identically for both turnaround classes. `rush_fee_amount` is 0.00 for a standard case; for a rush case it is 120.00 plus 35.00 multiplied by the case's `unit_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places — so a rush case of three units carries a rush fee of 225.00. That rush fee is added to the parent case's `billed_amount`, the case's money fields are recomputed as described in §3.9, and the lab case's `version` increments by 1. The lab case's is the **only** increment the dispatch produces: the recomputation is a pure re-derivation of the case's money and, as §2.4 sets out, leaves the `ProsthesisCase` row's own `version` and `updated_at` where they were, and the parent arch is not written at all. The recomputation runs on a standard dispatch too, where the rush fee it folds in is 0.00.

**Receiving** is the laboratory's own act, and only a `LabTechnician` whose `X-User-ID` equals the lab case's `dental_lab_id` may perform it — anyone else's laboratory identity is refused with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. Receiving is outside the write gate: work already at the laboratory must come back regardless of the patient's balance. From `DISPATCHED` or from `RESENT` the case moves to `RETURNED`, `received_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the parent arch's `last_lab_received_at` is set to that same instant — which is what arms the 24-hour buffer for the next dispatch on that arch — and the lab case's `version` increments by 1. Writing `last_lab_received_at` is a by-product, not a transition of the arch's: the arch's own `arch_status` does not move and **its `version` and `updated_at` do not advance**, so the lab case's is again the only increment the call produces. A client holding an `expected_case_arch_version` across a receive therefore still holds a current one. If the logical clock is strictly after `shelf_life_expires_at`, the mutate-then-reject behaviour of §2.12 takes over instead: the row is flipped to `EXPIRED`, `expired_at` is stamped, both occupations are released, the write is committed, and the call raises `ERR_LAB_CASE_EXPIRED`. A second attempt on that same row returns `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` instead, and the arch — if it was in `LAB_FABRICATION` — stays there so a fresh lab case can be drafted.

**Clinic QC** happens on a `RETURNED` case and is outside the write gate. A `Prosthodontist` supplies a `qc_result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, and the result is persisted on the row. A pass moves the case to `QC_PASSED`; when the case's `purpose` is `FABRICATION` or `REMAKE` this also advances the parent arch `LAB_FABRICATION → READY_FOR_DELIVERY` with a `version` increment on the arch — and only from `LAB_FABRICATION`, so an arch that has meanwhile been pulled into `REMAKE_PENDING` or abandoned is left exactly where it is while the lab case still passes. When the `purpose` is `RELINE`, `REBASE`, `REPAIR`, or `ADDITION` the pass instead makes the post-delivery service linked to this lab case completable, as §3.7 describes; that is a change in what the service will *accept*, not a change to the service row — the service is not written, stays in `AT_LAB`, and its `version` does not move. A fail moves the case to `QC_FAILED`, leaves the parent arch in `LAB_FABRICATION`, and opens the resend leg. Either way the lab case's `version` increments by 1 and `qc_result` is persisted on the row, on the failing verdict as much as on the passing one.

**Resending** a QC-failed case is inside the write gate and is available from `QC_FAILED` only. The case's `resend_count` must be strictly below 2, so a case may be resent twice and a third resend is refused with `ERR_QC_RESEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; and the logical clock must still be at or before `shelf_life_expires_at`, a resend attempted after that instant being refused with `ERR_LAB_CASE_EXPIRED` — without, on this path, any accompanying write, because the mutate-then-reject behaviour belongs to the receive path alone. A permitted resend moves the case `QC_FAILED → RESENT`, increases `resend_count` by one, clears `received_at` back to null, and — critically — **leaves `shelf_life_expires_at` exactly where it was**, so the 45-day shelf-life clock keeps running from the original dispatch and is not renewed by the resend. `dispatched_at` and `promised_due_at` are likewise left untouched, so the bench-line interval the case occupies is the one struck at the original dispatch and does not extend. That is why a resend runs **neither** of the two overlap rungs: rung 20's arch possession and rung 21's bench-line overlap belong to the dispatch endpoint alone, and a resend re-occupies its old slot without re-checking either — the arch's own still-open lab case is this very row, and the interval being re-entered is one this row already holds. Nothing about the arch is written on a resend, so no arch `version` moves. The cycle `QC_FAILED → RESENT → RETURNED → QC_FAILED` can therefore repeat at most twice before the ceiling stops it.

Lab cases are also cancelled from the outside. A case cancellation, an approved remake on the arch, the voiding of the post-delivery service that a lab case was created for, and the drafting of a newer lab case on the same arch all move an open lab case to `CANCELLED`, provided its `lab_status` is not already `QC_PASSED`, `EXPIRED`, or `CANCELLED`. The `DRAFT`, `DISPATCHED`, and `RESENT` legs explicitly release the laboratory bench-line reservation the case was holding; in every leg the row leaves the set of statuses that counts as occupying, and each cancelled row's `version` increments by 1. `QC_PASSED`, `EXPIRED`, and `CANCELLED` are the three terminal states of the lab-case axis. Lab cases can be listed and filtered by `case_arch_id`, by `dental_lab_id`, and by `lab_status`.

### 3.6. Delivery, the Two Clocks That Disagree, and the Recall Cycle

Delivery is the hinge of the whole system. A `Prosthodontist` delivers the finished appliance for one arch by naming the `case_arch_id`; on a remake the delivery additionally names the `superseded_prosthesis_id` of the appliance being replaced, and on an original delivery that field is omitted or null. Delivery is inside the write gate, and the named arch must be sitting in `READY_FOR_DELIVERY` or the call is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_STATE`. `superseded_prosthesis_id`, when supplied, is checked for existence at rung 6 and for nothing else: no rung requires it to belong to this arch, to this case or to this patient, and none requires it to be the arch's most recent appliance or to be in any particular warranty or recall state. Whatever row it names is the one whose `frequency_anchor_at` the new appliance inherits and the one that gets closed out below.

What follows is the **delivery cascade**, four links deep:

- **Link 1** creates the prosthesis row itself, in `warranty_status` `IN_WARRANTY` and `recall_status` `RECALL_SCHEDULED`, with `adjustments_used` at zero, `adjustments_remaining` at 4, `in_patient_possession` true, and `delivered_at` stamped with the logical clock. `prosthesis_type` is copied from the arch's parent case at this moment, and `is_remake` is true exactly when `superseded_prosthesis_id` is not null.
- **Link 2**, caused by link 1, moves the parent arch `READY_FOR_DELIVERY → DELIVERED` and increments the arch's `version` by 1.
- **Link 3**, caused by link 2, bubbles the parent case `IN_FABRICATION → DELIVERED` once every arch of that case is either `DELIVERED` or `ARCH_ABANDONED` and at least one of them is `DELIVERED`; while any arch of the case is still in `PLANNED`, `IMPRESSION_PENDING`, `MILESTONE_PENDING`, `LAB_FABRICATION`, `READY_FOR_DELIVERY`, or `REMAKE_PENDING`, the case stays in `IN_FABRICATION`. The bubble-up stamps the case's `delivered_at` with the logical clock, recomputes the case's `billed_amount` and `patient_responsibility_amount`, and increments the case's `version` by 1.
- **Link 4**, caused by link 3, auto-creates exactly one claim for the case, in `DRAFT`, with its `coverage_id` set to the coverage of the case's patient whose `rank` is `PRIMARY` — but only when exactly one such coverage exists. **When the patient holds no primary coverage, no claim is created and delivery is otherwise unaffected.** This leg fires only on the case-level transition to `DELIVERED`; a delivery that leaves the case in `IN_FABRICATION` mints no claim.

On a remake delivery there is one further effect: the superseded appliance named by `superseded_prosthesis_id` has its `warranty_status` set to `WARRANTY_VOIDED` and its `recall_status` set to `RECALL_CLOSED_SUPERSEDED`, with its own `version` increment. Each of those two moves is scoped to the axis's open states, exactly as the transition tables below declare them: the warranty axis moves only from `IN_WARRANTY` or `WARRANTY_CONSUMED`, so an appliance already read into `WARRANTY_EXPIRED` keeps that status rather than being voided, and an appliance already `WARRANTY_VOIDED` is left alone; the recall axis moves only from `RECALL_SCHEDULED`, `RECALL_DUE` or `RECALL_OVERDUE`, so one already `RECALL_COMPLETED` or `RECALL_CLOSED_SUPERSEDED` is left alone too. The `version` increment follows the writes rather than the attempt: **one** increment when either axis or both moved, and **none at all** when neither did. The superseded appliance's clocks are not re-evaluated first, so whether its warranty reads `IN_WARRANTY` or `WARRANTY_EXPIRED` at this moment depends on whether anything had already read the row since the warranty lapsed.

**The two clocks.** Delivery starts two long-running clocks on the new appliance, and they are deliberately built to disagree on a remake.

- `warranty_expires_at` is always `delivered_at` plus 365 calendar days — **this** delivery's `delivered_at`, whether the appliance is an original or a remake. A remake therefore buys a full fresh year of warranty.
- `frequency_anchor_at` is the anchor of the *insurance* replacement clock, and it depends on which kind of delivery this is. On an original delivery, where `superseded_prosthesis_id` is null, `frequency_anchor_at` equals this appliance's `delivered_at`. On a remake delivery, where `superseded_prosthesis_id` is set, `frequency_anchor_at` is copied verbatim from the superseded appliance's `frequency_anchor_at`. A remake therefore buys **no** new frequency eligibility at all.
- `replacement_eligible_date` is `frequency_anchor_at` plus 60 calendar months, keeping the day-of-month and clamping to the last day of a shorter target month.

The consequence is worth stating plainly: an appliance remade three years after its original delivery is under warranty for a year from the remake, but its `replacement_eligible_date` still counts from the original delivery five years earlier, so the carrier's replacement window is untouched by the clinic's own rework.

Delivery also fixes two smaller windows. `adjustment_window_ends_at` is `delivered_at` plus 90 calendar days, and it bounds the included-adjustment allowance of §3.7. `mandatory_reline_due_at` is `delivered_at` plus 180 calendar days **when the `prosthesis_type` is `IMMEDIATE_DENTURE`, and null for every other type** — an immediate denture is placed on a healing ridge and is expected to need a hard reline within six months.

Finally, the patient's own counters move: `delivered_prosthesis_count` rises by one for a non-remake delivery, and `last_frequency_anchor_at` becomes the greatest `frequency_anchor_at` across the patient's appliances. Both are derived rather than stored, so they move without the `Patient` row being written: **the patient's `version` and `updated_at` do not advance on a delivery**, and the delivery cascade's whole version footprint is the new prosthesis at 1, the arch by 1, the case by 1 where it bubbled, the auto-minted claim at 1 where one was minted, and the superseded appliance by 1 where one moved.

**The warranty axis.** Warranty and recall are two independent axes riding on the same prosthesis row; a movement on one never implies a movement on the other. The warranty axis starts at `IN_WARRANTY` and has four possible values.

- `IN_WARRANTY → WARRANTY_CONSUMED` happens when a warranty claim on this appliance reaches `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT`, provided the appliance is currently `IN_WARRANTY` and the logical clock is at or before `warranty_expires_at`. It also sets that claim's `warranty_consumed` to true. A `PATIENT_FAULT` verdict deliberately does **not** reach this transition.
- `IN_WARRANTY → WARRANTY_EXPIRED` and `WARRANTY_CONSUMED → WARRANTY_EXPIRED` are both evaluated lazily, on every read or handling of the row, and fire when the logical clock is strictly after `warranty_expires_at`.
- `IN_WARRANTY → WARRANTY_VOIDED` and `WARRANTY_CONSUMED → WARRANTY_VOIDED` fire when the parent arch enters `REMAKE_PENDING`, and apply to the most recent appliance of that arch.

Each of those transitions increments the prosthesis's `version` by 1 — subject to the per-request cap of §2.4, so a single request that moves this axis *and* the recall axis advances the row by 1 in total, not 2. `WARRANTY_EXPIRED` and `WARRANTY_VOIDED` are the two terminal states of the warranty axis.

**The recall axis.** The recall axis starts at `RECALL_SCHEDULED`. Its first due date is computed from the appliance type: 365 days after delivery for a `COMPLETE_DENTURE`, a `CAST_PARTIAL`, or a `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL`; 180 days for an `OVERDENTURE` or an `IMPLANT_RETAINED_OVERDENTURE`; and only 90 days for an `IMMEDIATE_DENTURE`, which needs watching soonest. More precisely, `recall_due_at` is `delivered_at` plus the appliance type's interval multiplied by one more than the appliance's `recall_cycle_count`, so the first cycle is one interval out, the second is two intervals out, and so on.

Banding is evaluated lazily against the logical clock whenever the row is read or handled. A row moves `RECALL_SCHEDULED → RECALL_DUE` once the logical clock is **at or after** `recall_due_at` — the boundary instant itself counts as due. A row moves `RECALL_DUE → RECALL_OVERDUE` once the logical clock is **at or after** `recall_due_at` plus 30 days; that boundary is inclusive too, so the instant exactly 30 days after the due instant is already overdue and not merely due. The two steps are evaluated in sequence within one re-evaluation, so a row that has sat unread past both boundaries goes `RECALL_SCHEDULED → RECALL_DUE → RECALL_OVERDUE` inside a single read and is projected in `RECALL_OVERDUE`, never observably resting in `RECALL_DUE`; both steps together still cost the row one `version` increment.

Closing a recall visit is a `Prosthodontist` act, sits outside the write gate because recall is a safety follow-up on an appliance the patient already holds, and carries one `recall_outcome` input with three permitted values:

- **`NO_ACTION`** — the appliance is fine. The row returns to `RECALL_SCHEDULED`, `recall_cycle_count` increases by one, and `recall_due_at` therefore advances by one further interval of the appliance type, recomputed from `delivered_at` rather than from the old due instant. This is the recall *cycle*: a clean recall reschedules the same row instead of terminating it, and a complete denture recalled cleanly twice is next due three years after delivery. Like the two completing outcomes, this leg is accepted from `RECALL_SCHEDULED`, `RECALL_DUE` and `RECALL_OVERDUE` alike — a no-action recall closed early, on a row still sitting in `RECALL_SCHEDULED`, is not refused; it returns the row to `RECALL_SCHEDULED` and pushes the cycle forward exactly as it would from the two later bands.
- **`RELINE_INDICATED`** — the row moves to `RECALL_COMPLETED` and a post-delivery service of `service_type` `HARD_RELINE_LAB` is auto-created on this appliance in `REQUESTED`. Because it is minted by the system rather than requested by a caller, it carries no laboratory routing of its own and stays in `REQUESTED`: its `lab_case_id` is null and **no lab case is minted**, `is_emergency_repair` is false, `charge_basis` is null and `charge_amount` 0.00, and — this being the whole point of it stopping short of `AT_LAB` — the appliance's `in_patient_possession` stays true. Being system-minted it also skips everything a caller's request would face: no version guard, no supersede of an older un-completed hard reline, no type-eligibility rung and no frequency rung, so it is minted even inside the 24-month reline window.
- **`REMAKE_EVALUATION`** — the row moves to `RECALL_COMPLETED` and a remake request is auto-created on the parent arch in `PENDING_APPROVAL` with `reason_code` of `RECALL_EVALUATION`, `requested_by_user_id` set to the calling `X-User-ID`, `warranty_claim_id` null and `charge_basis` `CHARGEABLE`. As with the try-in rejection, it is subject to neither rung 11 nor rung 22 and does not write the arch.

All three outcomes are accepted from `RECALL_SCHEDULED`, `RECALL_DUE`, and `RECALL_OVERDUE` alike. Separately, a row in any of those three bands moves to `RECALL_CLOSED_SUPERSEDED` when the parent arch enters `REMAKE_PENDING`, provided it is the most recent appliance of that arch. Every recall transition increments the prosthesis's `version` by 1, again subject to the per-request cap of §2.4: the lazy re-banding that runs at the head of a recall close-out and the close-out's own movement together cost the row 1, not 2. `RECALL_COMPLETED` and `RECALL_CLOSED_SUPERSEDED` are the two terminal states of the recall axis, and rung 10 is checked against the re-banded value, so a row that this very call has just moved into `RECALL_COMPLETED`-adjacent territory is judged on where it now stands.

Reading one prosthesis re-evaluates both clock-driven axes against the logical clock before the row is projected, so a caller who advances the clock and then reads sees the new bands. **Listing prostheses does the same**, and does it to every stored appliance before the filters are applied, not merely to the page the caller will see — so a `recall_status` filter matches on the re-banded value rather than the stale one, and a list call is by itself enough to persist the new bands and their `version` increments. The recall close-out below re-evaluates its target before its own state rungs run, and so do the two adjudication endpoints of §3.7, before their warranty rungs. Those four — read one, list, recall-complete, adjudicate or clinically review — are the complete set: delivery does not re-evaluate the appliance it supersedes, and a remake approval does not re-evaluate the appliance it voids, so both of those act on whatever bands were last persisted. Prostheses can be listed and filtered by `case_arch_id`, by `superseded_prosthesis_id`, by `warranty_status`, and by `recall_status`.

**The overdue boundary is inclusive.** The instant at exactly `recall_due_at` plus 30 days could be read either way — as the first overdue instant, or as the last merely-due one. It is **the first overdue instant**: the row moves `RECALL_DUE → RECALL_OVERDUE` once the logical clock is at or after `recall_due_at` plus 30 days, so a clock parked precisely on that instant re-bands the row to `RECALL_OVERDUE`, and only a clock strictly before it leaves the row in `RECALL_DUE`. This matches the other boundary on the same axis, where `recall_due_at` itself already counts as due, so both steps of the recall banding are inclusive on their lower edge and neither uses a strict comparison.

### 3.7. Post-Delivery Services, Warranty Fault and Remake Authorisation

An appliance in a patient's mouth generates work for years. A **post-delivery service** is one unit of that work, requested by a `Prosthodontist` or a `FrontOffice` operator against a delivered appliance. The request names the `prosthesis_id`, the `service_type`, and the `expected_prosthesis_version` that guards the write; it may also carry `is_emergency_repair`, which defaults to false, and — for the service types that go out to a laboratory — a `dental_lab_id` and a `bench_line`. Those last two are required when the service type is a laboratory type. On a chairside type `bench_line` is genuinely ignored, but `dental_lab_id` is only ignored *after* rung 6: a `dental_lab_id` supplied on a chairside request is still checked for existence there, so naming a laboratory that does not exist is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404 even on a service that would never have used it.

The seven service types split into two branches that behave differently in every respect that matters:

- **Chairside**: `ADJUSTMENT`, `SOFT_RELINE_CHAIRSIDE`, and `REPAIR`. The service moves immediately from `REQUESTED` to `IN_CHAIR`, and the appliance's `in_patient_possession` stays true — the patient keeps the denture.
- **Laboratory**: `HARD_RELINE_LAB`, `REBASE`, `TOOTH_ADDITION`, and `CLASP_ADDITION`. The service moves immediately from `REQUESTED` to `AT_LAB`, a lab case is auto-created in `DRAFT` on the appliance's parent arch, the service's `lab_case_id` is set to that new lab case, and `in_patient_possession` is set to **false** — the appliance has left the patient. The auto-created lab case takes its `dental_lab_id` and `bench_line` from the service request, and is minted with `turnaround_class` `STANDARD`, `unit_count` 1, and `lab_invoice_amount` 450.00. Its `purpose` is derived from the service type: a hard reline becomes `RELINE`, a rebase becomes `REBASE`, and both a tooth addition and a clasp addition become `ADDITION`. It is born in `DRAFT` with the same null clocks a caller-drafted lab case has, on the arch behind the appliance, and it differs from a caller-drafted one in three ways beyond not being subject to rung 13: it does **not** supersede an existing `DRAFT` lab case on that arch, it does **not** increment the arch's `version`, and it must still be dispatched by the ordinary dispatch endpoint, at which point both overlap rungs apply to it normally.

That divergence of side effects is the entire point of the fork: only the laboratory branch mints a lab case and only the laboratory branch takes the appliance out of the patient's possession.

Requesting a service also **supersedes**. Any older service of the same `service_type` on the same appliance that has not completed — one sitting in `REQUESTED`, `IN_CHAIR`, or `AT_LAB` — is moved to `VOIDED` with `voided_at` stamped and its own `version` incremented by 1; if that superseded row was `AT_LAB`, its linked lab case is cancelled with a `version` increment of its own, its bench-line reservation released, and `in_patient_possession` restored to true — a restoration the laboratory branch below then immediately undoes. The supersede leaves the older row's `charge_basis`, `charge_amount` and `lab_case_id` exactly as they were. This supersede is why the create is guarded by `expected_prosthesis_version`, as §2.8 explains. Every service request increments the appliance's `version` by 1, once, whether or not anything was superseded and whichever branch of the fork it took.

Two eligibility rules stand between a request and its acceptance, evaluated in this order. First, **type eligibility**: a `CLASP_ADDITION` is only accepted on a partial — that is, on a `CAST_PARTIAL` or a `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL` — and a `REBASE` is refused outright on a `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL`, both refusals carrying `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Second, **frequency**: each service type has a minimum interval, expressed in whole calendar months, that must have elapsed since the most recent completed service of that same type on that same appliance. A hard reline may be repeated only every 24 months; a chairside soft reline only every 6 months; a rebase only every 36 months. Adjustments, repairs, tooth additions, and clasp additions carry an interval of zero months, which means no limit at all. Where the interval is non-zero, the server finds the most recent completed service of that type on that appliance — ordering candidates by `completed_at` descending and breaking ties by `created_at` descending, and ignoring voided rows entirely — and requires the logical clock to be at or after that row's `completed_at` plus the interval. The boundary is inclusive: exactly 24 months after a hard reline, another hard reline is permitted. When no completed service of that type exists on the appliance, the request is eligible. A request inside the interval is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`.

Requesting a service is inside the global write gate — with the emergency carve-out of §2.11, which requires both that `service_type` is `REPAIR` and that `is_emergency_repair` is true. Completing and voiding a service both sit outside the gate.

**Completing a service** is a `Prosthodontist` act. A chairside service completes straight from `IN_CHAIR`. A laboratory service completes from `AT_LAB` only once its linked lab case has reached `QC_PASSED`; completing earlier is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_STATE`. Completion fixes the money classification on the row, and the rule has a dual character on adjustments:

- For an `ADJUSTMENT`, two conditions are tested. The count condition is that the appliance's `adjustments_used` is **strictly below 4**; the window condition is that the logical clock is **at or before** `adjustment_window_ends_at`, which is inclusive of the boundary instant. When both hold, `charge_basis` is `INCLUDED` and `charge_amount` is 0.00, and the completion consumes one unit of the allowance: `adjustments_used` rises by one and `adjustments_remaining` falls by one. When either fails — the fifth adjustment, or any adjustment after the 90-day window closes — `charge_basis` is `CHARGEABLE`, `charge_amount` is 85.00, and **neither allowance counter moves**, which is what keeps the conservation invariant of §2.13 intact on that path.
- Otherwise, when the service is one a warranty claim in `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT` was raised over — a claim citing this service through its `post_delivery_service_id` — `charge_basis` is `WARRANTY_NO_CHARGE` and `charge_amount` is 0.00.
- Otherwise `charge_basis` is `CHARGEABLE` and `charge_amount` is the fee for the service type: 260.00 for a chairside soft reline, 420.00 for a laboratory hard reline, 590.00 for a rebase, 180.00 for a repair, 155.00 for a tooth addition, and 195.00 for a clasp addition. All fees are carried to two decimal places.

Completion also stamps `completed_at`, adds `charge_amount` to the parent case's `billed_amount` and recomputes the case's patient responsibility as described in §3.9, restores `in_patient_possession` to true when the row was a laboratory service, and increments the service's `version` by 1. The service's is the only increment: consuming an adjustment unit and restoring the possession flag are by-product writes on the appliance, so **the `Prosthesis` row's `version` and `updated_at` do not advance on a completion**, and neither do the case's — a client's `expected_prosthesis_version` therefore survives a completion unchanged, in deliberate contrast to a service *request*, which does advance it.

**Voiding a service** is available to a `FrontOffice` or `Prosthodontist` operator. From `REQUESTED` or `IN_CHAIR` it simply stamps `voided_at` and moves the row to `VOIDED`. From `AT_LAB` it additionally cancels the linked lab case, releases its bench-line reservation, and restores `in_patient_possession` to true. From `COMPLETED` it is the declared **compensating action**: when the voided row was an adjustment classified as `INCLUDED`, `adjustments_used` falls by one and `adjustments_remaining` rises by one, restoring the allowance; the row's `charge_amount` is subtracted from the case's `billed_amount` and the case's patient responsibility recomputed; `charge_amount` is then set to 0.00; and `voided_at` is stamped. What the void deliberately leaves alone is as load-bearing as what it clears: **`charge_basis` is not reset** — a voided adjustment still reads `INCLUDED` or `CHARGEABLE`, a voided lab service still reads `WARRANTY_NO_CHARGE` — and **`completed_at` is not cleared**, so the row keeps the instant it was finished at even though it no longer counts as finished. Neither survives into any calculation, because every sum in §3.9 and every frequency lookup in this section filters on `service_status` being `COMPLETED` and a voided row no longer is. Every void increments the service's `version` by 1, and only the service's: restoring an allowance unit or a possession flag does not advance the appliance, and re-balancing does not advance the case.

`COMPLETED` is deliberately **not** a terminal state of the service axis — void is its declared compensating action and must stay reachable. `VOIDED` is the only terminal state. Every trigger other than void attempted from `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Services can be listed and filtered by `prosthesis_id`, by `lab_case_id`, by `service_type`, and by `service_status`.

When an appliance fails and somebody has to pay for it, a **warranty claim** decides who. A `Prosthodontist` or a `FrontOffice` operator opens one against a delivered appliance, outside the write gate, supplying the `prosthesis_id` and a free-text `reported_issue`, and optionally a `post_delivery_service_id` naming the service whose result the patient disputes and an `attributed_lab_case_id` naming the lab case whose workmanship is under suspicion. The claim is born in `SUBMITTED` with `fault_verdict` null, `lab_credit_amount` 0.00, `patient_charge_amount` 0.00, `warranty_consumed` false, and `adjudicated_at` null. Opening a claim runs no state check of any kind: the appliance's warranty and recall bands are not consulted and not re-evaluated, so a claim may be opened against an appliance already `WARRANTY_EXPIRED`, `WARRANTY_CONSUMED` or `WARRANTY_VOIDED` — rungs 23 and 24 speak at adjudication, not here — and the two optional foreign keys are checked for existence at rung 6 and for nothing else, so neither is required to belong to this appliance or its arch. Any number of claims may be open against one appliance at once. The create writes nothing but the new row: the appliance is not touched and its `version` does not move.

Adjudication belongs to the `ClinicalDirector` alone and is outside the write gate — fault adjudication decides who owes the money, and letting the clinic's own receivable block it would let the clinic profit from its own delay. The adjudicating director supplies a `fault_verdict` taking one of four values, and the claim fans out four ways:

- **`CLINIC_FAULT`** sets `warranty_consumed` to true, `lab_credit_amount` to 0.00, and `patient_charge_amount` to 0.00, stamps `adjudicated_at`, and moves the referenced appliance `IN_WARRANTY → WARRANTY_CONSUMED`. The clinic absorbs the whole cost.
- **`LAB_FAULT`** sets `warranty_consumed` to true and `patient_charge_amount` to 0.00, stamps `adjudicated_at`, moves the appliance `IN_WARRANTY → WARRANTY_CONSUMED`, and computes a credit: `lab_credit_amount` is the attributed lab case's `lab_invoice_amount` multiplied by 0.60, rounded half up to two decimal places — so a lab case invoiced at the default 450.00 yields a credit of 270.00. That amount is added to the attributed laboratory's `credit_issued_total`, and its `credit_available_balance` is re-derived as `credit_issued_total` minus `credit_applied_total`, leaving `credit_applied_total` untouched. The laboratory reached is the one behind the *attributed lab case*, whatever laboratory that is, and its ledger moves without the `DentalLab` row's `version` or `updated_at` advancing. A credit computed at 0.00 — an attributed lab case invoiced at 0.00 — moves no ledger at all. This leg requires the claim to carry an `attributed_lab_case_id`.
- **`PATIENT_FAULT`** sets `warranty_consumed` to **false** — the warranty is deliberately *not* consumed — leaves `lab_credit_amount` at 0.00, sets `patient_charge_amount` to 180.00, adds that amount to the case's `billed_amount` and recomputes the case's patient responsibility, stamps `adjudicated_at`, and leaves the appliance in `IN_WARRANTY`. The patient broke it, the patient pays, and the warranty survives for a future genuine failure.
- **`INDETERMINATE`** moves the claim to `CLINICAL_REVIEW` rather than to any terminal verdict, records `INDETERMINATE` in `fault_verdict`, **stamps `adjudicated_at` with the logical clock exactly as the three deciding verdicts do**, and leaves `lab_credit_amount`, `patient_charge_amount`, and `warranty_consumed` at their defaults. No money moves, the case balance of §3.9 is not re-run, and the appliance is untouched. The later clinical review re-stamps `adjudicated_at` over this value, so a claim that took the review route carries the instant of its *resolution*, not of its first adjudication.

Every one of the four increments the claim's `version` by 1. The appliance advances by 1 only on the two legs that actually move its warranty axis, and only when the move happens — a `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT` verdict on an appliance not sitting in `IN_WARRANTY` sets the claim's `warranty_consumed` true without writing the appliance, so nothing there advances. Two ladder rungs guard the whole family, and both read the appliance's bands **after** the lazy re-evaluation of §3.6 has been applied to it, so an adjudication is judged against the appliance's current bands and not against whatever was last persisted; that re-evaluation is itself a write and can advance the appliance's `version` on a call that then goes on to be refused. Rung 23 refuses adjudication against an appliance whose `warranty_status` is `WARRANTY_CONSUMED` or `WARRANTY_VOIDED` with `ERR_WARRANTY_CONSUMED`; an appliance in `WARRANTY_EXPIRED` deliberately passes that rung so the next one can speak. Rung 24 then refuses a `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT` verdict recorded after the appliance's `warranty_expires_at` with `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED`, and is deliberately not applied to a `PATIENT_FAULT` or `INDETERMINATE` verdict, because a chargeable verdict does not depend on the warranty window at all.

A claim parked in `CLINICAL_REVIEW` is resolved by the same role through a second decision carrying a `resolved_verdict` of `CLINIC_FAULT`, `LAB_FAULT`, or `PATIENT_FAULT`. Each resolution applies **exactly** the side-effect set of the corresponding adjudication leg above — the same money fields, the same warranty movement, the same credit arithmetic on a lab-fault resolution, the same `adjudicated_at` stamp — and increments the claim's `version` by 1. The lab-fault resolution likewise requires an `attributed_lab_case_id`. Both adjudication and clinical review are subject to rungs 23 and 24 in the same way. `CLINIC_FAULT`, `LAB_FAULT`, and `PATIENT_FAULT` are the three terminal states of the claim axis. Warranty claims can be listed and filtered by `prosthesis_id`, by `post_delivery_service_id`, by `attributed_lab_case_id`, and by `claim_status`.

*A lab-fault verdict without an attributed lab case is refused, not admitted at 0.00.* The lab-fault legs above require `attributed_lab_case_id` to be present, while the credit rule could be read as describing a zero-credit outcome for "a lab-fault verdict with no attributed lab case". The requirement wins: such a verdict is treated as a **missing required input** and refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400 at rung 7, on **both** the adjudication path and the clinical-review path alike, and refused before the enum-membership, terminal-state, from-state and warranty rungs have run. Because the refusal is absolute, the zero-credit branch is unreachable for a lab-fault verdict: no claim can ever settle in `LAB_FAULT` carrying `lab_credit_amount` 0.00 through this route, and no laboratory-less lab-fault claim exists for a remake to cite. The 0.00 credit figure survives only where no lab fault was found at all — every other verdict leaves `lab_credit_amount` at its default 0.00.

**Remaking an arch** is the last resort, and it is deliberately a two-person operation. A `Prosthodontist` requests the remake, inside the write gate, naming the `case_arch_id`, a `reason_code` of `TRYIN_REJECTED`, `FIT_FAILURE`, `LAB_DEFECT`, `PATIENT_DISSATISFACTION`, or `RECALL_EVALUATION`, and optionally the `warranty_claim_id` of the adjudicated fault the remake answers. The row is created in `PENDING_APPROVAL` with `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the calling `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` and `approved_by_user_id` null, and `attempt_number` set to the arch's `remake_request_count` plus one — after which the arch's `remake_request_count` itself rises by one. It rises because it is derived from the requests themselves, so on the accepted path the arch is not written at all and **its `version` and `updated_at` do not move**; the only arch write this endpoint ever performs is the eligibility flip of the rung 22 refusal below. Nor is the arch's own state consulted: a remake may be requested against an arch in any status, including one still in `PLANNED` or already abandoned. Its `charge_basis` is `NO_CHARGE` when the cited warranty claim is in `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT`, and `CHARGEABLE` in every other case, including when no claim is cited at all. The system mints requests of this shape for itself in two places already described: a rejected try-in mints one with `reason_code` `TRYIN_REJECTED`, and a recall closed for remake evaluation mints one with `reason_code` `RECALL_EVALUATION`.

The counter that the ceiling watches is `remake_request_count`, and it counts the arch's remake requests whose `authorization_status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, or `DENIED`. Rows in `FINAL_DENIED` are deliberately excluded from it, so the counter cannot run away once the ceiling has been hit. An arch may accumulate at most two such requests; a third request trips the mutate-then-reject behaviour of §2.12, and every request after that is pre-empted by the arch's `remake_eligibility` of `FINAL_DENIED`. Separately, the arch carries `remake_count`, which counts only the requests that were actually approved.

Deciding a remake belongs to the `ClinicalDirector` and sits outside the write gate, because the request creation was already gated and deciding an existing request is not a new start. Both decisions are subject to segregation of duties: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the request's `requested_by_user_id`, and a self-decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. **Denial** — which may carry an optional `denial_reason`, which must be a string when supplied but is **not persisted and appears on no response**, the request row carrying no field for it — moves the row to `DENIED`, records `approved_by_user_id` (yes, on a denial: the field holds whoever decided, whichever way they decided) and `decided_at`, leaves the parent arch entirely untouched, and increments the request's `version` by 1. It runs neither of the approval's two cross-entity checks, so a remake may be denied on an arch in any state and whatever the cited claim's status.

**Approval** requires, beyond segregation of duties, that the parent arch is in `DELIVERED` or `MILESTONE_PENDING`, and — when the request cites a `warranty_claim_id` — that the cited claim's `claim_status` is `CLINIC_FAULT` or `LAB_FAULT`, failing which the approval is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_STATE`. Both are rung 13 checks and are evaluated in that rung's printed order: the cited claim first, the arch second, so a request that fails both reports the claim. Segregation of duties is rung 14 and therefore runs after both, so a director who both requested and is now approving a remake on a wrongly-stated arch is told about the arch, not about the conflict. Approval then runs the **remake cascade**, six links deep, each caused by the one before it:

- **Link 1** sets `authorization_status` to `APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, and increments the request's `version` by 1.
- **Link 2**, caused by link 1, moves the parent arch to `REMAKE_PENDING` — from `DELIVERED` when the appliance was already handed over, or from `MILESTONE_PENDING` when a try-in was rejected before any delivery ever happened — increases the arch's `remake_count` by one, and increments the arch's `version` by 1.
- **Link 3**, caused by link 2, sets the arch's most recent appliance — the one whose row was minted last — to `warranty_status` `WARRANTY_VOIDED` and `recall_status` `RECALL_CLOSED_SUPERSEDED`, and moves every lab case of that arch whose `lab_status` is `DRAFT`, `DISPATCHED`, `RETURNED`, `QC_FAILED`, or `RESENT` to `CANCELLED`, each with a `version` increment of its own. Both halves of this link have a skip condition. The appliance half is skipped entirely when the arch has never delivered anything — which is exactly the `MILESTONE_PENDING` route, where a try-in was rejected before any delivery — and where an appliance does exist each of its two axes moves only from its open states, so an appliance already in `WARRANTY_EXPIRED` keeps that status, one already `RECALL_COMPLETED` keeps that, and an appliance on which neither axis moves is not written and takes no `version` increment. The lab-case half touches nothing already in `QC_PASSED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED`. What link 3 deliberately does **not** do is clear the arch's `last_lab_received_at`: the 24-hour dispatch buffer of rung 20 keeps running from the pre-remake return, so a dispatch on the remade arch inside that window is still refused with `ERR_ARCH_OCCUPIED`.
- **Link 4**, caused by link 3, moves laboratory credit — but only when the request's `charge_basis` is `NO_CHARGE` and the cited warranty claim is in `LAB_FAULT`. The amount applied is the lesser of the claim's `lab_credit_amount` and the attributed laboratory's `credit_available_balance`. The laboratory's `credit_applied_total` rises by that amount and its `credit_available_balance` is re-derived as `credit_issued_total` minus `credit_applied_total`, with `credit_issued_total` untouched — the conservation of §2.13 holding across the move, and the `DentalLab` row's own `version` again not moving. The link is skipped outright, with the applied amount standing at 0.00, whenever the request cites no claim at all, or its `charge_basis` is `CHARGEABLE`, or the cited claim settled anywhere other than `LAB_FAULT`, or the laboratory's `credit_available_balance` has already been spent down to 0.00.
- **Link 5**, caused by link 4, applies that credit to the case's payment plan whose `plan_status` is `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`: the plan's `principal_amount` falls by the applied amount, its `applied_lab_credit_amount` rises by it, and its still-open installments are re-struck as described in §3.9. This link fires only when link 4 actually moved a non-zero amount; a skipped link 4 skips link 5 with it, and the plan and its installments are then left completely untouched, at their existing amounts and versions. Where more than one plan of the case somehow qualifies, the credit goes to the earliest-created one and to that one alone.
- **Link 6**, caused by link 5, moves the re-struck plan to `PAID_OFF` when its `outstanding_amount` reaches 0.00 — which, when it is the patient's only delinquent plan, lowers the global write gate. It follows link 5's fate: no credit applied, no re-strike, no settlement. Re-striking that leaves a residue outstanding leaves `plan_status` exactly as it was, so a `DELINQUENT` plan whose every missed installment was waived away by the credit stays `DELINQUENT` — and the write gate stays up — unless the re-strike also drove `outstanding_amount` to 0.00.

Alongside those six, approval moves the parent case back from `DELIVERED` to `IN_FABRICATION` when the case was in `DELIVERED`, clearing the case's `delivered_at` back to null and incrementing the case's `version` by 1. That is the case-level cycle: a case that already delivered re-enters fabrication when one of its arches is remade, while the sibling arch stays exactly where it was. From `REMAKE_PENDING` the arch restarts through the fabrication-start action of §3.2, closing the per-arch cycle `DELIVERED → REMAKE_PENDING → IMPRESSION_PENDING`. `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, and `FINAL_DENIED` are the three terminal states of the authorisation axis. Remake requests can be listed and filtered by `case_arch_id`, by `warranty_claim_id`, and by `authorization_status`; that listing is also the only way to discover a `FINAL_DENIED` row minted by the ceiling.

### 3.8. Insurance: Predetermination, Claims and Coordination of Benefits

Expensive removable prosthetics are rarely started without asking the carrier first. When a case's `fee_schedule_total` reaches 1500.00 the case's `predetermination_required` becomes true, and from that moment neither fabrication nor claim submission can proceed without a live carrier answer in hand.

A `FrontOffice` operator submits a preauthorization by naming the `case_id` and the `coverage_id` it is sent to. The row is created in `SUBMITTED`, with `requested_amount` fixed at the case's `fee_schedule_total` as observed at that instant, `allowed_amount`, `estimated_plan_payment`, and `estimated_patient_responsibility` all at 0.00, and `alternate_benefit_type`, `determined_at`, and `expires_at` all null. Predeterminations are outside the write gate, because insurance work reduces the balance and is part of the remedy path. The submission runs no state check: the case's `case_status` is not consulted, so a preauthorization may be raised on a case still in `INTAKE` — where `fee_schedule_total` is still 0.00 and the snapshot therefore captures 0.00 — as readily as on one priced at close-out. Nor is the coverage scoped: it is checked for existence at rung 6 and for nothing else, so it need not belong to the case's patient and need not be the one at `PRIMARY`. Any number of predeterminations may be raised for one case, which is why the resolution rule at submission below has to pick between them. They can be listed and filtered by `case_id`, by `coverage_id`, and by `determination_status`.

The carrier's answer arrives as a `determination` of `APPROVED`, `ALTERNATE_BENEFIT`, or `DENIED`, accepted only while the row is still in `SUBMITTED`, and the three answers fan out:

- **`APPROVED`** sets `allowed_amount` equal to `requested_amount` and leaves `alternate_benefit_type` null. The plan pays coinsurance on what was actually requested.
- **`ALTERNATE_BENEFIT`** is the carrier downgrading the treatment to a cheaper alternative it is willing to fund. It requires the cited coverage's `alternate_benefit_enabled` to be true and requires the case's `prosthesis_type` to have a declared downgrade target; an implant-retained overdenture and a plain overdenture both downgrade to `COMPLETE_DENTURE`, and a cast partial downgrades to `FLEXIBLE_PARTIAL`, while the other three types have no declared target. A determination that cannot satisfy both conditions is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400 — but that check is evaluated *after* the terminal-state and from-state rungs rather than at rung 7's usual position, so a downgrade attempted on a row that has already been determined reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when the alternate benefit would also have been unavailable. When it can, `alternate_benefit_type` is set to the downgrade target and `allowed_amount` is set to the fee of that target type — 1800.00 for a complete denture, 1200.00 for a flexible partial — multiplied by the number of the case's arches whose `arch_status` is not `ARCH_ABANDONED`. A two-arch implant-retained overdenture requested at 7200.00 is therefore allowed at 3600.00, and the patient owes the whole difference.
- **`DENIED`** sets `allowed_amount` to 0.00, `estimated_plan_payment` to 0.00, and `estimated_patient_responsibility` to the full `requested_amount`. It stamps `determined_at` and leaves `expires_at` null — a denial never enters a validity window.

For the two paying answers the estimate is computed the same way from whichever allowed basis the branch produced. The uncapped estimate is `allowed_amount` multiplied by the cited coverage's `coinsurance_major_rate`, rounded half up to two decimal places; `estimated_plan_payment` is then the lesser of that rounded figure and the coverage's `benefit_remaining` — the cap is applied **after** the rounding, never before. `estimated_patient_responsibility` is `requested_amount` minus `estimated_plan_payment`, rounded half up to two decimal places. On a standard coverage paying half of an approved 1800.00 case, the estimate is 900.00 unless only 700.00 of annual benefit is left, in which case it is 700.00.

Both paying answers stamp `determined_at` with the logical clock and compute `expires_at` as `determined_at` plus 180 calendar days. All three answers increment the predetermination's `version` by 1. Once the logical clock passes `expires_at` strictly, an `APPROVED` or `ALTERNATE_BENEFIT` row moves lazily to `EXPIRED` — evaluated on every read or handling of the row, including a read of the row itself, a listing (which re-evaluates every stored predetermination before applying its filters, so a `determination_status` filter matches on the re-banded value), the determination endpoint before its own state rungs, and every consultation of the rung 30 gate, which re-evaluates all of the case's predeterminations before choosing among them — with a further `version` increment. `DENIED` and `EXPIRED` are the two terminal states of the determination axis, and only `APPROVED` or `ALTERNATE_BENEFIT` rows still inside their validity window satisfy the predetermination gate at rung 30.

**Claims** run a two-stage coordination of benefits. A claim is opened for a case against its primary coverage — either by the `FrontOffice` naming a `case_id` and a `coverage_id`, or automatically by the delivery cascade of §3.6 — and starts in `DRAFT` with `submission_target` `PRIMARY`, every money field at 0.00, `predetermination_id`, `cob_convention_applied`, `submitted_at`, `adjudicated_at`, and `reversed_at` all null. "One per case" is the intended shape, not an enforced one: on the manual path nothing rejects a second claim for a case that already has one, and nothing checks that the named `coverage_id` is the case's patient's coverage or that it sits at `PRIMARY` rank — the coverage is validated for existence at rung 6 and for nothing else. The automatic path is stricter only because the cascade chooses the coverage itself. Nor does the manual create consult the case's `case_status`, so a claim may be opened on a case still in `INTAKE` or already `CANCELLED`. Claims are outside the write gate and can be listed and filtered by `case_id`, by `coverage_id`, by `predetermination_id`, and by `adjudication_status`.

Submitting a claim is one action that behaves differently depending on the stage the claim has reached. Its first stage moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED_PRIMARY` and runs four ladder rungs in strict order — 28, 29, 30, then 31:

1. **Waiting period.** The logical clock must be at or after the primary coverage's `effective_date` plus its `waiting_period_days` days; earlier is `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD`. On a coverage left at its default of 180 days, a claim submitted 179 days after enrolment fails and one submitted on day 180 passes.
2. **Replacement frequency.** Consider the appliances belonging to this patient through cases **other than** this claim's own case. When that set is non-empty, the logical clock must be at or after the greatest `replacement_eligible_date` across it; earlier is `ERR_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`. Because a remake never advances `frequency_anchor_at`, a remade appliance buys the patient no new replacement window here.
3. **Predetermination gate.** When the case's `predetermination_required` is true, a determination for the case must exist in `APPROVED` or `ALTERNATE_BENEFIT` with `expires_at` strictly after the logical clock; otherwise `ERR_PREDETERMINATION_REQUIRED`.
4. **Annual maximum.** The primary coverage's `benefit_remaining` must be strictly greater than 0.00; a coverage at exactly 0.00 yields `ERR_ANNUAL_MAX_EXHAUSTED`.

A first-stage submission that clears all four sets `billed_amount` to the case's `fee_schedule_total` — the priced total from the exam close-out, deliberately not the case's `billed_amount`, so neither rush fees nor post-delivery charges are ever billed to the carrier — resolves `predetermination_id` to the case's determination whose `determination_status` is `APPROVED` or `ALTERNATE_BENEFIT` with the latest `created_at`, ties among those broken by taking the **last-minted** one, or to null when none exists; sets `allowed_amount` to that determination's `allowed_amount` when one was resolved and to `billed_amount` otherwise, keeps `submission_target` at `PRIMARY`, stamps `submitted_at`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1. The candidates are re-evaluated for expiry first, so a determination that has just lapsed is `EXPIRED` by the time the resolution runs and is not eligible to be picked — meaning a case with a live determination and a lapsed one always resolves to the live one, whichever was minted later.

The second stage moves `PRIMARY_PAID → SUBMITTED_SECONDARY` and requires that exactly one coverage exists for the case's patient at `rank` `SECONDARY`; a patient holding none is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400, which is a plain missing-prerequisite failure and not one of the four insurance rungs. The waiting-period rung is then re-run against **that** coverage rather than the primary one, and it is the only one of the four that is re-run. The stage sets `submission_target` to `SECONDARY`, copies the secondary coverage's `cob_convention` into the claim's `cob_convention_applied`, stamps `submitted_at` afresh, and increments the claim's `version` by 1. `billed_amount`, `allowed_amount` and `predetermination_id` are left exactly as the first stage struck them — the secondary is adjudicated against the same allowed basis the primary was.

Recording the carrier's adjudication carries an `adjudication_outcome` of `PAID` or `DENIED`, and what it does depends on which stage is outstanding.

**Primary paid.** The uncapped payment is the claim's `allowed_amount` multiplied by the primary coverage's `coinsurance_major_rate`, rounded half up to two decimal places; `primary_paid_amount` is the lesser of that rounded figure and the primary coverage's `benefit_remaining`, the cap again applied after the rounding. `plan_paid_amount` is set equal to `primary_paid_amount`. The claim's own `patient_responsibility_amount` is `billed_amount` minus `plan_paid_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places and floored at 0.00. The primary coverage's `benefit_used_ytd` rises by `primary_paid_amount` and its `benefit_remaining` falls by the same, without the `Coverage` row's own `version` or `updated_at` moving. The case's `insurance_paid_amount` and `patient_responsibility_amount` are recomputed as described in §3.9 — again without advancing the case's `version` — `adjudicated_at` is stamped, and the claim's `version` increments by 1. When the patient holds no coverage at `SECONDARY`, the claim then moves straight on to `CLOSED` with a further `version` increment, so that call leaves the claim two versions higher than it found it; this is the single declared exception to the one-bump-per-row rule of §2.4. That auto-close is also one of the four moments at which case closure is evaluated.

**Primary denied.** `primary_paid_amount` and `plan_paid_amount` stay at 0.00, the claim's `patient_responsibility_amount` becomes the full `billed_amount`, `adjudicated_at` is stamped, the claim moves to `DENIED`, and its `version` increments by 1.

**Secondary paid.** This is the dual-variant leg, and the convention recorded in `cob_convention_applied` selects the formula. Two intermediate quantities are computed first, both rounded half up to two decimal places: the amount the secondary *would have* paid as a primary, which is the claim's `allowed_amount` multiplied by the secondary coverage's `coinsurance_major_rate`; and the patient's outstanding balance, which is `billed_amount` minus `primary_paid_amount`, floored at 0.00. Then:

- Under the **`STANDARD`** convention, the secondary tops the patient up towards the allowed amount: the raw figure is the lesser of (the claim's `allowed_amount` minus `primary_paid_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places) and the patient's outstanding balance.
- Under the **`NON_DUPLICATION`** convention, the secondary pays only the excess of what it would have paid over what the primary actually paid: the raw figure is the amount it would have paid as a primary minus `primary_paid_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places and floored at 0.00. Where the primary already paid at least as much, the secondary pays nothing.

In both conventions, `secondary_paid_amount` is the raw figure floored at 0.00 and then capped at the secondary coverage's `benefit_remaining`. `plan_paid_amount` becomes `primary_paid_amount` plus `secondary_paid_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and the claim's `patient_responsibility_amount` becomes `billed_amount` minus `plan_paid_amount`, rounded half up and floored at 0.00. The secondary coverage's `benefit_used_ytd` rises by `secondary_paid_amount` and its `benefit_remaining` falls by the same — again with no `version` movement on the `Coverage` row — the case's money fields are recomputed without advancing the case's `version`, `adjudicated_at` is stamped, the claim's `version` increments by 1, and the claim then moves on to `CLOSED` with a further increment. `SECONDARY_PAID` is therefore never an observable resting state: the same call carries the claim through it to `CLOSED`, two versions higher, and evaluates case closure on the way out.

**Secondary denied.** `secondary_paid_amount` stays at 0.00, `plan_paid_amount` stays equal to `primary_paid_amount`, `adjudicated_at` is stamped, the claim moves to `DENIED`, and its `version` increments by 1.

A closed claim can still be **reversed**, optionally carrying a `reversal_reason` — which must be a string when supplied but is **not persisted and appears on no response** — and that reversal is the declared compensating action of the coverage-benefit conservation. It decreases the primary coverage's `benefit_used_ytd` by `primary_paid_amount` and the secondary coverage's `benefit_used_ytd` by `secondary_paid_amount`, restoring both `benefit_remaining` figures to their pre-adjudication values without advancing either `Coverage` row's `version`, and skipping each side entirely where that side paid nothing; sets `primary_paid_amount`, `secondary_paid_amount`, and `plan_paid_amount` all to 0.00; recomputes the case's `insurance_paid_amount` and `patient_responsibility_amount`; stamps `reversed_at`; evaluates case closure; and increments the claim's `version` by 1. Two fields on the claim are deliberately **not** unwound: `patient_responsibility_amount` keeps the figure the adjudication left on it rather than reverting to `billed_amount`, and `adjudicated_at` keeps its stamp rather than being cleared — a reversed claim carries both a `reversed_at` and the `adjudicated_at` of the payment it reversed. Neither is read by anything, since the case balance of §3.9 sums `plan_paid_amount` over claims in the paying statuses only and `REVERSED` is not one of them. `CLOSED` is deliberately **not** a terminal state of the adjudication axis, precisely so that reversal stays reachable; every trigger other than reversal attempted from `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `DENIED` and `REVERSED` are the two terminal states.

### 3.9. The Case Balance, Payment Plans and Collections

Everything the patient owes is derived, never stored by hand, and it is derived in a fixed nine-step order. The order matters most at step 7, where a discount tier is chosen: **the tier is keyed on the responsibility figure computed at step 6, before any discount is struck, and it is never re-derived from the post-discount total.** The whole computation re-runs whenever the exam is closed out, a lab case is dispatched, a delivery bubbles the case up to `DELIVERED`, a post-delivery service is completed, a post-delivery service is voided, a warranty claim is adjudicated, a claim payment is posted, a claim is reversed, and a payment plan is created. Those nine are the complete list; nothing else re-runs it, so in particular a case cancellation and a remake approval leave the four money fields exactly as they stood. One of the nine has an internal exception: an adjudication returning `INDETERMINATE` moves no money and does not re-run the computation. And however it is triggered, the re-run is a pure re-derivation — it never advances the case's own `version` or `updated_at`, which move only when the same request also drives a declared transition of the case.

1. The **gross fee** is the case's `fee_schedule_total`.
2. The **rush total** is the sum of `rush_fee_amount` over the case's lab cases whose `lab_status` is `DISPATCHED`, `RETURNED`, `QC_PASSED`, `QC_FAILED`, `RESENT`, or `EXPIRED`. A lab case still in `DRAFT` has no rush fee yet, and a `CANCELLED` lab case's rush fee drops out of the total.
3. The **service total** is the sum of `charge_amount` over the case's post-delivery services whose `service_status` is `COMPLETED`, plus the sum of `patient_charge_amount` over the case's warranty claims whose `claim_status` is `PATIENT_FAULT`.
4. `billed_amount` is the gross fee plus the rush total plus the service total, rounded half up to two decimal places.
5. `insurance_paid_amount` is the sum of `plan_paid_amount` over the case's claims whose `adjudication_status` is `PRIMARY_PAID`, `SECONDARY_PAID`, or `CLOSED`.
6. The **pre-discount responsibility** is `billed_amount` minus `insurance_paid_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places and floored at 0.00.
7. The **discount rate** is chosen from the pre-discount responsibility of step 6 alone, with both tier boundaries inclusive on their lower edge: 0.05 when it is at or above 2500.00; 0.03 when it is at or above 1000.00 but below 2500.00; and 0.00 below 1000.00.
8. `prompt_pay_discount_amount` is the pre-discount responsibility multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimal places — **but only when the case has a payment plan whose `plan_status` is `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` and whose `pay_in_full` is true.** In every other situation it is 0.00.
9. `patient_responsibility_amount` is the pre-discount responsibility minus `prompt_pay_discount_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

All of the sums above are order-independent, so no tie-break is required anywhere in this computation.

A **payment plan** finances what is left. A `FrontOffice` operator creates one — outside the write gate, because establishing or replacing a plan is precisely the remedy for a gated account — by supplying `patient_id`, `case_id`, `installment_count`, `down_payment_amount`, `first_due_date`, `frequency`, and `expected_prosthesis_case_version`, and optionally `pay_in_full`, which defaults to false. `installment_count` must be at least 2 and at most 12. `frequency` is `MONTHLY` or `BIWEEKLY`. Both `patient_id` and `case_id` are checked for existence at rung 6 and for nothing beyond it: the two are not cross-checked against each other, so the plan is written with whatever `patient_id` the body carried even where the case belongs to someone else — and it is *that* value the ownership rung and the write gate then read. The case's `case_status` is not consulted either, so a plan may be struck on a case in any state, including `CANCELLED`.

The sequence of checks and effects at creation is pinned, because the down-payment floor and the discount interact. The floor is checked **first**, and it is checked against the **pre-discount** responsibility: `down_payment_amount` must be at least 0.20 times the case's `patient_responsibility_amount` as it stands before any prompt-pay discount is struck, and a smaller down payment is a rung 7 failure carrying `ERR_VALIDATION`. Only once that check has passed is the prompt-pay discount applied to the case — steps 7 and 8 above, which now see a live plan with `pay_in_full` true — and only then is the plan's `principal_amount` struck as the resulting `patient_responsibility_amount` minus `down_payment_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The floor is the only bound on the down payment: nothing caps it at the responsibility figure, so a down payment larger than what is owed yields a negative `principal_amount` and a schedule of negative installments rather than a refusal. The plan row is minted before its `principal_amount` is struck and its schedule laid out, but all of that is one request, so the plan comes back at `version` 1 and each installment at `version` 1.

Creating a plan also **supersedes**. Any existing plan on the same case whose `plan_status` is `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, has its `superseded_by_plan_id` set to the new plan's identifier, and has every one of its own installments still sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `MISSED` moved to `WAIVED`; where the superseded plan was `DELINQUENT`, its `missed_count` is recomputed to zero and the patient's `write_gate_active` falls back to false unless some other plan of that patient is still `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULTED` — both of those being derived readings that follow from the waivers rather than separate writes, so neither the plan nor the patient takes a `version` increment on their account beyond the one the supersede itself carries. Each superseded plan advances by 1 and each waived installment advances by 1. **The case's `version` increments by 1 on every successful payment-plan create**, whether or not a prior plan was there to supersede, exactly as the parent arch advances on every lab-case create in §3.5. This supersede is what `expected_prosthesis_case_version` guards, as §2.8 explains.

**Minting the schedule** follows a rounding order that is easy to get wrong. The per-installment base is `principal_amount` divided by `installment_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Every installment from the first up to the second-to-last carries exactly that base as its `amount`. The final installment — the one whose `sequence_number` equals `installment_count` — carries `principal_amount` minus the base multiplied by one less than `installment_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is the only row whose `is_residual_installment` is true. Summing first and rounding once at the end produces a different schedule and is wrong here. A principal of 1000.00 over 3 installments therefore yields 333.33, 333.33, and 333.34.

Due dates are struck from `first_due_date`: installment number *k* falls due at `first_due_date` plus one less than *k* multiplied by the spacing, where the spacing is 30 days for a `MONTHLY` plan and 14 days for a `BIWEEKLY` plan. Each installment is born in `SCHEDULED` with `paid_at` null. The plan itself is born in `ACTIVE` with `paid_amount` at 0.00, `outstanding_amount` equal to `principal_amount`, `missed_count` at zero, `applied_lab_credit_amount` at 0.00, and `superseded_by_plan_id` null. Throughout the plan's life, `paid_amount` is the sum of `amount` over its installments in `PAID` plus the sum over those in `WAIVED`, and `outstanding_amount` is `principal_amount` minus `paid_amount`. Plans can be listed and filtered by `patient_id`, by `case_id`, by `superseded_by_plan_id`, and by `plan_status`; installments by `payment_plan_id` and by `installment_status`.

**Paying** an installment is available to the owning `Patient` and to a `FrontOffice` operator, and it sits outside the write gate for the obvious reason: gating the cure would make the gate unrecoverable. The payer supplies `amount_paid`, which must be strictly greater than zero and must equal the installment's `amount` to the cent; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A payment moves the row `SCHEDULED → PAID` or `MISSED → PAID`, stamps `paid_at`, raises the plan's `paid_amount` by the installment's `amount`, lowers its `outstanding_amount` by the same, and increments the installment's `version` by 1. Because `paid_amount`, `outstanding_amount` and `missed_count` are all derived from the installments rather than stored on the plan, those three move without the plan being written: **a payment that changes no plan status leaves the plan's `version` and `updated_at` exactly where they were**, and the installment's is the only increment the call produces. The plan advances by 1 only when the payment also moves its `plan_status`. Paying a row that was `MISSED` additionally lowers the plan's `missed_count` by one, and when `missed_count` reaches zero the plan **cures** back from `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULTED` to `ACTIVE` — which sets the patient's `write_gate_active` to false unless another plan of that patient is still delinquent or defaulted, lowering the global write gate. When `outstanding_amount` reaches 0.00 the plan moves to `PAID_OFF`; from `DELINQUENT` that move additionally requires `missed_count` to be zero, and it likewise lowers the write gate where no other broken plan remains. A single payment may do both — cure a plan and then settle it — and still costs the plan one increment in total. The payment is also one of the four moments at which case closure is evaluated. The amount checks sit at rung 7 and therefore *above* the state rungs, so a payment carrying the wrong figure against an already-waived installment reports `ERR_VALIDATION`, not `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

**Reversing** a posted payment belongs to the `FrontOffice`, sits outside the write gate so a mis-posted payment can be undone even on a gated account, and is the declared compensating action of the payment-plan conservation. It is available from `PAID`, and the destination is decided by where the logical clock sits relative to the grace boundary rather than by anything the caller says: when the clock is **at or before** the installment's `due_date` plus 10 days the row returns to `SCHEDULED`, and when the clock is **strictly after** that instant the row goes to `MISSED` instead and the plan's `missed_count` rises by one, which will re-raise the write gate through the plan's delinquency transition at the next sweep. Either way the plan's `paid_amount` falls by the installment's `amount`, its `outstanding_amount` rises by the same, `paid_at` is cleared back to null, and the installment's `version` increments by 1. The plan itself is not written at all: **`plan_status` is left exactly as it was, and the plan's `version` and `updated_at` do not move**, which is precisely why the re-raising of the gate has to wait for the next sweep. That has a consequence worth stating plainly: reversing a payment on a plan that had already reached `PAID_OFF` leaves it in `PAID_OFF` with a non-zero `outstanding_amount`, and since `PAID_OFF` is a terminal state of the plan axis the sweep can no longer touch it — the reversal must be cured by paying the installment again rather than by any plan-level action. Case closure is not evaluated here, so a case closed off the back of that plan stays closed.

**The delinquency sweep** is what raises and lowers the gate, so it necessarily sits outside the gate itself. A `PlatformAdmin` or `FrontOffice` operator runs it against one plan. It walks that plan's installments in `SCHEDULED` only — rows in `PAID` and `WAIVED` are never swept — and moves to `MISSED` every row whose `due_date` plus 10 days is **strictly before** the logical clock. The grace boundary is strict on that side: an installment observed at exactly 10 days after its `due_date` is still on time and is not swept. Each swept row increments the plan's `missed_count` by one and its own `version` by 1. The plan's `missed_count` is then re-derived as the count of its installments sitting in `MISSED`, and the plan's status follows from it: at 3 or more the plan is `DEFAULTED`; at 1 or 2 it is `DELINQUENT`; at zero the plan's status is left exactly as it was, so the sweep can raise a plan's severity but never lower it to `ACTIVE`. A sweep that leaves the plan `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULTED` sets the patient's `write_gate_active` to true. The declared movements are `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT`, `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED`, and `DELINQUENT → DEFAULTED`; because the status is re-derived from the count rather than stepped through those movements, a `DEFAULTED` plan whose count has since fallen to 1 or 2 — a credit re-strike having waived a missed row away — is walked **back** to `DELINQUENT` by the next sweep. And the sweep increments the plan's `version` by exactly 1 **unconditionally**: a sweep that finds nothing past its grace boundary, moves no installment and changes no status still advances the plan by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`, which is what makes the sweep the one endpoint whose repetition is observable on the row it targets.

**Re-striking a schedule after a laboratory credit** is the last piece, and it is the fifth link of the remake cascade of §3.7. The amount actually applied is the lesser of the warranty claim's `lab_credit_amount` and the laboratory's `credit_available_balance`. The plan's `principal_amount` falls by that amount and its `applied_lab_credit_amount` rises by it, both rounded half up to two decimal places; on the laboratory side `credit_applied_total` rises by the same amount and `credit_available_balance` falls by it. The credit is then consumed against the plan's still-open installments — those in `SCHEDULED` or `MISSED`, with rows in `PAID` and `WAIVED` excluded — **processed in descending `sequence_number` order, from the last open row backwards, so that the earliest due dates are preserved**. `sequence_number` is unique within a plan, so no tie-break is needed. Each row absorbs the lesser of its own `amount` and whatever credit remains: its `amount` falls by that much, rounded half up to two decimal places, the remaining credit falls by the same, and a row driven to 0.00 moves to `WAIVED`. A row waived out of `SCHEDULED` simply counts as satisfied in the plan's `paid_amount`; a row waived out of `MISSED` counts as satisfied and additionally lowers `missed_count` by one. Finally the plan's `outstanding_amount` is re-derived as `principal_amount` minus `paid_amount`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and a plan whose `outstanding_amount` reaches 0.00 moves to `PAID_OFF` — from `ACTIVE` or from `DELINQUENT`, and from nowhere else. The version accounting of the re-strike is: **1** on the plan, whether it merely re-principals or also settles; **1** on each installment that actually absorbed some credit, and **none at all** on the open rows the credit ran out before reaching, which keep their amounts, their statuses and their versions untouched.

`PAID_OFF` and `SUPERSEDED` are the two terminal states of the plan axis; `WAIVED` is the only terminal state of the installment axis, since `PAID` must remain reversible and `MISSED` must remain curable.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The status code follows the **response body**, not the side effects: an endpoint answers with HTTP 201 exactly when the resource it returns is one it has just minted, and with HTTP 200 in every other case — every read, every endpoint that modifies an existing resource, and every endpoint that returns a row named in its own path. That last clause matters, because three endpoints do mint rows and still answer 200, since the body they return is the pre-existing row rather than the new one: `POST /prosthetics/impressions/{impression_id}/request-retake` mints the successor record but answers with the superseded record; `POST /prosthetics/try-ins/{try_in_id}/decide` on a rejecting decision mints a remake request but answers with the milestone; and `POST /prosthetics/prostheses/{prosthesis_id}/recall-complete` mints a service or a remake request but answers with the appliance. Conversely, an endpoint answering 201 may have minted more than the one row it returns — a delivery returns the appliance at 201 while also minting a claim, and a service request returns the service at 201 while also minting a lab case. Exactly fifteen endpoints answer 201: the eleven plain creates (patient, coverage, laboratory, case, arch, impression, try-in, lab case, warranty claim, predetermination, claim) plus delivery, service request, remake request and payment plan. The clock-setting endpoint answers 200 as well, since it creates no resource and its body is a bare `current_utc` rather than a resource projection.
