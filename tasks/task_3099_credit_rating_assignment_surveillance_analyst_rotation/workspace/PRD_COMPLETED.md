# Credit Rating Agency API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A credit rating agency lives or dies on the credibility of its opinions. This document specifies the rating-agency service that carries a rating from the moment an issuer is registered through the solicitation of an engagement, the assembly and conflict-screening of an analytical team, the convening and voting of a rating committee, publication with rationale and watch designations, and the long surveillance tail that follows every published opinion. The service is mounted under `/rating-agency`, and every workflow it exposes is designed to leave an auditable, reproducible trail.

The domain is shaped by two forces that pull in opposite directions. Commercially, the agency must intake engagements, allocate fees across instruments, and keep issuers moving through the pipeline quickly. Analytically and regulatorily, the agency must keep the people who decide ratings insulated from the people who sell engagements: analysts rotate off long-standing leads, conflicts are screened before a team is confirmed, appeals are admissible only on genuinely new information, and compensation never varies with fee revenue. The service therefore models segregation of duties as a first-class concern rather than an afterthought — separate roles own commercial intake, compliance screening, committee decisions, surveillance work, and compensation, and no single actor can carry a rating from intake to publication alone.

Because rating opinions change and are re-examined, the pipeline is deliberately cyclic rather than a straight line: a screening can be sent back for re-intake, a decision can be reopened by an accepted appeal and re-decided, a committee can be reopened, a completed surveillance review can be reopened on the strength of a withdrawal event, and an appearance reconciliation can cycle between a confirmed breach and the analyst's own appeal. Where several writers can act on the same aggregate, optimistic concurrency control protects the pipeline from a client overwriting state it has not observed. The remainder of this document describes the response contract and cross-cutting principles first, then the business workflows, then the endpoint and error catalogues.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Identity, Authentication and Segregation of Duties

Every request carries two headers that together establish who is acting and in what capacity. `X-User-Role` names the acting role and must be present and must name one of the roles declared below; when either header is missing or malformed the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). When the role is present but the operation is not one the role is permitted to perform — including a request whose `X-User-ID` does not satisfy an entity-backed ownership check — the request is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS` (HTTP 403).

The declared roles are `EngagementManager` (commercial owner of engagement letters and request intake, who may never vote or decide), `RatingAnalyst` (analytical staff who serve on teams, vote, and work surveillance, and who are forbidden from commercial and committee-chair operations), `ComplianceOfficer` (conflict screening, methodology publication, rotation and look-back compliance, who never decides ratings), `CommitteeChair` (who convenes committees, proposes teams, and publishes ratings, but cannot screen conflicts or file appeals), `CompensationCommittee` (who owns analytical compensation separation from fee generation), `Anonymous` (an unauthenticated issuer-side filer who may only file an appeal against a published-pending decision), and `PlatformAdmin` (an operational role owning the logical clock and administrative reads, with no rating decisions).

Role identity resolves differently per role, and this matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `RatingAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Analyst`; any check on a resource that references an `Analyst` compares that resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. For every other role — `EngagementManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `CommitteeChair`, `CompensationCommittee`, `Anonymous` and `PlatformAdmin` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for instance as `reviewer_user_id` on a `ConflictScreening`, as `filer_user_id` on an `IssuerAppeal`, or as the recorder of a `LookBackReview`) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. A missing or invalid `X-User-Role`, and an operation attempted by a role outside its permitted set, are still rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS` respectively regardless of whether the role is entity-backed.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. List Retrieval, Pagination and Ordering

List endpoints are page-addressed by two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned and defaults to 10, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the zero-based number of records to skip and defaults to 0; the lowest admissible value is 0. A non-integer `limit` reads as the default and a non-integer or negative `offset` reads as 0; a `limit` outside its range is clamped into it (below the minimum to 1, above the maximum to 100) rather than refused. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. Every list endpoint accepts query-parameter filters over its own record fields — an unrecognised parameter is ignored rather than refused — and when a filter is supplied the filtered set is still ordered ascending by `created_at`. Unless a caller supplies a filter, records are returned in ascending `created_at` order, with ties broken by the rows' insertion order.

### 2.4. Addressable Resources and Path Resolution

Resources are reached through a left-to-right hierarchy of identifiers, and path parameters are validated in the order they appear in the URL: a request first resolves the top-level resource, then any nested resource beneath it, and only then evaluates business rules. If any identifier in the path fails to resolve to a row, the request is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Because the sweep covers every path segment before any business rule is consulted, a request naming a nonexistent parent fails on the parent and never on a child-level check. Once every path resource has resolved, the state guard runs: an action endpoint whose target resource is not in the state the transition requires is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and only after that guard passes are the transition-specific business rules evaluated.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Entity Shapes

Most persisted resources share a common shape. Their `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps carrying three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of the row. Two collections are the exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`: a `ConflictScreening` has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and a `RatingActionEvent` likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`. A `CommitteeVote` is the same shape — `id` and `created_at` only, no `updated_at` and no `version`.

An `EngagementLetter`, `RatingRequest`, `Analyst`, `AnalyticalTeamAssignment`, `RatingCommittee`, `RatingDecision`, `IssuerAppeal`, `SurveillanceReview`, `AppearanceReconciliation`, `ReviewSlot`, `Methodology`, `AnalystCompensation`, `LookBackReview` and `RatedEntity` all carry the full common shape. The `RatedEntity` row is the anchor of the model: it exposes `legal_name`, `sector`, `domicile_country`, `revenue_band`, `methodology_id`, `solicitation_status`, `surveillance_posture`, `first_rated_at` and `current_grade`, and its `solicitation_status` defaults to `SOLICITED` while `surveillance_posture` defaults to `NORMAL`. A `RatedEntity` is registered once and its `current_grade` stays null until its first `PUBLISHED` decision records a grade, at which point `first_rated_at` is stamped. Its `revenue_band` is one of `LARGE`, `MID` or `SMALL`; registration must name a `methodology_id` that resolves to an existing row, or it is rejected with `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

### 2.6. Precision, Rounding and Conserved Quantities

Monetary amounts are stored as integer cents. Money derived from a formula is produced with half-up rounding to whole cents, and any score or ratio that is expressed as a number is rounded half-up to the decimal places named in the workflow that computes it — `dissent_score` carries 2 decimal places and `compensation_separation_pct` carries 4 decimal places. Where a calculation names a rounding order (round the per-item value first, then aggregate), that order is normative and not interchangeable with aggregating raw values and rounding once. Several quantities are conservative in the accounting sense and must balance exactly at every state change: an engagement's credited and reversed fee allocations reconcile against the contracted `fee_amount_cents`, and a compensation record's `total_compensation_cents` is the sum of its parts at all times.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Rating Request

Two write paths are protected against a client acting on a stale view of its parent `RatingRequest`. Confirmation of an analytical team, and publication of a rating decision, each require the request to carry a field naming the client's last observed request version: the confirmation call must carry `expected_rating_request_version`, and the publication call must likewise carry `expected_rating_request_version`. When the supplied value no longer matches the `RatingRequest`'s current `version`, the operation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no state is changed. A request's `version` advances during an accepted appeal round, so a confirmation or publication payload assembled before that round is stale by construction. When the version field is omitted altogether it is not compared and the operation proceeds against the request's current state. No endpoint outside these two takes a version field in its payload.

### 2.8. Effective Dating and the Logical Clock

The service does not consult wall-clock time for its business dating. A single logical clock governs every deadline and every temporal gate, and its startup default is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`; until an operator advances it, all date comparisons read that instant. The clock is advanced by an administrative capability that accepts a `current_utc` value. Because deadlines such as a `review_due_at` or an `effective_at` are compared against the logical clock, moving the clock is how a test-drive advances a review into `OVERDUE` or a rating action into an applicable state, and every derived day-count is recomputed on the next read after the clock moves. A rating action's `effective_at` may legitimately sit in the future of the clock, and it stays inapplicable until the clock reaches it.

### 2.9. Methodology Pinning and Public Disclosure

A `Methodology` is authored as a draft and moves through `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` in its `status` field; each version is identified by a human-readable `name` and a `version_code` label alongside its globally unique `methodology_code`. Publishing stamps `effective_from` and makes the version citable; superseding stamps `effective_to`. Publishing is rejected with `ERR_METHODOLOGY_CODE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) when another `PUBLISHED` methodology already claims that `methodology_code` in the same `sector`. Publishing a version that is not `DRAFT`, disclosing a version that is not `PUBLISHED`, and superseding a version that is not `PUBLISHED` are each rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Superseding is rejected with `ERR_NO_REPLACEMENT_METHODOLOGY` (HTTP 409) unless a replacement methodology for the same `sector` has already reached `PUBLISHED`. When an issuer is pinned to a `methodology_id`, that pin is version-stable: superseding the methodology does not move existing issuers off it, and only future pins are affected. Public disclosure is a separate flag, `is_public`, which is false until explicitly disclosed for a published version; disclosing an already-public version is rejected with `ERR_METHODOLOGY_ALREADY_PUBLIC` (HTTP 409).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Engagement Letters, Fee Arrangements and the Cooling-Off Period

Commercial intake begins when an `EngagementManager` drafts an engagement letter for an issuer, supplying the `rated_entity_id`, the contracted `fee_amount_cents`, the `fee_structure`, whether the payer is the issuer via `fee_payer_is_issuer`, and the countersignature instant `signed_at`. A `rated_entity_id` that names no registered issuer is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The `fee_amount_cents` must be a non-negative integer; a negative amount is rejected with `ERR_FEE_AMOUNT_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 400). The `fee_structure` is one of `FLAT_ANNUAL`, `MILESTONE`, or `TRANSACTION`, and the letter is created in `DRAFT`. It also carries `cooling_off_end_at`, `fee_credited_cents` and `fee_reversed_cents`; the credited and reversed totals begin at zero and move only as requests against the engagement are opened, returned, or terminated.

Submitting the drafted letter for signature takes it to `SIGNED`, but only from `DRAFT` and only when the letter is not already `TERMINATED` — an attempt against a terminated letter is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409) and a submit against a letter that is neither `DRAFT` nor `TERMINATED` is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The submission stamps `cooling_off_end_at` at exactly ninety days after `signed_at`. That ninety-day cooling-off period is a hard gate on publication: no rating may be published while the cooling-off window is still open. Activation, which takes the letter from `SIGNED` to `ACTIVE`, enforces two conditions. The letter must actually be in `SIGNED`, or the call is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_SIGNED` (HTTP 409), and the cooling-off period must have elapsed according to the logical clock — the clock must have reached `cooling_off_end_at`, since the ending instant itself is admissible — or the call is rejected with `ERR_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Activation against an already-`TERMINATED` letter reports `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409) rather than the non-`SIGNED` state error. Only an `ACTIVE` letter unlocks `RatingRequest` intake under that engagement.

Before any request exists under the engagement, a signed letter may be reverted to `DRAFT`. The revert is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409) as soon as a `RatingRequest` exists under that `engagement_letter_id`, and that precondition is evaluated before the letter's `SIGNED`-state requirement — so an `ACTIVE` letter carrying a request reports the terminal error rather than a bare non-`SIGNED` state — while a letter that is not `SIGNED` and carries no request is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A revert against a `TERMINATED` letter also reports `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). On success the revert clears `cooling_off_end_at` back to null and reverses the cooling-off clock advance. Termination of an `ACTIVE` letter is where the model's deepest cascade lives. Termination requires the letter to be `ACTIVE`, or it is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), and once it succeeds the letter becomes `TERMINATED` and `fee_reversed_cents` is set equal to `fee_credited_cents`. Every `RatingRequest` under the engagement whose `request_status` is not `PUBLISHED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; every `AnalyticalTeamAssignment` on those withdrawn requests moves from `PROPOSED`, `SCREENED` or `CONFIRMED` to `REVOKED` and, because those rows revoke, each affected analyst's `consecutive_lead_months` recomputes over the lead's surviving rows so that lead credit for the vanished window is removed. As a parallel branch, every `RatingCommittee` for a withdrawn request that sits in `CONVENED` or `IN_SESSION` moves to `DISSOLVED`, and each `RatingDecision` still in `DRAFT` for such a request moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Existing `SurveillanceReview` rows for an entity that never published are untouched, but newly created reviews for such an entity are blocked by the entity's `surveillance_posture`. This is a depth-four transitive cascade running `EngagementLetter` → `RatingRequest` → `AnalyticalTeamAssignment` → `Analyst`, with a parallel committee-to-decision branch.

### 3.2. Request Intake, Screening and the Fee Allocation Waterfall

A rating request is submitted under an engagement by supplying the `engagement_letter_id`, the `rated_entity_id`, the `instrument_type`, the `instrument_notional_cents`, and the `requested_rating_scale`. The `instrument_type` is one of `SENIOR_UNSECURED`, `SUBORDINATED`, `SHORT_TERM`, or `REVOLVER`, and the `requested_rating_scale` is one of `GLOBAL_LONG_TERM`, `GLOBAL_SHORT_TERM`, or `NATIONAL_SCALE`. The intake payload must carry every one of the fields named above; an intake that omits any of them is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400) before anything is persisted. Intake requires the referenced engagement letter to resolve; an unresolvable one is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a `rated_entity_id` that does not resolve is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The `received_at` timestamp is stamped by the server, and the request starts in `SUBMITTED` with its `allocated_fee_cents` at zero until the screening stage computes it.

Opening the compliance screening stage moves the request from `SUBMITTED` to `SCREENING` and does two things at once. First, it validates that the parent engagement is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) — and that the issuer's pinned methodology resolves to a row that has reached `PUBLISHED`, since a pin that resolves to nothing is rejected with `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) while one that resolves to a non-published version is rejected with `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). A request that is not `SUBMITTED` is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Second, it computes the request's `allocated_fee_cents`, its share of the engagement fee under a waterfall that spans every request on the engagement. The denominator of that waterfall is the sum of the `instrument_notional_cents` over all `RatingRequest` rows that share the same `engagement_letter_id`, at every `request_status` including `WITHDRAWN` rows, and the row being opened is included in its own denominator. The base share is the engagement's `fee_amount_cents` multiplied by this request's `instrument_notional_cents` and divided by that denominator.

The base share is then adjusted by a size tier. When the base share is below 2,500,000 cents the multiplier is the full 1.00; when the base share is at least 2,500,000 cents but below 10,000,000 cents the multiplier is 0.95; and when the base share reaches 10,000,000 cents or more the multiplier is 0.90. The raw allocated amount is the base share multiplied by the tier multiplier, and `allocated_fee_cents` is that raw amount rounded half-up to whole cents — rounded per request, before any summing. The engagement's `fee_credited_cents` is the sum of `allocated_fee_cents` across the letter's requests. The pinned order matters: round each request's own share first and sum afterward, never sum the raw unrounded shares and round once, because with two or more requests the two orders can differ by a cent, whereas a single-request engagement collapses the distinction entirely. The allocation is also capped: it may never push the engagement's `fee_credited_cents` above its `fee_amount_cents`, and a request whose allocation would exceed the uncredited remainder is rejected with `ERR_FEE_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_ENGAGEMENT` (HTTP 402).

Screening may be reversed. Returning a request to intake takes it from `SCREENING` back to `SUBMITTED`, requires that it actually sit in `SCREENING` or the call is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SCREENING` (HTTP 409), and it reverses the request's `allocated_fee_cents` into the engagement's `fee_reversed_cents` while resetting `allocated_fee_cents` to zero. Because this route exists, a request can be bounced between `SUBMITTED` and `SCREENING` as often as intake needs without being abandoned.

An `EngagementManager` may also withdraw a request outright before it publishes, but only while its `request_status` is still `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING` or `TEAM_CONFIRMED`; a withdraw attempted from any other state is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE` (HTTP 409). A withdrawal moves the request to `WITHDRAWN`, reverses its allocation into the engagement's `fee_reversed_cents`, revokes every `AnalyticalTeamAssignment` on the request so that each affected analyst's lead credit is recomputed, and dissolves any `CONVENED` or `IN_SESSION` committee for the request while withdrawing any `DRAFT` decision attached to it.

The request only advances to `TEAM_CONFIRMED` once the analytical branch has also cleared, which is described next.

### 3.3. Analytical Team Proposal, Conflict Screening and the Rotation Rules

Analytical staffing starts on the roster. A `ComplianceOfficer` onboards an analyst by recording the `full_name`, the `primary_sector`, the `analyst_tier`, and the `independence_declared_at` timestamp of the most recent signed independence declaration. Both `primary_sector` and the issuer's `sector` draw on the same five-value classification: `CORPORATE_FINANCE`, `FINANCIAL_INSTITUTIONS`, `SOVEREIGN`, `STRUCTURED_FINANCE`, or `PROJECT_FINANCE`. The `analyst_tier` is one of `LEAD`, `SUPPORT`, or `COMMITTEE_ELIGIBLE`, and it governs what the analyst may do — only a `LEAD` may be named as a team lead, and only a `COMMITTEE_ELIGIBLE` analyst may cast a committee vote. An onboarded analyst starts with `rotation_eligible` true, `is_departed` false, `first_assignment_at` and `last_lead_assignment_at` null, and `consecutive_lead_months` at zero. When an analyst later leaves the agency the departure is recorded against the analyst, setting `is_departed` true and the destination `departed_to_entity_id`, and it also records the `departure_date` the departure names; that date is what the look-back window is measured from and it is never part of the analyst's serialised shape.

An analytical team is proposed for a request by a `CommitteeChair`, supplying the `rating_request_id`, the `lead_analyst_id`, the `support_analyst_id`, and the occupancy window through `window_start` and `window_end`. The proposal carries an optional `rotation_exemption_granted` flag and, when that flag is set, a `rotation_exemption_reason`. A `lead_analyst_id` or `support_analyst_id` that names no analyst row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The named lead must hold the `LEAD` tier, or the proposal is rejected with `ERR_ANALYST_NOT_LEAD` (HTTP 400). A granted exemption without a reason of at least twenty characters is rejected with `ERR_ROTATION_EXEMPTION_REASON_REQUIRED` (HTTP 400). The proposal is created in `PROPOSED` with `conflict_status` of `PENDING_SCREEN`, and its `rotation_exemption_granted` defaults to false. Its `status` walks the assignment lifecycle `PROPOSED`, `SCREENED`, `CONFIRMED`, `REVOKED`, and its `assigned_at` timestamp is stamped by the server when the proposal is recorded — the `window_start` and `window_end` it carries describe the occupancy interval the team would hold, with `window_start` inclusive and `window_end` exclusive.

Running the conflict screen is where the analytical and compliance views are joined, and its checks are evaluated in a fixed order so that an earlier failure is not masked by a later one. The screen first confirms that the request's issuer is pinned to a `PUBLISHED` methodology, because that gate must run before any analyst conflict check; a stale or draft pin yields `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). Only then does it examine the analysts. Each of the lead and the support must have at least one `ConflictScreening` row whose `screening_result` is not `DISQUALIFYING`, or the screen is rejected with `ERR_CONFLICT_SCREENING_DISQUALIFYING` (HTTP 409). The lead and the support must be different people, or the screen is rejected with `ERR_DUAL_ROLE_ASSIGNMENT` (HTTP 400). No team member may have departed to a rated issuer within the last twenty-four months, or the screen is rejected with `ERR_LOOKBACK_WINDOW_BLOCKING` (HTTP 409). Finally, the lead's own sector must match the request entity's `sector`, or a sector waiver must be on record, or the screen is rejected with `ERR_SECTOR_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). Running the screen requires the assignment to be `PROPOSED`, or it is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). When the screen succeeds the assignment becomes `SCREENED`, `conflict_status` is recomputed over the assignment's screening rows by taking the most severe result present, ordering `DISQUALIFYING` above `POTENTIAL` above `CLEAR`, and the lead's `first_assignment_at` is set if it was still null. A screened assignment may be sent back to `PROPOSED`, which requires it to be `SCREENED` or be rejected with `ERR_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SCREENED` (HTTP 409), and which resets `conflict_status` to `PENDING_SCREEN` so a different analyst pair can be tried.

A `ConflictScreening` row is itself a recorded artifact: it names the `team_assignment_id` and `analyst_id` it covers, the `conflict_type`, the `screening_result`, the opaque `reviewer_user_id` drawn from `X-User-ID`, and a server-stamped `screened_at`. A `team_assignment_id` or `analyst_id` that names no row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The `conflict_type` is one of `NONE`, `PRIOR_EMPLOYMENT`, `FAMILY_TIE`, `SECURITY_HOLDING`, or `FEE_DEPENDENCE`, and the `screening_result` is one of `CLEAR`, `POTENTIAL`, or `DISQUALIFYING`. These rows are how a compliance decision is attributed to a named reviewer, and a screening row is immutable once written.

Confirmation by a `CommitteeChair` is the point at which rotation becomes real, and it takes the assignment from `SCREENED` to `CONFIRMED`. Confirmation requires the assignment to be `SCREENED` or it is rejected with `ERR_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SCREENED` (HTTP 409); it requires `conflict_status` to be `CLEAR` or it is rejected with `ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 409); and it enforces the rotation rule. The lead's `consecutive_lead_months` is the number of whole thirty-day months in the current unbroken lead streak, floored to an integer. On every confirmation and every revocation the streak is recomputed by walking the lead's assignments in `assigned_at` ascending order; because `assigned_at` has one-second resolution, ties are ordinarily impossible, and on the rare tie the ordering falls back to `created_at` ascending rather than to `id`. The start of the streak is the earliest `assigned_at` among rows whose status is not `REVOKED`; the end is the latest `window_start` among `CONFIRMED` rows, or the current instant when any `CONFIRMED` row is open-ended. A `REVOKED` row neither extends the end bound nor resets the start bound. The difference between end and start is divided by thirty days and floored to a whole number of months. Confirmation is refused with `ERR_ANALYST_ROTATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409) when the lead's `consecutive_lead_months` has reached sixty months — the boundary is inclusive, so exactly sixty months is not eligible — unless the assignment carries a granted rotation exemption. A granted exemption suppresses the rejection for that confirmation but does not reset the streak, so the months keep accruing underneath it. Confirmation also requires the lead's new window to overlap no other non-`REVOKED` assignment of that same analyst, or it is rejected with `ERR_ANALYST_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). On success the lead's `last_lead_assignment_at` is set to the assignment instant and `consecutive_lead_months` is updated, and once that streak reaches sixty months the lead's `rotation_eligible` flips to false.

Revoking an assignment is available to a `ComplianceOfficer` from any of `PROPOSED`, `SCREENED` or `CONFIRMED`, and a revoke against an assignment already `REVOKED` is rejected with `ERR_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_REVOKED` (HTTP 409). A `revocation_reason` accompanies the revoke. Revoking a `CONFIRMED` assignment recomputes the lead's `consecutive_lead_months` over the surviving `SCREENED` and `CONFIRMED` rows, and when the shortened streak drops below sixty months the lead's `rotation_eligible` returns to true. If the revoked assignment had been `CONFIRMED` while its request was still `TEAM_CONFIRMED`, the parent request returns to `SCREENING` and its committee is dissolved. `CONFIRMED` is deliberately not a terminal state precisely because a withdrawal or a termination can revoke it.

### 3.4. Team Confirmation, Committee Convening and Quorum

Advancing the request to `TEAM_CONFIRMED` is a `ComplianceOfficer` action that requires the request to be `SCREENING` — otherwise the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and requires a `CONFIRMED` assignment on the request, or it is rejected with `ERR_TEAM_NOT_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 409), and that assignment's `conflict_status` must be `CLEAR`, or it is rejected with `ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 409). The request may sit at `SCREENING` with a `CONFIRMED`, `CLEAR` team and still not be decidable until this join occurs, which is why a decision attempted while the team branch is still `PROPOSED` or `SCREENED` finds no decidable request and is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_TEAM_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 409). Confirmation of the team is also one of the two write paths protected by optimistic concurrency: it must carry `expected_rating_request_version` matching the parent request's current `version`, or it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and on success the parent request's `version` increments by one.

A `CommitteeChair` convenes a rating committee for a request by supplying the `rating_request_id`, the `committee_date`, and the `required_voting_members` quorum size. The quorum size is an integer between 2 and 9 inclusive, and a `rating_request_id` that names no request is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The committee begins in `CONVENED` with `votes_cast` at zero, and it carries a `quorum_met` flag that is true only once `votes_cast` has reached `required_voting_members`, a `dissent_count`, and a `committee_status`. Opening the session takes it from `CONVENED` to `IN_SESSION`; a committee that is neither `CONVENED` nor `IN_SESSION` — including one already `DECIDED`, `DISSOLVED` or `QUORUM_FAILED` — is rejected with `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_IN_SESSION` (HTTP 409), the same error the decision routes report for a committee that is not decidable. It also enforces that the `committee_date` has been reached according to the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409) — and enforces that the sitting interval overlaps no other `CONVENED` or `IN_SESSION` committee, otherwise `ERR_COMMITTEE_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The session interval opens at `committee_date`.

Votes are cast by `RatingAnalyst` members while the committee is in session. Casting a vote requires the committee to be `IN_SESSION`, or it is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Each vote names the committee it belongs to through `rating_committee_id`, the `analyst_id`, the `vote_value`, and — unless the vote is an abstention — the `assigned_grade` the voter assigns; an `analyst_id` that names no analyst row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); a `dissent_rationale` is required when the vote differs from the eventual committee majority and must run at least forty characters. The `vote_value` is one of `UPGRADE`, `AFFIRM`, `DOWNGRADE`, or `ABSTAIN`. A voter must hold the `COMMITTEE_ELIGIBLE` tier and must not have already voted on this committee; a repeat or ineligible vote is rejected with `ERR_VOTE_NOT_ELIGIBLE_MEMBER` (HTTP 409). Casting a vote increments the committee's `votes_cast` and recomputes `quorum_met`, and the vote records its `voted_at` instant, which is the chronological key on which vote ordering rests. If the committee reaches its `committee_date` with `votes_cast` still short of `required_voting_members`, the system automatically moves it from `CONVENED` to `QUORUM_FAILED` and returns the parent request to `SCREENING`, at which point a replacement committee may be convened. This sweep is evaluated whenever the committee is read or listed, so a committee that has reached its date with the quorum still unmet is observed as `QUORUM_FAILED` on its next read.

### 3.5. Committee Decisions, Dissent Scoring and the Publication Discipline

Closing the vote and deciding is a `CommitteeChair` action that requires the committee to be `CONVENED` or `IN_SESSION` — otherwise `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_IN_SESSION` (HTTP 409) — the parent request to be `TEAM_CONFIRMED` or `APPEALED` — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_TEAM_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 409) — and requires `quorum_met` to be true, or it is rejected with `ERR_COMMITTEE_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409). No abstention may be outstanding when the committee decides; an outstanding `ABSTAIN` vote blocks the decision with `ERR_COMMITTEE_ABSTENTION_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 409). Every appearing voter must hold an independence declaration dated within the last 180 days, or the decision is rejected with `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_DECLARATION_STALE` (HTTP 409). The deciding payload carries the `assigned_grade`, the `outlook`, the `watch_status`, and the `rationale`; the `outlook` is one of `POSITIVE`, `STABLE`, `NEGATIVE`, or `DEVELOPING`, the `watch_status` is one of `NO_WATCH`, `WATCH_UPGRADE`, or `WATCH_DOWNGRADE`, and the `rationale` must run at least 200 characters or the call is rejected with `ERR_RATIONALE_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 400).

A successful decision creates a `RatingDecision` in `DRAFT` and moves the committee to `DECIDED` and the parent request to `COMMITTEE_DECIDED`. The new decision records its `assigned_grade` on the ordered scale `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BAA`, `BA`, `B`, `CAA`, `CA`, `C`, its `prior_grade` as the issuer's `current_grade` at the moment of decision (null on a first rating), its `outlook`, its `watch_status`, its `rationale`, and a `decision_status` of `DRAFT`. It also records a dissent tally. Two values are derived at decision time. The `dissent_count` is simply the number of votes on the committee whose `is_dissent` flag is true. The `dissent_score` is a weighted sum over exactly those dissent votes, and the weighting depends on both the vote's direction and on whether the decision is still internal or is being prepared for publication. While the decision remains internal — that is, in `DRAFT` — every dissent contributes a weight of 2 regardless of direction. Once the decision is being prepared for publication, at `APPEAL_WINDOW` or later, a `DOWNGRADE` dissent carries a weight of 3 while an `UPGRADE` or `AFFIRM` dissent continues to carry a weight of 2. In both modes each dissent's contribution is its weight multiplied by the grade-step distance between that voter's `assigned_grade` and the decision's `assigned_grade`, where the grade-step distance is the number of positions separating the two grades on the ordered scale and is zero when they are equal. The published-mode score therefore exceeds the internal-mode score by exactly the summed grade-step distance of the `DOWNGRADE` dissents, and nothing else. The `dissent_score` is the sum of those contributions rounded half-up to two decimal places; an abstention is never a dissent, and abstentions cannot occur at decision time in any case because they are barred by the decide precondition.

Issuing the rating passes through two distinct gates before anything reaches the market. First, a `CommitteeChair` opens the appeal window on the draft decision, which requires the decision to be `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_DECISION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409) — and the parent request to be `COMMITTEE_DECIDED`, otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_COMMITTEE_DECIDED` (HTTP 409). Opening the window moves the decision to `APPEAL_WINDOW` and records `appeal_window_end_at` four days after the window opens; the decision's `dissent_score` is restated at that point under the publication weighting so the record already carries its published-mode value while it waits, and appeals are admissible while — and only while — the decision remains in `APPEAL_WINDOW`, and the window closes once the decision leaves that state. Publication itself requires the decision to be in `APPEAL_WINDOW`, or it is rejected with `ERR_DECISION_NOT_IN_APPEAL_WINDOW` (HTTP 409). It also enforces the cooling-off gate on the parent engagement's `cooling_off_end_at`, rejecting with `ERR_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) while the ninety-day window is open, and enforces the unsolicited-disclosure gate: unless the issuer's `solicitation_status` is `SOLICITED` or its pinned methodology's `is_public` flag is true, publication is rejected with `ERR_UNSOLICITED_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` (HTTP 409). The rationale must again satisfy the 200-character minimum, or the publication is rejected with `ERR_RATIONALE_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 400).

Publication is the second write path guarded by optimistic concurrency: the publication payload must carry `expected_rating_request_version` matching the request's current `version`, or it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because an accepted appeal reopens the decision and advances the request's `version`, so a publish built on the pre-appeal view of the request is stale by construction. When publication succeeds, the decision moves to `PUBLISHED` and `published_at` is stamped, the issuer's `current_grade` is set to the `assigned_grade`, and `first_rated_at` is stamped if this is the issuer's first publication. The publication also records whether the decision was unsolicited through the `is_unsolicited` flag, which mirrors the issuer's `solicitation_status` being `UNSOLICITED` at decision time, and it fixes the version-pinned `methodology_code` from the issuer's pinned methodology so the published opinion names the exact methodology version it used. Finally, publication has cascading effects on surveillance: a successor `SurveillanceReview` is auto-created for the issuer with its `review_due_at` at the `review_cycle_months` after `published_at`, and the issuer's `surveillance_posture` is recomputed from the published `watch_status`, moving to `ACTIVE_WATCH` when the `watch_status` is `WATCH_UPGRADE` or `WATCH_DOWNGRADE`.

Publication is not the only way a decision settles. Following an accepted appeal the committee may re-decide, replacing the superseded decision: a fresh `RatingDecision` is created in `DRAFT` with the same payload shape as a first decision — `assigned_grade`, `outlook`, `watch_status` and a compliant `rationale` — the superseded decision moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and the parent request returns to `COMMITTEE_DECIDED`. Re-deciding requires the request to be `TEAM_CONFIRMED` on the compliance-and-analytical branch, the committee to be `DECIDED` or `IN_SESSION`, `quorum_met` to be true, and no outstanding abstention, each failure raising its own error as for a first decision. A draft decision that is never published can be withdrawn outright by a `CommitteeChair` — the decision moves to `WITHDRAWN` — and a decision is withdrawn automatically when its parent request is withdrawn or its committee is dissolved. A withdraw against a decision that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

### 3.6. Issuer Appeals, New Information and Appearance Reconciliation

An appeal against a published-pending decision is filed by an `Anonymous` issuer-side filer, who supplies the `filer_user_id` and a `new_information_summary`. The summary must run at least 100 characters and must not restate information already on file. Filing is admissible only while the linked decision is in `APPEAL_WINDOW`; an appeal against a decision that is no longer in that state — for instance one that has already been published — is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The appeal must introduce information absent from the decision's filed materials, or it is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_NEW_INFORMATION` (HTTP 409); the `contains_new_information` flag is recomputed at filing time to record whether that test passed. A filed appeal starts in `PENDING` with `appeal_outcome` unset, and a server-stamped `submitted_at` records its intake instant.

Filing can also be blocked by an appearance problem. When an `AppearanceReconciliation` for the same decision sits in a blocking state — `BREACH_CONFIRMED` or `APPEAL_PENDING` — the appeal is refused with `ERR_APPEALS_BLOCKED_PENDING_RECONCILIATION` (HTTP 409) until that reconciliation is cleared. This is how the model ties an analyst's past appearance to the admissibility of an issuer's appeal on the decision that analyst touched. An `AppearanceReconciliation` is recorded by a `ComplianceOfficer` against an `analyst_id` and a `rating_decision_id`, naming the `stated_role` the analyst actually held (one of `LEAD_ANALYST`, `SUPPORT_ANALYST`, or `COMMITTEE_VOTER`) and an `evidence_reference` of at least ten characters. An `analyst_id` or `rating_decision_id` that names no row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and an `evidence_reference` shorter than ten characters is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Its `reconciliation_status` starts at `PENDING` with `confirmed_at` null. A `CommitteeChair` may confirm a breach, which stamps `confirmed_at`, moves the row to `BREACH_CONFIRMED`, and blocks the linked decision's appeals; the analyst may then move their own case to `APPEAL_PENDING`, which keeps the row blocking while their appeal is examined; and a `CommitteeChair` may clear the row from either `PENDING` or `APPEAL_PENDING`, moving it to `CLEARED` and reopening the decision's appeal admissibility. The cycle `BREACH_CONFIRMED` ↔ `APPEAL_PENDING` can repeat as evidence is re-examined. Both the clear and the confirm-breach actions require the row to be in `PENDING` or `APPEAL_PENDING`, and an action against an already-`CLEARED` row is rejected with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_ALREADY_CLEARED` (HTTP 409); the analyst's appeal against a row that is not `BREACH_CONFIRMED` is rejected with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_BREACHED` (HTTP 409).

Resolving a pending appeal is a `CommitteeChair` action that carries the `appeal_outcome` and `resolution_notes`. The notes must run at least thirty characters, or the resolution is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_RESOLUTION_NOTES_REQUIRED` (HTTP 400); the appeal must still be `PENDING`, or the resolution is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); and the appeal must still be admissible — its linked decision must be in `APPEAL_WINDOW` or `DRAFT`, or the resolution is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409) — and an `ACCEPTED` appeal must carry new information, or it is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_NEW_INFORMATION` (HTTP 409). Resolution takes the outcome to `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`. On `ACCEPTED` three things happen together: the decision returns to `DRAFT`, the committee for the request reopens to `IN_SESSION` so a fresh decision round begins, and the parent request moves to `APPEALED`. On `REJECTED` the decision's appeal window simply continues to run to its normal close and nothing else changes. The accepted route leaves the decision at `COMMITTEE_DECIDED` on the request side only after the committee re-decides, which is the cyclic `APPEALED` → `COMMITTEE_DECIDED` round described earlier; a committee may be reopened only while its request is `APPEALED`, and a reopen attempted otherwise is rejected with `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_REOPENABLE` (HTTP 409). Because the accepted appeal moves the committee to `IN_SESSION` as part of resolving the appeal, an explicit `reopen` against that request while it is `APPEALED` is accepted whether the committee is still `DECIDED` or has already returned to `IN_SESSION`; a reopen is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_REOPENABLE` (HTTP 409) only for a request that is not `APPEALED` or a committee that is on neither of those two states (for example one that is still `CONVENED`).

### 3.7. Surveillance Cadence, Overdue Escalation and Calendar Buffers

Every published opinion enters surveillance immediately. A successor `SurveillanceReview` is auto-created at publication, and additional reviews can be scheduled by a `RatingAnalyst` with a `rated_entity_id`, an optional `rating_decision_id` — a `rated_entity_id` or `rating_decision_id` that names no row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — a `review_type` of either `PERIODIC` or `EVENT_DRIVEN`, and an explicit `review_due_at`. The review's `review_cycle_months` is derived from the issuer's `revenue_band`: a `LARGE` issuer is reviewed every 6 months, a `MID` issuer every 12 months, and a `SMALL` issuer every 24 months. A review begins in `SCHEDULED`.

A scheduled review that passes its due date is swept to `OVERDUE` automatically once the logical clock advances beyond its `review_due_at`, and its `overdue_days` — defined as the whole-day count since the due date rounded half-up to a whole day, never below zero — is recomputed on read. This cadence is swept whenever a review is read or listed, so a review that falls due while sitting `SCHEDULED` is observed as `OVERDUE` on its next read. Once `overdue_days` reaches thirty or more, the review is escalated to `ESCALATED`, and that escalation does two things: the issuer's `surveillance_posture` moves to `ACTIVE_WATCH`, and the review's `escalation_index` is raised by 25. The `escalation_index` is otherwise a derived priority: its base is `overdue_days`, and a `review_type` of `EVENT_DRIVEN` adds a tier bonus of 25 while a `PERIODIC` review adds none, so a periodic review's index equals its overdue days exactly. When an issuer's `surveillance_posture` flips to `ACTIVE_WATCH`, every open review of that issuer — one whose `review_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `OVERDUE`, `ESCALATED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — has its `escalation_index` raised to the larger of its current value and 25. This floor raises the review's derived `escalation_index` itself rather than adding a separate bonus to the overdue-plus-tier sum, so an open review whose own base is already 25 or more keeps that larger value. This floor is idempotent: applying it a second time does not compound the index.

Work on a review begins when a `RatingAnalyst` starts it. Starting requires the issuer's `surveillance_posture` not to be `RESTRICTED`, or the start is rejected with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409), and it requires the review to be in `SCHEDULED`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, or it is rejected with `ERR_SURVEILLANCE_NOT_STARTABLE` (HTTP 409). Starting moves the review to `IN_PROGRESS`. Completing a review requires it to be `IN_PROGRESS`, or the completion is rejected with `ERR_SURVEILLANCE_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409), and on success the review becomes `COMPLETED` and a successor review is auto-created with its `review_due_at` advanced by the cycle length. A completed review may be reopened only when a rating action of type `WITHDRAWAL` exists for its linked decision; a reopen without that grounding is rejected with `ERR_SURVEILLANCE_REOPEN_NOT_GROUNDED` (HTTP 409), and a grounded reopen returns the review to `IN_PROGRESS`.

Review work is booked against an analyst's calendar through a `ReviewSlot`, created with the `analyst_id`, the `surveillance_review_id`, and the half-open booking interval from `slot_start` through `slot_end`. An `analyst_id` or `surveillance_review_id` that names no row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Every slot reserves a fixed preparation buffer of 30 minutes before the booking and a fixed wrap-up buffer of 30 minutes after it, recorded as `pre_buffer_minutes` and `post_buffer_minutes`. A new booking whose interval falls inside another `BOOKED` slot's buffered interval on the same analyst is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_SLOT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A slot is `BOOKED` when created, can be cancelled from that state — a cancel against a non-`BOOKED` slot is rejected with `ERR_SLOT_NOT_BOOKED` (HTTP 409) — and moves to `CANCELLED` on cancellation, releasing its buffered interval so it no longer blocks new bookings. A slot also moves to `UNFILLED` automatically when its linked review is reopened or its action event is cancelled, freeing the time for rebooking.

### 3.8. Event-Driven Rating Actions

Beyond periodic review, a rating can be moved by an event-driven action. A `RatingAnalyst` raises a `RatingActionEvent` with the `surveillance_review_id` that surfaced it, the `rating_decision_id` it acts upon, the `event_type`, an `effective_at` instant that may sit in the future of the logical clock, and — for an upgrade or downgrade — the `target_grade`. The `event_type` is one of `UPGRADE`, `DOWNGRADE`, `AFFIRM`, `WATCH_PLACEMENT`, or `WITHDRAWAL`; a `target_grade` is required for `UPGRADE` and `DOWNGRADE` and ignored otherwise. A `surveillance_review_id` or `rating_decision_id` that names no row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A new event begins with its `event_status` at `PENDING` and `applied_at` null.

Applying a due event evaluates its gates in a fixed order: it first requires the issuer's `surveillance_posture` not to be `RESTRICTED`, or it is rejected with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409); it then requires the `effective_at` instant to have been reached on the logical clock, or it is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_EFFECTIVE_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 409) — so this gate outranks the pending-state gate; and only then requires the event still to be `PENDING`, or the apply is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409). On success the event moves to `APPLIED`, `applied_at` is stamped, the decision's `outlook` and `watch_status` are updated according to the `event_type` — an `UPGRADE` sets `POSITIVE` and `NO_WATCH`, a `DOWNGRADE` sets `NEGATIVE` and `NO_WATCH`, an `AFFIRM` sets `STABLE` and `NO_WATCH`, and a `WATCH_PLACEMENT` sets `WATCH_DOWNGRADE` — the issuer's `current_grade` is set to the `target_grade` for an `UPGRADE` or `DOWNGRADE`, and the issuer's `surveillance_posture` is recomputed, moving to `ACTIVE_WATCH` when the watch designation is a watch placement. Cancelling an event is available only while it is still in the future: the event must be `PENDING`, or the cancel is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409), and its `effective_at` must still be ahead of the clock, or the cancel is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_ALREADY_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 409). A successful cancel moves the event to `REVERTED` and raises the linked review's `escalation_index` by 25.

### 3.9. Analyst Departures, Look-Back Reviews and Compensation Separation

When an analyst leaves for a rated issuer, the agency runs a look-back over the opinions that analyst touched. A `ComplianceOfficer` records the departure against an `analyst_id`, setting the destination `departed_to_entity_id` and the `departure_date`; the analyst's `is_departed` flag flips to true and `departed_to_entity_id` is recorded. That destination feeds the screening rule described earlier, which blocks any team containing an analyst who left for a rated issuer within the last twenty-four months. A `LookBackReview` is then initiated with the `analyst_id`, the `departed_to_entity_id`, the `departure_date`, and a `lookback_period_months` window; an `analyst_id` or `departed_to_entity_id` that names no row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and it starts in `INITIATED`. Starting the review requires a recorded `departure_date` — on the analyst or on the review itself — or it is rejected with `ERR_LOOKBACK_NOT_GROUNDED` (HTTP 409); a review that is not `INITIATED` is rejected with the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the review moves to `IN_PROGRESS` and its `affected_decisions_count` — the number of published decisions the analyst led or supported inside the look-back window — is computed. Closing the review requires it to be `IN_PROGRESS`, or it is rejected with `ERR_LOOKBACK_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409), and it requires that `affected_decisions_count` has been computed, which any value of zero or more satisfies. A close moves the review to `CLOSED`, flags the departed analyst's surviving team assignments on the affected decisions for re-screening, and moves the joined issuer's `surveillance_posture` to `ACTIVE_WATCH` — the agency treats a high-profile hire as a live conflict signal on the issuer's ratings.

Analytical compensation is deliberately insulated from the commercial side of the house. A `CompensationCommittee` creates an `AnalystCompensation` record for an `analyst_id`, carrying a `base_salary_cents`, a `rating_accuracy_score` between 0 and 100, and a `comp_period` label such as a half-year. An `analyst_id` that names no analyst row is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The record begins in `DRAFT` with `fee_generation_bonus_cents` pinned at zero. Approving a record requires the analyst to hold no `CONFIRMED` team assignment in the current `comp_period` — otherwise `ERR_COMPENSATION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 402) — and requires the analyst not to have departed, or the approval is rejected with `ERR_COMPENSATION_ANALYST_DEPARTED` (HTTP 409). It also rejects any record that carries a fee-generation component with `ERR_COMPENSATION_FEE_LINKAGE` (HTTP 402); the model's whole point is that such a component can never legitimately exist. Approval computes an `accuracy_bonus_cents` and holds `fee_generation_bonus_cents` at zero, then sets `total_compensation_cents` as the sum of `base_salary_cents`, the accuracy bonus, and the zero fee bonus. The `accuracy_bonus_cents` is the base salary multiplied by the amount by which `rating_accuracy_score` exceeds a floor of 70 — floored at zero when the score is at or below 70 — and divided by 200, rounded half-up to whole cents, with the bonus rounded once before the total is formed. Because the fee bonus is unconditionally zero, the `compensation_separation_pct` — the fraction of total compensation that is fee-independent — is always 1.0, since the base salary plus the accuracy bonus is the whole total. The total is therefore invariant under any change to the engagements the analyst touched; a fee-linked variant would move the total with `fee_credited_cents` and is the explicitly forbidden shape, because it would break the conservation invariant that the total equals base salary plus accuracy bonus plus the zero fee bonus. Paying an approved record moves it to `PAID` while conserving the total, and returning an approved record to `DRAFT` for revision recomputes the total with the same conserved formula; a pay or revise against a record that is not `APPROVED` is rejected with `ERR_COMPENSATION_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409).

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every operation that creates a new resource responds with HTTP 201, and every operation that modifies an existing resource or performs a state transition responds with HTTP 200. A request that reaches no declared route — including one that names a valid path with an unsupported method — is answered with the standard error envelope carrying `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The table below lists each endpoint with the role or roles authorized to invoke it; a role marked `Any` is exercised by any authenticated caller, while `Anonymous` operations require no roster membership.
