# CDFI Certification & Lending API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Community development financial institutions occupy an unusual position in the credit market: they are asked to lend where conventional underwriting will not, and simultaneously asked to prove — continuously, and with evidence — that they are still doing so. Certification is not a one-time badge. A certified lender must demonstrate a board-adopted primary mission, a statutorily recognised target market, a governance structure genuinely accountable to that market, a financing-activity profile, development services delivered alongside credit, and non-governmental control. It must then keep proving, period after period, that a supermajority of its lending actually reaches the market it named. Failing that proof does not merely produce a bad report — it strips certification and, with it, access to the federal award programmes that fund the subsidy.

This document specifies the API for a system that carries all of that at once. The platform is mounted under `/cdfi` and manages the full arc: institution registration and target-market definition, accountability seat rosters, the certification docket with its six per-test verdicts and bounded information-request cycle, borrower intake with address geocoding and target-market attribution, loan products built for underserved and credit-invisible borrowers, a pinned pricing and grading chain, disbursement and servicing with delinquency bucketing and charge-off, borrower-supplied impact data, periodic target-market lending measurement under two competing weighting bases, and federal award applications with matching-fund verification, performance goals and posture-gated draw control.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below and should be read as standing constraints rather than incidental detail. First, **higher-risk lending demands a funded reserve**, so the institution's loan-loss reserve adequacy is re-derived on every event that moves a balance, and an inadequate reserve mechanically suspends federal draws without any operator deciding to do so. Second, **snapshots must be honest**, so several determinations — the accountability score copied onto an application, the target-market flag copied onto a loan at first disbursement, the eligible match booked at verification — are frozen at a specific moment and are never silently re-derived afterwards. Where a client's view of a parent record may have drifted before it takes such a snapshot, the write is rejected outright rather than accepted against a stale reading.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role under which the principal acts. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the seven roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This authentication gate is evaluated before every other check in the system: the header-presence test is the very first evaluation, and the role-recognition test is the second, so an unrecognised role never reaches an authorisation or existence check.

The seven roles are `InstitutionOfficer`, `Borrower`, `CertificationReviewer`, `CreditUnderwriter`, `CreditCommittee`, `ComplianceAnalyst` and `AwardAdministrator`. An `InstitutionOfficer` is the operating officer of the lender itself: it registers the institution, defines target markets, seats and retires board members, files certification and award paperwork, onboards borrowers, publishes products, books technical assistance, disburses principal and applies payments. A `Borrower` is the obligor of record and may only read its own loans and file, read or correct the impact data collected on them. A `CertificationReviewer` acts for the certifying authority: it approves target-market definitions, opens review, records the six certification-test verdicts, requests further information and signs the certification decision. A `CreditUnderwriter` enters verified capacity figures and runs the pricing and grading chain, and is barred from deciding the loans it underwrites. A `CreditCommittee` member approves or declines underwritten loans subject to the technical-assistance prerequisite and the product cap. A `ComplianceAnalyst` geocodes borrowers, runs the servicing checkpoint, charges off unrecoverable loans, cuts, finalizes and voids target-market lending measurements, and verifies or disputes impact reports. An `AwardAdministrator` obligates federal awards, opens and resumes draw windows, and verifies matching funds.

Third in evaluation order, after the two authentication checks, comes endpoint authorisation: if the acting role does not appear in the authorised-role list for the endpoint being called (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the seven roles are entity-backed, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `InstitutionOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Institution`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Institution` compare the resource's owning-institution field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `InstitutionOfficer` must be the owner of the addressed resource — the path institution itself, or the owning institution of the addressed target market, board seat, borrower, loan product, loan, technical-assistance engagement, impact report, compliance measurement, award application or matching-fund commitment — and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_INSTITUTION_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Institution registration is the single exemption from this check, because the institution does not exist until the call succeeds and therefore has no `id` to compare against. When `X-User-Role` is `Borrower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Borrower`; a borrower filing an impact report against a loan must match that loan's borrower of record, and a borrower correcting a disputed report must match that report's borrower of record. Either mismatch is rejected with `ERR_BORROWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The institution-scope check is evaluated before the borrower-scope check.

The remaining five roles — `CertificationReviewer`, `CreditUnderwriter`, `CreditCommittee`, `ComplianceAnalyst` and `AwardAdministrator` — carry an opaque identity: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for an audit anchor (for example as `underwritten_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a `Loan`, as `submitted_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on a certification application, as `decided_by_user_id` on an award application, or as `verified_by_user_id` on a matching-fund commitment) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Those recorded identities exist to support **segregation of duties**, which the platform enforces at three points using the same identifier space. First, the credit-committee member deciding a loan must not be the underwriter recorded on that loan. Second, the reviewer who signs a certification decision must not be the reviewer who opened review on that application. Third, the award administrator who verifies a matching-fund commitment must not be the administrator recorded as having decided that commitment's award. All three violations are reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and all three are evaluated within the authorisation band — after endpoint authorisation and the two scope checks, and before any resource-existence lookup — in the order just given: loan decision, then certification decision, then match verification.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10 when omitted, and its accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and its minimum is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and place the page's rows themselves in `data` as a JSON array, so a caller reads a row as an indexed element of `data` and reads the page envelope from `meta`. Four pagination fields appear in `meta` on every list response: `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values in force for that page, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter across all pages, and `has_more` indicating whether any row remains beyond the current page. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Version Tokens and Timestamps

Every persisted resource in the system carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted. `updated_at` is the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation was requested directly by a client or applied indirectly by a cascade originating in another entity's workflow. All twelve resource types — institutions, target markets, certification applications, board seats, borrowers, loan products, loans, technical-assistance engagements, impact reports, compliance measurements, award applications and matching-fund commitments — carry all four properties.

The `version` field is not merely an audit convenience. Because the institution's accountability and reserve figures, and the loan's servicing balances, are both mutated by cascades that no single client initiated, `version` is the only reliable signal that a client's cached reading has gone out of date, and two write endpoints require the client to present it (see §2.7).

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host's wall clock. A single server-side logical UTC instant supplies every date-derived value: the instant a certification decision is signed, the instant a target market is approved, the instant a board seat is retired, the instant a report is verified, the date a loan is first disbursed and the vintage year derived from it, the installment due date, the days-past-due arithmetic in the servicing checkpoint, the close instant of a technical-assistance engagement, the finalization instant of a measurement, and the upper bound a measurement window is checked against.

Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant carried in the request field `to_iso8601`; the call mutates no entity, returns HTTP 200, and is available to every role except `Borrower`.

### 2.6. Rounding, Money and Rate Units

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; there are no fractional-cent fields anywhere in the system. Interest rates are integers denominated in basis points, where one basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, so an annual rate of 7.5% is expressed as 750. Hours are decimal numbers carried to two decimal places, and percentages are decimal numbers carried to two decimal places except where a subsection below specifies four.

Every rounding operation in the system uses **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Two determinations are carried to four decimal places: the institution's accountability representation score and its reserve coverage ratio. Everything else that is not a whole-cent or whole-day integer is carried to two decimal places: the borrower's income as a percentage of area median income, the debt-to-income percentage, the accumulated technical-assistance hours, the two target-market lending percentages and the selected one among them, and the award's goal attainment percentage.

**Where the rounding happens matters, and it differs deliberately between two calculations.** For the institution's required loan-loss reserve, each loan's contribution is computed exactly and the exact products are summed, with a single rounding to whole cents applied once at the end — explicitly not once per loan. For an award's eligible matching funds, the opposite order is pinned: each commitment's face amount is multiplied by its source factor and rounded to whole cents on its own, and only then are the per-commitment rounded amounts summed. These two orders can disagree by whole cents, and each calculation must use the order specified for it. Likewise, the accountability score applies its single rounding to the final sum and never to the intermediate ratios that feed it.

Wherever a threshold comparison follows a rounding step, the comparison uses the **rounded** value, not the unrounded one. A borrower whose income works out to 80.004 percent of area median income is treated as 80.00 percent and therefore satisfies a ceiling of 80.00. A reserve coverage ratio computing to 0.79996 is treated as 0.8000 and therefore lands in the middle posture band rather than the lowest.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Snapshot Writes

Two write endpoints freeze a value copied from a parent record, and both therefore require the client to prove that its reading of that parent is current. In each case the version field is a **required, non-optional** request field; omitting it is a malformed request.

Opening a certification application — `POST /cdfi/institutions/{institution_id}/certification-applications` — must carry `expected_institution_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Institution`. The create snapshots the institution's accountability representation score onto the new application, and that snapshot would be dishonest if taken from a view the client never saw: approving a target market, seating a board member or retiring one all move the score and all bump the institution's `version`. If `expected_institution_version` does not equal the institution's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSTITUTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Note that the operation is otherwise legitimate — a fresh application may properly supersede a previously denied one — so the stale version is the sole distinguishing defect.

Filing an impact report — `POST /cdfi/loans/{loan_id}/impact-reports` — must carry `expected_loan_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Loan`. Filing a report supersedes the loan's prior report for the same period and, when that prior report had been verified, removes its counts from the institution's impact totals; a filer whose reading of the loan is stale does not know which report is current or where the loan's servicing position now stands. Servicing checkpoints, payments and charge-offs all advance the loan's `version` behind the filer's back. If `expected_loan_version` does not equal the loan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for a version mismatch.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must carry the error belonging to the earliest band below, and within a band the earliest listed check. This ordering is part of the contract: a request that is both unauthorised and malformed reports the authorisation failure, and a request that both addresses a missing loan and carries a bad enum reports the missing loan. The bands are:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, then `X-User-Role` naming one of the seven declared roles. Either failure is `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The acting role appearing in the endpoint's authorised-role list (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`); then, on every endpoint whose authorised roles include `InstitutionOfficer`, the institution-ownership check (`ERR_INSTITUTION_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, with institution registration exempt); then, on impact-report filing and impact-report correction, the borrower-ownership check (`ERR_BORROWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`); then the three segregation-of-duties checks in the order loan decision, certification decision, matching-fund verification (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
3. **Existence**, each yielding HTTP 404, in exactly this order: `institution_id` resolving to an institution (`ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying an institution identifier in path or body); `application_id` resolving to a certification application (`ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, on every certification-application endpoint addressed by identifier); `target_market_id` resolving to a target market of the same institution (`ERR_TARGET_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, on the target-market read and approve actions and on board-seat creation); `board_seat_id` resolving to a board seat (`ERR_BOARD_SEAT_NOT_FOUND`, on the board-seat read and the resignation action); `borrower_id` resolving to a borrower of the same institution (`ERR_BORROWER_NOT_FOUND`, on every borrower endpoint addressed by identifier, on loan filing and on engagement booking); `product_id` resolving to a loan product — of the same institution when an institution is addressed (`ERR_LOAN_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, on loan filing and the loan-product read); `loan_id`, and `refinance_of_loan_id` when supplied, resolving to loans (`ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, on every loan endpoint addressed by identifier, on loan filing and on engagement booking); `engagement_id` resolving to an engagement (`ERR_TA_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on the engagement read and close actions); `impact_report_id` resolving to a report (`ERR_IMPACT_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, on every impact-report endpoint addressed by identifier); `measurement_id` resolving to a measurement (`ERR_MEASUREMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on every compliance-measurement endpoint addressed by identifier); `award_id` resolving to an award application (`ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, on every award endpoint addressed by identifier); and `commitment_id` resolving to a matching-fund commitment (`ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on every commitment endpoint addressed by identifier).
4. **Request shape.** On every write endpoint, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) covers a missing required body field, a field of the wrong type, an enum value outside its declared set, a numeric field outside its declared range, and each conditionally required field that is absent while its condition holds — a target market of type `INVESTMENT_AREA` without tract codes, a target market of type `LOW_INCOME_TARGETED_POPULATION` without an income ceiling, a board seat declaring market representation without naming a market, and a geocoding outcome of `MATCHED` without a resolved tract code. On every list endpoint, `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400) covers an out-of-range `limit` or a negative `offset`.
5. **Concurrency.** `expected_institution_version` on certification-application creation (`ERR_STALE_INSTITUTION_VERSION`), then `expected_loan_version` on impact-report filing (`ERR_STALE_LOAN_VERSION`), both HTTP 412.
6. **State.** First, the generic check that the requested transition is declared for the resource's current state, reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — this applies to every state-changing endpoint for which no more specific state error exists. Then the specific state guards, all HTTP 409, in this order: the application is `UNDER_REVIEW` (`ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, guarding test evaluation, information requests and the certification decision); the loan's `underwriting_state` is `UNDERWRITING` (`ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_UNDERWRITING`, guarding the credit decision); the loan's `servicing_state` is `DELINQUENT_90` (`ERR_LOAN_NOT_DELINQUENT_90`, guarding charge-off); the measurement is `DRAFT` (`ERR_MEASUREMENT_NOT_DRAFT`, guarding both finalization and voiding); the award is `AWARDED` (`ERR_AWARD_NOT_AWARDED`, guarding draw activation); the commitment is `PLEDGED` (`ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_PLEDGED`, guarding verification); the engagement is `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`, guarding close); and the report is `DISPUTED` (`ERR_REPORT_NOT_DISPUTED`, guarding correction).
7. **Business safety**, in this order: the product's technical-assistance prerequisite on loan approval (`ERR_TA_PREREQUISITE_UNMET`, HTTP 409); the debt-to-income ceiling and risk-tier resolution on underwriting (`ERR_UNDERWRITING_TIER_UNMET`, HTTP 422); the institution's compliance posture on both draw activation and draw resumption (`ERR_POSTURE_NOT_COMPLIANT`, HTTP 409); and the institution's certification state on award submission (`ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, HTTP 409).
8. **Business referential**, in this order: an approved target market exists (`ERR_TARGET_MARKET_NOT_APPROVED`, HTTP 422, guarding both application submission and test evaluation); accountability is satisfied (`ERR_ACCOUNTABILITY_NOT_SATISFIED`, HTTP 422, guarding application submission and evaluation of the accountability test specifically); the borrower is geocoded (`ERR_BORROWER_NOT_GEOCODED`, HTTP 422, guarding underwriting); no test verdict remains pending (`ERR_TEST_VERDICT_PENDING`, HTTP 422, guarding the certification decision); no commitment of the award is still pledged or held (`ERR_MATCH_COMMITMENTS_UNVERIFIED`, HTTP 409, guarding draw activation); and the award carries at least one commitment (`ERR_NO_MATCH_COMMITMENTS`, HTTP 422, guarding award submission).
9. **Business constraint**, in this order: employer-identification-number uniqueness on institution registration (`ERR_DUPLICATE_EIN`, HTTP 409); measurement-window overlap on measurement creation (`ERR_MEASUREMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, HTTP 409); engagement slot conflict on engagement booking (`ERR_TA_SLOT_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409); an empty measurement input set on finalization (`ERR_NO_MEASURABLE_LOANS`, HTTP 422); and the claimed financing-activity floor on application submission (`ERR_FINANCING_ACTIVITY_BELOW_MINIMUM`, HTTP 422).
10. **Temporal**, in this order: window and slot bounds ordering (`ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`, HTTP 422, guarding measurement creation, award-application creation and engagement booking); then the measurement window's upper bound against the logical clock (`ERR_PERIOD_END_IN_FUTURE`, HTTP 422, guarding measurement creation only).
11. **Fiscal**, in this order: the product cap on loan approval (`ERR_PRODUCT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422); the approved-principal ceiling on disbursement (`ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_APPROVED`, HTTP 422); the outstanding-balance ceiling on payment (`ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`, HTTP 422); the zero-shortfall requirement on draw activation (`ERR_MATCH_SHORTFALL`, HTTP 422); and the federal-source exclusion on commitment verification (`ERR_FEDERAL_SOURCE_INELIGIBLE`, HTTP 422).

Two consequences of this ladder deserve explicit statement because they are counter-intuitive. On underwriting, the tier-and-debt-to-income evaluation is performed **before** the borrower-geocoded check, so an ungeocoded borrower whose figures also fail the tier table is reported as `ERR_UNDERWRITING_TIER_UNMET`. On engagement booking and measurement creation, the conflict and overlap checks are performed **before** the bounds-ordering check, so a request whose end instant precedes its start instant and which also collides with an existing window is reported as the collision, not as `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`.

### 2.9. Nested Paths and Reverse-Lookup Filters

Several resources are created beneath their owner and read back through a flat address. Where a path carries more than one identifier, segments are validated strictly left to right: the outer identifier is resolved before the inner one, so a request naming a nonexistent institution and a nonexistent nested resource reports `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_FOUND`, never the inner resource's error. The path identifiers in use across the API are `institution_id`, `application_id`, `target_market_id`, `board_seat_id`, `borrower_id`, `product_id`, `loan_id`, `engagement_id`, `impact_report_id`, `measurement_id`, `award_id` and `commitment_id`.

Because ownership is a graph rather than a strict tree — a loan points at an institution, a borrower and a product, and optionally at another loan it refinances; a board seat joins an institution to a target market; an engagement joins an institution, a borrower and optionally a loan — most collections are also exposed as flat lists carrying explicit filters. Institutions may be filtered by `certification_state` and `compliance_posture`. Target markets may be filtered by `institution_id`, `market_type` and `state`. Board seats may be filtered by `institution_id`, `target_market_id`, `structure` and `status`. Borrowers may be filtered by `institution_id`, `geocode_state` and `in_target_market`. Loan products may be filtered by `institution_id` and `requires_ta_completion`. Loans may be filtered by `institution_id`, `borrower_id`, `product_id`, `refinance_of_loan_id`, `underwriting_state` and `servicing_state`. Engagements may be filtered by `institution_id`, `borrower_id`, `loan_id` and `state`. Impact reports may be filtered by `loan_id`, `borrower_id` and `state`. Compliance measurements may be filtered by `institution_id`, `weighting_basis`, `state` and `verdict`. Certification applications may be filtered by `institution_id` and `state`. Award applications may be filtered by `institution_id`, `state` and `program`. Matching-fund commitments may be filtered by `award_application_id`, `status` and `source_type`. All of these lists honour the pagination contract of §2.3.

### 2.10. Dual-Variant Determinations and Their Selectors

Two of the platform's determinations are computed **both ways, every time**, with a stored selector naming which result is authoritative. This is deliberate: an examiner must be able to see what the other basis would have concluded on identical data.

The institution's required loan-loss reserve is computed under both a migration-analysis basis and a static-pool basis, and both results are persisted and returned — `loss_reserve_required_migration_cents` and `loss_reserve_required_static_cents`. The institution's `reserve_method`, which is either `MIGRATION_ANALYSIS` or `STATIC_POOL` and defaults to `MIGRATION_ANALYSIS`, names which of the two is copied into `loss_reserve_required_cents`, and only that copied figure drives coverage and posture. The two bases can diverge dramatically, and they respond to a write-off in opposite directions: migration analysis is forward-looking on the live book, so charging a loan off removes its contribution entirely and the required reserve falls sharply, whereas the static-pool basis is anchored to originally disbursed principal by vintage cohort, so charging a loan off leaves the required reserve completely unchanged.

Target-market lending is computed the same way. Each measurement persists a dollar-weighted percentage in `dollar_weighted_pct` and a count-weighted percentage in `count_weighted_pct`, and the measurement's `weighting_basis` — either `DOLLAR_WEIGHTED` or `COUNT_WEIGHTED` — names which becomes `selected_pct` and therefore which decides the verdict. Dollar weighting lets a single large loan outside the target market sink the percentage even when most loans are inside it; count weighting gives every loan an equal vote, so many small target-market loans outvote one large loan outside it. The same portfolio can pass under one basis and fail under the other.

### 2.11. Reserve Adequacy, Compliance Posture and Cascade Propagation

Higher-risk lending is only responsible lending when the losses it anticipates are funded, so the institution's reserve adequacy is not a periodic report — it is a standing posture re-derived on every event that moves a loan balance. Four operations trigger the re-derivation: a disbursement, a payment, the servicing checkpoint and a charge-off. Each of them recomputes both reserve variants, selects the authoritative one, recomputes `reserve_coverage_ratio`, and re-derives `compliance_posture`.

`compliance_posture` takes one of three values. A coverage ratio of at least 1.0000 is `COMPLIANT`. A ratio of at least 0.8000 but below 1.0000 is `WATCH`. A ratio below 0.8000 is `PROBATION`. Both band boundaries are inclusive on their lower edge, and both comparisons use the ratio after it has been rounded to four decimal places. An institution whose required reserve is exactly zero — a lender with no live book at all — has its coverage ratio pinned to 1.0000 rather than treated as a division by zero or as 0.0000, and is therefore `COMPLIANT`. A newly registered institution begins at `COMPLIANT` with a coverage ratio of 1.0000.

Posture is not advisory. Entering `PROBATION` from either of the other two bands immediately suspends draws on every award application of that institution currently in `DISBURSING`, and each award so suspended in turn puts every one of its still-pledged matching-fund commitments on hold. Restoring the ratio to at least 1.0000 returns the posture to `COMPLIANT` but does **not** auto-resume anything: a suspended award stays suspended until an award administrator resumes it explicitly. `WATCH` never suspends draws.

Cascades of this kind are ordinary mutations for bookkeeping purposes. Whenever a cascade writes a field on a row — the institution's derived figures, an award's state and suspension reason, a commitment's status, a certification application's accountability verdict and state — that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1, exactly as if a client had written it directly. The institution's two independent state dimensions do not constrain each other: `certification_state` is driven by certification decisions and recertification measurements, `compliance_posture` is derived purely from reserve coverage, and neither one's transitions are gated by the other. Posture gates award draws; certification state gates award submission.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Lender and Its Accountability Structures

An `InstitutionOfficer` brings a candidate lender onto the platform by registering it with its registered `legal_name`, its `ein` — an employer identification number that must be exactly nine digits and must not already be on file for another institution, on pain of `ERR_DUPLICATE_EIN` — and its charter form in `entity_type`, which is one of `LOAN_FUND`, `CREDIT_UNION`, `BANK_HOLDING` or `VENTURE_FUND`. The board-adopted `primary_mission_statement` must run to at least 40 characters, since it is the evidence the primary-mission certification test is scored against. Total assets at registration are declared in `asset_size_cents` and must not be negative. Two reserve inputs are also supplied at registration: `reserve_method`, which selects the authoritative reserve basis and defaults to `MIGRATION_ANALYSIS` when omitted, and the optional `loss_reserve_balance_cents`, the funded reserve on hand, which defaults to 0 and may never be negative.

A freshly registered institution is deliberately inert. Its `certification_state` is `UNCERTIFIED` and its `compliance_posture` is `COMPLIANT`; `accountability_score` is 0.0000 and `accountability_satisfied` is false; `governing_seats_active` and `advisory_seats_active` are both 0; `portfolio_principal_outstanding_cents`, `portfolio_principal_disbursed_cents`, `loss_reserve_required_migration_cents`, `loss_reserve_required_static_cents`, `loss_reserve_required_cents` and `reserve_deficit_cents` are all 0; `reserve_coverage_ratio` is 1.0000 under the empty-portfolio pin; `target_market_lending_pct` is 0.00; `jobs_created_total`, `jobs_retained_total`, `housing_units_total` and `businesses_financed_total` are all 0; and both `certified_at` and `recertification_due_at` are null.

Before it can apply for anything, the lender must say who it serves. A target market is defined by its statutory `market_type` — one of `INVESTMENT_AREA`, `LOW_INCOME_TARGETED_POPULATION` or `OTHER_TARGETED_POPULATION` — together with a human-readable `name`. An `INVESTMENT_AREA` is composed of eleven-character tract codes supplied in `census_tract_codes`, which must be non-empty for that market type and must be empty for the other two; the field defaults to an empty collection. A `LOW_INCOME_TARGETED_POPULATION` is defined instead by `ami_threshold_pct`, a household-income ceiling expressed as a percentage of area median income, which must be supplied for that market type and must be null for the other two. A new definition is created in `state` `DRAFT` with `approved_at` null. Only a `CertificationReviewer` can move a definition `DRAFT → APPROVED`, and only when it is currently `DRAFT`, when an `INVESTMENT_AREA` carries at least one tract code, and when a `LOW_INCOME_TARGETED_POPULATION` carries a non-null income ceiling. Approval stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock, and because seats pointing at that definition now begin to count, it also recomputes the institution's accountability figures and advances the institution's `version` by 1.

Accountability is demonstrated through seats. A board seat records the `structure` it sits on — `GOVERNING_BOARD` or `ADVISORY_BOARD` — the `holder_name`, and a `represents_target_market` flag that defaults to false. A seat claiming representation must name the market it is accountable to in `target_market_id`, and that identifier must be null when the flag is false. Seating a member creates the seat with `status` `ACTIVE` and `resigned_at` null, increments whichever of `governing_seats_active` or `advisory_seats_active` matches the declared structure, recomputes the accountability figures and advances the institution's `version` by 1.

The **accountability representation score** is a weighted measure computed over the institution's `ACTIVE` seats only; `RESIGNED` seats leave both the numerator and the denominator entirely. A seat counts as *representing* only when its `represents_target_market` flag is true **and** the target market it names is in state `APPROVED` — a seat pointing at a definition still in `DRAFT` is counted in the seat totals but not among the representing seats. Four counts feed the score: the number of active governing-board seats, the number of those that represent an approved market, the number of active advisory-board seats, and the number of those that represent an approved market. The governing share is the representing governing count divided by the total governing count, taken as an exact ratio and not rounded; when there are no active governing seats at all, the governing share is treated as zero rather than as undefined. Advisory seats earn partial credit at half weight: the advisory credit is the representing advisory count multiplied by one half and divided by the **combined** count of governing and advisory seats, and that quotient is then capped at 0.2000; when the combined count is zero, the advisory credit is zero. Because the divisor spans both structures, seating an advisory member who does not represent an approved market can *lower* the advisory credit rather than raise it. The governing share and the advisory credit are added, the sum is capped at a ceiling of 1.0000, and the capped value is rounded half up to four decimal places exactly once — never at the intermediate ratios — producing `accountability_score`. Finally, `accountability_satisfied` is true when and only when `accountability_score` is at least 0.5000.

Retiring a seat is the head of the accountability cascade and has consequences well beyond the seat itself. Resignation sets the seat's `status` to `RESIGNED`, stamps `resigned_at` from the logical clock, decrements the matching active-seat counter on the institution, recomputes `accountability_score` and `accountability_satisfied`, and advances the institution's `version` by 1. If — and only if — that recomputation flips `accountability_satisfied` from true to false, the cascade continues into the certification docket: every certification application of that institution whose `state` is `UNDER_REVIEW` has its `test_accountability_state` set to `FAILED`, and each such application whose `revision_round` is still below 2 is additionally pushed backwards from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED` with `revision_round` incremented by 1 and `info_requested_reason` recorded as `ACCOUNTABILITY_TEST_FAILED`. No reviewer action is involved; a resignation alone can knock an in-flight application out of review.

### 3.2. The Certification Docket

Opening a certification application is the one place where an officer must prove its reading of the institution is current, because the application permanently snapshots the institution's accountability score. The payload carries `expected_institution_version` alongside the application's own `primary_mission_statement` (again at least 40 characters), the `financing_activity_pct_claimed` — the share of the lender's activity it claims is financing activity, expressed to two decimal places between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive — and a `development_services_description` narrating the technical assistance and borrower support delivered alongside credit, which must run to at least 20 characters. A stale version token is refused with `ERR_STALE_INSTITUTION_VERSION`, as described in §2.7. On success the application is created in `state` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, all six per-test verdict fields at `PENDING`, `accountability_score_snapshot` copied from the institution's current `accountability_score` and carried to four decimal places, `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `info_requested_reason` and `decided_at` all null; the institution's `version` advances by 1.

Submission moves the docket `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and records the acting officer in `submitted_by_user_id`. Three substantive gates stand in front of it, and they are evaluated in this order after the state check: the institution must have at least one target market in `state` `APPROVED`, or the submission fails with `ERR_TARGET_MARKET_NOT_APPROVED`; `accountability_satisfied` must be true, or it fails with `ERR_ACCOUNTABILITY_NOT_SATISFIED`; and `financing_activity_pct_claimed` must be at least 60.00, or it fails with `ERR_FINANCING_ACTIVITY_BELOW_MINIMUM`. A `CertificationReviewer` then takes the file up, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and recording its own identity in `reviewed_by_user_id` — the identity that the eventual signature is barred from reusing.

Review consists of six independent tests, each recorded one call at a time. The reviewer supplies a `test_code`, which is one of `PRIMARY_MISSION`, `FINANCING_ENTITY`, `TARGET_MARKET`, `DEVELOPMENT_SERVICES`, `ACCOUNTABILITY` or `NON_GOVERNMENT`, together with a `verdict` of `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `WAIVED` and an optional `evidence_note`. Recording a verdict writes the one matching field — `test_primary_mission_state`, `test_financing_entity_state`, `test_target_market_state`, `test_development_services_state`, `test_accountability_state` or `test_non_government_state` — and leaves the application's own `state` at `UNDER_REVIEW`; this is a self-loop that never changes state. Two of the six tests carry preconditions of their own: recording a verdict for `TARGET_MARKET` requires that the institution already has at least one `APPROVED` target market, failing with `ERR_TARGET_MARKET_NOT_APPROVED`, and recording a verdict for `ACCOUNTABILITY` requires `accountability_satisfied` to be true, failing with `ERR_ACCOUNTABILITY_NOT_SATISFIED`. Recording a verdict on an application that is not `UNDER_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, which likewise guards the information request and the final signature.

The review cycle is bounded at two revisions, and both boundaries are enforced. A reviewer returning an application for more information supplies `info_requested_reason`; while `revision_round` is below 2 this moves the docket `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`, increments `revision_round` by 1 and records the reason. If `revision_round` has already reached 2 when the reviewer asks again, the same action terminates the cycle instead: the docket goes `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, `revision_round` increments to 3, the reason is still recorded, and `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock. When the officer answers an information request, it may supply a corrected `primary_mission_statement`, a corrected `financing_activity_pct_claimed` and a corrected `development_services_description`; each value supplied overwrites the stored one, and values omitted are left untouched. While `revision_round` is at most 1 the answer returns the docket `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and clears `info_requested_reason` to null; when `revision_round` has already reached 2 the answer instead settles the docket at `DENIED` with `decided_at` stamped. Returning to review also partially clears the slate: every test verdict currently sitting at `FAILED` is reset to `PENDING`, while `PASSED` and `WAIVED` verdicts are retained untouched. Answering a docket that is not in `INFO_REQUESTED` is an undeclared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Signing the decision is a single action with two outcomes and a hard segregation rule. The signing `CertificationReviewer` must present an `X-User-ID` different from `reviewed_by_user_id`, or the signature is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the docket must be `UNDER_REVIEW`; and no verdict may still be `PENDING`, or the signature is refused with `ERR_TEST_VERDICT_PENDING`. Given all six verdicts settled, the outcome is mechanical: when every one of the six is either `PASSED` or `WAIVED` the docket goes `UNDER_REVIEW → CERTIFIED`, and when at least one of the six is `FAILED` it goes `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`. Either way `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Certification is the moment the institution itself changes: on the `CERTIFIED` outcome the institution moves `UNCERTIFIED → CERTIFIED`, `certified_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `recertification_due_at` is set to the logical clock plus 1,095 days — three years, the interval at which target-market performance must be re-proved — and the institution's `version` advances by 1. On the `DENIED` outcome the institution's `certification_state` is left exactly as it was.

### 3.3. Borrower Intake, Geocoding and Target-Market Attribution

Borrower intake collects the `full_name`, the `street_address` and the five-digit `postal_code` that geocoding will work from, the annual `household_income_cents` which may not be negative, the `area_median_income_cents` for the borrower's area which must be strictly greater than zero, and an optional `credit_score`. A supplied score must lie between 300 and 850 inclusive; a borrower with no scoreable credit file simply omits the field, leaving it null, and such credit-invisible borrowers are a first-class case rather than an error. At intake the platform computes `income_pct_ami` as one hundred multiplied by `household_income_cents`, divided by `area_median_income_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The borrower is created with `geocode_state` `UNGEOCODED`, `geocode_attempts` 0, `census_tract_code` null, `in_target_market` false, `matched_target_market_type` null and `ta_hours_total` 0.00.

Attribution to a target market runs off the address, and it may take several tries. Each attempt reports an `outcome` of either `MATCHED`, which must carry the resolved `census_tract_code`, or `UNMATCHED`, which carries nothing. Every attempt increments `geocode_attempts` by 1 regardless of outcome, and every attempt advances the borrower's `version` by 1. A `MATCHED` outcome stores the tract code and puts the borrower at `GEOCODED` — this is the path from `UNGEOCODED → GEOCODED`, the retry path from `GEOCODE_FAILED → GEOCODED`, and a re-geocode of an already-matched borrower as `GEOCODED → GEOCODED` with the tract code overwritten. An `UNMATCHED` outcome puts the borrower at `GEOCODE_FAILED`, forces `in_target_market` to false and `matched_target_market_type` to null, and leaves the borrower retryable indefinitely.

Whenever geocoding runs, target-market attribution is re-derived. If the borrower is not `GEOCODED`, attribution stops immediately with `in_target_market` false and `matched_target_market_type` null. Otherwise the candidate set is the target markets of the borrower's own institution that are in `state` `APPROVED`; definitions still in `DRAFT` never match. Candidates are ranked by market type, with `INVESTMENT_AREA` taking first precedence, `LOW_INCOME_TARGETED_POPULATION` second and `OTHER_TARGETED_POPULATION` third, and ties among candidates of the same market type broken by `created_at` ascending. Each candidate is tested against the predicate for its own type, and the first candidate whose predicate holds wins: an `INVESTMENT_AREA` matches when the borrower's resolved `census_tract_code` is one of the market's `census_tract_codes`; a `LOW_INCOME_TARGETED_POPULATION` matches when the borrower's `income_pct_ami` is less than or equal to that market's `ami_threshold_pct`, using the already-rounded two-decimal income figure so that a computed 80.004 reads as 80.00 and satisfies a ceiling of 80.00; and an `OTHER_TARGETED_POPULATION` matches when the borrower's `credit_score` is null. If a winner exists, `in_target_market` becomes true and `matched_target_market_type` records the winner's market type; if the candidate set is empty or no predicate holds, `in_target_market` becomes false and `matched_target_market_type` becomes null — an empty candidate set is not an error.

One asymmetry is deliberate and load-bearing. Re-geocoding a borrower updates the borrower's own attribution but **never** rewrites the origination snapshot already stored on loans that have been disbursed. A borrower who is corrected out of the target market a year after closing does not retroactively change what those loans counted for.

### 3.4. Product Design and the Technical-Assistance Programme

Products for underserved borrowers are defined by the flexibility they allow rather than the flexibility they deny, so the product record is mostly a set of underwriting knobs. Publishing a product records its `name`, its `base_rate_bps` — the annual rate in basis points before any risk spread or technical-assistance discount, constrained to at least 0 and at most 3600 — and the amortization `term_months`, at least 1 and at most 360. The flexible-underwriting box is `max_dti_pct`, a debt-to-income ceiling to two decimal places that must be strictly greater than 0.00 and at most 60.00; `min_credit_score`, the lowest scoreable file the product will accept, between 300 and 850 inclusive; and `allow_no_credit_score`, defaulting to false, which decides whether a borrower with no score at all may be graded. Credit-building affordances are listed in `credit_building_features`, a collection defaulting to empty whose members are drawn from `REPORTS_TO_BUREAUS`, `SAVINGS_MATCH`, `RATE_STEP_DOWN` and `DEFERRED_FIRST_PAYMENT`. The `requires_ta_completion` flag, defaulting to false, makes borrower coaching a precondition of approval. Finally `product_cap_cents`, which must be strictly positive, is a hard ceiling on committed principal across the product, and `committed_principal_cents` — which starts at 0 — is the running total of approved principal across the product's loans whose `underwriting_state` is `APPROVED` or `CLOSED`.

Technical assistance is scheduled per borrower and, optionally, per loan. Booking a session names the `borrower_id` receiving assistance, the curriculum `topic` — one of `CREDIT_BUILDING`, `BUSINESS_PLANNING`, `HOMEBUYER_EDUCATION` or `FINANCIAL_COACHING` — the inclusive `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` bounds of the slot, and `planned_hours` to two decimal places, which must be strictly greater than 0.00 and at most 8.00. An optional `loan_id` attaches the session to a specific loan; pre-application coaching leaves it null. `scheduled_end_at` must be strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, and a violation is reported as `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`. New sessions are created in `state` `SCHEDULED` with `actual_hours` 0.00, `competency_verified` false and `closed_at` null.

The borrower — not the coach — is the occupied resource, so two sessions for the same borrower may not collide even when different staff deliver them. The requested closed interval is padded by a half-hour buffer on each side, and the booking is refused with `ERR_TA_SLOT_CONFLICT` when it intersects any existing session of the **same** borrower whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. Stated exactly: the booking is rejected when the new `scheduled_start_at` is at or before the existing session's `scheduled_end_at` advanced by 30 minutes, and simultaneously the existing session's `scheduled_start_at` is at or before the new `scheduled_end_at` advanced by 30 minutes. Sessions of other borrowers, and sessions already closed as no-shows, are irrelevant to the test.

Closing a session forks into two outcomes with disjoint consequences, driven by the `outcome` field. An `outcome` of `COMPLETED` requires `actual_hours` to be supplied and strictly greater than 0.00, and accepts `competency_verified` as the coach's attestation; it moves the session `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, stores both values, stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, and recomputes the borrower's `ta_hours_total` as the sum of `actual_hours` over that borrower's `COMPLETED` sessions, carried to two decimal places. An `outcome` of `NO_SHOW` moves the session `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`, stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, sets `actual_hours` to 0.00 and `competency_verified` to false, leaves the borrower's `ta_hours_total` unchanged, and — because only `SCHEDULED` and `COMPLETED` sessions occupy the borrower's calendar — releases the interval so that it becomes bookable again. Either fork advances the session's `version` by 1. Closing a session that is not `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. Only a session that is `COMPLETED` **and** carries `competency_verified` true satisfies a product's technical-assistance prerequisite; hours delivered under a session still `SCHEDULED`, or one closed as `NO_SHOW`, contribute nothing to `ta_hours_total` and nothing to the prerequisite.

### 3.5. Origination: Screening, Underwriting, Pricing and the Credit Decision

Filing a loan request names the `institution_id` booking it, the `borrower_id` obligated on it, the `product_id` whose underwriting box governs it, and the `requested_principal_cents`, which must be strictly positive. A refinance names the prior obligation in `refinance_of_loan_id`, which must resolve to another loan of the same borrower; new-money requests leave it null, and this self-reference is how credit-building step-ups are traced. A new request lands with `underwriting_state` `SCREENING` and `servicing_state` `NOT_DISBURSED`, `approved_principal_cents` null, `days_past_due` 0, and every money field — `principal_disbursed_cents`, `principal_repaid_cents`, `principal_outstanding_cents` and `charged_off_principal_cents` — at 0.

A `CreditUnderwriter` advances the request `SCREENING → UNDERWRITING` by entering the verified capacity figures: `verified_monthly_income_cents`, `existing_monthly_debt_cents`, and optionally `credit_score_at_underwriting`, the score used for tiering, which is left null for a borrower with no scoreable file. Underwriting requires the borrower to be `GEOCODED`, failing with `ERR_BORROWER_NOT_GEOCODED`; it requires either a score to be present or the product's `allow_no_credit_score` to be true; and it requires the pricing chain below to resolve a grade. The action records `underwritten_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` — the identity the credit decision may not reuse — and advances the loan's `version` by 1.

The pricing chain looks circular and is not. The contractual rate depends on the risk spread, which depends on the grade, which depends on the debt-to-income ratio, which depends on a monthly payment, which depends on a rate. The resolution is a **pinned evaluation order that is never iterated to a fixed point**, and the debt-to-income ratio is computed exactly once and then frozen. In order:

1. A **provisional monthly payment** is computed at the product's base rate under a simple declining method: one month of simple interest on the full requested principal — the requested principal multiplied by the base rate expressed as a decimal fraction, meaning `base_rate_bps` divided by ten thousand, then divided by twelve — plus straight-line principal, meaning the requested principal divided by `term_months`. The two components are summed and the sum is rounded half up to whole cents once, at the end of the step, giving `provisional_monthly_payment_cents`.
2. `dti_pct` is then one hundred multiplied by the sum of `existing_monthly_debt_cents` and `provisional_monthly_payment_cents`, divided by `verified_monthly_income_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. **This value is frozen here** and is never recomputed from the contractual rate later in the chain.
3. Before any tier is considered, `dti_pct` must be at or below the product's `max_dti_pct`; if it exceeds the ceiling, underwriting is refused with `ERR_UNDERWRITING_TIER_UNMET`. The `risk_grade` is then assigned by walking five tiers top to bottom and taking the first that matches. Tier `A` requires a non-null credit score of at least 720 and a `dti_pct` at or below 36.00. Tier `B` requires a non-null score of at least 660 and a `dti_pct` at or below 43.00. Tier `C` requires a non-null score of at least 600 and a `dti_pct` at or below 45.00. Tier `D` requires a non-null score at or above the product's `min_credit_score` and a `dti_pct` at or below 50.00. Tier `E` is the credit-invisible path: it requires the score to be null, the product's `allow_no_credit_score` to be true, the borrower's `ta_hours_total` to be at least 12.00 hours of completed coaching, and a `dti_pct` at or below 45.00. If no tier matches, underwriting is refused with `ERR_UNDERWRITING_TIER_UNMET`.
4. `risk_spread_bps` follows mechanically from the grade: 0 basis points for `A`, 75 for `B`, 150 for `C`, 275 for `D`, and 200 for `E` — note that the credit-invisible tier prices below tier `D`, deliberately rewarding the coaching that qualified it.
5. `ta_discount_bps` is a step function on coaching already delivered at the moment of underwriting: 25 basis points when the borrower's `ta_hours_total` is at least 8.00 hours, and 0 otherwise. Hours from sessions still `SCHEDULED` or closed as `NO_SHOW` count as zero.
6. `final_rate_bps` is the product's `base_rate_bps` plus `risk_spread_bps` minus `ta_discount_bps`.
7. `monthly_payment_cents` re-quotes the payment at the contractual rate using the same simple declining formula as step 1 — one month of simple interest on the full requested principal at `final_rate_bps` divided by ten thousand and then by twelve, plus the requested principal divided by `term_months`, rounded half up to whole cents once. `dti_pct` is deliberately left at its step-2 value.

A `CreditCommittee` member then settles the request with a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`. The loan must be in `underwriting_state` `UNDERWRITING`, failing with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_UNDERWRITING`, and the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `underwritten_by_user_id`, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An approval takes an optional `approved_principal_cents`; when it is omitted, the approved amount is the full `requested_principal_cents`. Two further gates apply to approvals only, in this order: when the product's `requires_ta_completion` is true, a session must exist for the loan's borrower in `state` `COMPLETED` with `competency_verified` true, or the approval is refused with `ERR_TA_PREREQUISITE_UNMET`; and the product's `committed_principal_cents` plus the amount being approved must not exceed `product_cap_cents`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A successful approval moves the loan `UNDERWRITING → APPROVED`, records `approved_principal_cents` and `decided_by_user_id`, and adds the approved amount to the product's `committed_principal_cents`. A decline moves the loan `UNDERWRITING → DECLINED`, records the supplied `decline_reason` and `decided_by_user_id`, and leaves `committed_principal_cents` untouched. Either outcome advances the loan's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Disbursement, Servicing, Delinquency and Charge-Off

A loan row carries **two independent state dimensions** that advance on disjoint trigger sets. `underwriting_state` runs `SCREENING → UNDERWRITING → APPROVED` or `DECLINED`, and `APPROVED → CLOSED` at first disbursement. `servicing_state` runs `NOT_DISBURSED → CURRENT`, then through `DELINQUENT_30`, `DELINQUENT_60` and `DELINQUENT_90` to `CHARGED_OFF`, with payment-driven cures back to `CURRENT` and a `CURRENT → PAID_OFF` retirement. A `CLOSED` loan is still actively servicing, and a `PAID_OFF` loan keeps `underwriting_state` at `CLOSED` permanently. Exactly one request advances both dimensions at once: the first disbursement, which performs `APPROVED → CLOSED` and `NOT_DISBURSED → CURRENT` together.

Disbursement supplies `amount_cents` against an `APPROVED` loan and is dated by the logical clock rather than by any request field, which is how a test positions a vintage. The advance is refused with `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_APPROVED` when `principal_disbursed_cents` plus `amount_cents` would exceed `approved_principal_cents`. On success, `amount_cents` is added to `principal_disbursed_cents` and `principal_outstanding_cents` is recomputed as disbursed principal less `principal_repaid_cents` less `charged_off_principal_cents`. On the **first** advance only, `first_disbursed_on` is stamped with the logical clock's date, `vintage_year` is set to the calendar year of that date, `next_payment_due_on` is set to that date advanced by 30 days, and `borrower_in_target_market_at_origination` is frozen from the borrower's `in_target_market` value as observed at that instant — this snapshot is the only field target-market lending measurement ever reads. The disbursement also adds the amount to the institution's `portfolio_principal_disbursed_cents` and `portfolio_principal_outstanding_cents`, then re-derives both reserve variants, `loss_reserve_required_cents`, `reserve_coverage_ratio` and `compliance_posture`, and advances both the loan's `version` and the institution's `version` by 1.

Payments also carry `amount_cents` and are dated by the logical clock. A payment exceeding `principal_outstanding_cents` is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. Every payment adds its amount to `principal_repaid_cents` and reduces `principal_outstanding_cents` correspondingly. Beyond that the behaviour depends on where the loan stands. A payment of at least one full installment — that is, `amount_cents` at or above the loan's `monthly_payment_cents` — cures a loan sitting at `DELINQUENT_30`, `DELINQUENT_60` or `DELINQUENT_90` outright and identically from all three: `servicing_state` returns to `CURRENT`, `days_past_due` is reset to 0, and `next_payment_due_on` is re-dated to the logical clock's date advanced by 30 days. A partial payment on a `CURRENT` loan reduces the balance and re-dates the installment without changing `servicing_state`. A payment that brings `principal_outstanding_cents` exactly to zero retires the loan: `servicing_state` becomes `PAID_OFF`, `underwriting_state` becomes `CLOSED`, `next_payment_due_on` is cleared and `days_past_due` is set to 0. Every payment subtracts its amount from the institution's `portfolio_principal_outstanding_cents`, re-derives the reserve variants, `reserve_coverage_ratio` and `compliance_posture`, and advances both the loan's `version` and the institution's `version` by 1.

The **servicing checkpoint** is the operations run that ages the book. It is scoped to one institution and processes that institution's loans in `created_at` ascending order, precisely so that the reserve recompute at the end of the run is order-independent and reproducible. Loans whose `servicing_state` is `NOT_DISBURSED`, `CHARGED_OFF` or `PAID_OFF` are skipped. For each remaining loan, `days_past_due` is recomputed as the number of whole days from `next_payment_due_on` to the current logical clock, truncated toward zero and floored at 0. A recomputed value of 90 days or more assigns `DELINQUENT_90`; at least 60 but under 90 assigns `DELINQUENT_60`; at least 30 but under 60 assigns `DELINQUENT_30`; and under 30 days leaves `servicing_state` unchanged. Exactly 30 days past due lands in `DELINQUENT_30`, while 29 days leaves the loan `CURRENT`. Buckets may be skipped in a single run — a `CURRENT` loan can go straight to `DELINQUENT_60` or `DELINQUENT_90`, and a `DELINQUENT_30` loan straight to `DELINQUENT_90` — and the assignment never moves a loan to a *lower* bucket: only a payment can do that. Every loan the run re-buckets has its `days_past_due` stored. The same run also evaluates the certification clock: when the institution's `certification_state` is `CERTIFIED`, its `recertification_due_at` is not null, and the logical clock has reached or passed that instant, the institution moves `CERTIFIED → RECERT_DUE`. The run finishes by recomputing both reserve variants, then `reserve_coverage_ratio` and `compliance_posture`, in that order, and advances the institution's `version` by 1.

The **required loan-loss reserve** is what the run recomputes, and both bases are always produced. Under the migration-analysis basis, roll rates are applied to *current* outstanding principal: 2 percent for a `CURRENT` loan, 10 percent for `DELINQUENT_30`, 35 percent for `DELINQUENT_60` and 75 percent for `DELINQUENT_90`. Each qualifying loan's `principal_outstanding_cents` is multiplied by the roll rate for its bucket, the exact products are summed across the four live buckets, and the sum is rounded half up to whole cents once, giving `loss_reserve_required_migration_cents`; loans at `NOT_DISBURSED`, `PAID_OFF` or `CHARGED_OFF` contribute nothing. Under the static-pool basis, vintage cumulative loss rates are applied to *originally disbursed* principal and the cohort never shrinks: a loan whose `vintage_year` equals the calendar year of the current logical clock carries a rate of 4.5 percent, and every older vintage carries 3 percent. Each loan with `principal_disbursed_cents` above zero contributes that figure multiplied by its vintage rate, the exact products are summed, and the sum is rounded half up to whole cents once, giving `loss_reserve_required_static_cents` — loans already `PAID_OFF` or `CHARGED_OFF` remain in this base. Consider a book of one 1,000,000-cent `CURRENT` loan and one 2,000,000-cent `DELINQUENT_90` loan, both first disbursed in the clock's calendar year: the migration basis requires 1,520,000 cents and the static-pool basis requires 135,000 cents on identical data. `loss_reserve_required_cents` then takes whichever variant the institution's `reserve_method` names, `reserve_coverage_ratio` is `loss_reserve_balance_cents` divided by `loss_reserve_required_cents` rounded half up to four decimal places — pinned to 1.0000 when the required figure is 0 — and `compliance_posture` falls out of the bands in §2.11.

Charging a loan off is the sharpest event in the system, and it is the head of a four-link cascade. Only a `ComplianceAnalyst` may do it, only against a loan whose `servicing_state` is `DELINQUENT_90` — any other position is refused with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_DELINQUENT_90` — and the request carries a `charge_off_reason`. The first link writes the loan: the entire `principal_outstanding_cents` moves into `charged_off_principal_cents`, `principal_outstanding_cents` becomes 0, `servicing_state` becomes `CHARGED_OFF` and `underwriting_state` becomes `CLOSED`. The second link, caused by the outstanding balance changing, writes the institution: the reserve absorbs the write-off first, so `loss_reserve_balance_cents` becomes the greater of zero and the prior balance less the charged-off principal, and `reserve_deficit_cents` records the portion that could not be absorbed — the greater of zero and the charged-off principal less the balance as it stood before absorption. `portfolio_principal_outstanding_cents` is then recomputed, both reserve variants are recomputed, `reserve_coverage_ratio` is recomputed, and if the new ratio is below 0.8000 the institution's `compliance_posture` becomes `PROBATION`. The third link, caused by the posture change, suspends federal money: every award application of that institution whose `state` is `DISBURSING` moves `DISBURSING → DRAW_SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` recorded as `POSTURE_PROBATION`. The fourth link, caused by each suspension, freezes the match: every matching-fund commitment of each newly suspended award whose `status` is `PLEDGED` moves `PLEDGED → HELD`. The charge-off advances both the loan's `version` and the institution's `version` by 1, and each cascaded award and commitment row advances its own `version` as well.

### 3.7. Impact Data Collection and the Institution Impact Ledger

Impact data comes from borrowers, one report per reporting period per loan. Filing a report carries `expected_loan_version` — the optimistic-concurrency token described in §2.7 — together with `reporting_period_end` marking the end of the outcome period covered, and the four outcome counts: `jobs_created` and `jobs_retained`, both expressed as full-time-equivalent figures, `housing_units` financed or preserved, and `businesses_financed`. None of the four may be negative. The report is created in `state` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_count` 0, `dispute_reason` null, `verified_at` null, and `borrower_id` copied from the loan's borrower of record rather than taken from the request. Filing a report advances the loan's `version` by 1. A `Borrower` may only file against its own loan, and an `InstitutionOfficer` only within its own institution, per §2.1.

Filing supersedes. When an earlier report of the same loan carries the same `reporting_period_end` and sits in `state` `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `DISPUTED`, that earlier report moves to `SUPERSEDED`. The consequence for the institution's ledger depends on where the superseded report stood: only a report that was `VERIFIED` had ever entered the counters, so only in that case are its four counts subtracted from `jobs_created_total`, `jobs_retained_total`, `housing_units_total` and `businesses_financed_total`, and only in that case is the goal-attainment figure recomputed. A report superseded out of `SUBMITTED` or out of `DISPUTED` leaves the counters exactly as they were.

A `ComplianceAnalyst` accepts or challenges each report. Verification moves it `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at` from the logical clock, and increases each of the institution's four counters by this report's corresponding field. A dispute records the analyst's `dispute_reason`, clears `verified_at` back to null, and is permitted from two positions with deliberately different ledger consequences: `VERIFIED → DISPUTED` subtracts exactly the same four values that verification added, reversing the entry precisely, while `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` leaves the counters untouched because an unverified report never entered them. A disputed report is corrected by the borrower, who supplies fresh `jobs_created`, `jobs_retained`, `housing_units` and `businesses_financed` values that overwrite the stored ones; the correction moves the report `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`, increments `revision_count` by 1 and clears `dispute_reason` to null. Correcting a report that is not `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DISPUTED`.

Because verification and dispute are exact compensating actions, the loop `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED → DISPUTED → SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` may run any number of times without drift, and the following invariant must hold after every single request: each of the institution's four totals equals the sum of the corresponding field over the reports in `state` `VERIFIED` whose loan belongs to that institution. Reports in `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded, and no transition other than verification, dispute-from-verified and supersede-of-a-verified-row ever touches the counters.

Each of those three counter-moving events also refreshes the award side. Every award application of the institution whose `state` is `AWARDED`, `DISBURSING` or `DRAW_SUSPENDED` has its `goal_attainment_pct` recomputed as one hundred multiplied by the sum of the institution's `jobs_created_total` and `housing_units_total`, divided by the greater of 1 and the sum of that award's `performance_goal_jobs` and `performance_goal_housing_units`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The floor of 1 in the divisor is what keeps an award with both goals set to zero from dividing by zero; jobs retained and businesses financed do not enter this measure. Verification and dispute each advance the report's `version` and the institution's `version` by 1.

### 3.8. Target-Market Lending Measurement and Recertification

Certification is provisional in practice, and the proof is a periodic measurement of how much of the lender's disbursed lending actually reached its target market. A `ComplianceAnalyst` cuts a measurement over a half-open window bounded by an inclusive `period_start` and an exclusive `period_end`, and names the `weighting_basis` — `DOLLAR_WEIGHTED` or `COUNT_WEIGHTED` — that will decide the verdict. `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, or the cut is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`, and `period_end` must not be later than the current logical clock, or it is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_END_IN_FUTURE` — an institution cannot be measured over a period that has not finished.

Each weighting basis forms its own independent series, and the overlap rule is scoped accordingly. A new window is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when it intersects the window of any existing measurement of the same institution **and the same** `weighting_basis` whose `state` is `DRAFT` or `FINALIZED`. Two consequences follow directly: adjacency is permitted, so a new window whose `period_start` equals an existing window's `period_end` is accepted; and a window that duplicates an existing one under the *other* basis is also accepted, which is precisely how the same period is measured both ways for comparison. Measurements in `state` `VOIDED` are excluded from the overlap candidate set entirely. A new measurement is created in `state` `DRAFT` with `verdict` `PENDING`, `measured_loan_count`, `target_market_loan_count`, `measured_principal_cents` and `target_market_principal_cents` all 0, `dollar_weighted_pct`, `count_weighted_pct` and `selected_pct` all null, and `void_reason` and `finalized_at` null.

Finalization is where the arithmetic happens, and the input set is pinned. It comprises the loans of the measured institution that have ever been disbursed — that is, whose `first_disbursed_on` is not null — and whose `first_disbursed_on` falls at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. `servicing_state` is deliberately **not** filtered: loans that have since been paid off or charged off remain in the set. Loans never disbursed are excluded, whether they are still `SCREENING`, in `UNDERWRITING`, `DECLINED`, or `APPROVED` but undrawn. Target-market membership reads **only** the origination snapshot `borrower_in_target_market_at_origination` and never the borrower's present `in_target_market` value. If the input set is empty, finalization is refused with `ERR_NO_MEASURABLE_LOANS`, and finalizing a measurement that is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_NOT_DRAFT`.

Given the input set, `measured_loan_count` is the number of loans in it and `target_market_loan_count` is the number of those whose origination snapshot is true; `measured_principal_cents` is the sum of `principal_disbursed_cents` across the set and `target_market_principal_cents` is that sum restricted to the loans whose origination snapshot is true. `dollar_weighted_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `target_market_principal_cents`, divided by `measured_principal_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `count_weighted_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `target_market_loan_count`, divided by `measured_loan_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Both are always stored. `selected_pct` is then whichever of the two the measurement's `weighting_basis` names, and `verdict` is `PASS` when `selected_pct` is at least 60.00 and `FAIL` otherwise — the threshold is inclusive at exactly 60.00, and the comparison uses the already-rounded percentage. That the two bases can diverge is not hypothetical: a portfolio of one 6,000,000-cent target-market loan and one 4,000,000-cent loan outside the target market yields 60.00 under dollar weighting and 50.00 under count weighting, which is `PASS` against `FAIL` on identical data. Finalization moves the measurement `DRAFT → FINALIZED`, stamps `finalized_at` from the logical clock, copies `selected_pct` into the institution's `target_market_lending_pct`, and advances both the measurement's `version` and the institution's `version` by 1.

Recertification hangs off that verdict. A verdict of `PASS` sets the institution's `recertification_due_at` to the measurement's `period_end` advanced by 1,095 days — the same three-year interval used at initial certification — and, when the institution was standing at `RECERT_DUE`, returns it `RECERT_DUE → CERTIFIED`. A verdict of `FAIL` on an institution standing at `RECERT_DUE` moves it `RECERT_DUE → DECERTIFIED`, which is terminal. Decertification does not retroactively unwind money already flowing: award applications already in `DISBURSING` or `DRAW_SUSPENDED` keep the state they are in. What it does is close the door on new federal money, because award submission requires a `CERTIFIED` institution and a decertified lender is refused with `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_CERTIFIED`. An institution reaches `RECERT_DUE` in the first place through the servicing checkpoint described in §3.6, which moves it `CERTIFIED → RECERT_DUE` once the logical clock reaches `recertification_due_at`.

A draft measurement that was cut wrongly can be withdrawn rather than finalized. Voiding requires the measurement to be `DRAFT`, carries a `void_reason`, moves it `DRAFT → VOIDED`, and releases the window: because voided rows leave the overlap candidate set, an overlapping period may then be cut afresh. Voiding advances the measurement's `version` by 1 and touches nothing on the institution.

### 3.9. Federal Awards, Matching Funds and Draw Control

A federal award application declares the `program` applied for — one of `CDFI_PROGRAM_FA`, `CDFI_PROGRAM_TA`, `NEW_MARKETS` or `CMF` — the `requested_amount_cents`, which must be strictly positive, and the `match_ratio_pct`, the required non-federal match expressed as a percentage of the request to two decimal places between 0.00 and 200.00 inclusive. It also declares what the money is supposed to achieve: `performance_goal_jobs` and `performance_goal_housing_units`, neither of which may be negative, over a performance period bounded by an inclusive `period_start` and an exclusive `period_end` that must be strictly after it, on pain of `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`. At creation, `required_match_cents` is computed as `requested_amount_cents` multiplied by `match_ratio_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents; `match_eligible_cents` starts at 0 and `match_shortfall_cents` therefore starts equal to `required_match_cents`. The application is created in `state` `DRAFT` with `awarded_amount_cents` 0, `goal_attainment_pct` 0.00, `suspension_reason` null and `decided_by_user_id` null.

Matching funds are recorded one pledge at a time. A pledge names its `source_name`, its `source_type` — one of `CASH_PRIVATE`, `CASH_PUBLIC_NON_FEDERAL`, `IN_KIND`, `DEFERRED_PLEDGE` or `FEDERAL` — the strictly positive face `amount_cents`, and the `committed_on` date on which the pledge was executed. A pledge is created with `status` `PLEDGED`, `eligible_cents` 0, `verified_by_user_id` null and `withdraw_reason` null. Pledges may be recorded while the award is in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `AWARDED` or `DISBURSING`, which means a source pledged after the draw window has opened starts out unsettled and matters for the activation gate below.

Verification is what converts a face amount into eligible match, and the haircut depends entirely on the source class. Cash from private sources and cash from public non-federal sources count in full, at a factor of 1.00. In-kind contributions count at 0.50, and deferred pledges at 0.75. Federal sources count at 0.00 and are in fact never verifiable at all: verification of a commitment whose `source_type` is `FEDERAL` is refused with `ERR_FEDERAL_SOURCE_INELIGIBLE`. Verification also requires the commitment to be `PLEDGED`, failing with `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_PLEDGED`, and requires the verifying `AwardAdministrator` to present an `X-User-ID` different from the `decided_by_user_id` recorded on the award, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the commitment moves `PLEDGED → VERIFIED`, `verified_by_user_id` is recorded, and `eligible_cents` is set to the commitment's `amount_cents` multiplied by its source factor and rounded half up to whole cents.

The rounding order in the match ledger is pinned and is the **opposite** of the reserve calculation's. Each commitment is rounded to whole cents on its own first, and only the already-rounded per-commitment figures are summed into the award's `match_eligible_cents`, which counts commitments in `status` `VERIFIED` only — `PLEDGED`, `HELD` and `WITHDRAWN` commitments contribute zero. `match_shortfall_cents` is then the greater of zero and `required_match_cents` less `match_eligible_cents`. The order matters by whole cents, and the difference decides real outcomes: pledges of 20,000,001 cents of `CASH_PRIVATE`, 15,000,001 of `IN_KIND`, 10,000,001 of `DEFERRED_PLEDGE` and 30,000,003 of `IN_KIND` round individually to 20,000,001 plus 7,500,001 plus 7,500,001 plus 15,000,002, totalling 50,000,005 cents, whereas summing the exact products first would give 50,000,003.75 and round to 50,000,004 — one cent short of a 50,000,005-cent requirement.

Withdrawing a source is permitted from either `PLEDGED` or `VERIFIED` and records a `withdraw_reason`. Withdrawing a `VERIFIED` source reverses its contribution exactly: `eligible_cents` on the commitment is set back to 0, the award's `match_eligible_cents` is reduced by what that commitment had contributed, and `match_shortfall_cents` is recomputed. Withdrawing a merely `PLEDGED` source leaves `match_eligible_cents` untouched, because an unverified pledge never entered the ledger. Either path moves the commitment to `WITHDRAWN` and advances both the commitment's `version` and the award's `version` by 1; verification does the same.

Submission of an award application moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is gated twice, in this order: the institution's `certification_state` must be `CERTIFIED`, failing with `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and the application must carry at least one matching-fund commitment of any status, failing with `ERR_NO_MATCH_COMMITMENTS`. An `AwardAdministrator` then settles it with a `decision` of `AWARD` or `REJECT`. An award records `awarded_amount_cents` from the supplied value or, when omitted, from the full `requested_amount_cents`, records `decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and moves the application `SUBMITTED → AWARDED`. A rejection moves it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, clears `awarded_amount_cents` to null, and still records `decided_by_user_id`. Either outcome advances the application's `version` by 1, and the recorded decider is thereafter barred from verifying this award's matching funds.

Opening the draw window is the tightest gate in the system, and its four checks are evaluated in this order. The application must be in `state` `AWARDED`, failing with `ERR_AWARD_NOT_AWARDED`. The institution's `compliance_posture` must be `COMPLIANT` or `WATCH`, failing with `ERR_POSTURE_NOT_COMPLIANT`. Every matching-fund commitment of the award must be settled, meaning none is left in `PLEDGED` or `HELD`, failing with `ERR_MATCH_COMMITMENTS_UNVERIFIED`. And `match_shortfall_cents` must be exactly 0, failing with `ERR_MATCH_SHORTFALL`. Activation moves the application `AWARDED → DISBURSING`, clears `suspension_reason` to null and advances its `version` by 1.

Draws do not stay open unconditionally. Any reserve recompute that drives the institution's `compliance_posture` to `PROBATION` — from a disbursement, a payment, a servicing checkpoint or a charge-off — moves every award application of that institution in `state` `DISBURSING` to `DRAW_SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `POSTURE_PROBATION`, and moves every `PLEDGED` commitment of each such award to `HELD`. Repairing the reserve returns the posture to `COMPLIANT` but resumes nothing on its own. An `AwardAdministrator` must lift the suspension explicitly, and the gate to do so is strictly tighter than the activation gate: the application must be in `DRAW_SUSPENDED` and the institution's `compliance_posture` must be exactly `COMPLIANT` — `WATCH` is not sufficient here, and an attempt under `WATCH` or `PROBATION` is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_NOT_COMPLIANT`. Resumption moves the application `DRAW_SUSPENDED → DISBURSING`, clears `suspension_reason` to null, returns every commitment of that award whose `status` is `HELD` back to `PLEDGED`, and advances the application's `version` by 1.
