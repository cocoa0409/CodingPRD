# Child Care Quality Rating Improvement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

State child care agencies are asked to do two contradictory things at once: publish a single, legible quality number that a parent can compare across the street, and defend that number against every provider who believes the number is wrong. This platform is the system of record that makes both possible. It carries a licensed child care provider from enrollment, through a self-assessment backed by documentary evidence, through an on-site observation conducted with a standardized environment rating tool by a reliability-certified observer, to an awarded quality tier with a fixed validity window. That tier is not decorative. It sets the multiplier on every subsidy dollar the provider bills the state, it gates the size of the quality improvement grant the provider may draw against, it caps the coaching hours the state will fund, and it is the number families see.

Because the tier moves money and reputation, the platform treats the rating cycle as an auditable spine rather than a form. Advancement is deliberately harder than accumulating points: quality standards are grouped into three sequential advancement blocks, and a provider cannot be rated above the ceiling implied by the highest block it has fully cleared, no matter how strong its weighted score. Observation scores are admissible only from observers whose reliability certification is current on the day they score, and a second observer's scores exist purely to test the first observer's agreement rather than to contribute to the composite. Verification of claimed points is performed by state staff, not asserted by the provider. Every one of these gates exists because the downstream consequences — retroactive subsidy corrections, grant recovery, a published tier — are expensive to unwind.

The remedy side of the platform is equally deliberate. A provider that disagrees with an award may appeal it, and a provider that disagrees with what families are being shown may dispute the published data; both flows are cyclic, allowing a bounded number of information-request rounds, and both can loop back into the rating spine — a sustained appeal remands the provider into a fresh cycle carrying the ordered tier. Segregation of duties is structural: the state administrator who issues an award is never permitted to review the appeal of that award. Finally, because tier changes are retroactive by nature, the platform reconciles already-paid subsidy claims day by day against the tier actually in force, and records the signed difference as an amount owed to or recoverable from the provider.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the public family-facing reads must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role under which the principal is acting. When either header is absent from a request that requires authentication, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER`. When the headers are present and well-formed but the named role is not permitted to perform the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. When the role is permitted in general but the acting principal does not own the targeted resource, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Six roles exist, and their names must be spelled exactly as follows: `StateAdministrator`, `Provider`, `Observer`, `Coach`, `AppealReviewer`, and `FamilyViewer`.

The `StateAdministrator` is state quality-rating agency staff. They configure quality standards, run provider enrollment, verify submitted documentation, schedule and finalize observations, issue and publish awards, administer improvement grants, allocate coaching, adjudicate subsidy claims and expenditures, and drive the logical clock and the scheduled expiry sweep. When `X-User-Role` is `StateAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — for example as `awarded_by_user_id` on the rating cycle that the administrator awards — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. A `StateAdministrator` may not take appeal review actions; awarding and appeal adjudication are segregated duties.

A `Provider` is a licensed child care operator acting on its own record. When `X-User-Role` is `Provider`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a provider compare the resource's `provider_id` to `X-User-ID`, and for resources that reach the provider only through a rating cycle, the check follows `rating_cycle_id` to its cycle and compares that cycle's `provider_id`. A provider submits self-assessments and documentation, registers staff credentials, opens rating cycles, files appeals and published-data disputes, accepts or declines grants and coaching allocations, submits expenditures, and submits subsidy claims.

An `Observer` is a state-certified environment rating tool observer. When `X-User-Role` is `Observer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Observer` entity, and it must equal the targeted visit's `lead_observer_id` or its `secondary_observer_id` for a score write to be accepted. Observers enter subscale scores but can never finalize their own visit; finalization is reserved to `StateAdministrator`.

A `Coach` is a quality improvement coach who schedules and delivers on-site coaching sessions against an allocation. When `X-User-Role` is `Coach`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `coach_user_id` on the `CoachingAllocation` the administrator creates, and session writes require the acting `X-User-ID` to equal that allocation's `coach_user_id`; it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

An `AppealReviewer` is an independent hearing officer who reviews and decides provider appeals of issued awards. When `X-User-Role` is `AppealReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `reviewer_user_id` on the appeal at the moment of the first review action; from then on every further review action on that appeal must present the same `X-User-ID`, and in all cases the acting identifier must differ from the awarding administrator's identifier recorded on the cycle.

A `FamilyViewer` is a member of the public consuming published ratings. When `X-User-Role` is `FamilyViewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and it is the only role for which the `X-User-ID` header may be omitted entirely. A `FamilyViewer` sees only cycles whose publication state is `PUBLISHED`; suppressed, unpublished, and withdrawn publication snapshots are never exposed to that role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position in the result set: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside its permitted band or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC format with exactly three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, formatted identically to `created_at` and refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that resource. Client-supplied identifiers are never honoured; a create request that includes an `id` has it ignored.

One resource family is deliberately leaner. An observation score attempt carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because score rows are append-only. A correction to a subscale score never edits an existing row; it appends a new row and demotes the previous one. Every other resource family in the system — quality standard domains, providers, rating cycles, self-assessments, staff credentials, observers, observation visits, appeals, published-data disputes, improvement grants, grant expenditure lines, coaching allocations, coaching sessions, and subsidy claims — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

Versioning is not merely informational: a mutation that touches a parent resource bumps that parent's `version` too. Registering a staff credential, recording a registry response, opening a rating cycle, publishing a cycle, filing an appeal, and closing a grant all increment the affected provider's `version` by 1, because each changes something a client would have read from the provider representation. The computed credential-compliance read is the single exception to unconditional bumping: it recomputes and persists `credential_compliance_pct` before responding, and increments the provider's `version` by 1 only when the recomputed value actually differs from the stored one.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Cycle Opening and Grant Spending

Two write operations require the client to prove it has read the current state of a parent record before it may change that parent's derived balances. Both use the same rejection code, `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, returned with HTTP status 412.

Opening a rating cycle — the `POST /providers/{provider_id}/rating-cycles` request — requires a non-optional `expected_provider_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the targeted provider. If that number does not equal the provider's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This guard exists because a newly opened cycle legitimately supersedes the provider's prior active cycle once it reaches `ACTIVE`; a second create carrying a stale `expected_provider_version` would otherwise silently discard a cycle another writer has already attached to the same provider.

Submitting an expenditure against an improvement grant — the `POST /improvement-grants/{improvement_grant_id}/expenditures` request — requires a non-optional `expected_grant_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the targeted grant. A value that does not equal the grant's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Submission commits funds against the grant's remaining availability and against a per-category ceiling, so a stale `expected_grant_version` means the client's picture of `committed_cents` and `category_spend_cents` no longer describes the balances the commit would consume.

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and one supplied anyway is ignored.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and the Not-Found Families

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right. On a nested route the outer segment is checked first: for a request under a provider path the provider is resolved before the nested resource, and only if the outer resource exists does resolution proceed inward. The specific not-found code depends on which family failed to resolve. An unresolvable `provider_id` yields `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable `rating_cycle_id`, `self_assessment_id`, `observation_visit_id`, or `observation_score_id` — whether it arrived in the path or in a request body — yields `ERR_RATING_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable `observer_id`, including one supplied as `lead_observer_id` or `secondary_observer_id`, yields `ERR_OBSERVER_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable `appeal_id` or `data_dispute_id` yields `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable `improvement_grant_id`, `grant_expenditure_id`, `coaching_allocation_id`, `coaching_session_id`, or `subsidy_claim_id` yields `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable `credential_id` yields `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`. All six carry HTTP status 404.

Body-level foreign keys are resolved after the request body has passed schema checking, and they resolve into the same families: on visit scheduling, `lead_observer_id` and `secondary_observer_id` resolve against registered observers; on grant offering, the `rating_cycle_id` in the body resolves against rating cycles; on coaching allocation, `funding_grant_id` resolves against improvement grants and an unresolvable value is `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

Because most write operations are guarded by several checks at once, the order of rejection is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. The framing checks run first, in this sequence.

First, authentication: the `X-User-Role` header must be present and must name one of the six declared roles, and `X-User-ID` must be present for every role except `FamilyViewer`. A failure here is `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` with HTTP 401. Second, role authorization: the acting role must appear in the target endpoint's permitted-role list, or the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. Third, path existence: every path-templated resource must resolve, left to right, producing one of the 404 family codes described above. Fourth, ownership: for the two entity-backed roles the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the owning entity's `id` — a `Provider` acts only on resources whose provider linkage resolves to its own identifier, whether that linkage is direct, or reached through a rating cycle, or reached from a self-assessment through its cycle to the provider, or reached from a grant or allocation to its provider; an `Observer` may write scores only for a visit that names it as `lead_observer_id` or `secondary_observer_id`. A failure is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403. Fifth, segregation of duties on appeal review actions: the acting `AppealReviewer` identifier must differ from the awarding administrator's identifier stored on the cycle, and on every review action after the review has been started it must equal the identifier already recorded on the appeal; a failure is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

Only then does the request body itself come under scrutiny. Sixth, body schema and range checking on every creating or acting request produces `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 422: required fields must be present, enum-typed fields must carry a declared member, integers must respect their declared minimums, a subscale score must lie between 1.0 and 7.0 inclusive and sit exactly on a half-point step, weights and fractional shares must lie inside their declared bounds, a service month must match the four-digit-year-hyphen-two-digit-month form, a secondary observer must differ from the lead observer, child-day maps may carry only the three declared age-band keys, and a claimed-points map must supply all five domain codes with no value above that domain's point ceiling. Seventh, body foreign keys are resolved. Eighth, the optimistic-concurrency version fields described in §2.5 are compared. Ninth, uniqueness: a duplicate provider license number, a duplicate quality standard domain code, a duplicate staff registry identifier within one provider, or a second self-assessment on a cycle that already has one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` and HTTP 409. Tenth, the target resource's own state must be one from which the requested transition is declared, producing the state-family code for that entity — `ERR_PROVIDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_EXPENDITURE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`, or `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` — each with HTTP 409. Eleventh, the owning provider's own standing is checked: opening a rating cycle, submitting a subsidy claim, taking a grant lifecycle decision, and scheduling a coaching session all require the provider to be `ACTIVE`, except that a provider still in `ENROLLED` may open its first rating cycle; a `SUSPENDED` or `WITHDRAWN` provider may do none of these four, and the rejection is `ERR_PROVIDER_STATE_INVALID` with HTTP 409.

After this eleven-step ladder, the domain-specific rules run: the observation-safety family, then the referential family, then the temporal family, then the business-constraint family, then the fiscal family, in that order. Those rules are described in §3 alongside the workflows they guard, and where several of them apply to the same operation the relative ordering is stated there.

### 2.8. The Logical Clock

Every date comparison, window test, and expiry decision in this system reads a server-held **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until an administrator sets it explicitly it reads that instant; it must never fall back to the machine's actual time. A `StateAdministrator` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, which may move the clock forward or backward. Setting the clock performs no state transition and fires no cascade; it merely changes what every subsequent request believes "now" to be.

Separately, an administrator may run a **quality-rating expiry run**, an operational checkpoint that sweeps the whole system in one request. That run advances awarded cycles whose effective date has arrived to `ACTIVE`, expires active cycles whose expiry date has passed, freezes open grants whose spend deadline has passed, and recomputes every provider's `credential_compliance_pct`. The cascades each of those triggers are described in §3.

### 2.9. Money, Hours and Rounding Conventions

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents; no monetary field is ever a fractional value. Percentages and observation scores are decimal numbers. The system uses **round half up** as its only rounding mode: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero.

The precision attached to each derived quantity is fixed. Credential compliance is expressed as a percentage with two decimal places, capped so that it never exceeds 100.00. An observation composite is a number between 1.00 and 7.00 with two decimal places. An inter-rater reliability percentage has two decimal places. A weighted total has two decimal places on a 0.00-to-100.00 scale. A subsidy tier multiplier is carried to four decimal places. Cent-denominated results are whole integers.

Two rounding-order rules matter enough to state as principles. First, **single rounding at the end of an aggregation**: where a formula sums several terms and then divides or scales, the terms are summed unrounded and only the final quantity is rounded. Rounding intermediate terms first is forbidden and produces different answers. Second, **pinned rounding for per-line money**: where a formula produces one cent amount per line or per bucket and then adds those amounts, each line or bucket amount is rounded to whole cents first and the sum of those rounded amounts is never re-rounded. Section 3 states, for each calculation, which of these two regimes applies.

### 2.10. Conservation and Cascade Discipline

Several quantities in this system are conserved ledgers, and every operation that touches them must leave the ledger balanced. On an improvement grant, the awarded amount always equals the sum of committed funds, reimbursed funds, and available funds; submitting an expenditure and then rejecting it must return the balances exactly to where they were, with no drift. On a coaching allocation, the allocated hours always equal the sum of scheduled hours, delivered hours, and remaining hours; scheduling a session and then cancelling it must likewise leave that identity intact. On a provider, the cumulative subsidy correction is conserved across a reconciliation pair: the cascade that flags a paid claim for reconciliation moves the provider's correction balance, and the subsequent reconciled payment returns it to its pre-cascade value.

Cascades run to completion inside the request that triggers them; the system offers no eventual consistency and no deferred settlement. When a single operation changes several resources, every affected resource has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, not just the resource named in the URL. Section 3 spells out every hop of every cascade, including the second- and third-order hops that follow from the first.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Standards and Enrolling a Provider

Before any provider can be rated, the state must declare its quality standards. A `StateAdministrator` declares exactly five **standard domains**, one per `domain_code`, drawn from `LEARNING_ENVIRONMENT`, `STAFF_QUALIFICATIONS`, `FAMILY_PARTNERSHIP`, `ADMINISTRATION`, and `CHILD_ASSESSMENT`; each code may be declared once only, and a second declaration of the same code is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Each domain declares the `block_level` it belongs to, an integer from 1 to 3 naming its advancement block; a `max_points` ceiling that is at least 1 and at most 100; a `block_min_points` floor of at least 1 and no greater than that domain's own `max_points`, which is the raw point count the domain must reach for its block to clear; and a `weight`, a fraction greater than 0 and no greater than 1 giving the domain's share of the weighted total. The weights of the five domains must sum to exactly 1.00, and no cycle will accept a self-assessment until all five rows exist with weights summing to exactly that value.

Two further attributes distinguish observation-derived domains from self-reported ones. The `observation_linked` flag defaults to false; when it is true, that domain's awarded points come from the finalized observation composite rather than from verified self-assessment points, and the domain also declares a `min_composite_for_block` — the minimum observation composite, between 1.00 and 7.00 with two decimal places, that the observation must reach before that domain's block can clear. When `observation_linked` is false, `min_composite_for_block` is null. In the configuration the state operates, `LEARNING_ENVIRONMENT` is the observation-linked domain and `STAFF_QUALIFICATIONS` is the credential-derived domain. Administrators may browse the declared domains and narrow the list by `block_level` or by `observation_linked`; the domain list is also readable by providers and by family viewers.

Provider onboarding starts with an application registered by a `StateAdministrator`, carrying the site's `legal_name`, its `license_number`, the `county_code` of the licensed site, a `region_code` of `URBAN`, `RURAL`, or `FRONTIER` naming the reimbursement region band, a `licensed_capacity` of at least one child, and an `active_staff_fte` figure greater than zero with two decimal places representing the total full-time-equivalent count of classroom and administrative staff. That figure is the permanent denominator of credential compliance. An optional `weekly_onsite_cap_hours` value, greater than zero with one decimal place, sets how many on-site coaching hours the site is willing to host in any ISO calendar week and defaults to 12 when omitted. The license number must be unique across all providers regardless of their standing, and a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. A newly registered provider begins in `APPLIED` with `credential_compliance_pct` at 0, `reimbursement_adjustment_cents` at 0, `public_listing_visible` false, `enrolled_on`, `current_rating_cycle_id` and `current_tier` all null, and `version` 1. Providers may be listed and filtered by `status`, by `county_code`, or by `current_rating_cycle_id`.

An enrollment decision resolves the application. The request carries a `decision` of either approval or denial, and the provider must still be in `APPLIED` — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_STATE_INVALID` is returned. Approval moves the provider `APPLIED → ENROLLED`, stamps `enrolled_on` with the logical clock date, and recomputes `credential_compliance_pct`; it also re-confirms that no other provider holds the same `license_number`. Denial moves the provider `APPLIED → WITHDRAWN`, leaving `current_tier` null and `public_listing_visible` false. Either way, the provider's `version` increments by 1.

A provider does not become `ACTIVE` by administrative action. That transition happens by itself: when the provider's first rating cycle reaches `ACTIVE` with a non-null awarded tier, the provider moves `ENROLLED → ACTIVE`, `current_rating_cycle_id` is pointed at that cycle, and `current_tier` is set to that cycle's awarded tier. A provider is `ACTIVE` exactly when it holds an active rating cycle.

Later standing changes are driven by a status-change request from a `StateAdministrator`, which carries an `action` and an optional `reason`. Suspension is available only from `ACTIVE` and is a three-hop cascade: the provider's active cycle has its publication state set to `SUPPRESSED` and the provider's `public_listing_visible` becomes false; then, as a second hop, every coaching allocation of that provider whose own state is `ACTIVE` becomes `SUSPENDED`; then, as a third hop caused by each allocation's suspension, every coaching session under that allocation still in `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED` and its scheduled hours return to that allocation's remaining hours. Reinstatement is available only from `SUSPENDED` and additionally requires that `current_rating_cycle_id` be non-null and that the cycle it names still be `ACTIVE`; it moves the provider `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, restores that cycle's publication state to `PUBLISHED` when it had been published before the suspension and otherwise leaves it `UNPUBLISHED`, and returns the provider's `SUSPENDED` allocations to `ACTIVE` — but sessions cancelled during the suspension are never recreated. Withdrawal is available from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and only when the provider has no subsidy claim sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, or `RECONCILE_REQUIRED`; it moves the provider to `WITHDRAWN`, drives every non-terminal rating cycle of that provider to `WITHDRAWN` with publication state `WITHDRAWN`, and nulls both `current_rating_cycle_id` and `current_tier`. All three actions increment the provider's `version` by 1, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for a provider.

### 3.2. Staff Credentials and the Professional Development Registry

Credentialing is the one part of the rating that the state verifies against an external system of record. Either the provider itself or a `StateAdministrator` registers a staff member by supplying that person's `staff_registry_id` — the identifier the professional development registry knows them by, which must be unique within the provider and otherwise raises `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — a `role_type` of `DIRECTOR`, `LEAD_TEACHER`, or `ASSISTANT`, a `credential_level` from 1 to 6, and an `fte` share greater than 0 and no greater than 1 with two decimal places. An optional `employment_status` of `ACTIVE` or `SEPARATED` defaults to `ACTIVE`. The record is created with `registry_status` at `UNLINKED`, which contributes nothing to the numerator of compliance, and the provider's `credential_compliance_pct` is recomputed and its `version` incremented anyway.

Recording the registry's answer is administrator-only. That request carries a `registry_status` of `UNLINKED`, `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, or `EXPIRED`, and optionally `verified_on`, `expires_on`, and a corrected `credential_level`. Moving a record to `VERIFIED` requires both dates: `verified_on`, the logical-clock date the linkage was confirmed, and `expires_on`, the inclusive last date the verified credential remains valid. Each registry write recomputes the provider's `credential_compliance_pct` and increments the provider's `version` by 1. Credential records may be listed and filtered by `provider_id`, by `registry_status`, or by `role_type`, and read individually.

Compliance itself is a weighted head-count expressed as a percentage. Each role carries a minimum credential level: a `DIRECTOR` must hold level 4 or higher, a `LEAD_TEACHER` level 3 or higher, and an `ASSISTANT` level 1 or higher. The numerator is the sum of the `fte` values of the provider's staff credential records that satisfy all four of these conditions at once: the record belongs to that provider, its `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`, its `registry_status` is `VERIFIED`, its `expires_on` is on or after the logical clock date, and its `credential_level` is at least the minimum for its `role_type`. Records that are `UNLINKED`, `PENDING`, or `EXPIRED` contribute nothing, and `SEPARATED` records are excluded from the numerator but never from the denominator. The denominator is always the provider's declared `active_staff_fte`. The percentage is one hundred multiplied by the numerator and divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places, and then capped so the stored `credential_compliance_pct` never exceeds 100.00. The sum has no ordering sensitivity. This figure is recomputed on every credential registration, on every registry write, on approval of an enrollment application, on every expiry run, on the computed compliance read, and immediately before the per-domain points award is computed inside any scoring request.

### 3.3. Opening a Rating Cycle and Verifying the Self-Assessment

The rating cycle is the spine of the system, and only the provider may open one. The request names a `cycle_type` of `INITIAL`, `RERATING`, or `REMAND` and carries `expected_provider_version` as described in §2.5. Four gates apply, evaluated in this order: first the stale-version comparison, which rejects with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; then the provider's own standing, which must be `ACTIVE`, or `ENROLLED` when this is the provider's first cycle, and otherwise yields `ERR_PROVIDER_STATE_INVALID`; then the re-rating window, which requires for a `RERATING` cycle that the logical clock date be on or after the `rerating_window_opens_on` date of the provider's currently active cycle and otherwise yields `ERR_RERATING_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` — a `REMAND` cycle is exempt from that window entirely; and finally the single-open-cycle rule, which rejects with `ERR_ACTIVE_CYCLE_EXISTS` when the provider already holds a cycle in any of `DRAFT`, `SELF_ASSESSMENT_SUBMITTED`, `DOCS_VERIFIED`, `OBSERVATION_SCHEDULED`, `OBSERVATION_FINALIZED`, `AWARDED`, or `UNDER_APPEAL`. Note the asymmetry that makes this workable: a cycle in `ACTIVE` does not block a new cycle, because the whole point of a re-rating is to run alongside the rating it will eventually replace.

A cycle that passes those gates is created in `DRAFT` with `opened_on` stamped from the logical clock, with `provider_id` recording the owning provider, and with `supersedes_cycle_id` pointed at the provider's currently active cycle when one exists and left null when this is the provider's first cycle. The provider's `version` increments by 1. A cycle's `publication_status` begins at `UNPUBLISHED`, its `appeal_adjusted` flag begins as false, and its `superseded_by_cycle_id`, `awarded_tier`, `awarded_on`, `awarded_by_user_id`, `published_at`, `published_snapshot`, `observation_composite`, `reliability_pct`, `effective_date`, `expires_on`, `validity_months`, and `rerating_window_opens_on` all begin null. Cycles may be listed and narrowed by `provider_id`, by `status`, by `publication_status`, by `supersedes_cycle_id`, or by `superseded_by_cycle_id`, and read individually together with their point maps, block clearance, tier and validity fields.

The provider's next move is to submit its self-assessment, which advances the cycle `DRAFT → SELF_ASSESSMENT_SUBMITTED`. The submission carries two things. `claimed_points` is a map from domain code to a claimed integer point count; all five domain codes must be present and no value may exceed that domain's `max_points`, or the submission fails schema checking with `ERR_VALIDATION`. `documentation` is an array of evidence records that must not be empty, each record naming the `domain_code` it supports, an `evidence_type` of `POLICY`, `ROSTER`, `LESSON_PLAN`, `FAMILY_SURVEY`, or `TRAINING_LOG`, a `reference_label` identifying the document, and a `page_count`. Submission also requires that the cycle still be in `DRAFT`, that all five standard domains exist with weights summing to exactly 1.00, and that the cycle not already have a self-assessment — a second submission on the same cycle is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The created self-assessment starts in `SUBMITTED` with `return_count` at 0, `verified_points` null, `verified_on` null, and `documented_domains` derived as the set of distinct domain codes appearing at least once in the evidence array. Self-assessments may be listed and filtered by `rating_cycle_id` and by `status`, and read individually.

Verification is the state's job. A `StateAdministrator` submits a `decision` of either approval or return, optionally with a free-text `note`, and on approval with a `verified_points` map. Approval requires the cycle to be in `SELF_ASSESSMENT_SUBMITTED` and the self-assessment to be in either `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED`; the `verified_points` map must supply every one of the five domain codes with no value above that domain's `max_points`; and every domain code whose verified value is greater than zero must appear in `documented_domains`, since the state will not confirm points for a domain the provider never evidenced — a violation is `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_COVERAGE_GAP` with HTTP 422. On approval the self-assessment moves to `VERIFIED`, `verified_on` is stamped from the logical clock, the cycle advances `SELF_ASSESSMENT_SUBMITTED → DOCS_VERIFIED`, and the cycle's `domain_points` map is provisionally recomputed under the provisional scoring variant described in §3.5.

Return is the revision loop. It is available while the cycle is in `SELF_ASSESSMENT_SUBMITTED` or `DOCS_VERIFIED`, and it requires that `return_count` be less than 2; a third return attempt is refused with `ERR_SELF_ASSESSMENT_RETURN_LIMIT` and HTTP 409. A return sets the self-assessment's `status` to `RETURNED`, increments `return_count` by 1, clears `verified_points` back to null, drives the cycle back to `SELF_ASSESSMENT_SUBMITTED` when it had reached `DOCS_VERIFIED`, and clears the cycle's `domain_points` to an empty map. This loop is entirely independent of the observation revision loop described in §3.4.

Throughout the cycle, both the state and the provider may pull a **score preview** — a computed, non-persisting projection that runs the per-domain points award and the tier determination in whichever variant is asked for. The preview accepts an optional `variant` naming either the provisional or the final scoring run, defaults to the provisional run, persists nothing at all, and returns a cycle representation carrying that variant's `weighted_total`, `block_clearance`, `highest_block_cleared`, `points_tier`, and `awarded_tier` as a projection rather than as stored state.

### 3.4. On-Site Observation, Observer Reliability, and Score Admission

Observation is the part of the rating the provider cannot self-report, so the platform is strict about who is allowed to produce a number. A `StateAdministrator` registers an observer with a `full_name` and a `tool_code` naming the standardized environment rating tool the observer is certified on, either `ERS_INFANT_TODDLER` or `ERS_EARLY_CHILDHOOD`. A new observer starts in `PROVISIONAL` standing with `anchor_agreement_pct` and `reliability_certified_until` both null, and a provisional observer's scores are not admissible.

Reliability standing is changed by recording the outcome of an anchor check. That request carries a `decision`, and optionally an `anchor_agreement_pct` measuring the observer's agreement against the state anchor observer and a `certified_until` date. A certify decision whose `anchor_agreement_pct` is at least 85.00 moves the observer to `RELIABLE`, storing the agreement figure and writing `reliability_certified_until` — the inclusive last date on which that observer's scores are admissible. A certify decision whose `anchor_agreement_pct` is below 85.00 moves the observer to `LAPSED` and clears `reliability_certified_until` to null. A lapse decision moves the observer to `LAPSED` directly. A retire decision moves the observer to `RETIRED`, which is terminal: a retired observer may never be named on a new visit. Observers may be listed and filtered by `status` and by `tool_code`.

Scheduling a visit is administrator-only and is available only while the cycle sits in `DOCS_VERIFIED`. The request names a `lead_observer_id`, whose scores will produce the composite; optionally a `secondary_observer_id`, whose scores exist solely to test the lead observer's agreement and which must differ from the lead observer or the request fails with `ERR_VALIDATION`; and the on-site occupation interval, `window_start` inclusive and `window_end` exclusive. Two safety rules then apply, in order. The reliability rule requires that every observer named on the visit be in `RELIABLE` standing with a `reliability_certified_until` date on or after the calendar date of `window_start`; a `LAPSED`, `RETIRED`, or `PROVISIONAL` observer, or one whose certification ends before the visit begins, is refused with `ERR_OBSERVER_NOT_RELIABLE` and HTTP 422. That same rule is re-checked at score submission and again at finalization, so an observer whose certification lapses mid-cycle cannot slip a number into the record.

The occupation rule is then checked independently for the lead observer and for the secondary observer. Observation windows are treated as half-open intervals that include their start and exclude their end, so an existing visit that ends exactly at the new `window_start` does not conflict. The candidate set is every observation visit naming that observer as lead or as secondary whose state is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SCORES_ENTERED`, `RELIABILITY_CHECK`, or `FINALIZED`; a `CANCELLED` visit never conflicts. On top of the geometric overlap, a 48-hour cool-down buffer applies only when the conflicting existing visit belongs to the same provider: the request is refused when the new `window_start` falls earlier than the existing visit's `window_end` plus 48 hours. A violation of either the overlap test or the same-provider buffer is `ERR_OBSERVER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` with HTTP 409. A visit that survives all of this is created in `SCHEDULED` with `rescore_count` at 0, with `composite` and `reliability_pct` null, and with `finalized_on` null, and the cycle advances `DOCS_VERIFIED → OBSERVATION_SCHEDULED`. Visits may be listed and filtered by `rating_cycle_id`, by `lead_observer_id`, by `secondary_observer_id`, or by `status`, and read individually along with their composite, reliability percentage, and rescore count.

Scoring is performed by the observer, and only by an observer the visit names. Each submission carries one `subscale_code` — one of `SPACE_FURNISHINGS`, `PERSONAL_CARE`, `LANGUAGE_REASONING`, `ACTIVITIES`, `INTERACTION`, or `PROGRAM_STRUCTURE` — and one `raw_score` between 1.0 and 7.0 inclusive, sitting exactly on a half-point step. Scores are append-only: a submission records a new row whose `observation_visit_id` names the visit, whose `observer_id` names the acting observer, whose `attempt_no` is one greater than the highest attempt already recorded for that combination of visit, observer, and subscale, and whose `is_current` flag is true; the previously current row for that same combination has its `is_current` set to false. The first attempt for a combination carries `attempt_no` 1. Score rows may be listed and filtered by `observation_visit_id`, by `observer_id`, by `subscale_code`, or by `is_current`, and read individually.

Score submissions drive the visit through its states without any explicit administrative action. The first accepted score advances the visit `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, and that first score is admitted only when the logical clock instant is at or after `window_start`. Once the lead observer holds a current row for all six subscale codes the visit advances `IN_PROGRESS → SCORES_ENTERED` and the visit's `composite` is computed. Once the secondary observer likewise holds a current row for all six codes — which can only happen when the visit named a `secondary_observer_id` — the visit advances `SCORES_ENTERED → RELIABILITY_CHECK` and the visit's `reliability_pct` is computed. From `RELIABILITY_CHECK`, either observer may replace a subscale score; that submission returns the visit `RELIABILITY_CHECK → SCORES_ENTERED`, appends the new attempt row, demotes the prior row, increments `rescore_count` by 1, and recomputes both the composite and the reliability percentage. This rescore loop is bounded: a submission from `RELIABILITY_CHECK` is admitted only while `rescore_count` is less than 2, and the third rescore is refused with `ERR_RESCORE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409.

The composite is the lead observer's average, and only the lead observer's. For each of the six subscale codes the system takes the current row for that subscale under the lead observer — the row with the highest `attempt_no`, and where two rows somehow share the highest attempt number, the one with the later `created_at`. Secondary observer rows never enter the composite. If fewer than six such rows exist, the computation cannot proceed and the guarded operation is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_SCORES_INCOMPLETE`. Otherwise the six raw scores are added together without any intermediate rounding, the sum is divided by six, and only that quotient is rounded half up to two decimal places. Rounding the individual subscale values first is forbidden.

Inter-rater reliability measures how often the two observers landed close to each other. For each of the six subscale codes the system compares the lead observer's current raw score with the secondary observer's current raw score and counts the subscale as matched when the absolute difference between the two is no greater than 1.0. The reliability percentage is one hundred multiplied by the matched count, divided by six, and rounded half up to two decimal places. Scores are admissible when that percentage is at least 83.33 — the comparison is inclusive. Because there are exactly six subscales, only seven values are reachable: 0.00, 16.67, 33.33, 50.00, 66.67, 83.33, and 100.00. Five matched subscales produce exactly 83.33 and therefore pass; four matched subscales produce 66.67 and fail. The computation requires six current rows on the lead side and six on the secondary side; fewer on either side is `ERR_OBSERVATION_SCORES_INCOMPLETE`.

Finalization is reserved to the `StateAdministrator` — an observer may never finalize the visit whose scores they entered — and it forks into two branches that carry deliberately different side effects. Both branches require the parent cycle to be in `OBSERVATION_SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`, and a visit in any state other than the two branch origins is refused with `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`. On both branches the checks run in this order: first the observer reliability rule from earlier in this section, yielding `ERR_OBSERVER_NOT_RELIABLE`; then the secondary-observer requirement, yielding `ERR_SECONDARY_OBSERVER_REQUIRED`; then the reliability threshold, yielding `ERR_RELIABILITY_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; then the score-completeness rule, yielding `ERR_OBSERVATION_SCORES_INCOMPLETE`.

The reliability-checked branch runs from `RELIABILITY_CHECK → FINALIZED`. It requires the visit's `reliability_pct` to be at least 83.33, and it requires both named observers to be in `RELIABLE` standing with certification covering the visit's `window_start` date. It stamps `finalized_on` from the logical clock, keeps the computed `reliability_pct` on the visit, propagates both the composite and the reliability percentage onto the parent cycle as `observation_composite` and `reliability_pct`, advances the cycle `OBSERVATION_SCHEDULED → OBSERVATION_FINALIZED`, and reruns the per-domain points award under the final variant.

The single-observer branch runs from `SCORES_ENTERED → FINALIZED` and is legal only below the upper tiers. It requires that the visit have no `secondary_observer_id` at all, that the tier implied by a provisional tier-determination run be no greater than 3, and that the lead observer be in `RELIABLE` standing with certification covering `window_start`. It stamps `finalized_on`, sets the visit's `reliability_pct` to null rather than to a computed value, propagates the composite onto the cycle while leaving the cycle's `reliability_pct` null, advances the cycle to `OBSERVATION_FINALIZED`, and likewise reruns the final points award. The asymmetry is intentional: a visit whose provisional run implies a tier of 4 or higher may not be finalized without a secondary observer, because tier 4 and tier 5 awards require a reliability-checked observation. That rule is enforced both at finalization and again at award time, and in both places the refusal is `ERR_SECONDARY_OBSERVER_REQUIRED` with HTTP 422.

Cancellation is the second revision loop. An administrator may cancel a visit that is still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, optionally supplying a `reason`. Cancellation drives the visit to `CANCELLED`, sets `is_current` to false on every score row that visit had accumulated, releases the observer's occupied interval so the observer is free for other visits, and returns the cycle `OBSERVATION_SCHEDULED → DOCS_VERIFIED` so a replacement visit can be scheduled. A visit in `SCORES_ENTERED`, `RELIABILITY_CHECK`, or already `FINALIZED` cannot be cancelled; `FINALIZED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal for a visit.

### 3.5. Turning Points Into a Tier: Domain Awards, Block Gates and Validity

Scoring runs in two stages that are always executed together: first each domain's awarded points are settled, then those points are folded into a weighted total, block clearances, and a final tier. Both stages exist in a **provisional** and a **final** variant, and the same stored data can legitimately produce a higher tier under the provisional variant than under the final one.

Settling a domain's points begins with the number the provider is credited with. Under the final variant that number is the verified value the state confirmed for that domain code; under the provisional variant it is the value the provider claimed. Three cases then apply. For the observation-linked domain, if the cycle has no observation composite yet the credited number is used unchanged, which is why a provisional run before finalization simply keeps the claimed points; once a composite exists, the domain's points are instead derived from it by multiplying the domain's `max_points` by an observation fraction and rounding half up to a whole integer. That fraction is 1.00 when the composite is at least 6.00, 0.80 when it is at least 5.00, 0.60 when it is at least 4.00, 0.40 when it is at least 3.00, and 0.20 otherwise. For the `STAFF_QUALIFICATIONS` domain, the credited number is capped: the cap is the domain's `max_points` multiplied by a credential fraction and rounded half up to a whole integer, and the awarded points are the smaller of the credited number and that cap. The credential fraction is 1.00 when `credential_compliance_pct` is at least 95.00, 0.80 when it is at least 85.00, 0.60 when it is at least 70.00, 0.40 when it is at least 55.00, and 0.20 otherwise. For every other domain the awarded points are simply the credited number. The result is the cycle's `domain_points` map, one whole-integer entry per domain code, and each entry is rounded to a whole integer before it enters the weighted total. All five domains must be present, and the provider's compliance percentage is always recomputed in the same request rather than read from a stale stored value. The final variant additionally requires the self-assessment to be `VERIFIED`, while the provisional variant will work from a self-assessment in `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, or `VERIFIED`.

The weighted total is then formed in a single rounding step. For each of the five domains the system divides that domain's awarded points by its `max_points`, multiplies the result by that domain's `weight`, and multiplies by one hundred; the five terms are added together unrounded and only the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places, yielding `weighted_total` on a 0.00-to-100.00 scale. From that total alone comes `points_tier`: 5 when the weighted total is at least 85.00, 4 when it is at least 70.00, 3 when it is at least 55.00, 2 when it is at least 40.00, and 1 otherwise. Every one of those comparisons is inclusive, so a weighted total of exactly 70.00 is tier 4 while 69.99 is tier 3.

Advancement, however, is gated by blocks rather than by points. For each block level 1, 2, and 3 the system marks `block_clearance` as `CLEARED` when every domain belonging to that block satisfies two conditions — its awarded points are at least that domain's `block_min_points`, and either the domain is not observation-linked, or the cycle has a non-null observation composite that is at least that domain's `min_composite_for_block` — and marks it `NOT_CLEARED` when any domain in the block fails either condition. The blocks are then folded sequentially: `highest_block_cleared` is 0 when block 1 is `NOT_CLEARED`, and otherwise the largest number K from 1 to 3 such that blocks 1 through K are all `CLEARED`. Clearing block 3 while block 2 remains `NOT_CLEARED` therefore still leaves `highest_block_cleared` at 1. Each level of block clearance implies a tier ceiling: clearing no blocks caps the rating at tier 1, clearing block 1 caps it at tier 2, clearing blocks 1 and 2 caps it at tier 4, and clearing all three permits tier 5. The `awarded_tier` is the smaller of `points_tier` and that ceiling — which is how a provider with an excellent weighted score can still be held at tier 2 because it never cleared its second block.

The two variants differ precisely at the observation gate. A provisional run consumes provisionally awarded points and treats a null observation composite as satisfying every observation-linked block minimum, so it is an optimistic projection. A final run consumes verified points and requires a non-null composite to clear any observation-linked block, so an observation-linked block whose composite falls below `min_composite_for_block` is `NOT_CLEARED` even when its points comfortably meet `block_min_points`.

Issuing the award is an administrator action against a cycle sitting in `OBSERVATION_FINALIZED`, and it carries a `score_variant` input recording which variant produced the tier. `FINAL` is the only variant permitted once the cycle has reached `OBSERVATION_FINALIZED`; a provisional-variant award is meaningful only against a cycle still in `DOCS_VERIFIED`. Six checks guard the award, evaluated in this order. First, the secondary-observer requirement: a provisional run implying tier 4 or higher against a visit with no secondary observer is `ERR_SECONDARY_OBSERVER_REQUIRED`. Second, registry completeness: no staff credential of the provider whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` may sit in `UNLINKED` or `PENDING` — every active staff record must be resolved against the professional development registry, whether resolved to `VERIFIED` or to `EXPIRED` — and a violation is `ERR_CREDENTIAL_REGISTRY_UNLINKED` with HTTP 422. Third, the compliance floor: the provider's `credential_compliance_pct` must reach the minimum for the tier the determination just produced — measured against `awarded_tier`, not against `points_tier` — where tier 1 requires 0.00, tier 2 requires 60.00, tier 3 requires 80.00, tier 4 requires 90.00, and tier 5 requires 95.00; a shortfall is `ERR_CREDENTIAL_COMPLIANCE_BELOW_MINIMUM` with HTTP 422. Fourth, the self-assessment must exist and be `VERIFIED`, or the award is refused with `ERR_SELF_ASSESSMENT_NOT_VERIFIED`. Fifth, no appeal of this cycle may be sitting in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`, or the award is refused with `ERR_OPEN_APPEAL_BLOCKS_ACTION` and HTTP 409. Sixth, the block floor: `highest_block_cleared` must be at least 1, because when block 1 is not cleared no rating may be issued at all regardless of the weighted total, and the refusal is `ERR_BLOCK_GATE_NOT_MET` with HTTP 422.

An accepted award recomputes credential compliance, settles the domain points, runs the tier determination, and then computes the validity window. The award date is the logical clock date of the awarding request. On a fresh award the `effective_date` is the first day of the month after the award date at midnight UTC with three fractional digits of zero. When the cycle carries `appeal_adjusted` as true — that is, on the rerun triggered by a sustained appeal — the `effective_date` is instead the value stored by the cycle's original award and is not moved by the appeal decision date. `validity_months` is 36 when `awarded_tier` is at least 4 and 24 otherwise. The `expires_on` instant is the `effective_date` advanced by that many calendar months and then pulled back by one day, at 23:59:59.999 UTC; because the effective date is always the first day of a month, month addition never needs day clamping. `rerating_window_opens_on` is the expiry date minus 90 calendar days at midnight UTC — the earliest date on which a re-rating cycle may be opened. The award stamps `awarded_on` from the logical clock and `awarded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and writes `weighted_total`, `points_tier`, `block_clearance`, `highest_block_cleared`, `awarded_tier`, `score_variant`, `effective_date`, `expires_on`, `validity_months`, and `rerating_window_opens_on` onto the cycle, which moves `OBSERVATION_FINALIZED → AWARDED`.

Whether the award takes effect immediately depends on the calendar. When the computed `effective_date` is already at or before the logical clock date, the cycle continues `AWARDED → ACTIVE` inside the same request; otherwise it waits, and the next expiry run occurring on or after the effective date promotes it. Promotion to `ACTIVE` is a three-part cascade. Any other rating cycle of the same provider that is currently `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, has its `superseded_by_cycle_id` pointed at the newer cycle, and has its publication state driven to `WITHDRAWN`. The provider's `current_rating_cycle_id` and `current_tier` are repointed at the new cycle, which is also what carries a first-time provider from `ENROLLED` to `ACTIVE`. And because the provider's tier has changed, every coaching allocation of that provider whose state is `ACTIVE` has its `spend_cap_cents` recomputed — a recomputation that can leave an existing allocation's cost above its own newly tightened ceiling, as §3.9 explains.

Expiry closes the loop. On an expiry run, every cycle in `ACTIVE` whose `expires_on` is strictly earlier than the logical clock date moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` — the expiry boundary is inclusive, so a cycle survives a run whose clock date equals `expires_on` exactly and expires on the first run whose clock date is strictly greater. That transition fires four hops. First, the cycle's publication state becomes `WITHDRAWN`. Second, because the cycle has left `ACTIVE`, the provider's `current_rating_cycle_id` and `current_tier` become null and `public_listing_visible` becomes false. Third, because the provider has lost its tier, every coaching allocation of that provider in `ACTIVE` becomes `SUSPENDED` and every improvement grant of that provider in `OPEN` becomes `FROZEN`. Fourth, each of those consequences has its own consequence: every `SCHEDULED` coaching session under a newly suspended allocation becomes `CANCELLED` with its scheduled hours returned to the allocation's remaining hours, and every `SUBMITTED` expenditure under a newly frozen grant becomes `VOIDED` with its amount returned to the grant's available balance. `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED`, and `WITHDRAWN` are the three terminal states of a rating cycle.

The expiry run is also where deferred promotions and deadline-driven freezes happen: awarded cycles whose `effective_date` has arrived advance to `ACTIVE` with the supersession cascade above, open grants whose `spend_deadline` has passed advance to `FROZEN` with their submitted expenditures voided, and every provider's `credential_compliance_pct` is recomputed.

### 3.6. Publishing to Families, Appeals, and Disputes of Published Data

Publication is what families see, and it is deliberately delayed. An administrator may publish a cycle's award only once the logical clock date has reached the award date plus 10 calendar days — the family-notice quiet period, inclusive of day 10 — and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_PUBLICATION_QUIET_PERIOD` and HTTP 422. Publication is also blocked while any appeal of that cycle sits in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`, which surfaces as `ERR_OPEN_APPEAL_BLOCKS_ACTION`; the quiet-period check is evaluated before the open-appeal check. Publishing drives the cycle's `publication_status` to `PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` from the logical clock, and freezes a `published_snapshot` containing the awarded tier, the `weighted_total`, the `domain_points` map, the provider's `licensed_capacity`, and its `county_code`. It also sets the provider's `public_listing_visible` to true and increments the provider's `version` by 1. That visibility flag is true exactly when the provider's `current_rating_cycle_id` is non-null and the cycle it names has publication state `PUBLISHED`.

The family-facing directory returns only cycles whose publication state is `PUBLISHED`, each carrying its frozen snapshot, and may be narrowed by `county_code` or by `tier`. A `FamilyViewer` never sees a `SUPPRESSED`, `UNPUBLISHED`, or `WITHDRAWN` snapshot. The four publication states are `UNPUBLISHED`, `PUBLISHED`, `SUPPRESSED`, and `WITHDRAWN`, and the difference matters: suppression is temporary and reversible, withdrawal is what happens when the underlying rating is gone for good.

A provider that disagrees with its award files an appeal. The filing names a statutory `grounds` of `SCORING_ERROR`, `OBSERVER_RELIABILITY`, `DOCUMENTATION_OMITTED`, or `PROCEDURAL`, and a `narrative` stating the provider's case. Filing is available while the cycle is in `AWARDED` or `ACTIVE`, only within 30 calendar days of the cycle's award date inclusive of day 30 — a later attempt is `ERR_APPEAL_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED` — and only when no appeal of that cycle is already open in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`. The new appeal is created in `FILED` with `filed_on` stamped, `info_request_count` at 0, and `reviewer_user_id`, `adjusted_tier`, and `decided_on` all null. Filing immediately drives the cycle to `UNDER_APPEAL`, drives its publication state to `SUPPRESSED`, sets the provider's `public_listing_visible` to false, and increments the provider's `version` by 1. As a consequence of that suppression, every subsidy claim of the provider still in `SUBMITTED` whose service month falls inside the cycle's validity window is held: any attempt to adjudicate such a claim is refused with `ERR_OPEN_APPEAL_BLOCKS_ACTION` until the appeal terminates. Appeals may be listed and filtered by `rating_cycle_id`, by `status`, or by `grounds`, and read individually.

Review actions belong exclusively to the `AppealReviewer` and carry an `action`, optionally an `adjusted_tier`, and optionally a `note`. Starting the review moves the appeal `FILED → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamps `reviewer_user_id` with the acting identifier; it requires that identifier to differ from the awarding administrator recorded on the cycle. Requesting information moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and increments `info_request_count` by 1, and it is available only while `info_request_count` is less than 2 — a third request is `ERR_APPEAL_INFO_ROUND_LIMIT` with HTTP 409 — and only to the reviewer already recorded on the appeal. The provider answers with its own action: responding moves the appeal `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, appending the supplied `narrative` and changing no counter, and it requires the acting identifier to be the cycle's provider. The negotiation loop `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` therefore runs at most twice.

Sustaining an appeal is the system's most consequential single action. It is available from `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires an `adjusted_tier` that is supplied and differs from the cycle's current `awarded_tier`, and requires that adjusted tier to respect the ceiling implied by the cycle's `highest_block_cleared` — unless the appeal's `grounds` are `OBSERVER_RELIABILITY`, in which case the block ceiling is waived because the observation that produced the block clearance is itself what is in dispute. The appeal moves `UNDER_REVIEW → SUSTAINED` and stamps `decided_on`, and a three-hop cascade follows. In the first hop the cycle returns `UNDER_APPEAL → AWARDED` with its `awarded_tier` replaced by the ordered `adjusted_tier`, `appeal_adjusted` set to true, and the validity arithmetic rerun while keeping the original `effective_date` — which means `validity_months`, `expires_on`, and `rerating_window_opens_on` may change while the effective date does not. In the second hop, caused by the tier change, every subsidy claim of that provider in `PAID` whose service month starts inside the cycle's validity window moves `PAID → RECONCILE_REQUIRED`. In the third hop, caused by each of those claims being flagged, the provider's `reimbursement_adjustment_cents` changes by the difference between what the claim would be worth under the reconciled variant and what was actually paid. Finally, the cycle's publication state becomes `UNPUBLISHED`, so a corrected snapshot has to be republished rather than the stale one silently persisting. The cycle path `AWARDED → UNDER_APPEAL → AWARDED` is genuinely cyclic.

Denial and withdrawal both restore the status quo, and they restore it identically. Denying an appeal from `UNDER_REVIEW` moves it to `DENIED`, stamps `decided_on`, and returns the cycle `UNDER_APPEAL → ACTIVE`; the cycle's publication state returns to `PUBLISHED` when `published_at` is non-null and otherwise stays `UNPUBLISHED`, and the held subsidy claims become adjudicable again. A provider may withdraw its own appeal from `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`; withdrawal moves the appeal to `WITHDRAWN`, stamps `decided_on`, and returns the cycle to `ACTIVE` with publication restored exactly as on a denial. `SUSTAINED`, `DENIED`, and `WITHDRAWN` are the terminal states of an appeal.

A dispute is the narrower remedy: it contests what was published rather than how the rating was computed. The provider names the `disputed_field` — one of `PUBLISHED_TIER`, `PUBLISHED_SCORE`, `DOMAIN_POINTS`, `LICENSED_CAPACITY`, or `COUNTY_CODE` — and a `claimed_value` it asserts is correct. The dispute is created in `FILED` with `filed_on` stamped, `triage_round` at 0, and `linked_appeal_id` and `resolved_on` null. Filing a dispute does not suspend publication; families continue to see the snapshot while the dispute is worked. Disputes may be listed and filtered by `rating_cycle_id`, by `linked_appeal_id`, by `status`, or by `disputed_field`, and read individually.

Disputes advance through triage actions, all of which are taken by a `StateAdministrator` except withdrawal, which belongs to the provider. Each action carries an `action` and an optional `resolution_note`. Opening triage from `FILED` requires the cycle's publication state to be `PUBLISHED` and increments `triage_round` by 1. Requesting information moves the dispute `TRIAGE → INFO_REQUESTED` and changes no counter. Reopening triage from `INFO_REQUESTED` moves it back to `TRIAGE` and increments `triage_round` by 1, and it is available only while `triage_round` is less than 3. Escalation moves the dispute `TRIAGE → ESCALATED` and is available only when the `disputed_field` is `PUBLISHED_TIER`, `PUBLISHED_SCORE`, or `DOMAIN_POINTS` — the two provider-attribute fields are never escalated — and it changes no counter. Returning from `ESCALATED` to `TRIAGE` likewise requires `triage_round` to be less than 3 and increments it by 1. Both re-entry paths into `TRIAGE` share the same ceiling, and exceeding it is `ERR_DISPUTE_TRIAGE_LIMIT` with HTTP 409, making the loop `TRIAGE → ESCALATED → TRIAGE` capped at three triage rounds.

Applying a correction is available from either `TRIAGE` or `ESCALATED` and behaves differently by field. When the disputed field is `PUBLISHED_TIER` or `PUBLISHED_SCORE`, a correction may only be applied when an appeal of the same cycle has reached `SUSTAINED` and that appeal's `decided_on` is at or before the logical clock date; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_REQUIRES_SUSTAINED_APPEAL` and HTTP 422. On success for those two fields the dispute's `linked_appeal_id` is set to that sustained appeal. When the disputed field is `LICENSED_CAPACITY` or `COUNTY_CODE`, the `claimed_value` is written onto the corresponding provider attribute and the cycle's `published_snapshot` is refreshed in place. Either way the dispute moves to `CORRECTION_APPLIED` and `resolved_on` is stamped. Rejecting a dispute from `TRIAGE` or `ESCALATED` moves it to `REJECTED`, stamps `resolved_on`, and leaves the published snapshot untouched. A provider may withdraw its dispute from `FILED`, `TRIAGE`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `ESCALATED`, which moves it to `WITHDRAWN` and stamps `resolved_on`. `CORRECTION_APPLIED`, `REJECTED`, and `WITHDRAWN` are the terminal dispute states.

### 3.7. Tiered Subsidy Reimbursement and Retroactive Reconciliation

The tier's most tangible effect is on money flowing out to the provider. Each month a provider submits a claim naming its `service_month` in four-digit-year-hyphen-two-digit-month form and a `child_days` map giving subsidised child-days by age band, with keys limited to `INFANT`, `TODDLER`, and `PRESCHOOL` and each value a non-negative integer. Only one live claim per month is allowed: another claim for the same provider and the same service month in any state other than `REJECTED` blocks the submission with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_PERIOD` and HTTP 409. The provider must itself be `ACTIVE` to submit. On acceptance the claim is created in `SUBMITTED`, its `base_amount_cents` is computed, and its `rating_cycle_id` is resolved to the cycle whose validity window contains the first calendar day of the service month, or left null when no cycle of that provider covers that day.

The unmultiplied base is straightforward: each age band's subsidised child-days are multiplied by that band's base daily rate — 4,500 cents per infant day, 3,800 cents per toddler day, and 3,200 cents per preschool day — and the three products are added to give `base_amount_cents`.

Turning that base into a payable amount requires knowing which tier was in force, and that question is answered day by day. For any given calendar day, the **covering cycle** is a rating cycle of that provider whose state is `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `EXPIRED`, whose `awarded_tier` is not null, and whose validity window contains that day with both endpoints inclusive. Cycles in `DRAFT` through `AWARDED`, in `UNDER_APPEAL`, or in `WITHDRAWN` are excluded from this lookup entirely. When several cycles cover the same day, the one with the greatest `effective_date` wins, and if those tie, the one with the later `created_at`. When no cycle covers the day at all, that day's tier is treated as 1, the unrated base multiplier.

A tier maps to a multiplier: tier 1 pays at 1.00, tier 2 at 1.05, tier 3 at 1.15, tier 4 at 1.30, and tier 5 at 1.50. The provider's `region_code` supplies a second factor: 1.00 for `URBAN`, 1.08 for `RURAL`, and 1.15 for `FRONTIER`. Which multiplier applies to the month depends on the variant. Under the **interim** variant a single multiplier is used for the whole month, taken from the tier in force on the first calendar day of the service month, so a mid-month tier change is ignored; that value is rounded half up to four decimal places and stored as `tier_multiplier_applied`. Under the **reconciled** variant the multiplier is day-weighted: the system adds up the tier multiplier for each calendar day of the service month, divides that sum by the number of calendar days in the month, and rounds half up to four decimal places. The reconciled weighting depends strictly on calendar days and never on how the child-days happen to be distributed inside the month, so a tier change effective mid-month produces a different result from the interim run on identical child-day inputs.

The payable amount is then assembled per age band with pinned rounding. For each of the three bands the system multiplies that band's child-days by that band's base daily rate, by `tier_multiplier_applied`, and by the provider's region factor, and rounds the product half up to whole cents. The three rounded band amounts are added together to give `reimbursement_cents`, and that sum is never rounded again.

Adjudication is an administrator action carrying a `decision` and, on approval, an optional `variant` naming either the interim or the reconciled run; the interim variant is the default at first approval. Approval requires the claim to be in `SUBMITTED` and applies its checks in this order: first the coverage rule, which requires the claim to have resolved a `rating_cycle_id` whose validity window contains the first calendar day of the service month and whose cycle is `ACTIVE` with publication state `PUBLISHED` — a claim submitted for an uncovered month is still accepted at submission time with a null cycle and tier 1 pricing, but its approval is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_MONTH_NOT_COVERED` and HTTP 422; then the open-appeal rule, which refuses approval with `ERR_OPEN_APPEAL_BLOCKS_ACTION` while any appeal of that cycle sits in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`; then the service-units rule, which requires the three age-band values to sum to more than zero and otherwise refuses with `ERR_ZERO_SERVICE_UNITS` and HTTP 422. An approved claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, and the reimbursement calculation writes `base_amount_cents`, `tier_multiplier_applied`, `reimbursement_cents`, and `variant_applied` recording which variant produced the number. Rejection is available from either `SUBMITTED` or `RECONCILE_REQUIRED`, moves the claim to `REJECTED`, sets `reimbursement_cents` to 0, and releases the provider-and-month uniqueness slot so a corrected claim can be filed. Claims may be listed and filtered by `provider_id`, by `rating_cycle_id`, by `status`, or by `service_month`, and read individually.

Payment settles the claim. From `APPROVED` it requires `reimbursement_cents` greater than 0 and the provider to be `ACTIVE`, and it moves the claim `APPROVED → PAID` stamping `paid_on`. From `RECONCILE_REQUIRED` — where a claim lands when a sustained appeal changed the tier under which it was originally paid — payment additionally requires the provider's `current_tier` to be non-null at payment time and requires that no appeal of the claim's cycle be sitting in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`. An approved claim whose provider lost its rating between approval and payment — through an expiry run, a supersession, or a withdrawal — is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_UNRATED` and HTTP 422 rather than paid at a stale multiplier. A reconciled payment rewrites `reimbursement_cents` under the reconciled variant and returns the provider's `reimbursement_adjustment_cents` to its pre-cascade value, so the pair of flagging-then-repaying leaves that conserved balance unchanged. `PAID` and `REJECTED` are the terminal claim states.

The provider's `reimbursement_adjustment_cents` is a signed cumulative figure: a positive balance is a retroactive correction owed to the provider, and a negative balance is an amount the state expects to recover. Appeal cascades push it up when a tier increase means past months were underpaid, and grant recovery pushes it down, as the next section describes.

### 3.8. Improvement Grants, Expenditure Compliance and Recovery

Money also flows in the other direction: the state funds quality improvement work, and it audits how that money was spent. A `StateAdministrator` offers a grant to a provider, naming in the payload the `rating_cycle_id` of the cycle whose award justified the grant, an `awarded_amount_cents` ceiling greater than zero, and a `spend_deadline` giving the inclusive last invoice date eligible for reimbursement. The grant is created in `OFFERED` with `compliance_status` at `COMPLIANT`, with `committed_cents`, `reimbursed_cents`, `available_cents`, and `recovery_amount_cents` all at 0, and with an empty `category_spend_cents` map. Grants may be listed and filtered by `provider_id`, by `rating_cycle_id`, by `status`, or by `compliance_status`, and read individually together with all money counters, both status dimensions, and the grant's `version`.

Every grant carries **two independent state dimensions**. The disbursement dimension runs through `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `OPEN`, `FROZEN`, `CLOSED`, and `DECLINED`, with `CLOSED` and `DECLINED` terminal. The compliance dimension runs through `COMPLIANT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `FINDING_ISSUED`, `REMEDIATED`, and `RECOVERY`, with `RECOVERY` terminal. The two evolve separately: a grant may be `OPEN` and `FINDING_ISSUED` at the same time, or `CLOSED` and still `COMPLIANT`.

Grant lifecycle decisions all arrive through a single decision-carrying request, but the permitted decisions differ by role. The provider accepts, declines, or remediates; the administrator reopens. Accepting is available from `OFFERED` to the owning provider only, and additionally requires that the funding cycle be `ACTIVE` with a publication state other than `WITHDRAWN`. Acceptance initialises `available_cents` to the full `awarded_amount_cents` and initialises `category_spend_cents` to zero for all four cost categories; the grant moves `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and then, when the logical clock date is at or before the `spend_deadline`, continues immediately `ACCEPTED → OPEN`, at which point expenditure submission becomes permitted. Declining is likewise available from `OFFERED` to the owning provider: the grant moves `OFFERED → DECLINED`, `available_cents` stays at 0, and any coaching allocation funded by this grant that is still in `PROPOSED` becomes `VOID`. Remediating is how a provider clears a compliance finding: it moves the compliance dimension `FINDING_ISSUED → REMEDIATED` and permits expenditure submission again once the grant is `OPEN`. Reopening is administrator-only, moves the grant `FROZEN → OPEN`, and requires both that the funding cycle be `ACTIVE` and that the logical clock date be at or before the `spend_deadline`; expenditures voided during the freeze are never restored, and the grant's `version` increments by 1.

Freezing happens without anyone asking for it. A grant in `OPEN` moves to `FROZEN` when an expiry run finds it, when the rating cycle expiry cascade reaches it, or simply when the logical clock date passes its `spend_deadline`; the precondition is that the grant be `OPEN` and that either its funding cycle is no longer `ACTIVE` or the clock date is strictly past the deadline. Freezing voids every expenditure of that grant still in `SUBMITTED`, returning each line's `amount_cents` to `available_cents` and reducing `committed_cents` by the same total, and it suspends every coaching allocation funded by that grant whose own state is `ACTIVE`.

Submitting an expenditure is the provider's action and the point at which funds are committed. The payload names a `category` of `EQUIPMENT`, `PROF_DEV`, `FACILITY`, or `ADMIN`, an `amount_cents` greater than zero, the vendor `invoice_date`, and `expected_grant_version`. Six checks apply, in this order: the stale-version comparison described in §2.5, yielding `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; the grant's disbursement state, which must be `OPEN` and otherwise yields `ERR_GRANT_NOT_OPEN` with HTTP 409; the compliance hold, which refuses submission with `ERR_GRANT_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` and HTTP 409 while the compliance dimension sits at `FINDING_ISSUED`, since a finding must be remediated first; the spend deadline, which requires the `invoice_date` to be on or before the `spend_deadline` and the logical clock date likewise to be on or before it at submission time, and otherwise yields `ERR_GRANT_SPEND_DEADLINE_PASSED` with HTTP 422; the category ceiling; and finally the grant balance. The same compliance hold also blocks adjudication of an expenditure line while a finding is outstanding.

Each cost category has a ceiling expressed as a share of the grant: equipment may consume up to 40 percent of `awarded_amount_cents`, professional development up to 30 percent, facility work up to 25 percent, and administrative costs up to 10 percent, each computed by multiplying the awarded amount by that share and discarding any fractional cent. A submission is admitted when the category's existing `category_spend_cents` entry plus the new `amount_cents` is no greater than that ceiling — the comparison is inclusive, so a submission landing exactly on the ceiling is accepted — and otherwise refused with `ERR_GRANT_CATEGORY_CEILING_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. The grant balance check requires `amount_cents` to be no greater than `available_cents`; a submission consuming exactly the remaining availability is accepted and leaves `available_cents` at 0, while a larger one is refused with `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and HTTP 422. An accepted submission creates the line in `SUBMITTED` with `reimbursable_cents` and `disallowed_cents` at 0, adds its amount to `committed_cents`, subtracts it from `available_cents`, adds it to that category's entry in `category_spend_cents`, and increments the grant's `version` by 1.

Adjudication of a line is administrator-only and carries a `decision` and an optional `note`. Approval moves the line `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and changes no money counter, because the funds stay committed. Rejection moves the line `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and unwinds the commitment exactly: `committed_cents` decreases by the line's `amount_cents`, `available_cents` increases by the same amount, and that category's `category_spend_cents` entry decreases by the same amount, so a submit-then-reject pair leaves the grant's ledger identity intact with zero drift. Rejection also drives the compliance dimension: a first rejection moves it `COMPLIANT → UNDER_REVIEW`, a second rejection on the same grant moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → FINDING_ISSUED`, and a rejection against a grant already `REMEDIATED` moves it back `REMEDIATED → UNDER_REVIEW`, making the compliance axis cyclic. Either decision increments the grant's `version` by 1. Expenditure lines may be listed and filtered by `improvement_grant_id`, by `status`, or by `category`, and read individually with their `reimbursable_cents` and `disallowed_cents`.

Reimbursement is a waterfall run by the administrator over the grant's approved lines. Only lines in `APPROVED` participate; lines in `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, or `VOIDED` are skipped and left untouched. The participating lines are processed in ascending `invoice_date` order, with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break; record identifiers are never consulted for ordering. Two running totals carry into the run: the grant-level running total starts at the grant's current `reimbursed_cents`, and each category's running total starts at that category's current `category_spend_cents` entry, which is what makes the category ceilings cumulative across successive reimbursement runs. Each line is then settled in turn. Its gross entitlement is its `amount_cents` multiplied by the match rate for its category — 0.90 for `EQUIPMENT`, 1.00 for `PROF_DEV`, 0.75 for `FACILITY`, and 0.50 for `ADMIN` — rounded half up to whole cents. Its category room is that category's ceiling minus that category's running total, and its grant room is the `awarded_amount_cents` minus the grant-level running total. The line's `reimbursable_cents` is the smallest of the gross entitlement, the category room, and the grant room, floored at zero so it can never be negative; its `disallowed_cents` is its `amount_cents` minus its `reimbursable_cents`; and the line moves to `REIMBURSED`. Both running totals then increase by that line's `reimbursable_cents` before the next line is considered. The rounding order here is pinned: each line's gross is rounded to whole cents before the three-way minimum is taken, and the per-line reimbursable values are summed afterwards without any further rounding — summing unrounded gross amounts and rounding the total instead is forbidden and produces a different answer. When the run finishes, the grant's `reimbursed_cents` becomes the grant-level running total, `committed_cents` decreases by the total `amount_cents` of the lines just processed, `available_cents` is recomputed as `awarded_amount_cents` minus `committed_cents` minus `reimbursed_cents`, `category_spend_cents` is updated, and the grant's `version` increments by 1.

Closing the grant is where compliance is finally priced. Close is available from `OPEN` or `FROZEN` and only when no expenditure of that grant is still in `SUBMITTED`. The recovery calculation considers only lines in `REIMBURSED`; rejected and voided lines contribute nothing. Two components are added. The first is the sum of `reimbursable_cents` over reimbursed lines whose `invoice_date` is strictly later than the grant's `spend_deadline` — an invoice dated exactly on the deadline is eligible and contributes nothing. The second is a professional-development shortfall penalty: the grant is expected to have reimbursed at least one quarter of its `awarded_amount_cents` in the `PROF_DEV` category, computed by multiplying the awarded amount by 0.25 and discarding any fractional cent; the shortfall is that requirement minus the total `reimbursable_cents` actually reimbursed in `PROF_DEV`, floored at zero; and the penalty is half of that shortfall, rounded half up to whole cents. Their sum is `recovery_amount_cents`, and the computation is not order-sensitive. Unspent `available_cents` is never recovered — it was never disbursed — so the grant's ledger identity still holds after close.

Closing moves the disbursement dimension to `CLOSED`. When `recovery_amount_cents` is greater than zero, the compliance dimension advances from wherever it stands — `COMPLIANT` or `UNDER_REVIEW` — through `FINDING_ISSUED` to `RECOVERY`, and the provider's `reimbursement_adjustment_cents` decreases by the recovered amount, recording a debt the state expects back. Closing also drives every coaching allocation funded by that grant whose state is `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE`, or `SUSPENDED` to `CLOSED`, and increments both the grant's `version` and the provider's `version` by 1.

### 3.9. Coaching Allocations and the On-Site Hours Ledger

Coaching is how the state spends staff time rather than money, and the allocation is the budget envelope for it. A `StateAdministrator` creates an allocation for a provider, naming the `coach_user_id` of the operator who will deliver it, a `funding_source` of either `GRANT` or `STATE_BASE`, a `funding_grant_id` identifying the improvement grant when the source is a grant and null when the source is the state base appropriation, the `period_start` and `period_end` dates bounding when sessions may be scheduled (both inclusive), an `allocated_hours` figure greater than zero with one decimal place, and an `hourly_rate_cents` billing rate greater than zero. An unresolvable `funding_grant_id` is `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_FOUND`.

Costing the allocation follows a pinned three-step order. First, `base_hours_cost_cents` is the `allocated_hours` multiplied by the `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. Second, `admin_fee_cents` is that base cost multiplied by an administrative fee rate and rounded half up to whole cents, where the rate is 0.12 when the base cost is below 50,000 cents, 0.09 when the base cost is below 200,000 cents, and 0.06 otherwise; both breakpoints are strict on their upper bound, so a base cost of exactly 50,000 cents falls in the 0.09 band while 49,999 cents falls in the 0.12 band. The fee band is keyed on the base cost and never on the total, which does not exist yet at that point. Third, `total_cost_cents` is the base cost plus the administrative fee.

Two ceilings then bind the allocation, and both are keyed to the provider's tier — with a deliberately inverse shape, because coaching exists to lift weak providers rather than to reward strong ones. The **effective tier** is the provider's `current_tier`, or 1 when the provider has no current tier. The hours ceiling is 40.0 hours at tier 1, 32.0 at tier 2, 24.0 at tier 3, 16.0 at tier 4, and 8.0 at tier 5; `allocated_hours` above the ceiling for the effective tier is refused with `ERR_COACHING_HOURS_CAP_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. The spend ceiling is 900,000 cents at tier 1, 750,000 at tier 2, 600,000 at tier 3, 400,000 at tier 4, and 250,000 at tier 5, and it is further limited by what the funding source can actually bear: the funding balance is the linked grant's `available_cents` when the source is a grant, and a flat 500,000 cents when the source is the state base appropriation. The allocation's `spend_cap_cents` is the smaller of the tier spend ceiling and that funding balance, and `total_cost_cents` above `spend_cap_cents` is refused with `ERR_COACHING_SPEND_CAP_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. The hours check is evaluated before the spend check. Because a higher tier means a tighter cap, a tier increase can leave an existing allocation's `spend_cap_cents` below its own unchanged `total_cost_cents` — which is exactly what the recomputation on cycle activation described in §3.5 can produce.

A newly created allocation sits in `PROPOSED` with `scheduled_hours` at 0, `delivered_hours` at 0, and `remaining_hours` equal to `allocated_hours`. The provider accepts it, which moves it `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and makes it schedulable; when the `funding_source` is `GRANT` the funding grant must be `OPEN` at that moment, and otherwise the acceptance is refused with `ERR_GRANT_NOT_OPEN`. Allocations otherwise move to `SUSPENDED` and back to `ACTIVE` under the provider-suspension and reinstatement cascades of §3.1 and the cycle-expiry cascade of §3.5, to `CLOSED` when the funding grant closes, and to `VOID` when an offered grant that would have funded them is declined. Allocations may be listed and filtered by `provider_id`, by `funding_grant_id`, by `coach_user_id`, or by `status`, and read individually with their cost breakdown and hours ledger.

Scheduling a session belongs to the coach, and only to the coach whose identifier the allocation records: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the allocation's `coach_user_id`. The request carries only the interval, `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`. From those the system computes the session's `scheduled_hours` as the length of the interval expressed in hours to one decimal place, and its `iso_week_key` as the ISO year-and-week of `scheduled_start` in the form of a four-digit year, a hyphen, the letter W, and the two-digit week number — for instance the thirty-eighth week of 2026 is written `2026-W38`. The provider must be `ACTIVE` for a session to be scheduled at all.

Three checks then apply, in this order. First, site occupation and weekly capacity. Coaching intervals are treated as **closed** intervals that include both endpoints, so two sessions that merely touch at an endpoint do conflict — the opposite convention from observation windows, and deliberately so, because a coach cannot physically leave one classroom and enter another in the same instant. The candidate set for the conflict test is every coaching session in `SCHEDULED` under any allocation of the same provider that carries the same `coach_user_id`; sessions in `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` never conflict. Independently of geometry, an aggregate weekly capacity test applies: the sum of `scheduled_hours` over all the provider's sessions in `SCHEDULED` plus `delivered_hours` over all its sessions in `DELIVERED`, taken across all of that provider's allocations that share the new session's `iso_week_key`, plus the new session's own `scheduled_hours`, must not be strictly greater than the provider's `weekly_onsite_cap_hours`. A failure of either test is `ERR_ONSITE_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` with HTTP 409. Second, the period bounds: both `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` must fall inside the allocation's period from `period_start` to `period_end` inclusive, and `scheduled_end` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_ALLOCATION_PERIOD` and HTTP 422. Third, the hours budget: the allocation's `remaining_hours` must be at least the new session's `scheduled_hours`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_COACHING_HOURS_CAP_EXCEEDED`. An accepted session is created in `SCHEDULED`, adds its hours to the allocation's `scheduled_hours`, and subtracts them from the allocation's `remaining_hours`.

Completion is logged by the coach with a `delivered_hours` figure, which must be greater than zero and no greater than the session's own `scheduled_hours` plus half an hour of tolerance. The session moves `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED`; the allocation's `scheduled_hours` decreases by the session's scheduled hours, its `delivered_hours` increases by the hours actually delivered, and its `remaining_hours` is recomputed as `allocated_hours` minus `scheduled_hours` minus `delivered_hours`. Cancellation may be performed by the coach or by a `StateAdministrator`, optionally with a `reason`: the session moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, the allocation's `scheduled_hours` decreases and its `remaining_hours` increases by the session's scheduled hours, and both the closed interval and the weekly capacity that session had consumed under its `iso_week_key` are released. A schedule-then-cancel pair therefore leaves the allocation's hours ledger exactly as it was. Sessions may be listed and filtered by `coaching_allocation_id`, by `status`, or by `iso_week_key`, and read individually. `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal session states.
