# Charity Accountability Rating API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Donors give away money on trust, and the organizations that publish charity ratings are the closest thing that trust has to an audit. This service is the production system behind one such rating operation: it ingests a charity's public tax filings, its own portal submissions and its audited financial statements, evaluates its governance practices against a fixed set of accountability standards, computes a weighted composite score across four methodology dimensions, and publishes a letter-style grade together with a historical trend. Because a published grade can move a charity's revenue by a material margin, the product is designed around a single premise: no figure becomes public until the rated charity has seen it, has had a bounded opportunity to correct it, and has had a right of appeal to a panel that did not produce it.

Three forces pull against each other in this domain, and most of the specification below exists to hold them apart. The first is **methodology stability**: a rating cycle is scored under the exact methodology version that was in force when it opened, and that pin survives the methodology's later retirement, so an organization is never re-graded by rules it could not have known. The second is **separation of duties**: the analyst who scores a cycle may not publish it, the administrator who seats an appeal panel may not be that analyst, and no panelist may be the cycle's analyst or its publishing reviewer. The third is **compensability**: every punitive action in the system — a waived standard, a substantiated complaint penalty, a rejected correction — has a documented inverse that returns the affected ledgers to their prior values with no residual drift.

The whole surface is mounted flat. There is no shared resource prefix: organizations, methodology versions, rating cycles, filings, governance profiles, corrections, appeals, complaints and publication records are all addressed at the root, and they are linked by foreign keys carried in request bodies and query filters rather than by nesting. Sub-resource reads exist only as reverse-lookup channels onto rows the system creates by cascade. Time is a first-class, server-controlled quantity: every deadline, grace period, comment window and policy-age check reads a single logical clock, never the host's wall clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If both headers are present but `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, or is a valid role that the target operation does not admit, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `RatingAnalyst`, `PublishingReviewer`, `AppealPanelist`, `MethodologyAdmin`, `OrganizationRep` and `PublicUser`, spelled exactly as shown.

A `RatingAnalyst` is a charity-evaluation analyst: they submit and validate filings, record governance disclosures, open rating cycles, run scoring, override dimension points, open organization review, claim and decide corrections, and triage and resolve complaints. A `PublishingReviewer` exists for one purpose, publishing a finished rating, and may never publish a rating they scored. An `AppealPanelist` sits on a three-person panel and may decide only the appeals whose panel they were seated on. A `MethodologyAdmin` is the standards administrator: they register organizations, draft, revise and adopt methodology versions, dispose public comments, waive and revoke governance standards, withdraw cycles, reverse complaint findings, seat appeal panels, run the transition sweep and drive the logical clock. An `OrganizationRep` acts for a rated charity: they submit filings, file corrections and appeals, and may comment on methodology revisions. A `PublicUser` is a donor or member of the public who reads published ratings and trends, comments on methodology revisions and files complaints; no pre-publication working artefact is reachable by this role.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `OrganizationRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Organization`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Organization` compare the resource's organization foreign key to `X-User-ID`, following one hop through a rating cycle's `organization_id` where the resource does not carry the organization directly. An `OrganizationRep` acting on a resource that belongs to a different organization is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is any of `RatingAnalyst`, `PublishingReviewer`, `AppealPanelist`, `MethodologyAdmin` or `PublicUser`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as a cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id` or `publishing_reviewer_user_id`, a correction's `claimed_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id`, a standard's `waived_by_user_id`, a dimension row's `overridden_by_user_id`, a comment's `commenter_user_id`, a complaint's `complainant_user_id`, an appeal's `filed_by_org_user_id` or a member of `panel_member_user_ids` — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer, and must lie between a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100 inclusive. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted, must be an integer, and must be 0 or greater; there is no upper bound on `offset`. A `limit` outside its permitted range, or a negative `offset`, or either value supplied in a non-integer form, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless an endpoint's own description in §4 names a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Where a different order is specified — the publication ledger by `sequence_number` ascending, an organization's filings by `fiscal_year` descending then `source_precedence` ascending then `created_at` ascending, an organization's rating trend newest-first, a profile's standard evaluations by `standard_code` ascending, a cycle's dimension rows in the fixed order `FINANCIAL_HEALTH`, `ACCOUNTABILITY`, `TRANSPARENCY`, `IMPACT` — that ordering is applied before the window is cut, so paging through a filtered read is positionally stable.

For list responses the `meta` object carries all four pagination fields: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit these four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, no client control and no reuse. Clients never supply an `id` on creation; supplying one is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was written, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Twelve of the thirteen entity types — `Organization`, `MethodologyVersion`, `PublicComment`, `TransitionGrant`, `RatingCycle`, `DimensionScore`, `FilingRecord`, `GovernanceProfile`, `StandardEvaluation`, `CorrectionRequest`, `Appeal` and `Complaint` — additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical-clock instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation came from a client request or from a cascade fired by an operation on another resource. `PublicationRecord` is the sole exception: the publication ledger is append-only, so a publication record has `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and there is no endpoint that updates or deletes one.

The `version` counter is not decorative. It is the observable evidence that a cascade fired, and it is the token the optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.7 compare against. When a single operation touches several rows, each touched row's own `version` advances independently: publishing a cycle advances the cycle's `version` and the organization's `version`; waiving a standard advances the standard evaluation's, the governance profile's and the rating cycle's; adopting a methodology advances both the adopted and the retired methodology row, plus a `version` on every organization that receives a transition grant.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, and Why Each Deadline Has Its Own Boundary Rule

There is exactly one time source in this service: a server-held **logical clock**. Every deadline, every grace period, every comment window, every policy-age comparison and every stored `*_at` timestamp reads it. Before the clock is ever set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host's wall-clock time under any circumstance. A `MethodologyAdmin` moves the clock with a test-driving operation that takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. The clock may move forward or stay where it is, never backwards: a requested instant earlier than the current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 409). Advancing the clock changes no stored row and re-arms no deadline retroactively.

Deadlines are computed once, at the moment of the transition that arms them, by adding a whole number of days to the clock reading at that moment; all subsequent comparisons are made to the second. Four windows exist, and they deliberately do **not** share one comparison rule.

The **correction window** is armed when an analyst opens organization review on a scored cycle: `correction_deadline_at` becomes the clock at that moment plus 21 days. A correction may be filed while the clock is at or before `correction_deadline_at` — the deadline instant itself is still open for filing. Publication, conversely, is permitted while the clock is at or after `correction_deadline_at`. The two windows therefore touch: at exactly `correction_deadline_at`, both filing a correction and publishing the cycle are legal.

The **appeal window** is armed when a correction is rejected: `appeal_deadline_at` becomes the clock at that moment plus 14 days. An appeal may be filed only strictly before `appeal_deadline_at`; at exactly that instant the window is shut and the filing is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). A cycle with no armed `appeal_deadline_at` at all likewise refuses appeals with the same code.

The **investigation clock** is armed when a complaint is triaged for investigation: `investigation_due_at` becomes the clock at that moment plus 30 days. It is a tracking figure and does not by itself bar any operation.

The **methodology comment window** is armed when comment opens: `comment_open_at` is the clock at that moment and `comment_deadline_at` is that instant plus the version's own `comment_period_days`, which defaults to 30 days and may be set anywhere from 7 to 120 days. A public comment is admissible while the version is in `COMMENT_OPEN` and the clock is at or before `comment_deadline_at`; the deadline instant is inclusive.

Finally, adoption imposes a forward buffer and mints grace. A methodology's `effective_from` must be at least 30 days after the clock reading at adoption time. A transition grant's `grace_until` is the adoption instant plus the adopting version's `transition_period_days`, which defaults to 90 days and may be set anywhere from 0 to 365 days. Where an organization already holds a grace deadline, its `transition_grace_until` becomes the later of the existing value and the new grant's `grace_until`.

An implementation that normalises all of these to one comparison operator will fail at least one of them: corrections are inclusive of their deadline, appeals are exclusive of theirs, the comment window is inclusive, and the publication gate is inclusive of the very instant the correction window still admits filings.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and the Pinned Rounding Order

All rounding in this service is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and every rounding operation is applied at the scale the figure declares. Ratios and weights carry **4 decimal places**: `program_expense_ratio`, `fundraising_efficiency`, `independence_ratio`, the four methodology weights and every renormalised weight, including a dimension row's `weight_applied`. Scores, deltas and penalties carry **2 decimal places**: `normalized_score`, `weighted_contribution`, `weighted_sum_provisional`, `weighted_sum_final`, `provisional_composite_score`, `final_composite_score`, `standards_penalty_points`, `data_completeness_pct`, `months_of_reserve`, `credibility_score`, `published_score`, `previous_published_score`, `score_delta` and `projected_score_delta`. Point counts — `raw_points`, `max_points`, `penalty_points`, `penalty_points_applied`, `accountability_penalty_points`, `failed_standard_count`, the four `standards_*_count` fields, `sequence_number`, `round_number`, `correction_round` and `source_precedence` — are plain integers and are never rounded.

Where a figure is a sum of products, **the order of rounding is part of the specification, not an implementation detail**. Two rules apply throughout. First, each individual term is rounded at its own declared scale *before* it enters the sum, and the completed sum is then rounded again at the same scale. Second, a renormalised weight is rounded to 4 decimal places at the moment it is derived, and it is that rounded weight — not the unrounded quotient — that multiplies the dimension's normalized score. Pooling numerators and denominators first and dividing once at the end is a defensible accounting practice that this methodology does not use, and it produces different published grades on identical inputs.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Parent Record

Two write operations in this service can silently destroy work the caller never observed, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the **parent** row's `version`. There is no other concurrency mechanism in the product: no locks, no if-match headers, and no version field on any endpoint other than the two named here.

`POST /filing-records` requires a non-optional `expected_organization_version` field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Organization` named by `organization_id`. If that value does not equal the organization's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no filing row is written. The product deliberately allows a newly validated filing to supersede the prior `VALIDATED` filing for the same organization, fiscal year and source kind, so a second submission made against an organization state the submitter has not re-read would otherwise succeed and displace data it never saw; the version check is what makes any such displacement a deliberate act.

`POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/dimension-overrides` requires a non-optional `expected_rating_cycle_version` field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RatingCycle`. If that value does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the override is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no dimension row is touched. This endpoint drives a `SCORED` to `SCORED` self-loop, so no state precondition can catch a later override that supersedes an earlier one — and complaint penalties, standard waivers, waiver revocations and correction decisions all advance the cycle's `version` between a client's read and its write. The check is the only thing standing between an analyst and a set of overrides computed from figures that no longer exist.

Every other endpoint in this service takes no version field whatsoever. Supplying `expected_organization_version` to an endpoint that does not declare it, or omitting it from one that does, is a malformed body.

### 2.8. Request Admission Order

Checks are evaluated in one fixed ascending order, and the **first** failing check determines the response. This ordering is observable and is part of the contract: when a request violates two rules at once, the lower-numbered rule's error is the one returned. The full ladder, in order:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-empty, on every endpoint without exception, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role admission.** `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and is among the roles the target endpoint admits, on every endpoint, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier appearing in the URL path resolves to a stored row, validated left to right, on every endpoint that has a path parameter. The code names the entity that failed to resolve: `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_METHODOLOGY_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUBLIC_COMMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSITION_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATING_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GOVERNANCE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STANDARD_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORRECTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PUBLICATION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.
4. **Organization ownership.** When `X-User-Role` is `OrganizationRep`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the `Organization` the target resource belongs to, reached directly or through the resource's rating cycle's `organization_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This check guards exactly four operations: `POST /correction-requests`, `POST /appeals`, `GET /organizations/{id}/filing-records` and `GET /organizations/{organization_id}/rating-trend`.
5. **Body well-formedness.** On every endpoint that takes a request body: every required input field is present, every enum value falls inside its declared set, every integer declared non-negative is non-negative, every timestamp is a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, and any unknown field is silently ignored rather than rejected. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Pagination shape.** On every list endpoint, `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of 0 or greater, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
7. **Body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, yielding `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_METHODOLOGY_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATING_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORRECTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DIMENSION_SCORE_NOT_FOUND` — the code belonging to the entity whose identifier failed to resolve — each HTTP 404. This check guards `POST /rating-cycles`, `POST /filing-records`, `POST /governance-profiles`, `POST /public-comments`, `POST /correction-requests`, `POST /appeals`, `POST /complaints` and `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/dimension-overrides`.
8. **State precondition.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, and where a transition names a related resource in its preconditions that related resource is in its required state too. This applies to every action endpoint, and the code names the entity: `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_FILING_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_METHODOLOGY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_GOVERNANCE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_STANDARD_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CORRECTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`, each HTTP 409.
9. **Stale parent version.** The supplied `expected_organization_version` or `expected_rating_cycle_version` equals the parent's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). This applies to exactly two endpoints: `POST /filing-records` and `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/dimension-overrides`.
10. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the user who performed the conflicting earlier step: a publishing reviewer may not be the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id`, and the `MethodologyAdmin` who seats an appeal panel may not be that same analyst either. Violations return `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) on the two endpoints this guards: `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/publish` and `POST /appeals/{appeal_id}/assign-panel`.
11. **Assigned-analyst identity.** The acting `RatingAnalyst`'s `X-User-ID` equals the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id`, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ANALYST` (403). This guards five endpoints: `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/score`, `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/open-org-review`, `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/dimension-overrides`, `POST /correction-requests/{correction_request_id}/claim` and `POST /correction-requests/{correction_request_id}/decide`.
12. **Related-resource state.** The related resource is in the specific state this operation depends on: the `MethodologyVersion` a new rating cycle pins is `ADOPTED` — or is `RETIRED` while `use_transition_grant` is true, in which case the verdict is deferred to check 13; the `CorrectionRequest` an appeal contests is `REJECTED`; and a cycle being scored has a `GovernanceProfile` whose `evaluation_status` is `EVALUATED`. The codes are `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_ADOPTED`, `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_REJECTED` and `ERR_GOVERNANCE_NOT_EVALUATED`, each HTTP 409, on `POST /rating-cycles`, `POST /appeals` and `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/score` respectively.
13. **Transition grace.** When a new rating cycle pins a `MethodologyVersion` whose status is `RETIRED`, `use_transition_grant` must be true **and** the organization must hold a `TransitionGrant` whose `superseded_version_label` equals that version's `version_label`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `grace_until` is at or after the logical clock. Otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_GRACE_UNAVAILABLE` (409), on `POST /rating-cycles`. A retired pin submitted *without* `use_transition_grant` never reaches this check, because check 12 has already rejected it as `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_ADOPTED`.
14. **Clock monotonicity.** The requested clock value is at or after the current logical clock, else `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (409), on `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`.
15. **Methodology effectivity.** The `MethodologyVersion` pinned to a new rating cycle is effective at the logical clock: its `effective_from` is at or before the clock, and either its `effective_until` is null or the clock is at or before it. Otherwise `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (409), on `POST /rating-cycles`. A `RETIRED` version already admitted by check 13's grant is exempt from this check, because a transition grant is precisely a licence to pin an interval that has closed.
16. **Appeal window.** An appeal is filed strictly before the cycle's `appeal_deadline_at`, else `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), on `POST /appeals`.
17. **Methodology window conflict.** At adoption, `effective_from` is at least 30 days after the logical clock; `effective_until`, when supplied, is strictly after `effective_from`; and the closed interval from `effective_from` to `effective_until` does not overlap the interval of any other `ADOPTED` version except the one named by `supersedes_version_id`, which this adoption is closing at `effective_from`. An `ADOPTED` version whose `effective_until` is null is open-ended and is therefore never overlapped by a successor's window — the adoption cascade closes it at the successor's `effective_from`, so only the clock buffer binds. Violations return `ERR_METHODOLOGY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409) on `POST /methodology-versions/{methodology_version_id}/adopt`, which is the sole owner of every effectivity-window check in the product.
18. **Cycle period overlap.** A new rating cycle's half-open interval from `period_start` up to but excluding `period_end` does not overlap the interval of any non-`WITHDRAWN` cycle of the same organization; adjacency, where a previous cycle's `period_end` equals the new `period_start`, is allowed with no buffer. Otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on `POST /rating-cycles`.
19. **Weight normalisation.** `weight_financial_health`, `weight_accountability`, `weight_transparency` and `weight_impact` sum to exactly 1.0000 and each one is strictly greater than 0.0000, else `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED` (422), on `POST /methodology-versions`.
20. **Unique tax id.** `ein` is unique across all organizations, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_EIN` (409), on `POST /organizations`.
21. **Unique methodology label.** `version_label` is unique across all methodology versions, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_VERSION_LABEL` (409), on `POST /methodology-versions`.
22. **One governance profile per cycle.** At most one `GovernanceProfile` exists per rating cycle, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_GOVERNANCE_PROFILE` (409), on `POST /governance-profiles`.
23. **Comment period open.** A public comment is submitted while its methodology version is `COMMENT_OPEN` and the logical clock is at or before `comment_deadline_at`, else `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409), on `POST /public-comments`.
24. **Comments disposed.** A methodology version is adopted only when its `unresolved_comment_count` is zero — every public comment has been disposed to `INCORPORATED` or `DECLINED` — else `ERR_UNRESOLVED_COMMENTS` (409), on `POST /methodology-versions/{methodology_version_id}/adopt`.
25. **Correction round limit.** A correction is accepted only while the cycle's `correction_round` is below the maximum of 2, and the request's `round_number` equals the cycle's `correction_round` plus 1. Otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_ROUND_LIMIT` (409), on both `POST /correction-requests` and `POST /correction-requests/{correction_request_id}/decide`.
26. **One live appeal per cycle.** At most one appeal exists per rating cycle in a state other than `DISMISSED`, else `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED` (409), on `POST /appeals`.
27. **Panel composition.** `panel_member_user_ids` holds exactly three distinct identifiers, none of them equal to the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id` or its `publishing_reviewer_user_id`, else `ERR_PANEL_COMPOSITION_INVALID` (422), on `POST /appeals/{appeal_id}/assign-panel`.
28. **Penalty range.** On a substantiated finding, `penalty_points` is an integer between 1 and 15 inclusive and does not exceed the ceiling for the severity tier derived from the *investigated* credibility score; on an unsubstantiated finding `penalty_points` is absent or zero. Otherwise `ERR_PENALTY_POINTS_RANGE` (422), on `POST /complaints/{complaint_id}/resolve`.
29. **Expense identity.** `total_expense_cents` equals the sum of `program_expense_cents`, `fundraising_expense_cents` and `admin_expense_cents`, and `joint_cost_allocated_cents` is at most `program_expense_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_EXPENSE_COMPONENTS_MISMATCH` (422), on both `POST /filing-records` and `POST /filing-records/{filing_record_id}/validate`.
30. **Filing sufficiency.** At least one `VALIDATED` filing record exists for the cycle's organization and the cycle's own `fiscal_year`, else `ERR_NO_VALIDATED_FILING` (409), on `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/score`.
31. **Completeness gate.** A cycle is published only when its `data_completeness_pct` is at least 60.00, else `ERR_DATA_COMPLETENESS_TOO_LOW` (409), on `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/publish`. An `UNRATED` cycle may be scored and reviewed but never published.
32. **Publishability.** A cycle is published only when the logical clock is at or after `correction_deadline_at`, no correction of the cycle is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW`, and no appeal of the cycle is `FILED` or `PANEL_ASSIGNED`. Otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_PUBLISHABLE` (409), on `POST /rating-cycles/{rating_cycle_id}/publish`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating explicitly. Because role admission (2) precedes path existence (3), a caller with the wrong role never learns whether a resource exists. Because the state precondition (8) precedes the stale-version check (9), an override aimed at a `WITHDRAWN` cycle reports `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID` even when the supplied `expected_rating_cycle_version` is also wrong.

### 2.9. A Flat Surface, Reverse Reads and Path Resolution

No resource in this service is nested for the purpose of writing. Every write targets a root-level collection, and relationships travel in request bodies: a filing names its `organization_id`, a rating cycle names its `organization_id` and its `methodology_version_id`, a governance profile names its `rating_cycle_id`, a public comment names its `methodology_version_id`, a correction names its `rating_cycle_id`, an appeal names both its `rating_cycle_id` and the `correction_request_id` it contests, and a complaint names its `organization_id`. Rows the system creates by cascade — dimension scores, standard evaluations, publication records, transition grants — are never posted by a client at all; they are reached only through reverse reads.

Where a path does carry more than one segment of identifier, resolution proceeds strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the 404 code. A read of a governance profile's standard evaluations under a `governance_profile_id` that does not exist answers `ERR_GOVERNANCE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, never a 200 with an empty array. A read of a cycle's dimension scores under an unknown `rating_cycle_id` answers `ERR_RATING_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`. A read of an organization's filings under an unknown organization answers `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`. Filters supplied alongside such a read narrow a resolved parent's children; they never substitute for resolving the parent.

The path variables the surface uses are named after the entity they address: `organization_id`, `methodology_version_id`, `public_comment_id`, `transition_grant_id`, `filing_record_id`, `governance_profile_id`, `standard_evaluation_id`, `rating_cycle_id`, `correction_request_id`, `appeal_id`, `complaint_id` and `publication_record_id`. Two reverse reads — an organization's filings and a cycle's dimension scores — spell their leading variable simply as `id`, which addresses the same organization and rating cycle respectively. A path variable that resolves to no row yields that entity's own not-found code, listed in §5.

Reverse reads accept the filters their §4 rows declare and no others: organizations by `org_type` and `trend_direction`; an organization's filings by `fiscal_year`, `source_kind` and `status`; methodology versions by `status` and by `supersedes_version_id`, which is the reverse lookup for the self-referential supersession chain; a version's comments by `status` and `commenter_kind`; transition grants by `organization_id`, `methodology_version_id` and `status`; governance profiles by `rating_cycle_id` and `evaluation_status`; standard evaluations by `standard_code` and `result`; rating cycles by `organization_id`, `methodology_version_id`, `status` and `fiscal_year`; dimension scores by `dimension` and `status`; corrections by `rating_cycle_id` and `status`; appeals by `rating_cycle_id`, `correction_request_id` and `status`; complaints by `organization_id`, `penalized_rating_cycle_id`, `status` and `category`; and publication records by `organization_id` and `rating_cycle_id`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties as a Standing Constraint

The credibility of a published rating rests on the claim that no single person carried it from evidence to publication. Three constraints enforce that claim, and each has its own error code so that a rejection is diagnosable.

Only the analyst named in a cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id` may score it, override its dimension points, open its organization review, or claim and decide its corrections; any other `RatingAnalyst` attempting those operations is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ANALYST`. Publication is reserved to a `PublishingReviewer`, and that reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id`; a reviewer who also scored the cycle is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Appeal panels are seated by a `MethodologyAdmin` who is likewise barred from being that analyst, under the same code, and the three seated identifiers in `panel_member_user_ids` must exclude both the analyst and the cycle's `publishing_reviewer_user_id` — a composition failure is `ERR_PANEL_COMPOSITION_INVALID`. Finally, an `AppealPanelist` may decide only an appeal whose `panel_member_user_ids` contains their own `X-User-ID`; a panelist deciding an appeal they were not seated on is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, the same code a wholly inadmissible role receives.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Methodology Versions: Drafting, Public Comment and Revision

A rating methodology is a versioned document, and everything the system computes is traceable to exactly one version of it. A `MethodologyAdmin` drafts a new one by supplying a `version_label` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across every methodology ever drafted — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VERSION_LABEL` — together with the four dimension weights `weight_financial_health`, `weight_accountability`, `weight_transparency` and `weight_impact`. Each weight is a number between 0.0000 and 1.0000 carried to four decimal places, each must be strictly greater than zero, and the four must sum to exactly 1.0000; anything else is `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED`. Three further inputs are optional and take documented defaults when omitted: `dimension_floor_score`, the minimum normalized score every included dimension must reach for the two highest grades, defaults to 40.0 and may be set anywhere from 0.00 to 100.00; `comment_period_days` defaults to 30 and is constrained to 7 through 120; and `transition_period_days` defaults to 90 and is constrained to 0 through 365. A draft may also name the `supersedes_version_id` of the currently adopted version it is intended to replace — this is null only for the very first methodology ever created — and may carry a `revision_note`. A freshly drafted version has `status` `DRAFT`, a `comment_count` of 0 and an `unresolved_comment_count` of 0, and all of `comment_open_at`, `comment_deadline_at`, `comment_closed_at`, `adopted_at`, `retired_at`, `effective_from` and `effective_until` are null.

Opening the public comment window moves the version `DRAFT` → `COMMENT_OPEN`; attempting it on a version in any other status is `ERR_METHODOLOGY_STATE_INVALID`. The transition stamps `comment_open_at` with the logical clock, derives `comment_deadline_at` as that instant plus the version's own `comment_period_days`, and advances the version's `version` counter by one. Members of the public and organization representatives may then file comments, each naming the `methodology_version_id` under discussion, a `commenter_kind` of `DONOR`, `RATED_ORGANIZATION` or `PUBLIC`, and a `body` of 1 to 2000 non-blank characters. The `commenter_user_id` recorded on the comment is always taken from the caller's `X-User-ID` and never from the body. A new comment lands with `status` `SUBMITTED` and a null `disposition_note`, and it advances its parent version's `comment_count` and `unresolved_comment_count` by one each and that version's `version` counter by one. A comment filed while the version is not `COMMENT_OPEN`, or filed after `comment_deadline_at` has passed, is refused with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED`; the deadline instant itself is still inside the window.

Closing the window moves the version `COMMENT_OPEN` → `COMMENT_CLOSED` — any other starting status yields `ERR_METHODOLOGY_STATE_INVALID` — stamps `comment_closed_at` with the logical clock, recomputes `comment_count` and `unresolved_comment_count`, and advances the version counter. Closing the window does **not** dispose of anything: the outstanding comments remain outstanding, and `unresolved_comment_count` continues to block adoption. Disposal is a separate act by a `MethodologyAdmin`, who supplies a `disposition` of `INCORPORATED` or `DECLINED` and an optional `disposition_note`; the comment moves from `SUBMITTED` to the chosen disposition, its parent's `unresolved_comment_count` falls by one, and both the comment's and the parent version's `version` counters advance by one. Disposing a comment that names a `public_comment_id` no reader can resolve is `ERR_PUBLIC_COMMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Comments frequently change minds, so the product provides a genuine revision loop. Returning a `COMMENT_CLOSED` version to `DRAFT` requires a `revision_note` explaining why, and it accepts optional replacements for `weight_financial_health`, `weight_accountability`, `weight_transparency`, `weight_impact` and `dimension_floor_score`. Weights are all-or-nothing here: when any one weight is supplied all four must be, and the replacement set must still sum to exactly 1.0000. The transition stores the note and any supplied values, clears `comment_open_at`, `comment_deadline_at` and `comment_closed_at` back to null, and advances the version counter — but it deliberately **preserves** `comment_count` and `unresolved_comment_count`, so comments filed against the earlier draft still gate adoption. Because a returned draft must run a fresh comment window before it is adoptable, `COMMENT_CLOSED` → `DRAFT` → `COMMENT_OPEN` is a real cycle, and a version can traverse it any number of times. Returning a version that is not `COMMENT_CLOSED` is `ERR_METHODOLOGY_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.2. Adoption, Impact Projection and Transition Grants

Adoption is the single most consequential operation in the service, and it fires a three-hop cascade. The acting `MethodologyAdmin` supplies `effective_from` and optionally `effective_until`. The version must be `COMMENT_CLOSED`, or the request is `ERR_METHODOLOGY_STATE_INVALID`; its `unresolved_comment_count` must be zero, or the request is `ERR_UNRESOLVED_COMMENTS`; and its effectivity window must satisfy every rule of admission check 17 — a `effective_from` at least 30 days beyond the logical clock, an `effective_until` strictly after `effective_from` when supplied, and no overlap with the closed effectivity interval of any other `ADOPTED` version other than the one this adoption is superseding — or the request is `ERR_METHODOLOGY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**Hop one** writes the adoption itself: `status` becomes `ADOPTED`, `adopted_at` takes the logical clock, and `effective_from` and `effective_until` take the supplied values. In the same hop, the methodology version that was `ADOPTED` — the one named by `supersedes_version_id` — moves `ADOPTED` → `RETIRED`, its `retired_at` takes the logical clock and its `effective_until` is closed at the new version's `effective_from`. Both rows' `version` counters advance by one. Retirement is not destructive to work in flight: a rating cycle already pinned to the retiring version keeps its pin and publishes normally, because its `methodology_pin_locked` flag was set when the cycle was created. Only a *new* cycle that wants to pin the superseded version needs special dispensation.

**Hop two**, caused by hop one's retirement, is the impact projection, and it runs once for every `Organization` whose `published_cycle_count` is at least 1. For each such organization the projection takes that organization's `PublicationRecord` with the greatest `sequence_number`, follows its `rating_cycle_id` to the cycle that produced it, and replays the four `DimensionScore` rows of that cycle exactly **as stored** — their `normalized_score` and `data_completeness_pct` are read from the rows, and the projection never re-reads a filing or a governance disclosure, which is what makes it deterministic and immune to data added after publication. Against those stored rows it evaluates the composite calculation of §3.6 in its final variant, but with the **new** version's four weights and the **new** version's `dimension_floor_score`, reusing the published cycle's own `failed_standard_count` and `data_completeness_pct`. The resulting projected composite minus the organization's `latest_published_score`, rounded half up to two decimal places, is the `projected_score_delta`. When that delta is at most negative 5.00 — the threshold is inclusive, so a delta of exactly negative 5.00 qualifies — the organization receives one `TransitionGrant` with `status` `ACTIVE`, its `organization_id` and its `methodology_version_id` set to the newly adopted version, its `superseded_version_label` set to the retiring version's `version_label`, its `grace_until` set to `adopted_at` plus the adopting version's `transition_period_days`, its `projected_score_delta` recorded, and its `trigger_reason` set to `WEIGHT_CHANGE` when any of the four weights differs from the retiring version's corresponding weight and to `THRESHOLD_TIGHTENED` otherwise, which in practice means the floor score was raised. Organizations with no publication at all are skipped entirely and never receive a grant.

**Hop three**, caused by hop two's grant creation, propagates the grace. Each granted organization's `transition_grace_until` is set to that grant's `grace_until` and the organization's own `version` counter advances by one. Every rating cycle of that organization whose `status` is neither `PUBLISHED` nor `WITHDRAWN` has its `methodology_pin_locked` flag set to true and keeps its pinned `methodology_version_id` untouched, which freezes the pin against the methodology change now in force.

A grant is a licence, and it is spent. When a `RatingAnalyst` opens a new cycle pinned to a `RETIRED` methodology version while passing `use_transition_grant` as true, the organization's `ACTIVE` grant whose `superseded_version_label` matches the pinned version's `version_label` moves `ACTIVE` → `CONSUMED` and its `version` counter advances by one; spending it also refreshes the organization's `transition_grace_until` roll-up, clearing it back to null when the organization holds no remaining `ACTIVE` grant, and that refresh does not advance the organization's own `version` counter. Where no such live grant exists, or its `grace_until` has already elapsed, the cycle creation is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_GRACE_UNAVAILABLE`. Grace also expires on its own: an operator-run sweep, available to a `MethodologyAdmin`, moves every `ACTIVE` grant whose `grace_until` lies strictly before the logical clock to `EXPIRED`, advances each such grant's `version` counter by one, and answers with an `expired_grant_count` and the matching list of `expired_grant_ids`. For every organization the sweep touches, `transition_grace_until` is cleared back to null when that organization holds no remaining `ACTIVE` grant, and this clearing likewise leaves the organization's own `version` counter untouched. The sweep exists so that expiry is observable without waiting on real time; a grant read that names a `transition_grant_id` no reader can resolve is `ERR_TRANSITION_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.3. Rated Organizations, Their Filings, and Which Filing Wins

Registration of a rated charity is a `MethodologyAdmin` act. It takes a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, an `ein` — the charity's public tax identifier, exactly nine digits, unique across every registered organization, with a collision refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_EIN` — an `org_type` drawn from `PUBLIC_CHARITY`, `PRIVATE_FOUNDATION`, `RELIGIOUS_CONGREGATION`, `DONOR_ADVISED_SPONSOR` and `MEMBERSHIP_ASSOCIATION`, a `fiscal_year_end_month` between 1 and 12 naming the calendar month in which the charity closes its books, and a `contact_email` that must contain an at-sign and which is the address that receives publication change notifications. The `org_type` is not decorative bookkeeping: it selects every methodology adjustment in the program-expense calculation and it decides which governance standards are inapplicable.

Six roll-up fields on an organization are maintained by the system and never written by a client. `latest_published_score` and `latest_published_grade` mirror the `published_score` and `published_grade` of that organization's `PublicationRecord` with the highest `sequence_number`, and both start null. `published_cycle_count` is the number of publication records whose organization is this one, and starts at 0. `trend_direction` is the `change_direction` of that same highest-sequence record, with a `FIRST_PUBLICATION` value mapped to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and takes the values `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `IMPROVED`, `UNCHANGED` and `DECLINED`; it starts at `NOT_APPLICABLE`. `transition_grace_until` mirrors the `grace_until` of the organization's `ACTIVE` transition grant — taking the later value when a further grant is minted while one is already live, as §2.5 describes — and is cleared back to null once the organization holds no `ACTIVE` grant at all; while any `ACTIVE` grant remains it is never recomputed downward. `substantiated_complaint_count` is the number of complaints against this organization whose `status` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, and starts at 0. `latest_published_grade` draws from the same five-value grade vocabulary as a cycle's grade — `ACCREDITED`, `MEETS_STANDARDS`, `NEEDS_IMPROVEMENT`, `DOES_NOT_MEET` and `UNRATED` — or is null before the first publication.

Financial evidence arrives as filings. Either a `RatingAnalyst` or the charity's own `OrganizationRep` submits one, naming the `organization_id` it describes, the `fiscal_year` it covers as an integer between 2000 and 2100, and a `source_kind` of `AUDITED_STATEMENT`, `PUBLIC_FILING` or `ORG_SUBMISSION`. Eight monetary figures are mandatory and are all expressed in whole cents, each of which must be zero or greater: `total_expense_cents`, `program_expense_cents`, `fundraising_expense_cents`, `admin_expense_cents`, `contributions_cents`, `total_revenue_cents`, `net_assets_cents` and `total_liabilities_cents`. Three more are optional and default to 0 when omitted: `grants_paid_cents`, the grants this charity paid onward to other organizations; `membership_services_cents`, member-services spend; and `joint_cost_allocated_cents`, the joint costs allocated out of fundraising appeals and into program services. Every submission also carries `expected_organization_version`, as §2.7 requires. Two arithmetic identities are enforced at submission and again at validation: `total_expense_cents` must equal the sum of `program_expense_cents`, `fundraising_expense_cents` and `admin_expense_cents`, and `joint_cost_allocated_cents` must not exceed `program_expense_cents`; a breach of either is `ERR_EXPENSE_COMPONENTS_MISMATCH`.

On acceptance the system derives the fields the client does not supply. `status` starts at `RECEIVED`. `source_precedence` is 1 for an `AUDITED_STATEMENT`, 2 for a `PUBLIC_FILING` and 3 for an `ORG_SUBMISSION`. `origin` records how the row arrived: `ORG_PORTAL` when the submitter was an `OrganizationRep`, `ANALYST_ENTRY` when it was a `RatingAnalyst`, and `CORRECTION_APPLIED` for the replacement rows the correction and appeal machinery synthesizes. `validated_at`, `rejection_code` and `superseded_at` all start null, and the submission advances the parent organization's `version` counter by one.

No calculation ever reads a filing that has not passed the analyst gate. Validation is a `RatingAnalyst` decision on a `RECEIVED` filing — any other starting status is `ERR_FILING_STATE_INVALID` — carrying a `decision` and, when that decision is to reject, a `rejection_code`; a rejection with no code supplied is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Accepting moves the filing `RECEIVED` → `VALIDATED` and stamps `validated_at` with the logical clock. Rejecting moves it `RECEIVED` → `REJECTED` and stores the `rejection_code`. Both outcomes advance the filing's `version` counter by one, and both are terminal in the sense that a filing never returns to `RECEIVED`.

Acceptance also displaces its predecessor. Any *other* filing sharing the same `organization_id`, the same `fiscal_year` and the same `source_kind` whose `status` is `VALIDATED` moves `VALIDATED` → `SUPERSEDED`, takes `superseded_at` from the logical clock, and has its own `version` counter advanced by one. The invariant this enforces is absolute: at most one filing per organization, fiscal year and source-kind triple holds `VALIDATED` at any instant. The same `VALIDATED` → `SUPERSEDED` edge is also travelled by the system when an accepted correction or a granted appeal synthesizes a replacement filing.

When a figure is needed for an organization and a fiscal year — during scoring, and again when the score-breakdown read reconstructs its intermediates — exactly one filing is chosen, by a selector whose rules are fixed. The candidate set is every filing for that organization and that fiscal year whose `status` is `VALIDATED`; filings in `RECEIVED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` are never read, no matter how recent they are or how authoritative their `source_kind` looks. Candidates are ranked first by `source_precedence` ascending, so an audited statement beats a public filing and a public filing beats an organization submission, and then, among candidates of equal precedence, by `created_at` ascending, so the earliest surviving submission wins. The first candidate in that ranking is the winner, and `created_at` is the only tie-break — a row's `id` is never consulted. When the candidate set for the cycle's own fiscal year is empty, scoring is refused with `ERR_NO_VALIDATED_FILING`; when it is empty for one of the two preceding fiscal years, that year is simply treated as unavailable, which shrinks the weight pool of the fundraising-efficiency calculation rather than contributing a zero. Because that ranking is also the order the organization's filing reverse-read returns rows in — `fiscal_year` descending, then `source_precedence` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — a caller can see which filing would win without recomputing anything.

### 3.4. Governance Disclosure, the Eight Standards, and Waivers

Financial ratios say nothing about whether a board is doing its job, so every rating cycle carries one governance disclosure. A `RatingAnalyst` records it against a `rating_cycle_id`, and at most one such profile may exist per cycle — a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_GOVERNANCE_PROFILE`. The disclosure captures `board_member_count`, the number of voting board members, between 0 and 100; `independent_board_member_count`, those with no compensated or family relationship to the charity, which must be between 0 and `board_member_count`; `conflict_of_interest_policy_on_file`, a boolean; `coi_policy_reviewed_at`, the optional date on which the board last reviewed that policy, null when it never has; `board_meetings_last_year`, the number of full-board meetings held, between 0 and 52; and four more booleans — `audit_committee_exists`, `whistleblower_policy`, `ceo_compensation_reviewed` and `impact_report_on_file`. Three of the captured fields feed the impact dimension rather than the accountability one: `impact_report_on_file`, `outcome_measures_count` (the number of published outcome measures, between 0 and 50) and `third_party_evaluation_on_file`, indicating an independent programme evaluation. Recording the disclosure evaluates nothing: the new profile lands with `evaluation_status` `PENDING`, a null `independence_ratio`, a null `evaluated_at`, and `standards_met_count`, `standards_not_met_count`, `standards_waived_count` and `standards_not_applicable_count` all zero.

Evaluation is a separate step, admissible only while the profile's `evaluation_status` is `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_GOVERNANCE_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the owning rating cycle is still in `DATA_COLLECTION`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`. It first computes `independence_ratio` as `independent_board_member_count` divided by `board_member_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, defined as 0.0000 when `board_member_count` is zero. It then mints exactly eight `StandardEvaluation` rows, one per `standard_code`, each carrying the `governance_profile_id` it belongs to, a `result`, a stringified `observed_value` taken from the profile, a stringified `required_value` naming the threshold, an `evaluated_at` stamped from the logical clock, and a null `waiver_reason` and null `waived_by_user_id`. The eight codes and their tests are:

- `BOARD_SIZE_MIN` — observed is the `board_member_count`, required is "5", and the standard is met when the board has at least 5 voting members. Never inapplicable.
- `BOARD_INDEPENDENCE` — observed is the computed `independence_ratio`, required is "0.5000", and the standard is met when that ratio is at least 0.5000. Never inapplicable.
- `COI_POLICY` — observed is `conflict_of_interest_policy_on_file`, required is "true", and the standard is met when a written conflict-of-interest policy is on file. Never inapplicable.
- `COI_POLICY_CURRENCY` — observed is `coi_policy_reviewed_at` or the literal "null" when it is absent, required is "reviewed within 730 days", and the standard is met when `coi_policy_reviewed_at` is present and falls at or after the logical clock less 730 days. Inapplicable when the organization's `org_type` is `RELIGIOUS_CONGREGATION`.
- `BOARD_MEETING_FREQUENCY` — observed is `board_meetings_last_year`, required is "3", and the standard is met when the board held at least 3 full meetings in the reported year. Never inapplicable.
- `AUDIT_COMMITTEE` — observed is `audit_committee_exists`, required is "true", and the standard is met when a standing audit committee is seated. Inapplicable when `org_type` is `RELIGIOUS_CONGREGATION`.
- `WHISTLEBLOWER_POLICY` — observed is `whistleblower_policy`, required is "true", and the standard is met when a written whistleblower policy is on file. Never inapplicable.
- `CEO_COMP_REVIEW` — observed is `ceo_compensation_reviewed`, required is "true", and the standard is met when the board documented a comparability review of chief-executive compensation. Inapplicable when `org_type` is `RELIGIOUS_CONGREGATION`.

A row's `result` is decided in a fixed order: `NOT_APPLICABLE` when the code's inapplicability condition holds, otherwise `MET` when its test passes, otherwise `NOT_MET`. `WAIVED` is never produced by evaluation; it is reachable only through the waiver operation below. Evaluation then sets `standards_met_count` to the number of `MET` rows, `standards_not_met_count` to the number of `NOT_MET` rows, `standards_waived_count` to zero, and `standards_not_applicable_count` to the number of `NOT_APPLICABLE` rows — the four counts always partition eight — copies `standards_not_met_count` onto the owning cycle's `failed_standard_count`, advances that cycle's `version` counter by one, moves the profile `PENDING` → `EVALUATED`, stamps `evaluated_at`, and advances the profile's own `version` counter by one. An inapplicable standard is emphatically **not** a failure: a `RELIGIOUS_CONGREGATION` with no audit committee scores exactly as one with an audit committee does, because inapplicability shrinks the accountability denominator rather than the numerator, leaving five gradeable standards rather than eight.

A failing standard can be forgiven but not erased. A `MethodologyAdmin` waives one by supplying a `waiver_reason`; the row moves `NOT_MET` → `WAIVED`, stores the reason and the acting `X-User-ID` as `waived_by_user_id`, and advances its own `version` counter. Waiving is admissible only on a row whose `result` is `NOT_MET`, otherwise `ERR_STANDARD_STATE_INVALID`, and only while the owning profile's rating cycle is in neither `PUBLISHED` nor `WITHDRAWN`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`. The waiver ripples upward: the profile's `standards_not_met_count` falls by one while its `standards_waived_count` rises by one and its `version` counter advances; the cycle's `failed_standard_count` falls by one and its `version` counter advances; and when the cycle's `ACCOUNTABILITY` dimension row is currently `COMPUTED` or `OVERRIDDEN`, that row's `raw_points`, `normalized_score` and `weighted_contribution` are recomputed and the cycle's provisional and final figures are rewritten. The recomputation matters because a `WAIVED` standard is worth 2 accountability points where a `MET` standard is worth 3 — waiving a failure improves the score without pretending the shortfall never happened.

Revoking a waiver is the exact inverse, and it is specified with the same density because tests read the two as a pair. A `MethodologyAdmin` supplies an optional `revocation_note`; the row moves `WAIVED` → `NOT_MET` and its `waiver_reason` and `waived_by_user_id` are cleared back to null. Revocation is admissible only on a row whose `result` is `WAIVED`, otherwise `ERR_STANDARD_STATE_INVALID`, and only while the owning cycle is in neither `PUBLISHED` nor `WITHDRAWN`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`. The profile's `standards_waived_count` falls by one while `standards_not_met_count` rises by one and its `version` counter advances; the cycle's `failed_standard_count` rises by one and its `version` counter advances; the `ACCOUNTABILITY` dimension row's `raw_points` and the cycle's provisional and final figures return to precisely their pre-waiver values; and the standard evaluation's own `version` counter advances by one. After any waiver and revocation pair the four `standards_*_count` fields still sum to eight and no figure carries residual drift.

One more edge leaves `EVALUATED`. When a correction whose `target_kind` is `GOVERNANCE_DISCLOSURE` is accepted — whether by the analyst's own decision or by a panel granting an appeal — the profile moves `EVALUATED` → `PENDING`: the field named by the correction's `target_field_name` is overwritten with its `proposed_value`, all eight `StandardEvaluation` rows are deleted so that the next evaluation re-mints them from the corrected disclosure, the four `standards_*_count` fields are zeroed, and the profile's `version` counter advances by one. `PENDING` → `EVALUATED` → `PENDING` is therefore a loop that mirrors the cycle's own correction rounds.

### 3.5. Opening a Rating Cycle and Running the Scoring Pipeline

A rating cycle is the unit of review. A `RatingAnalyst` opens one by naming the `organization_id` under review, the `methodology_version_id` that will score it, the `fiscal_year` whose filings feed it as an integer between 2000 and 2100, the `period_start` and `period_end` bounding the slice of the organization's timeline this review occupies, and the `assigned_analyst_user_id` of the analyst who will own it. An optional `use_transition_grant` flag, described in §3.2, is the only way to pin a retired methodology. The interval is half-open: `period_start` is inclusive, `period_end` is exclusive, and `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`. A new interval may not overlap the interval of any non-`WITHDRAWN` cycle of the same organization, though pure adjacency — a new `period_start` exactly equal to an earlier cycle's `period_end` — is permitted with no buffer; an overlap is `ERR_CYCLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The pinned methodology must ordinarily be `ADOPTED`, or the creation is `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_ADOPTED`, and it must be effective at the logical clock, or the creation is `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_EFFECTIVE`; a retired version admitted by a live transition grant is exempt from the effectivity test, since the grant is precisely a licence to pin a window that has closed.

A newborn cycle sits in `DATA_COLLECTION` with `correction_round` at 0, `methodology_pin_locked` already true, `publishing_reviewer_user_id` null, and every timing and score field null: `review_opened_at`, `correction_deadline_at`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `published_at`, `program_expense_ratio`, `fundraising_efficiency`, `months_of_reserve`, `data_completeness_pct`, `weighted_sum_provisional`, `provisional_composite_score`, `provisional_grade`, `weighted_sum_final`, `final_composite_score`, `grade`, `standards_penalty_points` and `withdrawal_reason`. Its `failed_standard_count` and `accountability_penalty_points` start at 0. The pinned `methodology_version_id` is written once at creation and is **never** rewritten afterwards, not even when that version is later retired — this is the whole point of the pin, and it is why a mid-cycle methodology revision cannot re-grade work in flight.

Scoring is the analyst's principal act, and it may be run only from `DATA_COLLECTION`; any other status is `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id`, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ANALYST`. At least one filing for the cycle's organization and the cycle's own `fiscal_year` must be `VALIDATED`, else `ERR_NO_VALIDATED_FILING`. And the cycle must own a governance profile whose `evaluation_status` is `EVALUATED`, else `ERR_GOVERNANCE_NOT_EVALUATED`. Because an accepted correction and a granted appeal both return the cycle to `DATA_COLLECTION`, a re-score is a fresh `DATA_COLLECTION` → `SCORED` traversal rather than a self-loop; on that re-score the four dimension rows already exist and travel `STALE` → `COMPUTED`, and **any override value previously written into a dimension row is discarded** in favour of a value recomputed from source data.

The pipeline begins with three financial figures, all read off the winning filing described in §3.3. The **program expense ratio** is stored on the cycle as `program_expense_ratio` at four decimal places, and its two adjustment tables are deliberately **not** mirror images of each other — applying one table to both sides of the fraction yields a plausible but wrong ratio. On the numerator side: for a `PUBLIC_CHARITY` or a `DONOR_ADVISED_SPONSOR`, the adjusted program figure is `program_expense_cents` less `joint_cost_allocated_cents`, stripping joint fundraising costs back out of program services; for a `PRIVATE_FOUNDATION` it is `program_expense_cents` plus `grants_paid_cents`; for a `MEMBERSHIP_ASSOCIATION` it is `program_expense_cents` plus `membership_services_cents`; and for a `RELIGIOUS_CONGREGATION` it is `program_expense_cents` unchanged. On the denominator side only two types adjust at all: for a `PRIVATE_FOUNDATION` the adjusted total is `total_expense_cents` plus `grants_paid_cents`, for a `DONOR_ADVISED_SPONSOR` it is `total_expense_cents` **less** `grants_paid_cents`, and for all three remaining types it is `total_expense_cents` unchanged. The adjusted program figure is then floored at zero — a negative adjustment becomes zero rather than a negative numerator. If the adjusted total is zero or below, the ratio is exactly 0.0000; otherwise it is the adjusted program figure divided by the adjusted total, rounded half up to four decimal places. Exactly one filing feeds this figure — the winner for the cycle's own fiscal year — never an average across filings.

The **three-year fundraising efficiency**, stored as `fundraising_efficiency` at four decimal places, is where the pinned rounding order does the most work. Three fiscal years are considered: the cycle's own `fiscal_year`, the year before it, and the year two before it, carrying declared weights of 0.50, 0.30 and 0.20 respectively. For each of those three years the selector of §3.3 is asked for a winning filing; a year with no winner is skipped entirely and contributes nothing. For each available year, the year's ratio is that filing's `fundraising_expense_cents` divided by its `contributions_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places at this point — before any weighting — except that a year whose `contributions_cents` is exactly zero takes the worst possible ratio of 1.0000 rather than dividing by zero. If no year at all has a winning filing, `fundraising_efficiency` is 1.0000, the worst possible value. Otherwise the declared weights of the available years are summed into a weight pool, and each available year's renormalised weight is its declared weight divided by that pool, rounded half up to four decimal places. Each available year then contributes a term equal to its rounded ratio multiplied by its rounded renormalised weight, itself rounded half up to four decimal places; those terms are summed, and the sum is rounded half up to four decimal places one final time. A missing year therefore shrinks the pool rather than dragging the figure toward zero — and pooling all three years' fundraising expenses over all three years' contributions and dividing once, though defensible accounting, is not this methodology and produces a different number.

The **months of reserve**, stored as `months_of_reserve` at two decimal places, is the simplest of the three. When the winning filing's `total_expense_cents` is zero or below, the figure is 0.00. Otherwise it is that filing's `net_assets_cents` multiplied by the twelve months in a year and divided by `total_expense_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Note carefully that `net_assets_cents` is already reported net of liabilities, so `total_liabilities_cents` is **not** subtracted a second time here; the liabilities figure is captured for disclosure, not for this quotient.

Those three figures, plus the governance profile and the cycle's own penalty ledger, feed the four dimension rows. Each `DimensionScore` row carries the `rating_cycle_id` it belongs to, its `dimension` — one of `FINANCIAL_HEALTH`, `ACCOUNTABILITY`, `TRANSPARENCY` and `IMPACT`, with exactly one row per dimension per cycle — and its `raw_points`, `max_points`, `normalized_score`, `weight_applied`, `weighted_contribution`, `data_completeness_pct`, `penalty_points`, `status`, `override_reason` and `overridden_by_user_id`.

`FINANCIAL_HEALTH` has a `max_points` of 40 and earns points on three independent ladders whose polarities are **not** aligned. The ratio ladder rewards larger values: 20 points when `program_expense_ratio` is at least 0.85, otherwise 15 at 0.75 or more, otherwise 10 at 0.65 or more, otherwise 5 at 0.50 or more, otherwise 0. The efficiency ladder rewards smaller values: 12 points when `fundraising_efficiency` is at most 0.10, otherwise 9 at 0.20 or less, otherwise 5 at 0.35 or less, otherwise 0. The reserve ladder rewards larger values again: 8 points when `months_of_reserve` is at least 12, otherwise 5 at 6 or more, otherwise 2 at 3 or more, otherwise 0. The three awards sum to the dimension's `raw_points`.

`ACCOUNTABILITY` is the one dimension whose ceiling moves with organization type. Its base is the smaller of 24 and the sum of 3 points for each `MET` standard on the cycle's governance profile plus 2 points for each `WAIVED` standard. Its `raw_points` is that base less the cycle's `accountability_penalty_points`, floored at zero so a large complaint penalty can drive the dimension to zero but never below it. Its `max_points` is the smaller of 24 and 3 points multiplied by the number of gradeable standards, that is eight less `standards_not_applicable_count`. Inapplicable standards therefore shrink the **denominator**, which is precisely how an organization type exempted from a standard is neither rewarded nor punished for it.

`TRANSPARENCY` has a `max_points` of 20 and sums four terms. The source term is 8 points when the cycle's fiscal year has a `VALIDATED` filing whose `source_kind` is `AUDITED_STATEMENT`, otherwise 4 points when it has a `VALIDATED` filing whose `source_kind` is `PUBLIC_FILING`, otherwise 0 — the two are alternatives and are never added together, and an organization holding both an audited statement and a public filing scores 8. This term reads the filing table directly rather than asking the winning-filing selector. Added to it are 6 points when the governance profile's `evaluation_status` is `EVALUATED`, 3 points when `conflict_of_interest_policy_on_file` is true, and 3 points when `whistleblower_policy` is true.

`IMPACT` has a `max_points` of 12 and sums three terms: 5 points when `impact_report_on_file` is true, plus the smaller of 4 and `outcome_measures_count`, plus 3 points when `third_party_evaluation_on_file` is true.

Every dimension's `normalized_score` is 0.00 when its `max_points` is zero, and otherwise its `raw_points` multiplied by 100 and divided by its `max_points`, rounded half up to two decimal places. A row's `weight_applied` and `weighted_contribution` **always** mirror the final composite variant, because that is the published figure: a dimension the final variant excludes carries a `weight_applied` of 0.0000 and a `weighted_contribution` of 0.00 even though the provisional variant still counted it at its full declared weight. Otherwise `weight_applied` is the final variant's four-decimal weight for that dimension and `weighted_contribution` is the `normalized_score` multiplied by `weight_applied`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The provisional variant's own per-dimension weights are not stored on any row; they are observable only through the cycle's score-breakdown read. A row's `penalty_points` records the complaint penalty currently deducted from its `raw_points` and is non-zero only on the `ACCOUNTABILITY` row.

Data sufficiency is measured per dimension and then aggregated. `FINANCIAL_HEALTH` is the only dimension graded in fractions: its `data_completeness_pct` is 100.00 multiplied by the number of the three fiscal years that have a winning filing, divided by 3, rounded half up to two decimal places — so it takes exactly one of 0.00, 33.33, 66.67 and 100.00. `ACCOUNTABILITY` is 100.00 when the cycle has a governance profile whose `evaluation_status` is `EVALUATED` and 0.00 otherwise. `TRANSPARENCY` is 100.00 when that same evaluated profile exists **and** at least one `VALIDATED` filing exists for the organization in any fiscal year whatsoever, and 0.00 otherwise. `IMPACT` is 100.00 when the profile exists and at least one of `impact_report_on_file` being true, `outcome_measures_count` being greater than zero, or `third_party_evaluation_on_file` being true holds, and 0.00 otherwise. The cycle's own `data_completeness_pct` is the sum, over the four dimensions, of each dimension's completeness multiplied by that dimension's **declared** weight from the pinned methodology version — never the renormalised final-variant weight — with the total rounded half up to two decimal places. A single-year filer consequently scores 33.33 on financial health and can fall below the publication gate even though its own fiscal year is fully documented. The aggregate figure is what gates the `UNRATED` grade; the per-dimension figures are what the final variant consults to decide which dimensions to exclude, and **only an exact 0.00 excludes**, so a 33.33 financial-health row still participates in the final variant.

Scoring closes by writing everything at once: the four dimension rows are created on a first score, travelling `PENDING` → `COMPUTED`, or rewritten on a re-score; `program_expense_ratio`, `fundraising_efficiency` and `months_of_reserve` are stored; `failed_standard_count` is refreshed from the governance profile; `data_completeness_pct` is stored; both composite variants are evaluated, writing `weighted_sum_provisional`, `provisional_composite_score` and `provisional_grade` from the provisional variant and `weighted_sum_final`, `final_composite_score` and `grade` from the final one, with `standards_penalty_points` recording the **final** variant's penalty; and the cycle's `version` counter advances by one. When `data_completeness_pct` is below 60.00, both `provisional_grade` and `grade` are forced to `UNRATED` regardless of what the arithmetic produced. The cycle moves `DATA_COLLECTION` → `SCORED`.

### 3.6. Composite Scoring: Two Variants, Six Steps, and the Analyst's Override

Every cycle carries **two** composite scores at all times, computed from the same dimension rows by the same six-step procedure under two different inclusion rules. The provisional family — `weighted_sum_provisional`, `provisional_composite_score` and `provisional_grade` — is rewritten at every score and every recompute, and it is the only figure the rated organization sees during its review window and the only figure an appeal can contest. The final family — `weighted_sum_final`, `final_composite_score` and `grade` — is likewise rewritten at every score and recompute, and it is what the publication ledger copies at publication time.

The six steps are pinned, and their order is part of the contract. **Step one** chooses the weight vector. The four declared weights come from the cycle's pinned methodology version: `weight_financial_health`, `weight_accountability`, `weight_transparency` and `weight_impact`. **Step two** forms the weighted sum: each included dimension's `normalized_score` is multiplied by that dimension's weight, each such product is rounded half up to two decimal places, the rounded products are summed, and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places again. **Step three** derives a band from that **pre-penalty** weighted sum: band A when the weighted sum is at least 90, band B at 75 or more, band C at 60 or more, band D below that. **Step four** computes the penalty, and the band is what sets its rate: 2.0 points per failed standard in band A, 1.5 in band B, 1.0 in band C and 0.5 in band D, multiplied by the cycle's `failed_standard_count` and rounded half up to two decimal places. **Step five** produces the composite score as the weighted sum less that penalty, floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimal places. **Step six** derives the grade from the **post-penalty** composite. First, a floor test: the smallest `normalized_score` among the included dimensions must be at least the pinned methodology version's `dimension_floor_score`. Then, in order: the grade is `UNRATED` when the cycle's `data_completeness_pct` is below 60.00, whatever the arithmetic said; otherwise `ACCREDITED` when the composite is at least 85 and the floor test passed; otherwise `MEETS_STANDARDS` when the composite is at least 70 and the floor test passed; otherwise `NEEDS_IMPROVEMENT` when the composite is at least 55; otherwise `DOES_NOT_MEET`. Note that the floor test gates only the top two grades — a cycle that fails the floor cannot be `ACCREDITED` or `MEETS_STANDARDS`, but it still lands on `NEEDS_IMPROVEMENT` or `DOES_NOT_MEET` by composite alone.

The two variants differ in exactly one respect: which dimensions are included, and whether the surviving weights are renormalised. The **provisional** variant includes all four dimensions at their declared weights with no renormalisation, even when a dimension's `data_completeness_pct` is 0.00 — a dimension with no data drags the provisional score down at its full declared weight. The **final** variant excludes every dimension whose `data_completeness_pct` is exactly 0.00 and renormalises the surviving declared weights, each becoming its declared weight divided by the sum of the surviving declared weights, rounded half up to four decimal places before it multiplies anything. An excluded dimension's `normalized_score` is also dropped from the floor test's minimum, so a zero-data dimension can never breach the floor. One consequence deserves emphasis: because step three's band comes from the renormalised weighted sum, excluding a weak dimension can **promote** the band and therefore **raise** the per-standard penalty rate, so the final score is not monotonically kinder than the provisional one.

Banding before the penalty rather than after it, or grading from the weighted sum rather than from the composite, produces different published grades on identical inputs; deriving the penalty rate from the post-penalty score is a fixed-point trap with no defined answer. The score-breakdown read exists so that this order can be inspected rather than reverse-engineered. It answers with the cycle's `rating_cycle_id` and the `methodology_version_label` of its pinned version, the three financial figures `program_expense_ratio`, `fundraising_efficiency` and `months_of_reserve`, the `data_completeness_pct`, `failed_standard_count` and `accountability_penalty_points`, then the provisional intermediates `provisional_band`, `provisional_penalty`, `weighted_sum_provisional`, `provisional_composite_score` and `provisional_grade`, and finally the final-variant intermediates `final_included_dimensions` — the list of dimensions the final variant kept — `final_band`, `final_penalty`, `weighted_sum_final`, `final_composite_score` and `grade`.

Judgement sometimes has to beat arithmetic. On a `SCORED` cycle, and only on a `SCORED` cycle, the assigned analyst may override dimension points in bulk. The request carries `overrides`, a non-empty array of objects each pairing a `dimension_score_id` with a replacement `raw_points`; a single `override_reason` covering the batch; and the `expected_rating_cycle_version` that §2.7 requires. Every `dimension_score_id` must resolve to a dimension row belonging to **this** cycle — a row that does not exist, or that belongs to a different cycle, is `ERR_DIMENSION_SCORE_NOT_FOUND` — and every `raw_points` must be an integer between 0 and that row's own `max_points` inclusive, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The operation is all-or-nothing: if any entry in the array is invalid, nothing at all is written. Attempting it on a cycle in any status other than `SCORED` is `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`, and attempting it as an analyst other than the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id` is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ANALYST`.

On success each named dimension row takes its supplied `raw_points`, recomputes `normalized_score` and `weighted_contribution`, stores the shared `override_reason` and the acting `X-User-ID` as `overridden_by_user_id`, and advances its own `version` counter by one. A row previously `COMPUTED` moves `COMPUTED` → `OVERRIDDEN`; a row previously `STALE` moves `STALE` → `OVERRIDDEN`, which lets an analyst override a row a complaint penalty left stale without re-running the whole pipeline; and a row already `OVERRIDDEN` moves `OVERRIDDEN` → `OVERRIDDEN`, overwriting both the earlier `raw_points` and the earlier `override_reason` and `overridden_by_user_id`. The cycle itself travels `SCORED` → `SCORED` and recomputes both composite variants and both grades. Because an override changes only `raw_points` and never any dimension's `data_completeness_pct`, the final variant's inclusion set is unchanged by an override. That self-loop is exactly why this write is guarded by the parent-version check instead of by a state precondition: nothing in the state machine can distinguish a legitimate second override from a blind overwrite of figures the analyst never read.

Staleness is the system's way of saying "this number no longer follows from its inputs". A dimension row moves `COMPUTED` → `STALE`, or `OVERRIDDEN` → `STALE`, whenever a complaint penalty lands on the `ACCOUNTABILITY` row, an accepted correction supersedes a source filing, or the cycle is withdrawn; in the `OVERRIDDEN` case the override value is discarded when the cycle is next re-scored. When a row goes stale the parent cycle immediately recomputes its **provisional** figures from the adjusted rows, while its final figures stand as last written until the next score. A row returns `STALE` → `COMPUTED` either on the next score after the cycle has returned to `DATA_COLLECTION`, recomputing `raw_points` from source data and discarding any prior override, or when a reversed complaint finding restores the `ACCOUNTABILITY` row. `STALE` → `COMPUTED` → `STALE` is therefore a genuine loop driven by complaint findings and their reversals. One consequence gates the review track: organization review cannot be opened while any dimension row of the cycle is `STALE`, and publication is likewise barred while any row is `STALE`.

### 3.7. Organization Review, Corrections and the Right of Appeal

No rating is published without the charity having seen it. Once the analyst is satisfied with a scored cycle, they open organization review, moving the cycle `SCORED` → `ORG_REVIEW`. Three conditions gate that move: the cycle must be in `SCORED`, or the request is `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`; the acting `X-User-ID` must be the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ANALYST`; and no dimension row of the cycle may be `STALE`, or the request is again `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID` — an organization must never be shown figures the system already knows are out of date. The transition stamps `review_opened_at` with the logical clock, derives `correction_deadline_at` as that instant plus 21 days, and advances the cycle's `version` counter by one.

During review the charity's `OrganizationRep` may dispute exactly one figure at a time. A correction request names the `rating_cycle_id` it concerns, a `target_kind` of either `FILING_FINANCIAL` or `GOVERNANCE_DISCLOSURE`, the `target_field_name` of the single field it wants changed — which must be one of the input fields of a filing record or of a governance profile — the `proposed_value` it asserts, rendered as a string and applied verbatim on acceptance, and a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 non-blank characters explaining the dispute. Two further fields are conditional: `target_fiscal_year` and `target_source_kind` are required when `target_kind` is `FILING_FINANCIAL`, because together with the organization they identify the filing an acceptance will supersede, and are null when `target_kind` is `GOVERNANCE_DISCLOSURE`. The new request lands with `status` `SUBMITTED`, its `submitted_by_org_user_id` taken from the caller's `X-User-ID`, its `round_number` set to the cycle's `correction_round` plus one, and `claimed_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_note` and `decided_at` all null. Filing it drives the cycle `ORG_REVIEW` → `CORRECTION_REQUESTED` and advances the cycle's `version` counter by one; the cycle must be in `ORG_REVIEW` for this to be admissible, else `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`, and its `correction_round` must still be below two, else `ERR_CORRECTION_ROUND_LIMIT`. Crucially, `correction_deadline_at` is **retained** across this transition, so the publication gate stays armed while the dispute is pending.

The cycle's own analyst — and nobody else, on pain of `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ANALYST` — takes ownership of a submitted request, moving it `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW` and recording the acting `X-User-ID` as `claimed_by_user_id`; claiming a request in any other status is `ERR_CORRECTION_STATE_INVALID`. The same analyst then decides it with a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`; deciding a request that is not `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW` is `ERR_CORRECTION_STATE_INVALID`. Either outcome stores `decided_by_user_id`, the `decision_note` and `decided_at` from the logical clock, and advances the request's `version` counter by one.

Acceptance moves the request `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW` → `ACCEPTED` and rewinds the rating, in four causally ordered hops. Hop one writes the acceptance itself; it is admissible only while the cycle's `correction_round` is below two, otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_ROUND_LIMIT`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves the cycle `CORRECTION_REQUESTED` → `DATA_COLLECTION`, recomputes `correction_round` — now one higher — and clears `review_opened_at`, `correction_deadline_at`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `weighted_sum_provisional`, `provisional_composite_score` and `provisional_grade` back to null. Hop three, caused by hop two reopening data collection, performs the substitution the `target_kind` selects. For a `FILING_FINANCIAL` target, the `VALIDATED` filing identified by the cycle's organization together with the request's `target_fiscal_year` and `target_source_kind` moves `VALIDATED` → `SUPERSEDED`, and a replacement filing is created carrying `origin` `CORRECTION_APPLIED`, `status` `VALIDATED`, and the field named by `target_field_name` overwritten with `proposed_value`. For a `GOVERNANCE_DISCLOSURE` target, the cycle's governance profile has the field named by `target_field_name` overwritten with `proposed_value`, its `evaluation_status` returns `EVALUATED` → `PENDING`, and its eight standard evaluation rows are deleted. In both cases every dimension row of the cycle that is `COMPUTED` or `OVERRIDDEN` moves to `STALE`. Hop four, caused by hop three deleting the standard rows, resets the cycle's `failed_standard_count` and the profile's four `standards_*_count` fields to zero, so the next score recomputes the accountability arithmetic from scratch. The cycle's and the request's `version` counters each advance by one. The complete loop — `DATA_COLLECTION` → `SCORED` → `ORG_REVIEW` → `CORRECTION_REQUESTED` → `DATA_COLLECTION` — may be travelled at most twice, because the third acceptance is what the round limit forbids.

Rejection moves the request `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW` → `REJECTED` and returns the cycle `CORRECTION_REQUESTED` → `ORG_REVIEW`, arming `appeal_deadline_at` as the logical clock plus 14 days and advancing the cycle's `version` counter by one. A rejection is admissible only when no *other* correction of the same cycle is still `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW`, since the cycle cannot simultaneously be back in review and awaiting another decision. Corrections can also be terminated without any merits decision at all: when the parent cycle is withdrawn, or when a substantiated complaint re-scores it, every correction of that cycle in `SUBMITTED` or in `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW` moves to `REJECTED` carrying the cascade's fixed `decision_note` and a `decided_at` from the logical clock, while `decided_by_user_id` stays null — the marker that no human decided it.

A rejected correction is the only thing an appeal can contest, and appeal rights are what make the rejection meaningful. The charity's `OrganizationRep` files one naming the `rating_cycle_id`, the `correction_request_id` being contested and the `grounds` for overturning the rejection, 1 to 2000 non-blank characters. The appeal lands with `status` `FILED`, `filed_by_org_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, `filed_at` from the logical clock, `deadline_at` as a retained copy of the cycle's `appeal_deadline_at` for audit purposes, an empty `panel_member_user_ids`, and null `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note`. Filing drives the cycle `ORG_REVIEW` → `APPEAL_OPEN` and advances the cycle's `version` counter by one. Four conditions gate the filing: the cycle must be in `ORG_REVIEW`, else `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`; the referenced correction's `status` must be `REJECTED`, else `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_REJECTED`; the logical clock must be strictly before the cycle's `appeal_deadline_at`, else `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and the cycle must not already hold an appeal in `FILED`, `PANEL_ASSIGNED`, `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, else `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED`. Only a `DISMISSED` appeal leaves room for another.

Seating the panel is a `MethodologyAdmin` act on an appeal that is `FILED` — any other status is `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`. The supplied `panel_member_user_ids` must hold exactly three distinct identifiers, none of them the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id` and none of them its `publishing_reviewer_user_id`, else `ERR_PANEL_COMPOSITION_INVALID`; and the seating admin may not themselves be that analyst, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The appeal moves `FILED` → `PANEL_ASSIGNED`, stores the panel and advances its `version` counter by one; the seating writes no further field on the appeal beyond the `updated_at` that §2.4 refreshes on every mutation, so there is no separate seating timestamp to read back.

A seated `AppealPanelist` — one whose own `X-User-ID` appears in `panel_member_user_ids`, others being refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` — then decides the appeal three ways, supplying a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`. Deciding an appeal that is not `PANEL_ASSIGNED` is `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`. All three branches store `decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` from the logical clock and the `decision_note`, and advance the appeal's `version` counter by one; they diverge sharply thereafter, and the three are specified here with equal weight because their differences are exactly what a reader must not guess.

Granting moves the appeal `PANEL_ASSIGNED` → `GRANTED` and rewinds the rating. The contested correction travels `REJECTED` → `ACCEPTED` — the only path out of that terminal state anywhere in the product — with its `decided_by_user_id` overwritten by the deciding panel member and its `decided_at` overwritten by the logical clock. The very same substitution the acceptance path performs is then applied: a filing superseded and replaced with a `CORRECTION_APPLIED` row, or a governance field overwritten with the profile returning `EVALUATED` → `PENDING` and its eight standard rows deleted. The cycle moves `APPEAL_OPEN` → `DATA_COLLECTION`, every dimension row goes to `STALE`, `review_opened_at`, `correction_deadline_at`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `weighted_sum_provisional`, `provisional_composite_score` and `provisional_grade` are cleared to null, `correction_round` is recomputed one higher, and the cycle's `version` counter advances by one. A grant is admissible only while the cycle's `correction_round` is below two, otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_ROUND_LIMIT`.

Denying moves the appeal `PANEL_ASSIGNED` → `DENIED`, advances the cycle `APPEAL_OPEN` → `APPEAL_RESOLVED`, and changes no score whatsoever; the contested correction stays `REJECTED` and the rating becomes publishable. Dismissing moves the appeal `PANEL_ASSIGNED` → `DISMISSED` and advances the cycle to the same `APPEAL_RESOLVED` destination with no score change and no substitution, the difference from a denial being that the panel records no finding on the merits — a dismissal is procedural. As with corrections, appeals can also end without a panel: when the parent cycle is withdrawn or a substantiated complaint re-scores it, every appeal of that cycle in `FILED` or `PANEL_ASSIGNED` moves to `DISMISSED` with the cascade's fixed `decision_note`, a `decided_at` from the logical clock and a `decided_by_user_id` left null.

### 3.8. Complaint Intake, Investigation and the Accountability Cascade

Complaints are filed against a charity, not against a rating, and by anyone: a `PublicUser` or an `OrganizationRep` may lodge one. The filing carries the `organization_id` complained about, a `complainant_kind` of `DONOR`, `BENEFICIARY`, `EMPLOYEE` or `ANONYMOUS`, a `category` of `MISUSE_OF_FUNDS`, `GOVERNANCE`, `SOLICITATION_PRACTICE`, `TRANSPARENCY` or `OTHER`, a `narrative` of 1 to 4000 non-blank characters, and an optional `corroborating_document_count` that defaults to 0 and must lie between 0 and 50 inclusive. The complaint lands with `status` `INTAKE`, its `complainant_user_id` taken from the caller's `X-User-ID`, `credibility_score`, `credibility_variant`, `severity_tier` and `investigation_due_at` all null, `penalty_points_applied` at 0 and `penalized_rating_cycle_id` null. Nothing is scored and nothing is penalised at intake, because no analyst has yet looked at it. Complaints are readable back by `organization_id`, by `penalized_rating_cycle_id` — the reverse lookup that answers "which complaints dragged this rating down" — and by `status` or `category`, and individually with their credibility figures and applied penalty.

A `RatingAnalyst` triages a complaint that is in `INTAKE`; any other status is `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. Triage supplies a `disposition`, an optional `triage_note` and an optional `referred_to`, and forks three ways with genuinely different consequences. `INVESTIGATE` moves the complaint `INTAKE` → `INVESTIGATING`, computes `credibility_score` under the intake variant, sets `credibility_variant` to `INTAKE`, derives `severity_tier`, and arms `investigation_due_at` at the logical clock plus 30 days. `CLOSE_NO_ACTION` moves it `INTAKE` → `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, a terminal status with no scoring side effect at all: `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, no `credibility_score` is computed and `investigation_due_at` stays null. `REFER_OUT` moves it `INTAKE` → `REFERRED_OUT`, also terminal, and sits between the other two — it stores `referred_to` and `triage_note`, stamps `resolved_at`, and does compute the intake-variant `credibility_score` and `severity_tier` so the referral carries a severity for the external authority, but it never penalises a rating. `referred_to` is required for this disposition and its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every branch advances the complaint's `version` counter by one.

Credibility is a weighted score built from three components plus a variant-specific adjustment. The base is the complainant's standing — `DONOR` 30 points, `BENEFICIARY` 25, `EMPLOYEE` 35, `ANONYMOUS` 10 — plus the category — `MISUSE_OF_FUNDS` 30, `GOVERNANCE` 25, `SOLICITATION_PRACTICE` 20, `TRANSPARENCY` 15, `OTHER` 5 — plus 8 points for each corroborating document, counting at most 3 of them, so documentary support contributes at most 24 points however many files arrive. An employee alleging misuse of funds with three documents therefore reaches 89 base points; an anonymous complaint in the `OTHER` category with no documents reaches 15. The intake variant is exactly that base and nothing else: at triage neither the investigation's outcome nor any corroborating pattern is knowable. The investigated variant adds two further terms — 5 points for each **other** complaint of the same organization already in `SUBSTANTIATED`, capped at 20 points in total, and then either 10 points when the finding is `SUBSTANTIATED` or a subtraction of 15 points when it is `UNSUBSTANTIATED`. The pattern count deliberately reads other complaints only and is taken before this complaint's own status is written, so an organization's first substantiated complaint contributes nothing to its own score. The result is clamped to the 0.00–100.00 range and only then rounded to 2 decimal places, so a clamped score is exactly `100.00`.

`severity_tier` follows from the score: `HIGH` at 80.00 or above, `MEDIUM` at 50.00 or above, and `LOW` otherwise. The tier is what caps the penalty — at most 5 accountability points at `LOW`, 10 at `MEDIUM` and 15 at `HIGH`. The subtle and load-bearing rule is that on resolution the tier is re-derived from the **investigated** score before the submitted `penalty_points` is checked against the ceiling, so the admissible ceiling is not knowable from the intake score alone: the pattern term and the finding adjustment can push a complaint across a severity boundary in either direction, raising or lowering the ceiling that the analyst's own request is then judged against.

Resolving an investigation takes a `finding`, an optional `penalty_points` and an optional `finding_note`, and requires the complaint to be `INVESTIGATING`, else `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. An `UNSUBSTANTIATED` finding moves the complaint to that terminal status, recomputes `credibility_score` under the investigated variant with its 15-point subtraction, sets `credibility_variant` to `INVESTIGATED`, re-derives `severity_tier` and stamps `resolved_at` — and changes no dimension row, no rating cycle field and no organization field. It requires `penalty_points` to be absent or 0, otherwise `ERR_PENALTY_POINTS_RANGE`; an unsubstantiated complaint cannot cost the charity a point.

A `SUBSTANTIATED` finding is the product's one route by which an outside voice moves a published-track rating, and it runs as four causally ordered hops. `penalty_points` must be present, within 1 to 15, and no greater than the ceiling of the freshly re-derived investigated tier, else `ERR_PENALTY_POINTS_RANGE`. Hop one writes the finding: the recomputed credibility figures, `penalty_points_applied`, `finding_note` and `resolved_at`, plus the choice of victim — `penalized_rating_cycle_id` is set by the penalty-target selector, which considers the rating cycles of the complained-about organization whose `status` is neither `PUBLISHED` nor `WITHDRAWN`, ranks them by `fiscal_year` descending so the most recent open cycle absorbs the penalty, breaks ties by `created_at` ascending and never consults `id`, and takes the first. When that candidate set is empty the field stays null, hops two through four are simply skipped, and **no error is raised** — a substantiated complaint against a charity with no open cycle is recorded, counts toward future pattern scoring, and penalises nothing. Published and withdrawn ratings are immune; a published rating is never retro-penalised.

Hop two, caused by hop one naming a cycle, adds `penalty_points` to the `penalty_points` field of that cycle's `ACCOUNTABILITY` dimension row, recomputes its `raw_points` as the base points less the accumulated penalty floored at 0 so the row can never go negative, and moves the row `COMPUTED` or `OVERRIDDEN` → `STALE`. Hop three, caused by that stale row, has the cycle recompute `accountability_penalty_points`, `weighted_sum_provisional`, `standards_penalty_points`, `provisional_composite_score` and `provisional_grade`; and if the cycle was anywhere on the review track — `ORG_REVIEW`, `CORRECTION_REQUESTED` or `APPEAL_OPEN` — it is dragged back to `SCORED`, clearing `review_opened_at`, `correction_deadline_at` and `appeal_deadline_at`. Hop four, caused by that departure from the review track, tidies up what the review track had in flight: appeals of the cycle in `FILED` or `PANEL_ASSIGNED` move to `DISMISSED` and corrections in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW` move to `REJECTED`, both carrying `decision_note` `cycle re-scored after substantiated complaint` and a null `decided_by_user_id`. Alongside the hops, the organization's `substantiated_complaint_count` is recomputed and its `version` counter advances by one, as does the penalised cycle's and the complaint's.

Findings can be wrong, and a `MethodologyAdmin` can reverse one, which is the exact compensating action for the cascade above. Reversal takes a `reversal_reason`, requires the complaint to be `SUBSTANTIATED` — else `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID` — and requires the cycle named by `penalized_rating_cycle_id` to be neither `PUBLISHED` nor `WITHDRAWN`, else `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`: once a rating has been published or abandoned the ledger behind it is frozen and the reversal is refused rather than silently skipped. The complaint returns `SUBSTANTIATED` → `INVESTIGATING`, stores the `reversal_reason`, sets `penalty_points_applied` back to 0 and clears `resolved_at` and `penalized_rating_cycle_id` to null. The penalised cycle's `ACCOUNTABILITY` row has the reversed points removed from its `penalty_points`, recomputes `raw_points` from source data and moves `STALE` → `COMPUTED`. The cycle's `accountability_penalty_points`, `standards_penalty_points` and provisional figures return to their pre-substantiation values, and its `version` counter advances by one, as do the organization's — with `substantiated_complaint_count` recomputed — and the complaint's. The accountability point ledger returns to exactly where it stood before the finding, with zero drift.

### 3.9. Publication, Change Notification and Withdrawal

Publication is the only act in this product that makes a number public, and it belongs exclusively to a `PublishingReviewer`, who may attach an optional `publishing_reviewer_note`. A cycle can be published from `ORG_REVIEW` or from `APPEAL_RESOLVED`, and the two paths carry identical preconditions and identical side effects — a cycle that survived an appeal publishes exactly as a cycle that was never appealed does. Seven conditions gate the act. The cycle's `status` must be one of those two, else `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the cycle's `assigned_analyst_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the reviewer may not publish a rating they themselves scored. No correction of the cycle may still be `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW`, no appeal of the cycle may still be `FILED` or `PANEL_ASSIGNED`, and no dimension row of the cycle may be `STALE`; each of those three is `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`. The logical clock must have reached `correction_deadline_at`, inclusively — a publication attempted at exactly that instant is admissible, which is the deliberate mirror image of the appeal window's strictly-before rule. And `data_completeness_pct` must be at least `60.00`, else `ERR_DATA_COMPLETENESS_TOO_LOW`; below that threshold the system refuses to publish rather than publishing an `UNRATED` grade.

Publishing runs as three causally ordered hops. Hop one computes the final score family — `weighted_sum_final`, `standards_penalty_points`, `final_composite_score` and `grade` under the final variant of the composite calculation — stamps `published_at` from the logical clock, records `publishing_reviewer_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, moves the cycle to `PUBLISHED` and advances its `version` counter by one. Note that the final family is (re)computed here, at publication, from whatever the inputs then are; the provisional family the organization saw during review is not what gets published.

Hop two, caused by the cycle reaching `PUBLISHED`, appends one row to the immutable publication ledger. It carries the `rating_cycle_id`, the `organization_id` denormalised for trend queries, and a `sequence_number` equal to the count of that organization's pre-existing publication records plus one, so numbering is 1-based and gap-free per organization. It copies `published_score` from the cycle's `final_composite_score`, `published_grade` from its `grade`, `weighted_sum_final` and `standards_penalty_points` from the cycle's like-named fields, `methodology_version_label` from the `version_label` of the methodology version pinned on the cycle, and `published_at` from the cycle. It then classifies the change. When `sequence_number` is 1, `previous_published_score` and `score_delta` are both null and `change_direction` is `FIRST_PUBLICATION`. Otherwise the predecessor is the row of the same organization whose `sequence_number` is exactly one lower — identified by sequence number, never by `created_at` ordering — `previous_published_score` is that row's `published_score`, `score_delta` is this publication's `published_score` minus it rounded to 2 decimal places, and `change_direction` is `IMPROVED` when the delta is positive, `DECLINED` when negative and `UNCHANGED` when exactly zero. Every field of this row is immutable thereafter: the entity is append-only, has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no update or delete operation anywhere in the surface.

Hop three, caused by that row's creation, does two things. It populates `notified_recipient_kinds` as an ordered list that always begins with `RATED_ORGANIZATION` and appends `MEDIA` only when `change_direction` is not `FIRST_PUBLICATION` and the absolute value of `score_delta` is at least `10.00`. The media threshold is symmetric, so a rise of `+10.00` notifies exactly as a fall of `-10.00` does, and inclusive at ten, so a delta of `8.75` does not notify; a first publication never notifies media however extreme its score. It then recomputes the organization's four publication roll-ups from the new row — `latest_published_score` and `latest_published_grade` from the row's `published_score` and `published_grade`, `published_cycle_count` from its `sequence_number`, and `trend_direction` as `NOT_APPLICABLE` on a first publication and otherwise `IMPROVED`, `DECLINED` or `UNCHANGED` by the sign of `score_delta` — and advances the organization's `version` counter by one. One further consequence is silent but material: because the cycle is now `PUBLISHED` it leaves the complaint penalty-target candidate pool, so a substantiated complaint arriving afterwards selects a different open cycle, or none at all. A published rating is never retro-penalised.

The ledger is the public face of the product and is readable by every role including `PublicUser`, either as a filtered list by `organization_id` or `rating_cycle_id` ordered by `sequence_number` ascending, or one row at a time. Alongside it, an organization's rating trend is a computed read returning the roll-up figures — `organization_id`, `latest_published_score`, `latest_published_grade`, `published_cycle_count`, `trend_direction` — together with a paginated `points` array of that organization's published history ordered newest-first, which is the display order a donor-facing trend chart wants and the reverse of the ledger's own ascending order.

Withdrawal is the other terminal fate, reserved to a `MethodologyAdmin` and requiring a `withdrawal_reason`. A cycle may be withdrawn from any of the six non-terminal statuses — `DATA_COLLECTION`, `SCORED`, `ORG_REVIEW`, `CORRECTION_REQUESTED`, `APPEAL_OPEN` and `APPEAL_RESOLVED` — and attempting it on a cycle already `PUBLISHED` or `WITHDRAWN` is `ERR_CYCLE_STATE_INVALID`. All six paths behave identically: the `withdrawal_reason` is stored, every dimension row of the cycle in `COMPUTED` or `OVERRIDDEN` moves to `STALE`, every correction in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ANALYST_REVIEW` moves to `REJECTED` and every appeal in `FILED` or `PANEL_ASSIGNED` moves to `DISMISSED` — each carrying `decision_note` `cycle withdrawn` and a null `decided_by_user_id` — and the cycle's `version` counter advances by one. Withdrawal also releases the organization's timeline: the withdrawn cycle's period stops occupying it, so a later cycle may reuse the same interval without tripping the period-overlap rule. `WITHDRAWN` and `PUBLISHED` are both terminal; no operation moves a cycle out of either, and no publication record is ever produced for a withdrawn cycle.

Every failed request answers with the error envelope of §2.2, whose `data.error_id` is one of the identifiers below and whose HTTP status is the one paired with it here. The identifier is the contract; the accompanying `message` is human-readable prose and is never parsed. When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned — the one belonging to the lowest-numbered check in the admission ladder of §2.8 — so the catalogue below must be read together with that ordering. Trigger conditions are not repeated here; each error's precise cause is stated in the §2 and §3 narratives that impose the rule.
