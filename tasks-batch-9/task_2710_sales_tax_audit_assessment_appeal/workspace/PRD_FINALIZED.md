# Sales Tax Audit Assessment Appeal API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state revenue department's sales-tax audit division lives or dies by defensibility. Every dollar it assesses must be traceable back to a documented finding, a methodology the taxpayer agreed to, a limitation period that had not yet run, and a computation an appeals officer can reproduce line by line. Today that chain of custody is held together by spreadsheets and paper files: risk scores are recalculated by hand and disagree with each other, sampling methodologies are negotiated over email with no record of which round superseded which, waiver dates are transcribed incorrectly, and penalty and interest are recomputed from scratch every time a finding moves. When a taxpayer protests, the division cannot always show why the assessed number is the number.

This document specifies the REST API for a single system of record that carries a matter from selection to collection. It covers audit selection by risk score and industry project, information-return matching, scope and statute-of-limitations management with taxpayer-signed waiver extensions, sampling-methodology agreement and projection of sample error to the population, exemption-certificate review under the good-faith acceptance standard, use-tax analysis of untaxed purchases, assessment issuance with penalty and interest computation and abatement for reasonable cause, the three-rung appeal ladder from informal conference through administrative appeal to the tax tribunal, and finally payment arrangements, offers in compromise and collection referral.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is arithmetic determinism: all money is integer cents, all rates are integer basis points, every division names its rounding step and its position in the evaluation order, and no computation ever depends on wall-clock time — the server keeps a logical clock that tests and operators set explicitly. The second is segregation of duties: the auditor who develops a matter may not close it, and no appeal forum may be presided over by the lead auditor or by the officer who already decided the rung below. Where those two commitments collide with convenience, they win.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` names the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the seven role names defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called — the authorization column of §4 is the definitive list — the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are:

- **`SelectionAnalyst`** — runs the selection pipeline: loads third-party information returns, rescores permit holders and works the lead queue.
- **`FieldAuditor`** — performs the examination: proposes and revises sampling methodology, draws the sample, determines exemption certificates and analyses untaxed purchases.
- **`AuditSupervisor`** — supervises the division: converts leads into audits, closes and reopens fieldwork, abandons lapsed audits, voids waivers, drafts, recomputes, issues, abates, finalizes assessments, reverses ledger rows and controls the logical clock.
- **`Taxpayer`** — the audited permit holder: signs waivers, counters and accepts methodology, cures certificates, files protests and requests payment arrangements.
- **`AppealsOfficer`** — presides over the informal conference and the administrative appeal.
- **`TaxTribunalJudge`** — presides over the tax tribunal, the last rung of the ladder.
- **`CollectionsAgent`** — collects issued assessments: refers matters to collections, approves arrangements, records payments and declares defaults.

Six of the seven roles are operator roles whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `SelectionAnalyst`, `FieldAuditor`, `AuditSupervisor`, `AppealsOfficer`, `TaxTribunalJudge` or `CollectionsAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It may, however, record it or compare it to a previously recorded operator identifier where a rule demands it: an audit stores the identifier of its assigned auditor in `lead_auditor_user_id`, a decided proceeding stores the identifier of the officer who decided it in `presiding_officer_user_id`, and the segregation-of-duties rules of §2.7 compare the acting `X-User-ID` against those two stored values.

`Taxpayer` is the one entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `Taxpayer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Taxpayer` record; ownership checks on any resource that is reachable to a `Taxpayer` compare the taxpayer reachable from that resource — directly through `taxpayer_id`, or transitively through an audit, a sampling agreement, an assessment, an adjustment, a proceeding or an arrangement — against `X-User-ID`. A `Taxpayer` caller that addresses a resource belonging to a different permit holder is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Because that check runs only after path existence has been established, a taxpayer probing a foreign identifier learns nothing beyond the fact that it exists.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. Both are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when they fall outside those bounds.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list is sorted by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the per-audit waiver listing, which is returned in statutory replay order — `signed_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — because that is the order in which the limitation-period arithmetic of §3.2 consumes the waivers.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts the records that match the filters before the limit and offset are applied.

Beyond pagination, list endpoints accept filter parameters named exactly after the field they filter on, and a filter narrows the result set to records whose field equals the supplied value. The filters available on each list are: `compliance_status` and `industry_naics_code` on the permit-holder list; `source_type` and `match_status` on a permit holder's information returns; `selection_mode` and `industry_naics_code` on the project list; `industry_project_id`, `taxpayer_id` and `status` on the lead list; `taxpayer_id`, `audit_lead_id`, `agreed_agreement_id`, `status` and `statute_status` on the audit list; `status` on an audit's waivers; `asset_class` on an audit's untaxed purchases; `audit_id`, `supersedes_agreement_id`, `status` and `methodology` on the sampling-agreement list; `stratum_index` and `error_class` on an agreement's sample units; `audit_id`, `sample_unit_id`, `status` and `claimed_exemption_type` on the certificate list; `audit_id`, `supersedes_assessment_id`, `status` and `collection_status` on the assessment list; `arrangement_type` and `status` on an assessment's arrangements; `assessment_id`, `appeal_proceeding_id`, `kind` and `status` on the ledger list; and `assessment_id`, `predecessor_proceeding_id`, `forum`, `status` and `outcome` on the proceeding list.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Trail and Versioning

Thirteen of the fourteen resource families carry the full system-managed property set:

- **`id`** (string) — a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client.
- **`created_at`** (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, e.g. `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`, recording when the record was first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as a cascade from some other request.
- **`version`** (integer) — a counter that increments by one on every mutation of the record, again including server-initiated cascades. It is the value the stale-version protection of §2.10 compares against.

The single exception is the **information return**. A third-party information return is immutable once loaded, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Any client reading an information return should expect both to be absent rather than null.

A derived field being recomputed counts as a mutation. When a rejection of an exemption certificate drives an audit's `projection_status` to `STALE`, or when a posted ledger row lowers an assessment's `balance_due_cents`, the affected audit or assessment has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if a client had written to it directly. This is what makes stale-version protection meaningful: a cascade the client never issued still advances the parent's `version`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, limitation date, interest-day count and window comparison in this system reads a server-held **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An `AuditSupervisor` moves it with `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`; the endpoint returns 200 with an empty data object and rewrites no stored field. Moving the clock changes only the instant against which subsequent requests evaluate `statute_status`, `protest_deadline_date`, `escalation_deadline_date`, `in_scoring_window` and interest days.

Date arithmetic throughout this specification is whole-calendar-day arithmetic: no time-of-day component participates in any comparison, and "the logical clock date" means the calendar date of the current logical clock instant in UTC. A rule that says a date must "equal the logical clock date" is satisfied by any instant on that calendar date.

Certain fields are derived at read time rather than stored. An audit's `statute_expiry_date` is recomputed and its `statute_status` re-derived on every read of that audit and at every validation that consults it, so a status is never served stale. An information return's `in_scoring_window` is likewise evaluated when the record is read.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents; a field whose name ends in `_cents` never carries a fractional part and is never transported as a string. All rates, ratios and error rates are integers denominated in basis points, one basis point being one hundredth of one percent, so 10,000 basis points is exactly 100 percent; a field whose name ends in `_bp` follows that convention. The prevailing state sales-and-use tax rate used by every tax computation below is 625 basis points, that is 6.25 percent.

Wherever a computation divides, the quotient is rounded half up to the unit named at that step — to a whole cent for money, to a whole basis point for a rate, to a whole percentage point for an abatement percentage, to a whole day for a date difference. Rounding half up means a remainder of exactly one half rounds away from zero. The order in which rounding steps occur is part of the specification and not an implementation detail: several of the calculations in §3 produce measurably different integers if the steps are reordered, and each of those calculations states its mandatory order explicitly. No computation may accumulate intermediate values as binary floating-point numbers where an exact integer result is specified.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties

The division's compliance posture rests on two separations that the API enforces rather than merely documents.

The first separates development from review. An audit records the operator identifier of the field auditor assigned when the lead was converted in `lead_auditor_user_id`. The supervisor who closes that audit's fieldwork may not be that same person: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the audit's `lead_auditor_user_id`, closing fieldwork is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The second separates adjudication from both audit and prior adjudication. Deciding an appeal proceeding is rejected with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the `lead_auditor_user_id` of the audit behind the assessment under protest, or equals the `presiding_officer_user_id` recorded on the predecessor proceeding this one escalates from. The same error also covers forum-role mismatch: an `AppealsOfficer` may decide only an `INFORMAL_CONFERENCE` or an `ADMINISTRATIVE_APPEAL`, and a `TaxTribunalJudge` may decide only a `TAX_TRIBUNAL` proceeding, so a `TaxTribunalJudge` deciding an informal conference and an `AppealsOfficer` deciding a tribunal matter are both refused.

These two endpoints — closing fieldwork and deciding a proceeding — are the complete scope of the segregation check. It is evaluated ahead of every deadline rule and every money rule, because a compliance separation must never be masked by a downstream arithmetic complaint.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of taste. Checks are evaluated in the fourteen tiers below, in ascending order; the first tier that finds a violation decides the response, and no later tier may pre-empt an earlier one. A test that expects a 409 conflict on a request that also carries a malformed body will get the 400 instead, because body shape is tier 5 and cross-entity state is tier 10.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the request path resolves to an existing record, validated left to right, producing that entity's own 404: `ERR_TAXPAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INFORMATION_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INDUSTRY_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_LEAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATUTE_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLING_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXEMPTION_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_USE_TAX_PURCHASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_PROCEEDING_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PAYMENT_ARRANGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
4. **Ownership.** When the acting role is `Taxpayer`, the addressed resource must belong to the permit holder named by `X-User-ID` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This tier applies to exactly seven operations: reading one permit holder (`GET /taxpayers/{id}`), reading one assessment (`GET /assessments/{id}`), listing assessments (`GET /assessments`), filing a protest (`POST /appeal-proceedings`), requesting an arrangement (`POST /payment-arrangements`), curing a certificate (`POST /exemption-certificates/{id}/cure`) and executing a waiver (`POST /statute-waivers/{id}/execute`). It deliberately runs after tier 3.
5. **Request shape.** Required body fields present; unknown enum values rejected; cents, basis-point and count fields non-negative; `in_state_usage_pct_bp` and `confidence_level_bp` within 0 through 10,000; `period_start` no later than `period_end`; dates parseable; `abatement_factors` non-empty and drawn only from the four recognized factor names. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
6. **Body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves to an existing record, producing that entity's 404 from the same catalogue as tier 3. Applies to every write endpoint whose body carries a foreign key.
7. **Own-state.** The addressed record is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — issuing a `DRAFT_STALE` assessment, recomputing a `DRAFT` assessment, scoping an already-`SCOPED` audit, closing fieldwork on an audit that is not in `FIELDWORK`, countering an `AGREED` or `SUPERSEDED` agreement, reviewing a `VALIDATED` certificate, curing a certificate that is not `REJECTED`, executing a `VOIDED` waiver, deciding a `DECIDED` proceeding or approving an arrangement that is not `REQUESTED` are all `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Two own-state checks carry their own code at this same tier instead of the generic one: recording a payment against an arrangement whose `status` is not `APPROVED` returns `ERR_ARRANGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), and converting a lead is exempt from the generic check entirely — its status test is deferred to tier 10, where a lead that is not `SELECTED` returns `ERR_LEAD_NOT_SELECTED`. Applies to every action endpoint.
8. **Segregation of duties.** The §2.7 rules, returning `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to exactly two endpoints: `POST /appeal-proceedings/{id}/decision` and `POST /audits/{id}/close-fieldwork`. Compliance separation outranks every deadline and money rule below it.
9. **Stale parent version.** The supplied expected-version field equals the parent's current `version`, per §2.10 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AUDIT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ASSESSMENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to exactly two endpoints: `POST /sampling-agreements` and `POST /assessment-adjustments`.
10. **Referenced-record state.** Sibling records that the operation depends on are in the state it requires: the lead being converted is `SELECTED` (`ERR_LEAD_NOT_SELECTED`); the audit has an `AGREED` sampling agreement and the unit being drawn names that same agreement (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_AGREED`); the audit's `projection_status` is `CURRENT` before an assessment is drafted or recomputed and before fieldwork closes (`ERR_PROJECTION_STALE`); the predecessor proceeding is `DECIDED` before an escalation is filed (`ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_DECIDED`); no proceeding of the assessment is `OPEN` before penalty abatement, finalization or arrangement approval, and the assessment is `ISSUED` or `FINAL` before an arrangement is approved (`ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`). All are HTTP 409. Applies to nine endpoints: `POST /audit-leads/{id}/convert`, `POST /sample-units`, `POST /audits/{id}/project-sample`, `POST /assessments`, `POST /assessments/{id}/recompute`, `POST /assessments/{id}/abate-penalty`, `POST /assessments/{id}/finalize`, `POST /appeal-proceedings` and `POST /payment-arrangements/{id}/approve`.
11. **Interval geometry.** Audit scope periods, sampling strata and sample invoice dates must sit correctly on the calendar: `ERR_AUDIT_SCOPE_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_TAXPAYER_AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS` (409), `ERR_STRATUM_OVERLAP` (422), `ERR_STRATUM_GAP` (422), `ERR_STRATUM_INDEX_INVALID` (422) and `ERR_INVOICE_DATE_OUT_OF_STRATUM` (422). The geometry rules themselves are stated in §3.2, §3.3 and §3.4. Applies to five endpoints: `POST /audits/{id}/scope`, `POST /audit-leads/{id}/convert`, `POST /sampling-agreements`, `POST /sampling-agreements/{id}/revise` and `POST /sample-units`.
12. **Deadlines and the clock.** Limitation-period, waiver, notice-date and appeal-window rules: `ERR_STATUTE_EXPIRED` (409), `ERR_STATUTE_NOT_EXPIRED` (409), `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_EXTENDING` (422), `ERR_WAIVER_ALREADY_PENDING` (409), `ERR_NOTICE_DATE_INVALID` (422), `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409) and `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to nine endpoints: `POST /sampling-agreements/{id}/agree`, `POST /audits/{id}/abandon`, `POST /audits/{id}/close-fieldwork`, `POST /statute-waivers`, `POST /statute-waivers/{id}/execute`, `POST /statute-waivers/{id}/void`, `POST /audits/{id}/project-sample`, `POST /assessments/{id}/issue` and `POST /appeal-proceedings`.
13. **Money rules.** Amounts must fit the balance and the branch they claim: `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, `ERR_REDUCTION_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, `ERR_PLAN_UNDERFUNDED`, `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_HARDSHIP_NOT_DOCUMENTED`, `ERR_CAPITAL_ASSET_FMV_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ERROR_AMOUNT_REQUIRED` and `ERR_ABATEMENT_NOT_ALLOWED`, all HTTP 422. Applies to eight endpoints: `POST /assessment-adjustments`, `POST /appeal-proceedings/{id}/decision`, `POST /payment-arrangements/{id}/approve`, `POST /use-tax-purchases`, `POST /use-tax-purchases/{id}/reclassify`, `POST /sample-units`, `POST /sample-units/{id}/redetermine` and `POST /assessments/{id}/abate-penalty`.
14. **Quotas, rounds, empty sets, sequence and uniqueness — checked last.** `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CANDIDATES` (422), `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (422), `ERR_SAMPLE_SET_EMPTY` (422), `ERR_SAMPLE_SIZE_NOT_MET` (422), `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_OPEN` (409), `ERR_FORUM_SEQUENCE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_KIND_SYSTEM_ONLY` (422), `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER` (409) and `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FINALIZABLE` (409). Applies to ten endpoints: `POST /taxpayers`, `POST /industry-projects/{id}/selection-runs`, `POST /sampling-agreements/{id}/revise`, `POST /sampling-agreements`, `POST /audits/{id}/project-sample`, `POST /audits/{id}/close-fieldwork`, `POST /assessments`, `POST /appeal-proceedings`, `POST /assessment-adjustments` and `POST /assessments/{id}/finalize`.

One consequence deserves emphasis. `ERR_GOOD_FAITH_STANDARD_NOT_MET` (422) belongs to the certificate-review determination described in §3.5 and is evaluated with the money-rule tier; it is the code returned when a reviewer asks for a favourable determination on a certificate that does not satisfy the good-faith standard.

### 2.9. Resource Layout and Path Resolution

The fourteen resource families are peers, each mounted at its own top-level collection under the API root: permit holders, information returns, industry projects, audit leads, audits, statute waivers, sampling agreements, sample units, exemption certificates, use-tax purchases, assessments, assessment adjustments, appeal proceedings and payment arrangements. One aggregate is never nested under another for the purpose of writing to it — relationships are declared by foreign keys carried in the request body, not by URL position.

Nesting appears only in read-only sub-collections that walk a relationship backwards: a permit holder's information returns, an audit's statute waivers, an audit's use-tax purchases, a sampling agreement's sample units, and an assessment's payment arrangements. For these, and for any path carrying more than one identifier, existence is validated strictly left to right and the first identifier that fails produces its own 404. Listing the waivers of a non-existent audit returns `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` and never a waiver-level error.

### 2.10. Stale-Version Protection on Shared Parents

Two write operations act on a parent record that other writers legitimately advance behind the client's back, and both require the client to prove it is working from a current read.

Proposing a sampling agreement requires `expected_audit_version` in the request body. The field is not optional. Its value must equal the current `version` of the audit named by `audit_id`; if the audit's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AUDIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because a proposal supersedes whichever `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` agreement the audit already carries, so a writer holding an out-of-date view of the audit would silently discard a methodology round it never saw. On success the audit's `version` increments by one.

Posting an assessment adjustment requires `expected_assessment_version` in the request body, again non-optional, and again it must equal the current `version` of the assessment named by `assessment_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSESSMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Adjustments accumulate against a moving balance, so a posting computed against an out-of-date `balance_due_cents` would credit an amount the ledger no longer supports; the check forces the writer to re-read the assessment first. On success the assessment's `version` increments by one.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two are processed without any version comparison, and supplying an expected-version field where none is specified has no effect.

### 2.11. Cascades and Conserved Quantities

Many operations in this system mutate more than one record. Those secondary effects are part of the contract, not an optimization: when a rule below says an operation also moves a parent's state, posts a ledger row or recomputes a derived total, that effect is required, immediate and observable on the very next read. Cascades may run several hops deep — a certificate rejection moves a sample unit, the moved unit staleness the audit's projection, and the stale projection freezes a draft assessment — and every record touched along the way has its `updated_at` and `version` advanced.

Four quantities are conserved by construction, and every mutation must leave them intact:

- **Selection quota.** For any industry project, `selection_quota` always equals `selections_used` plus `selections_remaining`. Declining a selected lead releases a slot and a waitlist promotion immediately re-consumes it, so the identity holds after each individual step, not merely at the end.
- **Assessed money.** For any assessment, `balance_due_cents` plus `total_adjustments_cents` plus `amount_paid_cents` always equals `total_due_cents`.
- **Limitation days.** For any audit, the difference between `statute_expiry_date` and `base_statute_expiry_date` is exactly `waiver_days_added` plus `tolled_days`. Voiding every executed waiver returns the audit precisely to its base expiry advanced only by tolled appeal days.
- **Open-audit count.** A permit holder's `open_audit_count` is the number of that holder's audits whose `status` is one of `PLANNED`, `SCOPED`, `FIELDWORK`, `REVIEW_COMPLETE` or `ASSESSED`; conversion increments it and closure or abandonment decrements it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Selection Pipeline: Permit Holders, Information Returns and Scored Leads

Everything begins with the universe of permit holders. A `SelectionAnalyst` or an `AuditSupervisor` registers one by supplying its `legal_name` (between 1 and 160 characters), its `permit_number`, its six-digit `industry_naics_code`, its `filing_frequency` — one of `MONTHLY`, `QUARTERLY` or `ANNUAL` — and its self-reported `gross_receipts_annual_cents`, which may be zero but never negative. A `permit_number` is a state sales-tax permit number written as the literal prefix `STP-` followed by exactly six digits, and it is unique across all permit holders: a second registration reusing one already on file is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER`. Three further inputs are optional. `delinquent_period_count`, the number of filing periods filed late or not filed at all, defaults to 0 and may not exceed 40. `prior_audit_error_rate_bp`, the error rate the most recent prior audit measured, defaults to null — meaning never audited — and when supplied must lie between 0 and 10,000 basis points. `compliance_status` defaults to `COMPLIANT` and may be set at registration to `DELINQUENT` or `REVOKED`; thereafter the system also writes it as a cascade from collection and arrangement outcomes.

Registration also seeds the derived block. `reported_total_cents` and `matched_total_cents` both start at 0 because no information return is yet on file, `open_audit_count` starts at 0, and the four persisted component scores — `info_variance_bp`, `delinquency_bp`, `magnitude_bp` and `prior_audit_bp` — are computed immediately along with `screening_score`, so a freshly registered holder already carries a triage score. With no returns loaded, `prior_audit_bp` for a never-audited holder takes the neutral value of 3,500 basis points, which is also its stored default.

Third-party information returns are what turn a permit holder from a name into a risk. A `SelectionAnalyst` loads one at a time, naming the `taxpayer_id` it belongs to, the `source_type` that produced it — `FEDERAL_1099K`, `WHOLESALER_REPORT` or `MARKETPLACE_REPORT` — the reported period as `period_start` and `period_end` (the end may equal but never precede the start, and both are calendar dates at midnight UTC), the `reported_amount_cents` the third party says it paid the holder, and the `matched_return_amount_cents` the holder's own filed returns account for over the same period. Neither amount may be negative. Loading computes `variance_cents` as the reported amount minus the matched amount, a signed value, and classifies the row in `match_status`: `MATCHED` when the variance is exactly zero, `UNDER_REPORTED` when it is positive, and `OVER_REPORTED` when it is negative. A third computed flag, `in_scoring_window`, is true when the row's `period_end` is on or before the logical clock date; it is evaluated when the record is read rather than frozen at load time, so moving the clock moves the flag. Information returns are immutable once loaded — there is no operation that changes one.

Each load rolls the holder's aggregates forward. `reported_total_cents` is the sum of `reported_amount_cents`, and `matched_total_cents` the sum of `matched_return_amount_cents`, over that holder's returns whose `period_end` falls inside the scoring window: on or before the logical clock date, and no earlier than 1,095 days — three years, the same span as the base limitation period — before the logical clock date. Both sums are recomputed on every information-return load and again whenever the logical clock moves, because the window's boundaries move with it. Addition over integer cents is order-insensitive, so no tie-break is needed. A return whose `matched_return_amount_cents` is zero contributes its full reported amount to the first sum and nothing to the second, which is exactly the signal the score is looking for. A `SelectionAnalyst` may also ask for a recomputation of a single holder on demand, which refreshes the two totals, the four component fields and `screening_score` without touching anything else.

The **risk score** is a weighted score out of 1,000, and it has two variants that share four components. The variance component measures how much of what the holder reported is unexplained: when `reported_total_cents` is zero the component is zero — a holder with no returns in the window scores nothing here rather than dividing by zero — and otherwise it is the absolute difference between `reported_total_cents` and `matched_total_cents`, multiplied by 10,000, divided by `reported_total_cents`, rounded half up to a whole basis point, and then capped at 10,000. The delinquency component is `delinquent_period_count` multiplied by 2,500 basis points — twenty-five percentage points per missed or late period, so four missed periods alone saturate the component — capped at 10,000. The magnitude component takes the smaller of `gross_receipts_annual_cents` and the magnitude cap of 5,000,000,000 cents ($50,000,000.00), multiplies it by 10,000, divides by that same cap, rounds half up and caps at 10,000, so a holder at or above fifty million dollars of receipts scores the full 10,000 and one at five million dollars scores 1,000. The prior-audit component is the neutral 3,500 basis points when `prior_audit_error_rate_bp` is null, and otherwise the smaller of that stored rate and 10,000. Each of these four is persisted on the permit holder as `info_variance_bp`, `delinquency_bp`, `magnitude_bp` and `prior_audit_bp` respectively.

The fifth component belongs only to the full variant: the industry component is the smaller of the project's `industry_error_rate_bp` and 10,000. The **screening variant** is a two-factor triage of a single holder, weighting variance 60 and delinquency 40; its result is stored in `screening_score` and nowhere else. The **full variant** is what a project scoring run uses, weighting variance 35, delinquency 20, industry 20, magnitude 15 and prior audit 10 — five weights that also sum to 100. In both variants the weights multiply the raw basis-point components, the whole weighted sum is divided by 1,000 exactly once, and only that single quotient is rounded half up; there is no per-component rounding, and introducing one produces a different integer. The result is an integer between 0 and 1,000. Because the two variants weight the same holder differently, they routinely disagree about the relative ordering of the same set of holders, and that is intended: triage and project selection are different questions.

An **industry project** is how the division works a sector. A `SelectionAnalyst` or `AuditSupervisor` opens one with a `project_name` of 1 to 120 characters, the six-digit `industry_naics_code` it targets, the historical `industry_error_rate_bp` for that sector between 0 and 10,000, and a `selection_mode` of `THRESHOLD`, `QUOTA` or `HYBRID`. Two further inputs govern how far down the ranking a run reaches: `selection_threshold_score`, an inclusive cutoff between 0 and 1,000, and `selection_quota`, at least 1, being the maximum number of leads that may hold `SELECTED` or `CONVERTED` status at any one time. On creation `selections_used` starts at 0 and `selections_remaining` starts equal to `selection_quota`, establishing the conserved identity of §2.11; `scored_lead_count` starts at 0 and both `last_run_at` and `last_cutoff_score` start null.

A scoring run, which only a `SelectionAnalyst` may start, scores every eligible permit holder in the project's industry and ranks them. Eligibility is threefold: the holder's `industry_naics_code` must be identical to the project's, its `compliance_status` must not be `REVOKED`, and its `open_audit_count` must be 0 — a holder already under audit is out of the running. Each eligible holder yields one **audit lead** in `SCORED` status, carrying `industry_project_id`, `taxpayer_id`, its `risk_score` from the full variant, `score_variant` set to `FULL`, a `score_breakdown` object recording the component basis points actually used (`info_variance_bp`, `delinquency_bp`, `industry_error_rate_bp`, `magnitude_bp`, `prior_audit_bp` and the `weighted_sum` before division), its `rank_position`, and `scored_at` set to the logical clock instant of the run. A project run always writes `FULL`; the only other permitted value, `SCREENING`, labels the taxpayer-level screening score described earlier in this section and is never written to a lead. `rank_position` is 1-based within the run, ordering by `risk_score` descending and breaking ties by the permit holder's `created_at` ascending.

The run then walks that ranking from the top, consuming `selections_remaining` as it goes. Under `THRESHOLD` it selects every lead whose `risk_score` is greater than or equal to `selection_threshold_score`; under `QUOTA` it selects the first `selection_quota` leads regardless of score; under `HYBRID` a lead must satisfy both conditions. A lead the walk selects moves `SCORED → SELECTED`, which increments `selections_used` by one and decrements `selections_remaining` by one. A lead the walk does not select moves `SCORED → NOT_SELECTED` when its `risk_score` is below `selection_threshold_score`, and otherwise stays in `SCORED` as a waitlist entry — a qualifying lead that arrives at the point where `selections_remaining` has reached zero is waitlisted, not rejected. When the walk finishes, the project records `scored_lead_count`, sets `last_run_at` to the run's clock instant, and sets `last_cutoff_score` to the `risk_score` of the lowest-ranked lead the run moved to `SELECTED`, or to `selection_threshold_score` when the run selected nobody. A `THRESHOLD` or `HYBRID` run that scored candidates but selected none of them, or that found no eligible holder at all, is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CANDIDATES` and leaves no leads behind at all — the run is all-or-nothing.

Leads are worked from the queue. Declining a lead, available to a `SelectionAnalyst` or an `AuditSupervisor`, records the mandatory `decline_reason` and is accepted while the lead is either `SELECTED` or `SCORED`; either way the lead ends in `DECLINED`. The two cases differ in their quota consequences, and the difference matters. Declining a `SELECTED` lead decrements `selections_used` and increments `selections_remaining`, then immediately promotes the highest-ranked remaining `SCORED` lead of the same project — highest `risk_score`, ties broken by the permit holder's `created_at` ascending — provided that lead's `risk_score` still meets `selection_threshold_score`; the promoted lead moves `SCORED → SELECTED` with `promoted_from_scored` set to true, and the promotion re-consumes the released slot so that the quota identity holds at every intermediate step. Declining a `SCORED` lead moves no quota counters and triggers no promotion.

Conversion is the supervisor's act. An `AuditSupervisor` converts a lead by supplying the `lead_auditor_user_id` of the field auditor who will develop the matter (1 to 64 characters) and, optionally, the `audit_type`. The lead must be `SELECTED` — a lead in any other status, including a waitlisted `SCORED` lead, is refused with `ERR_LEAD_NOT_SELECTED` — and the permit holder's `open_audit_count` must be 0, or the conversion is refused with `ERR_TAXPAYER_AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS`. On success the lead moves `SELECTED → CONVERTED`, a new audit is created in `PLANNED` status carrying `taxpayer_id`, `audit_lead_id` and `lead_auditor_user_id`, the holder's `open_audit_count` increments by one — which removes it from every future scoring run — and the project's `selections_used` does not move, because `SELECTED` and `CONVERTED` both consume a slot. The response to a conversion is the newly created audit, not the lead. `NOT_SELECTED`, `CONVERTED` and `DECLINED` are terminal lead states; no further operation is accepted on a lead in any of them.

### 3.2. Scope, the Limitation Period and Its Waivers

A converted audit arrives in `PLANNED` with almost nothing decided: `audit_type` is null, both `scope_period_start` and `scope_period_end` are null, `projection_method` is `NONE`, `projection_status` is `NOT_RUN`, `statute_status` is `UNSET`, `agreed_agreement_id` is null, `fraud_indicator` is false, `reopen_count` is 0 and every deficiency total is 0. Its full lifecycle runs `PLANNED → SCOPED → FIELDWORK → REVIEW_COMPLETE → ASSESSED → CLOSED`, with `ABANDONED` as the other terminal state and two cycles — one supervisor-driven and one remand-driven — described in §3.4 and §3.8.

Scoping, which a `FieldAuditor` or an `AuditSupervisor` performs while the audit is `PLANNED`, fixes the examination. It stores the `audit_type` — `FIELD`, `DESK` or `MANAGED` — together with `scope_period_start` and `scope_period_end`, the first and last calendar days of the audited period; the end may equal but never precede the start. A period is audited once and only once, so the proposed closed interval from `scope_period_start` through `scope_period_end` inclusive must not overlap the closed scope interval of any other audit of the same permit holder whose `status` is anything other than `ABANDONED` — `CLOSED` audits included. Sharing even a single endpoint day is an overlap, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_SCOPE_OVERLAP`. On success the audit moves `PLANNED → SCOPED`, and its `base_statute_expiry_date` is set to `scope_period_end` advanced by 1,095 calendar days — the three-year base limitation period — and fixed there permanently. Nothing afterwards moves the base date.

The live limitation date, `statute_expiry_date`, is derived from the base date by replaying the audit's waivers and then adding tolled appeal days, and it is recomputed whenever the scope is set, a waiver is executed, a waiver is voided, an appeal proceeding is decided, or the audit is read. The replay is chronological and never keyed on identifiers: executed waivers are ordered by `signed_date` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending. Starting from the base date, each waiver in turn contributes the whole number of calendar days from the running expiry to that waiver's `extended_to_date`; the waiver records the running expiry it found in `prior_expiry_date` and the days it contributed in `days_added`, and the running expiry then becomes its `extended_to_date`. The sum of all such contributions is stored on the audit as `waiver_days_added`. Separately, `tolled_days` on the audit is the sum of `tolled_days` over every `DECIDED` appeal proceeding reachable through the audit's assessments. The audit's `statute_expiry_date` is the running expiry after the waiver replay, advanced by `tolled_days`.

From that date the system derives `statute_status` against the current logical clock, on every read and at every validation that consults it, and never serves a stored stale value. Before the scope is set the status is `UNSET`. The status is `EXPIRED` when the logical clock date is strictly after `statute_expiry_date`. It is `EXPIRING_SOON` when the audit has not expired and the whole number of days from the logical clock date to `statute_expiry_date` is 90 or fewer — a full calendar quarter of warning. Otherwise it is `PROTECTED`. An `EXPIRED` audit is barred from agreeing a methodology, drawing or projecting a sample, closing fieldwork, issuing an assessment and executing a waiver, each refusal carrying `ERR_STATUTE_EXPIRED`.

Extending the limitation period requires the taxpayer's signature, and the paperwork comes in two steps. A `FieldAuditor` or an `AuditSupervisor` drafts a waiver naming the `audit_id`, the `covers_period_end` it covers — which must equal that audit's `scope_period_end` — the `extended_to_date` being requested, which must fall strictly after the audit's current `statute_expiry_date`, and the `signed_by_name` of the taxpayer officer who will sign. The draft lands in `DRAFTED`, contributes no days to anything, and carries `prior_expiry_date` null and `days_added` 0. Only one waiver per audit may sit in `DRAFTED` awaiting signature at a time; drafting a second while one is outstanding is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_ALREADY_PENDING`. A draft whose `extended_to_date` fails the strictly-after test above is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_EXTENDING`, and that test is evaluated after the outstanding-draft test, so an audit that already carries a `DRAFTED` waiver reports `ERR_WAIVER_ALREADY_PENDING` even when the second draft would not have extended anything either.

Execution belongs to the `Taxpayer`, and only to the permit holder the audit belongs to. The waiver must be `DRAFTED`, the audit's `status` must be one of `SCOPED`, `FIELDWORK` or `REVIEW_COMPLETE`, the audit must not be `EXPIRED`, and the supplied `signed_date` must equal the logical clock date. The waiver moves `DRAFTED → EXECUTED`, capturing `prior_expiry_date` as the audit's expiry at that moment and `days_added` as the whole number of days from that snapshot to `extended_to_date`. If, at its turn in the replay, a waiver's `extended_to_date` is on or before the running expiry, it does not extend anything and the execution is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_EXTENDING`. A successful execution then propagates in three observable steps: the waiver's own fields settle, then the audit's `statute_expiry_date` and `waiver_days_added` are recomputed by the replay, and then the audit's derived `statute_status` moves — typically from `EXPIRING_SOON` back to `PROTECTED` — which is precisely what re-enables closing fieldwork and drafting an assessment on a matter that was about to lapse.

Voiding is the compensating path and belongs to an `AuditSupervisor`, who must supply a `void_reason`. Voiding an `EXECUTED` waiver is permitted while the audit's `status` is `SCOPED`, `FIELDWORK` or `REVIEW_COMPLETE`; it sets the waiver's `days_added` back to 0 and re-runs the replay, so with no other executed waiver on file the audit returns exactly to its `base_statute_expiry_date` advanced only by `tolled_days`, and `waiver_days_added` returns to 0. A void that would leave the audit's recomputed expiry already behind the logical clock is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_EXPIRED`. Voiding a waiver that is still `DRAFTED` is also permitted and records the `void_reason`, but changes no date at all, because an unexecuted waiver never contributed a day. `VOIDED` is terminal: no execution is accepted afterwards, and attempting one is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

When a matter genuinely lapses, an `AuditSupervisor` abandons it, optionally recording an `abandon_reason`. Abandonment is available from `PLANNED`, `SCOPED`, `FIELDWORK` and `REVIEW_COMPLETE`, and it inverts the usual statute rule: the audit's `statute_status` must be `EXPIRED`, and abandoning an audit whose limitation period is still alive is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_NOT_EXPIRED`. Abandonment moves the audit to `ABANDONED`, supersedes every sampling agreement of that audit still in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`, and decrements the permit holder's `open_audit_count` by one, freeing it for future scoring runs. Because `ABANDONED` audits are excluded from the scope-overlap test, abandoning a matter also releases the period for a later audit.

### 3.3. Agreeing the Sampling Methodology

Sales-tax fieldwork rarely examines every invoice; it examines a sample and projects. Because the projection is the number the taxpayer will litigate, the methodology is negotiated before any invoice is pulled, and the negotiation is itself a tracked resource with a bounded number of rounds.

A `FieldAuditor` opens the negotiation by proposing a methodology against a `SCOPED` audit. The proposal names the `audit_id`, carries the `expected_audit_version` required by §2.10, and specifies the `methodology` — either `MEAN_PER_UNIT` or `RATIO_ESTIMATION` — the `confidence_level_bp`, which must be exactly one of 9,000, 9,500 or 9,900 basis points (any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), the `minimum_sample_size` of at least one unit that fieldwork must examine before it may close, and the `strata` array. A proposal may also name `supersedes_agreement_id`, the prior agreement it replaces; that named agreement is moved to `SUPERSEDED` as part of the same operation. A first proposal leaves `supersedes_agreement_id` null. The new agreement starts in `PROPOSED` with `round_number` 1, `examined_unit_count` 0, `counter_note` and `requested_minimum_sample_size` and `agreed_by_name` all null.

The `strata` array holds between 1 and 12 ordered stratum definitions, each an object with a `period_start`, a `period_end`, a `population_base_amount_cents` and a `population_unit_count` — the dollar base and the invoice count of the population that stratum represents. Stratum intervals are half-open: a stratum covers days from its `period_start` inclusive to its `period_end` exclusive. Taken together the strata must tile the audit's scope exactly. Sorted by start date, each stratum's `period_start` must equal the previous stratum's `period_end` — adjacency is required, not merely permitted — the first `period_start` must equal the audit's `scope_period_start`, and the last `period_end` must equal the audit's `scope_period_end` advanced by one day, so that the final scope day is covered by the half-open convention. Two strata covering a common day are rejected with `ERR_STRATUM_OVERLAP`; a hole between adjacent strata, a first start later than the scope start, or a last end earlier than the day after the scope end are all rejected with `ERR_STRATUM_GAP`. The agreement's `population_base_amount_cents` is the sum of the strata's `population_base_amount_cents` and is the denominator population for ratio estimation; its `population_unit_count` is the sum of the strata's `population_unit_count` and is the multiplier for mean-per-unit projection.

The taxpayer's answer may be a counter. Countering, which only the `Taxpayer` may do and only while the agreement is `PROPOSED` and the audit is still `SCOPED`, moves the agreement `PROPOSED → COUNTERED` and records the mandatory `counter_note` stating the objection along with an optional `requested_minimum_sample_size` of at least one. The requested size is advisory only: it changes nothing about the sampling plan until an auditor revision adopts it into `minimum_sample_size`.

Revision is the auditor's reply, available only while the agreement is `COUNTERED`. It moves the agreement `COUNTERED → PROPOSED` and increments `round_number` by one. Any of `methodology`, `confidence_level_bp`, `minimum_sample_size` and `strata` may be supplied, and each supplied value overwrites the stored one; a supplied `strata` array must still tile the audit's scope exactly under the same geometry rules, and the agreement's `population_base_amount_cents` and `population_unit_count` are recomputed from it. The negotiation cycle `PROPOSED → COUNTERED → PROPOSED` is bounded at three rounds: a revision attempted while `round_number` already stands at 3 is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.

Acceptance is the taxpayer's, and it is the event that opens fieldwork. Accepting requires the agreement to be `PROPOSED`, the audit to be `SCOPED` and the audit's `statute_status` not to be `EXPIRED`; the accepting party supplies `agreed_by_name`, the taxpayer officer who accepted the methodology. Acceptance moves the agreement `PROPOSED → AGREED` and cascades: the audit's `agreed_agreement_id` is set to that agreement, the audit moves `SCOPED → FIELDWORK`, and every other sampling agreement of the same audit still in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Both `AGREED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal — no counter, revision or acceptance is accepted against a superseded row, and attempting one is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Only the agreement named by the audit's `agreed_agreement_id` may receive sample units, and only its units ever feed a projection.

### 3.4. Drawing the Sample and Projecting to the Population

With the audit in `FIELDWORK`, the `FieldAuditor` records the invoices actually pulled. Each **sample unit** names the `sampling_agreement_id` it was drawn under, which must be the audit's `agreed_agreement_id`; a unit offered against any other agreement — a superseded round, or an audit with no accepted methodology at all — is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_AGREED`. The unit declares its `stratum_index`, a 0-based index into that agreement's `strata` array, and an index that is not a valid contiguous position in the array is refused with `ERR_STRATUM_INDEX_INVALID`. It carries the auditor's `invoice_reference` for the document, the `invoice_date`, which must fall inside the half-open interval of the declared stratum — on or after that stratum's `period_start` and strictly before its `period_end`, or the unit is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_DATE_OUT_OF_STRATUM` — and the `invoice_amount_cents`, which must be greater than zero and is the per-unit base for ratio estimation. Drawing a sample also requires that the audit's `statute_status` not be `EXPIRED`.

Two further inputs describe the finding. `exempt_sale_amount_cents` is the portion of the invoice claimed exempt; it defaults to 0, may not be negative and may not exceed `invoice_amount_cents`. `error_class` is the auditor's determination and defaults to `PENDING`, meaning undetermined; the other four values are `NO_ERROR`, `UNREPORTED_TAXABLE_SALE`, `CERTIFICATE_DEFECT` and `RATE_ERROR`. `UNREPORTED_TAXABLE_SALE` and `RATE_ERROR` both require `declared_error_amount_cents`, the auditor-stated error in cents; omitting it in either class is refused with `ERR_ERROR_AMOUNT_REQUIRED`, and supplying it in any other class is refused as a malformed request. From these the system computes `error_amount_cents`, which is 0 for both `NO_ERROR` and `PENDING`, equals `declared_error_amount_cents` for `UNREPORTED_TAXABLE_SALE` and `RATE_ERROR`, and for `CERTIFICATE_DEFECT` is `exempt_sale_amount_cents` multiplied by 625 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — the tax on the sale the defective certificate purported to exempt. Every new unit sets the audit's `projection_status` to `STALE`, which in turn moves any assessment of that audit still in `DRAFT` to `DRAFT_STALE`.

A determination can be changed. Redetermining a unit takes a new `error_class` and, where that class demands it, a new `declared_error_amount_cents`; the system recomputes `error_amount_cents` under the same rules, increments the unit's `redetermination_count` by one, sets the audit's `projection_status` to `STALE` and again cascades any `DRAFT` assessment of the audit to `DRAFT_STALE`. Both drawing and redetermining are refused once fieldwork has closed, because the finding set is frozen at that point.

**Projection** is what turns the examined sample into a population number, and only a `FieldAuditor` may run it. The examined set is exactly those sample units of the audit's agreed agreement whose `error_class` is not `PENDING`; undetermined units are excluded both from the sums and from the unit count, and units belonging to a superseded agreement never contribute anything. Let the examined count be the number of such units, the error sum be the total of their `error_amount_cents`, and the base sum be the total of their `invoice_amount_cents`. A projection requested when no unit of the agreed agreement has been determined at all — every unit still `PENDING` — is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_SET_EMPTY`, and a projection whose examined count is below the agreement's `minimum_sample_size` is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_SIZE_NOT_MET`. Projection also requires that the audit's `statute_status` not be `EXPIRED`.

The methodology recorded on the agreed agreement selects between two arithmetics, and they are not interchangeable. Under `MEAN_PER_UNIT`, the mean error per examined unit is computed first — the error sum divided by the examined count, rounded half up to whole cents — and that rounded mean is then multiplied by the agreement's `population_unit_count` to give the projected population deficiency; the population's dollar base plays no part. Under `RATIO_ESTIMATION`, the error rate is computed first — the error sum multiplied by 10,000, divided by the base sum, rounded half up to a whole basis point — and that rounded rate is then applied to the agreement's `population_base_amount_cents`, multiplying and dividing by 10,000 and rounding half up once more; the population's unit count plays no part. The order is mandatory in both branches. Multiplying an unrounded ratio by the population base and rounding only at the end is a different number and is not an acceptable implementation of ratio estimation.

Either way the audit records the results: `sample_error_rate_bp` is the error sum multiplied by 10,000, divided by the base sum and rounded half up to a whole basis point — computed and reported under both methodologies, even though only ratio estimation uses it arithmetically — `projected_deficiency_cents` holds the projected population deficiency, `examined_unit_count` holds the examined count, `projection_method` records the methodology just applied, and `projection_status` becomes `CURRENT`. The same run also re-sums the audit's other two deficiency components, `certificate_deficiency_cents` and `use_tax_deficiency_cents`, so that a successful projection leaves all three components mutually consistent. The agreement additionally tracks its own `examined_unit_count` as the number of sample units created against it.

Closing fieldwork is a supervisory act, and by §2.7 the closing `AuditSupervisor` may not be the audit's own lead auditor. The audit must be in `FIELDWORK`, its `statute_status` must not be `EXPIRED`, its `projection_status` must be `CURRENT` — a stale projection is refused with `ERR_PROJECTION_STALE` — and its `examined_unit_count` must be at least the agreed agreement's `minimum_sample_size`, or the close is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_SIZE_NOT_MET`. The closing supervisor supplies the optional `fraud_indicator`, which defaults to false and, when true, both forces the fraud penalty rate and disqualifies the resulting assessment from any abatement. The audit moves `FIELDWORK → REVIEW_COMPLETE` and the finding set freezes: no sample unit, exemption certificate or use-tax purchase of that audit may be created or changed while it remains in `REVIEW_COMPLETE`.

Reopening is the supervisor's other half of the cycle. An `AuditSupervisor` may move a `REVIEW_COMPLETE` audit back to `FIELDWORK`, optionally recording a `reopen_reason`, provided no assessment of that audit has reached `ISSUED` or `FINAL` — once a notice is out, reopening is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and provided the audit's `statute_status` is not `EXPIRED`. Reopening increments `reopen_count` by one and unfreezes the finding set. It deliberately leaves `projection_status` at `CURRENT`: reopening by itself changes no finding, so a `DRAFT` assessment of that audit stays `DRAFT` until an actual finding moves. This gives the audit machine its first cycle, `REVIEW_COMPLETE → FIELDWORK → REVIEW_COMPLETE`; the second, driven by an appeal remand, appears in §3.8.

### 3.5. The Other Two Deficiency Components: Certificates and Untaxed Purchases

A projected sample is only one third of what a sales-tax audit asserts. The other two components are the tax on sales the seller treated as exempt without a defensible certificate, and the use tax on purchases the seller consumed without paying tax to anyone.

**Exemption certificates.** Either a `FieldAuditor` or the `Taxpayer` may file a certificate for review against an `audit_id`. The filing states the `purchaser_name` on the certificate, the `purchaser_permit_number` — which may be null, and a null value alone is fatal to the good-faith standard — the `claimed_exemption_type`, one of `RESALE`, `MANUFACTURING`, `AGRICULTURAL`, `NONPROFIT` or `GOVERNMENT`, and the `covered_sale_amount_cents` the certificate purports to exempt, which must be greater than zero. Three booleans record the seller's diligence: `received_before_sale`, whether the seller held the certificate before the sale closed; `form_complete`, whether every mandatory field of the state form is filled in; and `claimed_exemption_matches_purchaser_business`, whether the claimed exemption is consistent with the purchaser's line of business. The filing may also name `sample_unit_id`, the sampled invoice this certificate supports; when that link is present a rejection flows into the population through the linked unit, and when it is absent a rejection is assessed directly. A new certificate lands in `ON_FILE` with `cure_count` 0, `review_note` null and `missing_certificate_tax_cents` 0 — that amount is only ever set on rejection.

The computed flag `good_faith_accepted` is true only when all four diligence conditions hold together: the certificate was received before the sale, the form is complete, a `purchaser_permit_number` is present, and the claimed exemption matches the purchaser's business. Any single failure makes it false.

A `FieldAuditor` determines the certificate while the audit is in `FIELDWORK`, supplying a `determination` of either `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED` and an optional `review_note`. A `VALIDATED` determination is available from `ON_FILE` and from `CURED`, and it requires `good_faith_accepted` to be true; asking for it on a certificate that fails the standard is refused with `ERR_GOOD_FAITH_STANDARD_NOT_MET`. Validation records the `review_note`, moves the certificate to its terminal `VALIDATED` state, and re-sums the audit's `certificate_deficiency_cents`, which drops this certificate's `missing_certificate_tax_cents` out of the total and resets that field to 0. If the certificate is linked to a sample unit whose `error_class` currently stands at `CERTIFICATE_DEFECT` — that is, if an earlier rejection had pushed the defect into the sample — validation reverses that too: the unit returns to `error_class` `NO_ERROR` with `error_amount_cents` 0, and the audit's `projection_status` becomes `STALE`.

A `REJECTED` determination is available from `ON_FILE` and from `CURED` and needs no good-faith precondition at all, since rejection is precisely the finding that the standard was not met. It propagates in four observable hops. First, the certificate records the `review_note`, takes status `REJECTED`, and computes `missing_certificate_tax_cents` as `covered_sale_amount_cents` multiplied by 625 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. Second, caused by the first, the tax is routed: when `sample_unit_id` is set, the linked sample unit is redetermined to `error_class` `CERTIFICATE_DEFECT` with its `error_amount_cents` recomputed as that unit's `exempt_sale_amount_cents` multiplied by 625 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up; when `sample_unit_id` is null, the audit's `certificate_deficiency_cents` is recomputed instead. Third, caused by the second, the audit's `sample_error_rate_bp` can no longer be trusted and `projection_status` is set to `STALE`. Fourth, caused by the third, every assessment of that audit whose `status` is `DRAFT` moves to `DRAFT_STALE`.

The audit-level roll-up carries the double-counting guard, and it is the single most misread rule in this domain. The audit's `certificate_deficiency_cents` is the sum of `missing_certificate_tax_cents` over the certificates of that audit whose status is `REJECTED` **and** whose `sample_unit_id` is null. Rejected certificates that are linked to a sample unit are deliberately excluded: their tax has already entered the population through the linked unit's `CERTIFICATE_DEFECT` `error_amount_cents`, and therefore through the projection. Adding them to the roll-up as well would assess the same exempt sale twice. Certificates that were rejected and later validated after a cure drop out of the sum and their `missing_certificate_tax_cents` returns to 0.

Curing belongs to the `Taxpayer` and only to the permit holder under audit. A cure is accepted while the certificate is `REJECTED` and the audit is in `FIELDWORK`; the taxpayer supplies whichever of `purchaser_permit_number`, `received_before_sale`, `form_complete` and `claimed_exemption_matches_purchaser_business` it can now substantiate, each supplied value overwriting the stored one. The certificate moves `REJECTED → CURED`, `cure_count` increments by one, and `good_faith_accepted` is recomputed from the updated values — so a cure that supplied the missing particular unblocks the `CURED → VALIDATED` edge, while a cure that did not still leaves only `CURED → REJECTED` available. A cure deliberately leaves the linked unit's determination and the audit's `certificate_deficiency_cents` untouched until the next review, so the money moves only when an auditor determines the cured certificate. `REJECTED → CURED → REJECTED` is the certificate revision cycle and may repeat.

**Untaxed purchases.** A `FieldAuditor` records each out-of-state purchase the seller consumed without tax, naming the `audit_id`, the `vendor_name` that invoiced it, the `invoice_date`, which must fall inside the audit's scope period under closed-interval semantics whenever that scope has already been fixed, and the `purchase_price_cents`, which must be greater than zero. Unlike a sample unit or a certificate, an untaxed purchase carries no `FIELDWORK` precondition of its own: the audit's `status` constrains neither recording nor reclassifying a purchase except through the `REVIEW_COMPLETE` freeze noted at the end of this section, so a purchase may equally be recorded against an audit that is still `SCOPED`. The `asset_class` selects the treatment: `CONSUMABLE` for supplies, `CAPITAL_ASSET` for equipment, `EXEMPT_MANUFACTURING` for an exempt manufacturing input. Four optional inputs describe the capital-asset story: `in_state_usage_pct_bp`, the in-state usage share in basis points, which defaults to 10,000 — full in-state use — and must lie between 1 and 10,000; `first_use_outside_state`, defaulting to false; `months_used_outside_state`, the whole months of documented out-of-state use before the asset entered the state, defaulting to 0 and capped at 600; and `fair_market_value_at_entry_cents`, the asset's value when it entered the state, which must be greater than zero when supplied. A fifth optional input, `tax_paid_other_state_cents`, defaults to 0 and records sales or use tax already paid to another state.

The liability of each purchase is computed in four steps, and the branch taken depends entirely on `asset_class`. A `CONSUMABLE` purchase takes `taxable_basis_cents` equal to `purchase_price_cents`; apportionment never applies to it, and any `in_state_usage_pct_bp` supplied on such a row is ignored by the arithmetic. An `EXEMPT_MANUFACTURING` purchase takes `taxable_basis_cents` of 0 — it is recorded for completeness and contributes nothing. A `CAPITAL_ASSET` purchase first chooses its pre-apportionment base: when `first_use_outside_state` is true **and** `months_used_outside_state` is at least 6, the asset entered the state already used and the base steps down to `fair_market_value_at_entry_cents`, which is mandatory in exactly that branch and whose omission is refused with `ERR_CAPITAL_ASSET_FMV_REQUIRED`; otherwise the base is `purchase_price_cents`. That base is then apportioned: `taxable_basis_cents` is the base multiplied by `in_state_usage_pct_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. From the basis, `gross_use_tax_cents` is `taxable_basis_cents` multiplied by 625 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up; `credit_applied_cents` is the smaller of `tax_paid_other_state_cents` and `gross_use_tax_cents`, so the credit is clamped at the gross tax and a purchase never produces a refund; and `use_tax_due_cents` is `gross_use_tax_cents` less `credit_applied_cents`.

The audit's `use_tax_deficiency_cents` is the sum of `use_tax_due_cents` over every untaxed purchase of that audit, including rows whose liability computes to 0. Recording a purchase sets the audit's `projection_status` to `STALE`, which cascades any `DRAFT` assessment of the audit to `DRAFT_STALE`. A `FieldAuditor` may also reclassify a purchase, supplying a new `asset_class` and any of the same four apportionment inputs plus `tax_paid_other_state_cents`; reclassification re-runs the whole computation in place, increments `reclassification_count` by one, re-sums the audit total, sets `projection_status` to `STALE` and cascades any `DRAFT` assessment to `DRAFT_STALE` exactly as a creation does. Like sample units and certificates, purchases may be neither created nor changed while the audit sits in `REVIEW_COMPLETE`.

### 3.6. Assessing the Liability: Drafting, Issuing and Finalizing a Notice

When the three deficiency components are settled and the audit is in `REVIEW_COMPLETE`, an `AuditSupervisor` drafts the notice of assessment against the `audit_id`. The audit's `projection_status` must be `CURRENT`; drafting against a `STALE` audit is refused with `ERR_PROJECTION_STALE`, which is the whole point of the staleness flag — no notice may assert a number that a later finding has already invalidated. An audit carries at most one live notice: if it already has an assessment whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `DRAFT_STALE` or `ISSUED`, a second draft is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_OPEN` unless the request names that existing row in `supersedes_assessment_id`, in which case the named row moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state and accepts no further recompute, issue, finalize, adjustment or proceeding. Superseding is available from `DRAFT`, from `DRAFT_STALE` and from `ISSUED` alike, and a superseded assessment contributes nothing to any total. A new assessment starts at `status` `DRAFT` on the notice axis and `collection_status` `NOT_DUE` on the collection axis, with `notice_mailed_date`, `protest_deadline_date` and `issued_at` all null.

**The five-step liability rollup.** Drafting runs the rollup, and its five steps must be performed in exactly this order, with the half-up rounding applied at each named step rather than once at the end.

Step one fixes the deficiency the notice asserts. The assessment's `tax_deficiency_cents` is the sum of the audit's three components — `projected_deficiency_cents`, `certificate_deficiency_cents` and `use_tax_deficiency_cents` — snapshotted onto the assessment at the moment the amounts are computed, so the notice keeps asserting the figure it was drafted on even if the audit later changes.

Step two selects the negligence penalty rate. The rate is an average rate applied to the whole deficiency, not a marginal rate applied to slices of it. When the audit's `fraud_indicator` is set, `penalty_rate_bp` is 5,000 basis points and no tier is consulted. Otherwise the tier is chosen from the whole `tax_deficiency_cents`: above 1,000,000,000 cents the rate is 2,500 basis points; above 100,000,000 cents it is 1,500; above 1,000,000 cents it is 1,000; and at 1,000,000 cents or below it is 500. Each threshold is strict, so a deficiency of exactly 1,000,000 cents takes the 500-point rate and a deficiency of 1,000,001 cents takes the 1,000-point rate. The `penalty_cents` is then `tax_deficiency_cents` multiplied by `penalty_rate_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up.

Step three computes simple interest. Interest never compounds and always uses a 365-day year. The `interest_days` is the whole number of calendar days from the audit's `scope_period_end` to the current logical clock date, floored at zero — so a rollup run on the scope's last day, or earlier, carries zero interest days — plus the sum of `tolled_days` over the `DECIDED` appeal proceedings of prior assessments of the same audit, which is how time spent under protest on a vacated notice follows the audit onto its replacement. The `interest_cents` is the sum of `tax_deficiency_cents` and `penalty_cents`, multiplied by the annual rate of 800 basis points, multiplied by `interest_days`, divided by the product of 10,000 and 365, rounded half up. Interest is therefore charged on penalty as well as on tax.

Step four tests the small-balance waiver, and it tests the provisional total rather than the tax alone. The `provisional_total_due_cents` is the sum of `tax_deficiency_cents`, `penalty_cents` and `interest_cents` as computed so far. If that provisional total is less than or equal to 5,000 cents — the comparison is inclusive, so a provisional total of exactly 5,000 cents is waived — then `penalty_waived_small_balance` becomes true, `penalty_cents` is set to 0, and `interest_cents` is recomputed from scratch on the bare tax: `tax_deficiency_cents` multiplied by 800 basis points, multiplied by `interest_days`, divided by the product of 10,000 and 365, rounded half up. Forgetting to recompute the interest after zeroing the penalty is the classic error here. If the provisional total exceeds 5,000 cents, `penalty_waived_small_balance` stays false and nothing is recomputed.

Step five states the totals. The `total_due_cents` is the sum of `tax_deficiency_cents`, `penalty_cents` and `interest_cents` as they stand after step four. The small-balance test is emphatically not re-applied to this recomputed total, and a waiver once granted never un-waives itself even though removing the penalty may have dropped the total further below the threshold. Finally `balance_due_cents` is `total_due_cents` less `total_adjustments_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`, which on a fresh draft equals `total_due_cents` because both aggregates start at 0. Note that `provisional_total_due_cents` is a stored field and keeps the pre-waiver figure the threshold was tested against; it is not overwritten by step five.

A supervisor or a `FieldAuditor` may also ask what an audit would assess right now without creating anything. That computed read returns `tax_deficiency_cents`, `penalty_rate_bp`, `penalty_cents`, `interest_days`, `interest_cents`, `provisional_total_due_cents`, `penalty_waived_small_balance` and `total_due_cents` from the same five-step rollup, together with the audit's current `projection_status` so the caller can see whether the preview rests on current findings. It creates no assessment, writes nothing and — unlike drafting — is not blocked by a `STALE` projection; the returned `projection_status` is the caller's warning instead.

**Staleness and recompute.** A `DRAFT` assessment is a live projection of the audit's findings, so any finding that moves the audit's `projection_status` to `STALE` also moves every `DRAFT` assessment of that audit to `DRAFT_STALE`: drawing or redetermining a sample unit, reviewing a certificate, and recording or reclassifying an untaxed purchase all do this. In `DRAFT_STALE` the stored amounts are frozen exactly as they were and issuance is blocked. An `AuditSupervisor` clears the flag by recomputing, which is accepted only from `DRAFT_STALE` and only when the audit's `projection_status` has returned to `CURRENT` — a stale audit yields `ERR_PROJECTION_STALE`, and recomputing an assessment that is already `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A recompute re-runs the whole five-step rollup and overwrites `tax_deficiency_cents`, `penalty_rate_bp`, `penalty_cents`, `interest_days`, `interest_cents`, `provisional_total_due_cents`, `penalty_waived_small_balance`, `total_due_cents` and `balance_due_cents`, then returns the assessment to `DRAFT`. Because `interest_days` is recomputed against the clock as it stands at that moment, a recompute after the clock has advanced legitimately produces more interest than the original draft.

**Issuing the notice.** Issuance is the supervisor's act of mailing the notice, and it takes a `notice_mailed_date` in the body which must equal the current logical clock date; any other value, past or future, is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_DATE_INVALID`. The assessment must be in `DRAFT` — a `DRAFT_STALE` notice is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — the audit must still be in `REVIEW_COMPLETE`, and the audit's derived `statute_status` must not be `EXPIRED`, since a notice mailed after the limitation period has run is void and is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_EXPIRED`. Issuance sets `issued_at` to the logical-clock timestamp and computes `protest_deadline_date` as `notice_mailed_date` plus 60 calendar days, the protest window. It then does two things on other axes: it kicks the collection axis once, moving `collection_status` from `NOT_DUE` to `DUE`, and it moves the audit from `REVIEW_COMPLETE` to `ASSESSED`. This is the one place where a notice-axis transition drives the collection axis; from here the two axes evolve independently, and an assessment may perfectly well sit at `status` `ISSUED` or `FINAL` while its `collection_status` reads `ARRANGED` or `REFERRED`.

**Finalizing.** Finalization is the end of the notice's life and the end of the audit. An `AuditSupervisor` may finalize an assessment whose `status` is `ISSUED` only when every avenue of protest has closed: no proceeding of the assessment may be `OPEN`, the logical clock date must be strictly after `protest_deadline_date`, and it must be strictly after the `escalation_deadline_date` of every `DECIDED` proceeding of that assessment. Finalizing on the deadline date itself is premature, because a filing made on the deadline is timely. A request that fails any of those three conditions is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FINALIZABLE`, except that an `OPEN` proceeding is reported as `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING` under the earlier precedence tier. Finalization moves `status` from `ISSUED` to the terminal `FINAL`, which cascades the audit from `ASSESSED` to `CLOSED`, which in turn decrements the taxpayer's `open_audit_count` by one and so frees that taxpayer to be audited again. There is one bypass: a `TAX_TRIBUNAL` decision of `SUSTAINED` or `REDUCED` moves the assessment to `FINAL` by cascade without consulting the deadline conditions at all, because the ladder is exhausted and there is nothing further to wait for. `FINAL` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal alongside `SUPERSEDED`; no recompute, issue or further decision touches them, though a `FINAL` assessment with an outstanding balance continues to move on the collection axis.

### 3.7. The Adjustment Ledger and Penalty Abatement

Every movement of money against an issued liability is a ledger row, never an in-place edit of the assessed total. `total_due_cents` is what the state asserted; the ledger records what has since been credited, written off or charged. Each row names the `assessment_id` whose balance it moves, and rows produced by an appeal also carry the `appeal_proceeding_id` of the proceeding that produced them — that field is null on every row a client posts and on every row produced by a collection or arrangement cascade.

A row's `kind` selects its meaning: `PAYMENT` for cash, `PENALTY_ABATEMENT` for reasonable-cause relief, `APPEAL_REDUCTION` for a credit an appeals decision granted, `ASSESSMENT_VACATED` for the wholesale cancellation of a remanded notice, `COLLECTION_FEE` for the statutory fee added on referral, and `OIC_WRITEOFF` for the balance forgiven when an offer in compromise is performed. The `amount_cents` is a signed credit against `total_due_cents`: positive for `PENALTY_ABATEMENT`, `PAYMENT`, `APPEAL_REDUCTION`, `ASSESSMENT_VACATED` and `OIC_WRITEOFF`, and negative for `COLLECTION_FEE`, which is a charge and therefore raises the balance rather than lowering it. The `source` distinguishes provenance: `MANUAL` for a row a client posted through the adjustments endpoint, `SYSTEM` for a row a cascade wrote. Every row carries `posted_at`, the logical-clock timestamp of posting, and starts at `status` `POSTED`.

Only `PAYMENT` may be posted by a client. An `AuditSupervisor` or a `CollectionsAgent` posts one by naming the `assessment_id`, the `expected_assessment_version`, the `kind` and the `amount_cents`. Naming any of the five `SYSTEM`-only kinds — `PENALTY_ABATEMENT`, `APPEAL_REDUCTION`, `COLLECTION_FEE`, `OIC_WRITEOFF` or `ASSESSMENT_VACATED` — is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_KIND_SYSTEM_ONLY`, because each of those kinds has exactly one lawful producer inside the system and permitting a client to forge one would break the correspondence between the ledger and the events that justify it. For the same reason `amount_cents` is a computed value on `PENALTY_ABATEMENT` and on every `SYSTEM` kind and is only ever supplied by a client on a `PAYMENT`. An `amount_cents` greater than the assessment's current `balance_due_cents` is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`; the ledger never over-credits an assessment into a negative balance. The `abatement_factors` array is never accepted on this endpoint at all — it is carried only on the abatement row that the abate-penalty operation creates — and `abatement_pct` is null on every non-abatement kind. A successful posting sets `status` to `POSTED` and `source` to `MANUAL`, re-derives the assessment's `total_adjustments_cents` and `balance_due_cents`, and, when the balance reaches exactly 0, moves the collection axis to `SATISFIED`.

Three aggregates hang off the ledger and each has a precise definition. `total_adjustments_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the rows of that assessment whose `status` is `POSTED`, with credits positive and the collection fee negative; `REVERSED` rows drop out of the sum entirely. `total_abated_cents` is the same sum restricted to rows whose `kind` is `PENALTY_ABATEMENT`. `amount_paid_cents` is a deliberately separate aggregate that accumulates only the cash recorded through an arrangement's payments, not the ledger's `PAYMENT` rows; a manually posted `PAYMENT` adjustment flows into `total_adjustments_cents` instead. Because the two aggregates draw from disjoint sources, the same money can never be counted twice. The conserved-money invariant closes the system: after every mutation, `balance_due_cents` plus `total_adjustments_cents` plus `amount_paid_cents` equals `total_due_cents`.

An `AuditSupervisor` may reverse a posted row, supplying a `reversal_reason`. Reversal moves `status` from `POSTED` to `REVERSED` and records the reason; it does not delete the row, so the history of what was credited and then unwound remains legible. Reversal then re-derives `total_adjustments_cents`, `total_abated_cents` and `balance_due_cents` so that the conserved-money invariant still holds exactly — reversing a credit restores the balance it had removed, and reversing a `COLLECTION_FEE` removes the charge it had added. A `SATISFIED` assessment whose balance becomes positive again as a result returns to `collection_status` `DUE`.

**Penalty abatement.** Abatement is relief for reasonable cause and it is the one ledger row a client asks for without naming its amount. An `AuditSupervisor` requests it against an assessment whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `penalty_cents` is greater than zero, supplying `abatement_factors`: a non-empty array of recognized factor names drawn from `FIRST_TIME_COMPLIANT`, `RELIANCE_ON_WRITTEN_ADVICE`, `RECORDS_LOST_IN_DISASTER` and `SUBSTANTIAL_COOPERATION`. An empty array or any unrecognized name is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Abatement is refused while any proceeding of the assessment is `OPEN`, with `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`, since the appeal may itself dispose of the penalty.

The relief percentage is a waterfall of additive factor points, then a series of caps applied in a fixed order. `FIRST_TIME_COMPLIANT` contributes 40 points, `RELIANCE_ON_WRITTEN_ADVICE` 35, `RECORDS_LOST_IN_DISASTER` 25 and `SUBSTANTIAL_COOPERATION` 20; the points of every supplied factor are added together and the sum is capped at 100, so all four factors together give 120 points and yield an `abatement_pct` of 100. The caps then run in order. First, if the audit's `fraud_indicator` is set the request is refused outright with `ERR_ABATEMENT_NOT_ALLOWED` — fraud is never abatable at any percentage. Second, if the taxpayer's `compliance_status` is `DELINQUENT` the `abatement_pct` is capped at 50, so a delinquent taxpayer who earned 60 points receives 50 and one who earned 40 still receives 40; the cap lowers a percentage, it never raises one. Third, if the assessment's `penalty_waived_small_balance` is true the request is refused with `ERR_ABATEMENT_NOT_ALLOWED`, because the small-balance pass already zeroed the penalty and there is nothing left to abate. An assessment whose `penalty_cents` is 0 for any reason is refused the same way.

The abated amount is `penalty_cents` multiplied by `abatement_pct`, divided by 100 and rounded half up, and it is computed against the **original** `penalty_cents` stored on the assessment rather than against any penalty net of an earlier abatement. Abatement posts a `SYSTEM` adjustment of kind `PENALTY_ABATEMENT` carrying that amount, the supplied `abatement_factors` and the computed `abatement_pct`, which raises `total_abated_cents` and `total_adjustments_cents` and lowers `balance_due_cents`. Because the computation always reads the original penalty, a second abatement on the same assessment can only be posted after the first has been reversed; otherwise the two rows would together credit more penalty than was ever assessed.

### 3.8. Protest and the Three-Rung Appeal Ladder

A taxpayer who disagrees with an issued notice protests it, and the protest ladder has three rungs which must be climbed in order: `INFORMAL_CONFERENCE`, then `ADMINISTRATIVE_APPEAL`, then `TAX_TRIBUNAL`. Only the `Taxpayer` who owns the assessment may file, naming the `assessment_id`, the `forum`, a `protest_basis` of between 1 and 500 characters stating the grounds, and, for anything above the first rung, the `predecessor_proceeding_id` of the forum being escalated from. The request body also accepts an optional `filed_date`, for symmetry with the paper form; it is never a required field, and when it is supplied it is only checked for being a parseable calendar date and is then ignored: the stored `filed_date` is always the logical clock date at the moment of filing.

Which rung is legal depends on what came before. A first proceeding must be an `INFORMAL_CONFERENCE` and must be filed on or before the assessment's `protest_deadline_date`; day 60 inclusive is still open, and a filing after it is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A proceeding at a higher forum must name a `predecessor_proceeding_id`, that predecessor must sit at the immediately preceding forum, and it must be `DECIDED`. Skipping a rung — going straight to the tribunal from an informal conference — or repeating a rung already used on that assessment is `ERR_FORUM_SEQUENCE_INVALID`; naming a predecessor that is still `OPEN` is `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_DECIDED`; and filing after the predecessor's `escalation_deadline_date` is `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A new proceeding starts at `status` `OPEN` with `outcome` `NONE`, and it sets the assessment's `collection_hold` to true. That flag is the taxpayer's practical protection: while it is true the assessment cannot be referred to collections, so the state does not pursue money that is under dispute.

The deciding act belongs to the forum's officer. An `AppealsOfficer` decides an `INFORMAL_CONFERENCE` or an `ADMINISTRATIVE_APPEAL`; a `TaxTribunalJudge` decides a `TAX_TRIBUNAL` proceeding. A role that does not match the forum is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even though both roles are listed on the endpoint, and so is a decision attempted by the user recorded as the audit's `lead_auditor_user_id`, or by the user recorded as the predecessor proceeding's `presiding_officer_user_id` — nobody reviews their own work, and nobody hears the appeal of a decision they themselves made one rung down. The proceeding must be `OPEN`; deciding a `DECIDED` proceeding is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The decision supplies an `outcome` and an optional `decision_rationale`, plus `reduction_amount_cents` on exactly one branch.

Whatever the branch, deciding moves `status` from `OPEN` to the terminal `DECIDED`, sets `decided_date` to the logical clock date, sets `tolled_days` to the whole number of days from `filed_date` to `decided_date`, records `presiding_officer_user_id` as the deciding user, clears the assessment's `collection_hold` when no other proceeding of that assessment remains `OPEN`, and recomputes the audit's `statute_expiry_date` so that the days the dispute consumed are added to the limitation period. The `escalation_deadline_date` is set to `decided_date` plus the escalation window of the next rung up — 30 calendar days to reach the `ADMINISTRATIVE_APPEAL` and 30 calendar days to reach the `TAX_TRIBUNAL` — and is null when the deciding forum is the `TAX_TRIBUNAL`, because there is no next rung, or when the outcome is `REMANDED`, because the notice being appealed no longer exists.

**Branch one: `SUSTAINED`.** The forum upholds the assessment as issued. No ledger row is written and no money moves; `reduction_amount_cents` must be absent. If the deciding forum is the `TAX_TRIBUNAL` the ladder is exhausted, so the assessment moves from `ISSUED` to `FINAL` immediately, without waiting for any deadline, and that cascades the audit from `ASSESSED` to `CLOSED` and decrements the taxpayer's `open_audit_count` by one. A `SUSTAINED` decision at a lower forum leaves the assessment `ISSUED` with a fresh escalation window running.

**Branch two: `REDUCED`.** The forum grants a partial credit, and this is the deepest cascade in the system. The `reduction_amount_cents` is required here and forbidden on the other two branches; it must not exceed the assessment's `balance_due_cents`, and a larger figure is refused with `ERR_REDUCTION_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. The effects proceed in five caused hops. First, the proceeding records `outcome` `REDUCED` along with `decided_date`, `tolled_days`, `presiding_officer_user_id` and `escalation_deadline_date`. Second, caused by the first, a `SYSTEM` adjustment of kind `APPEAL_REDUCTION` is posted for `reduction_amount_cents` with its `appeal_proceeding_id` pointing back at this proceeding. Third, caused by the second, the assessment's `total_adjustments_cents` and `balance_due_cents` are re-derived, and if `balance_due_cents` reaches 0 the collection axis moves to `SATISFIED`. Fourth, caused by the third, every payment arrangement of that assessment whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` moves to `COMPLETED` — there is nothing left to pay. Fifth, caused by the fourth, the taxpayer's `compliance_status` is recomputed to `COMPLIANT`, provided the taxpayer has no other assessment whose `collection_status` is `REFERRED`. A `REDUCED` decision at the `TAX_TRIBUNAL` additionally moves the assessment from `ISSUED` to `FINAL`, with the audit-closing cascade that entails. A partial reduction that leaves a positive balance stops after the third hop: the collection axis stays where it was and the arrangements keep running.

**Branch three: `REMANDED`.** The forum sends the matter back for more fieldwork, and this is the branch that closes the audit lifecycle's loop. The `TAX_TRIBUNAL` may not remand — it is the last word — and a remand attempted there is refused with `ERR_FORUM_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. The `reduction_amount_cents` must be absent. The remand sets `outcome` `REMANDED`, `decided_date`, `tolled_days`, `presiding_officer_user_id` and an `escalation_deadline_date` of null. It then vacates the notice: the assessment moves from `ISSUED` to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, a `SYSTEM` adjustment of kind `ASSESSMENT_VACATED` is posted for the whole remaining `balance_due_cents` so that the balance falls to exactly 0, `collection_status` is reset to `NOT_DUE`, `collection_hold` is cleared, and every payment arrangement of the assessment in `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` moves to the terminal `CANCELLED` — with an arrangement that had been `APPROVED` also returning the collection axis to `NOT_DUE` as part of that vacate. Finally it reopens the audit: the audit moves from `ASSESSED` back to `FIELDWORK`, its `reopen_count` increments by one and its `projection_status` becomes `STALE`, so the auditor must revisit the findings and project again before any replacement notice can be drafted. The audit's `statute_expiry_date` is recomputed with this proceeding's `tolled_days` included, which is what makes the reopened audit workable: the days the taxpayer spent protesting do not count against the state's limitation period. This is the second and larger of the audit's two cycles, `ASSESSED → FIELDWORK → REVIEW_COMPLETE → ASSESSED`; the replacement assessment drafted afterwards inherits the tolled days of this proceeding through step three of the liability rollup.

### 3.9. Collection: Referral, Installment Plans and Offers in Compromise

The collection axis begins to move the moment a notice is issued, and it moves independently of the notice axis for the rest of the assessment's life. `NOT_DUE` is a liability not yet demanded, `DUE` one demanded and unpaid, `ARRANGED` one covered by an approved arrangement, `REFERRED` one handed to enforced collection, and `SATISFIED` one whose balance has reached zero.

**Referral.** A `CollectionsAgent` refers an unpaid assessment, optionally recording a `referral_note`. Referral is accepted from `collection_status` `DUE` and from `ARRANGED` — the latter being how a broken arrangement's assessment is escalated — and it is refused while `collection_hold` is true, with the generic state code `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since the hold is a declared precondition of the transition and an assessment under active protest is not collectible. Referral proceeds in three caused hops. First, `collection_status` becomes `REFERRED`. Second, caused by the first, a `SYSTEM` adjustment of kind `COLLECTION_FEE` is posted whose `amount_cents` is the negative of `balance_due_cents` multiplied by the collection-fee rate of 1,000 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — ten percent of the balance at the moment of referral, charged as a negative credit so that it raises `balance_due_cents`. Third, caused by the second, the taxpayer's `compliance_status` becomes `DELINQUENT`, which is the same flag that later caps any penalty abatement at 50 percent and that raises the taxpayer's screening risk score.

**Requesting an arrangement.** Either the `Taxpayer` who owns the assessment or a `CollectionsAgent` may request one, naming the `assessment_id` and the `arrangement_type`. An `INSTALLMENT_PLAN` pays the liability down over time and therefore requires `monthly_installment_cents`, which must be greater than zero, and `term_months`, which must be between 1 and 72; `offer_amount_cents` is forbidden on it. An `OFFER_IN_COMPROMISE` settles the liability for less than its face amount and therefore requires `offer_amount_cents`, which must be greater than zero, and forbids both `monthly_installment_cents` and `term_months`. The optional `hardship_documented` flag defaults to false and records whether financial hardship documentation is on file. The request computes `scheduled_total_cents` — `monthly_installment_cents` multiplied by `term_months` for an installment plan, and `offer_amount_cents` for an offer in compromise — and lands at `status` `REQUESTED` with `payments_made_count` 0, `amount_paid_cents` 0 and `approved_at` null. Requesting costs nothing and moves no other state; the arrangements of one assessment may be listed through its reverse sub-collection, filtered by `arrangement_type` and by `status`.

**Approval.** Only a `CollectionsAgent` approves, and approval is where the state's terms are enforced. The arrangement must be `REQUESTED`; approving anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The assessment's `status` must be `ISSUED` or `FINAL` and its `collection_status` must be `DUE` or `REFERRED` — an arrangement may rescue an already-referred assessment — and no proceeding of the assessment may be `OPEN`, which is `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`. Then the type-specific test runs. An `INSTALLMENT_PLAN` must cover the whole liability: its `scheduled_total_cents` must be greater than or equal to the assessment's `balance_due_cents`, and a plan that would leave a shortfall is refused with `ERR_PLAN_UNDERFUNDED`. An `OFFER_IN_COMPROMISE` must first have `hardship_documented` true, without which it is refused with `ERR_HARDSHIP_NOT_DOCUMENTED`, and must then reach the statutory minimum of 2,000 basis points — twenty percent — of the balance. That minimum is tested on cross-multiplied integers with no rounding and no division anywhere: the offer is acceptable when `offer_amount_cents` multiplied by 10,000 is greater than or equal to 2,000 multiplied by `balance_due_cents`. An offer below that line is refused with `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Comparing cross-multiplied integers rather than computing twenty percent of the balance and rounding it matters at the boundary, where a rounded threshold would accept or refuse an offer that the exact comparison decides the other way. Approval sets `approved_at`, moves the arrangement from `REQUESTED` to `APPROVED`, and moves the assessment's `collection_status` to `ARRANGED` — from `DUE` or from `REFERRED` alike — while leaving the notice axis exactly where it was.

**Payments.** A `Taxpayer` or a `CollectionsAgent` records a payment of `amount_cents` against an arrangement. The arrangement must be `APPROVED`; a payment against a `REQUESTED`, `COMPLETED`, `DEFAULTED` or `CANCELLED` arrangement is refused with `ERR_ARRANGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, which occupies the state tier of the precedence order in place of the generic state code. A payment increments the arrangement's `amount_paid_cents` and `payments_made_count`, and increments the assessment's own `amount_paid_cents`, which lowers `balance_due_cents`. This is the only route by which the assessment's `amount_paid_cents` grows, which is why it can never double-count a manually posted `PAYMENT` ledger row.

A payment that fully performs the arrangement completes it, and the completion test differs by type. An `INSTALLMENT_PLAN` is fully performed when the assessment's `balance_due_cents` has reached 0. An `OFFER_IN_COMPROMISE` is fully performed when the arrangement's `amount_paid_cents` has reached or passed its `offer_amount_cents`, which will normally leave a positive balance outstanding — that is the nature of a compromise — so the completion first posts a `SYSTEM` adjustment of kind `OIC_WRITEOFF` for the whole remaining `balance_due_cents`, driving the balance to exactly 0. Either way the arrangement moves from `APPROVED` to the terminal `COMPLETED`, the assessment's `collection_status` becomes `SATISFIED`, every other arrangement of that assessment still in `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` also moves to `COMPLETED`, and the taxpayer's `compliance_status` is recomputed to `COMPLIANT` — but only when the taxpayer has no other assessment whose `collection_status` is `REFERRED`, since one settled liability does not clear a delinquency elsewhere. The same completion cascade fires whenever the collection axis reaches `SATISFIED` by any route, including a manually posted adjustment and an `APPEAL_REDUCTION` that exhausts the balance. `SATISFIED` is reachable from `DUE`, from `ARRANGED` and from `REFERRED` alike: the only condition is that `balance_due_cents` stands at exactly 0 after the posting, so even an assessment already in enforced collection is satisfied the moment its balance clears.

**Default.** A `CollectionsAgent` declares an `APPROVED` arrangement in default, recording a `default_reason`; the assessment's `balance_due_cents` must still be greater than zero, since there is nothing to default on once the liability is paid. Default proceeds in three caused hops that mirror a manual referral. First, the arrangement records the `default_reason` and moves to the terminal `DEFAULTED`. Second, caused by the first, the assessment's `collection_status` moves to `REFERRED` and a `SYSTEM` `COLLECTION_FEE` adjustment is posted for the negative of `balance_due_cents` multiplied by 1,000 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. Third, caused by the second, the taxpayer's `compliance_status` becomes `DELINQUENT`. The third terminal state, `CANCELLED`, is never reached by a collections act at all: it belongs to the remand cascade described in §3.8, which cancels every `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` arrangement of a vacated assessment.
