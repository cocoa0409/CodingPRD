# Travel Rewards Portal & Transfer Partner Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A card issuer's travel rewards programme is, at heart, two ledgers pointed at each other. On one side cardholders accumulate points from everyday spend, classified through merchant category codes and occasionally boosted by a rotating bonus category. On the other side the issuer discharges those points — as portal air and hotel bookings sourced from a contracted travel agency, as statement credits, or as transfers into airline and hotel loyalty programmes at negotiated ratios. Every point the issuer has issued and not yet discharged is a liability on the balance sheet, priced at an estimated redemption cost and discounted by the fraction of points that will never be redeemed at all. The service specified here is the system of record for both ledgers and for the controls that sit between them.

The programme's hardest problems are not arithmetic but governance. Portal inventory must stay in price and availability parity with what the agency advertises on its own public channel, or the issuer is selling seats it cannot honour at prices the agency will not accept. Transfer partner agreements carry ratio terms that change over time, and a cardholder who enrolled before a ratio cut is contractually protected for a grandfathering window that must be honoured to the day. Points are the most liquid thing on a compromised account, so redemption fraud controls have to be able to freeze a spending pipeline mid-flight — holding queued transfers, suspending lounge access, and rejecting in-flight requests — while still returning every reserved point to the cardholder's balance. And because drafting a partner agreement and approving it are different jobs, several operations require two distinct operators.

This document specifies the complete HTTP contract: fifteen resource collections mounted under `/rewards`, the state machines that govern them, the ten calculations that price points and cash, the admission checks that reject bad requests, and the deterministic sweep that advances every clock-driven transition. Implementations are expected to be deterministic: given the same logical clock and the same sequence of requests, every derived field, every ledger entry, and every cent of liability must come out identical.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries any string other than the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles listed for the addressed endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `Cardholder`, `AgencySupplier`, `ProgramAdmin`, `ProgramApprover`, and `FraudAnalyst`. Spelling is exact and case-sensitive.

Two of the roles are backed by persisted entities:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Cardholder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Account` compare the resource's `account_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Cardholder` may read its own account, and may address only its own redemption orders, transfer requests and benefit entitlements. Any attempt to read another account or to write against a resource whose `account_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). This check guards seven operations: reading one account, booking an offer, creating a redemption order, confirming a redemption order, cancelling a redemption order, creating a transfer request, and consuming a benefit entitlement.
- When `X-User-Role` is `AgencySupplier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TravelAgencyPartner`; ownership checks compare the addressed offer's `agency_partner_id` to `X-User-ID`, and for a redemption order they compare the `agency_partner_id` of the offer behind the order. An agency writing against inventory or a ticket belonging to another agency is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This check guards five operations: publishing an offer, resyncing an offer, withdrawing an offer, ticketing a redemption order, and refunding a redemption order.

The remaining three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `ProgramApprover`, or `FraudAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition nominates an operator field — `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on `PartnerAgreement`, `drafted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on `ProgramChangeNotice`, `opened_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on `FraudCase`, `held_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` on `TransferRequest`, and `generated_by_user_id` on `LiabilitySnapshot` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced across four operator pairings. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the opposing operator identifier already recorded on the row: from `PartnerAgreement.submitted_by_user_id` when approving or reinstating an agreement, from `ProgramChangeNotice.drafted_by_user_id` when announcing a notice, from `FraudCase.opened_by_user_id` when confirming or clearing a case, and from `TransferRequest.held_by_user_id` when releasing a hold. A match rejects the request with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The check guards exactly six operations: partner-agreement approve, partner-agreement reinstate, change-notice announce, fraud-case confirm, fraud-case clear, and transfer-request release.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint declares otherwise; the ledger listing is the one exception and is described in §2.7. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond `offset` plus `limit`.

Every list endpoint also accepts the filter parameters named for it in §3 and §4. Filters combine conjunctively: a request that supplies both `account_id` and `status` returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields, and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Thirteen of the fifteen collections additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation and formatted identically, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `PointsLedgerEntry` and `LiabilitySnapshot` are the two exceptions: both are immutable once written, so both carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on those two resources at all, and no request may address them for mutation.

Clients never supply `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, or `version` in a request body; a body that attempts to set them has those keys ignored.

All temporal rules in this specification read a single **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it; a companion read returns the clock's current `current_utc` value to any authenticated role. Setting the clock changes no domain row — it only changes what the temporal validations and the deterministic sweep will see on their next invocation. Wherever this document says "the clock instant", it means this value.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and body identifiers are resolved after path identifiers, again in declaration order. The first identifier that names no row rejects the request with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and the message names the resource type that failed to resolve. Because resolution precedes every business check, a request that names a nonexistent offer and also carries a stale version receives the 404, not the 412.

### 2.5. Points Arithmetic, Cash Precision, and Pinned Rounding Order

Points are always integers and are never rounded to a fractional value. Where a points figure is derived from a multiplication, the result is floored — truncated toward zero — and where a points figure is derived from a cash amount the result is rounded **up** to the next whole point, so that the issuer never under-charges points for a booking. Where cash is derived from points or from a rate, the result is an integer number of cents rounded **half up**: a computed value of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent.

Three rounding orders are pinned and are not interchangeable with any algebraically equivalent alternative.

1. **Earning legs are floored independently.** The base earn leg and the rotation bonus leg are each floored on their own, and only then added together. Flooring the sum instead of the two legs produces a different point total whenever both legs have fractional parts.
2. **Partner units are floored per 1,000-point block, then summed.** A transfer's partner units are computed one thousand-point block at a time, each block's unit count floored, and the floored per-block figures added. Computing the whole transfer in one division and flooring once yields a larger number whenever the ratio does not divide evenly, and that larger number is wrong.
3. **Liability buckets are rounded before they are summed.** The dormant and non-dormant halves of the points liability are each valued and rounded to whole cents separately, and the two rounded figures are then added; likewise each bucket's breakage credit is rounded at its own rate before the two credits are added. Summing first and rounding once yields a different cent total.

Dollar conversion, wherever spend in cents must become spend in dollars for a multiplier, is integer floor division of cents by 100. Ninety-nine cents of spend is zero billable dollars.

Day arithmetic — grandfathering windows, dormancy lookbacks, notice periods — is exact calendar-day addition or subtraction applied to a UTC instant, with no rounding and no truncation to midnight. Hour arithmetic — transfer latency, reversal windows, promotional buffers — is exact addition of hours to a UTC instant.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Offers and Accounts

Two write operations carry an optimistic-concurrency guard, and only those two.

Booking a portal inventory offer requires `expected_offer_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `PortalInventoryOffer`. If that number does not equal the offer's current `version`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OFFER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An offer with more than one portal seat legitimately accepts several bookings, so a second booking is not blocked by any state precondition; the stale version instead signals that the client is quoting a price the agency has already resynced away, and resync is precisely the operation that advances the offer's `version`.

Creating a transfer request requires `expected_account_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `Account`. If that number does not equal the account's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An account may legitimately hold several in-flight transfers at once, so a second create is not blocked by any state precondition; the stale version instead signals that the client's view of the available points balance predates an earlier debit, clawback, or posture cascade.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to any other endpoint that nonetheless carry one are unaffected by it.

### 2.7. The Points Ledger and the Conserved Quantity

`PointsLedgerEntry` rows are the sole authority on how many points an account holds. They are append-only: no endpoint creates, edits, or deletes one directly, and every entry arrives as the side effect of some other operation. Each entry records the `account_id` whose balance it moves, an `entry_type` drawn from `EARN_BASE`, `EARN_BONUS`, `CLAWBACK`, `REDEEM_DEBIT`, `REDEEM_REFUND`, `TRANSFER_DEBIT`, and `TRANSFER_REVERSAL`, a signed `points_delta` that is positive for earns and reversals and negative for debits and clawbacks, a `balance_after` showing the account's balance immediately after the entry was appended, an `occurred_at` set to the clock instant at which the entry was appended, and a short operator-readable `memo`.

Because entries originate from three different producers, the reference is polymorphic rather than a declared foreign key: `source_entity_type` is `TRANSACTION`, `REDEMPTION_ORDER`, or `TRANSFER_REQUEST`, and `source_entity_id` holds the identifier of the producing row, to be resolved against the collection that `source_entity_type` names. `balance_after` is computed in `occurred_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the ledger listing returns entries in that same order rather than the default ordering of §2.3. The listing may be narrowed by `account_id`, `entry_type`, `source_entity_type`, and `source_entity_id`.

An account's `points_balance` is the sum of `points_delta` over every entry bearing its `account_id`. Its `lifetime_points_earned` is the sum of `points_delta` over entries whose `entry_type` is `EARN_BASE` or `EARN_BONUS`; its `lifetime_points_redeemed` is the absolute value of the sum of `points_delta` over entries whose `entry_type` is `REDEEM_DEBIT` or `TRANSFER_DEBIT`.

Alongside the balance sits `points_pending_redemption`, the count of points a cardholder has committed but whose fate is undecided: the sum of `points_debited` over the account's redemption orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `QUOTED`, plus the sum of `points_out` over the account's transfer requests whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `HELD`, or `QUEUED`. The invariant the whole system preserves is that `points_balance` plus `points_pending_redemption` is conserved across every reservation and every release: reserving points moves them from one term to the other, and cancelling or rejecting moves them back, so the sum returns to exactly what it was before.

**Available points** are a narrower figure than the balance, and are what every spend admission check consults. Available points are `points_balance` less the sum of `points_debited` over the account's `QUOTED` redemption orders — and nothing else. Transfer reservations are deliberately *not* subtracted a second time, because submitting a transfer already appended a `TRANSFER_DEBIT` entry, so the balance is already net of it. Confirmed orders have likewise already been debited from the ledger and must not be counted twice. Only `QUOTED` redemption orders are subtracted, because they are the one reservation with no ledger entry standing behind them. A requested burn is admitted when it is less than or equal to available points; equality is admitted, and a burn strictly greater than available points is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_POINTS` (HTTP 402). That check guards four operations: booking an offer, creating a redemption order, confirming a redemption order, and creating a transfer request.

### 2.8. Request Admission Order

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must carry the error of the *earliest* rule in the following order. This ordering is observable and is part of the contract: a mid-flight posture lock must surface as `ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_LOCKED` rather than being masked by a later insufficient-points check, and a stale version must surface before any state check.

1. **Authentication.** Headers present and non-empty (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401), then the role recognised (`ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, 401). Both apply to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The role permitted on the endpoint (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403, every endpoint), then cardholder ownership (`ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`, 403), then agency scope (`ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403), then segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403). The scopes of these last three are enumerated in §2.1.
3. **Existence.** Every identifier resolves, path before body, left to right (`ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, 404, every endpoint).
4. **Shape.** Required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, the numeric constraints stated throughout §3 honoured, declared arrays non-empty, and no duplicate members in a fraud case's `signals` array (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400, every endpoint). Then pagination bounds on every list endpoint (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, 400).
5. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_offer_version` on an offer booking (`ERR_STALE_OFFER_VERSION`, 412), then `expected_account_version` on a transfer create (`ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION`, 412).
6. **State.** The addressed row's current state is a declared origin state for the requested transition (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409, every action endpoint). Then, in this order: the owning account is not `CLOSED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`, 409); the owning account's `fraud_posture` is not `LOCKED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_LOCKED`, 409); the addressed offer is `OPEN` (`ERR_OFFER_NOT_BOOKABLE`, 409); the referenced partner agreement is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409); the agency behind the offer is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, 409); the addressed entitlement is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_BENEFIT_NOT_CONSUMABLE`, 409); the addressed offer has no committed seats (`ERR_OFFER_HAS_COMMITTED_SEATS`, 409); the account being closed has no points in flight (`ERR_ACCOUNT_HAS_IN_FLIGHT_POINTS`, 409); the agreement being terminated has no transfers in flight (`ERR_AGREEMENT_HAS_IN_FLIGHT_TRANSFERS`, 409).
7. **Business safety.** The addressed offer is in parity (`ERR_OFFER_OUT_OF_PARITY`, 409), then the availability-parity rule on an offer create or resync (`ERR_AVAILABILITY_PARITY_BREACH`, 422).
8. **Temporal.** Rotation window overlap (`ERR_ROTATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409), promotional window conflict (`ERR_BONUS_EVENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409), transfer reversal window expiry (`ERR_TRANSFER_REVERSAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, 409), notice period too short (`ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`, 422), and snapshot cut-off in the future (`ERR_SNAPSHOT_AS_OF_IN_FUTURE`, 422), in that order.
9. **Referential.** Channel requires an offer (`ERR_CHANNEL_REQUIRES_OFFER`, 422), benefit requires a ticketed booking (`ERR_BENEFIT_REQUIRES_TICKETED_BOOKING`, 409), cited order belongs to another account (`ERR_ORDER_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`, 422), duplicate category mapping (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_MAPPING`, 409), and duplicate programme code (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE`, 409), in that order.
10. **Transfer and seat constraints.** Below the agreement minimum (`ERR_BELOW_MIN_TRANSFER`, 422), then not an exact increment multiple (`ERR_TRANSFER_INCREMENT_INVALID`, 422), then no seat available (`ERR_SEAT_UNAVAILABLE`, 409), then statement credit below the floor (`ERR_STATEMENT_CREDIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`, 422), then a ratio-change notice missing its target (`ERR_NOTICE_TARGET_REQUIRED`, 422), in that order.
11. **Fiscal.** Insufficient available points (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_POINTS`, 402), then benefit allowance exhausted (`ERR_BENEFIT_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED`, 409), then invalid settlement amounts (`ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`, 422).

The endpoint scope of each of these checks is stated in the §3 narrative that introduces the rule; §5 lists the error identifiers with their HTTP status codes and nothing else.

### 2.9. Cascade Semantics and Version Propagation

Many operations in this domain mutate more than one row. The rule is uniform: **every row a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by exactly 1**, and this applies to rows the caller never named. Suspending an agency bumps the agency's `version` and the `version` of every offer it pulls off the portal. Ticketing an order bumps both the order's `version` and the agency partner's `version`, because the agency's `payable_cents` moved. Holding a transfer bumps both the transfer's `version` and the account's `version`, because `transfer_hold_count` moved.

Cascades are described in this document hop by hop, and the distinction matters: a hop is caused by the mutation the previous hop performed, not by the originating request. A purchase reversal, for example, never touches a benefit entitlement directly — it appends a clawback entry, which lowers the balance, which holds underfunded transfers, which raises the hold count, which degrades the posture, which is what suspends the entitlements. Implementations must run the chain in that order, because each link reads state the previous link wrote.

Cascades are also deterministic in their iteration order. Wherever a cascade or a sweep processes a set of rows, it processes them in `created_at` ascending order, and where two rows share a `created_at` the tie is broken by `id` ascending.

### 2.10. The Deterministic Sweep

Clock-driven transitions never fire on their own. An administrator invokes a sweep, and the sweep evaluates every clock-driven rule against the current logical clock instant in this fixed order:

1. Rotations whose window has opened move `SCHEDULED` → `ACTIVE`.
2. Rotations whose window has closed move `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED`.
3. Promotional bonus events whose window has opened move `SCHEDULED` → `RUNNING`.
4. Promotional bonus events whose window has closed move `RUNNING` → `CLOSED`.
5. Programme change notices whose effective instant has arrived move `ANNOUNCED` → `EFFECTIVE`, and the grandfathering re-point of every active account runs immediately as part of that transition.
6. Accounts whose grandfather window has matured have their pending notice promoted to their governing notice.
7. Queued transfer requests whose scheduled posting instant has arrived move `QUEUED` → `COMPLETED`, and their partner units are computed at that moment.
8. Benefit entitlements whose expiry instant has arrived move to `EXPIRED`.

The ordering is load-bearing: a notice made effective in step 5 can change the ratio a transfer completed in step 7 uses, and a grandfather window matured in step 6 changes it again. The sweep returns a count for each step — `rotations_activated`, `rotations_expired`, `bonus_events_opened`, `bonus_events_closed`, `notices_made_effective`, `grandfather_windows_matured`, `transfers_completed`, and `entitlements_expired` — together with `clock_utc`, the instant the sweep ran against. Every row the sweep touches has its `version` incremented by 1. A sweep that finds nothing to do is not an error; it returns zeroes.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrolling, Reading, and Closing Card Accounts

A programme administrator enrols a card account by supplying the `cardholder_name` as it appears on the card, the `email` used for programme change communication, the `card_product_code`, and the `enrolled_at` instant at which the cardholder joined. The `card_product_code` is one of `PREMIER`, `CORE`, or `LEGACY_GOLD`, and it is the single most consequential input on the row: it fixes both the earn multiplier applied to every future purchase and the cents-per-point rate at which the cardholder's points buy travel. The `enrolled_at` instant matters later, when a programme change notice takes effect and the system must decide whether this cardholder falls inside a grandfathered cohort. A freshly enrolled account starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `fraud_posture` `CLEAR`, and every points aggregate at zero.

Nine fields on an account are derived rather than supplied, and none of them may be written directly. `points_balance`, `points_pending_redemption`, `lifetime_points_earned`, and `lifetime_points_redeemed` are the four ledger aggregates defined in §2.7. `transfer_hold_count` counts, monotonically, every transfer request belonging to this account whose `status` has ever been set to `HELD`; it is never decremented, not when a hold is released and not when the fraud case that motivated it is cleared. `fraud_posture` is the derived redemption-risk dimension described in §3.8. `terms_notice_id`, `pending_notice_id`, and `grandfather_expires_at` are the grandfathering triple described in §3.9. A tenth field, `closed_at`, is set by the system at closure and is null while the account is `ACTIVE`.

An account row therefore carries two independent dimensions at once. `status` is the lifecycle dimension and moves only `ACTIVE` → `CLOSED`. `fraud_posture` is the risk dimension and cycles among `CLEAR`, `WATCH`, and `LOCKED`. Neither dimension writes the other: a lifecycle closure never rewrites the posture, and a posture change never closes an account.

Reading a single account returns the derived aggregates and the posture as of that moment. A `Cardholder` may read only its own account; the unfiltered account list is closed to cardholders entirely and is available to `ProgramAdmin`, `ProgramApprover`, and `FraudAnalyst`, who may narrow it by `status`, `fraud_posture`, `card_product_code`, `terms_notice_id`, and `pending_notice_id` in addition to `limit` and `offset`.

Closing an account is a `ProgramAdmin` operation, and it is refused while any points remain undecided. The account must currently be `ACTIVE`; no transfer request belonging to it may be in `SUBMITTED`, `QUEUED`, or `HELD`; and no redemption order belonging to it may be in `QUOTED` or `CONFIRMED` fulfilment. A violation of either in-flight condition is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_HAS_IN_FLIGHT_POINTS`. On success the transition `ACTIVE` → `CLOSED` sets `closed_at` to the clock instant, moves every benefit entitlement of the account that is currently `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`, and increments the account's `version` by 1. Note the asymmetry with the posture cascade: closure *expires* entitlements permanently rather than suspending them, so nothing about a closed account can be reinstated.

A closed account is inert for spending. Posting a purchase to it, booking an offer for it, creating a redemption order or a transfer request on its behalf, granting it a new benefit entitlement, or consuming one of its existing entitlements are all rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED` — those six operations, and no others, consult the lifecycle dimension.

### 3.2. Merchant Classification and Bonus Category Rotations

Points earning begins with classification. A programme administrator publishes a mapping table whose rows each carry an `mcc_code` — the merchant category code the payment network presents on a purchase — a human-readable `display_name` shown on the earning statement, a coarse `base_category` drawn from `DINING`, `TRAVEL_AIR`, `TRAVEL_HOTEL`, `GROCERY`, `GAS`, and `OTHER`, and a `base_multiplier` giving points per billable dollar before any product or rotation multiplier is applied. The `base_multiplier` must be at least 1.0 and carry no more than two decimal places. Exactly one row in the whole table may set `is_fallback` to true; it is the catch-all used when an inbound purchase presents an `mcc_code` no row maps. The flag defaults to false when omitted. An `mcc_code` must be globally unique across the mapping table, and a second row claiming an already-mapped code — or a second row claiming `is_fallback` — is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_MAPPING`. The mapping list may be filtered by `mcc_code`, `base_category`, and `is_fallback`.

On top of that stable table sits a rotating promotion. A bonus category rotation names exactly one `merchant_category_id` to boost, carries an operator-facing `rotation_code` that must be globally unique across all rotations, a `bonus_multiplier` strictly greater than 1.0 with at most two decimal places, a `capped_spend_cents` strictly greater than zero, and a half-open window: `window_start` is inclusive, `window_end` is exclusive and must be strictly later than `window_start`. A purchase belongs to the window when its posting instant is at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`. The `capped_spend_cents` figure is a **per-account cumulative** cap on how much spend can earn the bonus, not a programme-wide budget — a rotation capped at 150,000 cents lets each cardholder earn the bonus on the first 1,500 dollars of qualifying spend and no more.

At most one live rotation may cover any given instant on any given category, and that is enforced at creation. A new rotation is rejected with `ERR_ROTATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when another rotation on the same `merchant_category_id` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, or `ACTIVE` has a half-open window intersecting the new one. Touching endpoints do not intersect: a new rotation whose `window_start` is exactly an existing rotation's `window_end` is accepted, and so is the mirror case. Only `EXPIRED` rotations are ignored by the check. A duplicate `rotation_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE`.

Rotations progress through four states. A newly created rotation is `DRAFT`. Publishing it — a `ProgramAdmin` action that moves `DRAFT` → `SCHEDULED` — requires that the rotation is currently `DRAFT` and that its `window_start` is strictly later than the clock instant; publishing sets `published_at` and increments `version` by 1. You cannot publish a rotation whose window has already opened. From there the sweep takes over: `SCHEDULED` → `ACTIVE` fires once the clock instant reaches or passes `window_start`, setting `activated_at` and incrementing `version`, and from that moment the rotation participates in points awards for matching purchases. `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED` fires once the clock instant reaches or passes `window_end`, setting `expired_at` and incrementing `version`, after which purchases in that category earn base points only. Rotations may be listed filtered by `merchant_category_id`, `status`, and `rotation_code`.

### 3.3. Posting a Purchase and Awarding Points

A posted purchase carries an `account_id`, the raw `mcc_code` the network presented, an `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, and a `posted_at` instant used for rotation window membership. Everything else on the row is derived, and the derivation runs in a fixed sequence.

**Classification first.** The system looks for the mapping row whose `mcc_code` equals the purchase's `mcc_code`; because that code is globally unique, at most one row can match. When one matches, `classification_source` is `MAPPED` and the effective base multiplier is that row's `base_multiplier`. When none matches, the system falls back to the single row carrying `is_fallback`, sets `classification_source` to `FALLBACK`, and uses that row's `base_multiplier`. Either way, `merchant_category_id` records the resolved category. In the degenerate case where no code matches *and* no fallback row has been configured, `merchant_category_id` is null and the effective base multiplier is 1.0.

**Then the base leg.** Billable dollars are the purchase's `amount_cents` divided by 100 using integer floor division, so a 1,999-cent purchase yields 19 billable dollars. Billable dollars are multiplied by the effective base multiplier and then by the card product's earn multiplier — 1.5 for `PREMIER`, 1.0 for `CORE`, and 2.0 for `LEGACY_GOLD` — and the result is floored to a whole number of `base_points`.

**Then the rotation leg.** The system looks for the rotation whose `merchant_category_id` equals the purchase's resolved category, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose half-open window contains `posted_at`. At most one can match, because overlapping windows on one category were refused at creation. When none matches, `rotation_id` is null, `rotation_eligible_cents` is 0, and `bonus_points` is 0. When one matches, `rotation_id` records it, and the cap is applied: the system sums `rotation_eligible_cents` over this account's earlier purchases against this same rotation — counting only rows whose `status` is `POSTED`, in `created_at` ascending order, and whose `created_at` precedes this purchase's `created_at`, so that reversed purchases drop out of the accumulator entirely. Headroom is the rotation's `capped_spend_cents` less that prior sum, floored at zero. `rotation_eligible_cents` is the smaller of the purchase's `amount_cents` and that headroom. Eligible dollars are `rotation_eligible_cents` divided by 100 using integer floor division; they are multiplied by the *incremental* part of the rotation multiplier — the `bonus_multiplier` less 1.0 — and then by the same card product earn multiplier, and floored to a whole number of `bonus_points`.

**Then the sum.** `total_points_earned` is `base_points` plus `bonus_points`, with each leg having been floored independently before the addition, per §2.5. Two ledger entries may follow: an `EARN_BASE` entry carrying `base_points` as a positive `points_delta` is always appended first, and a second `EARN_BONUS` entry carrying `bonus_points` is appended only when `bonus_points` is strictly greater than zero. The account's `points_balance` and `lifetime_points_earned` rise accordingly and the account's `version` increments by 1.

Note the interaction between fallback classification and rotations: a purchase that fell through to the fallback category can never match a rotation attached to any other category, so a `FALLBACK` purchase earns zero bonus points unless the fallback row itself carries the live rotation. A new purchase row starts with `status` `POSTED`, and `reversed_at` and `reversal_reason` null. Purchases may be listed filtered by `account_id`, `merchant_category_id`, `rotation_id`, `classification_source`, and `status`.

### 3.4. Reversing a Purchase: A Four-Hop Clawback

When the issuer reverses a purchase, the points it awarded must come back, and the consequences of that reversal ripple through the transfer pipeline. Reversal is a `ProgramAdmin` action taking a free-text `reversal_reason`, and it requires that the purchase is currently `POSTED` and that the owning account is `ACTIVE`. On success the `POSTED` → `REVERSED` transition runs four hops, each caused by the mutation the previous hop performed, and increments the purchase's `version` by 1.

The first hop sets `reversed_at` to the clock instant, records `reversal_reason`, and appends a `CLAWBACK` ledger entry whose `points_delta` is the negative of the purchase's `total_points_earned`, with `source_entity_type` `TRANSACTION` and `source_entity_id` naming the purchase.

The second hop is caused by that ledger entry, not by the reversal itself: the account's `points_balance` is recomputed from the ledger and falls by `total_points_earned`, and `lifetime_points_earned` is recomputed as well — recomputed strictly by the §2.7 formula, which sums `points_delta` only over `EARN_BASE` and `EARN_BONUS` entries, so the `CLAWBACK` entry does not enter that sum and `lifetime_points_earned` comes back unchanged. The reversed purchase also leaves the rotation accumulator, because that accumulator counts only `POSTED` rows — which means a subsequent purchase against the same rotation may now find more headroom than it would have.

The third hop is caused by the second. If the recomputed `points_balance` is strictly negative, every transfer request of this account whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `QUEUED` is moved to `HELD` with `hold_reason` `INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE_AFTER_CLAWBACK` and `held_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and the account's `transfer_hold_count` is incremented once for each newly held request. A recomputed balance of zero or above holds nothing at all — the test is strictly negative.

The fourth hop is caused by the third: the posture calculation re-runs against the new `transfer_hold_count`, and when that count reaches 2 the account moves `CLEAR` → `WATCH`, which suspends every `LOUNGE_ACCESS` benefit entitlement the account holds. The reversal never touches a benefit entitlement directly; it reaches them only through this chain.

### 3.5. Sourcing Portal Inventory: Agencies, Markup, and Parity Governance

Portal travel is not the issuer's inventory. It is sourced from contracted travel agencies, each onboarded with a legal `name`, a `contracted_markup_bps` giving the basis points the issuer adds to the agency's net rate to form the price shown on the portal, a `parity_tolerance_bps` giving the basis points by which the portal price may exceed the agency's own advertised price before parity is breached, and an optional `invoice_terms_days` stating how many days after ticketing the agency must invoice. Both basis-point figures must be at least 0 and no more than 5000 — half of the net rate is the maximum contractual markup and the maximum tolerated divergence. `invoice_terms_days` defaults to 30 when omitted. A newly onboarded agency is `ACTIVE` with `payable_cents` at 0, and `suspended_reason` null. `payable_cents` is the outstanding cash the issuer owes this agency; it is derived and moves only through the ticket, settle, and refund transitions of §3.6 — nothing else writes it. Agencies may be listed filtered by `status`.

Suspending an agency is a `ProgramAdmin` action that takes a `suspended_reason` and requires that the agency is currently `ACTIVE`. The `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` transition records the reason, increments the agency's `version` by 1, and cascades: every portal inventory offer of this agency whose `status` is `OPEN` is moved to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, and each touched offer's `version` is incremented by 1. Offers that were already `SUSPENDED`, `SOLD_OUT`, or `WITHDRAWN` are left alone.

An agency supplier publishes an itinerary to the portal by supplying its own `agency_partner_id`, an `itinerary_code` handle (several offers may share one code across resync generations, so it is not unique), a `depart_at` instant, an optional `return_at` instant that is null for one-way itineraries, the `net_rate_cents` the issuer pays the agency per seat, the `agency_public_price_cents` the agency advertises on its own public channel, the `agency_public_seats` it advertises publicly, the `portal_seats` it is exposing on the rewards portal, and the `tax_cents` of taxes and carrier fees. `net_rate_cents` and `agency_public_price_cents` must be strictly positive, `agency_public_seats` must be at least 0, `portal_seats` must be at least 1, and `tax_cents` must be at least 0. Publishing an offer for an agency that is `SUSPENDED` is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`.

**The portal price is computed, never supplied.** The offer's `portal_price_cents` is the `net_rate_cents` multiplied by one plus the agency's `contracted_markup_bps` divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents. The offer's `cash_copay_cents` is set equal to its `tax_cents`: taxes and carrier fees are always paid in cash by the cardholder and are never redeemable with points.

**Parity is a two-part test, and both parts must pass.** The price ceiling is the offer's `agency_public_price_cents` multiplied by one plus the agency's `parity_tolerance_bps` divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents; the price limb passes when `portal_price_cents` is less than or equal to that ceiling. The availability limb passes when `portal_seats` is less than or equal to `agency_public_seats`. `parity_status` is `IN_PARITY` when both limbs pass and `OUT_OF_PARITY` otherwise. The comparison is always between an offer's own figures — no other offer sharing the same `itinerary_code` participates in the test.

Separately from the derived verdict, a hard safety rule guards both offer publication and resync: the `portal_seats` in the payload may not exceed the `agency_public_seats` declared in that same payload, because the portal would then be advertising inventory the agency cannot honour. A violation is rejected with `ERR_AVAILABILITY_PARITY_BREACH` (HTTP 422). New offers are created `OPEN` with `seats_committed` at 0 and `last_resynced_at` null.

Resyncing an offer is the only way its parity verdict changes after creation. The agency supplier pushes a fresh snapshot of `net_rate_cents`, `agency_public_price_cents`, `agency_public_seats`, and `portal_seats`; the system recomputes `portal_price_cents` and re-evaluates `parity_status`, sets `last_resynced_at` to the clock instant, and increments the offer's `version` by 1 — which is exactly what invalidates any offer version a cardholder read earlier, per §2.6.

Offers carry a four-valued availability dimension, and it is cyclic in two places. A programme administrator may pull one offer off the portal without touching its agency, supplying a `suspension_reason`; that `OPEN` → `SUSPENDED` transition increments `version` by 1, and thereafter bookings against the offer are rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_BOOKABLE`. Redemption orders already `QUOTED` or `CONFIRMED` against the offer are untouched by the suspension. Reinstatement returns a `SUSPENDED` offer to `OPEN`, clearing `suspension_reason` and incrementing `version` by 1; it requires that the offer is currently `SUSPENDED`, that the offer's agency is `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`), and that `seats_committed` is strictly below `portal_seats` — an offer whose committed seats already fill every portal seat cannot be reinstated into a full state and is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_HAS_COMMITTED_SEATS`. An offer may be suspended and reinstated any number of times.

The capacity cycle is system-driven. `seats_committed` counts the redemption orders against this offer whose `fulfillment_status` is `QUOTED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `TICKETED`. When a new booking consumes the last seat — that is, when `seats_committed` before the booking plus one equals `portal_seats` — the offer moves `OPEN` → `SOLD_OUT` after `seats_committed` is recomputed, and `version` increments by 1. When a booking against a sold-out offer is later cancelled or refunded and `seats_committed` recomputes to strictly below `portal_seats`, the offer moves `SOLD_OUT` → `OPEN` and `version` increments by 1. Withdrawal is the terminal exit and belongs to the agency supplier: an offer that is `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `seats_committed` is exactly 0 may be withdrawn, incrementing `version` by 1 and removing it from bookable inventory permanently. An offer with even one committed seat cannot be withdrawn and is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_HAS_COMMITTED_SEATS`. Offers may be listed filtered by `agency_partner_id`, `itinerary_code`, `status`, and `parity_status`.

### 3.6. Redeeming Points: Quotes, Confirmation, Ticketing, and Agency Settlement

A redemption order is the unit of account for every points burn that is not a transfer, and it carries **two independent state dimensions on one row**. `fulfillment_status` tracks the traveller's experience: `QUOTED` → `CONFIRMED` → `TICKETED` → `REFUNDED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from either `QUOTED` or `CONFIRMED`. `settlement_status` tracks the agency's cash: `UNSETTLED` → `AGENCY_INVOICED` → `SETTLED`, with `CLAWED_BACK` reachable from either `AGENCY_INVOICED` or `SETTLED`. The two advance independently. Settling an invoice never moves `fulfillment_status`, and cancelling before ticketing never moves `settlement_status` off `UNSETTLED`.

There are two `channel` values and two ways in. A `PORTAL_BOOKING` order must be created against a specific inventory offer, because only then can an `inventory_offer_id` be attached; the cardholder supplies its own `account_id`, the `traveler_name` to appear on the ticket, and `expected_offer_version`. A `STATEMENT_CREDIT` order is created through the flat order collection, where the cardholder supplies `account_id`, `channel`, and `points_requested`. Attempting to create a `PORTAL_BOOKING` on the flat collection is rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_REQUIRES_OFFER` (HTTP 422). Both routes produce a row whose `fulfillment_status` is `QUOTED` and whose `settlement_status` is `UNSETTLED`.

**Pricing a portal booking.** The redemption rate is pinned from the account's `card_product_code` at quote time and stored on the order as `cents_per_point_applied`: 1.25 cents per point for `PREMIER`, 1.0 for `CORE`, and 1.5 for `LEGACY_GOLD`. Once pinned it is never re-read, even if the cardholder's product later changes. The order's `cash_copay_cents` is copied from the offer's `cash_copay_cents`, and `points_debited` is the offer's `portal_price_cents` less that copay, divided by `cents_per_point_applied` and rounded **up** to the next whole point. `statement_credit_cents` is 0 for this channel. The order's forward-looking cost to the issuer, `issuer_cost_provisional_cents`, is the offer's `net_rate_cents` less the order's `cash_copay_cents`, and `cost_per_1k_provisional_cents` is that provisional cost multiplied by 1,000, divided by `points_debited`, rounded half up to whole cents; when `points_debited` is 0 the per-thousand figure is 0 rather than an error.

**Pricing a statement credit.** Here `points_debited` is simply the `points_requested`, and `statement_credit_cents` is `points_requested` multiplied by one cent per point, rounded half up. `cash_copay_cents` is 0, `inventory_offer_id` and `traveler_name` are null, and both provisional cost fields are 0. On every newly created order, of either channel, `issuer_cost_final_cents`, `cost_per_1k_final_cents`, `invoice_amount_cents`, `ticketed_at`, `settled_at`, `closed_reason`, and `pnr_locator` are all null and `settlement_adjustment_cents` is 0; the final-cost pair stays null until settlement fills it. A statement credit must be for at least 2,500 points; anything smaller is rejected with `ERR_STATEMENT_CREDIT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422).

Booking is guarded on five fronts beyond the header and ownership checks. The account must be `ACTIVE` and its `fraud_posture` must not be `LOCKED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_LOCKED`). The offer's `status` must be `OPEN`, so a suspended, sold-out, or withdrawn offer is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_BOOKABLE`. The offer's `parity_status` must be `IN_PARITY`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_OFFER_OUT_OF_PARITY` — the issuer does not sell a seat it is out of parity on. `seats_committed` must be strictly below `portal_seats` at booking time, or the booking is refused with `ERR_SEAT_UNAVAILABLE`. And the points burn must be within available points per §2.7, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_POINTS`. On success the offer's `seats_committed` is recomputed, the offer flips to `SOLD_OUT` if the last seat has gone, the account's `points_pending_redemption` rises by `points_debited` **with no ledger entry whatsoever**, and both the offer's `version` and the account's `version` increment by 1.

**Confirmation turns a reservation into a debit.** Confirming is a cardholder action requiring no body. It requires that `fulfillment_status` is `QUOTED`, that the account's `fraud_posture` is not `LOCKED`, and that the account's `points_balance` is at least the order's `points_debited` — a shortfall is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_POINTS`. For a `PORTAL_BOOKING`, it additionally requires that the offer's `status` is `OPEN` or `SOLD_OUT` (a sold-out offer may still confirm its own bookings) and that the offer's `parity_status` is `IN_PARITY`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_OFFER_OUT_OF_PARITY`. The `QUOTED` → `CONFIRMED` transition appends a `REDEEM_DEBIT` ledger entry whose `points_delta` is the negative of `points_debited` and whose `source_entity_type` is `REDEMPTION_ORDER`; the account's `points_balance` falls by `points_debited` and its `points_pending_redemption` falls by the same amount, so the conserved sum of §2.7 falls by `points_debited` exactly once. `confirmed_at` is set to the clock instant, and both the order's `version` and the account's `version` increment by 1.

**Cancellation refunds points only when they were actually debited.** The cardholder supplies a `closed_reason`. Cancelling from `QUOTED` lowers `points_pending_redemption` by `points_debited` and appends no ledger entry at all, because nothing was ever debited. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` appends a `REDEEM_REFUND` ledger entry whose `points_delta` is positive `points_debited`, returning `points_balance` to its pre-confirmation value. Both branches record `closed_reason`, recompute the offer's `seats_committed` so that a `SOLD_OUT` offer returns to `OPEN`, leave `settlement_status` at `UNSETTLED` and the agency's `payable_cents` untouched, and increment `version` by 1.

**Ticketing is the agency's act and the moment cash appears.** The supplier posts a `ticket_number`; the order's `fulfillment_status` must be `CONFIRMED`, its `channel` must be `PORTAL_BOOKING`, the offer's agency must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`), and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the offer's `agency_partner_id`. The `CONFIRMED` → `TICKETED` transition stores `ticket_number` and sets `ticketed_at`, and — crossing into the other dimension — moves `settlement_status` from `UNSETTLED` to `AGENCY_INVOICED` while adding the order's `issuer_cost_provisional_cents` to the agency's `payable_cents`. Both the order's `version` and the agency partner's `version` increment by 1.

**Settlement replaces the estimate with the invoice.** A programme administrator supplies the `invoice_amount_cents` the agency billed and an optional signed `settlement_adjustment_cents` that defaults to 0. The order's `settlement_status` must be `AGENCY_INVOICED` and its `fulfillment_status` must be `TICKETED`. `issuer_cost_final_cents` is the `invoice_amount_cents` less the order's `cash_copay_cents` plus the `settlement_adjustment_cents`, and `cost_per_1k_final_cents` is that final cost multiplied by 1,000, divided by `points_debited`, rounded half up, with 0 substituted when `points_debited` is 0. The two cost legs are stored side by side and never overwrite each other: the provisional leg reads only the offer snapshot captured at booking, and the final leg reads only the settlement inputs, so a later offer resync can never change either. A negative `invoice_amount_cents`, or an adjustment that drives `issuer_cost_final_cents` below zero, is rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID` (HTTP 422). On success the agency's `payable_cents` is adjusted by the final cost less the provisional cost — a figure that is negative whenever the agency invoiced less than its booking-time net rate implied — `settled_at` is set, `settlement_status` becomes `SETTLED`, and both the order's `version` and the agency partner's `version` increment by 1. Only once an order is settled does it enter the cost-basis input set of the liability calculation in §3.11.

**Refunding a ticketed booking unwinds both ledgers.** The agency supplier supplies a `closed_reason`; the order's `fulfillment_status` must be `TICKETED` and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the offer's `agency_partner_id`. The `TICKETED` → `REFUNDED` transition appends a `REDEEM_REFUND` ledger entry of positive `points_debited`, moves `settlement_status` to `CLAWED_BACK`, and subtracts from the agency's `payable_cents` **exactly the amount previously added** — `issuer_cost_final_cents` when the order had already reached `SETTLED`, and `issuer_cost_provisional_cents` otherwise. The offer's `seats_committed` is recomputed and a `SOLD_OUT` offer returns to `OPEN`. Both the order's `version` and the agency partner's `version` increment by 1.

Orders may be read individually — the read returns both state dimensions and both cost legs — or listed filtered by `account_id`, `inventory_offer_id`, `channel`, `fulfillment_status`, and `settlement_status`.

### 3.7. Transfer Partner Agreements and Promotional Bonus Events

Transferring points into an airline or hotel loyalty programme is governed by a negotiated agreement, and drafting one is deliberately separated from approving it. A programme administrator drafts the agreement with the `partner_name` of the loyalty programme, a short `partner_program_code` handle, and seven commercial terms: `ratio_points_out`, the programme points consumed per ratio block, which must be strictly positive and an exact multiple of 1000; `ratio_partner_units_in`, the strictly positive count of partner units credited per block of that many points; `min_transfer_points`, the smallest accepted transfer size, strictly positive; `transfer_increment_points`, strictly positive, of which every transfer must be an exact multiple; `transfer_latency_hours`, at least 0, being the hours that elapse between screening clearance and partner posting; `reversal_window_hours`, at least 0, being the hours after completion during which the issuer may still reverse a posted transfer; and `settlement_cost_cents_per_1k`, strictly positive, being the cash the issuer pays the partner for every 1,000 points transferred. A fresh agreement is `DRAFT`, with `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `activated_at`, and `suspension_reason` all null. Drafting alone reserves nothing: two drafts may carry the same `partner_program_code`.

The code is reserved at submission. Submitting a `DRAFT` agreement for approval requires that no *other* agreement carrying the same `partner_program_code` is currently `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `ACTIVE`, or `SUSPENDED`; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE`. The `DRAFT` → `PENDING_APPROVAL` transition stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id` and increments `version` by 1.

From `PENDING_APPROVAL` the agreement forks. An approver may send it back — a `PENDING_APPROVAL` → `DRAFT` revision that clears `submitted_by_user_id` and increments `version` by 1, releasing the code again and allowing the draft/submit loop to repeat indefinitely. Or an approver may accept it: the `PENDING_APPROVAL` → `ACTIVE` transition requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the stored `submitted_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), stores the acting identifier as `approved_by_user_id`, sets `activated_at` to the clock instant, and increments `version` by 1. Only from that moment may transfers be submitted against the agreement; a transfer naming an agreement in any other state is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Suspension and reinstatement form the agreement's second cycle. A programme administrator suspends an `ACTIVE` agreement with a `suspension_reason`; the `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` transition records the reason, increments the agreement's `version` by 1, and cascades into promotions: every bonus event on this agreement whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_reason` `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED`, each with its own `version` incremented by 1. Transfer requests already `QUEUED` on the agreement stay `QUEUED` — the suspension does not reject them — but the sweep refuses to post them while the agreement is not `ACTIVE`. Reinstatement is an approver's act: the `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE` transition requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id`, clears `suspension_reason`, refreshes `activated_at` to the current clock instant, and increments `version` by 1. Cancelled promotions are *not* resurrected by reinstatement.

`TERMINATED` is the agreement's terminal state, and it is reachable only from `SUSPENDED` — never directly from `ACTIVE`. An approver may terminate a suspended agreement provided no transfer request on it is `SUBMITTED`, `QUEUED`, or `HELD`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_HAS_IN_FLIGHT_TRANSFERS`. The `SUSPENDED` → `TERMINATED` transition increments `version` by 1 and releases the `partner_program_code` for reuse by a new agreement. Agreements may be read individually — the read exposes the ratio terms and both operator identifiers — or listed filtered by `status` and `partner_program_code`.

Promotional transfer bonuses ride on top of an agreement. A programme administrator schedules one against a `partner_agreement_id`, with a marketing `event_code` that must be globally unique across all events, a `bonus_bps` strictly greater than 0 and no greater than 10000 giving the basis points of extra partner units on top of the base conversion, a `window_start` and a `window_end` strictly later than it, and a `cap_points_per_account` strictly greater than 0 capping how many of a single transfer's points may earn the bonus. Unlike a rotation window, a promotional window is **closed at both ends**: a transfer submitted exactly at `window_start` or exactly at `window_end` is inside it. The parent agreement must be `ACTIVE` at scheduling time, or the request is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and a duplicate `event_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE`.

Promotional windows on one agreement must be separated by a full day of dead air. A new event is accepted only when its `window_start` is strictly later than an existing live event's `window_end` plus 24 hours, or its `window_end` is strictly earlier than that event's `window_start` minus 24 hours; the test runs against every other event on the same `partner_agreement_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`, and `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` events are ignored. Windows that merely touch, or that sit adjacent inside the 24-hour buffer, are rejected with `ERR_BONUS_EVENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

An event is created `SCHEDULED` with `opened_at`, `closed_at`, and `cancelled_reason` null. The sweep moves it `SCHEDULED` → `RUNNING` once the clock instant reaches or passes `window_start`, provided the parent agreement is still `ACTIVE`, setting `opened_at` and incrementing `version` by 1; from then on transfers submitted inside the closed window attach the event. The sweep moves it `RUNNING` → `CLOSED` once the clock instant is strictly past `window_end`, setting `closed_at` and incrementing `version` by 1, after which later transfers attach no event. A programme administrator may cancel an event that is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` with a `cancelled_reason`, incrementing `version` by 1 and releasing the window for a replacement event; the agency-side equivalent is the agreement-suspension cascade described above, which reaches the same terminal state. Events may be listed filtered by `partner_agreement_id`, `status`, and `event_code`.

### 3.8. The Transfer Pipeline: Submission, Screening, Posting, and Reversal

A cardholder submits a transfer by naming its own `account_id`, the `partner_agreement_id` whose ratio terms are to be consulted, the `points_out` to burn, and `expected_account_version`. `points_out` must be strictly positive, must be at least the agreement's `min_transfer_points` — otherwise `ERR_BELOW_MIN_TRANSFER` (HTTP 422) — and must be an exact multiple of the agreement's `transfer_increment_points`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_INCREMENT_INVALID` (HTTP 422). The account must be `ACTIVE` and not `LOCKED`, the agreement must be `ACTIVE`, and the burn must be within available points.

Submission does two things beyond creating the row. First, it pins the promotion: the system looks for the bonus event on this agreement whose `status` is `RUNNING` and whose closed window contains the submission instant, and records its identifier as `bonus_event_id`. Should two such events somehow exist, the one with the earlier `window_start` wins, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. The identifier is pinned for good — an event cancelled after submission still pays out on this transfer, because the transfer no longer consults the event's state. When no event covers the instant, `bonus_event_id` stays null. Second, submission debits the ledger immediately: a `TRANSFER_DEBIT` entry of negative `points_out` is appended with `source_entity_type` `TRANSFER_REQUEST`, `points_pending_redemption` rises by `points_out`, and the account's `version` increments by 1. The conserved quantity of §2.7 is therefore unchanged by submission — the points have moved from balance into pending, not out of the account.

A new request is `SUBMITTED`, with `hold_reason`, `held_by_user_id`, `released_by_user_id`, `screening_cleared_at`, `scheduled_post_at`, `completed_at`, `reversed_at`, and `resolution_reason` all null, with `base_units`, `bonus_units`, and `partner_units_credited` at 0, and with `ratio_points_out_applied` and `ratio_partner_units_in_applied` unresolved until completion.

The agreement gate applies to exactly four operations, each rejecting with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` when the referenced agreement is in any state other than `ACTIVE`: creating a transfer request, clearing screening on one, scheduling a promotional bonus event, and submitting a `TRANSFER_RATIO_CHANGE` notice for approval.

**Screening.** A fraud analyst clears screening, moving `SUBMITTED` → `QUEUED`. The request must be `SUBMITTED`, the account's `fraud_posture` must not be `LOCKED`, and the agreement must be `ACTIVE`. Clearance sets `screening_cleared_at` to the clock instant and computes `scheduled_post_at` as that instant plus the agreement's `transfer_latency_hours`, then increments `version` by 1. An agreement with a latency of 48 hours therefore parks the transfer for two full days before the sweep will look at it.

**Holding is cyclic.** A fraud analyst may hold a request that is `SUBMITTED` or `QUEUED`, supplying a `hold_reason` of `ATO_SUSPECTED`, `INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE_AFTER_CLAWBACK`, or `MANUAL_REVIEW`. The transition to `HELD` records the reason and stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `held_by_user_id`; when the hold arrives from the purchase-reversal cascade of §3.4 instead of from an analyst, `held_by_user_id` is the literal string `SYSTEM`. Holding from `QUEUED` additionally clears `scheduled_post_at`, so the sweep skips the row. Points stay reserved throughout: `points_pending_redemption` is unchanged by a hold. Every hold increments the account's `transfer_hold_count` by 1 and then re-runs the posture calculation, which may move the account `CLEAR` → `WATCH` and suspend its lounge-access entitlements. Both the request's `version` and the account's `version` increment by 1.

Release closes the cycle. A programme approver returns a `HELD` request to `SUBMITTED`, which requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `held_by_user_id` and that the account's `fraud_posture` is not `LOCKED`. Release stores `released_by_user_id`, clears `scheduled_post_at`, and increments the request's `version` by 1 — and deliberately leaves both `hold_reason` and the account's `transfer_hold_count` standing as historical facts. A released request re-enters screening and may be held again, as many times as the analysts and approvers care to alternate.

**Rejection returns the points.** A fraud analyst rejects a `HELD` request with a `resolution_reason`. The `HELD` → `REJECTED` transition appends a `TRANSFER_REVERSAL` ledger entry of positive `points_out`, restoring `points_balance` to its pre-submission value, and lowers `points_pending_redemption` by `points_out`, so the conserved sum returns to exactly what it was before the transfer existed. `resolution_reason` is recorded and both the request's `version` and the account's `version` increment by 1. The same compensating ledger and pending-balance effects fire when a posture lock rejects a request that is `SUBMITTED`, `QUEUED`, or `HELD`, except that there the `resolution_reason` is `POSTURE_LOCKED`.

**Posting is the sweep's job.** A `QUEUED` request moves to `COMPLETED` when the clock instant has reached or passed its `scheduled_post_at`, the agreement is `ACTIVE`, and the account's `fraud_posture` is not `LOCKED`. Completion sets `completed_at`, lowers `points_pending_redemption` by `points_out` while leaving `points_balance` exactly where the `TRANSFER_DEBIT` left it — the points are now genuinely gone — and increments `version` by 1.

Completion is also where the partner units are finally computed, and the arithmetic is exacting. The ratio actually used is resolved first, through the grandfathering rule of §3.10, and stored on the row as `ratio_points_out_applied` and `ratio_partner_units_in_applied`. The transfer is then divided into blocks of 1,000 points each: the block count is `points_out` divided by 1,000 using integer floor division, which is always exact because `points_out` is a multiple of the agreement's increment, itself a multiple of 1,000. The units earned by one block are 1,000 multiplied by the applied ratio numerator, divided by the applied ratio denominator, floored to a whole number. `base_units` is that per-block figure added once for each block. This order is pinned and is not the same as dividing once at full size: 9,000 points at an applied ratio of 3,000 points to 2,000 units yields 666 units per block and therefore 5,994 `base_units`, where a single division would have yielded 6,000.

The bonus leg is layered on the already-floored per-block figure, never on the summed base. When `bonus_event_id` is null, `bonus_units` is 0. Otherwise the bonus-eligible block count is the smaller of the transfer's block count and the event's `cap_points_per_account` divided by 1,000 using integer floor division; the bonus units earned by one eligible block are the per-block base figure multiplied by the event's `bonus_bps` and divided by 10,000, floored; and `bonus_units` is that per-block bonus added once for each eligible block. `partner_units_credited` is `base_units` plus `bonus_units`.

**Reversal is time-boxed.** A programme administrator may reverse a `COMPLETED` transfer with a `resolution_reason`, but only while the clock instant is at or before `completed_at` plus the agreement's `reversal_window_hours`; once past that deadline the request is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_REVERSAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The `COMPLETED` → `REVERSED` transition appends a `TRANSFER_REVERSAL` ledger entry of positive `points_out`, zeroes `partner_units_credited`, `base_units`, and `bonus_units`, sets `reversed_at` and `resolution_reason`, and increments both the request's `version` and the account's `version` by 1. Transfer requests may be read individually — the read exposes the applied ratio and the credited units — or listed filtered by `account_id`, `partner_agreement_id`, `bonus_event_id`, `status`, and `hold_reason`.

### 3.9. Redemption Fraud Controls, Account Posture, and Benefit Administration

Points are the most liquid asset on a compromised card account, so the programme runs its own investigation queue. A fraud analyst opens a case against an `account_id` with a `case_type` of `ACCOUNT_TAKEOVER`, `POINTS_DRAIN`, or `REDEMPTION_ABUSE`, a non-empty `signals` array whose members are drawn from `DEVICE_CHANGE`, `EMAIL_CHANGE`, `PASSWORD_RESET`, `NEW_PAYEE`, and `GEO_ANOMALY`, and a `device_fingerprint_changed` boolean recording whether the device fingerprint changed inside the lookback window. Duplicate members in `signals` are rejected as a malformed body with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `opened_by_user_id`. A new case is `OPEN`, with `reviewed_at`, `resolved_by_user_id`, `resolution_note`, and `review_score` all null.

**Scoring runs in two legs, and the second can cross a threshold the first did not.** At case opening the `instant_score` is the sum of the weights of the case's distinct signals, capped at 100: a device change contributes 30, a new payee 25, an email change 20, a password reset 15, and a geographic anomaly 10. While the case is `OPEN`, `risk_score` equals `instant_score`.

Taking the case under review recomputes it. A fraud analyst moves an `OPEN` case to `UNDER_REVIEW`, which sets `reviewed_at` to the clock instant, increments the case's `version` by 1, and computes `review_score` from three terms. The first is the unchanged `instant_score`. The second is a transfer-velocity term: the system sums `points_out` over the account's transfer requests whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `HELD`, `QUEUED`, or `COMPLETED` and whose `created_at` falls strictly after the instant 30 days before the case's own `created_at` and at or before the case's `created_at`; that window total is divided by 50,000 using integer floor division, multiplied by 10, and capped at 40. Because the step is a floor division, 99,999 points in the window score a single step of 10, not two. The third term is a device-stability credit of 10, granted only when `device_fingerprint_changed` is false and suppressed entirely when it is true. `review_score` is the `instant_score` plus the velocity term less the credit, clamped below at 0 and above at 100. From `UNDER_REVIEW` onward `risk_score` equals `review_score`. Note that the velocity window is anchored on the case's own `created_at` rather than on the clock, so re-reviewing a case never widens it, and that rejected and reversed transfers are excluded because they never reached the partner.

**The posture is derived, never set.** After every case creation, review, confirmation, and clearance, after every transfer hold, and after the purchase-reversal cascade increments `transfer_hold_count`, the system recomputes the account's `fraud_posture` from four ordered tests. If any case on the account is `CONFIRMED`, the posture is `LOCKED`. Otherwise, if any case that is `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW` has a `risk_score` of at least 60, the posture is `LOCKED`. Otherwise, if any case that is `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW` has a `risk_score` of at least 30, or the account's `transfer_hold_count` is at least 2, the posture is `WATCH`. Otherwise the posture is `CLEAR`. All three thresholds are inclusive: a score of exactly 60 locks, a score of exactly 30 watches, and exactly two holds watch. Cases that are `CLEARED` never contribute to any of the tests. And because `transfer_hold_count` is monotonic, an account that has accumulated two holds can never fall back below `WATCH`, no matter how many cases are subsequently cleared.

Each posture movement has its own cascade, and they are not symmetrical. Moving `CLEAR` → `WATCH` suspends only the account's `LOUNGE_ACCESS` entitlements that are currently `ACTIVE`, stamping each with `suspension_reason` `POSTURE_WATCH`, and increments the account's `version` by 1. Moving to `LOCKED` — whether from `WATCH` or directly from `CLEAR` when a freshly opened case already scores at least 60 — is far heavier: every transfer request of the account whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `QUEUED`, or `HELD` is moved to `REJECTED`, every benefit entitlement of the account that is `ACTIVE` is moved to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `POSTURE_LOCKED` regardless of `benefit_type`, and the account's `version` increments by 1.

Recovery is likewise two-branched. When a clearance leaves no `CONFIRMED` case, no `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW` case scoring at least 30, and a `transfer_hold_count` strictly below 2, the account moves `LOCKED` → `CLEAR`: every `SUSPENDED` entitlement returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` cleared, and the account's `version` increments by 1. When instead a clearance leaves no `CONFIRMED` case and nothing scoring at least 60, but either a `transfer_hold_count` of at least 2 or some `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW` case scoring at least 30, the account moves `LOCKED` → `WATCH`: every `SUSPENDED` entitlement whose `benefit_type` is *not* `LOUNGE_ACCESS` returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` cleared, while `LOUNGE_ACCESS` rows remain `SUSPENDED` and have their `suspension_reason` restated as `POSTURE_WATCH`. The account's `version` increments by 1. Transfer requests already rejected by a lock are never resurrected by any recovery.

A `LOCKED` posture is also a live admission gate, and it blocks exactly seven operations, each rejecting with `ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_LOCKED`: booking a portal offer, creating a statement-credit redemption order, confirming a redemption order, creating a transfer request, clearing screening on a transfer request, releasing a held transfer request, and consuming a benefit entitlement. A `WATCH` posture gates none of them.

**Resolving a case is the approver's half of the control.** Confirming fraud requires that the case is `UNDER_REVIEW` and that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `opened_by_user_id`; the approver supplies a `resolution_note`. The `UNDER_REVIEW` → `CONFIRMED` transition runs a four-hop cascade. The first hop records `resolution_note` and stores the acting identifier as `resolved_by_user_id`. The second hop, caused by the first, pins the account's posture at `LOCKED` because a `CONFIRMED` case now exists. The third hop, caused by the second, moves every `SUBMITTED`, `QUEUED`, or `HELD` transfer request of the account to `REJECTED` with `resolution_reason` `POSTURE_LOCKED`. The fourth hop, caused by the third, appends a `TRANSFER_REVERSAL` ledger entry for each rejection and lowers `points_pending_redemption` accordingly, restoring `points_balance` to its pre-submission value for each. Alongside the transfer cascade, every `ACTIVE` benefit entitlement of the account moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `POSTURE_LOCKED`. Both the case's `version` and the account's `version` increment by 1.

Clearing a case shares the same origin state and the same segregation requirement — `UNDER_REVIEW`, and an acting identifier different from `opened_by_user_id` — and takes the same `resolution_note`, but its side-effect set is disjoint. The `UNDER_REVIEW` → `CLEARED` transition records `resolution_note` and `resolved_by_user_id`, re-runs the posture calculation, and reinstates whichever `SUSPENDED` entitlements the recovered posture no longer suppresses, following the `LOCKED` → `CLEAR` or `LOCKED` → `WATCH` branch as the remaining evidence dictates. Transfer requests already `REJECTED` stay rejected. Both the case's `version` and the account's `version` increment by 1. Cases may be read individually — the read exposes both score legs and the derived `risk_score` — or listed filtered by `account_id`, `case_type`, and `status`; the fraud queue is closed to cardholders entirely.

**Benefit entitlements are what the posture cascade actually touches.** A programme administrator grants one against an `account_id` with a `benefit_type` of `TRAVEL_INSURANCE`, `LOUNGE_ACCESS`, or `BAGGAGE_FEE_CREDIT`, an `annual_allowance` strictly greater than 0 giving the number of consumptions permitted before expiry, an `expires_at` instant at which the sweep will expire the row, and an optional `requires_portal_booking` flag that defaults to **true**. Granting an entitlement on a `CLOSED` account is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. A new entitlement is `ACTIVE` with `used_count` at 0, `remaining_allowance` equal to `annual_allowance`, and `last_consumed_at` and `suspension_reason` null. `remaining_allowance` is always the `annual_allowance` less `used_count`, floored at 0 so it never reports a negative figure, and `used_count` counts only consumptions that succeeded — a rejected consume call never increments it.

Consuming one unit is a cardholder action taking an optional `redemption_order_id`. Five conditions must all hold. The entitlement's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` row is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_NOT_CONSUMABLE`. The owning account must be `ACTIVE` and its `fraud_posture` must not be `LOCKED`. `remaining_allowance` must be at least 1, or the call is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED`. When `requires_portal_booking` is true the cited `redemption_order_id` must name an order whose `channel` is `PORTAL_BOOKING` and whose `fulfillment_status` is `TICKETED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_REQUIRES_TICKETED_BOOKING`; when the flag is false no order need be cited at all. And whenever an order is cited, its `account_id` must equal the entitlement's `account_id`, or the call is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. On success `used_count` rises by 1, `remaining_allowance` falls by 1, `last_consumed_at` is set to the clock instant, and `version` increments by 1.

Expiry is terminal and arrives two ways: the sweep expires an entitlement that is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` once the clock instant reaches or passes its `expires_at`, and the account-closure cascade of §3.1 expires every `ACTIVE` entitlement of the closing account. Either way `version` increments by 1 and every subsequent consume attempt is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_NOT_CONSUMABLE`. Entitlements may be listed filtered by `account_id`, `benefit_type`, and `status`.

### 3.10. Programme Change Communication and Grandfathering

When the issuer changes programme terms, it must tell cardholders in advance and must honour a protection window for those who joined under the old terms. A programme administrator drafts a notice with a `notice_code` that must be globally unique across all notices — a reused code is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE` — a `change_type` of `TRANSFER_RATIO_CHANGE`, `EARN_MULTIPLIER_CUT`, or `BENEFIT_REMOVAL`, the `effective_at` instant at which the change bites, a `grandfather_window_days` count of at least 0 giving the days of protection granted past that instant, and a `cohort_enrolled_before` instant that defines the protected cohort. A ratio change additionally carries a `target_agreement_id` naming the agreement it rewrites, a `new_ratio_points_out` that is strictly positive and a multiple of 1000 when present, and a `new_ratio_partner_units_in` that is strictly positive when present. Any notice may carry a `supersedes_notice_id` pointing at an earlier notice it replaces — a self-reference into the same collection. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `drafted_by_user_id` and the notice starts in `DRAFT`, with `announced_at`, `approved_by_user_id`, and `withdrawn_reason` null.

Submission for approval is the administrator's second act. The `DRAFT` → `PENDING_APPROVAL` transition increments `version` by 1, and for a `TRANSFER_RATIO_CHANGE` it enforces two conditions: `target_agreement_id`, `new_ratio_points_out`, and `new_ratio_partner_units_in` must all be present, or the request is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_TARGET_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); and the named agreement must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. An approver may bounce a pending notice back with a `PENDING_APPROVAL` → `DRAFT` revision that increments `version` by 1, and the draft/submit loop may repeat indefinitely.

Announcement is where the notice period is enforced. The `PENDING_APPROVAL` → `ANNOUNCED` transition requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `drafted_by_user_id`, and that the notice's `effective_at` is at least 45 days after the clock instant at the moment of announcement — a shorter runway is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). Announcement sets `announced_at` to the clock instant, stores the acting identifier as `approved_by_user_id`, and increments `version` by 1. It also cascades through the self-reference: when `supersedes_notice_id` is set and the notice it names is currently `ANNOUNCED`, that earlier notice moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_reason` `SUPERSEDED`. An administrator may also withdraw an `ANNOUNCED` notice directly with an explicit `withdrawn_reason`, which increments `version` by 1. Accounts already re-pointed are unaffected by a withdrawal, because a notice that reached `WITHDRAWN` was by definition never `EFFECTIVE`.

**Taking effect re-points the whole book, in two phases.** The sweep moves an `ANNOUNCED` notice to `EFFECTIVE` once the clock instant has reached or passed `effective_at`, incrementing the notice's `version` by 1. That transition immediately walks every account whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, in `created_at` ascending order. An account whose `enrolled_at` is strictly before the notice's `cohort_enrolled_before` is protected: it keeps whatever `terms_notice_id` it already had, records the new notice as its `pending_notice_id`, and receives a `grandfather_expires_at` equal to the notice's `effective_at` plus `grandfather_window_days` days. Every other active account is re-pointed at once: `terms_notice_id` becomes the new notice, and both `pending_notice_id` and `grandfather_expires_at` are cleared to null. Accounts whose `status` is `CLOSED` are skipped entirely and keep whatever terms they held. The iteration order is observable only through each account's `version` and never changes any account's resulting terms.

The second phase runs on a later sweep. Any account carrying a non-null `pending_notice_id` whose `grandfather_expires_at` the clock has reached or passed has that pending notice promoted into `terms_notice_id`, with `pending_notice_id` cleared to null; `grandfather_expires_at` is retained as an audit fact rather than cleared. From that moment the account's transfer arithmetic changes.

**The applied ratio is resolved from the account's governing notice, not from the agreement alone.** When a transfer completes, the system looks at the notice named by the account's `terms_notice_id`. If there is such a notice, and its `status` is `EFFECTIVE`, and its `change_type` is `TRANSFER_RATIO_CHANGE`, and its `target_agreement_id` equals the agreement being transferred into, then the applied ratio is that notice's `new_ratio_points_out` and `new_ratio_partner_units_in`. In every other case — a null `terms_notice_id` meaning the account is still on the base agreement terms, a notice of some other `change_type`, or a notice targeting a different agreement — the applied ratio is the agreement's own `ratio_points_out` and `ratio_partner_units_in`. That resolution is what §3.8 stores as `ratio_points_out_applied` and `ratio_partner_units_in_applied`. Notices may be read individually or listed filtered by `status`, `change_type`, `target_agreement_id`, and `supersedes_notice_id`.

### 3.11. Points Liability and Breakage Accounting

Finance values the outstanding points balance on demand. A programme administrator generates a snapshot by supplying an `as_of` cut-off instant and a `method` of either `COST_BASIS` or `FAIR_VALUE`. The cut-off may not be in the future: an `as_of` strictly after the clock instant is rejected with `ERR_SNAPSHOT_AS_OF_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). The acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `generated_by_user_id`, and the resulting row is immutable — it carries only `id` and `created_at` alongside its computed figures, has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and can never be amended. Snapshots may be listed filtered by `method`.

The computation has seven steps, and the order is pinned because the calculation would otherwise be circular.

**Step one** computes `gross_outstanding_points` as the sum of `points_delta` over *every* ledger entry whose `occurred_at` is at or before `as_of`, regardless of `entry_type`. This figure drives the breakage tier and nothing else.

**Step two** splits that total by dormancy, walking accounts in `created_at` ascending order. An account's own outstanding points are the sum of `points_delta` over its entries at or before `as_of`. An account is **dormant** when it has no `EARN_BASE` and no `EARN_BONUS` entry whose `occurred_at` falls strictly after the instant 365 days before `as_of` — a full year without earning. `dormant_outstanding_points` is the sum of the outstanding points of dormant accounts; the non-dormant bucket is `gross_outstanding_points` less that figure.

**Step three** selects the breakage tier, keyed on step one's gross figure only — never on the net liability the tier itself helps produce, and never on the points remaining after breakage is subtracted. Below 1,000,000 gross outstanding points the base breakage rate is 1200 basis points; from 1,000,000 up to but not including 10,000,000 it is 1800 basis points; at 10,000,000 and above it is 2400 basis points. The dormant bucket breaks at a higher rate: the base rate plus 1000 basis points, capped at 5000 basis points.

**Step four** computes `blended_cost_per_1k_cents`, and this is where the two methods diverge. Under `COST_BASIS` the input set is the redemption orders whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED` and whose `settled_at` is at or before `as_of`; the blended cost is the sum of their `issuer_cost_final_cents` multiplied by 1,000, divided by the sum of their `points_debited`, rounded half up — the actual invoiced cost. Under `FAIR_VALUE` the input set is the redemption orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `TICKETED`, or `REFUNDED` and whose `created_at` is at or before `as_of`; the blended cost is the sum of their `issuer_cost_provisional_cents` multiplied by 1,000, divided by the sum of their `points_debited`, rounded half up — the forward-looking estimate. Orders that were `CANCELLED` appear in neither set. Should the chosen input set be empty, the blended cost falls back to 1000 cents per 1,000 points. On identical data the two methods diverge exactly when some settled invoice differed from its booking-time estimate.

**Step five** values each bucket separately. The non-dormant bucket's value is its outstanding points multiplied by `blended_cost_per_1k_cents` and divided by 1,000, rounded half up to whole cents; the dormant bucket's value is computed the same way from its own points. `gross_liability_cents` is the sum of those two already-rounded figures.

**Step six** computes `breakage_credit_cents` — the value of points the issuer expects never to be redeemed — as the non-dormant bucket's value multiplied by the base rate and divided by 10,000, rounded half up, plus the dormant bucket's value multiplied by the dormant rate and divided by 10,000, rounded half up. Each bucket's credit is rounded at its own rate before the two are added.

**Step seven** reports the result. `net_liability_cents` is `gross_liability_cents` less `breakage_credit_cents`. `effective_breakage_rate_bps` is `breakage_credit_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by `gross_liability_cents`, rounded half up — and is reported as 0 when `gross_liability_cents` is 0 rather than raising an error.
