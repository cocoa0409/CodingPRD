# Orchestra Symphony Season API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A regional symphony orchestra runs on three clocks that rarely agree. The artistic clock decides, a year ahead, which programmes will be played and which soloists and conductors will be engaged for them. The labour clock — governed by a collectively bargained agreement with the players' union — decides how many services a musician may work in a day, how much rest must separate them, when a break must be scheduled, and what every minute past the contracted call is worth. The commercial clock decides when renewing subscribers get first refusal on their seats, when the remaining inventory opens to the general public, and what each of those seats is worth on the day it is sold. Today these three clocks live in separate spreadsheets, and every disagreement between them — a soloist who has not countersigned by the on-sale date, a rehearsal that quietly breaches the rest rule, a subscriber renewal that resells a seat already sold as a single ticket — is discovered late and resolved by hand.

This document specifies the Orchestra Symphony Season API, a single system of record that reconciles all three. It governs season programming and guest artist contracting, subscription package construction and seat renewal priority, single-ticket on-sale with demand-responsive pricing, musician contracting and rehearsal scheduling under union rules, donor and patron seating benefits, program book advertising sales, and the cancellation and substitution paths that unwind any of the above. Every business path is mounted under the `/orchestra` prefix, with a single administrative clock endpoint mounted outside it.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **seats and money are conserved quantities**: a seat that leaves the available pool must be accounted for as held or sold, and a cent a patron has paid must be recoverable as an order, a package, or house credit — before, during and after every compensating action. Second, **nothing expires on a wall clock**. Time advances only when an operator advances it: the logical UTC clock is set explicitly, and stale ticket holds and unpaid packages are swept only when an operator runs the housekeeping checkpoint. This makes the system fully deterministic and auditable, at the cost of requiring the box office to actually run its checkpoints.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts — authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity, precision, validation ordering, and the cross-cutting invariants that every workflow in Section 3 is obliged to preserve.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. Seven roles are recognised, and their names must be spelled exactly as follows: `ArtisticAdmin`, `OrchestraManager`, `BoxOfficeAgent`, `Patron`, `Musician`, `GuestArtist`, and `AdvertisingSales`. A request that omits either header, or that presents an `X-User-Role` value which is not one of those seven strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose role is well-formed but is not listed among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The seven roles are peers rather than a hierarchy — each owns a different family of resources. `ArtisticAdmin` owns seasons, halls, seating sections, concerts, programming decisions, guest artist offers, cancellations and substitutions. `OrchestraManager` owns the musician roster, the collective bargaining agreement, rehearsal services and the engagements booked onto them. `BoxOfficeAgent` registers patrons, records philanthropic gifts, and builds and settles subscription packages and single-ticket orders on a patron's behalf. `Patron` is a subscriber or single-ticket buyer acting for themself. `Musician` is a roster player acting for themself. `GuestArtist` is a visiting soloist or conductor negotiating one specific contract. `AdvertisingSales` owns program book advertising bookings and their approval.

Four of these roles carry an **opaque identity**. When `X-User-Role` is `ArtisticAdmin`, `OrchestraManager`, `BoxOfficeAgent`, or `AdvertisingSales`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may log it, but it never compares it to any entity's `id` and no ownership check is derived from it.

The remaining three roles carry an **entity-backed identity**, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Patron`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patron` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a patron compare that resource's `patron_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Musician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Musician` record; ownership checks on an engagement compare the engagement's `musician_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `GuestArtist`, the contract row itself is the identity: `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `GuestArtistContract`, and ownership checks compare the target contract's own `id` to `X-User-ID`. Whenever an entity-backed role acts on a resource whose owning identifier differs from `X-User-ID`, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from real elapsed time, and the `api_request_id` is `req_` followed by exactly twenty uppercase alphanumeric characters.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is not supplied it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip before the first returned item, defaults to 0 and must not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, a `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422; an empty string for either parameter is treated as if it had been omitted.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by the resource's creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order, with ties — which the logical clock makes common, since every row written during one request shares an instant — broken by the order in which the rows were inserted.

List responses carry the four pagination fields inside the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system — `Season`, `Venue`, `SeatSection`, `Concert`, `GuestArtistContract`, `Musician`, `UnionAgreement`, `RehearsalService`, `MusicianEngagement`, `Patron`, `SubscriptionPackage`, `TicketOrder` and `AdBooking` — carries the same four system-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format recording the most recent modification. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the resource; `created_at` and `updated_at` are both read from the logical clock described below, and a resource that one request mutates over several hops has its `version` incremented exactly once for that request, however many hops touched it, though its `updated_at` is refreshed by each of them. There are no resources in this system that omit `updated_at` or `version`; all thirteen entity families carry the complete set.

The word *mutation* is meant strictly here. Refreshing a derived counter is a mutation: when a concert's `seats_held_count` moves because a ticket hold was placed, or a season's `concerts_programmed_count` moves because a concert was programmed, or a musician's `ytd_earnings_cents` moves because a service was clocked out, that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments — even though no client sent a request naming that resource. Cascades therefore leave version trails on every entity they touch, and those trails are exactly what the optimistic concurrency rules of §2.12 detect.

All time in this system is read from a **logical UTC clock** rather than from real elapsed time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to wall-clock time. An administrative endpoint, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, accepts a single field `to_iso8601` and moves the logical clock to that instant, returning the new value as `current_utc`. It is available to `ArtisticAdmin`, `OrchestraManager`, `BoxOfficeAgent` and `AdvertisingSales`. Every deadline comparison, every UTC calendar-date grouping, and every seniority calculation in this document reads that clock and nothing else.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of **cents**. There are no fractional cents and no floating-point money: prices, fees, invoices, credit balances and lifetime ledgers are all whole-cent integers.

Every rate, premium, discount and multiplier is expressed in **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points is exactly a factor of one. A `price_multiplier_bp` of 12500 multiplies by 1.25; a discount of 500 basis points removes five percent. Wherever a monetary amount is scaled by a basis-point figure, the result is divided by 10,000 and then rounded to a whole number of cents using the **round half up** rule — a result of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, upward.

Rounding is applied at the points the calculation narratives in Section 3 identify and at no others. This matters because the system deliberately rounds *per line* rather than once over a raw total in subscription pricing, and rounds after each of several sequential multiplications in single-ticket pricing. A reader who collapses two rounding steps into one, or who defers all rounding to the end, will produce prices that differ by a cent or two from the specified result. Where a step is described as producing a value in cents, that value is rounded before it is fed into the next step.

Counters that are not monetary — seat counts, service counts, minutes, priority points and basis-point tiers — are exact integers and are never rounded.

### 2.6. Request Validation Order

Every write request is evaluated against a fixed ladder of checks, and the ladder is strictly ordered: the first check that fails determines the response, and no later check is evaluated. Ordering is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, because several checks in this system can fail simultaneously on a single request and only one error may be returned. The twenty-two rungs, in order, are:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` with HTTP 401. This applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-roles list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` with HTTP 403. This also applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every resource named in the URL path exists, validated left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable identifier is the one reported. Depending on which identifier failed, the response is `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MUSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATRON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AD_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. The path variables that participate are `season_id`, `venue_id`, `section_id`, `concert_id`, `contract_id`, `agreement_id`, `musician_id`, `service_id`, `engagement_id`, `patron_id`, `subscription_id`, `order_id` and `booking_id`; `agreement_id` is the one with no dedicated code of its own, and an unresolvable one is reported as `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_FOUND`. This applies to every path-parameterised endpoint.
4. **Body referential integrity.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — `season_id`, `venue_id`, `concert_id`, `seat_section_id`, `patron_id`, `musician_id`, `contract_id`, and every element of `concert_ids`. The applicable error is `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATRON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MUSICIAN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, with HTTP 404. This applies to every endpoint that carries foreign keys in its body.
5. **Ownership.** For the three entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` equals the identifier of the entity the request acts for, as described in §2.1, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403. This rung guards exactly eight operations: `POST /orchestra/subscriptions`, `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/pay`, `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew`, `POST /orchestra/ticket-orders`, `POST /orchestra/ticket-orders/{order_id}/pay`, `POST /orchestra/engagements/{engagement_id}/decline`, `POST /orchestra/guest-contracts/{contract_id}/counter` and `POST /orchestra/guest-contracts/{contract_id}/sign`.
6. **Request shape.** Required fields are present, enum-typed fields carry a value from their declared set, integer fields fall inside their declared ranges, `scheduled_end_utc` is strictly after `scheduled_start_utc`, `actual_end_utc` is strictly after `actual_start_utc`, and a patron's `email` is unique across all patrons. Any violation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 422. This applies to every write endpoint.
7. **Target-resource state.** The resource the request targets is itself in a state that permits the operation: a concert must be `DRAFT` to be programmed, a contract must be `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` to be negotiated, a service must be `DRAFT` to be published or to accept engagements, a service must be `PUBLISHED` to be clocked, an engagement must be `CONFIRMED` to be declined, an order must be `HELD` to be paid, a package must be `PENDING_PAYMENT` to be paid, a package must be `ACTIVE` to be renewed, a booking must be `SUBMITTED` to be decided, a concert must be `SCHEDULED` to be cancelled or settled, and a concert's `sale_status` must be `PUBLIC_ONSALE` before a ticket order may be created against it. The applicable error is one of `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_ON_SALE`, `ERR_CONCERT_ALREADY_CANCELLED`, `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_SETTLEABLE`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_NEGOTIABLE`, `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_CONFIRMED`, `ERR_TICKET_ORDER_NOT_HELD`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` or `ERR_AD_BOOKING_NOT_SUBMITTED`, each with HTTP 409. This applies to every state-changing endpoint.
8. **Cross-entity state gates.** Signing a contract requires the parent concert's `programming_status` to be `PROGRAMMED` or `LOCKED`; scheduling a service requires the same; programming a concert requires its season's `status` to be `PLANNING`; a season advance requires the declared from-state together with that transition's own preconditions; renewing requires the **target** season's `status` to be `RENEWAL_WINDOW`; and creating a new subscription package requires its season's `status` to be either `RENEWAL_WINDOW` or `SINGLE_ON_SALE`. The applicable error is `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_PROGRAMMED`, `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`, `ERR_UNION_AGREEMENT_MISSING`, `ERR_NO_PROGRAMMED_CONCERTS` or `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, each with HTTP 409. This rung guards ten operations: `POST /orchestra/guest-contracts/{contract_id}/sign`, `POST /orchestra/rehearsals`, `POST /orchestra/concerts/{concert_id}/program`, `POST /orchestra/seasons/{season_id}/advance`, `POST /orchestra/subscriptions` and `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew`, together with `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/engagements` and `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/publish`, which both require the concert's season to carry a union agreement, and `POST /orchestra/ad-bookings/{booking_id}/decide` and `POST /orchestra/concerts/{concert_id}/cancel`, which both require that season not to have reached `CLOSED`.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_concert_version` equals the target concert's current `version` and `expected_package_version` equals the prior package's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CONCERT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION`, each with HTTP 412. This rung guards exactly two operations: `POST /orchestra/ticket-orders` and `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew`. Note that it sits *below* the state gates: a package that is not `ACTIVE` reports `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` even when the supplied version is also stale.
10. **Union break rule.** A service whose scheduled duration is longer than 90 minutes must schedule at least `mandatory_break_minutes` of break, else `ERR_UNION_BREAK_VIOLATION` with HTTP 422. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/publish`.
11. **Union rest rule.** For the musician being booked, the gap between the end of any other of their `CONFIRMED` engagements' services and the start of this one — measured in whichever direction is applicable — must be at least `min_rest_minutes_between_services`; a gap exactly equal to the minimum passes. A shorter gap yields `ERR_UNION_REST_VIOLATION` with HTTP 422. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/engagements`.
12. **Union daily-service cap.** The musician may not hold more than `max_services_per_day` `CONFIRMED` engagements whose services start on the same UTC calendar date, whether or not those services overlap in time. Exceeding the cap yields `ERR_UNION_DAILY_SERVICE_CAP` with HTTP 409. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/engagements`.
13. **Doubling limit.** The `doubling_count` on an engagement must not exceed 2, else `ERR_DOUBLING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 422. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/engagements`.
14. **Venue changeover rule.** A service may not be published while another `PUBLISHED` service in the same hall collides with it once the hall's changeover buffer is added to both ends, yielding `ERR_VENUE_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT` with HTTP 409. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/publish`; the exact collision test is given in §2.8.
15. **Minimum complement.** At publication time the service's `confirmed_engagement_count` must be at least its `minimum_complement`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COMPLEMENT` with HTTP 409. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/rehearsals/{service_id}/publish`.
16. **Negotiation round cap.** An orchestra offer may not be revised once `negotiation_round` has reached 3, else `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` with HTTP 422. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/guest-contracts/{contract_id}/revise-offer`.
17. **Donor seating benefit.** A new subscription package seated in a section whose `donor_priority_rank` is 1 or 2 requires the patron's `donor_level` to be `BENEFACTOR` or `LAUREATE`, else `ERR_DONOR_BENEFIT_REQUIRED` with HTTP 422. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/subscriptions`; renewals inherit their prior package's section and skip the check entirely.
18. **Renewal priority.** The freshly recomputed `renewal_priority_score` of the patron must be at least the prior package's section's `min_renewal_priority_score`; a score exactly equal to the threshold passes. A lower score yields `ERR_RENEWAL_PRIORITY_INSUFFICIENT` with HTTP 422. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew`.
19. **Package membership.** `concert_ids` must be non-empty, must contain no duplicates, and every element must belong to the package's own `season_id` and have a `programming_status` of either `PROGRAMMED` or `LOCKED`. A violation yields `ERR_PACKAGE_CONCERT_MISMATCH` with HTTP 422. This applies to both `POST /orchestra/subscriptions` and `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew`.
20. **Section-venue match.** The requested `seat_section_id` must belong to the venue of the concert being ticketed, or to the venue of every concert listed in `concert_ids` for a package, else `ERR_SEAT_SECTION_VENUE_MISMATCH` with HTTP 422. This applies to `POST /orchestra/ticket-orders` and `POST /orchestra/subscriptions`.
21. **Renewal deadline.** The logical clock must be at or before the **target** season's `renewal_deadline_utc`; an instant exactly equal to the deadline still passes. A later instant yields `ERR_RENEWAL_DEADLINE_PASSED` with HTTP 409. This applies only to `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew`.
22. **Seat availability.** For every concert affected, the `available_count` on the relevant section inventory element must be at least the number of seats being claimed — `seat_count` for a ticket order, and `seat_count` on every concert of a package. A shortfall yields `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS` with HTTP 409. This applies to `POST /orchestra/ticket-orders`, `POST /orchestra/subscriptions` and `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew`.

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. Availability is the **last** thing checked, so a request that is both unaffordable in seats and defective in some earlier respect always reports the earlier defect. And the union safety rungs (10 through 15) sit above the commercial rungs (17 through 22) but below the concurrency rung, so a stale-version renewal is rejected before the priority gate ever runs.

### 2.7. The Three Status Dimensions of a Concert

A concert is not in one state; it is in three at once, and the three dimensions move independently of one another.

`programming_status` tracks artistic readiness and runs `DRAFT` → `PROGRAMMED` → `LOCKED`, with a revert edge back from `LOCKED` to `PROGRAMMED`. `sale_status` tracks commercial availability and runs `UNANNOUNCED` → `RENEWAL_ONLY` → `PUBLIC_ONSALE` → `SALES_CLOSED`. `performance_status` tracks whether the concert actually happened and runs `SCHEDULED` to either `PERFORMED` or `CANCELLED`. A newly created concert begins at `DRAFT`, `UNANNOUNCED` and `SCHEDULED` simultaneously. `PERFORMED`, `CANCELLED` and `SALES_CLOSED` are terminal within their own dimension.

The independence is deliberate and has sharp edges. Cancelling a concert drives `performance_status` to `CANCELLED` and `sale_status` to `SALES_CLOSED`, but it explicitly leaves `programming_status` untouched — a cancelled concert that had been `LOCKED` stays `LOCKED`. Conversely, the programming unlock edge — the revert from `LOCKED` back to `PROGRAMMED` — never touches `sale_status` or `performance_status`; a concert that reverts to `PROGRAMMED` while already `PUBLIC_ONSALE` remains on sale.

Alongside the three dimensions the system maintains a derived diagnostic, `readiness_posture`, whose value is `READY`, `AT_RISK` or `BLOCKED`. It is evaluated top-down and the first matching clause wins. The posture is `BLOCKED` when at least one guest artist contract on the concert is still in `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` **and** the concert's season has reached `SINGLE_ON_SALE` or `IN_PROGRESS` — that is, the orchestra is selling tickets to a concert whose soloist has not signed. Otherwise the posture is `AT_RISK` when at least one `PUBLISHED` service of the concert has a `compliance_status` of `UNDERSTAFFED`. Otherwise it is `READY`. Only guest contracts of this concert in any status and services of this concert whose status is `PUBLISHED` participate; `CANCELLED` and `DRAFT` services are ignored entirely.

`readiness_posture` is recomputed at the end of every operation whose narrative says so: programming a concert, an artist countering an offer, either branch of signing, a substitution, publishing a service, an engagement being declined, and the season advance that opens public on-sale. Concert cancellation is the one operation that deliberately does not recompute it. That last one is the reason a `BLOCKED` posture can appear without anything about the concert itself changing — the season crossing into `SINGLE_ON_SALE` is what makes an unsigned contract intolerable.

### 2.8. Hall Occupancy and the Changeover Buffer

Each hall carries a `changeover_buffer_minutes` value — the stage-turnaround time that must separate two published services in that room. It defaults to 60 minutes when a hall is created without one, and it may be zero.

Two services in the same hall collide when both of the following hold at once: the new service's `scheduled_start_utc` falls strictly before the other service's `scheduled_end_utc` advanced by the buffer, and the other service's `scheduled_start_utc` falls strictly before the new service's `scheduled_end_utc` advanced by the buffer. The intervals are half-open, so a new service starting at exactly the other service's end plus the buffer does **not** collide and is allowed. Only services whose status is `PUBLISHED` participate in this test; `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` services in the same hall are invisible to it, which is why a room may hold any number of draft services at overlapping times and only the second attempt to publish fails.

### 2.9. Seat Inventory as a Conserved Quantity

When a concert is created, the system snapshots a `section_inventory` array onto it — one element for each seating section belonging to the concert's hall **as those sections exist at that moment**. Adding a section to a hall afterwards does not retro-fit an inventory element onto concerts created earlier. Each element carries `seat_section_id`, `capacity` copied from the section, `held_count`, `sold_count`, `available_count`, `fill_bp` and `demand_band`, and the elements appear in the array in ascending order of their section's `created_at`. At creation the held and sold counts are zero, `available_count` equals `capacity`, `fill_bp` is 0 and `demand_band` is `LOW`.

The counts are not free-floating accumulators; they are defined by aggregation over live claims, and the definition is the invariant the system must re-establish after every mutation. For a given concert and a given inventory element, `held_count` equals the total `seat_count` across that concert's ticket orders in `HELD` status for that section, plus the total `seat_count` across subscription packages in `PENDING_PAYMENT` status seated in that section whose `concert_ids` include this concert. `sold_count` equals the total `seat_count` across that concert's ticket orders in `PAID` status for that section, plus the total `seat_count` across subscription packages in `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` status seated in that section whose `concert_ids` include this concert. `available_count` is the element's `capacity` less its `held_count` less its `sold_count`. Orders in `EXPIRED` or `REFUNDED` status and packages in `LAPSED` status contribute nothing to either count.

The conserved quantity that follows is simple and absolute: for every inventory element, `capacity` always equals `available_count` plus `held_count` plus `sold_count`. This identity must hold before and after every forward action — creating a package, holding a ticket, paying either — and again after every compensating action — a hold expiring, a payment being refunded, a package lapsing. A concert additionally publishes two roll-ups: `seats_held_count` is the sum of `held_count` over all its inventory elements and `seats_sold_count` is the sum of `sold_count` over all of them. Whenever an element's counts move, `available_count`, `fill_bp` and `demand_band` are recomputed for that element and both roll-ups are refreshed on the concert.

A hall also publishes `total_capacity`, the sum of `capacity` over the seating sections registered inside it, which grows as each section is added.

### 2.10. The Patron Cash Identity and House Credit

Patrons hold two money balances and one of them is a ledger that must always reconcile. `credit_balance_cents` is spendable house credit, created only by cancellation refunds and consumable only against ticket orders. `lifetime_cash_in_cents` is a conserved cumulative record of cash — never credit — that the patron has handed over for tickets and subscription packages.

The identity the system guarantees is this: for every patron, `lifetime_cash_in_cents` equals `credit_balance_cents`, plus the total `amount_paid_cents` across that patron's ticket orders in `PAID` status, plus the total `price_cents` across that patron's subscription packages in `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` status. A refund moves an order out of `PAID` and adds exactly its `amount_paid_cents` to `credit_balance_cents`, so the two sides of the identity move by the same amount in opposite directions and the equality survives with zero drift. Spending credit on a later order moves the same cents back the other way. `amount_paid_cents` on a refunded order is left exactly as it was — the refund is recorded by the status change and the credit grant, not by zeroing the order.

Philanthropic gifts sit entirely outside this identity. A donation increases `lifetime_donation_cents` and nothing else; it never touches `lifetime_cash_in_cents`, never touches `credit_balance_cents`, and is never counted as ticket or subscription revenue. Conversely, ticket and subscription payments never contribute to `lifetime_donation_cents`.

### 2.11. The Musician Service-Credit Ledger

Each musician carries `services_charged_ytd`, a conserved counter of how many engagements they currently hold against services that have been published or completed. It is defined by aggregation: `services_charged_ytd` equals the number of that musician's engagements whose status is `CONFIRMED` or `SETTLED` **and** whose `provisional_pay_cents` has been set to a non-null value.

Publishing a service is the forward action — it charges one credit for each engagement it prices. Declining or voiding an engagement is the compensating action and refunds exactly one credit, but only when that engagement was actually charged: an engagement on a service that never reached publication has a null `provisional_pay_cents`, was never charged, and is therefore never refunded. The parallel money ledger, `ytd_earnings_cents`, follows the same discipline; it equals the sum of `provisional_pay_cents` over the musician's `CONFIRMED` engagements plus the sum of `final_pay_cents` over their `SETTLED` engagements.

### 2.12. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system require the client to prove it is acting on a current read of a parent resource. Both are cases where the operation is perfectly legitimate to perform twice, so no state precondition can catch a client that is working from an out-of-date view; the version check is the only thing that rejects it.

The first is single-ticket hold creation. A request to `POST /orchestra/ticket-orders` must carry `expected_concert_version` — a required, non-optional field — holding the `version` of the target concert as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the concert's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONCERT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This matters because a hold prices itself from the concert's current `fill_bp` and `demand_band`; a client working from an advanced-past read would otherwise lock a price computed against inventory that no longer exists. A second hold on the same concert is an ordinary product operation, so nothing else would reject it.

The second is subscription renewal. A request to `POST /orchestra/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/renew` must carry `expected_package_version` — again required and non-optional — holding the `version` of the prior package as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the prior package's current `version`, the renewal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Renewal supersedes the prior package's seat claim into the next season while leaving that prior package `ACTIVE`, so a renewal issued from a stale read would otherwise succeed and mint a duplicate seat claim against the same prior package. A successful renewal increments the prior package's `version` by one, which is precisely what makes a second renewal from the same observed version fail.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Where a request body does not list `expected_concert_version` or `expected_package_version`, none is expected and none is honoured.

### 2.13. The Ops-Run Epoch and Deterministic Expiry

Nothing in this system expires on a timer. Ticket holds expire against a **housekeeping epoch counter**, `ops_run_index`, which lives on the season, starts at 0, and advances by exactly one each time an operator runs the season's ops-run checkpoint. When a hold is created the system stamps it with `hold_expires_at_ops_run`, computed as the season's `ops_run_index` at that instant plus 2 — in other words, a hold survives the next checkpoint and dies at the one after it. A held order becomes expired at the first checkpoint whose new epoch value has reached or passed that stamp.

The checkpoint is also the moment at which unpaid packages past their renewal deadline are swept and at which every patron holding a package in the season has their renewal priority recomputed and persisted. It never changes the season's own `status`; it only advances the epoch and runs its housekeeping. It is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION` when the season has already reached `CLOSED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows a season from its founding through to its closure. Each subsection describes the operations available at that stage, the information a caller must supply, the checks that guard the operation, and the consequences that ripple outward when it succeeds.

### 3.1. Founding a Season: Halls, Sections and the Collective Agreement

An `ArtisticAdmin` opens the year by creating a **Season**. The payload carries a display `name`, the `fiscal_year` the season belongs to, a `renewal_deadline_utc` instant after which subscription renewals are no longer accepted, and a `base_ticket_price_cents` figure — the season-wide base single-ticket price in cents, before any section multiplier or demand multiplier is applied, which must be at least 1. One optional field, `loyalty_discount_bp`, sets the base loyalty discount in basis points granted to renewing subscribers; it defaults to 500 basis points — five percent — and may be set anywhere from 0 to 2000 basis points inclusive. A new season opens in `PLANNING` with its `ops_run_index` at 0 and its three roll-up counters — `concerts_programmed_count`, `concerts_cancelled_count` and `program_book_revenue_cents` — all at zero.

Halls are created independently of seasons. A **Venue** carries only a `name` and an optional `changeover_buffer_minutes`, the stage-turnaround interval described in §2.8, which defaults to 60 minutes and must not be negative. Its `total_capacity` begins at 0 and grows as sections are registered inside it.

A **SeatSection** is registered against a hall by supplying that hall's `venue_id` together with a section `name` — labels such as ORCHESTRA, MEZZANINE or BALCONY are typical — and four commercial attributes. `capacity` is the physical seat count and must be at least 1. `price_multiplier_bp` is the section's price multiplier in basis points, where 10000 means the base price unmodified, and must be at least 1. `donor_priority_rank` ranks the section's desirability from 1, the most desirable, through 5; sections ranked 1 or 2 are the donor-benefit sections whose access is gated in §3.5. The optional `min_renewal_priority_score` sets the minimum priority score a patron must reach in order to renew a package seated here; it defaults to 0 and must not be negative. Registering a section adds its `capacity` to the hall's `total_capacity`, but — as noted in §2.9 — it does not retro-fit an inventory element onto concerts that already exist.

Before a season can leave `PLANNING`, an `OrchestraManager` must register the **UnionAgreement** that governs it. Exactly one agreement may exist per season; a second registration against a season that already has one is refused with `ERR_UNION_AGREEMENT_DUPLICATE`. The agreement is created against a `season_id` and requires `scale_service_fee_cents`, the base pay per service at union scale in cents, which must be at least 1. Everything else is optional and defaults to the terms of the standing agreement. `max_service_minutes` — the worked minutes included in scale, above which overtime begins — defaults to 150 minutes, or two and a half hours, and must be at least 1. `overtime_rate_cents_per_minute` defaults to 400 cents per minute, that is four dollars for every minute past the call, and must not be negative. `min_rest_minutes_between_services` defaults to 90 minutes and must not be negative. `max_services_per_day` — the ceiling on confirmed engagements a player may hold on one UTC calendar date — defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. `doubling_premium_bp` defaults to 1250 basis points, twelve and a half percent per doubled instrument, and must not be negative. `mandatory_break_minutes` defaults to 20 minutes and must not be negative. `principal_premium_bp` defaults to 2000 basis points, a twenty percent uplift for principal chairs, and must not be negative.

The roster is the manager's other founding responsibility. A **Musician** is added with a `full_name` and an `instrument` drawn from `VIOLIN`, `VIOLA`, `CELLO`, `BASS`, `FLUTE`, `OBOE`, `CLARINET`, `BASSOON`, `HORN`, `TRUMPET`, `TROMBONE`, `PERCUSSION` or `HARP`. Two optional fields record the individual's terms: `is_principal` marks a principal chair and defaults to false, and `overscale_bp` records an individually negotiated premium in basis points above union scale, defaulting to 0 and accepted anywhere from 0 to 10000 basis points inclusive. Both ledgers, `services_charged_ytd` and `ytd_earnings_cents`, begin at zero.

### 3.2. Programming the Repertoire and Contracting Guest Artists

A **Concert** is added to a season by naming the owning `season_id`, the `venue_id` of the hall it will be played in, a `title`, the scheduled downbeat `performance_start_utc`, the scheduled `performance_end_utc`, and `program_minutes`, the total printed programme duration in minutes, which must be at least 1. Both instants are required and must parse as ISO 8601, but no ordering constraint is enforced between them — the strict end-after-start rule of §2.6 governs service scheduling and clock-out only — and both are echoed back exactly as the caller supplied them. On creation the concert takes its three opening statuses — `programming_status` of `DRAFT`, `sale_status` of `UNANNOUNCED`, `performance_status` of `SCHEDULED` — a `readiness_posture` of `READY`, a `substitution_count` of 0, a `gross_ticket_revenue_cents` of 0, and the `section_inventory` snapshot described in §2.9.

Programming the concert moves `programming_status` from `DRAFT` to `PROGRAMMED`. It is an `ArtisticAdmin` action, it requires the concert to still be in `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_DRAFT` — and it requires the owning season to still be in `PLANNING`, since programming decisions are frozen once the renewal window opens; a season past `PLANNING` yields `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. Success increments the season's `concerts_programmed_count` by one and recomputes the concert's `readiness_posture`.

Guest engagements are negotiated as **GuestArtistContract** rows, and the negotiation is genuinely cyclic. An `ArtisticAdmin` opens one by supplying the `concert_id`, the `artist_name`, a `role_type` of either `SOLOIST` or `CONDUCTOR`, and an `offered_fee_cents` figure in cents which must not be negative. The contract opens in `OFFERED` with `negotiation_round` at 1, `countered_fee_cents` null and `agreed_fee_cents` null.

From `OFFERED` the artist may counter, moving the contract to `COUNTERED` and storing the demanded `countered_fee_cents` from the request — a required integer that must not be negative. Only a `GuestArtist` whose `X-User-ID` equals the contract's own `id` may do this — anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — and a contract that is not in `OFFERED` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_NEGOTIABLE`. Countering recomputes the parent concert's `readiness_posture`.

From `COUNTERED` the orchestra may answer with a fresh offer, closing the cycle back to `OFFERED`. This is an `ArtisticAdmin` action; it replaces `offered_fee_cents` with the newly supplied figure — again a required, non-negative integer — and increments `negotiation_round` by one. A contract not in `COUNTERED` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_NEGOTIABLE`, and the orchestra is capped at three rounds: once `negotiation_round` has reached 3 the revision is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`.

Signing has two branches, and the branch determines which fee becomes binding. Signing **from `OFFERED`** is the artist accepting the orchestra's standing offer, and `agreed_fee_cents` is set to the current `offered_fee_cents`. Signing **from `COUNTERED`** is the orchestra accepting the artist's demand, and `agreed_fee_cents` is set to the current `countered_fee_cents`. The branch is selected by the contract's current status alone and never by the acting role: either authorised role may call either branch, the sole identity constraint being the ownership rung, which requires a `GuestArtist`'s `X-User-ID` to equal the contract's own `id` and imposes nothing at all on an `ArtisticAdmin`. The request carries no body fields. In either branch the contract lands in `SIGNED`, the parent concert's promotion condition is re-evaluated, and the concert's `readiness_posture` is recomputed. Both branches require the parent concert's `programming_status` to be `PROGRAMMED` or `LOCKED`, refusing a still-`DRAFT` concert with `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_PROGRAMMED`, and both refuse a contract whose status does not permit signing with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_NEGOTIABLE`.

The promotion condition itself — the system-driven move of `programming_status` from `PROGRAMMED` to `LOCKED` — is evaluated at the close of every signature and at the close of every service publication, never on its own request. A concert locks when three things are true at once: it is currently `PROGRAMMED`; it has at least one guest artist contract, every one of its contracts is either `SIGNED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and at least one of them is `SIGNED`; and at least one of its rehearsal services has reached `PUBLISHED`. Locking recomputes `readiness_posture`. The reverse edge, from `LOCKED` back to `PROGRAMMED`, is likewise system-driven: it fires when a published service of the concert falls understaffed, and when a substitution withdraws a signed contract. It too recomputes `readiness_posture`, and — as §2.7 insists — it touches neither `sale_status` nor `performance_status`.

### 3.3. Calling Services: Rehearsal Scheduling, Union Compliance and Musician Pay

Every rehearsal, dress rehearsal and performance call is a **RehearsalService**. An `OrchestraManager` schedules one against a `concert_id` and a `venue_id`, choosing a `service_type` of `REHEARSAL`, `DRESS_REHEARSAL` or `PERFORMANCE`, and supplying `scheduled_start_utc`, a `scheduled_end_utc` that must fall strictly after it, and a `minimum_complement` — the number of confirmed players required before the service may go out — which must not be negative. One optional field, `break_minutes`, records the break scheduled inside the service and defaults to 0; it must not be negative and it is deducted from worked minutes at settlement. Scheduling requires the parent concert's `programming_status` to be `PROGRAMMED` or `LOCKED`, refusing a `DRAFT` concert with `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_PROGRAMMED`. The service opens in `DRAFT` with `compliance_status` of `COMPLIANT`, `confirmed_engagement_count` of 0, `actual_start_utc` and `actual_end_utc` null, `provisional_service_cost_cents` at 0 and `final_service_cost_cents` null. Note what is *not* checked here: neither the hall changeover rule nor any union rule is applied at scheduling time. Both are deferred to publication, so a manager may freely lay out a draft schedule that could never legally be published.

Booking a player onto a draft service creates a **MusicianEngagement**. The request names the `musician_id` and may carry `doubling_count`, the number of additional instruments that player will double on this service, which defaults to 0 and must not be negative. The engagement opens in `CONFIRMED` with `provisional_pay_cents` and `final_pay_cents` null and `overtime_minutes` at 0, and it increments the service's `confirmed_engagement_count` by one. The service must still be in `DRAFT` — a service already published or beyond is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_DRAFT` — and the concert's season must have a union agreement registered, without which the booking is refused with `ERR_UNION_AGREEMENT_MISSING` since none of the union arithmetic below would be defined.

Three union rules guard the booking, and §2.6 fixes the order in which they are tried. The **rest rule** looks at every other engagement this player holds in `CONFIRMED` status and measures the gap between that engagement's service and this one — from the earlier service's scheduled end to the later service's scheduled start, whichever way round the two services fall. Every such gap must be at least the agreement's `min_rest_minutes_between_services`; a gap exactly equal to that minimum is acceptable, a shorter one is `ERR_UNION_REST_VIOLATION`. Engagements the player already holds on *this same service* are excluded from the comparison entirely, and two services that actually overlap in time have no gap at all and are refused with `ERR_UNION_REST_VIOLATION` outright. The **daily-service cap** counts this player's `CONFIRMED` engagements whose services begin on the same UTC calendar date as this one, including any the player already holds on this very service; if the player already holds `max_services_per_day` of them the booking is `ERR_UNION_DAILY_SERVICE_CAP`, and this applies whether or not the services overlap in time — two well-separated calls on one day still count as two. The **doubling limit** caps `doubling_count` at 2; a third doubled instrument is `ERR_DOUBLING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Publishing a service moves it from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` and is the point at which the orchestra commits money. Three preconditions apply beyond the service being `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_DRAFT`). First, the **break rule**: if the interval from `scheduled_start_utc` to `scheduled_end_utc` is longer than 90 minutes, then `break_minutes` must be at least the agreement's `mandatory_break_minutes`, else `ERR_UNION_BREAK_VIOLATION`. A service of exactly 90 minutes is not long enough to require a break. Second, the **changeover rule** of §2.8 against every other `PUBLISHED` service in the same hall, else `ERR_VENUE_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT`. Third, the **complement rule**: `confirmed_engagement_count` must be at least `minimum_complement`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COMPLEMENT`. As at booking time, the concert's season must have a union agreement registered — publication prices the engagements from it — and a season without one refuses the publication with `ERR_UNION_AGREEMENT_MISSING`.

When publication succeeds, every engagement of the service still in `CONFIRMED` status is priced provisionally, `compliance_status` is set to `COMPLIANT`, `provisional_service_cost_cents` is set to the sum of those provisional figures, each of those players has `services_charged_ytd` incremented by one and `ytd_earnings_cents` increased by their own provisional figure, the parent concert's promotion condition is re-evaluated, and the concert's `readiness_posture` is recomputed. Engagements that have been declined or voided take no part in the sum.

**Musician pay** is built from a single premium figure and then extended at settlement. The premium in basis points is the sum of three additive terms: the agreement's `principal_premium_bp` if the player's `is_principal` is true and nothing if it is false, plus the player's own `overscale_bp`, plus the engagement's `doubling_count` multiplied by the agreement's `doubling_premium_bp`. These terms are added, never compounded — a principal on 2000 basis points who doubles once at 1250 basis points carries a premium of 3250 basis points, not a product of two uplifts. Base pay in cents is the agreement's `scale_service_fee_cents` multiplied by 10000 plus that premium, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The **provisional** figure, `provisional_pay_cents`, is exactly that base pay: scheduled minutes are ignored entirely at publication and no overtime is anticipated.

Clocking a service out moves it from `PUBLISHED` to `COMPLETED` and produces the **final** figures. The `OrchestraManager` supplies `actual_start_utc` and `actual_end_utc`, and the latter must fall strictly after the former or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a service that is not `PUBLISHED` is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Worked minutes are the number of whole minutes from `actual_start_utc` to `actual_end_utc`, truncated downward, less the service's `break_minutes`. `overtime_minutes` is the amount by which worked minutes exceed the agreement's `max_service_minutes`, or zero when they do not exceed it; the comparison is strict, so worked minutes exactly equal to `max_service_minutes` produce zero overtime. That figure is computed once for the service as a whole and stamped identically onto the `overtime_minutes` of every engagement it settles. `final_pay_cents` is the same base pay computed at publication plus `overtime_minutes` multiplied by the agreement's `overtime_rate_cents_per_minute`. Overtime is a flat per-minute rate and is never scaled by the premium — a principal and a rank-and-file player earn the identical overtime for the same minute. Each engagement so priced moves from `CONFIRMED` to `SETTLED`, each player's `ytd_earnings_cents` is increased by the difference between that engagement's `final_pay_cents` and its `provisional_pay_cents`, and the service's `final_service_cost_cents` is set to the sum of `final_pay_cents` over the settled engagements. Identical inputs therefore make the final figure strictly larger than the provisional one whenever the clocked worked minutes ran past `max_service_minutes`, and equal to it otherwise.

A player may decline a booking, moving the engagement from `CONFIRMED` to `DECLINED`. Only the `Musician` whose `X-User-ID` equals the engagement's `musician_id` may do so; anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. An engagement that is not `CONFIRMED` is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and the parent service must be either `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` — declining out of a cancelled or completed service is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

Declining runs a four-hop cascade. Before it begins, if the engagement had already been priced — that is, its `provisional_pay_cents` is not null — the player's `services_charged_ytd` is decremented by one and their `ytd_earnings_cents` is reduced by that provisional figure, per the ledger discipline of §2.11. Hop one decrements the parent service's `confirmed_engagement_count` by one. Hop two, caused by hop one: if the service is `PUBLISHED` and its `confirmed_engagement_count` has now fallen below its `minimum_complement`, the service's `compliance_status` becomes `UNDERSTAFFED` and its `provisional_service_cost_cents` is recomputed over the engagements that remain. Hop three, caused by hop two: the parent concert's `readiness_posture` is recomputed by the rules of §2.7 — which yields `AT_RISK` here — and that recomputation runs on every decline, whether or not the service actually fell understaffed. Hop four, caused by hop three: only when the service did fall understaffed, and only if that concert's `programming_status` is `LOCKED`, does it revert to `PROGRAMMED`, because the orchestra can no longer claim the concert is fully staffed.

### 3.4. Patrons, Philanthropy and Donor Standing

A `BoxOfficeAgent` registers a **Patron** with a `full_name` and an `email` that must be unique across all patrons — a duplicate address is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. One optional field, `subscriber_since_year`, records the first year this patron subscribed; when it is omitted the patron is treated as having no seniority at all. A new patron opens with a `donor_level` of `NONE`, with `lifetime_donation_cents`, `consecutive_renewal_years`, `credit_balance_cents` and `lifetime_cash_in_cents` all at zero, and with a `renewal_priority_score` computed from seniority alone.

Philanthropic gifts are recorded against the patron by supplying `amount_cents`, a required integer on which no minimum is enforced. The gift is added to `lifetime_donation_cents`, and the patron's **donor level** is then re-derived from the new cumulative total. The thresholds are inclusive at every step: a cumulative total of at least 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars — makes the patron a `LAUREATE`; otherwise at least 2,500,000 cents makes them a `BENEFACTOR`; otherwise at least 1,000,000 cents makes them a `SUSTAINER`; otherwise at least 250,000 cents, that is two thousand five hundred dollars, makes them a `FRIEND`; and anything below that leaves `donor_level` at `NONE`. Because the comparisons are inclusive, a total of exactly 250,000 cents is a `FRIEND` while 249,999 cents is not. Recording a gift also recomputes and persists the patron's `renewal_priority_score`. It does **not** touch `lifetime_cash_in_cents`, `credit_balance_cents`, or any ticket or subscription figure — §2.10 keeps philanthropy and commerce in separate books.

The **renewal priority score** is a weighted sum of three things and carries no rounding, being an integer point total. Seniority in years is zero when `subscriber_since_year` is null; otherwise it is the calendar year of the logical clock less `subscriber_since_year`, and never less than zero — a patron whose recorded first year lies in the future scores zero seniority rather than a negative figure. The score is then the patron's `consecutive_renewal_years` multiplied by 100, plus the points their `donor_level` carries — 0 for `NONE`, 50 for `FRIEND`, 150 for `SUSTAINER`, 400 for `BENEFACTOR` and 900 for `LAUREATE` — plus seniority in years multiplied by 10. Nothing about any season enters the calculation.

`renewal_priority_score` is recomputed and persisted at four moments: whenever a gift is recorded, whenever a subscription package is paid, at every ops-run checkpoint for each patron holding a package in that season, and freshly at the start of a renewal — in that last case the recomputed value is what the priority gate of §3.6 is tested against, not whatever was stored before.

### 3.5. The Renewal Window: Building and Paying Subscription Packages

An `ArtisticAdmin` opens the renewal window by advancing the season, naming `RENEWAL_WINDOW` as the `target_status`. Every advance in this system works the same way: `target_status` is required and must be one of the five season states or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it must name the season's immediate successor along `PLANNING` → `RENEWAL_WINDOW` → `SINGLE_ON_SALE` → `IN_PROGRESS` → `CLOSED`; naming the current status, a state already passed, or a state further ahead is `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`, decided before any of that transition's own preconditions are examined. The season must currently be in `PLANNING`; it must have exactly one union agreement registered against it, without which the advance is refused with `ERR_UNION_AGREEMENT_MISSING`; and its `concerts_programmed_count` must be at least 1, without which the advance is refused with `ERR_NO_PROGRAMMED_CONCERTS`. When it succeeds, every concert of the season whose `programming_status` is `PROGRAMMED` or `LOCKED` and whose `sale_status` is still `UNANNOUNCED` moves to `RENEWAL_ONLY`, processed in ascending order of `performance_start_utc` with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. Concerts still in `DRAFT` programming are left `UNANNOUNCED`. The season's own `version` increments by one, as it does on every advance.

With the window open, a **SubscriptionPackage** may be built. The request names the `patron_id` the package is for, the `season_id` it belongs to, the `seat_section_id` the subscriber will sit in, a `seat_count` of at least 1 held on every concert in the package, and `concert_ids`, an ordered list of the concerts the package includes, which is persisted and echoed back in exactly the order the caller supplied it — the ascending-performance ordering below governs pricing only, never storage. Such a package is created with a `package_type` of `NEW`, a `status` of `PENDING_PAYMENT` and a null `prior_package_id`. It may be built by a `BoxOfficeAgent` acting on the patron's behalf or by the `Patron` themself, in which case `X-User-ID` must equal the `patron_id` in the payload. The season must be in either `RENEWAL_WINDOW` or `SINGLE_ON_SALE`; any other season state refuses the build with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`.

Three commercial checks then apply, in the order §2.6 fixes. The **donor seating benefit** reserves the best rooms for the orchestra's principal supporters: if the chosen section's `donor_priority_rank` is 1 or 2, the patron's `donor_level` must be `BENEFACTOR` or `LAUREATE`, and a lesser standing is refused with `ERR_DONOR_BENEFIT_REQUIRED`. **Package membership** requires that `concert_ids` be non-empty, contain no duplicate entries, and consist entirely of concerts belonging to the same `season_id` as the package whose `programming_status` is `PROGRAMMED` or `LOCKED`; a violation of any of those is `ERR_PACKAGE_CONCERT_MISMATCH`. The **section-venue match** requires the chosen `seat_section_id` to belong to the hall of every concert in the list, since a subscriber holds the same seat all season; a mismatch is `ERR_SEAT_SECTION_VENUE_MISMATCH`. Finally, **seat availability** requires the `available_count` on the relevant inventory element of *every* concert in the list to be at least `seat_count`; a shortfall on any one of them refuses the whole package with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS`.

Pricing a package happens in four steps and the rounding order is pinned. Step one fixes the per-line unit price, `locked_unit_price_cents`: for a `NEW` package it is the season's `base_ticket_price_cents` multiplied by the section's `price_multiplier_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent. Step two sums the **already-rounded** lines: taking the concerts in ascending order of `performance_start_utc` with ties broken by ascending concert `created_at`, each concert contributes a line total of `locked_unit_price_cents` multiplied by `seat_count`, and those line totals are added to produce a subtotal. Rounding happens per concert line, not once over a raw sum, and the difference is real money. Step three applies the **package discount**, whose tier is keyed on how many concerts the package contains: a package of 8 or more concerts earns 1500 basis points off, one of 5 to 7 concerts earns 1000 basis points, one of 3 or 4 concerts earns 500 basis points, and one of fewer than 3 concerts earns nothing. The subtotal is multiplied by 10000 less that discount, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up. Step four is the loyalty discount, which a `NEW` package never receives — for a `NEW` package `price_cents` is simply the step-three result.

Paying a package moves it from `PENDING_PAYMENT` to `ACTIVE`; a package in any other status is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_PENDING`. Payment converts the claim from held to sold: on every concert in `concert_ids`, the package's `seat_count` is subtracted from `held_count` and added to `sold_count` on the inventory element for the package's `seat_section_id`. The patron's `lifetime_cash_in_cents` is increased by the package's `price_cents`. The patron's `consecutive_renewal_years` is then set — to exactly 1 when the package's `package_type` is `NEW`, or incremented by 1 when it is `RENEWAL` — and their `renewal_priority_score` is recomputed and persisted from the new figure. A package also publishes `fulfilled_concert_count`, the number of its `concert_ids` whose concert has a `performance_status` other than `CANCELLED`; it is computed the moment the package is created — new or renewal — and refreshed only when a concert it contains is cancelled.

An unpaid package does not survive the window. When the season leaves `RENEWAL_WINDOW`, and at any ops-run checkpoint taken after the season's `renewal_deadline_utc` has passed on the logical clock, packages still in `PENDING_PAYMENT` move to `LAPSED` and release every seat they were holding. A `LAPSED` package is terminal, contributes nothing to inventory, and is never revived.

### 3.6. Renewal Chains and Seat Priority

Renewal is how a subscriber carries a seat from one season into the next, and it deliberately creates a chain rather than editing anything in place. Renewing an `ACTIVE` package mints a **new** package whose `package_type` is `RENEWAL`, whose `prior_package_id` points at the package being renewed, and whose `seat_section_id` is inherited from that prior package rather than supplied by the caller. The new package opens in `PENDING_PAYMENT`. The prior package stays `ACTIVE` and untouched apart from its `version`, which is incremented by one.

The request supplies the `season_id` of the season being renewed into, the `concert_ids` for the new season's package, and the mandatory `expected_package_version` described in §2.12. `seat_count` is optional: when supplied it sets the new package's seat count, and when omitted the prior package's seat count carries over. Either a `BoxOfficeAgent` or the owning `Patron` may renew, with the same ownership rule as for a new build — the check comparing the **prior** package's `patron_id` to `X-User-ID`, since the renewal request carries no `patron_id` of its own.

Four gates guard the renewal beyond the ordinary ladder. The package being renewed must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The **target** season — the one being renewed into — must be in `RENEWAL_WINDOW`, else `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`; note that this is the destination season's status, not the prior package's season. The logical clock must be at or before that target season's `renewal_deadline_utc`, and an instant exactly equal to the deadline still qualifies; past it the renewal is `ERR_RENEWAL_DEADLINE_PASSED`. And the **renewal priority gate**: the patron's `renewal_priority_score`, freshly recomputed at this moment rather than read from storage — and persisted onto the patron there and then, so the refreshed score survives even when the gate that follows refuses the renewal — must be at least the `min_renewal_priority_score` of the prior package's seat section. A score exactly equal to the threshold passes; a lower one is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_PRIORITY_INSUFFICIENT`. This is how the orchestra rations its most desirable rooms year over year: the section's threshold rises, and only subscribers whose loyalty, giving and seniority have kept pace hold on to the seat.

Renewals do not re-run the donor seating benefit check — the seat is inherited, not chosen, so a `FRIEND` who was seated in a rank-1 section as a `BENEFACTOR` years ago may keep renewing it provided the priority gate is satisfied. Package membership and seat availability, however, apply exactly as they do to a new build: `concert_ids` must be non-empty, duplicate-free and drawn from programmed or locked concerts of the target season, and every one of those concerts must have enough `available_count` in the inherited section for the renewal's seat count. The section-venue match is not among a renewal's gates either, so a target-season concert played in a hall that does not contain the inherited section simply carries no inventory element for it, counts as zero available seats, and is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS` rather than `ERR_SEAT_SECTION_VENUE_MISMATCH`.

Pricing a renewal follows the same four steps as §3.5 with two substitutions. In step one, `locked_unit_price_cents` is **not** recomputed from the new season's base price; it is copied verbatim from the prior package's `locked_unit_price_cents`. That is the price lock a subscriber renews for. Steps two and three are identical — round each concert line, sum the rounded lines, then apply the package discount tier keyed on the number of concerts. Step four then adds the **loyalty discount** that a new package never receives. The loyalty rate in basis points is the **target** season's `loyalty_discount_bp` — the season being renewed into, not the prior package's — plus 100 basis points for each of the patron's `consecutive_renewal_years` as they stand at the moment of renewal, capped at 2000 basis points — a twenty percent ceiling no length of subscription can exceed. `price_cents` is the step-three figure multiplied by 10000 less that loyalty rate, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up. The consequence is the one the orchestra advertises: for an identical concert set, section and seat count, a renewal costs less than a fresh purchase whenever the base price has moved between seasons, and never costs more.

Creating a renewal holds seats immediately: on every concert in the new `concert_ids`, the seat count is added to `held_count` on the inherited section's inventory element, exactly as a new package does. Those held seats convert to sold when the renewal is paid, and lapse if it is not.

### 3.7. Public On-Sale: Demand Pricing, Holds and Settlement

The season moves from `RENEWAL_WINDOW` to `SINGLE_ON_SALE` on an `ArtisticAdmin` advance naming `SINGLE_ON_SALE` as the `target_status`. Two things must hold: the season is currently in `RENEWAL_WINDOW`, and the logical clock has reached or passed the season's `renewal_deadline_utc` — an earlier instant refuses the advance with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. The advance then runs a three-hop cascade. In hop one, every concert of the season whose `sale_status` is `RENEWAL_ONLY` moves to `PUBLIC_ONSALE`, and in parallel every subscription package of the season still in `PENDING_PAYMENT` moves to `LAPSED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Hop two, caused by hop one: each package entering `LAPSED` releases the seats it was holding — for every concert in its `concert_ids`, its `seat_count` is subtracted from `held_count` on the inventory element for its `seat_section_id`. Hop three, caused by hop two: each concert so touched recomputes `available_count`, `fill_bp` and `demand_band` on that element and refreshes its `seats_held_count`. Finally, every concert of the season recomputes its `readiness_posture`, which is where a concert still carrying an `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` contract turns `BLOCKED` — the orchestra is now selling seats to a programme whose soloist has not signed. The season's `version` increments by one.

**Single-ticket pricing** responds to how full the house already is, and every step of it is pinned. The inputs are exactly one inventory element — the one whose `seat_section_id` matches the requested section on the requested concert — together with the season's `base_ticket_price_cents`, the section's `price_multiplier_bp`, and optionally the patron's `donor_level`. Nothing else participates; subscription seats already counted into `held_count` and `sold_count` do participate, because they are part of that element.

The occupancy figure comes first. Seats taken is `held_count` plus `sold_count` on that element. `fill_bp` is seats taken multiplied by 10,000, divided by the element's `capacity`, and truncated downward to a whole number of basis points; when no seats are taken it is 0. The `demand_band` follows from `fill_bp` by inclusive thresholds evaluated from the top: at least 8000 basis points — eighty percent full — is `PEAK`; otherwise at least 5000 is `HIGH`; otherwise at least 2000 is `MODERATE`; otherwise `LOW`. Each band carries a multiplier in basis points: `LOW` discounts to 9000, `MODERATE` leaves the price alone at 10000, `HIGH` lifts it to 11500 and `PEAK` to 13000.

The price is then built in four rounded steps and clamped. The **raw unit** is the season's `base_ticket_price_cents` multiplied by the section's `price_multiplier_bp`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up — the section multiplier is applied and rounded *first*, before anything else touches the number. The **demand-adjusted price** is that raw unit multiplied by the band's multiplier, divided by 10,000, rounded half up. The **donor-adjusted price** is the demand-adjusted price multiplied by 10000 less the patron's donor discount, divided by 10,000, rounded half up; the discount in basis points is 0 for `NONE`, 250 for `FRIEND`, 500 for `SUSTAINER`, 1000 for `BENEFACTOR` and 1500 for `LAUREATE`, and it is 0 whenever no patron is identified at all. Finally the result is clamped against a floor and a ceiling derived from the raw unit and nothing else: the floor is the raw unit multiplied by 8500, divided by 10,000, rounded half up — eighty-five percent of the section price — and the ceiling is the raw unit multiplied by 12000, divided by 10,000, rounded half up — one hundred and twenty percent of it. The `unit_price_cents` charged is the donor-adjusted price raised to the floor if it fell below it, then lowered to the ceiling if it rose above it; the clamp is applied last, after every other step. `amount_due_cents` is `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the seat count.

A computed read available to a `BoxOfficeAgent` or a `Patron` returns this price without committing to anything. It takes, as query parameters, the `seat_section_id` to be priced — required, and `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when absent or empty — an optional `patron_id` whose `donor_level` personalises the donor discount, and an optional `seat_count` which is treated as 1 when omitted and must otherwise be an integer of at least 1. A `seat_section_id` or `patron_id` that resolves to nothing is `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PATRON_NOT_FOUND` respectively, checked in that order. The response reports `unit_price_cents`, `demand_band`, `fill_bp`, `donor_discount_bp`, `seat_count` and `amount_due_cents`. This variant is purely read-only: it never consults availability and never mutates inventory, so quoting 999 seats against a section that holds 100 still returns a price rather than an error. It does not run the section-venue match either: a section belonging to some other hall has no inventory element on this concert, so it is priced as a completely empty house — zero held, zero sold — against that section's own `capacity` and `price_multiplier_bp`.

Holding tickets is the committing variant. A `BoxOfficeAgent`, or a `Patron` whose `X-User-ID` matches, creates a **TicketOrder** by supplying the `concert_id`, the `patron_id` the seats are for, the `seat_section_id`, a `seat_count` of at least 1, and the mandatory `expected_concert_version` of §2.12. The concert's `sale_status` must be `PUBLIC_ONSALE`; a concert not yet or no longer publicly on sale is refused with `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_ON_SALE`. The chosen section must belong to the concert's hall, else `ERR_SEAT_SECTION_VENUE_MISMATCH`, and the element's `available_count` must be at least `seat_count`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS`. The order opens in `HELD`. The same arithmetic described above runs against the inventory **as it stands before this order's seats are held**, and its results are frozen onto the order as `quoted_unit_price_cents`, `amount_due_cents` and `demand_band_at_hold`; later demand movement never re-prices an existing hold. The seats are then added to `held_count`, and `available_count`, `fill_bp`, `demand_band` and `seats_held_count` are refreshed. The order's `hold_expires_at_ops_run` is set to the season's current `ops_run_index` plus 2, and the concert's `version` is incremented by one — which is precisely what makes a second hold created from the same observed version fail the concurrency check. `amount_paid_cents` and `credit_applied_cents` both start at 0.

Paying a held order moves it to `PAID`. It requires the order to be in `HELD`, else `ERR_TICKET_ORDER_NOT_HELD`, and it requires the concert's `performance_status` to still be `SCHEDULED` — an order against a cancelled concert cannot be paid and is refused with `ERR_CONCERT_ALREADY_CANCELLED`. One optional field, `use_credit`, decides whether house credit is spent: when it is true, `credit_applied_cents` is set to whichever is smaller of the patron's `credit_balance_cents` and the order's `amount_due_cents`; when it is false or absent, `credit_applied_cents` is 0. The patron's `credit_balance_cents` is then reduced by `credit_applied_cents`. `amount_paid_cents` is set to the full `amount_due_cents` regardless of how it was funded, and the patron's `lifetime_cash_in_cents` grows only by the cash portion — `amount_due_cents` less `credit_applied_cents` — which is exactly what keeps the identity of §2.10 balanced. On the inventory element the seats move from `held_count` to `sold_count`, and `fill_bp`, `demand_band`, `seats_held_count` and `seats_sold_count` are all recomputed.

Unpaid holds are swept at the **ops-run checkpoint**, which either an `ArtisticAdmin` or a `BoxOfficeAgent` may run against a season that has not yet reached `CLOSED`; running it on a closed season is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. The checkpoint increments the season's `ops_run_index` by one and then runs its own three-hop cascade. Hop one: every ticket order for a concert of this season that is still `HELD` and whose `hold_expires_at_ops_run` has been reached or passed by the new epoch value moves to `EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Hop two, caused by hop one: each expiring order releases its seats, subtracting its `seat_count` from `held_count` on its concert's element for its section. Hop three, caused by hop two: that concert recomputes `available_count`, `fill_bp`, `demand_band` and `seats_held_count`. Alongside this, every subscription package of the season still in `PENDING_PAYMENT` whose season's `renewal_deadline_utc` now lies strictly behind the logical clock moves to `LAPSED` and releases its held seats the same way, `renewal_priority_score` is recomputed and persisted for every patron holding a package in this season, and the season's `version` increments by one. The checkpoint never changes the season's `status`.

### 3.8. The Program Book: Advertising Bookings and Invoicing

Program book advertising is sold by `AdvertisingSales` and is the one revenue stream that touches neither seats nor patrons. An **AdBooking** is submitted against a `season_id` with an `advertiser_name`, an `ad_size` of `FULL_PAGE`, `HALF_PAGE` or `QUARTER_PAGE`, a `placement` of `BACK_COVER`, `INSIDE_FRONT_COVER` or `RUN_OF_BOOK`, and an `insertion_count` of at least 1 recording how many program books the advertisement runs in. The booking opens in `SUBMITTED` with `ad_subtotal_cents`, `volume_discount_bp`, `placement_fee_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` all null.

Deciding a booking carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. Either way the booking must currently be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_AD_BOOKING_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and the booking's season must not have reached `CLOSED`, else `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. A rejection moves the booking to `REJECTED` and computes nothing: all four invoice fields stay null.

An approval moves the booking to `APPROVED` and computes the invoice in a pinned order that resolves what looks at first like a circular dependency — the total is the discounted subtotal plus the placement fee, while the placement fee is itself a percentage of a discounted figure that depends on a tier. The order settles it. First, `ad_subtotal_cents` is the rate for the chosen size multiplied by `insertion_count`, where a full page is 120,000 cents per insertion, a half page 70,000 cents and a quarter page 40,000 cents. Second, `volume_discount_bp` is keyed on that **undiscounted** subtotal and never on any later figure: a subtotal of at least 500,000 cents earns 1200 basis points, otherwise at least 250,000 cents earns 800, otherwise at least 100,000 cents earns 400, otherwise nothing. Third, the discounted subtotal is `ad_subtotal_cents` multiplied by 10000 less `volume_discount_bp`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up. Fourth, `placement_fee_cents` is a percentage of that **discounted** subtotal rather than of `ad_subtotal_cents`: the discounted subtotal multiplied by the placement rate, divided by 10,000, rounded half up, where the back cover carries 2500 basis points, the inside front cover 1500, and run-of-book nothing at all. Fifth, `invoice_total_cents` is the discounted subtotal plus `placement_fee_cents`, with no further rounding. Each booking is priced entirely on its own; there is no aggregation of volume across a season's other bookings. Approval finally increases the season's `program_book_revenue_cents` by the new `invoice_total_cents`.

### 3.9. When Things Go Wrong: Substitution, Cancellation and Closing the Season

Soloists fall ill, and the orchestra substitutes rather than cancels. An `ArtisticAdmin` performs a substitution on a concert by naming the `contract_id` of the engagement being replaced together with a `replacement_artist_name` and a `replacement_fee_cents` figure, which must not be negative. The named contract must exist *and* belong to the concert named in the path — a contract of some other concert is reported as `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, exactly as an unknown identifier is — and it must be in `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED`; anything else is `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_NEGOTIABLE`. That contract moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and a fresh contract is created on the same concert in `OFFERED` with `negotiation_round` at 1, the same `role_type` as the contract it replaces, the `replacement_artist_name` as its `artist_name` and the `replacement_fee_cents` as its `offered_fee_cents` — the new offer is itself fully negotiable, so the replacement artist may counter it in turn. The concert's `substitution_count` increments by one, and if the concert was `LOCKED` it reverts to `PROGRAMMED`, because a contract that had been signed no longer is. The concert's `readiness_posture` is recomputed, and if the season has already reached `SINGLE_ON_SALE` or `IN_PROGRESS` that posture is now `BLOCKED` until the substitute signs. The response body is the newly minted replacement contract rather than the concert.

Cancelling a concert is the largest single operation in the system. An `ArtisticAdmin` performs it with a `reason` — a required, non-empty string, checked at the request-shape rung and therefore ahead of both gates below — on a concert whose `performance_status` is `SCHEDULED` — anything else is `ERR_CONCERT_ALREADY_CANCELLED` — and whose season has not yet reached `CLOSED`, else `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. The concert's `performance_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, its `sale_status` becomes `SALES_CLOSED`, and the season's `concerts_cancelled_count` increments by one. Two independent cascades then run.

**The personnel cascade** unwinds the orchestra's labour commitments. Hop one: every rehearsal service of this concert whose status is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` moves to `CANCELLED`, processed in ascending `scheduled_start_utc` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. Hop two, caused by hop one: every engagement of each service that just became `CANCELLED` whose status is `CONFIRMED` moves to `VOIDED`. Hop three, caused by hop two: each engagement entering `VOIDED` whose `provisional_pay_cents` is not null decrements its musician's `services_charged_ytd` by one and subtracts that same provisional figure from the musician's `ytd_earnings_cents`. An engagement whose service never reached publication carries a null `provisional_pay_cents`, was never charged, and so is voided without any ledger movement.

**The box-office cascade** unwinds the money. Hop one: every ticket order of this concert in `HELD` status moves to `EXPIRED`, and every ticket order in `PAID` status moves to `REFUNDED`. Hop two, caused by hop one: each order entering `REFUNDED` adds its `amount_paid_cents` to its patron's `credit_balance_cents` — the order's own `amount_paid_cents` is deliberately left untouched, so the audit trail survives — and each order entering `EXPIRED` or `REFUNDED` releases its seats on the concert's inventory, an expiring hold coming off `held_count` and a refunded order coming off `sold_count`. Hop three, caused by hop two: the concert recomputes `available_count`, `fill_bp`, `demand_band`, `seats_held_count` and `seats_sold_count`.

Two further effects sit outside both cascades. Every guest artist contract of this concert still in `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; contracts already `SIGNED` are left alone. And every subscription package whose `concert_ids` contain this concert recomputes its `fulfilled_concert_count`, which now excludes the cancelled date — but the package's `price_cents` is **not** reduced, because a cancelled concert is not removed from an existing package's price. What the cancellation explicitly does **not** change is the concert's `programming_status` or its `readiness_posture`: both are left exactly as they stood before the cancellation, so a cancelled concert that was `LOCKED` remains `LOCKED` and a posture of `BLOCKED` or `AT_RISK` survives the cancellation unchanged.

Concerts that do go ahead are settled. An `ArtisticAdmin` settles a concert whose `performance_status` is `SCHEDULED`, whose season has reached `IN_PROGRESS`, and every one of whose `PUBLISHED` services has already been clocked out to `COMPLETED`; a failure of any of those three conditions is reported as `ERR_CONCERT_NOT_SETTLEABLE`. Settlement moves `performance_status` to `PERFORMED`, drives `sale_status` to `SALES_CLOSED`, and freezes `gross_ticket_revenue_cents` as the sum of `amount_paid_cents` over that concert's ticket orders in `PAID` status.

The season's own last two advances are simple by comparison. Moving from `SINGLE_ON_SALE` to `IN_PROGRESS` requires only that the season currently be in `SINGLE_ON_SALE`, and increments its `version` by one; it is the state in which concerts may be settled. Moving from `IN_PROGRESS` to `CLOSED` requires that every concert of the season have reached a final `performance_status` of either `PERFORMED` or `CANCELLED` — a concert still `SCHEDULED` refuses the close with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. Closing drives every concert of the season to a `sale_status` of `SALES_CLOSED`, moves every subscription package of the season still in `ACTIVE` status to `EXPIRED`, and increments the season's `version` by one. `CLOSED` is terminal: the season accepts no further advance, no ops-run checkpoint, and no ad booking decision. Note that `EXPIRED` packages continue to count toward `sold_count` in the inventory identity of §2.9 and toward the cash identity of §2.10 — closing a season retires it, it does not erase its history.
