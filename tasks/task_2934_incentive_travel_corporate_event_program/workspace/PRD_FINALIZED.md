# Incentive Travel Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An incentive travel management company sells one promise to its corporate clients: that a sales force will chase a number all year because the reward at the end of it is a trip nobody wants to miss. Delivering on that promise is an unusually unforgiving piece of operations. The qualification rules must be settled before the selling season closes; destinations must be inspected and risk-scored before anyone commits; hotel and destination-management contracts must be negotiated round by round with attrition and force-majeure terms that decide how much money is exposed if the program is called off; winners must be adjudicated against a tier ladder and told what they have won; seats, guests, flights and on-site activities must be assembled against a fixed room block and a fixed budget; and when the last participant flies home, the taxable value of every award must be reported to the tax authority with a gross-up policy the client chose months earlier. Today that work lives in spreadsheets, and every one of those spreadsheets disagrees with the others.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that replaces them. It covers ten resource collections — programs, award tiers, qualifiers, destinations, supplier contracts, deposit milestones, registrations, on-site activities, activity signups and tax award reports — all mounted at the URL root, so that `/programs`, `/registrations` and `/tax-award-reports` are siblings rather than nested paths. Parent context always travels in the request body as a foreign key, never in the URL. The system is the arbiter of every number a client, a supplier or a tax authority will later see, which means the arithmetic in this document is normative: where a formula pins an evaluation order or a rounding step, that order and that step are part of the contract and a different-but-reasonable reading is a defect.

Two properties shape almost every rule that follows. First, money and capacity are **conserved**: once a program's budget rollup has run for the first time the committed budget plus the remaining headroom always equals the approved total, and the blocked room-nights always equal the confirmed room-nights plus the available room-nights, at every intermediate step and after every cancellation. Second, the high-value decisions are **segregated**: the user who proposed or countered a supplier contract may not be the user who executes it, and the user who filed a taxable award report may not be the user who voids it. Role separation alone is not sufficient for either rule — the acting user identity itself is compared.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If both headers are present but `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is valid but is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- **`ProgramDirector`** — owns program design, the award tier ladder, the gross-up policy decision, qualifier communication, lifecycle advancement, program cancellation and program measurement. Exclusively responsible for creating programs, advancing them, recording the gross-up policy decision, notifying qualifiers, cancelling programs and creating award tiers.
- **`SourcingManager`** — sources and inspects destinations, opens supplier contracts, runs the proposing side of negotiation, builds the deposit schedule and withdraws contracts that die before execution. This role cannot execute a contract.
- **`ContractApprover`** — counter-signs and executes supplier contracts and pays deposit milestones.
- **`SalesOpsAnalyst`** — loads qualifying-period sales results and adjudicates qualifiers against the tier ladder. This role cannot set budgets or advance the program.
- **`TravelDesk`** — runs the hospitality desk: seat confirmation, all air travel management, publishing and closing the on-site activity agenda, and the operational side of enrolments, signups and cancellations.
- **`TaxComplianceOfficer`** — owns taxable award reporting: drafting, filing and voiding reports.
- **`Participant`** — a qualifying salesperson acting on their own award: answering the invitation, enrolling themselves or a guest, choosing activities, cancelling and submitting the post-trip survey.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDirector`, `SourcingManager`, `ContractApprover`, `SalesOpsAnalyst`, `TravelDesk` or `TaxComplianceOfficer`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is, however, recorded wherever the specification says so — as `inspected_by_user_id` on a `Destination`, as `last_proposed_by_user_id`, `last_countered_by_user_id` or `executed_by_user_id` on a `SupplierContract`, and as `filed_by_user_id` or `voided_by_user_id` on a `TaxAwardReport` — and those recorded values are then compared against a later actor's `X-User-ID` to enforce segregation of duties (§2.9).

`Participant` is the one entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Qualifier` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Qualifier` compare that resource's qualifier reference to `X-User-ID`: a `Registration` is owned by the qualifier named in its `qualifier_id`, and an `ActivitySignup` is owned by the qualifier behind the registration named in its `registration_id`. A `Participant` whose `X-User-ID` does not match the owning qualifier is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This check guards nine operations: creating a registration, responding to an invitation, cancelling a registration, submitting a survey, creating an activity signup, cancelling an activity signup, reading a single registration, reading a single qualifier, and reading a single activity signup. Participants are deliberately excluded from the collection-level list of programs, qualifiers, destinations, contracts, milestones and tax reports; where a `Participant` may read a list at all (award tiers, on-site activities, registrations, activity signups), the ownership rule still governs the single-row reads.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional pagination parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and has a minimum of 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer, is below 1 or is above 100, and an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses always return their rows sorted by `created_at` ascending, so that a page boundary is stable across repeated reads. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for the request (defaults included), `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` as true when the resolved `offset` plus the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`.

Filters are combined with logical AND, and any filter that is omitted is not applied. Programs may be filtered by `fiscal_year` and `status`. Award tiers may be filtered by `program_id` and `tier_code`. Qualifiers may be filtered by `program_id`, `assigned_tier_id`, `award_status`, `tax_status` and `employee_code`. Destinations may be filtered by `program_id`, `status` and `inspection_status`. Supplier contracts may be filtered by `program_id`, `destination_id`, `supplier_kind` and `status`. Deposit milestones may be filtered by `contract_id`, `status` and `requires_finance_review`. Registrations may be filtered by `program_id`, `qualifier_id`, `guest_of_registration_id`, `registration_kind`, `attendance_status` and `air_status`. On-site activities may be filtered by `program_id`, `status` and `risk_level`. Activity signups may be filtered by `activity_id`, `registration_id` and `status`. Tax award reports may be filtered by `qualifier_id`, `tax_year`, `status` and `gross_up_method`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix; a client-supplied `id` is never honoured. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded when the row is first persisted and never changed afterwards.

Nine of the ten entities — `Program`, `Qualifier`, `Destination`, `SupplierContract`, `DepositMilestone`, `Registration`, `OnsiteActivity`, `ActivitySignup` and `TaxAwardReport` — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation came from a direct request or from a cascade triggered elsewhere. `AwardTier` is the single exception: a tier is immutable once created, has no update or state-change operation of any kind, and therefore carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from an `AwardTier` payload entirely.

Because `version` is the comparand for the stale-version guards described in §2.7, its increments matter to clients. Every cascade step listed in §3 that says a row moves state, gains a stamp or has a computed figure re-derived is a mutation of that row and increments that row's `version` by 1. When one request mutates a row twice — for example a registration that is confirmed and, in the same cascade, has its room-nights stamped — the row's `version` advances once per distinct mutation the specification names.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Deposit maturity, notification stamps, inspection stamps, waitlist promotion stamps and the cancellation-exposure waterfall all read a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. The clock starts at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` at server start and must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability sets it: the caller supplies `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant, and the response body reports the new setting as `current_utc`. Setting the clock changes no business state whatsoever; every subsequent logical-clock read simply returns the supplied instant. All six operator roles — `ProgramDirector`, `SourcingManager`, `ContractApprover`, `SalesOpsAnalyst`, `TravelDesk` and `TaxComplianceOfficer` — may set the clock; `Participant` may not.

The fields written from the logical clock are `notified_at` on a `Qualifier`, `inspected_at` on a `Destination`, `executed_at` on a `SupplierContract`, `paid_at` on a `DepositMilestone`, `promoted_at` on an `ActivitySignup`, and `filed_at` on a `TaxAwardReport`. The `created_at` / `updated_at` audit pair of §2.4 is independent of the logical clock.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in **whole cents**; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a request or a response. All rates are integers denominated in **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent — so a `service_load_bp` of 1275 is 12.75 percent, and an `attrition_allowance_bp` of 2000 is 20 percent.

Wherever a monetary result is derived from a rate, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero. Two rounding rules that are not half-up appear deliberately and are called out where they are used: the budget utilization figure uses integer floor division, so the 100-percent mark is reached only at or beyond exact parity; and the attrition floor uses a ceiling, so a fractional room-night floor always rounds up against the buyer.

The single most consequential precision rule in this specification is that **rounding happens where the formula says it happens**. Several formulas round intermediate components to whole cents before summing them, and combining those components into one composite rate and rounding once at the end produces a different — and incorrect — number. Each such formula states its pinned evaluation order explicitly in §3.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Seat and Slot Creation

Two creation endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`, because both of them are legitimately re-issuable operations that the state machines alone would happily accept a second time on identical state.

A request to `POST /registrations` must carry `expected_program_version` as a required, non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `Program` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The travel desk may legitimately enrol the same qualifier again after an earlier seat was cancelled, so nothing in the registration state machine would stop a second create; the guard exists so that a program-level decision the client never saw — a gross-up policy lock, a budget re-approval, the registration window closing — cannot be silently superseded. The request must carry the version the client actually read.

A request to `POST /activity-signups` must carry `expected_activity_version` as a required, non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `OnsiteActivity` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the activity's current `version`, the signup is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACTIVITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Signing a different registration up for the same activity is a legitimate operation on identical state, but the seat-versus-waitlist decision the client is acting on — `booked_count` measured against `capacity`, as read from the activity — may already have been invalidated by an intervening promotion, cancellation or capacity-consuming signup. The guard makes the client's read of the seat count part of the request's precondition.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. In particular, none of the action endpoints on programs, qualifiers, destinations, contracts, milestones, registrations, activities, signups or tax reports accepts an expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Validation Order and Error Precedence

When a single request would fail more than one check, the check with the lowest priority number below wins and its error is the one returned. The ordering is normative — an implementation that reports a budget failure on a request that also has a bad role has reported the wrong error. The full ladder, in evaluation order:

1. **Authentication headers** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, HTTP 401.
2. **Role recognition** — `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`, HTTP 401.
3. **Role authorization** — the supplied role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, HTTP 403.
4. **Path existence** — every templated id in the URL resolves to an existing row, validated strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable segment decides the error. Applies to every endpoint with a templated path segment. Violation: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` for `program_id`, `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND` for `tier_id`, `ERR_QUALIFIER_NOT_FOUND` for `qualifier_id`, `ERR_DESTINATION_NOT_FOUND` for `destination_id`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` for `contract_id`, `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND` for `milestone_id`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` for `registration_id`, `ERR_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND` for `activity_id`, `ERR_SIGNUP_NOT_FOUND` for `signup_id`, `ERR_TAX_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` for `report_id` — all HTTP 404.
5. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the order the fields are listed in that endpoint's input. Applies to `POST /award-tiers`, `POST /qualifiers`, `POST /destinations`, `POST /supplier-contracts`, `POST /deposit-milestones`, `POST /registrations`, `POST /onsite-activities`, `POST /activity-signups` and `POST /tax-award-reports`. Violation: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALIFIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESTINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate — all HTTP 404. A `guest_of_registration_id` that names no registration is an `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` at this step, not a guest-link failure.
6. **Participant ownership** — when the role is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the `Qualifier` that owns the target resource. Applies to `POST /registrations`, `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/respond`, `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/cancel`, `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/survey`, `POST /activity-signups`, `POST /activity-signups/{signup_id}/cancel`, `GET /registrations/{registration_id}`, `GET /qualifiers/{qualifier_id}` and `GET /activity-signups/{signup_id}`. Violation: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, HTTP 403.
7. **Request schema** — all required fields present, correct JSON types, enum values inside their declared sets and integers inside their declared ranges. An unknown field carrying a value the endpoint does not accept is ignored, not rejected. Applies to all POST endpoints. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400.
8. **Pagination schema** — `limit` is an integer from 1 to 100 and `offset` is an integer of at least 0. Applies to all GET list endpoints. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400.
9. **Stale parent version** — `expected_program_version` equals the current `Program.version` on `POST /registrations`; `expected_activity_version` equals the current `OnsiteActivity.version` on `POST /activity-signups`. Violation: `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ACTIVITY_VERSION`, HTTP 412. Because this sits above every state and business check, a stale version is reported even when the request would also have failed a later gate.
10. **Program lifecycle gate** — the program's `status` permits the operation, and any requested advance is a declared edge out of the current status. Applies to every POST endpoint except `POST /programs` and `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. Violation: `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409.
11. **Target entity state gate** — the directly targeted row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, across all twelve state dimensions: the registration attendance axis and air axis, the qualifier award axis and tax axis, contract status, milestone status, activity status, signup status, report status, and destination selection status and inspection status. Applies to all action endpoints. Violation: `ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_AIR_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_QUALIFIER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MILESTONE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SIGNUP_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_TAX_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_DESTINATION_STATE_INVALID` — all HTTP 409.
12. **Cross-entity state guards** — a deposit milestone may only be paid when its supplier contract is `EXECUTED` (`ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`); a tax award report may only be drafted when the referenced qualifier's `award_status` is `TRAVELLED` (`ERR_QUALIFIER_STATE_INVALID`); a registration may only be confirmed when its program has exactly one executed hotel contract (`ERR_NO_EXECUTED_HOTEL_CONTRACT`); an activity signup requires the registration's `attendance_status` to be `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELLED` (`ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`) and the activity's `status` to be `OPEN` (`ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`). Applies to `POST /deposit-milestones/{milestone_id}/pay`, `POST /tax-award-reports`, `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/confirm` and `POST /activity-signups`. All HTTP 409.
13. **Gross-up policy lock** — `gross_up_policy_locked` is false when a gross-up policy decision is submitted. Applies to `POST /programs/{program_id}/gross-up-policy`. Violation: `ERR_GROSS_UP_POLICY_LOCKED`, HTTP 409.
14. **Contract segregation of duties** — the executing `X-User-ID` differs from both `last_proposed_by_user_id` and `last_countered_by_user_id`. Applies to `POST /supplier-contracts/{contract_id}/execute`. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 422.
15. **Tax void segregation of duties** — the voiding `X-User-ID` differs from the report's `filed_by_user_id`. Applies to `POST /tax-award-reports/{report_id}/void`. Violation: `ERR_TAX_VOID_SELF_REVIEW`, HTTP 422.
16. **Activity waiver** — a signup for an activity whose `risk_level` is `HIGH` requires the registration's `waiver_on_file` to be true. Applies to `POST /activity-signups`. Violation: `ERR_ACTIVITY_WAIVER_REQUIRED`, HTTP 422.
17. **Destination risk ceiling** — the destination's `risk_score` is at most 60 at shortlisting. Applies to `POST /destinations/{destination_id}/shortlist`. Violation: `ERR_DESTINATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`, HTTP 422.
18. **Inspection score floor** — the destination's `inspection_score` is at least 70 at shortlisting. Applies to `POST /destinations/{destination_id}/shortlist`. Violation: `ERR_INSPECTION_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, HTTP 422. Note the ordering against priority 17: a destination that is both too risky and under-inspected reports the risk failure.
19. **Negotiation round budget** — the contract's `round_count` is strictly less than 5 before a proposing or countering round is accepted. Applies to `POST /supplier-contracts/{contract_id}/negotiate`. Violation: `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 422.
20. **Guest allowance** — the qualifier's `guest_count` is at most the assigned tier's `guest_allowance` at review, and the count of non-`CANCELLED` guest registrations for a winner never exceeds that allowance. Applies to `POST /qualifiers/{qualifier_id}/review` and `POST /registrations`. Violation: `ERR_GUEST_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422.
21. **Guest linkage** — a registration whose `registration_kind` is `GUEST` supplies a `guest_of_registration_id` pointing at a non-`CANCELLED` `WINNER` registration of the same `qualifier_id`; a registration whose `registration_kind` is `WINNER` supplies no `guest_of_registration_id`, or supplies null. Applies to `POST /registrations`. Violation: `ERR_GUEST_LINK_INVALID`, HTTP 422. Guest allowance (priority 20) is checked before guest linkage.
22. **Uniqueness** — `employee_code` is unique per `program_id`; `tier_code` and `tier_rank` are each unique per `program_id`; `sequence` is unique per `contract_id`; at most one non-`CANCELLED` `WINNER` registration exists per `qualifier_id`; at most one non-`CANCELLED` signup exists per activity-and-registration pair. Applies to `POST /qualifiers`, `POST /award-tiers`, `POST /deposit-milestones`, `POST /registrations` and `POST /activity-signups`. Violation: `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_KEY`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_WINNER_REGISTRATION` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVITY_SIGNUP` — all HTTP 409.
23. **Ordered date pairs** — `sales_period_end` is strictly after `sales_period_start`, `end_at` is strictly after `start_at`, and `departure_date` is strictly after `arrival_date`. Applies to `POST /programs`, `POST /onsite-activities` and `POST /registrations`. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, HTTP 422.
24. **Activity schedule conflict** — the participant's existing booked and waitlisted activity intervals do not collide with the requested one once both are padded by 45 minutes on each side. Applies to `POST /activity-signups`. Violation: `ERR_ACTIVITY_TIME_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409.
25. **Deviation ceiling** — the pre-program and post-program deviation days sum to at most 7. Applies to `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/air`. Violation: `ERR_DEVIATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422.
26. **Stay window** — `arrival_date` is at or after the executed hotel contract's `stay_start_at` and `departure_date` is at or before its `stay_end_at`. Applies to `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/confirm`. Violation: `ERR_STAY_WINDOW_VIOLATION`, HTTP 422.
27. **Room block capacity** — per-night room occupancy across the half-open interval from `arrival_date` up to but excluding `departure_date`, plus this registration's `rooms`, stays at or below the executed hotel contract's `room_block_count`. Applies to `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/confirm`. Violation: `ERR_ROOM_BLOCK_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409. This is checked after the stay-window gate, so a registration outside the window reports the window failure.
28. **Deposit schedule ceiling** — the sum of `amount_cents` over the contract's non-`CANCELLED` deposit milestones is at most the contract value. Applies to `POST /deposit-milestones` and `POST /supplier-contracts/{contract_id}/execute`. Violation: `ERR_DEPOSIT_SCHEDULE_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT_VALUE`, HTTP 422. On the execute endpoint this money check sits below the segregation-of-duties check at priority 14, so a self-execution attempt on an over-committed schedule reports the duty violation.
29. **Tier budget ceiling** — the committed cost computed at review is at most the assigned tier's `budget_per_qualifier_cents`. Applies to `POST /qualifiers/{qualifier_id}/review`. Violation: `ERR_TIER_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422. This sits below the guest-allowance check at priority 20, so a qualifier who both brings too many guests and costs too much reports the guest failure.
30. **Budget headroom** — the program's `budget_headroom_cents` is at least the registration's `cost_cents` at confirmation. Applies to `POST /registrations/{registration_id}/confirm`. Violation: `ERR_BUDGET_HEADROOM_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422. Money is checked last on confirmation: stay window (26), then room block (27), then headroom (30).
31. **Cross-aggregate scope agreement** — a contract's `destination_id` belongs to the same program as its `program_id`; a signup's activity and registration belong to the same program; a tax report's `tax_year` equals the `fiscal_year` of the program behind the referenced qualifier. Applies to `POST /supplier-contracts`, `POST /activity-signups` and `POST /tax-award-reports`. Violation: `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422.
32. **Tax reporting completeness** — every qualifier of the program whose `taxable_award_cents` is greater than 0 has `tax_status` equal to `REPORTED` before the program may reach `CLOSED`. Applies to `POST /programs/{program_id}/advance`. Violation: `ERR_TAX_REPORTING_INCOMPLETE`, HTTP 422.
33. **Advance prerequisites** — the requested program advance, already established as a declared edge at priority 10, satisfies its structural prerequisites. Applies to `POST /programs/{program_id}/advance`. Violation: `ERR_ADVANCE_PREREQUISITES_UNMET`, HTTP 422. The prerequisites per edge are stated in §3.1 and §3.9.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two operations in this system are protected by a duty-separation rule that compares user identities rather than roles. A `SupplierContract` may only be executed by a `ContractApprover` whose `X-User-ID` differs from both `last_proposed_by_user_id` and `last_countered_by_user_id` on that contract; if it matches either, the attempt fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422). Because `ContractApprover` is also one of the two roles permitted to run a countering round, the same human can easily end up on both sides of a contract — which is precisely what this rule forbids. A `TaxAwardReport` may only be voided by a `TaxComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the report's `filed_by_user_id`; if it matches, the attempt fails with `ERR_TAX_VOID_SELF_REVIEW` (HTTP 422).

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

A great deal of this system's behaviour is cascading: one accepted request can move rows across four or five entities. Three principles govern all of it.

Cascades are **ordered and total**. Where §3 lists the steps of a cascade, they are applied in the listed order, and every step that matches its selection criteria is applied — a cascade never stops early because one of its steps found nothing to do. Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as a direct mutation would.

Aggregates are **recomputed, never patched**. `committed_budget_cents`, `budget_headroom_cents`, `budget_posture`, `cancellation_exposure_cents`, `reported_award_total_cents`, `qualified_count`, `travelled_count`, `room_nights_confirmed`, `room_nights_available`, `attrition_posture`, `booked_count` and `waitlist_count` are all defined as functions of the current row set, and are re-derived from that row set whenever their inputs change rather than being incrementally adjusted. This is what makes the reversal paths exact.

Two quantities are **conserved at every step**. From the first time a program's budget rollup actually runs — the arming moment fixed by §3.1, whose triggers are the ones listed in §3.7 — that program's `committed_budget_cents` plus its `budget_headroom_cents` always equals its `budget_total_cents`, even when the headroom is negative; before that first rollup this budget identity is not yet in force and the charter's seeded zeros of §3.1 are what the program reports. An executed hotel contract's `room_nights_blocked` always equals its `room_nights_confirmed` plus its `room_nights_available`. Every compensating path in §3 — cancelling a registration, voiding a filed tax report, reversing a confirmation — is specified so that these two identities hold again once the compensation has run.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Cross-Program Scope

Because every collection is mounted at the URL root, a request never nests one resource id inside another's path. Path ids are nevertheless resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable id decides the 404 error code; a request that names both a nonexistent program and a nonexistent qualifier reports the program failure if the program segment appears first.

Cross-resource agreement is checked in the body instead. A supplier contract's `destination_id` must name a destination belonging to the same program as the contract's `program_id`. An activity signup's activity and registration must belong to the same program. A tax award report's `tax_year` must equal the `fiscal_year` of the program behind the referenced qualifier. All three failures share `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A `Registration` never accepts a `program_id` from the client at all: the field is denormalised from the referenced qualifier's `program_id` at creation, and any client-supplied value is ignored.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Design, Budget and the Award Tier Ladder

Everything begins with a `ProgramDirector` chartering a program. The charter carries a `name` (non-empty, at most 160 characters, for example "FY26 Presidents Circle"), a `fiscal_year` in the range 2000 to 2100 inclusive, a `qualification_metric` naming which sales measure the whole tier ladder will be expressed in — one of `NET_REVENUE_CENTS`, `UNITS_SOLD` or `MARGIN_CENTS` — the inclusive bounds `sales_period_start` and `sales_period_end` of the qualifying selling period as ISO 8601 UTC instants, and a `budget_total_cents` of at least 0 whole cents. The closing instant must be strictly after the opening instant; a period that ends at or before it starts is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.

A newly chartered program lands in `DESIGN` with `gross_up_policy` set to `NONE`, `gross_up_policy_locked` false, `budget_posture` `OK`, and every computed money and count field at 0 — that is `per_person_cost_basis_cents`, `committed_budget_cents`, `budget_headroom_cents`, `cancellation_exposure_cents`, `reported_award_total_cents`, `qualified_count` and `travelled_count`. Both measurement outputs, `sales_lift_pct` and `satisfaction_index`, start null, as does `cancellation_reason`. The budget identity described in §2.10 takes hold the first time the budget rollup runs; from that moment on, `committed_budget_cents` plus `budget_headroom_cents` equals `budget_total_cents` after every step. Until that first rollup, `committed_budget_cents`, `budget_headroom_cents` and `budget_posture` stay at their seeded 0, 0 and `OK` however large `budget_total_cents` is — the creation response itself already reports a `budget_headroom_cents` of 0 rather than the full budget — and reading the program does not arm the identity or re-derive those three fields; only the rollup events listed in §3.7 do.

The **award tier ladder** is the program's price list for winning. A `ProgramDirector` appends each rung to it with a payload naming the owning `program_id`, a `tier_code` of at most 40 characters such as "PLATINUM", and a `tier_rank` of at least 1 where rank 1 is the most senior tier. Both `tier_code` and `tier_rank` are unique within a program, and a collision on either reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_KEY`. Each rung then carries the commercial terms: a `qualification_threshold` of at least 0, expressed in the units of the program's chosen `qualification_metric`, that a salesperson must reach to clear the rung; a `budget_per_qualifier_cents` of at least 0 that caps what the program will commit per winner at that rung; a `guest_allowance` from 0 to 2 (defaulting to 0 when omitted) bounding how many companions a winner may bring; an `air_cabin_class` of `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM_ECONOMY` or `BUSINESS` that the rung entitles the winner to and that becomes the baseline for upgrade billing; an `upgrade_allowance_cents` of at least 0 (defaulting to 0) that the program will absorb before a winner is billed for a better cabin; and a `service_load_bp` from 0 to 5000 basis points (defaulting to 0) of service load added onto the destination's per-person cost for that rung. A tier is **immutable**: there is no operation that edits or retires one, which is why an `AwardTier` payload has no `version` and no `updated_at` at all.

Before registration can open, the client must settle the taxable-award **gross-up policy**. A `ProgramDirector` records the decision by supplying `gross_up_policy` as `NONE`, `FLAT_SUPPLEMENTAL` or `FULL_GROSS_UP`; the value is stored and `gross_up_policy_locked` is set to true in the same step, and the program's `version` increments. The decision is accepted exactly once — a second submission, whatever value it carries, is rejected with `ERR_GROSS_UP_POLICY_LOCKED`. Locking early is deliberate: the policy is snapshotted onto every tax report drafted months later, and a mid-flight change would make two winners of the same program incomparable.

Lifecycle advancement is a `ProgramDirector` capability that supplies the desired `to_status` and moves the program along exactly one declared edge. The eight statuses are `DESIGN`, `SOURCING`, `CONTRACTED`, `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, `ONSITE`, `RECONCILED`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. The declared director-driven edges are `DESIGN → SOURCING`, `CONTRACTED → REGISTRATION_OPEN`, `REGISTRATION_OPEN → ONSITE`, `ONSITE → RECONCILED` and `RECONCILED → CLOSED`. A requested `to_status` that is not a declared edge out of the current status — including any attempt to advance a terminal program — is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. `SOURCING → CONTRACTED` is a **system-driven** edge only: it happens as part of the contract-execution cascade of §3.4 and can never be requested directly, so an advance naming `CONTRACTED` as its `to_status` also reports `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`.

Each director-driven edge has structural prerequisites, and an edge that is declared but whose prerequisites are unmet reports `ERR_ADVANCE_PREREQUISITES_UNMET`. Moving into `SOURCING` requires at least one award tier to exist for the program and at least one of its destinations to be `SHORTLISTED` — sourcing cannot begin until both the tier ladder and an inspected, shortlisted destination exist. Moving into `REGISTRATION_OPEN` requires that no qualifier of the program is still `PENDING_REVIEW` and that `gross_up_policy_locked` is true. Moving into `ONSITE` requires at least one registration of the program to be `CONFIRMED`. Moving into `RECONCILED` requires that no on-site activity of the program is still `OPEN`; open activities block reconciliation, and every activity must have been closed or cancelled first. Moving into `CLOSED` requires that every qualifier of the program whose `taxable_award_cents` is greater than 0 carries `tax_status` `REPORTED`, and a program with an unreported taxable award reports `ERR_TAX_REPORTING_INCOMPLETE` rather than the generic prerequisite error. Every accepted advance increments the program's `version` by 1.

Two of these advances carry cascades of their own. `REGISTRATION_OPEN → ONSITE` closes the registration window: every registration of the program still sitting at `INVITED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `cancellation_reason` set to the literal string `REGISTRATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and each such row's `version` increments. The much larger `ONSITE → RECONCILED` cascade is specified in §3.9.

### 3.2. Qualifier Review: Tier Assignment and Award Commitment

A `SalesOpsAnalyst` loads each salesperson's qualifying-period result as a qualifier row, naming the `program_id` it competed in, the employer-assigned `employee_code` (non-empty, at most 40 characters, unique within the program — a repeat reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_CODE`), the salesperson's `full_name` (non-empty, at most 160 characters), a `baseline_metric` of at least 0 holding the prior-period value, an `actual_metric` of at least 0 holding the qualifying-period value, and an optional `guest_count` from 0 to 2 that defaults to 0. Loading is permitted while the program is in `DESIGN`, `SOURCING` or `CONTRACTED`; anywhere else the program gate reports `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. A new row lands with `award_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, `tax_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, a null `assigned_tier_id`, `committed_cost_cents` and `taxable_award_cents` both 0, and a null `notified_at`.

Each qualifier carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The award track, `award_status`, runs through `PENDING_REVIEW`, `QUALIFIED`, `DISQUALIFIED`, `NOTIFIED`, `REGISTERED`, `TRAVELLED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `DISQUALIFIED`, `TRAVELLED` and `WITHDRAWN` terminal. The tax track, `tax_status`, runs through `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `PENDING_REPORT`, `REPORTED` and `VOIDED` and has no terminal state at all, because a voided filing can be corrected. The two dimensions evolve independently; nothing about a move on one axis implies a move on the other except where §3.9 says so explicitly.

**Adjudication** is the analyst's single most consequential action, and it is one operation with two outcomes. It is permitted only while the program is in `DESIGN`, `SOURCING` or `CONTRACTED` and only from `PENDING_REVIEW`; a qualifier that has already been adjudicated reports `ERR_QUALIFIER_STATE_INVALID`. The adjudication first builds the **eligible tier pool**: every award tier of the qualifier's program whose `qualification_threshold` is less than or equal to the qualifier's `actual_metric`. The comparison is inclusive — a salesperson landing exactly on a threshold clears that rung.

If the eligible pool is empty, because the `actual_metric` fell below every rung on the ladder, the qualifier moves `PENDING_REVIEW → DISQUALIFIED`, `assigned_tier_id` stays null and `committed_cost_cents` stays 0. If the pool is non-empty, the winner is the **richest rung actually cleared**: order the pool by `qualification_threshold` descending and, among equal thresholds, by `tier_rank` ascending, then take the first entry. Because `tier_rank` is unique within a program, that pair is a total order and no further tie-break is needed — and in particular no tie-break may fall back on a row `id`. The chosen rung is stamped onto `assigned_tier_id`, `committed_cost_cents` is stamped from the award-commitment computation below, and the qualifier moves `PENDING_REVIEW → QUALIFIED`. Either outcome increments the qualifier's `version` by 1; the qualifying outcome additionally re-derives the program's `qualified_count` and re-runs the budget rollup of §3.7.

The program's `qualified_count` is the number of its qualifiers whose `award_status` is `QUALIFIED`, `NOTIFIED`, `REGISTERED` or `TRAVELLED`; its `travelled_count` is the number whose `award_status` is exactly `TRAVELLED`.

The **award commitment** for a qualifier is built from three independently rounded components plus the tier's upgrade allowance. Its per-person basis is the program's `per_person_cost_basis_cents`, which is 0 until a hotel contract has been executed (§3.4) and is the selected destination's per-person land cost thereafter. Working from the qualifier's assigned tier:

- The **winner component** is the per-person basis multiplied by the sum of 10000 and the tier's `service_load_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. A tier with a `service_load_bp` of 1275 therefore loads the basis by 12.75 percent.
- The **guest component** is the per-person basis multiplied by 6250 and divided by 10000 — a guest is costed at 62.5 percent of a winner's land cost — rounded half up to a whole cent.
- The **gratuity component** is the per-person basis multiplied by 350 and divided by 10000, that is 3.5 percent of the basis, rounded half up to a whole cent.

`committed_cost_cents` is then the winner component, plus the guest component multiplied by the qualifier's `guest_count`, plus the gratuity component, plus the tier's `upgrade_allowance_cents`. **The evaluation order is pinned**: each of the three rate-driven components is rounded to a whole cent *before* anything is summed. Adding the three rates together into one composite basis-point figure and rounding once at the end yields a different number and is wrong — with a per-person basis of 431757 cents, a `service_load_bp` of 1275 and a `guest_count` of 1, the pinned order gives 921765 cents while the naive single-rounding gives 921766. Qualifiers whose `award_status` is `PENDING_REVIEW`, `DISQUALIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN` carry a `committed_cost_cents` of 0.

Two gates guard adjudication once the tier has been chosen, and they are evaluated in this order. First, the qualifier's `guest_count` must be at most the chosen tier's `guest_allowance`; a winner who intends to bring two companions to a rung that allows one is rejected with `ERR_GUEST_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. Second, the computed `committed_cost_cents` must be at most the chosen tier's `budget_per_qualifier_cents`; exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_TIER_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Neither rejection changes the qualifier, which stays `PENDING_REVIEW` and can be adjudicated again after the ladder or the guest count has been corrected.

### 3.3. Destination Sourcing, Site Inspection and Shortlisting

A `SourcingManager` builds a candidate list of destinations for a program, each carrying the owning `program_id`, a `city` name (non-empty, at most 120 characters), a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `per_person_cost_cents` of at least 0 holding the all-in land cost per participant, a `risk_score` from 0 to 100 expressing travel risk, and an `air_gateway_code` of exactly three uppercase letters naming the arrival gateway. A fresh candidate lands with `status` `CANDIDATE`, `inspection_status` `NOT_INSPECTED`, a null `inspection_score`, a null `inspected_by_user_id` and a null `inspected_at`.

A destination carries two state dimensions. Selection runs `CANDIDATE`, `SHORTLISTED`, `SELECTED` and `REJECTED`, with `SELECTED` and `REJECTED` terminal. Inspection runs `NOT_INSPECTED` to `INSPECTED` and stops there.

**Site inspection** is recorded once and only once. The sourcing manager supplies an `inspection_score` from 0 to 100; the destination moves `NOT_INSPECTED → INSPECTED`, the score is stored, `inspected_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, `inspected_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the destination's `version` increments. Inspection is permitted only while the destination is still a `CANDIDATE` and the program is in `DESIGN` or `SOURCING`. A second inspection of the same destination is rejected with `ERR_DESTINATION_STATE_INVALID` — the score is recorded once and is not revisable.

**Shortlisting** promotes an inspected candidate and takes no request body. It requires the program to be in `DESIGN` or `SOURCING`, the destination to still be a `CANDIDATE`, and its `inspection_status` to be `INSPECTED`; a destination that is not a candidate or has never been inspected reports `ERR_DESTINATION_STATE_INVALID`. Two commercial gates then apply. The destination's `risk_score` must be at most 60 — a score of exactly 60 passes, 61 does not, and exceeding the ceiling reports `ERR_DESTINATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`. Its `inspection_score` must be at least 70 — exactly 70 passes, 69 does not, and falling short reports `ERR_INSPECTION_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The risk ceiling is evaluated before the inspection floor, so a destination that is both too risky and under-inspected reports the risk failure. On success the destination moves `CANDIDATE → SHORTLISTED` and its `version` increments.

Neither `SELECTED` nor `REJECTED` is ever reached by a request. They are system outcomes of the contract-execution cascade described next: exactly one shortlisted destination becomes `SELECTED`, and every sibling of the same program still sitting at `CANDIDATE` or `SHORTLISTED` is turned down.

### 3.4. Supplier Negotiation, Execution and the Five-Level Cascade

Supplier contracting is where the program's cost basis is actually fixed, and it is deliberately modelled as a cycle rather than a straight line. A `SourcingManager` opens a contract with the owning `program_id`, the `destination_id` the supplier operates in, a `supplier_name` (non-empty, at most 160 characters), a `supplier_kind` of `HOTEL`, `DMC` or `AIR_CONSOLIDATOR`, an opening `room_block_count` of at least 1 room held per night, a `contracted_nights` count of at least 1, a `stay_start_at` instant marking the first contracted night (an ISO 8601 UTC instant at midnight), a `nightly_rate_cents` of at least 0 per room-night, an `attrition_allowance_bp` from 0 to 10000 basis points of blocked room-nights the supplier waives, a `force_majeure_clause` of `NONE`, `STANDARD` or `EXPANDED`, and an `exposure_basis` of `ATTRITION_BASIS` or `DEPOSIT_FORFEITURE_BASIS`. The named destination must belong to the named program, or the create fails with `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

A new contract lands in `DRAFT` with `round_count` 0, `room_nights_blocked`, `room_nights_confirmed` and `room_nights_available` all 0, `attrition_posture` `CLEAR`, and null values for `last_proposed_by_user_id`, `last_countered_by_user_id`, `executed_by_user_id`, `executed_at` and `withdrawal_reason`. The server computes `stay_end_at` as `stay_start_at` advanced by `contracted_nights` whole days; that instant is the **exclusive** upper bound of the stay window, meaning a registration must depart at or before it. Only `HOTEL` contracts drive destination selection and program advancement; `DMC` and `AIR_CONSOLIDATOR` contracts negotiate and execute on exactly the same machine but trigger neither.

**Negotiation** is one operation carrying an `action` of either `PROPOSE` or `COUNTER`, and it is the only place where a contract's commercial terms can change after creation. A `SourcingManager` proposing moves the contract `DRAFT → PROPOSED` or, closing the loop, `COUNTERED → PROPOSED`; a `ContractApprover` countering moves it `PROPOSED → COUNTERED`. Any other pairing of current status and action — countering a `DRAFT`, proposing from `PROPOSED`, negotiating an `EXECUTED` or `WITHDRAWN` contract — is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. Negotiation is permitted while the program is in `DESIGN`, `SOURCING` or `CONTRACTED`.

Each round may carry revised terms, and every supplied term **overwrites** the contract: `room_block_count`, `contracted_nights`, `stay_start_at`, `nightly_rate_cents`, `attrition_allowance_bp`, `force_majeure_clause` and `exposure_basis` are all optional on a negotiation round, and `stay_end_at` is recomputed from whatever `stay_start_at` and `contracted_nights` end up being. An optional free-text `note` may accompany the round for the negotiator's own record; it is accepted and ignored, and it appears nowhere on the contract payload. A proposing round stamps `last_proposed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`; a countering round stamps `last_countered_by_user_id`. Every accepted round increments `round_count` by 1 and the contract's `version` by 1.

The negotiation cycle terminates because `round_count` is monotonic and never resets. Five rounds is the ceiling: a proposing or countering round attempted while `round_count` has already reached 5 is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, so a contract can traverse `PROPOSED → COUNTERED → PROPOSED` at most until the budget is spent, and a deadlocked negotiation must be withdrawn rather than looped forever.

**Execution** is a `ContractApprover` capability with no request body, and it is the single most far-reaching operation in the system. It is only available from `PROPOSED` — executing a `DRAFT`, a `COUNTERED`, an already-`EXECUTED` or a `WITHDRAWN` contract reports `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the program is in `SOURCING` or `CONTRACTED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from both `last_proposed_by_user_id` and `last_countered_by_user_id`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; being a `ContractApprover` is not enough, because the same approver may well have countered this very contract. Finally, the sum of `amount_cents` over the contract's deposit milestones that are not `CANCELLED` must be at most the contract value — `room_block_count` multiplied by `contracted_nights` multiplied by `nightly_rate_cents` — and a schedule exceeding it reports `ERR_DEPOSIT_SCHEDULE_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT_VALUE`.

An accepted execution runs a cascade five levels deep, in this fixed order:

1. **The contract itself.** Status moves `PROPOSED → EXECUTED`; `executed_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID` and `executed_at` from the logical clock; `room_nights_blocked` is stamped as `room_block_count` multiplied by `contracted_nights`; the room-night ledger of §3.7 is run, filling `room_nights_confirmed`, `room_nights_available` and `attrition_posture`; the contract's `version` increments.
2. **The destination — hotel contracts only.** The referenced destination moves `SHORTLISTED → SELECTED` and its `version` increments.
3. **The losing destinations — hotel contracts only.** Every sibling destination of the same program whose `status` is `CANDIDATE` or `SHORTLISTED` moves to `REJECTED`, each with its own `version` increment.
4. **The program — hotel contracts only, and only when the program is in `SOURCING`.** The program moves `SOURCING → CONTRACTED`. Its `per_person_cost_basis_cents` is stamped from the newly `SELECTED` destination's `per_person_cost_cents`, which is the moment the award ladder stops being theoretical. Every qualifier of the program whose `award_status` is `QUALIFIED`, `NOTIFIED` or `REGISTERED` then has its `committed_cost_cents` recomputed against the now-known basis, the budget rollup of §3.7 is re-run to refresh `committed_budget_cents`, `budget_headroom_cents` and `budget_posture`, the cancellation-exposure projection of §3.5 is re-run into `cancellation_exposure_cents`, and the program's `version` increments. When the program is already `CONTRACTED` — a second hotel contract, or a `DMC` contract executed after the hotel one — this level is skipped entirely and the per-person basis is left as it stands.
5. **The deposit schedule.** If the rollup at level 4 left `budget_posture` at `OVERCOMMITTED`, every deposit milestone of *this* contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` has `requires_finance_review` set to true, each with a `version` increment. If the posture is `OK` or `WATCH`, no milestone is touched.

**Withdrawal** is the exit for a contract that dies before execution. A `SourcingManager` supplies a `withdrawal_reason`, and the contract moves to `WITHDRAWN` from `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`; the reason is stored, every deposit milestone of the contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its own `version` increment, and the contract's `version` increments. Withdrawal is permitted in every program status except `CLOSED`. An `EXECUTED` contract can never be withdrawn — that attempt reports `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.5. Deposit Schedules, Cancellation Exposure and Force-Majeure Relief

A supplier contract is paid down through an ordered **deposit schedule**. A `SourcingManager` appends each milestone with the owning `contract_id`, a 1-based `sequence` of at least 1 that fixes the milestone's position in the waterfall, a `due_at` instant at which the milestone matures, an `amount_cents` of at least 0, and a `refundable_bp` from 0 to 10000 basis points of that amount which is recoverable if the program is cancelled strictly before `due_at` — 10000 basis points meaning fully refundable, 0 meaning nothing comes back. The `sequence` is unique within a contract; a repeat reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE`. The cumulative `amount_cents` of the contract's non-`CANCELLED` milestones may not exceed the contract value of `room_block_count` multiplied by `contracted_nights` multiplied by `nightly_rate_cents`; a schedule summing to exactly the contract value is accepted, and one cent more reports `ERR_DEPOSIT_SCHEDULE_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT_VALUE`. A new milestone lands `SCHEDULED`, with `requires_finance_review` false and a null `paid_at`.

Milestone status runs `SCHEDULED`, `PAID`, `FORFEITED`, `REFUNDED` and `CANCELLED`, of which the last three are terminal. Only the first move is request-driven: a `ContractApprover` pays a `SCHEDULED` milestone, which stamps `paid_at` from the logical clock, re-runs the exposure projection into the program's `cancellation_exposure_cents`, and increments the milestone's `version`. This is a guarded cross-entity transition — the parent contract must be `EXECUTED`, and paying a milestone that hangs off a `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` contract reports `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. Payment is also blocked while the program is `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED`, which the program gate reports as `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. A milestone that is not `SCHEDULED` when payment is attempted reports `ERR_MILESTONE_STATE_INVALID`. The three terminal moves — `PAID → FORFEITED`, `PAID → REFUNDED` and `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` — are all system outcomes, driven by the program-cancellation cascade of §3.9 or, for the last one, by the parent contract being withdrawn.

**Cancellation exposure** answers a question the client asks constantly: if we call this off today, what does it cost? The answer is deliberately computed two entirely different ways from the same contract, the same milestone set and the same logical clock, and which of the two is binding is a commercial term negotiated on the contract itself as `exposure_basis`.

The **attrition variant**, binding when `exposure_basis` is `ATTRITION_BASIS`, asks what the supplier can invoice for rooms that were blocked and never filled. Its figure, `attrition_basis_cents`, is the `shortfall_room_nights` from the room-night ledger of §3.7 multiplied by the contract's `nightly_rate_cents`. The deposit schedule and the force-majeure clause are ignored entirely under this variant.

The **deposit-forfeiture variant**, binding when `exposure_basis` is `DEPOSIT_FORFEITURE_BASIS`, asks how much of the money already scheduled or handed over is unrecoverable. It walks the contract's milestones whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PAID` in ascending `sequence` order — never in id order — and accumulates a pre-relief total. A milestone whose `due_at` is at or before the current logical instant has **matured**, and its whole `amount_cents` is added, because nothing about a matured milestone is recoverable. A milestone whose `due_at` is still in the future contributes only its non-refundable share: its `amount_cents` multiplied by the difference between 10000 and its `refundable_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. The accumulated figure is reported as `deposit_pre_relief_cents`. The force-majeure clause then buys relief on the whole basis: `force_majeure_relief_bp` is 0 for a clause of `NONE`, 2500 basis points — a quarter — for `STANDARD`, and 6000 basis points — three fifths — for `EXPANDED`. The final `deposit_forfeiture_basis_cents` is the pre-relief total multiplied by the difference between 10000 and `force_majeure_relief_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. Rounding is applied once per unmatured milestone and once more to the relieved total; milestones already `REFUNDED`, `FORFEITED` or `CANCELLED` are excluded from the walk altogether.

The two variants genuinely diverge, which is why both are exposed side by side. A computed read on a contract, available to `ProgramDirector`, `SourcingManager` and `ContractApprover`, reports `contract_id`, the `contract_value_cents`, both `attrition_basis_cents` and `deposit_forfeiture_basis_cents`, the intermediate `deposit_pre_relief_cents` and `force_majeure_relief_bp`, the `binding_basis` (which echoes the contract's `exposure_basis`) and the resulting `binding_exposure_cents`, plus the four room-night figures that feed the attrition side: `room_nights_blocked`, `room_nights_confirmed`, `attrition_floor_room_nights` and `shortfall_room_nights`. Consider a contract holding 12 rooms for 5 nights at 38500 cents a room-night with an `attrition_allowance_bp` of 2000 and 10 confirmed room-nights, a schedule of three milestones — 577500 due 2026-08-15 at 10000 basis points refundable and paid, 577500 due 2026-09-15 at 5000 basis points refundable and paid, and 1155000 due 2026-10-15 at 0 basis points refundable and still scheduled — and a `STANDARD` force-majeure clause: with the logical clock at 2026-09-20 the attrition variant yields 1463000 cents and the deposit-forfeiture variant yields 1732500 cents.

The program's own `cancellation_exposure_cents` is the sum of `binding_exposure_cents` across the program's contracts whose `status` is `EXECUTED`. It is re-derived when a hotel contract is executed, whenever a milestone is paid, and inside the program-cancellation cascade.

### 3.6. Qualifier Communication, Enrolment and Guest Policy

Once the contract is signed and the ladder adjudicated, the `ProgramDirector` tells the winners. **Notification is a bulk operation with skip semantics**, permitted while the program is `CONTRACTED` or `REGISTRATION_OPEN`. Every qualifier of the program whose `award_status` is `QUALIFIED` moves `QUALIFIED → NOTIFIED`, has `notified_at` stamped from the logical clock, and has its `version` incremented. Qualifiers in any other award state — still `PENDING_REVIEW`, already `NOTIFIED`, `DISQUALIFIED`, `REGISTERED`, `TRAVELLED` or `WITHDRAWN` — are **skipped, not errored**, so a second notification run is harmless. The response reports `notified_count` and `skipped_count` as integers and `notified_qualifier_ids` and `skipped_qualifier_ids` as arrays of qualifier ids; every qualifier of the program appears in exactly one of the two arrays, and both arrays are ordered by the qualifiers' `created_at` ascending.

**Enrolment** creates a seat. Both a `TravelDesk` operator and the winning `Participant` may create one, and the payload names the `qualifier_id` the seat is charged to, a `registration_kind` of `WINNER` or `GUEST`, an optional `guest_of_registration_id`, an `arrival_date` and a `departure_date` as ISO 8601 UTC instants at midnight, an optional `rooms` count from 1 to 3 that defaults to 1, and the `expected_program_version` guard of §2.7. The seat's `program_id` is not a client input: it is denormalised from the referenced qualifier's own `program_id`, and any value the client sends for it is ignored. Enrolment requires the program to be `REGISTRATION_OPEN` and the referenced qualifier's `award_status` to be exactly `NOTIFIED`; a qualifier who has not been notified, or who has already travelled or withdrawn, reports `ERR_QUALIFIER_STATE_INVALID`. Creating a seat increments the **program's** `version` by 1 as well as minting the registration at `version` 1 — which is exactly why the next enrolment must carry a freshly read `expected_program_version`.

The night interval a seat occupies is **half-open**: `arrival_date` is the first occupied night and `departure_date` is the exclusive upper bound, so the departure date's night is not occupied. The departure instant must be strictly after the arrival instant, or the request reports `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. A fresh seat lands with `attendance_status` `INVITED`, `air_status` `NOT_REQUESTED`, `waiver_on_file` false, `room_nights` 0, `cost_cents` 0, a null `air_cabin_class`, `air_deviation_pre_days` and `air_deviation_post_days` both 0, `air_base_fare_cents`, `air_upgrade_cost_cents`, `air_deviation_fee_cents`, `air_change_count` and `air_change_fee_total_cents` all 0, and a null `satisfaction_score` and `cancellation_reason`.

Guest policy is enforced at enrolment in two distinct ways. **The allowance** caps how many companions a winner may bring: the number of that winner's guest seats that are not `CANCELLED` may never exceed the `guest_allowance` of the tier stamped on the qualifier's `assigned_tier_id`, and an enrolment that would breach it reports `ERR_GUEST_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. **The linkage** governs how a guest seat is attached: a seat whose `registration_kind` is `GUEST` must supply a `guest_of_registration_id` naming a non-`CANCELLED` `WINNER` seat carrying the same `qualifier_id`, and a seat whose `registration_kind` is `WINNER` must supply either no `guest_of_registration_id` at all or an explicit null. Omitting the link on a guest seat, pointing it at a cancelled seat, at another guest seat, or at a seat belonging to a different qualifier, or supplying a non-null link on a winner seat, all report `ERR_GUEST_LINK_INVALID`. The allowance is checked before the linkage. Independently, a qualifier may hold at most one `WINNER` seat that is not `CANCELLED`; a second reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_WINNER_REGISTRATION`, and cancelling the first frees the slot for a legitimate re-enrolment.

Attendance runs `INVITED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CANCELLED` and `TRAVELLED`, with `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED` and `TRAVELLED` terminal. **The invitation response** is the one action reserved exclusively to the `Participant`, who must be the owner of the seat and may act only while the program is `REGISTRATION_OPEN`. The payload carries a `response` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE` plus an optional boolean `waiver_signed`. Accepting moves the seat `INVITED → ACCEPTED`, and sets `waiver_on_file` to true when and only when `waiver_signed` was supplied as true — omitting it, or sending false, leaves the waiver unsigned and will later block high-risk activity signups. Declining moves the seat `INVITED → DECLINED` and voids any air record the travel desk had already opened: an `air_status` of `REQUESTED`, `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING` becomes `VOIDED`. Either response increments the seat's `version`. Responding to a seat that is no longer `INVITED` reports `ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.7. Seat Confirmation, Room-Night Capacity, Budget Headroom and Air Travel

**Confirmation** is where a seat stops being a hope and starts consuming real capacity and real money, and it is reserved to the `TravelDesk`. It takes no request body, moves the seat `ACCEPTED → CONFIRMED`, and is permitted only while the program is `REGISTRATION_OPEN`. Confirming a seat that is not `ACCEPTED` — one still `INVITED`, already `CONFIRMED`, or terminal — reports `ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Confirmation books room-nights against exactly one contract, so the program must have **exactly one** supplier contract whose `supplier_kind` is `HOTEL` and whose `status` is `EXECUTED`. If there is none at all, or if there are two or more — which would leave the room-night basis ambiguous — confirmation reports `ERR_NO_EXECUTED_HOTEL_CONTRACT`. Against that single contract, three further gates apply in this order. The seat must fall inside the **stay window**: `arrival_date` must be at or after the contract's `stay_start_at` and `departure_date` at or before its `stay_end_at`, and a seat outside those bounds reports `ERR_STAY_WINDOW_VIOLATION`. The seat must fit the **room block** on every night it occupies, or it reports `ERR_ROOM_BLOCK_EXHAUSTED`. Finally the program must be able to afford it: `budget_headroom_cents` must be at least the seat's `cost_cents`, and a seat the program cannot fund reports `ERR_BUDGET_HEADROOM_EXCEEDED`.

The **room-night ledger** is the capacity accounting for the executed hotel contract. A seat's `room_nights` is its `rooms` multiplied by the number of whole days from `arrival_date` up to but excluding `departure_date` — two rooms arriving on the 10th and departing on the 14th consume eight room-nights, not ten. The contract's `room_nights_blocked` is its `room_block_count` multiplied by its `contracted_nights`, stamped at execution and 0 before that. Its `room_nights_confirmed` is the sum of `room_nights` over the program's seats whose `attendance_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELLED`; seats that are `INVITED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. Its `room_nights_available` is `room_nights_blocked` less `room_nights_confirmed`, and the identity of §2.10 — blocked equals confirmed plus available — holds after every confirmation and every cancellation.

Attrition is the supplier's protection against an under-filled block. The contract's `attrition_floor_room_nights` is `room_nights_blocked` multiplied by the difference between 10000 and its `attrition_allowance_bp`, divided by 10000, and rounded **up** to a whole room-night — a supplier waiving 2000 basis points of a 60-room-night block still expects 48 of them filled, and a fractional floor always rounds up against the buyer. The `shortfall_room_nights` is the floor less `room_nights_confirmed`, or 0 when the floor has already been met, and never negative. `attrition_posture` is `AT_RISK` whenever the shortfall is greater than 0 and `CLEAR` otherwise.

The **per-night occupancy gate** applied at confirmation is finer-grained than the ledger totals. For each night in the candidate seat's half-open interval, the server sums the `rooms` of every seat of the same program whose `attendance_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELLED` and whose own half-open interval covers that night — that is, whose `arrival_date` is at or before the night and whose `departure_date` is strictly after it — and requires that sum plus the candidate's own `rooms` to be at most the contract's `room_block_count`. Because the intervals are half-open, a departing seat and an arriving seat may share the same calendar date without contending for a room on that night.

The **budget rollup** keeps the program's money picture current, and it is re-derived — never incrementally patched — after every qualifier adjudication, every seat confirmation, every seat cancellation, every re-ticketing that moves a seat's `cost_cents`, and inside the contract-execution cascade. Its award side is the sum of `committed_cost_cents` over the program's qualifiers whose `award_status` is `QUALIFIED`, `NOTIFIED`, `REGISTERED` or `TRAVELLED`; qualifiers who are `PENDING_REVIEW`, `DISQUALIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded. Its air side is the sum of `cost_cents` over the program's seats whose `attendance_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELLED`; all other seats are excluded. `committed_budget_cents` is the award side plus the air side, and `budget_headroom_cents` is `budget_total_cents` less `committed_budget_cents` — a figure that may legitimately be negative. Utilization is then measured in basis points: it is 0 when `budget_total_cents` is 0, and otherwise `committed_budget_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `budget_total_cents`, using **integer floor division** so that the 10000-basis-point mark is reached only at or beyond exact parity. `budget_posture` is `OK` when utilization is at most 8500 basis points (85 percent), `WATCH` when it is above that but at most 10000 basis points, and `OVERCOMMITTED` above 10000.

A confirmed seat's other effects are cross-entity. `room_nights` is stamped on the seat, the room-night ledger is re-run on the executed hotel contract to refresh `room_nights_confirmed`, `room_nights_available` and `attrition_posture`, the budget rollup is re-run so the seat's `cost_cents` joins `committed_budget_cents`, and — for a `WINNER` seat only — the owning qualifier moves `NOTIFIED → REGISTERED` with its own `version` increment. A `GUEST` seat's confirmation touches no qualifier state. The seat's own `version` increments.

**Air travel management** is a `TravelDesk` operation carrying an `action` of `REQUEST`, `TICKET` or `CHANGE`, and it drives the seat's second, fully independent state dimension: `air_status` runs `NOT_REQUESTED`, `REQUESTED`, `TICKETED`, `CHANGE_PENDING` and `VOIDED`, with only `VOIDED` terminal. All three actions are permitted while the program is `REGISTRATION_OPEN` or `ONSITE`. Requesting moves the seat `NOT_REQUESTED → REQUESTED` and is deliberately allowed while attendance is still `INVITED`, `ACCEPTED` or `CONFIRMED`, because the travel desk collects air preferences with the invitation rather than after the answer. Ticketing moves it `REQUESTED → TICKETED` on first issue and `CHANGE_PENDING → TICKETED` on re-issue. Opening a change moves it `TICKETED → CHANGE_PENDING`. Any other pairing — requesting twice, ticketing straight from `NOT_REQUESTED`, changing a record that is not `TICKETED`, or acting on a `VOIDED` record at all — reports `ERR_AIR_STATE_INVALID`.

The air payload's optional fields carry the commercial detail: `cabin_class` sets the seat's `air_cabin_class` to `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM_ECONOMY` or `BUSINESS`; `deviation_pre_days` and `deviation_post_days` set `air_deviation_pre_days` and `air_deviation_post_days`, each from 0 to 7; and `base_fare_cents` sets `air_base_fare_cents`, the quoted fare that caps change fees. A request action stores all four; a change action overwrites whichever of them the caller supplies and, notably, charges nothing yet. The two deviation figures must sum to at most 7 days, both on the initial request and on the revised values of a change, and a longer deviation reports `ERR_DEVIATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Three figures are derived from those inputs against the tier stamped on the seat's qualifier, and their sum is the seat's `cost_cents`.

The **cabin upgrade** is priced off a step ladder in which `ECONOMY` is worth 0 cents, `PREMIUM_ECONOMY` 48000 cents and `BUSINESS` 165000 cents. The billable step is the ladder value of the seat's `air_cabin_class` less the ladder value of the tier's own `air_cabin_class`, floored at 0 so that a winner who flies below their entitlement earns no credit. `air_upgrade_cost_cents` is then that billable step less the tier's `upgrade_allowance_cents`, again floored at 0 so that an unused allowance produces no rebate. Both clamps are required; either one alone yields a negative figure somewhere.

The **deviation surcharge** gives the first 2 deviation days away free. Chargeable days are the sum of `air_deviation_pre_days` and `air_deviation_post_days` less 2, floored at 0, and `air_deviation_fee_cents` is that count multiplied by 9500 cents per day.

**Change fees** escalate geometrically under a fare-relative cap. `air_change_count` counts only completed re-ticketing loops — the initial move from `REQUESTED` to `TICKETED` is not a change and leaves the counter at 0, while every completed `CHANGE_PENDING → TICKETED` loop increments it by 1. The cap is `air_base_fare_cents` multiplied by 4000 and divided by 10000 — 40 percent of the quoted fare — rounded half up to a whole cent. The uncapped ladder is accumulated one round at a time: for each round from the first up to `air_change_count`, the round's fee is 12500 cents multiplied by the ratio of 10000 plus 5000 to 10000, raised to the power of one less than the round number — a 50 percent escalation per round — and **that per-round fee is rounded half up to a whole cent before it is added**. `air_change_fee_total_cents` is the smaller of the cap and the accumulated total, so the cap is applied **once to the accumulated total and never per round**. With an `air_base_fare_cents` of 214000 the cap is 85600, and the running totals across five loops are 12500, 31250, 59375, then 85600 where the uncapped 101563 is clamped, and 85600 again.

Re-ticketing after a change therefore does three things: it increments `air_change_count` by 1, re-derives `air_upgrade_cost_cents`, `air_deviation_fee_cents`, `air_change_fee_total_cents` and `cost_cents`, and — when the seat's `attendance_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELLED`, so that the seat is already inside the budget rollup's air side — re-runs the budget rollup. Every accepted air action increments the seat's `version` by 1.

### 3.8. The On-Site Agenda: Activities, Seats, Waitlists and Conflicts

While the group is on the ground, the `TravelDesk` publishes the agenda. Each activity carries the owning `program_id`, a `name` of at most 160 characters such as "Douro Valley Sail", a `start_at` and an `end_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, a `capacity` of at least 0 bounding how many signups may be `BOOKED` at once, and a `risk_level` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`. The end instant must be strictly after the start instant, or the request reports `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. Publishing is permitted while the program is `CONTRACTED`, `REGISTRATION_OPEN` or `ONSITE`, so the agenda can be built well before anyone arrives. A new activity lands `OPEN` with `booked_count` and `waitlist_count` both 0. A `capacity` of 0 is legal and meaningful: it sends every signup, from the very first, onto the waitlist.

Activity status runs `OPEN`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, with the latter two terminal. `booked_count` is always the number of the activity's signups whose `status` is `BOOKED` and `waitlist_count` the number whose `status` is `WAITLISTED`; cancelled signups count towards neither.

**Signing up** is available to both the `TravelDesk` and the owning `Participant`, and the payload names the `activity_id`, the `registration_id` consuming the slot, and the `expected_activity_version` guard of §2.7. Signups are permitted only while the program is `ONSITE`. The activity must be `OPEN`, or the request reports `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`; the registration's `attendance_status` must be `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELLED`, or it reports `ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`. The activity and the registration must belong to the same program, or it reports `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. An activity whose `risk_level` is `HIGH` additionally demands that the registration carry `waiver_on_file` as true — the waiver the participant signed when accepting the invitation — and a signup without it reports `ERR_ACTIVITY_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. At most one signup that is not `CANCELLED` may exist for a given activity-and-registration pair; a second reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVITY_SIGNUP`. Every accepted signup increments the parent activity's `version` by 1, which is what makes the next client's `expected_activity_version` go stale.

**Admission** is a strict comparison: the signup lands `BOOKED` when the activity's `booked_count` is strictly less than its `capacity`, and `WAITLISTED` otherwise. An activity whose `booked_count` already equals its `capacity` waitlists the next signup rather than overbooking it. Signup status runs `BOOKED`, `WAITLISTED` and `CANCELLED`, with `CANCELLED` terminal, and a new row's `promoted_at` is null.

**Schedule conflicts** are checked against the participant's own agenda, and every activity interval is padded by a 45-minute transfer buffer on both sides. For each of the participant's existing signups whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `WAITLISTED`, the requested activity conflicts when its `start_at` is strictly before the existing activity's `end_at` advanced by 45 minutes **and** the existing activity's `start_at` is strictly before the requested activity's `end_at` advanced by 45 minutes; a conflict reports `ERR_ACTIVITY_TIME_CONFLICT`. Because both comparisons are strict, a gap of exactly 45 minutes is accepted and a gap of 44 minutes is refused: against an activity ending at 2026-11-10T13:00:00Z, a signup starting at 13:44:00Z conflicts while one starting at 13:45:00Z does not. Cancelled signups never conflict with anything.

**Releasing a slot** is available to both the `TravelDesk` and the owning `Participant`, takes no request body, and is permitted while the program is `ONSITE` and the parent activity is `OPEN`; an activity that is no longer `OPEN` reports `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`, and a signup that is already `CANCELLED` reports `ERR_SIGNUP_STATE_INVALID`. Releasing a `BOOKED` slot moves it `BOOKED → CANCELLED`, decrements the activity's `booked_count`, and **runs one waitlist promotion**. Releasing a `WAITLISTED` slot moves it `WAITLISTED → CANCELLED`, decrements the activity's `waitlist_count`, and **promotes nobody** — only a booked seat being given back frees capacity. Either release increments the parent activity's `version` by 1 alongside the signup's own.

**Waitlist promotion** runs exactly once per released booked seat, and only while the parent activity is `OPEN`. The pool is every signup of that activity whose `status` is `WAITLISTED`. The pool is ordered by the signup's `created_at` ascending and, among signups created at the same instant, by the `employee_code` of the qualifier behind the signup's `registration_id` ascending — a business key, never a random row id. The first entry in that order moves `WAITLISTED → BOOKED`, has its `promoted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and increments its own `version`; the activity's `booked_count` rises by one and its `waitlist_count` falls by one. If the pool is empty, nothing is promoted and the freed capacity simply stands.

Promotion is deliberately **suppressed** in two situations, and both matter. **Closing an activity** is a `TravelDesk` action from `OPEN → CLOSED`, permitted while the program is `REGISTRATION_OPEN` or `ONSITE`; it cancels every `WAITLISTED` signup of the activity outright, drives `waitlist_count` to 0, leaves every `BOOKED` signup standing, and promotes nobody. Closing an activity that is not `OPEN` reports `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`. And during the program-cancellation cascade of §3.9, booked seats are released without any promotion at all, because there is no longer anything to be promoted into. Outside those two cases — a participant giving up a slot, or a registration being cancelled and taking its slots with it — every released booked seat pulls exactly one participant off the waitlist.

### 3.9. Reversals, Reconciliation, Measurement and Taxable Award Reporting

**Cancelling a seat** is the exact compensation of confirming one, and both the `TravelDesk` and the owning `Participant` may do it. The payload carries a `cancellation_reason`, which is stored on the seat in every branch. Cancellation is permitted while the program is `REGISTRATION_OPEN` or `ONSITE`, and from the seat's three non-terminal attendance states; cancelling a seat that is already `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED` or `TRAVELLED` reports `ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`.

- From `INVITED → CANCELLED`: the reason is stored, an `air_status` of `REQUESTED`, `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING` becomes `VOIDED`, and the seat's `version` increments. Nothing else moves, because an invited seat never consumed capacity or budget.
- From `ACCEPTED → CANCELLED`: identically, the reason is stored, a non-terminal `air_status` becomes `VOIDED`, and the seat's `version` increments. An accepted seat has still not entered the room-night ledger or the budget rollup, so neither is touched, and no guest seat or activity signup is disturbed.
- From `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`: the full reversal runs. The reason is stored and a non-terminal `air_status` becomes `VOIDED`. Every `GUEST` seat whose `guest_of_registration_id` names this row and whose `attendance_status` is still non-terminal moves to `CANCELLED`. Every activity signup of this seat whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and for each activity that thereby lost a `BOOKED` signup, one waitlist promotion runs exactly as described in §3.8, stamping the winner's `promoted_at`. The room-night ledger is re-derived, releasing this seat's `room_nights` back into `room_nights_available` and refreshing `attrition_posture`; the budget rollup is re-derived, removing the seat's `cost_cents` from `committed_budget_cents`. Finally, if this was a `WINNER` seat and no other non-`CANCELLED` `WINNER` seat remains for the qualifier, the qualifier moves `REGISTERED → NOTIFIED`. Every row touched increments its own `version`.

That last reversal is a **compensating** transition rather than a punitive one: because both `NOTIFIED` and `REGISTERED` sit inside the budget rollup's award-side input set, moving the qualifier back from `REGISTERED` to `NOTIFIED` leaves the award commitment exactly where it was and produces no budget drift. The seat's air-side `cost_cents`, by contrast, does leave the rollup, because the seat is no longer `CONFIRMED`. Both conserved identities of §2.10 hold again once the reversal has run.

**Cancelling a program** is the largest cascade in the system, driven by the `ProgramDirector` with a `cancellation_reason` that is stored on the program. It is available from `DESIGN`, `SOURCING`, `CONTRACTED`, `REGISTRATION_OPEN` and `ONSITE`. A `RECONCILED` or `CLOSED` program cannot be cancelled — the trip has already happened — and that attempt reports `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`, as does cancelling an already-`CANCELLED` program. The cascade is applied in exactly this order:

1. Every registration of the program whose `attendance_status` is `INVITED`, `ACCEPTED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and each such row whose `air_status` is `REQUESTED`, `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING` moves to `VOIDED`.
2. Every activity signup belonging to those registrations whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `CANCELLED`. **No waitlist promotion runs during program cancellation** — releasing a booked seat here promotes nobody.
3. Every on-site activity of the program whose `status` is `OPEN` moves to `CANCELLED`, taking any of its remaining `BOOKED` or `WAITLISTED` signups to `CANCELLED` with it.
4. Every qualifier of the program whose `award_status` is `QUALIFIED`, `NOTIFIED` or `REGISTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Qualifiers already `DISQUALIFIED`, `TRAVELLED` or `WITHDRAWN` are left alone.
5. Every deposit milestone of every `EXECUTED` supplier contract of the program is settled against the logical clock: a `PAID` milestone whose `due_at` is at or before the current logical instant moves to `FORFEITED`, a `PAID` milestone whose `due_at` is still in the future moves to `REFUNDED`, and a `SCHEDULED` milestone moves to `CANCELLED`.
6. The program's `cancellation_exposure_cents` is re-stamped from the exposure projection of §3.5 over the same contracts.

The program's own `status` becomes `CANCELLED` and its `version` increments, as does the `version` of every row the six steps touched.

**Reconciliation** closes the on-site phase. The `ProgramDirector` advances the program `ONSITE → RECONCILED` once no on-site activity of the program is still `OPEN`, and that single advance settles the whole travelling cohort: every registration whose `attendance_status` is `CONFIRMED` moves to `TRAVELLED`; every qualifier whose `award_status` is `REGISTERED` moves to `TRAVELLED`; each qualifier that just became `TRAVELLED` has its `taxable_award_cents` stamped from the taxable-award base below; each of those whose stamped `taxable_award_cents` is greater than 0 moves its `tax_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE → PENDING_REPORT`, while a traveller whose taxable base came out at 0 stays `NOT_APPLICABLE`; and the program's `sales_lift_pct` and `satisfaction_index` are computed and stamped. Every row involved increments its `version`.

The **taxable award base** is deliberately narrower than what the program spent. A qualifier's business-purpose exclusion is its `committed_cost_cents` multiplied by 1500 and divided by 10000 — 15 percent of the committed award, recognised as legitimate business purpose — rounded half up to a whole cent. `taxable_award_cents` is then `committed_cost_cents` less that exclusion, taken as the exact integer remainder with no further rounding. The air-side variance carried on a seat's `cost_cents` — cabin upgrades, deviation surcharges and change fees — is treated as business travel and is deliberately **not** part of the taxable base. A qualifier who ends `DISQUALIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN` keeps a `taxable_award_cents` of 0.

The **post-trip survey** is what feeds satisfaction. Either the participant themselves or a `TravelDesk` operator records a `satisfaction_score` from 1 to 10 on a seat, permitted while the program is `ONSITE` or `RECONCILED` and while the seat's `attendance_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELLED`; the score is stored and the seat's `version` increments. A seat that has never been surveyed carries a null `satisfaction_score`.

**Program measurement** is exposed as a computed read available to the `ProgramDirector` and the `SalesOpsAnalyst`, and the same two figures are stamped onto the program by the reconciliation advance. Sales lift is measured over the **travelling cohort only**: the qualifiers of the program whose `award_status` is exactly `TRAVELLED`, excluding anyone `DISQUALIFIED`, `WITHDRAWN` or merely `NOTIFIED`. Their `baseline_metric` values are summed into a baseline total and their `actual_metric` values into an actual total. If the cohort is empty, or if the baseline total is 0, `sales_lift_pct` is null. Otherwise the lift in basis points is the actual total less the baseline total, multiplied by 10000, divided by the baseline total and rounded half up to a whole basis point; `sales_lift_pct` is that basis-point figure divided by 100, which makes it exact to two decimal places by construction. The rule is **aggregate then rate, never rate then average**: the lift comes from the summed metrics of the cohort, not from the mean of per-qualifier lift percentages. Two travellers with baselines of 180000000 and 120000000 against actuals of 260000000 and 155000000 give 3833 basis points and therefore a `sales_lift_pct` of 38.33.

Satisfaction is measured over every registration of the program carrying a non-null `satisfaction_score`, regardless of `registration_kind`, so a guest's opinion counts as much as a winner's. If no seat has been surveyed, `satisfaction_index` is null. Otherwise it is the sum of those scores multiplied by 100, divided by the number of surveyed seats, rounded half up to a whole centi-point, and then divided by 100 — again exact to two decimal places. Scores of 9 and 8 give a `satisfaction_index` of 8.5. The measurement read returns `program_id`, `sales_lift_pct`, `satisfaction_index`, `baseline_metric_total`, `actual_metric_total`, `survey_count`, `qualified_count`, `travelled_count`, `committed_budget_cents`, `budget_headroom_cents`, `budget_posture`, `cancellation_exposure_cents` and `reported_award_total_cents`, where `baseline_metric_total` and `actual_metric_total` are the two cohort sums and `survey_count` is the number of surveyed seats.

**Taxable award reporting** is the last obligation of the program, and it belongs to the `TaxComplianceOfficer`. A report is drafted for a single travelled winner by supplying the `qualifier_id` and a `tax_year` in the range 2000 to 2100. Drafting is deliberately **not** gated on the program's lifecycle status — any status except `CANCELLED` is accepted — so that the only lifecycle gate is the cross-entity one: the referenced qualifier's `award_status` must be `TRAVELLED`, and drafting against a qualifier who has not travelled reports `ERR_QUALIFIER_STATE_INVALID`. The `tax_year` must equal the `fiscal_year` of the program behind that qualifier, or the draft reports `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

Drafting takes two snapshots that are frozen thereafter: `award_value_cents` copies the qualifier's `taxable_award_cents` as it stands at that instant, and `gross_up_method` copies the program's `gross_up_policy`. Neither is ever re-read afterwards, which is why the policy lock of §3.1 exists. The report lands in `DRAFT` with a null `filed_by_user_id`, `filed_at`, `voided_by_user_id` and `void_reason`. If the qualifier's `tax_status` was `VOIDED` at that moment, drafting a replacement moves it `VOIDED → PENDING_REPORT`, which is what closes the tax cycle and lets a mis-filed award be corrected before the program is closed.

The **gross-up computation** runs at draft creation and fills `combined_rate_bp`, `gross_up_cents` and `reported_value_cents` from the snapshotted award value. A tiered state supplemental rate underlies it: 300 basis points when the award value is below 500000 cents, 500 basis points when it is at least 500000 but below 1500000 cents, and 700 basis points when it is at or above 1500000 cents. The three policies then diverge sharply.

- With a `gross_up_method` of `NONE`, the employer contributes nothing: `combined_rate_bp` and `gross_up_cents` are both 0 and `reported_value_cents` equals `award_value_cents`.
- With `FLAT_SUPPLEMENTAL`, the employer covers only the federal supplemental rate of 2200 basis points and leaves the social-insurance and state components to the winner. `combined_rate_bp` is 2200, and `gross_up_cents` is `award_value_cents` multiplied by 2200, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. The computation is linear and non-iterative.
- With `FULL_GROSS_UP`, the employer solves the inverse-withholding problem so the winner nets the full award. `combined_rate_bp` is the sum of the federal supplemental 2200 basis points, the social-insurance 765 basis points, and the tiered state rate — **and the state tier is keyed on the pre-gross-up `award_value_cents`, not on the grossed-up result**. The reported figure is `award_value_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by the difference between 10000 and `combined_rate_bp`, rounded half up to a whole cent; `gross_up_cents` is that reported figure less `award_value_cents`. Re-deriving the state tier from the grossed-up figure is a circular ordering and is wrong: for an award value of 443198 cents the pinned order selects 300 basis points and reports 658052, whereas keying the tier on 658052 would select 500 basis points and report 678191.

In every case `reported_value_cents` is `award_value_cents` plus `gross_up_cents`. The two employer-funded policies genuinely diverge — for an award value of 443198 cents the flat policy produces a `gross_up_cents` of 97504 while the full policy produces 214854.

Report status runs `DRAFT`, `FILED` and `VOIDED`, with `VOIDED` terminal for the report itself. **Filing** is permitted from `DRAFT` only, while the program is `RECONCILED` or `CLOSED`, and only when the referenced qualifier's `tax_status` is `PENDING_REPORT`; filing anything that is not a `DRAFT` reports `ERR_TAX_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. Filing stamps `filed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `filed_at` from the logical clock, moves the qualifier's `tax_status` `PENDING_REPORT → REPORTED`, and adds the report's `reported_value_cents` to the program's `reported_award_total_cents`. **Voiding** is permitted from `FILED` only, again while the program is `RECONCILED` or `CLOSED`, and the payload carries a `void_reason`; voiding anything that is not `FILED` reports `ERR_TAX_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the report's `filed_by_user_id`, or the attempt reports `ERR_TAX_VOID_SELF_REVIEW`. Voiding stamps `voided_by_user_id` and `void_reason`, moves the qualifier's `tax_status` `REPORTED → VOIDED`, and subtracts the report's `reported_value_cents` from the program's `reported_award_total_cents` — an exact compensation, so that `reported_award_total_cents` always equals the sum of `reported_value_cents` over the program's reports that are currently `FILED`. Filing and voiding each increment the report's `version` and the qualifier's `version`.

The tax dimension therefore cycles `PENDING_REPORT → REPORTED → VOIDED → PENDING_REPORT`, and that cycle is precisely what allows a program with a mis-filed award to be corrected and then closed: a program may only reach `CLOSED` when every qualifier whose `taxable_award_cents` is greater than 0 sits at `tax_status` `REPORTED`.
