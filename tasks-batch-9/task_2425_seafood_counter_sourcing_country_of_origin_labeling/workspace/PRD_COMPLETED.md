# Seafood Counter Cool Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A supermarket service seafood counter is one of the most heavily regulated square metres in the store. Every tote that arrives at the dock carries a species declaration, a harvest method, a country of harvest and a farmed-or-wild designation, and every one of those facts must reappear — correctly — on the card sitting above the ice bed, in the weekly circular, and in the paperwork that a regulator may demand months later. When the facts diverge from the fish, the consequences are not cosmetic: mislabelled product must come off sale, advertising must be pulled, the dealer must be billed for the loss, and the department's licence to operate freely narrows until the finding is remediated.

The Seafood Counter Cool Ops API is the system of record for that chain of obligations. It is mounted under `/seafood-ops` and governs supplier lot receipt with full sourcing disclosure, mandatory country-of-origin labelling at the case and in advertising, laboratory-backed species substitution sampling, fresh-versus-previously-frozen disclosure, seafood import monitoring documentation for covered species, ice-bed temperature verification, sustainability program certification claims, and the valuation of shrink from unsold fresh product including its diversion into cook-and-sell recovery. Each of those concerns produces evidence, and the evidence feeds back into a single gating variable — the counter's operating **posture** — which progressively removes capabilities from a department that is accumulating open cold-chain excursions or unremediated compliance findings.

Two design commitments shape the whole specification. First, correctness of disclosure outranks throughput: a label cannot be posted while the case is under an open temperature excursion, an advertisement cannot be published unless two separate compliance reviewers have independently cleared its origin content and its sustainability claim, and a covered-species lot cannot enter the ice bed without an accepted import-monitoring record. Second, every financial consequence is reversible without drift: chargebacks assessed against a dealer, and shrink posted against the department's ledger, each have an explicit compensating action that leaves the underlying ledger identities intact.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identifier semantics, rounding discipline, stale-version rejection, and the order in which the server rejects a bad request.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ChainAdmin`, `SeafoodManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `MerchandisingLead`, and `SupplierRep`. If either header is absent — a header present but empty counting as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five strings, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the addressed operation in §4, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the five roles are entity-backed, which means their `X-User-ID` is not a free-form operator string but a primary key that the server compares against resource ownership.

- When `X-User-Role` is `SeafoodManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Counter`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Counter` compare the resource's counter foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the manager's `X-User-ID` must equal the `Counter` `id` itself when the counter is addressed directly, the `counter_id` of an addressed `SupplierLot`, the `counter_id` of the lot behind an addressed `CaseLabel`, the `counter_id` of the lot behind an addressed `ShrinkEvent`, the `counter_id` of an addressed or submitted `IceBedReading`, and the `counter_id` of an addressed `ComplianceViolation`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_COUNTER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Supplier` compare the resource's supplier foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the representative's `X-User-ID` must equal the `supplier_id` of an addressed `SupplierLot`, the `supplier_id` of the lot behind an addressed `SimpDocument`, the `supplier_id` of an addressed or filed `SustainabilityCertification`, and the `supplier_id` of the lot behind an addressed `VerificationSample`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

The other three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ChainAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does record it — as `cool_reviewer_user_id` or `claim_reviewer_user_id` on an `AdvertisedItem` when that officer decides the corresponding review branch — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `MerchandisingLead`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is neither persisted nor compared against any entity's `id`. Both ownership comparisons bite only on a resource a request actually addresses by path identifier or submits as a body foreign key; list endpoints perform no ownership comparison at all and are never implicitly narrowed to the caller's own counter or supplier, so a `SeafoodManager` or `SupplierRep` listing a collection sees every matching record on the system.

Because the two advertisement review branches exist precisely to obtain two independent opinions, the counter operates under a segregation-of-duties rule: the `ComplianceOfficer` who decides the sustainability-claim branch of an advertisement must not be the same principal who decided that advertisement's country-of-origin branch. When the acting `X-User-ID` on the claim branch equals the advertisement's recorded `cool_reviewer_user_id`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and like every other instant the server stamps it reads the logical clock of §2.4 rather than host wall-clock time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object — the logical-clock capability of §4 is the only operation with no data, and it answers `200` with `"response_type": "object"` and an empty `data` object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the first returned record; when it is omitted it defaults to 0. A parameter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted; a parameter that is not parseable as an integer, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties — which are routine, because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock and many records therefore share an instant — broken by ascending server-side creation sequence, so that records written earlier in the same logical instant sort earlier. Every documented list filter is an exact match on the stored value, filters combine conjunctively, and a filter value the server cannot interpret as the field's type — an unparseable integer for `case_slot`, or anything other than `true`/`false`/`1`/`0` for `breach` — matches no record at all and yields an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than an error. Unrecognised query parameters are ignored.

Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching record remains beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Each persisted record carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that record — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including refreshes of purely derived fields such as a counter's posture or shrink ledger totals. Both are settled per request rather than per individual field write: a record touched any number of times while serving one request has its `updated_at` set once to the logical clock and its `version` incremented by exactly one, a record created by a request stays at `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to its `created_at` even if the same request goes on to mutate it, and a re-derivation that leaves every derived value on the record unchanged — a posture recomputation that moves neither `posture` nor `open_excursion_count` nor `violation_points`, a supplier index recomputation that moves neither index nor `status`, an excursion replay that reproduces the same exposure figures — is not a mutation and does not touch `updated_at` or `version` at all. That rendering is uniform across the whole API: every timestamp field a response carries — `created_at` and `updated_at`, every field stamped from the logical clock, every instant the server copies or derives from another record's timestamp, and every client-supplied instant a record stores and echoes back, such as `reading_at`, `ad_start_at`, `ad_end_at`, `valid_from`, `valid_until`, `harvest_date` and `landing_date` — is emitted, whenever it is non-null, as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, normalised to UTC regardless of the offset or the fractional precision the client submitted.

Two record types are exceptions. A `Species` record is immutable configuration data and an `IceBedReading` is an append-only observation; both carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a `Species` or on an `IceBedReading`, and no request may expect those fields to appear on either.

All temporal reasoning in this system runs off a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any request sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `ChainAdmin` may move the clock forward or backward by submitting a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability described in §4 — a required string parsed as an ISO 8601 instant and normalised to UTC, a value that cannot be parsed as one being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; from that point on, every timestamp the server stamps, every certificate validity comparison and every advertising-window comparison uses the new instant. Fields documented below as being "stamped from the logical clock" — `received_at`, `cased_at`, `vacated_at`, `published_at`, `withdrawn_at`, `reviewed_at`, `result_recorded_at`, `posted_at`, `reversed_at`, `cured_at` and `expunged_at` — all read that clock, not the host's system time.

### 2.5. Identifier Resolution for Addressed Resources

Requests address resources through path identifiers and, for creates, through foreign keys carried in the body. Every entity in this system is a peer addressed directly under the API mount — there are no nested collections — but a single request routinely names several records at once, and the order in which the server resolves them is fixed. Identifiers in the URL path are resolved left to right, and a path identifier is always resolved before any body foreign key. The path identifier names in use are `counter_id`, `supplier_id`, `species_id`, `lot_id`, `label_id`, `item_id`, `reading_id`, `excursion_id`, `sample_id`, `document_id`, `certification_id`, `event_id` and `violation_id`. Among body foreign keys, resolution follows the ordering given in §2.9: the counter first, then the supplier, then the species, then the lot, then the remaining record types. The first identifier that fails to resolve determines the returned 404 error, and no later check runs. A body foreign key counts as *supplied* only when the field is present and not null: a key that is present but carries a non-string value is treated as an identifier that resolves to nothing and therefore yields that record type's 404, whereas a key that is absent or explicitly null is not a resolution failure at all and instead surfaces later as the `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` of a missing required field. For the same reason a request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as a JSON object is treated as an empty object, so the 404 existence checks of a request whose identifiers live in the path still run — and still report — ahead of any body-field complaint.

### 2.6. Counter Posture: The Gating Surface

A `Counter` carries a computed `posture` whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`, and which defaults to `OPEN` at registration. Posture is never set directly by a client; it is re-derived by the server on every cold-chain excursion creation or resolution, on every compliance-violation creation, cure or expungement, and as the fourth step of a verification-cycle close.

The derivation reads two aggregates over the counter's own records. The first is `open_excursion_count`, the number of `ColdChainExcursion` records for this counter whose `status` is `OPEN`. The second is `violation_points`, computed by taking every `ComplianceViolation` for this counter whose `status` is `OPEN` and adding that category's point weight, then taking every `ComplianceViolation` for this counter whose `status` is `CURED` and adding that category's point weight divided by two and rounded up to the next whole number; violations whose `status` is `EXPUNGED` contribute nothing at all. The rounding-up is applied to each cured violation individually before the additions, not to the total. The point weight is fixed per `category`: a `SPECIES_SUBSTITUTION` finding is worth 6 points, a `COOL_MISSING` finding 4 points, a `FROZEN_DISCLOSURE` finding 4 points, a `SIMP_DEFICIENT` finding 5 points, a `COLD_CHAIN` finding 3 points, and an `UNSUBSTANTIATED_CLAIM` finding 6 points. Each violation's `points` field records the weight assigned to it at creation.

Posture then follows from those two aggregates, evaluated in this order. If `open_excursion_count` is at least 2, or `violation_points` is at least 12, the posture is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if `open_excursion_count` is at least 1, or `violation_points` is at least 6, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Because the derivation is a pure function of the current aggregates, remediation moves the posture back down the same ladder: a counter in `SUSPENDED` returns to `RESTRICTED` as soon as no suspension condition holds but a restriction condition still does, a counter in `RESTRICTED` returns to `OPEN` once `open_excursion_count` is 0 and `violation_points` is below 6, and a counter in `OPEN` may go straight to `SUSPENDED` without passing through `RESTRICTED` when a suspension condition is met in a single step.

What the posture actually gates is spelled out in §2.9 items 34 and 35. In short, `RESTRICTED` closes exactly two doors — receiving a new lot and publishing an advertisement — while `SUSPENDED` closes the counter's whole mutating surface except for the remediation actions the department needs in order to dig itself out: dispositioning an open excursion, curing or expunging a violation, withdrawing a case label, retracting or closing an advertisement, and closing the verification cycle.

### 2.7. Weights, Money and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is always an integer count of cents; no monetary field is ever a decimal number. Weights are decimal numbers carried to exactly 3 decimal places (`received_weight_kg`, `remaining_weight_kg`, `shrink_weight_kg`, `conversion_requested_kg`, `approved_conversion_kg`, `allowance_kg`, `cook_yield_kg`, `sold_weight_kg`). Probe temperatures are decimal numbers carried to exactly 1 decimal place. Substitution risk indices are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places. Those declared precisions are enforced by normalisation rather than by refusal: a client-submitted decimal carrying more places than its field declares is quantised — round half up — to the declared precision on ingest, and it is the quantised value that is stored, echoed and used in every subsequent comparison and calculation, so a `temperature_c` of 4.44 is stored as 4.4 and does not breach, and a submitted weight is bounds-checked only after quantisation. The one submitted weight carrying no declared precision is `sampled_weight_g`, which is stored and echoed exactly as submitted.

Every rounding in this specification is **round half up**: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Monetary results round to whole cents, weight results round to 3 decimal places, and risk indices round to 2 decimal places. Where a calculation is composed of several parts, the point at which rounding is applied is stated explicitly in that calculation's narrative and must be honoured exactly — rounding each component before summation and rounding once after summation are different answers, and the specification always says which one applies.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Rejection on Parent Records

Two write operations in this system can silently destroy a decision that another actor has already made, because neither of them can be screened by an ordinary state precondition. Both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of the record they logically belong to.

The first is case-label publication — `POST /seafood-ops/case-labels`, whose parent record is the `SupplierLot` named by `lot_id`. A publish request must carry `expected_lot_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `SupplierLot` as the client last observed it. Publishing a label legitimately supersedes any prior `PUBLISHED` label for the same lot, so a second create is valid product behaviour rather than an invalid-state request; what must be caught instead is a writer whose view of the lot's species, frozen disclosure or `label_status` predates a change the label is obliged to reflect. If `expected_lot_version` does not equal the lot's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful publication itself increments that lot's `version` by one.

The second is verification-cycle closure — `POST /seafood-ops/counters/{counter_id}/close-verification-cycle`, whose parent record is the addressed `Counter`. A close request must carry `expected_counter_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `Counter` as the client last observed it. A cycle close is legitimate from any posture, so it cannot be screened by a state precondition; the version check instead detects that the sampling plan the caller intends to freeze was computed from an out-of-date candidate set. If `expected_counter_version` does not equal the counter's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COUNTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful close increments that counter's `version` by one as its final step.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.9. Order of Rejection

When a single request violates more than one rule, the server reports the violation that comes first in the following list and performs no further checking. The list is exhaustive and its order is normative.

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) — on every endpoint: both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles.
2. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) — on every endpoint: the acting role appears among the roles authorised for that endpoint in §4.
3. `ERR_COUNTER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (403) — on every endpoint a `SeafoodManager` may call: the counter-ownership comparison of §2.1 holds.
4. `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (403) — on every endpoint a `SupplierRep` may call: the supplier-ownership comparison of §2.1 holds.
5. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — on the sustainability-claim review branch only: the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the advertisement's `cool_reviewer_user_id`.
6. `ERR_COUNTER_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every endpoint carrying a counter identifier in the path or a `counter_id` in the body: the `Counter` resolves.
7. `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every endpoint carrying a supplier identifier in the path or a `supplier_id` in the body: the `Supplier` resolves.
8. `ERR_SPECIES_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every endpoint carrying a species identifier, whether as `species_id`, `observed_species_id` or `corrected_species_id`: the `Species` resolves.
9. `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every endpoint carrying a lot identifier in the path or a `lot_id` in the body: the `SupplierLot` resolves.
10. `ERR_CASE_LABEL_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on case-label retrieval by identifier and on case-label withdrawal: the `CaseLabel` resolves.
11. `ERR_AD_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every advertisement endpoint addressing a specific item: the `AdvertisedItem` resolves.
12. `ERR_READING_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on ice-bed reading retrieval by identifier: the `IceBedReading` resolves.
13. `ERR_EXCURSION_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every excursion endpoint addressing a specific excursion: the `ColdChainExcursion` resolves.
14. `ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every verification-sample endpoint addressing a specific sample: the `VerificationSample` resolves.
15. `ERR_SIMP_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every import-monitoring endpoint addressing a specific document: the `SimpDocument` resolves.
16. `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every endpoint carrying a certification identifier in the path or a `certification_id` in the body: the `SustainabilityCertification` resolves.
17. `ERR_SHRINK_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every shrink endpoint addressing a specific event: the `ShrinkEvent` resolves.
18. `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND` (404) — on every violation endpoint addressing a specific violation: the `ComplianceViolation` resolves.
19. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) — on every endpoint: every required body field is present, of the declared type, within its declared numeric bounds, and — for enumerated fields — one of the declared members.
20. `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (412) — on case-label publication: `expected_lot_version` equals the lot's `version`.
21. `ERR_STALE_COUNTER_VERSION` (412) — on verification-cycle closure: `expected_counter_version` equals the counter's `version`.
22. `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on casing, sales recording, verification-sample creation and shrink-event creation: the lot's `intake_status` is one from which the requested write is legal.
23. `ERR_LABEL_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on relabelling: the lot's `label_status` is one from which the requested transition departs.
24. `ERR_CHARGEBACK_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on chargeback settlement and chargeback reversal: the lot's `chargeback_status` is one from which the requested transition departs.
25. `ERR_CASE_LABEL_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on case-label withdrawal: the label's `status` is `PUBLISHED`.
26. `ERR_AD_ITEM_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on the country-of-origin review, the sustainability-claim review, publication, closure and retraction of an advertisement: the advertisement's `status` and the addressed review dimension are ones from which the requested transition departs.
27. `ERR_EXCURSION_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on excursion disposition: the excursion's `status` is `OPEN`, and a `RELEASE` disposition additionally requires the excursion's `severity` to be `MINOR` or `MAJOR`.
28. `ERR_SAMPLE_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on sending a sample to the laboratory, recording a result and disputing a result: the sample's `status` is one from which the requested transition departs.
29. `ERR_SIMP_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on import-monitoring submission and review: the document's `status` is one from which the requested transition departs.
30. `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on certificate review and certificate enforcement: the certificate's `status` is one from which the requested transition departs.
31. `ERR_SHRINK_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on conversion approval, conversion, posting and reversal of a shrink event: the event's `status` is one from which the requested transition departs.
32. `ERR_VIOLATION_STATE_INVALID` (409) — on violation cure and violation expungement: the violation's `status` is one from which the requested transition departs.
33. `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) — on disputing a sample, and on sending a disputed sample back to the laboratory: the sample's `retest_count` is below 2.
34. `ERR_COUNTER_SUSPENDED` (409) — the owning counter's `posture` is not `SUSPENDED`. This guard applies to exactly ten operations: lot receipt, casing a lot, recording sales, relabelling a lot, case-label publication, ice-bed reading submission, verification-sample creation, shrink-event creation, advertisement drafting, and advertisement publication. Excursion disposition, violation cure, violation expungement, case-label withdrawal, advertisement retraction, advertisement closure and verification-cycle closure remain available while a counter is `SUSPENDED`.
35. `ERR_COUNTER_RESTRICTED` (409) — the owning counter's `posture` is not `RESTRICTED`. This guard applies to exactly two operations: lot receipt and advertisement publication.
36. `ERR_OPEN_EXCURSION_BLOCKS` (409) — on case-label publication: the counter owning the lot has no `ColdChainExcursion` whose `status` is `OPEN`.
37. `ERR_SIMP_DOCUMENT_MISSING` (409) — on casing a lot: when the lot's `Species` has `simp_covered` true, a `SimpDocument` for that lot exists whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`.
38. `ERR_FROZEN_DISCLOSURE_CONFLICT` (422) — on lot receipt: `previously_frozen` and `fresh_claim` are not both submitted as true.
39. `ERR_RELABEL_SPECIES_MISMATCH` (422) — on relabelling: `corrected_species_id` equals the `observed_species_id` of the most recent `VerificationSample` for the lot whose `status` is `MISMATCH`, where "most recent" means ordered by `result_recorded_at` descending and, for ties, by `created_at` descending.
40. `ERR_MARKET_NAME_NOT_ACCEPTABLE` (422) — on relabelling: `corrected_market_name` is a member of the `acceptable_market_names` array of the species named by `corrected_species_id`.
41. `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` (422) — on case-label publication, advertisement drafting and the sustainability-claim review: when a `certification_id` is supplied, that certificate's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `counter_id`, `supplier_id` and `species_id` all equal the lot's `counter_id`, `supplier_id` and `species_id`.
42. `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` (422) — on case-label publication, advertisement drafting and the sustainability-claim review: when a `certification_id` is supplied, the logical clock is at or after that certificate's `valid_from` and strictly before its `valid_until`.
43. `ERR_SPECIES_NOT_CLAIM_ELIGIBLE` (422) — on approving a filed certificate: the certified species has `sustainability_program_eligible` true.
44. `ERR_CASE_SLOT_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422) — on casing a lot and on ice-bed reading submission: the submitted `case_slot` is at least 1 and at most the counter's `case_slot_count`.
45. `ERR_CASE_SLOT_OCCUPIED` (409) — on casing a lot: the requested slot is free under the closed-interval occupancy rule of §3.3.
46. `ERR_AD_WINDOW_INVALID` (422) — on advertisement drafting: `ad_end_at` is strictly after `ad_start_at`.
47. `ERR_AD_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) — on advertisement drafting: the requested window does not overlap the window of another advertisement for the same `lot_id` under the half-open rule of §3.6.
48. `ERR_READING_IN_FUTURE` (422) — on ice-bed reading submission: `reading_at` is not later than the logical clock.
49. `ERR_SIMP_DATE_ORDER` (422) — on import-monitoring document creation and on submission: `harvest_date` is not later than `landing_date`.
50. `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REMAINING_WEIGHT` (422) — on recording sales: `sold_weight_kg` is not greater than the lot's `remaining_weight_kg`.
51. `ERR_SHRINK_EXCEEDS_REMAINING` (422) — on shrink-event creation: `shrink_weight_kg` is not greater than the lot's `remaining_weight_kg`.
52. `ERR_CONVERSION_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED` (422) — on conversion approval: `conversion_requested_kg` is not greater than the `allowance_kg` derived in §3.9.
53. `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_PERMITTED` (422) — on conversion approval: the event's `reason` is `UNSOLD_FRESH`, the lot's `previously_frozen` is false, and `conversion_requested_kg` is greater than 0.000.
54. `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422) — on chargeback settlement: `payment_amount_cents` equals the supplier's `chargeback_outstanding_cents` exactly.
55. `ERR_SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD` (402) — on lot receipt: the shipping supplier's `chargeback_outstanding_cents` is strictly below 75,000 cents at the moment of receipt.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Multi-Entity Side Effects

Many operations in this system mutate records the caller never named. Two multi-hop chains dominate the design and are referred to throughout §3 as the **substitution cascade** and the **cold-chain cascade**.

The substitution cascade begins when a laboratory result contradicts the declared species. Recording that result drives the sample to `MISMATCH` and the lot's `label_status` to `MISMATCHED`; the transition into `MISMATCHED` raises a `SPECIES_SUBSTITUTION` compliance violation worth 6 points and withdraws every `PUBLISHED` case label for that lot with a `withdraw_reason` of `SPECIES_MISMATCH`; withdrawing a published label for that particular reason creates a `ShrinkEvent` for the lot with a `reason` of `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION`; creating a `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` shrink event drives the lot's `chargeback_status` from `NONE` to `ASSESSED`; and the assessment increases the dealer's `chargeback_assessed_total_cents` and `chargeback_outstanding_cents` and recomputes the dealer's `status`. Five hops, each of which mutates a different record and therefore refreshes that record's `updated_at` and increments its `version`.

The cold-chain cascade begins when a probe reading exceeds the ice-bed limit. The breaching reading opens (or extends) the slot's excursion; a newly opened excursion holds every lot currently `CASED` in that slot, moving each to `HELD`; each newly held lot has every `PUBLISHED` case label withdrawn with a `withdraw_reason` of `COLD_CHAIN_HOLD`; and each such label withdrawal retracts every `PUBLISHED` advertisement pointing at that lot, setting `retract_reason` to `LABEL_WITHDRAWN` and raising a `COOL_MISSING` compliance violation worth 4 points.

Three discipline rules govern all cascades. First, the conditional hop is genuinely conditional: the `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` shrink event fires only for a withdrawal whose `withdraw_reason` is `SPECIES_MISMATCH`, and never for a withdrawal reasoned `MANUAL`, `SUPERSEDED`, `COLD_CHAIN_HOLD` or `CLAIM_REVOKED`. Second, forward edges are idempotent at their source dimension: because the substitution cascade hangs off the lot's entry into `MISMATCHED`, a second mismatch result on a lot whose `label_status` is already `MISMATCHED` — the retest case — advances the sample alone. No second violation, no second label withdrawal, no second shrink event and no second chargeback are produced, and the dealer's `lifetime_sample_count` and `lifetime_mismatch_count` are counted once per sample rather than once per result. Third, a cascade never silently skips a hop it cannot complete; the preconditions stated in §3 for each hop are conditions on which records participate, not permission to abandon the chain.

### 2.11. Ledger Identities: Counter Shrink and Supplier Chargeback

The counter's shrink ledger consists of three running integer totals: `shrink_gross_cents`, the sum of `provisional_value_cents` over the counter's posted shrink events; `shrink_net_cents`, the sum of `final_value_cents` over the same events; and `recovered_conversion_cents`, the sum of `recovery_credit_cents` over the same events. Because a shrink event's `recovery_credit_cents` always equals its `provisional_value_cents` minus its `final_value_cents` by construction, the identity that gross equals net plus recovered holds at all times, and survives both the forward posting of an event and its later reversal with zero drift.

The dealer's chargeback ledger consists of four running integer totals: `chargeback_assessed_total_cents`, the lifetime sum of every amount ever assessed against the dealer; `chargeback_outstanding_cents`, the assessed amount neither settled nor reversed; `chargeback_settled_cents`, the assessed amount closed by dealer payment; and `chargeback_reversed_cents`, the assessed amount voided by a compliance reversal. A reversal never reduces `chargeback_assessed_total_cents`, so the identity that the assessed total equals outstanding plus settled plus reversed holds permanently.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Chain Configuration and Lot Receipt

Configuration is the `ChainAdmin`'s domain. Registering a seafood service counter takes a `store_number` — exactly 4 characters, all digits — a `department_name` for display, and a `case_slot_count` between 1 and 12 inclusive giving the number of addressable ice-bed slots in the service case. A freshly registered counter starts with `posture` `OPEN`, `verification_cycle_index` 0, `open_excursion_count` 0, `violation_points` 0, `last_cycle_required_samples` 0, `last_cycle_max_supplier_risk_index` 0.00, and all three shrink ledger totals — `shrink_gross_cents`, `shrink_net_cents` and `recovered_conversion_cents` — at 0.

Registering a dealer takes the supplier's legal `name` and its dealer `permit_number`. No natural key in this system is enforced as unique — a second counter may be registered under an already-used `store_number`, a second dealer under an already-used `permit_number`, and a second lot under an already-used `lot_code`, each attempt succeeding and minting its own distinct record. A new dealer starts in `GOOD_STANDING` with `lifetime_sample_count` 0, `lifetime_mismatch_count` 0, both risk indices — `substitution_risk_index_rolling` and `substitution_risk_index_lifetime` — at 0.00, and all four chargeback totals at 0.

Species reference data is immutable once written. Each species record carries the `market_name` printed on the case label, the `scientific_name` used as the comparison basis for laboratory verification, an `acceptable_market_names` array listing every market name permitted for that species — a required array of non-empty strings which may legitimately be empty and which the server does not require to contain the species' own `market_name` — and three booleans, each of them required that drive gating elsewhere in the system: `simp_covered` marks a species covered by the seafood import monitoring program, `high_risk_substitution` marks a species on the substitution watchlist, and `sustainability_program_eligible` marks a species for which counter sustainability claims may be made at all.

Receiving a lot is the `SeafoodManager`'s first act, and it is the moment at which every sourcing disclosure is captured. The receipt payload names the `counter_id` taking the lot, the `supplier_id` that shipped it and the `species_id` as declared by that supplier, plus the `lot_code` printed on the tote. The disclosures themselves are `harvest_method`, one of `WILD_NET`, `WILD_LONGLINE`, `WILD_TRAP`, `AQUACULTURE_POND` or `AQUACULTURE_PEN`; `production_method`, either `WILD` or `FARMED`; `origin_country_code`, the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country of harvest or grow-out given as exactly 2 uppercase letters; and `processing_country_code`, an optional ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code used when processing happened somewhere other than the country of origin, given as exactly 2 uppercase letters and defaulting to null when omitted. Two booleans capture the freezing history: `previously_frozen` states that the lot was frozen and thawed before display, and `fresh_claim` states that the counter intends to advertise the lot as never-frozen fresh. Submitting both as true is a contradiction in terms and is rejected with `ERR_FROZEN_DISCLOSURE_CONFLICT`. Finally the payload carries the commercials: `received_weight_kg`, greater than zero and carried to 3 decimal places; `unit_cost_cents_per_kg`, the landed cost per kilogram in cents and greater than zero; `retail_price_cents_per_kg`, the fresh case retail price per kilogram in cents and greater than zero; and `cooked_retail_price_cents_per_kg`, the cook-and-sell retail price per kilogram in cents and greater than zero.

Receipt is refused when the department is not operating freely — a `RESTRICTED` counter fails with `ERR_COUNTER_RESTRICTED` and a `SUSPENDED` counter with `ERR_COUNTER_SUSPENDED` — and it is also refused on credit grounds. A dealer whose `chargeback_outstanding_cents` has reached 75,000 cents, which is to say 750 dollars of unsettled mislabelling recovery, is on credit hold, and any attempt to receive one of its lots fails with `ERR_SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD`. The threshold is inclusive at 75,000 cents: exactly that balance is enough to block receipt.

A successfully received lot has `remaining_weight_kg` initialised to the full `received_weight_kg`, `received_at` stamped from the logical clock, and its three independent status dimensions initialised to `intake_status` `RECEIVED`, `label_status` `UNVERIFIED` and `chargeback_status` `NONE`, with `chargeback_assessed_cents` at 0 and `case_slot`, `cased_at` and `vacated_at` all null. Those three dimensions move independently for the rest of the lot's life: physical custody, label verification, and dealer recovery. Lots can be listed with filters on `counter_id`, `supplier_id`, `species_id`, `intake_status`, `label_status` and `chargeback_status`, and counters can be listed with filters on `store_number` and `posture`.

### 3.2. Import-Monitoring Documentation for Covered Species

For a species whose `simp_covered` flag is true, the paperwork is a precondition for sale, not an afterthought. A `SupplierRep` opens an import-monitoring record against the lot by naming its `lot_id` and supplying the `harvesting_vessel_name` — the name of the harvesting vessel or farm unit — the `harvest_date`, the `landing_date` of landing or first transfer, and a `chain_of_custody_ref` pointing at the custody documentation. The record is created in `DRAFT` with `amendment_count` 0, `review_note` null and `reviewed_at` null. An import-monitoring record carries no supplier identifier of its own — the dealer behind it is always reached through its `lot_id`, as §2.1 describes — so beyond the common identity, timestamp and `version` fields of §2.4 its projection is exactly `lot_id`, `harvesting_vessel_name`, `harvest_date`, `landing_date`, `chain_of_custody_ref`, `status`, `review_note`, `reviewed_at` and `amendment_count`. A `harvest_date` later than the `landing_date` is chronologically impossible and is rejected with `ERR_SIMP_DATE_ORDER`, both at creation and again on submission.

Submitting a record advances it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, again requiring that `harvest_date` be no later than `landing_date`; the submission may optionally carry a `chain_of_custody_ref`, and when it does, the supplied value replaces the stored one. The same submission action is also the amendment path: a record sitting in `DEFICIENT` returns to `SUBMITTED` and its `amendment_count` increases by one. Attempting to submit from any other state fails with `ERR_SIMP_STATE_INVALID`.

Review is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s call and takes a `decision` plus an optional `review_note` that is recorded on the record. A `decision` of `ACCEPT` moves the record from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `reviewed_at` from the logical clock, unblocks casing for the covered-species lot, and cures any `ComplianceViolation` for that lot whose `category` is `SIMP_DEFICIENT` and whose `status` is `OPEN` — those violations move to `CURED` with `cured_at` stamped from the logical clock and `cure_note` left null, which in turn re-derives the counter's posture at the halved weight, the posture re-derivation being skipped entirely when no such finding was open. A `decision` of `DEFICIENT` moves the record from `SUBMITTED` to `DEFICIENT`, stamps `reviewed_at`, and raises a `ComplianceViolation` with `category` `SIMP_DEFICIENT` and `points` 5 against the counter. The optional `review_note` is written on either decision and is written as null when the field is omitted, so a later review overwrites whatever note an earlier one left. `ACCEPTED` is the terminal state of an import-monitoring record; `DEFICIENT` is not, because the amendment loop exists precisely so a dealer can correct and resubmit.

### 3.3. Ice-Bed Casing, Sales and Slot Discipline

Casing is what makes a lot saleable, and it is the only route by which a lot becomes eligible to carry a case label. The `SeafoodManager` names the target `case_slot` and the lot advances from `RECEIVED` to `CASED`, at which point the lot's `case_slot` is set to the requested slot and `cased_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Casing also refreshes the counter's verification-sampling candidate membership, since the candidate set is defined over cased and held lots.

Casing is legal only from `intake_status` `RECEIVED`, so a lot already `CASED`, `HELD`, `DEPLETED` or `DISPOSED` cannot be cased again and the attempt fails with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. Four further conditions guard the transition. The counter's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`. The requested `case_slot` must be a required integer — a missing or non-integer value is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and must be at least 1 and at most the counter's `case_slot_count`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_CASE_SLOT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The slot must be genuinely free, which the system evaluates as a **closed interval** running from `cased_at` through `vacated_at` with a 30-minute sanitation buffer appended: no other lot on the counter may currently hold the requested slot with an `intake_status` of `CASED` or `HELD`, and for the slot's most recent previous occupant — the lot with the latest `vacated_at` among those the counter has ever vacated out of that same slot, ties going to the later-created record — the logical clock must be at or after that occupant's `vacated_at` plus 30 minutes. Either failure yields `ERR_CASE_SLOT_OCCUPIED`. And when the lot's species has `simp_covered` true, an import-monitoring record for that lot must exist with `status` `ACCEPTED`; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_SIMP_DOCUMENT_MISSING`.

Sales are recorded against a cased lot — `intake_status` exactly `CASED`, any other custody state failing with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` — as a `sold_weight_kg` figure greater than zero and carried to 3 decimal places, which decreases the lot's `remaining_weight_kg` by that amount. A `sold_weight_kg` greater than the lot's current `remaining_weight_kg` is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REMAINING_WEIGHT`. When a sale brings `remaining_weight_kg` to exactly 0.000, the lot advances from `CASED` to `DEPLETED`: `vacated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `case_slot` is cleared to null, and every `PUBLISHED` case label for that lot is withdrawn with a `withdraw_reason` of `MANUAL`. `DEPLETED` and `DISPOSED` are the terminal custody states; a write against a lot already in either of them fails with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, as does verification-sample creation against a lot that is not `CASED` or `HELD`.

The third route out of `CASED` runs through mislabelling. When a `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` shrink event whose `shrink_weight_kg` equals the lot's entire `remaining_weight_kg` is posted, the lot's `remaining_weight_kg` is set to 0.000, `vacated_at` is stamped and `case_slot` is cleared, and the lot moves from `CASED` to `DISPOSED`. Posting is only legal from a shrink event whose `status` is `VALUED` or `CONVERTED`.

### 3.4. Case Labels and Mandatory Disclosure Composition

The card above the ice bed is the customer-facing embodiment of everything captured at receipt, and the system composes it rather than accepting free text. A `SeafoodManager` publishes a label by naming the `lot_id`, optionally a `certification_id` carrying a sustainability claim, and the `expected_lot_version` required by §2.8. Publication is refused while the counter has any excursion whose `status` is `OPEN`, with `ERR_OPEN_EXCURSION_BLOCKS` — a label may not go up over a case whose temperature history is unresolved.

Four statement fields plus the claim text are derived at publication from the lot, its species and the referenced certificate. `display_market_name` is copied verbatim from the species' `market_name`. `production_method_statement` is the literal string `WILD CAUGHT` when the lot's `production_method` is `WILD`, and the literal string `FARM RAISED` otherwise. `origin_statement` depends on the same designation: for a `WILD` lot it is the words `Wild caught in `, then the lot's `origin_country_code`, then a space, then the lot's `harvest_method` enclosed in parentheses — for example, `Wild caught in US (WILD_LONGLINE)`. For a `FARMED` lot whose `processing_country_code` is non-null and differs from its `origin_country_code`, it is the words `Farm raised in `, then the `origin_country_code`, then `, processed in `, then the `processing_country_code` — for example, `Farm raised in EC, processed in CN`. For any other `FARMED` lot, meaning one whose `processing_country_code` is null or equal to the `origin_country_code`, it is the words `Farm raised in ` followed by the `origin_country_code` alone. `frozen_disclosure_statement` is resolved by a three-way ladder tested in this order: if `previously_frozen` is true the statement is the literal `PREVIOUSLY FROZEN`; otherwise if `fresh_claim` is true it is the literal `FRESH - NEVER FROZEN`; otherwise it is the literal `FRESH`. `sustainability_claim_text` is null when no `certification_id` was supplied, and otherwise is copied verbatim from the referenced certificate's `claim_text`. The derivation reads the single named lot regardless of that lot's `intake_status` and `label_status`, its species, and — when non-null — the named certificate; nothing else.

A published label starts with `status` `PUBLISHED`, `published_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `withdraw_reason` and `withdrawn_at` both null. Publishing a new label for a lot that already has one is normal product behaviour: the prior `PUBLISHED` label for that lot moves to `WITHDRAWN` with a `withdraw_reason` of `SUPERSEDED`. Publication also increments the parent lot's `version` by one, which is why a client holding a stale `expected_lot_version` cannot publish twice off one read.

A label leaves the case in five distinguishable ways, and the `withdraw_reason` records which. An operator-initiated withdrawal — available to both the `SeafoodManager` and a `ComplianceOfficer` — carries a required `reason` in the payload, drawn from the same five members the server itself uses (`MANUAL`, `SUPERSEDED`, `SPECIES_MISMATCH`, `COLD_CHAIN_HOLD` and `CLAIM_REVOKED`; any other value is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), which is stored verbatim in `withdraw_reason`, and stamps `withdrawn_at` from the logical clock. An operator-initiated withdrawal never carries a cascade of any kind: whichever of those five reasons it names — `SPECIES_MISMATCH` included — it creates no shrink event, retracts no advertisement and raises no violation, because the destruction, cold-chain and claim-revocation hops described next hang off the server-driven withdrawals alone. Superseding by a newer label sets `SUPERSEDED`. The substitution cascade sets `SPECIES_MISMATCH`, and this is the one reason that triggers destruction accounting: the withdrawal creates a `ShrinkEvent` for the lot with `reason` `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION`, `shrink_weight_kg` equal to the lot's `remaining_weight_kg`, `conversion_requested_kg` 0.000 and `status` `VALUED`. The cold-chain cascade sets `COLD_CHAIN_HOLD`; no shrink event is created for that reason, but this branch does carry the onward hop that retracts dependent advertisements described in §3.6. And a certificate leaving `ACTIVE` sets `CLAIM_REVOKED` on any label whose `certification_id` is not null and which is still `PUBLISHED`; that withdrawal raises a `ComplianceViolation` with `category` `UNSUBSTANTIATED_CLAIM` and `points` 6, and creates no shrink event. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal — a second withdrawal attempt on the same label fails with `ERR_CASE_LABEL_STATE_INVALID`. Labels can be listed with filters on `lot_id`, `certification_id`, `status` and `withdraw_reason`.

Whenever a `certification_id` is supplied on a label, the claim must actually be substantiated. The certificate must have `status` `ACTIVE` and its `counter_id`, `supplier_id` and `species_id` must each match the lot's `counter_id`, `supplier_id` and `species_id`; a failure on any of those four points is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`. Separately, the logical clock must fall at or after the certificate's `valid_from` and strictly before its `valid_until`, failing which the rejection is `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`.

### 3.5. Sustainability Program Certifications and Claim Enforcement

A dealer files a certificate for display at a specific counter. The filing names the `counter_id` permitted to display the claim, the certified `supplier_id`, the certified `species_id`, the certifying `program_name`, the `certificate_number` the program issued, the inclusive `valid_from` and exclusive `valid_until` bounds of validity, and the exact `claim_text` that may be shown at the counter. Filing itself compares neither bound against the other nor against the logical clock — an inverted or already-elapsed window is accepted at filing and only bites at review — and a filed certificate begins in `PENDING_REVIEW` with `review_note` null.

Review takes a `decision` plus an optional `review_note`. A `decision` of `APPROVE` moves the certificate from `PENDING_REVIEW` to `ACTIVE`, after which its `claim_text` becomes usable on case labels and advertisements. Approval requires two things beyond the state check, tested in this order: the certified species must have `sustainability_program_eligible` true, failing which the decision is rejected with `ERR_SPECIES_NOT_CLAIM_ELIGIBLE`, and only then must the logical clock fall at or after `valid_from` and strictly before `valid_until`, failing which the rejection is `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. A `decision` of `REJECT` moves the certificate from `PENDING_REVIEW` straight to `REVOKED`. Either decision records the optional `review_note` on the certificate, writing it as null when the field is omitted. A review decision addressed to a certificate that is not in `PENDING_REVIEW` fails with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Enforcement is the mechanism by which an already-active certificate stops being usable, and it accepts a `decision` plus an optional `note`. A `decision` of `REVOKE` represents the program withdrawing the certificate and moves it from `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED`. A `decision` of `SWEEP` represents the expiry sweep and moves it from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`, but only when the logical clock has reached or passed the certificate's `valid_until`; a sweep attempted while the clock is still strictly before `valid_until` is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`. The optional `note` is recorded in the certificate's `review_note` field — the certificate carries no separate enforcement-note field — and a `note` that is omitted leaves whatever `review_note` the certificate already held untouched. Both dispositions carry the identical downstream cascade, and it is deliberately asymmetric between the two carriers of the claim. Every `CaseLabel` whose `certification_id` names this certificate and whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with a `withdraw_reason` of `CLAIM_REVOKED`, and each such withdrawal raises a `ComplianceViolation` with `category` `UNSUBSTANTIATED_CLAIM` and `points` 6. Every `AdvertisedItem` whose `certification_id` names this certificate and whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` moves to `RETRACTED` with a `retract_reason` of `CLAIM_REVOKED`, and that retraction raises no violation of its own — the label-side finding already accounts for the unsubstantiated claim. `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal. Certificates can be listed with filters on `counter_id`, `supplier_id`, `species_id` and `status`.

### 3.6. The Advertising Review Fork and Publication Window

Advertising is where a labelling error becomes a public statement, so an advertisement's path to publication forks into two independent compliance reviews that must both clear before it can be published at all. A `MerchandisingLead` drafts the advertisement by naming the `counter_id` whose posture gates publication, the `lot_id` the advertisement points at, optionally a `certification_id` carrying a sustainability claim, the `headline` copy, and the advertising window as an inclusive `ad_start_at` and an exclusive `ad_end_at`. The draft is created with `status` `DRAFT`, `cool_review_status` `COOL_PENDING`, `claim_review_status` `CLAIM_PENDING`, `cool_statement` null, both reviewer identifiers null and `retract_reason` null. The named `counter_id` and the named `lot_id` are resolved independently and are not required to agree: the suspension gate and the later publication gates read the advertisement's own `counter_id`, while claim substantiation and the origin statement read the lot, so an advertisement may legitimately name a counter other than the one holding its lot. Drafting also imposes no condition on the lot's `intake_status` — that condition arrives at the country-of-origin review.

Two window rules apply at drafting. `ad_end_at` must be strictly after `ad_start_at`, failing which the draft is rejected with `ERR_AD_WINDOW_INVALID`. And the advertising window is a **half-open interval** running from `ad_start_at` inclusive to `ad_end_at` exclusive, with no buffer whatsoever: it must not overlap the window of any other `AdvertisedItem` for the same `lot_id` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `APPROVED` or `PUBLISHED`. Two windows that merely touch, where one ends at exactly the instant the next begins, are permitted; a genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_AD_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Note the contrast with the ice-bed slot rule of §3.3, which is a closed interval carrying a 30-minute buffer — the two occupancy rules are deliberately different, because a sanitation gap matters physically and an advertising gap does not.

The country-of-origin branch is decided by a `ComplianceOfficer` submitting a `decision`, optionally a `rejection_category`, and optionally a `note` — a note the server validates as a string but does not persist, the `AdvertisedItem` carrying no field for it. Both dispositions require the advertisement to still be in `DRAFT` *and* its `cool_review_status` to still be `COOL_PENDING`, failing either of which the request is rejected with `ERR_AD_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. A `decision` of `CLEAR` additionally requires the lot behind the advertisement to be in `CASED` or `HELD` custody, a lot in any other custody state being answered with `ERR_AD_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`; it advances `cool_review_status` from `COOL_PENDING` to `COOL_CLEARED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` in `cool_reviewer_user_id`, and composes the advertisement's `cool_statement`. That statement is the same four derived facts as the case label, concatenated in exactly this order and separated by a space, a vertical bar and a space: the `display_market_name`, then the `production_method_statement`, then the `origin_statement`, then the `frozen_disclosure_statement`. A `decision` of `REJECT` requires a `rejection_category` to be supplied and to be either `COOL_MISSING` or `FROZEN_DISCLOSURE`, an omitted or out-of-enumeration value being an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the membership test applies to the field whenever it is present, even under a `CLEAR` decision that will then ignore the value; it advances `cool_review_status` to `COOL_REJECTED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` in `cool_reviewer_user_id`, and raises a `ComplianceViolation` whose `category` is exactly the submitted `rejection_category` — worth 4 `points` in either case.

The sustainability-claim branch is decided by a different `ComplianceOfficer` submitting a `decision` and optionally a `note`, which like the origin branch's note is validated as a string and then discarded. Both dispositions require the advertisement to still be in `DRAFT` *and* its `claim_review_status` to still be `CLAIM_PENDING`, failing either of which the rejection is `ERR_AD_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, and both require the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the recorded `cool_reviewer_user_id`, per §2.1 — a comparison made before the state test, so a same-officer request against an already-decided advertisement reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `decision` of `CLEAR` additionally requires that either the advertisement carries no `certification_id` at all, or the named certificate satisfies the full substantiation test of §2.9 item 41 — `status` `ACTIVE` and `counter_id`, `supplier_id` and `species_id` all equal to those of the advertisement's lot, failing which the rejection is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` — with the logical clock at or after its `valid_from` and strictly before its `valid_until`; it advances `claim_review_status` from `CLAIM_PENDING` to `CLAIM_CLEARED` and records the acting `X-User-ID` in `claim_reviewer_user_id`. A `decision` of `REJECT` advances `claim_review_status` to `CLAIM_REJECTED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` in `claim_reviewer_user_id`, and raises a `ComplianceViolation` with `category` `UNSUBSTANTIATED_CLAIM` and `points` 6. The two branches raise different findings by design: the origin branch raises `COOL_MISSING` or `FROZEN_DISCLOSURE`, the claim branch raises `UNSUBSTANTIATED_CLAIM`.

The publication dimension is a join over those two branches, and the server drives it without a client request. Once `cool_review_status` is `COOL_CLEARED` **and** `claim_review_status` is `CLAIM_CLEARED`, the advertisement's `status` moves from `DRAFT` to `APPROVED` and it becomes eligible for publication; neither branch alone advances `status` out of `DRAFT`. Conversely, as soon as either `cool_review_status` becomes `COOL_REJECTED` or `claim_review_status` becomes `CLAIM_REJECTED`, the advertisement's `status` moves from `DRAFT` to `REJECTED` and it is permanently unpublishable.

Publishing an `APPROVED` advertisement is the `MerchandisingLead`'s act and makes the window live. It requires the counter's `posture` to be exactly `OPEN` — a `RESTRICTED` counter fails with `ERR_COUNTER_RESTRICTED` and a `SUSPENDED` one with `ERR_COUNTER_SUSPENDED` — and it requires a `CaseLabel` for the advertisement's `lot_id` to exist with `status` `PUBLISHED`, because an advertisement may not stand in front of product that carries no label at the case; the absence of such a label is reported as `ERR_AD_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, and the posture gates are tested before it. Closing retires a live window normally, moving `status` from `PUBLISHED` to `CLOSED`, and is legal only once the logical clock has reached or passed the advertisement's `ad_end_at` — a close attempted while the clock is still strictly before `ad_end_at` fails with `ERR_AD_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, as does a close addressed to an advertisement that is not `PUBLISHED`. Retracting pulls a live window early, moving `status` from `PUBLISHED` to `RETRACTED`; the request carries a required non-empty `reason` string that may hold any text and is not itself stored, because for an operator-initiated retraction — available to both the `MerchandisingLead` and a `ComplianceOfficer` — the recorded `retract_reason` is always the literal `MANUAL`. The server also retracts on its own initiative in two situations already described: when a `PUBLISHED` case label for the advertisement's lot is withdrawn as part of the cold-chain cascade, in which case `retract_reason` is `LABEL_WITHDRAWN` and a `ComplianceViolation` with `category` `COOL_MISSING` and `points` 4 is raised; and when the certificate the advertisement carries leaves `ACTIVE`, in which case `retract_reason` is `CLAIM_REVOKED` and no violation is raised. `CLOSED`, `RETRACTED` and `REJECTED` are all terminal. Advertisements can be listed with filters on `counter_id`, `lot_id`, `certification_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Cold Chain Monitoring and Excursion Disposition

Ice-bed temperature is verified by probe. A `SeafoodManager` submits a reading naming the `counter_id` whose case was probed, the `case_slot` that was probed, the `reading_at` instant of the probe, the `temperature_c` in degrees Celsius carried to 1 decimal place — unbounded in either direction, so a deep-chill negative reading is accepted — and the `taken_by_user_id` of the associate who took it, a required non-empty string recorded verbatim and never compared against any principal or persisted entity. The `case_slot` must be at least 1 and at most the counter's `case_slot_count`, and `reading_at` must not be later than the logical clock — a reading from the future is rejected with `ERR_READING_IN_FUTURE`. Each stored reading carries a computed `breach` flag, true exactly when its `temperature_c` is **strictly greater** than the ice-bed limit of 4.4 degrees Celsius; a reading of exactly 4.4 is compliant and its `breach` is false. Readings can be listed with filters on `counter_id`, `case_slot` and `breach`.

Every submitted reading re-derives the slot's exposure picture by full chronological replay, so that a reading arriving out of order yields the same excursion as if it had arrived in time order. The replay first establishes the slot's cut-off: the latest `ended_at` among the excursions for this same `counter_id` and `case_slot` whose `status` is `RESOLVED_RELEASED` or `RESOLVED_DISPOSED`, or no cut-off at all when the slot has never had a resolved excursion. The breach set is then every `IceBedReading` for that `counter_id` and `case_slot` whose `temperature_c` exceeds 4.4 degrees and whose `reading_at` is strictly later than the cut-off, or all such breaching readings when there is no cut-off. If the breach set is empty, the slot has no open excursion and nothing further happens.

When the breach set is non-empty, the slot's open `ColdChainExcursion` is created if absent and recomputed if present, always from the whole breach set ordered by `reading_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Its `counter_id` is copied from the triggering reading and its `case_slot` is the probed slot; a newly created excursion opens with `status` `OPEN`, `resolution` null, `resolution_note` null, `exposure_minutes` at its computed value and `severity` derived as described below. Its `started_at` is the earliest `reading_at` in the breach set and its `ended_at` the latest; its `exposure_minutes` is the elapsed time between those two instants expressed in whole minutes, rounded half up; and its `peak_temperature_c` is the highest `temperature_c` in the breach set. Severity follows from exposure alone: an exposure of at most 60 minutes — one hour — is `MINOR`; an exposure above that but at most 180 minutes — three hours — is `MAJOR`; anything longer is `CRITICAL`. A non-breaching reading never closes an open excursion; only an explicit disposition does.

A newly opened excursion is what puts product on hold. Every lot on that counter whose `case_slot` equals the excursion's `case_slot` and whose `intake_status` is `CASED` moves to `HELD`, which holds it out of sale while leaving its `remaining_weight_kg` untouched, and each newly held lot has all of its `PUBLISHED` case labels withdrawn with `withdraw_reason` `COLD_CHAIN_HOLD`, which in turn retracts the dependent published advertisements as described in §3.6. Opening an excursion also re-derives the counter's posture, and since one open excursion is enough to satisfy the restriction condition, a single breach immediately narrows the department's operating surface.

Disposition is a `ComplianceOfficer` act carrying a `resolution` of either `RELEASE` or `DISPOSE` plus an optional `note`; the submitted disposition is retained in the excursion's `resolution` field and the note in its `resolution_note`, which is written as null when no note is supplied. A `resolution` of `RELEASE` requires the excursion's `status` to be `OPEN` and its `severity` to be `MINOR` or `MAJOR` — a `CRITICAL` excursion may not be released, and the attempt fails with `ERR_EXCURSION_STATE_INVALID`. Release moves the excursion to `RESOLVED_RELEASED` and returns every lot on the counter in that slot whose `intake_status` is `HELD` back to `CASED`, restoring it to sale; case labels are deliberately **not** auto-republished, so a new label must be created before that lot can be sold under a label again. A `resolution` of `DISPOSE` requires only that the excursion's `status` be `OPEN` and is therefore the only route available for a `CRITICAL` excursion. Disposal moves the excursion to `RESOLVED_DISPOSED` and, for every lot on the counter in that slot whose `intake_status` is `HELD`, moves that lot to `DISPOSED`, stamps its `vacated_at` from the logical clock, clears its `case_slot` to null, creates a `ShrinkEvent` against it with `reason` `COLD_CHAIN_DISPOSAL`, `shrink_weight_kg` equal to that lot's `remaining_weight_kg`, `conversion_requested_kg` 0.000 and `status` `VALUED`, and raises a `ComplianceViolation` with `category` `COLD_CHAIN` and `points` 3. Either disposition decreases the counter's `open_excursion_count` by one and re-derives the posture, which is how a suspended department climbs back toward `OPEN`. `RESOLVED_RELEASED` and `RESOLVED_DISPOSED` are both terminal. Excursions can be listed with filters on `counter_id`, `case_slot`, `status` and `severity`.

### 3.8. Verification Sampling, the Substitution Cascade and Supplier Recovery

Label verification is the counter's defence against species substitution, and it runs as a repeating cycle rather than a one-shot check. A `ComplianceOfficer` draws a sample from a lot by naming the `lot_id` and the `sampled_weight_g` mass of the drawn sample in grams, which must be greater than zero. Drawing requires the lot's `intake_status` to be `CASED` or `HELD`. The sample is created with `status` `DRAFT`, its `cycle_index` stamped from the owning counter's current `verification_cycle_index`, and `observed_species_id`, `lab_reference`, `dispute_reason` and `result_recorded_at` all null with `retest_count` 0. Drawing the sample also advances the lot's `label_status` — from `UNVERIFIED` to `SAMPLED` on a first pass, and from `RELABELED` back to `SAMPLED` when a corrected lot is re-sampled, which is the edge that closes the verification loop `SAMPLED` → `MISMATCHED` → `RELABELED` → `SAMPLED`. In both cases the new sample's `cycle_index` is stamped from the counter's `verification_cycle_index` at the moment of drawing. Drawing from a lot whose `label_status` is anything else — `SAMPLED` already, `VERIFIED` or `MISMATCHED` — is equally permitted and simply leaves that `label_status` where it stands: the only precondition on the lot is the custody one, and nothing about the label dimension blocks a further sample.

Sending a sample to the laboratory takes a `lab_reference` accession string and moves the sample from `DRAFT` to `IN_LAB`. The same action serves as the retest path: a sample in `DISPUTED` returns to `IN_LAB`, its `retest_count` increases by one, and the submitted `lab_reference` replaces the stored one. Both the dispute transition and the retest transition require `retest_count` to be strictly below 2, so the loop `IN_LAB` → `MISMATCH` → `DISPUTED` → `IN_LAB` is bounded at two retests; a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Recording the result takes an `observed_species_id` naming the species the laboratory actually identified, stamps `result_recorded_at` from the logical clock, and requires the sample to be `IN_LAB`. When `observed_species_id` equals the lot's `species_id`, the sample reaches `MATCH` — a terminal state — the lot's `label_status` becomes `VERIFIED`, the dealer's `lifetime_sample_count` increases by one, both substitution risk index variants are recomputed for that dealer, and the lot leaves the sampling candidate set. When `observed_species_id` differs from the lot's `species_id`, the sample reaches `MISMATCH` and the substitution cascade of §2.10 fires: the dealer's `lifetime_sample_count` and `lifetime_mismatch_count` each increase by one with both risk index variants recomputed, the lot's `label_status` becomes `MISMATCHED`, a `ComplianceViolation` with `category` `SPECIES_SUBSTITUTION` and `points` 6 is raised, every `PUBLISHED` case label for the lot is withdrawn with `withdraw_reason` `SPECIES_MISMATCH`, that withdrawal creates the `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` shrink event, and the shrink event's creation drives the lot's `chargeback_status` from `NONE` to `ASSESSED`. The idempotency guard of §2.10 applies in full: because the cascade hangs off the lot's *entry* into `MISMATCHED`, a repeat mismatch result on a lot already sitting in `MISMATCHED` advances the sample and nothing else. Because the dealer's counters are keyed to the sample rather than to the result, a second result recorded against the same sample after a retest leaves `lifetime_sample_count` alone and adjusts `lifetime_mismatch_count` only when the sample's own verdict flips: a retest that turns a former `MISMATCH` into a `MATCH` decreases `lifetime_mismatch_count` by one, and a retest that confirms the mismatch changes neither counter. A `MATCH` result always drives the lot's `label_status` to `VERIFIED`, including when the lot was sitting in `MISMATCHED` before the retest cleared it.

A `SupplierRep` may contest a mismatch by submitting a `dispute_reason`, which moves the sample from `MISMATCH` to `DISPUTED` and stores the stated grounds. Disputes become moot once the lot has actually been corrected: relabelling voids every `DISPUTED` sample on that lot, moving each to `VOIDED`, which along with `MATCH` is a terminal sample state.

Relabelling is the `SeafoodManager`'s correction path and takes a `corrected_species_id` and a `corrected_market_name`. It requires the lot's `label_status` to be `MISMATCHED`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_LABEL_STATE_INVALID`. The `corrected_species_id` must equal the `observed_species_id` of the most recent `VerificationSample` for that lot whose `status` is `MISMATCH`, where recency is decided by `result_recorded_at` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break and, when those too are equal, the later-created record winning; a different species — or the absence of any `MISMATCH` sample on the lot at all — is rejected with `ERR_RELABEL_SPECIES_MISMATCH`. The `corrected_market_name` must be a member of the `acceptable_market_names` array of the species named by `corrected_species_id`, failing which the rejection is `ERR_MARKET_NAME_NOT_ACCEPTABLE`. On success the lot's `species_id` is replaced by `corrected_species_id`, the `label_status` moves from `MISMATCHED` to `RELABELED`, and disputed samples are voided. Any `ComplianceViolation` for that lot with `category` `SPECIES_SUBSTITUTION` and `status` `OPEN` deliberately stays `OPEN` — curing a finding is a separate compliance act, not a by-product of fixing the sign.

Two risk indices are maintained per dealer, both recomputed whenever a result is recorded and again during cycle close, and they answer different questions. The **rolling** index sizes the next cycle's sampling load and decays. Its input is the set of `VerificationSample` records whose `status` is exactly `MISMATCH` and whose lot belongs to this dealer; samples sitting in `DISPUTED`, `VOIDED`, `MATCH`, `IN_LAB` or `DRAFT` are excluded entirely. Walking that set in `created_at` ascending order, each mismatch contributes a base weight of 40 points halved once for every complete verification cycle that has elapsed since it was drawn — the elapsed count being the current `verification_cycle_index` of the counter owning that sample's lot minus the sample's own `cycle_index`, which is never negative. A mismatch drawn in the current cycle therefore contributes its full 40, one cycle later 20, two cycles later 10, and three cycles later 5. To that decayed sum a flat bonus of 10 points is added, once and only once, if any mismatch in the set sits on a lot whose species has `high_risk_substitution` true; the bonus is never decayed. The total is capped at 100.00 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and stored as `substitution_risk_index_rolling`.

The **lifetime** index drives dealer standing and never decays. When the dealer's `lifetime_sample_count` is 0, `substitution_risk_index_lifetime` is 0.00. Otherwise it is 100 multiplied by the dealer's `lifetime_mismatch_count`, divided by the dealer's `lifetime_sample_count`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — a pure mismatch-rate percentage with no decay and no high-risk bonus. The two variants diverge precisely because of decay: an identical sample history that yields 50.00 lifetime yields only 30.00 rolling one cycle after the mismatch. Dealer standing is derived from the lifetime variant alone. If `lifetime_sample_count` is at least 2 and `substitution_risk_index_lifetime` is at least 60.00, the dealer's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED`; otherwise, if `lifetime_sample_count` is at least 2 and `substitution_risk_index_lifetime` is at least 25.00, the `status` becomes `PROBATION`; otherwise it is `GOOD_STANDING`. A dealer with exactly one sample on record therefore never leaves `GOOD_STANDING`, however that single sample resolved.

The dealer's monetary exposure is computed once, at the moment the lot's `chargeback_status` moves from `NONE` to `ASSESSED`, and stored in the lot's `chargeback_assessed_cents`. The computation runs in five numbered steps, each reading only values frozen by the earlier steps, and rounds half up to whole cents once per step. **Step one** values the product: the lot's `remaining_weight_kg` multiplied by its `retail_price_cents_per_kg`, rounded to whole cents. The weight read here is the one observed *before* any shrink event has been posted, which is consistent with the rule that creating a shrink event never decrements `remaining_weight_kg`. **Step two** builds the pre-credit subtotal: a flat base penalty of 25,000 cents — 250 dollars — plus the step-one lot value, plus a relabelling labour charge of 4,000 cents. **Step three** selects the goodwill rate from that subtotal, and from that subtotal only: a subtotal below 50,000 cents earns a 15 percent goodwill rate, a subtotal from 50,000 cents inclusive up to but not including 120,000 cents earns 8 percent, and a subtotal of 120,000 cents or more earns nothing at all. The boundary at exactly 50,000 cents belongs to the 8 percent band. The goodwill credit is the subtotal multiplied by the selected rate, rounded to whole cents. It is essential that the tier is keyed on the pre-credit subtotal and never on the post-credit net, even though the credit lowers the bill. **Step four** nets the credit off: the pre-credit subtotal minus the goodwill credit. **Step five** applies dealer standing: when the dealer's `status` is `PROBATION` the net is multiplied by 1.25, and otherwise by 1.00, with the result rounded to whole cents and stored as the lot's `chargeback_assessed_cents`. The dealer standing read in step five is the standing observed *before* this cascade's own recomputation of that dealer's `status`.

The assessment then updates the dealer's ledger: `chargeback_assessed_total_cents` and `chargeback_outstanding_cents` each increase by the assessed amount, and the dealer's `status` is recomputed. From `ASSESSED` the lot's chargeback dimension can close in exactly two ways. A `SupplierRep` settles by submitting a `payment_amount_cents` — a required integer of at least 0, a negative or non-integer figure being an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a settlement mismatch — that must equal the dealer's `chargeback_outstanding_cents` exactly, partial payment being rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; settlement moves `chargeback_status` to `SETTLED`, decreases the dealer's `chargeback_outstanding_cents` by the lot's `chargeback_assessed_cents` and increases the dealer's `chargeback_settled_cents` by the same amount, and leaves the dealer's `status` exactly where it stood, since the standing ladder reads only the lifetime sample history that a payment does not touch. A `ComplianceOfficer` reverses by submitting a `reversal_note` — required and non-empty, but not retained, the `SupplierLot` carrying no field for it — which moves `chargeback_status` to `REVERSED`, decreases the dealer's `chargeback_outstanding_cents` by the lot's `chargeback_assessed_cents`, increases the dealer's `chargeback_reversed_cents` by the same amount, and recomputes the dealer's `status`. Reversal is the compensating action for the forward assessment, and it deliberately never reduces `chargeback_assessed_total_cents`, so the ledger identity of §2.11 continues to hold. `SETTLED` and `REVERSED` are both terminal; addressing either action to a lot in any other chargeback state fails with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_STATE_INVALID`.

Closing the verification cycle is how the department freezes an audit-ready sampling plan and moves on, and it remains available from every posture. It carries only `expected_counter_version` and runs five steps in this pinned order. **Step one** computes the sampling plan at the counter's *current* `verification_cycle_index`. The candidate set is every lot on this counter whose `intake_status` is `CASED` or `HELD` and whose `label_status` is `UNVERIFIED` or `RELABELED`. If that set is empty, the plan short-circuits: `last_cycle_max_supplier_risk_index` is written as 0.00 and `last_cycle_required_samples` as 0 — not the base sample count — and step one stops. Otherwise the high-risk count is the number of candidate lots whose species has `high_risk_substitution` true, and `last_cycle_max_supplier_risk_index` is the greatest `substitution_risk_index_rolling` among the dealers behind those candidate lots, read before the index is advanced in step two and before the dealer indices are re-decayed in step three. `last_cycle_required_samples` is then a base of 1 sample, plus the high-risk count divided by 3 and rounded up to the next whole number, plus a surcharge of 2 further samples when `last_cycle_max_supplier_risk_index` is at least 40.00 — the surcharge comparison is inclusive, so exactly 40.00 triggers it. **Step two** increments the counter's `verification_cycle_index` by one. **Step three** recomputes both `substitution_risk_index_rolling` and `substitution_risk_index_lifetime` for every dealer with at least one lot on this counter, using the new cycle index, which is what makes older mismatches decay. **Step four** re-derives the counter's `posture`. **Step five** increments the counter's `version` by one. Samples can be listed with filters on `lot_id`, `observed_species_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Shrink Valuation, Cook-and-Sell Recovery and Violation Remediation

Fish that does not sell still costs money, and the shrink record is where that cost is quantified. A `SeafoodManager` writes shrink against a lot by naming the `lot_id`, a `reason` of `UNSOLD_FRESH`, `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` or `COLD_CHAIN_DISPOSAL`, a `shrink_weight_kg` greater than zero and carried to 3 decimal places, and optionally a `conversion_requested_kg` — the weight the counter asks to divert into cook-and-sell — which defaults to 0.000, must be at least 0.000 and is likewise carried to 3 decimal places. Writing shrink requires the lot's `intake_status` to be `RECEIVED`, `CASED` or `HELD` — product may be written down before it ever reaches the ice bed — while a lot already `DEPLETED` or `DISPOSED` is rejected with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. The `shrink_weight_kg` may not exceed the lot's current `remaining_weight_kg`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_SHRINK_EXCEEDS_REMAINING`. Crucially, creating a shrink event does **not** decrement the lot's `remaining_weight_kg`; only posting does. Shrink events are also created by the server, with `conversion_requested_kg` 0.000, at two points already described: the `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` event produced by the substitution cascade, and the `COLD_CHAIN_DISPOSAL` event produced by disposing of a held lot. The chargeback hop of §2.10 attaches to the reason rather than to the writer, so a client-written event whose `reason` is `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` assesses the dealer exactly as the cascade-created one does, and — as ever — only when the lot's `chargeback_status` is still `NONE`.

Every shrink event, whether client-written or server-created, is valued immediately at cost and lands in `status` `VALUED` with `provisional_value_cents` populated and `approved_conversion_kg`, `allowance_kg`, `cook_yield_kg`, `final_value_cents` and `recovery_credit_cents` all at their zero defaults, `posted_at`, `reversed_at` and `reversal_note` null. The provisional valuation is the sum of three line components, and **each component is rounded to whole cents before the three are added** — rounding once after summation is a different and incorrect answer. The first component is the product cost: the `shrink_weight_kg` multiplied by the lot's `unit_cost_cents_per_kg`, rounded half up to whole cents. The second is handling: the `shrink_weight_kg` multiplied by a handling rate of 85 cents per kilogram, rounded half up to whole cents. The third is disposal: the `shrink_weight_kg` multiplied by a base disposal rate of 40 cents per kilogram, then multiplied by a disposal factor determined by the `reason` — 1.0 for `UNSOLD_FRESH`, 1.5 for `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` and 2.0 for `COLD_CHAIN_DISPOSAL` — with the whole product rounded half up to whole cents. Those three rounded figures are added to give `provisional_value_cents`. This provisional figure is computed from the single event under mutation and its lot alone; sibling events on the same lot never enter it. Once written it is frozen and never recomputed.

The final valuation is the same provisional figure minus the event's `recovery_credit_cents`, so that `provisional_value_cents` always equals `final_value_cents` plus `recovery_credit_cents` by construction. It is evaluated at exactly two moments: on conversion, where the credit is whatever the cook-and-sell computation produced, and on posting directly from `VALUED`, where no diversion was ever taken and the credit is therefore 0 and `final_value_cents` equals `provisional_value_cents`.

Cook-and-sell diversion is the recovery path for product that is merely unsold rather than unsafe or mislabelled, and it is gated tightly. Approving a conversion requires the event's `status` to be `VALUED`, any other state failing with `ERR_SHRINK_STATE_INVALID`; it further requires the event's `reason` to be `UNSOLD_FRESH`, its `conversion_requested_kg` to be greater than 0.000, and the lot's `previously_frozen` to be false; any of those three failing yields `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_PERMITTED`. Approval computes two weights, both rounded half up to 3 decimal places. The `allowance_kg` is the smaller of the event's `shrink_weight_kg` and 60 percent of the lot's `received_weight_kg` — no more than three fifths of an entire received lot may ever be diverted into the cooked case. The `approved_conversion_kg` is the smaller of the requested `conversion_requested_kg` and that `allowance_kg`, floored at 0.000 so it is never negative. A `conversion_requested_kg` that exceeds the computed `allowance_kg` is rejected outright with `ERR_CONVERSION_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`, and per the ordering of §2.9 that allowance ceiling is tested *before* the three permission conditions, so a request that both overshoots its allowance and names a reason other than `UNSOLD_FRESH` reports `ERR_CONVERSION_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. On success the event moves from `VALUED` to `CONVERSION_APPROVED` and both `allowance_kg` and `approved_conversion_kg` are written onto it; a rejected approval writes neither and leaves both at their 0.000 defaults.

Converting cooks the approved weight and books the credit, moving the event from `CONVERSION_APPROVED` to `CONVERTED`. The cooked weight is the `approved_conversion_kg` multiplied by a cook yield ratio of 0.65 — cooking loses just over a third of the mass — multiplied again by one minus a quality penalty, with the result rounded half up to 3 decimal places and stored as `cook_yield_kg`. The penalty is the sum of two independent adders: 0.15 when the lot's `previously_frozen` is true, and 0.05 when the lot's `fresh_claim` is true. Because approval already requires `previously_frozen` to be false, the 0.15 adder cannot be reached through the approval path, but the 0.05 adder applies whenever the lot was received under a never-frozen claim. The gross recovery is then the `cook_yield_kg` multiplied by the lot's `cooked_retail_price_cents_per_kg`, rounded half up to whole cents, and the labour charge is the `approved_conversion_kg` multiplied by a cook-line labour rate of 90 cents per kilogram, also rounded half up to whole cents. The event's `recovery_credit_cents` is the gross recovery minus the labour charge, floored at 0 so a loss-making cook never produces a negative credit, and then capped at the event's frozen `provisional_value_cents` so a credit can never exceed the loss it offsets. Conversion also writes `final_value_cents` as the frozen `provisional_value_cents` minus that credit.

Posting is what actually moves money and weight. An event may be posted from `CONVERTED`, carrying the credit it earned, or directly from `VALUED`, in which case its `final_value_cents` is set equal to its `provisional_value_cents` and its `recovery_credit_cents` to 0. Either way, the lot's `remaining_weight_kg` decreases by the event's `shrink_weight_kg`, the counter's `shrink_gross_cents` increases by the event's `provisional_value_cents`, the counter's `shrink_net_cents` increases by its `final_value_cents`, the counter's `recovered_conversion_cents` increases by its `recovery_credit_cents` — which for the no-conversion path is an increase of 0 — and `posted_at` is stamped from the logical clock as the event reaches `POSTED`. When the posted event's `reason` is `MISLABEL_DESTRUCTION` and its `shrink_weight_kg` equals the lot's entire `remaining_weight_kg` — the comparison reading the weight as it stood immediately before this posting decremented it — the lot itself moves from `CASED` to `DISPOSED` as described in §3.3. That custody move applies only to a lot actually sitting in `CASED` at the moment of posting: a full-destruction posting against a lot in `RECEIVED`, `HELD`, `DEPLETED` or `DISPOSED` still decrements the weight and still moves the ledger, but leaves that lot's `intake_status`, `case_slot` and `vacated_at` exactly as they were.

Reversal is the compensating action for a posting and is a `ComplianceOfficer` act carrying a `reversal_note`. It requires the event's `status` to be `POSTED`. It increases the lot's `remaining_weight_kg` back by the event's `shrink_weight_kg`, decreases the counter's `shrink_gross_cents` by the event's `provisional_value_cents`, decreases the counter's `shrink_net_cents` by its `final_value_cents`, decreases the counter's `recovered_conversion_cents` by its `recovery_credit_cents`, stamps `reversed_at` from the logical clock, stores the submitted `reversal_note`, and leaves the event in the terminal state `REVERSED`. Because the credit is by construction the difference between the provisional and final figures, the three-way ledger identity of §2.11 survives both the posting and the reversal exactly. Any of these shrink actions addressed to an event in the wrong state fails with `ERR_SHRINK_STATE_INVALID`; shrink events can be listed with filters on `lot_id`, `reason` and `status`.

Compliance findings are the last piece, and they are what keep the posture calculation honest. Every finding is raised by the server, never by a client: it records the `counter_id` charged, the `lot_id` it was raised against — null for a counter-wide finding — the `category` that selects its weight, the `points` weight assigned at creation, and a system-generated `detail` describing the finding, and it opens in `status` `OPEN` with `cure_note`, `expunge_note`, `cured_at` and `expunged_at` all null. A `SeafoodManager` cures an open finding by submitting a `cure_note`, which moves it from `OPEN` to `CURED`, stamps `cured_at` from the logical clock, and re-derives the counter's posture with that finding now contributing its weight halved and rounded up rather than in full. A `ComplianceOfficer` expunges a finding by submitting an `expunge_note`, which moves it from `OPEN` or from `CURED` to the terminal `EXPUNGED`, stamps `expunged_at` from the logical clock, and re-derives the posture with that finding contributing nothing. Either action addressed to a finding in the wrong state fails with `ERR_VIOLATION_STATE_INVALID`. Findings can be listed with filters on `counter_id`, `lot_id`, `category` and `status`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new record into existence — registering a counter, a dealer or a species, receiving a lot, publishing a case label, drafting an advertisement, submitting an ice-bed reading, drawing a verification sample, opening an import-monitoring record, filing a certificate, or writing a shrink event — return `201 Created`. Every other operation, including all state transitions, all dispositions, all ledger movements, all retrievals and the logical-clock capability, returns `200 OK`. Error responses carry the HTTP status listed in §5.
