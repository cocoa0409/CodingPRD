# Disaster Shelter Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a hurricane, wildfire, flood, earthquake or tornado forces families out of their homes, the American Red Cross must stand up sheltering capacity within hours and keep it running for weeks. That work is not a single decision. It is a chain: a facility that was identified and inspected months earlier under a standing site agreement becomes usable only if its agreement is current; a shelter can open only when enough trained volunteers have physically arrived to staff it; and the clients who walk through the door need a bed, meals, health services, a way to find missing family, and eventually a recovery case that connects them to money and to partner agencies without duplicating benefits they have already received.

This document specifies the API that backs that chain. It covers the full disaster relief operation lifecycle — declaring an operation, inspecting and approving shelter sites, activating shelters, deploying credentialed volunteers on bounded rotations, registering clients and managing dormitory occupancy, recording feeding and health services, running recovery casework with referrals, disbursing donor-restricted financial assistance, tracking operation costs against restricted fund pools, and preserving blood collection continuity in the affected region.

Three constraints shape almost every rule that follows. First, shelter posture is a gate rather than a status: a great many operations are admissible only while a shelter is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, and posture itself moves automatically as staffing rises and falls. Second, donor money is not fungible — a restricted pool may only fund the assistance categories and regions its donors permitted, and every movement of that money must be reconstructible from a cost ledger. Third, the system is driven by a logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that credential expiries, travel lead times, no-show grace periods, bed turnover buffers, card redemption windows and case closure holds are all deterministic and testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section collects the rules that hold across the whole API: who may act, what a response looks like, how lists are paged, and the cross-cutting principles — posture gating, credentialing, interval integrity, fund accounting, precision, clock evaluation, and optimistic concurrency — that every workflow in §3 inherits.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two HTTP headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. The header-presence requirement applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock administration capability described in §2.10. When either header is absent or empty, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` and HTTP status 401. A request that omits a required body field, supplies a field the endpoint does not declare, or supplies a value of the wrong type is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP status 400.

Role authorization is a separate check and applies to every endpoint without exception. The system recognizes exactly eight roles, spelled as follows: `NationalHQAdmin`, `RegionalCoordinator`, `ShelterManager`, `Volunteer`, `Caseworker`, `FinanceOfficer`, `BloodServicesCoordinator`, and `Client`. If the acting `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the endpoint authorizes, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP status 403. `NationalHQAdmin` holds the widest mandate — system configuration, region creation, fund pool creation, and the logical clock — while each of the remaining roles is confined to its own operational lane. `RegionalCoordinator` owns the disaster lifecycle decisions: declaring operations, advancing operation phase, opening and closing shelters, inspecting sites, and approving or recalling deployments. `ShelterManager` runs the shelter floor: registration, dormitory, feeding, health services and reunification release. `Volunteer` checks into shifts and ends their own rotation. `Caseworker` owns recovery casework — eligibility verification, recovery planning, referrals and assistance submission. `FinanceOfficer` disburses: approving awards, issuing and redeeming cards, and booking cost ledger entries. `BloodServicesCoordinator` schedules drives, records collections and cancels drives. `Client` has read-only access to their own record.

Three of the eight roles are backed by a persisted entity, and the remaining five are not, which matters because it determines what `X-User-ID` is compared against. When `X-User-Role` is `ShelterManager` or `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Volunteer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Volunteer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Client`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`, and the client-record read is scoped so that a client may only read the record whose `id` matches. When `X-User-Role` is `NationalHQAdmin`, `RegionalCoordinator`, `Caseworker`, `FinanceOfficer` or `BloodServicesCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records such an identifier where a workflow stamps it — `coordinator_user_id` on a region, `assigned_caseworker_id` on a case, `approved_by_user_id` and `issued_by_user_id` on an assistance request, `eligibility_verified_by_user_id` on a case, `reunification_verified_by_user_id` on a registration, `inspector_user_id` on an inspection — but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. Opaque identifiers are still compared against each other where a rule requires two distinct operators, as the segregation-of-duties rules in §2.7 and §3.7 do.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a list of resources is paged. The `limit` query parameter caps how many items are returned; when it is omitted the server uses 10, and it accepts any value from 1 through 100 inclusive. The `offset` query parameter states how many items to skip and defaults to 0 when omitted. Unless a workflow states otherwise, list endpoints return resources ordered by their creation timestamp ascending.

Paged responses carry four additional fields inside the `meta` object of the response envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of resources matching the request across all pages, and `has_more` is true when further pages remain.

### 2.4. Shelter Posture as the Master Operational Gate

A shelter operation is never simply "on" or "off". It carries a `posture` field whose value is one of `PENDING_OPEN`, `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `CONSOLIDATING` or `CLOSED`. Every shelter operation begins life in `PENDING_OPEN` and reaches its only terminal value, `CLOSED`, at the end of its life. Posture is not merely descriptive: it is the gate that decides which downstream operations are admissible at that shelter, so a client cannot be registered and a volunteer cannot be checked into a shift unless the shelter's posture currently permits it.

The two working postures are `OPEN` and `RESTRICTED`. While a shelter sits in `RESTRICTED`, any attempt to register a new client is refused with `ERR_SHELTER_RESTRICTED` at HTTP status 409. Checking a volunteer into a scheduled shift requires the shelter to be in either `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` posture. A shelter that is `PENDING_OPEN`, `CONSOLIDATING` or `CLOSED` admits neither operation. Posture is also what the disaster relief operation counts: an operation's `open_shelter_count` is the number of shelter operations beneath it whose posture is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, so `PENDING_OPEN`, `CONSOLIDATING` and `CLOSED` shelters do not count toward it.

Movement between the working postures is automatic and driven entirely by staffing, never by an explicit operator request. Whenever a shelter's `staffing_sufficiency` falls below 0.60, the shelter moves to `RESTRICTED` on its own. When `staffing_sufficiency` climbs back to 0.60 or above, it returns to `OPEN` on its own. Bed capacity deliberately plays no part in this gate. Capacity is enforced by a separate check at registration time — a registration that would push `occupied_beds` past the site's `capacity_verified_beds` is refused with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` at HTTP status 409 — so a shelter that is completely full but fully staffed stays `OPEN` and simply rejects the next registration on the capacity rule. The two mechanisms are independent, and the staffing gate must never be re-derived from occupancy.

A shelter leaves the working postures in one of two ways. An operator closing a shelter whose `registered_client_count` is greater than zero moves it to `CONSOLIDATING`, which is the wind-down posture: the shelter still exists and its remaining clients are still being served, but it is no longer taking new ones. Closing a shelter whose `registered_client_count` is zero moves it straight to `CLOSED`. `CONSOLIDATING` is not a one-way door. If a new client registration is created at a shelter that is currently `CONSOLIDATING` — which happens when a client is transferred in from a shelter that has already closed — the shelter returns to `RESTRICTED`, and from there the ordinary staffing gate can lift it back to `OPEN`. Reaching `CLOSED` is the end of the road: `CLOSED` is terminal and rejects every further transition with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` at HTTP status 409. The same terminal rule applies to any resource that has reached one of its terminal states, and a request to move a resource that is not in a state admitting the requested transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP status 409.

Shelter site health propagates downward into posture. When an inspection of a `ShelterSite` returns a `FAIL` result, every shelter operation at that site whose posture is currently `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` is moved to `RESTRICTED` as a cascade. Those shelters do not automatically recover when the site is later re-inspected and approved; they stay `RESTRICTED` until their own `staffing_sufficiency` reaches 0.60 or above and the ordinary open gate lifts them. Conversely, closing a shelter cascades downward too: when a shelter operation reaches `CLOSED`, every volunteer deployment assigned to it that is currently `ARRIVED` or `ON_SHIFT` is recalled, and each affected volunteer is returned to the deployable pool.

### 2.5. Credentialing, Site Agreements and Inspection Currency

Two independent credentialing chains must both be satisfied before a shelter can serve anyone. The first governs people, the second governs buildings.

On the people side, a volunteer carries a `credential_status` whose value is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`. A newly registered volunteer starts `PENDING` and is not deployable. Refreshing credentials stamps `credential_refreshed_at` from the logical clock and sets `credential_expires_at` to that instant plus 30 days; the volunteer becomes `ACTIVE`. From that moment the credential decays on its own: as soon as the logical clock reaches `credential_expires_at`, the volunteer's `credential_status` flips to `EXPIRED` and their availability is forced to `UNAVAILABLE`. A deployment request or an approval that involves a volunteer whose `credential_status` is not `ACTIVE`, or whose availability is not `AVAILABLE`, or whose requested `role_code` does not appear in that volunteer's `qualified_role_codes`, is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_CREDENTIALED` at HTTP status 409. The same code covers all three failure modes.

On the building side, a shelter site carries an `agreement_status` whose value is `PENDING_INSPECTION`, `APPROVED`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED`, together with an `inspection_result` whose value is `PASS`, `CONDITIONAL_PASS` or `FAIL`. A site is registered with the status `PENDING_INSPECTION` and is unusable until an inspection clears it. Opening a shelter whose site is not currently `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED` at HTTP status 409. Even an approved site can go stale: if the most recent inspection is more than 365 days old at the logical clock, the opening request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_STALE` at HTTP status 409. The 365-day currency window is measured from `last_inspection_at`, so a site whose agreement has not lapsed may still fail to open because nobody has re-inspected it recently.

The site's physical attributes recorded at registration — `facility_name`, `address_line`, `capacity_beds`, `accessibility_rating`, `functional_needs_support` and `generator_onsite` — describe what the facility claims to offer, where `accessibility_rating` is one of `FULL`, `PARTIAL` or `NONE`. Those claims are not trusted for operational purposes. An inspection replaces them with verified values: `capacity_verified_beds`, `accessibility_verified`, `functional_needs_verified`, `last_inspection_at` and `inspection_result`. Before a site's first inspection, `capacity_verified_beds` is zero and both verification booleans are false, which is why an un-inspected site can never satisfy the opening preconditions. An inspection that returns `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS` requires a verified bed count of at least 1 and a verified accessibility flag of true; if either precondition fails, the agreement does not advance.

Functional-needs support is a hard per-client gate rather than a site-wide one. A registration whose `functional_needs_flag` is true may only target a site whose `functional_needs_support` is true; when it does not, the registration is refused with `ERR_FUNCTIONAL_NEEDS_UNSUPPORTED` at HTTP status 409. This check is deliberately driven by the site's declared `functional_needs_support` attribute, not by the inspection's `functional_needs_verified` flag, and the two are recorded separately for that reason.

### 2.6. The Deployable Pool and Volunteer Readiness

A volunteer's ability to work is described by two dimensions that move independently of each other. The first is `credential_status`, covered in §2.5. The second is `deployment_availability`, whose value is `AVAILABLE`, `DEPLOYED`, `RESTING` or `UNAVAILABLE`. The two are orthogonal: a volunteer with `EXPIRED` credentials can still be `AVAILABLE`, and a volunteer with `ACTIVE` credentials can be `RESTING`. Only the combination rules constrain them, and every rule that consumes a volunteer's readiness must consult both dimensions.

The states of the second dimension mean specific things operationally. `AVAILABLE` is the deployable pool: the volunteer is trained, credentialed and free. `DEPLOYED` means the volunteer is committed to a deployment. `RESTING` is the state a volunteer enters immediately after finishing a rotation — they are off duty and not yet back in the pool, and they leave `RESTING` when their credentials are refreshed. `UNAVAILABLE` is the state a newly registered volunteer starts in and the state a volunteer falls into when their credentials expire. Refreshing credentials is the universal reset: it always leaves the volunteer `ACTIVE` and `AVAILABLE`, whether they were `PENDING`, `EXPIRED`, `RESTING` or `UNAVAILABLE` beforehand, which is how a resting volunteer is returned to the deployable pool.

Regional aggregates are derived from these two dimensions together. A region's `available_volunteer_count` counts only the volunteers whose `home_region_id` is that region, whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `deployment_availability` is `AVAILABLE` — a resting or deployed volunteer is not counted even if fully credentialed. The region's `deployed_volunteer_count` counts volunteers of that region whose `deployment_availability` is `DEPLOYED`. Each region also declares a `volunteer_pool_target` of at least 1, which expresses the region's intended deployable strength; it is a planning figure and does not by itself gate any operation.

A deployment may only be approved while its volunteer is genuinely free, and the approval also enforces the workload caps described in §2.7. Approval prices the trip and reserves the volunteer, moving their availability to `DEPLOYED`. Recall releases them: the volunteer returns to `AVAILABLE`, which is what makes a recalled volunteer immediately re-deployable. Ending a rotation does not release them the same way — the volunteer moves to `RESTING` and stays there until their credentials are refreshed.

### 2.7. Interval Integrity, Workload Caps and Occupancy Conflicts

Three resources in this system are booked in intervals — a volunteer's time, a dormitory bed, and a collection site's calendar — and each has its own collision rule. All three are enforced at the moment the booking is requested, and all three are refused at HTTP status 409.

A volunteer may not hold two overlapping shifts. A new shift assignment whose `shift_start_at` and `shift_end_at` interval intersects any non-cancelled shift already held by the same volunteer is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. The shift interval is inclusive at its start and exclusive at its end, and `shift_end_at` must be strictly after `shift_start_at`; a shift that ends at or before it starts is not a valid interval. A dormitory bed may not be double-booked. A requested bed code that is currently held by a `REGISTERED` occupant is refused with `ERR_BED_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`, and so is a bed whose previous occupancy ended inside the turnover buffer — that is, within 120 minutes before the requested `occupied_from_at`. The buffer exists so that a bed can be cleaned between clients. A blood drive window may not collide either: a new drive whose `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` interval intersects another non-cancelled drive at the same `site_name` is refused with `ERR_BLOOD_DRIVE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, and `scheduled_end_at` must be strictly after `scheduled_start_at`.

Workload caps bound how much a single volunteer or a single deployment can absorb. A rotation is at least 1 day and at most 21 days long, enforced when the deployment is requested; a value above 21 is refused with `ERR_ROTATION_TOO_LONG` at HTTP status 422. A volunteer may accumulate at most 40 rotation days in the year to date, enforced when a deployment is approved: if the volunteer's `rotation_days_used_ytd` plus the deployment's `rotation_days` exceeds 40, the approval is refused with `ERR_ANNUAL_ROTATION_CAP` at HTTP status 422. A single deployment may hold at most 3 shift assignments that have not reached a terminal state; a fourth is refused with `ERR_MAX_SHIFTS_EXCEEDED` at HTTP status 409. `rotation_days_used_ytd` is the sum of `rotation_days` across the volunteer's deployments that ended in `ROTATION_ENDED` or `RECALLED`, so an in-flight deployment does not yet count against the cap.

Shift assignments carry a `status` whose value is `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`; `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Deployments carry a `status` whose value is `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `ARRIVED`, `ON_SHIFT`, `ROTATION_ENDED`, `RECALLED` or `CANCELLED`; the last three are terminal. A deployment sitting in a terminal state rejects every further transition with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 2.8. Donor-Restricted Money and the Cost Ledger

Disaster money arrives with strings attached, and the system models those strings explicitly. Every `FundPool` declares a `restriction_type` whose value is `UNRESTRICTED`, `DONOR_RESTRICTED_DISASTER`, `DONOR_RESTRICTED_BLOOD` or `DONOR_RESTRICTED_LOCAL`, a `fund_code` that must be unique and non-empty, a `total_received_cents` of at least 0, and optionally a `region_id` for a locally restricted pool. A pool's `committed_cents` is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` across the assistance requests drawn on it that are in `APPROVED` or `CARD_ISSUED` status — money promised but not yet handed over. Its `disbursed_cents` is the same sum restricted to requests in `DISBURSED` status. Its `remaining_cents` is `total_received_cents` minus `committed_cents` minus `disbursed_cents`, so a promise consumes capacity exactly as a payment does.

An award may not be approved against a pool that cannot cover it, and it may not be approved against a pool whose restrictions forbid it. An approval whose `approved_amount_cents` exceeds the target pool's `remaining_cents` is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` at HTTP status 409. An approval whose pool `restriction_type` is incompatible with the request's `assistance_type`, or with the client's region, is refused with `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTION_MISMATCH` at HTTP status 409. The `assistance_type` vocabulary is `HOUSING`, `FOOD`, `MEDICAL`, `TRANSPORTATION`, `UTILITY`, `FUNERAL` and `OCCUPATIONAL`, and it is shared by referrals and assistance requests alike.

Movements of restricted money are reconstructible from the cost ledger. A `CostLedgerEntry` records a `cost_category` — one of `SHELTER_OPERATIONS`, `FEEDING`, `VOLUNTEER_TRAVEL`, `FINANCIAL_ASSISTANCE`, `HEALTH_SERVICES`, `BLOOD_COLLECTION` or `LOGISTICS` — an absolute `amount_cents` of at least 1, an `entry_type` of `CHARGE` or `REVERSAL`, the `incurred_at` instant, and the pool it is booked against. A disaster relief operation's `restricted_fund_balance_cents` is the sum of `remaining_cents` over every pool whose `restriction_type` is not `UNRESTRICTED` and which appears on at least one cost ledger entry of that operation. Unrestricted pools are excluded from that figure by definition. Reversing a disbursement must restore the pool balance exactly: the compensating ledger entry carries the same `amount_cents` as the original charge and the `entry_type` `REVERSAL`, so the pool's `remaining_cents` returns to precisely its pre-award value.

Segregation of duties protects the money. The operator who approves an award may not be the operator who issues its card: the issuing request is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` at HTTP status 403 when the incoming `X-User-ID` equals the request's `approved_by_user_id`. The same code and status protect reunification release, where the operator who verified the reunification may not be the one who releases the client.

### 2.9. Derived Fields, Precision and Common Resource Properties

Every resource in this API carries the same four system-managed properties. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded entity name; it is the only addressable handle for the resource. The `created_at` and `updated_at` values are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps carrying exactly three fractional digits, for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`; `created_at` is fixed at creation and `updated_at` moves on every change. The `version` is an integer that increments by exactly one on every mutation of the resource. All fifteen resource types in this API — regions, disaster relief operations, shelter sites, shelter operations, volunteers, volunteer deployments, shift assignments, clients, shelter registrations, client cases, referrals, assistance requests, fund pools, cost ledger entries and blood drives — carry all four properties; none of them is a reduced-shape resource, and none omits `updated_at` or `version`.

A large share of the fields these resources expose are derived rather than supplied. Counts such as `occupied_beds`, `registered_client_count`, `reunification_pending_count`, `staffed_volunteer_count`, `meals_served_total`, `health_service_count`, `open_shelter_count`, `available_volunteer_count` and `deployed_volunteer_count` are computed from child rows. Ratios such as `staffing_sufficiency` and `blood_continuity_index` are computed from those counts. Money figures such as `committed_cents`, `disbursed_cents`, `remaining_cents`, `restricted_fund_balance_cents`, `duplication_offset_cents`, `approved_amount_cents`, `entitlement_cents`, `travel_cost_cents`, `feeding_cost_cents` and `operational_cost_cents` are computed from child rows and pinned constants. Two rules govern all of them. First, a derived value is always recomputed from current child data rather than adjusted incrementally, except where a workflow explicitly states that a counter is maintained as a running total; this is what keeps the aggregates honest after a cascade. Second, recomputing a derived field that changes the stored value is itself a mutation: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` on the resource that owns the field. A cascade that touches a parent therefore bumps the parent's `version`, which in turn invalidates any client holding an older observed version — this is intentional and is the mechanism §2.12 depends on.

Precision is pinned per quantity and must not be improvised. All money in this system is expressed in integer cents, and every rounding operation uses the round-half-up rule — a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. The `staffing_sufficiency` ratio and the `blood_continuity_index` ratio are both stored and returned to exactly four decimal places, rounded half up. The `volunteer_deployment_priority_score` is an integer, and every division inside it discards the fractional part rather than rounding it. Bed codes are three-digit zero-padded strings of the form `B-001`. Instant arithmetic on the logical clock — travel lead days, redemption windows, closure holds, credential expiries — is exact and involves no rounding at all. Counts are plain integers. Where a calculation accumulates a running total, the rounding point is pinned inside the calculation definition and stated in §3; accumulating unrounded intermediates and rounding once at read time is a different, incorrect answer.

### 2.10. The Logical Clock and Automatic Transition Evaluation

Time in this system is not read from the host machine. The server maintains a logical clock whose startup default is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. Every deadline, expiry, grace period, lead time, trailing window and hold in this document is evaluated against that clock, and nothing else. A `NationalHQAdmin` may move the clock forward by invoking the clock-administration capability with a `to_iso8601` instant; the response echoes the accepted value back as `to_iso8601`. Moving the clock is the only way the passage of time is simulated, and it is why a deployment can be approved and then observed in transit without anyone waiting a day.

Automatic transitions are not background jobs. They are evaluated to a fixed point on every read and every write, which means a single request may carry a resource through several automatic transitions before the request itself is evaluated. A deployment that was approved and whose `rotation_start_at` has since passed may be found in `IN_TRANSIT`, or already in `ARRIVED`, depending on how far the clock has moved. The complete set of clock-driven transitions is: a volunteer deployment moving `APPROVED` to `IN_TRANSIT` and then `IN_TRANSIT` to `ARRIVED`; a shift assignment moving `SCHEDULED` to `NO_SHOW` and `CHECKED_IN` to `COMPLETED`; a blood drive moving `PLANNED` to `ACTIVE` and `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED`; a shelter site moving to `EXPIRED`; a volunteer's credentials moving `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`; and a client case moving `MONITORING` to `CLOSED`. Each of these is re-evaluated whenever the resource is read or written, so the observable state of the system always reflects the current clock.

### 2.11. Nested Resource Retrieval and Validation Order

When a request path names more than one resource, the identifiers are resolved from left to right along the path, and the first one that does not correspond to an existing resource determines the response. A request against a child of a parent that does not exist fails with the parent's not-found error, not the child's. The catalogue of existence failures is `ERR_REGION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPERATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHELTER_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHELTER_OPERATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUND_POOL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BLOOD_DRIVE_NOT_FOUND`, all at HTTP status 404. Body-supplied identifiers are resolved in the order they appear in the request body, after the path identifiers have all resolved.

Beyond existence, the system evaluates its checks in a fixed priority order, and the order is observable because a request that violates several rules at once reports the highest-priority one. The sequence is as follows. First, the presence and shape checks: both authentication headers must be present and non-empty and the body must carry every declared field with the correct type and no undeclared fields. Second, the role check: the acting role must be authorized for the endpoint. Third, existence, resolved left to right as described above. Fourth, state admissibility: the target must be in a state that admits the requested transition, a terminal resource rejects everything, and a shelter in `RESTRICTED` posture rejects registrations. Fifth, the credentialing and site-approval gates of §2.5. Sixth, the interval collision checks of §2.7. Seventh, the uniqueness and workload caps — `incident_code` and `fund_code` uniqueness, the 21-day rotation limit, the 40-day annual cap, the 3-shift ceiling, and the segregation-of-duties pairs. Eighth, cross-entity reference authorization. Ninth and last, capacity and donor money — the minimum-award floor, the pool balance, the pool restriction compatibility, the bed capacity ceiling, bed assignability, candidate availability, and the blood collection target. A caller that violates both a credentialing gate and a capacity rule therefore sees the credentialing error, and a caller that violates both an existence rule and a state rule sees the not-found error.

### 2.12. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and each names its own expected-version field. For these three, the version field is a required, non-optional part of the request body.

A `POST /api/v1/client-cases/{id}/recovery-plans` request, which writes a new recovery plan revision, requires `expected_client_case_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target client case. If the case's version has advanced since the client last read it, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP status 412. This is a stale-observation check rather than a one-shot guard: writing a second, later recovery plan revision is legitimate product behavior — a new revision supersedes the prior one — so a strictly later write succeeds provided the client observed the version it is superseding. Only an observation that is out of date fails.

A `POST /api/v1/shelter-sites/{id}/inspections` request, which records a shelter site inspection, requires `expected_shelter_site_version`, carrying the inspector's last observed `version` of the target shelter site. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP status 412. Re-inspecting a site is legitimate and supersedes the earlier result, so the check exists solely to stop an inspector from acting on a site state they have not seen.

A `POST /api/v1/disaster-relief-operations/{id}/cost-ledger-entries` request, which books a cost ledger entry, requires `expected_disaster_relief_operation_version`, carrying the bookkeeper's last observed `version` of the target disaster relief operation. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP status 412. Appending a further ledger entry is itself legitimate, so only a stale observation of the operation makes the write fail.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Endpoints that are not named in this subsection neither require nor honor one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Regional Footprint and Shelter Site Readiness

The geography of the response is established first. A `NationalHQAdmin` creates a region with a two-letter `code` that must be unique across all regions and non-empty, a human-readable `name`, the `state_codes` the region covers, and the opaque `coordinator_user_id` of the `RegionalCoordinator` accountable for it. Two planning attributes come along at the same time: `volunteer_pool_target`, which states the region's intended deployable strength and must be at least 1, and `authorized_agency_codes`, the list of partner agency codes that may receive referrals for clients of this region. A newly created region reports zero `available_volunteer_count` and zero `deployed_volunteer_count`, a `blood_continuity_index` of 0.0, and a `blood_continuity_status` of `CRITICAL` — a region with no collection history is, correctly, in the worst posture. Reading a region refreshes all four derived values, and the caller may select which blood-continuity variant is used by supplying the `continuity_mode` parameter described in §3.9.

Shelter capacity is contracted long before it is needed. Registering a `ShelterSite` captures the `facility_name` and `address_line`, the `region_id` of the region that holds the agreement, the `capacity_beds` the facility has contracted to provide (at least 1), the `accessibility_rating` of `FULL`, `PARTIAL` or `NONE`, whether `functional_needs_support` is available, whether a `generator_onsite` exists, and the `agreement_expires_at` instant at which the agreement lapses. Nothing is trusted yet: the site opens its life with `agreement_status` of `PENDING_INSPECTION`, `capacity_verified_beds` of 0, `accessibility_verified` and `functional_needs_verified` both false, and no `inspection_result` at all.

An inspection is what turns a paper agreement into usable capacity. A `RegionalCoordinator` records one by supplying the `inspected_at` instant, the `inspector_user_id`, the `capacity_verified_beds` actually confirmed, the `accessibility_verified` and `functional_needs_verified` confirmations, a `deficiency_count`, and a `result`. The payload also carries `expected_shelter_site_version`, whose stale-version behavior is specified in §2.12. The `result` decides where the agreement goes. A `PASS` or a `CONDITIONAL_PASS` recorded against a site that is `PENDING_INSPECTION` moves it to `APPROVED`, provided the inspection confirms at least one usable bed and confirms accessibility; a `FAIL` recorded against a `PENDING_INSPECTION` site moves it to `SUSPENDED` outright, with no bed or accessibility precondition to satisfy. A `FAIL` recorded against an already-`APPROVED` site also moves it to `SUSPENDED`, and this is the more consequential path: the suspension cascades two levels down, so every shelter operation at that site currently in `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` posture is forced into `RESTRICTED` immediately. Clients already sheltered there stay sheltered, but the shelter stops accepting new registrations until it recovers. Recovery is not automatic — re-inspecting the site and receiving a `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS` returns the agreement to `APPROVED`, but the shelters that were knocked into `RESTRICTED` stay there until their own staffing recovers and lifts them back to `OPEN`.

Agreements also lapse on their own. Whenever a site is read or written and the logical clock has reached its `agreement_expires_at`, an `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` site moves to `EXPIRED`, which is terminal. This evaluation happens on any read or write of the site, so a stale agreement cannot be used to open a shelter merely because nobody has touched the site recently. Every inspection, whatever its result, stamps `last_inspection_at`, records the `inspection_result`, and increments the site's `version`.

Activating a shelter is a separate act from approving a site. A `RegionalCoordinator` activates one by submitting the `shelter_site_id` and the `disaster_relief_operation_id` it serves, which creates a `ShelterOperation` in `PENDING_OPEN` posture with zero `occupied_beds`, zero `registered_client_count`, zero `staffed_volunteer_count` and a `staffing_sufficiency` of 0.0. The activation is refused with `ERR_REGION_MISMATCH` at HTTP status 409 if the site's region is not the same region as the disaster relief operation it is being activated for — a region does not shelter another region's disaster. Opening the shelter is a second step with four preconditions, all of which must hold: the site's `agreement_status` must be `APPROVED` (otherwise `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED`), the site's `inspection_result` must be `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS` (otherwise the shelter is not admissible), the parent operation's `phase` must be `MOBILIZATION` or `ACTIVE`, and the shelter's `staffing_sufficiency` must already be at least 0.60. The staffing precondition is the one that surprises operators: a shelter with no volunteers on site cannot open, no matter how good the building is. When the preconditions hold, `opened_at` is stamped from the logical clock, posture becomes `OPEN`, and the parent operation's `open_shelter_count` is recomputed.

Closing a shelter is the mirror image and depends on who is still inside. A `RegionalCoordinator` may close a shelter that is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` only while the parent operation is in `DEMOBILIZATION` phase and only if `registered_client_count` is greater than zero, in which case the shelter moves to `CONSOLIDATING` and begins winding down. Closing a shelter that is already `CONSOLIDATING` requires `registered_client_count` to be zero, and moves it to `CLOSED`. That final step cascades three levels: every volunteer deployment assigned to the shelter whose status is `ARRIVED` or `ON_SHIFT` is recalled, each affected volunteer's `deployment_availability` returns to `AVAILABLE`, and the `available_volunteer_count` and `deployed_volunteer_count` of every affected volunteer's home region are recomputed. The parent operation's `open_shelter_count` is recomputed as well.

### 3.2. Shelter Occupancy: Registration, Dormitory Beds and Client Departure

A client record is created before anyone can be sheltered. It carries a `full_name`, a `household_size` of at least 1, the `region_id` of the client's residence at the time of the disaster, and an optional `primary_client_id` pointing at the head-of-household record this one is attached to, which is how a family group is expressed. The linkage is optional: an unaccompanied client is a valid record with no `primary_client_id`.

Registration is where the shelter's posture, the site's capacity and the dormitory selector all converge. A `ShelterManager` registers a client at an open shelter by supplying the `client_id`, an optional `requested_bed_code`, the `occupied_from_at` instant at which the occupancy begins, an optional `occupied_until_at` at which it is expected to end, the `functional_needs_flag`, and an optional `reunification_reference` naming the person the client is trying to find. A registration attempted while the shelter's posture is `RESTRICTED` is refused with `ERR_SHELTER_RESTRICTED`; one whose `functional_needs_flag` is true at a site without `functional_needs_support` is refused with `ERR_FUNCTIONAL_NEEDS_UNSUPPORTED`. Capacity is checked separately from posture: if the shelter's `occupied_beds` plus one would exceed the site's `capacity_verified_beds`, the registration is refused with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A registration that specifies a `requested_bed_code` may also be refused with `ERR_BED_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` if that bed is held by a `REGISTERED` occupant or if its previous occupancy ended within the 120-minute turnover buffer before the requested `occupied_from_at`.

When `requested_bed_code` is omitted, the system chooses a bed. The candidate set is every bed code from `B-001` up to and including the site's verified capacity, each code being the letter `B`, a hyphen, and the bed number zero-padded to three digits. Two subsets are removed from that candidate set. The first is the set of codes currently occupied — registrations in `REGISTERED` status whose `dormitory_bed_code` is that code and whose `occupied_until_at` is either null or later than the requested `occupied_from_at`. The second is the set of codes that are still in their turnover buffer, meaning their previous occupancy has a non-null `occupied_until_at` that falls after the requested `occupied_from_at` minus 120 minutes and at or before the requested `occupied_from_at`. Whatever remains is eligible. The eligible beds are ranked by how many times each code has ever been assigned in this shelter — counting registrations in any status — ascending, with the bed code itself as the tie-breaker ascending, so the least-used bed wins and ties resolve to the lowest code. The first bed in that ranking is assigned as `dormitory_bed_code`. If no bed is eligible, the registration is refused with `ERR_NO_BED_AVAILABLE` at HTTP status 409.

A successful registration starts in `REGISTERED` status with `reunification_status` set to `PENDING` when a `reunification_reference` was supplied and to `NOT_REQUESTED` when it was not. `dormitory_bed_code` is null for a client who is registered without a bed, and such a client does not count toward `occupied_beds` even though they do count toward `registered_client_count`. The shelter's occupancy aggregates are recomputed at once: `occupied_beds` counts registrations in `REGISTERED` status that hold a non-null `dormitory_bed_code`, `registered_client_count` counts registrations in `REGISTERED` or `REUNIFIED` status, and `reunification_pending_count` counts registrations whose `reunification_status` is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`. The shelter's posture is then re-evaluated; because capacity is not a posture input, the re-evaluation is driven by staffing alone.

Departure has two flavors and one endpoint. A `ShelterManager` checks a client out by supplying an optional `reunification_verified_by_user_id`. In the ordinary case the registration moves from `REGISTERED` to `CHECKED_OUT` and `occupied_until_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The reunification case is stricter. A client whose `reunification_status` is `VERIFIED` is released as `REUNIFIED` rather than `CHECKED_OUT`, and the release is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` at HTTP status 403 when the `reunification_verified_by_user_id` already recorded on the registration equals the acting `X-User-ID` — the operator who confirmed the match may not be the operator who hands the client over. Both paths stamp `occupied_until_at`, recompute `occupied_beds`, `registered_client_count` and `reunification_pending_count`, and re-evaluate the shelter's posture, which is how a shelter that had been `RESTRICTED` returns to `OPEN` once beds free up and staffing is at or above 0.60. The three departure states — `CHECKED_OUT`, `TRANSFERRED` and `REUNIFIED` — are terminal, so a checked-out registration cannot be checked out again.

### 3.3. Feeding, Health Services and Shelter Cost Accounting

Two service lines are recorded against a shelter, and both feed the shelter's cost and activity totals.

Feeding is recorded per service event. A `ShelterManager` submits a `meal_type`, the `served_at` instant, the `meals_planned` for the event, the `meals_served` actually handed out, and a `unit_cost_cents` per meal. The cost of the event is computed line by line, and the rounding order is pinned: the raw line cost is `meals_served` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`, then multiplied by 10,625 and divided by 10,000 — the 10,625 being 10,000 plus the 625 basis-point supply surcharge — and that raw figure is rounded half up to a whole number of cents. That rounded line cost is then added to the shelter's `feeding_cost_cents`, and the `meals_served` count is added to `meals_served_total`. Rounding each line before accumulating is mandatory: summing the raw unrounded line costs and rounding once at the end produces a different total for identical events and is not the specified behavior. Note that the line cost uses `meals_served`, while the shelter's provisional operating cost described below uses `meals_planned` — the two are deliberately different figures.

Health services are recorded as encounters rather than costs. A `ShelterManager` submits the `client_id`, an `encounter_type`, and the `occurred_at` instant. The shelter's `health_service_count` increases by one, and the `health_service_count` on that client's registration at that shelter increases by one as well, so the shelter-level total and the per-client history stay in step.

The shelter's `operational_cost_cents` is a dual-variant figure selected by the `reporting_mode` parameter on the shelter read. The default variant is `PROVISIONAL`, used while the shelter is `PENDING_OPEN`, `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `CONSOLIDATING`; the `FINAL` variant is used after the shelter reaches `CLOSED`. Both variants build a direct cost and then add overhead, and both compute overhead identically: take the direct cost, multiply it by 750 and divide by 10,000, round that half up to a whole number of cents, and use whichever is larger between that result and 2,500 cents. The two variants differ only in what they count.

Under `PROVISIONAL`, the direct cost is the sum of three components. The first is, for every feeding-service event recorded on this shelter, the `meals_planned` for that event multiplied by its `unit_cost_cents` and then by 10,625 and divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent — a forward-looking figure that charges for meals planned rather than meals served. The second is the sum of `travel_cost_cents` over every volunteer deployment assigned to this shelter whose status is `APPROVED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `ARRIVED` or `ON_SHIFT` — that is, every deployment that is not yet finished. The third is the sum of `requested_amount_cents` over every assistance request whose client case belongs to a client registered at this shelter and whose status is `APPROVED` or `CARD_ISSUED` — money promised but not yet disbursed.

Under `FINAL`, the direct cost is the sum of three different components. The first is the shelter's accumulated `feeding_cost_cents`, which is already built from per-line rounded amounts. The second is the sum of `travel_cost_cents` over deployments of this shelter whose status is `ROTATION_ENDED` — completed rotations only. The third is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over assistance requests of this shelter's clients whose status is `DISBURSED`. Because the provisional variant counts planned meals, unfinished travel and merely approved assistance while the final variant counts served meals, completed rotations and disbursed assistance, the provisional figure is greater than or equal to the final figure for identical child rows. The `operational_cost_cents` field is recomputed whenever the shelter is read, and the `reporting_mode` value selects which variant is returned.

### 3.4. Volunteer Credentialing, Deployment and Rotation Management

A volunteer joins the roster with a `full_name`, a `home_region_id`, and the `qualified_role_codes` they are trained for. Registration alone does not make anyone deployable: the volunteer starts with `credential_status` of `PENDING`, `deployment_availability` of `UNAVAILABLE` and `rotation_days_used_ytd` of 0, and the home region's volunteer counts are recomputed to include them. A `RegionalCoordinator` then refreshes their credentials, which stamps `credential_refreshed_at` from the logical clock, sets `credential_expires_at` to that instant plus 30 days, and leaves the volunteer `ACTIVE` and `AVAILABLE`. That refresh is also the recovery path for every degraded condition — a `PENDING` volunteer becomes deployable, an `EXPIRED` volunteer is reinstated, and a volunteer who has been `RESTING` or `UNAVAILABLE` is returned to the deployable pool. The home region's `available_volunteer_count` and `deployed_volunteer_count` are recomputed each time. Left alone, credentials decay: once the logical clock reaches `credential_expires_at`, the volunteer's `credential_status` becomes `EXPIRED` and their `deployment_availability` is forced to `UNAVAILABLE`, automatically and without an explicit request.

A deployment begins as a request. A `RegionalCoordinator` submits the `volunteer_id`, the `disaster_relief_operation_id` the volunteer is being sent to, an optional `shelter_operation_id` naming the shelter they will report to, a `role_code`, a `travel_mode`, the `rotation_start_at` at which the rotation is scheduled to begin on site, and the `rotation_days` the rotation will last. The `role_code` is one of `SHELTER_MANAGER`, `REGISTRATION`, `DORMITORY`, `FEEDING`, `HEALTH_SERVICES`, `REUNIFICATION` or `CASEWORK`; the `travel_mode` is one of `AIR`, `GROUND` or `SELF`. The request is created in `REQUESTED` status with a `travel_cost_cents` of 0, and `rotation_days` must fall between 1 and 21 inclusive. When a `shelter_operation_id` is supplied, it must belong to the same disaster relief operation as the deployment's `disaster_relief_operation_id`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_SHELTER_MISMATCH` at HTTP status 409.

Approval is the gate that reserves a person. A `RegionalCoordinator` approves a requested deployment only when all five conditions hold: the volunteer's `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, the volunteer's `deployment_availability` is `AVAILABLE`, the requested `role_code` appears in the volunteer's `qualified_role_codes`, the `rotation_days` is at most 21, and the volunteer's `rotation_days_used_ytd` plus this deployment's `rotation_days` is at most 40. A failure of any of the first three is reported as `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_CREDENTIALED`; a rotation longer than 21 days is reported as `ERR_ROTATION_TOO_LONG` at HTTP status 422; a breach of the annual ceiling is reported as `ERR_ANNUAL_ROTATION_CAP` at HTTP status 422. When the conditions hold, the trip is priced into `travel_cost_cents`, the volunteer's `deployment_availability` moves to `DEPLOYED`, the volunteer's home region has its `available_volunteer_count` and `deployed_volunteer_count` recomputed, and the deployment becomes `APPROVED`.

Travel pricing depends on the mode and on whether the volunteer is deploying inside or outside their home region. A same-region deployment is treated as 120 miles of travel; a cross-region deployment is treated as 400 miles. An `AIR` deployment costs a base of 45,000 cents plus 2,500 cents for each day by which the rotation exceeds 14 days, counting only the excess and never a negative amount — so a rotation of 14 days or fewer pays the base fare alone. A `GROUND` deployment costs 55 cents for each of those miles plus 12,000 cents for each day by which the rotation exceeds 7 days, again counting only the excess. A `SELF` deployment costs nothing. All of these inputs are whole numbers, so the result is already an exact integer number of cents.

Once approved, a deployment advances through travel on its own. It moves from `APPROVED` to `IN_TRANSIT` as soon as the logical clock reaches `rotation_start_at` minus the lead time for its `travel_mode` — one day for `AIR`, two days for `GROUND`, and no lead time at all for `SELF` — and from `IN_TRANSIT` to `ARRIVED` as soon as the clock reaches `rotation_start_at`. Both transitions are evaluated automatically on any read or write of the deployment, and they chain, so a single request against a deployment whose start date has passed may observe it already in `ARRIVED`. A deployment reaches `ON_SHIFT` only when one of its shift assignments is checked in: when a shift belonging to the deployment transitions to `CHECKED_IN` and the deployment is `ARRIVED`, the deployment moves to `ON_SHIFT`, and the shelter's `staffed_volunteer_count` and `staffing_sufficiency` are recomputed.

Shifts are the unit of on-site work. A `RegionalCoordinator` or `ShelterManager` schedules one by submitting the `volunteer_deployment_id`, the `shelter_operation_id` where the shift is worked, a `functional_area` of `REGISTRATION`, `DORMITORY`, `FEEDING`, `HEALTH`, `REUNIFICATION` or `SECURITY`, and the `shift_start_at` and `shift_end_at` bounds of the interval. The shift starts `SCHEDULED`. Scheduling enforces the interval and workload rules of §2.7: a shift overlapping another non-cancelled shift of the same volunteer is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`, and a fourth non-terminal shift on the same deployment is refused with `ERR_MAX_SHIFTS_EXCEEDED`. A shift that is still `SCHEDULED` when the logical clock reaches `shift_start_at` plus 240 minutes is marked `NO_SHOW` automatically, and a shift that is `CHECKED_IN` when the clock reaches `shift_end_at` is marked `COMPLETED` automatically.

Checking in is the volunteer's own act, and it is doubly constrained. The acting `Volunteer` must own the parent deployment — the request's `X-User-ID` must equal the deployment's `volunteer_id` — and the check-in must occur no earlier than 60 minutes before `shift_start_at`, and the shelter must be in `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` posture. When those hold, the shift becomes `CHECKED_IN` and the parent deployment, if it is `ARRIVED`, moves to `ON_SHIFT`. When the parent deployment is recalled, any of its shifts that has not reached a terminal state is cancelled automatically.

Rotations end in one of two ways. The volunteer themselves may end a rotation, and only from `ON_SHIFT`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the deployment's `volunteer_id`. Ending a rotation sets the deployment to `ROTATION_ENDED`, moves the volunteer's `deployment_availability` to `RESTING` — they are off duty, not yet back in the pool — and adds the deployment's `rotation_days` to the volunteer's `rotation_days_used_ytd`. Recall is the coordinator's tool and works from any non-terminal state. A deployment that is still `REQUESTED` is cancelled rather than recalled and ends in `CANCELLED`; a deployment in `APPROVED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `ARRIVED` or `ON_SHIFT` ends in `RECALLED`. In every recall path the volunteer's `deployment_availability` returns to `AVAILABLE`, immediately re-deployable, and every non-terminal shift assignment of that deployment is cancelled. Both ending a rotation and recalling a deployment recompute the shelter's `staffed_volunteer_count` and `staffing_sufficiency`, since both remove a body from the floor. A deployment that has reached `ROTATION_ENDED`, `RECALLED` or `CANCELLED` is terminal and rejects any further transition.

Staffing sufficiency is what drives the posture gate of §2.4, and it is computed from clients and arrivals. The required number of volunteers is the number of registered clients divided by 20, rounded up to a whole volunteer, and then floored at a minimum of 1 — so even an empty shelter requires one volunteer. The staffed number is the count of volunteer deployments assigned to the shelter whose status is `ARRIVED` or `ON_SHIFT`. The ratio of staffed to required is capped at 1.0 and rounded half up to four decimal places, and that is the shelter's `staffing_sufficiency`. It is recomputed whenever the shelter's `registered_client_count` changes or whenever the set of deployments for the shelter changes.

### 3.5. Disaster Relief Operation Lifecycle and Deployment Candidate Selection

An operation is declared before anything is opened beneath it. A `NationalHQAdmin` or `RegionalCoordinator` declares one with an `incident_code` issued by emergency management that must be unique and non-empty, an `incident_type` of `HURRICANE`, `WILDFIRE`, `FLOOD`, `EARTHQUAKE` or `TORNADO`, the `declared_at` instant, and the `region_id` in which it is declared. A duplicate `incident_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INCIDENT_CODE` at HTTP status 409. The operation starts in `MOBILIZATION` phase with an `open_shelter_count` of 0 and a `restricted_fund_balance_cents` of 0.

Phase advancement is sequential and gated by shelter activity. Moving from `MOBILIZATION` to `ACTIVE` requires at least one shelter operation under the operation to exist with a posture of `PENDING_OPEN`, `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` — an operation cannot go active with nothing stood up under it. Moving from `ACTIVE` to `DEMOBILIZATION` requires `open_shelter_count` to be 2 or fewer, which is what forces a region to consolidate its footprint before winding down. Moving from `DEMOBILIZATION` to `CLOSED` requires `open_shelter_count` to be exactly 0. Each advancement increments the operation's `version`. Reading an operation recomputes its `open_shelter_count` — the number of shelter operations beneath it in `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` posture — and its `restricted_fund_balance_cents`.

When a coordinator needs to know whom to send, the system ranks the deployable pool rather than returning an arbitrary list. The caller supplies the `role_code` being staffed, along with the usual `limit` and `offset`. A volunteer is eligible only if their `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` and their `deployment_availability` is `AVAILABLE`; anyone else is excluded from the pool entirely rather than ranked low. Eligible volunteers receive a score built from a base of 100 points, adjusted by four terms. The recency bonus rewards recently refreshed credentials: the number of whole days between `credential_refreshed_at` and the logical clock is divided by 30, the fractional part is discarded, and that quotient is subtracted from 30, with the result floored at zero — so a refresh within the last 30 days earns the full 30 points and one 90 or more days old earns nothing. The distance penalty depends on where the volunteer lives relative to the operation: a volunteer whose `home_region_id` is the operation's region is treated as traveling 0 miles, and a volunteer from any other region is treated as traveling 400 miles; that mileage is divided by 50, the fractional part discarded, and the result capped at 25 points of penalty. The rotation penalty is the volunteer's `rotation_days_used_ytd` multiplied by 2, capped at 40 points. The role match bonus adds 20 points when the requested `role_code` appears in the volunteer's `qualified_role_codes`. The final score is the base of 100 plus the recency bonus minus the distance penalty minus the rotation penalty plus the role match bonus, floored at zero. Candidates are returned highest score first, and ties are broken by the earlier `credential_refreshed_at`, then by the earlier `created_at`. If no volunteer is eligible at all, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOLUNTEER` at HTTP status 409.

### 3.6. Recovery Casework: Eligibility, Recovery Planning and Referrals

Casework is the long tail of a disaster. A `Caseworker` opens a case for a client under an operation by supplying the `client_id`, the `disaster_relief_operation_id`, and the opaque `assigned_caseworker_id` of the caseworker who will own it. The case opens in `INTAKE` status with an `eligibility_status` of `PENDING`, a `recovery_plan_revision` of 1, and an `entitlement_cents` computed per §3.7.

Eligibility review is the first gate, and it is owned by the assigned caseworker: a verification request is refused unless the acting `X-User-ID` equals the case's `assigned_caseworker_id`. A case in `INTAKE` moves to `ELIGIBILITY_REVIEW` when eligibility is verified, and the payload's `eligibility_status` is recorded along with the acting operator as `eligibility_verified_by_user_id`. The status vocabulary is `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `INELIGIBLE` or `EXPIRED`. A case that has already been `CLOSED` can be reopened by the same operation: it moves to `REOPENED` with an `eligibility_status` of `PENDING`, which is the path taken when a client who had been settled suffers a second loss and needs a new assistance request.

A recovery plan is written only after eligibility is settled. The case must be in `ELIGIBILITY_REVIEW` or `REOPENED` and its `eligibility_status` must be `VERIFIED`; otherwise the write is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The request carries the `recovery_plan_summary` text and `expected_client_case_version`, whose stale-version rule is specified in §2.12 — the recovery plan is one of the three concurrency-guarded writes in this API, because a caseworker who has not seen the latest revision could otherwise overwrite it. The write moves the case to `RECOVERY_PLANNING`, sets `recovery_plan_revision` to 1 if the case was entering recovery planning for the first time and increments it by one if the case was already in `RECOVERY_PLANNING` or `REOPENED`, and recomputes `entitlement_cents`.

Referrals connect the client to partner agencies. A `Caseworker` raises one against the case with an `agency_code`, an `assistance_type`, and the `estimated_value_cents` the agency is expected to provide, which must be at least 0. Two rules guard the raise. The `agency_code` must appear in the `authorized_agency_codes` of the region the client resides in; an unauthorized agency is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_AUTHORIZED` at HTTP status 409. And a case may not hold two live referrals to the same agency for the same kind of help: if a non-cancelled referral with the same `agency_code` and `assistance_type` already exists for the case, the raise is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATION_BLOCKED` at HTTP status 409. When the raise succeeds, the referral is created in `SUBMITTED` status, attached to the case through its `client_case_id`, with a `duplication_check_result` of `CLEAR` or `PARTIAL_OVERLAP`, and the case moves to `REFERRED`. The referral's status vocabulary is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, of which `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Resolving a referral is a single operation driven by a `decision` field that takes one of five values. A `decision` of `ACCEPT` moves a `SUBMITTED` referral to `ACCEPTED`, and it is refused when the referral's `duplication_check_result` is `DUPLICATE_BLOCKED`. `DECLINE` moves a `SUBMITTED` referral to `DECLINED` with no precondition. `REQUEST_INFORMATION` sends an `ACCEPTED` referral back to `SUBMITTED` and puts it through the duplication check again the next time it is resolved. `COMPLETE` moves an `ACCEPTED` referral to `COMPLETED`. `CANCEL` moves a `SUBMITTED` referral to `CANCELLED`. Every resolution recomputes the parent case's status: when no referral of the case remains `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`, the case moves from `REFERRED` to `MONITORING`, which happens automatically rather than by an explicit request. Each resolution also recomputes the case's `case_closure_eligible_at`.

A case closes on a hold, not immediately. Once a case is in `MONITORING`, it becomes eligible for closure at the later of two instants — the most recent referral resolution among the case's `COMPLETED`, `DECLINED` and `CANCELLED` referrals, and the most recent update among the case's `DISBURSED` assistance requests — plus 14 days. That computed instant is `closure_eligible_at`. When the logical clock reaches it, the case moves automatically to `CLOSED`. A case that has neither a resolved referral nor a disbursed assistance request never becomes closure-eligible and never closes on its own. `CLOSED` is not terminal for a case: a subsequent eligibility verification by the assigned caseworker moves it to `REOPENED`, and from there a new recovery plan revision returns it to `RECOVERY_PLANNING` once eligibility is `VERIFIED`.

### 3.7. Immediate Financial Assistance: Entitlement, Awards and the Card Lifecycle

The size of a client's entitlement is a dual-variant figure. Both variants begin from a base amount: the household size multiplied by 30,000 cents per person, capped at 120,000 cents — so a household of four or more receives the same base as a household of exactly four. The `base_schedule` variant stops there and returns the base unchanged. The `expedited_schedule` variant returns 20,000 cents plus half of the base amount, where that half is computed by multiplying the base by 5,000 and dividing by 10,000 and rounding half up to a whole cent, with the whole result capped at 150,000 cents. Which variant applies is decided by two conditions: the expedited schedule is used when any shelter registration belonging to the case's client carries a `functional_needs_flag` of true, or when the case's own status is `REOPENED`; otherwise the base schedule applies. The two variants diverge sharply for identical data — for a household of five, the base schedule yields 120,000 cents and the expedited schedule yields 80,000 cents — which is why the selection rule is stated in terms of the client's registrations and the case's status rather than inferred from the amount. The result is stored on the case as `entitlement_cents` and recomputed whenever the case is created, when eligibility is verified, and when a recovery plan revision is written.

An assistance request is the client's ask. A `Caseworker` or `FinanceOfficer` submits one against a case with the `fund_pool_id` it should draw on, an `assistance_type`, and a `requested_amount_cents` of at least 1. The request is created in `SUBMITTED` status with `card_status` of `NOT_ISSUED`, and the duplication offset and award are computed for it.

The duplication-of-benefits offset exists so that a client is not paid twice for the same loss. The evaluation order is pinned and must not be re-derived from the post-floor award. First, the offset is computed as the sum of `estimated_value_cents` over every referral of the same case whose `assistance_type` matches the request's and whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `COMPLETED` — referrals that were declined or cancelled do not count, and referrals resolved after the assistance request was created do count. Second, the award is computed as the requested amount minus the offset, floored at zero. Third, the award is tested against the minimum: if the computed award is greater than zero but less than 5,000 cents, the approval is refused with `ERR_ASSISTANCE_BELOW_MINIMUM` at HTTP status 409 and the request remains in `ELIGIBILITY_VERIFIED`, awaiting either a change in the offset or a different request. Only when the offset fully covers the requested amount, leaving an award of exactly zero, does the request move to `DENIED`. The offset is never recomputed from the award after the floor has been applied.

Approval is a two-step operation against the same endpoint, and the case must be eligible before it can begin. The first call requires the case's `eligibility_status` to be `VERIFIED` and moves the request from `SUBMITTED` to `ELIGIBILITY_VERIFIED`, computing `duplication_offset_cents` and `approved_amount_cents`. The second call moves it from `ELIGIBILITY_VERIFIED` to `APPROVED`, and it must satisfy three conditions: the award is at least 5,000 cents, the target pool's `remaining_cents` is at least the award (otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`), and the pool's `restriction_type` is compatible with the request's `assistance_type` (otherwise `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTION_MISMATCH`). A second call whose computed award is exactly zero moves the request to `DENIED` instead, which is the terminal outcome for a fully offset request. On approval, `approved_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, the pool's `committed_cents` and `remaining_cents` are recomputed, and the parent operation's `restricted_fund_balance_cents` is recomputed. The request status vocabulary is `SUBMITTED`, `ELIGIBILITY_VERIFIED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `CARD_ISSUED`, `DISBURSED` and `REVERSED`, of which `DENIED`, `DISBURSED` and `REVERSED` are terminal.

Issuing the card is where segregation of duties bites. Only an `APPROVED` request can be issued, and issuing one whose `card_status` is anything other than `NOT_ISSUED` is refused with `ERR_CARD_ALREADY_ISSUED` at HTTP status 409 — the check is on the card, not the request status, so a request cannot be issued twice. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the request's `approved_by_user_id`; the same operator attempting both halves is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` at HTTP status 403. When it succeeds, the request moves to `CARD_ISSUED`, `issued_by_user_id` is stamped, `card_status` becomes `ISSUED`, a masked `card_number_masked` is generated, and `redemption_deadline_at` is computed. The card status vocabulary is `NOT_ISSUED`, `ISSUED`, `ACTIVE`, `REDEEMED` and `VOIDED`.

The redemption window depends on the conditions the client is living under at the moment of issuance. If any shelter registration belonging to the case's client sits in a shelter operation whose posture is `RESTRICTED` at issuance time, the window is 45 days; otherwise it is 90 days. The deadline is the issuance instant plus that many days, computed exactly on the logical clock. Redeeming the card requires the logical clock to be strictly before `redemption_deadline_at`; a redemption attempted at or after the deadline is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP status 409. A successful redemption moves the request to `DISBURSED` and the card to `REDEEMED`, recomputes the pool's `committed_cents` and `disbursed_cents`, appends a cost ledger entry with `cost_category` of `FINANCIAL_ASSISTANCE`, `entry_type` of `CHARGE` and an `amount_cents` equal to the award, and recomputes the case's `case_closure_eligible_at`.

Reversal unwinds a disbursement completely. Any `DISBURSED` request may be reversed, with no precondition beyond its state. The request moves to `REVERSED` and the card to `VOIDED`, and the unwind cascades three hops: the pool's `disbursed_cents` is recomputed, then the pool's `remaining_cents` is recomputed, then the parent operation's `restricted_fund_balance_cents` is recomputed and a compensating cost ledger entry with `entry_type` of `REVERSAL` and an `amount_cents` equal to the original award is appended. Because the compensating entry mirrors the charge exactly, the pool's remaining balance returns to precisely its pre-award value.

### 3.8. Donor Fund Pools and the Operation Cost Ledger

Fund pools are created before money can be moved. A `NationalHQAdmin` or `FinanceOfficer` creates one with a `fund_code` that must be unique and non-empty — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FUND_CODE` at HTTP status 409 — a `restriction_type`, an optional `region_id` for a locally restricted pool, and a `total_received_cents` of at least 0. A new pool reports zero `committed_cents`, zero `disbursed_cents`, and a `remaining_cents` equal to its total receipts. Reading a pool recomputes all three derived balances from the assistance requests drawn against it, so a pool's available capacity always reflects the promises and payments made against it rather than a stored running figure.

The cost ledger is how an operation's spending becomes auditable. A `NationalHQAdmin` or `FinanceOfficer` books an entry against an operation by supplying the `fund_pool_id`, the `cost_category`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, the `entry_type` of `CHARGE` or `REVERSAL`, and the `incurred_at` instant. The request also carries `expected_disaster_relief_operation_version`, whose stale-version rule is specified in §2.12 — booking against an operation whose version the bookkeeper has not observed is rejected, so an entry is only ever appended against a ledger state the bookkeeper has actually read. A successful booking recomputes the operation's `restricted_fund_balance_cents` and increments the operation's `version`. Because the operation's version advances on every booking, and because a shelter close, a phase advancement and an assistance approval all touch the same operation, the version a bookkeeper must present is a genuinely live value.

### 3.9. Blood Collection Continuity

Blood collection does not pause for a disaster, and the region's posture reflects that. A `NationalHQAdmin` or `BloodServicesCoordinator` schedules a drive with the `region_id` hosting it, the `site_name`, the `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` bounds of the collection window, and a `target_units` of at least 1. The window must be a real interval — `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at` — and it must not intersect another non-cancelled drive at the same `site_name`, which is refused with `ERR_BLOOD_DRIVE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` at HTTP status 409. A new drive starts `PLANNED` with `collected_units` and `usable_units` both zero.

A drive advances on its own. It becomes `ACTIVE` when the logical clock reaches `scheduled_start_at`, and `COMPLETED` when the clock reaches `scheduled_end_at`; reaching `COMPLETED` recomputes the region's `blood_continuity_index` and `blood_continuity_status`. Collection events are recorded against an active drive by supplying the `units` drawn. Each event adds to `collected_units`, and an event that would push `collected_units` past the drive's `target_units` is refused with `ERR_COLLECTION_TARGET_EXCEEDED` at HTTP status 409 rather than being truncated. Each accepted event also recomputes `usable_units`: the collected count is multiplied by 700 and divided by 10,000, the fractional part is discarded, and the result is subtracted from `collected_units`. The discarded portion represents units lost to deferrals — a drive that collects 100 units yields 93 usable ones.

A `BloodServicesCoordinator` may cancel a drive. A `PLANNED` drive may only be cancelled while `collected_units` is zero; an `ACTIVE` drive may be cancelled freely, and cancellation recomputes the region's continuity figures. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

The region's continuity posture is a dual-variant ratio selected by the `continuity_mode` parameter on the region read. Both variants share the same denominator: a baseline of 600 units plus the `target_units` of every drive in the region whose status is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE`. The numerator is where they differ. The `TRAILING` variant, which is the default, counts only what has actually been banked: the sum of `usable_units` over drives in the region whose status is `COMPLETED` and whose `scheduled_end_at` falls within the 42 days preceding the logical clock, inclusive of both the window's start and the clock itself. The `FORWARD_LOOKING` variant adds to that the `target_units` of every `PLANNED` drive in the region, and is used when briefing emergency management on projected supply. For identical drive rows the forward-looking numerator is the larger of the two. The index is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places. The accompanying `blood_continuity_status` is derived from the index by two thresholds applied in order: an index below 0.50 is `CRITICAL`, an index below 0.80 but at or above 0.50 is `STRAINED`, and an index of 0.80 or above is `NORMAL`.
