# Crash Preventability Review & Liability Claim Handling API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A motor carrier that runs freight across several terminals lives or dies by how it handles the hours and months after a crash. The first hour decides whether the telematics feed and the forward-facing camera clip still exist when a plaintiff's attorney asks for them. The first three days decide whether the carrier files its regulatory report on time or shows up in an enforcement report as a late filer. The following weeks decide whether the safety review committee's preventability finding will survive an appeal, whether the driver is coached or separated, and whether the reserve the claims department set on day one bore any relationship to the demand that eventually arrived in the mail. Today those decisions live in five disconnected systems and a shared spreadsheet, and the carrier cannot answer the one question its insurers keep asking: what is our real exposure, terminal by terminal, right now?

This document specifies the service that consolidates that workflow into a single auditable record. It covers crash notification intake with the driver's own statement, evidence preservation under litigation hold, preventability determination by a seated safety review committee operating under criteria and a bounded appeal, regulatory recordability and filing, liability claim setup with reserve estimation and counsel assignment, third-party property-damage and bodily-injury negotiation allocated across a policy tower that begins with a self-insured retention, driver discipline and continued-employment decisions, crash-rate analytics by terminal and route class and driver tenure, and subrogation recovery against at-fault third parties.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is that safety and evidence integrity outrank money: a determination may not be recorded on a spoliated record without the system saying so, a preserved artifact may not be released while a claim is open, and no settlement may pay out cents that were never reserved. The second is that every unwind is exact. A granted appeal must restore the driver's point total, the terminal's headcounts and the disciplinary record to precisely their pre-determination values; a rescinded settlement must return the money, the policy-layer capacity and the subrogation posture to precisely their pre-settlement values. Approximate reversal is not acceptable in a system whose output is read by regulators and reinsurers.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that operator is acting. If either header is missing, present but empty or containing nothing but whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` names something outside the eight declared roles — the role comparison being exact and case-sensitive, so a role name carrying stray surrounding whitespace is not recognised either — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). `X-User-ID` is inspected before `X-User-Role`, so a request supplying neither reports the identifier as the missing one. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not on the target endpoint's authorized list, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `SafetyAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, `Driver`, `CommitteeMember`, `SafetyManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `ClaimsManager` and `Auditor`. They divide into two peer families that own disjoint resource sets, plus one observer. The safety family — `SafetyAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, `Driver`, `CommitteeMember` and `SafetyManager` — owns terminals, drivers, crashes, evidence, review cases, ballots and discipline. The claims family — `ClaimsAdjuster` and `ClaimsManager` — owns claims, third-party demands, negotiation rounds and subrogation. `Auditor` is a read-only compliance observer with the widest read surface in the system and no write authority anywhere: an `Auditor` may read every collection, including the ballot ledger and the terminal crash-analytics projection, and may write nothing.

A `SafetyAdmin` is the fleet safety administrator. That role alone may advance the logical clock, register terminals, hire drivers, purge evidence and record retraining completion; it shares crash intake, evidence-preservation requests and the preservation action itself with `Dispatcher`, and shares maintenance of the policy tower with `ClaimsManager`. A `Dispatcher` is a terminal dispatcher who takes the crash notification and requests preservation of telematics and camera artifacts. A `CommitteeMember` is a seated member of the safety review committee whose sole write capability is casting a ballot. A `SafetyManager` opens and chairs review cases, opens deliberation, records determinations, grants appeals, files regulatory reports, issues discipline and makes continued-employment decisions. A `ClaimsAdjuster` opens claims, revises reserves, records third-party demands and runs the negotiation ledger. A `ClaimsManager` holds settlement, rescission, closure, counsel-assignment and recovery authority. Opening a subrogation demand is shared between `ClaimsAdjuster` and `ClaimsManager`.

Only one role is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Driver`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Driver` row; that row must exist, and the operation must concern that driver's own crash. A driver filing a statement about a crash whose `driver_id` is somebody else is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and the server records the acting identifier on the statement as `submitted_by_driver_id`. When `X-User-Role` is any of `SafetyAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, `CommitteeMember`, `SafetyManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `ClaimsManager` or `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server may record it for audit — as `member_user_id` on a ballot, as `decided_by_user_id` on a review case, as `issued_by_user_id` on a disciplinary action, as `offered_by_user_id` on a negotiation round — but those fields are not foreign keys and no existence check is performed against them.

Two same-user prohibitions apply inside the safety family, and both are checked on the acting `X-User-ID` rather than on the role. The operator who records a determination on a review case may not be the operator behind any live ballot on that same case; and the operator who grants an appeal may not be the operator who recorded the determination being appealed. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The same natural person is entirely free to hold both `SafetyManager` and `CommitteeMember` authority — the system does not police role assignment — but that person may not both vote on a case and decide it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and it is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time — so it is byte-identical on every response, success or error, until the clock is advanced.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped; so is a value that is not a base-ten integer literal at all — an empty value, a decimal such as `1.5` or a word — although surrounding whitespace is tolerated and stripped before the literal is read. The same treatment extends to the declared filters: a filter whose value falls outside its declared enum domain, or a boolean filter given anything other than `true` or `false` (matched case-insensitively), is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a query parameter that is not `limit`, `offset` or one of the endpoint's declared filters is ignored rather than rejected. Filters that name an identifier are never existence-checked: an identifier matching no row simply yields an empty page rather than a not-found error.

Unless a collection's own description says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first — so that pagination is stable while new rows are being written. Because the logical clock only moves when an administrator moves it, many rows routinely share a `created_at`; ties are broken by insertion order, so rows written under one clock value come back in the order they were created. Two collections carry a domain-specific ordering instead: the policy tower is returned ascending by `program_year` and then by `layer_index`, and a demand's negotiation ledger is returned ascending by `round_index`.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads and write responses omit those four keys.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and Append-Only Records

Every persisted row carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it and must never parse meaning out of it — and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits. Every instant the server renders — `created_at` included — is rendered in that one canonical form, zero-padded to three fractional digits and terminated by a literal `Z`, never with a numeric offset. Instants *accepted* from a client are more liberal: the date and time may be joined by `T` or `t`, the zone may be `Z`, `z` or a numeric `±HH:MM` offset that the server converts to UTC before storing, and one to nine fractional digits are accepted, with anything finer than a microsecond rounded to the nearest microsecond and anything finer than a millisecond retained for comparison but not shown on output. A calendar date field, by contrast, is accepted only in strict `YYYY-MM-DD` form and echoed in that same form.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row, including mutations that arrive as a cascade from an operation addressed at some other resource. `Terminal`, `Driver`, `Crash`, `EvidenceItem`, `ReviewCase`, `Claim`, `Demand`, `DisciplinaryAction` and `SubrogationDemand` all behave this way. In every serialised resource object the audit fields are the trailing keys, emitted after all of the entity's domain fields in the order `created_at`, then `updated_at`, then `version`; `id` is always the first key. Key order is otherwise fixed per entity, pinned where that entity is described, and stable from one response to the next — responses are never alphabetised, and the derived fields of §2.5 occupy their own fixed slots in the object rather than being appended at the end.

Four record types are append-only ledger rows and deliberately do not: `DriverStatement`, `CommitteeVote`, `PolicyLayer` and `NegotiationRound` carry only `id` and `created_at`, so on those four `created_at` is simply the final key of the object. They have no `updated_at` and no `version`, they have no update endpoint, and nothing in this specification exposes one. A driver statement is written once. A ballot is never edited — a member who changes his mind casts a fresh ballot and the earlier one is marked `superseded`. A policy layer is program configuration and is immutable once declared. A negotiation round is a ledger entry whose only mutable aspects are the claimant counter recorded against it and its `outcome` flag, both of which are driven by transitions on the parent demand rather than by any direct write to the round.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Re-Derived Fields

This service does not read wall-clock time. It keeps a logical clock whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and an administrative capability lets a `SafetyAdmin` set that clock to an arbitrary UTC instant by supplying `current_utc`. Before any such call the clock reads the startup instant, and it never falls back to real time. The instant supplied is accepted unconditionally — nothing compares it against the clock's present value, so the clock may be wound backwards as freely as forwards, and there is no upper bound on how far ahead it may be set. The call is not a resource write: it answers 200 with a `data` object carrying the single key `current_utc`, echoing the newly established clock value in the canonical three-fractional-digit `Z` form, and no persisted row's `updated_at` or `version` moves as a result. Every window, deadline, decay band and analytics range in this domain is measured against this clock, and every timestamp the server mints for itself — `notified_at`, `submitted_at`, `preserved_at`, `cast_at`, `decided_at`, `filed_at`, `offered_at`, `countered_at`, `settled_at`, `counsel_assigned_at`, `completed_at`, `closed_at`, `resolved_at` — takes the clock's current value.

A substantial set of fields is not stored at all but re-derived from the current clock on every read. On a terminal these are `active_driver_count`, `suspended_driver_count`, `preventable_crash_count`, `crash_rate_per_million_miles`, `risk_tier`, `staffing_posture` and `late_filing_count`. On a driver they are `tenure_band`, `preventable_points` and `required_action_tier`. On a crash it is `evidence_posture`. On a review case they are `active_vote_count` and `abstain_count`. On a demand it is `round_count`. On a claim they are `litigation_risk_score`, `litigation_risk_multiplier`, `counsel_required`, `total_incurred_cents` and `claim_exposure_cents`. The practical consequence is that moving the logical clock forward, with no write of any kind in between, can change a driver's point total and required action tier, a terminal's crash rate and risk tier, and a claim's litigation multiplier. Advancing the clock also moves the evaluation point of every temporal validation: the regulatory filing deadline `report_due_at`, the evidence purge deadline `retention_deadline_at`, the appeal window, the rescission window and the elapse of a suspension period.

Stored computed fields behave differently and are called out individually where they arise. `dot_recordable`, `recordability_codes` and `report_due_at` are evaluated once at crash intake and frozen. `provisional_reserve_cents` is computed once at claim setup and frozen. A subrogation demand's `recoverable_base_cents` and `max_recoverable_cents` are frozen at the moment the demand becomes pursuable. A ballot's `weighted_member_score` is computed when the ballot is cast and never recomputed.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Contended Parents

Two write operations in this system overwrite state that another operator may have advanced in the meantime, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control rather than by locking. Requests are processed one at a time in arrival order; a refused request leaves its parent completely untouched, so the caller must re-read the parent and retry against the fresh version.

A reserve revision on a claim requires a non-optional `expected_claim_version` field carrying the `version` of the `Claim` that the adjuster last observed — as returned by claim creation, by a single-claim read, or by any prior successful write on that claim. If the supplied value does not equal the claim's current `version`, the revision is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412), and `revised_reserve_cents`, `outstanding_reserve_cents`, `reserve_status` and `version` are all left exactly as they were. This guard exists because a revision is an absolute overwrite of the reserve rather than a delta: two adjusters working the same file from stale reads would silently discard each other's estimate. Note that settlements, rescissions, counsel assignment, demand creation and every cascade that touches the claim also advance `Claim.version`, so a revision prepared before any of those has become stale as well. On success the claim's `version` increases by 1 and the response body carries the new value.

A committee ballot requires a non-optional `expected_review_case_version` field carrying the `version` of the `ReviewCase` the member last observed — as returned when deliberation was opened, by a single-case read, or by the previous accepted ballot. A mismatch against the case's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412); no ballot row is created and neither `next_vote_sequence` nor `version` moves. A ballot mints its `sequence_number` from the case's counter and can supersede an earlier ballot from the same member, so a member who read the case before another member's ballot landed would be voting against a tally he never saw. On success both `version` and `next_vote_sequence` increase by 1, and the response is the updated review case, so the next voter can chain the returned value directly.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Settling a demand, granting an appeal, issuing discipline, assigning counsel, closing a claim and resolving a subrogation demand carry no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one is an unknown field.

### 2.7. Money, Precision and the Conserved Exposure Ledger

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field carrying one has a name ending in `_cents`. Fractional cents never appear in a stored value or in a response: wherever a computation produces a fraction, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. Where a non-monetary computation carries a declared precision, that precision is likewise applied with half-up rounding: a terminal's `crash_rate_per_million_miles` is rounded half up to three decimal places, a ballot's `weighted_member_score` and a case's `committee_score` to two decimal places, and a driver's `preventable_points` to one decimal place. The staffing ratio behind `staffing_posture` is the sole exception and is compared as an exact rational value without rounding of any kind.

Claim money obeys a conserved quantity. `claim_exposure_cents` is defined as the sum of `outstanding_reserve_cents`, `indemnity_paid_cents`, `alae_paid_cents` and `reserve_released_cents`, and it is deliberately unchanged by settlement, by rescission, by subrogation recovery and by claim closure. Settlement moves cents out of the outstanding reserve and into the paid buckets; rescission moves them back; closure moves the residual outstanding reserve into the released bucket. In every one of those operations the four terms shift among themselves and the total holds. Exactly one operation is licensed to change the total — a reserve revision, which re-bases the estimate on purpose. A subrogation recovery is treated as a receipt rather than as a reduction in exposure: it accumulates into `subrogation_recovered_cents` and lowers `total_incurred_cents`, which is `indemnity_paid_cents` plus `alae_paid_cents` plus `outstanding_reserve_cents` minus `subrogation_recovered_cents`, while leaving `claim_exposure_cents` untouched.

A parallel conservation applies on the safety side. A driver's `preventable_points`, the pair of terminal headcounts `active_driver_count` and `suspended_driver_count`, and a terminal's `preventable_crash_count` are conserved across the pairing of a determination and the appeal that vacates it, evaluated at a fixed logical clock. Those quantities change only when a new crash is notified, a new determination is recorded, an employment decision is made, or the logical clock crosses a decay or analytics-window boundary.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence: the Safety-First Ladder

Requests are validated in one strict ascending order, and the **first** rule that is violated determines the entire response. No side effect of any kind is applied when a rule fails, and no later rule is consulted. This matters because many requests in this domain violate several rules at once, and the benchmark for a correct implementation is that it reports the same one a compliance auditor would.

The ladder runs as follows, from highest precedence to lowest:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, with `X-User-Role` naming one of the eight declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization.** The supplied role appears on the target endpoint's authorized list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Driver ownership.** On a driver-statement submission, `X-User-ID` must be the `id` of an existing driver row and the statement must concern that driver's own crash — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
4. **Request shape.** The body parses as JSON, contains no unknown fields, contains every required input, and every present value matches its declared type, enum domain and range constraint — including the pagination bounds on list reads. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Four conventions govern this band throughout the system. An absent body, or a body that is empty or whitespace only, is read as the empty JSON object `{}`, so an endpoint that takes no input accepts a bodyless request and an endpoint with required inputs reports them as missing; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object — an array, a string, a number — is itself a shape violation. An explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as though the key were absent: it leaves an optional field unset and it makes a required field missing rather than mistyped. Every declared string input must be non-empty after surrounding whitespace is trimmed, and this holds for optional strings as well as required ones — though the value is stored and echoed exactly as supplied, untrimmed. And a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer or a number is declared, nor is a number accepted where a boolean is declared; an integer field rejects a fractional value outright, while a number field accepts an integral JSON literal. The unknown-field check runs before the missing-required check, which runs before any per-value type check, so a body that both names an unknown field and omits a required one reports the unknown field.
5. **Conditional field shape.** Four pairings of fields that are required in one branch and forbidden in another, all reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: `criterion_scores` must be present with exactly its five integer keys when the ballot is not an abstention and must be absent when it is; `suspension_start_at` and `suspension_end_at` are required when and only when the action type is a suspension; `retraining_course_code` is required when and only when the action type is retraining or suspension; `appeal_evidence_item_id` is required when and only when the appeal grounds code is new evidence; and `recovered_amount_cents` is required when and only when the subrogation resolution is a recovery.
6. **Path existence.** Every `{id}` in the path resolves to an existing row of the collection it addresses — otherwise the entity-specific not-found code at HTTP 404.
7. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, checked in the declaration order of the owning entity's fields — otherwise the entity-specific not-found code at HTTP 404.
8. **State legality.** The addressed entity is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the entity-specific state-invalid code at HTTP 409.
9. **Appeal exhaustion.** An appeal is refused once the crash's appeals are exhausted or the case has already used its permitted appeal round — `ERR_APPEALS_EXHAUSTED`.
10. **Segregation of duties.** The decider may not be behind any live ballot on the case, and the appeal grantor may not be the operator who decided — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
11. **Version staleness.** The supplied `expected_claim_version` or `expected_review_case_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise the corresponding stale-version code at HTTP 412.
12. **Business safety rules**, in this internal order: evidence posture sufficient for a determination, at least one preserved artifact before deliberation opens, the evidence retention deadline not yet passed at preservation time, the litigation hold not still active at release time, retraining not still outstanding when a driver is continued, and separation supported by the driver's required action tier.
13. **Business temporal rules**, in this internal order: the appeal, rescission and suspension-elapse windows; then the suspension-window overlap and buffer test; then the evidence-window well-formedness and duplication test; then the future-timestamp test.
14. **Business referential rules**, in this internal order: uniqueness and pairing constraints; the driver-statement prerequisite for opening a review case; the preventability, third-party-fault and appeal-evidence prerequisites; then the policy-tower structural test.
15. **Business constraint rules**, in this internal order: committee quorum; monotonic negotiation positions; the action-tier and staffing-override tests; the counsel-required and counsel-not-required tests; then the demand-population and closure-readiness tests.
16. **Business fiscal rules**, in this internal order: policy-tower coverage first, then the subrogation recovery ceiling, then the reserve-sufficiency test.

Because the ordering is literal rather than advisory, several cross-band precedences follow that an implementer would not otherwise guess, and each is deliberate. A determination request that both lacks quorum and sits on a merely partial evidence record reports the evidence failure, not the quorum failure, because deciding on an incomplete record is the graver defect. A settlement request against an already-settled demand that would also blow through the policy tower reports the state failure, because the workflow is malformed before the money is even considered. A stale version is checked after state legality and after segregation of duties but before every business rule, so staleness never masks a malformed workflow and never hides behind a business failure. A settlement that both overflows the tower and outruns the reserve reports the coverage failure, because coverage sits at a higher fiscal precedence than reserve sufficiency. And a second review case on a crash that already has one is reported as the duplicate-case referential failure rather than as a crash state failure, even though the crash is indeed no longer in its reported state — the referential rule owns that message.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Resources in this system are peers rather than a nesting hierarchy. A negotiation round is addressed through its demand, a demand is addressed directly by its own identifier, and a single-resource path never chains two identifiers: a demand is reached at the demands collection with one `{id}`, never as a demand nested inside a claim path. One level of sub-resource collection exists for children that are created through their parent — a crash's driver statements, a crash's evidence items, a review case's ballots, a claim's demands, a claim's subrogation demands, a demand's negotiation rounds. A trailing slash is insignificant, so `/terminals/` addresses the same collection as `/terminals`. A request whose path matches no declared route at all, and a request whose method is not declared on an otherwise valid path, are both answered with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — at HTTP 404 and HTTP 405 respectively rather than at the 400 that §5 tabulates for that identifier, since routing is resolved before any handler runs. That also means routing outranks authentication: a bogus path answers 404 even when the request carries no `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` at all.

Where a path does contain a parent identifier, the identifiers are validated left to right. Requesting the evidence items of a crash identifier that does not resolve is `ERR_CRASH_NOT_FOUND`; requesting the ballots of a review case identifier that does not resolve is `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_NOT_FOUND`. The parent is always resolved before anything about the child — including the child's own shape or state — is considered. Every entity type has its own not-found error, and it is used both for an unresolvable `{id}` in the path and for an unresolvable identifier supplied in a request body: `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CRASH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_LAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEMAND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_ACTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SUBROGATION_DEMAND_NOT_FOUND`, each answering HTTP 404.

Several children are reachable only through those sub-resource reads or through a filter on their own top-level collection, and the specification depends on that reachability. A `DriverStatement` is discovered through its crash. A `CommitteeVote` created as part of casting a ballot is never returned by the ballot-casting response — which returns the updated review case — and is observable only through the review case's ballot collection, which accepts `vote` and `superseded` filters. A `NegotiationRound` is discovered through its demand's round collection, which accepts an `outcome` filter. A claim is discoverable from its crash through the `crash_id` filter on the claims collection, a review case likewise through `crash_id` and additionally through `appeal_evidence_item_id`, and a subrogation demand through `claim_id`.

### 2.10. Cascades, Compensations and Version Propagation

Several operations in this system reach well beyond the resource named in their path, and every such reach completes inside the triggering request — there are no queues, no eventual convergence and no partially applied cascades. If any part of a cascade would violate a rule, the whole request fails and nothing is written.

Whenever a cascade mutates a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by exactly 1, exactly as though it had been written directly. Recording a determination therefore bumps the review case's `version` and the crash's `version` in the same request. Preserving an artifact bumps the evidence item's `version`. Settling a demand bumps the demand's `version` and the parent claim's `version`, and on the branch that completes the claim's settlements it also bumps the `version` of every subrogation demand it promotes. Granting an appeal bumps the review case, the crash, the driver and every disciplinary action it voids. Rows that carry no `version` — ballots, negotiation rounds, driver statements and policy layers — are exempt from this rule; a negotiation round's `outcome` flips without any version arithmetic.

Two cascades are large enough that the rest of this document refers to them by name. **The determination cascade** starts when a determination is recorded and runs: review case status and determination fields, then the crash's preventability dimension, then the driver's decayed point total and required action tier, then the terminal's preventable crash count, crash rate and risk tier, and finally the litigation risk of the crash's claim when one exists. **The settlement cascade** starts when a demand is settled and runs: the demand's settlement figures and the accepted negotiation round, then the parent claim's paid buckets, layer allocations and outstanding reserve together with its handling dimension, and — only on the branch where the claim's demands are now all settled — the promotion of every draft subrogation demand on that claim to pursuable status with its recovery base frozen.

Two cascades run in the opposite direction and are described as compensations because they are required to restore prior values exactly rather than merely to approximate them. Granting an appeal unwinds a determination. Rescinding a settlement unwinds a settlement. Both are specified in detail in the workflow sections below, and both are held to the conservation rules of §2.7.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Terminal Network and the Driver Roster

Everything in this system hangs off a terminal. A `SafetyAdmin` registers one by supplying its human-readable `name`, an operator-assigned `terminal_code` of three to ten uppercase alphanumeric characters that must be unique across the whole fleet, a two-letter `region_code` used purely as an analytics grouping key — exactly two ASCII letters of either case, stored and echoed verbatim with no upper-casing, which is why the `region_code` list filter matches case-sensitively — and `annual_miles_millions` — the exposure denominator, expressed in millions of miles driven per year, which must be greater than zero and carry at most four decimal places, and which is echoed back in exactly the numeric form the client supplied even though the rate arithmetic behind it is exact decimal. A second terminal claiming a code that already exists is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_CODE_DUPLICATE`; uniqueness is checked only after the whole body has passed its shape validation. A freshly registered terminal reports zero for every counter, a `crash_rate_per_million_miles` of 0.0, a `risk_tier` of `STANDARD` and an `OK` `staffing_posture`.

Drivers are hired into a terminal. The hiring request carries the `terminal_id` of the domicile, which must resolve to an existing terminal or the request fails with `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`; an `employee_code` that must be unique among the drivers of that same terminal, so the same payroll code may legitimately appear at two different terminals but never twice at one, with a collision refused as `ERR_EMPLOYEE_CODE_DUPLICATE`; the driver's legal `full_name`; his commercial licence number `cdl_number`; and `hire_date`, an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form that serves as the tenure baseline. Nothing ties `hire_date` to the logical clock — a date in the clock's future is accepted without complaint and simply bands as `UNDER_12_MONTHS` — and the duplicate-code check runs only after the supplied `terminal_id` has been resolved. A new driver starts with `status` of `ACTIVE`, a `continued_employment_decision` of `PENDING` and a null `decision_note`.

Tenure is not stored; it is banded against the logical clock on every read. The hire date is anchored at midnight UTC on that calendar day, and the age used for banding is the whole number of days from that anchor to the current logical instant with any part-day remainder discarded, exactly as crash age is counted in §3.5. A driver whose hire date is less than 365 days behind the current instant reports a `tenure_band` of `UNDER_12_MONTHS`; one at least 365 days and fewer than 1096 days in reports `MONTHS_12_TO_36`; one at 1096 days or more reports `OVER_36_MONTHS`. The bands matter twice: green drivers load a claim's litigation risk, and tenure is one of the two breakdown axes of terminal analytics.

A terminal's headcounts are likewise derived on every read, and their definitions are asymmetric in a way that is easy to get wrong. `active_driver_count` counts every driver domiciled at the terminal whose `status` is anything other than `TERMINATED` — which means `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` and `RETRAINING_REQUIRED` drivers are all counted as active. `suspended_driver_count` counts the drivers at that terminal whose `status` is `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` or `RETRAINING_REQUIRED`. A driver who is out of service is therefore counted in both the numerator and the denominator of the staffing ratio, and only separation removes him from either.

That ratio is what produces `staffing_posture`, the field that gates new suspensions at a terminal. When `active_driver_count` is zero the posture is `OK` — there are no drivers to strain. Otherwise the ratio is `suspended_driver_count` divided by `active_driver_count`, evaluated as an exact rational value and never rounded: a ratio of one half or more yields `CRITICAL`, a ratio of at least one fifth but below one half yields `STRAINED`, and anything lower yields `OK`. Because a suspended driver sits on both sides of the division, one suspended driver out of two non-terminated drivers is exactly one half and lands on `CRITICAL` at the boundary; one of five is exactly one fifth and is `STRAINED`; one of six falls short of one fifth and is `OK`.

One more terminal counter is worth stating here because it has no time window at all: `late_filing_count` is the number of crashes frozen to this terminal whose regulatory filing was recorded late, counted over the whole history of the terminal rather than over any trailing period. Terminals may be listed with `region_code`, `risk_tier` and `staffing_posture` filters, and drivers with `terminal_id`, `status`, `tenure_band` and `required_action_tier` filters. A serialised terminal presents its keys as `id`, `name`, `terminal_code`, `region_code`, `annual_miles_millions`, `active_driver_count`, `suspended_driver_count`, `preventable_crash_count`, `crash_rate_per_million_miles`, `risk_tier`, `staffing_posture`, `late_filing_count`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`; a serialised driver presents `id`, `terminal_id`, `employee_code`, `full_name`, `cdl_number`, `hire_date`, `status`, `tenure_band`, `preventable_points`, `required_action_tier`, `continued_employment_decision`, `decision_note`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version` — so on a driver the three re-derived fields sit between `status` and the employment-decision pair.

### 3.2. Crash Notification Intake and the Regulatory Filing Clock

A crash enters the system as a notification taken by a `Dispatcher` or a `SafetyAdmin`. The notification identifies the driver operating the unit through `driver_id` and the terminal through `terminal_id`, both of which must resolve — `driver_id` is resolved first, so a notification naming two unknown identifiers reports `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`; it carries `occurred_at`, the UTC instant of the crash, and `notified_at`, the instant the carrier learned of it; it carries free-text `location_text`; and it carries the classification fields that drive everything downstream. Although the request supplies a terminal, the value the crash keeps is copied from the driver's own `terminal_id` at intake and frozen there, so terminal analytics stay stable even if the driver later transfers to another domicile. Likewise, the `notified_at` the crash keeps is the logical-clock instant at which intake was processed.

Three temporal facts are checked before anything is created: `occurred_at` must not be after the current instant, `notified_at` must not be after the current instant, and `notified_at` must not precede `occurred_at`. Any of those three failing is `ERR_FUTURE_TIMESTAMP`.

The classification fields are `severity`, one of `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE` or `CATASTROPHIC`, which drives both the required evidence set and the reserve base; `route_class`, one of `LOCAL`, `REGIONAL` or `LINEHAUL`, which is purely an analytics grouping key; the boolean `tow_away` together with `tow_reason`, one of `NONE`, `CONVENIENCE` or `DISABLING_DAMAGE`; `injury_transported_count`, the number of people taken from the scene for immediate medical attention, which may not be negative; `fatality_count`, likewise never negative; the boolean `hazmat_release`, recording whether hazardous material escaped; the boolean `mechanical_factor`, recording whether an equipment factor is in play; and the boolean `third_party_at_fault`, recording whether an identified third party is alleged at fault. That last flag has no effect at intake but is a hard prerequisite for any recovery action later.

**Recordability is decided once, at intake, and then frozen.** Four independent triggers are tested. A fatality count of at least one contributes the code `FATALITY`. A transported-injury count of at least one contributes `INJURY_TRANSPORTED`. A tow contributes `DISABLING_TOW` only when `tow_away` is true *and* `tow_reason` is `DISABLING_DAMAGE` — a unit towed for convenience is not a trigger, and both conditions must hold together. A hazardous-material release contributes `HAZMAT_RELEASE`. The codes that fired are written to `recordability_codes` in one fixed sequence — fatality first, then transported injury, then disabling tow, then hazmat release — never in the order the triggers happened to be evaluated and never sorted alphabetically. `dot_recordable` is true when at least one code fired, and `recordability_codes` is an empty list when it is false.

When the crash is recordable the filing clock starts from the crash itself rather than from intake. `report_due_at` is 24 hours after `occurred_at` when a hazardous-material release occurred, and 72 hours — three days — after `occurred_at` otherwise, and `reporting_status` starts at `PENDING_FILING`. A hazmat crash with no injury and no tow is therefore recordable on the strength of the release alone and carries the short 24-hour window. When the crash is not recordable, `report_due_at` is null and `reporting_status` starts at `NOT_REQUIRED`. Regardless of recordability, a new crash starts with `preventability_status` of `REPORTED`, an `evidence_posture` of `PARTIAL` and `appeals_exhausted` false.

Filing the regulatory report is a `SafetyManager` action that carries a single input, the agency's `filing_reference`. It is legal only on a crash that is recordable and whose `reporting_status` is still `PENDING_FILING`; a non-recordable crash, or one already filed, is refused with `ERR_CRASH_STATE_INVALID`. Timeliness then selects the target state. Filing at or before the exact due instant moves `reporting_status` from `PENDING_FILING` to `FILED`; filing after it moves `PENDING_FILING` to `LATE_FILED`. The due instant itself is inclusive — a filing landing exactly on `report_due_at` is on time. Either way the supplied `filing_reference` is recorded and `filed_at` is set to the current logical instant. The late branch carries one extra consequence: the terminal frozen on the crash reports a `late_filing_count` one higher on its next read. `FILED`, `LATE_FILED` and `NOT_REQUIRED` are all final for this dimension — nothing in the system moves a crash out of them — and the filing dimension evolves entirely independently of the preventability dimension.

Crashes may be listed with `driver_id`, `terminal_id`, `preventability_status`, `reporting_status`, `route_class` and `severity` filters, and a single-crash read re-derives the evidence posture at response time. A serialised crash presents its keys as `id`, `driver_id`, `terminal_id`, `occurred_at`, `notified_at`, `location_text`, `severity`, `route_class`, `tow_away`, `tow_reason`, `injury_transported_count`, `fatality_count`, `hazmat_release`, `mechanical_factor`, `third_party_at_fault`, `dot_recordable`, `recordability_codes`, `report_due_at`, `reporting_status`, `filing_reference`, `filed_at`, `preventability_status`, `appeals_exhausted`, `evidence_posture`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version` — so the frozen recordability trio sits with the filing fields and the re-derived `evidence_posture` is the last domain key.

### 3.3. The Driver's Account and Evidence Custody

The involved driver's own account of the crash is filed by the `Driver` role and by nobody else. The ownership half of that test can only be applied to a crash that exists, so a submission addressed at an unresolvable crash identifier is not an authorization failure: it reports `ERR_CRASH_NOT_FOUND`, and only after the body's own shape has been validated. The submission carries a `narrative` of between 1 and 4000 characters, an optional `conditions_note` describing road, weather or light conditions, and `attested`, the driver's declaration that the narrative is true. Attestation is not optional in substance: a statement submitted with `attested` false is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_ATTESTED`. On acceptance the server copies the acting operator identifier into `submitted_by_driver_id` and stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock. The statement is append-only — there is no way to amend one — and although in practice a crash carries either zero or one, no uniqueness rule enforces that: a driver may file a further statement on the same crash and each one is accepted as its own ledger row. Filing it satisfies the prerequisite that a review case otherwise cannot clear. A serialised driver statement presents its keys as `id`, `crash_id`, `narrative`, `conditions_note`, `attested`, `submitted_by_driver_id`, `submitted_at`, `created_at`.

Evidence preservation is a two-stage affair, and the distinction between the stages is the whole point. A `Dispatcher` or a `SafetyAdmin` first *requests* preservation of an artifact by declaring its `kind` — one of `TELEMATICS_SNAPSHOT`, `FORWARD_CAMERA`, `DRIVER_FACING_CAMERA`, `ELD_LOG` or `SCENE_PHOTO` — the vendor identifier of the device or feed it will be pulled from as `source_device_id`, the data window it will cover as `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, and `retention_deadline_at`, the instant at which the vendor will purge the underlying data of its own accord. An `evidence_hash` may be supplied at request time but is optional there. A requested item starts in `preservation_state` `REQUESTED` with a null `preserved_copy_uri`, a null `preserved_at` and a null `purge_reason`.

Three conditions are checked on the request, all reported as `ERR_EVIDENCE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The window must be non-degenerate: `window_start_at` must be strictly earlier than `window_end_at`. The retention deadline must still lie in the future relative to the logical clock — requesting preservation of data the vendor has already agreed to destroy by now is pointless and is refused. And for the three kinds that participate in the required-evidence sets — `TELEMATICS_SNAPSHOT`, `FORWARD_CAMERA` and `ELD_LOG` — the requested window may not overlap the window of an existing item of the same kind on the same crash that is still in `REQUESTED` or `PRESERVED` state — an item that has since become `PURGED_BEFORE_HOLD` or `RELEASED` reserves nothing and its window may be requested again. Overlap is judged on half-open intervals that include their start instant and exclude their end instant, so two windows that merely touch at an instant, one ending exactly where the next begins, are accepted as adjacent rather than rejected as overlapping. `DRIVER_FACING_CAMERA` and `SCENE_PHOTO` items are not subject to the duplication test.

Preservation itself — the act that places the artifact under litigation hold in the immutable store — is the second stage, again available to a `Dispatcher` or a `SafetyAdmin`. It carries the `preserved_copy_uri` of the immutable-store copy and the `evidence_hash`, the SHA-256 hex digest of the artifact; the hash is optional at request time but required here, and the value given here is written over whatever hash the request stage recorded. The item must currently be `REQUESTED`; an item that is already preserved, already spoliated or already released is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. The retention deadline must not yet have passed — the current instant must be no later than `retention_deadline_at`, and preserving after that instant is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. On success the item moves `REQUESTED → PRESERVED`, the URI and hash are recorded, `preserved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the crash's evidence posture is re-derived, and the item's `version` increases by 1.

Purging is a `SafetyAdmin` capability and takes a single `purge_reason`. It branches on the item's current state into two outcomes with genuinely different meanings, and the branch is selected by whether the artifact was ever preserved.

An item that is still merely `REQUESTED` when it is purged moves `REQUESTED → PURGED_BEFORE_HOLD`. This is spoliation: the carrier let evidence go before it was secured. The `purge_reason` is recorded, and the consequences reach two aggregates away. The crash's `evidence_posture` becomes `COMPROMISED` — irreversibly, since a spoliated item is never un-spoliated — which in turn flips the preventability determination onto its criteria-based variant, and the crash's claim, if one exists, is re-scored so that its litigation risk rises by the difference between the compromised-evidence loading and the partial-evidence loading. The item's `version` increases by 1.

An item that is already `PRESERVED` when it is purged moves `PRESERVED → RELEASED`, which is routine records disposal rather than spoliation — but only once the litigation hold has lifted. Release is permitted only when the item's crash has no claim at all, or when that claim's `handling_status` is `CLOSED`; attempting it while a claim on the crash is still open is refused with `ERR_LITIGATION_HOLD_ACTIVE`, because the litigation hold outranks the retention schedule. On success the `purge_reason` is recorded, the crash's evidence posture is re-derived — and since a released item no longer satisfies any required kind, a crash that was complete can fall back to partial — and the item's `version` increases by 1. Both `PURGED_BEFORE_HOLD` and `RELEASED` are final; a further purge attempt on an item in either state is `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`.

**Evidence posture** is the derived field that decides whether the committee may rule at all, and it is recomputed from scratch on every read of the crash and after every preservation or purge. The required kind set depends on `severity`: a `MINOR` crash requires a `TELEMATICS_SNAPSHOT` alone; a `MODERATE` crash requires a `TELEMATICS_SNAPSHOT` and a `FORWARD_CAMERA`; a `SEVERE` crash and a `CATASTROPHIC` crash both require a `TELEMATICS_SNAPSHOT`, a `FORWARD_CAMERA` and an `ELD_LOG`. A required kind counts as satisfied only by an item that is currently in `PRESERVED` state *and* whose data window brackets the crash instant, where bracketing means `window_start_at` is at or before `occurred_at` and `occurred_at` is strictly earlier than `window_end_at`. An item whose window ends exactly at the crash instant therefore does not bracket it, because the end of the window is exclusive. Given that, the posture is decided in a fixed precedence: if any item on the crash is in `PURGED_BEFORE_HOLD` the posture is `COMPROMISED`; otherwise, if every required kind is satisfied the posture is `COMPLETE`; otherwise it is `PARTIAL`. Spoliation outranks completeness, so a crash with every required kind preserved *and* one spoliated item is `COMPROMISED`, not `COMPLETE`.

Evidence items are readable individually, listable fleet-wide with `crash_id`, `kind` and `preservation_state` filters, and listable per crash with `kind` and `preservation_state` filters. A serialised evidence item presents its keys as `id`, `crash_id`, `kind`, `source_device_id`, `window_start_at`, `window_end_at`, `retention_deadline_at`, `preservation_state`, `evidence_hash`, `preserved_copy_uri`, `preserved_at`, `purge_reason`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`.

### 3.4. The Safety Review Committee: Deliberation, Determination and Appeal

A `SafetyManager` opens a review case on a crash by supplying the `crash_id` and the `committee_name` of the seated committee, plus an optional `statement_waiver_reason`. The crash must resolve or the request is `ERR_CRASH_NOT_FOUND`. A crash may carry at most one review case for its whole life, so a second attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_EXISTS` — and this is precisely the case where the referential rule wins over the state rule, because a crash that already has a case is no longer in its `REPORTED` state yet the duplicate is what the operator needs to be told. That state rule is nonetheless real and is checked immediately after the duplicate test: a crash whose `preventability_status` is anything other than `REPORTED` cannot carry a new case and answers `ERR_CRASH_STATE_INVALID`. The driver's account is a prerequisite, tested last of the three: unless a driver statement already exists for the crash, a `statement_waiver_reason` must be supplied to explain why the committee will proceed without one, and a request offering neither is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_STATEMENT_REQUIRED`.

Opening a case creates it in `DRAFT` with `determination` of `PENDING`, `decision_basis` of `NOT_APPLICABLE`, a null `committee_score`, `appeal_round` at 0, `next_vote_sequence` at 1, and nulls in `appeal_grounds`, `appeal_grounds_code`, `appeal_evidence_item_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`. In the same request the crash moves `REPORTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, its `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed.

Seating the committee and opening balloting moves the case `DRAFT → DELIBERATION`, and it is gated on custody: at least one evidence item on the case's crash must already be in `PRESERVED` state, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_PRESERVED_EVIDENCE`. A case that is not in `DRAFT` — one where deliberation has already been opened, for instance — is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. Opening deliberation bumps the case's `version`, and that bumped value is the one the first ballot must quote.

**Balloting.** A `CommitteeMember` casts a ballot against a case that is in `DELIBERATION`; voting on a case still in `DRAFT`, or on one already `DECIDED`, is `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The ballot carries `expected_review_case_version` as described in §2.6, a `vote` of `PREVENTABLE`, `NON_PREVENTABLE` or `ABSTAIN`, an optional `comment`, and — when the vote is not an abstention — a `criterion_scores` object with exactly five keys, each an integer from 0 through 4 inclusive: `speed_and_spacing`, `hazard_recognition`, `vehicle_condition_precheck`, `regulatory_compliance` and `evasive_action`. An abstention must omit `criterion_scores` entirely; supplying scores with an abstention, or omitting them on a substantive vote, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

An accepted ballot creates a ledger row whose `review_case_id` is the case it was cast on, whose `sequence_number` is taken from the case's `next_vote_sequence`, whose `member_user_id` is the acting operator identifier, and whose `cast_at` is the current logical instant. Every earlier ballot on the same case from the same `member_user_id` is marked `superseded`, so a member always has exactly one live ballot no matter how many times he votes; `sequence_number` is the sole ordering key for deciding which of a member's ballots is the latest. The case's `next_vote_sequence` and `version` each increase by 1, and the response body is the updated review case rather than the ballot, carried at HTTP 201 because a ballot row was in fact created — the ballot itself becomes visible only through the case's ballot collection, where a serialised ballot presents its keys as `id`, `review_case_id`, `member_user_id`, `sequence_number`, `vote`, `criterion_scores`, `weighted_member_score`, `superseded`, `comment`, `cast_at`, `created_at`. The case's derived `active_vote_count` is the number of live, non-abstaining ballots and `abstain_count` is the number of live abstentions, both recomputed on every read.

Each substantive ballot carries its own `weighted_member_score`, computed at the moment it is cast and never recomputed thereafter. The five criteria are weighted: `speed_and_spacing` at 0.30, `hazard_recognition` at 0.25, `regulatory_compliance` at 0.20, `vehicle_condition_precheck` at 0.15 and `evasive_action` at 0.10, summing to 1.00 across all five. The subtlety is that `vehicle_condition_precheck` is **not applicable** when the crash's `mechanical_factor` is false, and in that case its weight is renormalised away rather than being treated as a score of zero. Concretely: the applicable criteria are the four others when `mechanical_factor` is false and all five when it is true; the denominator is the sum of the weights of the applicable criteria, which is 0.85 in the four-criterion case and 1.00 in the five-criterion case; the score is the sum, over the applicable criteria only, of each criterion's supplied integer multiplied by that criterion's weight, divided by that denominator, and the quotient is rounded half up to two decimal places. An abstaining ballot carries a null `weighted_member_score` and never enters any arithmetic.

**Recording the determination** is a `SafetyManager` action with no request body, and four conditions guard it, evaluated in this order. The case must be in `DELIBERATION`, or the response is `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The acting operator must not be the `member_user_id` behind any live ballot on the case, or the response is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The crash's `evidence_posture` must be `COMPLETE` or `COMPROMISED`, and a merely `PARTIAL` record is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_POSTURE_INSUFFICIENT`. And the case must have at least three live, non-abstaining ballots, or the response is `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; abstentions never count toward that quorum of three no matter how many are cast. The evidence check outranks the quorum check, so a case that both lacks quorum and rests on a partial record reports the evidence failure.

The arithmetic then runs in a pinned order. `committee_score` is the mean of the *already-rounded* `weighted_member_score` values of the live, non-abstaining ballots, and that mean is itself rounded half up to two decimal places. The rounding order is not incidental: the per-ballot scores are rounded to two decimals first and the mean is taken of those rounded figures, never of the unrounded quotients. Three ballots whose unrounded quotients are 1.82352941, 2.76470588 and 3.58823529 round to 1.82, 2.76 and 3.59, whose mean rounds to 2.72; averaging the unrounded quotients instead would yield 2.73, and that figure is wrong. `committee_score` is computed and stored on every determination under both decision variants, so the criterion arithmetic remains observable even when the ballots alone are dispositive.

Which rule turns that arithmetic into a `determination` depends on the crash's evidence posture, and the choice is recorded in `decision_basis` so the reader can see which rule applied. On a `COMPLETE` evidence record the basis is `UNWEIGHTED_MAJORITY`: the determination is `PREVENTABLE` when the count of live `PREVENTABLE` ballots strictly exceeds the count of live `NON_PREVENTABLE` ballots, and `NON_PREVENTABLE` otherwise — a tie therefore resolves to `NON_PREVENTABLE`, because the carrier bears the burden. With a complete record the committee's own judgement is dispositive and the criterion arithmetic is advisory. On a `COMPROMISED` evidence record the basis is `WEIGHTED_CRITERIA`: the determination is `PREVENTABLE` when `committee_score` is at least 2.50 and `NON_PREVENTABLE` when it falls below that threshold. Where evidence was spoliated the ballots are discounted and the recorded criterion scoring governs. The two rules genuinely disagree in practice: three ballots scoring 2.75, 2.50 and 2.95 with votes of non-preventable, non-preventable and preventable produce a `committee_score` of 2.73, which is `PREVENTABLE` under the criteria rule and `NON_PREVENTABLE` under the majority rule.

The determination request then branches on how many appeals the case has already consumed, and the two branches share their trigger, their four guards, their arithmetic and their entire determination logic — they differ only in target state and in one additional side effect. When `appeal_round` is still below the single permitted appeal round, the case moves `DELIBERATION → DECIDED`, recording `determination`, `decision_basis`, `committee_score`, `decided_by_user_id` from the acting operator and `decided_at` from the logical clock, and the case's `version` increases by 1. When `appeal_round` has already reached the permitted maximum of 1, the case moves `DELIBERATION → CLOSED_FINAL` instead, recording exactly the same five fields with exactly the same values, moving the crash exactly as the other branch does, incrementing `version` by 1 — and additionally setting the crash's `appeals_exhausted` to true, which permanently blocks any further appeal and makes the recorded preventability permanent. `CLOSED_FINAL` is the only state from which a review case never moves again.

Either branch drives the determination cascade. The crash moves `UNDER_REVIEW → PREVENTABLE` or `UNDER_REVIEW → NON_PREVENTABLE` according to the determination, and its `version` increases by 1. Because the crash's preventability changed, the driver's decayed point total and required action tier are re-derived — a `PREVENTABLE` outcome adds the crash's points while a `NON_PREVENTABLE` outcome contributes nothing at all — and the terminal's preventable crash count, crash rate and risk tier follow on their next read. If a claim exists on the crash, its litigation risk score, multiplier and counsel requirement are re-derived as well.

**Granting an appeal** returns a decided case to deliberation and unwinds every consequence of the vacated determination. It is a `SafetyManager` action carrying `appeal_grounds`, the narrative grounds, and `appeal_grounds_code`, one of `NEW_EVIDENCE`, `PROCEDURAL` or `CRITERIA_MISAPPLICATION`. Its guards run in the ladder's order. The case must be in `DECIDED`; appealing a case in `DRAFT`, in `DELIBERATION` or in `CLOSED_FINAL` is `ERR_REVIEW_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The crash's `appeals_exhausted` must be false and the case's `appeal_round` must still be below the maximum of 1, so exactly one appeal is available per case and a second attempt is `ERR_APPEALS_EXHAUSTED`. The acting operator must not be the case's `decided_by_user_id`, or the response is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the manager who ruled may not be the manager who reopens. The appeal must be filed no later than 14 days after `decided_at`, and one filed after that window is `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A `NEW_EVIDENCE` appeal must cite `appeal_evidence_item_id`, and because that citation is a body reference it is resolved at the ladder's band 7 — ahead of all four guards just described — so an unresolvable item reports `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` even on a case that is not `DECIDED`, has already exhausted its appeal or sits outside the appeal window. What is genuinely last is the citation's substance: the cited item must belong to the case's own crash and be in `PRESERVED` state or the response is `ERR_APPEAL_EVIDENCE_INVALID`. A `PROCEDURAL` or `CRITERIA_MISAPPLICATION` appeal must omit the citation altogether.

An accepted appeal moves the case `DECIDED → DELIBERATION`, increases `appeal_round` by 1, records `appeal_grounds`, `appeal_grounds_code` and — where cited — `appeal_evidence_item_id`, and resets the decision fields: `determination` back to `PENDING`, `decision_basis` back to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `committee_score` back to null, and both `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` back to null. Existing ballots are deliberately retained and remain live, so the pre-appeal tally still satisfies quorum unless a member chooses to re-ballot and supersede his own earlier vote. The case's `version` increases by 1. Because the case has now consumed its one appeal, the next determination recorded on it lands in `CLOSED_FINAL` and sets `appeals_exhausted`.

The compensating half of the appeal is the strict part. The crash moves back out of `PREVENTABLE` or `NON_PREVENTABLE` and into `UNDER_REVIEW`, with its `version` increased by 1. The driver's `preventable_points` are recomputed from scratch and must land on exactly the value they held before the determination — which is why only `PREVENTABLE` crashes ever contribute points, and why an appealed `NON_PREVENTABLE` determination leaves the point total unmoved. Every disciplinary action on the appealed crash that is still in `ISSUED` state moves `ISSUED → VOIDED` with its `retraining_status` reset to `NOT_REQUIRED`, releasing that action's suspension window so a later suspension may legitimately reuse the same interval; an action that had already reached `COMPLETED` is left untouched, because completed history is not rewritten. When the recomputed `required_action_tier` has fallen below the suspension tier and no non-voided suspension of that driver remains, a driver sitting in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` is returned to `ACTIVE`, restoring the terminal's `suspended_driver_count` to its pre-discipline value on the next read while `active_driver_count` — which never moved, since a suspended driver was already counted there — stays as it was. A driver sitting in `RETRAINING_REQUIRED` is not restored this way: his `status` is left exactly where it is even though the retraining action that put him there has just been voided, so the appeal releases the action without returning him to duty. If a claim exists on the crash, its litigation risk is re-derived to reflect the vacated determination. Evaluated at a fixed logical clock, the pairing of a determination and the appeal that vacates it leaves the driver's points, both terminal headcounts and the terminal's preventable crash count exactly where they started.

Review cases may be listed with `crash_id`, `appeal_evidence_item_id`, `status` and `determination` filters, and a case's ballots with `vote` and `superseded` filters. A serialised review case presents its keys as `id`, `crash_id`, `committee_name`, `statement_waiver_reason`, `status`, `determination`, `decision_basis`, `committee_score`, `active_vote_count`, `abstain_count`, `next_vote_sequence`, `appeal_round`, `appeal_grounds`, `appeal_grounds_code`, `appeal_evidence_item_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version` — so the two re-derived tally fields sit between `committee_score` and `next_vote_sequence`.

### 3.5. Driver Accountability: Points, Discipline and Continued Employment

A driver's accountability is expressed as a decaying point total, re-derived against the logical clock on every read of the driver and after every cascade that changes one of his crashes' preventability. Only crashes whose `preventability_status` is `PREVENTABLE` contribute; crashes still `UNDER_REVIEW` and crashes found `NON_PREVENTABLE` contribute nothing whatsoever, and that is exactly what makes the appeal compensation restorable to the cent.

Each contributing crash yields a base figure from its `severity` — 2 points for a `MINOR` crash, 4 for a `MODERATE` one, 8 for a `SEVERE` one and 12 for a `CATASTROPHIC` one — multiplied by a decay factor selected from the crash's age. Age is the whole number of days between the crash's `occurred_at` and the current logical instant, taking the whole days only and discarding any part-day remainder. A crash fewer than 365 days old keeps its full value at a factor of 1.0. One at least 365 days old but fewer than 730 days old is halved, at a factor of 0.5. One 730 days old or older contributes nothing, at a factor of 0.0. The bands are half-open on their upper edge, so a crash exactly 365 days old has already aged into the halved band and one exactly 730 days old has already expired. The products are summed across all contributing crashes and the total is rounded half up to one decimal place to give `preventable_points`.

`required_action_tier` is then a threshold band over that total, and it is the floor on how lightly the driver may be treated: at least 12 points requires `TERMINATION_REVIEW`, at least 8 requires `SUSPENSION`, at least 4 requires `RETRAINING`, at least 1 requires `COACHING`, and anything below 1 — including exactly zero — is `NONE`. Because the bands are read against the clock, advancing the clock alone can lower a driver's tier; it never retroactively voids a disciplinary action that has already been issued.

**Issuing discipline** is a `SafetyManager` action naming the `driver_id` of the disciplined driver, the `crash_id` of the preventable crash the action arises from, an `action_type`, and an `action_note` recording the manager's rationale. Both references must resolve, failing as `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CRASH_NOT_FOUND` respectively, with `driver_id` resolved first. A driver who has already been separated accepts no further action of any kind and yields `ERR_DRIVER_STATE_INVALID`, and because that is a state failure it outranks every rule below it. The suspension-window test described below runs next; only then comes the pairing test — the supplied `driver_id` must be the very driver recorded on that crash, and a mismatch is `ERR_DRIVER_CRASH_MISMATCH` — followed by the preventability prerequisite: the crash's `preventability_status` must be `PREVENTABLE`, and discipline arising from a crash that is still under review, was found non-preventable, or was never reviewed at all is refused with `ERR_CRASH_NOT_PREVENTABLE`.

The `action_type` is one of `COACHING`, `RETRAINING`, `SUSPENSION` or `TERMINATION_RECOMMENDATION`, and it may not be lighter than what the driver's points already demand. Ranking the action types from lightest to heaviest as coaching, retraining, suspension, termination recommendation, and ranking the required tiers from lowest to highest as none, coaching, retraining, suspension, termination review, the chosen action's rank must be at least as high as the driver's required tier's rank — where none is below coaching. A manager who offers coaching to a driver whose points already demand suspension is refused with `ERR_ACTION_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`; a manager is always free to go heavier than required.

Three conditional inputs attach to the action type. `suspension_start_at` and `suspension_end_at`, the inclusive bounds of the out-of-service period, are required when and only when the action is a suspension, must be absent otherwise, and the end must be later than the start — an equal or inverted pair is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, reported in the same conditional-shape band and therefore before either reference in the body is resolved. `retraining_course_code`, naming the course the driver must complete, is required when and only when the action is a retraining or a suspension, and must be absent for a coaching or a termination recommendation. `override_reason` is the escape hatch for staffing pressure, described below.

A new suspension window is tested against every non-voided suspension already on that driver's record. The windows are compared as half-open intervals including their start and excluding their end, and each existing interval is then widened by a mandatory separation of 24 hours on either side before the comparison; a new window that overlaps an existing one, or that falls inside that one-day separation, is refused with `ERR_SUSPENSION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Voided suspensions are excluded from the test, which is what lets a later suspension legitimately reuse an interval that an appeal released. Because consecutive out-of-service periods may legitimately stack on the same driver, a driver already sitting in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` may receive an additional suspension, and that second window is checked against the first.

Staffing pressure is the other gate. When the disciplined driver's terminal is reporting a `staffing_posture` of `CRITICAL`, a suspension may still be issued but only with an explicit `override_reason`; without one the request is refused with `ERR_STAFFING_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED`. When an `override_reason` is supplied the action records `override_flag` as true, leaving a visible marker that the terminal was already strained when the driver was pulled from duty. A `STRAINED` or `OK` posture imposes no such requirement. The field is not confined to that branch, though: `override_reason` is accepted on any action type under any posture, and `override_flag` simply mirrors its presence, so a coaching carrying an override reason records `override_flag` true to no further effect.

An accepted action is created in `ISSUED` state with `issued_by_user_id` set from the acting operator and a null `completed_at`. Its `retraining_status` starts at `PENDING` when a `retraining_course_code` was supplied and at `NOT_REQUIRED` when none was — so coaching and termination recommendations carry `NOT_REQUIRED` from birth and can never be completed as retraining. The driver's own state follows the action type. A retraining moves an `ACTIVE` driver `ACTIVE → RETRAINING_REQUIRED`. A suspension moves an `ACTIVE` driver `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION`, which raises the terminal's `suspended_driver_count` by 1 on its next read while leaving `active_driver_count` untouched — and that shift alone can move the terminal's `staffing_posture`. A suspension on a driver already in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` leaves him there: the driver is counted as suspended once, not once per action, so neither terminal count moves. Only an `ACTIVE` driver's `status` ever moves at all — a retraining or a suspension issued to a driver already in `RETRAINING_REQUIRED` or in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` leaves that status exactly as it stands, and since both of those statuses already count toward `suspended_driver_count`, neither headcount moves in those cases either. Coaching and termination recommendations leave the driver's `status` alone. In every case the driver's `version` increases by 1 alongside the created action.

**Completing retraining** is recorded by a `SafetyAdmin` with an optional `completion_note`. The action must be in `ISSUED` state with a `retraining_status` of `PENDING`; an action that is already `COMPLETED` or has been `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_ACTION_STATE_INVALID`, and so is one that never required retraining at all. When the action is a suspension there is an additional temporal condition: the out-of-service period must have elapsed, meaning the current logical instant must be strictly later than `suspension_end_at`. Reinstating a suspended driver early is not permitted and is refused with `ERR_SUSPENSION_NOT_ELAPSED`. On success the action moves `ISSUED → COMPLETED`, its `retraining_status` becomes `COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped from the clock and its `version` increases by 1. The driver returns to `ACTIVE` — from `RETRAINING_REQUIRED` or from `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` alike — provided no other action of that driver is still `ISSUED` with retraining pending; his `version` increases by 1, and where he was suspended the terminal's `suspended_driver_count` falls by 1 on the next read, restoring the staffing posture while `active_driver_count` again never moves. Where he is not returned — because another action of his is still `ISSUED` with retraining pending, or because his `status` is already something other than `RETRAINING_REQUIRED` or `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` — no field of the driver changes and his `version` does not move at all; only the completed action itself is written. Both `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED` are final states for a disciplinary action.

**The continued-employment decision** closes the loop and is a `SafetyManager` action carrying `decision`, either `CONTINUE` or `SEPARATE`, plus an optional `decision_note` recording the rationale. A driver who is already `TERMINATED` accepts neither, yielding `ERR_DRIVER_STATE_INVALID`. A `CONTINUE` decision is refused while any disciplinary action of that driver is still `ISSUED` with `retraining_status` of `PENDING`, on the reasoning that a driver cannot be affirmed back into the seat while a mandated course is outstanding; the refusal is `ERR_RETRAINING_INCOMPLETE`. A `SEPARATE` decision is refused unless the driver's `required_action_tier` currently reads `TERMINATION_REVIEW` — separation must be supported by the points, not merely by the manager's judgement — and the refusal is `ERR_SEPARATION_NOT_SUPPORTED`.

A recorded `CONTINUE` sets `continued_employment_decision` to `CONTINUE`, records the `decision_note` and leaves the driver's `status` where it was; its `version` still increases by 1, since the decision field itself was written. `decision_note` is an absolute overwrite rather than an accumulation, so a decision that omits the note records null and discards whatever note an earlier decision left behind. Nothing forbids repeating the decision: any number of `CONTINUE` decisions may be recorded on a driver who is not `TERMINATED`, each one re-evaluating the retraining guard and bumping his `version` again. A recorded `SEPARATE` sets `continued_employment_decision` to `SEPARATE`, records the `decision_note` and moves the driver to `TERMINATED` from whichever of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED_PENDING_ACTION` or `RETRAINING_REQUIRED` he occupied; his `version` increases by 1. Because both terminal headcounts are defined over non-terminated drivers, separation removes him from `active_driver_count` on the next read, and where he was suspended or in retraining it removes him from `suspended_driver_count` at the same time. `TERMINATED` is final: no discipline, no retraining completion and no further employment decision may follow.

Disciplinary actions may be listed with `driver_id`, `crash_id`, `status`, `action_type` and `retraining_status` filters, which is also how the actions arising from a particular crash are discovered. A serialised disciplinary action presents its keys as `id`, `driver_id`, `crash_id`, `action_type`, `action_note`, `suspension_start_at`, `suspension_end_at`, `retraining_course_code`, `retraining_status`, `status`, `override_reason`, `override_flag`, `issued_by_user_id`, `completed_at`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`.

### 3.6. Opening the Liability Claim: Litigation Risk, Reserve and Counsel

A `ClaimsAdjuster` opens a liability claim against a crash by supplying the `crash_id`, the `claimant_notice_date` — the calendar date on which the carrier received notice of a third-party claim, in year-month-day form — and an optional `adjuster_note`. That date carries no bound of any kind beyond its `YYYY-MM-DD` shape: it is compared neither against the logical clock nor against the crash's `occurred_at`, so a notice date in the future, or one earlier than the crash itself, is accepted and echoed exactly as supplied. The crash must resolve or the request is `ERR_CRASH_NOT_FOUND`, and a crash carries at most one claim, so a second attempt is `ERR_CLAIM_EXISTS`. A claim opens with `handling_status` of `SETUP` and `reserve_status` of `PROVISIONAL`; these are two genuinely parallel dimensions that advance independently, so a reserve revision moves the reserve dimension without touching the workflow dimension and recording a demand moves the workflow dimension without touching the reserve dimension.

A new claim also starts with `counsel_assigned` false, a null `counsel_firm_name`, a `counsel_type` of `NONE`, a null `counsel_assigned_at`, `litigation_flag` false, a null `closed_at`, a null `revised_reserve_cents`, an empty `layer_allocations` array, and zeros in `indemnity_paid_cents`, `alae_paid_cents`, `reserve_released_cents`, `subrogation_recovered_cents`, `sir_eroded_cents` and `insured_paid_cents`.

**Litigation risk** is scored on setup and thereafter re-derived on every read of the claim, as well as after every cascade that changes the crash's preventability, the crash's evidence posture or the claim's demand population. `litigation_risk_score` starts from the crash's preventability: 30 points when the crash has been found `PREVENTABLE`, 10 while it is still `UNDER_REVIEW`, 5 while it is merely `REPORTED`, and nothing at all once it has been found `NON_PREVENTABLE`. The crash's evidence posture adds 25 points when the record is `COMPROMISED`, 5 when it is `PARTIAL` and nothing when it is `COMPLETE`. A further 20 points are added when the crash's `fatality_count` is at least one, 10 when the crash involved a hazardous-material release, 5 when the driver's `tenure_band` currently reads `UNDER_12_MONTHS`, and 15 when the claim carries at least one `THIRD_PARTY_BODILY_INJURY` demand whose claimant is `represented_by_counsel`. The sum is then capped at 100. Crash `severity` deliberately plays no part here: severity already drives the reserve base, and counting it in both places would double-load the estimate.

Two fields fall straight out of that score. `counsel_required` is true when `litigation_risk_score` is at least 55 — a score of exactly 55 does require counsel. And `litigation_risk_multiplier` is a band over the same score: 1.00 at 24 points or below, 1.15 from 25 through 54, 1.35 from 55 through 79, and 1.60 at 80 or above. A preventable crash on a partial record scores 35 and multiplies at 1.15 without requiring counsel; the same crash on a compromised record scores exactly 55, multiplies at 1.35 and does require counsel; add a fatality, a represented bodily-injury demand, a hazmat release and a green driver and the raw sum of 105 is capped to 100 and multiplies at 1.60. Because the score is derived on read, preserving a missing evidence kind or granting an appeal moves the multiplier with no claim-side write at all — while the stored reserve stays exactly where it was, which is what makes multiplier-versus-reserve staleness visible to an adjuster.

**The reserve** is computed by one rule with two interchangeable front ends. Both variants produce a gross exposure figure, and the tail applied to that figure is identical in both cases and pinned in order. First, the indemnity component is the gross exposure multiplied by the claim's current `litigation_risk_multiplier`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Second, the expense component is taken **on the unmultiplied gross exposure**, not on the indemnity component: it is 18 percent of the gross exposure rounded half up to a whole cent when the claim has counsel assigned, and exactly zero when it does not. Loading the multiplied figure instead would count litigation risk twice. Third, the reserve is the greater of the sum of those two components and a floor of 250,000 cents.

The provisional variant estimates from the crash alone, and it is what runs at claim setup: the gross exposure is the severity base for the crash's `severity` — 500,000 cents for `MINOR`, 2,500,000 for `MODERATE`, 12,000,000 for `SEVERE` and 45,000,000 for `CATASTROPHIC` — plus 3,500,000 cents for each person recorded in `injury_transported_count`, plus 90,000,000 cents for each fatality recorded in `fatality_count`. Before any demand has been quantified the crash itself is the only defensible exposure signal. A `SEVERE` crash with one transported injury and no fatality therefore grosses 15,500,000 cents, which at a multiplier of 1.00 with no counsel reserves 15,500,000, at 1.15 reserves 17,825,000, and at 1.35 reserves 20,925,000.

The demand-weighted variant replaces the severity proxy entirely once written demands exist: the gross exposure is the sum, over every demand on the claim, of that demand's `settlement_total_cents` when the demand has already reached `SETTLED` or `CLOSED_PAID`, and of its original `demand_amount_cents` otherwise. An already-settled demand therefore contributes what it actually cost rather than what was originally asked. Two open demands of 20,000,000 and 6,000,000 cents gross 26,000,000, which at a multiplier of 1.15 gives an indemnity component of 29,900,000; if counsel has been assigned, the expense loading is 18 percent of the unmultiplied 26,000,000, or 4,680,000, for a reserve of 34,580,000. Loading the multiplied figure instead would have produced 5,382,000 and a reserve of 35,282,000, and that is the wrong answer. The 250,000-cent floor never binds under the provisional variant, since the lowest severity base already exceeds it; it becomes reachable only through the demand-weighted variant on a claim whose demands total less than that.

At setup the provisional variant runs and its result is frozen into `provisional_reserve_cents`, which is never recomputed again, and the same figure seeds `outstanding_reserve_cents`.

**Revising the reserve** is a `ClaimsAdjuster` action carrying `expected_claim_version`, a `basis` selecting the variant, and an optional `revision_note` — which is validated as a non-empty string when it is supplied but is not persisted anywhere on the claim and appears in no response. A `basis` of `PROVISIONAL_REFRESH` re-runs the severity estimate at the claim's current multiplier and counsel state; a `basis` of `DEMAND_BASED` re-bases the estimate on the written demands and requires at least one demand to exist on the claim, failing with `ERR_NO_DEMANDS_TO_WEIGHT` when none does. A closed claim accepts no revision and answers `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`; a stale version answers `ERR_CLAIM_VERSION_STALE` as described in §2.6.

An accepted revision writes the selected variant's output to `revised_reserve_cents`, moves `reserve_status` `PROVISIONAL → REVISED` on the first revision and holds it at `REVISED` on every subsequent one — the reserve may be re-estimated any number of times before the claim closes — and resets `outstanding_reserve_cents` to `revised_reserve_cents` less `indemnity_paid_cents` less `alae_paid_cents`, floored at zero so it never goes negative. The claim's `version` increases by 1. This is the single operation in the system licensed to change `claim_exposure_cents`, and it is expected to: re-basing an estimate is exactly what changing exposure means.

**Assigning counsel** is a `ClaimsManager` action carrying `counsel_firm_name`, the defence firm, and `counsel_type`, one of `NONE`, `PANEL`, `IN_HOUSE` or `LEAD_TRIAL`. It is refused on a claim whose `counsel_required` is false, with `ERR_COUNSEL_NOT_REQUIRED` — panel counsel is not assigned to files that do not warrant it — and refused on a closed claim with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. On success `counsel_assigned` becomes true, `counsel_assigned_at` is stamped from the clock, `litigation_flag` is set true to mark the file as being handled under litigation risk, and the claim's `version` increases by 1. Two consequences follow. The expense loading now applies — but only prospectively, entering the stored reserve on the next reserve revision rather than retroactively adjusting the reserve already on the books. And offers on the claim's bodily-injury demands are unblocked. Nothing forbids repeating the assignment: a claim that already has counsel accepts a further assignment for as long as its `counsel_required` is still true, and that assignment is an absolute overwrite — `counsel_firm_name` and `counsel_type` take the newly supplied values, `counsel_assigned_at` is re-stamped from the current clock value, `litigation_flag` stays true, and the `version` increases by 1 again.

Claims may be listed with `crash_id`, `handling_status`, `reserve_status` and `counsel_required` filters, and a single-claim read re-derives the risk score, the multiplier, the counsel requirement, `total_incurred_cents` and `claim_exposure_cents` at response time. Every write addressed at a claim — the reserve revision, the counsel assignment and the closure of §3.9 — answers 200 with that same updated claim object. The `counsel_required` filter is matched against that re-derived value rather than against anything stored, so the same claim can move in and out of the filtered page as the clock or the crash's record moves. A serialised claim presents its keys as `id`, `crash_id`, `claimant_notice_date`, `adjuster_note`, `handling_status`, `reserve_status`, `litigation_risk_score`, `litigation_risk_multiplier`, `counsel_required`, `counsel_assigned`, `counsel_firm_name`, `counsel_type`, `counsel_assigned_at`, `litigation_flag`, `closed_at`, `provisional_reserve_cents`, `revised_reserve_cents`, `outstanding_reserve_cents`, `indemnity_paid_cents`, `alae_paid_cents`, `reserve_released_cents`, `subrogation_recovered_cents`, `sir_eroded_cents`, `insured_paid_cents`, `layer_allocations`, `total_incurred_cents`, `claim_exposure_cents`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`.

### 3.7. The Policy Tower, Third-Party Demands and the Negotiation Ledger

The insurance program is declared one layer at a time, by a `ClaimsManager` or a `SafetyAdmin`, before any settlement can be paid. Each layer names its `program_year`, its 1-based `layer_index` giving its position in the tower, its `layer_type` — `SIR` for the self-insured retention, `PRIMARY` or `EXCESS` — its `attachment_point_cents`, the cumulative amount that must be consumed by the layers beneath it before this one attaches, its `limit_cents`, the per-occurrence capacity of the layer, and `insurer_name`, the carrier or retention holder standing behind it. `layer_index` must be at least 1 and is unique within its program year, so redeclaring an index is `ERR_POLICY_LAYER_DUPLICATE`. `limit_cents` must be greater than zero and `attachment_point_cents` may not be negative.

The tower's shape is validated when a layer is written and again before any settlement is allocated across it. Three conditions define a well-formed tower for a program year: the layer at index 1 must be of type `SIR` and must attach at zero; the indices must run contiguously from 1 with no gaps; and every layer's `attachment_point_cents` must equal the sum of the `limit_cents` of all the layers below it in the same program year. A tower that violates any of those is `ERR_POLICY_TOWER_MALFORMED`, and a program year with no declared layer at all is malformed on the same test. Because the write-time test is applied to the year's whole layer set with the newly supplied layer already in it, and a refused layer is not persisted, a tower can only be built upwards one index at a time: the first layer declared for a year must be `layer_index` 1 of type `SIR` attaching at zero, and each later layer must be the next index with `attachment_point_cents` equal to the running sum of the limits already declared beneath it. The duplicate-index test is reported ahead of the structural test, so redeclaring an existing index answers `ERR_POLICY_LAYER_DUPLICATE` even where the redeclared layer would also break the tower's shape. Layers are program configuration and are immutable once declared — there is no way to amend or retire one. A claim consumes the layer set whose `program_year` equals the UTC calendar year of its crash's `occurred_at`, so a crash that happened on the last day of a year is settled against that year's tower and not the next one's. Layers are readable individually and listable with `program_year` and `layer_type` filters. A serialised policy layer presents its keys as `id`, `program_year`, `layer_index`, `layer_type`, `attachment_point_cents`, `limit_cents`, `insurer_name`, `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` as §2.4 describes.

**A third-party demand** is recorded against a claim by a `ClaimsAdjuster`, naming the `claimant_name` of the demanding party, a `claimant_type` of either `THIRD_PARTY_PROPERTY` or `THIRD_PARTY_BODILY_INJURY`, the `demand_amount_cents` actually demanded, which must be greater than zero, whether the claimant is `represented_by_counsel`, and `demand_received_at`, the instant the written demand arrived, which may not be later than the current logical instant — a future-dated demand is `ERR_FUTURE_TIMESTAMP`. A closed claim accepts no new demand and answers `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

The demand is created in `DEMANDED` state with `next_round_index` at 1, `rescission_count` at 0, a null `settlement_reference` and nulls in `settled_amount_cents`, `alae_cents`, `settlement_total_cents` and `settled_at`; its derived `round_count` is the number of negotiation rounds recorded against it and reads 0 until the first offer. The parent claim reacts on every branch: only the `SETUP → NEGOTIATION` transition is declared, so the first demand moves the claim out of `SETUP`, each additional demand accumulates without leaving `NEGOTIATION`, and a demand recorded on a claim that has already reached `SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE` leaves the handling dimension sitting at `SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE` — the claim does not fall back to `NEGOTIATION` — while in every one of those cases the claim's `version` increases by 1 and its reserve dimension is untouched. One demand attribute reaches back into the claim's risk: a `THIRD_PARTY_BODILY_INJURY` demand whose claimant is represented adds 15 points to the claim's `litigation_risk_score` on the next read, which can tip a file over the counsel threshold without anybody writing to the claim.

**Negotiation** proceeds as an append-only ledger of rounds, each recording one carrier position and at most one claimant response. Opening a round is a `ClaimsAdjuster` action carrying a single `carrier_offer_cents`, which must be greater than zero. It is legal from exactly two demand states. From `DEMANDED` it opens the first round and moves the demand `DEMANDED → OFFER_OUTSTANDING`. From `COUNTER_OUTSTANDING` it opens a fresh round in answer to the claimant's counter and moves the demand `COUNTER_OUTSTANDING → OFFER_OUTSTANDING`. Offering while an offer already stands — that is, while the demand is in `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` — is not a declared transition and is refused with `ERR_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID`, as is offering on a demand that has already settled.

Two monotonicity rules bound the negotiation and are what force it to converge. `carrier_offer_cents` may never exceed the demand's `demand_amount_cents`, since the carrier cannot offer more than was asked; a violation is `ERR_OFFER_EXCEEDS_DEMAND`. And when a round already stands, the new `carrier_offer_cents` must strictly exceed the standing round's `carrier_offer_cents` — the carrier may not lower its position and may not repeat it — with a violation reported as `ERR_OFFER_NOT_MONOTONIC`. Both are checked before the counsel gate described next.

The counsel gate applies to both offer branches equally. When the demand's `claimant_type` is `THIRD_PARTY_BODILY_INJURY` and the parent claim's `counsel_required` is true, an offer may be made only once the claim's `counsel_assigned` is true; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_COUNSEL_REQUIRED`. Property-damage demands are never gated this way, and a bodily-injury demand on a claim whose risk score does not require counsel is not gated either.

An accepted offer flips the previous standing round's `outcome` to `SUPERSEDED` — whether that round was still `OPEN` or had already been `COUNTERED` — and creates a new ledger row whose `demand_id` is the demand being negotiated, whose `round_index` is taken from the demand's `next_round_index`, whose `outcome` is `OPEN`, whose `offered_by_user_id` records the acting operator and whose `offered_at` is stamped from the clock, with a null `claimant_counter_cents`, a null `gap_cents` and a null `countered_at`. The demand's `next_round_index` increases by 1, its `status` becomes `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` and its `version` increases by 1. `round_index` is the sole key for identifying the standing round: the standing round is always the one with the highest `round_index`, and a superseded row is excluded from every standing-offer lookup thereafter.

**Recording the claimant's counter** is likewise a `ClaimsAdjuster` action, carrying `claimant_counter_cents`. The demand must be in `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` with a standing round whose `outcome` is still `OPEN`; countering when no offer stands is `ERR_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID`. The counter must strictly exceed the standing round's `carrier_offer_cents` — a claimant who has come down to or below the carrier's number has effectively accepted rather than countered — and, when an earlier round carried a counter, the new counter may not exceed that previous `claimant_counter_cents`, because claimant positions may not increase. Either violation is `ERR_COUNTER_NOT_MONOTONIC`. On success the standing round records `claimant_counter_cents`, records `countered_at` from the clock, records `gap_cents` as the counter less the carrier's offer on that same round, and flips its `outcome` `OPEN → COUNTERED`; the demand moves `OFFER_OUTSTANDING → COUNTER_OUTSTANDING` and its `version` increases by 1. Both negotiation writes answer with the ledger row rather than with the demand: an accepted offer returns the newly created round at 201, and an accepted counter returns that same standing round, now carrying `claimant_counter_cents`, `gap_cents`, `countered_at` and an `outcome` of `COUNTERED`, at 200. The pair of states then cycles as the parties trade positions, bounded only by the two monotonicity rules, which squeeze the gap closed from both sides.

A round's `outcome` therefore takes one of four values. `OPEN` is a live carrier offer. `COUNTERED` is an offer the claimant has answered. `ACCEPTED` is the offer a settlement closed on. `SUPERSEDED` is final and marks a round that a later round replaced. Rounds are read through their demand's ledger, ascending by `round_index`, with an `outcome` filter available; demands themselves are readable individually and listable with `claim_id`, `status`, `claimant_type` and `represented_by_counsel` filters, or per claim with `status` and `claimant_type` filters. A serialised demand presents its keys as `id`, `claim_id`, `claimant_name`, `claimant_type`, `demand_amount_cents`, `represented_by_counsel`, `demand_received_at`, `status`, `next_round_index`, `round_count`, `settled_amount_cents`, `alae_cents`, `settlement_total_cents`, `settled_at`, `settlement_reference`, `rescission_count`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`; the per-demand record of which policy layers a settlement consumed is held internally and is not among a demand's keys, being observable only through the parent claim's `layer_allocations`. A serialised negotiation round presents its keys as `id`, `demand_id`, `round_index`, `carrier_offer_cents`, `claimant_counter_cents`, `gap_cents`, `outcome`, `offered_by_user_id`, `offered_at`, `countered_at`, `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` as §2.4 describes.

### 3.8. Settlement Across the Policy Tower, and Rescission

**Settling a demand** is reserved to a `ClaimsManager` and carries a single `settlement_reference`, the external payment or release reference. It means the claimant has accepted the standing carrier offer, so it is legal from exactly one demand state: `OFFER_OUTSTANDING → SETTLED`. Settling while the claimant's counter is the last word — that is, from `COUNTER_OUTSTANDING` — is not a declared transition, and neither is settling a `DEMANDED`, `SETTLED` or `CLOSED_PAID` demand; every one of those is `ERR_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID`. The parent claim must not be `CLOSED`, which is reported as `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

Two money guards then run, in this order. First the tower for the crash's program year must be well formed, or the request fails with `ERR_POLICY_TOWER_MALFORMED`; then the tower must have enough remaining capacity for the whole `settlement_total_cents`, or the request fails with `ERR_COVERAGE_EXHAUSTED`. Only after coverage is proven does the reserve-sufficiency guard run: the claim's `outstanding_reserve_cents` must be greater than or equal to `settlement_total_cents`, because money must be reserved before it is paid, and a shortfall is `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`. A file that is both uncovered and underreserved reports the coverage failure, never the reserve failure.

The settlement figures are computed in a pinned order. The indemnity, `settled_amount_cents`, is exactly the standing round's `carrier_offer_cents` — never the demand amount and never the claimant's last counter. The allocated loss adjustment expense rate is then selected **from the indemnity alone, before any expense is added**: an indemnity of 25,000,000 cents or more takes the rate 0.15, an indemnity of 5,000,000 cents or more but under 25,000,000 takes 0.12, and anything under 5,000,000 takes 0.08. Keying the tier off the settlement total instead would be circular and, near a tier edge, self-fulfilling. `alae_cents` is the indemnity multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `settlement_total_cents` is the indemnity plus `alae_cents`. Worked: an indemnity of 4,900,000 is under 5,000,000, so the rate is 0.08, `alae_cents` is 392,000 and `settlement_total_cents` is 5,292,000 — whereas keying the tier on the total would wrongly reach the 0.12 rate for an expense of 588,000 and a total of 5,488,000.

**The waterfall** then spends `settlement_total_cents` through the tower. The layers considered are those whose `program_year` equals the UTC calendar year of the crash's `occurred_at`, taken in ascending `layer_index` order so the retention is consumed before the primary and the primary before each excess layer. For each layer in turn, the amount already consumed on this claim is the sum of `allocated_cents` in the claim's `layer_allocations` entries whose `policy_layer_id` is that layer; the layer's remaining capacity is its `limit_cents` less that amount; the take is the lesser of the amount still unallocated and that remaining capacity. When the take is greater than zero, an allocation entry recording `policy_layer_id`, `layer_index`, `layer_type` and `allocated_cents` is produced, the unallocated amount falls by the take, and the walk stops as soon as nothing is left to allocate. A layer with no remaining capacity contributes no entry and is passed over. If anything is still unallocated after the last layer, the entire settlement is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_EXHAUSTED` and no field on the demand, the claim or any layer is modified — settlement is all-or-nothing, never partial.

Worked: a tower of a `SIR` layer at `layer_index` 1 with attachment 0 and `limit_cents` 10,000,000, plus a `PRIMARY` layer at `layer_index` 2 with attachment 10,000,000 and `limit_cents` 5,000,000, has a total capacity of 15,000,000. A standing offer of 13,392,857 lands in the middle tier, so `alae_cents` is the half-up rounding of 1,607,142.84, namely 1,607,143, and `settlement_total_cents` is exactly 15,000,000 — the tower is exhausted to the cent and the settlement succeeds, allocating 10,000,000 to the retention and 5,000,000 to the primary. A standing offer of 13,392,858 produces the same `alae_cents` of 1,607,143, since 1,607,142.96 also rounds half up to that figure, for a total of 15,000,001 — one cent over capacity — and is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_EXHAUSTED`.

**The settlement cascade** then fires as one atomic unit. The demand records `settled_amount_cents`, `alae_cents`, `settlement_total_cents`, the supplied `settlement_reference` and `settled_at` from the clock, moves to `SETTLED`, and its `version` increases by 1. The standing negotiation round flips its `outcome` `OPEN → ACCEPTED`, which is what makes that row's `carrier_offer_cents` the settled indemnity of record. The claim moves the indemnity into `indemnity_paid_cents` and the expense into `alae_paid_cents`, reduces `outstanding_reserve_cents` by exactly `settlement_total_cents` — never floored, because the reserve-sufficiency guard has already proven the reserve covers it — merges the new allocation entries into `layer_allocations`, summing `allocated_cents` per layer across successive settlements rather than appending duplicates for the same layer and keeping that array ordered ascending by `layer_index` however the settlements arrived, increases `sir_eroded_cents` by the allocations to the `SIR` layer and `insured_paid_cents` by the allocations to `PRIMARY` and `EXCESS` layers, and increases its own `version` by 1. `claim_exposure_cents` is unchanged throughout, because paying a claim moves money between buckets rather than creating or destroying exposure. The response to a settlement is the updated demand, so the claim-side and subrogation-side consequences of the cascade are observed by re-reading those resources rather than in the settlement's own response body.

The same trigger then reaches one of two branches on the claim's handling dimension, decided by the aggregate state of the settling demand's siblings. When at least one other demand on the claim is still in `DEMANDED`, `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` or `COUNTER_OUTSTANDING`, the claim stays in `NEGOTIATION` as a self-loop and nothing further happens. When every other demand on the claim is already `SETTLED` or `CLOSED_PAID`, the claim moves `NEGOTIATION → SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE` — or holds at `SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE` as a self-loop when it was already there, which is how a demand recorded after the claim had completed is absorbed — and only that branch carries the third cascade link: every subrogation demand on the claim still in `DRAFT` moves to `READY_TO_PURSUE`, freezing `recoverable_base_cents` as the claim's `indemnity_paid_cents` plus `alae_paid_cents` as they stand at that instant and `max_recoverable_cents` as that base multiplied by its `at_fault_percentage` divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent, each with its own `version` increased by 1. A claim with a single demand therefore reaches `SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE` on that demand's settlement, while a claim with two demands stays in `NEGOTIATION` for the first and completes on the second.

**Rescinding a settlement** is the compensating action, reserved to a `ClaimsManager` and carrying a `rescission_reason` — required as a non-empty string, but recorded nowhere on the demand and absent from every response, so the only trace a rescission leaves on the record is the incremented `rescission_count`. It is legal only from `SETTLED`, so `ERR_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID` answers any other demand state, and only while the current logical instant is no later than the demand's `settled_at` plus 168 hours — seven days; the boundary is inclusive, so a rescission at exactly 168 hours after `settled_at` is allowed and one instant later is `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A claim that is already `CLOSED` blocks rescission with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, which is what makes closure the point of no return.

Rescission must leave no ledger drift. The demand returns `SETTLED → OFFER_OUTSTANDING`, clears `settled_amount_cents`, `alae_cents`, `settlement_total_cents`, `settled_at` and `settlement_reference` back to null, increases `rescission_count` by 1 and increases its `version` by 1. The accepted round flips its `outcome` `ACCEPTED → OPEN`, so the very same offer stands again and may be re-accepted or superseded by a higher offer. On the claim, the rescinded indemnity and expense are returned from `indemnity_paid_cents` and `alae_paid_cents` to `outstanding_reserve_cents`, leaving `claim_exposure_cents` unchanged; the rescinded demand's allocations are subtracted from `layer_allocations`, `sir_eroded_cents` and `insured_paid_cents`, which restores the consumed capacity of every layer it touched so a later settlement can use it again, and an entry whose `allocated_cents` falls to zero is removed from `layer_allocations` outright rather than left behind as a zero row; the claim moves `SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE → NEGOTIATION`, or keeps the handling status it already had where a still-open sibling demand had left the claim in `NEGOTIATION`; every subrogation demand on the claim in `READY_TO_PURSUE` returns to `DRAFT` with `recoverable_base_cents` and `max_recoverable_cents` reset to null and its own `version` increased by 1; and the claim's `version` increases by 1. A settle-then-rescind pair therefore leaves every money field, every layer's consumed capacity and every subrogation ceiling exactly as they were before the settlement, with only the version counters and `rescission_count` bearing witness. The response is the updated demand, as it is for a settlement.

### 3.9. Subrogation Recovery, Claim Closure and Terminal Risk Analytics

**A subrogation demand** is the carrier's own recovery claim against a third party whose fault contributed to the crash. Either a `ClaimsManager` or a `ClaimsAdjuster` may open one on a claim, naming the `third_party_name` pursued, the `at_fault_percentage` attributed to that party, which must be an integer from 1 through 100 inclusive, and a `basis_note` recording the theory of recovery. It is permitted only when the underlying crash's `third_party_at_fault` is true; a crash that alleges no third-party fault answers `ERR_NO_THIRD_PARTY_AT_FAULT`. The record starts in `DRAFT` with `recoverable_base_cents`, `max_recoverable_cents`, `recovered_amount_cents`, `resolution_note` and `resolved_at` all null, because nothing is recoverable until the claim's own settlements have actually been paid. A subrogation demand may be opened at any point in the claim's life, including before the first third-party demand exists, and it simply waits in `DRAFT` until the settlement cascade promotes it — which means one opened after the claim has already reached `SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE`, or on a claim that is already `CLOSED`, stays in `DRAFT` indefinitely, since closure bars further demands and settlements but not the opening of a recovery claim and nothing else can fire the promotion. Opening one is a write on the subrogation record alone: the parent claim is not modified and its `version` does not move, unlike the recording of a third-party demand, which does advance it.

The promotion to `READY_TO_PURSUE` is what freezes the recovery ceiling, and it happens only through the settlement cascade described in §3.8, never on demand. `recoverable_base_cents` is the claim's `indemnity_paid_cents` plus `alae_paid_cents` at the instant of promotion, and `max_recoverable_cents` is that base multiplied by `at_fault_percentage` and divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent. Because the base is frozen rather than re-derived, a later reserve revision cannot inflate the recovery ceiling, and a rescission resets both figures to null rather than recomputing them.

**Resolving a recovery** is reserved to a `ClaimsManager` and carries a `resolution` of `RECOVERED`, `DENIED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, a `resolution_note`, and `recovered_amount_cents`. That amount is required when the `resolution` is `RECOVERED` and must be absent for the other two resolutions; supplying it with `DENIED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, or omitting it with `RECOVERED`, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Resolution is legal only from `READY_TO_PURSUE`, so a `DRAFT` record or an already-resolved one answers `ERR_SUBROGATION_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID`. When present, `recovered_amount_cents` must be greater than zero and may not exceed the frozen `max_recoverable_cents`; both halves of that test are the recovery-ceiling rule and both report `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_BASE`, so a zero or negative amount is reported the same way an over-ceiling amount is rather than as a request-shape violation, and both are evaluated only after the state guard has been cleared.

On success the record moves to its terminal state — `RECOVERED`, `DENIED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, none of which has any outgoing transition — records `resolution_note` and `resolved_at` from the clock, and increases its `version` by 1. A `RECOVERED` resolution also increases the claim's `subrogation_recovered_cents` by the recovered amount, which lowers the claim's `total_incurred_cents` by the same amount while leaving `claim_exposure_cents` completely untouched: a recovery is a receipt against a loss already incurred, not a change in exposure. Because that accumulation is a cascade onto the claim, a `RECOVERED` resolution also advances the claim's own `version` by 1. `DENIED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` leave `subrogation_recovered_cents` unchanged and write nothing on the claim at all, so its `version` and `updated_at` stay exactly where they were. On all three resolutions the response is the updated subrogation demand. Worked: a claim with `indemnity_paid_cents` 13,392,857 and `alae_paid_cents` 1,607,143 froze a base of 15,000,000; an `at_fault_percentage` of 40 gives a `max_recoverable_cents` of 6,000,000; recovering the whole 6,000,000 while `outstanding_reserve_cents` still stands at 5,000,000 leaves `total_incurred_cents` at 13,392,857 plus 1,607,143 plus 5,000,000 less 6,000,000, which is 14,000,000. Subrogation demands are readable individually, listable fleet-wide with `claim_id` and `status` filters and listable per claim with a `status` filter, and a serialised subrogation demand presents its keys as `id`, `claim_id`, `third_party_name`, `at_fault_percentage`, `basis_note`, `status`, `recoverable_base_cents`, `max_recoverable_cents`, `recovered_amount_cents`, `resolution_note`, `resolved_at`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`.

**Closing the claim** is a `ClaimsManager` action taking no request body, and it is the file's last word. Three guards run, in a fixed order. First, a claim already in `CLOSED` cannot be closed again, which is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` — a state failure outranking both readiness tests, as §2.8 requires. Then every demand on the claim must have reached `SETTLED` or `CLOSED_PAID`; any demand still in `DEMANDED`, `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` or `COUNTER_OUTSTANDING` blocks closure with `ERR_OPEN_DEMANDS_REMAIN`. Only then is the subrogation population examined: every subrogation demand on the claim must have reached `RECOVERED`, `DENIED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, and any still in `DRAFT` or `READY_TO_PURSUE` blocks closure with `ERR_OPEN_SUBROGATION_REMAINS`, so a claim carrying both an open demand and an unresolved recovery reports the open demand.

Closure reaches `CLOSED` from either of two handling states. The ordinary path is `SETTLEMENT_COMPLETE → CLOSED` once everything has been paid and pursued. The other is `SETUP → CLOSED`, for a claim that never received a single demand and is being closed as a no-liability file. Both set `closed_at` from the clock and both drive the reserve dimension to `FINAL` — from `REVISED` when the reserve was re-estimated at least once, or straight from `PROVISIONAL` when it never was. The whole residual `outstanding_reserve_cents` is moved into `reserve_released_cents`, so `outstanding_reserve_cents` becomes 0 while `claim_exposure_cents` is unchanged: releasing a reserve reclassifies exposure, it does not retire it. Every demand on the claim still in `SETTLED` moves to `CLOSED_PAID`, freezing each settlement beyond the reach of rescission with that demand's `version` increased by 1. Because the claim is now `CLOSED`, the litigation hold lifts and every `PRESERVED` evidence item on the crash becomes eligible for release. The claim's `version` increases by 1. `CLOSED` and `FINAL` are both terminal, so a closed claim accepts no further demand, revision, counsel assignment or settlement.

**Terminal crash-rate analytics** is a computed read available to a `SafetyAdmin`, a `SafetyManager` or an `Auditor`, and it is the only place the two counting bases are both reachable. The window is the trailing 365 days: it begins at the instant 365 days before the current logical instant and ends at the current logical instant, and it is half-open — the lower bound is inclusive and the upper bound exclusive. A crash whose `occurred_at` is exactly 365 days old sits on the inclusive lower bound and is therefore inside the window; a crash one second older is outside it; and a crash whose `occurred_at` equals the current instant is outside it too.

The `basis` query parameter selects what is counted and defaults to `AS_DETERMINED`. Under `AS_DETERMINED` only crashes whose `preventability_status` is `PREVENTABLE` are counted, so a crash awaiting determination or found non-preventable contributes nothing. Under `AS_REPORTED` every notified crash in the window is counted regardless of determination. The optional `route_class` and `tenure_band` parameters narrow the counted crash set before the rate is computed, not afterwards, so filtering to one route class produces that subset's own rate rather than a share of the whole terminal's rate. `crash_rate_per_million_miles` is the counted crash total divided by the terminal's `annual_miles_millions`, rounded half up to three decimal places. `risk_tier` is `CRITICAL` when that rate is 3.000 or greater, `ELEVATED` when it is 1.500 or greater but below 3.000, and `STANDARD` otherwise. Worked: one preventable crash at a terminal with `annual_miles_millions` 4.0 gives 0.250 and `STANDARD`; the same crash at a terminal with 0.6666 gives the half-up rounding of 1.50015 to three places, namely 1.500, which is exactly the `ELEVATED` boundary and so tiers as `ELEVATED`; the same crash with 0.5 gives 2.000 and `ELEVATED`.

The response carries `terminal_id`, the `basis` actually applied, the `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` instants that bound the window, `crash_count` for every notified crash in the window that survives the `route_class` and `tenure_band` narrowing — the narrowing applies to `crash_count` exactly as it does to the counted set, so a narrowed read does not report the terminal's whole in-window crash total — `preventable_crash_count` for the crashes the selected basis counted, the resulting `crash_rate_per_million_miles` and `risk_tier`, and the two breakdown arrays `by_route_class` and `by_tenure_band`. Each entry of `by_route_class` names one `route_class` value present in the window together with that subset's `crash_count`, `preventable_crash_count` and `crash_rate_per_million_miles`, in that key order and with no `risk_tier` of its own; each entry of `by_tenure_band` does the same for one `tenure_band` value, with the band taken from the involved driver's tenure as re-derived at read time. Both arrays are emitted in the fixed enumeration order of their axis — `LOCAL`, `REGIONAL`, `LINEHAUL` for route class and `UNDER_12_MONTHS`, `MONTHS_12_TO_36`, `OVER_36_MONTHS` for tenure band — never in order of frequency or of first appearance, and a value with no crash in the narrowed window is omitted entirely rather than reported as a zero row. Every subset rate divides by the terminal's full `annual_miles_millions`, never by a prorated share of it. The declared query parameters are validated for shape before the `{id}` in the path is resolved, so a malformed `basis` on an unknown terminal reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`; `limit` and `offset` are not accepted here, as this is a computed object read rather than a collection read. The `preventable_crash_count`, `crash_rate_per_million_miles` and `risk_tier` fields stored on the terminal itself always reflect the `AS_DETERMINED` basis with no route or tenure narrowing, so a terminal read and an unparameterised analytics read agree exactly.
