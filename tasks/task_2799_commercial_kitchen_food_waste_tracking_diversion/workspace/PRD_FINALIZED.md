# Commercial Kitchen Food Waste Diversion API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Commercial kitchens throw away money in plain sight. A prep cook trims two kilograms off a case of chicken, a hot line over-produces a pan of rice that nobody orders, and a dish return bin fills with plate scrapings — each of those losses has a weight, a cause, a food category and a cost, and in most operations none of it is measured. This document specifies the backend service for a food waste tracking and diversion platform that closes that gap: kitchen staff weigh every discard at an instrumented tracking station, the platform values each loss in cents using either catalog pricing or fully-loaded recipe cost, and the resulting record feeds shift-level coaching, site baselines and reduction targets, physical diversion of the material to donation, animal feed or composting partners, and a corporate sustainability rollup.

Three concerns dominate the design. The first is **valuation integrity**: the same discarded kilogram is worth two different amounts depending on whether it is priced from the food catalog or from the published cost of the recipe it came from, and the platform must carry both figures without ever letting a later catalog edit retro-value a historical capture. The second is **conservation**: waste weight is routed onto haulers in partial allocations, consignments are cancelled, captures are voided and corrected, and after every one of those compensating actions the totals on a site must return to exactly the value they would have held had the reversed action never happened — no drift, no residue. The third is **segregation of duties**: the operator who captured a loss may not be the operator who erases it, and the corporate lead who prepared a sustainability report may not be the one who publishes it.

The service is a flat, top-level HTTP API. Every collection is addressed at the root — there are no nested resource URLs — and relationships between records are carried entirely by identifier fields in request bodies and by filtered list reads. Time is not read from the host clock: the server maintains a **logical clock** that test harnesses and operators advance explicitly, and every timestamp the service writes, every calibration deadline it enforces and every reduction-target window it resolves is evaluated against that logical clock. At process start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller identity in two headers. `X-User-Role` names the role the caller is acting as, and `X-User-ID` is the acting identity within that role. There are exactly six roles, spelled exactly as follows: `KitchenStaff`, `ShiftLead`, `SiteSustainabilityManager`, `HaulerOperator`, `CorporateSustainabilityLead`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

If `X-User-Role` is absent or does not name one of those six roles, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. The same error is returned when `X-User-ID` is absent or empty on any endpoint that restricts itself to a named set of roles. When both headers are present and well-formed but the caller's role is not among the roles the target endpoint admits, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Exactly one endpoint — the logical-clock setter — is open to any caller and requires no identity headers at all; it is test-driving infrastructure rather than a business operation, and neither authentication error applies to it.

Two of the six roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the value in `X-User-ID` must resolve to a real row. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteSustainabilityManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Site`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Site` compare that resource's `site_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_SITE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. When `X-User-Role` is `HaulerOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Hauler`; ownership checks on any consignment or weigh ticket compare its `hauler_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_HAULER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. For either of these two roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing row of the backing entity — no such `Site`, no such `Hauler` — is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RECOGNIZED`.

The remaining four roles carry **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `KitchenStaff`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (as `captured_by_user_id` on a waste entry, as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on a coaching alert) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `ShiftLead`, whose opaque `X-User-ID` is recorded as `captured_by_user_id`, `resolved_by_user_id` or `created_by_user_id` depending on the action, and is the left-hand side of the void duty separation; for `CorporateSustainabilityLead`, whose opaque `X-User-ID` is recorded as `prepared_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a rollup and is the left-hand side of the publication duty separation; and for `PlatformAdmin`, whose opaque `X-User-ID` is used only for audit and is never compared to any entity's `id`.

Role capability, in brief. `KitchenStaff` are line and prep cooks at a station terminal: they capture losses and acknowledge coaching alerts, and they may read every resource except the hauler-scoped ones. `ShiftLead` supervises one service period: they open service periods and are the only role permitted to close one, and they exclusively curate the capture record by flagging, clearing flags, voiding and correcting captures, and resolving acknowledged alerts. `SiteSustainabilityManager` owns exactly one site and runs its baselines, diversion logistics, data-quality scans and weigh-ticket review, always restricted to rows carrying their own `site_id`. `HaulerOperator` files weigh tickets for consignments assigned to their hauler and may mark a staged consignment dispatched at pickup; they cannot read waste captures, service periods, baselines, targets, alerts, findings or rollups, and they may never accept or dispute their own ticket. `CorporateSustainabilityLead` certifies and rejects baselines, creates and activates reduction targets, approves haulers, and prepares and publishes rollups. `PlatformAdmin` maintains the shared catalog — sites, food types, recipes, haulers and stations — and deliberately holds no operational authority over captures, service-period closure, baselines, targets, settlements or rollups, so administrative rights cannot be used to route around either duty separation. No role is a superset of another.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and has a minimum accepted value of 0 with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer, is below 1 or is above 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix — a site id and a hauler id are indistinguishable by shape, and clients must never parse meaning out of one. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp recording when the row was inserted. Both fields are server-owned and cannot be supplied or altered by a client.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 each time the row is mutated. `ConsignmentLine` is the one exception: an allocation line is an immutable event record, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and nothing in the system ever bumps a line's version because it has none. All timestamps the service emits, including `created_at` and `updated_at`, are ISO 8601 UTC values with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`.

Wall-clock time plays no part in any business rule. The service keeps a single logical clock whose value is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` at process start, and an administrative capability sets it to a supplied `current_utc` instant. The clock may only move forward: a `current_utc` that is not strictly greater than the currently stored value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`. Every field described below as "the current logical clock" — `captured_at` defaults, `closed_at`, `published_at`, `submitted_at`, `certified_at`, `activated_at`, `resolved_at`, `staged_at`, `dispatched_at`, `accepted_at`, `detected_at` — reads this value, and every deadline comparison (station calibration lapse, donation shelf life, reduction-window close) is made against it.

### 2.5. Weights, Money and Rounding Discipline

Weights are integers in grams throughout the capture and routing pipeline; kilograms and tonnes appear only as derived, explicitly-rounded reporting figures. Money is always an integer count of whole cents — the platform stores no fractional cents and no floating-point currency. Wherever this document describes rounding, the method is **round half up**: a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

The rounding scales are fixed per quantity and are never negotiable. Per-ingredient line costs and a recipe's cost per kilogram are whole cents. A recipe's usable yield is rounded to 3 decimal places. Waste intensity figures, in kilograms per one thousand meals, are rounded to 3 decimal places. Compliance and pre-consumer share fractions are rounded to 4 decimal places. Diversion rate, reduction percentages and ticket variance percentages are rounded to 2 decimal places. Billable tonnage on a weigh ticket is rounded to 4 decimal places. A shift's leaderboard score is rounded to 1 decimal place. Where a formula performs several roundings in sequence, the sequence itself is part of the specification — rounding earlier or later than stated produces a different, wrong answer, and the workflow subsections below pin the order explicitly.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Aggregates

Two write operations require the client to prove it is acting on a version of a parent record it has actually observed. Both are stale-version comparisons against the parent's current `version` integer, and neither depends on requests overlapping in time.

Creating a correcting waste entry against an existing capture requires the request to carry `expected_entry_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `WasteEntry` being corrected. If that value does not equal the entry's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENTRY_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. The field is not optional. Because a correction legitimately supersedes whatever the entry currently says — including an earlier correction — a corrector working from an out-of-date version would otherwise silently overwrite a supersession it never saw.

Creating a reduction target requires the request to carry `expected_site_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Site` the target is being written for. If that value does not equal the site's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. The field is not optional. A new target legitimately supersedes any proposed or active target on the same site and window, so the corporate lead must demonstrate that the aggregate snapshot the target was sized against — captured waste totals, diversion rate, coaching posture — is still current. Every capture, void, service-period close and settlement bumps `Site.version`, so a stale `expected_site_version` is direct evidence that the sizing inputs have moved.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one elsewhere confers no meaning.

### 2.7. Derived Aggregates, Cascades and Compensation

A large fraction of every entity's payload is derived. Derived fields are never maintained by incremental delta arithmetic; each recompute reads the qualifying source rows and recalculates the value from scratch. This is what makes compensation exact: after a consignment is cancelled, a capture voided, or an allocation line deleted, the affected totals return to the byte-identical value they held before the reversed action, with zero accumulated drift.

Two conservation identities hold at all times and are the platform's primary integrity checks. First, for every capture, its `weight_grams` always equals `routed_weight_grams` plus `unrouted_weight_grams`. Second, a site's `valued_waste_cost_cents` always equals the sum of `effective_cost_cents` over exactly the captures the inclusion rule admits — so a correction that supersedes an entry and is then itself voided leaves the site's valued cost exactly where it started.

Cascades run to completion inside the request that triggers them, and every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. Where an operation reaches more than one entity, the workflow subsections in §3 enumerate every hop of the chain, in order, including the secondary and tertiary hops that follow only because an earlier hop changed a value. The deepest chains in the system are the closure of a service period and the voiding of a capture, both of which reach four levels; ticket acceptance reaches three.

### 2.8. Site Scoping, Hauler Scoping and Segregation of Duties

Two scoping rules and two duty separations restrict who may act, and all four are enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than on role alone.

A `SiteSustainabilityManager` may only write rows whose `site_id` equals their `X-User-ID`; every other write in their reach is refused with `ERR_SITE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. A `HaulerOperator` may only act on a consignment or weigh ticket whose `hauler_id` equals their `X-User-ID`, and violations are refused with `ERR_HAULER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On weigh-ticket submission the compared value is the ticket's own `hauler_id` — the one supplied in the body, or, when the body omits it, the value defaulted from the referenced consignment.

The two duty separations both yield `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `ShiftLead` may not void a capture whose `captured_by_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; a different `ShiftLead` identity must perform the void. A `CorporateSustainabilityLead` may not publish a rollup whose `prepared_by_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; a second `CorporateSustainabilityLead` identity must publish it. Neither separation can be bypassed by escalating to `PlatformAdmin`, because `PlatformAdmin` cannot void a capture and cannot publish a rollup at all.

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned — the one belonging to the earliest check in the following fixed evaluation order. This ordering is normative; reporting a later error in place of an earlier one is a defect even when both conditions genuinely hold.

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role admission for the target endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Resolution of an entity-backed role's `X-User-ID` to a real `Site` or `Hauler` row — `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RECOGNIZED`.
4. Body schema: parseability, presence of required fields, declared JSON types, enum membership, non-negativity where declared, decimal scale, `YYYY-MM-DD` dates, RFC 3339 UTC timestamps with a trailing `Z`, and the declared range invariants (`start_date` no later than `end_date`, `window_start` no later than `window_end`, `period_start` no later than `period_end`, `pickup_window_start` strictly before `pickup_window_end`, a non-empty `ingredients` array, a non-empty `site_ids` array) — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. Pagination parameter validity on list reads — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
6. Existence of every identifier appearing in the URL path — the owning entity's not-found error.
7. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body, including every member of a `site_ids` array — the referenced entity's not-found error.
8. Site scoping for a `SiteSustainabilityManager` — `ERR_SITE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
9. Hauler scoping for a `HaulerOperator` — `ERR_HAULER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
10. Optimistic-concurrency token match — `ERR_STALE_ENTRY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION`.
11. The target row's own state admits the requested transition — the entity's invalid-state error, or the more specific error named for that situation in §3.
12. Cross-entity state preconditions — station active and calibrated, service period open, recipe published, baseline certified, hauler approved.
13. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
14. Channel eligibility of the routed food — `ERR_CHANNEL_INELIGIBLE_FOOD`.
15. The donation posture block on staging — `ERR_SITE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_DONATION`.
16. Same-aggregate referential consistency — `ERR_ENTRY_SITE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TICKET_HAULER_MISMATCH`, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_IN_SCOPE`.
17. Capture timing — `ERR_CAPTURE_IN_FUTURE`, then `ERR_CAPTURE_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_DATE`.
18. Donation shelf life — `ERR_DONATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
19. Interval occupancy — `ERR_STATION_OCCUPIED` and `ERR_BASELINE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
20. Aggregate hauler pickup-window capacity — `ERR_HAULER_WINDOW_CAPACITY`.
21. Allocation against remaining unrouted grams — `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_UNROUTED`.
22. The consignment's channel is contracted with the hauler — `ERR_HAULER_CHANNEL_UNSUPPORTED`.
23. A target's performance window starts after its baseline ends — `ERR_TARGET_WINDOW_BEFORE_BASELINE`.
24. Uniqueness constraints — the specific duplicate error for the violated key.
25. Hauler per-pickup weight capacity at staging — `ERR_HAULER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
26. The rollup data-quality block — `ERR_ROLLUP_DATA_QUALITY_BLOCKED`.
27. Empty-set guards — `ERR_BASELINE_NO_DATA` and `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_EMPTY`.
28. Loss reason compatible with station placement — `ERR_LOSS_REASON_STATION_MISMATCH`.
29. Logical-clock monotonicity — `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because the optimistic-concurrency check sits at step 10, a correction request carrying a stale `expected_entry_version` against an already-superseded entry reports the stale-version error, not the invalid-state error. And because both duty separations sit at step 13, they are evaluated after target-state and cross-entity-state checks but before every business rule that follows: voiding a capture that is already `VOIDED` reports the invalid-state error even when the caller is also the original capturer.

### 2.10. Flat Collections and Reverse Navigation

All fifteen resource collections are peers addressed at the top level of the URL space — captures, service periods, allocation lines, consignments and the rest each live at their own root path. No resource is nested inside another resource's URL, so a request never carries more than one path identifier. That single identifier is resolved before any body content is inspected, and a path identifier that names no existing row yields that entity's not-found error.

Navigation from a parent to its children is always the filtered list form: a list read on the child collection with the parent's identifier supplied as a query filter. Filters on the same read combine conjunctively — supplying both `site_id` and `status` returns only rows matching both — and an unrecognised filter value for an enum-typed parameter is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Catalog Foundations: Sites, Food Types and Recipe Costing

Nothing can be weighed until a `PlatformAdmin` has registered the kitchen it will be weighed in. Registering a site takes a human-readable `name`, an operator-assigned short code in `site_code` that must be between 2 and 12 characters long and must be unique across every site in the platform, and a `region_code` naming the corporate reporting region the site rolls up into. An optional `target_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals` sets the site's own operating intensity goal — the figure the shift leaderboard measures each service period against — expressed to 3 decimal places, required to be greater than zero, and defaulting to 10.000 when omitted. A second site claiming an existing `site_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE`.

A freshly registered site is a clean slate: `total_captured_weight_grams`, `valued_waste_cost_cents`, `diverted_weight_grams_settled` and `open_alert_count` all read 0, `diversion_rate_pct` reads null because there is no captured weight to divide by, and `coaching_posture` reads `MONITORING`. Those six derived fields are the site's live scoreboard and every later section of this document eventually writes to one of them. `total_captured_weight_grams` is the sum of `weight_grams` over the site's captures whose status is either `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED`; `valued_waste_cost_cents` is the sum of `effective_cost_cents` over exactly that same set of captures; `diverted_weight_grams_settled` is the sum of `allocated_weight_grams` over the site's allocation lines whose consignment has reached `SETTLED` on the settlement dimension and has not been `CANCELLED` on the logistics dimension; `diversion_rate_pct` is the settled diverted grams divided by the total captured grams, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and is null whenever `total_captured_weight_grams` is 0; `open_alert_count` is the number of coaching alerts at the site whose status is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`; and `coaching_posture` is derived purely from that count — `MONITORING` when the count is exactly 0, `ESCALATED` when the count is 3 or more, and `INTERVENTION` for every count in between. An `ESCALATED` posture is not cosmetic: it blocks donation-channel material from leaving the site, as §3.7 explains. Sites may be listed with a `region_code` filter and a `coaching_posture` filter.

The catalog of discardable food is maintained by the same administrator. A food type carries a `name` that must be globally unique — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FOOD_TYPE_NAME` — and a reporting `category` drawn from `PRODUCE`, `PROTEIN`, `PREPARED`, `BAKERY` and `DAIRY`. Its `default_cost_per_kg_cents`, a whole number of cents per kilogram that must be greater than zero, is the catalog valuation basis every capture starts from. Three independent booleans govern where the material may legally go: `donation_eligible`, `animal_feed_eligible` and `compostable`. Finally, `donation_shelf_life_hours` states how many hours after capture a donation pickup may still legally collect this food type; it must be zero or greater and defaults to 0, which in practice means a food type left at the default can never satisfy the donation timing rule unless the pickup begins at the very instant of capture. Food types may be listed filtered by `category`, `donation_eligible` or `animal_feed_eligible`.

Recipes exist so that a discarded portion can be valued at what the kitchen actually spent producing it rather than at raw-ingredient list price. A recipe is created with a globally unique `recipe_code` — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECIPE_CODE` — a `name`, and an `ingredients` array of at least one element. Each element is an object naming the ingredient in `ingredient_name`, the `quantity_kg` purchased for the batch which must be greater than zero, the `unit_cost_cents_per_kg` paid for it which must be greater than zero, and a `trim_loss_pct` expressing the fraction of that ingredient lost to trimming, which must be at least zero and strictly less than one. **The array order is significant**: it is the pinned evaluation order of the costing pass, and reordering the array changes nothing about the arithmetic only because the arithmetic is defined per line before any summation occurs.

The costing pass runs in four steps and its step boundaries are normative. First, for each ingredient element taken in array order, the line's gross cost is the exact decimal product of `quantity_kg` and `unit_cost_cents_per_kg`, and that product is immediately rounded half up to a whole number of cents to become the line's cost; separately, the line's usable kilograms are the exact decimal product of `quantity_kg` and the fraction that survives trimming, which is one minus `trim_loss_pct`, and this product is not rounded at this step. Second, `total_ingredient_cost_cents` is the plain integer sum of those already-rounded per-line cent figures. Third, `usable_yield_kg` is the sum of the per-line usable kilograms, rounded half up to 3 decimal places once, at the end. Fourth, `cost_per_kg_cents` is `total_ingredient_cost_cents` divided by that rounded `usable_yield_kg`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents.

Rounding per line and then summing is not interchangeable with summing first and rounding once. Consider a recipe of three lines: 2.5 kilograms at 1201 cents per kilogram with a trim loss of 0.20, 1.5 kilograms at 407 cents per kilogram with no trim loss, and 0.5 kilograms at 999 cents per kilogram with no trim loss. Rounding each line first gives 3003, 611 and 500 cents, summing to 4114 cents against a usable yield of 4.000 kilograms, which divides to 1028.5 and rounds to a `cost_per_kg_cents` of 1029. Summing the unrounded products first would give 4112.5 cents, round to 4113, divide to 1028.25 and round to 1028 — a different and incorrect answer. The specified order is the per-line one.

A recipe always begins in `DRAFT` and its `status` may only ever hold `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`. While it is `DRAFT`, `published_cost_per_kg_cents` and `published_at` are both null and no capture may reference it. Publication, available only to a `PlatformAdmin`, advances `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`; it requires the recipe to currently be `DRAFT`, refusing anything else with `ERR_INVALID_RECIPE_STATE`, and requires the `ingredients` array to hold at least one element. On success the recipe's freshly computed `cost_per_kg_cents` is frozen into `published_cost_per_kg_cents`, `published_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, and the recipe's `version` is incremented by 1. That frozen figure — not the live `cost_per_kg_cents` — is what a capture pins, which is why editing a recipe after publication can never retro-value a historical capture. Recipes may be listed with a `status` filter.

### 3.2. Stations, Service Periods and the Capture of a Weighed Loss

A **tracking station** is a scale and terminal installed at a specific place in the kitchen, and either a `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager` installs one. Installation takes the `site_id` it is being installed at, a `station_code` printed on the terminal that must be unique among that site's stations — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATION_CODE` — a `station_kind` of `PREP`, `HOT_LINE` or `DISH_RETURN` describing its physical placement, and a `calibration_due_at` instant at which the scale's calibration lapses. Three optional inputs complete the profile: `tare_grams`, the container tare the scale firmware subtracts before a capture is posted, which must be zero or greater and defaults to 0; `capture_lock_seconds`, the length in seconds of the station occupation interval that each capture creates, which must be at least 0 and at most 600 and defaults to 30; and `status`, which defaults to `ACTIVE`. An `ACTIVE` station immediately enters the compliance denominator of that site's service periods on their next recompute. Stations may be listed filtered by `site_id`, `status` or `station_kind`.

A station's availability moves between three values, and only an `ACTIVE` station accepts captures. The `SiteSustainabilityManager` who owns the site, or a `PlatformAdmin`, drives the change by supplying the desired `status` together with an optional replacement `calibration_due_at` and an optional free-text `note`. Four transitions are declared. `ACTIVE → MAINTENANCE` requires the station to currently be `ACTIVE`; from that moment captures at the station are refused with `ERR_STATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, the compliance figure of the site's open service periods is deliberately *not* recomputed retroactively — it is recomputed on the next capture or at closure — and the station's `version` is incremented by 1. `MAINTENANCE → ACTIVE` requires the station to currently be `MAINTENANCE`; it sets `calibration_due_at` to the supplied value when one is supplied and leaves it untouched when none is, and increments `version` by 1. `ACTIVE → RETIRED` is permitted from either `ACTIVE` or `MAINTENANCE`, and `MAINTENANCE → RETIRED` from `MAINTENANCE`; both remove the station from the active-station denominator of the compliance figure and from missed-capture scanning altogether, and both increment `version` by 1. Any status change not among these four — retiring is terminal, so `RETIRED` back to `ACTIVE` is impossible, and so is `ACTIVE` to `ACTIVE` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE`.

A **service period** is one day's one service window at one site. It is opened by a `ShiftLead` or by the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager` with a `site_id`, a `shift_date` as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form treated as the site's local date with no offset arithmetic, a `shift_code` of `AM`, `PM` or `NIGHT`, and `meals_served`, the covers served during the period, which must be zero or greater and is the denominator of every intensity metric in the system. The combination of `site_id`, `shift_date` and `shift_code` is unique; a second attempt at the same triple is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SHIFT`. A newly opened period has `status` `OPEN` and a null `leaderboard_score`; `status` may only ever hold `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, and `closed_at` is null while `OPEN`. Only an `OPEN` period accepts captures. Service periods may be listed filtered by `site_id`, `shift_date`, `shift_code` or `status`, and that list read doubles as the shift-level leaderboard.

Capturing a loss is the platform's highest-volume operation and is available to both `KitchenStaff` and `ShiftLead`. The request names the `station_id` that weighed the loss, the `shift_id` the loss is booked to, the `food_type_id` of the discarded item, the net `weight_grams` which must be greater than 0 and no more than 200000, and a `loss_reason` drawn from `OVERPRODUCTION`, `SPOILAGE`, `TRIM`, `EXPIRATION`, `PLATE_WASTE` and `CONTAMINATION`. Three optional inputs follow: a `recipe_id` attributing the loss to a published recipe, a `captured_at` instant which defaults to the current logical clock when omitted, and a free-text operator `note`.

Nine preconditions guard the capture, and by §2.9's ordering they are judged in this sequence. The referenced station must be `ACTIVE`, or the capture is refused with `ERR_STATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The current logical clock must be strictly earlier than the station's `calibration_due_at`; once the clock has reached that instant the calibration has lapsed and the capture is refused with `ERR_STATION_CALIBRATION_OVERDUE`. The referenced service period must be `OPEN`, or the capture is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_STATE`. When a `recipe_id` is supplied it must reference a recipe whose status is `PUBLISHED`, or the capture is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The service period and the station must belong to the same site; a cross-site pairing is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_SITE_MISMATCH`. The `captured_at` instant must not be later than the current logical clock — a strictly future instant is refused with `ERR_CAPTURE_IN_FUTURE` — and the calendar date of `captured_at` must equal the period's `shift_date`, with any other date refused as `ERR_CAPTURE_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_DATE`. The `loss_reason` must be compatible with the station's `station_kind`, and the station must not already be occupied; both of those rules are set out below.

Loss reason and station placement are paired. `PLATE_WASTE` is accepted only at a `DISH_RETURN` station, because plate scrapings by definition arrive at the dish return. `TRIM`, `OVERPRODUCTION` and `SPOILAGE` are accepted only at a `PREP` or `HOT_LINE` station, because those are pre-service losses. `EXPIRATION` and `CONTAMINATION` are accepted at any station kind. Every other pairing — plate waste weighed at the prep bench, trim weighed at the dish return — is refused with `ERR_LOSS_REASON_STATION_MISMATCH`.

Each capture reserves the station for a stretch of time so two operators cannot double-post the same tray. The reserved stretch of a capture runs from its `captured_at` through `captured_at` plus the station's `capture_lock_seconds` plus a fixed one-minute turnaround allowance, and **both ends of that stretch are inclusive**. A new capture whose `captured_at` falls anywhere inside the reserved stretch of an existing capture at the same station whose status is `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` is refused with `ERR_STATION_OCCUPIED`; a capture landing exactly one second past the far end of the stretch is accepted. The stored `capture_window_end` on each capture is its `captured_at` plus the station's `capture_lock_seconds`, and is the upper bound of the occupation interval before the turnaround allowance is added.

Once the guards pass, the capture is written in status `RECORDED` and a substantial amount of derivation follows. Its `site_id` is copied from the station's own `site_id` and is never changed afterwards. Its `stream` is derived from placement and cause together: `POST_CONSUMER` when the station's `station_kind` is `DISH_RETURN` or when the `loss_reason` is `PLATE_WASTE`, and `PRE_CONSUMER` in every other case — which means a dish-return station always produces post-consumer records regardless of stated reason, and plate waste is post-consumer wherever it is weighed. `capture_window_end` is computed as described above. Two valuation bases are snapshotted so that later catalog or recipe edits can never disturb the record: `catalog_cost_basis_cents` takes the food type's `default_cost_per_kg_cents`, and `recipe_cost_basis_cents` takes the recipe's `published_cost_per_kg_cents` when a `recipe_id` was supplied and is null otherwise. `captured_by_user_id` records the capturing operator's `X-User-ID`, which is the comparison target of the void duty separation. The three review fields `flag_reason`, `void_reason` and `correction_note` are all null on an originally captured entry, as is `corrects_entry_id`.

Valuation at capture time uses the catalog basis only. The weight is converted to kilograms by dividing `weight_grams` by 1000, an exact decimal that is never rounded, and the cost is that kilogram figure multiplied by the chosen basis in cents per kilogram, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `provisional_cost_cents` is that product taken against `catalog_cost_basis_cents`, ignoring recipe attribution entirely. `final_cost_cents` stays null while the owning service period is `OPEN`, and so does `valuation_delta_cents`. `effective_cost_cents` — the figure that actually feeds every site, baseline and rollup total — is `final_cost_cents` when that field is non-null and `provisional_cost_cents` otherwise, so at capture time it equals the provisional figure. Routing starts empty: `routed_weight_grams` is 0 and `unrouted_weight_grams` equals the full `weight_grams`.

The capture then triggers recomputation. The owning service period recomputes `shift_waste_grams`, the sum of `weight_grams` over its captures whose status is `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED`; `waste_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals`, which is `shift_waste_grams` divided by `meals_served` rounded half up to 3 decimal places — grams per meal is numerically identical to kilograms per thousand meals — and is null whenever `meals_served` is 0; `pre_consumer_share_pct`, which is the summed `weight_grams` of the `PRE_CONSUMER` members of that same set divided by `shift_waste_grams`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and null whenever `shift_waste_grams` is 0; and `capture_compliance_pct`, which is the number of distinct stations that reported on this period divided by the number of the site's `ACTIVE` stations, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and fixed at 1.0000 when the site has no `ACTIVE` stations at all. The reporting-station numerator deliberately counts distinct `station_id` values over captures of this period whose status is `RECORDED`, `FLAGGED` **or** `SUPERSEDED` — a superseded capture still proves the station reported — while `shift_waste_grams` and the pre-consumer share exclude both `VOIDED` and `SUPERSEDED` records. The site then recomputes `total_captured_weight_grams`, `valued_waste_cost_cents` and `diversion_rate_pct`. Finally the coaching evaluation of §3.3 runs, after the intensity figures are up to date. Both the service period's `version` and the site's `version` are incremented by 1.

Captures are read individually — the single-record read exposes both valuations side by side along with the routed and unrouted split — and listed with the full filter surface used for pre- and post-consumer segmentation reporting: `site_id`, `shift_id`, `station_id`, `food_type_id`, `recipe_id`, `corrects_entry_id`, `status`, `stream` and `loss_reason`.

### 3.3. Coaching in the Moment: Alerts and the Site's Posture

Coaching is the product's behavioural lever: a cook who learns at the terminal that the line has already crossed its waste ceiling can change the next pan, whereas a cook who learns it in a monthly report cannot. Immediately after a capture has updated its service period's intensity figures — the ordering matters, because the rules read the post-capture values — two independent coaching rules are evaluated.

The first rule watches intensity. When the period's recomputed `waste_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals` is not null and has reached 12.000 kilograms per thousand meals or more — the comparison is inclusive and is made against the already-3-decimal-rounded value, so exactly 12.000 fires it — and no coaching alert for this service period with `alert_type` `INTENSITY_BREACH` is currently in status `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, one alert is created with that `alert_type`. Its `observed_intensity` records the intensity value at the instant it was raised, to 3 decimal places, and its `severity` is `HIGH` when that observed intensity has reached 18.000 or more and `MEDIUM` otherwise. The second rule watches single-drop overproduction: when the capture's `loss_reason` is `OVERPRODUCTION` and its `weight_grams` has reached five kilograms — 5000 grams, inclusive — and no alert for this service period with `alert_type` `OVERPRODUCTION_SPIKE` is currently `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, one alert is created with that `alert_type`, a `severity` of `MEDIUM` always, and a null `observed_intensity`. Both rules may fire on the very same capture; when they do, the intensity-breach alert is created first. The suppression condition in each rule is per service period and per alert type, so a second breach on the same period while the first alert is still open or merely acknowledged creates nothing.

Every alert records the `site_id` copied from the triggering service period, the `shift_id` of that period, the `waste_entry_id` of the capture whose posting produced the breach, and a human-readable `message` that is the coaching text shown at the station terminal. An alert opens in status `OPEN`, and its `status` may hold `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `RESOLVED`, `AUTO_RESOLVED` or `EXPIRED`. Creating one increments the site's `open_alert_count` by 1 and recomputes `coaching_posture` from the new count. Alerts may be listed filtered by `site_id`, `shift_id`, `waste_entry_id`, `status`, `alert_type` and `severity`.

Acknowledgement is the cook's action and is open to `KitchenStaff` and `ShiftLead` alike; it advances `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED`, stamps `acknowledged_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and increments the alert's `version` by 1. Crucially, the site's `open_alert_count` **does not change** on acknowledgement — an acknowledged alert still counts, because acknowledging a problem is not fixing it, and the posture must not soften just because someone tapped the screen. Resolution is the supervisor's action and belongs exclusively to `ShiftLead`: it advances `ACKNOWLEDGED → RESOLVED`, requires a `resolution_note`, stamps `resolved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `resolved_at` with the current logical clock, decrements the site's `open_alert_count` by 1, recomputes `coaching_posture`, and increments both the alert's `version` and the site's `version` by 1. Acknowledging an alert that is not `OPEN`, or resolving one that is not `ACKNOWLEDGED` — including trying to resolve an alert that is still merely `OPEN` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ALERT_STATE`.

Two further exits are never requested directly; they are consequences of other operations. An alert reaches `AUTO_RESOLVED` from either `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` when a void removes enough weight from its service period that the breach it reported no longer holds, and it reaches `EXPIRED` from `OPEN` only when the period closes. Both exits stamp `resolved_at`, decrement `open_alert_count` and recompute `coaching_posture`; §3.4 and §3.5 specify their exact preconditions. As the count falls, the posture walks back down the same ladder it climbed — from `ESCALATED` to `INTERVENTION` to `MONITORING` — and once it leaves `ESCALATED` the donation block on the site lifts.

### 3.4. Curating the Capture Record: Flags, Voids and Corrections

The four curation operations on a capture belong exclusively to `ShiftLead`. They exist because a scale terminal in a working kitchen produces bad data — a tray weighed twice, a wrong food type tapped, a number nobody trusts — and the platform's answer is to keep the original record and annotate or supersede it rather than to edit it in place.

Flagging marks a capture as needing review without removing a single gram from any total. It advances `RECORDED → FLAGGED`, requires a `flag_reason` in the request, and increments the capture's `version` by 1. The entry remains inside **every** aggregation set — `FLAGGED` is counted exactly like `RECORDED` throughout the platform — so no site or service-period aggregate changes at all. Clearing the flag advances `FLAGGED → RECORDED`, takes no request body, retains the previously recorded `flag_reason` for audit rather than nulling it, likewise changes no aggregate, and increments `version` by 1. These two edges together form a repeatable review cycle: a capture may be flagged, cleared, and flagged again. Flagging anything that is not `RECORDED`, or clearing a flag on anything that is not `FLAGGED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE`.

Voiding is how a capture is genuinely retracted, and it drives the deepest cascade in the system. A void is permitted from either `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` — from `VOIDED` or `SUPERSEDED` it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE` — and it requires a `void_reason`. Two further guards apply. The acting `ShiftLead`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from the capture's `captured_by_user_id`; a lead attempting to erase their own capture is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the capture's `routed_weight_grams` must be exactly 0; any capture with grams already routed onto a consignment is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_ROUTED_CANNOT_VOID`, and those grams must be released first by removing the allocation lines or cancelling the consignment.

The void cascade proceeds in four hops, each caused by the previous one. In the first hop the capture's status becomes `VOIDED`, `void_reason` is stored, and the entry leaves every `RECORDED`-or-`FLAGGED` aggregation set. In the second hop, caused by that departure, the owning service period recomputes `shift_waste_grams`, `waste_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals`, `capture_compliance_pct` and `pre_consumer_share_pct` — and, when the period has already been closed, its `leaderboard_score` as well. In the third hop, caused by the period's new intensity value, every coaching alert of that period whose `alert_type` is `INTENSITY_BREACH` and whose status is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` transitions to `AUTO_RESOLVED` with `resolved_at` set to the current logical clock — but only when the recomputed `waste_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals` is either null or has fallen strictly below the 12.000 coaching threshold. An overproduction-spike alert is never auto-resolved by a void. In the fourth hop, caused by those alerts leaving the open-or-acknowledged set, the site's `open_alert_count` is decremented once per auto-resolved alert and `coaching_posture` is recomputed from the new count. Independently of that chain, the first hop also directly forces a recompute of the site's `total_captured_weight_grams` and `valued_waste_cost_cents`. The capture's `version`, the service period's `version` and the site's `version` are each incremented by 1.

Correcting a capture is the alternative to voiding it: the bad record is superseded and a good record takes its place in the same breath. The request carries `expected_entry_version` as described in §2.6, a mandatory `correction_note`, and optional replacements for `weight_grams`, `food_type_id`, `loss_reason` and `recipe_id`. Preconditions are, in order, and consistently with the ladder of §2.9: `expected_entry_version` must equal the capture's current `version`, refused otherwise with `ERR_STALE_ENTRY_VERSION` (§2.9 step 10); the target capture must be `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE` (§2.9 step 11); the owning service period must still be `OPEN`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_STATE`; and the capture's `routed_weight_grams` must be 0, refused otherwise with `ERR_ENTRY_ROUTED_CANNOT_VOID`. A given capture may be corrected at most once — a second correction against a capture that already has a successor is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_ALREADY_CORRECTED`. That at-most-one-correction check is a uniqueness constraint sitting at §2.9's step 24, well after the target-state check of step 11, so a correction aimed at a capture that is not `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` reports `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE` even when a successor genuinely already exists; a successor row counts for this check whatever status it currently holds, including `VOIDED`.

On success the original capture's status becomes `SUPERSEDED`, which removes it from every `RECORDED`-or-`FLAGGED` aggregation set while leaving it visible to the compliance numerator, and its `version` is incremented by 1. A **new** capture is created in status `RECORDED` with `corrects_entry_id` pointing at the original's `id`. The new record copies `station_id`, `shift_id` and `captured_at` from the original, takes `weight_grams`, `food_type_id`, `loss_reason` and `recipe_id` from the request where supplied and falls back to the original's values for each field omitted, stores the supplied `correction_note`, and stamps `captured_by_user_id` with the correcting `ShiftLead`'s own `X-User-ID` — which means that lead becomes the identity barred from later voiding the correction. The new record re-runs the full capture valuation with fresh catalog and recipe snapshots, so `catalog_cost_basis_cents` and `recipe_cost_basis_cents` reflect the catalog as it stands now, and re-runs the coaching evaluation of §3.3. The loss-reason-versus-station-kind pairing, the future-capture check and the shift-date check all apply to the correcting entry exactly as they do to an original capture; the station-occupation check, however, is **skipped** for a correcting entry, because the original capture already reserved that interval and the correction inherits it. Service-period and site aggregates are recomputed once, after both status writes have landed, so no intermediate state is ever observable.

The correction chain has one more edge, and it is the platform's cleanest compensation. When a correcting capture is itself voided, the predecessor it superseded transitions `SUPERSEDED → RECORDED`, re-entering every aggregation set before the service period's and the site's aggregates are recomputed, and its `version` is incremented by 1. The restored status is always `RECORDED`, never `FLAGGED`, even when the entry happened to be `FLAGGED` at the moment it was superseded. Because every aggregate is a full recompute rather than a running delta, this restores the site's `valued_waste_cost_cents` to exactly its pre-correction value with zero drift. This edge together with the supersession edge forms the second cycle on a capture's lifecycle, `RECORDED → SUPERSEDED → RECORDED`, alongside the flag cycle `RECORDED → FLAGGED → RECORDED`.

### 3.5. Closing a Service Period: Final Valuation and the Leaderboard

Closing a service period is the accounting event of the kitchen day, and it belongs exclusively to `ShiftLead` — not to the site manager, not to the platform administrator. The period must currently be `OPEN`; closing an already-`CLOSED` period is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_STATE`. Closure advances `OPEN → CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` with the current logical clock, and increments the period's `version` by 1.

The substance of closure is the **final valuation pass**. Every capture on this period whose status is `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` is revalued, processed in ascending `captured_at` order with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break, and for each one the platform writes `final_cost_cents` and `valuation_delta_cents`. The final figure uses the recipe basis when the capture has one: `final_cost_cents` is the capture's kilogram weight — `weight_grams` divided by 1000, exact and unrounded — multiplied by `recipe_cost_basis_cents` and rounded half up to a whole number of cents, when `recipe_cost_basis_cents` is not null; when it is null the same multiplication is performed against `catalog_cost_basis_cents`, so a capture with no recipe attribution finishes with a final figure identical to its provisional one. `valuation_delta_cents` is `final_cost_cents` minus `provisional_cost_cents`. Because `effective_cost_cents` is defined as `final_cost_cents` whenever that field is non-null, closure is the moment the whole platform's notion of what the waste cost silently switches from provisional to final.

The size of that switch is the point of carrying two bases. Take 2450 grams of a food type priced at 640 cents per kilogram, attributed to a recipe whose published cost is 1029 cents per kilogram. The provisional valuation is 1568 cents; the final valuation is 2521 cents; the recorded `valuation_delta_cents` is 953 cents. The recipe figure is higher because it is fully loaded — it already carries the trim loss the kitchen paid for and the catalog price does not — and the platform reports the gap rather than hiding it.

Four further hops follow, and the causal chain matters. Closure recomputes the period's `shift_waste_grams`, `waste_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals`, `capture_compliance_pct` and `pre_consumer_share_pct`, then computes `leaderboard_score`, which stays null for as long as a period remains `OPEN`. The second hop is caused by the effective-cost switch: the owning site recomputes `valued_waste_cost_cents`. The third hop is caused by the period leaving `OPEN`: every coaching alert of this period whose status is `OPEN` transitions to `EXPIRED` with `resolved_at` set to the current logical clock, while alerts already in `ACKNOWLEDGED` are deliberately left untouched — someone took ownership of those, and closing the period does not absolve them. The fourth hop is caused by each alert leaving the open-or-acknowledged set in the third: the site's `open_alert_count` is decremented once per expired alert and `coaching_posture` is recomputed. Separately, every reduction target with status `ACTIVE` whose performance window contains this period's `shift_date` has its `performance_meals_served`, `performance_waste_grams`, reduction percentages and `attainment_state` recomputed, because a newly closed period is the only thing that ever adds meals to a performance window.

The leaderboard score is a weighted 100-point figure with a clamped penalty and a conditional bonus, and it is computed from three inputs. The **intensity excess** is the amount by which the period's `waste_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals` exceeds the site's `target_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals`, floored at zero so a period that beat its target scores no excess; a null intensity, which happens when `meals_served` is 0, is treated as zero excess. The **intensity penalty** is that excess multiplied by 4.0 points per kilogram per thousand meals, then capped so it can never exceed 60.0 points however far the period overshot. The **compliance penalty** is the shortfall in station reporting — one minus `capture_compliance_pct` — multiplied by a weight of 25.0 points, so a period where every active station reported loses nothing and a period where none did loses the full 25.0. The **pre-consumer bonus** is a flat 3.0 points, awarded when `pre_consumer_share_pct` is not null and has reached 0.60 or more, and zero otherwise; catching waste before it reaches a plate is what the bonus rewards. The raw score is 100 minus the intensity penalty, minus the compliance penalty, plus the bonus. That raw figure is then clamped into the range from 0 to 100 inclusive — first floored at 0, then capped at 100 — and rounded half up to 1 decimal place to become `leaderboard_score`.

### 3.6. Baselines and Reduction Targets

Reduction is meaningless without a measured starting point, so every site establishes **baseline periods** before it is given a target. Opening one is the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager`'s duty and takes the `site_id`, a `start_date` and an `end_date`, both calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, with `end_date` no earlier than `start_date`. The window is inclusive on both ends. A new period whose inclusive date interval intersects an existing period of the same site is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A period opens in status `OPEN` with `rejection_count` at 0, `rejection_note`, `submitted_at` and `certified_at` all null, and every frozen baseline metric null or zero. Baseline periods may be listed filtered by `site_id` and `status`.

Submission freezes the numbers. It advances `OPEN → SUBMITTED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BASELINE_STATE` from any other status, and requires at least one capture with status `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` whose `captured_at` date falls inside the inclusive window at this site — a window with no data is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_NO_DATA`. Six metrics are then computed and frozen. Over the qualifying capture set, `baseline_entry_count` is the number of captures, `baseline_waste_grams` is the sum of their `weight_grams`, `baseline_waste_kg` is those grams divided by 1000 and rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and `baseline_cost_cents` is the sum of their `effective_cost_cents`. Over a *different* set — the site's service periods whose status is `CLOSED` and whose `shift_date` falls inside the same inclusive window — `baseline_meals_served` is the sum of `meals_served`. Only closed periods contribute meals; an `OPEN` period is excluded from the meals total even when its captures fall squarely inside the window, and those captures still count toward the waste total. Finally `baseline_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals` is `baseline_waste_grams` divided by `baseline_meals_served`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and is null when `baseline_meals_served` is 0. Submission stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock and increments `version` by 1.

Corporate review, exercised by a `CorporateSustainabilityLead`, has two outcomes. Rejection advances `SUBMITTED → OPEN`, stores the supplied `rejection_note`, increases `rejection_count` by 1, and clears every frozen baseline metric back to null or zero so that the next submission recomputes them from current data rather than reusing a stale freeze; the `rejection_note` itself is retained after the period cycles back to `OPEN`, and `version` is incremented by 1. Because that edge returns the period to a submittable state, the revision cycle `OPEN → SUBMITTED → OPEN` is repeatable without limit and `rejection_count` is its loop counter. Certification advances `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`, additionally requires `baseline_meals_served` to be greater than 0 — certifying a window with frozen meals of zero is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_NO_DATA` — stamps `certified_at` with the current logical clock, makes the frozen metrics immutable, and increments `version` by 1. Certifying or rejecting a period that is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BASELINE_STATE`. `CERTIFIED` is the only status a reduction target may bind to.

A **reduction target** is proposed by a `CorporateSustainabilityLead` and names the `site_id` it applies to, the `baseline_period_id` of the baseline it is measured against, a `basis` of either `ABSOLUTE` or `INTENSITY`, a `target_reduction_pct` to 2 decimal places that must be greater than 0 and no more than 100, a `window_start` and a `window_end` bounding the inclusive performance window with `window_end` no earlier than `window_start`, and the `expected_site_version` token of §2.6. An optional `min_diversion_rate_pct`, also to 2 decimal places, sets a diversion-rate floor that must additionally be met for the target to count as attained; it must be at least 0 and no more than 100 and defaults to 0.00. The performance window must begin strictly after the baseline it is measured against ends: a `window_start` that is not strictly later than the bound period's `end_date` is refused with `ERR_TARGET_WINDOW_BEFORE_BASELINE`, which is what keeps a target from grading itself against overlapping data.

Proposing a target supersedes its predecessor. Any existing target on the same `site_id` with the identical `window_start` and `window_end` whose status is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, has `resolved_at` stamped with the current logical clock, and has `version` incremented by 1; from that moment it stops contributing to corporate on-track counting. The new target itself opens in status `PROPOSED` with `activated_at` and `resolved_at` null, `performance_waste_grams` and `performance_meals_served` at 0, and `attainment_state` at `BEHIND`. Targets may be listed filtered by `site_id`, `baseline_period_id`, `status`, `basis` and `attainment_state`.

Activation advances `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and is refused from any other status with `ERR_INVALID_TARGET_STATE`. It additionally requires the bound baseline period to be `CERTIFIED`; a target bound to a period still `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. On success `activated_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, the full attainment evaluation runs immediately, and `version` is incremented by 1. Activation never resolves a target to `ACHIEVED` or `MISSED`, however far past its window the clock already stands — only a clock advance can do that.

The attainment evaluation reads the performance side live and the baseline side frozen. Over captures at the target's site with status `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` whose `captured_at` date falls inside the inclusive performance window, `performance_waste_grams` is the sum of `weight_grams`. Over the site's `CLOSED` service periods whose `shift_date` falls inside that same inclusive window, `performance_meals_served` is the sum of `meals_served`. `performance_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals` is the performance grams divided by the performance meals, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and is null when the meals figure is 0. The baseline side always reads the bound period's frozen metrics and never recomputes them. `absolute_reduction_pct` is the baseline grams minus the performance grams, divided by the baseline grams, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places; it is null when the frozen `baseline_waste_grams` is 0. `intensity_reduction_pct` is the frozen baseline intensity minus the performance intensity, divided by the frozen baseline intensity, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places; it is null whenever either intensity is null. `effective_reduction_pct` is the absolute figure when `basis` is `ABSOLUTE` and the intensity figure when `basis` is `INTENSITY`.

`attainment_state` then holds `ACHIEVED_PENDING` when three conditions all hold — `effective_reduction_pct` is not null, it has reached `target_reduction_pct` or more with the comparison inclusive, and the diversion floor is satisfied — and `BEHIND` in every other case. The diversion floor reads the site's live `diversion_rate_pct`, with a null value read as 0.00, and is satisfied when that value has reached `min_diversion_rate_pct` or more. This is why accepting a weigh ticket can flip a target from `BEHIND` to `ACHIEVED_PENDING` without a single gram of waste changing.

The two bases genuinely disagree, and the platform makes the disagreement visible rather than picking a winner. Consider a baseline of 3000 grams over 250 meals, an intensity of 12.000, against a performance window of 2700 grams over 200 meals, an intensity of 13.500. Measured absolutely the site improved by 10.00 percent; measured by intensity it regressed by 12.50 percent. With a `target_reduction_pct` of 10.00, an `ABSOLUTE` target is attained on exactly the same underlying rows on which an `INTENSITY` target is missed. A dedicated computed read exposes both results side by side for one target, returning its `target_id`, `basis`, the frozen `baseline_waste_grams` and `baseline_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals`, the live `performance_waste_grams`, `performance_meals_served` and `performance_intensity_kg_per_1k_meals`, both `absolute_reduction_pct` and `intensity_reduction_pct`, the selected `effective_reduction_pct`, the configured `target_reduction_pct` and `min_diversion_rate_pct`, the site's current diversion rate as `site_diversion_rate_pct`, and the resulting `attainment_state`.

Resolution is driven solely by the logical clock. When the clock is advanced to a new `current_utc`, every target with status `ACTIVE` whose performance window has closed — meaning the new clock has reached midnight at the very start of the day after `window_end`, expressed as `window_end` plus one day at `00:00:00.000Z`, or any later instant — is processed in ascending `created_at` order. Each one is recomputed from scratch and then routed: a target whose fresh `attainment_state` is `ACHIEVED_PENDING` advances `ACTIVE → ACHIEVED`, and a target whose fresh state is `BEHIND` advances `ACTIVE → MISSED`. Either way `resolved_at` is stamped with the new clock value and `version` is incremented by 1. `ACHIEVED`, `MISSED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal. The clock response reports the accepted `current_utc`, a `reevaluated_target_ids` array listing the targets it recomputed in that processing order, and a `resolved_target_ids` array listing those that reached a terminal status.

### 3.7. Diverting the Material: Haulers, Consignments and Allocations

Weighing waste is only half the product; the other half is getting it out of the building to somewhere better than a landfill. That physical leg is modelled as a **diversion consignment** — one hauler, one channel, one reserved pickup window, and a set of allocation lines drawing grams from individual captures.

A hauler or recipient organisation is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a globally unique `name`, refused on collision with `ERR_DUPLICATE_HAULER_NAME`, and a `channels` array of at least one entry, without duplicates, drawn from `DONATION`, `ANIMAL_FEED`, `COMPOST` and `LANDFILL` — these are the channels the organisation is contracted for. Its rate card is a `rate_cents_per_tonne` greater than zero and a flat `stop_fee_cents` of zero or more added to every ticket's base fee. Two capacity limits complete the record: `capacity_kg_per_pickup`, greater than zero, is the maximum staged kilograms the hauler will accept on a single pickup, and `max_concurrent_pickups`, at least 1 and defaulting to 1, is how many of the hauler's non-terminal consignments may hold overlapping pickup windows. A hauler is created with `status` `PENDING`, which may only ever hold `PENDING` or `APPROVED`. A `PENDING` hauler may hold consignments in `DRAFT` or `STAGED` but may never receive a dispatch. Approval belongs to `CorporateSustainabilityLead`, advances `PENDING → APPROVED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_HAULER_STATE` when the hauler is already approved, makes every consignment already staged against that hauler dispatchable, and increments the hauler's `version` by 1. Haulers may be listed filtered by `status` and by `channel`.

A consignment is opened by the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager` with the `site_id` the material leaves from, the `hauler_id` collecting it, a `channel` drawn from the same four values, and a reserved pickup interval given by `pickup_window_start` inclusive and `pickup_window_end` exclusive, where the end must be strictly after the start. An optional `container_tare_grams`, zero or greater and defaulting to 0, records the tare of the transport container that will be subtracted from the hauler's gross weight later. The requested `channel` must be listed in the hauler's `channels`, or the request is refused with `ERR_HAULER_CHANNEL_UNSUPPORTED`. The pickup window is then checked against the hauler's aggregate concurrency: counting only the hauler's consignments whose `logistics_status` is `DRAFT`, `STAGED` or `DISPATCHED`, the number whose half-open interval from `pickup_window_start` up to but excluding `pickup_window_end` intersects the requested half-open interval must remain strictly below `max_concurrent_pickups`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_HAULER_WINDOW_CAPACITY`. Two windows that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending exactly where the next begins — do not intersect and do not consume capacity.

A consignment carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and confusing them is the single most common misreading of this domain. `logistics_status` tracks physical movement through `DRAFT`, `STAGED`, `DISPATCHED`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`, starting at `DRAFT`. `settlement_status` tracks billing through `UNBILLED`, `TICKET_SUBMITTED`, `SETTLED` and `DISPUTED`, starting at `UNBILLED`. The two axes evolve independently and there is exactly one coupling point between them, described in §3.8. A new consignment also has `staged_weight_grams` and `line_count` at 0, and `cancel_reason`, `staged_at` and `dispatched_at` all null. Consignments may be listed filtered by `site_id`, `hauler_id`, `channel`, `logistics_status` and `settlement_status`, so either dimension can be queried on its own.

**Allocation lines** attach captured grams to a consignment and may be created by either the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager` or a `ShiftLead`. A line names its `consignment_id`, the `waste_entry_id` whose weight is being routed, and the `allocated_weight_grams` being routed, which may be a partial slice of the capture — the same capture can be split across several consignments and channels. The platform stamps `created_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. A line is an immutable event record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` of its own. Lines may be listed filtered by `consignment_id` or by `waste_entry_id`, which is the reverse-navigation read from a capture to everywhere its grams went.

Adding a line is guarded by five business rules beyond existence and scoping. The consignment must still be in `DRAFT`; once it has left `DRAFT` no line may be added or deleted and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONSIGNMENT_STATE`. The referenced capture must not be `VOIDED` or `SUPERSEDED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE`. The capture's `site_id` must equal the consignment's `site_id`, refused otherwise with `ERR_ENTRY_SITE_MISMATCH`. The food must be eligible for the consignment's `channel`: a `DONATION` line requires the food type's `donation_eligible` flag to be true **and** the capture's `stream` to be `PRE_CONSUMER`, because post-consumer material can never be given to people however wholesome its food type; an `ANIMAL_FEED` line requires the food type's `animal_feed_eligible` flag to be true; a `COMPOST` line accepts any food type regardless of the `compostable` flag, and no food-type eligibility restriction is imposed on `LANDFILL`. A violation is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_INELIGIBLE_FOOD`. On a `DONATION` consignment the food must additionally still be inside its shelf life: the consignment's `pickup_window_start` must be no later than the capture's `captured_at` advanced by the food type's `donation_shelf_life_hours`, with that endpoint inclusive, and a later pickup start is refused with `ERR_DONATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Finally `allocated_weight_grams` must be at least 1 and no greater than the capture's current `unrouted_weight_grams`; over-allocation is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_UNROUTED`.

Every line insertion and deletion re-derives four figures from the surviving source rows. For the affected capture, `routed_weight_grams` becomes the sum of `allocated_weight_grams` over all its lines whose consignment's `logistics_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, and `unrouted_weight_grams` becomes its `weight_grams` minus that routed figure. For the consignment, `staged_weight_grams` becomes the sum of `allocated_weight_grams` over its lines and `line_count` becomes the number of lines attached. Deleting a line — permitted only while the consignment is `DRAFT`, and available to the same two roles — restores the capture's unrouted grams exactly, so the identity that a capture's `weight_grams` equals its routed plus its unrouted grams holds continuously and with zero drift.

Staging locks the line set for pickup. It advances `logistics_status` `DRAFT → STAGED`, refusing any other starting state with `ERR_INVALID_CONSIGNMENT_STATE`. It requires `line_count` to be at least 1, refusing an empty consignment with `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_EMPTY`, and requires `staged_weight_grams` not to exceed the hauler's `capacity_kg_per_pickup` converted to grams by multiplying by 1000 — an over-capacity load is refused with `ERR_HAULER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A `DONATION` consignment may not be staged while its site's `coaching_posture` is `ESCALATED`, which is refused with `ERR_SITE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_DONATION`: a kitchen with three or more unresolved coaching alerts has not earned the right to hand food to the public. On success `staged_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, `staged_weight_grams` is frozen for the later ticket variance comparison, no further line may be added or deleted, and `version` is incremented by 1.

Dispatch marks the material as collected and is available to the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager` and to the collecting `HaulerOperator`. It advances `logistics_status` `STAGED → DISPATCHED`, refusing any other starting state with `ERR_INVALID_CONSIGNMENT_STATE`. The hauler must be `APPROVED`; dispatching to a hauler still `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_HAULER_NOT_APPROVED`. The donation posture block applies here too: a consignment whose `channel` is `DONATION` may not be dispatched while its site's `coaching_posture` is `ESCALATED`, refused with `ERR_SITE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_DONATION`, while a consignment on any other channel is unaffected by posture. On success `dispatched_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, the consignment becomes ticket-eligible on the settlement dimension, and `version` is incremented by 1.

Cancellation is the compensating action on the logistics dimension. It is permitted from `DRAFT`, `STAGED` or `DISPATCHED` and requires a `cancel_reason`; it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONSIGNMENT_STATE` from `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, and also whenever `settlement_status` has already reached `SETTLED` — money that has settled cannot be un-shipped. Cancellation does **not** delete the attached lines; they are retained for audit and simply stop counting. Consequently, for each distinct `waste_entry_id` appearing on the consignment, that capture's `routed_weight_grams` and `unrouted_weight_grams` are recomputed and its grams become available for routing again, and the site's `diverted_weight_grams_settled` and `diversion_rate_pct` are recomputed. The consignment's `version` is incremented by 1. Cancelling from `DISPATCHED` has one additional consequence: any weigh ticket already in `SUBMITTED` on that consignment stays `SUBMITTED` forever and can no longer be accepted, and an attempt to accept it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONSIGNMENT_STATE`.

### 3.8. Weigh Tickets and Settlement Pricing

The settlement dimension exists because the hauler's scale and the kitchen's scale never quite agree, and the platform prices the haul from the hauler's ticket while measuring the disagreement.

Filing a ticket is the `HaulerOperator`'s exclusive operation. The request names the `consignment_id` being settled, the hauler's own `ticket_number`, the `gross_weight_grams` recorded at the hauler's scale with the container included, which must be greater than zero, and the `ticket_date` printed on the ticket as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date. An optional `hauler_id` asserts which ticket book the ticket was torn from; when omitted it defaults to the referenced consignment's `hauler_id`, and when supplied it must equal that value, with a mismatch refused as `ERR_TICKET_HAULER_MISMATCH`. That resolved `hauler_id` — supplied or defaulted — is also the value compared against the operator's `X-User-ID` for hauler scoping, and it is the value that scopes ticket-number uniqueness: the combination of `hauler_id` and `ticket_number` must be unique, and a reused number is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TICKET_NUMBER`.

Two state preconditions apply, and they are the **only** coupling point between the consignment's two dimensions. The consignment's `logistics_status` must be `DISPATCHED`; filing against anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONSIGNMENT_STATE`. Its `settlement_status` must be `UNBILLED` or `DISPUTED`; filing against `TICKET_SUBMITTED` or `SETTLED` is refused with `ERR_TICKET_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`, which is also the error when the consignment already carries a ticket whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` — a consignment may hold at most one ticket that is not `DISPUTED`. On success the ticket is created with `status` `SUBMITTED`, which may only ever hold `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `DISPUTED`, with `dispute_note` and `accepted_at` null, and the consignment's `settlement_status` advances to `TICKET_SUBMITTED` from either `UNBILLED` or `DISPUTED` while `logistics_status` stays `DISPATCHED` untouched; the consignment's `version` is incremented by 1. Tickets may be listed filtered by `consignment_id`, `hauler_id`, `status` and `ticket_date`.

Filing runs a seven-step settlement pricing pass whose step order is normative. **Step one**: `net_weight_grams` is the submitted `gross_weight_grams` minus the consignment's `container_tare_grams`. **Step two**: `billable_tonnes` is those net grams divided by one million — the number of grams in a metric tonne — rounded half up to 4 decimal places. **Step three**: `base_haul_fee_cents` is `billable_tonnes` multiplied by the hauler's `rate_cents_per_tonne`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and then increased by the hauler's flat `stop_fee_cents`. **Step four** selects the fuel surcharge tier **from `base_haul_fee_cents` alone**: `fuel_surcharge_pct` is 0.00 when the base fee is below 5000 cents, 0.06 when the base fee is at least 5000 cents and below 15000 cents, and 0.09 when the base fee has reached 15000 cents or more; `fuel_surcharge_cents` is `base_haul_fee_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. **Step five**: `settlement_subtotal_cents` is `base_haul_fee_cents` plus `fuel_surcharge_cents`. **Step six** measures the scale disagreement: `variance_pct` is the absolute difference between `net_weight_grams` and the consignment's frozen `staged_weight_grams`, divided by that `staged_weight_grams`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places; `variance_credit_cents` is 10 percent of `settlement_subtotal_cents`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, when `variance_pct` is **strictly greater** than a 5.00 percent tolerance, and 0 otherwise — a variance of exactly 5.00 percent earns no credit. **Step seven**: `settlement_total_cents` is `settlement_subtotal_cents` minus `variance_credit_cents`.

The tier selection in step four is the trap this pricing model is built to expose, and it is worth stating negatively. Suppose a net weight of 250000 grams against a staged weight of 240000 grams, a rate of 52000 cents per tonne and a stop fee of 1800 cents. The base fee is 13000 plus 1800, which is 14800 cents; 14800 sits in the middle tier, so the surcharge rate is 0.06 and the surcharge is 888 cents, giving a subtotal of 15688 cents. That subtotal is itself above the 15000-cent boundary, and keying the tier on it instead would produce a surcharge of 1332 and a total of 16132 — a self-referential result, because the subtotal already contains the surcharge being selected. The tier is keyed on the base fee, never on the subtotal and never on the total, so the correct answer here is 15688 cents.

Review of the ticket belongs to the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager` — never to the operator who filed it. Acceptance requires the ticket to be `SUBMITTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_TICKET_STATE`, and requires the consignment to be `DISPATCHED` on the logistics dimension and `TICKET_SUBMITTED` on the settlement dimension, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_CONSIGNMENT_STATE`. Acceptance advances the ticket `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at` with the current logical clock, and increments the ticket's `version` by 1. It then drives a three-hop cascade. The first hop flips both consignment dimensions in the same request: `settlement_status` advances `TICKET_SUBMITTED → SETTLED` and `logistics_status` advances `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED`. The second hop is caused by that settlement flip: the consignment's lines now qualify for the settled-diversion filter, so the site's `diverted_weight_grams_settled` and `diversion_rate_pct` are recomputed. The third hop is caused by the site's new diversion rate: every reduction target of that site with status `ACTIVE` re-evaluates its `attainment_state`, which can flip from `BEHIND` to `ACHIEVED_PENDING` purely because the diversion floor is now satisfied. The consignment's `version` and the site's `version` are each incremented by 1.

Disputing a ticket requires the ticket to be `SUBMITTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_TICKET_STATE`, and takes a `dispute_note`. It advances the ticket `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` and sets the consignment's `settlement_status` to `DISPUTED` while `logistics_status` stays `DISPATCHED` throughout. No site aggregate changes, because nothing was ever credited — a disputed haul was never settled. The ticket's `version` is incremented by 1. The disputed ticket itself stays `DISPUTED` forever; it is never reopened. Instead the hauler files a **replacement** ticket with a fresh `ticket_number` against the same consignment, which is permitted precisely because `settlement_status` is `DISPUTED`, and which returns the settlement dimension to `TICKET_SUBMITTED`. That gives the settlement axis its own cycle, `TICKET_SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → TICKET_SUBMITTED`, repeatable as often as the parties disagree, entirely independent of the logistics axis which remains `DISPATCHED` the whole time.

### 3.9. Data Quality Assurance

Before any number leaves the company, the platform interrogates its own capture record. A data-quality scan is requested by the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager` or by a `PlatformAdmin` for one `site_id` and one `scan_date`, and it materialises findings as durable rows. Its working sets are narrow and exact: the shifts considered are those of the scanned site whose `shift_date` equals the `scan_date` precisely — neither the day before nor the day after — and the stations considered are those of the scanned site whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, so `MAINTENANCE` and `RETIRED` stations are excluded from the missed-capture sweep. Captures in `VOIDED` are invisible to all three rules. The scan answers with an array of exactly the findings it created, in creation order; a scan of a site that has no `ACTIVE` station and no shift on that date is still a success carrying an empty array, not an error.

Three rules run, and their creation order is pinned: every `MISSED_CAPTURE` finding first, then every `DUPLICATE_ENTRY`, then every `WEIGHT_OUTLIER`. The row `id` is never used for ordering.

The `MISSED_CAPTURE` rule walks every pairing of an in-scope station with an in-scope shift, ordered by the station's `station_code` ascending and then by the shift's `shift_code` in the order `AM`, `PM`, `NIGHT`, and emits one finding for a pairing when no capture exists carrying that `station_id` and that `shift_id` with `status` `RECORDED`, `FLAGGED` or `SUPERSEDED` — a superseded capture still proves the station was used, so a corrected station is not also a missed one. The finding's `waste_entry_id` is null, because the defect is the absence of a row.

The `DUPLICATE_ENTRY` rule considers the captures of those shifts whose `status` is `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED`, sorted by `captured_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and emits one finding on a capture when some **earlier** capture in that sort order shares its `station_id`, its `food_type_id` and its `weight_grams`, and the elapsed time from the earlier capture's `captured_at` to this one's is 300 seconds or less, with that boundary inclusive — a gap of exactly 300 seconds is a duplicate. The finding is attached to the later capture only, never to the earlier one, so a run of three identical weighings produces two findings rather than three.

The `WEIGHT_OUTLIER` rule considers the captures of those shifts whose `status` is `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` and whose `weight_grams` has reached 25000 grams or more, that boundary also inclusive, sorted by `captured_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

Re-running a scan is idempotent, but only against live findings. A candidate is skipped when a finding already exists with `status` `OPEN` and an identical combination of `finding_type`, `site_id`, `station_id`, `shift_id` and `waste_entry_id`. A finding already `CONFIRMED` or `DISMISSED` does **not** suppress a re-emit: once a human has judged a defect, a later scan is entitled to raise it again as a fresh open item.

A finding records the scanned `site_id`, the `station_id` and `shift_id` it is about, the offending `waste_entry_id` where one exists, the `finding_type` it came from, a human-readable `detail` explaining the detection, and a `detected_at` stamped with the logical clock instant of the scan. Its `status` begins at `OPEN`, which may only ever hold `OPEN`, `CONFIRMED` or `DISMISSED`, and its `resolved_by_user_id` is null while open. Resolution is the owning `SiteSustainabilityManager`'s act: a `resolution` of `CONFIRMED` accepts the defect as real and a `resolution` of `DISMISSED` rejects it as noise, with an optional `resolution_note` recording the reasoning. Either advances the finding out of `OPEN`, refusing any already-resolved finding with `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`, stamps `resolved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and increments `version` by 1. Both outcomes have identical downstream force: a confirmed finding and a dismissed finding equally stop blocking corporate publication, because the block is on unexamined defects, not on defects. Findings may be listed filtered by `site_id`, `waste_entry_id`, `station_id`, `shift_id`, `finding_type` and `status`.

### 3.10. Corporate Reporting and the Publication Gate

A corporate rollup is the company-level report, prepared by a `CorporateSustainabilityLead` over an inclusive reporting period given by `period_start` and `period_end`, where the end must not precede the start, and an explicit `site_ids` array. That array is a list of site identifiers rather than a single foreign key: it must hold at least one element, every element must be an existing site, order is irrelevant, and a repeated site is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_IN_SCOPE`. The rollup's `status` begins at `DRAFT`, which may only ever hold `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, `prepared_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID` — the value that segregation of duties will later be measured against — and `published_by_user_id`, `published_at` and `published_metrics` are all null.

Preparation computes seven figures, all of them reading only the explicitly listed sites. `in_scope_site_count` is the length of `site_ids`. The capture set for the period is every capture belonging to an in-scope site whose `status` is `RECORDED` or `FLAGGED` and whose `captured_at` falls on a calendar date within the inclusive period; `total_captured_weight_grams` is the sum of `weight_grams` over that set and `total_valued_cost_cents` is the sum of `effective_cost_cents` over that same set, so the cost figure automatically reflects whichever shifts have closed and re-valued and whichever have not. The diverted set is every allocation line whose consignment belongs to an in-scope site, whose consignment's `settlement_status` is `SETTLED`, whose consignment's `logistics_status` is not `CANCELLED`, and whose consignment's `pickup_window_start` falls on a calendar date within the inclusive period; `total_diverted_weight_grams` is the sum of `allocated_weight_grams` over that set. Note the deliberate asymmetry in how the two sides are dated: captures are dated by when the food was weighed, and diversions by when the pickup window opened, so a haul settled long after the period still counts in the period whose window it belonged to. `diversion_rate_pct` is `total_diverted_weight_grams` divided by `total_captured_weight_grams`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and is null when the captured denominator is 0.

`sites_on_track_count` is the number of in-scope sites holding at least one reduction target whose `status` is `ACTIVE` **and** whose `attainment_state` is `ACHIEVED_PENDING`. Both conditions bind. A target already `ACHIEVED` does not count, nor does one `MISSED`, `PROPOSED` or `SUPERSEDED`; only a live target currently pending achievement counts, because the metric answers "are these kitchens on track right now", not "have they ever succeeded". A site with several qualifying targets still counts once. From that count, `goal_attainment_state` is `ALL_ON_TRACK` when `sites_on_track_count` equals `in_scope_site_count`, `NONE_ON_TRACK` when `sites_on_track_count` is 0, and `PARTIAL` in every other case. All grams and cents are integers.

While the rollup is `DRAFT` its metrics are live and recompute; publication freezes them. Publication is a `CorporateSustainabilityLead` action gated by three preconditions in that order. The rollup must still be `DRAFT`, refused otherwise with `ERR_ROLLUP_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, refused otherwise with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the lead who assembled a report may never be the lead who signs it off, however senior. And there must be zero findings with `status` `OPEN` whose `site_id` appears in the rollup's `site_ids`, refused otherwise with `ERR_ROLLUP_DATA_QUALITY_BLOCKED`; the finding's `detected_at` is irrelevant to this test, so an open finding from long before the reporting period blocks publication just as firmly as one raised this morning, and the remedy is to resolve it either way, not to argue about its date. On success the metrics are recomputed one final time and that fresh result is copied into `published_metrics` as a frozen object carrying `total_captured_weight_grams`, `total_valued_cost_cents`, `total_diverted_weight_grams`, `diversion_rate_pct`, `sites_on_track_count` and `goal_attainment_state`; `published_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, `published_at` with the current logical clock, `status` advances `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, and `version` is incremented by 1. `PUBLISHED` is terminal: the frozen snapshot never moves again, whatever the underlying sites do afterwards. Rollups may be listed filtered by `status` and by `goal_attainment_state`.

## 4. API Endpoint Specifications

All paths below are relative to the API root; there is no version prefix and no nesting of collections beneath their parents. Endpoints that create a resource answer with HTTP 201 and the created object; endpoints that drive a state transition, recompute a resource or modify one answer with HTTP 200 and the updated object; read endpoints answer with HTTP 200; the deletion of an allocation line answers with HTTP 200 and an empty data object; and the data-quality scan answers with HTTP 200 carrying the array of findings it created, which may be empty. Every response uses the envelope of §2.2. The Authorized Role(s) column lists the values of `X-User-Role` accepted by the endpoint; where a listed role is site-scoped or hauler-scoped, the scoping rules of §2.8 apply in addition.
