# Seasonal Tree Recycling API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every winter, the city faces the same logistical surge: tens of thousands of natural Christmas trees, wreaths, and holiday greens must be collected from curbs and drop-off sites inside a short seasonal window, kept free of contaminating stands, decorations, tinsel, and flocking, and processed into mulch that residents later claim. Running this program on spreadsheets and phone calls produces missed streets, overfilled stockpiles, contaminated mulch batches, and disputes with residents that staff cannot adjudicate consistently. The Seasonal Tree Recycling API is the operational backbone for the city tree program, covering the full arc of the season: defining the collection window and the drop-off site network, scheduling single-day curbside routes per service zone, recording gate intake, enforcing contamination rules through a ticket-and-appeal pipeline with household strikes, running chipping operations with yield reconciliation, distributing the resulting mulch through tiered claim events, coordinating the volunteers who staff the sites, and closing the season with a defensible diversion report.

The platform is built around two hard realities of the domain. The first is time: nearly every rule in the program is a window, a deadline, or a cutoff, and the server therefore evaluates every temporal rule against a deterministic logical clock rather than the wall clock, so a season can be rehearsed, tested, and audited predictably. The second is scarcity: route capacity is measured in trees, site capacity in cubic yards of stockpile, and the mulch pool is split into reserved and general tiers, so every allocation decision is explicit, checked, and — when something goes wrong — reversed through compensating actions rather than edits to history.

This document specifies the complete behavior of the API. Section 2 defines system-wide contracts — authentication, the response envelope, pagination, the logical clock, concurrency control, validation ordering, and measurement conventions. Section 3 walks the business workflows in seasonal order. Section 4 is the endpoint contract, and Section 5 the error catalogue.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that apply uniformly to every resource and every request in the system.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

All interactions with the API are authenticated through two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the caller's operational capacity. The system recognizes exactly six roles, spelled as follows: `ProgramAdmin`, `SiteSupervisor`, `HaulerPartner`, `Resident`, `Volunteer`, and `Anonymous`. A request whose headers are missing, or whose role value is not one of these six declared roles, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. This requirement applies to every endpoint without exception — including the self-service registration endpoints, whose callers must still present the headers with the `Anonymous` role. A caller whose role is authenticated but not on the endpoint's authorized list — including the per-transition role splits described in Section 3, such as only a `SiteSupervisor` or `ProgramAdmin` being allowed to mark a mulch claim as fulfilled, and only a `Resident` or `ProgramAdmin` being allowed to cancel one — is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN`.

The `ProgramAdmin` is the city program manager and holds a full superset of all operations: program creation, editing, and the operational checkpoint; site and zone creation; zone rescheduling; volunteer approval; chipping-run scheduling and cancellation; mulch-event administration; report generation and publication; and control of the logical clock. Two segregation-of-duties carve-outs bind even this role, as detailed in Section 3.4: an administrator may not adjudicate a contamination ticket they themselves reported, and may not resolve an appeal against their own adjudication. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as `adjudicated_by` on a ticket the administrator adjudicates) but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and it bypasses the ownership checks described below.

When `X-User-Role` is `SiteSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. Site supervisors staff the drop-off sites: they record and void gate intake, record curbside service outcomes, report and adjudicate contamination, fulfill mulch claims, mark shift outcomes, and share site lifecycle transitions (suspend, reopen, closure) with the administrator. When `X-User-Role` is `HaulerPartner`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier; the role represents the external hauling and processing partner's operator, who supplies scale data at chipping-run completion and reads program, site, run, intake, and report resources, and is not tied to any persisted partner entity.

When `X-User-Role` is `Resident`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Household`; ownership checks on any resource that references a household compare the resource's `household_id` to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a resident may cancel or reschedule a booking, create or cancel a mulch claim, or appeal a ticket only when the `household_id` of the booking, claim, or ticket equals their `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN`. When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Volunteer`, and the same entity-backed comparison applies to shift operations: signing up for or cancelling a shift requires the shift's `volunteer_id` to equal the caller's `X-User-ID`. The `ProgramAdmin` bypasses these ownership checks. Finally, the `Anonymous` role is an opaque, unauthenticated registrant identity: it may only register a household or a volunteer, and the server-minted `id` returned by that registration is the value the registrant later presents as `X-User-ID` under the `Resident` or `Volunteer` role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Endpoints that return lists of resources are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and it accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. The `offset` parameter specifies how many items to skip and defaults to 0. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list endpoints return resources sorted by creation timestamp in ascending order. The single deliberate exception is the program's notification log listing, which returns entries newest first. List endpoints also accept filter parameters drawn from their resource's references and status: programs filter by `status`; sites, zones, volunteers, chipping runs, and diversion reports filter by `program_id` (and by `status`); households additionally filter by `zone_id` and `curbside_eligible`; bookings by `household_id`, `zone_id`, and `status`; intake events by `site_id` and `status`; contamination tickets by `program_id`, `booking_id`, `site_id`, and `status`; chipping runs further by `site_id`; mulch events by `program_id` and `status`; mulch claims by `event_id`, `household_id`, and `status`; volunteer shifts by `volunteer_id`, `site_id`, and `status`; and notification entries by `kind` and `household_id`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Seasonal Time Discipline

The server maintains its own logical clock, and every temporal rule in this document — window membership, lead times, cutoffs, deadlines — evaluates "now" against that clock, never against the host wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this is a fixed startup default and the server never falls back to wall-clock time. An administrator-only test-driving capability advances the logical clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and returns the resulting `current_utc`. Advancing the clock is inert by itself: no automatic state transitions fire when time passes, except through the operational checkpoint described in Section 3.8.

Each program defines a **collection window** bounded by `window_start` and `window_end`, both instants inclusive, and the window must span at least seven days. Around that window sit the season's derived deadlines: gate intake must be recorded inside the window inclusive of both endpoints; chipping runs may be scheduled from `window_start` through fourteen days after `window_end`; a zone's collection day may be moved only with at least forty-eight hours of lead time (measured against the current collection date) or as a makeup-day move after the current date has already passed; a volunteer may cancel a shift no later than twenty-four hours before it starts; and an upheld contamination finding may be appealed within seventy-two hours of adjudication.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every primary resource in the system — programs, drop-off sites, service zones, households, curbside bookings, contamination tickets, chipping runs, mulch events, mulch claims, volunteers, volunteer shifts, and diversion reports — carries a standard set of system-managed fields:

- `id` (string): a unique, server-minted identifier formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the resource was created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the last modification; it is refreshed on every change to the resource, including recomputation of derived fields.
- `version` (integer): a system-managed integer that increments by one on every mutation of the resource, again including recomputation of derived fields, and serving as the basis for optimistic concurrency control.

Two resources are deliberately append-only exceptions to this contract. An `IntakeEvent` and a `NotificationLog` each carry only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent on these two resources, because intake history is corrected by compensating void events rather than by edits, and notifications are immutable records of what was sent.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Four write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and on each of them the version field is a non-optional input. A program update (`PATCH /programs/{id}`, whether editing fields or driving status transitions) must carry `expected_program_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the program; if the program's current version has advanced — for example because cascades such as ticket openings, posture flips, or mulch pool changes have mutated it since the client last read it — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A site update (`PATCH /sites/{id}`, covering capacity, date, and slot edits as well as suspend, reopen, and closure transitions) must likewise carry `expected_site_version`; a mismatch with the site's current version, including advances caused by cascaded stockpile or status changes, is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A zone update (`PATCH /zones/{id}` — a collection-date reschedule, a capacity change, or a status change) must carry `expected_zone_version`; a mismatch, for instance because another writer has already rescheduled the zone (which legitimately moves the date and cascades to bookings) since the client last read it, is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Finally, generating a diversion report (`POST /diversion-reports`) supersedes the program's currently published report and therefore must carry `expected_program_version`; when it no longer matches the program's current version, the client's basis for supersession is stale — another writer may just have created a report that superseded the prior one — and the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Endpoints not named in this paragraph do not take a version field at all.

### 2.7. Validation Order and Error Precedence

Every request is evaluated against an ordered validation pipeline. A failure at any stage short-circuits all later stages, and the relative order below is normative — it determines which error surfaces when several rules are violated at once. First, authentication: the `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` headers must be present and the role must be a declared role, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Second, role authorization: the caller's role must appear in the endpoint's authorized list, honoring per-transition role splits, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN`. Third, entity-backed ownership: a resident acting on a booking, claim, or ticket must match its `household_id`, and a volunteer acting on a shift must match its `volunteer_id` (the `ProgramAdmin` bypasses this check); violations are rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN`. Fourth, existence: every path identifier — all resource paths in this API are flat, addressed by a single `id` — and every foreign-key reference in the request body (program, site, zone, household, booking, event, volunteer, ticket, run, claim, report) must exist, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_FOUND`. Fifth, state legality: the target resource must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, including terminal-state lockdown; violations surface as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Sixth, optimistic concurrency: the `expected_program_version`, `expected_site_version`, or `expected_zone_version` on a guarded write must equal the parent's current version, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

Seventh, compliance rules: on booking creation `prep_attestation` must be true (`ERR_PREP_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`), and on contamination adjudication and appeal resolution the segregation-of-duties rules bind — the adjudicator must differ from the reporter, and the appeal resolver must differ from the original adjudicator — with violations rejected as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Eighth, referential coherence: a booking's zone must equal its household's zone (`ERR_ZONE_MISMATCH`); a contamination ticket must reference exactly one of a booking or a site and that source must belong to the given program (`ERR_TICKET_SOURCE_INVALID`); and a mulch claim's household and event, or a shift's volunteer and site, must belong to the same program (`ERR_VALIDATION`). Ninth, temporal gates, each carrying its own error: intake recorded outside the inclusive collection window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_COLLECTION_WINDOW`); a chipping run outside its allowed scheduling window or a completion attempted before its start (`ERR_RUN_WINDOW_INVALID`); a zone reschedule inside the forty-eight-hour lead (`ERR_NOTIFICATION_LEAD_TIME`); an appeal after its deadline (`ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`); a volunteer cancellation past the cutoff or a shift outcome recorded before the shift ends (`ERR_SHIFT_CUTOFF_PASSED`); a claim created outside the claim window (`ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_INVALID`); and a fulfillment outside the pickup window (`ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_CLOSED`).

Tenth, business constraints: the program's posture must be `NORMAL` for new bookings and intake (`ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`); the program must be `ACTIVE` where a workflow requires it (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`); the zone must be `ACTIVE` for booking creation and reschedule (`ERR_ZONE_SUSPENDED`); the site must be `OPEN` for intake (`ERR_SITE_NOT_OPEN`); the volunteer must be `APPROVED` for shift signup (`ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_APPROVED`); the household must be curbside-eligible (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_BARRED`); no duplicate open ticket may exist for the same booking or the same site (`ERR_DUPLICATE_TICKET`); no duplicate active claim may exist for the same household on the same event (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`); a volunteer's shifts must not overlap (`ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`); chipping runs at one site must respect the overlap-and-buffer rule (`ERR_RUN_OVERLAP`); site closure guards (`ERR_SITE_NOT_CLOSABLE`); program closure guards (`ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_ITEMS`); and the yield-variance review gate (`ERR_YIELD_VARIANCE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`). Eleventh and last, capacity and quota checks always run after every other rule has passed: route tree capacity (`ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), site stockpile capacity (`ERR_SITE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), a capacity edit not below the current stockpile (`ERR_CAPACITY_BELOW_STOCKPILE`), run input not above the stockpile (`ERR_RUN_INPUT_EXCEEDS_STOCKPILE`), claim size within its permitted band (`ERR_CLAIM_CAP_EXCEEDED`), and shift role-slot capacity (`ERR_SHIFT_CAPACITY_FULL`). Requests that are simply malformed — a missing required field, an invalid enum value, an out-of-range number, a window shorter than seven days, a contaminated tonnage exceeding the measured or gross figure, or a cross-program reference — are rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

### 2.8. Measurement Constants, Precision, and Rounding

All quantities in the system are decimal numbers, and every stored or reported result is rounded with the round-half-up method to the number of decimal places its rule prescribes: route tonnage and per-source diversion contributions use three decimal places; stockpile volumes, mulch pool figures, and theoretical chip yield use two decimal places; the yield variance ratio and the diversion rate use four decimal places. Where a rule gates on a ratio, the gate evaluates the unrounded value and only the reported figure is rounded.

Per-tree weight estimates follow the size-class table `EST_TONS`: a `SMALL` tree is estimated at 0.020 tons, a `MEDIUM` tree at 0.035 tons, a `LARGE` tree at 0.050 tons, and a `MIXED` load at 0.030 tons per tree. Gate intake volumes follow a per-size-class cubic-yard conversion table `CLASS_CY`, keyed by the same four size classes, which converts a delivered tree count into gross cubic yards. Chipping yield is expressed per material class through `YIELD_CY_PER_TON`: `MIXED_CONIFER` material yields 2.2 cubic yards per ton, `HARDWOOD` yields 1.8, and `MIXED_LOAD` yields 2.0. The mulch pool reserves sixty percent of everything produced for participating households, with a minimum claimable allocation of half a cubic yard and a default per-household cap of two cubic yards per event. A completed run whose measured output diverges from its theoretical output by more than eight percent — an unrounded ratio strictly greater than 0.08 — requires supervisory confirmation, while a divergence of exactly eight percent is accepted without it. On the enforcement side, a household is barred from curbside service once its strike count reaches two, a program's posture turns restricted once three contamination tickets are simultaneously open, and a volunteer is automatically suspended upon a third no-show. Diversion grades are anchored at inclusive thresholds of 0.9000 for an A and 0.7500 for a B.

### 2.9. Derived State, Cascades, and Cross-Entity Consistency

Many fields in this system are computed rather than stored from input, and consistency is immediate: whenever an event changes the inputs to a derived field, the derived value is recomputed as part of the same operation. Because derived-field recomputation is a mutation, every resource it touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. Several operations deliberately mutate more than one entity, and every hop of those cascades is part of the operation's contract. Adjudicating a contamination ticket can flip a booking to rejected, add a strike to a household, bar the household, auto-cancel the household's remaining confirmed bookings, and write one notification per cancelled booking. Completing a chipping run draws down a site's stockpile, credits the program's mulch pool, promotes waitlisted claims, and notifies each promoted household. Moving a site into closing cancels its future signed-up volunteer shifts and posts a closing notice; rescheduling a zone rewrites the service date of every confirmed booking on it and notifies each affected household. The server may also transition resources on its own when the stated criteria are met — through the operational checkpoint, on event closure, on a third no-show, or when a new report supersedes a published one — without any explicit client request against the affected resource.

A program carries a derived parallel dimension, its `posture`, alongside its lifecycle `status`. The posture is `NORMAL` while fewer than three contamination tickets for the program are in `OPEN` status, and flips to `RESTRICTED` the moment the count of open tickets rises to three or more; while `RESTRICTED`, new curbside bookings and new intake events are rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`. When adjudications and appeal resolutions bring the open-ticket count back below three, the posture returns to `NORMAL`. The posture is recomputed on every ticket creation, adjudication, and appeal resolution.

### 2.10. The Notification Ledger

Customer-facing notices are captured in an append-only `NotificationLog`. Each entry records the `program_id` it belongs to, a `kind` drawn from `PROGRAM_PUBLISHED`, `ZONE_RESCHEDULED`, `BOOKING_AUTO_CANCELLED`, `CLAIM_PROMOTED`, `SITE_CLOSING`, and `MULCH_EVENT_OPENED`, an informational `household_id` or `zone_id` reference where relevant — these references are not enforced as foreign keys — and the rendered notice `message`. Entries are created as side effects of the workflows described in Section 3, never directly. Administrators and site supervisors can list a program's notification entries, filtered by `kind` or `household_id`; as noted in Section 2.3, this listing returns the newest entries first.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Season: Programs, Zones, and Sites

A `ProgramAdmin` stands up a season by creating a **Program** with a display `name`, a `season_label` such as "Winter 2027", and the collection window `window_start` and `window_end`. The window end must fall at least seven days after the window start; a shorter window is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The program may also carry a `target_diversion_rate` between 0 and 1, which defaults to 0.9 when omitted. A new program begins with `status` of `DRAFT`, a `posture` of `NORMAL`, and all of its counters at zero: `open_ticket_count`, `mulch_produced_cy`, `mulch_allocated_participant_cy`, and `mulch_allocated_general_cy` all start empty, and the derived `participant_remaining_cy` and `general_remaining_cy` follow from them.

While the program is in `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, or `ACTIVE`, the administrator builds out its geography. A **ServiceZone** is created with a `zone_code` that must be unique within its program — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` — together with a single `collection_date`, which must lie inside the program window (again `ERR_VALIDATION` otherwise), and a `daily_tree_capacity` expressing the maximum number of trees the route can service that day. A zone is born with `status` of `ACTIVE`, and it continuously exposes four derived figures: `reserved_trees`, the sum of `tree_count` over its `CONFIRMED` bookings; `remaining_capacity`, the `daily_tree_capacity` minus the reserved trees; `projected_tons_provisional`; and `collected_tons_actual`, whose arithmetic Section 3.2 covers. A **DropOffSite** is created with a `program_id`, a `name`, a street `address`, a `capacity_cy` stockpile ceiling in cubic yards, an `open_date` and `close_date` that must both lie within the program window with the open date no later than the close date (`ERR_VALIDATION` otherwise), and an optional `volunteer_slots_per_role` that defaults to 3. A new site begins in `PLANNED` status with an empty `stockpile_cy` of zero.

Publishing moves the program `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and is the season's point of no return for structure: it requires at least one service zone and at least one drop-off site to exist for the program, and it re-checks that the window still spans at least seven days, with failures rejected as `ERR_VALIDATION`. Publication writes a `PROGRAM_PUBLISHED` entry to the notification ledger. Activation moves the program `PUBLISHED → ACTIVE` once the logical clock has reached `window_start`; attempting activation earlier is rejected with `ERR_OUTSIDE_COLLECTION_WINDOW`. Field edits to a program's `name`, `window_start`, `window_end`, or `target_diversion_rate`, and all of its status transitions, go through the same optimistic-concurrency-guarded update operation described in Section 2.6, which increments the program's `version` by one on success.

Sites and zones have operational lifecycles of their own once the season is live. A site moves `PLANNED → OPEN` only while its program is `ACTIVE` — otherwise the transition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and only while the logical clock sits between the site's `open_date` and `close_date` inclusive, failing which `ERR_OUTSIDE_COLLECTION_WINDOW` is raised. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `SiteSupervisor` may then suspend an `OPEN` site (`OPEN → SUSPENDED`), which blocks further gate intake with `ERR_SITE_NOT_OPEN` while leaving existing bookings and shifts untouched, and may reopen a suspended site (`SUSPENDED → OPEN`) provided the clock has not passed the site's `close_date` (`ERR_OUTSIDE_COLLECTION_WINDOW` otherwise). From either `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED`, a site can be moved to `CLOSING` — manually by an administrator or supervisor, or automatically by the operational checkpoint once the clock has passed `close_date` — which posts a `SITE_CLOSING` notification and cancels every `SIGNED_UP` volunteer shift at that site whose `start_at` still lies in the future. The final step, `CLOSING → CLOSED`, is guarded twice over: the site's `stockpile_cy` must be exactly zero, and no contamination ticket referencing the site may remain in `OPEN` or `APPEALED` status; either violation is rejected with `ERR_SITE_NOT_CLOSABLE`. Zone suspension is simpler: an administrator may move a zone `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, which rejects new bookings against it with `ERR_ZONE_SUSPENDED` while leaving existing `CONFIRMED` bookings unaffected, and may later reactivate it (`SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`). All site transitions, including capacity edits through `capacity_cy`, run under the site's optimistic concurrency guard; lowering a site's `capacity_cy` below its current `stockpile_cy` is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_BELOW_STOCKPILE`.

### 3.2. Household Registration and Curbside Booking

Participation begins with **Household** registration, which an `Anonymous` caller performs as self-service or a `ProgramAdmin` performs on a resident's behalf, supplying the `program_id`, the household's curbside `zone_id`, a street `address`, and a `contact_email`. Registration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` once the program is `CLOSED`, and with `ERR_VALIDATION` when the named zone does not belong to the named program. A household starts life with a `strike_count` of 0 and `curbside_eligible` set to true — two computed fields whose behavior Section 3.4 defines — and its record always exposes both.

With a household registered, a `Resident` booking for their own household (or a `ProgramAdmin` booking on its behalf) requests curbside collection by submitting the `household_id`, the `zone_id`, a `tree_count` between 1 and 5, a `size_class` of `SMALL`, `MEDIUM`, `LARGE`, or `MIXED`, and a `prep_attestation`. The attestation is the resident's sworn statement that the trees carry no stands, decorations, tinsel, or flocking; it must be true, and a missing or false attestation is rejected with `ERR_PREP_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`. The booking's `zone_id` must equal the household's own zone, failing which `ERR_ZONE_MISMATCH` is raised. Three state gates then apply before capacity is even considered: the program's posture must be `NORMAL` (`ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` while `RESTRICTED`), the zone must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ZONE_SUSPENDED`), and the household must still be curbside-eligible (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_BARRED` once barred). Finally the route's tree capacity is checked: the booking is admitted only while the zone's `reserved_trees` plus the new `tree_count` stays at or below `daily_tree_capacity` — exact equality is admitted, and a single tree over the limit is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A booking is created directly in `CONFIRMED` status. Its `service_date` is a snapshot of the zone's `collection_date` taken at creation, its `estimated_tons` is computed as the `tree_count` multiplied by the size-class estimate from `EST_TONS`, and its `actual_tons` stays null until a scale weight is recorded at service. Creation immediately adds the `tree_count` to the zone's `reserved_trees` and recomputes the zone's tonnage figures. Those figures are deliberately dual: `projected_tons_provisional` looks forward, summing over the zone's `CONFIRMED` bookings the product of each booking's `tree_count` and its size-class estimate, while `collected_tons_actual` reconciles after the fact, summing over `SERVICED` bookings only and preferring each booking's recorded `actual_tons` scale weight when one is present, falling back to the same per-tree estimate when it is not. Both figures are rounded half-up to three decimal places.

On the collection day, a `SiteSupervisor` or `ProgramAdmin` records the outcome of each `CONFIRMED` booking. Recording an `outcome` of `SERVICED` moves the booking `CONFIRMED → SERVICED` and stores the optional `actual_tons` scale weight if provided, recomputing the zone's `collected_tons_actual`; recording an `outcome` of `MISSED` moves it `CONFIRMED → MISSED` and releases the route capacity by subtracting the booking's `tree_count` from `reserved_trees`. The operational checkpoint applies the same expiry automatically: any booking still `CONFIRMED` whose `service_date` has fallen into the past is moved to `MISSED` with its capacity released. A missed pickup is not the end of the road — the resident (or an administrator) may reschedule by supplying a `new_service_date`. Rescheduling moves `MISSED → CONFIRMED` and is guarded four ways: the `new_service_date` must equal the zone's current `collection_date` (`ERR_BOOKING_DATE_MISMATCH` otherwise); that collection date must still lie in the future (`ERR_OUTSIDE_COLLECTION_WINDOW`); the household must be curbside-eligible (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_BARRED`); and the capacity check runs again before the `tree_count` is re-added to `reserved_trees` (`ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` if the route no longer fits). On success the booking's `service_date` is updated to the new date. Cancellation is available to the resident or an administrator from either `CONFIRMED` or `MISSED`, moving the booking to `CANCELLED`; when the booking was `CONFIRMED`, its `tree_count` comes off `reserved_trees`. `SERVICED`, `CANCELLED`, and `REJECTED_CONTAMINATED` are terminal, and any transition not declared here — from a terminal state above all — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Behind these flows sits the zone reschedule, an administrator operation guarded by `expected_zone_version`. Moving a zone's `collection_date` is permitted in exactly two situations: as a makeup-day move when the current collection date has already passed, or with at least forty-eight hours of lead time, meaning the clock reads no later than forty-eight hours before the current collection date — the boundary is inclusive, so exactly forty-eight hours ahead is still permitted. A move attempted inside that lead is rejected with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_LEAD_TIME`, and in either case the new date must lie within the program window and in the future. When a reschedule lands, every `CONFIRMED` booking on the zone has its `service_date` rewritten to the new date and one `ZONE_RESCHEDULED` notification is created per affected household. The same update can change `daily_tree_capacity`, but never below the zone's current `reserved_trees`; such a reduction is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

### 3.3. Site Operations and Intake Recording

Gate intake is the domain of the `SiteSupervisor` (or an administrator) recording an **IntakeEvent** against an `OPEN` site. Each event names the `site_id`, a `source_type` of `RESIDENT_SELF_HAUL`, `COMMERCIAL_LANDSCAPER`, or `CITY_CREW`, the delivered `tree_count`, the load's `size_class`, an optional `contaminated_cy` for the volume rejected at the gate (defaulting to 0), and an optional `weighed_tons` scale weight, which stays null at sites without a scale. Two gates precede the arithmetic: the site must be `OPEN` (`ERR_SITE_NOT_OPEN` otherwise, which is also how intake fails while a site is `SUSPENDED`), and the program posture must be `NORMAL` (`ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`). The event's `recorded_at` is stamped from the server's logical clock at creation and must fall within the program's collection window inclusive of both ends, failing which the recording is rejected with `ERR_OUTSIDE_COLLECTION_WINDOW`.

The volume arithmetic is fixed. The `gross_volume_cy` is the delivered `tree_count` multiplied by the per-size-class cubic-yard factor from `CLASS_CY`. The `contaminated_cy` may not exceed the `gross_volume_cy`; a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The `net_volume_cy` is the gross volume minus the contaminated volume, and that net figure is what the site absorbs: recording the event adds `net_volume_cy` to the site's `stockpile_cy`. Admission is capacity-checked — the resulting `stockpile_cy` must remain at or below the site's `capacity_cy`, with exact equality admitted and any excess rejected as `ERR_SITE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. For reporting, each event also computes an `estimated_tons` (the `tree_count` times the size-class estimate) and a `report_tons`, which is the `weighed_tons` when one was recorded and the `estimated_tons` otherwise. Intake events are append-only: as Section 2.5 states, they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Corrections happen through a compensating action, never an edit. Voiding a `RECORDED` intake — by a supervisor or administrator, supplying a `reason` that is stored on the event as `void_reason` — moves it to the terminal `VOIDED` status and subtracts its `net_volume_cy` from the site's `stockpile_cy`. The invariant this preserves is exact: recording an intake and later voiding it leaves the stockpile unchanged. A site's `stockpile_cy` is therefore always the sum of `net_volume_cy` over its `RECORDED` intake events minus the sum of `input_cy` over its `COMPLETED` chipping runs, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and chipping is the subject of Section 3.5.

### 3.4. Contamination Enforcement: Tickets, Adjudication, and Appeals

When a crew member, gate supervisor, volunteer, or administrator finds a decorated tree, an attached stand, a flocked tree, a wreath with wire, or any other contaminant, they report it by creating a **ContaminationTicket**. The report names the `program_id`, a `category` of `DECORATIONS`, `STAND_ATTACHED`, `FLOCKED_TREE`, `WREATH_WITH_WIRE`, or `OTHER`, optional free-text `notes`, and exactly one contamination source: a `booking_id` for curbside contamination or a `site_id` for material dumped at a site. Supplying both, neither, or a source that does not belong to the named program is rejected with `ERR_TICKET_SOURCE_INVALID`. A booking-linked ticket requires the booking to be in `CONFIRMED` or `SERVICED` status, and the system refuses to open a second `OPEN` ticket against the same booking or the same site with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TICKET`. The server stamps the ticket with the creator's `reporter_user_id` and `reporter_role` taken from the request headers, and the ticket opens in `OPEN` status with `adjudicated_by`, `adjudicated_at`, `appeal_deadline`, and `resolved_by` all still null. Every ticket creation adds one to the program's `open_ticket_count` and recomputes the posture — this is the flip point that can push a program into `RESTRICTED` under the rule of Section 2.9.

Adjudication is performed by a `ProgramAdmin` or `SiteSupervisor` other than the ticket's reporter; an adjudicator whose `X-User-ID` equals the ticket's `reporter_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and this segregation binds the administrator role too. The adjudicator submits a `verdict`, and the two branches diverge sharply. A `verdict` of `DISMISSED` moves the ticket `OPEN → DISMISSED`, subtracts one from `open_ticket_count` with the posture recomputed, and nothing else happens — no strike, no booking change. A `verdict` of `UPHELD` moves the ticket `OPEN → UPHELD` and fires the system's deepest cascade, in order. The count decrement and posture recompute come first. Then the linked booking, if any: one still `CONFIRMED` transitions to `REJECTED_CONTAMINATED` and releases its route capacity by subtracting its `tree_count` from the zone's `reserved_trees`, while one already `SERVICED` stays `SERVICED`. Next the strike: the booking's household gains one strike on its `strike_count`, which counts the tickets linked to that household's bookings now standing in `UPHELD` or `UPHELD_FINAL`. At the moment the count reaches two, `curbside_eligible` flips to false — exactly two strikes is already barred — and the bar cascades: every other `CONFIRMED` booking belonging to the household is automatically cancelled (`CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, releasing its reserved capacity as any cancellation would) and one `BOOKING_AUTO_CANCELLED` notification is written per cancelled booking. Finally the ticket records `adjudicated_by` and `adjudicated_at` from the acting user and the logical clock, and sets its `appeal_deadline` to seventy-two hours after `adjudicated_at`.

The resident of the ticket's household may appeal an `UPHELD` ticket, moving it `UPHELD → APPEALED`. Appeal is available only on booking-linked tickets — a site ticket cannot be appealed and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and only while the clock has not passed the `appeal_deadline`; the boundary is inclusive, and a later appeal is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Resolution of an appeal is administrator-only, submitted as a `resolution`, and carries the second segregation-of-duties rule: the resolver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the ticket's `adjudicated_by`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A resolution of `UPHELD_FINAL` closes the ticket with no further effect. A resolution of `OVERTURNED` runs the compensation path: the household's `strike_count` drops by one and its `curbside_eligible` is recomputed, but bookings auto-cancelled by the bar cascade are not resurrected — they stay `CANCELLED`. The one restoration is the directly linked booking: if it sits in `REJECTED_CONTAMINATED`, it returns to `CONFIRMED`, its `tree_count` is re-added to the zone's `reserved_trees` unconditionally — a documented exception to the capacity check, deliberately re-reserving even when the route has since filled — and its `service_date` is set to the zone's current `collection_date`. `DISMISSED`, `UPHELD_FINAL`, and `OVERTURNED` are the ticket's terminal states.

### 3.5. Processing: Chipping Runs and Yield Reconciliation

Stockpiled trees become mulch through **ChippingRun** operations scheduled by a `ProgramAdmin`. Scheduling a run names the `program_id`, the `site_id` whose stockpile will be chipped, a `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, and a `material_class` of `MIXED_CONIFER`, `HARDWOOD`, or `MIXED_LOAD`. The schedule must sit inside the processing window — from the program's `window_start` through fourteen days after `window_end` — and the end must be later than the start; either failure is rejected with `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_INVALID`. Runs at the same site must not overlap and must keep a two-hour buffer from one another; a gap of exactly two hours between runs is allowed, and any tighter packing is rejected with `ERR_RUN_OVERLAP`. A run is created in `SCHEDULED` status, and an administrator may cancel it (`SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`) without further effect as long as it has not completed.

Completion is the yield-reconciliation moment, performed by a `ProgramAdmin` or the `HaulerPartner` supplying scale data. The completion payload carries the `input_cy` drawn from the stockpile, the `measured_input_tons` scale weight of the chipped material, the `contaminated_input_tons` portion diverted to disposal (defaulting to 0), the `measured_output_cy` of finished mulch, and an optional `yield_review_confirmed` flag (defaulting to false). The guards run as follows. The clock must have reached `scheduled_start`, else `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_INVALID`. The `input_cy` may not exceed the site's current `stockpile_cy` — exact equality draws the pile to zero and is allowed, and anything more is rejected with `ERR_RUN_INPUT_EXCEEDS_STOCKPILE`. The `contaminated_input_tons` may not exceed the `measured_input_tons`, and the measured input must be greater than zero; violations are rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

The yield itself is computed in a fixed order. The contamination fraction is the `contaminated_input_tons` divided by the `measured_input_tons`. The `theoretical_output_cy` is then the `measured_input_tons` multiplied by the material class's `YIELD_CY_PER_TON` factor, multiplied by one minus the contamination fraction, with the result rounded half-up to two decimal places. The raw variance is the absolute difference between the `measured_output_cy` and the `theoretical_output_cy`, divided by the `theoretical_output_cy`, and is deliberately left unrounded for the gate; the stored `variance_pct` is that raw variance rounded half-up to four decimal places. When the raw variance is strictly greater than 0.08, completion is blocked unless `yield_review_confirmed` is true, in which case the supervisor's override is recorded on the run; without the flag the completion is rejected with `ERR_YIELD_VARIANCE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. A raw variance of exactly 0.08 needs no confirmation.

A successful completion cascades in three hops beyond the stockpile draw of `input_cy` and the storing of `theoretical_output_cy` and `variance_pct`. First, the run's `measured_output_cy` is added to the program's `mulch_produced_cy`, and the tier remainders `participant_remaining_cy` and `general_remaining_cy` are recomputed under the pool rules of Section 3.6. Second, the waitlist promotion sweep described in Section 3.6 runs against the enlarged pool. Third, one `CLAIM_PROMOTED` notification is written for each claim the sweep promotes.

### 3.6. The Mulch Giveback: Events, Claims, and the Waitlist

The season's mulch returns to residents through **MulchEvent** operations created by a `ProgramAdmin`. An event names its `program_id`, a display `name`, a claim window from `claim_open_at` to `claim_close_at` — claims are accepted at both instants, so the close is inclusive and must be later than the open — and a pickup window from `pickup_start` to `pickup_end`, where the pickup start must be no earlier than the claim close and the pickup end must be later than the pickup start and is likewise inclusive. An optional `per_household_cap_cy` caps how much any household may receive from the event and defaults to 2.0 cubic yards. Events whose timing contradicts these orderings are rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new event is `SCHEDULED`; the administrator opens it (`SCHEDULED → OPEN`) only while the program is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and opening writes a `MULCH_EVENT_OPENED` notification. Closing (`OPEN → CLOSED`) happens manually or automatically when the checkpoint finds the clock past `pickup_end`, and it expires every claim still `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` on the event to `EXPIRED`, releasing their allocations back to the tier pools and running the promotion sweep, which may in turn promote waitlisted claims on the program's other still-`OPEN` events.

A `Resident` (claiming for their own household) or a `ProgramAdmin` files a **MulchClaim** with the `event_id`, the `household_id`, and a `requested_cy`. Five gates stand in front of allocation: the event must be `OPEN` (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_OPEN`); the clock must sit inside the claim window inclusive of both ends (`ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_INVALID`); the `requested_cy` must be at least half a cubic yard and no more than the event's `per_household_cap_cy` (`ERR_CLAIM_CAP_EXCEEDED`); the household must not already hold a `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` claim on the same event (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`); and the household and the event must belong to the same program (`ERR_VALIDATION`). Only then does allocation begin, and its evaluation order is fixed: tier derivation first, then the tier-remaining lookup, then the minimum-of cap, and last the threshold test.

The claim's `tier` is derived, never supplied: a household with at least one `SERVICED` booking anywhere in the program is a `PARTICIPANT`; every other household is `GENERAL`. The program's mulch pool splits accordingly. The participant pool is sixty percent of `mulch_produced_cy`, rounded half-up to two decimal places first; the general pool is whatever remains, computed as `mulch_produced_cy` minus that already-rounded participant pool. The participant remainder (`participant_remaining_cy`) is the participant pool minus `mulch_allocated_participant_cy` — the sum of `allocated_cy` over `PARTICIPANT` claims in `CONFIRMED` or `FULFILLED` — and the general remainder (`general_remaining_cy`) is the general pool minus `mulch_allocated_general_cy` over `GENERAL` claims in those same two statuses. The claim's `allocated_cy` is then the smallest of three figures: the `requested_cy`, the event's `per_household_cap_cy`, and the current remainder for the claim's tier. If that allocation reaches at least 0.5 cubic yards, the claim is created `CONFIRMED` — partial allocation below the requested amount is explicitly allowed — and its `allocated_cy` joins the tier allocation counter immediately. Otherwise the allocation is zero and the claim is created `WAITLISTED`.

Claims resolve in three ways. Fulfillment — by a `SiteSupervisor` or `ProgramAdmin` — moves `CONFIRMED → FULFILLED` and is only valid while the clock sits inside the pickup window inclusive of both ends; outside it the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Cancellation — by the resident who owns the claim or an administrator — moves `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` to `CANCELLED`; when the claim was `CONFIRMED`, its `allocated_cy` is released from the tier allocation and a promotion sweep runs. Expiry is the system's counterpart: event closure or the checkpoint past `pickup_end` moves `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` claims to `EXPIRED` with the same release-and-sweep behavior for formerly confirmed claims.

The promotion sweep itself is chronological and never blocks. Its candidates are every `WAITLISTED` claim on the program's `OPEN` events, taken in ascending `created_at` order; because creation timestamps carry millisecond precision, ties cannot occur and `created_at` is the pinned ordering key. Each candidate in turn re-runs the full allocation algorithm against the tier remainder current at that moment; a candidate whose recomputed allocation reaches the half-cubic-yard minimum is promoted to `CONFIRMED` — again, partial allocation is allowed — and earns a `CLAIM_PROMOTED` notification, while a candidate that still falls short simply stays `WAITLISTED` and the sweep continues with the next candidate in line. The sweep fires whenever the pool can grow or allocations can be released: on chipping-run completion, on claim cancellation, on claim expiry, and on the closure of another event.

### 3.7. Volunteers and Shift Staffing

Sites are staffed by **Volunteer** resources, registered by an `Anonymous` applicant or a `ProgramAdmin` with a `program_id`, a `full_name`, and an `email`; registration is refused once the program is `CLOSED`. A volunteer starts in `APPLIED` status and becomes useful only after an administrator approves them (`APPLIED → APPROVED`). Administrators may also suspend a volunteer (`APPROVED → SUSPENDED`) and later reinstate them (`SUSPENDED → APPROVED`); the system itself suspends a volunteer whose reliability collapses, as described below, and auto-suspension cancels every one of the volunteer's future `SIGNED_UP` shifts.

An `APPROVED` volunteer signs up for a **VolunteerShift** — self-service by the `Volunteer` or on their behalf by a `ProgramAdmin` — naming the `volunteer_id`, the `site_id`, a `start_at` and `end_at` inside the program window with the end later than the start, and a `shift_role` of `GREETER`, `TRAFFIC_CONTROL`, or `LOADER`. Signup is rejected with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_APPROVED` while the volunteer holds any other status, and with `ERR_VALIDATION` when the volunteer and the site do not belong to the same program. Two scheduling rules then apply. First, personal overlap: the new shift's half-open interval — covering `start_at` up to but not including `end_at` — must not overlap any of the volunteer's existing `SIGNED_UP` or `COMPLETED` shifts; adjacency is allowed, so a shift starting exactly when another ends is fine, and a true overlap is rejected with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. Second, site staffing capacity: the count of `SIGNED_UP` or `COMPLETED` shifts at the same site on the same calendar date with the same `shift_role` must remain at or below the site's `volunteer_slots_per_role` — exact equality is admitted, and one shift beyond the limit is rejected with `ERR_SHIFT_CAPACITY_FULL`. A shift is created in `SIGNED_UP` status and increments the site-date-role occupancy on arrival.

Shifts end in three ways. Cancellation moves `SIGNED_UP → CANCELLED`, may be performed by the owning volunteer or an administrator, and decrements the site-date-role occupancy; the volunteer faces a twenty-four-hour cutoff — cancellation is allowed only while the clock reads no later than twenty-four hours before `start_at`, the boundary inclusive, with later attempts rejected as `ERR_SHIFT_CUTOFF_PASSED` — while the `ProgramAdmin` is exempt from that cutoff. A `SiteSupervisor` or `ProgramAdmin` marks outcomes once the shift has ended: marking `COMPLETED` (`SIGNED_UP → COMPLETED`) or `NO_SHOW` (`SIGNED_UP → NO_SHOW`) both require the clock to have reached `end_at`, and earlier marking is rejected with `ERR_SHIFT_CUTOFF_PASSED`. The operational checkpoint performs the same sweep, marking every `SIGNED_UP` shift whose `end_at` has passed as `NO_SHOW`. Each `NO_SHOW` adds one to the volunteer's `no_show_count`, and the count reaching three — exactly at the third no-show — automatically suspends the volunteer (`APPROVED → SUSPENDED`) and cancels all of their future `SIGNED_UP` shifts.

### 3.8. Closing the Books: Diversion Reporting and Program Closure

Two read paths expose the season's environmental tally. A live **diversion summary** — available to administrators, supervisors, and the hauler partner — computes the same rollup on the fly without persisting anything, returning `collected_tons`, `diverted_tons`, `disposed_tons`, `diversion_rate`, `grade`, and `below_target`. A persisted **DiversionReport** is created by a `ProgramAdmin` supplying the `program_id` and the `expected_program_version` under the optimistic concurrency rule of Section 2.6; creating a report increments the program's `version` by one, and any existing `PUBLISHED` report for the program is immediately moved to `SUPERSEDED`, its terminal state. A new report starts in `DRAFT`, copies `period_start` and `period_end` from the program's `window_start` and `window_end` at generation time, and snapshots the rollup described next.

The rollup pins its rounding order: every source is rounded first, and only the rounded contributions are summed — summing raw figures first and rounding once at the end is the rejected alternative, because the two orders can produce different totals. Each `SERVICED` booking contributes its `actual_tons` when one was recorded, else its `tree_count` multiplied by the size-class estimate, rounded half-up to three decimal places; each `RECORDED` (non-voided) intake event contributes its `report_tons`, likewise rounded to three decimals. The `collected_tons` is the sum of those rounded booking and intake contributions. The `diverted_tons` sums, over the program's `COMPLETED` chipping runs, each run's `measured_input_tons` minus its `contaminated_input_tons`, rounded half-up to three decimal places per run. The `disposed_tons` is the collected figure minus the diverted figure. The `diversion_rate` is the diverted figure divided by the collected figure, rounded half-up to four decimal places — and is defined as exactly 0.0000 when nothing was collected at all. The `grade` is `NO_DATA` when the collected tonnage is zero; otherwise it is `A` when the rate reaches 0.9000, `B` when it falls short of that but reaches 0.7500, and `C` below that — both thresholds inclusive. The `below_target` flag is true exactly when the `diversion_rate` is strictly less than the program's `target_diversion_rate`.

Publishing moves a report `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and is guarded twice: the clock must have passed the program's `window_end`, and no chipping run may remain in `SCHEDULED` for the program; either failure is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`. Publication stamps `published_at` from the logical clock. Once a published report exists and the rest of the season has wound down, the administrator closes the program (`ACTIVE → CLOSED`). Closure is guarded on five fronts, all reported as `ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_ITEMS`: every drop-off site must be `CLOSED`; at least one diversion report must be `PUBLISHED`; no booking may remain in `CONFIRMED` or `MISSED`; no contamination ticket may remain in `OPEN` or `APPEALED`; and every mulch event must be `CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is the program's terminal state.

The season's housekeeping runs through an administrator-only **operational checkpoint** against a program, which performs five reconciliations in one pass and then increments the program's `version` by one. Every booking still `CONFIRMED` whose `service_date` has fallen into the past is moved to `MISSED` with its reserved capacity released. Every volunteer shift still `SIGNED_UP` whose `end_at` has passed is marked `NO_SHOW`, incrementing the volunteer's `no_show_count` — and a volunteer reaching three no-shows is suspended with their future `SIGNED_UP` shifts cancelled, per Section 3.7. Every `OPEN` site whose `close_date` has passed is moved to `CLOSING`, posting the `SITE_CLOSING` notification and cancelling that site's future `SIGNED_UP` shifts. Every claim still `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` on an event whose `pickup_end` has passed is moved to `EXPIRED`, releasing allocations and running the promotion sweep. Finally, the program's posture is recomputed from the current open-ticket count.
