# Performing Arts Center Season API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A performing arts center lives two lives at once. On some nights it is a **presenter**: it signs a touring company, guarantees that company a fee, absorbs the artistic and financial risk of the evening, and keeps whatever the box office yields. On other nights it is a **landlord**: a dance school, a corporate client or a community choir rents the hall four-wall, and the center bills a facility fee plus the crew and equipment the event consumes. The same grid, the same union locals, the same load-in dock and the same changeover buffer serve both. The two business models nevertheless settle in opposite directions — one pays money out to an artist, the other collects money in from a client — and the season is planned months before either outcome is known.

This document specifies the API that runs that season end to end. It covers hall and inventory registration, engagement booking with hall-occupancy and artist-routing protection, artist fee negotiation as a genuine multi-round exchange between the center and an agency, technical rider review against the physical capability of the hall, union labor calls with call minimums and overtime tiering, financial risk assessment that can escalate or block a booking outright, deposit collection for rental clients, ticketing with allocated price tiers, dynamic single-ticket pricing and season subscription packages, education and community programming funded from a capital reserve, and finally settlement — the expense reconciliation and box-office statement that closes the engagement and sweeps the center's share into that same reserve.

Three product constraints shape everything that follows. First, **money is never approximate**: every monetary field is an integer count of cents, every percentage step has a pinned rounding moment, and the order in which steps are evaluated is part of the specification rather than an implementation detail. Second, **the booking, the risk decision and the box office advance independently**: an engagement can be contractually confirmed while its financial clearance is still escalated, and its ticketing can be closed while its settlement is still open, so the engagement carries three separate status dimensions rather than one. Third, **no one signs both sides of a deal**: the finance controller who accepted an artist's fee may not be the finance controller who finalizes the settlement that pays it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and versioning of resources, arithmetic precision, concurrency control, and the cross-cutting principles that govern cascades and validation order.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the declared role is not authorized for the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The system recognizes exactly eight roles: `CenterAdmin`, `ProgrammingDirector`, `RentalManager`, `ArtistRepresentative`, `RentalClientContact`, `TechnicalDirector`, `BoxOfficeManager`, and `FinanceController`. `CenterAdmin` is the facility operator, owning halls, union rate cards, equipment inventory, center-level reserve movements and the logical clock. `ProgrammingDirector` curates the presented season: artists, presented engagements, fee offers and education programs. `RentalManager` sells and administers hall rentals: rental clients, rental engagements and deposits. `ArtistRepresentative` is the agency side of a presented deal. `RentalClientContact` is the renting organization's contact. `TechnicalDirector` is the production department head, responsible for rider capability review, equipment reservations and union labor calls. `BoxOfficeManager` owns ticketing: price tiers, on-sale, single-ticket sales and subscription packages. `FinanceController` runs risk assessment and settlement.

Two of those roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the header value is itself a resource identifier. When `X-User-Role` is `ArtistRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Artist` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Artist` compare the resource's artist foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `ArtistRepresentative` may act on an engagement only when that engagement's `artist_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may read only its own artist record. When `X-User-Role` is `RentalClientContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RentalClient` row; a `RentalClientContact` may act on an engagement only when that engagement's `rental_client_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may read only its own client record. A mismatch in either direction is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining six roles — `CenterAdmin`, `ProgrammingDirector`, `RentalManager`, `TechnicalDirector`, `BoxOfficeManager` and `FinanceController` — carry an **opaque** identity: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to an entity's `id`. The server does, however, record it wherever an audit trail or a separation-of-duties rule requires one: `opened_by_user_id` and `accepted_by_user_id` on a negotiation round, `reviewed_by_user_id` on a technical rider, `fee_accepted_by_user_id` on an engagement, and `finalized_by_user_id` on a settlement all store the raw `X-User-ID` of the operator who performed the corresponding act. The one place where a recorded opaque identifier becomes a gate rather than an annotation is settlement finalization: the `FinanceController` whose `X-User-ID` appears in the engagement's `fee_accepted_by_user_id` may not finalize that engagement's settlement, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip before the window begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is ordered by the resource's `created_at` timestamp, ascending.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` reports the number of items matching the filter before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true when items remain beyond the current window.

Several list endpoints additionally accept domain filters, which narrow the collection before pagination is applied: a `status` filter on labor-call, equipment-rental-line, negotiation-round, price-tier, ticket-sale and education-program listings; `space_id`, `artist_id`, `rental_client_id` and `lifecycle_status` filters on the engagement listing; and `entry_type` plus `engagement_id` filters on the reserve ledger listing.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Version Counters

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`).

Almost every resource also carries two mutation-tracking fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, initialized to the creation instant and refreshed on every mutation of the resource. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that resource. Both fields are present on `PerformanceSpace`, `Artist`, `RentalClient`, `UnionDepartment`, `EquipmentItem`, `Engagement`, `FeeNegotiation`, `TechRider`, `EquipmentRentalLine`, `LaborCall`, `PriceTier`, `TicketSale`, `SubscriptionPackage`, `Settlement` and `EducationProgram`.

The single exception is the reserve ledger. A `ReserveLedgerEntry` is append-only and immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field at all.

Version counters are not incidental. Because the `Engagement` version is the anchor of this system's concurrency control (§2.6), the specification is explicit about which operations advance it and which do not. Confirming, cancelling, recording a deposit, assessing risk, going on sale, opening a negotiation round, opening a settlement and finalizing a settlement each bump `Engagement.version` by exactly 1. Adding a price tier, selling a single ticket, selling a subscription order and clearing a technical rider do **not** bump `Engagement.version`, even though each of them writes derived engagement fields. When adding the first price tier moves the engagement's ticketing dimension out of `TICKETING_UNSET`, that dimension change carries no version increment of its own; only the triggering operation's own declared increment applies, so confirmation bumps `Engagement.version` once in total even though it advances two dimensions, and tier creation bumps it not at all.

### 2.5. Monetary Values, Rounding and Arithmetic Order

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of cents, stored and returned as a JSON integer, never as a string and never as a decimal fraction. Seat counts, crew sizes, quantities, kilograms and amperages are likewise integers. Only four kinds of value are fractional: hour counts (`scheduled_hours`, `actual_hours`, `call_minimum_hours`), rate multipliers (`overtime_multiplier`, `doubletime_multiplier`, `dynamic_multiplier_applied`), and the share and sell-through fractions (`artist_share_pct`, `offered_artist_share_pct`, `counter_artist_share_pct`, `accepted_share_pct`, `historical_sell_through_pct`, `sell_through_pct_at_sale`).

Where a calculation converts a fraction into cents, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Where a formula in §3 pins the moment of rounding — per rate component before multiplying by crew size, per price tier before summing, per unit price before multiplying by quantity — that moment is normative. Summing first and rounding once at the end produces a different answer and is not an acceptable substitute. Two calculations use rounding modes other than half-up, and both say so where they are described: the deposit requirement rounds a subtotal **up** to the next whole multiple of 100 cents, and the subscription revenue allocation truncates each tier's share **downward** to a whole cent before a single remainder assignment restores the total.

Fractional intermediate values that are themselves persisted carry a declared precision: `scheduled_hours` is stored to 2 decimal places, `sell_through_pct_at_sale` to 4 decimal places, `historical_sell_through_pct` accepts at most 4 decimal places on input, and the offered, countered and accepted artist share fractions accept at most 2 decimal places on input.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Engagement Writes

Two engagement operations rewrite decisions the client believes it has already observed, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent `Engagement`.

A `POST /engagements/{id}/fee-negotiations` request must carry `expected_engagement_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the client's last observed `Engagement.version`. If that value does not equal the engagement's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Opening a new round legitimately supersedes any non-terminal earlier round, so without this guard a client working from a stale view would silently discard a round it never saw.

A `POST /engagements/{id}/assess-risk` request must likewise carry `expected_engagement_version` as a required field, and a mismatch against the engagement's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-running the assessment overwrites the previous clearance decision, so a client holding a stale version would replace a clearance derived from inputs it never observed.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field. Confirming, cancelling, recording a deposit, going on sale, selling tickets, clearing riders, reporting hours, reconciling expenses and finalizing settlements all take no `expected_engagement_version` and perform no concurrency comparison.

### 2.7. Three Independent Engagement Dimensions

An engagement does not have "a status". It has three, each with its own state set, its own triggers and its own terminal states, and they advance independently of one another.

`lifecycle_status` is the booking dimension. It starts at `DRAFT` and moves through `CONFIRMED` and `PERFORMED` to the terminal `SETTLED`, or leaves early for the terminal `CANCELLED`.

`financial_clearance` is the risk dimension. It starts at `UNASSESSED` and is written only by the risk assessment and by one cascade out of fee acceptance. Its states are `UNASSESSED`, `CLEARED`, `ESCALATED`, `ESCALATED_APPROVED` and `BLOCKED`, and none of them is terminal — a re-assessment may always move the dimension again, including from `BLOCKED` back to `CLEARED`.

`ticketing_status` is the box-office dimension. It starts at `TICKETING_UNSET`, reaches `TIERS_DRAFT` when tier drafting begins or the booking is confirmed, reaches `ON_SALE` when the box office opens, and ends in the terminal `CLOSED`.

Because the dimensions are independent, combinations that look contradictory are legal and must be supported. A `CONFIRMED` engagement may hold `ESCALATED` clearance, because fee acceptance can push a projected margin negative after confirmation-time clearance was granted. An engagement whose ticketing dimension is `CLOSED` may still be sitting at `PERFORMED` with an open settlement. And an engagement cancelled before any tier existed still parks its ticketing dimension in `CLOSED`, so that the cancel cascade never leaves a live ticketing dimension behind.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this system touch more than one resource. Where they do, the secondary effects are part of the contract, not an optimization, and the order in which they are applied is normative wherever one effect feeds another.

Three recompute chains recur. Changing the set of labor calls that count toward an estimate re-derives `Engagement.labor_estimate_total_cents` as the sum of `estimated_cost_cents` over the engagement's calls whose `status` is `ESTIMATED`, `WORKED` or `RECONCILED`. Changing the set of reserved equipment lines re-derives `TechRider.additional_equipment_cost_cents` as the sum of `line_cost_cents` over that rider's `RESERVED` lines, then re-derives `Engagement.additional_equipment_cost_cents` the same way across the engagement's riders, then re-derives `EquipmentItem.reserved_qty` as the sum of `qty` over all `RESERVED` lines pointing at that item. And because both the labor estimate and the equipment cost are deposit inputs, a change to either re-derives `Engagement.deposit_required_cents` for a rental engagement.

That last chain carries an idempotence requirement. When a single operation changes both the labor estimate and the equipment cost — as engagement cancellation does — the deposit requirement is recomputed **exactly once**, after both inputs have settled, not once per input.

Cascades never run backwards into their own inputs. The reserve contribution computed at settlement is derived from the center's net position; it is not an expense, and it is never subtracted back out of that net position.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and the Order of Validation

When a path contains more than one identifier, the server resolves them strictly left to right and fails on the first one that does not exist. A request naming a labor call under an engagement that does not exist yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` before the labor call is ever looked up. Body-referenced identifiers are resolved after path identifiers: `space_id` yields `ERR_SPACE_NOT_FOUND`, `artist_id` and `rental_client_id` yield `ERR_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`, `union_department_id` yields `ERR_DEPARTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `equipment_item_id` yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, and an element of `included_engagement_ids` that names nothing yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Path-referenced resources of other kinds yield `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECH_RIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABOR_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICE_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate, all with HTTP 404.

More generally, when several checks could all reject the same request, only the highest-priority one is reported. The evaluation order is fixed and applies system-wide:

1. **Headers present and role recognized** — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Acting role authorized for this endpoint** — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Entity-backed ownership** — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403. Applies to exactly six operations: submitting a technical rider for an engagement (`POST /engagements/{id}/tech-riders`), countering a negotiation round (`POST /fee-negotiations/{id}/counter`), accepting a negotiation round (`POST /fee-negotiations/{id}/accept`), recording a deposit (`POST /engagements/{id}/record-deposit`), reading an artist (`GET /artists/{id}`) and reading a rental client (`GET /rental-clients/{id}`).
4. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. Applies to exactly one operation: `POST /settlements/{id}/finalize`.
5. **Path-referenced resources exist**, left to right — the 404 family above. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
6. **Body-referenced resources exist** — the 404 family above. Applies to `POST /engagements`, `POST /engagements/{id}/labor-calls`, `POST /tech-riders/{id}/equipment-lines`, `POST /subscription-packages` and `POST /reserve-ledger-entries`.
7. **Request body satisfies the input schema** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Required fields present, enum values in range, numbers inside their declared bounds, `call_end_utc` strictly after `call_start_utc`, and the engagement's four instants in non-decreasing order: `load_in_start_utc` at or before `performance_start_utc`, which is at or before `performance_end_utc`, which is at or before `load_out_end_utc`. Applies to every write endpoint.
8. **Target resource is in a state that admits the transition** — HTTP 409, reported as `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_CONFIRMED`, `ERR_PERFORMANCE_NOT_COMPLETE`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_RIDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_LABOR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TICKETING_NOT_ON_SALE` or `ERR_TIER_CLOSED` depending on which dimension blocked the request. Applies to every state-transition endpoint. This step includes the rule that a rider must be `GAP_IDENTIFIED` before it will accept equipment lines, and the rule that confirmation requires some technical rider of the engagement to be `CLEARED`.
9. **Version pinning** — `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION`, 412, on the two operations named in §2.6.
10. **Engagement-type agreement** — `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to `POST /engagements`, `POST /engagements/{id}/fee-negotiations`, `POST /engagements/{id}/record-deposit`, `POST /engagements/{id}/education-programs` and `POST /subscription-packages`.
11. **Hall is online** — `ERR_SPACE_OFFLINE`, 422. Applies to `POST /engagements` and `POST /engagements/{id}/confirm`.
12. **Hall occupancy does not collide** — `ERR_SPACE_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`, 409, on `POST /engagements`.
13. **Artist routing is respected** — `ERR_ROUTING_WINDOW_VIOLATION`, 422, on `POST /engagements`.
14. **Labor call sits inside the engagement window** — `ERR_CALL_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT_WINDOW`, 422, on `POST /engagements/{id}/labor-calls`.
15. **Crew roster is not oversubscribed** — `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409, on `POST /engagements/{id}/labor-calls`.
16. **Seat allocations fit the hall and tier names are unique** — `ERR_SEAT_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY`, 422. Applies to both `POST /engagements/{id}/price-tiers` and `POST /engagements/{id}/on-sale`.
17. **Equipment stock is available** — `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STOCK_UNAVAILABLE`, 409, on `POST /tech-riders/{id}/equipment-lines`.
18. **Seats are available** — `ERR_TIER_SOLD_OUT`, 409. Applies to both `POST /price-tiers/{id}/ticket-sales` and `POST /subscription-packages/{id}/sell`.
19. **Negotiation round and revision limits** — `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`, 422. Applies to both `POST /engagements/{id}/fee-negotiations` and `POST /fee-negotiations/{id}/revise-offer`.
20. **Singleton children** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_TECH_RIDER` on `POST /engagements/{id}/tech-riders` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_SETTLEMENT` on `POST /engagements/{id}/settlements`, both 409.
21. **Guarantee floor** — `ERR_GUARANTEE_BELOW_FLOOR`, 422. Applies to both `POST /engagements/{id}/fee-negotiations` and `POST /fee-negotiations/{id}/revise-offer`.
22. **Capability gaps are covered** — `ERR_GAP_NOT_COVERED`, 409, on `POST /tech-riders/{id}/clear`.
23. **Financial clearance permits the act** — 422, reported as `ERR_CLEARANCE_REQUIRED` or `ERR_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED`. Applies to `POST /engagements/{id}/confirm` and `POST /fee-negotiations/{id}/accept`.
24. **Reserve floor is respected** — `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`, 402, on `POST /engagements/{id}/education-programs`.
25. **Rental deposit is covered** — `ERR_DEPOSIT_SHORTFALL`, 402, on `POST /engagements/{id}/confirm`.

One ordering inside step 8 deserves emphasis because two codes compete for the same request. When a single ticket sale is attempted, the tier-scoped check runs before the engagement-scoped check. A sale against a `CLOSED` price tier therefore reports `ERR_TIER_CLOSED` even when the engagement's `ticketing_status` is also `CLOSED`; `ERR_TICKETING_NOT_ON_SALE` is reserved for the case where the engagement's ticketing dimension is not `ON_SALE` while the target tier is still `ACTIVE`.

### 2.10. The Logical Season Clock

Time-dependent rules in this system read a **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. Before any client repositions it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance.

A `CenterAdmin` repositions the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity — it changes no status, recomputes no money and bumps no version — but it changes the answer given by every rule that reads it: the lead-time term of the risk score, the retained-percentage band of the cancellation fee, and the eligibility of an engagement to be treated as performed.

### 2.11. The Capital and Equipment Reserve

The center funds capital replacement and community programming from a single append-only ledger. Each `ReserveLedgerEntry` records an `entry_type` drawn from `OPENING_BALANCE`, `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`, `SETTLEMENT_CONTRIBUTION`, `EDUCATION_SUBSIDY_DRAW` and `EDUCATION_SUBSIDY_REFUND`; a signed `amount_cents` where a positive value credits the reserve and a negative value debits it; a free-text `memo` for audit; an optional `engagement_id` naming the engagement that caused the movement, left null for center-level adjustments; and a computed `balance_after_cents`.

`balance_after_cents` is the running total of `amount_cents` across every entry in the ledger, accumulated in `created_at` ascending order. It is therefore the reserve balance as of that entry, and the balance of the reserve as a whole is the `balance_after_cents` of the entry with the greatest `created_at`, or zero when the ledger is empty. A `CenterAdmin` may append opening balances and manual adjustments directly; the other three entry types are appended only as side effects of settlement finalization, education-program funding and engagement cancellation.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Season Infrastructure and the Act of Booking

Before a season can be programmed, the `CenterAdmin` describes the physical plant. A hall is registered with a `name` such as "Grand Hall", a fixed sellable `seat_capacity` of at least one seat, and two fee rates: `base_facility_fee_cents`, charged per performance day before discounts, and `load_in_day_fee_cents`, charged per load-in or load-out day before discounts. Both rates may be zero but never negative. The hall also declares its physical envelope — `rigging_capacity_kg` for the maximum total load the grid supports, `house_power_amps` for available company-switch amperage, and `dmx_universes` for installed lighting-control universes, each a non-negative integer — plus a `changeover_buffer_minutes` value counting the mandatory changeover minutes that follow one production's `load_out_end_utc` before the hall may be re-occupied. A hall's `status` may be supplied at registration and defaults to `ACTIVE`; the only other value is `OFFLINE`, and an `OFFLINE` hall refuses new bookings.

Union rate cards are registered the same way, one per department. The `department` value is one of `STAGEHANDS`, `ELECTRICS`, `AUDIO` or `WARDROBE`, and each department may be registered only once. A card declares a `roster_size` of at least one crew member — the total the local can supply at any instant — and a `straight_hourly_rate_cents` of at least one cent per crew member per straight-time hour. Four further fields have defaults, so a card may be registered without them: `overtime_multiplier` defaults to 1.5, `doubletime_multiplier` defaults to 2.0, `call_minimum_hours` defaults to 4 hours of guaranteed billable time per crew member per call, and `meal_penalty_cents` defaults to 3,500 cents — a flat $35 per crew member when a long call runs without a break.

Rental inventory rounds out the infrastructure. An `EquipmentItem` belongs to one of four families, given as its `category`: `POWER_DISTRO`, `DMX_NODE`, `RIGGING_HARDWARE` or `AUDIO_PACKAGE`. It carries a `model_name` label, an `on_hand_qty` count of units the center owns, a `daily_rate_cents` rental rate per unit per rental day, and a `replacement_value_cents` figure used by the center's equipment-reserve policy for insurance planning. A `reserve_funded` flag, defaulting to false, marks items whose rental income the center earmarks for the capital reserve; like `replacement_value_cents` it is descriptive metadata and is read by none of the money calculations in this specification. The one live counter on the item is `reserved_qty`, which starts at zero and always equals the sum of `qty` across every equipment rental line in `RESERVED` state that points at this item.

The two counterparties are registered separately. A `ProgrammingDirector` registers a touring artist with a `legal_name`, the `agency_name` of the booking agency of record, a `guarantee_floor_cents` value below which the agency will not entertain an offer, a `historical_sell_through_pct` expressed as a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive with at most four decimal places, a routing availability window bounded by `routing_window_start_utc` and `routing_window_end_utc`, and a `min_days_between_engagements` count of whole days the routing requires between two engagements of that artist. The artist also carries `lifetime_guarantee_paid_cents`, a running total that begins at zero and grows only when a settlement is finalized. A `RentalManager` registers a rental client with an `org_name`, a billing `contact_email`, a `credit_rating` of `A`, `B` or `C`, and an `is_nonprofit` boolean marking eligibility for the nonprofit facility-fee discount. A client's `outstanding_balance_cents` may be seeded at registration and otherwise begins at zero; thereafter it moves only through finalized settlements and forfeited deposits.

Booking itself is a single act available to both a `ProgrammingDirector` and a `RentalManager`. The booking declares an `engagement_type` of either `PRESENTED`, where the center takes the artistic and financial risk, or `RENTAL`, where a client rents the hall; a `space_id` naming the hall; a public `title`; the four occupancy instants `load_in_start_utc`, `performance_start_utc`, `performance_end_utc` and `load_out_end_utc`; a `performance_day_count` of at least one billable performance day; and a `load_in_day_count` of zero or more billable load-in and load-out days. The counterparty field is exclusive rather than optional: `artist_id` is required when the type is `PRESENTED` and must be omitted otherwise, and `rental_client_id` is required when the type is `RENTAL` and must be omitted otherwise. Supplying the wrong one, or both, is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`. A booking on a hall whose `status` is `OFFLINE` is rejected with `ERR_SPACE_OFFLINE`.

Two temporal guards protect the booking. The first is hall occupancy. An engagement occupies its hall over the half-open interval that begins at `load_in_start_utc` and ends at `load_out_end_utc` advanced by the hall's `changeover_buffer_minutes`; that buffer-extended interval may not overlap the same interval of any other engagement on the same hall whose `lifecycle_status` is `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED`, `PERFORMED` or `SETTLED`. Only a `CANCELLED` engagement releases its slot. A collision is rejected with `ERR_SPACE_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`. The second guard applies to presented engagements only: the closed performance interval running from `performance_start_utc` to `performance_end_utc` must lie inside the artist's routing window, and it must stand at least `min_days_between_engagements` whole days away from every other non-`CANCELLED` engagement of that same artist. Either failure is rejected with `ERR_ROUTING_WINDOW_VIOLATION`.

A new engagement is born in `DRAFT` on the booking dimension, `UNASSESSED` on the clearance dimension and `TICKETING_UNSET` on the ticketing dimension. Every derived money field starts at zero: `negotiated_fee_cents`, `artist_share_pct`, `labor_estimate_total_cents`, `additional_equipment_cost_cents`, `deposit_paid_cents`, `risk_score`, `projected_gross_cents`, `projected_margin_cents`, `realized_gross_cents`, `realized_margin_cents`, `negotiation_round_count` and `cancellation_fee_cents`. `risk_tier` starts at `LOW` and `fee_accepted_by_user_id` starts null.

**The facility fee.** For a rental engagement, and only at the moment the engagement is created, the center computes `facility_fee_cents`. It first forms an undiscounted base: the hall's `base_facility_fee_cents` multiplied by the engagement's `performance_day_count`, plus the hall's `load_in_day_fee_cents` multiplied by the engagement's `load_in_day_count`. Two discounts may then apply. The multi-day discount is 15 percent when `performance_day_count` is 5 or more, 10 percent when it is 3 or 4, and nothing otherwise. The nonprofit discount is a further 20 percent when the client's `is_nonprofit` flag is true, and nothing when it is false. The two are added together and then capped at a combined maximum of 30 percent — a five-day nonprofit booking is discounted 30 percent, not 35. The base is multiplied by one minus that combined percentage and the result is rounded half up to a whole cent; this is the only rounding step in the whole calculation. Finally a capacity surcharge is added: the hall's `seat_capacity` less 1,200 free seats, floored at zero so that small halls pay nothing, multiplied by 25 cents per surcharged seat. That surcharge is an exact integer and is never rounded. The sum of the rounded discounted base and the surcharge is `facility_fee_cents`. The fee depends only on the engagement's own day counts, its hall and its client; sibling engagements never participate. A presented engagement has `facility_fee_cents` of 0 — the center does not rent the hall to itself — and no event other than creation ever recomputes this figure.

**The deposit requirement.** Also for rental engagements only, `deposit_required_cents` is derived at creation and re-derived whenever `labor_estimate_total_cents` or `additional_equipment_cost_cents` changes. The client's `credit_rating` selects a deposit percentage: 25 percent for grade `A`, 50 percent for grade `B` and the full 100 percent for grade `C`. That percentage is applied to a core amount equal to `facility_fee_cents` plus `labor_estimate_total_cents`, and the product is rounded half up to a whole cent. Then, and only when the client's `outstanding_balance_cents` is strictly greater than zero — a balance of exactly zero adds nothing — the engagement's `additional_equipment_cost_cents` is added on top of that rounded amount in full, undiscounted by the percentage. The running total is next rounded **up** to the next whole multiple of 100 cents, and finally raised to a floor of 50,000 cents if it falls below $500. A presented engagement has `deposit_required_cents` of 0.

### 3.2. Negotiating the Artist Guarantee

A presented deal is closed by negotiation, not by fiat. A `ProgrammingDirector` opens a round against a `DRAFT` presented engagement by naming an `offered_fee_cents` guarantee and an `offered_artist_share_pct` overage split, the latter a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive with at most two decimal places, together with the `expected_engagement_version` pin described in §2.6. Opening a round against a rental engagement is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`; opening one against an engagement whose booking dimension has left `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`; and an offer beneath the artist's `guarantee_floor_cents` is refused with `ERR_GUARANTEE_BELOW_FLOOR`.

Rounds are counted. The engagement's `negotiation_round_count` increments as each round is created, and the new round's `round_index` is assigned to equal that count after the increment — so the first round is round 1. No engagement may open more than three rounds in total; the fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. Opening a round supersedes the past: every earlier round of the same engagement whose `status` is `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` is moved to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state, and each superseded round has its own `version` incremented by 1. The new round is created in `OFFERED` with `revision_count` at zero, `counter_fee_cents` and `counter_artist_share_pct` null, `accepted_fee_cents` and `accepted_share_pct` null, `accepted_by_user_id` null, and `opened_by_user_id` recording the `X-User-ID` of the director who opened it. The engagement's own `version` is incremented by 1.

Inside a round the two sides may cycle. An `ArtistRepresentative` whose `X-User-ID` matches the engagement's `artist_id` may counter a standing offer, supplying `counter_fee_cents` and `counter_artist_share_pct`; this drives the round `OFFERED → COUNTERED` and increments the round's `version` by 1. Countering is permitted only while the round is `OFFERED` — otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN` — and only while the engagement's booking dimension is still `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. The director may then answer a counter by revising the offer, which drives the round `COUNTERED → OFFERED`, overwrites `offered_fee_cents` and `offered_artist_share_pct` with the new terms, increments `revision_count` by 1, and resets both counter fields back to null; the round's `version` increments by 1. This is the cycle `OFFERED → COUNTERED → OFFERED` inside a single round, and it is bounded: a revision is permitted only while `revision_count` is strictly less than 2, so at most two revisions may occur within one round, and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. A revised offer is subject to the same guarantee floor as the original and is refused with `ERR_GUARANTEE_BELOW_FLOOR` when it falls below it. Revising a round that is not `COUNTERED` yields `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`.

Acceptance ends the round, and which side may accept depends on where the round stands. A round sitting at `OFFERED` is accepted by the `ArtistRepresentative` — the agency taking the center's standing offer — and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the engagement's `artist_id`. A round sitting at `COUNTERED` is accepted by the `ProgrammingDirector` — the center taking the agency's counter. In both cases the engagement's clearance dimension must not be `BLOCKED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED`, and no other round of the same engagement may already be `ACCEPTED`. Accepting a round in any other state yields `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`.

The two acceptance branches freeze different numbers, and this asymmetry is the heart of the negotiation record. Accepting from `OFFERED` sets `accepted_fee_cents` to the round's `offered_fee_cents` and `accepted_share_pct` to its `offered_artist_share_pct`. Accepting from `COUNTERED` sets `accepted_fee_cents` to the round's `counter_fee_cents` and `accepted_share_pct` to its `counter_artist_share_pct`. Both branches otherwise behave identically: `accepted_by_user_id` records the accepting `X-User-ID`, the round moves to the terminal `ACCEPTED` state, the engagement's `negotiated_fee_cents` and `artist_share_pct` are written from the frozen figures, the engagement's `fee_accepted_by_user_id` is set to the same `X-User-ID` — the value that will later bar that user from finalizing this engagement's settlement — and the engagement's `version` is incremented by 1.

Acceptance also has a financial consequence that reaches into the clearance dimension. Because `negotiated_fee_cents` has changed, the engagement's `projected_margin_cents` is immediately recomputed by the forward-looking margin calculation of §3.5. If that recomputed margin is negative while the clearance dimension currently reads `CLEARED`, the clearance flips `CLEARED → ESCALATED` and `risk_tier` is set to `HIGH`. This is the one clearance movement not driven by an explicit assessment, and it is deliberately independent of the booking dimension: a `CONFIRMED` engagement may end up holding `ESCALATED` clearance because a fee was accepted after confirmation was granted. When clearance is anything other than `CLEARED` at the moment of acceptance, no such flip occurs and the clearance dimension is left where it stands.

### 3.3. Technical Rider Review and Additional Equipment

Whichever side of the deal is bringing the production also files its technical rider: an `ArtistRepresentative` for a presented engagement, a `RentalClientContact` for a rental, and in both cases the acting `X-User-ID` must own the engagement's counterparty or the submission is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The rider declares `submitted_rigging_kg` for the total load the production hangs from the grid, `requested_power_amps` for the company-switch amperage it needs, `requested_dmx_universes` for the lighting-control universes it needs — each a non-negative integer — and a `requires_orchestra_pit` boolean set when the production needs the pit lift lowered. That last flag is recorded for production planning; the gap set written by the capability assessment is drawn exclusively from `RIGGING_OVER_CAPACITY`, `RIGGING_NEAR_LIMIT`, `POWER_SHORTFALL` and `DMX_SHORTFALL`, so a pit request never contributes a gap of its own. An engagement admits at most one rider; a second submission is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TECH_RIDER`. A new rider is created in `SUBMITTED` with an empty `capability_gaps` array, `additional_equipment_cost_cents` at zero and `reviewed_by_user_id` null.

Review is the `TechnicalDirector`'s act, and it is available only while the rider reads `SUBMITTED`; reviewing an already-reviewed rider is refused with `ERR_RIDER_INVALID_STATE`. The assessment compares the rider against the hall of its engagement and forks three ways.

The first branch is a hard stop. If `submitted_rigging_kg` exceeds the hall's `rigging_capacity_kg`, the rider goes `SUBMITTED → REJECTED_INFEASIBLE` with `capability_gaps` set to a single-element array holding `RIGGING_OVER_CAPACITY`. That state is terminal — an infeasible rider can never be cleared, and because confirmation requires a cleared rider, the engagement can never be confirmed on it.

Otherwise the assessment accumulates gap flags in a fixed emission order. First, a near-limit threshold in whole kilograms is derived as 90 percent of the hall's `rigging_capacity_kg`, rounded half up; if `submitted_rigging_kg` is greater than or equal to that threshold, `RIGGING_NEAR_LIMIT` is appended. Second, if `requested_power_amps` is strictly greater than the hall's `house_power_amps`, `POWER_SHORTFALL` is appended. Third, if `requested_dmx_universes` is strictly greater than the hall's `dmx_universes`, `DMX_SHORTFALL` is appended. The resulting array always lists its members in that order — near-limit, then power, then DMX — regardless of which combination fired.

If the accumulated array is empty the rider goes `SUBMITTED → CAPABLE` with `capability_gaps` set to the empty array. If it holds at least one flag the rider goes `SUBMITTED → GAP_IDENTIFIED` with the accumulated array written as-is. All three branches record `reviewed_by_user_id` from the reviewing `X-User-ID` and increment the rider's own `version` by 1.

A rider carrying gaps can buy its way out of them. Reserving an additional-equipment line is a `TechnicalDirector` act available **only** while the rider reads `GAP_IDENTIFIED`; every other rider state, `CAPABLE` and `CLEARED` included, is refused with `ERR_RIDER_INVALID_STATE`, which is precisely why the capable branch of the review fork can never carry equipment cost. A line belongs to the rider it is created under, recorded as its `tech_rider_id`, and names an `equipment_item_id`, a `covers_gap` value drawn from `RIGGING_NEAR_LIMIT`, `POWER_SHORTFALL`, `DMX_SHORTFALL` or `NONE` for a comfort item that covers nothing, a `qty` of at least one unit and a `rental_days` count of at least one billable day. The line is created in `RESERVED` status with `line_cost_cents` computed as the item's `daily_rate_cents` multiplied by `qty` multiplied by `rental_days`. Reserving is inventory-constrained: the requested `qty` may not exceed the item's `on_hand_qty` less its current `reserved_qty`, and an over-reservation is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STOCK_UNAVAILABLE`. After a line is created the item's `reserved_qty` is re-derived across all `RESERVED` lines. Notably, neither the rider's `additional_equipment_cost_cents` nor the engagement's changes at this point; both are written only when the rider is cleared.

Clearing is the join point of the review fork, and the two branches arrive at `CLEARED` carrying very different consequences. Clearing from `CAPABLE` moves the rider `CAPABLE → CLEARED`, sets the rider's `additional_equipment_cost_cents` to 0, re-derives the engagement's `additional_equipment_cost_cents` (which this branch leaves unchanged), leaves `deposit_required_cents` untouched, and increments the rider's `version` by 1. Clearing from `GAP_IDENTIFIED` moves the rider `GAP_IDENTIFIED → CLEARED` but only after a coverage test: every element of `capability_gaps` other than `RIGGING_NEAR_LIMIT` must be matched by at least one `RESERVED` line of this rider whose `covers_gap` equals that element. A near-limit flag is advisory and needs no line; an uncovered power or DMX shortfall is refused with `ERR_GAP_NOT_COVERED`. On success the rider's `additional_equipment_cost_cents` is set to the sum of `line_cost_cents` over its `RESERVED` lines; because that figure changed, the engagement's `additional_equipment_cost_cents` is re-derived; and because that engagement figure changed, `deposit_required_cents` is re-derived for a rental engagement by the deposit rule of §3.1. The rider's `version` increments by 1. Attempting to clear a rider that is neither `CAPABLE` nor `GAP_IDENTIFIED` is refused with `ERR_RIDER_INVALID_STATE`. In neither branch does clearing a rider increment `Engagement.version`.

### 3.4. Union Labor Calls, Call Minimums and Overtime

Crew is called by the `TechnicalDirector`, one call per department per window. A call names a `union_department_id` supplying the crew, a `crew_size` of at least one member, and the window bounded by `call_start_utc` and `call_end_utc`, where the end must fall strictly after the start. The call window must sit inside the engagement's occupancy: the half-open interval from `call_start_utc` to `call_end_utc` must be contained in the half-open interval from the engagement's `load_in_start_utc` to its `load_out_end_utc`, and a call that pokes outside is refused with `ERR_CALL_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT_WINDOW`.

The local can only supply so many bodies at once. For the call's department, the system sums `crew_size` across every call whose `status` is `ESTIMATED` or `WORKED` and whose half-open window overlaps the new one, adds the new `crew_size`, and requires the total to be no greater than the department's `roster_size`. Touching endpoints do not overlap — a call ending at 18:00 and another beginning at 18:00 make no demand on each other — and cancelled or reconciled calls do not count. Exceeding the roster is refused with `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the check is department-scoped and not engagement-scoped, an overlapping call on a *different* engagement in a different hall still consumes the same local's roster.

On creation the call's `scheduled_hours` is derived as the elapsed time from `call_start_utc` to `call_end_utc` expressed in hours to two decimal places, its `status` is set to `ESTIMATED`, `actual_hours` and `meal_break_taken` are null, and `actual_cost_cents` is 0. The `estimated_cost_cents` figure is then written by the labor cost rule below in its estimate form. Because the engagement's labor estimate has changed, `Engagement.labor_estimate_total_cents` is re-derived as the sum of `estimated_cost_cents` over the engagement's calls whose `status` is `ESTIMATED`, `WORKED` or `RECONCILED`, and because that figure is a deposit input, `deposit_required_cents` is re-derived for a rental engagement.

**How a call is priced.** Both the estimate and the actual reckoning share one tiering procedure over a single per-crew-member hour count. That hour count is first raised to the department's `call_minimum_hours` if it falls below it, so a two-hour call against a card with the default four-hour minimum bills four hours per crew member. The billable hours are then split into three bands: straight time is the billable hours capped at 8 hours; overtime is the billable hours in excess of 8 hours, itself capped at the 4-hour span between the 8-hour overtime threshold and the 12-hour doubletime threshold; and doubletime is whatever exceeds 12 hours. Each band is priced separately per crew member — straight-time hours at the department's `straight_hourly_rate_cents`, overtime hours at that same rate multiplied by `overtime_multiplier`, doubletime hours at that same rate multiplied by `doubletime_multiplier` — and each of those three products is rounded half up to a whole cent **before** the three are added together. That per-crew-member subtotal, plus any meal penalty, is then multiplied by `crew_size` to give the call's cost. The rounding order is normative: rounding once at the end after summing the bands and the crew multiplication is a different calculation and is not permitted.

The two variants differ in exactly two respects. The **estimate**, written as `estimated_cost_cents` when the call is created, feeds the tiering with `scheduled_hours` and never applies a meal penalty, even when the scheduled duration is long. The **actual**, written as `actual_cost_cents` when hours are reported, feeds the tiering with the reported `actual_hours` and does apply the penalty: when the reported hours exceed 5 hours and `meal_break_taken` is false, the department's `meal_penalty_cents` is added to the per-crew-member subtotal before the crew multiplication. Note the consequence: for two calls of identical duration, the actual cost exceeds the estimate by exactly `meal_penalty_cents` multiplied by `crew_size` when a break was skipped, and equals it otherwise. Note also that the penalty test reads the raw reported hours, not the minimum-adjusted billable hours, so a three-hour call with no break carries no penalty however the minimum inflates its billable time.

Reporting hours drives the call `ESTIMATED → WORKED`. The reporting payload supplies `actual_hours` and `meal_break_taken`, both stored on the call, and the call's `version` increments by 1. Reporting is permitted only while the call reads `ESTIMATED` — otherwise `ERR_LABOR_CALL_INVALID_STATE` — and only while the engagement's booking dimension reads `CONFIRMED` or `PERFORMED`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_CONFIRMED`. A worked call reaches its terminal `RECONCILED` state only as a consequence of settlement finalization, and reaches the alternative terminal `CANCELLED` state only as a consequence of engagement cancellation.

### 3.5. Financial Risk, Clearance, Deposits and Confirmation

Before a booking may be confirmed, a `FinanceController` must put a number on it. Risk assessment is version-pinned as described in §2.6 — the payload carries `expected_engagement_version` and optionally an `override_escalation` boolean — and it is permitted only while the engagement's booking dimension reads `DRAFT` or `CONFIRMED`. Every assessment writes four fields on the engagement: `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `projected_gross_cents` and `projected_margin_cents`, and increments `Engagement.version` by 1. Assessment may be repeated as often as the season demands; each run overwrites the previous decision, which is exactly why the version pin exists.

**The forward-looking margin.** The projection is computed from the engagement's `ACTIVE` price tiers and nothing else — no ticket sale, sold or refunded, ever participates. Each `ACTIVE` tier contributes its `base_price_cents` multiplied by its `seats_allocated` multiplied by a sell-through fraction, and that per-tier product is rounded half up to a whole cent before the tiers are added together; the tiers are visited in `created_at` ascending order. The sell-through fraction is the artist's `historical_sell_through_pct` for a presented engagement and exactly zero for a rental engagement, because on a rental night the client keeps the box office. The sum is `projected_gross_cents`.

Against that gross the projection sets four costs. The first is a fee basis, which resolves in a strict order of preference: if any negotiation round of the engagement is `ACCEPTED`, the basis is the engagement's `negotiated_fee_cents`; otherwise, if any round is still `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED`, the basis is the `offered_fee_cents` of the round with the highest `round_index` among those; otherwise the basis is zero. Note that the mid-negotiation basis reads the *offered* figure, never the countered one. To the fee basis are added the engagement's `labor_estimate_total_cents`, its `additional_equipment_cost_cents`, and a flat marketing allowance of $2,500 that the center books against every projection regardless of engagement type. `projected_margin_cents` is `projected_gross_cents` less that four-part cost total, and it may be negative.

**Scoring a presented engagement.** The presented score is a weighted blend of four normalized terms. The first is fee exposure: the fee basis divided by `projected_gross_cents`, using 1 as the divisor whenever the projected gross is zero or smaller so the ratio stays finite, and then capped at 1. It carries a weight of 0.45. The second is routing slack, measured in whole days as the smaller of two gaps — the gap from the artist's `routing_window_start_utc` forward to the engagement's `performance_start_utc`, and the gap from the engagement's `performance_end_utc` forward to the artist's `routing_window_end_utc` — each truncated downward to whole days. That slack is floored at zero, divided by a routing-slack target of 14 days, capped at 1, and then subtracted from 1 so that ample slack scores as low risk; the resulting term carries a weight of 0.25. The third is lead time: the whole days from the logical clock forward to `performance_start_utc`, truncated downward, floored at zero, divided by a lead target of 120 days, capped at 1, and subtracted from 1; weight 0.20. The fourth is commercial history: one minus the artist's `historical_sell_through_pct`; weight 0.10. The four weighted terms are added, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to an integer.

**Scoring a rental engagement.** The rental score uses different inputs entirely — routing and sell-through are not inputs at all. A credit factor is read straight off the client's `credit_rating`: 1.00 for grade `C`, 0.50 for grade `B` and 0.00 for grade `A`, weighted 0.50. An arrears ratio is the client's `outstanding_balance_cents` divided by the engagement's `deposit_required_cents`, again using 1 as the divisor whenever the deposit requirement is zero or smaller, and capped at 1; weight 0.30. The lead-time term is identical to the presented one — whole days from the logical clock to `performance_start_utc`, truncated downward, floored at zero, divided by 120 days, capped at 1, subtracted from 1 — and carries a weight of 0.20. As before the weighted sum is multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to an integer.

**Banding and clearance.** The resulting integer, always between 0 and 100, is banded on inclusive lower bounds: a score of 75 or more is `PROHIBITIVE`, a score of 55 or more is `HIGH`, a score of 35 or more is `MODERATE`, and anything below 35 is `LOW`. That band is written to `risk_tier`, and it in turn resolves the clearance dimension, evaluated in this order: a `PROHIBITIVE` tier yields `BLOCKED`; otherwise a `HIGH` tier yields `ESCALATED_APPROVED` when `override_escalation` is true **and** the clearance dimension already reads `ESCALATED`; otherwise a `HIGH` tier yields `ESCALATED`; otherwise the clearance is `CLEARED`.

The consequences of that ordering are worth spelling out. From `UNASSESSED`, a `LOW` or `MODERATE` tier moves the clearance `UNASSESSED → CLEARED`, a `HIGH` tier moves it `UNASSESSED → ESCALATED`, and a `PROHIBITIVE` tier moves it `UNASSESSED → BLOCKED`. An override flag presented while clearance still reads `UNASSESSED` accomplishes nothing, because the escalated-approval branch requires the clearance dimension to be `ESCALATED` already — the run still lands on `ESCALATED`. Once the clearance does read `ESCALATED`, a re-assessment carrying `override_escalation` set to true and yielding a tier of anything other than `PROHIBITIVE` moves the clearance `ESCALATED → ESCALATED_APPROVED`, which is the state that unblocks confirmation for a risky booking the controller has consciously accepted. A `BLOCKED` engagement is not condemned forever: a later re-assessment whose tier comes back `LOW` or `MODERATE` moves the clearance `BLOCKED → CLEARED`, while one that still scores `PROHIBITIVE` leaves it `BLOCKED`. And as §3.2 records, the clearance can also move `CLEARED → ESCALATED` with `risk_tier` forced to `HIGH` without any assessment at all, when accepting a fee drives `projected_margin_cents` negative.

**Rental deposits.** A rental client pays in before the hall is held. Recording a deposit is available to a `RentalManager` and to the engagement's own `RentalClientContact`, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the engagement's `rental_client_id`. The payload names an `amount_cents` figure, which is added to the engagement's `deposit_paid_cents`, and `Engagement.version` is incremented by 1. Deposits are meaningless on a presented engagement and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Note that recording a deposit does not recompute `deposit_required_cents` — the requirement moves only when the labor estimate or the equipment cost moves.

**Confirmation.** Confirming a booking is the act of a `ProgrammingDirector` or a `RentalManager` and drives the booking dimension `DRAFT → CONFIRMED`. Six preconditions must all hold. The booking dimension must read `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. Some technical rider of the engagement must read `CLEARED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RIDER_INVALID_STATE`. The clearance dimension must read `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED_APPROVED`: a clearance of `UNASSESSED` or `ESCALATED` is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_REQUIRED`, and a clearance of `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED`. For a presented engagement, some negotiation round must read `ACCEPTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`. For a rental engagement, `deposit_paid_cents` must be greater than or equal to `deposit_required_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_SHORTFALL`. Finally, the hall's `status` must still read `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SPACE_OFFLINE` — a hall taken offline after the booking was drafted blocks its confirmation.

On success, confirmation also opens the box office for drafting: when the ticketing dimension reads `TICKETING_UNSET` it is moved `TICKETING_UNSET → TIERS_DRAFT`, and when it already reads `TIERS_DRAFT` it is left alone. The engagement's `updated_at` is refreshed and `Engagement.version` is incremented by exactly 1 — one increment in total, not one per dimension.

### 3.6. Ticketing the House: Tiers, On-Sale, Dynamic Pricing and Subscriptions

Ticketing belongs to the `BoxOfficeManager`, and it applies to presented engagements only — the center sells no tickets to a rental client's event, so drafting a tier, going on sale or enrolling an engagement in a subscription package are all presented-only acts and are refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH` on a rental engagement.

A **price tier** slices the house. It is created with a `tier_name` label such as "ORCH" or "MEZZ", unique within its engagement; a `seats_allocated` count of at least one seat; a `base_price_cents` undiscounted face price; a `price_ceiling_cents` hard cap on the dynamically priced unit price, which may not be lower than `base_price_cents`; a `dynamic_pricing_enabled` boolean; and a `subscription_eligible` boolean. Tier creation is admitted while the engagement's booking dimension reads `DRAFT` or `CONFIRMED`. Two constraints are enforced together: the sum of `seats_allocated` across the engagement's `ACTIVE` tiers, counting the tier being added, may not exceed the hall's `seat_capacity`, and the `tier_name` may not duplicate an existing tier of the same engagement; either violation is refused with `ERR_SEAT_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY`.

A new tier starts in `ACTIVE` status with `sold_qty`, `subscription_held_qty`, `gross_cents` and `subscription_revenue_cents` all at zero, and `available_qty` derived as `seats_allocated` less `sold_qty` less `subscription_held_qty`. That derivation is an invariant, not a one-time initialization: at every instant the identity `seats_allocated` equals `available_qty` plus `sold_qty` plus `subscription_held_qty` must hold for every tier. Adding the first tier to an engagement whose ticketing dimension still reads `TICKETING_UNSET` moves that dimension `TICKETING_UNSET → TIERS_DRAFT`; adding tiers does not increment `Engagement.version`, and neither does the dimension change it triggers.

Going on sale drives the ticketing dimension `TIERS_DRAFT → ON_SALE`. It is admitted only from `TIERS_DRAFT` — any other ticketing state is refused with `ERR_TICKETING_NOT_ON_SALE` — and only while the booking dimension reads `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_CONFIRMED`. At least one `ACTIVE` tier must exist, and the sum of `seats_allocated` over the engagement's `ACTIVE` tiers is re-checked against the hall's `seat_capacity`, with a violation refused again by `ERR_SEAT_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY`. On success the engagement's `projected_gross_cents` and `projected_margin_cents` are recomputed by the forward-looking projection of §3.5 — now that the tier layout is final — and `Engagement.version` is incremented by 1.

**Selling a single ticket.** A sale names a `qty` of at least one seat and a `channel` of `WEB`, `PHONE` or `WINDOW`. Two state checks guard it, and their order matters: the tier-scoped check runs first, so a sale against a tier whose `status` is `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_TIER_CLOSED` even when the engagement's `ticketing_status` is also `CLOSED`; only when the tier is still `ACTIVE` does the engagement-scoped check fire, refusing a sale whose engagement is not `ON_SALE` with `ERR_TICKETING_NOT_ON_SALE`. The requested `qty` must then be no greater than the tier's `available_qty`, or the sale is refused with `ERR_TIER_SOLD_OUT`.

Pricing is dynamic and is evaluated against the tier's condition *before* this sale lands. The sale records `sell_through_pct_at_sale` as the tier's `sold_qty` divided by its `seats_allocated`, computed to four decimal places from the counters as they stood before this transaction; seats held by subscription orders deliberately do **not** count toward that sell-through figure. From it the system selects a multiplier, recorded on the sale as `dynamic_multiplier_applied`. When the tier's `dynamic_pricing_enabled` flag is false the multiplier is pinned to 1.00 for every sale on that tier and no band is consulted. Otherwise a sell-through of 0.85 or greater selects a multiplier of 1.30, a sell-through of 0.60 or greater but below 0.85 selects 1.15, and anything below 0.60 selects 1.00; the band comparisons are inclusive at their lower bounds and are made against the four-decimal ratio.

The per-seat price follows: `unit_price_cents` is the tier's `base_price_cents` multiplied by the selected multiplier, rounded half up to a whole cent, and then capped at the tier's `price_ceiling_cents` — the cap is applied after the rounding, and a ceiling below the multiplied price simply wins. `gross_cents` is `unit_price_cents` multiplied by `qty`; the rounding happens once per unit price, never after the quantity multiplication. Alongside it the sale records `restoration_fee_cents` as a flat capital-restoration charge of two dollars per seat multiplied by `qty` — money the patron pays toward the building rather than toward the show, which is why it is swept to the capital reserve at settlement rather than shared with the artist. A new sale is created in `SOLD` status, carrying the `price_tier_id` of the tier it was sold on; the only other status, `REFUNDED`, is reached solely through engagement cancellation.

A completed sale re-derives the tier's `sold_qty` and `gross_cents` as the sum of `qty` and of `gross_cents` over the tier's `SOLD` sales, re-derives `available_qty` from the seat identity, and re-derives the engagement's `realized_gross_cents`. That last figure is the running sum of `gross_cents` over every `SOLD` sale on the engagement's tiers, processed in `created_at` ascending order, plus the sum of `subscription_revenue_cents` over those same tiers. Selling a ticket does **not** increment `Engagement.version`.

**Season subscription packages.** A package spans several presented engagements rather than belonging to one. It is created with a `name` such as "Season Choose-3", a `package_price_cents` of at least one cent covering every included engagement, an `included_engagement_ids` array of at least two engagement identifiers, a `subscription_tier_name` naming the tier the package draws its seats from, and a `seats_per_order` count of at least one seat held per included engagement per sold order. Every element of `included_engagement_ids` must name an existing engagement — a dangling element yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` — whose `engagement_type` is `PRESENTED`, with a non-presented member refused by `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`, and whose `ticketing_status` is `ON_SALE`, with a member that has not opened its box office refused by `ERR_TICKETING_NOT_ON_SALE`. Each included engagement must additionally carry an `ACTIVE` price tier whose `tier_name` equals `subscription_tier_name` and whose `subscription_eligible` flag is true; a package that names a tier some included engagement cannot supply does not describe a sellable package and fails input validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A package is created with `sold_count` at zero.

At creation the package's `allocation_map` is written: the per-engagement revenue split for **one** order, keyed by engagement identifier. The split is a face-value waterfall. Each designated tier's face contribution is its `base_price_cents` multiplied by the package's `seats_per_order`, and the face total is the sum of those contributions across every included engagement. The tiers are then visited in a pinned order — by their engagement's `created_at` ascending, and where two engagements share a creation instant, by their engagement's `performance_start_utc` ascending; identifiers never participate in the ordering. Each tier's allocation is `package_price_cents` multiplied by that tier's face contribution divided by the face total, truncated **downward** to a whole cent. Truncation loses fractions of a cent, so a remainder is computed as `package_price_cents` less the sum of the truncated allocations, and the whole remainder is added to the **first** tier in the pinned ordering — never spread, never given to the largest tier.

Selling subscriptions supplies an `order_qty`. The seat test is all-or-nothing across the package: `seats_per_order` multiplied by `order_qty` must be no greater than the `available_qty` of **every** designated tier, and if a single member engagement cannot supply the seats, the entire sale is refused with `ERR_TIER_SOLD_OUT` and no tier is touched. On success the package's `sold_count` increases by `order_qty`; every designated tier's `subscription_held_qty` increases by `seats_per_order` multiplied by `order_qty`, with `available_qty` re-derived from the seat identity; and every designated tier's `subscription_revenue_cents` increases by that tier's single-order allocation multiplied by `order_qty`, after which each affected engagement's `realized_gross_cents` is re-derived. A subscription sale does **not** increment `Engagement.version`.

**The box-office statement.** At any point a `BoxOfficeManager` or `FinanceController` may pull a computed statement for an engagement. It reports the `engagement_id`; a `tier_count` of the engagement's price tiers; `seats_allocated_total`, `sold_qty_total` and `subscription_held_qty_total` as the sums of `seats_allocated`, `sold_qty` and `subscription_held_qty` across those tiers; `single_ticket_gross_cents` as the sum of the tiers' `gross_cents`; `subscription_revenue_cents` as the sum of the tiers' `subscription_revenue_cents`; `box_office_gross_cents` as those two revenue figures added together; `restoration_fees_cents` as the sum of `restoration_fee_cents` over the `SOLD` sales on those tiers; and `adjusted_gross_cents` as `box_office_gross_cents` less `restoration_fees_cents`. The statement is computed live from current counters and stores nothing.

### 3.7. Education and Community Programs

A presented engagement can carry community work alongside the ticketed performance, and the `ProgrammingDirector` attaches it. A program declares a `program_type` of `STUDENT_MATINEE`, `MASTERCLASS` or `COMMUNITY_WORKSHOP`; a `participant_capacity` of at least one participant; and a `subsidy_requested_cents` figure of at least one cent that the programming team asks the reserve to cover. Programs belong to presented engagements only, and attaching one to a rental engagement is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

The center does not simply grant what is asked. An allowance is derived from a per-family base plus a per-head component: the base is $4,000 for a `STUDENT_MATINEE`, $1,500 for a `MASTERCLASS` and $2,500 for a `COMMUNITY_WORKSHOP`, and to it is added $15 for each participant the program can host, counted from `participant_capacity`. The program's `subsidy_approved_cents` is then the smaller of `subsidy_requested_cents` and that allowance — a modest request is granted in full, an ambitious one is trimmed to the allowance.

Funding is drawn from the reserve immediately, and the reserve has a protective floor. The balance consulted is the balance **before** the draw: the `balance_after_cents` of the reserve entry with the greatest `created_at`, or zero when the ledger holds no entry at all. That balance less the approved subsidy must be greater than or equal to $50,000; a draw that would push the reserve beneath that floor is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402) and no program is created. When the guard passes, the program is created in `FUNDED` status and a reserve entry of type `EDUCATION_SUBSIDY_DRAW` is appended, carrying `amount_cents` equal to the approved subsidy negated, the causing `engagement_id`, and a recomputed `balance_after_cents`. A funded program leaves that status only through engagement cancellation, which moves it to the terminal `CANCELLED_WITH_ENGAGEMENT`.

### 3.8. Settlement, Reconciliation and Season Close

Settlement is the `FinanceController`'s domain and it proceeds in three acts: open the statement, reconcile the expenses, finalize the money.

**Opening the statement.** Opening a settlement is also what marks the engagement as performed. When the request arrives, the engagement's booking dimension must read `CONFIRMED` and the logical clock must have reached or passed the engagement's `performance_end_utc`; if either fails — the clock is still before curtain-down, or the booking is not `CONFIRMED` — the request is refused with `ERR_PERFORMANCE_NOT_COMPLETE`. There is no explicit mark-performed operation; the booking dimension advances `CONFIRMED → PERFORMED` here, as an automatic consequence, and that transition closes the box office: the ticketing dimension moves `ON_SALE → CLOSED` (or `TIERS_DRAFT → CLOSED` if the engagement never opened), every price tier whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is set to `CLOSED`, and `realized_gross_cents` is re-derived as the chronological sum of `gross_cents` over `SOLD` sales plus the tiers' `subscription_revenue_cents`. `Engagement.version` is incremented by 1. An engagement admits at most one settlement, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SETTLEMENT`.

The new settlement is created in `DRAFT` status with six figures frozen at that instant. `box_office_gross_cents` is the sum of `gross_cents` over the engagement's `SOLD` ticket sales plus the sum of `subscription_revenue_cents` over its tiers. `restoration_fees_cents` is the sum of `restoration_fee_cents` over those same `SOLD` sales. `adjusted_gross_cents` is `box_office_gross_cents` less `restoration_fees_cents` — the patron's capital contribution is not the show's revenue. `guarantee_cents` is a copy of the engagement's `negotiated_fee_cents`, which is zero for a rental engagement. `actual_labor_cents` is the sum of `actual_cost_cents` over the engagement's labor calls whose `status` is `WORKED`; calls still `ESTIMATED` and calls `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely. `actual_equipment_cents` is a copy of the engagement's `additional_equipment_cost_cents`. The input-supplied `actual_marketing_cents` starts at zero, and every computed money field of the settlement — `reimbursable_expense_cap_cents`, `reimbursable_expenses_cents`, `artist_share_cents`, `net_to_center_cents`, `reserve_contribution_cents`, `amount_payable_to_artist_cents` and `balance_due_from_client_cents` — starts at zero with `finalized_by_user_id` null.

**Reconciling expenses.** Reconciliation drives the settlement `DRAFT → EXPENSES_RECONCILED` and supplies the one figure the system cannot derive: `actual_marketing_cents`, the marketing and house-services spend. It is admitted only from `DRAFT`, and it additionally requires that no labor call of the engagement still reads `ESTIMATED` — every call must have reported its hours or been cancelled. A non-`DRAFT` settlement and a still-`ESTIMATED` call are both refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. On success `actual_labor_cents` is re-derived over the `WORKED` calls, the seven computed money fields are written, and the settlement's `version` is incremented by 1.

Those seven fields are computed in a **pinned order**, because each step consumes the previous one's output. For a **presented** engagement the waterfall runs as follows. Step one restates `adjusted_gross_cents` as `box_office_gross_cents` less `restoration_fees_cents`. Step two selects the reimbursable expense cap and writes it to `reimbursable_expense_cap_cents`: an adjusted gross below $20,000 caps reimbursement at $3,000; an adjusted gross of $20,000 or more but below $50,000 caps it at $6,000; an adjusted gross of $50,000 or more caps it at 12 percent of the adjusted gross, rounded half up to a whole cent. Step three forms the eligible expense pool as `actual_marketing_cents` plus `actual_equipment_cents` — labor is emphatically **not** reimbursable — and writes `reimbursable_expenses_cents` as the smaller of that pool and the cap. Step four computes the overage as the adjusted gross less `guarantee_cents` less `reimbursable_expenses_cents`, floored at zero, then writes `artist_share_cents` as the engagement's `artist_share_pct` multiplied by that overage, rounded half up. Step five writes `net_to_center_cents` as the adjusted gross less `guarantee_cents`, less `reimbursable_expenses_cents`, less `artist_share_cents`, less `actual_labor_cents`; the result may be negative. Step six selects a reserve percentage from the net position — 10 percent when `net_to_center_cents` is $10,000 or more, 5 percent when it is zero or more but below $10,000, and nothing at all when it is negative — and writes `reserve_contribution_cents` as that percentage of the net position floored at zero, rounded half up, **plus** the whole of `restoration_fees_cents`, which always reaches the reserve regardless of how the show performed. Step seven writes `amount_payable_to_artist_cents` as `guarantee_cents` plus `artist_share_cents`, and `balance_due_from_client_cents` as zero.

For a **rental** engagement the waterfall is a four-wall billing rather than a box-office split, and it runs to the same seven fields. Step one sets `adjusted_gross_cents`, `guarantee_cents`, `reimbursable_expenses_cents` and `artist_share_cents` all to zero, and `reimbursable_expense_cap_cents` remains zero as well — none of those concepts applies when the client keeps the house. Step two computes a service charge as `actual_labor_cents` plus `actual_equipment_cents`, marked up by 15 percent — that is, the sum multiplied by 1.15 — rounded half up to a whole cent. Step three forms the total charges as the engagement's `facility_fee_cents` plus that service charge plus `actual_marketing_cents`. Step four writes `balance_due_from_client_cents` as the total charges less the engagement's `deposit_paid_cents`; a negative result means the center owes the client a refund. Step five writes `net_to_center_cents` as the total charges less `actual_labor_cents` less `actual_equipment_cents` — the markup, in other words, is the center's margin on services. Step six selects the same reserve percentage from the same bands as the presented variant (10 percent at $10,000 of net or more, 5 percent from zero up to that band, nothing when negative) and writes `reserve_contribution_cents` as that percentage of the net position floored at zero, rounded half up; unlike the presented variant, no restoration fees are added, because a rental night sells no house tickets. Step seven writes `amount_payable_to_artist_cents` as zero.

One structural rule governs both variants: the reserve contribution is derived from the net position but is never treated as an expense and is never subtracted back out of `net_to_center_cents`. That back-edge would make the waterfall circular and is forbidden.

**Finalizing.** Finalization drives the settlement `EXPENSES_RECONCILED → FINALIZED` and the engagement `PERFORMED → SETTLED`. It is admitted only when the settlement reads `EXPENSES_RECONCILED` — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE` — and only when the engagement's booking dimension reads `PERFORMED`. And it is the segregation-of-duties gate: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the engagement's `fee_accepted_by_user_id`, and an attempt by the very controller who accepted the artist's fee is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Finalization's cascade runs in order. `finalized_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` and the settlement's `status` becomes `FINALIZED`. The engagement's `realized_margin_cents` is written by the chronological margin reckoning: `realized_gross_cents` — the running sum of `gross_cents` over the engagement's `SOLD` sales in `created_at` ascending order plus the tiers' booked `subscription_revenue_cents` — less the sum of the settlement's `guarantee_cents`, `artist_share_cents`, `actual_labor_cents`, `actual_equipment_cents` and `actual_marketing_cents`. Note what this reckoning omits and includes relative to the projection: it uses actual sales rather than historical sell-through, and it uses the reconciled marketing figure rather than the flat allowance, so the two variants diverge whenever real sell-through differs from historical sell-through or reconciled marketing differs from $2,500. Every labor call of the engagement whose `status` is `WORKED` is then moved to the terminal `RECONCILED`, each incrementing its own `version` by 1. Because the settlement produced a reserve contribution, a reserve entry of type `SETTLEMENT_CONTRIBUTION` is appended with `amount_cents` equal to the settlement's `reserve_contribution_cents` and a recomputed `balance_after_cents`. Because that contribution posted, the counterparty ledger moves next: for a presented engagement the artist's `lifetime_guarantee_paid_cents` increases by the settlement's `amount_payable_to_artist_cents`, and for a rental engagement the client's `outstanding_balance_cents` increases by the settlement's `balance_due_from_client_cents` — which, being possibly negative, may reduce that balance. Finally `Engagement.version` is incremented by 1 and the settlement's own `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.9. Cancellation and the Compensation Cascade

Seasons change. Either a `ProgrammingDirector` or a `RentalManager` may cancel an engagement, supplying a free-text `reason` and, optionally, a `cancelled_by_client` boolean that marks the cancellation as client-initiated for reporting purposes; that flag alters no retained percentage and no computed figure. Cancellation is available only while the booking dimension reads `DRAFT` or `CONFIRMED`; an engagement already `PERFORMED`, `SETTLED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. Both paths land on the terminal `CANCELLED` state, but they unwind different amounts of committed work, and the order of the unwinding is normative because later steps consume the results of earlier ones.

Cancelling from `DRAFT` runs ten steps in this sequence. **One**, `cancellation_fee_cents` is set to 0 — nothing is retained before confirmation, and the retained-percentage formula is never reached on this path. **Two**, every labor call of the engagement whose `status` is `ESTIMATED` is moved to the terminal `CANCELLED`, each incrementing its own `version` by 1. **Three**, every equipment rental line whose `status` is `RESERVED` under any of this engagement's riders is moved to `RELEASED`, and each referenced equipment item's `reserved_qty` is re-derived over the surviving `RESERVED` lines. **Four**, because step three released those lines, every technical rider of the engagement keeps its `review_status` exactly as it was but has its `additional_equipment_cost_cents` re-derived over its `RESERVED` lines — which is now zero — and the engagement's `additional_equipment_cost_cents` is re-derived the same way. **Five**, because step two removed calls from the estimate set, `labor_estimate_total_cents` is re-derived as the sum of `estimated_cost_cents` over the engagement's calls whose `status` is `ESTIMATED`, `WORKED` or `RECONCILED`. **Six**, because steps four and five both changed deposit inputs, `deposit_required_cents` is recomputed by the deposit rule of §3.1 **exactly once**, after both of them — a rental engagement gets one recomputation, not two, and a presented engagement keeps zero. **Seven**, every education program of the engagement whose `status` is `FUNDED` is moved to the terminal `CANCELLED_WITH_ENGAGEMENT`. **Eight**, because each of those programs left `FUNDED`, one reserve entry of type `EDUCATION_SUBSIDY_REFUND` is appended per program, carrying `amount_cents` equal to that program's `subsidy_approved_cents` as a positive credit, appended in the programs' `created_at` ascending order, each entry recomputing `balance_after_cents` in turn. **Nine**, the ticketing dimension is moved to the terminal `CLOSED` and every price tier whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is set to `CLOSED`. **Ten**, `Engagement.version` is incremented by 1.

Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` runs eleven steps and differs in three places. **One**, `cancellation_fee_cents` is written by the retained-fee formula rather than zeroed, because the center has already committed the hall. **Two** through **six** are identical to the draft path: estimated calls cancelled, reserved lines released with each item's `reserved_qty` re-derived, rider and engagement `additional_equipment_cost_cents` re-derived over the now-empty reserved set, `labor_estimate_total_cents` re-derived over the calls whose `status` is `ESTIMATED`, `WORKED` or `RECONCILED`, and `deposit_required_cents` recomputed exactly once after both of those inputs settle, for a rental engagement only. **Seven**, and this is new, every ticket sale whose `status` is `SOLD` under this engagement's tiers is moved to `REFUNDED`; each affected tier's `sold_qty`, `gross_cents` and `available_qty` are re-derived; and `realized_gross_cents` is re-derived as the sum of `gross_cents` over the surviving `SOLD` sales plus the tiers' `subscription_revenue_cents`. **Eight**, funded education programs are moved to `CANCELLED_WITH_ENGAGEMENT`, each appending an `EDUCATION_SUBSIDY_REFUND` reserve entry in the programs' `created_at` ascending order. **Nine**, and this too is new, for a rental engagement the client's `outstanding_balance_cents` is increased by the engagement's `cancellation_fee_cents` less its `deposit_paid_cents`; where the deposit already on hand exceeds the retained fee the result is negative, and the client is credited rather than billed. **Ten**, the ticketing dimension is moved to `CLOSED` and every `ACTIVE` price tier is set to `CLOSED`. **Eleven**, `Engagement.version` is incremented by 1.

**The retained fee.** On the confirmed path, the retained percentage is a function of proximity. The number of whole days from the logical clock forward to `performance_start_utc`, truncated downward, is compared against two bands using strict inequalities: fewer than 30 days out retains 75 percent, fewer than 90 days out (but 30 or more) retains 40 percent, and 90 days or more out retains 10 percent. The exposure that percentage is applied to is the engagement's `facility_fee_cents` plus its `negotiated_fee_cents` — the rental billing and the artist guarantee, whichever of the two is non-zero for this engagement type — and the product is rounded half up to a whole cent in a single rounding step. Sibling engagements never participate.

Two properties of the cascade deserve to be stated plainly, because they are easy to get wrong in both directions. First, cancelling an engagement that never reached tier drafting — which includes every rental engagement, since a rental cannot draft tiers at all — still parks the ticketing dimension in `CLOSED`, moving it `TICKETING_UNSET → CLOSED` with the tier sweep degenerating to a no-op. The cancel cascade never leaves a live ticketing dimension behind. Second, subscription holds are **not** unwound: `subscription_held_qty` and `subscription_revenue_cents` on the affected tiers are left exactly as they stand, because season subscription refunds are handled outside this API. That is deliberate, and it is what keeps the seat identity intact after cancellation — `seats_allocated` still equals `available_qty` plus `sold_qty` plus `subscription_held_qty` once the refunded single sales have released their seats.

List endpoints honour the pagination and filtering conventions of §2.3. Every endpoint requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers of §2.1, and the roles column is exhaustive — a role absent from a row is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
