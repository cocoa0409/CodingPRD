# Age-Restricted Delivery Verification API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retailers who put alcohol, tobacco and vaping products, cannabis, restricted over-the-counter pharmacy items or aerosol solvents into a last-mile delivery van inherit a regulator's problem: the legal act of selling a restricted product now happens at a stranger's doorstep, minutes after the merchant has lost sight of the transaction. A single handover to an underage or visibly intoxicated recipient can cost a delivery licence, and the evidence that the handover was handled correctly must survive an audit years later. This document specifies the Age-Restricted Delivery Verification API, the compliance backbone that a retail platform, its delivery partners and its regulators share.

The system is deliberately built around three checkpoints rather than one. At **order time**, a purchaser attests their own age, an identity pre-verification is run against external data sources, and the order is measured against the delivery jurisdiction's permissibility rules, restricted delivery hours and quantity ceilings. At **delivery time**, a certified courier re-runs the jurisdictional and hours evaluation against the instant they actually arrive, inspects or scans an acceptable identification document, and either completes the handover with signature and photo evidence retained for regulatory audit, or refuses it. **After the fact**, secret-shopper auditors probe courier behaviour, compliance officers investigate alleged violations, and each delivery partner carries a continuously derived licence-risk score whose worsening posture throttles that partner's ability to move restricted goods at all.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is that compliance state and physical fulfilment state are tracked independently: an order can be physically returning to the warehouse while its compliance record is still marked pre-verified, and a compliance block never rewrites the physical history of a shipment. The second is that every derived number — exposure in regulatory units, identity confidence, courier compliance score, partner licence risk — is recomputed from its source rows on every event that could change it, in a pinned evaluation order, so that two servers replaying the same request sequence produce byte-identical figures. Time itself is governed by a server-held logical clock rather than the host wall clock, so that expiry, decay and deadline behaviour is reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold across every resource and every operation. Where §3 describes what a business event means, this section describes the shape of the request and response, the order in which objections are raised, and the arithmetic conventions every calculation obeys.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER`. Six roles exist, and the role strings are exact: `PlatformAdmin`, `Customer`, `PartnerManager`, `Courier`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `SecretShopperAuditor`. Each endpoint in §4 lists the roles permitted to invoke it; a caller whose `X-User-Role` is not among them is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and that check happens before any resource is loaded.

Three of the six roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is the primary key of a persisted row and ownership is enforced by comparing that value to a foreign key on the addressed resource. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks compare the addressed customer's `id`, or the `customer_id` of any order or order line reached through it, to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DeliveryPartner`; ownership checks compare the addressed partner's `id`, or the `delivery_partner_id` of the courier, certification, order or violation case being touched, to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Courier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Courier`; ownership checks compare a delivery attempt's `courier_id` to `X-User-ID`. Any of these comparisons failing yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_SCOPE`, even when the role itself is authorised for the operation.

The remaining three roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, but the server does record it: the officer who decides a manual order clearance is stored in `clearance_actor_user_id`, the officer who substantiates a violation case is stored in `substantiated_by_user_id`, the officer who reviews a corrective action plan is stored in `reviewed_by_user_id`, and the officer who records a verification outcome is stored in `verified_by_user_id`. None of those stored values is ever compared to an entity `id`; they are compared only to each other. When `X-User-Role` is `SecretShopperAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque auditor identifier that the server neither persists nor compares to any entity's `id`.

That recording of officer identity exists to support a **segregation-of-duties** rule. A `ComplianceOfficer` who recorded the substantiating decision on a violation case is barred from reviewing, and barred from recording the verification outcome on, any corrective action plan filed against that same case — even though the role as a whole is authorised for both operations. Concretely, on plan review and on plan verification the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the parent violation case's `substantiated_by_user_id`; if they are equal the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, and the smallest accepted value is 1 while the largest is 100. `offset` skips a number of leading rows; it defaults to 0 when omitted, and negative values are not accepted. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Unless a collection's own description in §3 or §4 states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The single documented exception is the per-jurisdiction policy listing, which is ordered by `effective_from` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending so that a jurisdiction's regulatory timeline reads in chronological order. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it and must not infer a resource's kind from its shape. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was inserted. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical-clock instant on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on insert and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. A derived field being recomputed as a side effect of another operation counts as a mutation of the row that owns it: when a refusal cascade rewrites a partner's `open_case_count`, that partner's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed. That rendering convention is uniform: whenever the server itself mints an instant — stamping one from the logical clock, as `updated_at`, a recorded outcome's `recorded_at` or a decision's `decided_at` are stamped, or deriving one by arithmetic on another instant — it is written in the same ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional digits. An instant supplied in a request body is not re-spelled: it is stored and returned exactly as the caller wrote it, as is any field that copies such a stored value.

`CaptureArtifact` is the deliberate exception. A retained signature or photo is audit evidence, so the row is immutable: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely. There is no operation that updates or deletes a capture artifact.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Epoch Sweep

All expiry, decay, deadline and freshness rules in this specification read a server-held **logical clock**, never the host machine's wall clock. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it holds that value until an administrator moves it. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; moving the clock backwards is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND`, so the clock is monotonic for the lifetime of a server process.

Advancing the clock is not a passive assignment — it runs an epoch sweep in three fixed steps, in this order. Step one expires identity pre-verifications: every `IdentityCheck` whose `status` is `VALID` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the new clock value becomes `EXPIRED`, and then every affected customer's `latest_identity_check_result` is re-derived. Step two expires training credentials: every `CourierCertification` whose `state` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the new clock value becomes `EXPIRED`, and then every affected courier's `certification_state` and `dispatch_eligible` are re-derived. Step three re-scores partners: every `DeliveryPartner` has both `license_risk_score` and `lifetime_risk_score` recomputed, because risk decay is a function of elapsed time, after which the posture derivation runs and may worsen or improve `posture` — and a worsening posture cascades to that partner's dispatched orders in `created_at` ascending order exactly as it does when a case decision triggers it. The response reports `current_utc` together with `expired_identity_check_count`, `expired_certification_count` and `repostured_partner_count`, the last counting partners whose `posture` value actually changed during step three.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. A request addressing a policy list under a jurisdiction that does not exist fails with `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the policy lookup. The catalogue of not-found codes is one per entity: `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHOPPER_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved, and they are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. An order creation that names a nonexistent customer and a nonexistent jurisdiction reports the customer failure, because `customer_id` is declared ahead of `delivery_jurisdiction_id`. Body-level foreign-key failures reuse the same per-entity codes: `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_COURIER_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence: the Order in Which Objections Are Raised

A single request can violate many rules at once. The system evaluates rules in strictly ascending tier order and the **first** violated rule determines the response; no later tier is evaluated, and no partial mutation survives a rejection. The tiers, from earliest to latest, are:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` (and `X-User-Role`) must be present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` with HTTP 401.
2. **Role authorisation.** The caller's role must be listed for the endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` with HTTP 403.
3. **Peer-ownership scope.** A `Customer` caller may only touch orders whose `customer_id` equals `X-User-ID` and the customer row whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`. A `PartnerManager` caller may only touch delivery-partner rows, couriers, courier certifications, orders and violation cases whose owning `delivery_partner_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A `Courier` caller may only touch delivery attempts whose `courier_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A violation yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_SCOPE` with HTTP 403. This tier applies to every `Customer`-, `PartnerManager`- and `Courier`-scoped endpoint.
4. **Segregation of duties.** On corrective-action-plan review and on corrective-action-plan verification only, `X-User-ID` must not equal the parent violation case's `substantiated_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.
5. **Path existence.** Every id in the request path must resolve to a live row, left to right, else the corresponding not-found code with HTTP 404. This tier applies to every endpoint carrying path parameters.
6. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key in the request body must resolve, in the endpoint's declared field order, else the corresponding not-found code with HTTP 404. This tier applies to every creating operation that accepts foreign-key fields.
7. **Request shape.** Required fields present, JSON types correct, enum members recognised, declared numeric ranges respected, date and timestamp strings parseable, and `limit`/`offset` inside their bounds — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 400.
8. **Cross-field semantics within one body.** A policy whose `effective_to` precedes its `effective_from`; a policy whose `delivery_window_start_minute` equals its `delivery_window_end_minute`; a `source_signals` array in which some `source_type` value repeats; and a corrective action plan whose `due_at` is not later than the logical clock now — each yields `ERR_INCONSISTENT_FIELDS` with HTTP 422. This tier applies to policy publication, identity-check creation and corrective-action-plan filing.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_customer_version` or `expected_violation_case_version` must equal the parent row's current `version`, else the matching stale-version code with HTTP 412 (see §2.8).
10. **State precondition.** The target row must be in a state from which the requested transition exists. Because an order carries two independent state dimensions, submission requires `fulfillment_state` to be `CREATED`; dispatch requires `fulfillment_state` to be either `SUBMITTED` or `HELD_AT_HUB` *and* `compliance_state` to be one of `AUTO_CLEARED`, `PREVERIFIED` or `COMPLIANCE_CLEARED`; return settlement requires `fulfillment_state` to be `RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`. Failures in this tier carry HTTP 409 and one of `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ATTEMPT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE` or `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`.
11. **Policy resolution.** For every restriction class present on the order, exactly one governing policy must be resolvable, else `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_POLICY` with HTTP 422. This tier applies to order submission, delivery-attempt creation and the door-side identification check.
12. **Jurisdiction permissibility.** Evaluated per restriction class in the sub-order prohibition, then dry status, then permit posture, yielding `ERR_DELIVERY_PROHIBITED_IN_JURISDICTION`, `ERR_DRY_JURISDICTION_PROHIBITED` or `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED`, all HTTP 422. This tier applies to order submission and delivery-attempt creation.
13. **Partner and courier authority.** Yielding `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_COURIER_PARTNER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_COURIER_NOT_CERTIFIED` or `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING_FOR_RESTRICTION`, all HTTP 422. This tier applies to three operations: order dispatch, delivery-attempt creation and the door-side identification check.
14. **Door-side identification.** Yielding `ERR_DOCUMENT_TYPE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE`, `ERR_SCAN_REQUIRED`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED` or `ERR_RECIPIENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, all HTTP 422, and applying to the door-side identification check alone.
15. **Purchaser eligibility.** Yielding `ERR_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_DELIVERY_BLOCKED`, `ERR_IDENTITY_PREVERIFICATION_REQUIRED` or `ERR_PURCHASER_UNDERAGE`, all HTTP 422, and applying to order submission alone.
16. **Capture completeness.** Yielding `ERR_CAPTURE_INCOMPLETE` with HTTP 422, and applying to handover alone.
17. **Temporal admissibility of a single instant.** Yielding `ERR_OUTSIDE_RESTRICTED_HOURS`, `ERR_SCHEDULED_TIME_IN_PAST`, `ERR_RESPONSE_DUE_NOT_FUTURE` or `ERR_TRAINING_STALE`, all HTTP 422. This tier applies to four operations: order submission, delivery-attempt creation, requesting a partner response on a case, and certification renewal.
18. **Interval-occupation conflicts.** Yielding `ERR_COURIER_SLOT_CONFLICT` or `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, both HTTP 409, and applying to delivery-attempt creation and policy publication respectively (see §2.10).
19. **Structural constraints.** Global uniqueness of a jurisdiction `code` and a product `sku`; at most one capture artifact per attempt-and-`kind` pair; a violation case referencing exactly one subject; the revision ceiling on plan resubmission; and an order line `quantity` of at least 1 with at least one line on a submitted order. These yield `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CAPTURE_KIND`, `ERR_CASE_SUBJECT_INVALID` or `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, all HTTP 409, and apply to jurisdiction registration, product registration, capture storage, manual case creation and plan resubmission.
20. **Quantity ceilings.** Checked last, deliberately, so that every safety and temporal objection is reported before a merely commercial one: the per-order ceiling yields `ERR_ORDER_UNIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and the per-customer rolling-window ceiling yields `ERR_WINDOW_UNIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, both HTTP 422, on order submission.

Two consequences of that ordering are worth stating outright, because they are easy to get backwards. A submission into a dry jurisdiction outside the permitted delivery hours reports `ERR_DRY_JURISDICTION_PROHIBITED`, not `ERR_OUTSIDE_RESTRICTED_HOURS`, because jurisdiction permissibility sits at tier 12 and hours at tier 17. A submission on an order that is no longer in `CREATED` and that would also breach its per-order unit ceiling reports `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, not `ERR_ORDER_UNIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, because the state precondition sits at tier 10 and quantity ceilings at tier 20.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Rows

Two creating operations write a child row whose existence silently invalidates something the client may have read earlier, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent row's `version`.

The first guards identity pre-verification. A request that creates an `IdentityCheck` under a customer must carry `expected_customer_version`, a non-optional input field holding the `version` of that `Customer` row as the client last observed it. If it does not equal the customer's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard exists because a new pre-verification legitimately supersedes the customer's previous `VALID` identity check and rewrites `latest_identity_check_result`; a writer holding a stale view of the customer would silently discard a pre-verification it never saw.

The second guards corrective action plans. A request that files a `CorrectiveActionPlan` against a violation case must carry `expected_violation_case_version`, a non-optional input field holding the `version` of that `ViolationCase` row as the client last observed it. If it does not equal the case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard exists because a new plan supersedes every earlier non-terminal plan on the case, so a writer working from a stale view of the case would supersede a plan whose review it never observed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Where an operation is not listed above, the client does not send an expected version and the server does not check one.

### 2.9. Precision, Rounding and the Conservation of Money

Restricted-unit exposure is carried to two decimal places and rounded half up, and the rounding is applied **twice** in a pinned order: once to each individual line's contribution, and once again to the sum of those already-rounded line values. Summing the unrounded per-line products and rounding only at the end is forbidden and produces a different figure, which matters because the result is compared against a policy ceiling. To make the divergence concrete: lines of quantity 3, 2 and 1 whose per-item unit figures are 1.50, 0.75 and 0.05, under a jurisdiction scale factor of 1.15, round per line to 5.18, 1.73 and 0.06 and sum to 6.97; rounding only the unrounded total 6.9575 would give 6.96 instead. A ceiling of 6.96 therefore rejects that order under the required arithmetic and would wrongly accept it under the forbidden arithmetic.

Rolling-window consumption is likewise carried to two decimal places and rounded half up. Identity confidence is carried to four decimal places and rounded half up. Partner licence-risk scores are carried to two decimal places and rounded half up, applied per contributing case and then once more to the tier-multiplied total; the result is also capped at a ceiling of 100.00. Courier compliance scores are carried to two decimal places, rounded half up, and clamped into the inclusive range from 0.00 to 100.00. Posture thresholds compare two-decimal scores with an at-or-above test, so a score of exactly 25.00 is enough to restrict a partner and a score of exactly 60.00 is enough to suspend one.

Money is handled exclusively in whole integer cents; no monetary field is ever fractional. The restocking fee is **floored** to whole cents rather than rounded, deliberately, so that the platform can never over-refund by a rounding artefact. And every return settlement preserves an exact invariant: the amount returned to the customer in `refunded_cents` plus the amount kept in `retained_cents` equals the order's frozen `charged_cents`, exactly, with no tolerance.

### 2.10. Local Time Geometry, Effective Periods and Interval Occupation

Every jurisdiction declares `utc_offset_minutes`, a fixed offset from UTC between -720 and 840 inclusive. Daylight saving is not modelled: the offset is constant. To determine the local wall-clock minute of an instant, the system floors that instant to a whole minute, converts it to minutes since the Unix epoch, adds the jurisdiction's offset in minutes, and reduces the result modulo the 1440 minutes in a day. The local calendar date of an instant is derived the same way, by applying the offset before reading off the date.

A policy's permitted delivery window runs from `delivery_window_start_minute` to `delivery_window_end_minute` on that local minute axis and is **half-open**: the start minute is inside the window and the end minute is outside it. When the end minute is strictly greater than the start minute, an instant is inside the window when its local minute is at or after the start and strictly before the end. When the end minute is less than or equal to the start minute, the window wraps past local midnight, and an instant is inside it when its local minute is at or after the start **or** strictly before the end. As a boundary illustration, under an offset of -240 minutes and a window running from minute 600 to minute 1320, an instant of `2026-08-21T01:59:00Z` is local minute 1319 and is inside the window, while `2026-08-21T02:00:00Z` is local minute 1320 and is outside it.

A policy's `effective_from` and `effective_to` are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and the effective period they describe is **closed at both ends**: both the first and last date govern. Two policies for the same jurisdiction and the same restriction class may not share even a single calendar date, and because the interval is closed at both ends a new period whose `effective_from` equals an existing period's `effective_to` *is* an overlap and is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.

Courier occupation uses the opposite geometry on purpose. A delivery attempt occupies its courier for 20 minutes starting at the attempt's `started_at`, and that interval is half-open with a zero buffer: it runs from `started_at` up to but not including the instant 20 minutes later. A new attempt collides when its interval intersects the interval of any non-terminal attempt of the same courier — that is, any attempt in `INITIATED`, `ID_VERIFIED` or `ID_REJECTED` — and the collision is reported as `ERR_COURIER_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Exact adjacency is permitted here: a new attempt whose `started_at` equals the exact end of a previous attempt's dwell interval does not conflict.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Recomputation

Several operations in this system set off multi-step cascades in which each link fires only because the previous link changed something. Two properties are contractual. First, cascades run to completion inside the originating request; there is no deferred or eventual settling, and a caller reading any affected row immediately afterwards sees the final values. Second, whenever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, the rows are processed in a pinned order so that the outcome is reproducible: orders held by a partner reposturing are processed in `created_at` ascending order, delivery attempts aborted by a certification suspension are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and certifications suspended by a compliance-score collapse are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

Derived fields are never patched incrementally from a delta; they are recomputed from their source rows. A partner's `open_case_count` and `open_critical_case_count` are re-counted from that partner's cases, a customer's `refusal_strikes` is re-counted from that customer's refused attempts, a jurisdiction's `active_policy_count` is the count of policy rows pointing at it, and a courier's `compliance_score` is rebuilt from every contributing failed test and substantiated case. Posture derivation is always evaluated strictly *after* the risk score it reads has been rewritten, so the posture never feeds back into its own inputs within one request.

A final structural property applies to every state machine in the system: some states are terminal, and no transition of any kind — human or cascaded — leaves them. An order whose `compliance_state` is `COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED` is finished for compliance purposes, and an order whose `fulfillment_state` is `DELIVERED` or `RETURN_COMPLETED` is finished physically. A delivery attempt in `HANDED_OVER`, `REFUSED` or `ABORTED` is closed; a violation case in `CLOSED_VERIFIED` is closed; a corrective action plan in `VERIFIED` or `SUPERSEDED` is closed. Requests that would move a row out of one of these states are refused by the state guard of §2.7 with that entity's invalid-state error. The two derived dimensions — a courier's `certification_state` and a partner's `posture` — have no terminal state at all: both are pure functions of their source rows and may move in either direction as often as those rows change.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Regulatory Reference Data: Jurisdictions, Policies and the Catalogue

Nothing restricted can be sold until a `PlatformAdmin` has described the regulatory landscape. Registering a jurisdiction records its `code` — a regulatory identifier that must be globally unique across all jurisdictions and must match the shape of two uppercase letters, a hyphen, and then two to six characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits — together with a human-readable `name`, the jurisdiction's alcohol-delivery posture in `dry_status`, and its fixed `utc_offset_minutes`. The `dry_status` value is one of `WET`, `DAMP` or `DRY`. Reusing a `code` that already exists is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A freshly registered jurisdiction reports `active_policy_count` of 0; that counter is the number of restriction policy rows whose `jurisdiction_id` points at this jurisdiction, counted across **all** effective periods with no filtering by state or by whether the period is currently in force, and it is incremented as each policy is published. Jurisdictions can be listed with an optional `dry_status` filter.

Onto each jurisdiction an administrator publishes one **restriction policy** per restriction class per effective period. The policy names its `jurisdiction_id` and the `restriction_type` it governs, which is one of `ALCOHOL`, `TOBACCO_VAPE`, `CANNABIS`, `PHARMACY_OTC_RESTRICTED` or `AEROSOL_SOLVENT`. Its `permissibility` — one of `PERMITTED`, `PERMIT_REQUIRED` or `PROHIBITED` — says whether that class may be delivered there at all. Its `min_age_years` is the minimum completed age in years demanded of both purchaser and doorstep recipient, and it must lie between 18 and 25 inclusive. Its `delivery_window_start_minute` (from 0 through 1439) and `delivery_window_end_minute` (from 0 through 1440) bound the permitted local delivery hours on the minute axis described in §2.10; a policy whose two window minutes are equal is contradictory and is rejected with `ERR_INCONSISTENT_FIELDS`, as is a policy whose `effective_to` falls before its `effective_from`.

Quantity governance lives on the same row. `unit_scale_factor` is the jurisdiction-specific multiplier that converts a product's restricted units into local regulatory units; it lies between 0.01 and 5.00 inclusive and carries two decimal places. `max_units_per_order` caps a single order's exposure for that class, and `max_units_per_window` caps what one customer may receive across `rolling_window_days` days; both ceilings are positive with two decimal places, and `rolling_window_days` is between 1 and 365 inclusive. Door-side evidence rules live here too: `acceptable_document_types` is a subset of `DRIVERS_LICENSE`, `PASSPORT`, `STATE_ID`, `MILITARY_ID` and `TRIBAL_ID`, and it **may be the empty array**, in which case no document whatsoever is acceptable at the door for that class. The boolean `requires_scan` says whether an accepted document must be machine-scanned — read by barcode or machine-readable zone — rather than merely inspected by eye. `effective_from` and `effective_to` bound the closed calendar period over which the row governs, and a period sharing any date with an existing period for the same jurisdiction and class is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A policy read against an unknown identifier reports `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`. Per-jurisdiction policy listings accept an optional `restriction_type` filter.

The catalogue is simpler. A product carries a `sku` that is globally unique — a reused `sku` also reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — a display `name`, a `restriction_type` drawn from the same five classes plus the additional value `NONE` for unrestricted goods, a `restricted_units_per_item` figure of zero or more with up to two decimal places, and a `list_price_cents` that must be strictly positive. Products classified `NONE` must carry `restricted_units_per_item` of 0. Product listings accept an optional `restriction_type` filter.

### 3.2. Customers, Age Attestation and Identity Pre-Verification

A `PlatformAdmin` registers each customer with a `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique across all customers (a duplicate reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), and a `jurisdiction_id` naming the customer's home and billing jurisdiction. That home jurisdiction is not necessarily where deliveries land, and it is the *delivery* jurisdiction that governs permissibility. A new customer starts with `attestation_state` of `NOT_ATTESTED`, `latest_identity_check_result` of `NONE`, `refusal_strikes` of 0, and `blocked_until`, `attested_dob`, `attestation_at` and `attestation_channel` all null.

Attestation is the customer's own act. Supplying an `attested_dob` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form together with an `attestation_channel` of `WEB_CHECKBOX`, `MOBILE_APP` or `PHONE_AGENT` writes both values, stamps `attestation_at` from the logical clock, and advances the customer's `version` by one. The system then derives `attestation_state`: if the completed age implied by `attested_dob` as at `attestation_at` is at least 18 years, the state becomes `ATTESTED`; if it is below 18, the state becomes `ATTESTATION_REJECTED`. A self-declared minor is emphatically **not** an error at this point — the declaration is recorded faithfully, and it is order submission that later refuses to proceed. Re-attesting overwrites the previous declaration and re-derives the state.

Pre-verification is somebody else's act: a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` runs an identity check on the customer's behalf against an external data source. The request names the `provider` consulted — `STATE_DMV_GATEWAY`, `NATIONAL_ID_VENDOR` or `TELCO_VERIFY` — the `provided_dob` submitted to that provider, the `source_dob` the provider's authoritative record returned (which may disagree with what was submitted), a `validity_days` figure between 1 and 365 inclusive, and a `source_signals` array. Each element of `source_signals` is an object with a `source_type` drawn from `GOVERNMENT_ID_DB`, `CREDIT_BUREAU`, `MOBILE_CARRIER` and `WATCHLIST_NEGATIVE`, plus a boolean `matched`; a given `source_type` may appear at most once, and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_INCONSISTENT_FIELDS`. The request also carries `expected_customer_version` as described in §2.8.

The resulting `confidence` is a weighted sum, computed only from the signals supplied on this request — earlier identity checks for the same customer are never consulted. Starting from zero and visiting the four signal kinds in the fixed order government identity database, credit bureau, mobile carrier, negative watchlist, each signal whose `matched` is true adds its weight: half a point for `GOVERNMENT_ID_DB`, a quarter point for `CREDIT_BUREAU`, fifteen hundredths for `MOBILE_CARRIER` and one tenth for `WATCHLIST_NEGATIVE`. That running total is rounded half up to four decimal places. Then a single penalty applies: if `provided_dob` differs from `source_dob` **and** a `GOVERNMENT_ID_DB` signal was among those matched, two tenths of a point are subtracted, the result is rounded half up to four decimal places again, and the value is floored at 0.0000 so confidence is never negative.

Banding turns that number into `result`. A confidence of 0.8500 or above is `MATCH`; a confidence below 0.8500 but at or above 0.6000 is `PARTIAL`; anything lower is `NO_MATCH`. Both boundaries are inclusive at the bottom, so a confidence of exactly 0.8500 — the value produced when the government identity database, the credit bureau and the negative watchlist all match — is a `MATCH`, and all four signals matching alongside a date-of-birth disagreement yields 0.8000 and lands in `PARTIAL`. From `result` the system derives `verified_dob`: it is `source_dob` on a `MATCH`, `provided_dob` on a `PARTIAL`, and null on a `NO_MATCH`. That derived date of birth, and not the customer's own attestation, is the age of record at submission time.

Each new check is stored with `status` of `VALID` and an `expires_at` of its own `created_at` plus `validity_days` days, and it demotes every earlier check of that customer whose `status` was `VALID` to `SUPERSEDED`. The customer's `latest_identity_check_result` is then re-derived as the `result` of whichever of that customer's checks has `status` of `VALID` and the greatest `created_at`, falling back to `NONE` when no such row exists, and the customer's `version` advances by one. As §2.5 describes, a clock advance past a check's `expires_at` moves it from `VALID` to `EXPIRED` and re-derives the customer's `latest_identity_check_result` accordingly. Identity-check listings accept an optional `status` filter and are ordered oldest first.

### 3.3. Building an Order and the Order-Time Compliance Battery

A `Customer` opens an order by naming their own `customer_id`, the `delivery_jurisdiction_id` of the delivery address, the `delivery_partner_id` of the last-mile partner contracted to carry it, a promised `scheduled_delivery_at` instant in UTC, and a `delivery_fee_cents` amount of zero or more. A new order sits at `compliance_state` `DRAFT_UNVERIFIED` and `fulfillment_state` `CREATED`, with `restriction_types` and `applied_policy_ids` both empty, `charged_cents` and `item_subtotal_cents` at 0, `provisional_restricted_units` at 0.00, `restocking_fee_cents`, `refunded_cents` and `retained_cents` at 0, and `final_restricted_units`, `submitted_at`, `dispatched_at`, `delivered_at`, `hold_reason` and `clearance_actor_user_id` all null. Orders can be listed with any combination of `customer_id`, `delivery_jurisdiction_id`, `delivery_partner_id`, `compliance_state` and `fulfillment_state` filters.

Lines may be added only while the order's `fulfillment_state` is still `CREATED`; adding a line to an order that has moved on is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. A line names a `product_id` and a `quantity` between 1 and 500 inclusive. At the moment of creation the line snapshots two values from the catalogue and never re-reads them: `unit_price_cents` copies the product's `list_price_cents`, and `units_per_item_snapshot` copies the product's `restricted_units_per_item`. Its `line_total_cents` is the `quantity` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`. Its `line_restricted_units` starts at 0.00 and stays there until submission computes it, and its `line_state` starts at `INCLUDED`. Adding a line also refreshes the parent order's `item_subtotal_cents` and its `restriction_types`. That `restriction_types` array is the set of distinct `restriction_type` values other than `NONE` carried by the products of the order's lines whose `line_state` is `INCLUDED`, sorted ascending as strings. Order lines can be listed with `order_id`, `product_id` and `line_state` filters, and an unknown line identifier reports `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`.

Submission is where the two state dimensions part company. On every successful submission — whatever the compliance outcome — the physical dimension advances `CREATED → SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `item_subtotal_cents` is frozen as the sum of `line_total_cents` across **all** of the order's lines regardless of their `line_state`, and `charged_cents` is frozen as `item_subtotal_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents`. An order with no lines at all cannot be submitted and reports `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`; an order whose `fulfillment_state` is anything other than `CREATED` reports `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Meanwhile the compliance dimension leaves `DRAFT_UNVERIFIED` along exactly one of four branches.

The first branch is the unrestricted shortcut. When the order's `restriction_types` is empty — every line's product is classified `NONE` — the compliance dimension moves `DRAFT_UNVERIFIED → AUTO_CLEARED`, `provisional_restricted_units` is set to 0.00 and `applied_policy_ids` stays empty. Such an order bypasses attestation, pre-verification, policy resolution, restricted hours, quantity quotas and door-side identification entirely; there is nothing age-restricted in the box.

Every other branch runs the full battery. First, the purchaser must be eligible, and the eligibility checks are evaluated in a fixed sub-order: the customer's `attestation_state` must be exactly `ATTESTED` (both `NOT_ATTESTED` and `ATTESTATION_REJECTED` report `ERR_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`); then, if the customer's `blocked_until` is set and lies later than the logical clock now, the submission reports `ERR_CUSTOMER_DELIVERY_BLOCKED`; then the customer must have at least one identity check whose `status` is `VALID`, else `ERR_IDENTITY_PREVERIFICATION_REQUIRED`; and finally the completed age of the newest `VALID` check's `verified_dob`, measured as at the local calendar date of `scheduled_delivery_at` in the delivery jurisdiction, must be at least the largest `min_age_years` among the policies selected for this order, else `ERR_PURCHASER_UNDERAGE`. A newest `VALID` check whose `result` is `NO_MATCH` is deliberately *not* an eligibility failure — it routes the order rather than rejecting it, as described below, and so the purchaser-age check still runs against that check's `verified_dob`, which on a `NO_MATCH` is null and therefore cannot satisfy any minimum age.

Second, one governing policy must be resolvable per restriction class. For every element of `restriction_types`, exactly one policy must exist for the pair of the order's `delivery_jurisdiction_id` and that class whose closed effective period contains the local calendar date of `scheduled_delivery_at`; zero matching rows is a hard failure reported as `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_POLICY`, and the no-overlap rule of §2.10 guarantees there is never more than one. The resolved policy identifiers are written to `applied_policy_ids`, one per element of `restriction_types` and ordered to match it positionally.

Third, the jurisdiction must permit the delivery. Per restriction class, and in this sub-order: a selected policy whose `permissibility` is `PROHIBITED` reports `ERR_DELIVERY_PROHIBITED_IN_JURISDICTION`; then an `ALCOHOL` class delivered into a jurisdiction whose `dry_status` is `DRY` reports `ERR_DRY_JURISDICTION_PROHIBITED`; then a jurisdiction whose `dry_status` is `DAMP` combined with a selected policy whose `permissibility` is anything other than `PERMIT_REQUIRED` reports `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED`. A `WET` jurisdiction imposes no additional condition beyond the `PROHIBITED` test.

Fourth come the two temporal tests. The promised `scheduled_delivery_at` must lie strictly later than the logical clock now; an instant at or before now reports `ERR_SCHEDULED_TIME_IN_PAST`. And the local delivery minute of `scheduled_delivery_at` must fall inside every selected policy's half-open window under the geometry of §2.10, else `ERR_OUTSIDE_RESTRICTED_HOURS`.

Fifth and last come the quantity ceilings, computed from the order's **provisional** restricted-unit exposure — the provisional-at-submit variant of the exposure calculation, of which the handover-time figure of §3.6 is the second variant. Provisional exposure is evaluated once per element of `restriction_types`, against the policy effective on the local date of the *promised* delivery instant, and using each line's frozen `units_per_item_snapshot` rather than the live catalogue figure. Visiting the order's lines in `created_at` ascending order and skipping any line whose product does not carry the class under evaluation as well as any line whose `line_state` is not `INCLUDED`, the system multiplies the line's `quantity` by its `units_per_item_snapshot` and by the policy's `unit_scale_factor`, rounds that product half up to two decimal places, and writes it to the line's `line_restricted_units`; it then adds those rounded per-line values together and rounds the total half up to two decimal places again, exactly as §2.9 requires. The resulting figure per class is compared to that class's policy `max_units_per_order`, and exceeding it reports `ERR_ORDER_UNIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The order's `provisional_restricted_units` field carries this exposure figure.

The rolling window test is the aggregate half of the same rule. For the class under evaluation, the system anchors on `scheduled_delivery_at` and looks back over a half-open interval that begins `rolling_window_days` days before the anchor and ends at, but excludes, the anchor itself. Within that interval it visits the same customer's *other* orders — the order being submitted is always excluded — whose `fulfillment_state` is exactly `DELIVERED`, whose `delivered_at` falls at or after the window start and strictly before the anchor, and whose `restriction_types` contains the class under evaluation, in `delivered_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, and sums their `final_restricted_units`, rounding the sum half up to two decimal places. Orders sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `DISPATCHED`, `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`, `RETURN_IN_TRANSIT` or `RETURN_COMPLETED` contribute nothing. If the provisional exposure for the class plus that already-consumed figure exceeds the policy's `max_units_per_window`, the submission reports `ERR_WINDOW_UNIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Only once the whole battery has passed does the compliance dimension resolve, and it resolves purely on the `result` of the customer's newest `VALID` identity check. A `MATCH` routes `DRAFT_UNVERIFIED → PREVERIFIED`. A `PARTIAL` routes `DRAFT_UNVERIFIED → PENDING_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`, deferring the judgement to a human rather than blocking the order. A `NO_MATCH` routes `DRAFT_UNVERIFIED → COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED`. All three branches perform identically every other side effect of submission: the frozen subtotal and charge, the written `restriction_types` and `applied_policy_ids`, the provisional exposure with its per-line write-back, and the physical advance to `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_at` stamped. The difference between them is the compliance label alone. `COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED` is a terminal compliance state and renders the order permanently undeliverable: a dispatch request against an order in that compliance state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.

Because the battery is long and its failure mode is a single error code, the API also offers a **compliance evaluation** read: a non-mutating replay of the entire order-time battery against the *current* logical clock and the *current* policy rows, available to a `PlatformAdmin`, a `ComplianceOfficer` or the owning `Customer`. It returns the `order_id` it evaluated, the `restriction_types` and `applied_policy_ids` it resolved, the `provisional_restricted_units` total and a `per_type_exposure` array breaking that total down per restriction class, the `window_units_consumed` figure from the rolling look-back, the `local_delivery_minute` it derived and the boolean `in_delivery_window` verdict for it, the `purchaser_age_years` it computed alongside the `min_age_required` it compared that age against, the `identity_result` of the newest valid check, the `would_route_to` compliance state the order would land in, and `first_blocking_error_id` — the identifier of the first objection the battery would raise, or null when the replay finds none. Nothing is written; this is a diagnostic, not a rehearsal.

### 3.4. Clearance, Dispatch and the Delivery-Partner Posture Gate

A delivery partner is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `legal_name`, a `contract_tier` of `STANDARD`, `PREFERRED` or `PROBATIONARY`, and a `license_reference` that must be unique across all partners — a reused reference reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A new partner opens with `license_risk_score` and `lifetime_risk_score` at 0.00, `open_case_count` and `open_critical_case_count` at 0, and `posture` at `OPEN`. Partners can be listed with `posture` and `contract_tier` filters.

`posture` is never set by a request. It is a derived gate, recomputed immediately after every recomputation of the partner's rolling licence-risk score and after every change to either open-case counter, and it reads only the rolling-window score variant, never the lifetime one. The derivation is a two-step threshold ladder evaluated in this order: if the partner's `license_risk_score` is at or above 60.00, **or** its `open_critical_case_count` is at least 2, the posture becomes `SUSPENDED`; otherwise, if `license_risk_score` is at or above 25.00, **or** `open_case_count` is at least 3, the posture becomes `RESTRICTED`; otherwise it becomes `OPEN`. The two counters themselves are recounted from the partner's cases: `open_case_count` is the number of that partner's violation cases in `OPEN`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `PENDING_PARTNER_RESPONSE`, and `open_critical_case_count` is the number of cases in those same three states whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`.

When the derivation **worsens** a partner's posture — along `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, `RESTRICTED → SUSPENDED` or directly `OPEN → SUSPENDED` — a cascade reaches every order that partner is currently carrying. Each order of that partner whose `fulfillment_state` is `DISPATCHED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, is held: its `fulfillment_state` moves `DISPATCHED → HELD_AT_HUB`, its `dispatched_at` is cleared, and its `hold_reason` is set to the literal prefix `PARTNER_POSTURE_` followed by the partner's new posture value. In the same pass, an order whose `compliance_state` is `PREVERIFIED` moves `PREVERIFIED → PENDING_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` and an order whose `compliance_state` is `COMPLIANCE_CLEARED` moves `COMPLIANCE_CLEARED → PENDING_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`, so a held order needs a fresh human clearance before it can move again. Orders already `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` are deliberately **not** recalled — a courier standing at a door is left to finish or refuse — and orders in any other fulfilment state are untouched. Reaching `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED` also re-derives `dispatch_eligible` for every courier the partner employs, and reaching `SUSPENDED` forces `dispatch_eligible` to false for all of them.

When the derivation **improves** a posture — along `SUSPENDED → RESTRICTED` or `RESTRICTED → OPEN`, which happens when a reopen, a dismissal or a verified remediation lowers the score below the relevant thresholds — the partner's couriers have `dispatch_eligible` re-derived, but orders already sitting in `HELD_AT_HUB` are **not** automatically released. Releasing them requires a fresh dispatch.

Manual clearance is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s decision on an order parked in `PENDING_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`; from any other compliance state the request reports `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The `decision` field carries either `CLEAR` or `BLOCK`. A `CLEAR` decision requires the order's delivery partner to have a `posture` other than `SUSPENDED` — clearing an order into a suspended partner's hands reports `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED` — and moves `PENDING_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW → COMPLIANCE_CLEARED`, storing the acting `X-User-ID` in `clearance_actor_user_id` and clearing `hold_reason`. A `BLOCK` decision moves `PENDING_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW → COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED`, likewise storing the acting officer in `clearance_actor_user_id`, and additionally retracts the shipment: if the order's `fulfillment_state` is `DISPATCHED` or `HELD_AT_HUB` it is forced back to `SUBMITTED` and `dispatched_at` is cleared.

Dispatch is the partner's acceptance of the order for last-mile carriage, and only the `PartnerManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals the order's `delivery_partner_id` may perform it. It is admissible along `SUBMITTED → DISPATCHED` — the first dispatch — and along `HELD_AT_HUB → DISPATCHED`, which is the release edge that closes the `DISPATCHED → HELD_AT_HUB → DISPATCHED` cycle and may be traversed any number of times without ever touching the compliance dimension on the way back. Both edges require the order's `compliance_state` to be one of `AUTO_CLEARED`, `PREVERIFIED` or `COMPLIANCE_CLEARED`, and both require the partner's `posture` to be something other than `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`. On success the order's `fulfillment_state` becomes `DISPATCHED`, `dispatched_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `hold_reason` is cleared. A held order that a posture cascade also pushed into `PENDING_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` cannot be re-dispatched until an officer clears it first.

### 3.5. The Courier Workforce: Training, Certification and Eligibility

A `PartnerManager` registers each courier they employ with the `delivery_partner_id` of the employing partner, the courier's `full_name`, and a `hired_at` date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. A new courier opens at `compliance_score` 100.00, `compliant_handover_count` 0, `certification_state` `UNCERTIFIED` and `dispatch_eligible` false. Couriers can be listed with `delivery_partner_id` and `certification_state` filters.

Certification is per restriction class, not per courier. Issuing one records the `courier_id`, the `restriction_type` it authorises out of the five restricted classes, the `training_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive achieved on that class's training assessment, the `training_completed_at` date on which the assessment was taken, and a `valid_days` validity length between 30 and 1095 days inclusive. Issuance demands a `training_score` of at least 80; below that the request is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. On success the row stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, computes `expires_at` as `issued_at` plus `valid_days` days, opens in `state` `ACTIVE` with `renewal_count` 0 and `revocation_reason` null, and triggers a re-derivation of the courier's `certification_state` and `dispatch_eligible`. A courier's certifications can be listed with `restriction_type` and `state` filters, across every class and state.

A certification's `state` has four values and a cyclic lifecycle. `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` happens on its own, when a clock advance reaches or passes the row's `expires_at` during the sweep of §2.5. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` happens when the courier's recomputed `compliance_score` falls below 70.00 — a punitive edge described in §3.8. Both lapsed states can be repaired: renewal is admissible from `EXPIRED` and from `SUSPENDED`, must be requested by the `PartnerManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals the courier's employing `delivery_partner_id`, and carries a fresh `training_score`, `training_completed_at` and `valid_days`. The score bar differs by branch, and this asymmetry is the point of the two edges: reinstating an `EXPIRED` row demands a `training_score` of at least 80, while reinstating a `SUSPENDED` row demands remedial training scoring at least 90. Either bar being missed reports `ERR_TRAINING_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. Both branches additionally demand freshness — the logical clock now minus `training_completed_at` must be no more than 90 days, and staler training reports `ERR_TRAINING_STALE`. A successful renewal along `EXPIRED → ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` re-stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `expires_at` from the new `valid_days`, increments `renewal_count` by one, and re-derives the courier's `certification_state` and `dispatch_eligible`. Renewal requested against a row that is already `ACTIVE`, or that has been `REVOKED`, reports `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`.

Revocation is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s permanent act and is admissible from `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED` or `SUSPENDED`; only an already-`REVOKED` row refuses it, with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`. The request carries a `revocation_reason`, which is stored on the row. Beyond moving the `state` along `ACTIVE → REVOKED`, `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` or `EXPIRED → REVOKED` as the case may be, revocation re-derives the courier's `certification_state` and `dispatch_eligible` and aborts the courier's open delivery attempts in exactly the manner a suspension does — every attempt of that courier in `INITIATED`, `ID_VERIFIED` or `ID_REJECTED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, moves `INITIATED → ABORTED`, `ID_VERIFIED → ABORTED` or `ID_REJECTED → ABORTED` according to where it stood, and each such order moves `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`.

The courier-level `certification_state` is a pure derived function of the courier's certification rows and their `compliance_score`, re-derived after every event that touches either. When the `compliance_score` is below 40.00, the courier is `SUSPENDED` — the edge `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, when at least one of the courier's certifications is in `state` `ACTIVE`, the courier is `CERTIFIED`, which is how both `UNCERTIFIED → CERTIFIED` on a first issuance and `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` on a score recovery accompanied by a live certification are reached. Otherwise, with no `ACTIVE` certification but an acceptable score, the courier is `UNCERTIFIED`, which is the `CERTIFIED → UNCERTIFIED` edge taken when the last live certification expires, is suspended or is revoked. `dispatch_eligible` is then true exactly when `certification_state` is `CERTIFIED` **and** the employing partner's `posture` is something other than `SUSPENDED`; in every other combination it is false.

### 3.6. At the Door: the Attempt, the Identification Check and the Handover

A delivery attempt is opened by the courier who is physically at the address. The request names the `order_id` being delivered, the courier's own `courier_id`, and `started_at` — the courier-device instant the attempt began, which becomes the **delivery-time evaluation instant** for restricted hours, certification validity and recipient age alike. The order must be sitting in `fulfillment_state` `DISPATCHED`, from which it advances `DISPATCHED → OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`, and its `compliance_state` must be one of `AUTO_CLEARED`, `PREVERIFIED` or `COMPLIANCE_CLEARED`. The courier's employing `delivery_partner_id` must equal the order's `delivery_partner_id`, else `ERR_COURIER_PARTNER_MISMATCH`; the courier's derived `certification_state` must be `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_COURIER_NOT_CERTIFIED`; and the partner's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`.

Attempt creation is also where the jurisdictional and hours evaluation is run a *second* time, now against `started_at` rather than the promised time. Policy resolution repeats against the local calendar date of `started_at` and can report `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_POLICY` even for an order that submitted cleanly weeks earlier; the permissibility trio can report `ERR_DELIVERY_PROHIBITED_IN_JURISDICTION`, `ERR_DRY_JURISDICTION_PROHIBITED` or `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED`; and the half-open window test can report `ERR_OUTSIDE_RESTRICTED_HOURS` for a courier who arrived after closing. The attempt also reserves the courier's twenty-minute half-open dwell interval and can be refused with `ERR_COURIER_SLOT_CONFLICT` under the geometry of §2.10. A newly created attempt sits in `state` `INITIATED`, with `document_type`, `document_expires_on`, `scan_performed`, `scanned_dob`, `recipient_name`, `recipient_age_years`, `refusal_reason` and `abort_reason` all null, `observed_indicators` empty, and `id_check_compliant` false. Attempts can be listed with `order_id`, `courier_id` and `state` filters.

The door-side identification check is the fork at the heart of the system, and only the courier named by the attempt's `courier_id` may record it, from `state` `INITIATED` only — from any other state it reports `ERR_INVALID_ATTEMPT_STATE`. It carries the `document_type` presented, the `document_expires_on` date printed on it, a boolean `scan_performed`, the `scanned_dob` read from the document, and the `recipient_name` of whoever presented it. Before the document is examined, the courier's authority is confirmed once more: for every element of the order's `restriction_types` the courier must hold a certification for that class that is in `state` `ACTIVE` as at the attempt's `started_at`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING_FOR_RESTRICTION`.

The document itself is then examined in a fixed sub-order, and each of these four failures is a hard error rather than a branch. First, `document_type` must be a member of **every** selected policy's `acceptable_document_types`; if it is not, `ERR_DOCUMENT_TYPE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE` follows — and because an `acceptable_document_types` array may be empty, a policy with an empty list rejects every document presented to it, however well-formed. Second, if any selected policy has `requires_scan` true while `scan_performed` is false, `ERR_SCAN_REQUIRED` follows. Third, `document_expires_on` must be at or after the local calendar date of `started_at`; a document whose printed expiry falls strictly before that date reports `ERR_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`, so a document expiring on the delivery date itself is still acceptable. Fourth, `scanned_dob` must equal the purchaser's `attested_dob` exactly; any disagreement reports `ERR_RECIPIENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`.

Only when all four have passed does the age fork happen. The system computes `recipient_age_years` as the completed years between `scanned_dob` and `started_at` by calendar comparison, and writes it alongside the five document fields on both branches. If `recipient_age_years` is at least the largest `min_age_years` among the selected policies, the attempt takes `INITIATED → ID_VERIFIED` and `id_check_compliant` is set to true. If it falls below that threshold, the attempt takes `INITIATED → ID_REJECTED` and `id_check_compliant` is set to false. The `ID_REJECTED` branch is otherwise inert: **no violation case is opened**, because an attempt in `ID_REJECTED` represents a compliant courier reading the document correctly and declining to hand over. The order stays `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` until the courier separately records the refusal.

Evidence capture is a separate act, performed by the courier against the attempt, and it must happen before handover. Each capture records its `kind` — `SIGNATURE`, `PHOTO_ID` or `PHOTO_HANDOFF` — a `content_hash` that is the SHA-256 hex digest of the stored bytes as 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters and must be unique across every capture in the system (a repeat reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), and the `captured_at` instant the device captured it. The system computes `retention_until` as `captured_at` plus 2555 days — seven years — which is the regulatory audit retention horizon for the artefact. At most one capture of a given `kind` may exist per attempt, and a second of the same kind reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CAPTURE_KIND`. Captures are immutable audit rows: as §2.4 states, they carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no operation modifies or deletes them. An attempt's captures can be listed with an optional `kind` filter.

Handover completes the delivery, carries the `recipient_name` of the person who took it, and is admissible from exactly two attempt states. The ordinary path is `ID_VERIFIED → HANDED_OVER`, which requires a `SIGNATURE` capture and a `PHOTO_HANDOFF` capture to already exist for the attempt, plus a `PHOTO_ID` capture whenever any selected policy has `requires_scan` true; a missing artefact reports `ERR_CAPTURE_INCOMPLETE`. The second path is the empty-restriction edge `INITIATED → HANDED_OVER`, available only when the order's `restriction_types` is empty — nothing in the box is age-restricted, so no door-side identification is demanded at all — and requiring only the `SIGNATURE` and `PHOTO_HANDOFF` captures. Handover from any other attempt state reports `ERR_INVALID_ATTEMPT_STATE`.

Both handover paths advance the order `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → DELIVERED` and stamp the order's `delivered_at` with the attempt's `started_at` rather than with the logical clock. They differ in two respects. On the `ID_VERIFIED` path the system runs the **final** exposure evaluation and writes the result to the order's `final_restricted_units`; because `id_check_compliant` is true, it also increments the courier's `compliant_handover_count` by one and then recomputes that courier's `compliance_score`. On the empty-restriction path, `final_restricted_units` is set to 0.00, and because `id_check_compliant` remains false the courier's `compliant_handover_count` does **not** increase and no compliance credit is earned.

The final exposure evaluation — the final-at-handover variant of the same calculation — shares the arithmetic of the provisional one — same per-line rounding then sum rounding to two decimal places, same visit order by line `created_at` ascending, same restriction to lines whose `line_state` is `INCLUDED` — but differs in two inputs, and the difference is intentional. Its evaluation instant is the attempt's `started_at`, so it selects the policy effective on the local date of the **actual** doorstep instant rather than the promised one; and it multiplies by the **live** `restricted_units_per_item` read from the product row rather than the line's frozen `units_per_item_snapshot`. When a later policy row for the same jurisdiction and class carries a different `unit_scale_factor`, the two variants return different numbers from byte-identical order data — an order whose provisional exposure was 6.97 under a scale factor of 1.15 records a final exposure of 7.87 if the doorstep instant falls under a policy whose scale factor is 1.30.

### 3.7. Refusal, Abort, and the Return Settlement Waterfall

Refusal is mandatory rather than discretionary, and the reason the courier records determines whether an investigation follows. The `refusal_reason` field is one of `UNDERAGE_RECIPIENT`, `INTOXICATED_RECIPIENT`, `NO_ACCEPTABLE_ID`, `ID_MISMATCH`, `RECIPIENT_ABSENT` or `COURIER_FAULT`, and the courier may attach an `observed_indicators` array of free-text observations supporting an intoxication or impairment call. Which reasons are admissible depends on where the attempt stands, because the attempt's state records what the courier already knows. From `INITIATED`, before any document has been read, the permitted reasons are `NO_ACCEPTABLE_ID`, `INTOXICATED_RECIPIENT`, `RECIPIENT_ABSENT` and `COURIER_FAULT`. From `ID_REJECTED`, where the document was read and the recipient was too young, the permitted reasons are `UNDERAGE_RECIPIENT`, `ID_MISMATCH` and `NO_ACCEPTABLE_ID`. From `ID_VERIFIED`, where age has already been confirmed, the permitted reasons are `INTOXICATED_RECIPIENT`, `RECIPIENT_ABSENT` and `COURIER_FAULT` — an age-verified recipient who is visibly impaired must still be refused. A reason outside the set permitted from the current state is rejected with `ERR_REFUSAL_REASON_NOT_ALLOWED`, and a refusal recorded from any state other than those three reports `ERR_INVALID_ATTEMPT_STATE`.

A recorded refusal fires a four-link cascade in which each link exists only because the previous one did. **Link one** moves the attempt `INITIATED → REFUSED`, `ID_VERIFIED → REFUSED` or `ID_REJECTED → REFUSED` according to where it stood, and the order `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`; it happens on every refusal without exception. **Link two** fires only when link one recorded a *compliance* refusal — that is, when `refusal_reason` is one of `UNDERAGE_RECIPIENT`, `ID_MISMATCH`, `NO_ACCEPTABLE_ID` or `INTOXICATED_RECIPIENT` — and opens a violation case automatically: its `origin` is `AUTO_REFUSAL`, its `delivery_attempt_id` names this attempt, its `delivery_partner_id` copies the order's, its `state` opens at `OPEN`, and its `severity` is read off a fixed mapping from the refusal reason — `UNDERAGE_RECIPIENT` maps to `CRITICAL`, `ID_MISMATCH` to `MAJOR`, `INTOXICATED_RECIPIENT` to `MAJOR` and `NO_ACCEPTABLE_ID` to `MINOR`. **Link three** fires only because link two created a case: the partner's `open_case_count` and `open_critical_case_count` are recounted and the posture derivation of §3.4 runs. **Link four** fires only because link three changed the posture: that partner's `DISPATCHED` orders are held and pushed back to compliance review, in `created_at` ascending order. A refusal for `COURIER_FAULT` or `RECIPIENT_ABSENT` therefore stops after link one — no case is opened, so no counters change, so no posture moves and no orders are held.

Independently of the case cascade, every refusal recomputes the purchaser's `refusal_strikes`. That counter is the number of the customer's delivery attempts, across all of their orders, that sit in `REFUSED` with a `refusal_reason` of `UNDERAGE_RECIPIENT`, `INTOXICATED_RECIPIENT`, `ID_MISMATCH` or `NO_ACCEPTABLE_ID`; `RECIPIENT_ABSENT` and `COURIER_FAULT` refusals never count against the customer. When that count reaches 3, the customer's `blocked_until` is set to the strike-triggering attempt's `started_at` plus 30 days, which is what the purchaser-eligibility check of §3.3 later reads to raise `ERR_CUSTOMER_DELIVERY_BLOCKED`. Below the third strike, `blocked_until` remains null.

There is a second way an open attempt ends without a refusal. When a courier's certification is suspended by a compliance-score collapse, or revoked by an officer, every attempt of that courier in `INITIATED`, `ID_VERIFIED` or `ID_REJECTED` is aborted: the attempt moves `INITIATED → ABORTED`, `ID_VERIFIED → ABORTED` or `ID_REJECTED → ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to the literal `CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED`, the order moves `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`, and the order's `hold_reason` is set to the literal `COURIER_CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED`. Aborted attempts open no violation case and levy no customer strike; the fault lay with the credential, not the doorstep.

Whichever route sent the goods back, a `PlatformAdmin` closes the loop with a return settlement, admissible only while the order's `fulfillment_state` is `RETURN_IN_TRANSIT` and otherwise reporting `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The request carries `restock_confirmed`, which must be true; a settlement requested with it false is refused with `ERR_RESTOCK_NOT_CONFIRMED`. The settlement runs a waterfall whose **evaluation order is pinned**, and getting that order wrong changes the money. First the governing reason is identified: it is the `refusal_reason` of the `REFUSED` attempt of this order with the greatest `started_at`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. Second, the gross refundable amount is taken as the order's frozen `item_subtotal_cents`, **before** any fee is deducted. Third, the restocking tier is looked up **from that gross figure** and from nothing else: a gross below 5000 cents attracts no restocking fee at all, a gross of 5000 or more but below 20000 cents attracts 500 basis points, and a gross of 20000 cents or more attracts 800 basis points. Keying the tier on the post-fee refund — the reading invited by describing the fee as a percentage of a refund — is forbidden and yields a different number.

Fourth, the fee itself: a refusal for `COURIER_FAULT` waives the restocking fee entirely and sets `restocking_fee_cents` to 0, and for every other reason the fee is the gross figure multiplied by the tier's basis points, divided by ten thousand, and **floored** to whole cents. Fifth, the delivery fee is refunded only when the platform was at fault: a `COURIER_FAULT` refusal refunds the order's `delivery_fee_cents` in full, and every other reason refunds none of it. Finally the settlement is written: `refunded_cents` is the gross figure minus `restocking_fee_cents` plus any refunded delivery fee, and `retained_cents` is the order's `charged_cents` minus `refunded_cents`, which makes the conservation invariant of §2.9 hold by construction. Worked through: an order with `item_subtotal_cents` of 20400 and `delivery_fee_cents` of 600 refused for `UNDERAGE_RECIPIENT` takes a gross of 20400, lands in the 800-basis-point tier, is charged a floored fee of 1632, and settles at `refunded_cents` 18768 against `retained_cents` 2232.

Settlement also releases the regulatory units the order had consumed. Every line of the order moves `line_state` `INCLUDED → WITHDRAWN`, the order's `final_restricted_units` is reset to 0.00, and the order's `fulfillment_state` advances `RETURN_IN_TRANSIT → RETURN_COMPLETED`. Because the rolling-window aggregate of §3.3 counts only orders whose `fulfillment_state` is exactly `DELIVERED`, and because the reset zeroes the figure it would have summed, a refused-and-returned order stops consuming the customer's rolling quota on both counts.

### 3.8. Secret-Shopper Testing and the Courier Compliance Score

Independent assurance comes from the `SecretShopperAuditor`, who stages controlled compliance probes against individual couriers. Scheduling a test records the `courier_id` under test, the `order_id` of the decoy order used to stage it, the `scenario_type` staged at the door — one of `UNDERAGE_PRESENTER`, `NO_ID_PRESENTED`, `EXPIRED_DOCUMENT` or `INTOXICATION_CUES` — the planned `scheduled_at` instant, and the `expected_outcome`, which is the compliant behaviour the auditor expects and is either `REFUSAL` or `HANDOVER`. A newly scheduled test opens with `verdict` `PENDING`, and with `observed_outcome`, `findings` and `recorded_at` all null. Tests can be listed with `courier_id`, `order_id` and `verdict` filters, and an unknown identifier reports `ERR_SHOPPER_TEST_NOT_FOUND`.

Recording the outcome carries the `observed_outcome` actually seen — again `REFUSAL` or `HANDOVER` — and an optional `findings` narrative. The `verdict` is derived by comparison: `PASS` when `observed_outcome` equals `expected_outcome`, and `FAIL` when it does not, with `recorded_at` stamped from the logical clock. A `FAIL` verdict also opens a violation case automatically, with `origin` `AUTO_SHOPPER_FAIL`, the failed test named in `secret_shopper_test_id`, and `severity` `MAJOR`.

Beyond the case, a recorded outcome sets off a three-link cascade on the courier. **Link one** recomputes the courier's `compliance_score`. **Link two** fires only if that recomputed score falls below 70.00: every certification of that courier whose `state` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. **Link three** fires only because those certifications were suspended: every delivery attempt of that courier in `INITIATED`, `ID_VERIFIED` or `ID_REJECTED` is aborted and its order sent back as a return, exactly as §3.7 describes.

The `compliance_score` itself is rebuilt from scratch on every recomputation — never adjusted by a delta — and it is recomputed on four occasions: when a secret-shopper outcome is recorded, when a violation case is decided, when a decided case is reopened, and when a compliant handover completes. It starts from a base of 100.00. Then every secret-shopper test of that courier whose `verdict` is `FAIL`, with no time window at all and visited in `recorded_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, subtracts 25.00. Then every violation case whose `state` is `SUBSTANTIATED` or `CLOSED_VERIFIED`, whose `delivery_attempt_id` is not null, and whose named attempt's `courier_id` is this courier, visited in `decided_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, subtracts an amount keyed on its `assessed_severity`: 8.00 for `MINOR`, 18.00 for `MAJOR` and 35.00 for `CRITICAL`. Then a good-behaviour bonus is added: one point per compliant handover as counted by `compliant_handover_count`, capped at 10.00 however many handovers the courier has made. The base, the penalties and the bonus are summed, the total is rounded half up to two decimal places, and the result is clamped into the inclusive range 0.00 to 100.00. Two failed secret-shopper tests and nothing else therefore leave a courier at 50.00, which is under the 70.00 certification-suspension line and consequently suspends every `ACTIVE` certification that courier holds — while still being above the 40.00 line at which the courier's own `certification_state` would read `SUSPENDED`.

### 3.9. Violation Investigation, Licence Risk and Corrective Action

Cases arrive in three ways, recorded in `origin`: `AUTO_REFUSAL` for the refusal cascade of §3.7, `AUTO_SHOPPER_FAIL` for a failed secret-shopper test, and `MANUAL` when a `ComplianceOfficer` opens one directly. A manual case names the accountable `delivery_partner_id`, an asserted `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, a `summary` narrating the alleged violation, and exactly one subject: either a `delivery_attempt_id` or a `secret_shopper_test_id`, never both and never neither. Supplying both, or supplying neither, is refused with `ERR_CASE_SUBJECT_INVALID`. A new case opens with `state` `OPEN`, `investigation_rounds` 0, `risk_contribution` 0.00, and `assessed_severity`, `decided_at`, `substantiated_by_user_id`, `response_due_at`, `partner_response_text` and `notes` all null; opening it recounts the partner's `open_case_count` and `open_critical_case_count` and re-derives that partner's `posture`. Cases can be listed with `delivery_partner_id`, `delivery_attempt_id`, `secret_shopper_test_id`, `state`, `severity` and `origin` filters — the `severity` filter matching the asserted `severity` carried on the case rather than its `assessed_severity` — and an unknown identifier reports `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`.

The investigation is a cyclic workflow, and every transition out of a state the case is not in reports `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. An officer opens the investigation, moving `OPEN → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and incrementing `investigation_rounds`. From `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` an officer may put questions to the accountable partner, moving `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → PENDING_PARTNER_RESPONSE`, recording an optional `notes` narrative and a `response_due_at` deadline that must lie strictly later than the logical clock now — a deadline at or before now is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_DUE_NOT_FUTURE`. The `PartnerManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals the case's `delivery_partner_id` answers with a `partner_response_text`, moving `PENDING_PARTNER_RESPONSE → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and incrementing `investigation_rounds` again. This is the first of the case's two cycles and it may be traversed any number of times.

Deciding the case ends the investigation, at least for now. The `decision` field carries `SUBSTANTIATED` or `DISMISSED`, an optional `notes` narrative is recorded, and both branches are admissible only from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` — along `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → SUBSTANTIATED` and `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → DISMISSED` respectively — and stamp `decided_at` from the logical clock — the anchor from which risk decay is later measured. A `SUBSTANTIATED` decision additionally requires `assessed_severity` to be supplied, which is the severity confirmed at decision time and the one that drives scoring; it stores the acting `X-User-ID` in `substantiated_by_user_id`, writes the case's own `risk_contribution`, recounts the partner's `open_case_count` and `open_critical_case_count`, recomputes both of the partner's risk-score variants, re-derives the partner's `posture` with its cascade onto that partner's `DISPATCHED` orders in `created_at` ascending order, and — when the case's subject is a delivery attempt — recomputes the `compliance_score` of that attempt's courier. A `DISMISSED` decision is the mirror image: `decided_at` is stamped, `substantiated_by_user_id` is left null, `assessed_severity` is set to null and `risk_contribution` to 0.00, and the partner's counters, both risk-score variants and posture are recomputed all the same, because a case leaving the open states changes the counters even though it contributes no points.

Reopening is the compensating action, and it is admissible along `SUBSTANTIATED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and along `DISMISSED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, with a required `notes` narrative. It clears `decided_at`, clears `assessed_severity`, clears `substantiated_by_user_id` and resets `risk_contribution` to 0.00; it increments `investigation_rounds` and advances the case's `version` by one; it recomputes both partner risk-score variants with this case removed from the substantiated set and re-derives the posture; and it marks every corrective action plan of this case whose `state` is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `FAILED_VERIFICATION` as `SUPERSEDED` — `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` and likewise from each of the other three non-terminal plan states. The invariant this preserves is exact and is worth stating as an acceptance criterion: substantiating a case, reopening it, and then dismissing it must return the partner's `license_risk_score` to precisely the value it held before the first decision.

Licence risk is computed in two variants that share one skeleton. Both begin with the partner's tier multiplier, read from `contract_tier`: 1.00 for `STANDARD`, 0.80 for `PREFERRED` and 1.50 for `PROBATIONARY`. Both then walk that partner's violation cases whose `state` is `SUBSTANTIATED` or `CLOSED_VERIFIED`, visited in `decided_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending order and never by identifier; cases in `OPEN`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `PENDING_PARTNER_RESPONSE` or `DISMISSED` contribute no points at all and influence posture only through the two open-case counters. For each visited case the system computes its age in whole days as the elapsed time from `decided_at` to the logical clock now, divided by a day and rounded down. It then takes the severity points for the case's `assessed_severity` — 5 for `MINOR`, 15 for `MAJOR`, 40 for `CRITICAL` — multiplies them by the variant's age weight, halves the product when the case has reached `CLOSED_VERIFIED` (the verified-remediation credit factor of 0.50), rounds that per-case term half up to two decimal places, and accumulates it. Finally the accumulated total is multiplied by the tier multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places, and capped at a ceiling of 100.00.

The variants differ only in inclusion and weighting. The **rolling** variant, written to `license_risk_score`, includes only cases whose age is 90 days or fewer and weights every included case at 1.00 — a case older than ninety days vanishes from it entirely. This is the only variant that drives posture derivation. The **lifetime** variant, written to `lifetime_risk_score`, includes every substantiated case forever and weights each by a decaying factor: one full point minus a tenth for each complete 90-day step of age, floored at a quarter point, so the weight never decays below 0.25. A `STANDARD` partner holding a `CRITICAL` case decided 28 days ago, a `MAJOR` case decided 23 days ago and a `MINOR` case decided 240 days ago therefore scores 55.00 on the rolling variant and 59.00 on the lifetime variant from exactly the same rows. Each case's `risk_contribution` field carries that case's own per-case rounded term under the **rolling** variant, and is 0.00 whenever the case's `state` is anything other than `SUBSTANTIATED`.

Because the two variants diverge, a dedicated **risk assessment** read exposes them side by side against the current logical clock without mutating anything. It returns the `delivery_partner_id` it assessed, the `evaluated_at` instant it used, the partner's `contract_tier` and the `tier_multiplier` that tier implies, the `rolling_90d_score` and `lifetime_weighted_score` figures, the current `open_case_count`, `open_critical_case_count` and `posture`, and a `case_breakdown` array. Each breakdown element reports one case's `violation_case_id`, `assessed_severity` and `decided_at`, the `age_days` computed for it, the boolean `included_in_rolling` flag, its `rolling_points` contribution, the `lifetime_weight` applied to it and the resulting `lifetime_points`; the array is ordered by `decided_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending.

Remediation is the partner's obligation once a case is substantiated. The `PartnerManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals the case's `delivery_partner_id` files a corrective action plan against a case whose `state` is `SUBSTANTIATED` — from any other case state the filing reports `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` — supplying `expected_violation_case_version` as §2.8 requires, a `remediation_items` array of between 1 and 12 commitment strings inclusive, and a `due_at` instant by which every item will be complete. A `due_at` at or before the logical clock now is refused with `ERR_INCONSISTENT_FIELDS`. The new plan opens in `state` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` 0 and `reviewed_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id`, `verification_outcome` and `notes` all null; filing it marks every earlier plan of the same case whose `state` is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `FAILED_VERIFICATION` as `SUPERSEDED` — the edge `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` and its counterparts from the other three non-terminal plan states — and advances the case's `version` by one. A superseded plan is terminal and can never earn the verified-remediation credit. Plans of a case can be listed with a `state` filter, and an unknown plan identifier reports `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.

Review is the officer's first pass, admissible only from `SUBMITTED` and only while the parent case is still `SUBSTANTIATED`, and always subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1. Its `decision` field carries `ACCEPT`, moving the plan `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, or `REQUEST_REVISION`, moving it `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`; an optional `notes` narrative is recorded, and in both branches the acting `X-User-ID` is stored in `reviewed_by_user_id`. Transitions attempted from any other plan state report `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`.

Resubmission is the partner's revision loop and closes the plan's cycle. It is admissible along `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and along `FAILED_VERIFICATION → SUBMITTED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the parent case's `delivery_partner_id`, and requires the plan's `revision_round` to be strictly below 3; once it has reached 3 the request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, so the fourth resubmission never lands. A successful resubmission replaces the plan's `remediation_items` and `due_at`, increments `revision_round` by one, and returns the plan to `SUBMITTED` for another review.

Verification closes the file. Admissible only from `ACCEPTED`, again subject to segregation of duties, it carries a `verification_outcome` of `PASSED` or `FAILED` plus optional `notes`, and stores the acting `X-User-ID` in `verified_by_user_id`. A `PASSED` outcome moves the plan `ACCEPTED → VERIFIED` and drives the parent case `SUBSTANTIATED → CLOSED_VERIFIED`, which halves that case's contribution through the verified-remediation credit factor of 0.50, recomputes both of the partner's risk-score variants, and re-derives the partner's `posture` — the mechanism by which a partner earns its way back from `SUSPENDED` to `RESTRICTED` or from `RESTRICTED` to `OPEN`. A `FAILED` outcome moves the plan `ACCEPTED → FAILED_VERIFICATION` and leaves the parent case in `SUBSTANTIATED` with no credit applied, from where the partner may resubmit within the revision ceiling.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is exposed at the root of the service; there is no version or product path prefix. Requests that bring a new row into existence answer with HTTP 201 Created; every other successful request — reads, listings, state transitions, computed evaluations and clock control — answers with HTTP 200 OK. Path parameters are always written `{id}` and are always resolved left to right as §2.6 describes. Every listing endpoint accepts the `limit` and `offset` parameters of §2.3 in addition to the filters named in its description, and every endpoint requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers of §2.1.

| Method | Path | Description | Authorized Role(s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `POST` | `/_clock/set_current_utc` | Sets the logical clock to an arbitrary UTC instant and runs the three-step epoch sweep in the fixed order of §2.5. Rejects any instant earlier than the current clock value. | PlatformAdmin |
| `POST` | `/jurisdictions` | Registers a regulatory jurisdiction with its dry status and fixed UTC offset. | PlatformAdmin |
| `GET` | `/jurisdictions/{id}` | Reads one jurisdiction including its derived active policy count. | PlatformAdmin, ComplianceOfficer, PartnerManager |
