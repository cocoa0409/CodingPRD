# Build-To-Rent Leaseup API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A build-to-rent developer does something that neither a homebuilder nor a conventional apartment operator does: it builds a neighbourhood of detached homes, cottages and townhomes, keeps every one of them under a single ownership, and then leases them out section by section while the rest of the community is still a construction site. That overlap is the whole business problem. Concrete trucks and prospective residents share the same streets. A model home opens for tours before the clubhouse pool has water in it. A household signs a lease on a finished cul-de-sac while the phase two blocks away is still pouring foundations. Every commercial decision — how aggressively to concession, when to raise asking rents, when to hand the community to a third-party property manager, when to convert the construction loan to permanent financing — is made against numbers that are still moving.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that operation. The service tracks communities and their planned product mix, the entitlement and platting package that must be approved before a shovel moves, the construction phases and their dependency chain, the individual homesites and their certificates of occupancy, the published market rent schedules, the concession programs and their amortization, the households and leases, the move-out turn work orders, and the absorption reviews that tell the asset manager whether lease-up is running ahead of or behind plan. It also computes the two valuations the capital stack argues about — the lender's trailing-actual view and the sponsor's forward-stabilized view — from exactly the same stored data.

The organising idea in the design is a single derived gate. Each community carries a computed **posture** that is recalculated from its own children after every mutation and that decides, on its own, whether leasing, pricing and inventory operations may proceed at all. When a housing phase is halted, the posture freezes the community, homesites come off the market, unsigned leases are cancelled, and committed concession money is released back to its budgets — all inside the same request. When the halt is resumed, the freeze clears and the parked inventory comes back. Every rule in the sections that follow is written so that a caller can predict, from the state it can already read, exactly what the next request will do.

The service is mounted at the server root; every collection in this specification is addressed from its own top-level path, and resources are linked to one another by identifier fields carried in request bodies rather than by nesting.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` names the role that user is acting in. If `X-User-Role` is absent, blank, or names something outside the role catalogue below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); the same rejection applies when the caller's role is `Household` and `X-User-ID` is absent or blank. Once authenticated, the caller's role is checked against the set of roles authorised for the endpoint it is invoking, listed in the table in §4. A role outside that set receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Seven roles exist, and their names are used verbatim in the value of `X-User-Role`:

- **`DeveloperPrincipal`** — the owner-developer. Controls site setup, plat submissions, phase creation, homesite creation and the property-management transition.
- **`EntitlementOfficer`** — the municipal reviewer who decides plats. Must never be the same person who submitted the plat under review.
- **`ConstructionManager`** — runs construction phasing, records certificates of occupancy, performs incremental section releases, and halts and resumes phases.
- **`LeasingAgent`** — registers households, drafts and submits leases, and raises concession requests. May never decide a concession.
- **`AssetManager`** — owns pricing, absorption reviews, concession decisions, lease cancellations and portfolio financing.
- **`PropertyManager`** — the post-transition operator. Activates leases, posts concession credits and runs the turn process.
- **`Household`** — the resident-side actor. May sign and give notice on its own lease, and read its own lease and its own household record.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `DeveloperPrincipal`, `EntitlementOfficer`, `ConstructionManager`, `LeasingAgent`, `AssetManager` or `PropertyManager`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a rule requires an actor to be remembered — as `plat_submitted_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a community and a rent schedule, as `concession_requested_by_user_id` and `concession_decided_by_user_id` on a lease, and as `created_by_user_id` on an absorption review — but it never compares such an identifier against any entity's `id`.

The `Household` role is different: it is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Household`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Household` record, which is why the identifier returned when a household is registered is the value that household must then send. Ownership checks compare a resource's household foreign key to `X-User-ID`: a `Household` caller may read or act on a lease only when that lease's `household_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and may read a household record only when that record is its own. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards exactly three operations — fetching a single lease, signing a lease, and giving notice on a lease.

Two duties in this system are deliberately segregated, and both are enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` values rather than roles. The `EntitlementOfficer` who decides a plat must not be the operator recorded in that community's `plat_submitted_by_user_id`, and the `AssetManager` who decides a concession must not be the operator recorded in that lease's `concession_requested_by_user_id`. Either collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the pipeline is deterministic, two requests with the same `limit` and `offset` against unchanged data return the same page in the same order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were actually applied, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters before the page window is taken, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when records remain beyond the current window.

Filters, where an endpoint offers them, are combined conjunctively and are applied before pagination. Communities may be filtered by `lifecycle_status` and by `posture`. Phases may be filtered by `community_id`, by `predecessor_phase_id` — which is how a caller walks the phasing chain — and by `status` and `kind`. Homesites may be filtered by `phase_id`, `product_type` and `status`. Market rent schedules may be filtered by `community_id`, `product_type` and `status`, which is the normal way to locate the currently published one. Concession programs may be filtered by `community_id` and `status`. Leases may be filtered by `homesite_id`, `household_id`, `concession_program_id`, `priced_by_schedule_id`, `lease_status` and `concession_status`. Turn work orders may be filtered by `homesite_id`, `scope` and `status`. Absorption reviews may be filtered by `community_id` and `product_type`. Household listing takes no filters beyond pagination.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix, never supplied by the client — and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, formatted identically, and an integer `version`. A newly created resource has `version` equal to 1 and `updated_at` equal to `created_at`. Thereafter, every mutation that changes the resource increments `version` by exactly 1 and refreshes `updated_at`, including mutations that reach the resource indirectly as part of a cascade rather than being addressed directly by the request. Communities, phases, homesites, market rent schedules, concession programs, households, leases and turn work orders all behave this way.

`AbsorptionReview` is the single exception. An absorption review is an immutable snapshot: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither is present in its response payload.

All instants exchanged by this API — request fields and response fields alike — are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. This applies to `construction_window_start`, `construction_window_end`, `occupancy_certificate_issued_at`, `released_at`, `last_turn_ready_at`, `effective_from`, `superseded_at`, `pm_effective_from`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `executed_at`, `notice_date`, `move_out_date`, `window_start`, `window_end` and `completed_at`. A field that has not yet been set is present in the payload with a null value rather than omitted.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. A request addressed at a community that does not exist fails with `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before anything nested under it is considered; the same pattern gives `ERR_PHASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RENT_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONCESSION_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURN_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ABSORPTION_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404, for the collection actually addressed.

Identifier fields inside a request body are resolved with the same codes and the same 404 status. An unknown `community_id` on a phase, rent schedule or concession program yields `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `phase_id` on a homesite, or an unknown `predecessor_phase_id` on a phase, yields `ERR_PHASE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `homesite_id` on a lease or a turn work order, or any unknown member of the `homesite_ids` list on a section release, yields `ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `household_id` on a lease yields `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`; and an unknown `concession_program_id` on a concession request yields `ERR_CONCESSION_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`.

Nothing in this system is addressed through a nested collection path. A phase names its community by carrying `community_id`; a homesite names its phase by carrying `phase_id`; a lease names the home and the leaseholder by carrying `homesite_id` and `household_id`. The chain from a lease up to a community therefore runs through the homesite to its phase and from the phase to its community, and rules written in terms of "the lease's community" mean exactly that traversal.

### 2.6. The Community Posture Gate

`posture` is the single most important derived value in the system. It is a computed field on the community, it takes one of exactly three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — and it is never supplied by a client. A freshly created community starts at `FROZEN`. The value is recomputed at the end of every request that mutates the community itself or any of its phases, homesites or leases, and the recomputed value is what the response reports.

The recomputation is a three-way decision evaluated in the following order, and the first matching branch wins:

1. The posture is `FROZEN` if any of these three conditions holds: the community's `plat_status` is anything other than `APPROVED`; or its `single_ownership_covenant` is false; or at least one phase of the community whose `kind` is `HOUSING` currently has `status` equal to `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED`.
2. Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` if either of these two conditions holds: the community's `released_unit_count` is at least 1 while its `amenity_delivery_ratio` is strictly below 0.5000; or at least one phase of the community whose `kind` is `AMENITY` currently has `status` equal to `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED`.
3. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

The amenity gate in branch 2 is a strict comparison: a community whose `amenity_delivery_ratio` is exactly 0.5000 does **not** trip the gate, while 0.4999 does.

Five aggregate counters on the community are recomputed in the same pass, from all phases and homesites of the community regardless of their phase status. `released_unit_count` is the number of homesites of the community whose `released_at` is not null. `available_unit_count` is the number of homesites whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`. `leased_unit_count` is the number of homesites whose `status` is either `LEASED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` — a home on notice still counts as occupied. `occupancy_ratio` is `leased_unit_count` divided by `total_planned_units`, rounded half up to four decimal places. `amenity_delivery_ratio` is the sum of `amenity_weight` over the community's `AMENITY` phases whose `status` is `COMPLETE`, divided by the sum of `amenity_weight` over all of the community's `AMENITY` phases, rounded half up to four decimal places; when the community has no `AMENITY` phase at all the ratio is exactly 1.0000 and no division is attempted. Leases never feed the posture decision directly — they influence it only through the homesite statuses they cause.

What the posture gates is spelled out where each operation is described, but the shape of the gate is uniform. While the posture is `FROZEN`, no leasing, pricing, concession or inventory-release write may proceed, and the rejection carries `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). Six writes are subject to this freeze: `POST /phases/{id}/section-releases`, `POST /market-rent-schedules`, `POST /concession-programs`, `POST /leases`, `POST /leases/{id}/submissions` and `POST /leases/{id}/signature`. While the posture is `RESTRICTED`, lease drafting, lease submission and lease signature remain permitted, but two writes are still refused with `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409): `POST /concession-programs` and `POST /phases/{id}/section-releases`. The restricted check is evaluated immediately after the frozen check.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Community Writes

Two writes in this system create a child record that retires an existing one, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent community's `version`.

`POST /market-rent-schedules` must carry `expected_community_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` the client last observed on the target community. If that value does not equal the community's current `version`, the publication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMMUNITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters because publication supersedes the prior published schedule for the same community and product type, and an absorption-indexed publication derives its asking rent from that prior schedule — a client working from a stale view would reprice off a baseline that has already been retired.

`POST /concession-programs` likewise requires `expected_community_version` matching the community's current `version`, and likewise fails with `ERR_STALE_COMMUNITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412) on any mismatch. Here the new program legitimately supersedes the community's current active program, so a writer holding a stale view would retire a program it never observed and publish a concession tier derived from an absorption review it never saw.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Every other write in this specification, including the community's own state-transition writes, takes no `expected_community_version` and performs no version comparison. The current value a client should send is the `version` field returned by fetching the community.

### 2.8. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All money in this API is expressed as an integer number of cents, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. No monetary value is ever a decimal or a string. All growth and rate figures are expressed in basis points — hundredths of one percent — in fields whose names end in `_bps`; a value of 250 basis points is two and a half percent.

Wherever a calculation produces a non-integral cent amount, the result is rounded half up: a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. The two derived ratios on a community, `occupancy_ratio` and `amenity_delivery_ratio`, are held to four decimal places and rounded half up; so is an absorption review's `pacing_index`. Where a formula in §3 names more than one rounding step, those steps are performed in the order the prose states them, and no intermediate result is truncated or re-rounded out of order. Where a formula performs no division at all — because every factor is already an integer — no rounding is required and none is applied.

`*_cents` and `*_count` fields submitted in a request body must be non-negative integers. The two basis-point fields that represent growth, `recommended_growth_bps` on an absorption review and `applied_growth_bps` on a rent schedule, are the exceptions: they may legitimately be negative, because rent growth may be negative.

### 2.9. The Logical Clock

The service runs on a logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that window, effective-date and activation rules are testable. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. Every rule in this specification that speaks of "now", "the current instant" or "already in the past" reads this logical clock.

The clock is advanced by `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which takes a single field `current_utc` and returns the new clock value in a `current_utc` response field with HTTP 200. Any role may drive it. The clock may only move forward: a `current_utc` at or before the stored value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 409). Advancing the clock has one business side effect — any community parked in `PM_TRANSITION_PENDING` whose `pm_effective_from` is now at or before the new clock value flips to `PM_MANAGED`, with its `version` incremented.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by a fixed evaluation order. Checks are grouped and evaluated as follows, and the first group that produces a failure stops the request:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and role recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. **Authorisation** — the role is on the endpoint's list (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. **Ownership** — a `Household` caller acting on someone else's lease (`ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`).
4. **Segregation of duties** — the decider is the submitter or requester (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
5. **Path existence** — every path identifier resolves (the family of `..._NOT_FOUND` codes).
6. **Body reference existence** — every identifier field in the body resolves (the same family of codes).
7. **Schema validity** — the body parses, required fields are present and non-null, enum values are declared members, numeric and instant formats are correct (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — `expected_community_version` matches, on the two endpoints that carry it (`ERR_STALE_COMMUNITY_VERSION`).
9. **Own-state legality** — the addressed entity is in a state from which the requested transition exists (`ERR_INVALID_PLAT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_HOMESITE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CONCESSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TURN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MANAGEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_ALREADY_SUPERSEDED`).
10. **Posture gate** — frozen first (`ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`), then restricted (`ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`).
11. **Cross-entity state** — the related entities are in compatible states (`ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_LEASABLE`, then `ERR_CONCESSION_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_IN_TURN`).
12. **Business safety gates** — in this order: `ERR_OCCUPANCY_CERTIFICATE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_SINGLE_OWNERSHIP_COVENANT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_MAINTENANCE_STAFFING_REQUIRED`, `ERR_LEASE_UP_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`.
13. **Business constraints** — in this order: `ERR_PLAT_REVISION_LIMIT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_ALREADY_LEASED`, `ERR_PRODUCT_MIX_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_PREDECESSOR`.
14. **Temporal constraints** — in this order: `ERR_TURN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_PHASE_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT`, `ERR_INVALID_TURN_WINDOW`, `ERR_TURN_REWORK_BUFFER`, `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_WINDOW`, `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND`, `ERR_PHASE_GAP_VIOLATION`.
15. **Aggregate capacity** — `ERR_TURN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
16. **Referential business rules** — in this order: `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RENT_SCHEDULE`, `ERR_PRODUCT_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_CONCESSION_PERIOD_OUT_OF_ORDER`.
17. **Fiscal rules** — in this order: `ERR_CONCESSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_INCOME_QUALIFICATION_FAILED`, `ERR_LTV_COVENANT_BREACH`.

The practical consequence worth internalising is that a posture freeze surfaces as a posture error rather than being masked by a downstream business rule, and that a stale `expected_community_version` surfaces before any state or posture reasoning is done.

### 2.11. Cascades, Compensation and the Concession Ledger Invariant

Several writes in this system are not single-entity updates. A signature reaches the homesite, the phase, the community counters, the community lifecycle and the financing facility. A construction halt reaches the community posture, then the homesites, then their unsigned leases, then the concession programs backing those leases. These are described in §3 as ordered cascade hops, and the ordering matters: each hop is caused by the hop before it, so a hop whose precondition has not been created by its predecessor simply does not fire. All hops of a cascade complete inside the originating request — consistency in this system is immediate, never deferred — and every entity a hop touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed.

Where a cascade iterates over a collection of sibling records, it does so in `created_at` ascending order, so that a caller reading the affected records afterwards sees a deterministic result. Where a cascade iterates over a list supplied by the client, it does so in the order the client supplied.

Two of the writes are explicitly compensating actions: cancelling a lease and resuming a halted phase. A compensating action reverses the effects of the forward action it undoes, but it is not a rollback of history. In particular, a homesite's `released_at` is never rewritten, so a community's `released_unit_count` is unchanged by a halt, by a resume, by a cancellation or by a turn — once a home has been released to leasing it stays counted as released for the rest of its life.

One numeric invariant must hold after every forward action and every compensating action in this specification. A community's `committed_concession_cents` must always equal the sum of `concession_remaining_cents` across the leases of that community whose `concession_status` is `APPROVED` or `AMORTIZING`. The parallel invariant holds per program: a `ConcessionProgram`'s `committed_cents` must always equal the sum of `concession_remaining_cents` across the leases carrying that `concession_program_id` whose `concession_status` is `APPROVED` or `AMORTIZING`. Every rule in §3 that moves concession money — approval, credit application, revocation on cancellation, revocation via the freeze cascade — is written so that both totals stay consistent.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Community and Its Product Mix

A `DeveloperPrincipal` opens the file on a new neighbourhood by registering the community itself. The registration payload carries the community's trade name in `name` — between 1 and 120 characters and not blank — and a `market_code` of between 2 and 8 uppercase characters, which is the submarket label used for reporting. The combination of `name` and `market_code` must be unique across all communities; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The payload also states `total_planned_units`, the total number of rental homes the community will eventually hold across all of its housing phases, which must be at least 1.

The product mix is the commercial heart of the registration. `product_mix` is an object with exactly three keys — `DETACHED`, `COTTAGE` and `TOWNHOME` — each carrying the number of homes planned in that configuration. All three keys are required, none may be negative, and the three values must sum exactly to `total_planned_units`. A mix whose keys stray outside those three product configurations, or whose values do not reconcile to the planned total, is a schema failure and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Three further inputs set the community's commercial and operational envelope. `concession_budget_cents` is the lifetime concession budget for the whole community, in cents, and may not be negative; it is the ceiling every concession approval is tested against. `facility_commitment_cents` is the committed principal of the portfolio construction facility, also in cents and also non-negative, and it is the numerator of every loan-to-value test. `maintenance_tech_headcount` is the number of maintenance technicians staffed for turns; it is optional, defaults to 2 when omitted, and must be at least 1. That headcount is not decoration — it is the hard cap on how much concurrent turn labour the community can absorb. Finally, `single_ownership_covenant` is optional and defaults to true; it records that the plat keeps every home in the community under one owner with no fee-simple lot sales, and while it is false the community can never leave a frozen posture.

Everything else on a new community is derived or system-owned. `plat_status` starts at `DRAFT`, `plat_revision_round` at 0, `plat_submitted_by_user_id` and `plat_package_reference` at null, `lifecycle_status` at `PLANNING`, `management_mode` at `DEVELOPER_SELF_MANAGED`, `pm_company_name` and `pm_effective_from` at null, `facility_status` at `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW`, and `posture` at `FROZEN` — frozen precisely because the plat has not been approved. The counters `released_unit_count`, `available_unit_count`, `leased_unit_count`, `committed_concession_cents` and `construction_impact_credit_cents` all start at 0, `occupancy_ratio` at 0.0, and `amenity_delivery_ratio` at 1.0. The record's `version` is 1.

Phases are also created by the `DeveloperPrincipal`, one per delivery increment, each naming its owning `community_id`. A phase carries a `sequence_index` of at least 1 that must be unique within its community, and a `kind` of either `HOUSING` or `AMENITY`. A `HOUSING` phase delivers rentable homesites: its `planned_unit_count` must be at least 1 and its `amenity_weight` must be 0. An `AMENITY` phase delivers shared amenities: its `amenity_weight` must be at least 1 and its `planned_unit_count` must be 0. Both fields are optional in the request and both default to 0, which means a housing phase must state its unit count explicitly and an amenity phase must state its weight explicitly; a phase that violates either pairing is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each phase also declares a closed construction window through `construction_window_start` and `construction_window_end`, where the end must fall strictly after the start. A new phase begins at `status` `PLANNED` with `halt_count` 0, `pre_halt_status` null, and `released_homesite_count` and `leased_homesite_count` both 0. Creating an amenity phase changes the community's planned amenity weight, so the community's `amenity_delivery_ratio` and `posture` are recomputed as part of the same request.

The phasing chain is expressed through `predecessor_phase_id`, an optional self-referential link that defaults to null. A phase may not name itself as its own predecessor, and the predecessor must belong to the same community; either mistake yields `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_PREDECESSOR`. What the link *means* is a start gate, described in §3.3.

Homesites are created inside a housing phase by either a `DeveloperPrincipal` or a `ConstructionManager`, each naming its `phase_id`. A homesite carries a `lot_number` of 1 to 12 characters that must be unique within its phase — a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — a `product_type` of `DETACHED`, `COTTAGE` or `TOWNHOME`, a `bedrooms` count between 1 and 5 inclusive, and a `square_feet` figure of conditioned area between 400 and 4000 inclusive. Three counting rules apply together at creation, and all three are enforced with `ERR_PRODUCT_MIX_EXCEEDED`: the homesite's `product_type` must be a key of its community's `product_mix` whose planned count is at least 1; the number of homesites of that product type across the whole community must not exceed that planned count; and the number of homesites in the phase must not exceed that phase's `planned_unit_count`. A new homesite starts at `status` `PLANNED` with `off_market_reason`, `occupancy_certificate_number`, `occupancy_certificate_issued_at`, `released_at` and `last_turn_ready_at` all null and `turn_cycle_count` 0.

### 3.2. Entitlement, Platting and the Revision Loop

Nothing in a community can be built or leased until the plat is approved, because the posture stays `FROZEN` for as long as `plat_status` is anything other than `APPROVED`. The plat dimension of a community starts at `DRAFT` and moves through `SUBMITTED` to one of `APPROVED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `REJECTED`, with `REJECTED` terminal.

Submission is a `DeveloperPrincipal` action. The submitting payload carries a single field, `plat_package_reference`, which identifies the package lodged with the entitlement authority. Submission is legal from either `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; attempting it while the plat is already `SUBMITTED`, or after it has reached `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAT_STATE`. A successful submission moves `plat_status` to `SUBMITTED`, records the supplied reference in `plat_package_reference`, records the submitting operator's `X-User-ID` in `plat_submitted_by_user_id`, recomputes the community's `posture` — which stays `FROZEN`, since the plat is not yet approved — and increments the community's `version` by 1. Resubmission after a revision request is the same operation and closes the revision loop `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`; it additionally requires that `plat_revision_round` has not exceeded 2, and it deliberately does **not** reset `plat_revision_round`.

Deciding a submitted plat belongs exclusively to the `EntitlementOfficer`, who supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_REVISION` or `REJECT` together with an optional `decision_note`. Any decision requires that `plat_status` is currently `SUBMITTED` — deciding a plat in any other state is `ERR_INVALID_PLAT_STATE` — and that the deciding `X-User-ID` differs from the community's `plat_submitted_by_user_id`, failing which the decision is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The three decision branches diverge sharply. An `APPROVE` decision additionally requires that the community's `single_ownership_covenant` is true; platting that would break single-ownership operation of the whole community may not be approved, and the attempt draws `ERR_SINGLE_OWNERSHIP_COVENANT_REQUIRED`. On success `plat_status` becomes `APPROVED`, the community's `version` increments, and the posture is recomputed — which is the moment a community first leaves `FROZEN`, landing on `OPEN` when no housing phase is halted and the amenity gate is satisfied, or on `RESTRICTED` when it is not. A `REQUEST_REVISION` decision additionally requires that `plat_revision_round` has not yet reached 2; a third revision request on the same plat is refused with `ERR_PLAT_REVISION_LIMIT`. On success `plat_status` becomes `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `plat_revision_round` increases by 1, and the community's `version` increments. A `REJECT` decision carries no extra precondition beyond the two shared ones. On success `plat_status` becomes `REJECTED`, which is terminal, the posture becomes `FROZEN` permanently — no later action can lift it — and the community's `version` increments.

### 3.3. Construction Phasing, Section Releases and Amenity Delivery

The construction dimension of a phase runs `PLANNED → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION → SECTIONS_RELEASED → COMPLETE`, with a self-loop on `SECTIONS_RELEASED` and a detour through `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED`. `COMPLETE` is terminal.

A `ConstructionManager` starts construction on a phase that is still `PLANNED`; starting a phase in any other state is `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_STATE`. Four gates then apply. The community's `plat_status` must be `APPROVED` and its `posture` must not be `FROZEN`, so a phase cannot be started while another housing phase of the same community sits halted — that attempt is `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`. The predecessor gate says that if this phase carries a `predecessor_phase_id`, that predecessor's `status` must already be `SECTIONS_RELEASED` or `COMPLETE`; a phase with no predecessor passes trivially. The concurrency cap says that at most one *other* phase of the same community whose `status` is `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, `SECTIONS_RELEASED` or `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED` may have a construction window overlapping this phase's closed window — in other words the community may have at most two phases with mutually overlapping windows in flight at once. A predecessor that has not released sections and a third overlapping window both produce `ERR_PHASE_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT`. Separately, any phase of the same community with the same `kind` whose window does **not** overlap this one must still be separated from this window by at least two full days — 48 hours — measured between the windows; a gap of exactly 48 hours is acceptable, anything smaller is `ERR_PHASE_GAP_VIOLATION`. A successful start sets the phase `status` to `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, moves the community's `lifecycle_status` from `PLANNING` to `CONSTRUCTION` on the first start anywhere in that community (incrementing the community's `version`), and recomputes the community's `posture`.

Before a home can be released to leasing, the municipality has to sign off on it. A `ConstructionManager` records that sign-off against the homesite, supplying `occupancy_certificate_number` and the instant `issued_at`; the service stores them as `occupancy_certificate_number` and `occupancy_certificate_issued_at`. The homesite must still be `PLANNED` — issuing a certificate on a home that has already left `PLANNED` is `ERR_INVALID_HOMESITE_STATE` — and recording a certificate does not itself change the homesite's `status`, which stays `PLANNED`.

Releasing a finished section is the operation that makes build-to-rent work: leasing of completed streets proceeds while later streets are still under construction. A `ConstructionManager` names the homes to release in a `homesite_ids` list. The addressed phase must have `kind` equal to `HOUSING` and `status` equal to either `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` — which is the first release and drives the transition `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION → SECTIONS_RELEASED` — or `SECTIONS_RELEASED`, which is the incremental self-loop `SECTIONS_RELEASED → SECTIONS_RELEASED` for a later section. Releasing from `PLANNED` or from `COMPLETE`, or releasing an amenity phase, is `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_STATE`. The community's posture must be `OPEN`: a frozen community rejects the release with `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN` and a restricted one with `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. Every listed home must belong to the addressed phase and must currently be `PLANNED`, and a listed home that belongs to another phase or has already left `PLANNED` is `ERR_INVALID_HOMESITE_STATE`. Every listed home must have a non-null `occupancy_certificate_issued_at`, failing which the whole release is `ERR_OCCUPANCY_CERTIFICATE_REQUIRED`. And for every distinct `product_type` appearing among the listed homes, the community must already have a market rent schedule with `status` `PUBLISHED`, because a home cannot go on the market with no price; a missing one is `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RENT_SCHEDULE`.

A successful release walks the listed homes in the order the client supplied them, moving each from `PLANNED` to `AVAILABLE` and stamping `released_at` with the current logical clock. The phase's `released_homesite_count` is recomputed, the community's `released_unit_count` and `available_unit_count` are recomputed, and the community's posture is recomputed — which is where a community with an undelivered clubhouse moves `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, because `released_unit_count` has become at least 1 while `amenity_delivery_ratio` is still below 0.5000. On the very first release anywhere in the community, two further things happen in the same request: the community's `lifecycle_status` moves `CONSTRUCTION → LEASE_UP`, and its `facility_status` moves `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW → ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE`, because a community with rentable inventory can start drawing against the facility on an advance basis.

Completion is where the two phase kinds diverge, and the divergence is the amenity gate. Completing a `HOUSING` phase requires that its `status` is `SECTIONS_RELEASED` and that its `released_homesite_count` equals its `planned_unit_count` — every planned home in the phase must have been released — and a completion attempted from any other state, or with homes still unreleased, is `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_STATE`. The phase's `status` becomes `COMPLETE`, and the community's posture is recomputed, but a housing completion leaves `amenity_delivery_ratio` untouched and therefore cannot on its own lift a restriction. Completing an `AMENITY` phase requires only that its `status` is `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, since amenity phases never release sections and never pass through `SECTIONS_RELEASED`; a completion attempted from any other state is likewise `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_STATE`. The phase's `status` becomes `COMPLETE`, the community's `amenity_delivery_ratio` is recomputed upward because this phase's `amenity_weight` now counts as delivered, and the posture is recomputed — which is how a community moves `RESTRICTED → OPEN` once the delivered amenity weight reaches half of the planned amenity weight. Both branches increment the phase's `version`.

### 3.4. Halting Construction and the Freeze Cascade

Construction stops for weather, for a failed inspection, for a supply problem. A `ConstructionManager` halts a phase whose `status` is either `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` or `SECTIONS_RELEASED`, supplying a `halt_reason`; halting a `PLANNED`, `COMPLETE` or already-halted phase is `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_STATE`. The halt is the widest cascade in the system, and it runs in five ordered hops.

The zeroth hop touches the phase itself: `status` becomes `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED`, `pre_halt_status` records the status the phase held immediately before the halt — `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` for a phase halted mid-build, `SECTIONS_RELEASED` for a phase that had already released sections — and `halt_count` increases by 1. Both entry branches, `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION → CONSTRUCTION_HALTED` and `SECTIONS_RELEASED → CONSTRUCTION_HALTED`, run the identical cascade and differ only in the value stored in `pre_halt_status`.

The first hop, caused by the zeroth, recomputes the community's `posture`. Halting a phase whose `kind` is `HOUSING` drives the posture to `FROZEN`; halting a phase whose `kind` is `AMENITY` drives it only to `RESTRICTED`. In the same hop, resident experience is compensated: every lease in the community whose `lease_status` is `ACTIVE` accrues a further 7,500 cents — 75 dollars — added to its own `construction_impact_credit_cents`, and the community's `construction_impact_credit_cents` increases by 7,500 cents for each such lease.

The second hop fires only because the first hop produced a `FROZEN` posture. Every homesite of the community whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, moves `AVAILABLE → OFF_MARKET` with `off_market_reason` set to `POSTURE_FREEZE`. Their `released_at` values are left alone, so the community's `released_unit_count` does not move; only `available_unit_count` falls.

The third hop, caused by the second, cleans up the leases standing on the homes that just came off the market. Every lease on a just-delisted homesite whose `lease_status` is `DRAFT` or `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, again in `created_at` ascending order, moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `POSTURE_FREEZE`. If such a lease had a concession in flight — its `concession_status` being `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` — that concession moves to `REVOKED`, and the community's `committed_concession_cents` is reduced by that lease's `concession_remaining_cents`.

The fourth hop, caused by the third, settles the program-level ledger: each `ConcessionProgram` whose leases were revoked has its `committed_cents` recomputed downward by the same amounts and its `version` incremented.

Resuming is the compensating action and it is symmetrical. A `ConstructionManager` resumes a phase whose `status` is `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED` — resuming anything else is `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_STATE` — and the community's `plat_status` must still be `APPROVED`. The phase returns to whatever `pre_halt_status` holds, which is either `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` or `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED → SECTIONS_RELEASED`, and `pre_halt_status` is then cleared to null; `halt_count` is a running total and is never decremented. The community's posture is recomputed, and if no housing phase of the community remains `CONSTRUCTION_HALTED` the freeze clears. Only when the recomputed posture is no longer `FROZEN` does the compensating relist run: every homesite of the community whose `status` is `OFF_MARKET` and whose `off_market_reason` is `POSTURE_FREEZE`, in `created_at` ascending order, returns to `AVAILABLE` with `off_market_reason` set back to null, restoring `available_unit_count` to its pre-halt value. Leases cancelled by the freeze are **not** restored — cancellation is terminal — and `released_at` is never rewritten, so `released_unit_count` is unaffected by either the halt or the resume. Both resume branches perform the identical posture recompute and the identical relist.

### 3.5. Absorption Pacing, Published Rents and Indexed Rent Growth

Lease-up is a race against a business plan, and the instrument the `AssetManager` uses to score it is the absorption review. A review is scoped to one community and one `product_type`, and it measures a half-open window running from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive. The window must be well formed: `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, a span that is a positive whole multiple of seven days, and a `window_end` that is not after the current logical clock — you cannot review a period that has not finished. Any of those three failures is `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_WINDOW`. The review also carries `planned_absorption_units`, the business-plan absorption target for that window, which must be at least 1. The acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stored on the review as `created_by_user_id`. A review is an immutable snapshot and is never revised.

Creating a review computes four things and freezes them. `actual_absorption_units` is the number of leases in that community for that `product_type` whose `executed_at` falls at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, and whose `lease_status` is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `ENDING`; leases that were cancelled or have already ended never count, no matter when they were signed. `available_supply_units` is the number of homesites of that `product_type` in the community whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` **at the moment the review is created** — not at the window's end. `posture_at_review` snapshots the community's `posture` at that same moment, and it is a genuine input to the score rather than a note. From these, `pacing_index` is computed to four decimal places, rounded half up.

The pacing computation has one special case and then one product of three factors. If `actual_absorption_units` is zero, `pacing_index` is exactly 0.0000 and no division is attempted at all. Otherwise: the raw pace is `actual_absorption_units` divided by `planned_absorption_units`; the supply factor is `available_supply_units` divided by `planned_absorption_units` — using 1 in place of the divisor if `planned_absorption_units` were ever smaller than 1 — and then clamped so that it is never below 0.75 and never above 1.25; and the posture penalty is 0.90 when `posture_at_review` is `RESTRICTED` and 1.00 otherwise, including when the posture was `FROZEN`. `pacing_index` is the raw pace multiplied by the supply factor multiplied by the posture penalty, rounded half up to four decimal places.

The index then selects a `concession_tier` and a `recommended_growth_bps` together, on inclusive lower bounds evaluated from the top down. A `pacing_index` of 1.2000 or more gives tier `NONE` and a recommendation of 250 basis points. From 1.0000 up to but not including 1.2000 the tier is `LIGHT` and the recommendation is 125 basis points. From 0.8000 up to but not including 1.0000 the tier is `MODERATE` and the recommendation is 0 basis points. Below 0.8000 the tier is `AGGRESSIVE` and the recommendation is negative 150 basis points. The boundaries are inclusive at the bottom: 1.0000 is `LIGHT`, and 0.9999 is `MODERATE`.

Publishing a market rent schedule is also an `AssetManager` action, and it is one of the two writes that carry `expected_community_version` as described in §2.7. A schedule names its `community_id` and its `product_type`, states the instant `effective_from` at which it may begin pricing new leases, and chooses a `repricing_mode` of either `MANUAL` or `ABSORPTION_INDEXED`. The triple of `community_id`, `product_type` and `effective_from` must be unique across all schedules; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Publication is blocked while the community's posture is `FROZEN`, with `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`, but a `RESTRICTED` posture does not block it — repricing during an amenity delay is exactly what an asset manager needs to do. On success the new schedule is stored with `status` `PUBLISHED`, `superseded_at` null and `published_by_user_id` set from the acting `X-User-ID`; the community's previously `PUBLISHED` schedule for that same community-and-product-type pair moves to `status` `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped at the current logical clock; and the community's `version` increments.

Under `MANUAL` mode the published price is taken directly from the request's `asking_rent_cents`, which must be at least 1 cent — a `MANUAL` publication with `asking_rent_cents` absent or below 1 is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and `applied_growth_bps` is recorded as 0.

Under `ABSORPTION_INDEXED` mode the price is derived and any `asking_rent_cents` in the request is ignored. The baseline is the schedule for the same community and product type ranked by `effective_from` descending and, where two share the same `effective_from`, by `created_at` descending. If no schedule for that pair exists at all, the publication fails with `ERR_RENT_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`. If the baseline that wins that ranking has `status` `SUPERSEDED` rather than `PUBLISHED` — which can happen when an out-of-order manual publication leaves the latest-dated schedule retired while an earlier-dated one is live — the service refuses to index off a stale baseline and returns `ERR_SCHEDULE_ALREADY_SUPERSEDED`. The growth figure comes from the most recent absorption review for the same community and product type, ranked by `window_end` descending and then by `created_at` descending; if no such review exists, the publication fails with `ERR_ABSORPTION_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. That review's `recommended_growth_bps` is the applied growth. The step is the baseline's `asking_rent_cents` multiplied by that basis-point figure and divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents, and then clamped so that it never moves the rent by more than 200 dollars — 20,000 cents — in either direction. The new `asking_rent_cents` is the baseline's `asking_rent_cents` plus that clamped step, and `applied_growth_bps` records the basis-point figure that was applied. Note the order: the step is rounded first and clamped second. If the resulting `asking_rent_cents` would be below 1 cent, the publication is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 3.6. Concession Programs and Amortization Mode

A concession program is the community's standing offer, and only one is live at a time. An `AssetManager` creates one against a `community_id` with a `program_code` of 2 to 24 characters that is unique within the community, a `tier` label of `NONE`, `LIGHT`, `MODERATE` or `AGGRESSIVE`, and a `free_weeks` allowance between 0 and 12 inclusive that expresses how many weeks of gross charge the offer gives away. The `tier` is recorded for operator reporting only; it is informational and is never cross-checked against any absorption review's computed `concession_tier`. The program also declares `eligible_product_types`, a non-empty subset of `DETACHED`, `COTTAGE` and `TOWNHOME`, a `max_budget_cents` program-level cap that may not be negative, and an optional `restricted_posture_eligible` flag that defaults to false. Program creation is the second of the two writes carrying `expected_community_version`, and a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_COMMUNITY_VERSION` as described in §2.7. Creation is refused while the community's posture is `FROZEN` with `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and — uniquely among the leasing writes — also while it is `RESTRICTED`, with `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`; a community that has not delivered its amenities has no business writing new concession policy. A new program is stored with `status` `ACTIVE`, `committed_cents` 0 and `superseded_at` null, the community's previously `ACTIVE` program moves to `status` `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped at the current logical clock, and the community's `version` increments.

`amortization_mode` is the program field that changes the shape of every lease that attaches to it, and it takes one of two values. `STRAIGHT_LINE_NET_EFFECTIVE` spreads the concession evenly across the whole lease term, which keeps the resident's first cheque close to the full charge and reports a smooth net effective rent. `UPFRONT_FREE_RENT` consumes the concession in the earliest periods instead, giving the resident whole free months at the front of the term and only then reverting to the full charge. The `restricted_posture_eligible` flag is the other lever, and it is a leasing lever rather than a pricing one: while a community's posture is `RESTRICTED`, a lease may only be signed if it carries an approved concession whose program has this flag set to true — the operator's way of saying that residents moving in during an amenity delay must be compensated for it.

The arithmetic that turns a program into a lease's concession schedule is described in §3.8, where the concession request that triggers it lives.

### 3.7. Households, Lease Drafting and the Price Frozen at Creation

A `LeasingAgent` registers a prospective household before drafting anything. The registration carries `primary_applicant_name` — 1 to 120 characters, the name of the person who will hold the lease — an `email` that must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all households, with a repeat drawing `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, a `household_size` occupant count between 1 and 8 inclusive, a `monthly_income_cents` figure of verified combined monthly income that may not be negative, and a `credit_tier` underwriting label of `A`, `B` or `C`. A new household starts with `active_lease_count` at 0. That counter is derived: at any moment it is the number of leases for the household whose `lease_status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `ENDING`, and it is refreshed whenever any of that household's leases changes contract state — rising when a lease is signed, falling when one is cancelled. The `id` returned by registration is the value that household will send as `X-User-ID` when it signs or gives notice.

Drafting a lease is also a `LeasingAgent` action, and it is where the money is fixed. The draft payload names the home in `homesite_id` and the leaseholder in `household_id`, states a `term_months` of exactly 12, 18 or 24 — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and a `start_date` commencement instant. Four gates apply before pricing runs. The community must not be `FROZEN`, or the draft is `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`; a `RESTRICTED` posture does not block drafting. The home must have `status` `AVAILABLE` and its phase must be in `status` `SECTIONS_RELEASED` or `COMPLETE`, failing which the draft is `ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_LEASABLE` — which is what a caller sees when it tries to draft against a home that is still `PLANNED`, already `LEASED`, on `NOTICE_GIVEN`, `IN_TURN`, or parked `OFF_MARKET`. The home's community must have `lifecycle_status` of `LEASE_UP` or `STABILIZED`; a community still in `PLANNING` or `CONSTRUCTION` refuses the draft with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. And the household must not already hold an open lease: at most one lease per household may sit in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `EXECUTED` or `ACTIVE` across the whole system, and a second draft is `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_ALREADY_LEASED`.

Base rent is selected, not supplied. The service assembles the candidate schedules for the home: every `MarketRentSchedule` whose `community_id` is the community reached from the home's phase, whose `product_type` equals the home's `product_type`, whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, and whose `effective_from` is at or before the lease's `start_date`. Schedules with `status` `SUPERSEDED` are excluded from the candidate set even when their `effective_from` is later than every published one — supersession, not date, decides eligibility. The candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, and where two share the same `effective_from` the tie is broken by `created_at` descending. The winner's `asking_rent_cents` becomes the lease's `base_rent_cents` and the winner's identifier is stored as the lease's `priced_by_schedule_id`. If the candidate set is empty, the draft is rejected with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RENT_SCHEDULE`. Both values are frozen at creation and are never re-derived, so a later repricing of the community never disturbs a lease already drafted.

The community-services fee — the build-to-rent substitute for a homeowners' association charge — is then computed in a strictly pinned order, and the order is the point. Step one takes `base_rent_cents` from the selection just described. Step two picks the fee rate from `base_rent_cents` **alone**: a base rent below 200,000 cents uses 350 basis points, a base rent from 200,000 cents up to but not including 320,000 cents uses 300 basis points, and a base rent of 320,000 cents or more uses 250 basis points. The bracket boundaries are inclusive at the bottom and exclusive at the top, so a base rent of exactly 200,000 cents uses 300 basis points while 199,999 cents uses 350. Step three computes `community_services_fee_cents` as `base_rent_cents` multiplied by the selected rate in basis points and divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents, plus a flat product surcharge keyed on the home's `product_type`: 4,500 cents for `DETACHED`, 3,000 cents for `COTTAGE` and 2,000 cents for `TOWNHOME`. Step four computes `gross_monthly_charge_cents` as `base_rent_cents` plus `community_services_fee_cents`. The rate bracket in step two is never selected from `gross_monthly_charge_cents`, and the four steps are never iterated toward a fixed point — the gross charge is an output of the fee, never an input to it.

Creation also derives `end_date` as the lease's `start_date` advanced by `term_months` calendar months. The new lease sits at `lease_status` `DRAFT` and `concession_status` `NONE`, with `concession_program_id`, `concession_requested_by_user_id`, `concession_decided_by_user_id`, `cancellation_reason`, `signed_by_name`, `executed_at`, `notice_date` and `move_out_date` all null, with `concession_schedule` an empty list, and with `total_concession_cents`, `monthly_concession_credit_cents`, `first_period_charge_cents`, `net_effective_rent_cents`, `concession_applied_cents`, `concession_remaining_cents`, `credits_applied_count` and `construction_impact_credit_cents` all 0.

### 3.8. The Concession Ledger: Request, Decision, Amortization and Credits

The concession dimension of a lease evolves independently of its contract dimension. It begins at `NONE` and moves through `REQUESTED` to `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, from `APPROVED` to `AMORTIZING` and finally to the terminal `EXHAUSTED`, with `REVOKED` as the compensating exit — and both `REJECTED` and `REVOKED` loop back to `REQUESTED`, because a leasing agent is expected to try again.

A `LeasingAgent` raises a concession request against a lease, supplying the `concession_program_id` of the program to attach. The request is legal when `concession_status` is `NONE`, `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` — the transitions `NONE → REQUESTED`, `REJECTED → REQUESTED` and `REVOKED → REQUESTED` are the three entry paths and all three run identical arithmetic — and illegal while the concession is already `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `AMORTIZING` or `EXHAUSTED`, which is `ERR_INVALID_CONCESSION_STATE`. The lease's `lease_status` must be `DRAFT` or `PENDING_SIGNATURE`; a concession cannot be attached to a lease that is already executed. The named program must have `status` `ACTIVE` and must belong to the lease's community, so a request against a program that has been superseded is `ERR_CONCESSION_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The home's `product_type` must appear in the program's `eligible_product_types`, failing which the request is `ERR_PRODUCT_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. On success `concession_status` becomes `REQUESTED`, `concession_program_id` is stored, and `concession_requested_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`; on the re-request path after a rejection, `concession_decided_by_user_id` is reset to null.

Raising the request is also what builds the amortization schedule, and the rounding order in that computation is pinned. First, `total_concession_cents` is the lease's `gross_monthly_charge_cents` multiplied by the program's `free_weeks`, multiplied by 12 and divided by 52 — the weeks-per-month conversion that turns a weekly allowance into monthly charges — rounded half up to whole cents. The concession is rounded exactly once, here at the total.

The two amortization modes then lay out the periods differently, and both must satisfy the same invariant: the `credit_cents` values across `concession_schedule` sum to `total_concession_cents` exactly. Under `STRAIGHT_LINE_NET_EFFECTIVE`, a per-period credit is computed as `total_concession_cents` divided by `term_months`, rounded half up to whole cents; the schedule then holds that per-period credit repeated for one fewer period than the term, and a final period whose credit is `total_concession_cents` minus the per-period credit multiplied by the number of those earlier periods — the final period absorbs the entire rounding residual. Under `UPFRONT_FREE_RENT`, the schedule is built by consuming the concession in whole months of gross charge: while any concession remains unallocated, the next period takes whichever is smaller of `gross_monthly_charge_cents` and the amount still remaining, and that amount is deducted from the remainder; the loop therefore produces as many periods as it takes to exhaust the concession, with every period but the last carrying a full month of gross charge and the last carrying whatever is left. Rounding each period independently and summing, or summing exact per-period values and rounding at the end, is wrong in both modes.

Four further lease figures fall out of the schedule. `concession_schedule` itself is an ordered array of objects, each carrying a `period_index` numbered from 1 upward and the `credit_cents` for that period. `monthly_concession_credit_cents` is the `credit_cents` of period 1, or 0 when the schedule is empty — which under `STRAIGHT_LINE_NET_EFFECTIVE` is a modest figure and under `UPFRONT_FREE_RENT` is a full month of gross charge. `first_period_charge_cents` is `gross_monthly_charge_cents` minus that first-period credit, floored at 0 so it is never negative — which under `UPFRONT_FREE_RENT` is exactly 0. `net_effective_rent_cents` is `gross_monthly_charge_cents` multiplied by `term_months`, minus `total_concession_cents`, divided by `term_months`, rounded half up to whole cents; it is deliberately mode-independent, so the two modes report the same net effective rent from the same inputs and differ only in cash timing. Finally `concession_remaining_cents` is set equal to `total_concession_cents` and `concession_applied_cents` is set to 0.

Deciding the concession belongs to the `AssetManager`, who supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` with an optional `decision_note`. The concession must be `REQUESTED` — deciding anything else is `ERR_INVALID_CONCESSION_STATE` — and the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `concession_requested_by_user_id`, or the decision is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An `APPROVE` decision additionally tests two budgets. The program's `committed_cents` plus this lease's `total_concession_cents` must not exceed the program's `max_budget_cents`, and the community's `committed_concession_cents` plus the same amount must not exceed the community's `concession_budget_cents`; either overrun is refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. On approval `concession_status` becomes `APPROVED`, `concession_decided_by_user_id` records the deciding `X-User-ID`, and both ledgers grow by `total_concession_cents` — the community's `committed_concession_cents` and the program's `committed_cents`. A `REJECT` decision carries no budget test: `concession_status` becomes `REJECTED`, `concession_decided_by_user_id` records the deciding `X-User-ID`, and the lease's concession arithmetic is wound back — `total_concession_cents`, `monthly_concession_credit_cents`, `first_period_charge_cents` and `concession_remaining_cents` all return to 0 and `concession_schedule` returns to an empty list.

Credits are posted one period at a time by the `PropertyManager` once the lease is amortizing. The posting names a `period_index`, which must be exactly `credits_applied_count` plus 1 and must not exceed the number of periods in `concession_schedule`; anything else is `ERR_CONCESSION_PERIOD_OUT_OF_ORDER`. The concession must be `AMORTIZING`, or the posting is `ERR_INVALID_CONCESSION_STATE`. The credit taken is the `credit_cents` of the schedule entry at that `period_index`. That amount is added to the lease's `concession_applied_cents`; `concession_remaining_cents` is recomputed as `total_concession_cents` minus `concession_applied_cents`; `credits_applied_count` increases by 1; and the same amount is subtracted from both the community's `committed_concession_cents` and the program's `committed_cents`, which is what keeps the ledger invariant of §2.11 true. No rounding occurs at application time. When the posting brings `concession_remaining_cents` to 0, the concession moves `AMORTIZING → EXHAUSTED`, which is terminal, and this lease's contribution to both committed totals has reached 0.

Revocation is the compensating exit and it never claws back money already applied. When a lease is cancelled, or when the freeze cascade of §3.4 cancels it, a concession whose status is `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `AMORTIZING` moves to `REVOKED`, and both the community's `committed_concession_cents` and the program's `committed_cents` are reduced by the lease's `concession_remaining_cents` — the portion already applied stays applied.

### 3.9. Submission, Signature, Activation and the Cancellation Compensation

The contract dimension of a lease runs `DRAFT → PENDING_SIGNATURE → EXECUTED → ACTIVE → ENDING → ENDED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from the first three states. `ENDED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

Submission is the `LeasingAgent`'s handoff to the resident: once the concession position is settled, the lease moves `DRAFT → PENDING_SIGNATURE`. The lease must be in `DRAFT` — submitting a lease in any other contract state is `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE` — the community's posture must not be `FROZEN`, or the submission is `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and the home must still be `AVAILABLE`, or the submission is `ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_LEASABLE`. No payload is required.

Signature is the only write a `Household` may make against a lease apart from giving notice, and it carries a single field, `signed_by_name`, the signature name captured at execution. The lease must be in `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, or the signature is `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE`. The signing `X-User-ID` must equal the lease's `household_id`, or the request is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`. The community's posture must not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`), and if the posture is `RESTRICTED` the lease may only be signed when its `concession_status` is `APPROVED` and the attached program's `restricted_posture_eligible` flag is true — a resident moving in during an amenity delay must be carrying compensation — with a failure of either condition reported as `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. The home must still be `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_LEASABLE`). And the household must qualify on income: `monthly_income_cents` must be at least three times the lease's `gross_monthly_charge_cents`, failing which the signature is `ERR_INCOME_QUALIFICATION_FAILED`.

A successful signature runs the widest forward cascade in the leasing path, in five ordered hops. Hop zero writes the lease: `lease_status` becomes `EXECUTED`, `executed_at` is stamped at the current logical clock, and `signed_by_name` is recorded from the request; the household's `active_lease_count` reflects the new open lease. Hop one, caused by hop zero, moves the home `AVAILABLE → LEASED`. Hop two, caused by hop one, recomputes the phase's `leased_homesite_count` and the community's `leased_unit_count` and `occupancy_ratio`. Hop three, caused by hop two, moves the community's `lifecycle_status` `LEASE_UP → STABILIZED` if and only if the recomputed `occupancy_ratio` has reached 0.7500 or more — three quarters of the planned units occupied is the stabilization threshold. Hop four, caused by hop three, moves the community's `facility_status` `ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE → CONVERSION_PENDING`, opening the door to permanent financing. Hops three and four fire only when their triggering condition is actually met; a signature that leaves occupancy below the threshold stops after hop two.

Activation is a `PropertyManager` action taken on or after the lease's commencement. The lease must be `EXECUTED` and the logical clock must have reached the lease's `start_date`; activating a lease that is not `EXECUTED`, or activating one before its `start_date`, is `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE`. On success `lease_status` moves `EXECUTED → ACTIVE`, and if a concession is attached its status moves `APPROVED → AMORTIZING`, which is what makes credit postings legal. Activation is also the moment the resident-experience credit is decided: when the community's `posture` at that instant is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, a flat 7,500 cents — 75 dollars — is added to the lease's `construction_impact_credit_cents` and the same amount is added to the community's `construction_impact_credit_cents`; when the posture is `OPEN`, no credit accrues and both fields keep their prior values. Only the posture at the instant of activation matters: a later restriction never retroactively grants a credit, and a later return to `OPEN` never removes one.

Cancellation is the `AssetManager`'s compensating action against a lease that has not yet activated, and it carries a `cancellation_reason` of `POSTURE_FREEZE`, `APPLICANT_WITHDREW` or `OPERATOR_CANCELLED`. It is legal from `DRAFT`, from `PENDING_SIGNATURE` and from `EXECUTED` — the three branches `DRAFT → CANCELLED`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE → CANCELLED` and `EXECUTED → CANCELLED` — and illegal against a lease that is `ACTIVE`, `ENDING`, `ENDED` or already `CANCELLED`, which is `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE`. All three branches make `lease_status` `CANCELLED`, record the supplied `cancellation_reason`, revoke any concession as described at the end of §3.8, and refresh the household's `active_lease_count`. The `EXECUTED` branch does more, because it has more to undo: the home moves `LEASED → AVAILABLE` with its `released_at` left untouched, the phase's `leased_homesite_count` and the community's `leased_unit_count` and `occupancy_ratio` are recomputed, the community's `lifecycle_status` reverts `STABILIZED → LEASE_UP` when the recomputed `occupancy_ratio` has fallen below 0.7500, and in that same revert the community's `facility_status` moves `CONVERSION_PENDING → ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE`. A `facility_status` of `PERM_CONVERTED` is never reverted by anything.

### 3.10. Notice, the Turn Loop and Maintenance Capacity

Departure begins with the resident. A `Household` gives notice on its own lease, supplying `notice_date` and a `move_out_date` that must be strictly after the notice instant; a `move_out_date` at or before `notice_date` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The lease must be `ACTIVE`, or the notice is `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE`, and the noticing `X-User-ID` must equal the lease's `household_id`, or it is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`. On success `lease_status` moves `ACTIVE → ENDING` with both instants recorded, and the home moves `LEASED → NOTICE_GIVEN`. Because a home on notice still counts as occupied, the community's `leased_unit_count` and `occupancy_ratio` are unchanged by the notice, so no lifecycle revert follows from giving notice alone.

Opening a turn work order is what actually ends the tenancy. A `PropertyManager` schedules turn work against a `homesite_id` whose `status` is `NOTICE_GIVEN` or already `IN_TURN`; opening one against a home that is `AVAILABLE`, `PLANNED`, `LEASED` or `OFF_MARKET` is `ERR_HOMESITE_NOT_IN_TURN`. The order occupies the half-open interval from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive, declares a `scope` of `MAKE_READY`, `DETACHED_YARD_RESTORE` or `MAJOR_REHAB`, assigns a `tech_count` of between 1 and 3 technicians for the whole window, and estimates `material_cost_cents`, which may not be negative.

Four temporal and capacity rules police the window, and they are evaluated in the order given here. First, two work orders on the same home may not have overlapping windows; because the windows are half-open, one ending at exactly the instant the next begins is allowed, while any genuine intersection is `ERR_TURN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Second, the window must be well formed: `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, a span that is a positive whole number of hours and no longer than ten days — 240 hours — and a `window_start` at or after the current logical clock; any of those failures is `ERR_INVALID_TURN_WINDOW`. Third, a rework buffer applies per scope: a new order of the same `scope` on the same home may not begin within two full days — 48 hours — of a prior order's `window_end` for that scope, and a closer start is `ERR_TURN_REWORK_BUFFER`. Fourth, the community's maintenance staffing is a hard aggregate cap: summed across the scheduled work orders of the community, the technicians required at any hour covered by the requested window must not exceed the community's `maintenance_tech_headcount`, and an over-subscription is `ERR_TURN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Opening the order also derives its cost, in ordered buckets. `window_hours` is the length of the window expressed in whole hours. `labor_cost_cents` is `tech_count` multiplied by `window_hours` multiplied by the hourly rate for the scope: 6,500 cents per technician-hour for `MAKE_READY`, 8,000 for `DETACHED_YARD_RESTORE` and 11,000 for `MAJOR_REHAB`. `total_turn_cost_cents` is `labor_cost_cents` plus `material_cost_cents`. That total is then allocated across three buckets in a strict waterfall, each filling completely before the next receives anything. The recoverable cap is the `gross_monthly_charge_cents` of the most recent departing lease on that same home — the lease whose `lease_status` is `ENDING` or `ENDED`, ranked by `executed_at` descending and then by `created_at` descending — and the cap is 0 when no such lease exists. `tenant_recoverable_cents` is whichever is smaller of `total_turn_cost_cents` and that cap. Whatever remains after the tenant bucket flows to `community_opex_cents`, which takes whichever is smaller of that remainder and the community operating cap of 80,000 cents. `capital_reserve_cents` absorbs everything still left. The three buckets always sum to exactly `total_turn_cost_cents`, and because every factor is an integer no rounding is required anywhere in the allocation.

Opening the order carries the departure cascade with it. The home moves `NOTICE_GIVEN → IN_TURN`; the `ENDING` lease on that home moves `ENDING → ENDED`, which is terminal; the community's `leased_unit_count` and `occupancy_ratio` are recomputed, since a home in turn no longer counts as occupied; and the community's `lifecycle_status` reverts `STABILIZED → LEASE_UP` when the recomputed `occupancy_ratio` falls below 0.7500, taking `facility_status` `CONVERSION_PENDING → ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE` with it. A work order opened against a home that is already `IN_TURN` — a second scope on the same move-out — leaves the home and the outgoing lease where they are. The order itself is stored with `status` `SCHEDULED` and `completed_at` null.

Completion closes the loop. A `PropertyManager` completes a work order whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` once the logical clock has reached its `window_start`; completing an order that is already `COMPLETED` is `ERR_INVALID_TURN_STATE`. On completion `status` becomes `COMPLETED` and `completed_at` is stamped at the current logical clock, and `labor_cost_cents`, `total_turn_cost_cents` and the three allocation buckets are recomputed against the recoverable cap in force at completion — which can differ from the cap at scheduling time if the departing lease reached `ENDED` in between. Then, and only when no other work order on that home still has `status` `SCHEDULED`, the home moves `IN_TURN → AVAILABLE`, its `turn_cycle_count` increases by 1, its `last_turn_ready_at` is stamped at the current logical clock, and the community's `available_unit_count` and `posture` are recomputed. That closes the cyclic turn loop `AVAILABLE → LEASED → NOTICE_GIVEN → IN_TURN → AVAILABLE`, and the home is ready to be leased again.

### 3.11. Stabilization, the Management Transition, and Portfolio Financing

A community carries four independent state dimensions on the same record, and this subsection covers the two that the capital stack cares about. The management dimension — `management_mode` — runs `DEVELOPER_SELF_MANAGED → PM_TRANSITION_PENDING → PM_MANAGED`, with a direct `DEVELOPER_SELF_MANAGED → PM_MANAGED` shortcut, and `PM_MANAGED` is terminal. The financing dimension — `facility_status` — runs `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW → ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE → CONVERSION_PENDING → PERM_CONVERTED`, with `CONVERSION_PENDING → ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE` as its only revert and `PERM_CONVERTED` terminal.

Handing a community to a third-party operator is a `DeveloperPrincipal` action carrying `pm_company_name`, the name of the incoming property manager, and `effective_from`, the instant at which the handover takes effect. Four gates apply. The community's `lifecycle_status` must be `LEASE_UP` or `STABILIZED` and its `maintenance_tech_headcount` must be at least 1 — a community with nobody staffed to run turns cannot be handed over — and a failure of either is `ERR_MAINTENANCE_STAFFING_REQUIRED`. The community's `occupancy_ratio` must have reached at least 0.5000, half the planned units occupied, or the transition is `ERR_LEASE_UP_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. The posture must not be `FROZEN`, or it is `ERR_COMMUNITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`. And the transition may only be arranged once: a second attempt while `management_mode` is already `PM_TRANSITION_PENDING` or `PM_MANAGED` is `ERR_INVALID_MANAGEMENT_STATE`.

The two branches are chosen by comparing `effective_from` against the logical clock, and both record `pm_company_name` and store the supplied instant as `pm_effective_from`, incrementing the community's `version`. An `effective_from` strictly after the current clock parks the community at `management_mode` `PM_TRANSITION_PENDING`; the flip to `PM_MANAGED` then happens on its own, as the side effect described in §2.9, the first time the logical clock is advanced to or past `pm_effective_from`, with another `version` increment. An `effective_from` at or before the current clock converts the community straight to `PM_MANAGED` in the same request.

Resizing the portfolio facility is an `AssetManager` action carrying a replacement `facility_commitment_cents`. The new commitment must keep the community's loan-to-value ratio, measured on the trailing-actual basis described below, at or below 6,500 basis points — 65 percent — and a resize that would breach that covenant is `ERR_LTV_COVENANT_BREACH`. A facility that has already reached `PERM_CONVERTED` may not be resized, which is `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE`. On success the stored `facility_commitment_cents` is replaced and the community's `version` increments.

Converting the construction facility to permanent financing is the terminal financing act, taken by an `AssetManager` with no payload, and it has four preconditions that read as a checklist of everything lease-up was for. The community's `lifecycle_status` must be `STABILIZED`, or the conversion is `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. Its `facility_status` must be `CONVERSION_PENDING`, or the conversion is `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE`. Its `management_mode` must be `PM_MANAGED` — a lender will not convert a community the developer is still self-managing — and a conversion attempted before the handover is `ERR_INVALID_MANAGEMENT_STATE`. And the loan-to-value ratio on the trailing-actual basis must be at or below 6,500 basis points, failing which the conversion is `ERR_LTV_COVENANT_BREACH`. On success `facility_status` becomes `PERM_CONVERTED`, which is terminal and is never reverted by any later occupancy loss, and the community's `version` increments.

The valuation itself is a computed read available to the `DeveloperPrincipal` and the `AssetManager`. It takes a single input, `basis`, whose value is either `TRAILING_ACTUAL` or `FORWARD_STABILIZED`, and it stores nothing — it computes and returns `basis`, `in_place_monthly_cents`, `pro_forma_monthly_cents`, `annual_gross_cents`, `vacancy_deduction_cents`, `egi_cents`, `opex_cents`, `noi_cents`, `value_cents`, `loan_to_value_bps` and a boolean `covenant_satisfied`. The two bases produce materially different numbers from identical stored data, which is exactly the argument between a lender and a sponsor.

On the `TRAILING_ACTUAL` basis — the conservative lender view, and the basis every covenant test in this specification is measured against — `in_place_monthly_cents` is the sum, over the community's leases whose `lease_status` is `ACTIVE`, of each lease's `gross_monthly_charge_cents` less its `monthly_concession_credit_cents`; concessions burden the income. `pro_forma_monthly_cents` is 0, because nothing unleased is credited. `annual_gross_cents` is `in_place_monthly_cents` plus `pro_forma_monthly_cents`, multiplied by 12. `vacancy_deduction_cents` is 0, because actual figures already carry their own vacancy. `egi_cents` is `annual_gross_cents` minus the community's `construction_impact_credit_cents` — the resident-experience credits accrued during construction reduce effective gross income on this basis.

On the `FORWARD_STABILIZED` basis — the sponsor view — `in_place_monthly_cents` is the sum of `gross_monthly_charge_cents` over the community's leases whose `lease_status` is `EXECUTED` or `ACTIVE`, taken at unburdened gross with no concession credit deducted at all. A blended market rent is then computed: for each product type whose `product_mix` count is greater than zero, multiply that planned count by the `asking_rent_cents` of the community's schedule for that product type whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`; sum those products across the product types; divide the sum by `total_planned_units`; and round half up to whole cents. If any product type with a positive planned count has no published schedule, the read fails with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RENT_SCHEDULE`. `pro_forma_monthly_cents` is `total_planned_units` minus `leased_unit_count`, multiplied by that blended market rent. `annual_gross_cents` is `in_place_monthly_cents` plus `pro_forma_monthly_cents`, multiplied by 12. `vacancy_deduction_cents` is `annual_gross_cents` multiplied by 500 basis points — a 5 percent stabilized vacancy allowance — divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. `egi_cents` is `annual_gross_cents` minus `vacancy_deduction_cents`.

From effective gross income onward the two bases share one path, and each named step is rounded half up in the order written with no intermediate truncation. `opex_cents` is `egi_cents` multiplied by 3,400 basis points — a 34 percent operating expense ratio — divided by 10,000. `noi_cents` is `egi_cents` minus `opex_cents`. `value_cents` capitalises that income at 525 basis points, a 5.25 percent capitalisation rate: it is `noi_cents` multiplied by 10,000 and divided by 525, rounded half up. `loan_to_value_bps` is the community's `facility_commitment_cents` multiplied by 10,000 and divided by `value_cents`, rounded half up. `covenant_satisfied` is true when `loan_to_value_bps` is at or below 6,500 and false otherwise.
