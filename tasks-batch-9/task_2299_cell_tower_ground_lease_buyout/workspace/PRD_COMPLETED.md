# Cell Tower Lease Buyout API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Wireless infrastructure lease aggregation is a business of buying future rent. A tower stands on somebody else's dirt, a carrier pays the tower operator, the tower operator pays the fee owner of the ground, and that ground rent — escalating, long-dated, and only as good as the carriers standing above it — is the asset our firm acquires. The work is unglamorous and highly serial: model the stream, price it two different ways, approach the landowner with either a lump sum or a block of prepaid rent, negotiate within a hard bid ceiling, clear title, chase the mortgagee for consent, serve the tower company with a right-of-first-refusal notice and wait out its window, fund escrow, record an easement, and redirect the rent. Any one of those steps can kill a deal, and several of them can kill it after capital has already been committed.

This document specifies the API that runs that pipeline end to end. It is deliberately a *transaction-integrity* system rather than a workflow-convenience system. Capital is finite and pooled, so a portfolio that is nearly fully deployed must be able to refuse and freeze new commitments rather than overdraw. Valuations snapshot inputs that other operators are simultaneously editing, so a valuation computed against a tenancy roster that has since changed must be rejected rather than quietly written. The manager who blessed a price must not be the manager who signs the closing, because that is the single control that keeps a mispriced deal from being self-approved. And once a stream is owned, it becomes collateral: pledged into a warehouse facility, haircut by servicing condition, tested for carrier concentration, and capable of pushing that facility into a borrowing-base deficit if a landowner dispute is upheld three counterparties away from the lender who financed it.

The reader should treat every threshold, rounding step, and evaluation order in this document as contractual. Money is carried in integer cents; rates are carried in basis points; and where a formula pins the order in which rounding happens, an implementation that reorders it will produce different numbers on the same inputs and will be considered incorrect. Time is supplied by a logical clock rather than the host wall clock, so that offer expiries, consent validity, refusal windows, and cure deadlines are all reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every rule in this section applies across the whole surface. The entire API is mounted under `/lease-buyouts`.

### 2.1. Principals, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Two request headers establish the acting principal on every call. `X-User-ID` identifies the principal, and `X-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-Role` carries a value that is not one of the nine recognized roles, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, the refusal is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The nine roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `AcquisitionAnalyst`, `PortfolioManager`, `TreasuryOfficer`, `TitleOfficer`, `Lender`, `ServicingAgent`, `Landowner`, and `TowerCompanyAgent`.

Seven of these roles are **opaque principals**: when `X-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `AcquisitionAnalyst`, `PortfolioManager`, `TreasuryOfficer`, `TitleOfficer`, `Lender`, or `ServicingAgent`, the `X-User-ID` value is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `approved_by_user_id` when a `PortfolioManager` approves a valuation, as `closed_by_user_id` when a `PortfolioManager` closes a deal, as `decided_by_user_id` when an offer reaches a decided state, as `consent_decided_by_user_id` when a `Lender` rules on mortgagee consent, or as `raised_by_role` capturing the declared role of whoever opened a dispute — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

Two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-Role` is `Landowner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Landowner` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Landowner` compare the resource's landowner reference to `X-User-ID`, walking from an `Offer` through its deal to the deal's lease stream and from a `DisputeCase` through its lease stream, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_DEAL_LANDOWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-Role` is `TowerCompanyAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TowerCompany` record; responding to a right-of-first-refusal notice compares the tower company that owns the notice's tower site to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_ROFR_HOLDER` (HTTP 403).

Several endpoints accept a discriminated verb from more than one role. Transition surfaces take that verb in a required `action` field, and consent and refusal surfaces take it in a required `decision` field. The offer transition surface, for example, accepts one set of `action` values from an `AcquisitionAnalyst` and a different set from a `Landowner`, and the dispute transition surface splits its `action` values three ways. When a caller is authorized for the endpoint in general but submits a verb reserved for a different role, the refusal is `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) rather than the blanket `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. A verb that is not a recognized member of the endpoint's vocabulary at all is a shape violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The title review surface counts the `Lender`'s `GRANT` and `DENY` verbs as members of its `action` vocabulary for this purpose, so submitting either of them there yields `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH` rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty uppercase-alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the host clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.6.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filtering

Collection endpoints page through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 20, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds — or one whose text is not a plain canonical integer literal, so that `1.5`, `05`, and `abc` are all rejected — is a shape violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific narrative says otherwise, every collection is ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a collection response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Collections also accept exact-match filters, and a filter whose value is not a legal member of the underlying enumeration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Portfolios may be filtered by `capital_posture`. Financing facilities under a portfolio may be filtered by `compliance_state`. Collateral pledges may be filtered by `facility_id`, by `lease_stream_id`, and by `status`. Tower sites may be filtered by `tower_company_id`, by `state_code`, and by `structure_type`. Lease streams may be filtered by `tower_site_id`, by `landowner_id`, by `acquisition_status`, and by `servicing_status`. Carrier tenancies under a stream may be filtered by `status` and by `tenancy_type`. Stream valuations under a stream may be filtered by `status` and by `method`. Deals may be filtered by `portfolio_id`, by `lease_stream_id`, by `valuation_id`, by `stage`, and by `capital_state`. Offers under a deal may be filtered by `status`. Title reports under a deal may be filtered by `review_status` and by `consent_status`. Right-of-first-refusal notices under a deal may be filtered by `status`. Disputes under a stream may be filtered by `status` and by `dispute_type`. Amendment events under a stream may be filtered by `event_type` and by `status`. The identifier-valued filters in that list — `facility_id`, `lease_stream_id`, `tower_company_id`, `portfolio_id`, `valuation_id`, `landowner_id`, `tower_site_id` — and the free-text `state_code` are compared as opaque strings and are never checked for existence, so a value matching nothing yields an empty page rather than a 404; only the enumeration-valued filters are validated.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Audit Trail

Every persisted resource in this system — portfolios, financing facilities, tower companies, tower sites, landowners, lease streams, carrier tenancies, stream valuations, deals, offers, title reports, right-of-first-refusal notices, dispute cases, collateral pledges, and amendment events — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and clients never supply it.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the resource.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the resource.

A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation. When a carrier tenancy is added and the owning stream's `tenancy_quality_score` moves, the stream's `updated_at` and `version` both advance even though no client wrote to the stream directly. The same holds for a portfolio whose `utilization_bps` shifts because a deal reserved capital, and for a facility whose `borrowing_base_cents` shifts because a pledge became ineligible. This is what makes version pinning (§2.7) a usable serialization point rather than a formality. A recomputation that lands on the values the resource already holds is *not* a mutation and leaves both `updated_at` and `version` untouched; that change-gating covers portfolios, financing facilities, landowners, carrier tenancies, collateral pledges, a lease stream's own `acquisition_status` and `servicing_status` assignments, and a deal's settlement recomputation. Two resources are exempt from the gating and advance unconditionally: a `LeaseStream` on any roster write of §3.3, and an `Offer` on any transition the server accepts.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. There are no fractional cents in any stored field, in any request body, or in any response body. Field names ending in `_cents` are always integers.

All rates, shares, haircuts, advance rates, escalators, concentration limits, and utilization measures are expressed in basis points, where ten thousand basis points is the whole. Converting a basis-point rate into a decimal multiplier therefore means dividing it by ten thousand.

Wherever a calculation must land on an integer, the rounding method is **round half up**: a value whose fractional remainder is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next integer of larger magnitude. Where an intermediate step is specified to round to two decimal places before a later step rounds to a whole number, that two-decimal step is part of the contract and must not be collapsed into a single final rounding. Where a calculation is specified to round each element of a series before summing, summing first and rounding once is a different and incorrect answer. Each individual calculation below restates its own rounding order, and that order governs.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Lazy Expiry

Time-dependent behaviour reads a logical clock rather than the host clock. The clock's value, referred to throughout this document as the current instant, starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and is repositioned only by a `PlatformAdmin` supplying a `current_utc` instant to the administrative clock capability, which echoes back the resulting `current_utc`. Offer expiries, refusal windows, mortgagee-consent validity, cure deadlines, and every stored timestamp such as `sent_at`, `served_at`, `ordered_at`, `consent_requested_at`, `decided_at`, `responded_at`, `resolved_at`, `approved_at`, and `recorded_at` all derive from this clock.

Three expiries are applied **lazily** rather than by a background sweep. An `Offer` in `SENT` whose `expires_at` has been reached, a mortgagee consent in `REQUESTED` whose `consent_expires_at` has been reached, and a `RofrNotice` in `SERVED` whose `window_end` has been reached are each evaluated and moved to their expired state at the moment the resource is next read or a transition on it is next attempted. The practical consequence is that a client cannot observe a stale non-expired state: any read that would have returned `SENT`, `REQUESTED`, or `SERVED` past the deadline instead returns `LAPSED`, `EXPIRED`, or `EXPIRED` respectively, together with whatever cascades that lapse triggers. The sweep is global rather than per-resource: every request under `/lease-buyouts` first materializes, in `created_at` order, every lapse the clock implies across all offers, all title reports and all notices — not merely the resource being addressed — and repositioning the clock runs the same sweep before it answers.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Valuation and Pledge Admission

Two write paths in this system decide on inputs that other operators are concurrently editing, and both require the client to pin the version of the parent it read.

Creating a stream valuation under a lease stream requires `expected_lease_stream_version` as a mandatory field of the request body. Its value must equal the target `LeaseStream`'s current `version`. A valuation snapshots the stream's `tenancy_quality_score` and rent terms, and any write that changes those inputs — adding a tenancy, changing a tenancy's `status` — advances the stream's `version`. A create request carrying an older number was therefore computed against inputs that no longer hold, and it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_STREAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than written against changed inputs.

Creating a collateral pledge under a financing facility requires `expected_facility_version` as a mandatory field of the request body. Its value must equal the target `FinancingFacility`'s current `version`. Pledge admission depends on the facility's current borrowing base, concentration headroom, and `compliance_state`, all of which move on every pledge, release, and advance. A request pinned to an older facility version was decided against superseded headroom and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Sending a version field to an endpoint that does not declare one has no effect.

### 2.8. Request Validation Order

Many operations carry several checks at once, and which error a caller sees is itself part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the **first** failing check determines the response; later checks are not evaluated once an earlier one has failed.

1. **Authentication** — `X-Role` is present and names a known role; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Endpoint role permission** — the caller's role appears among the endpoint's authorized roles; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Path resource existence** — every identifier in the URL path resolves to a live resource, checked outermost segment first; otherwise the matching not-found error (404).
4. **Body foreign-key existence** — every reference carried in the request body resolves; otherwise the matching not-found error (404).
5. **Request shape** — required fields are present, types and enumerations are legal, minimum and maximum bounds hold, and dates and timestamps parse; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A body that is absent, unparseable, or not a JSON object is treated as an empty object, so its required fields simply read as missing; fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.
6. **Landowner ownership** — a `Landowner` caller acting on an offer or a dispute is the landowner of the underlying lease stream; otherwise `ERR_NOT_DEAL_LANDOWNER` (403).
7. **Refusal-holder ownership** — a `TowerCompanyAgent` caller responding to a notice is the tower company of the notice's tower site; otherwise `ERR_NOT_ROFR_HOLDER` (403).
8. **Action-to-role match** — the submitted action verb is one the caller's role may send on this endpoint; otherwise `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403).
9. **Segregation of duties** — the `PortfolioManager` closing a deal is not the user recorded as `approved_by_user_id` on the deal's pinned valuation; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. **Lease-stream version currency** — `expected_lease_stream_version` equals the stream's `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_LEASE_STREAM_VERSION` (412).
11. **Facility version currency** — `expected_facility_version` equals the facility's `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (412).
12. **Offer state admits the action** — the requested offer verb is declared from the offer's current `status`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE` (409).
13. **Offer not expired** — the current instant is strictly before `expires_at`, checked for the counter, accept, and reject verbs; otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (409). Because the lazy sweep of §2.6 has already moved a past-deadline `SENT` offer to `LAPSED`, one of those three verbs against a `LAPSED` offer reports `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` ahead of check 12, while an analyst verb against the very same offer still reports `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`.
14. **Deal stage admits the action** — the deal's `stage` permits the operation; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STAGE` (409).
15. **Capital state admits the action** — the deal's `capital_state` permits the capital operation; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CAPITAL_STATE` (409).
16. **Title state admits the action** — the requested title or consent verb is declared from the current `review_status` or `consent_status`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_STATE` (409). Check 28 below is the single documented inversion of this list: on the consent-request verb and on the consent decision, `ERR_MORTGAGE_CONSENT_NOT_REQUIRED` is evaluated *before* this state check. Where a title or consent verb additionally requires the owning deal to be outside `CLOSED` (or outside `CLOSED` and `DEAD`), that precondition is reported as `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STAGE` (409) and is evaluated after this state check.
17. **Dispute state admits the action** — the requested dispute verb is declared from the dispute's current `status`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE` (409).
18. **Notice still open** — the notice is still `SERVED` and the current instant lies inside its window; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ROFR_STATE` (409).
19. **Pledge state admits the action** — the pledge is not already `RELEASED`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLEDGE_STATE` (409).
20. **Amendment state admits the action** — the amendment event is `RECORDED` before it may be reversed; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE` (409).
21. **Pinned valuation approved** — a deal's pinned valuation is `APPROVED` before outreach may start; otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409). This error is reserved for a pinned valuation still sitting in `DRAFT`; one already in `STALE` or `SUPERSEDED` falls through to check 22 instead.
22. **Pinned valuation not stale** — a deal's pinned valuation is neither `STALE` nor `SUPERSEDED` when the deal advances; otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_STALE` (409).
23. **Landowner contactable** — no offer is sent to a landowner whose `outreach_status` is `DO_NOT_CONTACT`; otherwise `ERR_LANDOWNER_DO_NOT_CONTACT` (422).
24. **Refusal window closed** — a deal may not reach `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE` and may not close while its `rofr_state` is `PENDING`; this check runs on the operator-driven clearance evaluation and again at closing; otherwise `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_OPEN` (422).
25. **No unresolved dispute at close** — no dispute on the lease stream is `OPEN`, `CURE_PENDING`, or `REOPENED` at closing; otherwise `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTE_BLOCKS_CLOSE` (422).
26. **Mortgagee consent present** — when the title report has `mortgage_present` true, `consent_status` is `GRANTED` before clearance and before closing; this check runs on the operator-driven clearance evaluation and again at closing; otherwise `ERR_MORTGAGE_CONSENT_MISSING` (422).
27. **Title exceptions closed** — `open_exception_count` is zero before clearance and before closing; this check runs on the operator-driven clearance evaluation and again at closing; otherwise `ERR_TITLE_EXCEPTIONS_OPEN` (422).
28. **Consent only when mortgaged** — requesting consent and ruling on consent both require `mortgage_present` true; otherwise `ERR_MORTGAGE_CONSENT_NOT_REQUIRED` (422). As flagged at check 16, this check runs ahead of the title-state check on both of those surfaces, so an unmortgaged report reports the 422 even when its `consent_status` would also have refused the verb.
29. **Offer type matches valuation method** — a `LUMP_SUM` offer requires a pinned valuation whose `method` is `LUMP_SUM_PERPETUAL`, and a `PREPAID_RENT` offer requires a pinned valuation whose `method` is `PREPAID_RENT_TERM`; otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_METHOD_MISMATCH` (422).
30. **Stream has an active tenancy** — a valuation may not be approved for a stream with no tenancy in `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`; otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_TENANCY` (422).
31. **Stream acquired for servicing** — pledges and amendment events require the stream's `acquisition_status` to be `ACQUIRED`; otherwise `ERR_STREAM_NOT_ACQUIRED` (422).
32. **One active deal per stream** — at most one non-terminal deal exists per lease stream; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_DEAL` (422).
33. **One outstanding offer per deal** — at most one offer per deal sits in `SENT` or `COUNTERED`; otherwise `ERR_OFFER_ALREADY_OUTSTANDING` (422).
34. **Negotiation rounds available** — the deal's `negotiation_rounds` is strictly below the three-round ceiling before a revision; otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (422).
35. **Cure attempts available** — the dispute's `cure_attempts` is strictly below the three-attempt ceiling before a cure is started; otherwise `ERR_CURE_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (422).
36. **One open dispute per stream and type** — at most one non-terminal dispute exists per lease stream and `dispute_type` pair; otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN` (422).
37. **Refusal windows do not overlap** — a new notice may not be served while another notice on the same deal is still inside its half-open window; otherwise `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (422).
38. **Pledge periods do not overlap** — no two non-released pledges on the same lease stream cover overlapping or abutting closed date intervals; otherwise `ERR_PLEDGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (422).
39. **Offer within the bid guardrail** — the offer's `amount_cents` is less than or equal to the pinned valuation's `max_bid_cents`; otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXCEEDS_GUARDRAIL` (422).
40. **Portfolio not frozen** — the portfolio's `capital_posture` is not `FROZEN` when capital is reserved; otherwise `ERR_PORTFOLIO_CAPITAL_FROZEN` (422).
41. **Portfolio capital sufficient** — the portfolio's `available_capital_cents` is greater than or equal to the deal's `total_commitment_cents` at acceptance; otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PORTFOLIO_CAPITAL` (402).
42. **Withdrawal within available capital** — a negative capital adjustment may not exceed `available_capital_cents`; otherwise `ERR_CAPITAL_WITHDRAWAL_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` (422).
43. **Facility not left in deficit** — a pledge release that would leave `advances_outstanding_cents` above the recomputed borrowing base is refused; otherwise `ERR_BORROWING_BASE_DEFICIT` (422).
44. **Advance within the borrowing base** — `advances_outstanding_cents` plus the requested amount is less than or equal to `borrowing_base_cents`; otherwise `ERR_ADVANCE_EXCEEDS_BORROWING_BASE` (402).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because the version checks sit at positions 10 and 11, a request that is both stale and business-invalid reports staleness, not the business problem. And because segregation of duties sits at position 9, a closing attempt by the approving manager is refused on identity grounds even when the deal is also blocked by an open title exception.

### 2.9. Nested Path Resolution

Many paths in this API nest a child collection under a parent identifier. Identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the 404 returned. A request for the tenancies of a lease stream that does not exist yields `ERR_LEASE_STREAM_NOT_FOUND`; a request for the offers of a deal that does not exist yields `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`; a facility identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` before the body is examined at all. The not-found vocabulary is per entity: `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOWER_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOWER_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LANDOWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_STREAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TENANCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROFR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

The same vocabulary serves references carried in request bodies, one step later in the order. A pledge creation naming a `lease_stream_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_LEASE_STREAM_NOT_FOUND`; a deal creation naming an unresolvable `portfolio_id`, `lease_stream_id`, or `valuation_id` yields `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_STREAM_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND` respectively; a facility creation naming an unresolvable `portfolio_id` yields `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_FOUND`; a tower site naming an unresolvable `tower_company_id` yields `ERR_TOWER_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`; and a lease stream naming an unresolvable `tower_site_id` or `landowner_id` yields `ERR_TOWER_SITE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LANDOWNER_NOT_FOUND`. A path that matches no route at all, and a method the matched path does not implement, fall outside this per-entity vocabulary and are both answered with the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

### 2.10. Derived-Field Reconciliation and Cascade Propagation

A large fraction of the fields in this system are server-derived and are never accepted from a client. They are recomputed immediately — within the same request that caused the change — rather than on a schedule. Consistency is immediate everywhere; there is no eventual-consistency window and no deferred reconciliation job.

Counters are maintained by the writes that affect them: a tower company's `site_count`, a tower site's `lease_stream_count`, a lease stream's `active_tenancy_count`, a landowner's `open_dispute_count`, a portfolio's `active_deal_count`, and a facility's `pledged_stream_count` all move as their children are created, retired, or reclassified. A portfolio's `active_deal_count` specifically counts the deals on that portfolio whose `stage` is none of `CLOSED`, `DEAD`, `ROFR_LOST`, and `TERMINATED`.

Cascades cross aggregate boundaries. Editing a tenancy roster changes a stream's quality score, which invalidates that stream's valuations, which can strand a deal that pinned one of them. Upholding a landowner dispute impairs a stream, which zeroes the eligible value of every pledge of that stream, which shrinks a facility's borrowing base, which can flip that facility into `BORROWING_BASE_DEFICIT` even though the lender was never a party to the dispute. Adjusting a portfolio's capital can freeze the portfolio, which parks every one of its deals that was out on outreach. Each of these cascades is described in the workflow narrative that owns it, and each mutated resource independently refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`.

Where a state machine's precondition depends on a sibling's state — the join into clearance depends on both the title track and the refusal track — the server re-evaluates the join automatically as a side effect of whichever tracked change occurred, in addition to honouring the explicit operator-driven evaluation.

### 2.11. Interval Conventions: Half-Open Windows versus Closed Pledge Periods

This system uses two deliberately different interval conventions, and confusing them produces wrong answers at exactly one boundary each.

A right-of-first-refusal window is a **half-open instant interval**: it includes `window_start` and excludes `window_end`. A response landing exactly at `window_end` is outside the window. Two adjacent windows are therefore permitted — a fresh notice whose `window_start` equals a prior notice's `window_end` does not overlap that prior notice.

A collateral pledge period is a **closed date interval**: it includes both `pledge_start_date` and `pledge_end_date`, and adjacency is additionally forbidden. A candidate pledge starting on the exact day an existing pledge ends conflicts with it; a candidate starting the following day does not.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties

Pricing authority and closing authority are separated at the level of the individual user, not the role. Approving a stream valuation records the approving user in `approved_by_user_id`. Closing a deal records the closing user in `closed_by_user_id`. The two must differ: a `PortfolioManager` whose `X-User-ID` matches the `approved_by_user_id` of the deal's pinned valuation may not close that deal, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Any other `PortfolioManager` may close it. The check is against the pinned valuation specifically — not against whichever valuation on the stream is currently approved — because the pinned valuation is the price the deal was struck at.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Capital Pools and the Portfolio Capital Ledger

Everything in this system is funded out of a **Portfolio**, a named capital pool that only a `PlatformAdmin` may open. Creating one takes a human-readable `name`, a `program_code` that is expected to be unique across all portfolios but whose uniqueness the server does not enforce — a duplicate code is accepted — and a `total_capital_cents` figure that may be zero or greater. On creation the pool's derived ledger is fully initialized: `committed_capital_cents` and `deployed_capital_cents` at zero, `available_capital_cents` equal to `total_capital_cents`, `utilization_bps` at zero, `capital_posture` at `OPEN`, and `active_deal_count` at zero.

The ledger is recomputed from the deals on the portfolio, partitioned by their capital state, every time any of those deals moves. `committed_capital_cents` is the sum of `total_commitment_cents` across deals whose `capital_state` is `RESERVED` or `ESCROWED`. `deployed_capital_cents` is the sum of `total_commitment_cents` across deals whose `capital_state` is `DISBURSED`. `available_capital_cents` is `total_capital_cents` less `committed_capital_cents` less `deployed_capital_cents`. Utilization is measured against the whole pool: let *used* be the sum of `committed_capital_cents` and `deployed_capital_cents`; then `utilization_bps` is zero when `total_capital_cents` is zero, and otherwise is *used* multiplied by ten thousand, divided by `total_capital_cents`, and rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. At every moment the three balances must add back to the whole: `available_capital_cents` plus `committed_capital_cents` plus `deployed_capital_cents` equals `total_capital_cents`.

Posture follows utilization through a two-rung ladder evaluated from the top: when `utilization_bps` is at or above 9000 — that is, ninety percent of the pool or more is spoken for — `capital_posture` is `FROZEN`; otherwise, when `utilization_bps` is at or above 7500, it is `CONSTRAINED`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. Both rungs are inclusive, so a portfolio sitting at exactly 9000 basis points is frozen and one sitting at exactly 7500 is constrained.

A `PlatformAdmin` may resize the pool by submitting a `delta_cents` amount — any integer, zero included — together with an explanatory `memo`, which must be a non-empty string but is not persisted anywhere. A positive delta grows `total_capital_cents`; a negative delta shrinks it, and a withdrawal whose magnitude exceeds `available_capital_cents` is refused with `ERR_CAPITAL_WITHDRAWAL_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` — money already committed or deployed cannot be pulled back out. After the adjustment the whole ledger, including `utilization_bps` and `capital_posture`, is recomputed, and the adjustment returns the updated portfolio rather than an adjustment record.

Posture cascades into deals, and it does so on the **level** of the posture rather than on the edge into it. Every ledger recomputation, whether or not the posture actually changed value, parks each of that portfolio's deals sitting in stage `OUTREACH` — `OUTREACH → CAPITAL_HOLD` — while the recomputed posture is `FROZEN`, and resumes each of its deals sitting in `CAPITAL_HOLD` — `CAPITAL_HOLD → OUTREACH` — while the recomputed posture is anything else. The consequence worth naming is that starting outreach on an already-frozen portfolio is not refused: the deal is written to `OUTREACH` and the ledger recomputation that follows inside the same request immediately parks it at `CAPITAL_HOLD`, so the caller sees `CAPITAL_HOLD` in the response. Both movements are server-driven consequences of the ledger recomputation; no operator requests them and no other stage is affected by them.

### 3.2. The Asset Graph: Tower Companies, Sites, Landowners and Streams

Before any stream can be valued, the physical and contractual graph around it has to exist. A **TowerCompany** is registered with its `legal_name` and a `notice_email` to which refusal notices are addressed, plus two optional refusal settings: `rofr_required`, a boolean that defaults to false, and `rofr_window_days`, which defaults to 30 and must fall between 1 and 90 days inclusive. Its `site_count` is derived. Either a `PlatformAdmin` or an `AcquisitionAnalyst` may register one.

A **TowerSite** hangs off a tower company by way of a `tower_company_id` and describes the physical location: a `site_code` expected to be unique across all sites but whose uniqueness the server does not enforce, a free-form `state_code` string, a `ground_area_sqft` footprint of at least one square foot, and a `structure_type` drawn from `MONOPOLE`, `LATTICE`, `GUYED`, and `ROOFTOP`. Registering a site increments its tower company's `site_count`. The site's own `lease_stream_count` is derived from the streams recorded beneath it.

A **Landowner** is the fee owner of the ground and the counterparty to all outreach. The record carries a `legal_name`, an `entity_type` drawn from `INDIVIDUAL`, `LLC`, `TRUST`, `CORPORATION`, and `MUNICIPALITY`, and a `contact_email`. Its `outreach_status` is initialized to `NEW` and thereafter tracks the progression of offers and deals through `CONTACTED`, `NEGOTIATING`, `UNDER_CONTRACT`, and `CLOSED`. The landowner also exposes a derived `open_dispute_count`, which is recomputed from scratch as the number of non-terminal disputes across every lease stream that names this landowner — not only across the stream that triggered the recomputation.

One landowner field is deliberately operator-controlled. A `PlatformAdmin` or `AcquisitionAnalyst` may patch a landowner to correct `contact_email`, and may pass a `suppress_outreach` boolean; both fields are optional, and a patch carrying neither — or carrying only values the record already holds — is accepted as a no-op that leaves `updated_at` and `version` alone. Setting `suppress_outreach` to true forces `outreach_status` to `DO_NOT_CONTACT`, which is the one value the progression logic never produces on its own; setting it back to false restores whatever value the offer and deal progression implies, because `outreach_status` is never stored as such but derived on every read from the suppression flag laid over the separately maintained progression value. While a landowner sits at `DO_NOT_CONTACT`, no offer may be sent to them and no deal on their stream may begin outreach — both attempts are refused with `ERR_LANDOWNER_DO_NOT_CONTACT`.

A **LeaseStream** is the asset itself: the ground rent under one tower. Recording one requires the `tower_site_id` it sits on, the `landowner_id` who receives the rent, a `current_annual_rent_cents` of at least one cent, an `escalator_type` drawn from `NONE`, `FIXED_PCT`, and `CPI_CAPPED`, and a `remaining_term_months` between 1 and 600 inclusive. An optional `escalator_pct_bps` defaults to 0 and must fall between 0 and 1000 basis points inclusive. Recording a stream increments the site's `lease_stream_count` and initializes the stream's derived surface: `tenancy_quality_score` at zero, `anchor_carrier_name` at the literal string `UNASSIGNED`, `acquisition_status` at `PROSPECT`, `servicing_status` at `PERFORMING`, `rent_redirection_status` at `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `active_tenancy_count` at zero, and both `cumulative_revenue_share_landowner_cents` and `cumulative_revenue_share_acquirer_cents` at zero.

The stream's `acquisition_status` is a server-maintained mirror of whatever deal is running on it, and it cycles. When a deal on the stream reaches outreach, `PROSPECT → UNDER_OFFER`. When a deal on the stream closes, `UNDER_OFFER → ACQUIRED`, and that same closing sets `rent_redirection_status` to `SENT` and `servicing_status` to `PERFORMING`. When a deal on the stream is lost to an exercised right of first refusal, or dies on a title failure or a consent denial, `UNDER_OFFER → LOST`. When a deal on the stream is terminated outright, the stream is released back to the funnel: `UNDER_OFFER → PROSPECT`. Each of these movements is an unconditional assignment of the new value at the moment the triggering deal event fires, not a guarded edge, so the recorded `acquisition_status` is always whatever the latest deal event implies regardless of what it held before. At most one non-terminal deal may exist per stream at a time; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_DEAL`.

### 3.3. Carrier Tenancy and the Tenancy Quality Score

A tower's ground rent is only as creditworthy as the carriers hanging off the structure, so each stream carries a roster of **CarrierTenancy** records. Either an `AcquisitionAnalyst` or a `ServicingAgent` may add one, supplying the `carrier_name`, a `carrier_credit_tier` drawn from `INVESTMENT_GRADE`, `CROSSOVER`, and `SPECULATIVE`, a `tenancy_type` of either `ANCHOR` or `COLOCATION`, a `monthly_rent_share_cents` figure of zero or more, and the `term_end_date` on which that carrier's occupancy runs out. A new tenancy begins life with `status` `ACTIVE`. A `ServicingAgent` may later reclassify a tenancy's `status` to `ACTIVE`, `HOLDOVER`, or `TERMINATED`.

The **tenancy quality score** is the number that drives pricing, and it is computed only from tenancies whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` — terminated occupancies contribute nothing. The score is built by walking that input set in ascending `created_at` order and, for each tenancy:

1. Take the tenancy's base credit points from its `carrier_credit_tier`: forty points for `INVESTMENT_GRADE`, twenty-five for `CROSSOVER`, ten for `SPECULATIVE`.
2. Multiply those base points by the type multiplier for its `tenancy_type`: one exactly for `ANCHOR`, six tenths for `COLOCATION`.
3. Round that product half up to two decimal places. This intermediate two-decimal rounding is pinned and must not be skipped or deferred.
4. If the tenancy's `status` is `HOLDOVER`, subtract five points from the rounded figure as a holdover penalty.
5. If the result is negative, replace it with zero.
6. Store on the tenancy, as its `quality_points`, that same figure rounded half up to a whole number. A tenancy outside the input set — one whose `status` is `TERMINATED` — carries `quality_points` of zero, and is driven back to zero by the next roster recomputation if it previously carried more.

The stream's `tenancy_quality_score` is then the sum of the **two-decimal figures produced at step five** — not the sum of the whole-number `quality_points` values written onto the tenancies — with that sum rounded half up to a whole number and finally clamped into the inclusive range from zero to one hundred. A stream with no `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` tenancy scores zero. The distinction between summing the two-decimal figures and summing the rounded per-tenancy values is real and observable whenever a colocation contributes a fractional value, and the two-decimal series is the contractual one.

Alongside the score, two other derived stream fields are maintained from the same roster. `active_tenancy_count` counts the tenancies in `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`. `anchor_carrier_name` is the `carrier_name` of the earliest-created tenancy whose `tenancy_type` is `ANCHOR` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`; when no such tenancy exists, `anchor_carrier_name` is the literal string `UNASSIGNED`. That anchor identity matters far downstream, because losing it on an acquired stream is what degrades servicing to `DELINQUENT` and so strips the stream's collateral of its eligibility; it is reported for information on the stream and is never used to group or pool collateral in the concentration test of §3.9.

Every write to the roster — adding a tenancy, or submitting a reclassification even when it names the `status` the tenancy already holds — recomputes `tenancy_quality_score`, `anchor_carrier_name`, and `active_tenancy_count`, and advances the stream's `version` whether or not any of those three figures actually moved. It also invalidates pricing unconditionally: every valuation on that stream in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` is moved to `STALE` on any roster write, even when the recomputed score is identical to the snapshotted one, on the reasoning that its snapshot can no longer be trusted to match the live score. Adding a tenancy to a stream that has already been acquired additionally opens a rent-redirection obligation — the new tenancy's `redirection_notice_status` becomes `PENDING` and the stream's `rent_redirection_status` is assigned `PARTIAL` outright, whatever it held before, reflecting that one tenant has not yet been told where to send its rent. On a stream that has not been acquired, a new tenancy's `redirection_notice_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

Reclassification can also break a stream. The trigger is measured on `anchor_carrier_name` across the roster write itself: when an acquired stream carried a named anchor immediately before the write and carries `UNASSIGNED` immediately after it — which is what happens when the anchor tenancy moves to `TERMINATED` and no `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` anchor remains behind it — the stream's servicing condition degrades to `DELINQUENT`, but only from `PERFORMING → DELINQUENT` or `WATCHLIST → DELINQUENT`; a stream already sitting at `DELINQUENT` or `IMPAIRED` is left exactly where it is. When the degradation fires, every pledge of that stream is made ineligible with the facility's borrowing base recomputed behind it.

### 3.4. Pricing a Stream: Two Methods and One Ceiling

An `AcquisitionAnalyst` prices a stream by creating a **StreamValuation** against it. The request declares a `method` — either `LUMP_SUM_PERPETUAL`, which buys the rent forever, or `PREPAID_RENT_TERM`, which buys a defined block of it — carries the mandatory `expected_lease_stream_version` described in §2.7, and may carry `prepaid_term_months`, which defaults to 120 and must fall between 12 and 600 inclusive; it is stored and echoed under both methods even though only `PREPAID_RENT_TERM` reads it. Nothing about the stream gates the computation — a stream with an empty tenancy roster prices perfectly well at a snapshotted score of zero rather than being refused, and neither the stream's `acquisition_status` nor any existing valuation on it is consulted. A valuation is born in `DRAFT`.

Computation begins by snapshotting the stream's live `tenancy_quality_score` into `tenancy_quality_score_snapshot`. That snapshot picks the base discount tier from a four-rung ladder evaluated from the top down: a score of 80 or more prices at 700 basis points; failing that, a score of 60 or more prices at 850; failing that, a score of 40 or more prices at 1000; and anything below 40 prices at 1250. Because the ladder is evaluated top down with inclusive thresholds, a score of exactly 60 lands on 850 while a score of 59 falls through to 1000 — a one-point difference in tenancy quality that moves the price materially.

`growth_rate_bps` is derived from the stream's escalator. An `escalator_type` of `NONE` gives a growth rate of zero. `FIXED_PCT` gives the stream's `escalator_pct_bps` verbatim. `CPI_CAPPED` gives the lesser of `escalator_pct_bps` and an assumed inflation rate of 250 basis points, so a stream with a 400-basis-point CPI-linked escalator is modelled at 250.

**Under the `LUMP_SUM_PERPETUAL` method**, `discount_rate_bps` is the base tier unmodified and `horizon_years` is fixed at forty years. The number of years the rent is actually contracted for is `remaining_term_months` divided by twelve and truncated toward zero. The model then walks each year from one through the horizon. For a year *y*, the modelled rent is `current_annual_rent_cents` multiplied by the quantity one plus `growth_rate_bps` divided by ten thousand, raised to the power of *y* minus one. The discount factor for that year is one divided by the quantity one plus `discount_rate_bps` divided by ten thousand, raised to the power of *y*. The year's present value is the modelled rent multiplied by the discount factor. If *y* is strictly greater than the contracted-years count, the year lies beyond the signed term and its present value is reduced by a renewal-risk haircut of 2000 basis points — that is, multiplied by the quantity one less two thousand divided by ten thousand. The year's present value is then rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `value_cents` is the sum of those rounded per-year figures.

**Under the `PREPAID_RENT_TERM` method**, the prepaid block is first clipped to what the lease actually still has: the effective month count is the lesser of `prepaid_term_months` and the stream's `remaining_term_months`, and `horizon_years` is that effective month count divided by twelve and truncated toward zero. Because a prepayment carries term risk that a perpetual purchase does not, `discount_rate_bps` is the base tier plus a term-risk premium of 150 basis points. The per-year walk is identical to the perpetual case — same growth compounding, same discount factor, same half-up rounding of each year's present value to whole cents — except that **no renewal-risk haircut is ever applied**, since every year inside the clipped horizon is contracted by construction. The rounded per-year figures are summed to a gross present value, and `value_cents` is that gross multiplied by the quantity one less five hundred divided by ten thousand — a single prepaid haircut of 500 basis points — and rounded half up to a whole number of cents.

The rounding order in both variants is pinned: each year's present value is rounded before the summation, and the prepaid haircut is applied once to the finished sum. Summing unrounded years, or applying the prepaid haircut year by year, produces divergent numbers and is incorrect.

From the value the system derives the negotiating ceiling. `max_bid_cents` is `value_cents` multiplied by 10500 basis points divided by ten thousand — one hundred five percent of model value — rounded half up to a whole number of cents. That five percent of headroom above model is the entire discretionary room the negotiating team has, and it is enforced mechanically: no offer may ever exceed it.

A valuation's `status` is a small branching machine. A `PortfolioManager` approves a `DRAFT`: `DRAFT → APPROVED`. Its three checks run in exactly this order. First the valuation must be in `DRAFT`; any other `status` — `APPROVED`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `STALE` — is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_STALE`. Then the stream's `active_tenancy_count` must be at least one, otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_TENANCY`. Then `tenancy_quality_score_snapshot` must still equal the stream's current `tenancy_quality_score`, otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_STALE` again. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `approved_at` from the current instant, and demotes every other valuation on the same stream that is currently `APPROVED`: `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`. Approval touches nothing on the stream itself. A draft whose stream's score has moved since the draft was computed is invalidated instead, `DRAFT → STALE`, and an approved valuation whose stream's score moves after approval likewise goes `APPROVED → STALE`, provided no deal that pinned it has already reached `CLOSED`. A deal pinning a stale valuation cannot advance beyond `ORIGINATION`; the attempt is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_STALE`, and an attempt to start outreach on a valuation still in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_APPROVED`. A pinned valuation in `SUPERSEDED` is treated by both of those gates exactly as `STALE` is.

### 3.5. Originating a Deal and Running the Negotiation

A **Deal** binds three things together: the capital pool that funds it, the asset being bought, and the price it was struck at. An `AcquisitionAnalyst` opens one by supplying the `portfolio_id`, the `lease_stream_id`, and the `valuation_id` of the valuation being relied on, plus an `easement_type` of either `PERPETUAL_EASEMENT` or `LONG_TERM_EASEMENT`. Two optional terms round out the paperwork: `easement_term_years`, defaulting to 0 and bounded between 0 and 99 inclusive, which is what a long-term easement is measured in; and `revenue_share_bps`, defaulting to 2500 and bounded between 0 and 5000 inclusive, which is the landowner's participation in future upside after closing.

The `valuation_id` is **pinned at creation and never re-resolved**. The three references are resolved independently and are never cross-checked against one another, so the pinned valuation need not belong to the named `lease_stream_id`. If a fresher valuation is later approved on the same stream, this deal continues to answer to the one it pinned — that is what makes the guardrail, the segregation-of-duties check, and the staleness check all deterministic. A new deal starts at `stage` `ORIGINATION` and `capital_state` `UNCOMMITTED`, increments its portfolio's `active_deal_count`, and takes its initial `rofr_state` from the tower company standing over the stream's site: `PENDING` when that company's `rofr_required` is true, and `NOT_REQUIRED` when it is false. Its `negotiation_rounds` starts at zero, its `easement_status` at `NOT_DRAFTED`, its `agreed_consideration_cents` unset, and every escrow figure at zero.

Starting outreach moves the deal `ORIGINATION → OUTREACH`, and is permitted only when the pinned valuation's `status` is `APPROVED` and the landowner is not at `DO_NOT_CONTACT`. The transition pushes the stream from `PROSPECT` to `UNDER_OFFER`.

**Offers** are the negotiating instrument. An `AcquisitionAnalyst` drafts one against the deal with an `offer_type` of either `LUMP_SUM` or `PREPAID_RENT`, an `amount_cents` of at least one cent, an `expires_at` instant after which the landowner can no longer act on it, and an optional `prepaid_term_months` defaulting to 0 and bounded between 0 and 600 inclusive. A drafted offer sits at `status` `DRAFT` with `round_number` zero. Drafting is gated on nothing beyond the method agreement below: the deal may be at any `stage`, any number of drafts may coexist, and `expires_at` may be any parseable instant, including one already behind the current instant. The offer's type must agree with the deal's pinned valuation method — a `LUMP_SUM` offer against a `PREPAID_RENT_TERM` valuation, or a `PREPAID_RENT` offer against a `LUMP_SUM_PERPETUAL` valuation, is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_METHOD_MISMATCH`.

Offers advance through a single transition surface whose `action` vocabulary splits by role. An `AcquisitionAnalyst` may submit `SEND`, `REVISE`, or `WITHDRAW`; a `Landowner` may submit `COUNTER`, `ACCEPT`, or `REJECT`. A verb sent by the wrong role is refused with `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH`, and a verb that is not declared from the offer's current `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. `LAPSE` is not part of the client vocabulary; it is applied by the system alone.

**`SEND`** takes the offer `DRAFT → SENT`. It requires the deal's `stage` to be `OUTREACH` or `NEGOTIATION`, requires the pinned valuation not to be `STALE` or `SUPERSEDED` — otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_STALE` — requires the landowner not to be at `DO_NOT_CONTACT`, requires that no other offer on the deal is currently sitting in `SENT` or `COUNTERED`, since only one proposal may be live at a time and a second is refused with `ERR_OFFER_ALREADY_OUTSTANDING`, and requires the offer's `amount_cents` to be less than or equal to the pinned valuation's `max_bid_cents` — a violation is `ERR_OFFER_EXCEEDS_GUARDRAIL`. It carries no round ceiling of its own: only `REVISE` is bounded by the three-round limit, so a send always succeeds against the counter. Sending stamps `sent_at` from the current instant, increments the deal's `negotiation_rounds` and then copies the new count into the offer's `round_number`, moves the deal `OUTREACH → NEGOTIATION` while leaving a deal already at `NEGOTIATION` there, and lifts the landowner's `outreach_status` from `NEW` to `CONTACTED` if it was still `NEW`.

**`COUNTER`** takes the offer `SENT → COUNTERED` and is available only to the deal's own landowner, only while the current instant is strictly before `expires_at` — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` — and only with a `counter_amount_cents` greater than zero. The counter amount is recorded on the offer, and the landowner's `outreach_status` moves to `NEGOTIATING`.

**`REVISE`** takes the offer back `COUNTERED → SENT` and is the analyst's answer to a counter. It requires the deal's `negotiation_rounds` to be strictly below three, the maximum number of rounds any single deal may consume; a fourth round is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The revised `amount_cents` is optional; when supplied it must still respect the pinned valuation's `max_bid_cents`, and when omitted the offer simply keeps the amount it already carried. A revision increments the deal's `negotiation_rounds`, copies the new count into `round_number`, replaces `amount_cents` with the revised figure, clears `counter_amount_cents`, and re-stamps `sent_at` from the current instant. Nothing about the deal's `stage` gates a revision.

**`ACCEPT`** takes the offer `SENT → ACCEPTED` and is the pivot of the whole pipeline. Only the deal's own landowner may accept, and only while the current instant is strictly before `expires_at`. Acceptance additionally requires the deal's `stage` to be exactly `NEGOTIATION` — a deal parked at `CAPITAL_HOLD`, for instance, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STAGE` — and requires that the funding portfolio's `capital_posture` is not `FROZEN` — refused with `ERR_PORTFOLIO_CAPITAL_FROZEN` — and that the portfolio's `available_capital_cents` is greater than or equal to the deal's computed `total_commitment_cents`, refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PORTFOLIO_CAPITAL` and HTTP 402 when the pool cannot fund the purchase. On success the offer stamps `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id`, the deal's `agreed_consideration_cents` is set to the accepted `amount_cents`, the settlement statement described in §3.7 is computed, the deal's capital state moves `UNCOMMITTED → RESERVED`, the deal's stage moves `NEGOTIATION → DILIGENCE`, the landowner's `outreach_status` moves to `UNDER_CONTRACT`, the deal's `rofr_state` is confirmed as `PENDING` when the tower company's `rofr_required` is true and `NOT_REQUIRED` otherwise, and the portfolio's `committed_capital_cents`, `available_capital_cents`, `utilization_bps`, and `capital_posture` are all recomputed.

**`REJECT`** is available to the deal's landowner from either live state — `SENT → REJECTED` and `COUNTERED → REJECTED` — and stamps `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id`. **`WITHDRAW`** is the analyst's mirror image, available from either live state with no preconditions at all — `SENT → WITHDRAWN` and `COUNTERED → WITHDRAWN` — and stamps `decided_at`. Lapsing is not requested by anyone: an offer in `SENT` whose `expires_at` has been reached by the current instant goes `SENT → LAPSED`, applied lazily on the next read of the offer or the next transition attempt against it.

When the rounds are used up and the last offer has landed in a terminal state without acceptance, the deal simply sits where it is at `NEGOTIATION`: no endpoint retires a negotiating deal, and `DEAD` is reachable only through the title-failure and consent-denial cascades of §3.6. The `PortfolioManager`'s termination described in §3.7 is the only operator-driven exit, and it is not available before `DILIGENCE`.

### 3.6. Diligence: Title, Mortgagee Consent, and the Right of First Refusal

A deal in `DILIGENCE` runs two independent tracks that must both finish before it can be cleared for closing.

**The title track.** Either an `AcquisitionAnalyst` or a `TitleOfficer` orders a **TitleReport** against the deal, optionally flagging `snda_required` — a subordination, non-disturbance and attornment requirement, defaulting to false. A fresh report is stamped with `ordered_at` from the current instant, opens at `review_status` `ORDERED` and `consent_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, and starts with `mortgage_present` false, `lender_name` unset, and `exception_count` and `open_exception_count` at zero. Ordering is gated on nothing — any deal `stage` will do — and a deal may accumulate several reports; wherever a rule reads "the deal's title report" it means the most recently created one, and a deal carrying no report at all reads as blocked with `ERR_TITLE_EXCEPTIONS_OPEN`.

Only a `TitleOfficer` drives the review, submitting an `action` to the report's transition surface. That surface's vocabulary is `RECORD_FINDINGS`, `CURE_EXCEPTION`, `CLEAR`, `FAIL`, and `REQUEST_CONSENT`. A verb that is not declared from the report's current `review_status` or `consent_status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_STATE`. The system-only lapse verbs are not part of this vocabulary.

**`RECORD_FINDINGS`** carries the searched `exception_count`, which is required and must be zero or greater, an optional `mortgage_present` boolean defaulting to false, and the `lender_name` when a mortgage is of record. Both `mortgage_present` and `lender_name` are overwritten wholesale by every recording, so omitting `lender_name` clears it. The consent track follows the same recording: `mortgage_present` false forces `consent_status` back to `NOT_REQUIRED` whatever it was holding, while `mortgage_present` true lifts `consent_status` to `PENDING_REQUEST` only when it was `NOT_REQUIRED` and otherwise leaves an already-progressed consent alone. From `ORDERED`, findings with an `exception_count` greater than zero take the report `ORDERED → EXCEPTIONS_FOUND`, setting both `exception_count` and `open_exception_count` to the reported figure. From `ORDERED`, findings with an `exception_count` of exactly zero take the report `ORDERED → CLEARED` instead, and immediately re-evaluate the deal's clearance join. Findings may also be recorded against an already-cleared report, which reopens it: `CLEARED → EXCEPTIONS_FOUND`, permitted only while the deal's `stage` is not `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STAGE` — and only with an `exception_count` greater than zero, a zero on that path being refused with `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_STATE`; it reopens the deal's diligence when the deal had already reached clearance. `RECORD_FINDINGS` is declared from `ORDERED` and `CLEARED` only: from `EXCEPTIONS_FOUND` or `FAILED` it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_STATE`.

**`CURE_EXCEPTION`** decrements the open count. While more than one exception remains open the report stays put — `EXCEPTIONS_FOUND → EXCEPTIONS_FOUND` with `open_exception_count` reduced by one. When exactly one exception remains open, the same verb finishes the job: `EXCEPTIONS_FOUND → CLEARED`, driving `open_exception_count` to zero and re-evaluating the deal's clearance join. A `CURE_EXCEPTION` against a report that is not in `EXCEPTIONS_FOUND`, or whose `open_exception_count` is already zero, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_STATE`. **`CLEAR`** is the explicit finalization verb over the same edge, `EXCEPTIONS_FOUND → CLEARED`, and requires `open_exception_count` to already be zero — a non-zero open count on that verb is likewise `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_STATE`. A `TitleOfficer` may also give up on the search entirely: **`FAIL`** takes the report `EXCEPTIONS_FOUND → FAILED` with no precondition, and that failure kills the deal from either `DILIGENCE` or `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE` — the deal's stage moves to `DEAD`, its capital moves `RESERVED → RELEASED` (or `ESCROWED → RELEASED`, in which case no escrow refund is recorded), the stream reverts to `LOST`, and the portfolio's capital ledger is recomputed. A deal already outside those two stages is left untouched while the report still records `FAILED`.

**The consent track** runs in parallel on the same report and has its own status. Discovering a mortgage takes it `NOT_REQUIRED → PENDING_REQUEST`. A `TitleOfficer` then submits **`REQUEST_CONSENT`**, `PENDING_REQUEST → REQUESTED`, permitted only when `mortgage_present` is true; a request against an unmortgaged property is refused with `ERR_MORTGAGE_CONSENT_NOT_REQUIRED`. Requesting stamps `consent_requested_at` from the current instant and sets `consent_expires_at` to twenty-one whole days after that instant, exclusive of the boundary itself.

A `Lender` — and only a `Lender` — rules on the request by submitting a `decision` of either `GRANT` or `DENY`, in each case only while the current instant is strictly before `consent_expires_at`; past that instant the lazy sweep has already moved the consent to `EXPIRED`, so a late ruling is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_STATE` rather than with any dedicated expiry error. A grant takes the consent `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, stamps `consent_decided_by_user_id`, and re-evaluates the deal's clearance join. A denial takes it `REQUESTED → DENIED`, stamps `consent_decided_by_user_id`, and kills the deal on the same terms as a title failure: stage to `DEAD` from either `DILIGENCE` or `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE`, capital to `RELEASED` without an escrow refund, stream to `LOST`, portfolio ledger recomputed. A consent left unanswered simply runs out: `REQUESTED → EXPIRED` once the current instant reaches `consent_expires_at`, applied lazily on the next read of the report or the next transition attempt against it. Expiry is recoverable — a `TitleOfficer` may submit `REQUEST_CONSENT` again, `EXPIRED → REQUESTED`, so long as the deal's `stage` is not `CLOSED`, which resets both `consent_requested_at` and `consent_expires_at`. Even a denial is recoverable in principle: `DENIED → REQUESTED` is permitted while the deal's `stage` is neither `CLOSED` nor `DEAD`, resetting the consent window on the way through. Both of those stage preconditions are reported as `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STAGE`, and every accepted `REQUEST_CONSENT` — including a first request from `PENDING_REQUEST` — reopens the diligence of a deal that had already reached `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE`, because putting consent back into flight regresses a join condition.

**The refusal track.** When the tower company standing over the site holds a right of first refusal, it must be given a chance to buy the stream on the same terms. An `AcquisitionAnalyst` serves a **RofrNotice** against the deal. Serving is gated on nothing beyond the overlap rule below: neither the deal's `stage` nor the tower company's `rofr_required` flag is consulted, so a notice may be served against a deal at any stage and against a stream whose tower company does not in fact hold a refusal right. Serving snapshots the tower company's `rofr_window_days` into `window_days_snapshot` — later edits to the tower company do not move an already-served window, and thirty days is used as the snapshot when no tower company resolves behind the stream at all — sets `served_at` and `window_start` to the current instant, sets `window_end` to `window_days_snapshot` days after `window_start`, opens the notice at `status` `SERVED`, and moves the deal's `rofr_state` to `PENDING`. The window is the half-open interval described in §2.11: `window_start` counts as inside, `window_end` counts as outside. A second notice may not be served while an earlier notice on the same deal is still inside its window — refused with `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` — but a notice whose `window_start` falls exactly on a prior notice's `window_end` is permitted, since adjacency is not overlap.

Only the `TowerCompanyAgent` of the notice's own tower site may respond, submitting a `decision` of either `WAIVE` or `EXERCISE`, and only while the current instant is at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`. An agent whose `X-User-ID` is not the identifier of the tower company standing over the notice's stream — including the case where no tower company resolves at all — is refused with `ERR_NOT_ROFR_HOLDER`, and that identity check precedes the state and window checks. A response outside those bounds, or against a notice that is no longer `SERVED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROFR_STATE`. A waiver takes the notice `SERVED → WAIVED`, stamps `responded_at` and `responded_by_user_id`, moves the deal's `rofr_state` to `WAIVED`, and re-evaluates the clearance join. An exercise takes the notice `SERVED → EXERCISED`, stamps the same two fields, moves the deal's `rofr_state` to `EXERCISED`, and takes the deal away from us: the stage becomes `ROFR_LOST` from whatever stage the deal was in, capital `RESERVED → RELEASED` — or `ESCROWED → RELEASED` with no escrow refund recorded — stream to `LOST`, portfolio ledger recomputed. Silence works in our favour: once the current instant reaches `window_end` the notice goes `SERVED → EXPIRED` lazily on the next read or transition attempt, the deal's `rofr_state` becomes `WAIVED_BY_LAPSE`, and the clearance join is re-evaluated.

### 3.7. Clearance, Escrow, Closing and the Settlement Statement

**The settlement statement** is computed the moment an offer is accepted, and recomputed at exactly one other moment: when a dispute is opened on a stream that still carries a non-terminal deal, the holdback of that deal is recalculated. Nothing else recomputes it — resolving, reopening, upholding, or dismissing a dispute leaves the already-computed figures exactly where they stand, even though the open-dispute count that produced them has moved, and a recompute is skipped altogether while `agreed_consideration_cents` is still null. Let *gross* be the deal's `agreed_consideration_cents`, which is the accepted offer's `amount_cents`. The broker tier is keyed on **gross**, on a ladder evaluated from the top: a gross at or above 30,000,000 cents — three hundred thousand dollars — sets `broker_fee_bps` to 150; failing that, a gross at or above 10,000,000 cents — one hundred thousand dollars — sets it to 200; anything below that sets it to 250. `broker_fee_cents` is gross multiplied by `broker_fee_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole number of cents.

Next comes the dispute holdback. Count the disputes on the deal's lease stream whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CURE_PENDING`, or `REOPENED`. The holdback rate is that count multiplied by 250 basis points, capped at 1000 basis points — so from four unresolved disputes onward the holdback stops growing at ten percent of gross. `holdback_cents` is gross multiplied by that capped rate, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `net_landowner_proceeds_cents` is gross less `holdback_cents`, and `total_commitment_cents` — the figure the portfolio actually has to set aside — is gross plus `broker_fee_cents`.

The order of that computation is pinned: the broker tier is selected on gross, then the fee is computed, then the holdback, then the net. Selecting the broker tier on gross-less-holdback is a different and incorrect answer, and a deal straddling a tier boundary will expose the difference. A read-only settlement projection is available for any deal at any stage — before an offer is accepted it simply reports a null `agreed_consideration_cents` and zeros throughout — returning `deal_id`, `agreed_consideration_cents`, `broker_fee_bps`, `broker_fee_cents`, `holdback_cents`, `net_landowner_proceeds_cents`, `total_commitment_cents`, `escrow_funded_cents`, `escrow_disbursed_cents`, `escrow_holdback_cents`, and `escrow_refunded_cents`.

**The clearance join.** A deal leaves `DILIGENCE` for `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE` only when three conditions hold simultaneously: the title report's `review_status` is `CLEARED`, its `consent_status` is either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `GRANTED`, and the deal's `rofr_state` is one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `WAIVED`, and `WAIVED_BY_LAPSE`. The join is evaluated automatically as a side effect of title clearance, of a consent grant, and of a refusal resolution — but because a silently blocked deal is hard to diagnose, either a `PortfolioManager` or an `AcquisitionAnalyst` may also demand an explicit evaluation from stage `DILIGENCE`. On success the deal moves `DILIGENCE → CLEAR_TO_CLOSE` and its `easement_status` moves `NOT_DRAFTED → DRAFTED`. An evaluation demanded from any stage other than `DILIGENCE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STAGE` before any blocker is examined. On failure the explicit evaluation returns the specific blocker, and the three are tested in a fixed order so that only the first surfaces: first `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_OPEN` while `rofr_state` is still `PENDING`; then `ERR_MORTGAGE_CONSENT_MISSING` while a mortgage is of record and consent has not been granted; then `ERR_TITLE_EXCEPTIONS_OPEN`, which covers not only an `open_exception_count` above zero but also a `review_status` that is anything other than `CLEARED` and the case of no title report having been ordered on the deal at all.

Clearance is reversible. If a join condition regresses — a consent expires, or a new title exception is recorded — the deal falls back `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE → DILIGENCE` and its `easement_status` reverts to `NOT_DRAFTED`.

**Escrow funding** is the `TreasuryOfficer`'s step, and it is permitted only from `capital_state` `RESERVED` while the deal's `stage` is `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE`. It takes capital `RESERVED → ESCROWED` and sets `escrow_funded_cents` equal to `total_commitment_cents`.

**Closing** belongs to a `PortfolioManager` and takes a `recording_ref` — the county recording reference for the easement. Closing requires `capital_state` to be `ESCROWED`, requires that no dispute on the lease stream sits in `OPEN`, `CURE_PENDING`, or `REOPENED` — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTE_BLOCKS_CLOSE` — and requires the closing user to differ from the `approved_by_user_id` of the pinned valuation, per §2.12. The clearance conditions are re-checked at this point too, so `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_OPEN`, `ERR_TITLE_EXCEPTIONS_OPEN`, and `ERR_MORTGAGE_CONSENT_MISSING` are all live possibilities at closing. The order in which closing rejects is pinned: the segregation-of-duties check comes first, then `stage`, then `capital_state`, then the refusal window, then the open-dispute count, then the mortgagee consent, and last the title exceptions.

A successful close is the widest cascade in the system. The deal moves `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE → CLOSED` and records `closed_by_user_id`. Capital moves `ESCROWED → DISBURSED`, with `escrow_disbursed_cents` set to the sum of `net_landowner_proceeds_cents` and `broker_fee_cents` and `escrow_holdback_cents` set to `holdback_cents`, so that the disbursement and the retained holdback together account for everything that was funded. `easement_status` moves `DRAFTED → RECORDED`, with `recording_ref` stored and `recorded_at` stamped from the current instant. The stream moves to `acquisition_status` `ACQUIRED` and `servicing_status` `PERFORMING`, and because the servicing move is a real servicing-condition change it revalues any pledge already standing against that stream and recomputes the holding facility's borrowing base. Every carrier tenancy on the stream in `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` has its `redirection_notice_status` set to `SENT`, and the stream's own `rent_redirection_status` becomes `SENT`. The landowner's `outreach_status` becomes `CLOSED`. Finally the portfolio's `deployed_capital_cents` rises by the commitment, its `committed_capital_cents` falls by the same amount, and `available_capital_cents`, `utilization_bps`, and `capital_posture` are all recomputed.

**Termination** is the `PortfolioManager`'s escape hatch, taking a `reason`, and it is available from either `DILIGENCE → TERMINATED` or `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE → TERMINATED` with no further precondition. In both cases the deal's capital moves to `RELEASED`, the stream returns to `PROSPECT`, and the portfolio's ledger is recomputed. Terminating from `DILIGENCE` releases a merely reserved commitment; terminating from `CLEAR_TO_CLOSE`, where escrow may already be funded, additionally sets `escrow_refunded_cents` equal to `escrow_funded_cents` so the money is visibly returned.

The deal's `capital_state` is its own parallel machine and worth restating on its own terms. It starts `UNCOMMITTED`. Offer acceptance reserves it — `UNCOMMITTED → RESERVED`, requiring `available_capital_cents` to be at least `total_commitment_cents` and `capital_posture` not to be `FROZEN`, and raising the portfolio's `committed_capital_cents` by the commitment. Escrow funding takes `RESERVED → ESCROWED`. Closing takes `ESCROWED → DISBURSED`. A deal that dies, loses its refusal contest, or is terminated releases what it held: `RESERVED → RELEASED` lowers the portfolio's `committed_capital_cents` by the commitment, and `ESCROWED → RELEASED` likewise lowers it. Only one of those releases refunds the escrow: a `PortfolioManager`'s termination of a deal already in `ESCROWED` sets `escrow_refunded_cents` equal to `escrow_funded_cents`. A title failure, a consent denial, or an exercised refusal that catches a deal in `ESCROWED` releases the capital without recording any refund, leaving `escrow_funded_cents` standing and `escrow_refunded_cents` at zero. `DISBURSED` and `RELEASED` are both terminal. A capital verb attempted from a state that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAPITAL_STATE`, just as a stage-inappropriate operation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STAGE`.

### 3.8. Post-Closing Servicing: Landowner Disputes and Revenue Share

Ownership does not end the relationship with the landowner; it changes it. Two servicing surfaces run against an acquired stream.

**Disputes.** A `Landowner`, a `ServicingAgent`, or a `PlatformAdmin` may open a **DisputeCase** against a lease stream with a `dispute_type` of `ACCESS`, `INTERFERENCE`, or `PROPERTY_USE` and a free-text `description`. The system records the declared role of whoever opened it in `raised_by_role`. Opening a dispute does not require the stream to be `ACQUIRED` — a landowner grievance can predate any closing — but a caller acting as `Landowner` must be the landowner the stream itself names, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_DEAL_LANDOWNER`; a `ServicingAgent` or `PlatformAdmin` faces no such ownership test. A new case opens at `status` `OPEN` with `cure_attempts` at zero and no `cure_deadline`, increments the landowner's `open_dispute_count`, and — when the stream still carries a deal outside the terminal stages `CLOSED`, `DEAD`, `ROFR_LOST`, and `TERMINATED` — triggers a recomputation of that deal's settlement holdback, since an unresolved dispute is exactly what the holdback exists to cover. A stream whose only deal has already closed gets no holdback recomputation. At most one non-terminal dispute may exist per stream and `dispute_type` pair; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN`.

The dispute transition surface's `action` vocabulary splits three ways: a `ServicingAgent` may submit `START_CURE`, a `Landowner` may submit `CONFIRM_CURE` or `REOPEN`, and a `PlatformAdmin` may submit `UPHOLD` or `DISMISS`. A verb sent by the wrong role is refused with `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH`. **`START_CURE`** takes the case `OPEN → CURE_PENDING` or `REOPENED → CURE_PENDING`. Either path requires `cure_attempts` to be strictly below three attempts; a fourth is refused with `ERR_CURE_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. Starting a cure increments `cure_attempts` and sets `cure_deadline` to thirty days after the current instant — on the first pass from `OPEN` the deadline is set, and on a repeat pass from `REOPENED` it is reset. The first-pass transition out of `OPEN` also degrades the stream's servicing condition, but only from `PERFORMING`: `PERFORMING → WATCHLIST` re-haircuts every pledge of that stream and recomputes the borrowing base of the facility holding them, while a stream already on `WATCHLIST`, `DELINQUENT`, or `IMPAIRED` is left exactly as it stands rather than being lifted or pushed anywhere.

The deal's own landowner then judges the cure. **`CONFIRM_CURE`** takes the case `CURE_PENDING → RESOLVED`, stamps `resolved_at`, recomputes `open_dispute_count`, and — if no other dispute on the stream remains in `OPEN`, `CURE_PENDING`, or `REOPENED` — restores the stream `WATCHLIST → PERFORMING`, which recomputes its pledges at full basis. **`REOPEN`** takes the case `CURE_PENDING → REOPENED` and leaves the stream on `WATCHLIST`, awaiting another attempt.

A `PlatformAdmin` adjudicates the endgame. **`UPHOLD`** is available from either `OPEN → UPHELD` or `REOPENED → UPHELD` with no precondition; it stamps `resolved_at`, drives the stream's servicing condition to `IMPAIRED` — `PERFORMING → IMPAIRED` or `WATCHLIST → IMPAIRED` — makes every pledge of that stream that was in `PLEDGED` become `INELIGIBLE` with its `eligible_value_cents` driven to zero, and recomputes the holding facility's `pledged_eligible_total_cents`, `borrowing_base_cents`, and `compliance_state`. **`DISMISS`** is likewise available from either `OPEN → DISMISSED` or `REOPENED → DISMISSED` with no precondition; it stamps `resolved_at`, and in both cases the landowner's `open_dispute_count` is recomputed, with a dismissal from `REOPENED` additionally restoring the stream `WATCHLIST → PERFORMING` when no other non-terminal dispute remains on it. `UPHELD`, `DISMISSED`, and `RESOLVED` are all terminal.

**Revenue share on future upside.** After closing, a new colocation, a lease amendment, or a rent increase on an acquired stream is a shared windfall. A `ServicingAgent` records an **AmendmentEvent** against the stream with an `event_type` of `COLOCATION_ADDED`, `AMENDMENT`, or `RENT_INCREASE`, a `gross_amount_cents` of at least one cent, an `effective_date`, and an optional `pass_through_cost_cents` defaulting to 0 and required to be zero or greater, with no upper bound of its own — a claim far exceeding gross is accepted at the shape check and dealt with by the cap below. Recording an event on a stream whose `acquisition_status` is not `ACQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_STREAM_NOT_ACQUIRED`. The event opens at `status` `RECORDED`.

The share rate is drawn from the stream's own history: it is the `revenue_share_bps` of the most recently created deal on that stream whose `stage` is `CLOSED`, and where no closed deal exists on the stream the rate falls back to the 2500-basis-point default rather than to zero. The split then depends on the event type. For a `COLOCATION_ADDED` event, `effective_pass_through_cents` is zero and `share_bps_applied` is the closed deal's `revenue_share_bps` in full. For an `AMENDMENT`, pass-through costs are recognized but capped: the cap is `gross_amount_cents` multiplied by 2000 basis points divided by ten thousand — twenty percent of gross — rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and `effective_pass_through_cents` is the lesser of the claimed `pass_through_cost_cents` and that cap; `share_bps_applied` is again the deal's `revenue_share_bps` in full. For a `RENT_INCREASE`, `effective_pass_through_cents` is zero and `share_bps_applied` is the deal's `revenue_share_bps` divided by two and truncated toward zero, on the reasoning that an escalation the acquirer already paid for warrants only half participation.

From there, `net_amount_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` less `effective_pass_through_cents`. `landowner_share_cents` is `net_amount_cents` multiplied by `share_bps_applied`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `acquirer_share_cents` is `net_amount_cents` less `landowner_share_cents`, so the two shares always exhaust the net exactly. The stream's `cumulative_revenue_share_landowner_cents` and `cumulative_revenue_share_acquirer_cents` then accumulate these **per-event rounded** figures. Rounding is per event and never on a summed gross: summing several events' nets and rounding once at the end produces a different cumulative total and is incorrect. The invariant to hold is that the two cumulative fields together equal the sum of `net_amount_cents` across all `RECORDED` amendment events on the stream.

An event booked in error can be undone. A `ServicingAgent` reverses it — `RECORDED → REVERSED` — supplying a `reversal_reason`, which is mandatory; reversal decreases the stream's `cumulative_revenue_share_landowner_cents` by that event's `landowner_share_cents` and its `cumulative_revenue_share_acquirer_cents` by that event's `acquirer_share_cents`. `REVERSED` is terminal, and a second reversal attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE`.

### 3.9. Portfolio Financing: Facilities, Pledges, Advances and the Borrowing Base

Acquired streams are not dead capital; they are collateral. A `TreasuryOfficer` opens a **FinancingFacility** against a portfolio by supplying the `portfolio_id`, a `facility_name`, an `advance_rate_bps` between 1 and 9500 basis points inclusive, a `valuation_basis` of either `MARK_TO_MODEL` or `COST_BASIS`, and optionally a `carrier_concentration_limit_bps` that defaults to 5000 and must fall between 1 and 10000 inclusive. A fresh facility carries `pledged_eligible_total_cents`, `concentration_excess_cents`, `borrowing_base_cents`, `advances_outstanding_cents`, and `pledged_stream_count` at zero, and `compliance_state` at `COMPLIANT`.

**Pledging.** A `TreasuryOfficer` pledges a stream into a facility by naming the `lease_stream_id`, a `pledge_start_date`, a `pledge_end_date`, and the mandatory `expected_facility_version` of §2.7. The stream's `acquisition_status` must be `ACQUIRED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STREAM_NOT_ACQUIRED`. The period must not collide with any existing pledge of the same stream: the candidate is tested against every pledge of that stream whose `status` is `PLEDGED` or `INELIGIBLE`, with `RELEASED` pledges excluded from the test, and a collision is declared when the candidate's `pledge_start_date` falls on or before the existing pledge's `pledge_end_date` **and** the candidate's `pledge_end_date` falls on or after the existing pledge's `pledge_start_date`. Because these are closed intervals with adjacency forbidden, a candidate beginning on the exact day an existing pledge ends collides with it and is refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, while a candidate beginning the following day is accepted. A stream may therefore be pledged to at most one facility on any given day. The two dates are never tested against each other: a `pledge_end_date` falling before its own `pledge_start_date` is accepted as shape-valid and simply describes an interval that collides with nothing.

An accepted pledge opens at `status` `PLEDGED` — unless the stream's `servicing_status` is already `IMPAIRED` or `DELINQUENT` at the moment of pledging, in which case it opens directly at `INELIGIBLE` — and immediately values itself. `valuation_basis_snapshot` copies the facility's `valuation_basis` at the moment of pledging, so later edits to the facility do not revalue existing collateral. `basis_value_cents` follows that snapshot: under `MARK_TO_MODEL` it is the `value_cents` of the earliest-created `APPROVED` valuation on the stream; under `COST_BASIS` it is the `total_commitment_cents` of the most recently created `CLOSED` deal on that stream. Either lookup that finds nothing yields a `basis_value_cents` of zero rather than a refusal, so a pledge of an acquired stream that has no surviving `APPROVED` valuation is admitted at zero basis. `haircut_bps` is read from the stream's `servicing_status` — zero basis points while `PERFORMING`, 3000 while on `WATCHLIST`, 5000 while `DELINQUENT`, and the full 10000 while `IMPAIRED` — and it is recorded on the pledge from that ladder even when the pledge is opening `INELIGIBLE` and its eligible value is therefore going to be zero anyway. `eligible_value_cents` is then zero whenever the pledge's `status` is `INELIGIBLE` or `RELEASED` or the stream's `servicing_status` is `IMPAIRED`, and otherwise is `basis_value_cents` multiplied by the quantity one less `haircut_bps` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Creating a pledge recomputes the facility's `pledged_eligible_total_cents`, `concentration_excess_cents`, `borrowing_base_cents`, `compliance_state`, and `pledged_stream_count`.

**The borrowing base** is recomputed from the facility's `PLEDGED` collateral on every pledge, release, reclassification, and advance. First, `pledged_eligible_total_cents` is the sum of `eligible_value_cents` across the facility's pledges in `PLEDGED`. Call that figure the gross eligible pool. Second comes the concentration test. The ceiling is the gross eligible pool multiplied by `carrier_concentration_limit_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and it is applied **per pledge holding** rather than per anchor carrier: `concentration_excess_cents` is the sum, across the facility's individual `PLEDGED` pledges, of the amount by which a single pledge's own `eligible_value_cents` strictly exceeds that ceiling, counting zero for any pledge at or below it. Pledges are never grouped by their streams' `anchor_carrier_name`, and that field plays no part in the calculation at all, so two pledges of streams naming the same anchor carrier are measured separately and never pooled. A facility holding exactly one `PLEDGED` pledge is exempt from the test outright: with a single holding `concentration_excess_cents` is zero however small the limit, so one pledge of 44,627,011 cents at the default 5000-basis-point limit and a 6500-basis-point advance rate yields a `borrowing_base_cents` of 29,007,557 — sixty-five percent of the whole pool, undiminished. Third, subtract `concentration_excess_cents` from the gross eligible pool to get the eligible pool after concentration. Finally, `borrowing_base_cents` is that concentration-adjusted pool multiplied by `advance_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `compliance_state` is `BORROWING_BASE_DEFICIT` whenever `advances_outstanding_cents` is strictly greater than `borrowing_base_cents`, and `COMPLIANT` otherwise. The same recomputation refreshes `pledged_stream_count`, which counts the facility's pledges whose `status` is anything other than `RELEASED` — so an `INELIGIBLE` pledge still counts even though it contributes nothing to the pool, and two pledges of the same lease stream over non-overlapping periods count twice.

Concentration bites at a fine boundary. Two pledges of the same size each sit exactly at a 5000-basis-point ceiling — half the pool apiece — and so produce no excess at all; drop the limit by a single basis point and each pledge pokes a few cents above the new ceiling, a small excess appears, and the borrowing base shrinks. That sensitivity is intentional — it is the lender's protection against a facility that is really a bet on one holding.

**Advances.** A `TreasuryOfficer` draws on the facility by requesting an `amount_cents` of at least one cent. The draw is permitted only when `advances_outstanding_cents` plus the requested amount is less than or equal to `borrowing_base_cents`; an over-draw is refused with `ERR_ADVANCE_EXCEEDS_BORROWING_BASE` and HTTP 402. A successful draw increases `advances_outstanding_cents` and re-evaluates `compliance_state`, and the response is the updated facility rather than an advance record.

**Pledge lifecycle.** A pledge's `status` moves on servicing condition. When the pledged stream's `servicing_status` becomes `IMPAIRED` or `DELINQUENT`, the pledge is reclassified `PLEDGED → INELIGIBLE`, its `eligible_value_cents` is driven to zero, and the facility's `pledged_eligible_total_cents`, `borrowing_base_cents`, and `compliance_state` are recomputed. When the stream recovers to `PERFORMING` or `WATCHLIST`, the pledge is restored `INELIGIBLE → PLEDGED`, its `eligible_value_cents` is recomputed at the haircut appropriate to the recovered condition, and the facility's borrowing base is recomputed. Both movements are server-driven consequences of the stream's servicing machine, not operator requests, and both re-read `haircut_bps` from the ladder for the stream's new condition. A pledge already in `RELEASED` is passed over entirely by this sweep: its `status`, `haircut_bps`, and zero `eligible_value_cents` never move again.

A `TreasuryOfficer` may **release** a pledge. Releasing from `PLEDGED → RELEASED` is permitted only when the facility's `compliance_state` would still be `COMPLIANT` after the release recomputation; a release that would leave `advances_outstanding_cents` above the recomputed `borrowing_base_cents` is refused with `ERR_BORROWING_BASE_DEFICIT`, because a lender cannot be stripped of collateral it has already advanced against. Releasing from `INELIGIBLE → RELEASED` carries no such precondition, since ineligible collateral was contributing nothing to the base. The projection that decides this is the whole borrowing-base calculation run again over the facility's remaining `PLEDGED` pledges with the candidate excluded — gross pool, per-holding concentration excess, and advance rate alike — not merely the current base less the departing pledge's contribution; dropping to a single remaining holding therefore lifts the concentration test off the facility entirely and can leave the projected base *higher* than the release deserved. A release sets `eligible_value_cents` to zero, recomputes the facility's `pledged_eligible_total_cents`, `concentration_excess_cents`, `borrowing_base_cents`, and `compliance_state`, and drops the pledge out of `pledged_stream_count`. `RELEASED` is terminal, and any further verb against a released pledge is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLEDGE_STATE`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that mint a new resource return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource — including transition surfaces, decisions, releases, reversals, capital adjustments, and advances — return `200 OK` and echo back the affected resource. Read operations return `200 OK`. The clock capability is the one write that mints nothing and echoes no resource: it returns `200 OK` with a `data` object carrying `current_utc` alone, and none of the common resource properties of §2.4.
