# Supplement Label Claim Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A dietary supplement firm carries regulatory exposure on three fronts at once, and today those three fronts are tracked in three different places. Ingredient eligibility — whether an article is grandfathered into the food supply or is a new dietary ingredient that must be notified to the agency before marketing — lives in a scientific affairs spreadsheet. Structure-function claims and their substantiation dossiers live in a shared drive where nobody can prove that the person who reviewed the evidence was not the person who wrote it. Supplement facts panels, allergen warnings and contraindication language are assembled by a graphic designer working from an email thread. When an adverse event arrives, or a certificate of analysis fails identity testing, nobody can answer the only question that matters: which marketed products are now non-compliant, and which claims must come off the label.

This document specifies the Supplement Label Claim Compliance API, a single system of record that unifies ingredient eligibility determination, new dietary ingredient notification, claim substantiation, label panel construction, supplier qualification, incoming-lot disposition, certificate-of-analysis review, and adverse event reporting. The service is mounted under the `/supplement-compliance` path prefix. Its organizing idea is that every product carries a continuously recomputed **compliance posture** derived from its adverse event history, its late agency filings, its rejected certificates of analysis, the qualification standing of every supplier that has shipped material into it, and the eligibility standing of every ingredient printed on its panel. When that posture degrades, the system does not merely raise a flag — it retracts approved claims, supersedes printed panels, and returns a label-locked product to draft, automatically and in the same request that caused the degradation.

Two design constraints shape the whole specification. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the author of a substantiation dossier may not review it, and the reviewer who cleared a dossier may not approve the claim that dossier supports. Second, **every deadline is evaluated against a server-side logical clock**, never against wall-clock time, so that the 75-day premarket waiting period, the 30-day post-market claim notification window, the 15-business-day serious adverse event deadline, and the 6-year recordkeeping horizon are all reproducible and testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including the read-only list and fetch endpoints and the test-driving clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is missing, present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` holds a string outside the six roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This is the first check performed on any request, before the URL is resolved to a resource. Once the headers are accepted, the role is matched against the authorized-role list of the target endpoint given in §4; a role that does not appear on that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist`, `SubstantiationReviewer`, `ClaimApprovalOfficer`, `QualityAssuranceManager`, `SupplierRepresentative`, and `ComplianceAuditor`.

A `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` builds the regulatory record: it registers ingredients and runs their eligibility determinations, drafts and files new dietary ingredient notifications and records agency responses, creates products and their supplement-facts lines, drafts claims, opens substantiation dossiers and appends evidence to them, drafts and validates label panels, and files serious adverse event reports with the agency. A `SubstantiationReviewer` exists for exactly one purpose — rendering the accept-or-reject decision on a dossier under review — and is deliberately barred from authoring the evidence it judges and from approving the claim it cleared. A `ClaimApprovalOfficer` signs off approved claims, locks validated panels for print, starts marketing, and files the post-market claim notification. A `QualityAssuranceManager` owns the incoming-material and safety side: supplier registration and qualification decisions, lot release and recall, certificate-of-analysis review, and adverse event triage. A `ComplianceAuditor` is a read-only oversight role with access to every fetch, list and computed-read endpoint and no mutating capability whatsoever.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this difference is load-bearing. For `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist`, `SubstantiationReviewer`, `ClaimApprovalOfficer`, `QualityAssuranceManager` and `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a rule demands an audit stamp — as `authored_by_user_id` on a claim or a dossier, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a dossier or a certificate of analysis, as `approved_by_user_id` on a claim — and compares recorded stamps against each other for segregation-of-duties purposes, but it never compares the value to any entity's `id`. For `SupplierRepresentative`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `Supplier`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Supplier` compare the resource's supplier foreign key to `X-User-ID`: when a `SupplierRepresentative` receives a lot, the `supplier_id` in the body must equal `X-User-ID`, and when it files a certificate of analysis, the `supplier_id` of the certificate's lot must equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). No other role is scoped this way; a `QualityAssuranceManager` may receive lots and file certificates for any supplier.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of matching records to skip before the returned window begins and defaults to 0. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the applied filters before the pagination window is taken and `has_more` indicates whether any matching record remains beyond the returned window.

Unless a list endpoint states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — that is, oldest record first. Unrecognized query parameters are ignored rather than rejected; the alternate spellings `page` and `page_size` in particular carry no meaning in this API, because pagination is expressed only through `limit` and `offset`.

Each list endpoint accepts a small set of equality filters over the resource's own columns, and combining filters narrows the result set conjunctively. Ingredients are filterable by `eligibility_status`, `ingredient_class` and `predecessor_ingredient_id`; NDI notifications by `ingredient_id` and `notification_status`; products by `lifecycle_status` and `label_posture`; supplement-facts lines by `product_id`, `ingredient_id` and `line_status`; claims by `product_id`, `ingredient_id`, `substantiation_status` and `notification_status`; substantiation dossiers by `claim_id` and `dossier_status`; label panels by `product_id`, `panel_status` and `supersedes_panel_id`; adverse event reports by `product_id`, `aer_status` and `is_serious`; suppliers by `qualification_status` and `country_code`; ingredient lots by `supplier_id`, `ingredient_id` and `lot_status`; and certificates of analysis by `lot_id` and `coa_status`. Because the data model is flat — every collection is addressed at its own top-level path and no resource is created underneath a parent path — these filters are the only way to walk a foreign key in reverse, and they are therefore part of the contract rather than a convenience.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Regulatory Audit Trail

Every resource in this system — products, ingredients, supplement-facts lines, NDI notifications, claims, substantiation dossiers, label panels, adverse event reports, suppliers, ingredient lots and certificates of analysis — carries the same four common properties. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client control; a client-supplied identifier is never honoured. The `created_at` timestamp is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, fixed at creation and never rewritten. The `updated_at` timestamp uses the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. The `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation was requested directly or arrived as a cascade from an operation on some other resource. All eleven resource types carry all four properties; none of them is exempt.

The "cascade counts as a mutation" rule matters more here than in most systems, because a single request routinely rewrites four or five rows. Locking a panel, for example, advances the panel's own `version` and also advances the `version` of the product it belongs to, because the product's `lifecycle_status` changed as a consequence. Creating a replacement panel advances the product's `version` even though the request body never mentions the product's own attributes. Releasing a lot advances both the lot's `version` and the supplier's, because the supplier's released-mass ledger moved. A client that wants to reason about staleness therefore has to re-read a parent after any operation that touches it, and §2.6 turns that requirement into an enforced precondition on the two endpoints where a stale write would silently destroy regulatory evidence.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and each endpoint in this API carries at most one path identifier. When the identifier names no existing row, the request fails with 404 and the type-specific code for the collection addressed: `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_INGREDIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOSSIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NDI_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COA_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys arriving in a request body are resolved with the same codes, and §2.7 pins the order in which competing body references are resolved.

### 2.5. The Logical Regulatory Clock and Calendar Arithmetic

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Instead the server maintains a single **logical clock** whose value is the authoritative "now" for every deadline, waiting period, effective window and timeliness determination in the specification. At process start the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real time, not even before the clock has been set explicitly for the first time. A test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it for all subsequent requests. Moving the clock does not by itself re-evaluate any deadline, recompute any due date or change any state: timeliness is always decided by the next business request that asks the question. Every role may drive the clock.

Two flavours of temporal value coexist and must not be confused. **Instants** — `determined_at`, `submitted_at`, `waiting_period_end`, `agency_response_at`, `approved_at`, `notified_at`, `reviewed_at`, `validated_at`, `locked_at`, `triaged_at`, `released_at`, `recalled_at`, `last_decision_at` — are full ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with millisecond resolution, taken directly from the logical clock at the moment the corresponding transition fired. **Calendar dates** — `marketing_start_date`, `notification_due_date`, `effective_from`, `effective_until`, `received_date`, `submission_due_date`, `retention_until` — are UTC dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component. Whenever a rule compares a date to the logical clock, it is the clock's UTC calendar date that participates in the comparison, and the comparison is inclusive on the deadline: filing on exactly the due date is on time, and filing one calendar day later is late.

Calendar arithmetic in this system is plain day counting unless a rule says otherwise. Adding 30 days to a date advances the calendar date by 30 days; adding 75 days to an instant advances the instant by 75×24 hours; adding years for the recordkeeping horizon keeps the month and day and moves only the year, with the single exception that a February 29 anniversary maps to February 28. Only one rule in the system counts business days rather than calendar days, and it is described with the adverse event workflow.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency: Stale-Version Guards on Panel and Dossier Creation

Exactly two write endpoints in this API demand that the client prove it has observed the current state of a parent resource before it is allowed to write. Both guards are stale-version comparisons against a stored integer; neither has anything to do with requests overlapping in time.

The first guard protects panel creation. A request that drafts a supplement facts panel must carry `expected_product_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` the client last read from the target `Product`. If that value does not equal the product's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created. The guard exists because panel creation is destructive in two ways at once: naming a `supersedes_panel_id` retires an existing panel and vacates its effective window, and superseding a locked panel drags the product's `lifecycle_status` from `LABEL_LOCKED` back to `DRAFT`. A client whose view of the product predates a lock, a posture change or another panel creation would therefore replace a printed label it has never seen.

The second guard protects substantiation dossier creation. A request that opens a dossier must carry `expected_claim_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` the client last read from the target `StructureFunctionClaim`. If that value does not equal the claim's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Opening a dossier supersedes whatever draft dossier the claim already had, so a client whose view of the claim predates a submission for review, a rejection cascade or another dossier creation would discard evidence it never observed.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for version staleness. The two reads that supply these values are the single-product fetch and the single-claim fetch, both of which return the resource's current `version`.

### 2.7. Request Screening Order

When a request would fail more than one check, the response must carry the error of the check that comes first in the ordering below. This is not a documentation convenience: an implementation that evaluates checks in a different order will report a plausible but wrong error code, and a caller cannot distinguish "the panel is frozen" from "the panel does not exist" if the order is not fixed.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present, non-empty, and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears on the target endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Path resource existence.** The row named by the endpoint's single path identifier exists — otherwise the type-specific 404 from §2.4.
4. **Body shape.** Every required input field is present; every enum-typed field holds a value from its declared set; every numeric field has the declared type and sign, so that `net_quantity_g`, `serving_weight_g`, `amount_per_serving_mg` and `quantity_kg` are each strictly greater than zero, `panel_position` is at least 1, `assay_percent_of_label` is zero or greater, and `audit_score` lies between 0 and 100 inclusive; every date field is a `YYYY-MM-DD` string; and `outcome_codes` and `declared_warning_codes` are arrays of strings. A failure here is `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). This screen applies to every creating and amending request.
5. **Body foreign keys, resolved in input order.** `product_id` must name an existing product (`ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`) on line creation, claim creation, panel creation and adverse event intake. Then `ingredient_id` and, after it, `predecessor_ingredient_id` must each name an existing ingredient when supplied (`ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_FOUND`) on ingredient registration, ingredient amendment, line creation, claim creation, NDI notification drafting and lot intake. Then `claim_id` must name an existing claim (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`) on dossier creation. Then `lot_id` must name an existing lot (`ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`) on certificate intake. Then `supplier_id` must name an existing supplier (`ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`) on lot intake. Then `supersedes_panel_id`, when supplied on panel creation, must name an existing panel whose own `product_id` equals the incoming `product_id` — a panel belonging to a different product is reported as `ERR_PANEL_NOT_FOUND`, not as a validation error.
6. **Supplier ownership scope.** The `SupplierRepresentative` restriction of §2.1, enforced on lot intake and certificate intake, yielding `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
7. **Field-pair sanity.** On panel creation and panel amendment, `effective_from` must not be later than `effective_until`; equal dates describe a legal one-day window. A reversed pair is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (HTTP 422).
8. **Stale-version guards.** The two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.6, yielding `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` on panel creation and `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` on dossier creation. These two share a rank because no endpoint carries both.
9. **State preconditions on the target resource.** The eleven resource-state screens listed immediately below, each yielding its own 409. They also share a rank, because each applies to a disjoint set of operations.
10. **The claim notification dimension.** Filing a claim notification requires the claim's notification dimension to be exactly `DUE`; `ERR_CLAIM_NOTIFICATION_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409) otherwise.
11. **The frozen-label gate**, then **the supplier qualification gate**, then **the ingredient eligibility gate** — in that order, yielding `ERR_PRODUCT_LABEL_FROZEN`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` and `ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` respectively.
12. **Mandatory label content and panel capacity**, evaluated in this order: serving-size format, then servings-per-container reconciliation, then mandatory warning coverage, then disclaimer presence, then the printed line budget.
13. **Incoming-material quality**: the approved identity-method screen at certificate intake, then the identity-verification screen at lot release, then the acceptability screen at certificate acceptance.
14. **Temporal windows**: the elapsed-waiting-period screen on an acknowledgement, then the NDI window-overlap screen on a submission, then the panel effective-window overlap screen on panel creation.
15. **Uniqueness**: printed panel position, supplier lot code, and claim text.
16. **Substantiation sufficiency and segregation of duties**: dossier readiness at submission, dossier acceptance at approval, the segregation-of-duties comparison, the substantiation score threshold, and the revision-round cap.
17. **Supplier decision prerequisites and label composition floors**: the audit score floor on a qualification, the remediation reference requirement on a reinstatement, the ineligible-ingredient screen on line creation, and the active-lines floor on panel validation.

The eleven resource-state screens at rank 9 are these. A product's `lifecycle_status` must permit the operation — starting marketing requires `LABEL_LOCKED`, amending a product requires `DRAFT`, and both adding and removing a supplement-facts line require the parent product to be in `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_STATE_INVALID`. An ingredient's `eligibility_status` must be `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT` to run an eligibility determination, and must be `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT` or `NDI_REQUIRED` to amend the monograph, otherwise `ERR_INGREDIENT_STATE_INVALID`. An NDI notification must be in `DRAFT` to be submitted and in `SUBMITTED` to receive an agency response, otherwise `ERR_NDI_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`. A claim's `substantiation_status` must be `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` to be submitted for review or reworded, `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW` to be approved, and `APPROVED` both to be withdrawn and to be notified to the agency, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. A dossier must be in `DRAFT` to accept an evidence append and in `UNDER_REVIEW` to accept a review decision, otherwise `ERR_DOSSIER_STATE_INVALID`. A panel must be in `DRAFT` to be validated or amended and in `VALIDATED` to be locked, otherwise `ERR_PANEL_STATE_INVALID`. An adverse event report must be in `RECEIVED` to be triaged and in `TRIAGED_SERIOUS` to be filed with the agency, otherwise `ERR_AER_STATE_INVALID`. A supplier qualification decision must be legal from the supplier's current `qualification_status` — `QUALIFY` only from `UNQUALIFIED`, `PROBATION` only from `QUALIFIED`, and both `REINSTATE` and `DISQUALIFY` only from `PROBATION` — otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_STATE_INVALID`. A lot must be `QUARANTINED` to be released and `RELEASED` to be recalled, otherwise `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. A certificate of analysis must be in `RECEIVED` to be reviewed, otherwise `ERR_COA_STATE_INVALID`. And a supplement-facts line must have `line_status` equal to `ACTIVE` to be amended or removed, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_INGREDIENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision, Rounding, and Conserved Ledgers

Wherever this specification rounds, it rounds **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint moves away from zero, so 12.5 becomes 13 and 0.125 becomes 0.13. Two precisions are in play. Substantiation scores and lot masses in kilograms are carried to **2 decimal places**; percentages printed on a panel, line counts, risk indices and counters are **integers**. Where a rule prescribes the order of rounding relative to summation, that order is part of the rule and not an implementation detail: rounding after summing produces a different answer than summing after rounding whenever the individual fractions cluster near a midpoint, and the specification always says which one applies.

Two invariants must hold after every operation that touches them, and an implementation that satisfies them will produce the same numbers as one that recomputes from scratch.

The **lot mass identity** says that mass is neither created nor destroyed. For every ingredient lot, `quarantined_quantity_kg` plus `released_quantity_kg` plus `rejected_quantity_kg` equals `quantity_kg`, exactly, at 2-decimal precision on kilograms. `quantity_kg` itself is immutable after intake. Every lot transition therefore *moves* mass between the three buckets rather than adjusting one of them in isolation. The supplier ledger reconciles against those buckets: a supplier's `total_released_kg` always equals the sum of `released_quantity_kg` over that supplier's lots whose `lot_status` is `RELEASED` — lots in any other disposition contribute nothing. The consequence that matters most is the compensating pair: releasing a lot and then recalling it must leave `total_released_kg` at exactly its pre-release value, with zero drift at 2-decimal precision.

The **claim ledger identity** says that a product's two claim counters are always literal counts. `active_claim_count` equals the number of that product's claims whose `substantiation_status` is `APPROVED`, and `withdrawn_claim_count` equals the number whose `substantiation_status` is `WITHDRAWN`. Neither counter is affected by a claim's notification dimension. Here too the compensating pair is the load-bearing case: approving a claim and then withdrawing it leaves `active_claim_count` back at its pre-approval value with the unit moved into `withdrawn_claim_count`. The posture cascade that pulls an approved claim back for recheck also decrements `active_claim_count`, so the identity holds after cascades as well as after direct actions.

### 2.9. The Compliance Risk Index and the Label Posture Gate

Each product carries two independent status dimensions on the same row. Its `lifecycle_status` — one of `DRAFT`, `LABEL_LOCKED` and `MARKETED` — records where the label and the marketing programme stand. Its `label_posture` — one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN` — is a purely derived compliance gate. Posture moves on its own and gates lifecycle transitions; a lifecycle transition never changes posture.

Posture is derived from an integer **compliance risk index** which in turn is derived from four denormalized counters maintained on the product row. `serious_ae_count` is the number of that product's adverse event reports whose `aer_status` is `TRIAGED_SERIOUS` or `SUBMITTED_TO_FDA`. `nonserious_ae_count` is the number whose `aer_status` is `TRIAGED_NONSERIOUS`. `late_notification_count` is the number of that product's claims whose `notification_status` is `SUBMITTED_LATE`, plus the number of its adverse event reports whose `submission_timeliness` is `LATE`. `rejected_coa_count` is the number of rejected certificates of analysis whose lot's ingredient appears on an `ACTIVE` supplement-facts line of the product. The counters are maintained incrementally by the transitions that cause them to move — triage, agency filing, certificate rejection, claim notification and claim withdrawal — and the index calculation reads the stored counters rather than recounting the underlying rows.

The index is assembled in two stages, and the stage boundary is the part most easily got wrong. First a **base** is formed: start from a floor of 10, add 20 for each serious adverse event, add 6 for each non-serious adverse event, add 15 for each late agency filing, and add 8 for each rejected certificate of analysis. Second, an **escalation surcharge** is chosen *from the base alone* — the pre-surcharge figure, never from the running total that already includes it. A base below 30 attracts no surcharge; a base of at least 30 but below 60 attracts a surcharge of 5; a base of 60 or more attracts a surcharge of 12. The `compliance_risk_index` is then the base plus the surcharge, capped at 100. Selecting the tier from the post-surcharge total looks equally plausible and is wrong: a base of 56 yields an index of 61 under the pinned order, but 68 under the other.

Posture is then decided against two thresholds and two cross-aggregate conditions. The **derived supplier set** of a product is the set of suppliers reachable as follows: take every supplement-facts line of the product whose `line_status` is `ACTIVE`, collect those lines' ingredients, then collect every ingredient lot of any of those ingredients whose `lot_status` is `RELEASED`, `RECALLED` or `REJECTED`, and take those lots' suppliers. A lot still in `QUARANTINED` never contributes a supplier, because nothing from it has entered manufacturing. An empty derived supplier set satisfies every supplier condition vacuously.

A product's posture is `FROZEN` if its `compliance_risk_index` is 90 or more, **or** any supplier in its derived supplier set has `qualification_status` equal to `DISQUALIFIED`, **or** any ingredient on an `ACTIVE` line has `eligibility_status` equal to `NDI_OBJECTED`. Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` if the `compliance_risk_index` is 70 or more, **or** any supplier in the derived supplier set has `qualification_status` equal to `PROBATION`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. The boolean `requires_label_revision` is simply the statement that the posture is not `OPEN`: it is set to true whenever the posture leaves `OPEN` and cleared to false only when the posture returns to `OPEN`.

Posture is recomputed immediately after every recomputation of the compliance risk index, after every supplier qualification transition, and after every ingredient transition into `NDI_OBJECTED`. A `FROZEN` posture is an absolute stop on forward labelling and claim work: starting marketing, drafting a panel, validating a panel, locking a panel, submitting a claim for substantiation review and approving a claim are all refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_LABEL_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) while the governing product sits in `FROZEN`. Those six operations are the complete list; nothing else consults the frozen gate. What each posture transition *does* to the product's claims, dossiers and panels is described in §3.9.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three signatures are collected along a claim's path to the label, and no two adjacent signatures may come from the same principal. The `authored_by_user_id` stamp is written from `X-User-ID` when a claim is drafted and again when a substantiation dossier is opened. The `reviewed_by_user_id` stamp is written from `X-User-ID` when a `SubstantiationReviewer` renders a decision on a dossier. The `approved_by_user_id` stamp is written from `X-User-ID` when a `ClaimApprovalOfficer` approves a claim.

Two comparisons are enforced, both reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). At dossier review time, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the dossier's own `authored_by_user_id`: an author may not review its own evidence. At claim approval time, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on the accepted dossier backing the claim: the reviewer who cleared the evidence may not also sign off the claim. Because roles are distinct anyway — only a `SubstantiationReviewer` may review and only a `ClaimApprovalOfficer` may approve — these comparisons bite when the same human operator holds two roles and reuses one identifier across them, which is exactly the audit finding the rule exists to prevent.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Ingredient Monograph and Settling Eligibility

Everything in this system begins with an ingredient record, and a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` opens one by registering the article's monograph data. The `ingredient_name` is the common or usual name that will eventually be printed on the panel. The `ingredient_class` places the article in one of six taxonomy classes — `VITAMIN`, `MINERAL`, `BOTANICAL`, `AMINO_ACID`, `PROBIOTIC` or `EXCLUDED_SUBSTANCE` — and that choice reaches far beyond taxonomy, because it also selects which analytical methods are acceptable for identity confirmation on incoming material. Two booleans capture the submitter's regulatory assertions: `pre_dshea_marketed` asserts that the article was present in the food supply before the statutory grandfather date, and `chemical_alteration` asserts that the article as intended for use has been chemically altered relative to its pre-statute form. `daily_intake_mg` records the highest recommended daily intake in milligrams under the intended conditions of use.

Three further monograph attributes are optional at registration and drive label composition later. `rdi_mg` carries the reference daily intake in milligrams; leaving it null is a positive statement that no Daily Value has been established for the article, and it propagates all the way into the printed panel. `allergen_code` names the major food allergen the ingredient carries and takes one of `MILK`, `EGG`, `FISH`, `CRUSTACEAN_SHELLFISH`, `TREE_NUT`, `PEANUT`, `WHEAT`, `SOYBEAN` or `SESAME`, or is null when the ingredient carries none. `contraindication_code` names the population or interaction requiring cautionary language and takes one of `ANTICOAGULANT`, `PREGNANCY`, `PEDIATRIC` or `IMMUNOSUPPRESSANT`, or is null when none applies. Finally, `predecessor_ingredient_id` may name another ingredient record that this one supersedes, which is how a reformulation is traced back to the article it replaced; the reverse direction is walked by filtering the ingredient list on that same attribute.

A freshly registered ingredient starts in `eligibility_status` `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT` with `ndi_notification_required` false and `determined_at` null. While it remains unsettled, its monograph may be amended — `rdi_mg`, `allergen_code`, `contraindication_code`, `daily_intake_mg` and `predecessor_ingredient_id` are all revisable — and amendment is permitted in `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT` and in `NDI_REQUIRED` but nowhere else. An amendment has a quiet but important consequence: every `ACTIVE` supplement-facts line that references the amended ingredient has its `percent_dv_declared` and `dv_established` recomputed on the spot, so a corrected reference daily intake immediately re-prices every panel line that depends on it.

The eligibility determination itself is a single decisive operation, available only while the ingredient is in `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT`, and it reads nothing but the ingredient's own monograph data. If the `ingredient_class` is `EXCLUDED_SUBSTANCE`, the determination lands on `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT → INELIGIBLE`: the article may never be printed on a panel, `ndi_notification_required` stays false, and no notification path is available to rescue it. Otherwise the article qualifies as grandfathered — `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT → GRANDFATHERED`, with `ndi_notification_required` set false — only when all three of the following hold: `pre_dshea_marketed` is true, `chemical_alteration` is false, and the recommended daily intake is at most five grams, that is `daily_intake_mg` no greater than 5000. If any one of those three fails — the article was not in the pre-statute food supply, or it has been chemically altered, or its daily intake exceeds 5000 milligrams — the determination lands on `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT → NDI_REQUIRED` and `ndi_notification_required` becomes true. All three branches stamp `determined_at` with the logical clock and advance the ingredient's `version`. Attempting a second determination on an already-settled ingredient is refused with `ERR_INGREDIENT_STATE_INVALID`, and the same code guards an amendment attempted after the determination has settled the article into `GRANDFATHERED`, `NDI_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `NDI_OBJECTED` or `INELIGIBLE`.

`ndi_notification_required` is not an independent input; it is true exactly when `eligibility_status` is one of `NDI_REQUIRED`, `NDI_PENDING`, `NDI_ACKNOWLEDGED` or `NDI_OBJECTED`, and false in the other three states. Only two eligibility values ever clear an ingredient for labelled use: `GRANDFATHERED` and `NDI_ACKNOWLEDGED`. Every other value blocks something, and the two most consequential blocks are described in §3.3 and §3.5.

### 3.2. New Dietary Ingredient Notification and the Premarket Waiting Period

An ingredient whose determination came back `NDI_REQUIRED` cannot reach the label until the agency has been notified and the statutory premarket waiting period has run. A `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` drafts the notification package by naming the `ingredient_id` it covers, its own docket identifier as `submitter_reference`, and the history-of-use and safety basis narrative as `safety_narrative`. The package is drafted for an ingredient sitting in `NDI_REQUIRED` or already in `NDI_PENDING` from an earlier filing. A new package begins in `notification_status` `DRAFT`, with `submitted_at`, `waiting_period_end`, `agency_response_at` and `agency_objection_reason` all null.

Filing the package with the agency drives `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and mints the waiting period. `submitted_at` is set to the logical clock, and `waiting_period_end` is set to exactly 75 days after `submitted_at` — an instant, at millisecond resolution, not a calendar date. The notification then **occupies** the interval that begins at `submitted_at` inclusive and ends at `waiting_period_end` exclusive, on its ingredient. Only notifications whose `notification_status` is `SUBMITTED` occupy anything; a package still in `DRAFT`, or one that has already reached `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `OBJECTED`, releases its interval.

That occupancy is enforced as an overlap check. A filing attempted at some instant would itself occupy the 75 days beginning at that instant, and it is refused with `ERR_NDI_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) if that prospective interval intersects the occupied interval of any *other* submitted notification on the same ingredient. Concretely, a collision exists when the filing instant falls strictly before an existing notification's `waiting_period_end` and that existing notification's `submitted_at` falls strictly before the filing instant plus 75 days. Because the upper bound is exclusive, a filing made at exactly the instant an existing notification's `waiting_period_end` falls is *adjacent*, not overlapping, and is accepted. Filing also cascades onto the ingredient: an ingredient in `NDI_REQUIRED` moves `NDI_REQUIRED → NDI_PENDING`, and one already in `NDI_PENDING` stays there. A filing attempted on a package that is not in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_NDI_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`.

The agency's answer is recorded as a `response` of either `ACKNOWLEDGE` or `OBJECT` on a package that is in `SUBMITTED`; the same state code guards a response recorded against any other status. An `ACKNOWLEDGE` may only be recorded once the waiting period has actually run — the logical clock must be at or after the package's `waiting_period_end` — and a premature acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (HTTP 409). An `OBJECT` is exempt from that timing rule and may be recorded at any point after filing, because the agency is free to object early. An acknowledgement drives `SUBMITTED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, stamps `agency_response_at` from the logical clock, and cascades the ingredient `NDI_PENDING → NDI_ACKNOWLEDGED`, at last clearing the article for labelled use.

An objection is the harshest event in the system. It drives `SUBMITTED → OBJECTED`, stamps `agency_response_at`, records the supplied `agency_objection_reason` on the package, and cascades the ingredient `NDI_PENDING → NDI_OBJECTED`. That ingredient state is terminal — the objected article can never re-enter the eligibility flow — and it immediately propagates outward: every product carrying that ingredient on an `ACTIVE` supplement-facts line has its `label_posture` recomputed, and because an `NDI_OBJECTED` ingredient on an active line is one of the three frozen conditions of §2.9, each of those products reaches `FROZEN` with all the consequences described in §3.9. A single agency letter can therefore supersede the printed panels of an entire product family and drag label-locked products back to draft.

### 3.3. Registering a Product and Composing Its Supplement-Facts Lines

A finished supplement is registered by a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` with four facts about the pack: the `brand_name` under which it is sold, the `product_name` that will serve as the statement of identity on the principal display panel, the `net_quantity_g` net contents of the container in grams, and the `servings_per_container` figure the container is declared to hold. Net quantity must be strictly greater than zero and the serving count must be a positive integer. A new product opens in `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT` with `label_posture` `OPEN`, `marketing_start_date` null, all four risk counters at zero, `compliance_risk_index` at its floor value of 10, `active_claim_count` and `withdrawn_claim_count` at zero, and `requires_label_revision` false. Pack facts remain amendable — `brand_name`, `product_name`, `net_quantity_g` and `servings_per_container` may each be revised — but only while the product is still in `DRAFT`; an amendment attempted once the label is locked or the product is marketed is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_STATE_INVALID`. Net quantity is worth amending carefully, because it silently controls how many printed lines the panel is allowed to carry.

Alongside the standard product read, a computed **risk profile** read exposes the compliance picture of §2.9 for one product: its `serious_ae_count`, `nonserious_ae_count`, `late_notification_count` and `rejected_coa_count`, the `compliance_risk_index` those four counters produce, the escalation surcharge tier that was applied to reach it, the derived supplier set that participates in the posture decision, and the resulting `label_posture`. It is the read a `ComplianceAuditor` uses to explain a frozen label without having to reconstruct the cascade by hand.

The supplement facts panel is composed one line at a time. A line is created by naming the `product_id` it belongs to and the `ingredient_id` it declares, together with `amount_per_serving_mg` — the quantitative amount per serving in milligrams as it will be printed — and `panel_position`, the 1-based print order of the line inside the panel. The amount must be strictly positive and the position at least 1. Lines may only be added to, amended on, or removed from a product still in `DRAFT`; any of the three attempted against a `LABEL_LOCKED` or `MARKETED` product is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_STATE_INVALID`. `panel_position` must be unique among the product's lines whose `line_status` is `ACTIVE`, both when a line is created and when an existing line's position is changed; a collision is refused with `ERR_PANEL_POSITION_TAKEN` (HTTP 409). Notably, a `REMOVED` line does not hold its position, so a slot freed by removal may be reused immediately.

Not every ingredient may be printed. Adding a line whose ingredient has `eligibility_status` of `INELIGIBLE` or `NDI_OBJECTED` is refused with `ERR_INGREDIENT_INELIGIBLE_FOR_LABEL` (HTTP 409). Note the asymmetry with the lock-time gate of §3.5: at line-creation time an ingredient still working through its notification — `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT`, `NDI_REQUIRED` or `NDI_PENDING` — is perfectly acceptable, because the firm is expected to build the panel while the notification is pending. Only the two terminal failures are refused up front; the stricter requirement that every printed ingredient be `GRANDFATHERED` or `NDI_ACKNOWLEDGED` is deferred to the moment the panel is locked for print.

Each line carries two computed attributes derived from its ingredient's reference daily intake. `dv_established` is true exactly when the referenced ingredient's `rdi_mg` is not null, and false when it is null. When no Daily Value has been established — `rdi_mg` null — `dv_established` is false and `percent_dv_declared` is null, and the printed panel will instead carry a footnote symbol. When a Daily Value does exist, `dv_established` is true and the declared percentage is computed from the raw ratio of the line's `amount_per_serving_mg` to the ingredient's `rdi_mg`, expressed as a percentage — that is, the amount divided by the reference intake and multiplied by 100. That raw percentage is then reduced to a whole number by two rules applied in sequence: if it is greater than zero but less than 0.5, the printed value is 1 rather than 0, because a trace contribution is never printed as zero; otherwise it is rounded half up to the nearest whole percent, so a raw 12.5 prints as 13. Both attributes are computed when the line is created, recomputed when the line's `amount_per_serving_mg` or `panel_position` is amended, and recomputed again whenever the underlying ingredient's monograph is amended.

Removing a line is a soft delete, and the removal responds with the retained row rather than an empty body. `line_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `REMOVED`, `percent_dv_declared` is cleared to null, `dv_established` is cleared to false, and the row's `version` advances. The row stays fully queryable so that a superseded panel remains auditable years later, but a `REMOVED` line leaves every panel computation at once: it contributes no printed line, no warning code, no Daily Value coverage, and no supplier to the product's derived supplier set. Amending or removing a line that is already `REMOVED` is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_INGREDIENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.4. Claim Development, Substantiation Evidence, and the Revision Loop

A claim ties one product to one of its ingredients and asserts something about the effect of that ingredient. A `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` drafts it by naming the `product_id` and the `ingredient_id`, supplying the verbatim `claim_text` exactly as it will appear in labelling and advertising, and choosing a `claim_type` of `STRUCTURE_FUNCTION`, `NUTRIENT_CONTENT` or `GENERAL_WELLBEING`. The first and third of those categories carry a regulatory consequence that surfaces on the panel: a product with an approved claim of either type must print the FDA disclaimer, while a `NUTRIENT_CONTENT` claim does not trigger that requirement. Drafting stamps `authored_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, sets `revision_round` to 0 and `requires_recheck` to false, and opens the claim with `substantiation_status` `DRAFT` and `notification_status` `NOT_DUE`. `claim_text` must be unique among the product's claims whose `substantiation_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`, checked both at drafting and at rewording; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_TEXT` (HTTP 409). Withdrawn text is deliberately excluded from the uniqueness scope, so a retired claim's wording can be reused. Rewording a claim — changing `claim_text`, `claim_type`, or both — is permitted only while the claim is in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`.

The two status dimensions on a claim evolve independently and must not be collapsed into one. `substantiation_status` runs `DRAFT`, `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` and `WITHDRAWN`, and its middle two states form a loop. `notification_status` runs `NOT_DUE`, `DUE`, `SUBMITTED_TIMELY` and `SUBMITTED_LATE`, and is governed entirely by §3.6. A claim can sit in `APPROVED` on the substantiation dimension and `DUE` on the notification dimension and then move only on the notification dimension; it can equally be dragged from `APPROVED` back to `REVISION_REQUESTED` while its notification dimension, `notification_due_date` and `notification_due_basis` all stay exactly where they were.

Evidence lives in a **substantiation dossier**, and a claim may have many dossiers over its life but only one live draft at a time. Opening a dossier requires the `claim_id`, the `expected_claim_version` stale-version guard of §2.6, and a `dossier_summary` narrating the totality of evidence relied on. A new dossier begins in `dossier_status` `DRAFT` with `authored_by_user_id` stamped from `X-User-ID`, an empty `evidence_studies` array, `study_count` at 0, `screening_score` at 0.00, and `final_score`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at`, `review_decision` and `rejection_reason` all null. Opening a dossier also advances the parent claim's `version` — which is precisely why the guard exists — and drives any other dossier of the same claim that is still in `DRAFT` along `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, retiring it without deleting it.

Studies are appended to a draft dossier one at a time, never edited and never removed; `evidence_studies` is append-only. Each append supplies a `study_type` of `RCT_HUMAN`, `OBSERVATIONAL_HUMAN`, `ANIMAL` or `IN_VITRO`, a `subject_count`, a `dose_match` boolean stating whether the study dose matched the intended conditions of use, a `peer_reviewed` boolean, and a `citation`. The append pushes the study onto `evidence_studies` in arrival order, advances `study_count` by 1, and recomputes `screening_score`. Appending to a dossier that has left `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_DOSSIER_STATE_INVALID`.

Both scores come from the same weighted-scoring procedure, differing only in which studies participate and how two of the four factors behave. For each participating study, four factors are multiplied together. The **base weight** comes from the study type: 30 for `RCT_HUMAN`, 18 for `OBSERVATIONAL_HUMAN`, 8 for `ANIMAL` and 4 for `IN_VITRO`. The **subject factor** is fixed at 1.0 for an `ANIMAL` or `IN_VITRO` study, since subject counts are not comparable there; for a human study it is the study's `subject_count` divided by a variant-specific denominator, capped at 1.0 so that an over-powered study earns no bonus. The **dose factor** is 1.0 when `dose_match` is true and 0.60 when it is false. The **peer factor** is 1.0 when peer review is not being enforced; when it is enforced, it is 1.0 for a study whose `peer_reviewed` is true and 0.50 for one whose `peer_reviewed` is false. Each study's contribution is the product of its four factors, **rounded half up to two decimal places before it is added to anything**. The score is then the sum of those already-rounded per-study contributions, itself rounded half up to two decimal places, and capped at 100.00. The rounding order is pinned: rounding each study first and then summing is the rule, and summing the raw products before a single final rounding is not.

The **provisional screening score** is what `screening_score` holds while the dossier is in `DRAFT`, and it is recomputed from scratch on every evidence append. Every study in `evidence_studies` participates regardless of peer-review status, the human-study denominator is 50 subjects, and peer review is not enforced, so the peer factor is 1.0 for every study including those whose `peer_reviewed` is false. It is a deliberately generous early read: it tells the author whether the file is heading in the right direction while the peer-review status of the citations is still being verified.

The **competent-and-reliable final score** is a far stricter measure, computed exactly once and frozen into `final_score` at the moment the claim enters substantiation review; it is never recomputed afterwards, no matter what happens to the dossier. Only studies whose `peer_reviewed` is true participate at all — a non-peer-reviewed study is excluded from the set entirely rather than down-weighted — the human-study denominator rises to 100 subjects, and peer review is enforced. Neither variant looks at ingredient, product or supplier state; both read only `evidence_studies` in append order. The practical effect is that a file which screens at a comfortable score can freeze far lower, because half its studies vanish and the surviving human studies need twice the subjects to earn full credit.

Submitting a claim for substantiation review is the transition that freezes the score, and it has five preconditions. The claim must be in `DRAFT` or in `REVISION_REQUESTED`; from `REVISION_REQUESTED` it must additionally have a `revision_round` strictly below 3, and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The governing product must not be `FROZEN`. The claim's ingredient must have `eligibility_status` of `GRANDFATHERED` or `NDI_ACKNOWLEDGED`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409). And the claim must have exactly one dossier in `DRAFT` whose `study_count` is at least 1 — no draft dossier, more than one, or an empty one is refused with `ERR_DOSSIER_NOT_READY` (HTTP 409). When all five hold, the claim moves `DRAFT → UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED → UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW`, that draft dossier moves `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, its `final_score` is computed and frozen, and the claim's `version` advances. The resubmission edge additionally advances `revision_round` by 1: the counter records completed revision loops, so a claim that has looped three times sits at `revision_round` 3 and can never be submitted again.

A `SubstantiationReviewer` renders the verdict on a dossier under review by supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, with `rejection_reason` accompanying a rejection. Both outcomes stamp `reviewed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, stamp `reviewed_at` from the logical clock, and record the verdict in `review_decision`. An `ACCEPT` carries two further preconditions: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the dossier's `authored_by_user_id` per §2.10, and the frozen `final_score` must be at least 65.00 — the competent-and-reliable evidence threshold — with a shortfall refused as `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409). Acceptance drives `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED`. A `REJECT` is exempt from the score threshold, since rejecting weak evidence is the point, but the segregation-of-duties comparison still applies; rejection drives `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, records `rejection_reason`, and cascades the parent claim `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, advancing the claim's `version`. The author then opens a fresh dossier — superseding nothing, since the rejected one is terminal — and resubmits, consuming one of the three permitted revision loops.

Approval is reserved to a `ClaimApprovalOfficer` and requires the claim to be in `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW`, the product not to be `FROZEN`, and the claim to have exactly one dossier in `ACCEPTED`; a missing acceptance is refused with `ERR_DOSSIER_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409). The acting `X-User-ID` must also differ from that accepted dossier's `reviewed_by_user_id`. Approval drives `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `approved_at` from the logical clock, clears `requires_recheck` to false, advances the product's `active_claim_count` by 1, and advances the claim's `version`. Whether approval also opens the notification window depends on the product's `lifecycle_status`, and that is the subject of §3.6.

Withdrawal is the compensating action for approval and may be performed by either a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` or a `ClaimApprovalOfficer` on a claim in `APPROVED`. It requires a `withdrawal_reason`, drives `APPROVED → WITHDRAWN`, decrements the product's `active_claim_count` by 1 and increments its `withdrawn_claim_count` by 1 so that the ledger identity of §2.8 continues to hold, recomputes the product's `compliance_risk_index` and then its `label_posture`, and advances the claim's `version`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal on the substantiation dimension. Withdrawal is the ordinary route back to `OPEN` for a product whose posture degraded because of a claim.

### 3.5. Constructing, Validating and Locking the Supplement Facts Panel

A **label panel** is one printed supplement facts panel over one dated window. Drafting one is a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` operation and requires, besides the `product_id` and the `expected_product_version` guard of §2.6, a `panel_format` of `STANDARD`, `SIMPLIFIED` or `LINEAR`; a `serving_size_text` giving the printed serving statement; a `serving_weight_g` mass for one serving in grams, strictly positive; a `servings_per_container_declared` figure of at least 1; an `effective_from` and an `effective_until` calendar date bounding the window over which this panel is the printed label; and a `declared_warning_codes` array listing the warning statement codes the submitter has actually printed. Two further inputs are optional: `disclaimer_text` carries the verbatim FDA disclaimer sentence pair when the submitter prints it and is null when none is printed, and `supersedes_panel_id` names the panel this one replaces. A new panel opens in `panel_status` `DRAFT` with `required_warning_codes` empty, `panel_lines_required`, `panel_lines_allowed` and `panel_total_dv_coverage_pct` all null, and `validated_at` and `locked_at` null. Before validation, a draft panel's printed content is freely amendable: `serving_size_text`, `serving_weight_g`, `servings_per_container_declared`, `effective_from`, `effective_until`, `declared_warning_codes` and `disclaimer_text` may all be revised, and each amendment advances the panel's `version`.

Panels of the same product may not fight over the calendar. A panel **occupies** the closed calendar-date interval running from its `effective_from` through its `effective_until`, both bounds inclusive, and only panels in `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `LOCKED` occupy anything — a `SUPERSEDED` panel has vacated its window. Before an incoming panel is created, its own window is widened by a seven-day changeover buffer on **both** sides — the buffer exists because printed stock takes about a week to flush through a co-packer — and that buffered candidate interval is intersected against the occupied interval of every other live panel of the same product. A collision exists when the buffered candidate's start falls on or before an existing panel's `effective_until` **and** that existing panel's `effective_from` falls on or before the buffered candidate's end; because both comparisons are inclusive, windows that merely touch do collide. The panel named by `supersedes_panel_id` is excluded from the comparison set, since it is being retired by this very request and vacates its window. A collision is refused with `ERR_PANEL_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Separately and earlier, a window whose `effective_from` is later than its `effective_until` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`; equal dates are a legal one-day window.

Supplying `supersedes_panel_id` is how a marketed label is revised, and it makes panel creation a mutating operation on three rows at once. The named panel must exist and must belong to the same product as the incoming panel, or the request fails with `ERR_PANEL_NOT_FOUND`. When the request succeeds, the named panel moves `VALIDATED → SUPERSEDED` or `LOCKED → SUPERSEDED` depending on where it stood, the product's `version` advances, and — if the superseded panel was the locked one that had put the product into `LABEL_LOCKED` — the product itself moves `LABEL_LOCKED → DRAFT`. That cyclic edge is the heart of the label revision loop: a product that has been label-locked returns to draft the moment a replacement panel is drafted, and it stays there until the replacement is validated and locked in turn. A product whose `label_posture` is `FROZEN` may not have a panel drafted at all.

Validation is where the panel is actually checked against the printed-label rules, and it may only run on a panel in `DRAFT` whose product is not `FROZEN`. The product must also have at least one supplement-facts line in `ACTIVE`; a panel for a product with no active lines is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_HAS_NO_ACTIVE_LINES` (HTTP 409). Five content checks then run in a fixed order.

First, `serving_size_text` must be a printed serving statement of the required shape: a number — one or more digits, optionally followed by a decimal point and one or more further digits — then exactly one space, then exactly one of the unit words `capsule`, `capsules`, `tablet`, `tablets`, `scoop`, `scoops`, `mL` or `g`, and nothing else, matching the pattern `^[0-9]+(\.[0-9]+)? (capsule|capsules|tablet|tablets|scoop|scoops|mL|g)$`. A malformed statement is refused with `ERR_SERVING_SIZE_FORMAT_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

Second, the declared serving count must reconcile in two directions at once. `servings_per_container_declared` must equal the product's `net_quantity_g` divided by the panel's `serving_weight_g`, rounded half up to a whole number, **and** it must equal the product's own `servings_per_container`. Failing either comparison is refused with `ERR_SERVING_COUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Third, mandatory warning coverage is checked against a computed set. The panel's `required_warning_codes` always contains the code `PEDIATRIC_KEEP_OUT_OF_REACH`, which every supplement panel must print. Then, for each of the product's `ACTIVE` supplement-facts lines, the line's ingredient contributes a code for each of its two cautionary attributes: an ingredient whose `allergen_code` is non-null contributes the code formed by prefixing `ALLERGEN_` to that allergen value — so a soy-bearing ingredient contributes `ALLERGEN_SOYBEAN` — and an ingredient whose `contraindication_code` is non-null contributes the code formed by prefixing `CONTRAINDICATION_` to that value, so an anticoagulant-interacting ingredient contributes `CONTRAINDICATION_ANTICOAGULANT`. The collection has set semantics: two lines carrying the same allergen collapse to one code. `required_warning_codes` is then that set sorted in ascending lexicographic order, which makes the stored array byte-stable. `REMOVED` lines contribute nothing. The panel passes when every member of `required_warning_codes` also appears in the submitter's `declared_warning_codes`; extra declared codes are permitted and are not an error. A missing required code is refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_WARNING_MISSING` (HTTP 422).

Fourth, the disclaimer requirement. If the product has at least one claim whose `substantiation_status` is `APPROVED` and whose `claim_type` is `STRUCTURE_FUNCTION` or `GENERAL_WELLBEING`, then the panel's `disclaimer_text` must be present and must equal, verbatim, the sentence pair `This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.` An absent or altered disclaimer in that circumstance is refused with `ERR_DISCLAIMER_MISSING` (HTTP 422). A product with no approved claim of either type, or with only an approved `NUTRIENT_CONTENT` claim, has no disclaimer obligation.

Fifth, the panel must physically fit. `panel_lines_required` is assembled by counting: a fixed header block of 4 lines, plus one line for each `ACTIVE` supplement-facts line of the product, plus one dagger-footnote line if at least one of those active lines has `dv_established` false — one footnote covers all of them, so the value is 1 or 0 and never more — plus one line for each code in the computed `required_warning_codes`, plus one disclaimer line if the product has an approved `STRUCTURE_FUNCTION` or `GENERAL_WELLBEING` claim. Claims in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `WITHDRAWN` never add the disclaimer line. Extra codes declared beyond the required set consume no budget. `panel_lines_allowed` depends on the `panel_format` and the pack size. A `LINEAR` panel has a flat allowance of 8 lines. A `STANDARD` panel takes the product's `net_quantity_g` divided by 10 with the fraction discarded, adds 12 to that whole number, caps the result at 40, and then raises it to 20 if it fell below — the floor division first, then the upper cap, then the lower floor, in exactly that order. A `SIMPLIFIED` panel does the same with different constants: `net_quantity_g` divided by 20 with the fraction discarded, plus 8, capped at 24, then raised to 12 if below. Validation fails with `ERR_PANEL_LINE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) when `panel_lines_required` exceeds `panel_lines_allowed`; equality passes.

Validation also computes the panel's Daily Value coverage. `panel_total_dv_coverage_pct` is the sum of the `percent_dv_declared` values of the product's supplement-facts lines that are both `ACTIVE` and have `dv_established` true, walked in ascending `panel_position` order — and here too the rounding order is pinned. Each line's percentage has **already** been rounded to a whole number by the per-line rule of §3.3, and it is those whole numbers that are added together; summing the unrounded ratios and rounding the total once is explicitly not the rule, and yields a different integer whenever the per-line fractions cluster around a half percent. `REMOVED` lines and lines with no established Daily Value are excluded, and a product whose active lines all lack a Daily Value yields a coverage of 0. When every check passes, the panel persists `required_warning_codes`, `panel_lines_required`, `panel_lines_allowed` and `panel_total_dv_coverage_pct`, moves `DRAFT → VALIDATED`, and stamps `validated_at` from the logical clock.

Locking is reserved to a `ClaimApprovalOfficer` and is the point at which the label becomes committed to print. The panel must be in `VALIDATED`, the product must not be `FROZEN`, and — the stricter gate deferred from §3.3 — **every** ingredient referenced by an `ACTIVE` supplement-facts line of the product must have `eligibility_status` of `GRANDFATHERED` or `NDI_ACKNOWLEDGED`. A single line whose ingredient is still in `DRAFT_ASSESSMENT`, `NDI_REQUIRED` or `NDI_PENDING` blocks the lock with `ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A successful lock moves the panel `VALIDATED → LOCKED`, stamps `locked_at` from the logical clock, advances the panel's `version`, and cascades onto the product: `DRAFT → LABEL_LOCKED`, with the product's `version` advanced. That cascade has its own preconditions — the product must be in `DRAFT` and must not be `FROZEN` — so the panel lock and the product lifecycle move together or not at all.

### 3.6. Going to Market and the Thirty-Day Claim Notification Window

Marketing begins with a single `ClaimApprovalOfficer` action on a product whose label is locked. The request carries `marketing_start_date`, the declared launch date; the product's stored `marketing_start_date` is set to the UTC calendar date of the logical clock at the instant the transition fires, and the product moves `LABEL_LOCKED → MARKETED`, advancing its `version`. Three preconditions guard the transition. The product's `lifecycle_status` must be exactly `LABEL_LOCKED` — a `DRAFT` product has no committed label and a `MARKETED` product has already launched, and either is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_STATE_INVALID`. The product's `label_posture` must not be `FROZEN`. And every supplier in the product's derived supplier set, as defined in §2.9, must have `qualification_status` equal to `QUALIFIED`; a supplier sitting in `UNQUALIFIED`, `PROBATION` or `DISQUALIFIED` blocks the launch with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 409). A product whose derived supplier set is empty — nothing has been released, recalled or rejected against any of its ingredients yet — satisfies that check vacuously and may launch. `MARKETED` is terminal on the lifecycle dimension: there is no un-marketing.

Launching is also the event that starts the regulatory notification clock on the product's claims. Every claim of the product whose `substantiation_status` is `APPROVED` and whose `notification_status` is still `NOT_DUE` moves `NOT_DUE → DUE` in the same request, and each such claim has its `notification_due_date` and `notification_due_basis` computed at that moment. Claims that are not yet approved are untouched, and will pick up their own window if and when they are approved later.

The window is always 30 days wide but is anchored differently depending on which came first, the approval or the launch, and the choice of anchor is made once at the `NOT_DUE → DUE` transition and never re-evaluated. When a claim reached `APPROVED` while its product was still in `DRAFT` or `LABEL_LOCKED`, the basis is `PRODUCT_LAUNCH_BASIS` and `notification_due_date` is the product's `marketing_start_date` plus 30 days; the approval date is irrelevant, because the notification obligation does not exist until the product is actually on the market. When a claim reaches `APPROVED` while its product is already `MARKETED`, the basis is `CLAIM_ACTIVATION_BASIS` and `notification_due_date` is the UTC calendar date of the claim's `approved_at` plus 30 days; the notification obligation starts the day the claim goes live. The same claim on the same product therefore gets a later due date under the activation basis than under the launch basis whenever approval follows marketing. Approval of a claim on a product still in `DRAFT` or `LABEL_LOCKED` leaves `notification_status` at `NOT_DUE`, with `notification_due_date` and `notification_due_basis` both null, until the launch sweeps it up.

Filing the notification is a `ClaimApprovalOfficer` action carrying a `submission_reference`, the agency's receipt identifier. It requires the claim's `substantiation_status` to be `APPROVED` and its `notification_status` to be exactly `DUE`; filing against a claim whose notification dimension is `NOT_DUE`, `SUBMITTED_TIMELY` or `SUBMITTED_LATE` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOTIFICATION_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409), while a claim that is not approved is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Timeliness is decided by comparing the logical clock's UTC calendar date against `notification_due_date`, inclusively. On or before the due date the claim moves `DUE → SUBMITTED_TIMELY`; strictly after it, `DUE → SUBMITTED_LATE`. Filing on exactly the due date is timely. Either outcome stamps `notified_at` from the logical clock, records `submission_reference`, and advances the claim's `version`; both are terminal on the notification dimension.

A late filing is not merely recorded, it is charged. Reaching `SUBMITTED_LATE` increments the product's `late_notification_count` by 1, which recomputes `compliance_risk_index` and then `label_posture`. Because a single late claim notification is worth 15 index points, two late filings on a product that has also absorbed a serious adverse event will push it past the restricted threshold on their own — which is exactly the intended feedback: a firm that misses agency deadlines loses the freedom to make new claims.

### 3.7. Supplier Qualification, Lot Disposition and Certificate Review

Incoming material is governed by a chain of three resources, and the compliance consequences of that chain reach all the way to the printed label. A `QualityAssuranceManager` registers a supplier with a `supplier_name` legal name and a `country_code` ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code for the manufacturing site, optionally supplying an `audit_score` up front. A new supplier opens in `qualification_status` `UNQUALIFIED` with `coa_rejection_count` at 0, `total_released_kg` at 0.00, `remediation_reference` null and `last_decision_at` null.

Qualification standing moves only through recorded decisions, and every decision carries a `decision` value with its own legality and its own prerequisites. `QUALIFY` is legal only from `UNQUALIFIED` and requires an `audit_score` — supplied in the request or already stored on the supplier — of at least 70; a missing or insufficient score is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_SCORE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). It drives `UNQUALIFIED → QUALIFIED`, records the score in `audit_score`, and stamps `last_decision_at`. `PROBATION` is legal only from `QUALIFIED` and drives `QUALIFIED → PROBATION`. `REINSTATE` is legal only from `PROBATION`, requires a non-empty `remediation_reference` naming the corrective-action file — a missing one is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_REFERENCE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) — and drives `PROBATION → QUALIFIED`, recording the reference and resetting `coa_rejection_count` to 0. `DISQUALIFY` is legal only from `PROBATION` and drives `PROBATION → DISQUALIFIED`, which is terminal. Every decision stamps `last_decision_at` from the logical clock and advances the supplier's `version`. A decision that is not legal from the supplier's current standing is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_STATE_INVALID`. Each of the four decisions also recomputes `label_posture` on every product whose derived supplier set contains this supplier: a move into `PROBATION` restricts those products, a move into `DISQUALIFIED` freezes them, and a reinstatement may relax a frozen product back toward `RESTRICTED` or all the way to `OPEN` — the precise cascade rules are in §3.9. The `QUALIFIED ↔ PROBATION` pair is a genuine cycle, so a supplier may be put on probation, remediated, and put on probation again.

Material arrives as an **ingredient lot**. Intake names the `supplier_id` that shipped it, the `ingredient_id` it contains, the supplier's own `lot_code` as printed on the container, and a `quantity_kg` total mass strictly greater than zero. `lot_code` must be unique per supplier across all of that supplier's lots regardless of their `lot_status`, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_LOT_CODE_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). Either a `QualityAssuranceManager` or the material's own `SupplierRepresentative` may take a lot in, subject to the ownership scope of §2.1. A new lot opens in `lot_status` `QUARANTINED` with `identity_verified` false, its full `quantity_kg` sitting in `quarantined_quantity_kg`, and `released_quantity_kg` and `rejected_quantity_kg` both at 0.00, satisfying the mass identity of §2.8 from the first instant. `released_at`, `recalled_at` and `recall_reason` are null.

A **certificate of analysis** is the laboratory's characterization of a lot, and it is where identity testing is actually adjudicated. Filing one names the `lot_id` it characterizes, the `lab_name` of the issuing laboratory, the `identity_test_method` used, an `identity_match` boolean stating whether the test confirmed the labelled article, an `assay_percent_of_label` result expressed as a percentage of the label amount, a `heavy_metals_pass` boolean and a `microbial_pass` boolean. The method must be one of `HPTLC`, `HPLC`, `MASS_SPEC`, `DNA_BARCODE`, `MICROSCOPY` or `ORGANOLEPTIC`, and it must be **approved for the ingredient class of the certificate's lot**, which is where the `ingredient_class` choice made back in §3.1 finally bites. A `VITAMIN` accepts `HPLC` or `MASS_SPEC`. A `MINERAL` accepts only `MASS_SPEC`. A `BOTANICAL` accepts `HPTLC`, `DNA_BARCODE` or `MASS_SPEC`. An `AMINO_ACID` accepts `HPLC` or `MASS_SPEC`. A `PROBIOTIC` accepts only `DNA_BARCODE`. An `EXCLUDED_SUBSTANCE` has no approved method at all, so every certificate filed against a lot of such an ingredient is refused. `MICROSCOPY` and `ORGANOLEPTIC` are valid method values but are approved for no class, so a certificate naming either is always refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_METHOD_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422), as is any other class-method mismatch.

A certificate that clears the method screen opens in `coa_status` `RECEIVED` with `review_decision`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at` and `rejection_reason` all null, and with `coa_acceptable` computed immediately at intake. `coa_acceptable` is true exactly when all four of the following hold: `identity_match` is true, `assay_percent_of_label` is at least 95.0 and at most 120.0 with both bounds inclusive so that exactly 95.0 and exactly 120.0 both pass, `heavy_metals_pass` is true, and `microbial_pass` is true. Acceptability is computed but does not by itself dispose of anything — the certificate still requires review.

A `QualityAssuranceManager` reviews a received certificate with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, plus a `rejection_reason` on a rejection. Both outcomes stamp `reviewed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `reviewed_at` from the logical clock and record the verdict in `review_decision`. An `ACCEPT` requires `coa_acceptable` to be true, and an acceptance of an unacceptable certificate is refused with `ERR_COA_NOT_ACCEPTABLE` (HTTP 409); acceptance drives `RECEIVED → ACCEPTED` and, when `identity_match` is true, sets the lot's `identity_verified` to true. A `REJECT` is exempt from the acceptability requirement — rejecting a failing certificate is the whole point — and drives `RECEIVED → REJECTED`, launching the four-hop cascade described at the end of this subsection.

Releasing a quarantined lot to manufacturing is the payoff of all that testing, and it has three preconditions. The lot's `lot_status` must be `QUARANTINED`. The lot's own supplier must have `qualification_status` equal to `QUALIFIED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. And the lot's `identity_verified` must be true, meaning at least one certificate on that lot has reached `ACCEPTED` with a confirmed identity; a lot with no accepted certificate is refused with `ERR_LOT_IDENTITY_UNVERIFIED` (HTTP 409). Release drives `QUARANTINED → RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` from the logical clock, moves the mass so that `released_quantity_kg` becomes the full `quantity_kg` and `quarantined_quantity_kg` falls to 0.00, increments the supplier's `total_released_kg` by that mass, and advances both the lot's and the supplier's `version`.

Recall is the compensating action for release. It requires the lot to be in `RELEASED` and a `recall_reason`, drives `RELEASED → RECALLED`, stamps `recalled_at`, and moves the mass again: `rejected_quantity_kg` becomes the full `quantity_kg` and `released_quantity_kg` returns to 0.00, so the per-lot identity of §2.8 still holds. The supplier's `total_released_kg` is decremented by the same mass, restoring it to exactly its pre-release value with no drift at two decimal places, and both `version` counters advance. Because a recalled lot's `lot_status` remains one of the three dispositions that populate a product's derived supplier set, recall does **not** remove the supplier from the posture calculation — the material did reach manufacturing, and the regulatory record says so — so recall also recomputes `label_posture` on every product with an `ACTIVE` line on the recalled lot's ingredient. `RECALLED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal lot dispositions.

A rejected certificate is the single most consequential event in the incoming-material chain, because it propagates through four hops in one request. First, every product with an `ACTIVE` supplement-facts line on the lot's ingredient has its `rejected_coa_count` incremented by 1, and its `compliance_risk_index` recomputed. Second, the lot itself is disposed of according to where it stood: a lot in `QUARANTINED` moves `QUARANTINED → REJECTED` with its full mass moved from `quarantined_quantity_kg` into `rejected_quantity_kg`, while a lot already in `RELEASED` moves `RELEASED → RECALLED` with `recall_reason` recorded as `COA_REJECTED`, its mass moved into `rejected_quantity_kg`, and the supplier's `total_released_kg` compensated downward exactly as in a manual recall. Third, the supplier's `coa_rejection_count` is incremented by 1, and that count drives its qualification standing directly: a count reaching 1 moves a `QUALIFIED` supplier `QUALIFIED → PROBATION`, and a count reaching 2 moves a supplier already on probation `PROBATION → DISQUALIFIED`. Fourth, that supplier transition recomputes `label_posture` on every product whose derived supplier set contains the supplier — `RESTRICTED` for the probation case, `FROZEN` for the disqualification case — with the full consequences of §3.9. One failing laboratory result can therefore retract a product's approved claims, and two can supersede its printed panel.

### 3.8. Adverse Event Intake, Triage, Agency Filing and Recordkeeping

An adverse event report enters the system as received, not as adjudicated. Either a `QualityAssuranceManager` or a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` takes it in against a `product_id`, recording the `reporter_type` — one of `CONSUMER`, `HEALTHCARE_PROVIDER` or `DISTRIBUTOR` — the `received_date` on which the firm actually received the report, the verbatim `narrative` as received, and an `outcome_codes` array drawn from `DEATH`, `LIFE_THREATENING`, `HOSPITALIZATION`, `DISABILITY`, `CONGENITAL_ANOMALY`, `INTERVENTION_REQUIRED` and `OTHER`. The report opens in `aer_status` `RECEIVED` with `is_serious` false, `submission_due_date`, `submitted_at`, `submission_timeliness`, `submission_reference` and `triaged_at` all null. Seriousness is deliberately not decided at intake, because intake is a clerical act and triage is a medical judgement.

`retention_until` is the recordkeeping horizon and is minted from `received_date` at intake: the same month and day, six years later. The single edge case is a leap-day intake — a report received on 29 February retains until 28 February six years on. The report and its narrative must remain retrievable through that date, which is why nothing in this system ever deletes an adverse event report.

Triage is a `QualityAssuranceManager` action on a report still in `RECEIVED`, and it forks into two branches whose side effects differ sharply. The serious branch fires when the report's `outcome_codes` array contains at least one of `DEATH`, `LIFE_THREATENING`, `HOSPITALIZATION`, `DISABILITY` or `CONGENITAL_ANOMALY` — note that `INTERVENTION_REQUIRED` and `OTHER` are not serious outcomes on their own — or when the triaging manager supplies `serious_override` as true, which forces the serious branch even when no serious outcome code is present. That branch sets `is_serious` true, drives `RECEIVED → TRIAGED_SERIOUS`, stamps `triaged_at` from the logical clock, mints a `submission_due_date`, increments the product's `serious_ae_count` by 1, and advances the report's `version`. The non-serious branch fires when the array contains none of the five serious outcomes **and** `serious_override` is absent or false: it sets `is_serious` false, drives `RECEIVED → TRIAGED_NONSERIOUS`, stamps `triaged_at`, leaves `submission_due_date` null, increments the product's `nonserious_ae_count` by 1, and advances the report's `version`. Only the serious branch mints a deadline, and only a report on the serious branch can ever be filed with the agency; `TRIAGED_NONSERIOUS` is terminal. Both branches recompute the product's `compliance_risk_index` and then its `label_posture`, and the weights differ by more than a factor of three — a serious event is worth 20 index points and a non-serious one 6 — so a single serious report moves a pristine product from an index of 10 to an index of 30, and the escalation tier that base selects pushes the stored index to 35.

The serious-event deadline is the only rule in this system that counts **business days** rather than calendar days. Starting from `received_date` as day zero, which is itself never counted, the calendar is walked forward one day at a time; a day that falls on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday increments the count, while a Saturday or a Sunday is skipped without counting. `submission_due_date` is the date on which the count reaches 15, and it is therefore always a weekday. Public holidays are not modelled at all — only Saturdays and Sundays are skipped — so a report received on a Friday is due on the Friday three weeks later.

Filing the report with the agency is a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` action on a report in `TRIAGED_SERIOUS`, carrying the agency's `submission_reference`. It drives `TRIAGED_SERIOUS → SUBMITTED_TO_FDA`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, records the reference, and advances the report's `version`. `submission_timeliness` is then set by comparing the logical clock's UTC calendar date against `submission_due_date`: on or before the due date it is `TIMELY`, strictly after it is `LATE`. A `LATE` filing increments the product's `late_notification_count` by 1 — the same counter a late claim notification feeds — which recomputes `compliance_risk_index` and then `label_posture`. `SUBMITTED_TO_FDA` is terminal, and because it is one of the two statuses counted by `serious_ae_count`, filing a report never reduces the product's risk index.

### 3.9. Posture Cascades, Retraction, and the Road Back to Open

A product's posture is never set directly by any request. It is recomputed as a consequence, and every recomputation that lands on a different value fires a posture transition with its own side effects. Six transitions exist, and their side effects are asymmetric on purpose: degradation retracts work, while recovery does not restore it.

The first degradation step is `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, which fires when a recomputation finds either a `compliance_risk_index` of 70 or more, or at least one supplier in the derived supplier set sitting in `PROBATION`, while none of the frozen conditions holds. It sets `label_posture` to `RESTRICTED` and `requires_label_revision` to true, and then it retracts claim work in three coordinated moves. Every claim of the product whose `substantiation_status` is `APPROVED` is dragged `APPROVED → REVISION_REQUESTED` with `requires_recheck` set true, and the product's `active_claim_count` is decremented by 1 for each such claim, keeping the ledger identity of §2.8 intact. For every one of those claims, a substantiation dossier sitting in `ACCEPTED` is dragged `ACCEPTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and in the process its `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at` and `review_decision` are all cleared back to null — a re-reviewed dossier must be judged afresh, and may be accepted or rejected again by any reviewer other than its author. Crucially, the notification dimension of each dragged claim is left completely untouched: `notification_status`, `notification_due_date` and `notification_due_basis` all keep their values, so a claim that was `DUE` when the cascade hit is still `DUE` afterwards and its 30-day deadline keeps running even though the claim is no longer approved. The product's `version` advances. `requires_recheck` stays true on the claim until it next reaches `APPROVED`, at which point the approval clears it to false.

`OPEN → FROZEN` and `RESTRICTED → FROZEN` are the same event reached from two different places, and they fire when a recomputation finds a `compliance_risk_index` of 90 or more, **or** a supplier in the derived supplier set in `DISQUALIFIED`, **or** an ingredient on an `ACTIVE` line in `NDI_OBJECTED`. Both set `label_posture` to `FROZEN` and `requires_label_revision` to true, and both then dismantle the printed label: every panel of the product whose `panel_status` is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `LOCKED` is driven to `SUPERSEDED`, and if the product's `lifecycle_status` was `LABEL_LOCKED` it is returned to `DRAFT`. The product's `version` advances. A frozen product cannot start marketing, cannot have a panel drafted, validated or locked, and cannot have a claim submitted for review or approved — those six operations all report `ERR_PRODUCT_LABEL_FROZEN`. Note that a product already `MARKETED` stays `MARKETED` when it freezes; freezing blocks forward work but does not pull a product off the market, and the compliance record then shows a marketed product with no live panel, which is exactly the alarm state the firm needs to see.

Recovery runs the same calculation in reverse, and it is driven by compensating actions: recalling a lot, withdrawing a claim, or reinstating a supplier. `RESTRICTED → OPEN` fires when a recomputation finds `compliance_risk_index` below 70, no supplier in the derived supplier set in either `PROBATION` or `DISQUALIFIED`, and no ingredient on an `ACTIVE` line in `NDI_OBJECTED`. It sets `label_posture` back to `OPEN`, clears `requires_label_revision` to false, and advances the product's `version` — and that is all it does. Claims dragged into `REVISION_REQUESTED` are not restored to `APPROVED`; they must be resubmitted and re-approved through the ordinary loop of §3.4, consuming a revision round.

A frozen product recovers only when the condition that froze it is genuinely gone, and a supplier reinstatement is the usual trigger. `FROZEN → RESTRICTED` fires when no supplier in the derived supplier set is `DISQUALIFIED`, no ingredient on an `ACTIVE` line is `NDI_OBJECTED`, and the `compliance_risk_index` sits at 70 or more but below 90 — the product is out of the frozen band but still restricted. It sets `label_posture` to `RESTRICTED` and advances the `version`; `requires_label_revision` stays true, because a revision is still required. `FROZEN → OPEN` fires when no supplier in the derived supplier set is in `PROBATION` or `DISQUALIFIED`, no ingredient on an `ACTIVE` line is `NDI_OBJECTED`, and the `compliance_risk_index` is below 70; it sets `label_posture` to `OPEN`, clears `requires_label_revision` to false, and advances the `version`. In neither case are the superseded panels resurrected. A product that froze must have a fresh panel drafted, validated and locked before it can be label-locked again, and an ingredient that reached `NDI_OBJECTED` can never be cleared at all — the only route forward for that product is to remove the objected ingredient's line, which takes the ingredient out of the active line set and therefore out of the frozen condition.
