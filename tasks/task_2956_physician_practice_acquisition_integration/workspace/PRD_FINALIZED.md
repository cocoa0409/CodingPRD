# Practice Acquisition Integration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A health system's practice acquisition team does not fail because it cannot find targets. It fails in the eighteen months after signature, when the fair market value opinion that justified the price has quietly expired, when a physician's compensation drifts above the survey's ninetieth percentile because actual productivity outran the projection, when a commercial payer's credentialing committee seats the last package three weeks after the closing date, or when nobody notices that a claims-made malpractice policy left an uncovered tail that the indemnity escrow cannot fund. Each of these is a small clerical fact with a large regulatory consequence, and each of them is discovered today by a spreadsheet owner reading a column of dates.

This document specifies the API for a system that replaces those spreadsheets. The system tracks a physician practice acquisition from sourcing through post-close performance measurement, and it treats the compliance record as the primary artifact rather than as a by-product. Valuation opinions are prepared by one individual and accepted by another. Compensation models are designed by one individual and approved by a third, who may not be the same officer who accepted the valuation the compensation is benchmarked against. Payer novation, provider re-credentialing, chart migration, staff transition, and billing conversion each run as their own bounded workflow, and each reports upward into a single day-one readiness posture that gates the close. Nothing about the transaction advances on assertion alone: every gate is a stored precondition evaluated against stored state and a server-controlled logical clock.

The reader should expect a system that is deliberately unforgiving about ordering. The deal's workflow status, its regulatory clearance, and its operational readiness are three separate dimensions that move independently, and a request that is simultaneously out of sequence and economically impossible reports the sequencing failure first. Money is always integer cents, rates are always basis points, and every derived figure is recomputed and stored by the server rather than accepted from a client. Where a calculation admits two plausible evaluation orders, this document pins exactly one.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User Identity, Roles and the Segregation of Duties

Every request to every endpoint — including read-only ones — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that user is acting under. If either header is absent, is present but holds the empty string, or if `X-User-Role` holds a value that is not one of the six recognised roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check runs before anything else about the request is examined. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403; because that gate runs before the request body is parsed, a caller in a forbidden role never learns whether its payload was well formed.

The six roles are `DealLead`, `ValuationAnalyst`, `CompensationDesigner`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `IntegrationManager`, and `PracticePhysician`, spelled exactly as shown.

A `DealLead` owns the transaction itself: opening the acquisition, registering the physicians who will join it, scheduling and allocating the liability schedule, and driving the deal's workflow dimension from sourcing through close or abandonment. A `DealLead` may neither author nor accept a valuation, and may not approve compensation. A `ValuationAnalyst` prepares fair market value and commercial reasonableness opinions and submits them for review, but is structurally barred from accepting its own work. A `CompensationDesigner` designs physician compensation plans and submits them for compliance review, and can neither approve nor execute them. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the regulatory gate: it accepts or rejects valuation opinions, approves compensation plans, moves the acquisition's regulatory clearance dimension, waives readiness checklist lines, and places executed plans on compliance hold — while authoring nothing itself. An `IntegrationManager` runs the operational integration: payer contracts and their novation, re-credentialing packages, chart migration batches, staff transition offers, billing conversions, the day-one checklist, and post-close performance reporting. A `PracticePhysician` is the acquired physician acting for themselves; they may execute an approved compensation plan addressed to them and read their own records, and nothing else.

Five of the six roles carry an **opaque identity**. When `X-User-Role` is `DealLead`, `ValuationAnalyst`, `CompensationDesigner`, `ComplianceOfficer`, or `IntegrationManager`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow step demands an audit trail — as `prepared_by_user_id` on a valuation opinion, as `accepted_by_user_id` on an accepted one, as `approved_by_user_id` on an approved compensation plan, as `verified_by_user_id` on a readiness checklist line that reached a terminal outcome — but it never compares that value against any entity's `id`.

`PracticePhysician` is the one exception and carries an **entity-backed identity**. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticePhysician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Physician` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Physician` compare that resource's physician foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a physician executes a compensation plan, the server resolves `X-User-ID` to a `Physician` and requires it to equal the plan's `physician_id`; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_PLAN_OWNER` with HTTP 403.

Segregation of duties is enforced at the level of the individual user id, not merely at the level of the role, and it produces `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403 in two distinct situations. First, the `ComplianceOfficer` who accepts or rejects a valuation opinion must be a different user id from the one recorded in that opinion's `prepared_by_user_id`. Second, the `ComplianceOfficer` user id recorded in the pinned fair market value opinion's `accepted_by_user_id` may not approve any compensation plan on that same acquisition. Separately, and with its own code, a valuation opinion may only be submitted for review by the analyst who prepared it: the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal `prepared_by_user_id`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_PLAN_OWNER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint is paginated by two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and has a minimum accepted value of 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400 rather than being silently clamped.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where two rows share a `created_at` instant, the ordering between them is unspecified but must be stable within a single response. Every list response populates all four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows returned in this page is less than `total_count`.

Filters on a list endpoint combine conjunctively: a request that supplies two filter parameters returns only rows matching both. Every filter parameter is optional, and an omitted filter constrains nothing. Because this API uses a flat URL topology in which no child collection is nested beneath its parent, the filtered list endpoints are the sole mechanism for discovering a parent's children — each child collection accepts every one of its own foreign-key fields as an optional filter.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Mutation Semantics

Eleven of the twelve resource types in this system share a common property set. Each carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client-supplied component; a `created_at` and an `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with exactly three fractional digits; and a `version`, a plain integer that starts at 1 when the row is inserted and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Clients never supply `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, or `version` on a create, and never supply them on an update either.

`updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant on every mutation, and `version` is bumped, whether the mutation was requested directly or arrived as a cascade from another entity's transition. This matters throughout §3: when a payer's denial withdraws a set of credentialing packages, each of those packages has its own `version` bumped and its own `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been withdrawn by an explicit request.

A row is mutated only when the operation actually changes at least one of that row's own stored fields, and a request that changes several fields of one row advances that row's `version` by exactly one rather than once per field. Creating a child row is therefore not by itself a mutation of the parent acquisition its body names: `POST /physicians`, for one, inserts a `Physician` and leaves the named acquisition's `version` and `updated_at` exactly where they were, and no create advances the acquisition's `version` merely because a new child now points at it — the two optimistically concurrent creates of §2.7 advance it because §2.7 says they do. What does advance it is any change to a stored acquisition field, the server-derived ones included: a dimension moving, a consideration figure being written or cleared, a ledger balance shifting, `hr_staffing_coverage_pct` changing, or `readiness_posture` being recomputed to a *different* posture. A recomputation that lands back on the value already stored changes nothing, so it advances no `version` and refreshes no `updated_at`.

`PerformanceReport` is the single exception. A performance report is append-only and immutable once written: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither key appears in its representation.

### 2.5. URL Shape and Path Parameter Resolution

All collections are mounted at the root of the API; there are no nested collection paths. A child resource is created by posting its foreign keys in the request body — `acquisition_id`, `physician_id`, `payer_contract_id`, `applied_comp_plan_id` — and is discovered afterwards through the corresponding filtered list endpoint.

Single-resource and action endpoints therefore take exactly one path parameter, named for the entity it addresses: `acquisitionId`, `physicianId`, `opinionId`, `planId`, `contractId`, `packageId`, `batchId`, `offerId`, `conversionId`, `taskId`, `itemId`, and `reportId`. Path parameters are resolved left to right before the request body is inspected. An identifier that does not resolve to a stored entity of the expected type yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404; a single shared code covers every entity type, and only the human-readable `message` names which entity was not found.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved with the same code, `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, but strictly after path parameters and strictly before any cross-reference consistency check. An unknown identifier is therefore never reported as a scope mismatch.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Every date-sensitive rule in this specification — valuation opinion expiry, credentialing lead time, staff offer expiry, patient-notice lead time, physician licence expiry, the close instant, the execution instant — is evaluated against a server-controlled logical clock, never against real wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to the host's real time.

An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to an arbitrary instant supplied as `to_iso8601` and returns the new value as `current_utc`. It is available to `IntegrationManager`, `DealLead`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. Once set, all subsequent reads of "the current logical date" and "the current logical instant" use that value. Where a rule speaks of a *date* comparison, the logical clock's calendar date in UTC is used; where it speaks of an *instant*, the full ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits is used.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Acquisition Aggregate

Two create operations are optimistically concurrent on the `Acquisition` aggregate, and both of them require a non-optional `expected_acquisition_version` field in the request body carrying the `version` the client last read from that acquisition.

A `POST /valuation-opinions` request must carry `expected_acquisition_version` equal to the target acquisition's current stored `version`. If the value does not match — because some other writer has advanced the acquisition since the client read it — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACQUISITION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. On success the server increments the acquisition's `version` by 1. This is what keeps an already-accepted fair market value opinion from being silently displaced by a filing made from a stale read.

A `POST /compensation-plans` request must likewise carry `expected_acquisition_version` equal to the target acquisition's current stored `version`, is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACQUISITION_VERSION` and HTTP 412 on any mismatch, and increments the acquisition's `version` by 1 on success. The effect is that a plan designer must re-read the acquisition after any preceding write, so no plan can be built on a superseded valuation, a stale retention pool balance, or a stale regulatory clearance value.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Requests to any other endpoint neither require nor accept `expected_acquisition_version`.

### 2.8. Money, Rates, Precision and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents, and every field name carrying money ends in `_cents`. All rates, shares, and multipliers are integers denominated in basis points, where 10000 basis points is exactly 100 percent; every such field ends in `_bps`. A market multiple of six and a half times EBITDA is therefore recorded as `market_multiple_bps` of 65000.

Where a computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. Where a computation produces a percentile or a percentage, the result is rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. These are the only two rounding modes used for rounding in the system, and they apply at exactly the points the individual calculations in §3 name — several calculations deliberately round intermediate components independently before summing them, and in those cases rounding the sum instead of the components is wrong.

Two quantities are **truncated toward zero** rather than rounded, and this distinction is load-bearing: the migration reconciliation match rate in basis points and the patient-notice coverage in basis points both take the floor of their quotient. A batch or a notice campaign therefore cannot round its way up to a completeness threshold it has not actually met.

Calendar arithmetic uses whole days with no time component. Adding a number of calendar months to a date preserves the day-of-month, and where the target month has no such day the last day of the target month is used instead. Adding a number of calendar years preserves month and day-of-month, and 29 February maps to 28 February in a non-leap target year. Day differences between two dates are whole-day counts.

### 2.9. The Three Dimensions of an Acquisition

An acquisition does not have a single status. It has three, and they move independently.

The **workflow dimension** is `deal_status`, taking the values `SOURCING`, `DILIGENCE`, `BOARD_REVIEW`, `SIGNED`, `CLOSED`, and `ABANDONED`. A new acquisition begins in `SOURCING`; `CLOSED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal. This dimension is driven exclusively by a `DealLead`.

The **regulatory dimension** is `compliance_clearance`, taking the values `PENDING`, `CLEARED`, `FLAGGED`, and `SUSPENDED`. A new acquisition begins in `PENDING`, and this dimension has no terminal state — a suspended deal can always be brought back. It is driven exclusively by a `ComplianceOfficer`, and it also moves by cascade when a compensation plan lands on compliance hold. Its value is never derived from `deal_status`, and `deal_status` is never derived from it, although two `deal_status` transitions read it as a precondition.

The **operational dimension** is `readiness_posture`, taking the values `BLOCKED`, `AT_RISK`, and `READY`. Unlike the other two it is never set by a request: it is recomputed by the server from the acquisition's blocking day-one checklist lines every time one of them changes, every time one is added, and on the workflow transitions into `DILIGENCE` and into `SIGNED`. A new acquisition, having no checklist at all, starts at `BLOCKED`.

### 2.10. The Compliance-Clearance Freeze

Whenever an acquisition's `compliance_clearance` is `FLAGGED` or `SUSPENDED`, the deal is frozen for new work. Any request that would create or advance a resource belonging to that acquisition is rejected with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED` and HTTP 403. The freeze covers exactly these sixteen operations: `POST /physicians`, `POST /valuation-opinions`, `POST /compensation-plans`, `POST /payer-contracts`, `POST /credentialing-packages`, `POST /credentialing-packages/{packageId}/status`, `POST /ehr-migration-batches`, `POST /ehr-migration-batches/{batchId}/progress`, `POST /staff-transition-offers`, `POST /staff-transition-offers/{offerId}/status`, `POST /billing-conversions`, `POST /billing-conversions/{conversionId}/status`, `POST /liability-items`, `POST /liability-items/{itemId}/disposition`, `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status`, and `POST /performance-reports`.

Four categories of operation are deliberately exempt, because a freeze that could not be lifted would be a deadlock. Every `GET` endpoint remains readable. `POST /acquisitions` is exempt because a brand-new acquisition has no clearance problem to inherit. `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/compliance-clearance` is exempt because it is the only way out of the freeze. And `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status`, `POST /readiness-tasks`, and `POST /readiness-tasks/{taskId}/status` are exempt because remediating the compensation defect and the day-one checklist is precisely the work that a flagged deal needs to do. The clock-setting capability is likewise unaffected.

### 2.11. Ledger Conservation

The system maintains two conserved ledgers on every acquisition, and both obey the same shape: a client-supplied total, a server-derived committed balance, and a server-derived available balance that is the total minus the committed amount. Neither committed nor available balance is ever accepted from a client.

The **retention-bonus pool** is `retention_pool_total_cents`, authorised when the acquisition is opened. `retention_pool_committed_cents` is at all times the sum of `retention_bonus_cents` over the acquisition's staff transition offers whose `status` is `EXTENDED` or `ACCEPTED`; `retention_pool_available_cents` is `retention_pool_total_cents` minus `retention_pool_committed_cents`. Offers in `DRAFT` reserve nothing. After every offer transition the invariant holds that the total equals the committed amount plus the available amount, and because every non-acceptance exit releases exactly the amount that was committed, the pool balance after a rescission is identical to the balance before the offer was extended, with no residue. No rounding occurs in this ledger; it only adds and subtracts amounts that were already rounded when the offer was drafted.

The **indemnity escrow** is `escrow_total_cents`, funded at signing and supplied when the acquisition is opened. `escrow_committed_cents` is at all times the sum of `escrowed_amount_cents` over the acquisition's liability items whose `disposition` is `ESCROWED`; `escrow_available_cents` is `escrow_total_cents` minus `escrow_committed_cents`. After every disposition change the invariant holds that the total equals the committed amount plus the available amount, and additionally that every liability item whose `disposition` is anything other than `ESCROWED` carries `escrowed_amount_cents` of exactly 0. Releasing an escrowed item returns its full escrowed amount, so no drift can accumulate. No rounding occurs in this ledger either.

Both ledgers are initialised when the acquisition is created: `retention_pool_committed_cents` and `escrow_committed_cents` are set to 0, and each available balance is set equal to its total.

### 2.12. Rejection Precedence

A single request frequently violates more than one rule. The system evaluates rules in a strict total order and returns the error belonging to the first rule violated; no two rules share a rank. The order is as follows.

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` both present and non-empty and the role recognised, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role gate** — the caller's `X-User-Role` appears in the endpoint's authorised list, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Schema** — on every endpoint: required fields present, types correct, enum values recognised, dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, instants in ISO 8601 UTC form, money and count fields non-negative, `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` non-negative. This rank also carries the intra-body constraints: `terminates_on` on or after `effective_on`; `discount_rate_bps` strictly greater than `terminal_growth_bps`; `youngest_patient_age_years` present when `contains_minor_records` is true; and `indemnity_cap_cents` present and at least `estimated_amount_cents` when a disposition of `INDEMNIFIED` is requested. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Path existence** — every identifier in the URL path resolves, on every endpoint that has a path parameter. Otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
5. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key in the request body (`acquisition_id`, `physician_id`, `payer_contract_id`, `applied_comp_plan_id`) resolves, on `POST /physicians`, `POST /valuation-opinions`, `POST /compensation-plans`, `POST /payer-contracts`, `POST /credentialing-packages`, `POST /ehr-migration-batches`, `POST /staff-transition-offers`, `POST /billing-conversions`, `POST /readiness-tasks`, `POST /liability-items`, and `POST /performance-reports`. Otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. **Acquisition scope agreement** — all foreign keys in one request body belong to the same acquisition, on `POST /compensation-plans`, `POST /credentialing-packages`, and `POST /performance-reports`. Otherwise `ERR_ACQUISITION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (409).
7. **Unique NPI**, on `POST /physicians`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI` (409).
8. **Unique checklist line per deal**, on `POST /readiness-tasks`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TASK_CODE` (409).
9. **Unique batch label per deal**, on `POST /ehr-migration-batches`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_LABEL` (409).
10. **One live credentialing package per provider per payer**, on `POST /credentialing-packages`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREDENTIALING_PACKAGE` (409).
11. **One performance report per physician per period**, on `POST /performance-reports`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD` (409).
12. **Record ownership**, on `POST /valuation-opinions/{opinionId}/status` and `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status`. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_PLAN_OWNER` (403).
13. **Optimistic concurrency** — `expected_acquisition_version` equals the stored acquisition `version`, on `POST /valuation-opinions` and `POST /compensation-plans`. Checked after existence and scope but before any state or business rule, so a stale reader is told to re-read rather than being handed a business error derived from a version it never observed. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_ACQUISITION_VERSION` (412).
14. **State transition legality** — the requested transition is declared for the entity's current state, on every action endpoint. This rank absorbs every terminal-state lockdown and every repeat of an already-applied transition, and it outranks every business rule below it: an action that is both out of sequence and economically impossible reports the sequencing failure. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).
15. **Compliance-clearance freeze**, on the sixteen operations enumerated in §2.10. Otherwise `ERR_COMPLIANCE_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED` (403).
16. **Segregation of duties**, on `POST /valuation-opinions/{opinionId}/status` and `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
17. **No referral-linked compensation**, on `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status` with a target status of `SUBMITTED` and on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/compliance-clearance` with a target clearance of `CLEARED`. This is the highest-priority business rule in the domain. Otherwise `ERR_REFERRAL_LINKED_COMPENSATION` (422).
18. **A referral-prohibition exception is named**, on `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status` with a target status of `SUBMITTED`. Otherwise `ERR_STARK_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED` (422).
19. **Fair market value safe harbour**, on `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status` with a target status of `APPROVED` or of `EXECUTED`. Otherwise `ERR_COMP_EXCEEDS_FMV_SAFE_HARBOR` (422).
20. **Physician licence currency**, on `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status` with a target status of `SUBMITTED` and on `POST /credentialing-packages/{packageId}/status` with a target status of `SUBMITTED`. Otherwise `ERR_PHYSICIAN_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (422).
21. **Minimum tail coverage purchased**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `BOARD_REVIEW`. Otherwise `ERR_TAIL_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
22. **Valuation inputs complete for the chosen method**, on `POST /valuation-opinions`. Otherwise `ERR_MISSING_VALUATION_INPUT` (422).
23. **Method compatible with opinion type**, on `POST /valuation-opinions`. Otherwise `ERR_OPINION_METHOD_MISMATCH` (422).
24. **Valuer independence attested**, on `POST /valuation-opinions/{opinionId}/status` with a target status of `SUBMITTED`. Otherwise `ERR_VALUER_INDEPENDENCE_NOT_ATTESTED` (422).
25. **Disposition legality for the deal structure and exposure category**, on `POST /liability-items/{itemId}/disposition`. Otherwise `ERR_LIABILITY_DISPOSITION_NOT_PERMITTED` (422).
26. **Waiver justification present**, on `POST /readiness-tasks/{taskId}/status` with a target status of `WAIVED`. Otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (422).
27. **Batch not empty**, on `POST /ehr-migration-batches/{batchId}/progress` with an action of `EXTRACT`. Otherwise `ERR_EMPTY_MIGRATION_BATCH` (422).
28. **Record conservation on reconcile**, on `POST /ehr-migration-batches/{batchId}/progress` with an action of `RECONCILE`. Otherwise `ERR_MIGRATION_COUNT_INVALID` (422).
29. **Reporting period is one whole calendar month at or after the close date**, on `POST /performance-reports`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_PERIOD` (422).
30. **Governing valuation not expired**, on `POST /valuation-opinions/{opinionId}/status` with a target status of `ACCEPTED`, on `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status` with a target status of `APPROVED`, and on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `BOARD_REVIEW`. Ranked below the safe-harbour rule, so a plan that is both over the percentile and approved against a stale opinion reports the compensation defect rather than the staleness. Otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_OPINION_EXPIRED` (422).
31. **No overlapping live term for the same payer**, on `POST /payer-contracts`. Otherwise `ERR_PAYER_CONTRACT_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (409).
32. **Committee cycle has a free slot**, on `POST /credentialing-packages/{packageId}/status` with a target status of `SUBMITTED`. Otherwise `ERR_COMMITTEE_CYCLE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
33. **Credentialing lead time achievable**, on `POST /credentialing-packages/{packageId}/status` with a target status of `SUBMITTED`. Otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIALING_LEAD_TIME_MISSED` (422).
34. **Patient-notice lead time sufficient**, on `POST /billing-conversions/{conversionId}/status` with a target status of `NOTICES_ISSUED`. Otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_SHORT` (422).
35. **Patient-notice coverage complete**, on `POST /billing-conversions/{conversionId}/status` with a target status of `NOTICES_ISSUED`. Otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_NOTICE_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` (422).
36. **A pinned accepted fair market value opinion exists**, on `POST /compensation-plans/{planId}/status` with a target status of `APPROVED`. Otherwise `ERR_FMV_OPINION_NOT_ACCEPTED` (422).
37. **Commercial reasonableness established**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `BOARD_REVIEW`. Otherwise `ERR_COMMERCIAL_REASONABLENESS_NOT_ESTABLISHED` (422).
38. **Every physician has a signed-off arrangement**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `SIGNED`. Otherwise `ERR_COMP_PLAN_MISSING` (422).
39. **The depended-upon compensation plan is executed**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `CLOSED` and on `POST /performance-reports`. Otherwise `ERR_NO_EXECUTED_COMP_PLAN` (422).
40. **No unallocated exposure remains**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `SIGNED`. Otherwise `ERR_LIABILITY_UNASSIGNED` (422).
41. **Readiness posture is ready**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `CLOSED`. Otherwise `ERR_READINESS_NOT_READY` (422).
42. **Payer novation complete**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `CLOSED` and on `POST /billing-conversions/{conversionId}/status` with a target status of `CONVERTED`. Otherwise `ERR_PAYER_NOVATION_INCOMPLETE` (422).
43. **Chart migration complete**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `CLOSED` and on `POST /readiness-tasks/{taskId}/status` with a target status of `COMPLETE`. Otherwise `ERR_MIGRATION_INCOMPLETE` (422).
44. **The deal has actually closed**, on `POST /performance-reports`. Otherwise `ERR_ACQUISITION_NOT_CLOSED` (422).
45. **Retention pool sufficient**, on `POST /staff-transition-offers/{offerId}/status` with a target status of `EXTENDED`. Ranked below the state rule, so re-extending an already-extended offer against a depleted pool reports the invalid transition rather than the shortfall. Otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_POOL_EXCEEDED` (422).
46. **Escrow sufficient**, on `POST /liability-items/{itemId}/disposition` with a target disposition of `ESCROWED`. Otherwise `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
47. **Escrow can fund the tail premium**, on `POST /acquisitions/{acquisitionId}/status` with a target status of `SIGNED`. This is the last rule in the order: a deal that fails several rules at once never reports this one. Otherwise `ERR_TAIL_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` (422).

The substantive content of each of these rules — what exactly is being compared, against what threshold, and with what boundary inclusivity — is set out in the workflow narratives of §3, alongside the business step it guards.

A handful of transition preconditions described in §3 have no dedicated error code of their own: for example, a deal may not leave `SOURCING` until at least one physician is registered against it, and a deal may not be signed or closed while its `compliance_clearance` is anything other than `CLEARED`. Any such precondition, when it is not satisfied and no more specific code in the table above applies, is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` with HTTP 409, at the same position in the precedence order as rule 14.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Deal, Registering Physicians, and the Two Deal-Level Dimensions

A `DealLead` opens the file. The creation payload names the target with `target_practice_name` and records the practice's dominant `specialty`, which must be one of `PRIMARY_CARE`, `CARDIOLOGY`, `ORTHOPEDICS`, or `ONCOLOGY` — this single choice later selects both the compensation survey benchmark table and the malpractice tail rate. The deal's `structure` is either `ASSET` or `EQUITY`, and it governs which liability dispositions are legal later on. The practice's professional liability policy is recorded as `malpractice_policy_form`, either `CLAIMS_MADE` or `OCCURRENCE`, together with its `annual_malpractice_premium_cents`, which may be zero but never negative. The buyer states how many months of prior-acts coverage it intends to purchase in `tail_coverage_months`, an integer from 0 through 120 inclusive that defaults to 0 when omitted. The trailing twelve-month collections of the practice are captured as `trailing_annual_collections_cents`, the planned closing date as `target_close_on` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and the non-physician headcount the day-one plan requires as `required_staff_headcount`, which must be at least 1. Finally, the two ledger totals are authorised: `retention_pool_total_cents` for staff retention bonuses and `escrow_total_cents` for the indemnity escrow, each of which may be zero but never negative.

Creation is where `tail_coverage_required` is decided, once and permanently: it is true when `malpractice_policy_form` is `CLAIMS_MADE` and false when it is `OCCURRENCE`. An occurrence policy leaves nothing uncovered, so such a deal is exempt from every tail rule that follows. A newly opened acquisition sits in `deal_status` `SOURCING` with `compliance_clearance` `PENDING` and `readiness_posture` `BLOCKED`, carries `hr_staffing_coverage_pct` of 0.00, and has `tail_premium_cents` not yet computed. Its five consideration fields — `purchase_price_cents`, `integration_reserve_cents`, `gross_consideration_cents`, `synergy_credit_bps`, `synergy_credit_cents`, and `net_consideration_cents` — are all null until the board reviews the deal, and `closed_at` and `abandoned_reason` are likewise null.

Registering a physician is also the `DealLead`'s work. The payload carries the `acquisition_id` of the deal being joined, the provider's `npi`, their `full_name`, their own `specialty` from the same four-value enumeration, and their `state_license_expires_on`. Board certification is recorded in `board_certified`, which defaults to false when the field is omitted. An `npi` is exactly ten decimal digits and must be globally unique across every physician in the system, not merely within one deal: a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI`. A physician's `employment_status` is never supplied by a client. It is derived from that physician's compensation plans and is recomputed every time one of them moves: it reads `EMPLOYED` when one of the physician's plans is in `EXECUTED`, `OFFERED` when one is in `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` when the most recently created plan for that physician is in `WITHDRAWN`, and `PROSPECTIVE` otherwise. A freshly registered physician therefore reads `PROSPECTIVE`.

Advancing from `SOURCING` to `DILIGENCE` is the `DealLead`'s first workflow step and its only precondition is substantive: at least one physician must already be registered against the acquisition. On success the server recomputes `readiness_posture`. The workflow dimension moves by a request naming the desired state in `target_status`.

Abandonment is available from three points in the lifecycle and behaves differently at each. `SOURCING → ABANDONED` has no precondition and simply stores the free-text `abandoned_reason` supplied on the request. `DILIGENCE → ABANDONED` also has no precondition, stores `abandoned_reason`, sets every valuation opinion on the deal that is still in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and sets every staff transition offer in `EXTENDED` to `RESCINDED`, releasing each one's `retention_bonus_cents` from `retention_pool_committed_cents` back into the available balance. `BOARD_REVIEW → ABANDONED` performs exactly the same three side effects as the `DILIGENCE` case — storing `abandoned_reason`, superseding `DRAFT` and `SUBMITTED` opinions, and rescinding `EXTENDED` offers with full pool release — but adds one precondition: no compensation plan on the acquisition may be in `EXECUTED`. A deal with an executed physician arrangement cannot be walked away from at the board table.

The regulatory dimension is moved only by a `ComplianceOfficer`, who names the desired value in `target_clearance` and may attach a `clearance_note`. Six transitions exist. `PENDING → CLEARED` requires two things of the acquisition's compensation plans: none may be in `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`, and none may have `includes_referral_credit` true — a referral-linked plan anywhere on the deal blocks clearance with `ERR_REFERRAL_LINKED_COMPENSATION`. `PENDING → FLAGGED` has no precondition and no side effect. `CLEARED → FLAGGED`, whether requested directly or arriving as a cascade from post-close performance reporting, returns every compensation plan on the acquisition that is in `SUBMITTED` back to `DRAFT` and increments each of those plans' `returned_count` by 1. `FLAGGED → CLEARED` requires only that no compensation plan on the acquisition is in `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`; unlike the transition out of `PENDING`, it does not re-examine `includes_referral_credit`. `FLAGGED → SUSPENDED` has no precondition and sets every staff transition offer still in `DRAFT` to `RESCINDED` — a draft offer reserves no pool money, so nothing is released. `SUSPENDED → FLAGGED` has no precondition and no side effect, and is the only way out of suspension.

### 3.2. Valuation: Fair Market Value and Commercial Reasonableness

Two independent opinions govern the transaction, and a single entity records both kinds. `opinion_type` is either `FMV` or `COMMERCIAL_REASONABLENESS`. A `ValuationAnalyst` files one by supplying the `acquisition_id`, the `expected_acquisition_version` the analyst last observed, the `opinion_type`, the `method`, the valuation date as `effective_on`, an `independence_attested` flag, and whichever numeric inputs the chosen method requires. The server stores the caller's `X-User-ID` as `prepared_by_user_id`, sets `status` to `DRAFT` and `is_pinned` to false, and increments the acquisition's `version`.

`method` takes one of `INCOME_APPROACH`, `MARKET_APPROACH`, or `NARRATIVE`, and it must be compatible with `opinion_type`: an `FMV` opinion requires `INCOME_APPROACH` or `MARKET_APPROACH`, and a `COMMERCIAL_REASONABLENESS` opinion requires `NARRATIVE`. Any other pairing is `ERR_OPINION_METHOD_MISMATCH`. Beyond compatibility, the method dictates which inputs must be present, and a gap is `ERR_MISSING_VALUATION_INPUT`: `INCOME_APPROACH` requires `normalized_ebitda_cents`, `projection_years`, `ebitda_growth_bps`, `discount_rate_bps`, and `terminal_growth_bps`; `MARKET_APPROACH` requires `normalized_ebitda_cents` and `market_multiple_bps`; `NARRATIVE` requires `cr_conclusion`, which is either `REASONABLE` or `NOT_REASONABLE`. `projection_years` is a whole number of years from 1 through 5 inclusive, and `discount_rate_bps` must be strictly greater than `terminal_growth_bps` — a Gordon-growth denominator of zero or less is not a valuation, and this particular pairing is caught at the schema rank as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than as a missing input.

The concluded value is computed once at filing time and is never recomputed afterwards. For a `COMMERCIAL_REASONABLENESS` opinion, `concluded_value_cents` is exactly 0 — a narrative opinion concludes no value at all.

For an `FMV` opinion filed under the **market approach**, `concluded_value_cents` is `normalized_ebitda_cents` multiplied by `market_multiple_bps`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The growth rate, the discount rate, the terminal growth rate, and the projection horizon are ignored entirely under this variant, which is why the same normalised EBITDA can conclude at a materially different value depending on which method the analyst chose.

For an `FMV` opinion filed under the **income approach**, the concluded value is a discounted cash flow over the explicit horizon plus a Gordon-growth terminal value, and the rounding points are pinned. Let the growth rate be `ebitda_growth_bps` divided by 10000, the discount rate be `discount_rate_bps` divided by 10000, the terminal growth rate be `terminal_growth_bps` divided by 10000, and the horizon be `projection_years` whole years. For each year of the horizon, counting from year one through the final year, the projected EBITDA for that year is `normalized_ebitda_cents` multiplied by the quantity one plus the growth rate raised to the power of that year number, and that projected figure is rounded half up to a whole cent before anything else is done with it. That rounded yearly EBITDA is then divided by the quantity one plus the discount rate raised to the power of the same year number, the quotient is rounded half up to a whole cent, and only then is it added into the running explicit-period present value. The terminal value starts from the final year's projected EBITDA — `normalized_ebitda_cents` multiplied by one plus the growth rate raised to the power of the horizon, rounded half up to a whole cent — which is then multiplied by one plus the terminal growth rate and divided by the discount rate minus the terminal growth rate, with that result rounded half up to a whole cent. The rounded terminal value is then divided by one plus the discount rate raised to the power of the horizon and rounded half up to a whole cent a second time. `concluded_value_cents` is the sum of the accumulated explicit-period present value and that discounted terminal value. Each projected year and the terminal value are rounded independently before accumulation; accumulating unrounded figures and rounding once at the end is a different number and is not the pinned answer.

Every opinion, of either type, is stamped with an `expires_on` computed as `effective_on` plus twelve calendar months, keeping the same day-of-month and falling back to the last day of the target month where that day does not exist. A valuation therefore goes stale exactly twelve months after its effective date, and an opinion whose `expires_on` equals the current logical date is still usable — only an `expires_on` strictly before the current logical date is expired, which surfaces as `ERR_VALUATION_OPINION_EXPIRED`.

An opinion's lifecycle runs `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, with `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` as its terminal states. Submitting is the preparer's own act and nobody else's: the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal `prepared_by_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_NOT_PLAN_OWNER`, and `independence_attested` must be true, or it fails with `ERR_VALUER_INDEPENDENCE_NOT_ATTESTED`. An opinion may perfectly well be drafted without an independence attestation; it simply cannot leave `DRAFT`. Reviewers may attach a `review_note` to any of their decisions.

A `ComplianceOfficer` disposes of a `SUBMITTED` opinion in one of three ways. Returning it to `DRAFT` has no precondition and no side effect. Accepting it, `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, has three preconditions: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the opinion's `expires_on` must not be before the current logical date, or it fails with `ERR_VALUATION_OPINION_EXPIRED`; and no other opinion on the same acquisition with the same `opinion_type` may already be in `ACCEPTED`. On acceptance the caller's `X-User-ID` is stored in `accepted_by_user_id`, which is the field the compensation-approval segregation check later reads. Rejecting it, `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, carries the single precondition that the caller's `X-User-ID` differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, again with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` on violation, and has no side effect.

An `ACCEPTED` opinion may be retired by a `ComplianceOfficer` with the transition `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED`, permitted only while `is_pinned` is false. Once the board has pinned an opinion, that opinion cannot be superseded out from under the price it justified. Separately, both `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` and `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` exist as cascade-only transitions: they occur when the parent acquisition moves into `CLOSED` or into `ABANDONED`, never by direct request.

### 3.3. Board Review: Pinning the Valuation and Pricing the Transaction

The move from `DILIGENCE` to `BOARD_REVIEW` is the moment the deal acquires a price, and it is the most heavily gated transition in the system. A `DealLead` requests it, and four preconditions are evaluated. First, exactly one valuation opinion with `opinion_type` `FMV` must be in `ACCEPTED` on the acquisition. Second, at least one opinion with `opinion_type` `COMMERCIAL_REASONABLENESS` must be in `ACCEPTED` and carry `cr_conclusion` of `REASONABLE`; a missing opinion and a `NOT_REASONABLE` conclusion are equally fatal, and both report `ERR_COMMERCIAL_REASONABLENESS_NOT_ESTABLISHED`. Fair market value alone is not sufficient — the arrangement must also be commercially reasonable. Third, the accepted `FMV` opinion's `expires_on` must not be before the current logical date, or the transition fails with `ERR_VALUATION_OPINION_EXPIRED`. Fourth, when `tail_coverage_required` is true, `tail_coverage_months` must be at least 60 — the standard five-year tail term — or the transition fails with `ERR_TAIL_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. A deal on an occurrence policy skips that fourth check entirely.

Four side effects then fire, in this order. The accepted `FMV` opinion has `is_pinned` set to true; it is now the single opinion the rest of the deal is measured against. `purchase_price_cents` is copied from that pinned opinion's `concluded_value_cents`. The malpractice tail premium is computed and stored in `tail_premium_cents`. And the consideration chain is computed and stored across `integration_reserve_cents`, `gross_consideration_cents`, `synergy_credit_bps`, `synergy_credit_cents`, and `net_consideration_cents`.

**Pricing the malpractice tail.** When `tail_coverage_required` is false, `tail_premium_cents` is exactly 0 and no rate is consulted. When it is true, the tail rate is selected from the acquisition's `specialty`: 15000 basis points for `PRIMARY_CARE`, 22500 for `CARDIOLOGY`, 30000 for `ORTHOPEDICS`, and 20000 for `ONCOLOGY` — that is, one and a half times the annual premium for primary care, rising to three times it for orthopaedics. `tail_premium_cents` is then `annual_malpractice_premium_cents` multiplied by that rate, divided by 10000, multiplied by `tail_coverage_months`, divided by 60, with the whole expression rounded half up to a whole cent exactly once at the end. Purchasing precisely the standard sixty-month term therefore prices at the specialty rate applied to the annual premium with no proration; purchasing more months scales the premium up proportionally.

**Pricing the transaction.** The consideration chain is evaluated in five pinned steps. The base is `purchase_price_cents`. The integration reserve, `integration_reserve_cents`, is a flat 350 basis points — three and a half percent — of that base alone, rounded half up to a whole cent; the reserve is never levied on the tail premium or on the escrow. `gross_consideration_cents` is then the sum of four terms: the base, `integration_reserve_cents`, `tail_premium_cents`, and `escrow_committed_cents` as it stands at the moment of the transition. The synergy credit rate is selected from a tier ladder keyed on the **gross** figure: a gross below 800,000,000 cents earns `synergy_credit_bps` of 0; a gross of 800,000,000 cents or more but below 1,500,000,000 cents earns 150; a gross of 1,500,000,000 cents or more but below 2,500,000,000 cents earns 275; and a gross of 2,500,000,000 cents or more earns 400. `synergy_credit_cents` is `gross_consideration_cents` multiplied by `synergy_credit_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. `net_consideration_cents` is `gross_consideration_cents` minus `synergy_credit_cents`.

The tier is selected once, from the gross, and is never re-derived from the net. A deal whose gross sits just above a tier ceiling can fall back below that ceiling once the credit is netted out; the credit still stands at the gross-derived rate. Re-deriving the tier from `net_consideration_cents` lands in a lower band and produces a different answer, and that answer is wrong.

Board review can be reversed. `BOARD_REVIEW → DILIGENCE` is requested by the `DealLead` and carries one precondition: no compensation plan on the acquisition may be in `EXECUTED`. Reverting performs three side effects. The pinned `FMV` opinion has `is_pinned` set back to false, freeing it to be superseded. All six consideration fields — `purchase_price_cents`, `integration_reserve_cents`, `gross_consideration_cents`, `synergy_credit_bps`, `synergy_credit_cents`, and `net_consideration_cents` — are cleared back to null. And every compensation plan on the acquisition that is in `SUBMITTED` is returned to `DRAFT`, with each of those plans' `returned_count` incremented by 1. That last effect is deliberate: a plan submitted against a price the board has withdrawn must be redrafted, and it consumes one of the plan's bounded revision allowances to do so.

### 3.4. Physician Compensation: Design, Benchmarking, Approval and Execution

A `CompensationDesigner` builds a plan by naming the `acquisition_id`, the `expected_acquisition_version` last observed, the `physician_id` being compensated, and a `model_type` of `STRAIGHT_SALARY`, `WRVU_PRODUCTIVITY`, or `BASE_PLUS_INCENTIVE`. Four economic inputs are optional and each defaults to 0 when omitted: `base_salary_cents`, the annual guaranteed base; `conversion_factor_cents_per_wrvu`, the cents paid per work RVU; `threshold_wrvu`, the annual work-RVU threshold above which the conversion factor applies under the base-plus-incentive model only; and `projected_annual_wrvu`, the projected annual work-RVU volume. None may be negative.

Three compliance inputs travel with the design. `includes_referral_credit` states whether any component of the model varies with the volume or value of designated health service referrals, and defaults to false. `stark_exception` names the referral-prohibition exception being relied upon and is one of `BONA_FIDE_EMPLOYMENT`, `PERSONAL_SERVICE_ARRANGEMENT`, or `NONE`. `fmv_justification_on_file` states whether a written above-ninetieth-percentile fair market value justification exists, and defaults to false. On creation the plan lands in `DRAFT` with `returned_count`, `actual_ytd_wrvu`, `months_elapsed`, and `comp_true_up_cents` all set to 0, and the acquisition's `version` is incremented.

**Projected total cash compensation.** `projected_total_cash_comp_cents` is computed at creation from the model. Under `STRAIGHT_SALARY` it is simply `base_salary_cents`. Under `WRVU_PRODUCTIVITY` it is `conversion_factor_cents_per_wrvu` multiplied by `projected_annual_wrvu`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Under `BASE_PLUS_INCENTIVE` the incentive volume is `projected_annual_wrvu` minus `threshold_wrvu`, floored at zero so a physician projected below the threshold earns no incentive rather than a negative one, and the total is `base_salary_cents` plus the product of `conversion_factor_cents_per_wrvu` and that incentive volume, rounded half up to a whole cent. `comp_per_wrvu_cents` is then `projected_total_cash_comp_cents` divided by `projected_annual_wrvu`, rounded half up to a whole cent — except when `projected_annual_wrvu` is 0, in which case `comp_per_wrvu_cents` is exactly 0 and no division is attempted.

**Where the plan sits in the survey.** `benchmark_basis` is derived from the model: it is `COMP_PER_WRVU` when `model_type` is `WRVU_PRODUCTIVITY`, and `TOTAL_CASH_COMP` for both `STRAIGHT_SALARY` and `BASE_PLUS_INCENTIVE`. The basis selects both the compared value and the survey quartiles. Under `TOTAL_CASH_COMP` the compared value is `projected_total_cash_comp_cents` and the quartiles are the specialty's total-cash-compensation figures; productivity is irrelevant to the percentile. Under `COMP_PER_WRVU` the compared value is `comp_per_wrvu_cents` and the quartiles are the specialty's dollars-per-work-RVU figures, so two plans with identical total cash compensation land at different percentiles whenever their work-RVU volumes differ. The specialty used is the **physician's** `specialty`, not the acquisition's.

The published survey quartiles, given in cents as the twenty-fifth, fiftieth, seventy-fifth, and ninetieth percentile points, are as follows. For total cash compensation: `PRIMARY_CARE` 22000000, 26500000, 31000000, 36000000; `CARDIOLOGY` 45000000, 55000000, 68000000, 82000000; `ORTHOPEDICS` 50000000, 62000000, 76000000, 92000000; `ONCOLOGY` 40000000, 49000000, 60000000, 72000000. For compensation per work RVU: `PRIMARY_CARE` 4200, 5200, 6300, 7600; `CARDIOLOGY` 5600, 6900, 8300, 9800; `ORTHOPEDICS` 6100, 7400, 9000, 10800; `ONCOLOGY` 5200, 6400, 7800, 9300.

`benchmark_percentile` interpolates the compared value linearly between those published points, using whichever band the value falls in. If the value is at or below the twenty-fifth-percentile point, the percentile is 25 multiplied by the value and divided by that point. If the value is above the twenty-fifth point and at or below the fiftieth, the percentile is 25 plus 25 multiplied by the excess of the value over the twenty-fifth point divided by the gap between the fiftieth and twenty-fifth points. If it is above the fiftieth and at or below the seventy-fifth, the percentile is 50 plus 25 multiplied by the excess over the fiftieth point divided by the gap between the seventy-fifth and fiftieth points. If it is above the seventy-fifth and at or below the ninetieth, the percentile is 75 plus 15 multiplied by the excess over the seventy-fifth point divided by the gap between the ninetieth and seventy-fifth points. And if it is above the ninetieth point, the percentile is 90 plus 9 multiplied by the excess over the ninetieth point divided by the ninetieth point, with the result capped so that it never exceeds 99, whichever of the two is smaller. The final figure is rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. A value landing exactly on the ninetieth-percentile point therefore yields exactly 90.00.

The fair market value safe harbour is the ninetieth percentile, and exactly 90.00 is **inside** it. Only a `benchmark_percentile` strictly greater than 90.00 requires `fmv_justification_on_file` to be true; where the percentile is above the safe harbour and no justification is on file, the offending operation is rejected with `ERR_COMP_EXCEEDS_FMV_SAFE_HARBOR`.

**Submitting a plan.** A `CompensationDesigner` moves a plan `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and three preconditions are evaluated in the order the rejection precedence dictates. `includes_referral_credit` must be false — compensation varying with the volume or value of referrals is categorically prohibited, and the rejection is `ERR_REFERRAL_LINKED_COMPENSATION`. `stark_exception` must not be `NONE`, or the rejection is `ERR_STARK_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`. And the linked physician's `state_license_expires_on` must not be before the current logical date, or the rejection is `ERR_PHYSICIAN_LICENSE_EXPIRED`; a licence expiring exactly today is still valid. A `CompensationDesigner` may also abandon its own draft outright with `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, which has no precondition and recomputes the linked physician's `employment_status`.

**The bounded revision cycle.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may send a `SUBMITTED` plan back for redrafting, and the plan is allowed two returns. When the request arrives and `returned_count` is less than 2, the plan takes the transition `SUBMITTED → DRAFT` and `returned_count` is incremented by 1. When the request arrives and `returned_count` already equals 2, the very same request instead takes the transition `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`, still increments `returned_count` by 1, and recomputes the linked physician's `employment_status`. The counter is incremented on both outcomes, so the terminal row carries a `returned_count` of 3. The observable sequence for a single plan is therefore a `returned_count` of 0 becoming 1 with the plan back in `DRAFT`, then 1 becoming 2 with the plan back in `DRAFT` again, then 2 becoming 3 with the plan `WITHDRAWN`. `returned_count` starts at 0 when the plan is created and is never reset; a fresh allowance requires designing a new plan. Cascaded returns count against the same allowance — both the return triggered when the acquisition's `compliance_clearance` moves `CLEARED → FLAGGED` and the return triggered when board review is reverted increment `returned_count` exactly as a directly requested return does.

**Approval.** A `ComplianceOfficer` moves a plan `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` against four preconditions. A valuation opinion with `opinion_type` `FMV` on the same acquisition must be in `ACCEPTED` with `is_pinned` true, or the request fails with `ERR_FMV_OPINION_NOT_ACCEPTED` — which in practice means the deal must already have reached `BOARD_REVIEW`. The caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from that pinned opinion's `accepted_by_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`: the officer who accepted the valuation may not also bless the compensation benchmarked against it. `benchmark_percentile` must not be greater than 90.00 unless `fmv_justification_on_file` is true, or the request fails with `ERR_COMP_EXCEEDS_FMV_SAFE_HARBOR`. And the pinned opinion's `expires_on` must not be before the current logical date, or the request fails with `ERR_VALUATION_OPINION_EXPIRED`. On approval the caller's `X-User-ID` is stored in `approved_by_user_id`, the linked physician's `employment_status` is recomputed to `OFFERED`, and any other compensation plan for the same `physician_id` that is currently in `EXECUTED` is set to `SUPERSEDED` — a physician may hold only one live executed arrangement.

**Execution.** Only the physician can execute. A `PracticePhysician` moves the plan `APPROVED → EXECUTED` under two preconditions: the caller's resolved physician `id` must equal the plan's `physician_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_NOT_PLAN_OWNER`; and the parent acquisition's `deal_status` must be `BOARD_REVIEW`, `SIGNED`, or `CLOSED`. On execution `executed_at` is stamped with the current logical instant and the physician's `employment_status` is recomputed to `EMPLOYED`. An `APPROVED` plan may alternatively be pulled by a `ComplianceOfficer` with `APPROVED → WITHDRAWN`, which has no precondition and recomputes the physician's `employment_status`.

**Compliance hold.** An `EXECUTED` plan can be frozen. `EXECUTED → COMPLIANCE_HOLD` is available to a `ComplianceOfficer` with no precondition, and also arrives as a cascade from post-close performance reporting. In either case the hold has a second effect on the parent acquisition: when that acquisition's `compliance_clearance` is currently `CLEARED`, it is moved to `FLAGGED` — which in turn returns every `SUBMITTED` plan on the acquisition to `DRAFT` and increments each of those plans' `returned_count` by 1. A single hold therefore ripples into the whole deal's compensation queue, and the deal is frozen for new work until an officer clears it again. A held plan can be released back with `COMPLIANCE_HOLD → EXECUTED`, which requires that `benchmark_percentile` is not greater than 90.00 or that `fmv_justification_on_file` is true, failing otherwise with `ERR_COMP_EXCEEDS_FMV_SAFE_HARBOR`. It can instead be retired with `COMPLIANCE_HOLD → SUPERSEDED`, which has no precondition and recomputes the physician's `employment_status`. Reviewers may attach a `review_note` to any of these decisions. `SUPERSEDED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the plan's terminal states.

### 3.5. The Liability Schedule, Asset-versus-Equity Legality, and the Indemnity Escrow

Diligence surfaces exposures, and a `DealLead` schedules each one. The payload carries the `acquisition_id`, a `category` drawn from `MALPRACTICE_TAIL`, `PAYER_REFUND_EXPOSURE`, `EQUIPMENT_LEASE`, `UNPAID_PTO`, and `STARK_SELF_DISCLOSURE`, a narrative `description`, and the best estimate of the exposure as `estimated_amount_cents`. An `indemnity_cap_cents` may optionally be supplied at creation, recording the seller's indemnity ceiling. A newly scheduled item is created in `disposition` `UNASSIGNED` and holds no escrow: `escrowed_amount_cents` is 0.

Allocating an exposure is the `DealLead`'s decision, expressed as a `target_disposition` with an optional `disposition_note`. Whether a given allocation is legal at all depends on the interaction of the acquisition's `structure` with the item's `category`, and an illegal pairing is rejected with `ERR_LIABILITY_DISPOSITION_NOT_PERMITTED`. When `structure` is `EQUITY`, a disposition of `EXCLUDED` is not permitted for `MALPRACTICE_TAIL`, `STARK_SELF_DISCLOSURE`, or `PAYER_REFUND_EXPOSURE`, because an equity buyer takes the entity together with its whole history and cannot leave those behind. When `structure` is `ASSET`, a disposition of `ASSUMED` is not permitted for `STARK_SELF_DISCLOSURE`. Every other combination of structure and category is permitted.

Six allocations exist. `UNASSIGNED → ASSUMED` and `UNASSIGNED → EXCLUDED` each check only the structure-and-category legality described above and have no side effect. `UNASSIGNED → INDEMNIFIED` requires that `indemnity_cap_cents` is present and is at least `estimated_amount_cents`; a cap below the exposure it purports to cover is not an indemnity, and this failure is reported at the schema rank as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `UNASSIGNED → ESCROWED` and `ASSUMED → ESCROWED` behave identically to one another: each requires that `estimated_amount_cents` does not exceed the acquisition's `escrow_available_cents`, failing with `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT` otherwise, and each sets the item's `escrowed_amount_cents` to `estimated_amount_cents`, adds that amount to `escrow_committed_cents`, and subtracts it from `escrow_available_cents`. Finally, `ESCROWED → EXCLUDED` releases an escrowed item: it checks the structure-and-category legality for `EXCLUDED`, subtracts the item's `escrowed_amount_cents` from `escrow_committed_cents`, adds the same amount back to `escrow_available_cents`, and resets the item's own `escrowed_amount_cents` to 0. Escrow is committed item by item and released in full, so no drift accumulates. `EXCLUDED` and `INDEMNIFIED` are terminal dispositions; an item in either cannot be reallocated.

### 3.6. Payer Contract Novation and Provider Re-Credentialing

An `IntegrationManager` records each payer agreement carried into the transaction. The payload names the `acquisition_id`, the `payer_name`, a `payer_type` of `COMMERCIAL`, `MANAGED_MEDICARE`, `MEDICARE`, or `MEDICAID`, and a `contract_form` of `ASSIGNABLE`, `CONSENT_REQUIRED`, or `NON_ASSIGNABLE`. The contractual notice period the payer must be given is `notice_days_required`, a non-negative whole number of days. The term runs from `effective_on` through `terminates_on`, both calendar dates, with `terminates_on` on or after `effective_on`. The share of practice collections flowing through this payer is `annual_revenue_share_bps`, an integer from 0 through 10000 inclusive. How many credentialing packages the payer's committee will seat in one cycle is `committee_slots_per_cycle`, which must be at least 1 and defaults to 2 when omitted. A new contract lands in `novation_status` `NOT_REQUESTED` with `notice_sent_on` and `consent_received_on` both null.

Two agreements with the same payer on the same acquisition may not cover overlapping terms. At creation the server compares the new term against every other contract that shares both the `acquisition_id` and the `payer_name` and whose `novation_status` is neither `DENIED` nor `TERMINATED` — denied and terminated contracts cover nothing and are excluded from the comparison. The term is a closed interval on both endpoints, so two terms collide whenever the new `effective_on` is on or before the peer's `terminates_on` and the peer's `effective_on` is on or before the new `terminates_on`. Two contracts that merely touch — one terminating on the very day the next becomes effective — do collide, and the collision is reported as `ERR_PAYER_CONTRACT_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. A single day of gap is required: a new term beginning the day after a peer's last day is accepted.

Novation runs as a branching workflow driven by an `action` on the contract, optionally accompanied by a `note`. `SEND_NOTICE` moves `NOT_REQUESTED → NOTICE_SENT`, requires the parent acquisition's `deal_status` to be `BOARD_REVIEW` or `SIGNED`, and stamps `notice_sent_on` with the current logical date. `RECORD_CONSENT` moves `NOTICE_SENT → CONSENT_RECEIVED`, requires that the current logical date is at least `notice_days_required` days after `notice_sent_on` — the payer's contractual notice period must actually have run — and stamps `consent_received_on` with the current logical date. `NOVATE` moves either `CONSENT_RECEIVED → NOVATED` or `NEW_AGREEMENT_PENDING → NOVATED`, and in both cases requires at least one credentialing package on this contract to be in `APPROVED`: a payer relationship is not novated until at least one provider is credentialed under it. `NOVATED` and `TERMINATED` are the terminal novation states.

`DENY` moves `NOTICE_SENT → DENIED` with no precondition, and it is the most consequential action in this subsection because it cascades in three directions. Every credentialing package on this contract that is in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, or `IN_REVIEW` is set to `WITHDRAWN`. The acquisition's readiness checklist line with `task_code` `PAYER_CREDENTIALING` is set to `FAILED` when it is currently in `IN_PROGRESS` or in `COMPLETE`, and its `failure_reason` is recorded. And the acquisition's `readiness_posture` is recomputed, which will read `BLOCKED` for as long as that failed line stands.

From `DENIED` the manager has two exits. `START_NEW_AGREEMENT` moves `DENIED → NEW_AGREEMENT_PENDING` and requires that `contract_form` is not `NON_ASSIGNABLE`; a contract the payer will not paper afresh has no such path. `TERMINATE` moves `DENIED → TERMINATED` with no precondition. `TERMINATE` is also available from `NEW_AGREEMENT_PENDING`, again with no precondition, giving `NEW_AGREEMENT_PENDING → TERMINATED`.

**Re-credentialing.** An `IntegrationManager` opens one package per provider per payer, naming the `acquisition_id`, the `physician_id` of the provider being re-credentialed, the `payer_contract_id` of the payer that must credential them, and the `submitted_on` date on which the package will be filed. The `acquisition_id` in the body must match both the physician's and the contract's own acquisition, and a mismatch anywhere among the three is `ERR_ACQUISITION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. At most one live package may exist for a given physician-and-payer pair: if another package for the same `physician_id` and `payer_contract_id` is in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW`, or `APPROVED`, the create is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREDENTIALING_PACKAGE`. A package that was denied or withdrawn does not block a fresh filing. New packages are created in `DRAFT`.

Three derived values are computed at creation. The payer's committee meets on the first and the fifteenth of every month, and `committee_cycle_key` is the earliest such meeting date falling on or after `submitted_on`, stored as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date. The assignment is lower-inclusive: a package with `submitted_on` of 2026-09-15 belongs to the 2026-09-15 cycle, while one with `submitted_on` of 2026-09-16 belongs to the 2026-10-01 cycle. `lead_days` is the payer's processing lead time, looked up from the linked contract's `payer_type`: 90 days for `COMMERCIAL`, 75 for `MANAGED_MEDICARE`, 60 for `MEDICARE`, and 120 for `MEDICAID`. `projected_effective_on` is then computed by adding `lead_days` days to `submitted_on` and pushing the result forward to the earliest committee meeting date on or after that intermediate date — again the first or fifteenth of a month, and again lower-inclusive, so a lead time landing exactly on a meeting date uses that meeting. `retro_billing_allowed` is true exactly when the payer's retro-billing window is greater than zero days; only `MEDICARE` carries a non-zero window, of 30 days, while `COMMERCIAL`, `MANAGED_MEDICARE`, and `MEDICAID` all carry zero.

Filing the package, `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, is where the lead-time arithmetic becomes a gate, and it carries four preconditions. The linked contract's `novation_status` must be `NOTICE_SENT`, `CONSENT_RECEIVED`, `NEW_AGREEMENT_PENDING`, or `NOVATED` — there is no point filing with a payer who has not been noticed or who has walked away. The linked physician's `state_license_expires_on` must not be before the current logical date, or the filing fails with `ERR_PHYSICIAN_LICENSE_EXPIRED`. The package's `projected_effective_on` must not fall after `target_close_on` extended by the payer's retro-billing window, or the filing fails with `ERR_CREDENTIALING_LEAD_TIME_MISSED`; landing exactly on that limit is viable and one day past it is not, which means an identical filing date can be perfectly viable for a `MEDICARE` contract and fatal for a `COMMERCIAL` one. And the committee cycle must still have a free seat: strictly fewer than the linked contract's `committee_slots_per_cycle` other packages on the same `payer_contract_id` may already share this package's `committee_cycle_key` in `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW`, or `APPROVED`, or the filing fails with `ERR_COMMITTEE_CYCLE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This last is an aggregate limit rather than a per-package one: each package can be individually viable while the cycle as a whole is full.

From `SUBMITTED` the package moves to `IN_REVIEW` with no precondition and no side effect, and from `IN_REVIEW` it reaches either `APPROVED` or `DENIED`, neither of which carries a precondition. `IN_REVIEW → DENIED` has no side effect. `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED` has two: when every credentialing package on the acquisition is in `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, or `WITHDRAWN` and the acquisition's `PAYER_CREDENTIALING` checklist line is currently in `IN_PROGRESS`, that line is set to `COMPLETE`; and the acquisition's `readiness_posture` is recomputed regardless.

Withdrawal is available from three states and it is how a seat is given back. `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN` has no side effect, because a draft package never consumed a seat. `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` and `IN_REVIEW → WITHDRAWN` each free the package's seat in its `committee_cycle_key` on the linked payer contract, so a subsequent filing into that cycle succeeds where it would previously have exceeded capacity. All three withdrawal transitions may be requested directly by an `IntegrationManager` or may arrive as the cascade from the payer's denial. `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the package's terminal states.

### 3.7. Chart Migration and Legacy Record Retention

Charts move in batches, each staged by an `IntegrationManager`. The payload names the `acquisition_id`, a human-readable `batch_label` that must be unique within that one acquisition — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_LABEL`, and the same label may be reused freely across different acquisitions — the `source_system` the charts are extracted from, one of `ATHENA`, `ECLINICALWORKS`, or `PAPER_CHART`, the `chart_type`, one of `STRUCTURED`, `IMAGING`, or `SCANNED_DOCUMENT`, the `record_count` staged in the batch, and the `last_encounter_on` date of the most recent clinical encounter represented in the batch. `record_count` may be 0 — a batch may be staged empty as a placeholder. Whether any record belongs to a patient who is still a minor is stated in `contains_minor_records`, which defaults to false; when it is true, `youngest_patient_age_years` becomes mandatory and must be a whole number of years from 0 through 17 inclusive, and its absence is caught at the schema rank as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Statutory retention is decided at staging time. The base period is 7 years when `chart_type` is `IMAGING` and 10 years for both `STRUCTURED` and `SCANNED_DOCUMENT` — imaging studies carry a shorter statutory base than other records. When `contains_minor_records` is false, `retention_years` is simply that base. When it is true, a second candidate is computed as 21 years minus `youngest_patient_age_years`, holding the record until the youngest patient would reach the age of majority, and `retention_years` is whichever of the base period and that minor-derived period is larger. `legacy_purge_eligible_on` is then `last_encounter_on` plus `retention_years` calendar years, preserving month and day-of-month, with 29 February mapping to 28 February in a non-leap target year.

A newly staged batch reads `STAGED` with `validated_count` and `defect_count` at 0, `unprocessed_count` equal to `record_count`, `match_rate_bps` at 0, and `retry_count` at 0. `unprocessed_count` is always `record_count` minus `validated_count` minus `defect_count` — it is the conserved remainder, and the invariant that validated plus defect plus unprocessed equals the original record count holds after every action on the batch.

The pipeline is driven by an `action` on the batch, optionally with a `note`. `EXTRACT` moves `STAGED → EXTRACTED` and requires `record_count` to be greater than 0, failing with `ERR_EMPTY_MIGRATION_BATCH` otherwise: a placeholder batch may sit staged forever but can never be extracted, because an empty batch has no defined match rate. `LOAD` moves `EXTRACTED → LOADED` with no precondition and no side effect.

`RECONCILE`, issued against a `LOADED` batch, carries `validated_count` and `defect_count` in the request and branches three ways. In every branch the sum of `validated_count` and `defect_count` must not exceed `record_count`, or the request fails with `ERR_MIGRATION_COUNT_INVALID` — conservation is enforced on input so that `unprocessed_count` can never go negative. The match rate is then computed as `validated_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by `record_count`, and **truncated** toward zero to a whole number of basis points; it is never rounded up, so a batch cannot round its way to the reconciliation floor. The floor is 9950 basis points, that is 99.5 percent.

When the computed rate is at or above 9950 basis points, the batch takes `LOADED → RECONCILED`. It stores `validated_count`, `defect_count`, the recomputed `unprocessed_count`, and `match_rate_bps`; it sets the acquisition's readiness checklist line with `task_code` `IT_DATA_MIGRATION` to `COMPLETE` when every migration batch on the acquisition is in `RECONCILED` or `ABORTED` and that line is currently in `IN_PROGRESS`; and it recomputes the acquisition's `readiness_posture`.

When the computed rate falls below 9950 basis points and `retry_count` is still less than 2, the batch takes `LOADED → FAILED`. It stores the same four figures — `validated_count`, `defect_count`, `unprocessed_count`, and `match_rate_bps` — and increments `retry_count` by 1.

When the computed rate falls below 9950 basis points and `retry_count` has already reached 2, the same request instead takes `LOADED → ABORTED`. It stores the same four figures, still increments `retry_count` by 1, sets the acquisition's `IT_DATA_MIGRATION` checklist line to `FAILED` when that line is in `IN_PROGRESS` or in `COMPLETE` and records its `failure_reason`, and recomputes the acquisition's `readiness_posture`. The retry allowance is therefore two failures, with the third failing reconcile abandoning the batch outright.

A `FAILED` batch has two exits. `REQUEUE` moves `FAILED → STAGED`, resetting `validated_count` and `defect_count` to 0 — which restores `unprocessed_count` to exactly `record_count` — and resetting `match_rate_bps` to 0. `retry_count` is not reset, so the allowance genuinely bounds the loop. `ABORT` moves `FAILED → ABORTED` with no precondition, and recomputes the acquisition's `readiness_posture`. `RECONCILED` and `ABORTED` are the batch's terminal states.

### 3.8. Staff Transition Offers, Wage Harmonisation and the Retention Pool

An `IntegrationManager` drafts a transition offer for each non-physician staff member. The payload names the `acquisition_id`, the `staff_member_name`, the `role_code` — one of `MA`, `RN`, `FRONT_DESK`, `BILLER`, or `PRACTICE_MANAGER` — the `legacy_annual_wage_cents` earned at the target practice, and the `offer_expires_on` date after which the staff member may no longer accept. Two optional inputs each default to 0: `legacy_pto_hours`, the accrued but unused paid-time-off hours carried across, and `credited_service_months`, the months of prior service credited for benefits harmonisation. A drafted offer lands in `DRAFT`, with `extended_on` and `accepted_on` both null, and the retention pool is untouched — a draft reserves nothing.

**Harmonising the wage.** Four steps run at creation time, and the rounding points are pinned because rounding the sum instead of the components can differ by a cent. First, the market uplift: `uplift_component_cents` is `legacy_annual_wage_cents` multiplied by the role's uplift rate, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole cent on its own. The role uplift rates are 400 basis points for `MA`, 600 for `RN`, 250 for `FRONT_DESK`, 300 for `BILLER`, and 800 for `PRACTICE_MANAGER` — a four percent bump for a medical assistant, an eight percent bump for a practice manager. Second, the paid-time-off buy-out: the legacy hourly rate is `legacy_annual_wage_cents` divided by 2080 annual hours and is deliberately **not** rounded at this point; `pto_component_cents` is `legacy_pto_hours` multiplied by that unrounded hourly rate, multiplied by 5000 basis points and divided by 10000 — the buy-out is valued at half the legacy hourly rate — and only then rounded half up to a whole cent on its own. Third, the harmonised sum is `legacy_annual_wage_cents` plus `uplift_component_cents` plus `pto_component_cents`, each component already independently rounded. Fourth, the system pay-scale floor is applied last: the floor is 4200000 cents for `MA`, 7600000 for `RN`, 3600000 for `FRONT_DESK`, 4400000 for `BILLER`, and 9500000 for `PRACTICE_MANAGER`. If the harmonised sum is strictly below the role's floor, `offered_annual_wage_cents` becomes the floor and `wage_floor_applied` is true; otherwise `offered_annual_wage_cents` is the harmonised sum and `wage_floor_applied` is false. A harmonised sum landing exactly on the floor is not displaced, and `wage_floor_applied` reads false.

**Awarding the retention bonus.** `retention_bonus_cents` is computed immediately after the wage, in four steps nested on top of it. The service tier is banded on `credited_service_months`: below 12 months earns 200 basis points, from 12 months up to but not including 60 months earns 500, from 60 months up to but not including 120 months earns 800, and 120 months or more earns 1100. The raw award is `offered_annual_wage_cents` multiplied by that tier rate, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. A criticality multiplier is then applied to that **already-rounded** raw award: 12500 basis points — a twenty-five percent premium — when `role_code` is `RN`, `BILLER`, or `PRACTICE_MANAGER`, and 10000 basis points, leaving the award unchanged, for `MA` and `FRONT_DESK`. The product is divided by 10000 and rounded half up to a whole cent a second time. Finally the result is clamped in a fixed order: the floor of 100000 cents is applied first, raising any smaller award up to it, and the cap of 1500000 cents is applied second, bringing any larger award down to it.

**Committing against the pool.** Extending an offer, `DRAFT → EXTENDED`, is where money is actually reserved, and it carries three preconditions. The parent acquisition's `deal_status` must be `DILIGENCE`, `BOARD_REVIEW`, or `SIGNED`. The offer's `offer_expires_on` must not be before the current logical date — an offer that has already lapsed cannot be extended. And the offer's `retention_bonus_cents` must not exceed the acquisition's `retention_pool_available_cents`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_RETENTION_POOL_EXCEEDED`. Because the pool is checked at commitment time rather than at drafting time, an offer may quite legitimately be drafted for more than the pool can bear and only fail when someone tries to extend it. On success the offer's `retention_bonus_cents` is added to `retention_pool_committed_cents` and subtracted from `retention_pool_available_cents`, `extended_on` is stamped with the current logical date, and the acquisition's `hr_staffing_coverage_pct` is recomputed.

Accepting, `EXTENDED → ACCEPTED`, requires only that `offer_expires_on` is not before the current logical date. It stamps `accepted_on` with the current logical date, recomputes `hr_staffing_coverage_pct`, sets the acquisition's readiness checklist line with `task_code` `HR_STAFFING_PLAN` to `COMPLETE` when the recomputed coverage is at least 80.00 percent and that line is currently in `IN_PROGRESS`, and recomputes the acquisition's `readiness_posture`. An accepted offer keeps its commitment against the pool; the money is spent, not released.

Three exits release the commitment in full and behave identically to one another in fiscal terms. `EXTENDED → DECLINED` and `EXTENDED → RESCINDED` each carry no precondition; `EXTENDED → EXPIRED` requires that `offer_expires_on` is strictly before the current logical date. All three subtract the offer's `retention_bonus_cents` from `retention_pool_committed_cents`, add the same amount back to `retention_pool_available_cents`, and recompute `hr_staffing_coverage_pct`. Because the released amount is exactly the amount committed, the pool balance after a rescission is identical to the balance before the offer was ever extended. Separately, `DRAFT → RESCINDED` withdraws an offer that was never extended; it has no precondition and no side effect, because a draft held neither pool money nor a headcount seat. That transition may be requested directly or may arrive as the cascade from the acquisition's `FLAGGED → SUSPENDED` clearance move. `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `RESCINDED`, and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

**Workforce coverage.** `hr_staffing_coverage_pct` counts the offers that hold a seat — those in `EXTENDED` or in `ACCEPTED`, since an extended offer holds a seat as surely as it holds pool money — multiplies that count by 100, divides by `required_staff_headcount`, and rounds half up to exactly two decimal places. Offers still in `DRAFT` do not count. The `HR_STAFFING_PLAN` checklist line may only be completed while coverage is at least 80.00 percent, and exactly 80.00 is sufficient.

### 3.9. Provider-Based Billing Conversion and Patient Notification

Each practice site whose billing status changes at close is registered by an `IntegrationManager` with the `acquisition_id`, the `site_name`, a `conversion_type` of either `PROVIDER_BASED_DEPARTMENT` or `FREESTANDING_CLINIC`, the `required_notice_count` of patients who must receive a written notice of the billing change — at least 1 — and the `effective_on` date on which the new billing status takes effect. Two optional inputs record notification progress: `notified_patient_count`, defaulting to 0, and `notices_issued_on`, the date the notices actually went out, which may be left null at registration but is required before the site can leave `NOT_STARTED` in earnest. A newly registered site reads `NOT_STARTED` with `attested_on` null.

Three derived values are computed at registration and re-derived whenever the notification figures are re-examined. `notice_coverage_bps` is `notified_patient_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by `required_notice_count`, and **truncated** toward zero to whole basis points; notifying all but one patient never rounds up to complete coverage, so one missed patient out of twelve hundred reports 9991 basis points and fails. `notice_lead_days` is the whole number of days from `notices_issued_on` to `effective_on`, and is null for as long as `notices_issued_on` is null. `provider_based_uplift_bps` is 4000 when `conversion_type` is `PROVIDER_BASED_DEPARTMENT` and exactly 0 when it is `FREESTANDING_CLINIC`.

Attesting the provider-based criteria, `NOT_STARTED → ATTESTED`, requires the parent acquisition's `deal_status` to be `BOARD_REVIEW`, `SIGNED`, or `CLOSED`, and stamps `attested_on` with the current logical date. Recording that the notices went out, `ATTESTED → NOTICES_ISSUED`, carries three preconditions: `notices_issued_on` must not be null; `notice_lead_days` must be at least 30, or the request fails with `ERR_PATIENT_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_SHORT` — exactly thirty days of lead time is adequate and twenty-nine is not; and `notice_coverage_bps` must be at least 10000, or the request fails with `ERR_PATIENT_NOTICE_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. On success, the acquisition's readiness checklist line with `task_code` `PATIENT_NOTIFICATION` is set to `COMPLETE` when every billing conversion on the acquisition has left both `NOT_STARTED` and `ATTESTED` behind and that line is currently in `IN_PROGRESS`, and the acquisition's `readiness_posture` is recomputed.

Completing the conversion, `NOTICES_ISSUED → CONVERTED`, carries two preconditions. When `conversion_type` is `PROVIDER_BASED_DEPARTMENT`, some payer contract on the same acquisition with `payer_type` `MEDICARE` must be in `novation_status` `NOVATED`, or the request fails with `ERR_PAYER_NOVATION_INCOMPLETE` — a provider-based department bills Medicare under the health system's number, and that requires the Medicare relationship to have novated. A `FREESTANDING_CLINIC` is exempt from that check. In addition, the parent acquisition's `deal_status` must be `SIGNED` or `CLOSED`.

A conversion may be abandoned from any of its three non-terminal states. `NOT_STARTED → WITHDRAWN` and `ATTESTED → WITHDRAWN` each carry no precondition and no side effect. `NOTICES_ISSUED → WITHDRAWN` likewise carries no precondition, but it does recompute the acquisition's `readiness_posture`, because a site that had satisfied its notification obligation no longer does. `CONVERTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

### 3.10. The Day-One Checklist, Signing and Closing

The day-one readiness checklist is a small, fixed set of lines. Either an `IntegrationManager` or a `DealLead` adds one, naming the `acquisition_id`, the `task_code`, and the `due_on` date by which the line must be complete, and optionally setting `is_blocking`, which defaults to true. `task_code` is one of `HR_STAFFING_PLAN`, `PAYER_CREDENTIALING`, `IT_DATA_MIGRATION`, `REVENUE_CYCLE_GO_LIVE`, `COMPLIANCE_TRAINING`, or `PATIENT_NOTIFICATION`, and at most one line per code may exist on a given acquisition — a second attempt at the same code is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TASK_CODE`. The functional owner is never supplied by a client: `domain` is derived from `task_code`, with `HR_STAFFING_PLAN` mapping to `HR`, `PAYER_CREDENTIALING` mapping to `REVENUE_CYCLE`, `IT_DATA_MIGRATION` mapping to `IT`, `REVENUE_CYCLE_GO_LIVE` mapping to `REVENUE_CYCLE`, `COMPLIANCE_TRAINING` mapping to `COMPLIANCE`, and `PATIENT_NOTIFICATION` mapping to `CLINICAL`. A new line reads `OPEN`, with `waiver_justification`, `failure_reason`, and `verified_by_user_id` all null, and adding it recomputes the acquisition's `readiness_posture`.

**How the posture is derived.** Only lines with `is_blocking` true participate; non-blocking lines never affect the posture, in any status. If the acquisition has no blocking lines at all, `readiness_posture` is `BLOCKED` — an empty checklist is treated as an unbuilt checklist rather than as a vacuously satisfied one, which is why a brand-new acquisition starts `BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if any blocking line is in `FAILED`, the posture is `BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if any blocking line is in `OPEN` or in `IN_PROGRESS`, the posture is `AT_RISK`. Only when every blocking line is in `COMPLETE` or in `WAIVED` does the posture read `READY`.

Starting a line, `OPEN → IN_PROGRESS`, is available to an `IntegrationManager` and to a `ComplianceOfficer`, has no precondition, and recomputes the posture. Completing it, `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE`, is available to the same two roles and carries two conditional preconditions: when `task_code` is `HR_STAFFING_PLAN`, the acquisition's `hr_staffing_coverage_pct` must be at least 80.00; when `task_code` is `IT_DATA_MIGRATION`, every migration batch on the acquisition must be in `RECONCILED` or `ABORTED`, or the request fails with `ERR_MIGRATION_INCOMPLETE`. The other four codes carry no such gate. Completion stores the caller's `X-User-ID` in `verified_by_user_id` and recomputes the posture. This same transition also arrives as a cascade when the corresponding domain workstream reaches its terminal condition — the last credentialing package approving, the last migration batch reconciling, the coverage threshold being met on acceptance of an offer, or the last billing conversion issuing its notices.

Waiving a line is a `ComplianceOfficer`'s privilege alone and is available from two states, `OPEN → WAIVED` and `IN_PROGRESS → WAIVED`. Both carry the identical precondition that the request supplies a non-empty `waiver_justification` — whitespace alone is not a justification, and its absence is `ERR_WAIVER_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` — and both perform the identical side effects of storing that `waiver_justification`, storing the caller's `X-User-ID` in `verified_by_user_id`, and recomputing the posture. A waived blocking line counts as satisfied for posture purposes, which is the whole point of the waiver.

Failure comes in two shapes. `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED` may be requested directly by an `IntegrationManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, supplying a `failure_reason`, and it also arrives as the cascade from a payer's denial or from a migration batch aborting; it stores `failure_reason` and recomputes the posture. `COMPLETE → FAILED` exists **only** as that same cascade and can never be requested directly — a direct request to fail a completed line is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. When it fires it stores `failure_reason`, clears `verified_by_user_id` back to null since the earlier verification no longer stands, and recomputes the posture. A `FAILED` line can be reopened with `FAILED → IN_PROGRESS`, which has no precondition, clears `failure_reason` back to null, and recomputes the posture. `COMPLETE` and `WAIVED` are terminal for direct requests; only the cascade may move a line out of `COMPLETE`.

**Signing.** A `DealLead` moves the deal `BOARD_REVIEW → SIGNED` against four preconditions. Every physician on the acquisition must have at least one compensation plan in `APPROVED` or in `EXECUTED`, or the request fails with `ERR_COMP_PLAN_MISSING` — no physician may be left without a signed-off arrangement at signature. No liability item on the acquisition may still be in `UNASSIGNED`, or the request fails with `ERR_LIABILITY_UNASSIGNED`; an acquisition with no liability items at all satisfies this rule vacuously. When `tail_coverage_required` is true, `escrow_total_cents` must be at least the computed `tail_premium_cents`, or the request fails with `ERR_TAIL_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` — the escrow must be able to fund the tail it is there to fund. And the acquisition's `compliance_clearance` must be `CLEARED`. Signing recomputes `readiness_posture`.

**Closing.** `SIGNED → CLOSED` is likewise the `DealLead`'s act and carries five preconditions. `readiness_posture` must be `READY`, or the request fails with `ERR_READINESS_NOT_READY` — and because an acquisition with no blocking checklist lines reads `BLOCKED`, such a deal fails here rather than closing on an empty checklist. Every payer contract on the acquisition must be in `NOVATED` or `TERMINATED`, or the request fails with `ERR_PAYER_NOVATION_INCOMPLETE`. Every migration batch must be in `RECONCILED` or `ABORTED`, or the request fails with `ERR_MIGRATION_INCOMPLETE`. Every physician must have at least one plan actually in `EXECUTED` — approval is not enough at this gate — or the request fails with `ERR_NO_EXECUTED_COMP_PLAN`. And `compliance_clearance` must be `CLEARED`. On success the server stamps `closed_at` with the current logical instant and sets every valuation opinion on the deal that is still in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` to `SUPERSEDED`; the pinned `ACCEPTED` opinion is left exactly as it is. `CLOSED` and `ABANDONED` are the deal's terminal workflow states.

### 3.11. Post-Close Performance Tracking and the Compensation True-Up

Post-close tracking exists only after the deal has actually closed. An `IntegrationManager` appends one month of actuals at a time, naming the `acquisition_id`, the `physician_id` whose period is being reported, the `applied_comp_plan_id` of the executed plan the period is measured against, the `period_start_on` and `period_end_on` bounding the period, the `actual_wrvu` generated in the period, and the `actual_collections_cents` posted in it. Reports are append-only and immutable: they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`, and a period may never be restated.

Five conditions guard the append, and they are evaluated in the order §2.12 fixes. The `physician_id` and the `applied_comp_plan_id` must both belong to the named acquisition, and the plan must belong to that physician, or the request fails with `ERR_ACQUISITION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. No other report may already exist for the same `physician_id` and the same `period_start_on`, or the request fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD`. The period must be a single whole calendar month — `period_start_on` the first day of a month, `period_end_on` the last day of that same month — and `period_start_on` must not precede the calendar date of the acquisition's `closed_at`; any of these failing is `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_PERIOD`. The plan named by `applied_comp_plan_id` must be in `EXECUTED` or in `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`, or the request fails with `ERR_NO_EXECUTED_COMP_PLAN`. And the acquisition's `deal_status` must be `CLOSED`, or the request fails with `ERR_ACQUISITION_NOT_CLOSED`.

Appending a report folds it into the plan. `CompensationPlan.actual_ytd_wrvu` becomes the sum of `actual_wrvu` across every report filed against that plan, and `months_elapsed` becomes the count of those reports. The true-up then runs in five steps against the elapsed month count.

First, the plan's annual `threshold_wrvu` is pro-rated over the elapsed months: it is multiplied by `months_elapsed`, divided by 12, and rounded half up to a whole work RVU. Second, the earned compensation for the elapsed period is computed from the plan's `model_type`. Under `STRAIGHT_SALARY`, earned compensation is `base_salary_cents` multiplied by `months_elapsed`, divided by 12, rounded half up to a whole cent. Under `WRVU_PRODUCTIVITY`, it is `conversion_factor_cents_per_wrvu` multiplied by `actual_ytd_wrvu`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Under `BASE_PLUS_INCENTIVE`, it is the sum of two independently rounded terms: `base_salary_cents` multiplied by `months_elapsed` and divided by 12, rounded half up to a whole cent, plus `conversion_factor_cents_per_wrvu` multiplied by the excess of `actual_ytd_wrvu` over the pro-rated threshold — floored at zero when actual volume has not yet reached the pro-rated threshold — again rounded half up to a whole cent. Third, the amount the plan has already paid on its projection is `projected_total_cash_comp_cents` multiplied by `months_elapsed`, divided by 12, rounded half up to a whole cent. Fourth, `comp_true_up_cents` is the earned compensation minus that paid-to-date figure; a negative value is a clawback and is stored as a negative number rather than being floored at zero.

Fifth, the plan is re-benchmarked, and it is re-benchmarked on the **annualised run-rate**, never on the year-to-date figures. The annualised work-RVU volume is `actual_ytd_wrvu` multiplied by 12, divided by `months_elapsed`, rounded half up to a whole work RVU. The annualised total cash compensation is then computed by the very same model-driven projection described in §3.4, but with `projected_annual_wrvu` replaced by that annualised volume — so a straight-salary plan annualises to its base salary, a productivity plan annualises to the conversion factor times the annualised volume, and a base-plus-incentive plan annualises to the base plus the conversion factor applied to the annualised volume in excess of the plan's full annual `threshold_wrvu`. The annualised compensation per work RVU is the annualised total cash compensation divided by the annualised volume, rounded half up to a whole cent, or exactly 0 when the annualised volume is 0. `benchmark_percentile` is then recomputed by the same interpolation described in §3.4, evaluated on the annualised total cash compensation when the plan's `benchmark_basis` is `TOTAL_CASH_COMP` and on the annualised compensation per work RVU when it is `COMP_PER_WRVU`, against the same specialty quartile tables. The plan's `projected_annual_wrvu`, `projected_total_cash_comp_cents`, and `comp_per_wrvu_cents` are design inputs and are **never** overwritten by this reconciliation; only `benchmark_percentile`, `comp_true_up_cents`, `actual_ytd_wrvu`, and `months_elapsed` change.

The report itself then snapshots the outcome: `true_up_applied_cents` records the plan's `comp_true_up_cents` as it stands after this report was folded in, and `benchmark_percentile_after` records the plan's `benchmark_percentile` after the same fold, to two decimal places.

Finally, the safe-harbour trip fires. When the re-benchmarked `benchmark_percentile` is strictly greater than 90.00, `fmv_justification_on_file` is false, and the plan is in `EXECUTED`, the plan moves `EXECUTED → COMPLIANCE_HOLD`. That hold sets the acquisition's `compliance_clearance` to `FLAGGED` when it was `CLEARED`, and that flag in turn returns every compensation plan on the acquisition that is in `SUBMITTED` back to `DRAFT`, incrementing each of those plans' `returned_count` by 1. One month of unexpectedly strong productivity on one physician can therefore freeze the whole deal for new work and send every pending compensation design back to the drawing board — which is the intended serialisation point between post-close reality and in-flight compensation design.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint listed below sits at the root of the URL space: there is no version prefix and no path segment before the collection name. Successful creation of a resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`. Successful reads and successful state-changing actions return HTTP 200 with the affected resource — for an action endpoint, the resource whose state changed, refreshed to include every field the action recomputed. List endpoints return HTTP 200, `response_type` `array`, and the four pagination fields in `meta`. Setting the logical clock returns HTTP 200, `response_type` `object`, and — exactly as §2.6 states, rather than an empty object — a `data` of `{"current_utc": "…"}` carrying the instant the clock now holds, rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits.

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3, and every one of them also accepts a set of exact-match filters, all optional, which may be combined freely and are conjunctive when combined. Acquisitions may be filtered by `deal_status`, `compliance_clearance`, `readiness_posture`, `specialty`, and `structure`. Physicians may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `employment_status`, `specialty`, and `board_certified`. Valuation opinions may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `opinion_type`, `method`, `status`, and `is_pinned`. Compensation plans may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `physician_id`, `status`, `model_type`, and `benchmark_basis`. Payer contracts may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `payer_type`, `payer_name`, `novation_status`, and `contract_form`. Credentialing packages may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `physician_id`, `payer_contract_id`, `status`, `committee_cycle_key`, and `retro_billing_allowed`. Migration batches may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `status`, `chart_type`, `source_system`, and `contains_minor_records`. Staff transition offers may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `status`, `role_code`, and `wage_floor_applied`. Billing conversions may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `status`, and `conversion_type`. Readiness tasks may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `status`, `task_code`, `domain`, and `is_blocking`. Liability items may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `category`, and `disposition`. Performance reports may be filtered by `acquisition_id`, `physician_id`, `applied_comp_plan_id`, and `period_start_on`. Because every child collection can be filtered by its parent identifier, every child of an acquisition is reachable from that acquisition without a nested route.
