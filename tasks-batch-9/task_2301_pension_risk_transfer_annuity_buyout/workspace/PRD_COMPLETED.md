# Pension Risk Transfer API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A defined benefit plan sponsor who decides to de-risk is not making one decision but a chain of them, and each link in the chain is fiduciary. The sponsor must first price the alternatives — a lump-sum election window, an annuity buyout, a liability-driven investing glidepath, or some hybrid — on two irreconcilable bases: the accounting basis the sponsor reports and the settlement basis an insurance company will actually charge. The gap between those two numbers is the real cost of de-risking, and it is invisible until someone computes both. This API exists to make that gap, and every downstream commitment it triggers, an auditable record rather than a spreadsheet.

The operational reality behind a pension risk transfer is unglamorous and unforgiving. Participant records assembled over forty years contain missing addresses, unverified deaths, and benefit amounts that were mis-keyed by a prior administrator; a carrier will not price a tranche it cannot underwrite, and a regulator will not approve a termination filing for a plan whose data was never cleansed. Layered on top is the safest-available-annuity standard: the fiduciary who selects a carrier must be able to show that the choice was made on claims-paying strength, that an independent expert with no disclosed conflict endorsed it, and that the person who decided was not the person who proposed. The system therefore treats data cleansing, expert engagement, and duty segregation as hard gates rather than advisory workflow.

What follows specifies the whole arc: strategy evaluation and the plan amendment that authorizes settlement activity; participant data cleansing across three independent verification dimensions; benefit calculation audits; lump-sum notice, election, cap allocation and disbursement; tranche assembly, the quote process, carrier selection, pricing lock and asset transfer in kind or in cash; settlement accounting recognition and its effect on funded status; and finally post-transfer complaint handling, residual plan management, and the standard termination filing. Every monetary figure is an integer number of cents, every rate is an integer number of basis points, and every deadline is measured against a server-controlled logical clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every business resource in this system is mounted beneath the `/prt` prefix. This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that applies uniformly across that surface.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent — a header whose value is empty or only whitespace counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before any comparison. If the headers are well formed but the declared role does not appear in the target endpoint's authorized-role list (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight recognized roles are `PlanSponsor`, `PlanFiduciary`, `Actuary`, `PlanAdministrator`, `Regulator`, `InsurerRep`, `Participant`, and `IndependentExpertUser`.

Five of those roles are institutional functions rather than persisted entities. When `X-User-Role` is `PlanSponsor`, `PlanFiduciary`, `Actuary`, `PlanAdministrator`, or `Regulator`, the `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It does, however, record it where a transition demands an audit trail of who acted: `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a de-risking strategy, and `proposed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on an independent expert engagement. Those recorded values are what the segregation-of-duties rules compare against.

The remaining three roles are entity-backed peers who may only touch their own resources.

- When `X-User-Role` is `InsurerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Insurer`. Ownership checks compare the resource's carrier reference to `X-User-ID`: on a quote submission or decline, `X-User-ID` must equal the quote's `insurer_id`; on an asset-transfer review, `X-User-ID` must equal the funded tranche's `selected_insurer_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ParticipantRecord`. On an election or a revocation, `X-User-ID` must equal the election's `participant_id`; on a complaint filing, `X-User-ID` must equal the participant identifier in the request path.
- When `X-User-Role` is `IndependentExpertUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `IndependentExpert`. On an opinion issuance, `X-User-ID` must equal the expert engagement identifier in the request path.

Any of those ownership comparisons failing yields `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Separately, two operations enforce segregation of duties between two opaque operators, and a violation there yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): the fiduciary who records a decision on a strategy must not be the actuary who submitted it, and the fiduciary who clears or disqualifies an expert must not be the sponsor user who proposed that expert.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty random lower-case alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is negative or not an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints declare their own ordering: the benefit-audit history of a participant is ordered by `audit_sequence` ascending, and the asset-transfer history of a tranche is ordered by `created_at` ascending.

List endpoints also accept the domain filters named in §4's descriptions — `strategy_status`, `window_status`, `expert_status`, `plan_id`, `tranche_id`, `window_id`, `participant_id`, `cohort`, `data_status`, `mortality_status`, `address_status`, `participant_status`, `audit_status`, `election_status`, `selected_quote_id`, `selected_insurer_id`, `tranche_status`, `transfer_status`, `insurer_id`, `quote_status`, `entry_type`, `complaint_status`, and `responsibility_determination`. When a filter names an identifier that does not exist, the request is rejected with the corresponding not-found error rather than returning an empty page. The status- and enum-valued filters are not validated against their declared sets: an unrecognised value simply matches nothing and yields an empty page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock, Day Arithmetic and Interval Conventions

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a logical clock whose value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and which may only be moved by an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a `current_utc` instant and sets the clock to it. Before that capability is ever invoked the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the real system time. Moving the clock changes no entity state and fires no transitions, is open to all eight roles, accepts any instant — including one earlier than the current reading — and answers `200 OK` with an empty `data` object. Every timestamp the API parses out of a request body, `current_utc` included, is read the same way: an ISO 8601 string, with a trailing `Z` or an explicit offset both accepted and a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` date read as midnight, any offset normalised to UTC and a value with no offset assumed to be UTC already; an absent, non-string or unparseable value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every deadline, expiry, notice period, quiet period, revocation period and pricing-lock boundary described in this document is evaluated against this logical clock, and all day arithmetic is exact whole days added to a logical instant.

Two interval conventions coexist and must not be confused. A lump-sum election window is **half-open**: the interval runs from `opens_at` inclusive to `closes_at` exclusive, so the window is live while the clock is at or after `opens_at` and strictly before `closes_at`. A pricing lock is **closed**: it runs from `lock_start_at` inclusive to `lock_expires_at` inclusive, so two locks that touch at a single instant do overlap.

### 2.5. Integer Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer numbers of cents, and every field name carrying money ends in `_cents`. All rates, ratios, factors and scores are integer numbers of basis points, and every field name carrying one ends in `_bp` or `_bps`. No floating-point arithmetic appears anywhere in the specification, and no amount is ever stored with a fractional part.

Wherever this document says an amount is rounded, the method is **round half up on the magnitude**: an exact half rounds away from zero, never toward it. Applying a basis-point rate to an amount means multiplying the amount by the rate and then rounding the product half up over a divisor of 10,000; applying a rate expressed per annum-month means the divisor stated for that specific rule. Two quantities are computed with **floor division** rather than rounding, and are called out as such where they arise: the capital-strength points of an insurer, and the price points of an annuity quote.

Ordering matters as much as precision. Where a total is the sum of per-record amounts, this document states explicitly whether each record is rounded before the summation or whether the sum is rounded once at the end; the two produce different integers and only one is the contract.

### 2.6. Common Resource Properties and Versioning

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp that is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits taken from the logical clock at creation.

All resources except one additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the resource, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation. Where this document says a transition "bumps the version" of an entity, it means both that increment and the `updated_at` refresh. Crucially, a derived recomputation counts as a mutation: when a participant's liability figures or data-confidence score are re-derived by a cascade, that participant's `version` increments and its `updated_at` moves, even though no client sent a request naming that participant.

The single exception is the settlement accounting entry. Settlement entries are append-only and are never mutated after they are written, so a `SettlementEntry` carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. It records its own write instant separately in `posted_at`, taken from the logical clock when the entry was appended.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Referential Existence

Identifiers embedded in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve decides the error. A request naming a benefit audit under a participant that does not exist fails with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND` before the audit is ever looked up. The full set of not-found errors is `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSURER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.

The same discipline extends to identifiers supplied in a request body or as a list filter rather than in the path. A quote request naming an `insurer_id` that does not exist fails with `ERR_INSURER_NOT_FOUND`; a tranche assignment naming a `participant_id` that does not exist fails with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`. Path resolution always precedes body-identifier resolution. Route resolution itself sits outside the §5 catalogue: a path matching no route at all is answered with an `error_id` of `ERR_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404, and a method the route does not support with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` and HTTP 405, while a trailing slash is tolerated everywhere, so `/prt/plans/` and `/prt/plans` are the same route.

Foreign keys never appear as path segments. An entity's own identifier is always the `{id}` segment of its path, and every reference to another entity travels either in the request body or as a list filter. Consequently the children created by a cascade — the elections a window fans out, the settlement entries a settlement appends, the audits a new audit supersedes — are reachable only through the reverse-lookup list endpoints in §4.

### 2.8. Cascade Propagation, Parallel State Dimensions and Compound Bubble-Up

Three structural properties of this domain shape almost every workflow in §3.

**Parallel state dimensions.** A participant record carries three status fields that advance independently on the same row: `participant_status` tracks settlement disposition, `mortality_status` tracks death verification, and `address_status` tracks address cleansing. A participant may be `ASSIGNED` to a tranche while still `UNVERIFIED` for mortality and `UNCONFIRMED` for address; advancing one dimension never advances another. The derived `benefit_audit_status` field mirrors a fourth concern — the state of the participant's latest non-superseded benefit audit — and the `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` fields aggregate all of them into a single readiness verdict.

**Cascade propagation.** When an operation changes more than one entity, every affected entity is mutated in the same request, each bumping its own `version`. The `PensionPlan` is the single exception: its liability, asset, funded-status, participant-liability-total and settlement-charge figures are updated in place by a cascade *without* bumping the plan's `version` or moving its `updated_at`. The plan's `version` is bumped only by a request that names the plan directly — a valuation update, a settlement amendment or rescission, a termination filing, a filing review — and by the residual transition of §3.9. So loading a participant, abandoning a tranche, settling a window or a tranche, and resolving or re-triaging a complaint all leave the plan's `version` where it was even though they rewrite the plan's figures. Cascades are described hop by hop in §3, and each hop is part of the contract: an implementation that performs the primary transition but skips the third hop is incorrect. Cascades are also compensating where the domain demands it — abandoning a tranche releases the insurer capacity its lock consumed and returns its participants to the ongoing plan, and re-triaging a complaint away from sponsor responsibility reverses the remediation charge already booked, so that in both cases the conserved quantity returns to its pre-commitment value.

**Compound bubble-up.** The plan's own status is partly derived from the aggregate status of its children. At the end of a lump-sum window settlement and at the end of a buyout tranche settlement, the server re-evaluates whether the plan has become residual, and transitions it without any client request naming the plan. The precise precondition is given in §3.9.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Rejection on Supersede-Style Writes

Three write operations legitimately supersede something the client may not have seen, and all three are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent entity's `version`. In each case the version field is a required, non-optional member of the request body, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is rejected before any state is examined or changed.

- A `POST /prt/plans/{id}/valuation-updates` request must carry `expected_plan_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `PensionPlan`. If the plan's current `version` differs, the update is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A valuation update overwrites liability, asset, cost and discount-rate figures wholesale, so a client working from a stale plan view would silently erase measurements produced by an intervening update or by a settlement cascade.
- A `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/asset-transfers` request must carry `expected_tranche_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `BuyoutTranche`. If the tranche's current `version` differs, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRANCHE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new transfer supersedes any transfer still awaiting the insurer's answer on that tranche, so a stale client would supersede a composition it never saw and fund against an out-of-date `locked_premium_cents`.
- A `POST /prt/participants/{id}/benefit-audits` request must carry `expected_participant_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `ParticipantRecord`. If the participant's current `version` differs, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new audit supersedes every prior audit of that participant, so a stale client would discard an audit computed from earnings or service data that has since been corrected.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.10. Order of Evaluation Across Validation Families

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The families below are evaluated in the order listed; the first family that finds a violation decides the response, and later families are not consulted. The domain rationale is that fiduciary safety and participant protection outrank referential tidiness, scheduling constraints, quote timing, and money limits — under a prudence standard an imprudent purchase is a worse outcome than an overspend — so capacity and asset-sufficiency checks sit at the very bottom.

1. **Header authentication** — both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the eight roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Entity-backed ownership** — for the three entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` matches the owning entity, else `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Applies to exactly six operations: `POST /prt/quotes/{id}/submission`, `POST /prt/asset-transfers/{id}/insurer-review`, `POST /prt/elections/{id}/election`, `POST /prt/elections/{id}/revocation`, `POST /prt/participants/{id}/complaints`, and `POST /prt/independent-experts/{id}/opinion`. Two of those compare `X-User-ID` against the raw path segment and so genuinely precede family 4: on `POST /prt/participants/{id}/complaints` and `POST /prt/independent-experts/{id}/opinion` an unknown identifier in the path still yields `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` when it differs from `X-User-ID`. The other four must resolve their target before they can read the carrier or participant reference off it, so on `POST /prt/quotes/{id}/submission`, `POST /prt/asset-transfers/{id}/insurer-review`, `POST /prt/elections/{id}/election`, and `POST /prt/elections/{id}/revocation` the not-found error of family 4 is returned first.
4. **Path existence** — every `{id}` segment resolves, left to right, else the corresponding not-found error from the §2.7 set — `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable plan segment, and likewise for every other entity. Applies to every path containing an `{id}` segment.
5. **Body and filter existence** — every identifier supplied in a body or list filter resolves, else the corresponding not-found error — `ERR_INSURER_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `insurer_id`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `participant_id`, and so on. Applies to `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/quotes`, `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/participant-assignments`, and every filtered list endpoint.
6. **Request shape** — every required field present, every value of the correct JSON type, every enum value drawn from its declared set, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). A request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as JSON is treated as an empty JSON object, so it surfaces as whichever required field is missing; a body that parses to something other than an object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A string field also fails this family when it is present but blank, when it is not of the declared type, or when it falls outside a declared character-length bound. Unknown fields are ignored, and fields whose value the server computes or assigns are ignored when a client supplies them. Applies to every POST endpoint.
7. **Numeric ranges** — money fields at least 0, `discount_rate_bps` within 1 through 2000, `credited_service_months` within 0 through 600, `capital_ratio_bp` within 0 through 20000, `lock_days` within 1 through 180, `quote_valid_days` within 1 through 90, `plan_ein` exactly nine digits, and `remediation_amount_cents` consistent with the responsibility determination; else `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /prt/plans`, `POST /prt/plans/{id}/participants`, `POST /prt/plans/{id}/lump-sum-windows`, `POST /prt/insurers`, `POST /prt/quotes/{id}/submission`, `POST /prt/participants/{id}/benefit-audits`, and `POST /prt/complaints/{id}/resolution`. The two amount bounds of an asset transfer are the exception: `gross_value_cents` below 1 and a `security_lot_count` outside 1 through 500 are rejected under family 6 as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not as `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`.
8. **Interval well-formedness** — a lump-sum window requires `opens_at` strictly before `closes_at`, a quote submission requires `lock_days` of at least 1, and an amendment's `amendment_effective_date` must not precede the logical clock; else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /prt/plans/{id}/lump-sum-windows`, `POST /prt/plans/{id}/settlement-amendment`, and `POST /prt/quotes/{id}/submission`. Because numeric ranges are checked first, a `lock_days` of 0 surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` rather than `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.
9. **Notice period** — opening a window must leave participants at least 30 days between the logical clock and `closes_at`, else `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /prt/lump-sum-windows/{id}/opening`.
10. **Stale parent version** — the optimistic-concurrency comparisons of §2.9, yielding `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_TRANCHE_VERSION`, or `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` respectively, on `POST /prt/plans/{id}/valuation-updates`, `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/asset-transfers`, and `POST /prt/participants/{id}/benefit-audits`.
11. **Own-state precondition** — the target resource's own status field permits the requested transition, else the entity-specific conflict error: `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_STRATEGY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ELECTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_TRANCHE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_EXPERT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_TRANSFER_STATE_INVALID`, or `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`, all HTTP 409. Applies to every state-transition endpoint.
12. **Named-state precondition** — the specialised variants that name the *required* state instead of complaining about the current one: `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_SELECTED`, `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_TRANSFERRED`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_REQUESTED`, `ERR_EXPERT_NOT_ENGAGED`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_INITIATED`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_TRIAGED`, and `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, all HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/pricing-lock`, `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/settlement`, `POST /prt/quotes/{id}/submission`, `POST /prt/independent-experts/{id}/opinion`, `POST /prt/asset-transfers/{id}/insurer-review`, `POST /prt/complaints/{id}/resolution`, `POST /prt/elections/{id}/election`, and `POST /prt/elections/{id}/revocation`.
13. **Cross-entity state dependency** — an ancestor or peer entity is in the wrong state even though the target is fine: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` when a complaint resolution is attempted while the participant's audit is reopened, and `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID` when a termination filing is attempted with complaints still outstanding. Applies to `POST /prt/lump-sum-windows/{id}/opening`, `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/quoting`, `POST /prt/plans/{id}/settlement-amendment`, `POST /prt/complaints/{id}/resolution`, and `POST /prt/plans/{id}/termination-filing`.
14. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on `POST /prt/strategies/{id}/decision` and `POST /prt/independent-experts/{id}/independence-review`.
15. **Expert opinion required** — `ERR_EXPERT_OPINION_REQUIRED`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/insurer-selection`.
16. **Disclosed conflict** — `ERR_EXPERT_CONFLICT_DISCLOSED`, on `POST /prt/independent-experts/{id}/independence-review`.
17. **Carrier rating floor** — `ERR_INSURER_RATING_BELOW_FLOOR`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/quotes` and `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/pricing-lock`.
18. **Participant data cleanliness** — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_DATA_NOT_CLEAN`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/quoting`, `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/insurer-selection`, and `POST /prt/elections/{id}/election`.
19. **Mortality confirmation** — `ERR_MORTALITY_NOT_CONFIRMED`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/quoting` and `POST /prt/elections/{id}/election`.
20. **Cross-entity ownership coherence** — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_PLAN_MISMATCH` on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/participant-assignments`, and `ERR_QUOTE_TRANCHE_MISMATCH` on `POST /prt/participants/{id}/complaints`. `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/insurer-selection` accepts no quote reference of any kind, so no coherence check arises there and `ERR_QUOTE_TRANCHE_MISMATCH` is never raised by selection.
21. **Single-tranche membership** — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/participant-assignments`.
22. **Settled-participant precondition for complaints** — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_TRANSFERRED`, on `POST /prt/participants/{id}/complaints`.
23. **Window scheduling constraint** — `ERR_WINDOW_QUIET_PERIOD_CONFLICT`, on `POST /prt/plans/{id}/lump-sum-windows`.
24. **Duplicate solicitation** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUOTE_REQUEST`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/quotes`.
25. **Empty tranche** — `ERR_TRANCHE_EMPTY`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/quoting`.
26. **Empty candidate pool** — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_QUOTE`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/insurer-selection`.

32. **Carrier capacity** — `ERR_INSURER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/pricing-lock`.
33. **Transfer value shortfall** — `ERR_TRANSFER_VALUE_SHORTFALL`, on `POST /prt/tranches/{id}/asset-transfers`.
34. **Plan asset sufficiency** — `ERR_PLAN_ASSETS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402), on `POST /prt/lump-sum-windows/{id}/settlement`, `POST /prt/complaints/{id}/resolution`, and `POST /prt/plans/{id}/filing-review`.
35. **Pagination well-formedness** — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, on every list endpoint.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Plan Registration, Valuation and the Settlement Amendment

A `PlanSponsor` registers a defined benefit plan by supplying the sponsor's `sponsor_name`, the plan's `plan_name`, and its `plan_ein` — the sponsor's employer identification number, which must be exactly nine digits and must be unique across all registered plans; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and uniqueness is tested only after the nine-digit shape and every numeric range have passed. Both names may be between 1 and 200 characters. The sponsor also supplies the opening actuarial measurement: `total_liability_cents` for the projected benefit obligation on the accounting basis, `plan_assets_cents` for the fair value of plan assets, `service_cost_cents` and `interest_cost_cents` for the current period, `unrecognized_actuarial_loss_cents` for the balance sitting in accumulated other comprehensive income, and `discount_rate_bps` for the accounting discount rate in basis points, which must be between 1 and 2000 inclusive. All five monetary inputs must be at least 0. A new plan starts with a `plan_status` of `ONGOING`, has its `participant_liability_total_cents` set to 0 because it has no participants yet, and has its `accounting_funded_status_bp` derived immediately.

**Funded status** is the plan's headline measurement, and it is re-derived whenever assets or liabilities move. Its accounting figure, `accounting_funded_status_bp`, is 0 whenever `total_liability_cents` is 0; otherwise it is `plan_assets_cents` multiplied by 10,000 and rounded half up over `total_liability_cents`, so a fully funded plan reads 10000 basis points. Alongside it the system computes a settlement-basis view: `settlement_basis_liability_cents` is `total_liability_cents` multiplied by 10,700 basis points and rounded half up over 10,000 — that is, the accounting obligation uplifted by seven percent to reflect what a carrier would charge to take it on — and `settlement_funded_status_bp` is 0 when that uplifted liability is 0 and otherwise `plan_assets_cents` multiplied by 10,000 and rounded half up over `settlement_basis_liability_cents`. The `unfunded_settlement_gap_cents` is the amount by which `settlement_basis_liability_cents` exceeds `plan_assets_cents`, or 0 when assets already reach or exceed the uplifted liability. Finally `participant_liability_total_cents` is the sum of `accounting_liability_cents` across the plan's participant records whose `participant_status` is `IN_PLAN` or `ASSIGNED`, and is 0 when no participant qualifies. A funded-status read returns all of these together with the plan's `cumulative_settlement_charge_cents` and `deferred_settlement_charge_cents`, computes them fresh, and persists nothing.

Recording a later actuarial valuation overwrites the same six input figures — `total_liability_cents`, `plan_assets_cents`, `service_cost_cents`, `interest_cost_cents`, `unrecognized_actuarial_loss_cents`, and `discount_rate_bps` — and re-derives both `participant_liability_total_cents` and `accounting_funded_status_bp`. Either a `PlanSponsor` or an `Actuary` may record it, and because the write supersedes the prior measurement it carries `expected_plan_version` under the stale-version rule of §2.9. No `plan_status` precondition applies: a valuation may be recorded against a plan in any status, including a terminated one.

Settlement activity is not permitted merely because the sponsor wants it; the plan document must first be amended. Adopting the amendment moves the plan `ONGOING → SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED` and requires the sponsor to declare an `amendment_action` of `ADOPT`, an `amendment_scope` of `LUMP_SUM_ONLY`, `BUYOUT_ONLY`, or `BOTH`, and an `amendment_effective_date`. The scope must be backed by evidence that the fiduciary actually evaluated that lever: at least one de-risking strategy of the plan must be `APPROVED` and its `strategy_type` must belong to the set the requested scope maps to. A scope of `LUMP_SUM_ONLY` is backed by an approved `LUMP_SUM_WINDOW` or `HYBRID_WINDOW_AND_BUYOUT` strategy; a scope of `BUYOUT_ONLY` is backed by an approved `ANNUITY_BUYOUT` or `HYBRID_WINDOW_AND_BUYOUT` strategy; a scope of `BOTH` is backed only by an approved `HYBRID_WINDOW_AND_BUYOUT` strategy. If no approved strategy of a compatible type exists, the adoption is rejected with `ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_APPROVED`. The `amendment_effective_date` must be at or after the logical clock, and an earlier date is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; because the interval family outranks the own-state family, that check runs before the plan's own status is examined, and adoption from any status other than `ONGOING` is then rejected with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. On success the plan stores `amendment_scope` and `amendment_effective_date` from the payload — the date is parsed as a UTC instant and echoed back as a full ISO 8601 timestamp with three fractional digits — stamps `amendment_adopted_at` with the logical clock, and bumps the plan's `version`.

Rescission is the reverse move, `SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED → ONGOING`, requested with an `amendment_action` of `RESCIND`; a rescission needs neither `amendment_scope` nor `amendment_effective_date`, and ignores them when supplied. A rescission requested against a plan that is not `SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED` is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. It is reachable only while nothing is in flight. If any lump-sum window of the plan is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, the rescission is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`; if any buyout tranche of the plan is `QUOTING`, `INSURER_SELECTED`, `PRICING_LOCKED`, or `ASSETS_TRANSFERRED`, it is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_STATE_INVALID`. When it succeeds, `amendment_scope`, `amendment_adopted_at`, and `amendment_effective_date` are all cleared, and two cascades fire: every window of the plan still in `PLANNED` transitions to `VOIDED`, and every tranche of the plan still in `DRAFT` transitions to `ABANDONED`, stamping `abandoned_at` from the logical clock while leaving `abandonment_reason` null — which in turn returns each of that tranche's assigned participants from `ASSIGNED` to `IN_PLAN` with `tranche_id` cleared and each such participant's `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` recomputed. The rescinded tranche's own `assigned_participant_count`, liability figures and `data_readiness_flag` are left as they stand. Every entity touched by those cascades bumps its own `version`, as does the plan.

### 3.2. Evaluating the De-Risking Levers

An `Actuary` drafts a de-risking option by supplying a `strategy_name` — recorded on the resource as `strategy_label`, between 1 and 120 characters — and a `strategy_type` drawn from `LUMP_SUM_WINDOW`, `ANNUITY_BUYOUT`, `LDI_GLIDEPATH`, and `HYBRID_WINDOW_AND_BUYOUT`. A drafted strategy starts in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0, its `ldi_hedge_ratio_bp` taken from the optional `ldi_hedge_ratio_bp` of the payload and defaulting to 0 when omitted — the target liability-driven-investing hedge ratio in basis points, nominally between 0 and 10000, recorded for glidepath and hybrid options, and stored exactly as supplied because no range check is applied to it — and its `projected_accounting_cost_cents`, `projected_settlement_cost_cents`, `projected_basis_gap_cents`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, and `decision_rationale` all unset, with `in_scope_participant_count` at 0.

Submitting the strategy moves it `DRAFT → UNDER_EVALUATION` and is the moment the projection is priced. The strategy must itself be in `DRAFT`, otherwise the submission is rejected with `ERR_STRATEGY_STATE_INVALID`, and that check precedes the plan's. The plan must be in `ONGOING` or `SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED` status, otherwise the submission is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Submission records `submitted_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and bumps the strategy's `version`.

**The projected settlement cost** is computed twice, once on each measurement basis, from the same in-scope population. That population consists of every participant record of the strategy's plan whose `participant_status` is `IN_PLAN` or `ASSIGNED`, whose `mortality_status` is anything other than `DECEASED_CONFIRMED`, and whose `cohort` is one the strategy's type addresses. A `LUMP_SUM_WINDOW` strategy addresses only `DEFERRED_VESTED` participants; an `ANNUITY_BUYOUT` strategy addresses only `RETIREE_IN_PAY` participants; a `HYBRID_WINDOW_AND_BUYOUT` strategy addresses both cohorts; an `LDI_GLIDEPATH` strategy addresses neither cohort, so its in-scope population is always empty. The population is walked in `created_at` ascending order — the order does not change the total, but it pins the audit trail — and its size is stored as `in_scope_participant_count`.

For each participant in the population, and separately for each basis, the effective monthly benefit is multiplied by twelve to give an annual amount, that annual amount is multiplied by the basis factor for the participant's `cohort`, and the product is rounded half up over 10,000. On the accounting basis the factor is 118,000 basis points for a `RETIREE_IN_PAY` participant and 95,500 basis points for a `DEFERRED_VESTED` one — eleven-point-eight and nine-point-five-five times the annual benefit respectively. On the settlement basis the factor is 126,250 basis points for `RETIREE_IN_PAY` and 104,500 basis points for `DEFERRED_VESTED`, the uplift covering carrier profit, adverse selection and expenses. The per-participant amounts are rounded **before** they are summed; summing the exact products and rounding the total once produces a different integer and is explicitly not the contract. The base amount is the sum of those rounded per-participant amounts, and it is 0 when the population is empty.

On the settlement basis only, an expense load is then added, and it is applied **per participant** rather than to the base total: each participant's rounded settlement amount is multiplied by 350 basis points, that product is rounded half up over 10,000, and the load is added to that participant's own amount before the population is summed — an additional three and a half percent for the carrier's transaction expenses, charged record by record so that the rounding happens once per participant and not once on the total. On the accounting basis the expense load contributes nothing. The accounting total is stored in `projected_accounting_cost_cents`, the settlement total in `projected_settlement_cost_cents`, and `projected_basis_gap_cents` is the settlement total minus the accounting total — the de-risking premium the sponsor must fund out of its own resources.

A `PlanFiduciary` then records a decision, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, or `RETURN_FOR_REVISION` together with a `decision_rationale`. The strategy must be `UNDER_EVALUATION`, and the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `submitted_by_user_id`; an actuary who tries to approve their own submission is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval moves the strategy `UNDER_EVALUATION → APPROVED` and rejection moves it `UNDER_EVALUATION → REJECTED`; both record `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_rationale` and bump the `version`. Returning the strategy for revision moves it `UNDER_EVALUATION → DRAFT`, and carries the same segregation-of-duties requirement plus a bound on how many times the loop may run: `revision_count` must be below 2, and a third attempt to return the same strategy is rejected with `ERR_STRATEGY_STATE_INVALID`. A successful return increments `revision_count` by one, records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_rationale` exactly as the approve and reject branches do, clears `projected_accounting_cost_cents`, `projected_settlement_cost_cents`, and `projected_basis_gap_cents`, resets `in_scope_participant_count` to 0, clears `submitted_by_user_id` so the next submitter is captured afresh, and bumps the `version`. The `revision_count` bound is tested before the segregation-of-duties comparison, so a third return attempted by the original submitter surfaces as `ERR_STRATEGY_STATE_INVALID`. An approved strategy is finally moved `APPROVED → EXECUTED` by the system when the plan becomes residual, as described in §3.9.

### 3.3. Cleansing Participant Data: Locator Searches and Mortality Verification

A `PlanAdministrator` loads participants into the plan one at a time, supplying a `participant_reference` between 1 and 40 characters that must be unique within the plan — a reuse within the same plan is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, tested after the numeric ranges have passed — a `cohort` of either `RETIREE_IN_PAY` or `DEFERRED_VESTED`, the `stated_monthly_benefit_cents` from the sponsor's records (at least 1), the `final_average_monthly_earnings_cents` (at least 1), the `credited_service_months` (an integer from 0 through 600), and the participant's `date_of_birth`, which is a required member of the creation payload and is parsed as a UTC instant and echoed as a full ISO 8601 timestamp with three fractional digits. A participant's `ssn_last4` is optional at creation and, when recorded, holds exactly four digits of the tax identifier, anything else being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; both `date_of_birth` and `ssn_last4` are nullable on the stored record and form the identity data a locator or death-master match is run against. At creation, `audited_monthly_benefit_cents` is initialised to `stated_monthly_benefit_cents`, `tranche_id` is null, `date_of_death` is null, `confirmed_address_line1` and `confirmed_postal_code` are null, `address_search_tier` is `NONE`, `address_search_attempts` is 0, `benefit_audit_status` is `NOT_AUDITED`, and the three status dimensions sit at `IN_PLAN`, `UNVERIFIED`, and `UNCONFIRMED`. Both liability figures are derived immediately, and the plan's `participant_liability_total_cents` is recomputed.

**Per-participant liability** exists on both bases and is the operand every downstream aggregation consumes. The effective monthly amount is `audited_monthly_benefit_cents` when that is set, falling back to `stated_monthly_benefit_cents` when it is not. If the participant's `mortality_status` is `DECEASED_CONFIRMED`, both liability figures are 0 and no further arithmetic runs. Otherwise the effective monthly amount is multiplied by twelve, the annual amount is multiplied by the factor for the participant's `cohort`, and the product is rounded half up over 10,000. For `accounting_liability_cents` the factor is 118,000 basis points for `RETIREE_IN_PAY` and 95,500 for `DEFERRED_VESTED`; for `settlement_liability_cents` it is 126,250 for `RETIREE_IN_PAY` and 104,500 for `DEFERRED_VESTED`. Both figures are re-derived whenever the effective monthly amount, the `cohort`, or the `mortality_status` changes.

**The data-confidence score.** Every participant carries a `data_confidence_score` between 0 and 100 that starts from a perfect 100 and loses points for each unresolved defect, and a `data_status` derived from it. An `address_status` of `UNCONFIRMED` costs 40 points, while an `address_status` of `UNLOCATABLE` costs only 25 — an exhausted search is a documented outcome, an un-started one is not, so a participant whose locator ladder is exhausted can still reach 75 while one who has never been searched sits at 60. A `mortality_status` of `UNVERIFIED` or `DEATH_DISPUTED` costs 20 points. A `benefit_audit_status` of `NOT_AUDITED`, `AUDIT_OPEN`, `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, or `REOPENED` costs 15 points. The score is floored at 0, and `data_status` is `CLEAN` when the score is at least 80 and `NEEDS_REVIEW` otherwise. A freshly loaded participant therefore scores 25 and is `NEEDS_REVIEW`; a participant who is `CONFIRMED`, `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`, and `PASSED_CLOSED` scores 100; one who is `CONFIRMED`, `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`, and `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` scores 85 and is still `CLEAN`; and one who is `CONFIRMED` but `UNVERIFIED` with a closed audit sits at exactly 80, which is `CLEAN` because the boundary is inclusive. The score is recomputed on every mutation of `address_status`, `mortality_status`, `benefit_audit_status`, `date_of_birth`, or `ssn_last4`.

**Address cleansing** proceeds by recorded commercial locator attempts, each supplying a `search_result` of either `NO_MATCH` or `CONFIRMED_MATCH`. The ladder holds at most three attempts. Each recorded attempt increments `address_search_attempts` by one and sets `address_search_tier` to the tier corresponding to the new attempt count — `TIER_1` after the first, `TIER_2` after the second, `TIER_3` after the third — reflecting the escalation from cheap batch matching to premium skip tracing. A `NO_MATCH` recorded while fewer than two attempts have already been made leaves the participant in `UNCONFIRMED`, a self-loop that only advances the tier and bumps the participant's `version`. A `NO_MATCH` recorded when exactly two attempts already stand is the third and final one: it moves the participant `UNCONFIRMED → UNLOCATABLE`, sets `address_search_attempts` to 3 and `address_search_tier` to `TIER_3`, and recomputes `data_confidence_score` and `data_status`. Neither `NO_MATCH` branch ever re-evaluates a tranche's `data_readiness_flag`, so a tranche holding a participant who has just become `UNLOCATABLE` keeps the flag it had until some other transition recomputes it. A `CONFIRMED_MATCH` moves the participant `UNCONFIRMED → CONFIRMED`; it requires both `confirmed_address_line1` and `confirmed_postal_code` in the payload, and their absence is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It advances the tier, stores the two address fields, recomputes `data_confidence_score` and `data_status`, and — when the participant is currently assigned to a tranche whose `tranche_status` is `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, or `INSURER_SELECTED` — recomputes that tranche's `data_readiness_flag`. Any search attempted once three attempts already stand is rejected with `ERR_ADDRESS_SEARCH_TIER_EXHAUSTED`, and both `CONFIRMED` and `UNLOCATABLE` are terminal for this dimension. The exhaustion test is applied before the terminal-state test, so which of the two errors surfaces depends on the attempt count: a participant sitting at three attempts — necessarily `UNLOCATABLE`, or `CONFIRMED` on the third try — yields `ERR_ADDRESS_SEARCH_TIER_EXHAUSTED`, while a participant already `CONFIRMED` with fewer than three attempts standing yields `ERR_PARTICIPANT_STATE_INVALID`.

**Mortality verification** is a separate dimension driven by a `match_result` of `NO_MATCH`, `DEATH_MATCH`, `DISPUTE_FILED`, `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_ALIVE`, or `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_DECEASED`, each recorded against the death master file. Each outcome accepts only the source states named below, and one recorded from any other state is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_STATE_INVALID` after the payload shape has been checked. A `NO_MATCH` from either `UNVERIFIED` or `DEATH_DISPUTED` affirms life and lands the participant in `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`; it recomputes `data_confidence_score` and `data_status`, recomputes the assigned tranche's `data_readiness_flag` when that tranche is `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, or `INSURER_SELECTED`, and bumps the participant's `version`.

A `DEATH_MATCH` is the heaviest of these operations. It is accepted from `UNVERIFIED`, from `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`, or from `DEATH_DISPUTED`, and in every case lands the participant in `DECEASED_CONFIRMED` with an identical set of consequences. The payload must carry `date_of_death`, whose absence is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The recorded date of death is stored as a UTC instant and echoed as a full ISO 8601 timestamp with three fractional digits; every election of that participant whose `election_status` is `NOTICE_SENT`, `ELECTED_LUMP_SUM`, or `ELECTED_ANNUITY` transitions to `VOIDED`; `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` are recomputed; and if the participant is assigned to a tranche in `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, or `INSURER_SELECTED`, that tranche's `accounting_liability_cents` and `settlement_liability_cents` are recomputed and its `data_readiness_flag` is set to `BLOCKED`. Because a confirmed death zeroes both of the participant's own liability figures, the tranche's recomputed liabilities drop by exactly the departed participant's former contribution.

Deaths are sometimes wrong, and the dimension is cyclic to accommodate that. Filing a dispute moves the participant `DECEASED_CONFIRMED → DEATH_DISPUTED` and requires a non-empty `dispute_reason`, whose absence is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it recomputes `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` and bumps the `version`. Resolving the dispute in the participant's favour moves them `DEATH_DISPUTED → ALIVE_CONFIRMED`: `date_of_death` is cleared, `accounting_liability_cents` and `settlement_liability_cents` are recomputed — restoring exactly the non-zero amounts the death confirmation had zeroed, which makes the whole cycle compensating — `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` are recomputed, and the assigned tranche's liabilities and `data_readiness_flag` are recomputed when that tranche is `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, or `INSURER_SELECTED`. Resolving the dispute against the participant moves them `DEATH_DISPUTED → DECEASED_CONFIRMED`, recomputing `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` and bumping the `version` — and nothing else: unlike a `DEATH_MATCH` it leaves both liability figures at the non-zero amounts they carried while the dispute was live, voids no election, and touches no tranche. Note that this dimension has no terminal state: a record can move between confirmation and dispute indefinitely, and it evolves entirely independently of `participant_status` and `address_status` on the same row.

### 3.4. Auditing the Benefit Calculation

Sponsor records are the starting point, not the truth, so an `Actuary` opens a `BenefitAudit` to recompute a participant's monthly benefit independently. The audit request carries `expected_participant_version` under the stale-version rule of §2.9, an optional `audit_basis` of `PLAN_DOCUMENT`, `COLLECTIVE_AGREEMENT`, or `PRIOR_ADMINISTRATOR_FILE` (defaulting to `PLAN_DOCUMENT` when omitted, and naming the source document the recomputation was performed against), and optionally revised `final_average_monthly_earnings_cents` and `credited_service_months` figures to recompute from — the revised earnings figure must be at least 0 and the revised service figure within 0 through 600, else `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`, and each falls back to the participant's own stored figure when omitted. Opening an audit has no state precondition of its own: any participant, in any of its four state dimensions, may be audited again. Before the new audit exists, every non-superseded audit of that participant transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, each bumping its own `version`; this create-with-supersede pattern is exactly what the stale-participant guard protects. The new audit is assigned an `audit_sequence` equal to the participant's prior audit count plus one, a 1-based ordinal that serves as the deterministic tie-break whenever "the latest audit" must be identified, and it snapshots the participant's `stated_monthly_benefit_cents` as it stood at audit creation. It also carries a `resolution` field, null until the audit is closed, an `override_rationale` and a `reopened_by_complaint_id`, both likewise null at creation. The new audit's `audit_status` is `OPEN` at the instant of creation, and it immediately leaves that state within the same request by the fork described below; `OPEN` is therefore never observable to a client.

**The recomputation** first caps service: the `credited_service_months` are capped at 420 months — thirty-five years, the plan's maximum credited service — before any multiplication happens. The `recomputed_monthly_benefit_cents` is then the `final_average_monthly_earnings_cents` multiplied by an accrual rate of 150 basis points and by the capped service months, with that product rounded half up over 120,000 — the divisor being 10,000 basis points times the twelve months of a year, because the accrual rate is expressed per year of service while the result is a monthly benefit. The `variance_cents` is the participant's `stated_monthly_benefit_cents` minus the `recomputed_monthly_benefit_cents`, and may be negative when the sponsor was understating the benefit.

The audit then forks on the magnitude of that variance, and the two branches have deliberately different consequences. When the absolute value of `variance_cents` is at most 500 cents, the audit lands in `PASSED` — the bound is inclusive, so a variance of exactly five dollars in either direction passes — the participant's `audited_monthly_benefit_cents` is left untouched, the participant's `benefit_audit_status` becomes `AUDIT_OPEN` and stays there until the audit is resolved, and the participant's `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` are recomputed. When the absolute value of `variance_cents` exceeds 500 cents, the audit lands in `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` instead, the participant's `benefit_audit_status` is set to `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` are recomputed, and — the branch-specific extra hop — if the participant is assigned to a tranche whose `tranche_status` is `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, or `INSURER_SELECTED`, that tranche's `data_readiness_flag` is set to `BLOCKED`.

Resolving an audit requires the actuary to state a `resolution` of `APPLY_CORRECTION` or `ACCEPT_AS_IS`, and the accepted resolution is stored on the audit in a `resolution` field. Only five pairings of resolution and current status are accepted: `ACCEPT_AS_IS` from `PASSED`, `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` or `REOPENED`, and `APPLY_CORRECTION` from `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` or `REOPENED`. Every other pairing — `APPLY_CORRECTION` against a `PASSED` audit, and either resolution against an audit already `PASSED_CLOSED`, `CORRECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` — is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID`. Accepting a `PASSED` audit as it stands moves it `PASSED → PASSED_CLOSED`: the participant's `audited_monthly_benefit_cents` is set to the audit's `stated_monthly_benefit_cents`, the participant's `benefit_audit_status` becomes `PASSED_CLOSED`, and the participant's `accounting_liability_cents`, `settlement_liability_cents`, `data_confidence_score`, and `data_status` are all recomputed before the audit's `version` is bumped. Applying the correction to an audit in `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` moves it `CORRECTION_REQUIRED → CORRECTED`: the participant's `audited_monthly_benefit_cents` is set to the audit's `recomputed_monthly_benefit_cents`, `benefit_audit_status` becomes `CORRECTED`, both liability figures and the confidence fields are recomputed, and — when the participant is assigned to a tranche in `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, or `INSURER_SELECTED` — that tranche's `accounting_liability_cents`, `settlement_liability_cents`, and `data_readiness_flag` are recomputed too. Accepting a `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` audit as it stands is also permitted but is a deliberate override: it moves the audit `CORRECTION_REQUIRED → PASSED_CLOSED` and demands a non-empty `override_rationale`, whose absence is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That branch sets `audited_monthly_benefit_cents` to the stated figure, sets `benefit_audit_status` to `PASSED_CLOSED`, and recomputes both liabilities plus `data_confidence_score` and `data_status`. Neither `PASSED_CLOSED` branch reaches any tranche: only the `CORRECTED` branch recomputes the assigned tranche's liabilities and readiness, so a tranche whose participant has just been closed out as `PASSED_CLOSED` keeps its previous `data_readiness_flag` and liability figures until something else recomputes them.

Audits can also be reopened after the fact by complaint triage, as §3.9 describes. An audit in `PASSED`, `PASSED_CLOSED`, `CORRECTED`, or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` — provided it is the participant's latest non-superseded audit, selected by the highest `audit_sequence` — transitions to `REOPENED`, records the triggering complaint's identifier in `reopened_by_complaint_id`, sets the participant's `benefit_audit_status` to `REOPENED`, and recomputes the participant's `data_confidence_score` and `data_status`. A `CORRECTED` audit reopened this way carries an identical set of consequences, and an audit already sitting in `REOPENED` is simply re-stamped with the new complaint's identifier and bumps its `version` again. From `REOPENED` the actuary may apply the correction again, moving the audit `REOPENED → CORRECTED` with exactly the same consequences as the correction applied from `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, or accept as is with a non-empty `override_rationale`, moving it `REOPENED → PASSED_CLOSED` with the same consequences as the override from `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`. The `SUPERSEDED` status is terminal, and the audit history read exposes superseded audits alongside live ones so that the whole audit trail remains inspectable.

### 3.5. Scheduling a Lump-Sum Window and Fanning Out Notices

A `PlanAdministrator` schedules an election window by supplying a `window_name` — recorded as `window_label`, between 1 and 120 characters — the `opens_at` and `closes_at` bounds of the half-open election interval, the `lump_sum_cap_cents` giving the aggregate cash the sponsor will release through the window, and the `eligibility_max_lump_sum_cents` giving the per-participant ceiling above which no notice is sent. Both amounts must be at least 1, else `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`, and `closes_at` must be strictly after `opens_at`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` — the amounts being checked first. A window starts in `PLANNED` with `notice_count` at 0, `approved_lump_sum_total_cents` and `paid_lump_sum_total_cents` at 0, and `opened_at`, `closed_at`, and `settled_at` all null.

Windows cannot be stacked on top of each other. A proposed window's half-open interval must not overlap the interval of any other window of the same plan that is not `VOIDED`, and it must additionally clear a quiet period of 90 days measured after the `closes_at` of every such window — three months of silence between offers, so that participants are not pressured by back-to-back deadlines. The quiet-period test is one-directional and unconditional: the proposed `opens_at` must be at or after every other live window's `closes_at` plus 90 days, so a proposal that sits entirely *before* an existing window conflicts just as surely as one that crowds it from behind. Either failure is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_QUIET_PERIOD_CONFLICT`.

Opening the window moves it `PLANNED → OPEN` and is gated on the plan's own authorization. The three gates are evaluated in the §2.10 order rather than the order they are described here: the 30-day notice runway is tested first, then the window's own state and the clock's position inside the half-open interval, and the plan's authorization last. The plan must be in `SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED` status and its `amendment_scope` must be `LUMP_SUM_ONLY` or `BOTH`; either failure is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED`. The logical clock must be at or after `opens_at` and strictly before `closes_at`, and a clock outside that half-open interval is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. Finally, the gap between the logical clock and `closes_at` must be at least 30 days, so that participants have a full statutory month to consider the offer; a shorter runway is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`.

Opening stamps `opened_at` with the logical clock and then fans out notices. One election is created for every participant of the window's plan who satisfies all four of these predicates simultaneously: `participant_status` is `IN_PLAN`, `mortality_status` is `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`, `address_status` is `CONFIRMED`, and the participant's raw present value is at most the window's `eligibility_max_lump_sum_cents`. Participants failing any predicate are skipped silently — no error, no election. Each created election starts in `NOTICE_SENT`, records `notice_sent_at` and its `window_id` and `participant_id`, and stores its `notice_estimated_lump_sum_cents` as the raw present value computed at notice time. The number of elections created is stored in the window's `notice_count`, and the window's `version` is bumped.

**The lump-sum present value** is computed in four steps, and it is computed twice in the lifetime of an election: once at notice time to fill `notice_estimated_lump_sum_cents` and test eligibility, and again at election time to fill the amounts that will actually be paid.

First, the raw present value. The effective monthly amount is the participant's `audited_monthly_benefit_cents` when set, falling back to `stated_monthly_benefit_cents`. It is multiplied by twelve to give an annual amount, that annual amount is multiplied by the lump-sum factor for the participant's `cohort` — 121,000 basis points for `RETIREE_IN_PAY` and 100,000 basis points for `DEFERRED_VESTED`, meaning a deferred-vested participant's raw present value is exactly 120 times the monthly benefit — and the product is rounded half up over 10,000 to give `raw_pv_cents`.

Second, the administrative fee tier, keyed strictly on that **pre-fee** `raw_pv_cents` and never on the net amount. A `raw_pv_cents` of at most 2,400,000 (twenty-four thousand dollars) sets `admin_fee_bp` to 0. A `raw_pv_cents` above 2,400,000 and at most 12,000,000 sets `admin_fee_bp` to 75. A `raw_pv_cents` above 12,000,000 sets `admin_fee_bp` to 40. Both tier ceilings are inclusive. This ladder is deliberately non-monotonic — the largest offers are charged the *lower* rate of 40 basis points, because the administrative work does not scale with the size of the cheque — so keying the tier off a post-fee amount would push a large offer down into the 75-basis-point band and overcharge it.

Third, the fee itself. The uncapped fee is `raw_pv_cents` multiplied by `admin_fee_bp` and rounded half up over 10,000, and `admin_fee_cents` is that uncapped fee or 500,000 cents — a five-thousand-dollar ceiling — whichever is smaller.

Fourth, `net_lump_sum_cents` is `raw_pv_cents` minus `admin_fee_cents`. That net figure is what the cap waterfall allocates and what the participant is actually paid.

### 3.6. Participant Elections and Revocation

A `Participant` responds to a notice by supplying an `election_choice` of either `LUMP_SUM` or `ANNUITY`. Both choices share the same gates, and they are evaluated in the §2.10 order, which puts the audit-status gate ahead of the mortality gate rather than in the order they are narrated here. The election must currently be in `NOTICE_SENT`, otherwise `ERR_ELECTION_STATE_INVALID`; the window must be `OPEN` and the logical clock strictly before the window's `closes_at`, with either failure rejected as `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`; the participant's `benefit_audit_status` must be `PASSED_CLOSED` or `CORRECTED`, otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_DATA_NOT_CLEAN` — nobody elects against an unaudited benefit figure; and the participant's `mortality_status` must be `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_MORTALITY_NOT_CONFIRMED`. The participant's raw present value must also remain at or below the window's `eligibility_max_lump_sum_cents`, and an offer that has grown past the ceiling since the notice went out is rejected with `ERR_LUMP_SUM_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

Choosing the lump sum moves the election `NOTICE_SENT → ELECTED_LUMP_SUM`, stamps `elected_at` with the logical clock, records `election_choice`, and prices the offer immediately, storing `raw_pv_cents`, `admin_fee_bp`, `admin_fee_cents`, and `net_lump_sum_cents`. Choosing the annuity moves the election `NOTICE_SENT → ELECTED_ANNUITY` and likewise stamps `elected_at` and records `election_choice`, but no pricing runs: `raw_pv_cents`, `admin_fee_bp`, `admin_fee_cents`, and `net_lump_sum_cents` all stay null, because the participant is keeping their annuity and staying in the plan. Both branches bump the election's `version`. An annuity election is terminal for the election but not for the participant, who remains `IN_PLAN` and may still be handed to a carrier in a later buyout.

Participants get one chance to change their mind. A revocation returns an election from either `ELECTED_LUMP_SUM` or `ELECTED_ANNUITY` back to `NOTICE_SENT`, with identical preconditions and identical consequences in both directions. The window must still be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The logical clock must be at or before seven days after `elected_at` and strictly before the window's `closes_at`; a late attempt is rejected with `ERR_REVOCATION_PERIOD_ELAPSED`. And `revocation_count` must be below 1, so a second revocation of the same election is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_STATE_INVALID` — a state-family rejection, tested together with the election's own status and therefore *before* the window-open and revocation-period gates. A successful revocation clears `elected_at`, `election_choice`, `raw_pv_cents`, `admin_fee_bp`, `admin_fee_cents`, and `net_lump_sum_cents`, increments `revocation_count` by one, and bumps the `version` — leaving the participant free to elect again from a clean slate within the same window.

### 3.7. Closing the Window: Cap Allocation and Disbursement

Closing a window moves it `OPEN → CLOSED` and may only be requested once the logical clock has reached `closes_at`; an early attempt is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. Closing does three things in order. First, every election of the window still sitting in `NOTICE_SENT` transitions to `DEFAULTED_NO_RESPONSE` — silence is a decision, and the participant keeps their annuity. Second, the cash cap is allocated. Third, `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the window's `version` is bumped.

**The cap waterfall** visits every election of the window whose `election_status` is `ELECTED_LUMP_SUM`, in `elected_at` ascending order, ties broken by the election's participant's `created_at` ascending and then by the election's own `created_at` ascending; the elections' identifiers are never used as an ordering key. A running honoured total starts at 0. For each election in turn, the running total plus that election's `net_lump_sum_cents` is compared against the window's `lump_sum_cap_cents`. If the candidate total is at most the cap, the election stays `ELECTED_LUMP_SUM` and the running total becomes the candidate total. If the candidate total exceeds the cap, the election transitions `ELECTED_LUMP_SUM → CAP_DEFERRED`, the running total is left unchanged, and **the walk continues to the next election** — it does not stop. That continuation is the whole point: a large election that breaches the cap is deferred, and a later, smaller election can still be honoured out of the headroom the deferral preserved. Each deferred election bumps its own `version`. When the walk finishes, the running total is stored as `approved_lump_sum_total_cents`. An empty set of lump-sum elections yields an approved total of 0, and the window still closes and still settles.

Settlement is the `PlanSponsor`'s act, moving the window `CLOSED → SETTLED`, and it re-checks the cap before releasing any money. The re-verification re-prices every election of the window still in `ELECTED_LUMP_SUM` from each participant's **current** `audited_monthly_benefit_cents`, and the resulting total must be at most `lump_sum_cap_cents`; if it is not, the settlement is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_CAP_EXCEEDED`. This is not a redundant check: a benefit correction recorded after the window closed can lift an elected participant's net lump sum above the total the waterfall approved at closing. The re-pricing is read-only — it never overwrites the elections' stored `net_lump_sum_cents`, so each honoured election is still disbursed the figure recorded when it was elected. The plan must also hold enough cash, with `plan_assets_cents` at least `approved_lump_sum_total_cents`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_ASSETS_INSUFFICIENT`.

A successful window settlement cascades through four hops. In the first hop, every election of the window still in `ELECTED_LUMP_SUM` transitions to `PAID`, with `paid_at` stamped from the logical clock and `paid_amount_cents` set to that election's `net_lump_sum_cents`. In the second hop, because those elections became `PAID`, each corresponding participant transitions `IN_PLAN → SETTLED_LUMP_SUM` and has its `tranche_id` cleared. In the third hop, because those participants have left the `IN_PLAN` and `ASSIGNED` population, the plan's `total_liability_cents` is reduced by the sum of the paid participants' `accounting_liability_cents`, the plan's `plan_assets_cents` is reduced by `paid_lump_sum_total_cents`, and the plan's `participant_liability_total_cents` and `accounting_funded_status_bp` are recomputed. In the fourth hop, because the plan's liability changed and every window and tranche of the plan may now be terminal, the plan is re-evaluated for the residual transition described in §3.9 and moves `SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED → RESIDUAL` when that transition's preconditions hold.

Alongside the cascade, one settlement accounting entry is appended with an `entry_type` of `LUMP_SUM_RECOGNITION` and its `window_id` set. Its `pbo_settled_cents` is the sum of the paid participants' `accounting_liability_cents`, its `amount_cents` is `paid_lump_sum_total_cents` minus that `pbo_settled_cents`, its `gain_loss_cents` repeats that same difference, and its `deferred_amount_cents` is 0 — a lump-sum disbursement is recognised immediately and nothing is held back in accumulated other comprehensive income. Its `source_reference` reads `LumpSumWindow CLOSED -> SETTLED` and its `tranche_id` stays null. The window itself stores `paid_lump_sum_total_cents` as the cash actually disbursed, stamps `settled_at`, and bumps its `version`. A window still in `PLANNED` when the sponsor rescinds the settlement amendment instead transitions `PLANNED → VOIDED`, bumping its `version` and nothing more.

### 3.8. The Annuity Buyout: Carriers, Tranches, Selection and Funding

**The carrier registry** is the `Regulator`'s to keep. Registering an admitted carrier records its `insurer_name` (1 to 200 characters, unique across the registry), its `rating_grade` from the set `AAA`, `AA_PLUS`, `AA`, `AA_MINUS`, `A_PLUS`, `A`, and `BBB`, its `capital_ratio_bp` risk-based capital ratio in basis points between 0 and 20000, and its `capacity_cents` — the total premium volume the carrier is willing to write, at least 1. A duplicate `insurer_name` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because the numeric bounds are checked first a registration that is both out of range and duplicated surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`. A `state_guaranty_coverage_cents` figure records the per-participant state guaranty association coverage for the fiduciary's file, is optional and defaults to 0 when omitted, and must be at least 0. A newly registered carrier has `insurer_status` `ACTIVE`, `committed_capacity_cents` 0, `available_capacity_cents` equal to its `capacity_cents`, and `rating_changed_at` null.

Rating grades are not decorative; they map to a points scale that the safest-available standard is expressed in. `AAA` is worth 100 points, `AA_PLUS` 92, `AA` 85, `AA_MINUS` 78, `A_PLUS` 70, `A` 60, and `BBB` 40. The floor for participating in a pension risk transfer is 78 points, which is to say `AA_MINUS` or better — a carrier at `A_PLUS` or below is out of scope no matter how attractive its price.

A rating action supplies a new `rating_grade`, optionally a revised `capital_ratio_bp` — bounded, when supplied, by the same 0 through 20000 range as at registration — and optionally a new `insurer_status` of `ACTIVE`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `WITHDRAWN`; either optional field simply leaves the stored value alone when it is omitted, and the registry standing is moved only by these regulator actions. The action stamps `rating_changed_at`, bumps the carrier's `version`, and cascades when the new grade maps below 78 points. Every quote of that carrier whose `quote_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED` transitions to `INELIGIBLE`, bumping each quote's `version`. Where the ineligible quote was `SHORTLISTED` — that is, where it is still named as some tranche's `selected_quote_id` and that tranche is still in `INSURER_SELECTED` — that tranche falls back `INSURER_SELECTED → QUOTING`: its `selected_quote_id`, `selected_insurer_id`, `selection_score_bp`, and `selection_basis` are all cleared, its `data_readiness_flag` is forced to `BLOCKED` without re-deriving it from the population, its `assigned_participant_count` and both liability figures are left untouched, and its `version` is bumped. A downgrade therefore un-picks a carrier mid-process rather than letting an imprudent purchase proceed.

**Assembling a tranche.** A `PlanFiduciary` drafts a buyout tranche by supplying a `tranche_name` — stored as `tranche_label`, 1 to 120 characters — and a `target_cohort` of `RETIREE_IN_PAY` or `DEFERRED_VESTED`. A drafted tranche starts in `DRAFT` with `data_readiness_flag` `BLOCKED`, `assigned_participant_count` 0, both `accounting_liability_cents` and `settlement_liability_cents` 0, `transfer_expense_cents` 0, and `selection_basis`, `selected_quote_id`, `selected_insurer_id`, `selection_score_bp`, `locked_premium_cents`, `lock_start_at`, `lock_expires_at`, `settlement_gain_loss_cents`, `settled_at`, `abandoned_at`, and `abandonment_reason` all null.

A `PlanAdministrator` then assigns participants one at a time by supplying a `participant_id`, moving that participant `IN_PLAN → ASSIGNED` and setting its `tranche_id`. A participant who is not `IN_PLAN` — because they already belong to a tranche or have already settled — is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`. A participant whose plan differs from the tranche's plan, or whose `cohort` differs from the tranche's `target_cohort`, is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_PLAN_MISMATCH`. The tranche must still be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_TRANCHE_STATE_INVALID`. A successful assignment recomputes the tranche's `assigned_participant_count`, `accounting_liability_cents`, `settlement_liability_cents`, and `data_readiness_flag`, and bumps both the participant's and the tranche's `version`; the response body is the updated tranche, not the participant. The three rejections are ordered by §2.10 rather than by the order they are narrated here, so the tranche's own `DRAFT` requirement is tested first, then the plan-and-cohort coherence, and the single-tranche membership rule last.

**Tranche liability and readiness** are always derived from the tranche's currently assigned population — every participant whose `tranche_id` is this tranche and whose `participant_status` is `ASSIGNED`. The tranche's `accounting_liability_cents` is the sum of those participants' `accounting_liability_cents` and its `settlement_liability_cents` is the sum of their `settlement_liability_cents`, both 0 when the population is empty; because the per-participant amounts are already rounded, no further rounding happens at the tranche level. The `assigned_participant_count` is the size of that population, except that participants who have already moved on to `TRANSFERRED_TO_INSURER` continue to be counted as belonging to the tranche. The `data_readiness_flag` is `BLOCKED` if the population is empty, `BLOCKED` if any assigned participant's `data_status` is anything other than `CLEAN`, `BLOCKED` if any assigned participant's `mortality_status` is anything other than `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`, `BLOCKED` if any assigned participant's `benefit_audit_status` is outside `PASSED_CLOSED` and `CORRECTED`, and `READY` only when none of those conditions holds. The stored copies of these figures are refreshed only by the operations that say they recompute them; in particular the tranche settlement below leaves `assigned_participant_count`, both liability figures and `data_readiness_flag` at the values they held before its participants moved on to `TRANSFERRED_TO_INSURER`.

**Engaging an independent expert** is a prerequisite for going out for quote. A `PlanSponsor` proposes an expert by supplying an `expert_firm_name` between 1 and 200 characters, an `engagement_scope` describing what the firm is retained to opine on, and an optional `conflict_disclosed` boolean that defaults to false and states whether the firm disclosed a relationship with any candidate carrier. The proposal starts in `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW`, records `proposed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and leaves `reviewed_by_user_id`, `safest_available_opinion`, and `opinion_issued_at` null. A `PlanFiduciary` then supplies a `review_decision` of `ENGAGE` or `DISQUALIFY`. Either decision requires the engagement to be in `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_EXPERT_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the reviewing `X-User-ID` to differ from `proposed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An `ENGAGE` decision additionally requires `conflict_disclosed` to be false; a firm that disclosed a conflict cannot be engaged and is rejected with `ERR_EXPERT_CONFLICT_DISCLOSED`. `ENGAGE` moves the engagement `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW → ENGAGED` and `DISQUALIFY` moves it `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW → DISQUALIFIED`; both record `reviewed_by_user_id` and bump the `version`, and both `ENGAGED` and `DISQUALIFIED` are reached with equal finality — `DISQUALIFIED` is terminal.

The engaged firm itself, acting as `IndependentExpertUser`, then issues its opinion by supplying a `safest_available_opinion` of `AFFIRMATIVE`, `QUALIFIED`, or `ADVERSE`. The engagement must be in `ENGAGED`, otherwise `ERR_EXPERT_NOT_ENGAGED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be this engagement's own identifier, otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Issuing the opinion moves the engagement `ENGAGED → OPINION_ISSUED`, stores the opinion, stamps `opinion_issued_at` from the logical clock, and bumps the `version`.

**Going out for quote** moves a tranche `DRAFT → QUOTING` and is the densest gate in the system. The plan must be `SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED` with an `amendment_scope` of `BUYOUT_ONLY` or `BOTH`, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED`. The tranche must hold at least one assigned participant, else `ERR_TRANCHE_EMPTY`. Every assigned participant must have `mortality_status` `ALIVE_CONFIRMED`, else `ERR_MORTALITY_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and `data_status` `CLEAN`, else `ERR_PARTICIPANT_DATA_NOT_CLEAN`. And at least one independent expert of the plan must be in `ENGAGED` or `OPINION_ISSUED`, else `ERR_EXPERT_STATE_INVALID` — a gate evaluated last of all, after the empty-tranche test, despite the state-shaped error it raises. The tranche's own `DRAFT` requirement is checked before any of them, with `ERR_TRANCHE_STATE_INVALID` for a tranche that has already moved on. On success the tranche's `accounting_liability_cents` and `settlement_liability_cents` are recomputed over the assigned population, its `data_readiness_flag` is set to `READY`, and its `version` is bumped.

**Soliciting quotes.** With the tranche out for quote, the fiduciary requests a quote from a named carrier by supplying an `insurer_id`. A carrier whose live `rating_grade` maps below 78 points, or whose `insurer_status` is anything other than `ACTIVE`, may not be solicited at all and is rejected with `ERR_INSURER_RATING_BELOW_FLOOR`. A carrier that already holds a non-terminal quote on this tranche — one whose `quote_status` is `REQUESTED`, `SUBMITTED`, `SHORTLISTED`, `LOCKED`, or `BOUND` — may not be solicited a second time, and the duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUOTE_REQUEST`. A created quote starts in `REQUESTED` with `requested_at` stamped from the logical clock, records its `tranche_id` and `insurer_id`, leaves `premium_cents`, `lock_days`, `quote_valid_days`, `submitted_at`, `expires_at`, `rating_at_submission`, `composite_score_bp`, and `decline_reason` null, and bumps the tranche's `version`.

The carrier's own representative answers, supplying a `submission_decision` of `SUBMIT` or `DECLINE`. Either answer requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the quote's `insurer_id`, otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, and requires the quote to still be in `REQUESTED`. The status error for a quote that has moved on is deliberately split: a quote that is still live but no longer awaiting an answer — `SUBMITTED`, `SHORTLISTED`, or `LOCKED` — yields `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_REQUESTED`, whereas a quote that has already reached a terminal status of `DECLINED`, `INELIGIBLE`, `EXPIRED`, or `BOUND` yields `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`.

A `SUBMIT` moves the quote `REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and requires `premium_cents`, `lock_days`, and `quote_valid_days` to all be present, their absence being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `premium_cents` is the single premium quoted for the whole tranche and must be at least 1; `lock_days` is the number of days the carrier will hold its pricing once locked and must be between 1 and 180; `quote_valid_days` is the number of days the submitted quote remains selectable and must be between 1 and 90. Submission stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, sets `expires_at` to `submitted_at` plus `quote_valid_days` days, snapshots the carrier's current `rating_grade` into `rating_at_submission` for the fiduciary record, and bumps the quote's `version`. A `DECLINE` moves the quote `REQUESTED → DECLINED` instead and requires a non-empty `decline_reason`, whose absence is likewise rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it records the reason and bumps the `version`.

**Selecting the carrier** moves the tranche `QUOTING → INSURER_SELECTED` and is where the safest-available standard bites; a tranche in any other status is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_STATE_INVALID` before the gates below are consulted. At least one independent expert of the plan must be in `OPINION_ISSUED` with a `safest_available_opinion` of `AFFIRMATIVE`; a qualified or adverse opinion, or no opinion at all, is rejected with `ERR_EXPERT_OPINION_REQUIRED`. Every assigned participant must have a `benefit_audit_status` of `PASSED_CLOSED` or `CORRECTED`, else `ERR_PARTICIPANT_DATA_NOT_CLEAN`. The request names no quote at all — the pool is drawn from the tranche's own quotes alone, so a quote belonging to a different tranche can never enter it and `ERR_QUOTE_TRANCHE_MISMATCH` is not raised here. And the candidate pool must be non-empty, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_QUOTE` and no state changes at all — an empty pool is a rejection, not a zero score.

The candidate pool consists of every quote of this tranche whose `quote_status` is `SUBMITTED`, whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, whose `rating_at_submission` maps to at least 78 points, and whose carrier's `insurer_status` is `ACTIVE`. The fiduciary declares a `selection_basis` of either `SAFETY_WEIGHTED` or `PRICE_WEIGHTED`, and the two bases can name different winners on identical data.

Scoring proceeds as follows. The lowest `premium_cents` across the candidate pool is identified first. For each candidate quote, three component scores on a 0-to-100 scale are computed: its rating points, taken from the points value of its `rating_at_submission`; its capital points, being the carrier's `capital_ratio_bp` divided by 100 with the remainder discarded, then capped at 100; and its price points, being the lowest premium in the pool multiplied by 100 and divided by this quote's own `premium_cents` with the remainder discarded, which gives 100 to the cheapest quote and proportionally less to dearer ones. The `composite_score_bp` is then the rating points multiplied by the rating weight, plus the capital points multiplied by the capital weight, plus the price points multiplied by the price weight. Under `SAFETY_WEIGHTED` the weights are 55 for rating, 25 for capital, and 20 for price — claims-paying strength dominates. Under `PRICE_WEIGHTED` they are 25 for rating, 15 for capital, and 60 for price — premium dominates, though the same 78-point rating floor still governs who is even in the pool. Because each weight is a percentage, the weighted sum is already expressed in basis points of a 0-to-10000 scale and no rounding is performed or needed.

The winner is the candidate with the highest `composite_score_bp`; ties are broken by the lower `premium_cents`, then by the higher rating points, then by the earlier `submitted_at`, then by the earlier `created_at`, and finally by insertion order. Selection stores the declared `selection_basis`, writes every candidate quote's `composite_score_bp`, and records the winner's identifiers and score in the tranche's `selected_quote_id`, `selected_insurer_id`, and `selection_score_bp`. The winning quote transitions `SUBMITTED → SHORTLISTED`; every other quote of the tranche still in `SUBMITTED` transitions to `DECLINED`, carrying its `composite_score_bp` when it was in the candidate pool. Each quote bumps its own `version`, as does the tranche.

**Locking the price** moves the tranche `INSURER_SELECTED → PRICING_LOCKED` and is where carrier capacity is actually consumed. The tranche must be in `INSURER_SELECTED`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_SELECTED` — as is a tranche whose `selected_quote_id` or `selected_insurer_id` no longer resolves. The selected carrier's **live** `rating_grade` — not the `rating_at_submission` snapshot — must map to at least 78 points, else `ERR_INSURER_RATING_BELOW_FLOOR`; a carrier downgraded between submission and lock is caught here even if the cascade has not reached the quote, and this rating test precedes the expiry test. The selected quote's `expires_at` must then be strictly after the logical clock, else `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Finally the premium must fit inside the carrier's remaining capacity over the candidate lock interval, which runs as a closed interval from the logical clock through the logical clock plus the quote's `lock_days` days.

**Committed capacity** is an aggregate, not a pairwise test. For a candidate interval, the carrier's `committed_capacity_cents` is the sum of `locked_premium_cents` across every tranche whose `selected_insurer_id` is this carrier, whose `tranche_status` is `PRICING_LOCKED`, `ASSETS_TRANSFERRED`, or `SETTLED`, and whose own closed lock interval intersects the candidate interval — that is, whose `lock_start_at` is at or before the candidate interval's end and whose `lock_expires_at` is at or after the candidate interval's start. Because both intervals are closed, two locks that touch at a single instant do intersect and their premiums are added together. The tranche being evaluated is excluded from its own aggregate. The carrier's `available_capacity_cents` is its `capacity_cents` minus that committed sum, and it equals the full `capacity_cents` when no lock intersects. The premium is admitted only when it is at most `available_capacity_cents`; otherwise the lock is rejected with `ERR_INSURER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A successful lock sets the tranche's `locked_premium_cents` to the selected quote's `premium_cents`, sets `lock_start_at` to the logical clock and `lock_expires_at` to `lock_start_at` plus the quote's `lock_days` days, moves the selected quote `SHORTLISTED → LOCKED`, recomputes the carrier's `committed_capacity_cents` and `available_capacity_cents` — over the interval just locked, and this time including the newly locked tranche in the aggregate rather than excluding it — and bumps the tranche's, the quote's, and the carrier's `version`. Because the stored carrier figures are always the aggregate over the most recently evaluated interval, they are a snapshot of that interval rather than a running lifetime total.

**Funding the tranche** is the `PlanSponsor`'s act. The sponsor initiates a delivery by supplying `expected_tranche_version` under the stale-version rule of §2.9, a `transfer_mode` of `IN_KIND` or `CASH`, the `gross_value_cents` being delivered, and — for an in-kind delivery — a `security_lot_count`. An in-kind delivery stores its gross figure in `in_kind_market_value_cents` and its lot count in `in_kind_lot_count`, which must be between 1 and 500; a cash delivery stores its gross figure in `cash_amount_cents`, is not required to supply a lot count at all, and has its `in_kind_lot_count` stored as 0. Whichever mode is used, the other mode's amount field stays null. Either way the delivered amount must be at least 1 and is mirrored into `gross_value_cents`, and the tranche must be in `PRICING_LOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANCHE_STATE_INVALID`. Any delivery still awaiting the carrier's answer on the same tranche transitions `INITIATED → SUPERSEDED` before the new one is created, bumping its own `version`; that supersede happens only once the shortfall test below has been passed, so a rejected delivery attempt leaves the in-flight delivery untouched in `INITIATED`.

**Credited value** is what the carrier will actually count against the locked premium, and the two funding modes are penalised quite differently. An in-kind delivery suffers two frictions: a valuation haircut of 125 basis points applied to `gross_value_cents` and rounded half up over 10,000, plus a per-lot custody fee of 25,000 cents for each security lot delivered. Its `transfer_expense_cents` is the haircut plus the custody fees. A cash delivery suffers only a wire fee of 8 basis points applied to `gross_value_cents` and rounded half up over 10,000, and its `transfer_expense_cents` is that wire fee alone — no haircut, no per-lot component. In both modes `credited_value_cents` is `gross_value_cents` minus `transfer_expense_cents`, and `shortfall_cents` is the amount by which the tranche's `locked_premium_cents` exceeds `credited_value_cents`, or 0 when the credited value reaches or passes the premium. A shortfall above 0 is fatal: the request is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_VALUE_SHORTFALL` and no delivery record is created at all. A delivery that survives therefore always stores `shortfall_cents` as 0, starts in `INITIATED` with `insurer_decision`, `rejection_reason`, and `settled_at` null, and bumps the tranche's `version`; the created delivery is the response body.

The carrier's representative reviews the delivery, supplying an `insurer_decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. Either answer requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the tranche's `selected_insurer_id`, otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, and requires the delivery to be in `INITIATED`. Here too the status error is split: a delivery that has already been answered, being `SETTLED` or `REJECTED`, yields `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_INITIATED`, while a delivery that was `SUPERSEDED` by a later delivery on the same tranche yields `ERR_TRANSFER_STATE_INVALID`. An `ACCEPT` additionally requires the logical clock to be at or before the tranche's `lock_expires_at`, since the carrier cannot honour pricing it no longer holds; a late acceptance is rejected with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`.

The two answers diverge sharply. An `ACCEPT` moves the delivery `INITIATED → SETTLED`, stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock, and cascades: the tranche transitions `PRICING_LOCKED → ASSETS_TRANSFERRED` and copies this delivery's `transfer_expense_cents` onto its own `transfer_expense_cents`, bumping the tranche's `version` as well as the delivery's. A `REJECT` moves the delivery `INITIATED → REJECTED`, requires a non-empty `rejection_reason` whose absence is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, records that reason, and bumps only the delivery's `version` — the tranche stays in `PRICING_LOCKED`, consumes no additional capacity, and the sponsor may initiate a replacement delivery against the same lock. Either way the response body is the delivery record, never the tranche, even on the accept path where the tranche moves too.

**Recognising the buyout** is the final sponsor act on a tranche, moving it `ASSETS_TRANSFERRED → SETTLED`. Any other starting status is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_TRANSFERRED`.

The settlement accounting follows the pro-rata recognition model. The projected benefit obligation being extinguished, `pbo_settled_cents`, is the sum of `accounting_liability_cents` across the tranche's assigned participants. The settlement cost is the tranche's `locked_premium_cents` plus the settled delivery's `transfer_expense_cents`, the settled delivery being the most recently created delivery of the tranche whose `transfer_status` is `SETTLED`, with both that expense and the gross figure delivered read as 0 if no such delivery can be found. The recognition threshold is the plan's `service_cost_cents` plus its `interest_cost_cents`, both read as they stood immediately before the cascade. The `gain_loss_cents` is the settlement cost minus `pbo_settled_cents`, where a positive figure is a settlement loss and a negative one a settlement gain. The pro-rata share of the unrecognised actuarial loss is 0 when the plan's `total_liability_cents` is 0, and otherwise the plan's `unrecognized_actuarial_loss_cents` multiplied by `pbo_settled_cents` and rounded half up over the plan's `total_liability_cents`. The total charge is the `gain_loss_cents` plus that pro-rata share. Whether the total charge hits current earnings turns on one **strict** comparison: only when the settlement cost is strictly greater than the threshold is the total charge recognised immediately, with the deferred amount set to 0. At exact equality — and at anything below — the whole total charge is deferred into accumulated other comprehensive income instead, and the recognised amount is 0. The tranche's `settlement_gain_loss_cents` records the gain-or-loss figure, positive meaning a loss.

One settlement accounting entry is appended with an `entry_type` of `BUYOUT_RECOGNITION` and its `tranche_id` set, carrying the recognised charge in `amount_cents`, the deferred charge in `deferred_amount_cents`, both `gain_loss_cents` and `pbo_settled_cents` populated, a null `window_id`, and a `source_reference` of `BuyoutTranche ASSETS_TRANSFERRED -> SETTLED`. Four cascade hops then run. First, the plan's `cumulative_settlement_charge_cents` increases by the entry's `amount_cents` and its `deferred_settlement_charge_cents` increases by the entry's `deferred_amount_cents`. Second, every assigned participant transitions `ASSIGNED → TRANSFERRED_TO_INSURER`. Third, because those participants have left the `IN_PLAN` and `ASSIGNED` population, the plan's `total_liability_cents` is reduced by their `accounting_liability_cents`, the plan's `plan_assets_cents` is reduced by the settled delivery's `gross_value_cents`, and the plan's `participant_liability_total_cents` and `accounting_funded_status_bp` are recomputed. Fourth, because the plan's liability changed and every window and tranche may now be terminal, the plan is re-evaluated for the residual transition of §3.9. Alongside all of that the selected quote transitions `LOCKED → BOUND`, the tranche stamps `settled_at`, and every entity touched bumps its `version`.

**Abandoning a tranche** is the compensating exit, available to the fiduciary while the tranche is in `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, `INSURER_SELECTED`, or `PRICING_LOCKED`; from any other status it is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_STATE_INVALID`. It requires an `abandonment_reason`. Abandonment reverses every reservation the tranche made, and each reversal is stated in full. If `locked_premium_cents` was set, the selected quote transitions `LOCKED → EXPIRED` and the carrier's `committed_capacity_cents` and `available_capacity_cents` are recomputed over the lock interval being released — the tranche's own `lock_start_at` through `lock_expires_at` as they stood before the clearing — restoring the invariant that the carrier's `capacity_cents` equals its committed plus available capacity at the pre-lock values. Every quote of the tranche whose `quote_status` is `REQUESTED`, `SUBMITTED`, or `SHORTLISTED` transitions to `EXPIRED`. Every assigned participant transitions `ASSIGNED → IN_PLAN` with `tranche_id` cleared. The tranche clears `selected_quote_id`, `selected_insurer_id`, `selection_score_bp`, `locked_premium_cents`, `lock_start_at`, and `lock_expires_at`, stamps `abandoned_at` and `abandonment_reason`, recomputes `assigned_participant_count` and both liability figures over the population it has just released, and bumps its `version` — as does every quote, participant, and carrier the reversal touched. Its `selection_basis` and `data_readiness_flag` are left exactly as they stood, so an abandoned tranche keeps the basis it declared and the readiness flag it last derived. Abandonment from `DRAFT` carries the same compensation set restricted to the effects that apply, which in practice means participant unassignment alone. Note what abandonment does *not* touch: the plan's `total_liability_cents` is never adjusted, because liability is only reduced at settlement — though the plan's `participant_liability_total_cents` is recomputed in place to pick up the participants handed back to `IN_PLAN`, under the no-version-bump rule of §2.8.

### 3.9. Post-Transfer Complaints, the Residual Plan and Termination

A participant who has actually left the plan through a settlement may complain about the benefit they now receive. Filing requires the participant's `participant_status` to be `TRANSFERRED_TO_INSURER`, `SETTLED_LUMP_SUM`, or `RESIDUAL_ANNUITY_PURCHASED`; a complaint about a participant still in the plan is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_TRANSFERRED`. The filing carries a `complaint_category` of `BENEFIT_AMOUNT`, `MISSING_PAYMENT`, `ADDRESS_OR_IDENTITY`, or `ANNUITY_FORM`, a `complaint_narrative` between 1 and 1000 characters that is recorded as the complaint's `complaint_detail`, and optionally a `tranche_id`. When a `tranche_id` is supplied it must equal the participant's **current** `tranche_id`, compared as an opaque string with no check that any such tranche exists, and any other value — including a real tranche the participant has since been cleared from — is rejected with `ERR_QUOTE_TRANCHE_MISMATCH`; when omitted, the complaint snapshots the participant's `tranche_id` as it stood at filing, which is null for lump-sum and residual participants. A filed complaint starts in `OPEN` with `triage_count` 0, `remediation_amount_cents` 0, `reversed_amount_cents` 0, and `responsibility_determination`, `determination_rationale`, and `resolved_at` null, and receives a server-minted sequential `complaint_reference` within the plan of the form `CMP-0001` — the literal `CMP-` followed by the count of the plan's existing participant complaints plus one, zero-padded to four digits.

**Triage** is the `PlanAdministrator`'s determination of who owns the correction, supplying a `responsibility_determination` of `SPONSOR_RESPONSIBLE`, `INSURER_RESPONSIBLE`, or `NO_ERROR` together with a `determination_rationale`. Triage is accepted from `OPEN` and, as a re-triage, from `RESOLVED`; from any other status it is rejected with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_TRIAGED`. One consistency rule binds the determination to the audit record: when the `complaint_category` is `BENEFIT_AMOUNT` and the participant's latest non-superseded benefit audit has an absolute `variance_cents` above 500 cents, the determination must be `SPONSOR_RESPONSIBLE`, and any other determination is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the sponsor cannot disclaim an error its own audit already documented.

A triage from `OPEN` moves the complaint `OPEN → TRIAGED`, records the determination and rationale, and increments `triage_count` by one. When the determination is `SPONSOR_RESPONSIBLE`, three further hops fire. First, the participant's latest non-superseded benefit audit, selected by the highest `audit_sequence`, transitions to `REOPENED` with `reopened_by_complaint_id` set to this complaint. Second, because that audit reopened, the participant's `benefit_audit_status` becomes `REOPENED` and the participant's `data_confidence_score` and `data_status` are recomputed. Third, because that recomputation drops the participant's score below the 80-point cleanliness floor, if the participant is assigned to a tranche whose `tranche_status` is `DRAFT`, `QUOTING`, or `INSURER_SELECTED`, that tranche's `data_readiness_flag` is set to `BLOCKED` while its count and liability figures are left alone. Every entity in those hops bumps its `version`. A participant with no live audit to reopen is not an error: the first hop is simply skipped and the participant's `benefit_audit_status` still becomes `REOPENED`.

**Resolution** moves a complaint `TRIAGED → RESOLVED` and is where money moves. The complaint must be `TRIAGED`, otherwise `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_TRIAGED`. The participant's `benefit_audit_status` must not be `REOPENED`, otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` — a complaint cannot be closed while the audit it reopened is still outstanding. The supplied `remediation_amount_cents` must be consistent with the determination: at least 1 when the determination is `SPONSOR_RESPONSIBLE`, and exactly 0 when it is `INSURER_RESPONSIBLE` or `NO_ERROR`; any other combination is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`. And when the sponsor is responsible, the plan's `plan_assets_cents` must be at least the remediation amount, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_ASSETS_INSUFFICIENT`. Resolution stores `remediation_amount_cents`, stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock, and bumps the `version`. When the determination is `SPONSOR_RESPONSIBLE` it additionally appends one settlement accounting entry with an `entry_type` of `REMEDIATION` and `amount_cents` equal to the remediation amount, increases the plan's `cumulative_settlement_charge_cents` by that amount, decreases the plan's `plan_assets_cents` by that amount, and recomputes the plan's `accounting_funded_status_bp`.

**Re-triage** exists because responsibility determinations are sometimes revised, and it is the compensating action for the remediation ledger. A re-triage moves the complaint `RESOLVED → TRIAGED` and requires the payload's `responsibility_determination` to differ from the stored one; repeating the same determination is rejected with `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. When the stored determination was `SPONSOR_RESPONSIBLE`, the recorded `remediation_amount_cents` is above 0, and the new determination is `NO_ERROR`, the money is unwound exactly: one settlement accounting entry is appended with an `entry_type` of `REMEDIATION_REVERSAL` and an `amount_cents` equal to the negative of the recorded remediation amount, the complaint's `reversed_amount_cents` is set to that remediation amount, the plan's `cumulative_settlement_charge_cents` is decreased by it, the plan's `plan_assets_cents` is increased by it, and the plan's `accounting_funded_status_bp` is recomputed. In every case the re-triage records the new determination and `determination_rationale`, increments `triage_count` by one, clears `resolved_at`, leaves `remediation_amount_cents` at the figure already recorded, and bumps the `version`. A re-triage never performs the audit-reopening hops of a first triage — no audit changes status, no participant's `benefit_audit_status` moves, and no tranche readiness is re-derived — even when the new determination is `SPONSOR_RESPONSIBLE`.

**Settlement entries** are the plan's append-only accounting ledger, and they are only ever written by the transitions described above — never created directly by a client. Each entry records the `plan_id` whose accounting it charges, an `entry_type` of `BUYOUT_RECOGNITION`, `LUMP_SUM_RECOGNITION`, `REMEDIATION`, `REMEDIATION_REVERSAL`, or `PLAN_TERMINATION`, and a `source_reference` — a human-readable provenance string naming the triggering transition and, for remediation entries, the complaint's reference. The five strings the ledger ever carries are `LumpSumWindow CLOSED -> SETTLED`, `BuyoutTranche ASSETS_TRANSFERRED -> SETTLED`, `ParticipantComplaint TRIAGED -> RESOLVED (CMP-0001)`, `ParticipantComplaint RESOLVED -> TRIAGED (CMP-0001)`, and `PensionPlan TERMINATION_FILED -> TERMINATED`, the two complaint forms substituting the complaint's own reference inside the parentheses. Which of the two cross-references is populated follows from the type: a `BUYOUT_RECOGNITION` entry carries a `tranche_id` and a null `window_id`, a `LUMP_SUM_RECOGNITION` entry carries a `window_id` and a null `tranche_id`, and the remaining three types carry neither. Its `amount_cents` is the charge recognised immediately in earnings, negative for a reversal, and every entry's `amount_cents` sums into the plan's `cumulative_settlement_charge_cents`. Its `deferred_amount_cents`, defaulting to 0, is the charge held in accumulated other comprehensive income because the recognition threshold was not exceeded, and sums into the plan's `deferred_settlement_charge_cents`. Its `gain_loss_cents` and `pbo_settled_cents` default to 0 and are populated only on the two recognition types; remediation and termination entries leave both at 0. Its `posted_at` records the logical-clock instant of the append.

**Becoming residual.** The plan transitions `SETTLEMENT_AUTHORIZED → RESIDUAL` by itself, evaluated at the end of every lump-sum window settlement and every buyout tranche settlement, with no client request naming the plan. Three conditions must all hold: every lump-sum window of the plan must be `SETTLED` or `VOIDED`; every buyout tranche of the plan must be `SETTLED` or `ABANDONED`; and at least one window must be `SETTLED` or at least one tranche must be `SETTLED`, so that a plan whose every settlement channel was voided or abandoned does not drift into residual status having transferred nothing. The transition stamps `residual_effective_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `accounting_funded_status_bp` and `participant_liability_total_cents`, cascades every de-risking strategy of the plan still in `APPROVED` to `EXECUTED`, and bumps the plan's `version` along with each strategy's.

**Filing for termination.** Once residual, the `PlanSponsor` submits the standard termination filing with a `filing_reference`, which is required on every filing including every re-filing. The plan must be in `RESIDUAL` or `FILING_DEFICIENT`, and the count of the plan's participant complaints whose `complaint_status` is `OPEN` or `TRIAGED` must be exactly 0; any outstanding complaint is rejected with `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. Filing moves the plan to `TERMINATION_FILED`, stores `filing_reference`, stamps `filing_submitted_at` from the logical clock, clears any `filing_deficiency_reason`, and bumps the `version`.

The `Regulator` reviews the filing with a `review_decision` of `APPROVE` or `RETURN_DEFICIENT`, and the plan must be in `TERMINATION_FILED` for either. A `RETURN_DEFICIENT` moves the plan `TERMINATION_FILED → FILING_DEFICIENT` and requires a non-empty `filing_deficiency_reason` in the payload, whose absence is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it stores that reason and bumps the `version`. The sponsor may then re-file from `FILING_DEFICIENT` back to `TERMINATION_FILED` any number of times, but a re-filing must carry a non-empty `deficiency_response` explaining how the deficiency was cured — its absence is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the same zero-outstanding-complaints requirement applies. A re-filing overwrites `filing_reference`, refreshes `filing_submitted_at`, clears `filing_deficiency_reason`, and bumps the `version`.

An `APPROVE` moves the plan `TERMINATION_FILED → TERMINATED`, its only terminal status, and is gated on solvency: the plan's `accounting_funded_status_bp` must be at least 10000, meaning assets at least equal the accounting liability, otherwise the approval is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_ASSETS_INSUFFICIENT`. Approval stamps `terminated_at` from the logical clock and cascades three ways. Every participant of the plan still in `IN_PLAN` transitions to `RESIDUAL_ANNUITY_PURCHASED` — the residual annuity contract that closes out the plan. Every complaint of the plan's participants still in `RESOLVED` transitions to `CLOSED_FINAL`. And one settlement accounting entry is appended with an `entry_type` of `PLAN_TERMINATION`, an `amount_cents` of 0, and a `pbo_settled_cents` equal to the plan's `participant_liability_total_cents` measured **before** the participant cascade ran. With the entry posted, the plan's `participant_liability_total_cents` is recomputed over the population that survived the cascade — which is 0, since no participant is left in `IN_PLAN` or `ASSIGNED` — and its `accounting_funded_status_bp` is recomputed from that new figure. Every participant, complaint, and the plan itself bumps its `version`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that mint a new resource return `201 Created`. Operations that transition or otherwise modify an existing resource — including those that create cascade-owned children as a side effect, such as window opening and the settlement operations — return `200 OK`. Read operations return `200 OK`. Every transition returns the single resource named in the description below, even where the transition also moves other entities: an address search and a mortality check return the participant, a benefit-audit resolution returns the audit, a participant assignment returns the tranche, and a carrier's review of a delivery returns the delivery. Every business path below is mounted beneath the `/prt` prefix; the single administrative clock capability sits outside it by design.
