# Brand Recommerce & Resale Platform API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Brands that sell durable goods are sitting on an asset they do not control: the items they already sold. Those items are traded, resold and eventually discarded on third-party marketplaces where the brand has no say over price, no visibility into condition, and no credit for the environmental benefit of keeping a product in circulation. This document specifies the API for a **brand-operated resale programme** — a Resale-as-a-Service platform that takes physical custody of used goods through the brand's own mail-in and in-store channels, authenticates and grades them, pays the surrendering customer in brand currency rather than cash, refurbishes what is economically worth refurbishing, and re-sells the result on channels the brand itself governs.

The platform exists because every one of those steps is a decision with money and reputation attached, and every one of them is currently made off-ledger. What is a scuffed handbag worth as a trade-in credit? Is a ninety-minute repair justified by the price the item will fetch? May a refurbished jacket be listed alongside the new season at all, or does that cannibalize a full-price sale? When a buyer returns a resale purchase two weeks later, how much of the brand currency they spent comes back, and what happens if the grant it came from has since expired? The system answers each question from stored policy rather than from operator judgement, and it records the answer so that both the finance function and the sustainability report can be reconstructed from primary data.

Three properties shape the whole design and are worth stating up front. First, **brand currency is a conserved quantity**: every minor unit issued, redeemed, released or expired is represented by an immutable ledger entry, and no operation may leave a customer's balance disagreeing with the sum of their grants. Second, **physical mass is also conserved**: every gram taken in at intake stays accounted for, either in the diverted bucket or in the salvage bucket, which is what makes the diversion metrics auditable rather than aspirational. Third, **resale inventory is accounted for separately from new goods**: a resale unit carries an acquisition cost equal to the brand currency the programme paid for it plus whatever refurbishment was capitalized into it, and it tracks its accounting posture on a dimension entirely independent of where it physically is. Everything in the sections that follow is mounted under `/recommerce`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Programme Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller's identity in two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. Both are required on every endpoint of the platform with a single exception: the logical-clock setter, `POST /recommerce/_clock/set_current_utc`, is a test-driving capability and performs neither authentication nor authorization. Everywhere else, a `X-User-ID` header that is absent, an empty string, or made up only of whitespace is treated as unauthenticated and the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A caller who is authenticated but whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles that the target endpoint admits is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Exactly six roles exist, spelled as follows.

- **`ProgramAdmin`** — owns programme configuration: customer enrolment and standing, condition-tier policy, brand-currency policy, store locations, refurbishment bays, sales channels, the brand-currency expiry sweep, and sustainability reporting. It is also the sole holder of the viability-override authority described in §3.5.
- **`IntakeAgent`** — the store-side operator who takes physical custody of surrendered goods and records receipt inside the intake window for the item's channel.
- **`Authenticator`** — the independent grader who rules on authenticity and condition, and who dispositions an authenticated trade-in.
- **`RefurbTechnician`** — the refurbishment operator who schedules bay windows, logs cleaning, repair and repackaging effort, and submits finished work to quality control.
- **`MerchandisingManager`** — owns the resale assortment: listing refurbished units on resale-eligible channels, running markdown cadences over aged inventory, and settling returns.
- **`Customer`** — the consumer participating in the programme: surrendering items, appealing grades, redeeming brand currency against resale purchases, and opening returns.

Five of those roles are **operator roles** whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeAgent`, `Authenticator`, `RefurbTechnician` or `MerchandisingManager`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is, however, recorded where the workflow demands an accountable actor: the grader's identifier lands in `graded_by_user_id` on a trade-in, the technician's in `performed_by_user_id` on a refurbishment order and in `logged_by_user_id` on each task line, and the quality-control signer's in `qc_by_user_id`. Those recorded values are compared to a later caller's `X-User-ID` only for the segregation-of-duties rules of §2.10 — never resolved as an entity reference.

The sixth role is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare that resource's customer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A `Customer` caller who addresses a trade-in, a brand-currency grant, a resale order, or a list filter belonging to a different customer is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A third authorization-family code, `ERR_OVERRIDE_ROLE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403), guards one specific privilege escalation and is described where it is earned, in §3.5.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by creation timestamp in ascending order.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object, alongside the request identifier and timestamp.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and How Time Windows Are Read

Nothing in this platform reads the host's wall clock. Every deadline, expiry, ageing calculation and window comparison is evaluated against a **logical clock** that the server holds. Before any client has set it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. A test-driving endpoint accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it, echoing the value it now holds back as `current_utc`; any role may call it, and it is the one endpoint exempt from the header checks of §2.1. Because the clock only ever moves when it is told to, two requests issued back to back observe the same instant, and a workflow can be walked deterministically across days or months.

Two window conventions coexist and must not be confused. A **refurbishment bay window**, delimited by `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, is a *closed* interval: both endpoints belong to the booking, which is why two bookings that merely touch at one instant are still in conflict. A **markdown campaign window** and a **diversion report period**, delimited by `window_start_at`/`window_end_at` and `period_start_at`/`period_end_at` respectively, are *half-open*: the start instant is inside the window and the end instant is outside it. In every one of those four cases the end must fall strictly after the start, and for markdown campaigns and diversion reports the span must additionally be expressible as a whole number of days; a window that violates either shape is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).

Deadline comparisons in this system are inclusive unless stated otherwise. A trade-in received at exactly its `receive_deadline_at` is on time. A brand-currency grant whose `expires_at` equals the logical clock is expired — the expiry boundary is inclusive on the expiry side, while the redemption-eligibility test is strict, admitting only grants whose `expires_at` lies strictly after the clock. A buyer-remorse return raised at exactly fourteen days after fulfilment is in time, and a warranty claim raised at exactly `warranty_expires_at` is in time. All instants exchanged with the API, including `received_at`, `accepted_at`, `issued_at`, `occurred_at`, `listed_at`, `fulfilled_at`, `return_requested_at`, `last_run_at` and `published_at`, are ISO 8601 UTC with millisecond precision.

### 2.5. Integer Money, Integer Mass, and Basis Points

All monetary amounts in this platform are **integer minor units** — cents. There is no floating-point money anywhere in the system, in storage, in intermediate arithmetic, or in a response body. Every field whose name ends in `_minor` is an integer, and any request that supplies a negative value for a money field, a mass field or a basis-point field is malformed. Physical mass is likewise an integer number of grams.

Percentages are expressed in **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is one hundred percent; every field whose name ends in `_bp` or `_pct_bp` carries such a value. The bounds that individual policy fields accept are stated with the workflows that consume them in §3.

Rounding is never left to the implementation's discretion, because different roundings of the same inputs produce materially different money. Three modes are used, and each calculation in §3 names the mode it applies at each step:

- **Round down (floor)** — used for every basis-point application that derives a credit, a baseline resale value, a channel ceiling, a redemption cap, a markdown step, and for every ratio expressed in basis points.
- **Round up (ceiling)** — used only where rounding down would undershoot a protective floor: the minimum-margin floor on a list price, the markdown price floor, and the buyer-remorse restocking fee.
- **Round half up** — used for the per-line refurbishment labour cost and for the channel fee on a resale order.

Where a calculation both multiplies several factors and sums several terms, the order of rounding is part of the specification: a value that is rounded per line and then summed is not the same value as one summed and then rounded, and §3 pins the order for each case.

### 2.6. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource the platform persists carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources — customers, condition-tier policies, brand-currency policies, store locations, refurbishment bays, channels, trade-ins, brand-currency grants, resale units, refurbishment orders, markdown campaigns, resale orders and diversion reports — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that is initialized to 1 when the row is created and incremented by exactly one on every mutation. A recomputation of a derived field is a mutation: when a customer's `credit_balance_minor` or `diverted_grams_total` is recalculated as a consequence of some other entity's transition, that customer's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, exactly as if the customer had been edited directly.

Two resources are append-only records rather than mutable rows and therefore behave differently. A **brand-currency ledger entry** and a **refurbishment task line** carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field — those two fields are absent from their representations entirely, because neither row is ever mutated, voided or deleted after it is written.

### 2.7. Policy Supersession: ACTIVE and RETIRED Rule Rows

Pricing and currency terms are stored as versioned policy rows rather than as configuration, and the platform always reads exactly one live row per question. A **condition-tier policy** is keyed on the pair (`product_category`, `condition_grade`) and is created with `status` `ACTIVE`; publishing a new policy for the same pair flips the previously `ACTIVE` row for that pair to `RETIRED`. A **brand-currency policy** has no key beyond itself: publishing a new one flips the previously `ACTIVE` brand-currency policy to `RETIRED`, so at most one is live programme-wide. `RETIRED` rows are never consulted by any calculation; they remain readable purely for audit.

Because policies can be superseded mid-flight, brand currency pins the terms it was issued under. When a grant is created it records both `credit_policy_id` and `credit_policy_version` — the `version` the brand-currency policy carried at the instant of issuance, always at least 1. Retiring or replacing that policy afterwards does not change the grant's expiry or its redemption restrictions; the grant continues to be governed by the pinned row. Where a calculation needs an `ACTIVE` policy and none exists, the request is refused: `ERR_GRADE_POLICY_MISSING` (HTTP 409) when no `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy covers the (`product_category`, grade) pair being priced, and `ERR_CREDIT_POLICY_MISSING` (HTTP 409) when brand currency must be issued or redeemed and no `ACTIVE` brand-currency policy is live. Those two checks guard trade-in submission, trade-in disposition, unit listing, resale-order placement, quality-control decisions and return settlement.

### 2.8. Stale-View Rejection on Scheduling and Purchase Writes

Two write operations in this platform can silently destroy work that the caller never observed, and both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control against the parent row they depend on. In each case the version field is a **required** input, not an optional hint — omitting it is a malformed request, not a waiver of the check.

**Scheduling a refurbishment order** requires `expected_resale_unit_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the target resale unit as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because a second refurbishment order for the same unit is a legitimate re-scheduling: it supersedes the earlier `SCHEDULED` order and releases the bay window that order had booked. Without the check, a caller working from a stale view of the unit would cancel a bay booking it had never seen.

**Placing a resale order** requires `expected_customer_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the buying customer as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the customer's current `version`, the order is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the risk is arithmetic rather than structural: because brand currency may be redeemed partially, a second order is perfectly legal on its own terms, and nothing in any state machine would notice that the caller computed `requested_credit_minor` from a `credit_balance_minor` that has since been superseded. The stale-version check is the only thing that notices.

No other endpoint in the platform accepts an expected-version input, and no other endpoint performs an optimistic-concurrency check. Endpoints that are not named above take no version field at all.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and the Append-Only Ledger

Several operations in this platform mutate more than one entity, and in some cases the cascade runs four links deep. The rule the platform follows is that all links of a cascade complete inside the same request or none of them do: there is no partially applied acceptance, no partially settled return, and no state in which a ledger entry exists without its grant having been adjusted to match. Each entity that a cascade touches independently refreshes its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version`, including entities the caller never named in the request — a customer whose balance moved because a grant was drained, a resale unit whose lifecycle advanced because its refurbishment order passed quality control, or an earlier refurbishment order that was superseded by a newer one.

Brand-currency ledger entries are strictly append-only. Every movement of brand currency — issuance, redemption, release on a refund, and expiry — is a new row, and no existing row is ever amended or removed. A movement is signed: `amount_minor` is positive for `ISSUE` and `RELEASE` entries and negative for `REDEEM` and `EXPIRE` entries, and `balance_after_minor` records the parent grant's `remaining_minor` immediately after that entry was applied. Compensation therefore never takes the form of deleting a redemption; it takes the form of appending an offsetting release, and if that release lands in a grant that has already expired the platform appends the compensating expiry in the same breath rather than resurrecting dead currency.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Ownership Scoping

Two points in the workflow require a second pair of hands, and both are enforced on the opaque operator identifier in `X-User-ID` rather than on the role. The first is the appeal re-grade: when an appealed trade-in is graded again, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `graded_by_user_id` already recorded on that trade-in, so the grader whose assessment was contested cannot be the one who reviews it. The second is the quality-control signature: the `X-User-ID` deciding a refurbishment order's quality-control outcome must differ from the `performed_by_user_id` recorded on that order, so a technician cannot pass their own work. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Note that the technician identifier is overwritten on every start of the work, including rework rounds, so the partner who may legitimately sign quality control can change from round to round.

Ownership scoping is the other identity-derived restriction. A caller acting as `Customer` may only read or act on rows whose customer reference equals the `Customer` row bound to their `X-User-ID`; that applies to trade-ins, brand-currency grants, resale units, resale orders, ledger-entry reads and list filters alike, on every endpoint whose permitted roles include `Customer`. Attempting to reach another customer's data is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Operator roles are not ownership-scoped and see the whole programme.

### 2.11. Request Admission Order

When a single request could fail several checks, the platform evaluates them in a fixed order and reports the first failure it reaches. The order is normative, because a request that is both unauthenticated and addressed at a non-existent row must answer 401, not 404, and a request that is both stale and in the wrong state must answer with the state error rather than the stale-version error.

1. **Authentication.** The `X-User-ID` header must be present and non-blank, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization.** The role in `X-User-Role` must be admitted by the target endpoint, otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Body shape.** On every creating and modifying request, all required fields must be present, every enumerated value must be inside its declared set, every money, mass and basis-point field must be a non-negative integer, and every timestamp must be a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Where a window's endpoints are ill-ordered or a campaign or report span is not a whole number of days, the dedicated code `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (422) is emitted instead, on refurbishment order creation, markdown campaign creation and diversion report creation.
4. **Path existence.** Every entity named by an `{id}` path segment must exist, resolved strictly left to right, so that the outermost missing segment is the one reported. The code is the per-entity not-found code: `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRADE_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFURB_BAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESALE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKDOWN_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESALE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DIVERSION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, each answering 404. A request for the bays of a store that does not exist reports the store's absence and never looks for a bay.
5. **Body foreign keys.** Every identifier supplied in a request body must reference an existing row of its declared entity — `customer_id`, `store_location_id`, `channel_id`, `resale_unit_id`, `refurb_bay_id`, `credit_policy_id` and `supersedes_report_id` — reported with the same per-entity 404 codes.
6. **Ownership.** The `Customer` scoping rule of §2.10 must hold, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
7. **State.** The target entity must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. The code is the domain-specific state error for that entity and action, always 409: `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_RECEIVABLE`, `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_AUTHENTICATABLE`, `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_APPEALABLE`, `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_DISPOSITIONABLE`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_READY_FOR_LISTING`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_LISTED`, `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_STARTABLE`, `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_LOGGABLE`, `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_QC_SUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_QC_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLABLE`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RETURN_REQUESTABLE`, `ERR_ORDER_RETURN_NOT_SETTLEABLE`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_RUNNABLE` and `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`.
8. **Stale version.** Where an expected-version input is declared, it must equal the parent row's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` on refurbishment order creation and `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` on resale order creation, both 412.
9. **Segregation of duties**, enforced with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409), on the re-grade of an appealed trade-in and on a quality-control decision.
10. **Counterfeit bar**, enforced with `ERR_COUNTERFEIT_NOT_ACCEPTABLE` (409), on trade-in disposition.
11. **Referenced-party standing.** The referenced customer must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_SUSPENDED` (409), on trade-in submission and on resale order placement. Then the referenced infrastructure must be usable: a store must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_STORE_LOCATION_CLOSED`; a bay must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_BAY_OUT_OF_SERVICE`; a channel must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ACTIVE`; each of the three carries HTTP 409. Those three are checked on trade-in submission, refurbishment order creation, unit listing, resale order placement and markdown campaign creation, as applicable to the identifiers each of those requests carries.
12. **Bay ownership**, enforced with `ERR_BAY_STORE_MISMATCH` (409), on refurbishment order creation.
13. **Live policy availability**, enforced with `ERR_GRADE_POLICY_MISSING` and `ERR_CREDIT_POLICY_MISSING` (409), on trade-in submission, trade-in disposition, unit listing, resale order placement, quality-control decisions and return settlement.
14. **Assortment separation**, enforced with `ERR_ASSORTMENT_NOT_PERMITTED` (409), on unit listing.
15. **Price feasibility**, enforced with `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_ABOVE_CHANNEL_CEILING` (409), on unit listing.
16. **Temporal conflicts** — bay window conflict, reported as `ERR_BAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on refurbishment order creation; campaign overlap on the same channel and category, reported as `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CATEGORY_OVERLAP` (409), and then channel-wide campaign capacity, reported as `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), both on markdown campaign creation; then the intake window, reported as `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), on trade-in receipt; then the after-sale windows, reported as `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_CLOSED` and `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED` (409), on a return request.
17. **Counted limits** — `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409) on a quality-control decision, then `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409) on an appeal.
18. **Refurbishment viability**, enforced with `ERR_REFURB_NOT_VIABLE` (409), then the override-privilege check `ERR_OVERRIDE_ROLE_REQUIRED` (403), both on a quality-control decision.
19. **Redemption restrictions**, on resale order placement, in this order: channel eligibility of the credit, reported as `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_REDEEMABLE_HERE` (409); the redemption cap, reported as `ERR_REDEMPTION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422); available credit, reported as `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT` (422); minimum cash remainder, reported as `ERR_MIN_CASH_REMAINDER` (422).
20. **Replacement availability**, enforced with `ERR_NO_REPLACEMENT_UNIT` (409), on return settlement.
21. **Report period overlap**, enforced with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on diversion report creation.

### 2.12. Conservation Invariants

Two quantities in this platform are conserved, and both are auditable after every request that could have moved them. They are not implementation advice; they are the correctness criterion for the ledger and for the sustainability report.

**Brand currency.** After every request that appends a ledger entry — a trade-in acceptance, a resale order placement, a return settlement and an expiry sweep — the following must hold for every customer: their `credit_balance_minor` equals the sum of `remaining_minor` over their grants whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, and equals the sum of `amount_minor` over all ledger entries belonging to those same grants. And for every grant: its `remaining_minor` equals the sum of `amount_minor` across all of that grant's ledger entries, and the `balance_after_minor` of its most recent entry — the latest by `occurred_at`, tie-broken by the later `created_at` — equals its `remaining_minor`. The compensating pair is redemption at order placement and release at refund settlement; a warranty settlement that releases into a grant which has already expired must append both the release and the offsetting expiry, so that every sum above is byte-identical to its value before the order was ever placed.

**Tracked mass.** After every request that creates or salvages a resale unit — a trade-in acceptance, a salvage quality-control decision, and a replacement settlement — the total `mass_grams` across all resale units equals the total `item_mass_grams` across all trade-ins whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Per customer, `diverted_grams_total` equals the sum of `mass_grams` over resale units that are not in lifecycle state `SALVAGED` and whose originating trade-in belongs to that customer with status `ACCEPTED`. And for any diversion report, `diverted_grams` plus `salvage_grams` equals the total `item_mass_grams` of the trade-ins accepted inside that report's period. Resale units are never deleted, so mass only ever moves between the diverted bucket and the salvage bucket. The compensating pair here is the warranty replacement: the defective unit leaves the diverted bucket for salvage while the substitute moves from listed to sold, leaving the first total unchanged and dropping the customer's diverted total by exactly the defective unit's `mass_grams`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Programme: Participants, Policy and Physical Estate

Nothing can be traded in until a `ProgramAdmin` has enrolled the participant. Enrolment captures an `email`, which must be non-empty and unique across all customers, and a `full_name`, which must also be non-empty. A `loyalty_tier` may be supplied and is one of `STANDARD` or `PLUS`; when it is omitted the customer is enrolled as `STANDARD`. The customer's `status` is set to `ACTIVE` on enrolment and is only ever `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. Two derived quantities start at zero and are never written directly by a client: `credit_balance_minor`, the customer's live brand-currency balance, and `diverted_grams_total`, their lifetime diverted mass. Neither may ever go negative. A `ProgramAdmin` may later revise a customer's `status`, their `loyalty_tier`, or both, and each such edit is a mutation of the customer row. A customer whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` may neither surrender an item nor buy one: both trade-in submission and resale-order placement reject a suspended participant with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SUSPENDED`.

`credit_balance_minor` is never incremented by arithmetic on its previous value. Whenever brand currency moves, the balance is **recomputed from scratch** as the sum of `remaining_minor` over that customer's grants whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`. `diverted_grams_total` is recomputed the same way: as the sum of `mass_grams` over the resale units whose originating trade-in belongs to this customer with status `ACCEPTED` and whose lifecycle state is anything other than `SALVAGED`.

**Condition-tier policy** is the pricing spine of the programme. A `ProgramAdmin` publishes one policy row per (`product_category`, `condition_grade`) pair, where `product_category` is one of `FOOTWEAR`, `OUTERWEAR` or `BAG` and `condition_grade` is one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `SALVAGE`. The row carries five economic terms and one warranty term. `base_credit_pct_bp` is the share of the reference MSRP paid out as the base trade-in credit, in basis points between 0 and 10000 inclusive. `resale_price_pct_bp` is the share of the reference MSRP that forms the baseline resale price, again 0 to 10000 inclusive. `refurb_budget_cap_pct_bp`, in the same range, is the maximum refurbishment spend the brand will tolerate on that tier, expressed as a share of the baseline resale value; a refurbishment whose cost ratio lands strictly above this value is non-viable. `restock_fee_pct_bp`, also 0 to 10000 inclusive, is the buyer-remorse restocking fee as a share of a resale order's gross price. `warranty_days` is the resale warranty length, a whole number of days of at least zero, measured from the instant an order is fulfilled. The row's `status` is system-managed as described in §2.7. Read access to a condition-tier policy is open to `ProgramAdmin`, `MerchandisingManager` and `Authenticator`, because the grader needs to know what a grade is worth.

**Brand-currency policy** governs how the programme's own money behaves. It is published with a human-readable `code` that must be non-empty and five terms: `expiry_days`, a whole number of at least 1 counting the days from issuance to expiry; `mail_in_bonus_pct_bp`, from 0 to 10000 inclusive, the bonus paid on the base credit when the item arrived through the mail-in channel; `max_redemption_pct_bp`, from 1 to 10000 inclusive, the largest share of a resale order's total that may be settled in brand currency; `min_cash_remainder_minor`, at least zero, the minimum cash that must still be due whenever any brand currency is applied; and `eligible_assortment`, the redemption restriction, which is one of `NEW_ONLY`, `RESALE_ONLY` or `BOTH`. That restriction is read against the purchase channel's commercial identity: `RESALE_ONLY` permits redemption only where the channel's `channel_type` is `RESALE_MARKETPLACE` or `OUTLET`, `NEW_ONLY` permits it only where the `channel_type` is `FULL_PRICE_DTC`, and `BOTH` permits it anywhere. Brand-currency policies are readable by `ProgramAdmin` and `MerchandisingManager` only.

The **physical estate** is two levels deep. A store location is registered with a `code` unique across all stores and non-empty, and a `region_code` naming the region it belongs to, also non-empty; its `status` begins `ACTIVE` and may be flipped between `ACTIVE` and `CLOSED`. A closed store refuses to take physical custody of a traded item, which surfaces as `ERR_STORE_LOCATION_CLOSED`. Inside a store, a `ProgramAdmin` adds refurbishment bays; a bay takes its `store_location_id` from the store segment of the creation path rather than from the body, and carries a `code` that must be non-empty and unique within its own store. Each bay declares a `bay_type` of `CLEANING`, `REPAIR` or `REPACKAGING`, which is the work class of the bay and — importantly — the thing that sets the labour multiplier for everything costed inside it. A bay's `status` begins `AVAILABLE` and may be flipped to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`; a bay out of service cannot be booked, which surfaces as `ERR_BAY_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Bays within a store may be listed with optional filters on `bay_type` and `status` alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

**Sales channels** are where the anti-cannibalization boundary is actually drawn. A channel is registered with a `code` unique across channels, a `channel_type` of `FULL_PRICE_DTC`, `RESALE_MARKETPLACE` or `OUTLET`, an `allowed_assortment` of `NEW_ONLY`, `RESALE_ONLY` or `BOTH`, a `max_resale_price_ratio_bp` between 1 and 10000 inclusive, and a non-empty `region_code`. `allowed_assortment` is the separation rule: a resale unit may be listed only where it reads `RESALE_ONLY` or `BOTH`. `max_resale_price_ratio_bp` is the cannibalization ceiling: a listed unit's price may never exceed that share of the unit's reference MSRP, no matter what the tier policy would otherwise justify. A channel's `status` begins `ACTIVE` and may be paused; a `PAUSED` channel accepts neither new listings nor new resale orders, and both refusals carry `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ACTIVE`.

### 3.2. Intake: Submission, the Provisional Quote, and Physical Receipt

A trade-in is opened either by the `Customer` themself or by an `IntakeAgent` acting at the counter. The submission names the participant through `customer_id`, declares the intake `channel` as either `MAIL_IN` or `IN_STORE`, states the `product_category` of the surrendered item, states the `declared_grade` the participant claims — one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `SALVAGE` — supplies `msrp_minor`, the reference MSRP of the equivalent new good, which must be at least 1, and supplies `item_mass_grams`, the weighed mass of the item, also at least 1. Mass matters here more than it looks: `item_mass_grams` is the conserved sustainability quantity that every later diversion figure is derived from. The `store_location_id` field completes the picture and is channel-dependent — it is required when the `channel` is `IN_STORE` and must be null when the `channel` is `MAIL_IN`.

On submission the trade-in enters `SUBMITTED` with `authenticity_result` set to `PENDING`, `assessed_grade` and `graded_by_user_id` both null, and `appeal_count` at 0. The server also computes `receive_deadline_at`, the instant by which the physical item must be in the programme's hands. A mail-in submission gets three full weeks — twenty-one days from creation — because the item still has to travel; an in-store submission gets only two days, since the customer is standing at the counter. Both are counted from the trade-in's own creation timestamp.

The submission response is not a promise, it is a quote. The **provisional appraisal** runs immediately and writes `appraised_credit_minor`, and it is deliberately built from what the participant *claims* rather than from what an expert has seen. It reads the single `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for the pair (`product_category`, `declared_grade`) and the single `ACTIVE` brand-currency policy; if either is absent, submission fails with `ERR_GRADE_POLICY_MISSING` or `ERR_CREDIT_POLICY_MISSING` respectively. The base credit is `msrp_minor` multiplied by that tier's `base_credit_pct_bp`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded down to whole minor units. When the intake `channel` is `MAIL_IN`, a channel bonus is added: the base credit multiplied by the brand-currency policy's `mail_in_bonus_pct_bp`, divided by ten thousand, rounded down; for an `IN_STORE` submission the channel bonus is zero. The provisional variant contributes **no loyalty bonus at all**, even for a `PLUS` customer — that term is reserved for issuance. The base credit and the channel bonus are then added together, and the result is capped: `appraised_credit_minor` is the lesser of that sum and sixty thousand minor units, which is the programme's ceiling on what any single trade-in may earn. Because the loyalty term is withheld here, a `PLUS` customer's quote is always strictly below what the same grade will actually issue.

Taking physical custody is the `IntakeAgent`'s exclusive act, and it advances the trade-in from `SUBMITTED` to `RECEIVED`. The trade-in must actually be in `SUBMITTED` — anything else is `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_RECEIVABLE`. The logical clock must be at or before `receive_deadline_at`; a receipt attempted strictly after the deadline is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and the trade-in does not change state, so an expired mail-in stays `SUBMITTED` rather than moving to some dead-letter status. For an `IN_STORE` trade-in the store named at submission must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_STORE_LOCATION_CLOSED`. Receipt writes `received_at` from the logical clock and increments the trade-in's version. Note what receipt does *not* do: it does not re-run the appraisal, and `appraised_credit_minor` still holds the provisional quote.

Trade-ins are readable individually by `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeAgent`, `Authenticator` and `Customer`, and listable by the same four roles with optional filters on `customer_id`, `store_location_id`, `status` and `channel`, plus `limit` and `offset`. A `Customer` reading either way sees only their own rows, per §2.10.

### 3.3. Grading, Contesting a Grade, and the Independent Re-Grade

Authentication is the `Authenticator`'s exclusive act and it is the same operation whether it is happening for the first time or as the resolution of an appeal — which is why it appears twice in the trade-in's state machine, once as the leg from `RECEIVED` to `AUTHENTICATED` and once as the leg from `APPEALED` to `AUTHENTICATED`. The request carries `authenticity_result`, which the grader sets to `GENUINE`, `COUNTERFEIT` or `INCONCLUSIVE` (`PENDING` being the pre-authentication value the field starts at), and optionally `assessed_grade`, the condition tier the grader assigns from `A`, `B`, `C` or `SALVAGE`. Grading is refused unless an `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy exists for the pair (`product_category`, `assessed_grade`), which surfaces as `ERR_GRADE_POLICY_MISSING`; the platform will not record a grade it cannot price. Attempting to grade a trade-in whose status is neither `RECEIVED` nor `APPEALED` is `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_AUTHENTICATABLE`.

A successful grading writes `authenticity_result` and `assessed_grade` from the request, stamps the acting operator's identifier into `graded_by_user_id`, and increments the trade-in's version. It deliberately leaves `appraised_credit_minor` alone: even though the assessed grade may differ sharply from the declared one, the trade-in continues to display the provisional quote right up until disposition. That is the point at which the participant learns the real number, and it is also the only point at which the loyalty bonus becomes visible.

Grading is contestable exactly once. An appeal takes the trade-in from `AUTHENTICATED` to `APPEALED` and may be raised by the `Customer` who owns it or, on their behalf, by a `ProgramAdmin`. The request carries an `appeal_reason` recording why the grade is being contested. A `Customer` caller must be the trade-in's own participant, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The trade-in must be in `AUTHENTICATED` — an appeal against a `RECEIVED`, `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` trade-in is `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_APPEALABLE`. And `appeal_count`, which starts at 0 and may never exceed 1, must still be below the programme's single-appeal allowance; a second appeal is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A successful appeal increments `appeal_count` by one and bumps the version.

The re-grade leg closes the cycle that runs from `RECEIVED` to `AUTHENTICATED`, on to `APPEALED`, and back to `AUTHENTICATED`. It is the same grading operation with one additional guard: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `graded_by_user_id` currently recorded on the trade-in, so the grader whose verdict was contested cannot review their own work. A self-review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The re-grade overwrites `authenticity_result`, overwrites `assessed_grade`, and replaces `graded_by_user_id` with the second grader's identifier, then bumps the version. It still requires an `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for the newly assigned pair. Because the appeal allowance is one, a trade-in can pass through `APPEALED` at most once, and the cycle therefore cannot spin.

### 3.4. Disposition: the Acceptance Cascade and the Disjoint Rejection Branch

Disposition is where the fork in the trade-in's lifecycle happens, and either the `Authenticator` or a `ProgramAdmin` may pull the lever. The request carries a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, an optional `reject_reason` free-text note captured on the rejection branch, and an optional `ownership_treatment` that decides the accounting posture of whatever unit is about to come into existence. In both branches the trade-in must currently be `AUTHENTICATED`; any other status is `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_DISPOSITIONABLE`. The two branches have deliberately **disjoint** side-effect sets, and neither leaks into the other.

**Accepting** requires three things beyond the state check. The `authenticity_result` must be either `GENUINE` or `INCONCLUSIVE`; a trade-in the grader marked `COUNTERFEIT` can never be accepted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COUNTERFEIT_NOT_ACCEPTABLE`, leaving rejection as the only permitted disposition for a counterfeit. An `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy must exist for the pair (`product_category`, `assessed_grade`), otherwise `ERR_GRADE_POLICY_MISSING`. And an `ACTIVE` brand-currency policy must exist, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_POLICY_MISSING`.

When those hold, acceptance triggers the deepest cascade in the platform, four links long, plus a parallel branch. The **first link** moves the trade-in to `ACCEPTED`, writes `accepted_at` from the logical clock, overwrites `appraised_credit_minor` with the *final* appraisal, and bumps the trade-in's version. The final appraisal differs from the provisional quote in exactly two ways. It prices the item at `assessed_grade` rather than at `declared_grade`, reading the `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for that pair. And it adds a loyalty term: when the participant's `loyalty_tier` is `PLUS`, five percent of the base credit — the base credit multiplied by five hundred basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded down — is added on top; for a `STANDARD` participant that term is zero. The evaluation order is pinned and the bonuses never compound: the base credit is computed first from `msrp_minor` and the tier's `base_credit_pct_bp`, rounded down; the mail-in channel bonus is then computed against **that base**, rounded down, and applies only when the intake `channel` was `MAIL_IN`; the loyalty bonus is likewise computed against that same base, rounded down, independently of the channel bonus. The three terms are summed, and the sum is finally capped at sixty thousand minor units before it is written. Applying the mail-in bonus to a base already inflated by the loyalty bonus, or capping before summing, produces a different and incorrect figure.

The **second link**, caused by the first, creates one brand-currency grant. It copies `customer_id` from the trade-in, records `trade_in_id` as the acceptance that produced it, records `credit_policy_id` as the `ACTIVE` brand-currency policy and `credit_policy_version` as that policy's current `version`, sets `issued_amount_minor` to the freshly computed final appraisal and `remaining_minor` to the same figure, sets `issued_at` equal to the trade-in's `accepted_at`, computes `expires_at` as `issued_at` plus the pinned policy's `expiry_days` whole days, and enters `status` `ISSUED`. `remaining_minor` is thereafter never less than zero nor greater than `issued_amount_minor`, and is only ever moved by ledger entries.

The **third link**, caused by the second, appends the grant's opening ledger entry: `credit_grant_id` pointing at the new grant, `resale_order_id` null because no order is involved, `entry_type` `ISSUE`, `amount_minor` the full issued amount as a positive number, `balance_after_minor` equal to that same issued amount, and `occurred_at` from the logical clock.

The **fourth link**, caused by the third, recomputes the participant's `credit_balance_minor` as the sum of `remaining_minor` over that customer's grants whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, and increments the customer's version.

Running in parallel off the first link, a **resale unit** is created. It records `trade_in_id`, copies `product_category` straight across, copies `condition_grade` from the trade-in's `assessed_grade` — not from the declared grade — copies `reference_msrp_minor` from `msrp_minor` and `mass_grams` from `item_mass_grams`, giving the unit its own denormalized pricing input and its own conserved mass. Its `ownership_treatment` comes from the request's optional `ownership_treatment` field and is `OWNED_INVENTORY` when that field is omitted; the only other value is `CONSIGNED`. The unit begins in lifecycle state `INTAKE`, accounting state `UNCAPITALIZED`, with `capitalized_cost_minor` at 0, `list_price_minor` and `current_price_minor` and `listed_at` and `listed_channel_id` and `replacement_for_unit_id` all null, `markdown_step_count` at 0 and `aged_days` at 0. Creating the unit in turn causes the participant's `diverted_grams_total` to be recomputed.

There is one twist on the parallel branch. When the `assessed_grade` is `SALVAGE`, the unit that was just created is moved, **inside the very same request**, to lifecycle state `SALVAGED`, and to accounting state `EXPENSED` when its `ownership_treatment` is `OWNED_INVENTORY` — a `CONSIGNED` unit stays `UNCAPITALIZED`, since consigned stock never capitalizes anything. It therefore never spends a moment in the diverted bucket, and the participant's `diverted_grams_total` does not rise by that item's mass even though the trade-in was accepted and the credit was issued. The mass is still tracked — it simply lands on the salvage side of the ledger from birth.

**Rejecting** is the disjoint branch. The trade-in moves to `REJECTED`, `reject_reason` is written from the request body, `appraised_credit_minor` is zeroed out — the provisional quote is explicitly withdrawn rather than left standing — and the version is bumped. Nothing else happens at all: no brand-currency grant is created, no ledger entry is appended, no resale unit is created, and the participant's `credit_balance_minor`, `diverted_grams_total` and `version` are all left exactly as they were. `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal for a trade-in.

### 3.5. Refurbishment: Bay Windows, Logged Effort, Viability and Quality Control

A resale unit carries **two independent state dimensions on one row**, and confusing them is the most common way to get this domain wrong. Its `lifecycle_state` is the physical and commercial dimension and runs through `INTAKE`, `IN_REFURB`, `READY`, `LISTED`, `RESERVED`, `SOLD`, `RETURNED` and the terminal `SALVAGED`. Its `accounting_state` is the inventory-accounting dimension and runs through `UNCAPITALIZED`, `CAPITALIZED`, `WRITTEN_DOWN` and `EXPENSED`. Neither dimension is ever derived from the other. Listing a unit and selling a unit move only the lifecycle dimension and leave the accounting dimension untouched; passing quality control, contacting a markdown floor and scrapping a defective unit move the accounting dimension. A `CONSIGNED` unit is the sharpest illustration: it never capitalizes anything and stays `UNCAPITALIZED` for its entire life, while its lifecycle dimension advances through listing and sale exactly like any other unit.

Scheduling refurbishment is open to a `RefurbTechnician` or a `ProgramAdmin`. The request names the unit through `resale_unit_id`, books a bay through `refurb_bay_id`, delimits the booking with `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, and — as §2.8 requires — carries `expected_resale_unit_version`. The unit must still be in `INTAKE`; scheduling against a unit that has moved on is `ERR_UNIT_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. The bay must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_BAY_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. The bay must also belong to the very store that took physical custody of this unit's trade-in — booking a bay in some other store is `ERR_BAY_STORE_MISMATCH`. And the requested window must not conflict with any other non-terminal order in the same bay. Two windows conflict either when they overlap as closed intervals, endpoints included, or when the gap between them is less than a full hour, because the platform enforces a sixty-minute sanitation buffer between consecutive jobs in the same bay; a conflict is `ERR_BAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The `window_end_at` must be strictly after `window_start_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

A newly scheduled order enters `SCHEDULED` with `rework_count`, `logged_cost_minor` and `viability_ratio_bp` all at 0, `performed_by_user_id`, `qc_by_user_id` and `viability_override_reason` all null. Scheduling does **not** move the unit out of `INTAKE`; it only books capacity. It does, however, increment the unit's version — which is precisely why the expected-version input exists. And it has one further consequence: any other order still sitting in `SCHEDULED` for the same unit is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, its own version is incremented, and the bay window it had booked is released and stops participating in overlap detection. That is the legitimate re-scheduling path, and it is the reason a create against a stale view of the unit would otherwise cancel a booking the caller never saw.

Starting work is exclusive to a `RefurbTechnician` and serves two legs of the refurbishment cycle. From `SCHEDULED` it moves the order to `IN_PROGRESS` and, as a cross-entity effect, moves the unit's `lifecycle_state` from `INTAKE` to `IN_REFURB` and increments the unit's version, leaving `accounting_state` at `UNCAPITALIZED`. From `QC_FAILED` it moves the order back to `IN_PROGRESS` as a rework round: `rework_count` is incremented by one, and on this leg there is **no** cross-entity move at all — the unit was already `IN_REFURB` and stays there. A rework round is only admissible while `rework_count` is still below two. Either leg requires the booked bay to be `AVAILABLE`, and either leg writes the acting operator's identifier into `performed_by_user_id`, overwriting whatever was there, so the technician on record — and hence the partner who is allowed to sign quality control — can change from round to round. Starting an order whose status is neither `SCHEDULED` nor `QC_FAILED` is `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_STARTABLE`.

Effort is captured as **immutable task lines**, appended only by a `RefurbTechnician` and only while the order is `IN_PROGRESS`; logging against an order in any other status is `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_LOGGABLE`. Each line takes its `refurb_order_id` from the order segment of the creation path and declares a `task_type` of `CLEANING`, `REPAIR` or `REPACKAGING`, the `labor_minutes` booked to the line — a whole number of at least zero — and the `parts_cost_minor` spent on it, also at least zero. The server assigns `sequence_no`, the 1-based append position of the line within its order, records `logged_by_user_id` as the acting operator, and computes `cost_minor`. Task lines are never amended, never voided and never deleted; there is no exclusion status, so every line an order has ever accumulated — including lines appended during rework rounds — is part of its cost forever. Lines may be listed with an optional `task_type` filter plus `limit` and `offset`.

Costing a line is where the rounding order bites. Labour is booked at 145 minor units per labour minute, and that rate is then scaled by the multiplier of the **bay** the order booked — not by the line's own `task_type`. A `CLEANING` bay multiplies by ten thousand basis points, that is, leaves the labour rate unchanged; a `REPAIR` bay multiplies by thirteen thousand five hundred basis points, a thirty-five percent premium; and a `REPACKAGING` bay multiplies by nine thousand basis points, a ten percent discount. So the raw labour value of a line is its `labor_minutes` multiplied by 145, multiplied by the bay's multiplier in basis points, divided by ten thousand. That raw labour value is rounded **half up to whole minor units first**, and only then is the line's `parts_cost_minor` added; the sum is the line's `cost_minor`. The order's `logged_cost_minor` is then the plain integer sum of the `cost_minor` values of every task line belonging to it, taken in ascending `sequence_no` order. The sequence matters for reproducibility, and the per-line rounding matters for the answer: summing the unrounded labour products across lines and rounding once at the end yields a different, incorrect total. An order with no task lines at all has a `logged_cost_minor` of 0.

Alongside the cost, the platform maintains `viability_ratio_bp` — the logged cost expressed as basis points of what the unit is expected to fetch. The baseline resale value is the unit's `reference_msrp_minor` multiplied by the `resale_price_pct_bp` of the `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for the unit's own (`product_category`, `condition_grade`) pair, divided by ten thousand and rounded down. When that baseline is greater than zero, `viability_ratio_bp` is the order's `logged_cost_minor` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the baseline, rounded down. When the baseline is zero — which is what a `SALVAGE`-tier policy with a `resale_price_pct_bp` of zero produces — the ratio is defined as 10001, a value that is by construction above any possible cap, so a zero-baseline unit is never viable to refurbish. Both `logged_cost_minor` and `viability_ratio_bp` are re-materialized on every task-line append and again when the order is submitted for quality control, and each re-materialization increments the order's version.

Submitting for quality control is again exclusive to a `RefurbTechnician` and moves the order from `IN_PROGRESS` to `QC_PENDING`; any other starting status is `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_QC_SUBMITTABLE`. The submission recomputes `logged_cost_minor` and `viability_ratio_bp` over all task lines — an order that logged nothing submits with both at zero — and bumps the version.

The **quality-control decision** may be signed by a `RefurbTechnician` or a `ProgramAdmin`, carries a `decision` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `SALVAGE`, and optionally carries a `viability_override_reason`. The order must be `QC_PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_QC_DECIDABLE`. Whatever the decision, the signer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `performed_by_user_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and a successful decision records the signer in `qc_by_user_id`.

A **pass** carries the viability gate. The order's `viability_ratio_bp` must be at most the `refurb_budget_cap_pct_bp` of the `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for the unit's (`product_category`, `condition_grade`) pair. The comparison is inclusive: a ratio exactly equal to the cap is viable, and only a strictly greater ratio trips the gate with `ERR_REFURB_NOT_VIABLE`. There is one escape hatch. A non-viable order may still be passed if a `viability_override_reason` justification is supplied *and* the caller's role is `ProgramAdmin`; that reason is then persisted on the order as the audit record of the override. Supplying `viability_override_reason` as a `RefurbTechnician` or a `MerchandisingManager` is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_ROLE_REQUIRED` — the override authority is the `ProgramAdmin`'s alone and cannot be borrowed. A pass moves the order to `COMPLETED`, bumps its version and releases the bay window. As a caused second link it advances the unit's `lifecycle_state` from `IN_REFURB` to `READY`, and — only when the unit's `ownership_treatment` is `OWNED_INVENTORY` — advances its `accounting_state` from `UNCAPITALIZED` to `CAPITALIZED` and writes `capitalized_cost_minor`; the unit's version is incremented either way.

The **carrying cost** written at that moment is what distinguishes resale inventory from new goods. The acquisition cost is the `issued_amount_minor` of the single brand-currency grant whose `trade_in_id` matches the unit's own — there is exactly one grant per accepted trade-in — and it is zero if for some reason no such grant exists. For an `OWNED_INVENTORY` unit, `capitalized_cost_minor` is that acquisition cost plus the completing order's `logged_cost_minor`: the brand currency the programme paid at intake, plus the refurbishment capitalized into the same carrying value. For a `CONSIGNED` unit, `capitalized_cost_minor` is 0. Note that the grant is read in **any** status, including `EXPIRED`: unredeemed currency lapsing later does not retroactively reduce an acquisition cost the books already recognized.

A **fail** sends the order to `QC_FAILED` and leaves the unit sitting in `IN_REFURB`. It is admissible only while `rework_count` is below two; once two rework rounds have been consumed, a further fail is refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and the order must be passed or salvaged instead. That cap is what makes the cycle that runs from `IN_PROGRESS` to `QC_PENDING`, on to `QC_FAILED`, and back to `IN_PROGRESS` finite: a third failure is unreachable.

A **salvage** decision writes the unit off. The order moves to `SALVAGED`, its version is bumped and the bay window is released; as a cross-entity effect the unit's `lifecycle_state` goes from `IN_REFURB` to `SALVAGED`, its `accounting_state` goes from `UNCAPITALIZED` to `EXPENSED` when its `ownership_treatment` is `OWNED_INVENTORY` — with `capitalized_cost_minor` staying at 0, since nothing was ever capitalized — and its version is incremented. The originating participant's `diverted_grams_total` is then recomputed and drops by exactly that unit's `mass_grams`. `COMPLETED`, `SALVAGED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal for a refurbishment order.

Orders are readable and listable by `ProgramAdmin`, `RefurbTechnician` and `MerchandisingManager`, with optional list filters on `resale_unit_id`, `refurb_bay_id` and `status`, plus `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.6. Bringing Units to Market: Listing, Pricing and the Markdown Cadence

Listing is the `MerchandisingManager`'s exclusive act, and it is the point at which the anti-cannibalization policy of §3.1 becomes an enforced constraint rather than a stated intention. The request names the destination through `channel_id`. The unit must be in `lifecycle_state` `READY` — its first listing after refurbishment — or `RETURNED`, which is the re-listing of a unit that came back on a refund; a unit in any other lifecycle state is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_READY_FOR_LISTING`. The destination channel must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ACTIVE`. The channel's `allowed_assortment` must read `RESALE_ONLY` or `BOTH`; a channel restricted to `NEW_ONLY` refuses the listing with `ERR_ASSORTMENT_NOT_PERMITTED`, which is the assortment-separation gate that keeps refurbished stock out of the full-price shop window. An `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for the unit's (`product_category`, `condition_grade`) pair is also required, otherwise `ERR_GRADE_POLICY_MISSING`.

Pricing is a three-way constraint problem evaluated top to bottom, with no step reading a later step's output. The **baseline resale value** is the unit's `reference_msrp_minor` multiplied by the tier's `resale_price_pct_bp`, divided by ten thousand and rounded down. The **margin floor** is the unit's `capitalized_cost_minor` multiplied by eleven thousand five hundred basis points — a hundred and fifteen percent, meaning the brand insists on at least a fifteen percent margin over carrying cost — divided by ten thousand and rounded **up**, so the floor is never undershot by a fractional cent. The **channel ceiling** is the unit's `reference_msrp_minor` multiplied by that channel's `max_resale_price_ratio_bp`, divided by ten thousand and rounded down. If the margin floor comes out strictly above the channel ceiling, no compliant price exists on that channel at all and the listing is refused with `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_ABOVE_CHANNEL_CEILING`. Otherwise `list_price_minor` is the greater of the baseline resale value and the margin floor, then clamped down to the channel ceiling — that is, the lesser of the channel ceiling and that greater-of pair. `current_price_minor` is set equal to `list_price_minor`. A `CONSIGNED` unit has a `capitalized_cost_minor` of 0, which makes its margin floor 0 and leaves the channel ceiling as the only binding constraint.

A successful listing writes `listed_channel_id` to the chosen channel, freezes `list_price_minor` and `current_price_minor` as just computed, resets `markdown_step_count` to 0, stamps `listed_at` from the logical clock, moves `lifecycle_state` to `LISTED`, and increments the unit's version. `accounting_state` is not touched by listing at all. Re-listing a `RETURNED` unit behaves identically in every respect — same channel checks, same pricing, `markdown_step_count` back to 0 and a fresh `listed_at`, so the markdown cadence restarts from step zero — with one asymmetry worth spelling out: a unit whose `accounting_state` had already been driven to `WRITTEN_DOWN` is **not** reversed to `CAPITALIZED` by re-listing. The write-down is permanent.

Ageing is measured from the current listing only. A unit's `aged_days` is the whole number of complete days between `listed_at` and the logical clock — the elapsed seconds divided by eighty-six thousand four hundred, rounded down — and is 0 whenever `listed_at` is null. Because `listed_at` is reset on every listing, a returned and re-listed unit ages from zero again.

Aged inventory is discounted through **markdown campaigns**, declared by a `MerchandisingManager` or a `ProgramAdmin`. A campaign names its `channel_id` and its `product_category`, delimits its half-open window with `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, and states three cadence terms: `cadence_days`, the whole number of at least 1 giving the days of listing age required per markdown step; `step_bp`, the per-step reduction as basis points of the current price, between 1 and 9999 inclusive; and `floor_pct_bp`, the price floor as basis points of the unit's own `list_price_minor`, between 1 and 10000 inclusive. The channel must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ACTIVE`. The window must not overlap, on the half-open interval, another `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` campaign covering the same (`channel_id`, `product_category`) pair — that collision is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CATEGORY_OVERLAP`. Separately and more broadly, at most two non-terminal campaigns may overlap the requested window on the same channel counting across *all* product categories; a third is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A campaign is created in `SCHEDULED` with `run_count` at 0 and `last_run_at` null. Campaigns on a channel are listable by `ProgramAdmin` and `MerchandisingManager` with optional filters on `product_category` and `status`, plus `limit` and `offset`.

A **cadence run** is executed against a campaign at the current logical clock by a `MerchandisingManager` or a `ProgramAdmin`, and its behaviour depends entirely on where the clock sits relative to the campaign window. A run while the clock is at or after `window_start_at` and strictly before `window_end_at` is a working run: from `SCHEDULED` it moves the campaign to `ACTIVE`, and from `ACTIVE` it leaves it `ACTIVE`. A run at or after `window_end_at` is a closing run: from `ACTIVE` or from `SCHEDULED` — the latter being a campaign that expired without ever having been run — it moves the campaign to the terminal `CLOSED` and **steps no price whatsoever**. A run requested on a campaign that is already `CLOSED`, or at a logical clock strictly before `window_start_at`, is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_RUNNABLE`. Every run that is not refused performs its bookkeeping unconditionally: `run_count` is incremented by one, `last_run_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the campaign's version is incremented — that holds even when the eligible set turns out to be empty and even for a closing run.

On a working run the campaign selects its eligible units, and every one of the following must hold for a unit to qualify: its `lifecycle_state` is `LISTED`; its `listed_channel_id` equals the campaign's `channel_id`; its `product_category` equals the campaign's `product_category`; and its `aged_days` is at least the campaign's `cadence_days` multiplied by one more than its current `markdown_step_count`. That last predicate is the cadence itself — a unit that has taken two steps needs three full cadence periods of listing age before it earns a third. `RESERVED` and `SOLD` units are frozen at their sale price and never eligible; `RETURNED` units are not listed and never eligible; and a unit one day short of its next step boundary is excluded until the next run. Eligible units are processed in ascending `listed_at` order, ties broken by ascending `created_at`.

For each eligible unit the run computes two numbers. The **floor price** is the unit's `list_price_minor` multiplied by the campaign's `floor_pct_bp`, divided by ten thousand and rounded **up**. The **stepped price** is the unit's `current_price_minor` multiplied by ten thousand minus the campaign's `step_bp`, divided by ten thousand and rounded **down**. The new price is the greater of those two, so the step never cuts below the floor. If that new price equals the unit's existing `current_price_minor`, the unit is skipped entirely: its `markdown_step_count`, its `current_price_minor` and its version are all left untouched, and it is counted as having been skipped at the floor. Otherwise `current_price_minor` becomes the new price, `markdown_step_count` is incremented by one, and the unit's version is incremented. And when that new price lands *exactly* on the floor price and the unit's `accounting_state` is `CAPITALIZED`, the accounting dimension moves to `WRITTEN_DOWN` in the same step. A unit sitting at `UNCAPITALIZED` — every `CONSIGNED` unit, for instance — that touches the floor is **not** written down; its accounting dimension stays exactly where it was while its `lifecycle_state` remains `LISTED`.

A cadence run reports what it did rather than returning the campaign row. Its response carries `run_sequence_no`, the campaign's `run_count` after this run's increment, so the first run reports 1; `evaluated_unit_count`, the number of eligible units the run examined; `marked_down_unit_count`, how many of those actually had their price changed; `skipped_at_floor_count`, how many were skipped because the computed new price equalled the price they already had; `written_down_unit_count`, how many were flipped from `CAPITALIZED` to `WRITTEN_DOWN` by landing on the floor; and `total_markdown_minor`, the summed reduction in minor units across every unit whose price actually moved. A closing run and a working run with no eligible units both report zeros across all five counters while still reporting their `run_sequence_no`.

Resale units are readable individually and listable by `ProgramAdmin`, `MerchandisingManager`, `RefurbTechnician` and `Customer`. The list accepts optional filters on `trade_in_id`, on `replacement_for_unit_id` to trace warranty-replacement lineage, on `listed_channel_id`, on `lifecycle_state`, on `accounting_state` and on `product_category`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.7. Buying a Resale Unit: Settlement, Redemption Restrictions and Fulfilment

A purchase may be placed by the `Customer` buying the item or by a `MerchandisingManager` transacting on their behalf. The order names the buyer through `customer_id`, the selling channel through `channel_id`, the item through `resale_unit_id`, states how much brand currency the buyer wants to apply through `requested_credit_minor`, and — as §2.8 requires — carries `expected_customer_version`. Before any money is computed, the buyer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or the order is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SUSPENDED`; the channel must be `ACTIVE` or the order is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ACTIVE`; the unit must be in `lifecycle_state` `LISTED` **and** its `listed_channel_id` must equal the order's `channel_id`, either failure surfacing as `ERR_UNIT_NOT_LISTED`; and an `ACTIVE` brand-currency policy must be live, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_POLICY_MISSING`, since it is that policy that supplies the redemption terms.

Settlement then runs in a pinned order, and the order matters because the dependency *looks* circular and is not. First, `gross_price_minor` is snapshotted from the unit's `current_price_minor` — whatever the markdown cadence had brought it down to at that moment. Second, the channel fee is derived from that gross price through a three-band schedule, and the band is selected from **`gross_price_minor` alone, never from the order total**. The bands are upper-inclusive and are tried in declared order: a gross price of at most twenty thousand minor units is charged nine hundred basis points; a gross price of at most sixty thousand minor units is charged six hundred and fifty basis points; anything above sixty thousand is charged four hundred and fifty basis points. A gross price of exactly twenty thousand therefore selects the nine-hundred-basis-point band, not the next one. `channel_fee_minor` is the gross price multiplied by the selected rate in basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded **half up**. Third, `order_total_minor` is `gross_price_minor` plus `channel_fee_minor`. Fourth, the redemption cap is `order_total_minor` multiplied by the `ACTIVE` brand-currency policy's `max_redemption_pct_bp`, divided by ten thousand and rounded down. Selecting the fee band from `order_total_minor` rather than from the gross price yields a different fee, a different total and a different cap, and is wrong.

Four redemption restrictions are then evaluated, in this order. The first is channel eligibility: every grant the buyer would draw upon must be redeemable in this channel, meaning its pinned brand-currency policy's `eligible_assortment` must admit the channel's `channel_type`; a buyer holding credit that its policy forbids here is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_REDEEMABLE_HERE`. The second is the cap: `requested_credit_minor` must not exceed the redemption cap just computed, and the boundary is inclusive, so a request exactly equal to the cap is accepted while a request strictly above it is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The third is availability: `requested_credit_minor` must not exceed the buyer's available credit, which is the sum of `remaining_minor` over their eligible unexpired grants; exceeding it is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT`. The fourth is the cash-remainder rule: `credit_applied_minor` is `requested_credit_minor` persisted verbatim, `cash_due_minor` is `order_total_minor` minus `credit_applied_minor`, and whenever any brand currency at all is applied the residual `cash_due_minor` must be at least the policy's `min_cash_remainder_minor`; falling below it is `ERR_MIN_CASH_REMAINDER`. An order that applies no credit is exempt from that last rule.

The eligible grant set is defined tightly. A grant qualifies only if its `customer_id` is the buyer's, its `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, its `expires_at` lies strictly after the logical clock, and its pinned brand-currency policy's `eligible_assortment` admits this channel's `channel_type`. `EXHAUSTED` and `EXPIRED` grants are excluded, as are grants whose pinned policy forbids this `channel_type`. Redemption then walks that set as a **waterfall**, ordered by ascending `expires_at` and, where two grants expire together, by ascending `created_at` — never by identifier. Each grant is drained to zero before the next one is touched, so only the last grant in the walk may be left partially drawn. Every touched grant receives exactly one ledger entry with `entry_type` `REDEEM`, a negative `amount_minor` equal to the amount drawn from it, a `balance_after_minor` equal to that grant's `remaining_minor` after the draw, `occurred_at` from the logical clock, and `resale_order_id` set to the order that caused it.

Each drained grant then settles its own posture. A grant drawn down but not emptied moves from `ISSUED` to `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`; a grant taken to a `remaining_minor` of exactly zero moves to `EXHAUSTED`, whether it started at `ISSUED` or at `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`. Either way its `remaining_minor` is reduced by the allocated amount and its version is incremented. Only unexpired grants participate, so redemption never touches a grant whose `expires_at` has been reached. With every grant settled, the buyer's `credit_balance_minor` is recomputed and their version incremented.

The order itself is created in `status` `PLACED` with `reason_type` `NONE`, `fulfilled_at`, `warranty_expires_at` and `return_requested_at` all null, and `restock_fee_minor`, `refund_cash_minor` and `refund_credit_minor` all 0. As the last cross-entity effect, the purchased unit moves from `LISTED` to `RESERVED` and its version is incremented; its `current_price_minor` is now frozen inside the order's `gross_price_minor` and the unit is no longer eligible for any markdown step.

Fulfilment is performed by a `MerchandisingManager` or a `ProgramAdmin` and advances the order from `PLACED` to `FULFILLED`; any other starting status is `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLABLE`, and the unit must be sitting in `RESERVED`. Fulfilment stamps `fulfilled_at` from the logical clock and computes `warranty_expires_at` as `fulfilled_at` plus the `warranty_days` of the `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for the unit's (`product_category`, `condition_grade`) pair — so the warranty a buyer gets is a property of the tier they bought, not of the order. The order's version is incremented, and as a cross-entity effect the unit moves from `RESERVED` to `SOLD` with its own version incremented.

Orders are readable and listable by `ProgramAdmin`, `MerchandisingManager` and `Customer`, with optional filters on `customer_id`, `channel_id`, `resale_unit_id` and `status`, plus `limit` and `offset`. The ledger entries an order produced are separately listable by the same three roles with an optional `entry_type` filter, `limit` and `offset` — a `Customer` seeing only their own, per §2.10.

### 3.8. After the Sale: Refunds, Warranty Replacement, and Denial

A buyer opens an after-sale claim on a fulfilled order, either themselves or through a `ProgramAdmin` acting for them, and the claim moves the order from `FULFILLED` to `RETURN_REQUESTED`. The request carries `reason_type`, which is `BUYER_REMORSE` or `WARRANTY_DEFECT`, the third value `NONE` being the resting state of the field on an order with no live claim. A `Customer` caller must be the order's own buyer, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. An order in any status other than `FULFILLED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RETURN_REQUESTABLE`. The two reasons then face different deadlines, both inclusive. A `BUYER_REMORSE` claim must be raised no later than fourteen days after `fulfilled_at` — the programme's two-week remorse window — and a claim raised after that is refused with `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A `WARRANTY_DEFECT` claim must be raised no later than `warranty_expires_at`, which the tier's `warranty_days` fixed at fulfilment; a claim raised after that instant is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED`. A successful claim writes `reason_type` from the request, stamps `return_requested_at` from the logical clock and increments the order's version. The unit is left completely untouched while the claim is pending — it stays `SOLD`.

Settlement is the `MerchandisingManager`'s or the `ProgramAdmin`'s decision and carries a `resolution` of `REFUND`, `REPLACE` or `DENY`. The order must be in `RETURN_REQUESTED`; anything else is `ERR_ORDER_RETURN_NOT_SETTLEABLE`. The three resolutions fork the order into `RETURNED`, into `REPLACED`, or back into `FULFILLED`, and the money each one moves is very different.

**Refunding** computes three figures whose values depend entirely on which `reason_type` the claim carried, reading the `ACTIVE` condition-tier policy for the unit's (`product_category`, `condition_grade`) pair. For a `BUYER_REMORSE` claim, `restock_fee_minor` is the order's `gross_price_minor` multiplied by that tier's `restock_fee_pct_bp`, divided by ten thousand and rounded **up**; the non-refundable amount is that restocking fee plus the order's `channel_fee_minor`, because the brand does not eat the channel fee on a change of mind. For a `WARRANTY_DEFECT` claim, `restock_fee_minor` is 0 and the non-refundable amount is 0 as well — the channel fee **is** refunded, because the fault was the brand's. From there both variants share the same arithmetic. `refund_cash_minor` is the order's `cash_due_minor` less the non-refundable amount, floored at zero so it can never go negative. Any part of the non-refundable amount that the cash leg could not absorb is a shortfall: the non-refundable amount less `cash_due_minor`, again floored at zero. `refund_credit_minor` is the order's `credit_applied_minor` less that shortfall, floored at zero — so a remorse buyer who paid mostly in brand currency has the unrecovered fee taken out of their credit refund rather than being invoiced for it. For a warranty claim the two consequences of a zero non-refundable amount are that `refund_cash_minor` equals `cash_due_minor` exactly and `refund_credit_minor` equals `credit_applied_minor` exactly. Identical orders can therefore settle to very different cash depending only on the reason.

A refund's cascade runs four links. The **first link** writes `restock_fee_minor`, `refund_cash_minor` and `refund_credit_minor` onto the order, moves the order to `RETURNED`, increments its version, and moves the unit from `SOLD` to `RETURNED` with `listed_channel_id` cleared to null and its own version incremented. The unit's `current_price_minor` and `list_price_minor` are retained until it is re-listed, and its `accounting_state` is left exactly as it was — a `CAPITALIZED` unit stays `CAPITALIZED`, and a `WRITTEN_DOWN` unit stays `WRITTEN_DOWN`.

The **second link**, caused by the first, returns the brand currency. Release entries totalling `refund_credit_minor` are appended against the very grants that funded this order, walked in **reverse redemption order** — this order's own `REDEEM` entries taken by descending `occurred_at` and, where two share an instant, by descending `created_at`, never by identifier. One `RELEASE` entry per grant is appended with a positive `amount_minor`, its `balance_after_minor` reflecting that grant's restored `remaining_minor`, and `resale_order_id` naming this order; the last grant reached may be only partially restored. Each touched grant then has its `remaining_minor` increased by the released amount and its `status` recomputed: an `EXHAUSTED` grant that is partly refilled becomes `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, a `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` grant whose `remaining_minor` is restored all the way back to its `issued_amount_minor` returns to `ISSUED`, and even an `EXPIRED` grant that funded part of this order accepts its release and becomes `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`. Every touched grant's version is incremented.

The **third link**, caused by the second, closes the loophole the previous paragraph would otherwise open. Every grant the release touched whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock is **immediately re-expired inside the same request**: an `EXPIRE` entry is appended with an `amount_minor` equal to the negative of the grant's just-restored `remaining_minor` and a `balance_after_minor` of 0, `remaining_minor` returns to 0, the grant's `status` returns to `EXPIRED` and its version is incremented again. The release-then-expire pair is what keeps the conserved quantity exact instead of silently resurrecting currency that had already lapsed. The **fourth link**, caused by the second and the third together, recomputes the buyer's `credit_balance_minor` and increments the customer's version — and when a release has been immediately re-expired, that recomputed balance is identical to what it was before the settlement began.

**Replacing** is reserved for genuine defects. The order's `reason_type` must be `WARRANTY_DEFECT`; a buyer-remorse claim cannot be settled with a substitute unit and the attempt is refused at the state gate with `ERR_ORDER_RETURN_NOT_SETTLEABLE`. The platform then picks a substitute. A candidate qualifies only if its `lifecycle_state` is `LISTED`, its `listed_channel_id` equals the order's `channel_id`, its `product_category` matches the defective unit's, its `condition_grade` matches the defective unit's, and it is not the defective unit itself. Candidates are ranked by ascending `current_price_minor` — the cheapest suitable substitute wins, since the brand is making the buyer whole rather than upgrading them — with ties broken by ascending `listed_at` and then by ascending `created_at`, never by identifier. `RESERVED`, `SOLD`, `READY` and `RETURNED` units are all ineligible. If no candidate exists at all, the whole settlement is rejected with `ERR_NO_REPLACEMENT_UNIT` and the operator must settle with a refund or a denial instead.

When a substitute is found, the order moves to `REPLACED` with `refund_cash_minor`, `refund_credit_minor` and `restock_fee_minor` all set to 0, and its version incremented. The defective unit moves from `SOLD` to `SALVAGED`, has its `listed_channel_id` cleared to null, and has its `accounting_state` driven to `EXPENSED` — from `CAPITALIZED` or from `WRITTEN_DOWN`, whichever it held. The substitute unit moves from `LISTED` to `SOLD` and records `replacement_for_unit_id` as the defective unit's identifier, which is the lineage link a later audit follows. Both units' versions are incremented. Deliberately, **no ledger entry of any kind is appended**: a replacement moves goods, not money, and the conserved brand-currency quantity is untouched. The buyer's `diverted_grams_total` is recomputed, dropping by the salvaged unit's mass while the substitute's mass stays where it was, and the customer's version is incremented.

**Denying** the claim is the cheapest resolution and the only one that is not terminal. The order returns to `FULFILLED`, `reason_type` is reset to `NONE`, `return_requested_at` is cleared back to null, and the version is incremented. No refund is computed, no ledger entry is appended and no unit moves. Because the order is `FULFILLED` again, the buyer may raise a fresh claim — but only while the applicable window is still open, since the deadline is measured from `fulfilled_at` or against `warranty_expires_at` and a denial does not restart either. That is the deny cycle that runs from `FULFILLED` to `RETURN_REQUESTED` and back to `FULFILLED`, and `RETURNED` and `REPLACED` are the two terminal settlements.

### 3.9. Currency Expiry and Sustainability Reporting

Unredeemed brand currency does not sit on the books forever. A `ProgramAdmin` runs the **expiry sweep**, which takes every grant whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock — the boundary is inclusive, so a grant expiring exactly now is swept. The sweep walks its set by ascending `expires_at` and then by ascending `created_at`, never by identifier. For each grant it appends a ledger entry with `entry_type` `EXPIRE`, an `amount_minor` equal to the negative of that grant's `remaining_minor`, a `balance_after_minor` of 0, a null `resale_order_id` and `occurred_at` from the logical clock; then sets `remaining_minor` to 0, moves `status` to `EXPIRED`, and increments the grant's version. Once every grant is done, each affected customer's `credit_balance_minor` is recomputed as the sum of `remaining_minor` over their grants whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, and their version is incremented. `EXHAUSTED` grants are skipped because their `remaining_minor` is already zero, and grants already `EXPIRED` are skipped too, which makes a repeated sweep idempotent — it expires nothing and reports zeros. The run reports `expired_grant_count`, `expired_amount_minor` and `affected_customer_count`.

Grants are readable individually and listable by `ProgramAdmin`, `MerchandisingManager` and `Customer`, with optional filters on `customer_id`, `trade_in_id`, `credit_policy_id` and `status`, plus `limit` and `offset`; the immutable ledger entries of a single grant are separately listable by the same three roles with an optional `entry_type` filter, `limit` and `offset`. A `Customer` sees only their own grants and entries.

**Diversion reporting** is how the programme turns tracked mass into a published sustainability number, and only a `ProgramAdmin` may generate or publish one. A report declares its half-open period through `period_start_at` and `period_end_at`, and may optionally name `supersedes_report_id`, the earlier published report this one restates; that field is null for an original report. A period must not overlap the period of any `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` report other than the row being superseded, judged on the half-open interval, and an overlap is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The window must satisfy the shape rules of §2.4 or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

Membership in a report's population is decided by one thing only: a trade-in's `accepted_at`, which must be at or after `period_start_at` and strictly before `period_end_at`, with the trade-in's `status` being `ACCEPTED`. Neither unit creation time nor listing time enters into it. The report's population is then every resale unit whose originating trade-in is in that accepted set, and five metrics are computed over it. `units_diverted_count` is the number of those units whose `lifecycle_state` is anything other than `SALVAGED`. `diverted_grams` is the summed `mass_grams` of those same non-salvaged units. `salvage_grams` is the summed `mass_grams` of the units in the population whose `lifecycle_state` is `SALVAGED`. `landfill_diversion_rate_bp` is the diverted share of the total in-period accepted mass: when `diverted_grams` and `salvage_grams` are both zero it is 0, and otherwise it is `diverted_grams` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the sum of `diverted_grams` and `salvage_grams`, rounded down to whole basis points. `credit_liability_minor` is the unredeemed brand currency issued inside the period — the summed `remaining_minor` over grants whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` and whose `issued_at` is at or after `period_start_at` and strictly before `period_end_at`. An empty period yields zeros across all five, rate included. Because the metrics are evaluated at generation time and then frozen on publication, a unit salvaged *after* the period still counts as salvage for the period in which its trade-in was accepted.

A generated report is created in `DRAFT`, with `published_at` null. Publishing moves it from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` and stamps `published_at` from the logical clock, incrementing its version; publishing a report whose status is not `DRAFT` — one already published, or one that has itself been superseded — is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`. Publication also carries a cross-entity effect: when the new report names a `supersedes_report_id`, that earlier report is moved to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` from whichever of `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` it held, and its own version is incremented. A superseded report's computed metric fields are frozen and never recomputed, so the restated figures live in the new row and the historical figures remain readable in the old one. Reports are readable and listable by `ProgramAdmin` and `MerchandisingManager`, with optional filters on `supersedes_report_id` and `status`, plus `limit` and `offset`.

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/recommerce` prefix and returns the envelope described in §2.2. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence return HTTP 201; endpoints that modify, transition or read an existing resource, and endpoints that run a batch operation, return HTTP 200. Authorization is evaluated exactly as described in §2.1 and §2.11: authentication first, then the role list in the table below, and only then the body, path, state and business gates of §2.11.
