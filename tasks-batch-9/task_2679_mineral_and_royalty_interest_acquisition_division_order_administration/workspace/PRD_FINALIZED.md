# Mineral Royalty Acquisition & Division Order Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A mineral and royalty acquisition firm makes money in the gap between what a county courthouse says and what an operator's revenue check pays. Between those two facts sits an enormous amount of clerical risk: a chain of title replayed in the wrong order produces net mineral acres that no bank will lend against; a valuation filed on stale acreage produces a bid band that authorises an overpayment; a division order executed against an amended unit produces a decimal interest that silently underpays the owner for years. This document specifies the API that removes that risk by making every one of those facts a first-class, versioned, auditable resource.

The platform covers the whole arc of a royalty acquisition. Title analysts open a tract file and file each county instrument — patents, deeds, probates and overriding-royalty assignments — into an append-only chain, then certify it, at which point the system replays the chain chronologically to establish the tract's defensible net mineral acreage and its aggregate outstanding burden. Acquisition managers carve a seller's interest out of that certified acreage, price it with a five-year production-decline model that yields both a walk-away floor and a maximum authorised bid, and negotiate an offer letter through bounded rounds of counters and revisions. When an owner accepts, a purchase and sale agreement papers the deal, opens a defect curative period, and — after the defect basket has been settled, the wire has cleared and the deed has been filed with the county clerk — moves the interest into the firm's ownership.

From that point the system becomes a reconciliation engine. Operators form units, commit tract acreage to them across half-open occupancy intervals, and exercise pugh clauses that hand non-producing depth back. Division order administrators compute the decimal interest the firm believes it is owed, send it to the operator, and pin it at execution. Every monthly revenue check detail the operator reports is then reconciled twice — once against the pinned decimal to decide whether the operator underpaid, and once against the live acreage picture to decide whether the pinned decimal is still the right one. Any line that does not settle cleanly, or that belongs to an owner the firm cannot locate, is routed into a suspense account that accrues statutory interest until it is released to the owner or escheated to the state. Two hard separations of duty run through the whole design: the manager who prices an interest may not be the manager who bids on it, and the administrator who issues a division order may not be the administrator who releases the money it held back.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold across every resource in the API: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, resource identity, rounding, concurrency, precedence of validation, and the conserved quantities the system must never let drift. Collections are mounted at the root — there is no shared API prefix, and each collection is its own top-level path segment.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. If `X-User-ID` is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is missing or is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the target operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `TitleAnalyst`, `AcquisitionManager`, `DivisionOrderAdmin`, `Operator`, `RoyaltyOwner` and `PlatformAdmin`.

- A `TitleAnalyst` runs county-records research: opening tract files, filing chain instruments, certifying acreage and burdens, raising title defects, adjudicating submitted cures and recording the deed. A `TitleAnalyst` may not certify a bid, and may not fund an agreement.
- An `AcquisitionManager` owns the buy side: carving interests, filing valuations, issuing and revising offer letters, papering agreements, waiving defects, closing the curative period and wiring funds. An `AcquisitionManager` may not certify title and may not record the deed.
- A `DivisionOrderAdmin` owns the revenue side: registering owners of record, maintaining their locatability and tax-identification status, issuing division orders, and resolving suspense by release or escheat. A `DivisionOrderAdmin` may neither value nor bid on an interest.
- An `Operator` is the unit operator: it forms and amends units, commits tract acreage, exercises pugh releases, responds to division orders, corrects disputed ones and reports monthly revenue check detail.
- A `RoyaltyOwner` is the mineral owner of record: it counters, accepts or rejects offers on interests it owns, submits curative instruments, and reads its own offers, division orders, revenue detail, suspense accounts and annual tax statement.
- A `PlatformAdmin` has no business authority whatsoever. Its single capability is moving the server's logical clock so that time-gated rules are exercisable.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `RoyaltyOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RoyaltyOwner` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `RoyaltyOwner` compare the resource's owner reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, every `RoyaltyOwner` write against an offer letter or a title defect is additionally gated on `X-User-ID` matching the `seller_owner_id` of the mineral interest the offer or defect hangs off; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403), so one owner can never accept, counter, reject or cure on another owner's interest.

When `X-User-Role` is `TitleAnalyst`, `AcquisitionManager`, `DivisionOrderAdmin`, `Operator` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque staff identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is nevertheless recorded where the audit trail requires it: as `approved_by_user_id` on a valuation, `issued_by_user_id` on an offer letter, `created_by_user_id` on an agreement and on a division order, `raised_by_user_id` and `waived_by_user_id` on a title defect, `operator_user_id` on a unit, and `released_by_user_id` on a suspense account. The `Operator` case deserves emphasis: `X-User-ID` for an operator is a staff identifier, never a unit `id`, and the server compares it to the `operator_user_id` that was stamped onto the unit from whichever `X-User-ID` created that unit. Any operator write against a unit, one of its allocations, or one of its division orders that presents a different `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint is paginated with the same two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and must not be negative. A `limit` outside its bounds or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether further rows exist beyond the current page. Two listings deviate from the default ordering because a consumer depends on the order: a tract's chain of title is returned in `instrument_date` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — the exact order the chronological replay uses — and an interest's valuations are returned newest first.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form, with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row.

One resource is deliberately different. A chain-of-title entry is append-only and immutable: once a county instrument has been filed it is never edited and never deleted, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither key appears in its representation. Every other resource in this specification — tracts, owners of record, mineral interests, valuations, offer letters, agreements, title defects, units, unit-tract allocations, division orders, revenue check details and suspense accounts — carries all four common properties.

Time in this system is a logical clock, never the host's wall clock. Every expiry date, curative deadline, pugh deadline, occupancy interval, months-held count and effective-month comparison reads that logical clock. Before any adjustment the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it by supplying a new `current_utc` instant; moving the clock mutates no entity and fires no deferred transition on its own — a lazy transition such as offer expiry only materialises when the next write touches the affected row.

### 2.5. Precision: Acres, Dollars, Fractions and Decimal Interests

All rounding in this system is round-half-up. There is no banker's rounding anywhere, and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity kind rather than per endpoint:

- **Acreage** is carried to 4 decimal places. This covers gross surface acres, certified net mineral acres, an interest's net mineral acres, allocated acres, released acres, unallocated acres and defect-affected acreage. In the chronological chain replay the rounding is applied *after every step*, not only at the end of the replay.
- **Dollar amounts** are carried to 2 decimal places. Where a calculation sums several dollar figures, the rounding order is pinned per calculation and stated in §3; in every such case the individual terms are rounded to cents first and the rounded terms are then accumulated, never the reverse.
- **Rates and fractions** — conveyed fractions, seller share fractions, lease royalty rates, decline rates, royalty rate caps and severance tax rates — are carried to 6 decimal places. Burden fractions are carried to 4 decimal places.
- **Decimal interests** — the expected, pinned, operator-supplied, corrected and audit decimals — are carried to 8 decimal places, with the rounding applied once at the very end of the computation.

Because these precisions are contractual, an equality comparison between two figures of the same kind is an exact comparison at that precision. A corrected decimal must equal the recomputed expected decimal to all 8 places; a wire amount must equal the defect-adjusted purchase price to the cent.

### 2.6. Segregation of Duties

Two duties in this system are split, and both splits are enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than on role. Both rejections carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The first split is on the buy side. The `X-User-ID` issuing an offer letter must differ from the `approved_by_user_id` recorded on the interest's live valuation. A manager who priced an interest cannot then bid against their own price, which means at least two distinct `AcquisitionManager` user identifiers are needed before any interest can reach the offered posture.

The second split is on the disbursement side. The `X-User-ID` releasing a suspense account must differ from the `created_by_user_id` recorded on the division order whose held revenue accrued into that account. The administrator who issued an order cannot then release the money that order's own variance held back, which means at least two distinct `DivisionOrderAdmin` user identifiers are needed before any account can reach the released posture.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Interests and Units

Two creation operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent resource's `version`. In both cases the version field is a required, non-optional member of the request body, and in both cases the point of the guard is not that a second create would be nonsensical — it is that the client's picture of the parent is out of date.

Papering a purchase and sale agreement requires `expected_mineral_interest_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent mineral interest. If it does not equal the interest's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INTEREST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The product legitimately allows a fresh agreement to be papered on an interest whose prior agreement terminated, so a second agreement is not inherently invalid; it is invalid only when the client's view of the interest — its valuation, its accepted offer, its acquisition posture — has been superseded.

Issuing a division order requires `expected_unit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent unit. If it does not equal the unit's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A further division order on the same interest-and-unit pairing legitimately supersedes an earlier one once the unit's allocation set has been amended, so the create itself is valid; it is rejected only because the acreage picture the client priced the decimal against is no longer the acreage picture of record.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field of any kind. Endpoints not named in this subsection do not take one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. Each resource kind has its own not-found code, all at HTTP 404: `ERR_TRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROYALTY_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MINERAL_INTEREST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_LETTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PSA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_DEFECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIVISION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVENUE_DETAIL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SUSPENSE_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`.

The same catalogue is used for references supplied inside a request body. Filing a chain instrument, carving an interest, papering an agreement, committing acreage and issuing a division order all name other resources in their payloads — `tract_id`, `seller_owner_id`, `mineral_interest_id`, `offer_letter_id`, `unit_id`, `royalty_owner_id` and `division_order_id` — and every such reference must resolve to an existing row before any business rule is evaluated. An unresolvable reference produces the not-found code of the referenced kind, not a generic bad-request.

Beyond existence, request bodies are shape-checked: all required fields must be present, enum fields must hold a declared value, numeric fields must be numbers inside their declared range, dates must be well-formed ISO 8601, and any field declared as a fraction must lie within 0 through 1. A shape failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities

Two quantities in this system are conserved exactly, and every operation that appears to move them is paired with a compensating action that restores the total.

The first is **unit acreage**. For any unit, the sum of `allocated_acres` across its allocations whose `allocation_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `PUGH_RELEASED`, plus the unit's `unallocated_acres`, rounded to 4 decimal places, always equals that unit's `unit_gross_acres`. A unit is created with `unallocated_acres` equal to `unit_gross_acres`, which is what makes the identity hold from the first instant. Committing acreage draws it out of `unallocated_acres`; a pugh release moves exactly `released_acres` from the allocation back to `unallocated_acres`, so the identity also holds mid-cycle; terminating an allocation returns the whole of its remaining `allocated_acres` and drops the row out of the summed status set. `TERMINATED` allocations are excluded from the sum precisely because their acreage has already gone home. This identity is asserted after every acreage commitment, every pugh release and every allocation termination.

The second is **division-order net revenue**. For any division order, the sum of `net_amount` across all of its revenue check details — regardless of `check_status` — rounded to 2 decimal places, always equals its `paid_net_total` plus the sum of `accrued_amount` across its suspense accounts, again rounded to 2 decimal places. Suspense accounts that have been released or escheated carry an `accrued_amount` of exactly 0.00 and therefore contribute nothing. A variance routes a whole line's `net_amount` into `accrued_amount` instead of `paid_net_total`; a release moves exactly `accrued_amount` into `paid_net_total` and zeroes `accrued_amount`, leaving the total untouched. Interest accrued on a suspense account is deliberately outside this conserved quantity: it is newly created money the firm owes, not reallocated operator revenue. This identity is asserted after every revenue check detail is reported and after every suspense release.

### 2.10. Order of Validation

When more than one rule could reject a request, the rule that fires is always the lowest-numbered rule in the ladder below, and the client sees that rule's error and status. Text order elsewhere in this document does not imply evaluation order; this ladder does. The ladder is global — every endpoint walks it, skipping rungs that do not apply to it.

1. `X-User-ID` present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` present and one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. The acting role is authorised for the target operation — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
4. Owner-bound and operator-bound identity match — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rung guards exactly eight operations: an owner countering an offer, an owner accepting an offer, an owner rejecting an offer, an owner submitting a cure, an operator exercising a pugh release, an operator terminating an allocation, an operator responding to a division order, and an operator correcting a disputed division order.
5. Every resource named by a path parameter exists, checked left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code (404).
6. Every reference supplied in the request body resolves — otherwise the matching not-found code (404). This rung guards filing a chain instrument, carving an interest, papering an agreement, committing acreage and issuing a division order.
7. Request body shape, required fields, enum membership, numeric ranges, ISO dates and fraction bounds — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
8. Pagination bounds on every list endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
9. Optimistic concurrency on the parent mineral interest when papering an agreement — otherwise `ERR_STALE_INTEREST_VERSION` (412).
10. Optimistic concurrency on the parent unit when issuing a division order — otherwise `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (412).
11. (Priority 20) The tract underlying the interest is certified — otherwise `ERR_TRACT_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409). Guards carving an interest, filing a valuation and issuing an offer letter.
12. (Priority 21) The tract's chain has at least one entry and exactly one patent instrument — otherwise `ERR_TITLE_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE` (409). Guards certification.
13. (Priority 22) The tract is still in research posture, so a certified tract cannot be certified twice — otherwise `ERR_TRACT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Guards certification.
14. (Priority 23) The interest is in the posture the requested operation transitions out of — otherwise `ERR_INTEREST_STATE_INVALID` (409). Guards issuing an offer letter and papering an agreement.
15. (Priority 24) A live valuation exists for the interest — otherwise `ERR_NO_CURRENT_VALUATION` (409). Guards issuing an offer letter and revising one.
16. (Priority 25) The offer letter's posture is in the from-set of the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_STATE_INVALID` (409). Guards countering, revising, accepting and rejecting an offer, and papering an agreement.
17. (Priority 26) The agreement's curative dimension is still open — otherwise `ERR_CURATIVE_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409). Guards raising a defect, closing the curative period, submitting a cure, deciding a cure and waiving a defect.
18. (Priority 27) The agreement's curative dimension is closed before money moves or a deed is filed — otherwise `ERR_CURATIVE_PERIOD_OPEN` (409). Guards funding and deed recording.
19. (Priority 28) The agreement's funding dimension is in the required posture — otherwise `ERR_PSA_ALREADY_FUNDED` (409). Guards funding and deed recording.
20. (Priority 29) No deed has been recorded yet — otherwise `ERR_DEED_ALREADY_RECORDED` (409). Guards deed recording.
21. (Priority 30) The defect's posture is in the from-set of the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_DEFECT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Guards submitting a cure, deciding a cure and waiving a defect.
22. (Priority 31) The allocation's posture is in the from-set of the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID` (409). Guards pugh release and allocation termination.
23. (Priority 32) The division order's posture is in the from-set of the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_DIVISION_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (409). Guards the operator response, the correction and reporting a revenue check detail.
24. (Priority 33) The suspense account is still accruing — otherwise `ERR_SUSPENSE_STATE_INVALID` (409). Guards release and escheat.
25. (Priority 50) Segregation of duties on acquisition — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Guards issuing an offer letter.
26. (Priority 51) Segregation of duties on disbursement — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Guards suspense release.
27. (Priority 52) The owner of record is locatable — otherwise `ERR_OWNER_UNLOCATABLE` (409). Guards suspense release.
28. (Priority 53) The owner of record has a tax identification on file — otherwise `ERR_OWNER_TAX_ID_MISSING` (409). Guards suspense release.
29. (Priority 54) Escheat is only lawful for an unlocatable-owner account — otherwise `ERR_ESCHEAT_REASON_INVALID` (409). Guards escheat.
30. (Priority 60) The offer has not lapsed — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (409). Guards countering, revising and accepting an offer.
31. (Priority 61) The curative window is still open in time — otherwise `ERR_CURATIVE_DEADLINE_PASSED` (409). Guards submitting a cure and waiving a defect.
32. (Priority 62) A recorded deed does not pre-date the agreement — otherwise `ERR_RECORDING_DATE_BEFORE_EFFECTIVE` (422). Guards deed recording.
33. (Priority 63) The pugh deadline has arrived — otherwise `ERR_PUGH_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED` (409). Guards pugh release.
34. (Priority 64) The statutory dormancy period has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_ESCHEAT_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (409). Guards escheat.
35. (Priority 65) The buffered occupancy window on a tract does not intersect an existing agreement's window — otherwise `ERR_PSA_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Guards papering an agreement.
36. (Priority 66) The allocation interval does not overlap an existing interval on the same unit-and-tract pair — otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (409). Guards committing acreage.
37. (Priority 67) The negotiation is not exhausted — otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Guards countering an offer.
38. (Priority 68) The chronological chain replay never drives running acreage below zero — otherwise `ERR_CHAIN_ACREAGE_NEGATIVE` (422). Guards certification.
39. (Priority 69) The aggregate overriding-royalty burden on a tract stays within its cap — otherwise `ERR_BURDEN_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Guards certification.
40. (Priority 70) A pugh release requires an active pugh clause and at least one non-producing depth — otherwise `ERR_PUGH_CLAUSE_NOT_APPLICABLE` or `ERR_PUGH_ALL_DEPTHS_PRODUCING` (both 409). Guards pugh release.
41. (Priority 71) At most one revenue check detail exists per order, production month and product stream — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCTION_MONTH` (409). Guards reporting a revenue check detail.
42. (Priority 72) Where the interest's tract does participate in the unit, at least one of its allocation rows covers the logical clock date — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ALLOCATION` (422). Guards issuing a division order and correcting one.
43. (Priority 73) The selected allocation is in an acreage-bearing posture — otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Guards issuing a division order and correcting one.
44. (Priority 74) The interest's tract participates in the named unit at all — otherwise `ERR_TRACT_UNIT_MISMATCH` (422). Guards issuing a division order.
45. (Priority 79) A county book-and-page citation is unique within a tract's chain — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_BOOK_PAGE` (409). Guards filing a chain instrument.
46. (Priority 80) Unit acreage capacity is not oversubscribed — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_ACREAGE_OVERSUBSCRIBED` (422). Guards committing acreage.
47. (Priority 81) The offered amount is not below the walk-away floor — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_BID_FLOOR` (422). Guards issuing an offer letter and revising one.
48. (Priority 82) The offered amount is within bidding authority — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_ABOVE_MAX_BID` (422). Guards issuing an offer letter and revising one.
49. (Priority 83) The wire settles the defect-adjusted price exactly — otherwise `ERR_FUNDING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Guards funding.
50. (Priority 84) A defect cannot impair more acreage than was bought — otherwise `ERR_DEFECT_ACREAGE_EXCEEDS_INTEREST` (422). Guards raising a defect.
51. (Priority 85) A correction must state the recomputed decimal — otherwise `ERR_CORRECTED_DECIMAL_MISMATCH` (422). Guards correcting a division order.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Rungs 42 and 44 are disjoint by construction: rung 44 fires only when the interest's tract has *never* been allocated to the named unit in any posture and any interval, while rung 42 fires only once at least one such row exists but none of them covers the logical clock date. And because the money checks sit at the bottom of the ladder, an offer priced outside the band on an interest whose tract has been de-certified surfaces as `ERR_TRACT_NOT_CERTIFIED`, not as a band violation.

### 2.11. Cascade Semantics and Version Propagation

Several operations in this system reach beyond the row named in the request. Where they do, the reach is specified transition by transition in §3, and three global rules apply to all of them. First, every entity a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and, unless the transition text says otherwise, its `version` incremented by 1 — a client holding a previously read `version` of a cascaded-into parent will therefore fail the concurrency guards of §2.7. Second, cascades are applied immediately and synchronously within the triggering request: by the time a response is returned, every hop has landed, and a read of any touched resource reflects the finished state. Third, cascades never partially apply — if any hop would violate a conserved quantity of §2.9, the whole request fails and nothing is written.

The deepest chain in this system is four hops long and starts at deed recording: the agreement closes, which moves the mineral interest into the firm's ownership, which disputes every live division order on that interest, which in turn opens or reuses a suspense account for each such order. The second-deepest is three hops and starts at a curative termination: the agreement terminates, which reverts the interest to a prospect, which voids its accepted offer letter and marks its live valuation stale.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The subsections below follow the life of an acquisition: from a courthouse file, through pricing and negotiation, into an agreement and a recorded deed, and out the other side into unit acreage, division orders and monthly revenue reconciliation. Rules are stated where the business narrative puts them, so a complete picture of any single operation is assembled from more than one subsection; §2.10 remains the sole authority on which rule fires first when several could.

### 3.1. Chain of Title: Tract Files, County Instruments and Certification

A `TitleAnalyst` begins by opening a tract file. The payload names the `county` whose deed records will be searched — between 1 and 80 characters and never empty — the `state_code` in which the tract lies, and the tract's surveyed `gross_acres`, which must be strictly greater than zero and is carried to 4 decimal places. `state_code` is one of `TX`, `OK`, `NM` or `ND`. An optional `operator_activity_tier` classifies how active the surrounding operator is; it is one of `HIGH`, `MODERATE` or `LOW` and defaults to `MODERATE` when omitted. A newly opened tract file is stamped with `title_status` of `RESEARCH`, a `chain_entry_count` of 0, and a `net_mineral_acres_certified`, `existing_burden_fraction` and `certified_at` that are all null. The tract's `gross_acres` is the denominator of every ownership fraction downstream, which is why it can never be zero.

Research proceeds one county instrument at a time. Each filing records an `instrument_type` — `PATENT`, `DEED`, `PROBATE` or `ASSIGNMENT` — an `instrument_date` given as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, a `county_book_page` citation matching the clerk's format of one to six digits, a forward slash, then one to six digits, and the `grantor_name` and `grantee_name` as recited on the instrument. Two mutually exclusive numeric operands complete the picture. `conveyed_fraction` is a value strictly above zero and at most 1, carried to 6 decimal places; it is required for a `PATENT` (the mineral fraction severed to the patentee), for a `PROBATE` (the fraction devised to the subject line) and for an `ASSIGNMENT` (the overriding-royalty burden fraction), and it must be null for a `DEED`. `conveyed_acres` is an absolute net mineral acreage of at least zero, carried to 4 decimal places; it is required for a `DEED` and must be null for a `PATENT`, a `PROBATE` and an `ASSIGNMENT`. A `county_book_page` that already appears on this tract's chain is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BOOK_PAGE`; uniqueness is scoped per tract, so the same citation may legitimately appear on two different tracts.

Chain entries are append-only and immutable — there is no edit and no delete surface, and, as §2.4 notes, they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`. Filing one increments the tract's `chain_entry_count` by 1 and the tract's `version` by 1. Filing one against a tract that is already `CERTIFIED` additionally reverts it: `title_status` moves `CERTIFIED → RESEARCH`, and `net_mineral_acres_certified`, `existing_burden_fraction` and `certified_at` are all set back to null. This is deliberate — the certified acreage figure that every downstream interest was carved from is no longer defensible once a further instrument surfaces. It is equally deliberate that the revert does *not* retroactively adjust anything: mineral interests already carved from the tract keep the `net_mineral_acres` they froze at creation, and the only practical effect of the revert is that new interests, new valuations and new offers on that tract are blocked until it is re-certified. Note also that filing an instrument never rejects for negative acreage; the chronological replay is a certification-time check only.

Certification is the `RESEARCH → CERTIFIED` transition and the analyst's most consequential act. It requires the tract to be in `RESEARCH` — a second certification of an already `CERTIFIED` tract is `ERR_TRACT_STATE_INVALID` — and it requires the chain to hold at least one entry and to hold exactly one `PATENT` instrument. An empty chain, a chain with no patent, or a chain with two or more patents is `ERR_TITLE_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE`.

The replay itself establishes the acreage. Every entry filed against the tract participates; there is no status filter, because entries are immutable. They are processed in ascending `instrument_date` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and an entry's `id` is never used as a sort key. A running net mineral acreage begins at zero. A `PATENT` entry *replaces* the running figure with the tract's `gross_acres` multiplied by that entry's `conveyed_fraction`. A `DEED` entry subtracts its `conveyed_acres` from the running figure. A `PROBATE` entry multiplies the running figure by its `conveyed_fraction`. An `ASSIGNMENT` entry leaves the running figure exactly as it was, because it carves an overriding royalty out of the revenue stream rather than acreage out of the tract. After every individual step the running figure is rounded half-up to 4 decimal places — not merely once at the end — and if at any step the running figure is below zero the whole certification is rejected with `ERR_CHAIN_ACREAGE_NEGATIVE`. Whatever survives the final entry is written to `net_mineral_acres_certified`. Because a deed subtracts an absolute acreage while a probate multiplies by a fraction, the same set of instruments in two different date orders yields two different acreages, and one of those orders can drive the running figure negative where the other does not; this is why the sort key is contractual rather than incidental.

The same certification computes the tract's burden. Only `ASSIGNMENT` entries contribute: their `conveyed_fraction` values are summed and the sum is rounded half-up to 4 decimal places. Patents, deeds and probates are excluded entirely, and because a plain sum is order-insensitive no tie-break is needed here. If that sum is strictly greater than 0.2500 — a quarter of the royalty stream, the most the firm will underwrite — the certification is rejected with `ERR_BURDEN_CAP_EXCEEDED`; a sum of exactly 0.2500 is accepted. Otherwise the sum is written to `existing_burden_fraction`. On success the tract's `title_status` becomes `CERTIFIED`, `certified_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant, and the tract's `version` increments by 1.

Reads round out this area. A single tract can be read by a `TitleAnalyst`, an `AcquisitionManager`, a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or an `Operator`, and the same four roles may list tracts, optionally filtering by `state_code` and by `title_status`. A tract's chain of title is readable by a `TitleAnalyst` or an `AcquisitionManager` and may be filtered by `instrument_type`; as §2.3 records, it is returned in replay order rather than plain creation order. The set of allocations that have ever drawn acreage from a tract — in any posture — is readable by a `TitleAnalyst`, a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or an `Operator` and may be filtered by `allocation_status`.

### 3.2. Owners of Record, Mineral Interests and the Decline-Curve Valuation

Before an interest can be carved, its seller must exist as an owner of record. A `DivisionOrderAdmin` registers one with a `legal_name` — the name that will appear on the division order and on the annual tax statement — a `state_code` of residence drawn from `TX`, `OK`, `NM` or `ND`, and a `mailing_address` of record for check and tax-form delivery. Two optional fields govern how the owner is treated downstream: `contact_status`, which is one of `LOCATED`, `UNLOCATABLE` or `DECEASED_ESTATE_OPEN` and defaults to `LOCATED`, and `tax_id_on_file`, a boolean recording whether a signed tax-identification form has been received, which defaults to false. A newly registered owner starts with a `suspense_total` of 0.00. That figure is not client-supplied: it is always the sum of `accrued_amount` across the owner's suspense accounts whose `suspense_status` is `ACCRUING`, carried to 2 decimal places.

Owner contact data changes, and the update surface is the business event that makes it change. A `DivisionOrderAdmin` may amend any combination of `contact_status`, `tax_id_on_file` and `mailing_address`; this is how an owner becomes unlocatable, how a found owner is re-located, and how a received tax-identification form is recorded. Each such amendment increments the owner's `version` by 1. Critically, it never retroactively re-routes revenue: revenue lines already settled stay settled, and lines already held stay held. Marking an owner unlocatable changes where *future* revenue goes, not where past revenue went. A single owner is readable by a `TitleAnalyst`, an `AcquisitionManager`, a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or the `RoyaltyOwner` itself; the owner list is available to an `AcquisitionManager` or a `DivisionOrderAdmin` and can be filtered by `contact_status` and `state_code`.

An `AcquisitionManager` then carves the interest. The payload names the `tract_id` the interest comes out of, the `seller_owner_id` of the owner of record who would convey it, a `seller_share_fraction` strictly above zero and at most 1 at 6 decimal places, a `lease_royalty_rate` strictly above zero and at most 1 at 6 decimal places (0.1875, for a three-sixteenths lease, being a typical value), and `undeveloped_net_acres` of at least zero at 4 decimal places representing the undeveloped upside the firm sees at intake. The tract must be `CERTIFIED` at this moment, or the request is `ERR_TRACT_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Two figures are frozen from the tract at creation and never move again: `net_mineral_acres` is the tract's `net_mineral_acres_certified` multiplied by `seller_share_fraction`, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places, and `existing_burden_fraction` is a straight copy of the tract's `existing_burden_fraction`, carried at 4 decimal places. The interest opens with `interest_status` of `PROSPECT`, a null `current_valuation_id` and a null `acquiring_deed_book_page`. A single interest is readable by a `TitleAnalyst`, an `AcquisitionManager` or a `DivisionOrderAdmin`, and the same three roles may list interests filtered by `tract_id`, `seller_owner_id` and `interest_status`.

Pricing is a decline-curve exercise, and it too requires the tract to be `CERTIFIED`. An `AcquisitionManager` files a valuation naming `current_monthly_net_revenue` (the trailing month's net royalty revenue attributable to the interest, at least zero, at 2 decimal places), an `annual_decline_rate` at least zero and strictly below 1 at 6 decimal places, and the `undeveloped_net_acres` this valuation chooses to price as upside — which may restate the figure captured at intake. The valuation copies the tract's `operator_activity_tier` onto itself at filing time, so a later re-classification of the tract can never retroactively move a valuation that has already been filed.

Four figures are then computed, in this order. First, the present value of proved-developed-producing cash flow, `pdp_pv`, starts at 0.00 and accumulates over a five-year horizon. For each whole year from the first through the fifth: that year's undiscounted cash flow is `current_monthly_net_revenue` multiplied by twelve and then multiplied by one-minus-`annual_decline_rate` raised to the power of the year number minus one, rounded half-up to cents; that year's discounted cash flow is the undiscounted figure divided by 1.10 raised to the power of the year number — a ten percent annual discount applied at each year's end — again rounded half-up to cents; and the running `pdp_pv` becomes the previous running total plus that year's discounted figure, rounded half-up to cents. The rounding order here is pinned: each year is rounded to cents *before* it enters the running total, and summing the five raw yearly figures and rounding once at the end is not the sanctioned computation.

Second, `undeveloped_pv` is the valuation's `undeveloped_net_acres` multiplied by an upside allowance of 1800.00 dollars per net acre, then multiplied by a risking factor of 0.45, then multiplied by the activity multiplier selected by the valuation's copied `operator_activity_tier` — 1.20 for `HIGH`, 1.00 for `MODERATE` and 0.80 for `LOW` — with the product rounded half-up to cents.

Third and fourth come the two edges of the bid band, and both apply the same burden haircut: one minus the interest's `existing_burden_fraction`. The `bid_floor` is `pdp_pv` multiplied by that haircut, rounded half-up to cents; this is the proved-developed-producing-only variant, the walk-away number below which the firm will not bid. The `max_bid` is the sum of `pdp_pv` and `undeveloped_pv` multiplied by that same haircut, rounded half-up to cents; this is the risked-upside variant, the maximum authorised bid. For identical valuation inputs, the two variants differ by exactly `undeveloped_pv` multiplied by the haircut, rounded half-up to cents — which is to say the entire difference between the floor and the ceiling of the band is the risked value of undeveloped acreage.

Filing a valuation sets its `valuation_status` to `CURRENT` and stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the filing manager's `X-User-ID` — the counterparty for the segregation-of-duties check of §2.6. It also supersedes whatever came before: any prior valuation of the same interest whose `valuation_status` was `CURRENT` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, the interest's `current_valuation_id` is repointed at the new valuation, and the interest's `version` increments by 1. That `current_valuation_id` is a convenience pointer only; the authoritative link is the valuation's own `mineral_interest_id`. A third posture, `STALE`, exists for valuations invalidated by an agreement termination, and is described in §3.5. An interest's valuations are readable — newest first, and filterable by `valuation_status` — by an `AcquisitionManager` or a `DivisionOrderAdmin`, as is any single valuation including the bid band the offer surface enforces.

### 3.3. Offer Negotiation: Sending, Countering, Revising and Closing

Issuing an offer letter drives the interest from `PROSPECT` to `OFFERED`, and it is the single most heavily guarded write on the buy side. The interest must be sitting in `PROSPECT` — an interest already `OFFERED`, `UNDER_PSA`, `ACQUIRED` or `RELEASED` yields `ERR_INTEREST_STATE_INVALID`. The underlying tract must still be `CERTIFIED`, so an offer attempted after a late-surfacing county instrument reverted the tract is `ERR_TRACT_NOT_CERTIFIED`. A valuation whose `valuation_status` is `CURRENT` must exist for the interest, or the request is `ERR_NO_CURRENT_VALUATION`. The issuing `X-User-ID` must differ from that valuation's `approved_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the money must sit inside the band: an `offer_amount` strictly below the valuation's `bid_floor` is `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_BID_FLOOR`, an `offer_amount` strictly above the valuation's `max_bid` is `ERR_OFFER_ABOVE_MAX_BID`, and an amount equal to either edge is accepted.

The payload carries only two fields: `offer_amount`, strictly greater than zero at 2 decimal places, and `expires_on`, a `YYYY-MM-DD` date that is the last day on which the offer may be countered or accepted, inclusive of that day itself. On success the letter opens with `offer_status` of `SENT`, a `current_amount` equal to `offer_amount`, a `counter_round_count` of 0, a null `last_counter_amount`, a null `accepted_at`, and `issued_by_user_id` stamped with the issuing manager's `X-User-ID`. The interest moves to `OFFERED` and its `version` increments by 1. A single offer letter — including its live amount and its counter tally — is readable by an `AcquisitionManager` or a `RoyaltyOwner`, as is the list of an interest's offer letters, filterable by `offer_status`.

Negotiation then loops. A `RoyaltyOwner` counters with a `counter_amount`; the letter moves `SENT → COUNTERED` on the first counter and `REVISED → COUNTERED` on every subsequent one, which is the loop-closing edge of the cycle. Countering is permitted only from `SENT` or `REVISED` (anything else is `ERR_OFFER_STATE_INVALID`), only while the logical clock's date is on or before `expires_on`, only while `counter_round_count` is strictly below the ceiling of three owner counters, and only when `X-User-ID` equals the interest's `seller_owner_id`. A fourth counter attempt once `counter_round_count` has reached three is `ERR_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. A counter records `last_counter_amount` as the amount named, increments `counter_round_count` by 1 and sets `offer_status` to `COUNTERED`. It pointedly does *not* move `current_amount`: the number on the table is the firm's number, and only a revision moves it.

An `AcquisitionManager` answers a counter by revising, taking the letter `COUNTERED → REVISED`. Revising is permitted only from `COUNTERED`, only while the logical clock's date is on or before the letter's current `expires_on`, and only when the interest still has a `CURRENT` valuation — a revision attempted after a terminated agreement marked the valuation `STALE` is `ERR_NO_CURRENT_VALUATION`. The payload carries `revised_amount` and a fresh `expires_on`. The `revised_amount` faces exactly the same band test as an opening offer against the interest's `CURRENT` valuation: strictly below `bid_floor` is `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_BID_FLOOR`, strictly above `max_bid` is `ERR_OFFER_ABOVE_MAX_BID`, and either edge exactly is fine. On success `current_amount` becomes `revised_amount`, `expires_on` becomes the supplied date — this is how a negotiation buys itself more time — and `offer_status` becomes `REVISED`.

Acceptance closes the negotiation. A `RoyaltyOwner` accepts from either `SENT` or `REVISED`, with no request body at all; accepting from `REVISED` accepts the revised `current_amount` rather than the original `offer_amount`, which is the only difference between the two edges. Acceptance requires the logical clock's date to be on or before `expires_on` and requires `X-User-ID` to equal the interest's `seller_owner_id`. It sets `offer_status` to `ACCEPTED` and stamps `accepted_at` with the logical-clock instant.

Rejection is the owner's other exit and behaves quite differently. A `RoyaltyOwner` may reject from `SENT`, from `COUNTERED` or from `REVISED`, optionally supplying a `rejection_reason`. Rejection sets `offer_status` to `REJECTED` and — this is the cross-entity effect that is easy to miss — sets the interest's `interest_status` to `RELEASED`, incrementing the interest's `version` by 1. An interest that has been `RELEASED` is out of the acquisition pipeline. Note that rejection carries no expiry test: an owner may reject a lapsed offer, whereas a counter, a revision or an acceptance may not act on one.

Expiry is lazy, not scheduled. At the start of a counter, a revision or an acceptance, if the letter's `offer_status` is `SENT`, `COUNTERED` or `REVISED` and the logical clock's date is strictly after `expires_on`, the system first drives the letter `SENT → EXPIRED` (or from whichever of those three postures it held), sets the interest's `interest_status` to `RELEASED`, and only then rejects the triggering write with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. The expiry is therefore durable — a subsequent read shows `EXPIRED` — even though the client's request failed. `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal postures for an offer letter; the fourth of them, `VOIDED`, is reached only by the cascade described in §3.5.

### 3.4. Papering the Agreement and its Exclusive Occupancy Window

An `AcquisitionManager` papers a purchase and sale agreement on an accepted offer, driving the interest `OFFERED → UNDER_PSA`. The payload names the `offer_letter_id` being papered, the `effective_date` of the agreement as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date, an `individual_defect_threshold` of at least zero at 2 decimal places, an `aggregate_defect_basket` of at least zero at 2 decimal places, the mandatory `expected_mineral_interest_version` described in §2.7, and an optional `curative_days` between 1 and 180 inclusive that defaults to 45 when omitted. The interest must be in `OFFERED` (otherwise `ERR_INTEREST_STATE_INVALID`) and the named letter must be in `ACCEPTED` (otherwise `ERR_OFFER_STATE_INVALID`).

Creation copies and computes a great deal. The agreement's `mineral_interest_id` is copied from the offer letter and its `tract_id` from that interest. Its `purchase_price` freezes the offer letter's `current_amount` at this instant, and `adjusted_purchase_price` is initialised to the same figure, with `defect_adjustment_amount` at 0.00 — the price only moves at curative close. Its `curative_deadline` is `effective_date` advanced by `curative_days` days, and is the last date, inclusive, on which any defect activity is permitted. Its `window_end_with_buffer` is `curative_deadline` advanced by a further thirty days, and is the inclusive end of the agreement's exclusive occupancy window on the tract. The two independent state dimensions are initialised to `CURATIVE_OPEN` and `UNFUNDED`, the derived `psa_status` reads `EXECUTED`, `created_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `wire_reference`, `recording_county`, `recorded_book_page` and `recording_date` are all null. The interest moves to `UNDER_PSA` and its `version` increments by 1.

Exclusivity on the tract is the last gate. The new agreement's buffered window is the closed, inclusive span running from its `effective_date` through its `window_end_with_buffer`. That span must not intersect the equivalent span of any other agreement on the same `tract_id` whose `psa_status` is `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATED`. Two spans that merely touch at a single endpoint count as intersecting and are rejected. A conflict is `ERR_PSA_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Note which postures participate: an agreement that has reached `CLOSED` no longer blocks the tract, because the interest it bought is already the firm's, while an agreement that `TERMINATED` still blocks — the curative fight it lost is a matter of record the next buyer must sit outside of.

The `psa_status` is never client-supplied and is a pure function of the other two dimensions plus the recording. It reads `TERMINATED` whenever `curative_status` is `CURATIVE_TERMINATED`. It reads `CLOSED` when `curative_status` is `CURATIVE_CLOSED`, `funding_status` is `FUNDED`, and `recorded_book_page` is not null. In every other combination it reads `EXECUTED`. Recomputing it is part of every transition that touches either dimension. A single agreement — showing both dimensions and the derived posture side by side — is readable by a `TitleAnalyst` or an `AcquisitionManager`, and the same two roles may list agreements filtered by `offer_letter_id`, `mineral_interest_id`, `tract_id`, `psa_status` and `curative_status`. The agreement's representation carries no defect counter of any kind — no tally of open, raised, uncured, cured or waived defects appears on it — so a caller who needs such a count lists the agreement's defects and reads `total_count` out of that listing's `meta`.

### 3.5. The Curative Period: Defects, Cures, Waivers and the Basket Waterfall

While an agreement's curative dimension is `CURATIVE_OPEN`, a `TitleAnalyst` raises title defects against it. Each defect records the agreement it belongs to as its `psa_id`, taken from the agreement the defect is raised inside, and names a `defect_type` drawn from `MISSING_HEIR`, `UNRELEASED_LEASE`, `TAX_SALE_GAP`, `OVERCONVEYANCE` and `UNLOCATABLE_OWNER`, an `affected_nma` giving the net mineral acres the defect clouds — strictly greater than zero, at 4 decimal places — and a free-text `description` of the defect as it will be asserted to the seller. A defect cannot impair more acreage than the firm is buying: an `affected_nma` greater than the interest's `net_mineral_acres` is rejected with `ERR_DEFECT_ACREAGE_EXCEEDS_INTEREST`, while an `affected_nma` exactly equal to it is permitted. Any defect activity attempted once the curative dimension has left `CURATIVE_OPEN` is `ERR_CURATIVE_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Two dollar figures are attached to the defect the moment it is raised. Its `allocated_value` — the gross exposure — is the agreement's `purchase_price` multiplied by the defect's `affected_nma`, divided by the interest's `net_mineral_acres`, rounded half-up to cents. Its `net_defect_value` — the figure that will actually enter the basket — is `allocated_value` less the agreement's `individual_defect_threshold`, floored at zero (so a defect smaller than the per-defect deductible contributes exactly 0.00 rather than a negative number), rounded half-up to cents. The defect opens with `defect_status` of `RAISED`, a `cure_round_count` of 0, a null `cure_instrument_book_page`, a null `waived_by_user_id`, and `raised_by_user_id` stamped from the analyst's `X-User-ID`.

The cure loop then runs, potentially several times. A `RoyaltyOwner` submits a curative instrument by naming its `cure_instrument_book_page`; this drives the defect `RAISED → CURE_SUBMITTED` the first time and `CURE_REJECTED → CURE_SUBMITTED` on every subsequent attempt, which is the loop-closing edge of the cycle. Submission is permitted only from `RAISED` or `CURE_REJECTED` (anything else is `ERR_DEFECT_STATE_INVALID`), only while the agreement's curative dimension is `CURATIVE_OPEN`, only while the logical clock's date is on or before the agreement's `curative_deadline` — a later attempt is `ERR_CURATIVE_DEADLINE_PASSED` — and only when `X-User-ID` equals the interest's `seller_owner_id`. It records the supplied `cure_instrument_book_page`, increments `cure_round_count` by 1, and sets `defect_status` to `CURE_SUBMITTED`.

A `TitleAnalyst` then adjudicates, supplying a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`. Adjudication is permitted only from `CURE_SUBMITTED` and only while the curative dimension is `CURATIVE_OPEN`; unlike a submission, it is not gated on the `curative_deadline`, so an analyst can still rule on a cure that landed on the deadline's last day. A `decision` of `ACCEPTED` drives the defect `CURE_SUBMITTED → CURED`, and a cured defect never enters the price adjustment again. A `decision` of `REJECTED` drives it `CURE_SUBMITTED → CURE_REJECTED`, handing the ball back to the seller for another round.

An `AcquisitionManager` has a third path: waiving. A waiver drives the defect from `RAISED`, from `CURE_SUBMITTED` or from `CURE_REJECTED` straight to `WAIVED`, takes an optional `waiver_note`, stamps `waived_by_user_id` with the waiving manager's `X-User-ID`, and — exactly like a cure — removes the defect from the price adjustment without any curative instrument being produced. A waiver requires the curative dimension to be `CURATIVE_OPEN` and requires the logical clock's date to be on or before the `curative_deadline`, so `ERR_CURATIVE_DEADLINE_PASSED` guards it too. `CURED`, `WAIVED` and `UNCURED` are all terminal for a defect; any attempt to submit, adjudicate or waive against a defect already in one of them is `ERR_DEFECT_STATE_INVALID`. A single defect, including both of its dollar figures, is readable by a `TitleAnalyst`, an `AcquisitionManager` or a `RoyaltyOwner`, and the same three roles may list an agreement's defects filtered by `defect_status`.

Closing the curative period is where the money is settled, and an `AcquisitionManager` does it with an empty request body. It is permitted only while the curative dimension is `CURATIVE_OPEN`. The first thing it does is sweep: every defect of this agreement still sitting in `RAISED`, in `CURE_SUBMITTED` or in `CURE_REJECTED` is driven to `UNCURED`. Defects that had already reached `CURED` or `WAIVED` are untouched and stay excluded — that exclusion is exactly what makes the cure loop economically worth running.

The waterfall then runs over the uncured set. Only defects of this agreement whose posture after the sweep is `UNCURED` participate; they are processed in ascending `created_at` order, never by `id`. For each one in turn, its `allocated_value` and `net_defect_value` are recomputed by the two rules stated earlier in this subsection, and the running total accumulates as the previous running total plus that defect's `net_defect_value`, rounded half-up to cents at each addition. The rounding order is pinned: each defect is rounded to cents first and the rounded per-defect figures are then accumulated. Summing the raw allocated values and rounding once at the end is not the sanctioned order and can differ by cents once three or more defects are in play.

Next the basket absorbs the first tranche. If the running total is less than or equal to the agreement's `aggregate_defect_basket`, the adjustment is exactly 0.00 — the boundary is inclusive, so a total equal to the basket produces no price movement at all. Otherwise the adjustment is the running total less the `aggregate_defect_basket`, rounded half-up to cents. Whichever branch is taken, `defect_adjustment_amount` is set to that adjustment and `adjusted_purchase_price` becomes `purchase_price` less `defect_adjustment_amount`, rounded half-up to cents.

The adjustment then decides which of two branches the curative dimension takes, and the two branches diverge sharply. The termination ceiling is the agreement's `purchase_price` multiplied by twenty percent, rounded half-up to cents. If the adjustment is less than or equal to that ceiling, the curative dimension moves `CURATIVE_OPEN → CURATIVE_CLOSED`, `psa_status` is recomputed and stays `EXECUTED` because the money has not been wired and no deed has been filed, and the deal proceeds. If the adjustment is strictly greater than that ceiling, the curative dimension moves `CURATIVE_OPEN → CURATIVE_TERMINATED` instead. Equality does not terminate — an adjustment landing exactly on the ceiling closes the period normally.

The termination branch fires a three-hop cascade. The agreement's `psa_status` becomes `TERMINATED`. Its mineral interest falls all the way back, `UNDER_PSA → PROSPECT`, with the interest's `version` incremented by 1. Caused by that second hop rather than by the close itself, the third hop reaches two more rows: the offer letter of that interest whose `offer_status` is `ACCEPTED` is driven `ACCEPTED → VOIDED`, and the valuation of that interest whose `valuation_status` is `CURRENT` is driven `CURRENT → STALE`. The practical consequence is that the interest can be offered again, but not immediately — it now has no live valuation, so the next offer attempt is `ERR_NO_CURRENT_VALUATION` until a fresh valuation is filed, which in turn re-opens the segregation-of-duties question with a new `approved_by_user_id`.

### 3.6. Funding, Deed Recording and the Ownership-Change Cascade

Funding moves the second, independent state dimension of the agreement from `UNFUNDED` to `FUNDED`, and an `AcquisitionManager` performs it by naming a `wire_amount` and a `wire_reference`. The curative dimension must already read `CURATIVE_CLOSED`; wiring against an agreement whose curative period is still open is `ERR_CURATIVE_PERIOD_OPEN`. The funding dimension must read `UNFUNDED`; a second wire is `ERR_PSA_ALREADY_FUNDED`. And the money must be exact: a `wire_amount` that is not equal to the agreement's `adjusted_purchase_price` — the defect-adjusted figure, not the original `purchase_price` — is `ERR_FUNDING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. On success `funding_status` becomes `FUNDED`, the supplied `wire_reference` is recorded, and `psa_status` is recomputed, remaining `EXECUTED` until the deed is on file. The asymmetry between the two dimensions is intentional: funding is gated on the curative dimension, but the curative dimension is never gated on funding.

Recording the deed is a `TitleAnalyst` act and the derived `EXECUTED → CLOSED` transition. The payload names the `recording_county` in which the deed was filed, the `recorded_book_page` the clerk assigned, and the `recording_date`. Four gates apply. The curative dimension must be `CURATIVE_CLOSED`, or the request is `ERR_CURATIVE_PERIOD_OPEN`. The funding dimension must be `FUNDED`, and an attempt to record while the agreement is still `UNFUNDED` is rejected with `ERR_PSA_ALREADY_FUNDED` — the same code that guards a double wire, reused here for the mirror-image funding-dimension violation. The agreement's `recorded_book_page` must still be null, or the request is `ERR_DEED_ALREADY_RECORDED`. And the `recording_date` must not fall earlier than the agreement's `effective_date`; a deed that pre-dates its own agreement is `ERR_RECORDING_DATE_BEFORE_EFFECTIVE`, while a `recording_date` equal to the `effective_date` is accepted.

Recording is the deepest cascade in the system, four hops from end to end, and every hop is part of the same synchronous request. The first hop writes `recording_county`, `recorded_book_page` and `recording_date` onto the agreement and sets `psa_status` to `CLOSED`. The second hop drives the mineral interest `UNDER_PSA → ACQUIRED` — its terminal posture — copies the deed's `recorded_book_page` into the interest's `acquiring_deed_book_page`, and increments the interest's `version` by 1. The third hop, caused by the second rather than by the recording itself, reaches every division order of that interest whose `do_status` is `SENT` or `EXECUTED`: each such order is driven to `DISPUTED` with a `dispute_reason` of `OWNERSHIP_CHANGE`, and each one's `expected_decimal` is recomputed against present-day state. This is the ownership-change notice the operator must act on — the payee of record has changed, so no revenue may settle at the old decimal. The fourth hop, caused by the third, opens or reuses a suspense account for each of those orders, keyed on the pairing of the order and its payee of record: the account's `suspense_status` is `ACCRUING`, its `suspense_reason` is `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER`, and, when the account is being created rather than reused, its `first_suspended_month` is the logical clock's own year and month in `YYYY-MM` form. A `suspense_reason` is written once by whichever cascade opened the account and is never rewritten afterwards, so an account opened earlier for a decimal variance keeps that reason even when an ownership transfer later reuses it.

### 3.7. Unitization, Acreage Commitments and Pugh Elections

An `Operator` forms a unit by naming the operator's own `unit_name` designation for the pooled area, the target `formation`, the producing `state_code` (one of `TX`, `OK`, `NM` or `ND`, and the value that later selects the severance-tax row), the `unit_gross_acres` inside the unit boundary (strictly greater than zero, at 4 decimal places), and the `royalty_rate_cap` the unit agreement will honour (strictly greater than zero and at most 1, at 6 decimal places). Two integers describe the depth stack: `total_depth_count`, the number of depth intervals the unit's leases hold, which must be at least 1, and `producing_depth_count`, the number actually on production, which must be at least zero and must not exceed `total_depth_count`. Two optional fields describe the pugh arrangement: `pugh_clause_active`, a boolean defaulting to false, and `pugh_deadline`, a `YYYY-MM-DD` date that is the first date on which a pugh release may be exercised and that is required whenever `pugh_clause_active` is true. On creation the unit's `unallocated_acres` is set equal to `unit_gross_acres`, its `active_allocation_count` is 0, and its `operator_user_id` is stamped from the creating `X-User-ID` — the identifier every later operator action on this unit, on its allocations and on its division orders must present.

The `Operator` may amend the unit later, supplying any combination of `producing_depth_count`, `pugh_clause_active`, `pugh_deadline` and `royalty_rate_cap`. This is the business surface for bringing a further depth interval onto production or for activating a pugh clause mid-life. Each amendment increments the unit's `version` by 1, which is precisely what makes a previously read `expected_unit_version` stale for the next division-order issuance described in §3.8. What an amendment deliberately does *not* do is re-run any existing division order's decimal — a pinned decimal moves only through the correction path. A single unit, including its live `unallocated_acres`, is readable by a `TitleAnalyst`, an `AcquisitionManager`, a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or an `Operator`; the unit list is available to a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or an `Operator` and can be filtered by `state_code` and `formation`.

Committing tract acreage to a unit is the junction between the title world and the revenue world, and an `Operator` does it by naming the `unit_id` that draws the acreage, the `tract_id` that contributes it, the `allocated_acres` committed (strictly greater than zero, at 4 decimal places), an `effective_from` date, an `effective_to` date that must be strictly later than `effective_from`, and the owner's `pooling_election` for this commitment — one of `POOLED`, `NON_CONSENT` or `LEASED_ROYALTY_ONLY`. The occupancy interval is half-open: it includes `effective_from` and excludes `effective_to`. The new interval must not overlap the interval of any existing allocation on the same unit-and-tract pairing whose `allocation_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PUGH_RELEASED`; an overlap is `ERR_ALLOCATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Because the interval is half-open, exact adjacency is explicitly legal: a new allocation whose `effective_from` equals an existing allocation's `effective_to` does not conflict.

Capacity is the other gate. The sum of `allocated_acres` across the unit's allocations whose `allocation_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PUGH_RELEASED`, plus the requested `allocated_acres`, rounded to 4 decimal places, must not exceed the unit's `unit_gross_acres`. Equality is allowed — a unit may be committed to the acre. Exceeding it is `ERR_UNIT_ACREAGE_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. On success the allocation opens with `allocation_status` of `ACTIVE`, a `released_acres` of 0.0000 and a null `termination_reason`; the unit's `unallocated_acres` becomes its previous value less `allocated_acres`, rounded to 4 decimal places; the unit's `active_allocation_count` increments by 1; and the unit's `version` increments by 1. The acreage identity of §2.9 holds across this write. A single allocation, including its released acreage, is readable by a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or an `Operator`, as is a unit's allocation list filtered by `allocation_status` and `tract_id`.

Exercising the pugh clause hands back the non-producing depth share and drives the allocation `ACTIVE → PUGH_RELEASED`. It takes no request body and carries four preconditions beyond the operator identity match. The allocation must be `ACTIVE`; a release attempted on an allocation that is already `PUGH_RELEASED` or `TERMINATED` is `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`. The unit's `pugh_clause_active` must be true, or the request is `ERR_PUGH_CLAUSE_NOT_APPLICABLE`. The logical clock's date must be on or after the unit's `pugh_deadline`, or the request is `ERR_PUGH_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED` — the boundary is inclusive, so the deadline day itself is releasable. And there must be something to release: `producing_depth_count` must be strictly less than `total_depth_count`, because a unit producing from every depth it holds has no dead depth to hand back, and an attempt in that situation is `ERR_PUGH_ALL_DEPTHS_PRODUCING`.

The release arithmetic conserves acreage exactly. The retained figure is the allocation's `allocated_acres` multiplied by `producing_depth_count` and divided by `total_depth_count`, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places. The allocation's `released_acres` becomes its original `allocated_acres` less that retained figure, rounded to 4 decimal places, and only then is `allocated_acres` itself overwritten with the retained figure. The allocation's `allocation_status` becomes `PUGH_RELEASED`; the unit's `unallocated_acres` becomes its previous value plus the allocation's `released_acres`, rounded to 4 decimal places; and the unit's `version` increments by 1. The unit's `active_allocation_count` is *not* decremented, because a pugh-released allocation still carries acreage and still counts. Two downstream consequences matter: the acreage identity of §2.9 is preserved to the digit, and because the depth ratio is applied to `allocated_acres` in place, every subsequent live-variant decimal computation shrinks automatically. There is no separate pugh factor anywhere in the decimal formula, which is what guarantees the depth ratio is never applied twice.

Terminating an allocation is the compensating action for creating one. An `Operator` supplies a `termination_reason`, and the allocation moves to `TERMINATED` from either `ACTIVE` or `PUGH_RELEASED` — attempting it on an already `TERMINATED` allocation is `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`. The `termination_reason` is recorded, the unit's `unallocated_acres` becomes its previous value plus whatever `allocated_acres` remained on the allocation, rounded to 4 decimal places, the unit's `active_allocation_count` decrements by 1, and the unit's `version` increments by 1. Across a create-then-terminate pair no acreage drifts: the unit's total returns to exactly what it was. `TERMINATED` is terminal for an allocation.

### 3.8. Division Orders: the Expected Decimal, the Operator's Response and Corrections

A `DivisionOrderAdmin` issues a division order by naming only three things: the `mineral_interest_id` whose decimal the order sets out, the `unit_id` the order is issued against, and the `expected_unit_version` that §2.7 requires. Everything else on the order is derived. The `royalty_owner_id` — the payee of record — is copied from the interest's `seller_owner_id` and is never client-supplied. The order opens with `do_status` of `SENT`, a `dispute_reason` of `NONE`, a null `pinned_decimal`, a null `operator_decimal`, a null `effective_production_month`, a `cumulative_variance_amount` of 0.00, a `paid_net_total` of 0.00, a `correction_round_count` of 0, and `created_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID` — the counterparty for the disbursement segregation of duties. Issuing an order increments the unit's `version` by 1.

Before any arithmetic happens, the system must pick the allocation the decimal will be built from, and the selector is fully deterministic. The candidate set is every allocation whose `unit_id` matches the order's unit, whose `tract_id` matches the interest's tract, and whose half-open occupancy interval covers the logical clock's date — that is, whose `effective_from` is on or before the clock date and whose `effective_to` is strictly after it. Candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, with ties broken by `created_at` descending, and an allocation's `id` is never used as a ranking key. The first candidate wins, and its identifier is stored on the order as `allocation_id`; that stored pointer is a convenience, since the authoritative link is the allocation's own `unit_id` and `tract_id` pairing, and it is recomputed on every correction. Three failure modes are distinguished here and their ordering is fixed by §2.10: if no allocation row exists for that unit-and-tract pairing in any posture and over any interval, the request is `ERR_TRACT_UNIT_MISMATCH`; if rows exist for the pairing but none of them covers the clock date, the request is `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ALLOCATION`; and if the selected candidate's `allocation_status` is `TERMINATED` rather than `ACTIVE` or `PUGH_RELEASED`, the request is `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The `expected_decimal` is then built from four factors. The **ownership** factor is the interest's `net_mineral_acres` divided by its tract's `gross_acres`. The **participation** factor is the selected allocation's `allocated_acres` divided by the unit's `unit_gross_acres`. The remaining two factors depend entirely on that allocation's `pooling_election`, and the three branches are genuinely different. Under `LEASED_ROYALTY_ONLY` the owner is not a unit participant at all, so the unit's royalty cap does not bind: the effective rate is the interest's `lease_royalty_rate` exactly as written, and the election factor is 1. Under `POOLED` the owner is a participant, so the effective rate is the lesser of the interest's `lease_royalty_rate` and the unit's `royalty_rate_cap`, and the election factor is 1. Under `NON_CONSENT` the effective rate is likewise the lesser of `lease_royalty_rate` and `royalty_rate_cap`, but the election factor is one minus a non-consent penalty of thirty percent, rounded to 4 decimal places — that is, 0.7000.

From the effective rate the **net burden** factor is the effective rate less the interest's `existing_burden_fraction`, rounded half-up to 6 decimal places first, then floored at zero so an overriding-royalty burden heavier than the lease rate produces a net burden of exactly zero rather than a negative one. The `expected_decimal` is the product of the ownership factor, the participation factor, the net burden factor and the election factor, rounded half-up to 8 decimal places once at the very end. Nothing else enters this computation: pugh effects reach it purely through the allocation's already-rewritten `allocated_acres`. A single division order — showing `expected_decimal`, `pinned_decimal` and `operator_decimal` side by side — is readable by a `DivisionOrderAdmin`, an `Operator` or a `RoyaltyOwner`, and a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or an `Operator` may list orders filtered by `mineral_interest_id`, `unit_id`, `royalty_owner_id` and `do_status`.

The operator's single response forks the order into one of two outcomes with markedly different consequences. The `Operator` supplies an `operator_decimal` and an `effective_production_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, and may do so only from `SENT`. On **branch A**, where the supplied `operator_decimal` equals the order's `expected_decimal`, the order moves `SENT → EXECUTED`: the supplied `operator_decimal` is recorded, `pinned_decimal` is set to `expected_decimal` — this is the version-pinning act that fixes the decimal every future revenue check will be reconciled against — the supplied `effective_production_month` is recorded as the first production month the executed order governs — production months earlier than it are not reported against this order — `do_status` becomes `EXECUTED` and `dispute_reason` becomes `NONE`. The revenue channel is now open. On **branch B**, where the supplied `operator_decimal` differs from `expected_decimal`, the order moves `SENT → DISPUTED` instead: the supplied `operator_decimal` is still recorded, `do_status` becomes `DISPUTED`, `dispute_reason` becomes `DECIMAL_MISMATCH`, and — the crucial asymmetry — `pinned_decimal` stays null and `effective_production_month` stays null. Nothing is pinned, and no revenue channel opens.

A disputed order is re-issued through a correction, which is the loop-closing edge `DISPUTED → SENT`. The `Operator` supplies a single `corrected_decimal`, and the correction is permitted only from `DISPUTED`. The system re-runs the allocation selector and the decimal computation against present-day state — the same three failure modes listed above apply — and the supplied `corrected_decimal` must equal that freshly recomputed figure to all 8 decimal places; anything else is `ERR_CORRECTED_DECIMAL_MISMATCH`. On success `expected_decimal` and `allocation_id` are rewritten from the fresh computation, `operator_decimal` is cleared back to null, `do_status` returns to `SENT`, `dispute_reason` returns to `NONE`, and `correction_round_count` increments by 1. The order is then awaiting a fresh operator response, and the cycle may run again. An order also reaches `DISPUTED` from `EXECUTED` without any operator involvement at all, through the ownership-change cascade of §3.6; and an executed order can be pushed to `SUSPENDED` and pulled back, which §3.9 describes.

### 3.9. Revenue Reconciliation, Suspense Resolution and Owner Reporting

Once an order is `EXECUTED`, the `Operator` reports revenue one month and one product stream at a time. Each report names the `production_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, the `product_code` — one of `OIL`, `GAS` or `NGL` — the `gross_volume` for the month (at least zero, at 4 decimal places), the `gross_value` of unit sales for the month before any owner decimal is applied (at least zero, at 2 decimal places), and the `operator_decimal_used`, the decimal the operator actually paid on, at 8 decimal places. Reporting is permitted only against an order whose `do_status` is `EXECUTED`; a `SENT`, `DISPUTED` or `SUSPENDED` order yields `ERR_DIVISION_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. At most one line may exist for any given combination of order, `production_month` and `product_code`; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCTION_MONTH`.

The line's `severance_tax_rate` is looked up from the statutory table by the unit's `state_code` and the line's `product_code`. Texas withholds 4.6000 percent on oil and on natural-gas liquids and 7.5000 percent on gas; Oklahoma withholds a flat 7.0000 percent across all three streams; New Mexico withholds a flat 3.7500 percent across all three; and North Dakota withholds 5.0000 percent on oil and on liquids and 6.5000 percent on gas. The rate is carried at 6 decimal places.

Three parallel computations then run over the same line, and all of them are always performed. The first is what the operator actually paid: `reported_gross_royalty` is `gross_value` multiplied by `operator_decimal_used`, rounded half-up to cents; `severance_tax_amount` is `reported_gross_royalty` multiplied by `severance_tax_rate`, rounded half-up to cents; and `net_amount` is `reported_gross_royalty` less `severance_tax_amount`, rounded half-up to cents. Note carefully that the tax is computed on the *already rounded* gross royalty, never on the raw product of value and decimal — that ordering is contractual throughout this calculation and in both variants below.

The second computation is the settlement variant, built from the order's pinned decimal, and it is the one that moves money. `expected_gross_royalty` is `gross_value` multiplied by the order's `pinned_decimal`, rounded half-up to cents. `expected_net_amount` is `expected_gross_royalty` less the tax on it — that tax being `expected_gross_royalty` multiplied by `severance_tax_rate`, rounded half-up to cents — with the difference again rounded half-up to cents. And `variance_amount` is `expected_net_amount` less `net_amount`, rounded half-up to cents; it may legitimately be negative when the operator overpaid. This variant deliberately ignores every allocation, pooling and pugh change that occurred after the order was executed, because that is exactly what pinning a decimal means.

The third computation is the audit variant, built from the live acreage picture, and it never moves money. `audit_decimal` is the expected-decimal computation of §3.8 re-evaluated *now* against the current allocation, unit and interest state, at 8 decimal places. `audit_gross_royalty` is `gross_value` multiplied by `audit_decimal`, rounded half-up to cents. The audit net is `audit_gross_royalty` less the tax on it — `audit_gross_royalty` multiplied by `severance_tax_rate`, rounded half-up to cents — the difference rounded half-up to cents. And `audit_variance_amount` is that audit net less `net_amount`, rounded half-up to cents; it too may be negative. The audit figures are computed even for lines that settle cleanly, and they exist to answer a different question from the settlement variant: not "did the operator underpay?" but "is the pinned decimal still the right decimal?". After a pugh release the live decimal is strictly smaller than the pinned decimal, so `audit_gross_royalty` and `expected_gross_royalty` diverge for byte-identical inputs — and that divergence is the firm's signal that the order needs correcting.

Routing the line is where precedence matters, and owner status wins over money. If the payee of record's `contact_status` is anything other than `LOCATED`, the line's `check_status` is `VARIANCE_HELD` and the suspense it feeds carries a `suspense_reason` of `UNLOCATABLE_OWNER` — this holds even when `variance_amount` is exactly 0.00. Otherwise, if `variance_amount` is not exactly 0.00, `check_status` is `VARIANCE_HELD` and the `suspense_reason` is `DECIMAL_VARIANCE`. Only when the owner is `LOCATED` and the variance is exactly 0.00 does the line settle: `check_status` becomes `PAID` and the line's `net_amount` is added to the order's `paid_net_total`.

A held line fires a two-hop cascade. The order itself moves `EXECUTED → SUSPENDED` and its `cumulative_variance_amount` becomes its previous value plus the line's `variance_amount`, rounded to cents — a running sum over the order's held lines. Caused by that hop, the second hop opens or reuses the suspense account keyed on the pairing of the order and its payee: the line's `net_amount` is added to `accrued_amount`, `held_detail_count` increments by 1, and `suspense_status` is `ACCRUING`. When the account is newly created by a variance, its `first_suspended_month` is the `production_month` of that first held line. A single revenue line, showing both the settlement variance and the audit variance, is readable by a `DivisionOrderAdmin`, an `Operator` or a `RoyaltyOwner`, as is an order's revenue-line list filtered by `production_month` and `check_status`.

A suspense account is never a static row. Four of its figures are recomputed against the logical clock on every read of the account and on every write that touches it. `accrued_amount` is the sum of `net_amount` across the parent order's revenue lines whose `check_status` is `VARIANCE_HELD`, rounded to cents — a plain order-insensitive sum, and lines already flipped to `PAID` by a previous release are excluded, which is exactly why a release drives `accrued_amount` to precisely 0.00. `held_detail_count` is the count of that same set. `months_held` is a whole count of calendar months: the clock's year minus the year of `first_suspended_month`, multiplied by twelve, plus the clock's month minus the month of `first_suspended_month`. `interest_accrued` is `accrued_amount` multiplied by an annual statutory rate of six percent, multiplied by `months_held`, divided by twelve, rounded half-up to cents. And `release_amount` — what a release would pay out — is `accrued_amount` plus `interest_accrued`, rounded half-up to cents. As §2.9 records, that interest sits deliberately outside the conserved revenue quantity. A single suspense account, with all four figures fresh as of the logical clock, is readable by a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or a `RoyaltyOwner`, and both roles may list accounts filtered by `division_order_id`, `royalty_owner_id`, `suspense_status` and `suspense_reason`.

Releasing an account is the compensating action for a variance hold, and a `DivisionOrderAdmin` performs it with an empty body. Four gates apply. The account's `suspense_status` must be `ACCRUING`; an already `RELEASED` or `ESCHEATED` account is `ERR_SUSPENSE_STATE_INVALID`. The payee of record's `contact_status` must be `LOCATED`, or the request is `ERR_OWNER_UNLOCATABLE`. That owner's `tax_id_on_file` must be true, or the request is `ERR_OWNER_TAX_ID_MISSING` — the firm does not disburse without a signed tax-identification form. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the parent order's `created_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success `months_held`, `interest_accrued` and `release_amount` are recomputed against the logical clock, `suspense_status` moves `ACCRUING → RELEASED`, `released_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `accrued_amount` and `held_detail_count` are zeroed to 0.00 and 0. The cascade then reaches the parent order: every one of its revenue lines whose `check_status` was `VARIANCE_HELD` becomes `PAID`, the order's `paid_net_total` becomes its previous value plus the released `accrued_amount` rounded to cents, its `cumulative_variance_amount` resets to 0.00, and its `do_status` returns `SUSPENDED → EXECUTED` so the revenue channel re-opens. The revenue identity of §2.9 is preserved exactly across this pair.

Escheat is the other exit and is emphatically *not* a compensating action. A `DivisionOrderAdmin` supplies an `escheat_state_code` — the owner's own `state_code` of residence being the natural default destination — and the account moves `ACCRUING → ESCHEATED`. Escheat is lawful only for an account whose `suspense_reason` is `UNLOCATABLE_OWNER`; an account held for a `DECIMAL_VARIANCE` or an `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER` yields `ERR_ESCHEAT_REASON_INVALID`. And it is lawful only once the statutory dormancy period has run: `months_held` must be at least thirty-six whole calendar months — three full years — and a shorter holding period is `ERR_ESCHEAT_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. The boundary is inclusive, so exactly thirty-six months qualifies. On success the accrual figures are recomputed, `suspense_status` becomes `ESCHEATED`, the supplied `escheat_state_code` is recorded, and `accrued_amount` and `held_detail_count` are zeroed. The parent division order deliberately stays `SUSPENDED`: the money left for the state, the owner was never paid, and the revenue channel does not re-open. `RELEASED` and `ESCHEATED` are both terminal for a suspense account.

The last piece of the revenue side is the owner's annual statement, a purely computed read available to a `DivisionOrderAdmin` or to the `RoyaltyOwner` itself. It takes a single `tax_year` and returns eight figures: the `royalty_owner_id` it was computed for, the `tax_year` echoed back, `gross_royalty`, `severance_tax_withheld`, `taxable_before_depletion`, `depletion_allowance`, `net_taxable` and `detail_count`. Its source set is every revenue line whose `check_status` is `PAID`, belonging to any division order whose `royalty_owner_id` is this owner, whose `production_month` falls in the twelve months from the January of the requested year through the December of that year, both ends inclusive. `gross_royalty` is the sum of `reported_gross_royalty` over that set, rounded to cents; `severance_tax_withheld` is the sum of `severance_tax_amount` over the same set, rounded to cents; and `detail_count` is the count of the set. Because held lines are excluded until a release flips them to `PAID`, a suspense release retroactively enlarges an owner's statement — which is intentional.

Percentage depletion looks circular and is not, so its evaluation order is normative and single-pass. First, `taxable_before_depletion` is `gross_royalty` less `severance_tax_withheld`, rounded to cents, with depletion *not* yet subtracted. Second, `depletion_allowance` is the lesser of two figures — fifteen percent of `gross_royalty`, and sixty-five percent of `taxable_before_depletion` — rounded to cents; the statutory rate on gross revenue is thus capped by a share of income measured *before* depletion. Third, `net_taxable` is `taxable_before_depletion` less `depletion_allowance`, rounded to cents. Feeding `net_taxable` back into the second step is forbidden: the fixed point is defined by this one pass and never by iteration. When the owner has no paid line in the requested year, the statement is not an error — it returns successfully with `gross_royalty`, `severance_tax_withheld`, `taxable_before_depletion`, `depletion_allowance` and `net_taxable` all 0.00 and `detail_count` 0.
