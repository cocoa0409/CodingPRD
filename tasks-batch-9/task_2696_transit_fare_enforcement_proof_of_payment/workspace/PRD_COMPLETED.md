# Transit Fare Enforcement & Proof of Payment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Barrier-free boarding is the operational premise of every modern proof-of-payment network: riders board without passing a turnstile, and compliance is verified after the fact by inspectors working sampled deployments inside legally designated zones. That premise only holds if the agency can prove three things at once — that the zone was properly signed and designated at the moment of the encounter, that the inspection was a statistically defensible sample rather than a discretionary sweep, and that the money assessed against a rider was calculated from a published progressive schedule rather than an inspector's judgement. This service is the system of record for all three.

The platform this document specifies covers the full compliance chain. Enforcement planners designate proof-of-payment zones only after a physical signage audit demonstrates that the statutory notices are actually posted; they attach transit lines to those zones, plan sampled inspection deployments with an explicit random-sampling design and target sample size, and close each deployment only once the achieved sample is adequate. Field inspectors record encounters, derive a fare-media verdict from the proof the rider presented, and resolve each encounter into one of four dispositions — compliant, warning, citation, or unresolved identity. Citations carry a progressive penalty, an administrative fee, and a reduction floor; riders may contest them before a hearing officer, appeal a hearing outcome to a board member who did not decide the hearing, and settle a balance by direct payment, an installment plan, or supervised community service. Two read-only analytics surfaces close the loop: an equity report that measures how citation rates vary by demographic band and residence tract, and a revenue-recovery report that reconciles what the agency billed or banked against what enforcement cost to staff.

Two design commitments run through every rule below. The first is **decriminalization discipline**: a citation is a revocable administrative instrument, so any finding that the zone was not properly designated, that the fare was in fact valid, or that identity was mistaken must unwind not only the citation but every settlement artifact hanging off it — the active plan, the active service assignment, and the cash already banked. The second is **evidentiary determinism**: every temporal rule reads a single logical clock the test harness controls, every money figure is an integer number of cents, and every rate is an integer number of basis points, so two readers of the same record set always compute the same answer.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, empty, or holds nothing but whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the six declared roles exactly — the comparison is case-sensitive and surrounding whitespace is not trimmed before it — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the role is declared but is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). These two checks precede all others: authentication first, then role membership, then everything else.

The six roles are `EnforcementPlanner`, `FareInspector`, `Rider`, `HearingOfficer`, `AppealsBoardMember`, and `EquityAnalyst`. Their remits are deliberately disjoint, because the three decision stages of a citation — issuance, administrative adjudication, and appellate review — are held by three different roles.

An `EnforcementPlanner` is the agency's enforcement planner. Planners designate and suspend proof-of-payment zones, file signage audits, maintain the line network and the inspector and rider registries, plan, activate, close and void sampled deployments, reissue citations dismissed on procedural grounds, assess plan delinquency, log community-service hours, and read both analytics reports. A `FareInspector` works an active deployment in the field: recording sampled encounters, validating presented fare media, handling identity capture, and issuing either a warning or a citation. A `Rider` is a cited rider who contests a citation, files, supplements and withdraws appeals, and settles a balance. A `HearingOfficer` adjudicates contested citations, upholding, reducing or dismissing them. An `AppealsBoardMember` requests supplementation of an appeal and grants or denies it. An `EquityAnalyst` is strictly read-only: the analyst measures citation distribution by demographic band and geographic tract and reconciles revenue against enforcement cost, and holds no write authority over any business record.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against foreign keys on the target resource. When `X-User-Role` is `FareInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector` record; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Inspector` compare that resource's inspector foreign key to `X-User-ID`, so recording or resolving an encounter and issuing a citation from it require `X-User-ID` to equal the encounter's `inspector_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Rider`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Rider` record; ownership checks compare the resource's rider foreign key to `X-User-ID`, so a rider may only act on a citation whose `rider_id` is their own, on an appeal whose `appellant_rider_id` is their own, and on a payment plan whose citation's `rider_id` is their own.

When `X-User-Role` is `EnforcementPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a scaffolded transition writes it — as `auditor_user_id` on a `SignageAudit` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `HearingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it is recorded as `hearing_officer_user_id` on the hearing the officer decides, but is not matched against any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `AppealsBoardMember`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier; it is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` on the appeal the member decides, and — uniquely in this system — it is compared against the `hearing_officer_user_id` stored on the hearing under appeal, but never against an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `EquityAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, is not recorded on any business record, and is not compared to any entity's `id`.

Two ownership failures carry dedicated codes. A `Rider` acting on a resource that belongs to a different rider is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `FareInspector` acting on an encounter whose `inspector_id` is a different inspector is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ON_ENCOUNTER` (HTTP 403). The segregation-of-duties rule for appellate review carries its own code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), described in §2.10 and §3.8.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it does not move when a caller sets that clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no lower than 1, and may be no higher than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no lower than 0. Either parameter supplied with an empty value is treated as omitted and falls back to its default; either one that is not an integer, or that falls outside its declared bounds, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order — the oldest record first, and where two records share the same `created_at` instant, in the order in which they were created — and this default ordering applies to every list surface in the API unless a specific report defines its own ordering. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered collection before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any records remain beyond the current page.

Beyond pagination, several list surfaces accept filters that narrow the collection before paging: zones by `designation_status`; zone-line assignments by `zone_id` and `line_id`; deployments by `line_id`, `zone_id` and `status`; encounters by `deployment_id`, `inspector_id`, `rider_id` and `outcome`; citations by `rider_id`, `zone_id`, `encounter_id`, `issuing_inspector_id`, `reissued_from_citation_id`, `adjudication_status` and `settlement_status`; hearings by `citation_id`, `requested_by_rider_id` and `status`; appeals by `citation_id`, `hearing_id`, `appellant_rider_id` and `status`; payment plans by `citation_id` and `status`; and community-service assignments by `citation_id` and `status`. Every one of these filters is optional; omitting it, or supplying it with an empty value, leaves the collection unfiltered. A filter value is compared literally against the stored field and is not validated against the enum it draws from, so a `status` or `outcome` filter naming a value outside the declared set is not rejected — it simply matches no record and returns an empty page with `total_count` 0.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted record is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix — a citation's `id` and a fare zone's `id` are indistinguishable in form, and clients must not attempt to infer a record's kind from its identifier. Every record also carries a `created_at` timestamp, expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock at the moment the record came into being. Wherever this document describes a field as empty — an unstamped instant such as `designated_at` or `decided_at`, an unset code such as `suspension_reason` or `void_reason`, a `plan_id` on a ledger row that belongs to no plan, an omitted optional string such as `deficiency_note` or `media_reference` — the field is nevertheless always present in the serialized representation and carries the JSON value `null`; it is never omitted from the payload and never rendered as an empty string or a zero.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. `FareZone`, `TransitLine`, `Inspector`, `Rider`, `InspectionDeployment`, `InspectionEncounter`, `Citation`, `AdjudicationHearing`, `Appeal`, `PaymentPlan` and `CommunityServiceAssignment` all carry the full set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Three record kinds are immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations entirely. `ZoneLineAssignment` is an immutable junction row attaching a line to a zone. `SignageAudit` is an append-only physical count. `PenaltyPayment` is an append-only ledger row. None of the three is ever rewritten; corrections are made by appending a new row, and in the ledger's case by appending a reversal.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Temporal Windows

Every temporal rule in this system — contest deadlines, hearing scheduling, appeal and supplement windows, reissue windows, installment due dates, inspector certification validity, and deployment window admission — is evaluated against a single deterministic logical clock rather than wall-clock time. At process start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Any role may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response echoes the value now in effect as `current_utc`. Moving the clock advances or rewinds it to exactly the supplied instant and modifies no business record whatsoever.

Two interval conventions are used, and they are not interchangeable. A **half-open** interval includes its start instant and excludes its end instant: a deployment's operating window, an encounter-admission window, a deployment-overlap comparison, and both analytics report windows are all half-open. A **closed** interval includes both endpoints: the zone concurrency-capacity comparison in §3.4 uses closed intervals, so two deployments that merely touch at an instant do count as intersecting for capacity purposes even though they do not overlap for the same-line collision rule. Where a report or a deployment declares a window, `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`; a request that violates this, or that carries an instant which is unparseable or not expressed in UTC, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Deployment windows carry the additional requirement that `window_start` and `window_end` be a whole number of minutes apart, enforced under the same code. Instants that are not part of a declared window — a signage audit's `audited_at`, an assignment's `effective_from`, an inspector's `certification_expiry`, an encounter's `occurred_at`, `media_validated_at` and `media_expires_at`, and the clock-setting `to_iso8601` — are screened by the request-body schema step instead, so one that is unparseable or not expressed in UTC is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) and never with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. By the same division, a required query-string parameter that is absent or supplied empty — a report's `window_start`, `window_end`, `group_by` or `basis` — is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and only a window parameter that is present but malformed or non-UTC reaches `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. An instant is accepted with or without fractional digits and with either a `Z` or a `+00:00` designator; any non-zero offset is treated as not being UTC. An instant supplied with finer than millisecond precision keeps that precision for every comparison the rules make, but is rendered back to three fractional digits by truncation rather than rounding, so a clock set to `…:00.123456Z` echoes `…:00.123Z`.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error returned. Each resource kind has its own not-found code, all at HTTP 404: `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CITATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SERVICE_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Reading the signage audits filed against a zone, the payments applied to a citation, or the payments booked against a plan therefore resolves the parent identifier first and returns that parent's not-found code before it considers the sub-collection. The one thing that precedes that resolution is the page window itself: the `limit` and `offset` parameters of §2.3 are screened before the parent identifier is looked up, so a malformed page window supplied against a parent that does not exist reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than the parent's not-found code.

The same discipline governs foreign-key identifiers arriving in a request body, with one refinement: they are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and again the first unresolvable reference decides the response. Creating a zone-line assignment resolves `zone_id` before `line_id`; planning a deployment resolves `line_id` before `zone_id`; recording an encounter resolves `deployment_id`, then `inspector_id`, then `rider_id` when one is supplied; filing an appeal resolves `citation_id` before `hearing_id`. Body-borne reference failures reuse the same 404 codes as path failures.

### 2.7. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer numbers of **cents**. There are no fractional cents anywhere: penalties, administrative fees, totals, balances, installments, service credits and enforcement costs are all whole-cent integers. All rates and scaling factors are integer **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is exactly one whole unit — so a `penalty_multiplier_bps` of 10000 leaves a penalty unchanged, and 12500 raises it by one quarter.

Wherever a division or a multiplicative scaling produces a non-integer, the result is rounded **half up** — a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Ratios that are exposed as decimals rather than basis points — `signage_compliance_pct`, `sample_adequacy_ratio`, and the equity report's `systemwide_citation_rate`, `citation_rate`, `rate_delta` and `disparity_index` — are carried to exactly **4 decimal places**, also rounded half up. The two families differ in how they are serialized, and the difference is deliberate: `signage_compliance_pct` and `sample_adequacy_ratio` are emitted as JSON **numbers**, so a computed 0.9000 appears in the payload as `0.9` and a computed 1.0000 as `1.0`, whereas the equity report's four figures are emitted as decimal **strings** that retain all four places, as §3.9 restates. The one place where a different rounding mode is mandated is the installment schedule, which divides with a floor and pushes the whole remainder into the final installment; §3.9 states that rule in full.

Rounding **order** is normative throughout and is never a matter of implementer discretion. Where a calculation applies two multiplicative steps in sequence, each step is rounded before the next consumes its result; aggregating unrounded intermediates and rounding once at the end produces observably different figures and is forbidden. The three places where this bites hardest — the penalty ladder, the enforcement-cost legs, and the equity disparity index — each restate the pinned order at the point of use.

### 2.8. Version Pinning on Settlement Writes

A citation's money identity moves for reasons a client cannot see: a hearing officer may reduce the penalty, a planner may log service hours, a dismissal may write the balance off. Two settlement writes therefore pin the client's view of the citation with an optimistic-concurrency check.

Opening an installment plan against a citation requires the request to carry `expected_citation_version`, a non-optional input field holding the `version` value the client last observed on that citation. If that value does not equal the citation's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CITATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The check cannot be replaced by a state precondition, because a new plan legitimately supersedes a `DEFAULTED` or `CANCELLED` predecessor and may also forfeit an active community-service alternative; only the version comparison pins the client's reading of `outstanding_balance_cents` and `settlement_status` at the instant the plan terms were computed.

Opening a community-service alternative against a citation is guarded identically: the request requires `expected_citation_version` carrying the client's last observed citation `version`, and a value that does not equal the citation's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CITATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the exposure is `required_hours`, which is derived from `outstanding_balance_cents` at creation time — a stale view would lock in an hour count the ledger no longer supports — and again a new assignment legitimately follows a `FORFEITED` or `CANCELLED` predecessor, so state alone cannot screen the write.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Direct payments, installment payments, hearing decisions, appeal decisions, delinquency assessments, service-hour logs, zone transitions and deployment transitions are all unversioned writes.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate more than one record, and the secondary effects are as much part of the contract as the primary one. Three cascades recur, and each is stated in full at its point of origin in §3, but their shape is worth fixing here.

The **zone-suspension cascade** has three links. A zone leaving `DESIGNATED` for `SUSPENDED` — whether by an administrative hold or by a signage audit that drops compliance below threshold — voids every deployment in that zone whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE`, stamping `void_reason` as `ZONE_SUSPENDED`; each deployment voided by that first link then voids its own encounters whose `outcome` is still `PENDING`; and each such deployment then recomputes `achieved_sample_count`, `voided_encounter_count` and `sample_adequacy_ratio` from its surviving encounter set. The zone's own `version` is incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed.

The **zone-revocation cascade** extends further, because revocation is a finding that the zone was never validly designated. In addition to voiding `PLANNED` and `ACTIVE` deployments with `void_reason` `ZONE_SUSPENDED`, it voids every live citation written in that zone and then unwinds each citation's settlement artifacts and its banked cash.

The **dismissal-unwind cascade** is the common tail shared by an administrative dismissal, a granted appeal, and a revocation-driven voiding. Its links run in order: the citation's `adjudication_status` moves to a terminal value and `dismissal_reason_code` and `dismissed_at` are stamped; then `settlement_status` becomes `WRITTEN_OFF` and `outstanding_balance_cents` is set to 0, while every `ACTIVE` payment plan for that citation becomes `CANCELLED` and every `ACTIVE` community-service assignment for that citation becomes `CANCELLED`; then, if the citation's `amount_paid_cents` was greater than zero immediately before the cascade, a reversal ledger row is appended whose amount equals that pre-cascade paid figure, driving `amount_paid_cents` to zero; and finally the affected rider's `prior_citation_count` and the issuing inspector's `citations_issued_count` are recomputed. Each record the cascade touches has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When several rules could reject the same request, they are evaluated in a fixed order and the first failure is the one reported. The ordering is not an implementation detail: a test that supplies a request violating two rules expects the earlier rule's code. From first to last, the precedence chain runs as follows.

Header presence and role recognition come first (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then endpoint role membership (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`), then request-body schema — required fields present, correct JSON types, enum members drawn from their declared sets, and numeric fields within their declared ranges (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400, on every POST endpoint). Four schema conventions are fixed. A field supplied with an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, so a required field sent as `null` fails as missing and an optional field sent as `null` falls back to its default. An integer field accepts only a JSON integer — a boolean and a number carrying a fractional part are both type failures, even where the fractional part is zero. A required string field rejects the empty string, since its minimum length is 1. And a POST body that is absent, unparseable as JSON, or parseable but not a JSON object is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an absent or unparseable body is reported through whichever required field it fails to supply. Next comes time-window well-formedness (`ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`) on deployment planning and on both report reads. Then path-identifier resolution, then body foreign-key resolution, both returning the resource-specific 404 codes of §2.6.

Version pinning is evaluated next: `expected_citation_version` is compared against the citation's current `version` on plan creation and on service-assignment creation, yielding `ERR_STALE_CITATION_VERSION`. The three ownership and separation checks follow in order — rider ownership (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), inspector-on-encounter (`ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ON_ENCOUNTER`), and appellate segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, raised when the acting `AppealsBoardMember`'s `X-User-ID` equals the `hearing_officer_user_id` recorded on the hearing the appeal was filed against; this guard covers both requesting a supplement and deciding an appeal).

State-machine admissibility comes next, in this order: zone state, deployment state, deployment-must-be-active for encounter recording, encounter-must-be-pending, no-open-encounters-on-close, citation adjudication state, citation settlement state, hearing state, hearing appealability, appeal state, plan state, and service-assignment state. That listing carries one refinement: where an endpoint is addressed at a hearing or at an appeal and also inspects the citation standing behind it, the addressed record's own state is screened first, so deciding or cancelling a hearing whose `status` is not `SCHEDULED` reports `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULED` even when that hearing's citation is also outside `CONTESTED`, and deciding an appeal that is not in a live status reports `ERR_APPEAL_STATUS_INVALID` even when the citation is also outside `UPHELD` and `REDUCED`. Business-safety rules follow: zone designation, signage threshold, inspector duty status, inspector certification currency, identity establishment, the warning-first policy gate, and disposition support. Then business-referential rules: line-to-zone assignment, duplicate assignment, duplicate codes, encounter-already-cited, hearing-citation match, appeal-already-open, alternative-already-active, and reissue eligibility. Then temporal rules: encounter-in-window, deployment window overlap, zone deployment capacity, contest window, appeal window, supplement window, and reissue window. Then remaining constraint rules: appeal round limit, sample adequacy, installment-count range, decision-field consistency, diversion eligibility, and service-hours validity. Fiscal rules are evaluated last, in this order: down-payment minimum, down-payment ceiling, reduction floor, payment amount, and nothing-outstanding.

The practical consequences of this ordering are worth stating explicitly. Because version pinning precedes every state check, a plan request carrying a stale `expected_citation_version` against a citation that is also in the wrong `settlement_status` reports `ERR_STALE_CITATION_VERSION`, not `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATUS_INVALID`. Because state checks precede business-safety checks, an attempt to cite an encounter that is no longer `PENDING` inside a zone that has since been suspended reports `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_PENDING`, not `ERR_ZONE_NOT_DESIGNATED`. Because referential checks precede temporal ones, a reissue of a citation that was dismissed for the wrong reason and outside the reissue window reports `ERR_REISSUE_NOT_PERMITTED`, not `ERR_REISSUE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And because fiscal checks come last, a plan request whose down payment is below the minimum but whose `installment_count` is also out of range reports `ERR_INSTALLMENT_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` first.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designating a Proof-of-Payment Zone

A proof-of-payment zone is the legal container for barrier-free enforcement, and nothing may be enforced inside it until the agency can demonstrate that riders were told the rules. Registration therefore produces a zone in `DRAFT` and nothing more. The planner supplies a `zone_code` — the code printed on platform signage, between 3 and 32 characters long and unique across every zone record regardless of that zone's `designation_status`, so a revoked zone's code is not recycled — together with a human-readable `name` and a `required_sign_count` of at least 1, being the number of proof-of-payment notices statute requires inside the zone. A duplicate `zone_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Three further inputs are optional and carry defaults: `penalty_multiplier_bps`, the zone's penalty scaling factor in basis points, defaults to 10000 and must be at least 10000 and no more than 15000; `enforcement_policy`, the citation-versus-warning stance in force inside the zone, defaults to `CITATION_IMMEDIATE` and must be either `WARNING_FIRST` or `CITATION_IMMEDIATE`; and `max_concurrent_deployments`, the aggregate cap on overlapping inspection activity, defaults to 2 and must be at least 1 and no more than 5. A newly registered zone opens with `verified_sign_count` at 0 and `signage_compliance_pct` at 0.0, with `designated_at` and `suspension_reason` both empty.

Before the zone can enter the signage queue at least one transit line must be attached to it; any attachment naming that zone satisfies the requirement, regardless of its `effective_from`, which is not compared against the logical clock or against anything else at this gate. Submitting a zone for signage moves `designation_status` from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_SIGNAGE` and simultaneously clears `suspension_reason`; a zone in `DRAFT` with no line attachment is not yet submittable and the transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_STATE`, as is any attempt to submit a zone whose current status is neither `DRAFT` nor `SUSPENDED`. The `SUSPENDED` arm is the system's recovery path: a suspended zone re-enters the signage queue by the same operation, with only its current status checked, and may be designated again once its signage is fixed. Either way the zone's `version` increments by 1 and `updated_at` is refreshed.

Signage evidence arrives as an append-only physical count. Filing one names the `zone_id` whose notices were counted, the `audited_at` instant the count was taken, a `signs_verified_count` of compliant notices physically verified — which may be 0 — and an optional `deficiency_note` describing missing or illegible notices. The server stamps `auditor_user_id` with the acting planner's `X-User-ID`; because a signage audit is immutable, that stamp is the only provenance the record carries. `audited_at` is screened only for well-formedness and is never compared against the logical clock, so an audit may be filed bearing an instant in the future and, being the greatest `audited_at` on the zone, becomes the authoritative count immediately.

Filing an audit immediately recomputes the zone's two derived signage fields, and the selection rule is precise. Among all audits filed against the zone — there is no status filter, since audits have no lifecycle — exactly one is authoritative: the audit with the greatest `audited_at`, and where two audits share the same `audited_at`, the one with the later `created_at`, and where both instants are identical, the one filed later in the request sequence. The tie-break is `created_at` and never `id`. That audit's `signs_verified_count` becomes the zone's `verified_sign_count`, and `signage_compliance_pct` becomes `verified_sign_count` divided by `required_sign_count`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, then capped so that a zone with more verified notices than statute requires reports exactly 1.0000 rather than a figure above one. When no audit exists for the zone at all, `verified_sign_count` is 0 and `signage_compliance_pct` is 0.0000. The same recomputation happens when a single zone is read, so a zone's compliance figure is always current with its audit history. Filing an audit also registers a mutation on the named zone unconditionally: the zone's `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed on every accepted filing, whether or not the new count is the authoritative one, whether or not it changes the derived figures, and whether or not it trips the automatic suspension described below.

The statutory signage floor is 90 percent, expressed as a compliance figure of 0.9000. Designating a zone moves `designation_status` from `PENDING_SIGNAGE` to `DESIGNATED`, and requires both that at least one signage audit exists for the zone and that `signage_compliance_pct` is at least 0.9000; a zone that fails either condition is refused with `ERR_SIGNAGE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, and a zone whose status is not `PENDING_SIGNAGE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_STATE`. On success the zone's `designated_at` is stamped with the logical clock — recording the instant of the most recent entry into `DESIGNATED`, so a zone designated twice carries the later instant — and its `version` increments by 1.

Signage decay is policed automatically, with no operator call. When an audit is filed that becomes the authoritative audit for a zone whose `designation_status` is `DESIGNATED`, and the recomputed `signage_compliance_pct` falls below 0.9000, the zone transitions `DESIGNATED → SUSPENDED` inside that same request with `suspension_reason` set to `SIGNAGE_DEFICIENCY`, and the three-link zone-suspension cascade fires: every deployment in the zone whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `ZONE_SUSPENDED`; every encounter under each of those voided deployments whose `outcome` is still `PENDING` becomes `VOIDED`; and each affected deployment then recomputes `achieved_sample_count`, `voided_encounter_count` and `sample_adequacy_ratio`. The zone's `version` increments by 1.

An `EnforcementPlanner` may also suspend a `DESIGNATED` zone deliberately, which sets `suspension_reason` to `ADMINISTRATIVE_HOLD` rather than `SIGNAGE_DEFICIENCY` but fires exactly the same three-link cascade with the same `void_reason` `ZONE_SUSPENDED` on the affected deployments and the same counter recomputation on each. A suspend request against a zone whose status is not `DESIGNATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_STATE`.

Revocation is the terminal and most consequential zone transition, admissible from either `DESIGNATED` or `SUSPENDED` and refused with `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_STATE` from any other status. Moving `designation_status` to `REVOKED` is a finding that enforcement inside the zone was never validly grounded, so it voids far more than deployments. Every deployment in the zone whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `ZONE_SUSPENDED`. Every citation written in the zone whose `adjudication_status` is `ISSUED` or `CONTESTED` moves to `VOID` with `dismissal_reason_code` set to `ZONE_NOT_DESIGNATED` and `dismissed_at` stamped from the logical clock; where such a citation had a hearing in `SCHEDULED`, that hearing becomes `CANCELLED`. Each voided citation then takes the dismissal-unwind tail: `settlement_status` becomes `WRITTEN_OFF` with `outstanding_balance_cents` set to 0, every `ACTIVE` payment plan on that citation becomes `CANCELLED`, every `ACTIVE` community-service assignment on that citation becomes `CANCELLED`, and — where the citation's `amount_paid_cents` was greater than zero immediately before the cascade — a reversal ledger row is appended whose `source` is `DISMISSAL_REVERSAL`, whose `amount_cents` equals that pre-cascade `amount_paid_cents`, whose `plan_id` is empty and whose `applied_at` is the logical clock, driving `amount_paid_cents` to 0. Finally, `prior_citation_count` is recomputed for every rider whose citation was touched and `citations_issued_count` is recomputed for every inspector who issued one, and the zone's own `version` increments by 1. Revocation neither clears nor writes `suspension_reason`: a zone revoked out of `SUSPENDED` keeps whichever reason suspended it, and a zone revoked directly out of `DESIGNATED` keeps the empty value it already held. `designated_at` is likewise left exactly as it stood. `REVOKED` is terminal: no transition leaves it.

### 3.2. The Line Network, the Inspector Roster and the Rider Registry

Three registries feed enforcement planning, and all three are maintained by the `EnforcementPlanner`.

A transit line is registered with a `line_code` unique across all lines, a `mode` drawn from `BUS`, `LIGHT_RAIL`, `COMMUTER_RAIL` and `STREETCAR`, a `daily_boardings` figure of at least 1 that serves as the sampling-frame size for that line, and a `baseline_evasion_rate_bps` between 0 and 10000 inclusive, being the prior-survey evasion rate in basis points that planners use to size a random sample. A duplicate `line_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Attaching a line to a zone creates an immutable junction record naming the `zone_id` of the proof-of-payment zone the line segment sits inside, the `line_id` of the line whose platforms carry the zone's signage, a `platform_segment_count` of at least 1 counting the line's platform segments inside the zone, and an `effective_from` instant from which the line becomes enforceable inside the zone, inclusive of that instant. Only one attachment may exist for a given zone-and-line pair; a second attempt at the same pair is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. These records are never edited — they carry no revision counter and no modification timestamp — so a change of geometry is expressed by attaching a different line rather than rewriting an attachment.

An inspector is registered with a `badge_number` unique across all inspectors, a `display_name` that is printed on the citations the inspector writes, and a `certification_expiry` instant at which the inspector's fare-enforcement certification lapses. That instant is an exclusive upper bound on validity: certification is current strictly before it. The optional `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and must be either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. A newly registered inspector's `citations_issued_count` opens at 0; thereafter it is the count of citations whose `issuing_inspector_id` is that inspector and whose `adjudication_status` is one of `ISSUED`, `CONTESTED`, `UPHELD` or `REDUCED` — so a citation that reaches `DISMISSED` or `VOID` stops counting against the inspector the moment it does. A duplicate `badge_number` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

A rider record carries a `rider_ref` unique across all riders, a `display_name` recorded during identity capture, a `demographic_band` drawn from `BAND_A`, `BAND_B`, `BAND_C`, `BAND_D` and `UNDISCLOSED`, and a `residence_geo_tract` holding the census tract code of the rider's residence. The demographic band is self-reported and is used only for aggregate equity analysis; the tract code serves the geographic arm of the same analysis. The optional `fare_assistance_enrolled` flag defaults to false and is true when the rider is enrolled in the agency's reduced-fare assistance programme — a fact that halves the penalty ladder, as §3.5 explains. Two counters open at 0 and are maintained thereafter: `prior_warning_count` is the number of encounters for this rider whose `outcome` is `WARNING_ISSUED`, and `prior_citation_count` is the number of citations for this rider whose `adjudication_status` is one of `ISSUED`, `CONTESTED`, `UPHELD` or `REDUCED`. A duplicate `rider_ref` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Reading a rider record is not owner-scoped: a `Rider` is authorised for the read by role alone and may read any rider's record, not merely their own, so `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` never arises on this endpoint.

### 3.3. Planning, Activating and Closing a Sampled Deployment

An inspection deployment is the agency's unit of sampling design: one team, one line, one zone, one time window, one statistical target. Planning one names the `line_id` of the line the team will work and the `zone_id` of the proof-of-payment zone being enforced, bounds the shift with a `window_start` that is included and a `window_end` that is excluded, funds it with a `planned_inspector_count` of at least 1 and no more than 20, declares a `sampling_method` drawn from `SYSTEMATIC_INTERVAL`, `CLUSTER_RANDOM` and `STRATIFIED_TIME`, and states a `target_sample_size` of at least 1 — the number of encounters the design requires for a statistically usable evasion estimate. The window must be well formed: `window_end` strictly later than `window_start`, and the two instants a whole number of minutes apart, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. An absent `window_start` or `window_end`, or one supplied as something other than a string, fails earlier at the schema step with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; only an instant that is present as a string but is unparseable or not expressed in UTC reaches `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

Three admission rules then apply, and each has its own code. First, the line must actually be enforceable inside the zone: an attachment must exist for that exact zone-and-line pair whose `effective_from` is at or before the requested `window_start`, and where none does the request is refused with `ERR_LINE_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_ZONE`. Second, no two live deployments may collide on the same line: if any other deployment on the same `line_id` whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED` has a half-open window that shares any instant with the requested half-open window, the request is refused with `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because both windows are half-open, adjacency is permitted — a deployment ending at 14:00 and another starting at 14:00 do not collide. Third, the zone's own concurrency cap is respected: the count of deployments on the same `zone_id` whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED` and whose window treated as a **closed** interval — both endpoints included — shares any instant with the requested window treated as a closed interval must be strictly less than that zone's `max_concurrent_deployments`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_DEPLOYMENT_CAPACITY`. The deliberate asymmetry between the two interval conventions means two touching deployments on different lines in the same zone do count against the zone cap even though touching deployments on one line do not collide.

A planned deployment opens in `status` `PLANNED` with `achieved_sample_count` at 0, `voided_encounter_count` at 0 and `sample_adequacy_ratio` at 0.0. Its three closing measurements — `measured_evasion_rate_bps`, `resolved_evasion_rate_bps` and `enforcement_cost_cents` — are empty until the deployment closes, and `void_reason` is empty unless the deployment is voided.

Activation moves `status` from `PLANNED` to `ACTIVE` and is the moment enforcement authority actually attaches. Two conditions guard it beyond the from-state. The governing zone's `designation_status` must be `DESIGNATED` at that moment, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_DESIGNATED`; and the logical clock must have reached `window_start`, so a shift cannot be opened early. A deployment whose `status` is not `PLANNED`, or whose window has not yet begun, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEPLOYMENT_STATE`. The three guards are evaluated in exactly the order just given — from-state first, then zone designation, then the clock — so although the clock guard reports a state code it is the last of the three to run, and a `PLANNED` deployment whose window has not begun inside a zone that is not `DESIGNATED` reports `ERR_ZONE_NOT_DESIGNATED` rather than `ERR_INVALID_DEPLOYMENT_STATE`. Activation carries no upper temporal bound: the clock is compared against `window_start` alone, so a shift whose `window_end` has already passed may still be activated.

A planner may abandon a shift from either `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE`, moving `status` to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `PLANNER_CANCELLED` — distinguishing a planner's own cancellation from the `ZONE_SUSPENDED` voiding the cascades produce. Voiding also moves every encounter under the deployment whose `outcome` is `PENDING` to `VOIDED`, and then recomputes `achieved_sample_count`, `voided_encounter_count` and `sample_adequacy_ratio`. The deployment's `version` increments by 1. A void request against a deployment already `CLOSED` or `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEPLOYMENT_STATE`.

Sample adequacy is the gate on closing, and it is computed continuously rather than only at close: after every encounter created under a deployment and after every encounter voided beneath it, again when the deployment is closed, and again on every read of the deployment — so all three counters are always current with the deployment's surviving encounter set, including after the deployment has reached `CLOSED` or `VOIDED`, `achieved_sample_count` is recomputed as the count of that deployment's encounters whose `outcome` is anything other than `VOIDED`, `voided_encounter_count` as the count whose `outcome` is `VOIDED`, and `sample_adequacy_ratio` as `achieved_sample_count` divided by `target_sample_size`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Unlike `signage_compliance_pct`, this ratio is **not** capped at 1.0000: a deployment that over-samples its target reports the figure above one that the division actually yields, so 12 surviving encounters against a target of 10 reads 1.2. Note that encounters still sitting in `PENDING` do count toward `achieved_sample_count`, even though a deployment holding any such encounter cannot be closed.

Closing moves `status` from `ACTIVE` to `CLOSED` and demands two things. No encounter under the deployment may still be in `PENDING`; if one is, the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_ENCOUNTERS_REMAIN`. And `sample_adequacy_ratio` must be at least 0.8000 — that is, the achieved sample must have reached at least 80 percent of the target. The gate is inclusive: a ratio of exactly 0.8000 closes the deployment, while 0.7999 is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_SIZE_INADEQUATE`. A close request against a deployment whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEPLOYMENT_STATE`.

Closing is also the moment the agency's two evasion figures are frozen, and the two are deliberately different. An encounter counts as non-compliant when its `validation_result` is any of `EXPIRED`, `UNVALIDATED_TAP`, `WRONG_ZONE` or `NO_PROOF`. For `measured_evasion_rate_bps` — the statutory rate the agency publishes — the pool is every encounter of the deployment whose `outcome` is not `VOIDED`; the figure is the number of non-compliant encounters in that pool, multiplied by 10000 and divided by the size of the pool, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points, and is 0 when the pool is empty. For `resolved_evasion_rate_bps` — the rate used for equity-adjusted internal planning — the pool additionally excludes every encounter whose `outcome` is `UNRESOLVED_IDENTITY`, and that exclusion applies to the count of non-compliant encounters and to the pool size alike; the arithmetic and the empty-pool rule are otherwise identical. The consequence is that the published rate treats an identity refusal as an observed rider — inflating the pool, and inflating the non-compliant count as well wherever that encounter's own `validation_result` was non-compliant — whereas the internal rate drops those encounters entirely, so the two figures diverge whenever at least one `UNRESOLVED_IDENTITY` encounter exists and the surviving pool is not uniformly non-compliant. An `UNRESOLVED_IDENTITY` encounter is not necessarily non-compliant: as §3.4 sets out, that disposition is admitted on the strength of `identity_capture_method` alone, so an encounter whose verdict is `VALID` may carry it, and such an encounter enlarges the published pool without adding anything to the published numerator.

Enforcement cost is frozen at the same instant and is a function of the shift, not of how many riders were checked; encounter counts have no effect on it whatsoever. Take the whole number of minutes between `window_start` and `window_end`. Of those minutes, the first six hours — 360 minutes — are regular time, and everything beyond 360 minutes is overtime, with the overtime figure floored at zero for shorter shifts. One inspector's cost is the regular minutes multiplied by the regular hourly rate of 4800 cents and divided by 60, rounded half up to whole cents, plus the overtime minutes multiplied by the overtime hourly rate of 7200 cents and divided by 60, also rounded half up to whole cents; the two legs are rounded separately. That per-inspector figure is then multiplied by `planned_inspector_count` to give the labour total. A zone premium of 500 basis points — 5 percent of the labour total, rounded half up — is added when and only when the governing zone's `penalty_multiplier_bps` is strictly greater than 10000; for a zone at exactly 10000 the premium is zero. `enforcement_cost_cents` is the labour total plus that premium. The order is pinned: round each per-inspector leg first, then multiply by head count, then apply the premium to the multiplied labour total — never premium first, and never a single rounding at the end.

### 3.4. The Inspection Encounter: Fare Media Validation and Identity Handling

Recording an encounter is the field inspector's core act. The submission names the `deployment_id` of the deployment the encounter was sampled under and the `inspector_id` of the inspector who conducted it, states the `occurred_at` instant, describes the proof presented, and declares how identity was established. A `rider_id` is supplied whenever identity was established at all and is omitted when the rider refused to identify themselves — that is, whenever `identity_capture_method` is `REFUSED`. `identity_capture_method` itself is one of `GOVT_ID`, `SELF_REPORTED` or `REFUSED`. That correspondence is a recording convention rather than an admission rule: `rider_id` is an ordinary optional field and no guard couples it to `identity_capture_method`, so an encounter submitted with `REFUSED` capture together with a `rider_id`, or with `GOVT_ID` capture and no `rider_id`, is accepted exactly as submitted and the inconsistency is felt only later, at the disposition guards of §3.4 and the identity guard of §3.5.

The fare-media description has five fields. `fare_media_type` is the class of proof presented, one of `SMARTCARD`, `MOBILE_QR`, `PAPER_TRANSFER`, `CASH_RECEIPT` or `NONE`. `media_reference` is the serial, token or receipt number of the presented media and is omitted when `fare_media_type` is `NONE` — again a convention only, since no guard couples the two and `media_reference` never participates in the verdict. `media_validated_at` is the instant the media was tapped or otherwise validated; its absence means no validation record exists. `media_expires_at` is the instant the presented media stops being valid; its absence means the media carries no expiry record at all. `media_zone_code` is the zone code the presented media was sold for, and is compared against the `zone_code` of the deployment's zone.

Four guards run before anything is recorded. The named deployment must be in `status` `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; because a zone leaving `DESIGNATED` voids its `ACTIVE` deployments, this rule is also what makes it impossible to record an encounter inside a zone that is no longer designated. The inspector named by `inspector_id` must have `status` `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_SUSPENDED`. That inspector's `certification_expiry` must be strictly after the encounter's `occurred_at` — certification lapsing at exactly `occurred_at` is already lapsed — failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_CERT_EXPIRED`. And `occurred_at` must fall at or after the deployment's `window_start`, strictly before its `window_end`, and at or before the current logical clock, so an inspector can neither log a rider outside the shift nor post-date an encounter into the future; a violation is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`.

Once admitted, the server derives `validation_result` from the presented media and freezes it on the row; it is never recomputed afterwards, so a later change to the zone or the media has no effect on an existing encounter. The verdict is one of `VALID`, `EXPIRED`, `UNVALIDATED_TAP`, `WRONG_ZONE` or `NO_PROOF`, and the evaluation order is normative and must not be reordered, because two media classes check the same conditions in different sequence.

If `fare_media_type` is `NONE`, the verdict is `NO_PROOF` and nothing further is examined. If `fare_media_type` is `SMARTCARD` or `MOBILE_QR` — the electronic classes — the checks run in this order: when `media_validated_at` is absent the verdict is `UNVALIDATED_TAP`; otherwise when `media_zone_code` differs from the deployment zone's `zone_code` the verdict is `WRONG_ZONE`; otherwise when `media_expires_at` is absent the verdict is `NO_PROOF`; otherwise when `media_expires_at` is strictly earlier than `occurred_at` the verdict is `EXPIRED`; otherwise the verdict is `VALID`. If `fare_media_type` is `PAPER_TRANSFER` or `CASH_RECEIPT` — the paper classes — expiry is examined **before** zone: when `media_expires_at` is absent the verdict is `NO_PROOF`; otherwise when `media_expires_at` is strictly earlier than `occurred_at` the verdict is `EXPIRED`; otherwise when `media_zone_code` differs from the deployment zone's `zone_code` the verdict is `WRONG_ZONE`; otherwise the verdict is `VALID`. A paper transfer that is both expired and sold for the wrong zone therefore reads `EXPIRED`, while an equivalent smartcard reads `WRONG_ZONE`. Two boundary rules complete the definition: media whose `media_expires_at` equals `occurred_at` exactly is **not** expired, since the comparison is strictly earlier; and `media_validated_at` is never compared to `occurred_at` — its presence or absence is all that matters. Only fields on the encounter row plus the `zone_code` of the deployment's zone participate in the verdict.

A new encounter opens with `outcome` `PENDING`, and creating it refreshes the parent deployment's `achieved_sample_count` and `sample_adequacy_ratio`. That refresh is not itself counted as a mutation of the deployment: the deployment's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they were, and the same holds when an encounter is resolved, so a deployment's revision counter moves only on its own activate, close and void transitions. From `PENDING` the encounter forks four ways, and the fourth fork is not a resolution call at all.

Resolving an encounter carries a single `disposition` field and covers three of the four branches. The acting inspector's `X-User-ID` must equal the encounter's `inspector_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ON_ENCOUNTER`, and the encounter's `outcome` must still be `PENDING`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_PENDING`. Each branch demands that the disposition be consistent with what the media actually showed, and a mismatch on any branch is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_SUPPORTED`.

The first branch, `PENDING → COMPLIANT`, requires `validation_result` to be exactly `VALID`. It sets `outcome` to `COMPLIANT`, increments the encounter's `version` by 1, and changes no rider counter at all. The second branch, `PENDING → WARNING_ISSUED`, requires `validation_result` to be one of `EXPIRED`, `UNVALIDATED_TAP`, `WRONG_ZONE` or `NO_PROOF`, and additionally requires the encounter to have a `rider_id` — a warning must be attributable to somebody. It sets `outcome` to `WARNING_ISSUED`, recomputes that rider's `prior_warning_count`, and increments the encounter's `version` by 1. This is the only branch that raises the warning-inclusive rung of the progressive ladder for that rider's future citations. The third branch, `PENDING → UNRESOLVED_IDENTITY`, requires `identity_capture_method` to be `REFUSED`. It sets `outcome` to `UNRESOLVED_IDENTITY` and increments the encounter's `version` by 1; no rider counter moves, because there is no identified rider. This branch is counted in the published evasion rate and excluded from the internal one, as §3.3 describes.

The fourth branch, `PENDING → CITATION_ISSUED`, is reached only by actually issuing a citation, described next; it is the only branch that mints a citation record. The link between the two records is carried by the citation alone, whose `encounter_id` names the encounter it was written from: the encounter row records no reference back to the citation, and no citation identifier appears among the encounter's fields. A fifth terminal value, `VOIDED`, is never chosen by an inspector: an encounter reaches `VOIDED` only when its parent deployment is voided, whether by a planner's cancellation or by the zone-suspension or zone-revocation cascade, and only while its `outcome` is still `PENDING`. That automatic transition also causes the parent deployment to recompute `achieved_sample_count` and `voided_encounter_count`. All five values other than `PENDING` — `COMPLIANT`, `WARNING_ISSUED`, `CITATION_ISSUED`, `UNRESOLVED_IDENTITY` and `VOIDED` — are terminal; an encounter never returns to `PENDING`.

### 3.5. Citation Issuance and the Progressive Penalty Ladder

Issuing a citation is the heaviest act in the system, and the request that does it is deliberately minimal: the inspector supplies only the `encounter_id` of the encounter the citation is written from. Everything else — who the rider is, which zone governs, which inspector is credited, what the penalty is — is derived by the server from the encounter and the records behind it, so an inspector cannot negotiate any of it.

Seven guards stand in front of issuance. The encounter's `outcome` must still be `PENDING`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_PENDING`. The acting inspector's `X-User-ID` must equal the encounter's `inspector_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ON_ENCOUNTER`. That inspector's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_SUSPENDED`, and their `certification_expiry` must be strictly after the encounter's `occurred_at`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_CERT_EXPIRED` — an inspector whose certification lapsed between the encounter and the paperwork cannot write the citation. The encounter's `validation_result` must be one of `EXPIRED`, `UNVALIDATED_TAP`, `WRONG_ZONE` or `NO_PROOF`; a citation written against a `VALID` verdict is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_SUPPORTED`. Identity must have been established: the encounter must carry a `rider_id` and its `identity_capture_method` must be either `GOVT_ID` or `SELF_REPORTED`, so an encounter recorded with `REFUSED` identity capture can never be cited and is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED`. The zone governing the encounter's deployment must have `designation_status` `DESIGNATED` at the moment of issuance, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_DESIGNATED`. And no citation may already reference that encounter — one encounter, one live citation — failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_ALREADY_CITED`; the single exception is a reissue of a citation dismissed for a procedural defect, described in §3.6. The order in which those guards are listed above is narrative rather than normative; the evaluation order is the precedence chain of §2.10, which for this endpoint runs: inspector-on-encounter, then encounter-must-be-`PENDING`, then zone designation, then inspector duty status, then inspector certification currency, then identity establishment, then the warning-first gate of the next paragraph, then disposition support, and last the one-citation-per-encounter rule. Two consequences are observable: an encounter that is no longer `PENDING` recorded by a different inspector reports `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ON_ENCOUNTER`, and an encounter whose verdict is `VALID` and whose `identity_capture_method` is `REFUSED` reports `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED` rather than `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_SUPPORTED`.

A zone whose `enforcement_policy` is `WARNING_FIRST` adds an eighth guard, and it is the decriminalization lever the agency actually tunes. Inside such a zone a rider's first contact must be a warning, not a citation: the warning-inclusive ladder position computed for this rider must be at least 2. A first-contact rider in a `WARNING_FIRST` zone is therefore refused with `ERR_WARNING_REQUIRED_FIRST_OFFENSE`, and the inspector's only lawful disposition is a warning. Inside a zone whose `enforcement_policy` is `CITATION_IMMEDIATE` this guard does not apply and a first offence may be cited directly.

The ladder itself is computed over a rolling one-year lookback — 365 days — and it is computed twice, in two variants that differ only in whether warnings count as rungs. The lookback window is half-open at its lower edge and inclusive at its upper edge: it opens strictly after the instant 365 days before the citation's `issued_at` and closes at `issued_at` itself. Within that window the server counts two things. **Prior citations** are the citations for the same rider, excluding the citation being written, whose `issued_at` falls inside that window and whose `adjudication_status` is one of `ISSUED`, `CONTESTED`, `UPHELD` or `REDUCED` — a citation that has reached `DISMISSED` or `VOID` stops counting the moment it is dismissed, which is exactly why a successful dismissal lowers the rung of every subsequent citation. **Prior warnings** are the encounters for the same rider whose `outcome` is `WARNING_ISSUED` and whose `occurred_at` falls inside the same window. Both counts are ordered strictly by those timestamps and never by `id`.

From those two counts the two variants follow. `offense_sequence`, the warning-inclusive variant, is 1 plus the prior-warning count plus the prior-citation count; it is written onto the citation and it is the figure that drives the assessed penalty and the `WARNING_FIRST` gate above. `citation_only_sequence`, the citation-only variant, is 1 plus the prior-citation count alone; it ignores warnings entirely and is written onto the citation as the basis for the reduction floor. For a rider whose only prior contact in the window was a warning, the citation-only variant returns 1 where the warning-inclusive variant returns 2 — a strictly lower rung and therefore, for identical inputs, a strictly lower money figure.

Penalty assessment turns a rung into cents through four pinned steps. **Step one, tier lookup**: rung 1 draws a base of 7500 cents, rung 2 draws 15000 cents, and every rung of 3 or higher draws 25000 cents — the ladder flattens at the third offence rather than growing without bound. **Step two, zone scaling**: that base is multiplied by the zone's `penalty_multiplier_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents, so a zone at 12000 basis points assesses one fifth more than the statutory base. **Step three, the assistance discount**: when the rider's `fare_assistance_enrolled` flag is true, the scaled figure is multiplied by the residual of 10000 basis points less the 5000-basis-point assistance discount and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents — in practice exactly half the scaled figure — and when the flag is false the scaled figure passes through untouched. **Step four, the clamp**: the result is raised to the penalty floor of 2500 cents if it falls below it, and lowered to the penalty cap of 27500 cents if it rises above it. Each multiplicative step is rounded before the next consumes its result; computing the whole chain and rounding once at the end is forbidden.

That same four-step assessment is run twice with different rungs. Fed the `offense_sequence`, it yields `assessed_penalty_cents`, the penalty actually charged. Fed the `citation_only_sequence`, it yields `reduction_floor_cents`, the lowest penalty an adjudicator may later reduce the citation to. Both figures use the same zone multiplier and the same assistance flag, so the floor is never above the assessed penalty and is strictly below it whenever a warning contributed a rung.

The administrative fee is banded, and the band's key is the single most easily mistaken detail in the whole schedule. The fee schedule is published as a band on what the rider owes, which looks circular because the total owed includes the fee — but the band is keyed on `assessed_penalty_cents`, the pre-fee penalty, and never on `total_due_cents`. An assessed penalty at or below 10000 cents attracts 800 basis points; an assessed penalty above 10000 but at or below 20000 cents attracts 1200 basis points; anything above 20000 cents attracts 1500 basis points. `admin_fee_cents` is the assessed penalty multiplied by the applicable basis points and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents, and `total_due_cents` is `assessed_penalty_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`. At an assessed penalty of exactly 20000 cents the pinned order yields a fee of 2400 and a total of 22400; keying the band on the total instead would yield 3000 and 23000. The pinned order is normative.

A freshly issued citation is stamped with the encounter it came from and copies of the parties involved: `rider_id` and `issuing_inspector_id` are copied from the encounter, and `zone_id` is copied from the encounter's deployment. Its `issued_at` is the logical clock at issuance, and `reissued_from_citation_id` is empty for an originally issued citation. Its `contest_deadline_at` is `issued_at` plus 30 days and is the inclusive last instant at which a hearing may be requested. It opens with `amount_paid_cents` at 0 and `service_credit_cents` at 0, with `outstanding_balance_cents` equal to `total_due_cents`, with `dismissal_reason_code` and `dismissed_at` both empty, with `adjudication_status` `ISSUED` and `settlement_status` `OUTSTANDING`. Issuance also flips the source encounter's `outcome` to `CITATION_ISSUED` and increments that encounter's `version` by 1, and recomputes the rider's `prior_citation_count` and the inspector's `citations_issued_count` — both of which rise by one as the new citation joins the live set.

The two status fields on a citation form a **parallel state machine**: `adjudication_status` tracks whether the citation stands as a legal finding, `settlement_status` tracks how the money is being handled, and the two dimensions evolve independently. A citation can be `UPHELD` and `PLAN_ACTIVE` at the same time, or `ISSUED` and `SATISFIED`. Only one coupling runs between them, and it runs one way: whenever `adjudication_status` reaches `DISMISSED` or `VOID`, `settlement_status` is forced to `WRITTEN_OFF`.

Money on a citation obeys one identity, recomputed after every ledger append, after every reduction, after every settlement transition, and again on every read of the citation. `amount_paid_cents` is the sum of the amounts of that citation's ledger rows whose `source` is `DOWN_PAYMENT`, `PLAN_INSTALLMENT` or `DIRECT`, less the sum of those whose `source` is `DISMISSAL_REVERSAL`. `service_credit_cents` is the sum of the amounts of that citation's ledger rows whose `source` is `SERVICE_CREDIT`, kept separate from cash because service credit is worked off rather than paid. `outstanding_balance_cents` is `total_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents` less `service_credit_cents`, floored at 0 so it can never be negative — and it is unconditionally 0 whenever `settlement_status` is `SATISFIED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, regardless of what the ledger sums to. Because the ledger is append-only and carries no status of its own, the order in which rows are summed cannot affect the result, and every figure is exact integer cents with no rounding at any point.

### 3.6. Administrative Adjudication, Dismissal and Reissuance

A rider who disputes a citation has 30 days from issuance to say so. Contesting requires the citation's `adjudication_status` to be exactly `ISSUED` — a citation already `CONTESTED`, `UPHELD`, `REDUCED`, `DISMISSED` or `VOID` is refused with `ERR_CITATION_STATUS_INVALID` — and requires the logical clock to be at or before the citation's `contest_deadline_at`, that instant itself still counting as inside the window; a later request is refused with `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Only the cited rider may contest: the acting `Rider`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the citation's `rider_id`, and any other rider is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Because an `ISSUED` citation by construction has no hearing in `SCHEDULED`, the requirement that no scheduled hearing already exist for the citation is enforced by the same status check.

A successful contest moves the citation `ISSUED → CONTESTED`, increments its `version` by 1, and creates the administrative hearing that will decide it. The new hearing records the `citation_id` of the contested citation and a `requested_by_rider_id` copied from the citation's `rider_id`, opens in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `decision` `PENDING`, and has `scheduled_for` set to the logical clock plus 14 days — the statutory lead time between a contest and its hearing. Its `hearing_officer_user_id` and `decided_at` are both empty for as long as the hearing remains `SCHEDULED`, and so are its `dismissal_reason_code` and `reduced_penalty_cents`. Because the resource brought into existence by a contest is the hearing, the call returns **201 Created** carrying that new hearing record in `data` rather than the contested citation; a client that needs the citation's new `adjudication_status` reads the citation back from its own endpoint.

A `HearingOfficer` may cancel a `SCHEDULED` hearing, which is the system's only reverting arm on the adjudication dimension. Cancellation requires the hearing's `status` to be `SCHEDULED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULED` — and the citation to be `CONTESTED`. It sets the hearing's `status` to `CANCELLED`, moves the citation's `adjudication_status` back `CONTESTED → ISSUED`, and increments both records' `version` by 1. The citation is then contestable again, subject to the same 30-day window it always had.

Deciding a hearing is a three-way branch, and the branch is chosen by the `decision` field, which must be `UPHOLD`, `REDUCE` or `DISMISS`. The hearing's `status` must be `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULED`) and the referenced citation's `adjudication_status` must be `CONTESTED` (otherwise `ERR_CITATION_STATUS_INVALID`). The two optional inputs are branch-bound, and the binding is strict in both directions: `dismissal_reason_code` must be supplied when the decision is `DISMISS` and must be absent for `UPHOLD` and `REDUCE`; `reduced_penalty_cents` must be supplied when the decision is `REDUCE` and must be absent for `UPHOLD` and `DISMISS`; an `UPHOLD` therefore carries neither. A request that omits a field its branch requires, or supplies one its branch forbids, is refused with `ERR_DECISION_FIELDS_INVALID`.

Whichever branch is taken, the hearing itself is finalised identically: `status` moves `SCHEDULED → HELD`, `decision` is set to the submitted value, `decided_at` is stamped with the logical clock — starting the appeal window — and `hearing_officer_user_id` is stamped with the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, which is what later makes appellate segregation of duties enforceable. The hearing's `version` and the citation's `version` each increment by 1. Both of the hearing transitions — deciding and cancelling — answer **200 OK** carrying the updated hearing record in `data` whichever branch was taken, never the citation they moved.

The `UPHOLD` branch moves the citation `CONTESTED → UPHELD` and changes no money field. The penalty stands, the fee stands, and the settlement dimension is untouched.

The `REDUCE` branch moves the citation `CONTESTED → REDUCED` and rewrites the money. `reduced_penalty_cents` must be at least the citation's `reduction_floor_cents` and strictly below its current `assessed_penalty_cents` — a reduction to the floor is permitted, a reduction to the existing penalty is not a reduction, and anything below the floor is refused with `ERR_REDUCTION_BELOW_FLOOR`. On success `assessed_penalty_cents` becomes the submitted figure, `admin_fee_cents` is re-derived from scratch by re-banding on the **new** pre-fee penalty — so a reduction that crosses a band boundary changes the fee rate as well as the fee — and `total_due_cents` and `outstanding_balance_cents` are recomputed from the identity in §3.5. A rider who had already paid more than the reduced total therefore sees the balance floor at 0 rather than going negative.

The `DISMISS` branch moves the citation `CONTESTED → DISMISSED`, stamps `dismissal_reason_code` with the submitted reason and `dismissed_at` with the logical clock, and then runs the dismissal-unwind cascade in full. The reason must be one of `SIGNAGE_DEFICIENT`, `VALID_FARE_PRESENTED`, `IDENTITY_ERROR`, `ZONE_NOT_DESIGNATED`, `FIRST_OFFENSE_DIVERSION` or `PROCEDURAL_DEFECT`, and one of those six carries an eligibility test: `FIRST_OFFENSE_DIVERSION` may be used only where the citation's `offense_sequence` is exactly 1, and using it on any higher rung is refused with `ERR_DIVERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The cascade that follows a dismissal runs in order: `settlement_status` becomes `WRITTEN_OFF` and `outstanding_balance_cents` becomes 0; every payment plan on this citation whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `CANCELLED` and every community-service assignment on this citation whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `CANCELLED`; then, where the citation's `amount_paid_cents` immediately before the cascade was greater than zero, a ledger row is appended with `source` `DISMISSAL_REVERSAL`, `amount_cents` equal to that pre-cascade paid figure, an empty `plan_id` and `applied_at` set to the logical clock, which drives `amount_paid_cents` to 0; and finally the rider's `prior_citation_count` and the issuing inspector's `citations_issued_count` are recomputed, both falling by one as the citation leaves the live set. Service credit already earned is not reversed — only cash is.

Reissuance exists for exactly one situation: the citation was dismissed because the paperwork was defective, not because the rider was innocent. An `EnforcementPlanner` may reissue only a citation whose `adjudication_status` is `DISMISSED`, and any other status is refused with `ERR_CITATION_STATUS_INVALID`. That citation's `dismissal_reason_code` must be `PROCEDURAL_DEFECT`; a citation dismissed for signage deficiency, a valid fare, an identity error, an undesignated zone or first-offence diversion may never be reissued, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REISSUE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The window is 14 days: the logical clock must be at or before the dismissed citation's `dismissed_at` plus 14 days, and a later request is refused with `ERR_REISSUE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Because the target citation is dismissed rather than live, it does not itself block the reissue under the one-citation-per-encounter rule — but any other citation written from the same encounter that has not itself reached `DISMISSED` or `VOID` does, and such a live sibling is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_ALREADY_CITED`. That sibling test is evaluated immediately after the from-status check and therefore ahead of both the `PROCEDURAL_DEFECT` reason test and the 14-day window test, so a second reissue attempted while the first reissued citation is still live reports `ERR_ENCOUNTER_ALREADY_CITED` rather than `ERR_REISSUE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

A reissue mints a new citation rather than resurrecting the old one. The new record carries `reissued_from_citation_id` pointing at the citation it replaces — the only place in the system where a citation references another citation — and inherits the original citation's `issued_at`, so the rider gains no advantage and suffers no prejudice from the agency's clerical delay; `contest_deadline_at` is consequently 30 days after that inherited instant. Both ladder positions are recomputed at that original issuance instant, the penalty is reassessed through the same four pinned steps, and the administrative fee band is re-derived from the reassessed penalty. The new citation opens with `adjudication_status` `ISSUED` and `settlement_status` `OUTSTANDING`, and the rider's `prior_citation_count` and the inspector's `citations_issued_count` are incremented as it joins the live set. `DISMISSED` and `VOID` are terminal on the adjudication dimension; the original citation stays dismissed forever, is not mutated by the reissue in any way, and is not the record the call returns — a reissue answers **201 Created** carrying the newly minted citation in `data`.

### 3.7. Appellate Review

Appellate review sits above the hearing, and its defining constraint is who may conduct it. Per the agency's segregation-of-duties rule, the officer who decided a citation's administrative hearing may not act on the appeal of that same hearing: whenever an `AppealsBoardMember`'s `X-User-ID` equals the `hearing_officer_user_id` recorded on the hearing the appeal was filed against, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. That guard applies to both board-member operations — requesting a supplement and deciding the appeal — and it is why the hearing stamps the deciding officer's identifier at all.

Filing an appeal is the rider's act. The submission names the `citation_id` under appellate review, the `hearing_id` of the hearing being appealed, a `grounds_code` drawn from `SIGNAGE_DEFICIENCY`, `IDENTITY_ERROR`, `VALID_FARE_EVIDENCE`, `PROCEDURAL_DEFECT` and `HARDSHIP`, and a written `statement` for the current round. Only the cited rider may file: the acting `Rider`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the citation's `rider_id`, and anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Only an adverse outcome is appealable — the named hearing's `status` must be `HELD` and its `decision` must be `UPHOLD` or `REDUCE`, so a hearing still `SCHEDULED`, one that was `CANCELLED`, one whose `decision` is still `PENDING`, and one that already dismissed the citation are all refused with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_APPEALABLE`. The two identifiers must agree: the named hearing's own `citation_id` must equal the `citation_id` in the body, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_HEARING_CITATION_MISMATCH`. Appellate review is a single bite of the apple, so a citation that already carries an appeal in `SUBMITTED` or `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_OPEN` — and the same code covers a citation whose earlier appeal already reached `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, since a decided appeal exhausts review of that hearing. `WITHDRAWN` is the one earlier status that does not block: a withdrawn appeal is skipped by this test entirely, so the rider may file a fresh appeal on the same citation for as long as the window below remains open. Finally the window: the logical clock must be at or before the hearing's `decided_at` plus 21 days, and a later filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

A filed appeal opens in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `round_index` at 1 and `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and its `appellant_rider_id` is copied from the citation's `rider_id`. Its `supplement_deadline_at` is empty — that field carries a value only while the appeal is awaiting supplementation — and `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `dismissal_reason_code` are all empty while the appeal is undecided.

The supplementation cycle is the appeal's one loop. A board member may return a `SUBMITTED` appeal for more material, moving it `SUBMITTED → SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` and setting `supplement_deadline_at` to the logical clock plus 14 days; the appeal's `version` increments by 1. The appellant then resupplies, moving it back `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` with a fresh `statement`, `round_index` raised by 1, `submitted_at` reset to the logical clock, and `supplement_deadline_at` cleared; again `version` increments by 1. Resubmission is the rider's own act — the acting `Rider`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the appeal's `appellant_rider_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — and must arrive at or before `supplement_deadline_at`, a later resubmission being refused with `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The loop is bounded at 2 rounds: both the supplement request and the resubmission require `round_index` to be strictly below 2, so an appeal that has already been resubmitted once cannot cycle again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUND_LIMIT`. A supplement request against an appeal whose `status` is not `SUBMITTED`, and a resubmission against an appeal whose `status` is not `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED`, are both refused with `ERR_APPEAL_STATUS_INVALID`.

Deciding an appeal is a two-way branch on a `decision` of `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, and the appeal may be decided out of either live state — `SUBMITTED` or `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` — with any other status refused as `ERR_APPEAL_STATUS_INVALID`. The citation must be standing on an adverse finding: its `adjudication_status` must be `UPHELD` or `REDUCED`. Field binding mirrors the hearing's: `dismissal_reason_code` must be supplied when the decision is `GRANTED` and must be absent when it is `DENIED`, and a violation is refused with `ERR_DECISION_FIELDS_INVALID`; where the supplied reason is `FIRST_OFFENSE_DIVERSION`, the citation's `offense_sequence` must be exactly 1 or the request is refused with `ERR_DIVERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Both branches stamp `decided_at` with the logical clock and `decided_by_user_id` with the acting member's `X-User-ID`, and increment the appeal's `version` by 1. Every one of the four appeal transitions — supplement request, resubmission, decision and withdrawal — answers **200 OK** carrying the updated appeal record in `data`, never the citation, even where a grant dismissed that citation and unwound its settlement.

A grant is the widest cascade a single request can trigger, and it runs in four ordered links. First, the appeal's own `status` becomes `GRANTED`. Second, caused by that, the citation moves from `UPHELD` or from `REDUCED` to `DISMISSED`, with `dismissal_reason_code` taken from the appeal's own `dismissal_reason_code` and `dismissed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Third, caused by that, `settlement_status` becomes `WRITTEN_OFF` with `outstanding_balance_cents` set to 0, every `ACTIVE` payment plan on the citation becomes `CANCELLED`, and every `ACTIVE` community-service assignment on the citation becomes `CANCELLED`. Fourth, caused by that, where the citation's `amount_paid_cents` immediately before the cascade was greater than zero, a ledger row is appended with `source` `DISMISSAL_REVERSAL`, `amount_cents` equal to that pre-cascade paid figure, an empty `plan_id` and `applied_at` set to the logical clock, driving `amount_paid_cents` to 0. The rider's `prior_citation_count` and the issuing inspector's `citations_issued_count` are then recomputed downward, and the citation's `version` increments by 1.

A denial is deliberately inert on the citation. The appeal moves to `DENIED`, and the citation's `adjudication_status` and `settlement_status` are both left exactly as they were — an upheld citation stays `UPHELD`, a reduced one stays `REDUCED`, and any live plan or service assignment keeps running. A rider may also abandon an appeal from either live state, moving it to `WITHDRAWN` with `decided_at` stamped from the logical clock and `version` incremented by 1 while `decided_by_user_id` and `dismissal_reason_code` are both left empty, since a withdrawal is the appellant's own act rather than a board decision; withdrawal likewise leaves the citation with its hearing outcome intact, and requires the acting rider to be the appeal's `appellant_rider_id`. `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

### 3.8. Settling a Balance: Direct Payment, Installment Plans and Community Service

A citation's money is settled through a single append-only ledger. Every row records the `citation_id` it settles or reverses, a `plan_id` naming the installment plan it belongs to for down payments and installments and empty for everything else, an `amount_cents` that is always a strictly positive magnitude, a `source` drawn from `DOWN_PAYMENT`, `PLAN_INSTALLMENT`, `DIRECT`, `SERVICE_CREDIT` and `DISMISSAL_REVERSAL`, an `hours_credited` figure that is 0 on every row except a service credit, and an `applied_at` instant taken from the logical clock, which is the instant the cash-basis revenue report keys on. Reversals are recorded as positive rows with `source` `DISMISSAL_REVERSAL` and are subtracted by the balance identity rather than stored as negatives. No row is ever amended or deleted.

Three settlement routes are open, and the settlement dimension of the citation records which is in play. Each of the three writes addressed at the citation itself — a direct payment, opening an installment plan, and opening a community-service assignment — requires the citation to be adjudicatively live: `adjudication_status` must be one of `ISSUED`, `UPHELD` or `REDUCED`, and a citation that is `CONTESTED`, `DISMISSED` or `VOID` is refused with `ERR_CITATION_STATUS_INVALID`. The two writes addressed at a plan instead — paying an installment and assessing delinquency — do not inspect the citation's `adjudication_status` at all; they are gated on the plan's own `status` alone, so a rider who contests the citation after opening a plan may still pay the plan's remaining installments while the citation sits in `CONTESTED`. When a `Rider` is the actor rather than an `EnforcementPlanner`, the acting rider's `X-User-ID` must match the citation's `rider_id`, or the plan's citation's `rider_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

**Direct payment.** A rider or a planner may pay against a citation whose `settlement_status` is `OUTSTANDING`; any other settlement state is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATUS_INVALID`. The submitted `amount_cents` must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than the citation's current `outstanding_balance_cents`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`; the field itself carries no declared range at the schema step, so a zero or negative amount reaches this fiscal test rather than failing as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Only after that does the requirement that the citation have something left to pay apply — `outstanding_balance_cents` strictly greater than 0, otherwise `ERR_NOTHING_OUTSTANDING`. Because the amount test runs first and already rejects every amount that exceeds the balance, a payment posted against a citation that is still `OUTSTANDING` but whose balance a reduction has floored at 0 reports `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`, and `ERR_NOTHING_OUTSTANDING` is in practice unreachable on this endpoint. A partial payment is allowed and simply appends a `DIRECT` ledger row with `applied_at` set to the logical clock, raising `amount_paid_cents` by the paid amount and lowering `outstanding_balance_cents` accordingly while the citation stays `OUTSTANDING`. A payment that exactly equals the outstanding balance closes the citation out: `settlement_status` moves `OUTSTANDING → SATISFIED` and `outstanding_balance_cents` is set to 0. Either way the citation's `version` increments by 1.

**Opening an installment plan.** The request names the `citation_id` being financed, an `installment_count` of scheduled installments after the down payment, a `down_payment_cents` collected at plan creation, the `expected_citation_version` described in §2.8, and an optional `supersede_alternative` flag that defaults to false. `installment_count` must be at least 2 and no more than 6; anything outside that range is refused with `ERR_INSTALLMENT_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422) and never with the schema step's `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The schema step checks this field only for presence and for JSON integer type; its range is a constraint rule evaluated late, after version pinning, after rider ownership, after both citation status checks and after the alternative-already-active rule — so a plan request naming an `installment_count` of 9 against a citation whose `settlement_status` is `SATISFIED` reports `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATUS_INVALID`, not the range code. The down payment must be at least 10 percent of the citation's current `outstanding_balance_cents`, computed as that balance multiplied by 1000 basis points and divided by 10000 with half-up rounding to whole cents; a smaller down payment is refused with `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. It must also be strictly less than `outstanding_balance_cents`, since a plan with nothing left to finance is not a plan; a down payment equal to or above the balance is refused with `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, and this bound also guarantees the financed amount is at least as large as the installment count.

The settlement-state rule for plan creation carries the system's one deliberate exception. Ordinarily `settlement_status` must be `OUTSTANDING`, and `SATISFIED`, `WRITTEN_OFF` and `PLAN_ACTIVE` are all refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATUS_INVALID`. `SERVICE_ACTIVE`, however, passes the settlement-state rule regardless of what `supersede_alternative` says, because the live community-service alternative is adjudicated by a later rule instead: where the citation has an `ACTIVE` community-service assignment or an `ACTIVE` payment plan, a request with `supersede_alternative` false is refused with `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, while a request with `supersede_alternative` true is admitted and supersedes it. The practical effect is that a rider switching from service to cash is refused with `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_ALREADY_ACTIVE` rather than `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATUS_INVALID` when they forget the flag.

The schedule is computed from the balance at the moment the plan opens. `financed_cents` is the citation's `outstanding_balance_cents` less `down_payment_cents`. `base_installment_cents` is `financed_cents` divided by `installment_count` and rounded **down** to whole cents. The remainder — `financed_cents` less `base_installment_cents` multiplied by `installment_count` — is pushed entirely into the last installment, so `final_installment_cents` is `base_installment_cents` plus that remainder. The schedule is therefore the base amount repeated for every installment but the last, followed by the final amount, and it sums to `financed_cents` exactly. This ordering is normative because the obvious alternative over-collects: financing 17660 cents over 3 installments yields the pinned schedule 5886, 5886, 5888 summing to exactly 17660, whereas rounding each installment half up would yield 5887, 5887, 5887 — 17661 cents, one cent of over-collection — and is forbidden. Installment `k`, counted from 1, falls due 30 days times `k` after the plan's `created_at`, so `first_due_at` is the plan's `created_at` plus 30 days.

A new plan opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `installments_paid_count` at 0, `missed_installment_count` at 0 and `remaining_balance_cents` equal to `financed_cents`. Where `down_payment_cents` is greater than 0, a ledger row is appended with `source` `DOWN_PAYMENT` and the new plan's identifier as its `plan_id`, raising the citation's `amount_paid_cents` and lowering `outstanding_balance_cents` by that amount. The citation's `settlement_status` moves to `PLAN_ACTIVE` — from `OUTSTANDING` in the ordinary case, and from `SERVICE_ACTIVE` when the plan supersedes a service alternative — and its `version` increments by 1. When the plan does supersede, that citation's `ACTIVE` community-service assignment moves `ACTIVE → FORFEITED`, and the service-credit ledger rows it already earned are retained, so the citation's `service_credit_cents` is unchanged by the switch and the rider keeps credit for hours already worked.

**Paying installments.** Each payment against an `ACTIVE` plan submits an `amount_cents`, and the plan must be `ACTIVE` — a `COMPLETED`, `DEFAULTED` or `CANCELLED` plan is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. Installments are consumed strictly in ascending order, and the submitted amount must equal the scheduled amount of the next unpaid installment exactly: the base amount for every installment but the last, and `final_installment_cents` for the last. Any other amount is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`. A successful payment appends a ledger row with `source` `PLAN_INSTALLMENT`, `plan_id` set to the plan and `applied_at` set to the logical clock, raises `installments_paid_count` by 1, and recomputes `remaining_balance_cents` as `financed_cents` less the sum of that plan's `PLAN_INSTALLMENT` amounts. The citation's `amount_paid_cents` rises and its `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed from the identity in §3.5, and its `version` increments by 1; the plan's own `version` likewise increments by 1 on every accepted installment payment, whether or not that payment completes the schedule. When the payment is the last scheduled one — that is, when `installments_paid_count` plus 1 equals `installment_count` and the amount equals `final_installment_cents`, leaving `remaining_balance_cents` at 0 — the plan moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` with `remaining_balance_cents` at 0 and the citation's `settlement_status` moves `PLAN_ACTIVE → SATISFIED` with `outstanding_balance_cents` set to 0.

**Delinquency.** An `EnforcementPlanner` evaluates an `ACTIVE` plan against the logical clock; a plan in any other status is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The assessment recomputes `missed_installment_count` as the number of installment positions from 1 through `installment_count` whose due instant — the plan's `created_at` plus 30 days times the position — has arrived and which remain unpaid, where "unpaid" means `installments_paid_count` is lower than that position. The due-date comparison is inclusive: an installment falling due at exactly the current clock instant already counts as due. Where the recomputed count reaches 2 or more, the plan moves `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED` and the citation's `settlement_status` reverts `PLAN_ACTIVE → OUTSTANDING`, returning the citation to the collectible pool so that a fresh plan may be opened against it later — this is the cyclic arm of the settlement dimension. Both the plan's and the citation's `version` increment by 1 on default. Where the count is below 2, the recomputed `missed_installment_count` is still written and the plan stays `ACTIVE`; such a sub-threshold assessment registers a mutation on the plan — `version` incremented by 1 and `updated_at` refreshed — only where the recomputed count actually differs from the stored one, so re-assessing a plan whose missed count has not moved returns the plan with its `version` and `updated_at` exactly as they were. `missed_installment_count` is the one plan counter that is not refreshed when the plan is read: it reports whatever the last delinquency assessment wrote, so a plan whose due dates have passed but which has never been assessed still reads 0, whereas `installments_paid_count` and `remaining_balance_cents` are recomputed from the plan's `PLAN_INSTALLMENT` ledger rows on every read.

**Community service.** The service alternative converts a balance into supervised hours at 1500 cents per hour. Opening one names the `citation_id` being worked off, the `partner_org_name` of the community partner supervising the service, and the `expected_citation_version` described in §2.8. The citation's `settlement_status` must be `OUTSTANDING` — there is no superseding path into service, so `PLAN_ACTIVE`, `SERVICE_ACTIVE`, `SATISFIED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are all refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATUS_INVALID` — it must have no `ACTIVE` payment plan and no `ACTIVE` community-service assignment, otherwise `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, and only then must its `outstanding_balance_cents` be strictly greater than 0, otherwise `ERR_NOTHING_OUTSTANDING` — the fiscal test being the last of the three, as §2.10 requires. `required_hours` is the citation's `outstanding_balance_cents` divided by 1500 and rounded **up** to a whole hour, so a balance of 8100 cents requires 6 hours rather than 5.4. The assignment opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `hours_completed` at 0 and `credited_cents` at 0, the citation's `settlement_status` moves `OUTSTANDING → SERVICE_ACTIVE`, and the citation's `version` increments by 1.

Hours are credited by a planner, who posts an `hours` figure that must be a whole number of at least 1. Only that field's presence is screened at the schema step: an omitted or explicitly `null` `hours` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), whereas an `hours` that is present but is a string, a boolean, a number carrying a fractional part, or an integer below 1 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_HOURS` (HTTP 422) rather than as a schema type failure. That validity test is evaluated only after the assignment identifier has been resolved and after the assignment's own state has been checked, so an assignment whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_ACTIVE` even when the posted `hours` is also invalid. The raw credit is the posted hours multiplied by 1500 cents, and that figure is then capped at the citation's `outstanding_balance_cents` at the moment of the call, so the ledger identity can never be driven negative by over-logging; the cap is applied per call, before any ledger row is written. A ledger row with `source` `SERVICE_CREDIT` is appended for the capped amount only when that amount is greater than 0, with `hours_credited` set to the posted hours, an empty `plan_id` and `applied_at` from the logical clock. `hours_completed` rises by the posted hours and `credited_cents` is recomputed as the sum of that citation's `SERVICE_CREDIT` amounts; the citation's `service_credit_cents` rises correspondingly and `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed. When `hours_completed` has reached `required_hours`, or when `outstanding_balance_cents` reaches 0, the assignment moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` and the citation's `settlement_status` moves `SERVICE_ACTIVE → SATISFIED` with `outstanding_balance_cents` set to 0. Both records' `version` increment by 1 — the assignment's and the citation's alike, on every accepted hours posting and not only on the one that completes the assignment. The call answers **200 OK** carrying the updated assignment in `data`, never the citation and never the service-credit ledger row it appended.

An assignment's two other terminal states are reached only by events outside itself. `ACTIVE → FORFEITED` happens when a plan supersedes the alternative, as described above, and retains the credit already earned. `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` happens when the citation's `adjudication_status` becomes `DISMISSED` or `VOID`, alongside the citation's own move to `WRITTEN_OFF`. A payment plan's `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` arm is driven by the same dismissal event. `SATISFIED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are the two terminal values of the settlement dimension; `OUTSTANDING`, `PLAN_ACTIVE` and `SERVICE_ACTIVE` are all live.

### 3.9. Equity Distribution and Revenue Recovery Analytics

Two read-only analytics surfaces exist, both available to an `EquityAnalyst` and an `EnforcementPlanner` and to nobody else. Both take a reporting window whose `window_end` must be strictly later than its `window_start`, failing which the read is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`, and both treat that window as half-open — a record at exactly `window_start` is inside, a record at exactly `window_end` is outside. Both echo `window_start` and `window_end` back in their payloads verbatim — the raw query-string text exactly as the caller supplied it, never the parsed instant re-rendered into the three-fractional-digit form of §2.4 — so a window bound supplied as `2026-08-01T00:00:00+00:00` echoes with that offset intact and one supplied without fractional digits echoes without them. Each report's third query parameter — `group_by` for the equity report, `basis` for the revenue report — is mandatory and is screened **before** either window bound is looked at: absent or supplied empty it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and present but naming a value outside its declared two-member set it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` as well, never with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. Because that screening precedes the window, a read that both names an unknown `group_by` and carries an unparseable `window_start` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two window bounds are themselves screened left to right — `window_start` for presence and then for well-formedness, then `window_end` the same way, and only then the strict-ordering comparison — so a read carrying two malformed bounds reports the failure of `window_start`, and one whose `window_start` is missing while its `window_end` is malformed reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

**The equity distribution report** measures whether citations fall evenly across the riding public. Its third input, `group_by`, selects the axis of comparison and is either `DEMOGRAPHIC_BAND` — grouping riders by their `demographic_band` — or `GEO_TRACT`, grouping them by their `residence_geo_tract`; the chosen value is echoed back as `group_by`. The encounter pool is every encounter that has a `rider_id`, whose `outcome` is anything other than `VOIDED`, and whose `occurred_at` falls inside the half-open window; its size is reported as `encounter_pool_count`. Within that pool an encounter counts as cited when a citation exists that references it and whose `adjudication_status` is one of `ISSUED`, `CONTESTED`, `UPHELD` or `REDUCED`; a citation that has reached `DISMISSED` or `VOID` does not mark its encounter as cited, which is precisely how a successful dismissal shows up in the equity numbers. The count of cited encounters in the pool is reported as `citation_count`.

`systemwide_citation_rate` is the cited count divided by the pool size, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and is exactly 0.0000 when the pool is empty. The groups are the distinct values of the selected key over that pool, emitted in ascending order of the key and reported as `groups_evaluated`, which counts only groups whose own `encounter_pool_count` is greater than zero. Each entry in the `groups` array carries its `group_key`, its own `encounter_pool_count` and `citation_count`, its `citation_rate` — the group's cited count divided by the group's pool size, rounded half up to 4 decimal places — and its `rate_delta`, being that group's `citation_rate` less the `systemwide_citation_rate`, also carried to 4 decimal places and negative for groups cited less often than the system as a whole.

`disparity_index` is the mean absolute deviation of the group rates from the systemwide rate: take each group's rounded `citation_rate`, subtract the systemwide rate, discard the sign, sum those absolute deviations across all groups, divide by `groups_evaluated`, and round half up to 4 decimal places. It is exactly 0.0000 when `groups_evaluated` is 0. The rounding order is normative and observable: each group rate is rounded to 4 decimal places **before** its deviation is taken, and only then is the mean rounded again. For two groups whose raw rates are one third and one half against a systemwide rate of two fifths, the pinned order yields 0.0834 whereas aggregating the raw unrounded rates and rounding once at the end yields 0.0833; the latter is forbidden. An empty encounter pool is a legitimate answer rather than an error: the report returns `groups_evaluated` of 0, a `systemwide_citation_rate` of 0.0000 and a `disparity_index` of 0.0000. All four rate figures — `systemwide_citation_rate`, `disparity_index`, each `citation_rate` and each `rate_delta` — are rendered as decimal strings with exactly 4 decimal places.

**The revenue recovery report** reconciles what enforcement brought in against what it cost, and its third input, `basis`, selects between two genuinely different accounting readings — either `ACCRUAL` or `CASH`, echoed back as `basis`. The cost leg is identical under both. It gathers every deployment whose `status` is `CLOSED` and whose own `window_start` falls inside the half-open reporting window, reports how many there were as `deployments_counted`, and sums their frozen `enforcement_cost_cents` into the report's `enforcement_cost_cents`.

Under the `ACCRUAL` basis the recovery leg is what the agency billed. It gathers every citation whose `issued_at` falls inside the window and whose `adjudication_status` is one of `ISSUED`, `CONTESTED`, `UPHELD` or `REDUCED`, sums their `total_due_cents` into `recovered_cents`, and reports how many there were as `citations_counted`. The payment ledger is ignored entirely, so a citation issued and never paid contributes its full total. Under the `CASH` basis the recovery leg is what the agency actually banked. It gathers every ledger row whose `applied_at` falls inside the window, sums the amounts of those whose `source` is `DOWN_PAYMENT`, `PLAN_INSTALLMENT`, `DIRECT` or `SERVICE_CREDIT`, subtracts the amounts of those whose `source` is `DISMISSAL_REVERSAL`, and reports the result as `recovered_cents`; `citations_counted` is the number of distinct `citation_id` values appearing on those rows. Under this basis an unpaid citation contributes nothing and a dismissal reverses cash banked earlier, so the two bases answer different questions and are expected to disagree.

Both bases then close the same way. `net_recovery_cents` is `recovered_cents` less `enforcement_cost_cents` and may be negative — a zone whose enforcement costs more than it recovers is exactly the finding the report exists to surface. `recovery_ratio_bps` is `recovered_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by `enforcement_cost_cents`, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points, and is 0 whenever `enforcement_cost_cents` is 0, since no ratio against a zero cost leg is meaningful. All money figures are integer cents and the ratio is integer basis points.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions
