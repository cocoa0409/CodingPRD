# Bowling League & Sanctioned Average Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A certified bowling league is, administratively, three overlapping ledgers that must never disagree. The first is a *membership* ledger: who has been registered with the national body, whose fees have actually been forwarded and receipted, and who is therefore eligible to occupy a lineup slot on a given night. The second is a *scoring* ledger: which games have been certified, what average each bowler has earned from those certified games, and what handicap that average entitles them to under the bylaws the league adopted at its organizational meeting. The third is a *money* ledger: weekly dues split between league expenses and the prize fund, sanction fees remitted upward to the national body, and prize money eventually distributed downward to the teams that finished in the paying places. Today these three ledgers live in a secretary's spreadsheet, a treasurer's cash box and a shoebox of receipts, and they drift apart the moment a match is protested or a remittance is rejected.

This document specifies the REST API that replaces those artefacts. The service is the authoritative record for league formation and bylaw adoption, membership sanctioning and fee remittance, average and handicap derivation from certified games, lineup legality including vacancy, substitute, absentee and pre-bowl handling, forfeit preallocation, weekly dues collection and prize-fund accumulation and distribution, honor-score award applications with their verification chain, and the protest and rules-interpretation escalation path that reaches the association. Every collection is mounted at the root of the URL space — there is no versioning prefix and no top-level namespace segment — and the only nested read in the entire surface is the league-scoped team listing.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **derived values are never client-supplied**: averages, handicaps, points, standings figures, fee totals and prize amounts are computed by the server from primary records, and the client's only lever over them is the primary record itself. Second, **every compensating action must land exactly back on the figures that preceded the action it compensates**: voiding a certified match must return every affected bowler's certified pin count to the precise integer it held before certification, and reversing every paid award must return the prize fund's undistributed balance to the precise cent it held before disbursement. A great deal of the specification below exists to make those two commitments enforceable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Officers, Bowlers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is missing, or is present but empty — a header whose value consists only of whitespace counts as empty here — the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. Both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before they are used, so the role spelling and the identifier comparisons below are made against the trimmed value.

Exactly five roles are declared, and their spellings are exact: `Bowler`, `TeamCaptain`, `LeagueSecretary`, `LeagueTreasurer` and `AssociationOfficer`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those five strings, or that is one of them but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). A plain `Bowler` attempting to submit a team lineup, or a `LeagueSecretary` attempting to record the national body's decision on a fee remittance, both fall to this code.

Four of the five roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Bowler`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's member-bearing field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `TeamCaptain`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Member`, and the captaincy is asserted by the header rather than recorded on a roster field — a `TeamCaptain` is accepted for a team when the acting `Member` holds some `Membership` of that league whose `team_id` is the team being acted for, and that membership's `roster_role` may be either `REGULAR` or `SUBSTITUTE` and need not yet be sanctioned. When `X-User-Role` is `LeagueSecretary`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member` and must equal the target league's `secretary_member_id`; when it is `LeagueTreasurer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member` and must equal the target league's `treasurer_member_id`. For all four of these roles, an `X-User-ID` that is not the `id` of any existing `Member` is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (HTTP 403) on every endpoint whose authorised roles include an entity-backed role.

`AssociationOfficer` is the one exception. When `X-User-Role` is `AssociationOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — the officer identifier is never compared to any entity's `id`, and no `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` check applies to it — while entity-backed audit fields such as `submitted_by_user_id`, `plan_approved_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` capture whatever `X-User-ID` the acting officer or league official presented, purely for audit and duty-segregation comparisons.

Officer binding is checked once the target resource has been loaded, which is why it follows existence resolution rather than preceding it. A mutation performed as `LeagueSecretary` against a league the actor does not serve as secretary is refused with `ERR_NOT_LEAGUE_SECRETARY` (HTTP 403); the treasurer's equivalent is `ERR_NOT_LEAGUE_TREASURER` (HTTP 403). Read endpoints carry no officer binding and no ownership binding at all: any of the five roles may read a member, a league, a team, a membership, a lane pair, a match, a series entry, a prize fund, a prize award, an award application or a protest — and may list any of those the surface lists — without standing in any relationship to the row read, while the fee-remittance and dues-payment reads, single and listed alike, are restricted to `LeagueSecretary`, `LeagueTreasurer` and `AssociationOfficer`. When a `Bowler` acts on a membership, a dues payment, an award application or a protest that is not their own, or a `TeamCaptain` submits a lineup for a team on which the acting member holds no membership, the refusal is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from real time — it therefore does not advance from one request to the next unless the clock is moved.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. A resource object carries exactly the fields §2 and §3 name for that entity — including the client-supplied inputs recorded on it, such as a membership's `prior_season_average` and `prior_season_games` or a remittance's ordered `membership_ids` array — together with the identity, timestamp and `version` fields of §2.4, and no others; a field the entity has not yet acquired a value for is present and null rather than omitted. The same projection is used whether the object is read singly, returned from a write or listed.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when absent, may not be below 1 and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when absent and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-10 integer at all, and the `limit` and `offset` echoed in `meta` are the effective values actually applied, which are the defaults when the parameters are absent.

Unless a list endpoint's own description states a different ordering, results are sorted by `created_at` ascending, and rows that share the same `created_at` — which the logical clock of §2.4 makes routine, since a whole cascade stamps one instant — are ordered by the order in which the server inserted them. Two orderings depart from that default and are stated where they apply: the lineup-slot listing is ordered by the owning `team_id` compared as a string and then by lineup `position` ascending, and the prize-award listing is ordered by `place` ascending; each of those two falls back to insertion order for rows that tie on all of its stated keys.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix and no domain-flavoured decoration. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied value is ignored.

Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits, stamped when the row is first written. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format, stamped equal to `created_at` when the row is first written and refreshed on every mutation that touches the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `DuesPayment` is the single exception: a weekly dues payment is immutable, has no update surface at all, and therefore carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Time is not read from the wall clock. The server maintains a **logical clock** whose value is the sole source of "now" for every timestamp the server stamps and every window comparison it makes. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must never fall back to real time. An `AssociationOfficer` may move the clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying `current_utc`, and every subsequent stamp and comparison uses that instant. The clock's reach is deliberately broad: lane-pair certification expiry, the twenty-one-day fee-remittance filing window, the seventy-two-hour protest window and the one-hundred-and-sixty-eight-hour honor-score window are all measured against it, so all of them are fully deterministic. Note in particular that a lane pair's derived `certification_status` is re-evaluated on every read, so advancing the clock past a pair's `certified_through` changes that pair from `CERTIFIED` to `EXPIRED` without any request ever being addressed to the pair itself.

Instants supplied by clients — `organizational_meeting_at`, `certified_through`, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `prebowled_at`, `current_utc` — must parse as ISO 8601 and must carry an explicit UTC offset. Instants that do not are refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a value that is not a string at all, or is a blank string, fails the same way. An offset other than `Z` is a legitimate explicit offset: it is accepted and the instant is converted to UTC before it is stored or compared, so `2026-08-01T05:00:00+05:00` and `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` are the same instant to this server. Every instant the server renders back is written in UTC with a trailing `Z` and exactly three fractional-second digits, any finer precision being truncated rather than rounded.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on the League, the Match and the Prize Fund

Three write surfaces in this system legitimately *supersede* what came before them: a bylaw re-adoption replaces the entire prior bylaw set, a lineup re-submission deletes and replaces the submitting side's previous slots, and a fresh distribution plan overwrites every figure of the prior draft. Because each of those writes would otherwise succeed against state the client never observed — silently discarding an amendment, a revised lineup or a plan computed from a different basis — each is guarded by optimistic concurrency control on its parent resource's `version`.

Bylaw adoption requires `expected_league_version` as a non-optional input carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `League`. If the value does not equal the league's current `version`, the adoption is refused with `ERR_STALE_LEAGUE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is the guard that prevents a client from overwriting a `handicap_base` amendment — and therefore every membership handicap derived from it — that the client never saw.

Lineup submission requires `expected_match_version` as a non-optional input carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Match`. A mismatch with the match's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_MATCH_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The `version` returned on the response to a successful submission is the value the next submission against that match must echo.

Distribution-plan drafting requires `expected_prize_fund_version` as a non-optional input carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PrizeFund`. A mismatch with the fund's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_PRIZE_FUND_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

In precise endpoint terms, `POST /leagues/{id}/bylaws` takes `expected_league_version`, `POST /matches/{id}/lineups` takes `expected_match_version`, and `POST /prize-funds/{id}/distribution-plans` takes `expected_prize_fund_version`. No other endpoint in this API takes a version field of any kind, and no other endpoint may reject a request on version grounds.

### 2.6. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

A single request frequently violates more than one rule at once. To keep responses predictable, all checks are ordered, they are evaluated strictly in that order, and **the first violated check decides the response** — no later check is consulted, so a request that breaks several rules always returns the error belonging to the earliest check it breaks. The complete order is:

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) — both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty. Every endpoint.
2. `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403) — `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles and that role is authorised for the endpoint. Every endpoint.
3. `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (403) — for the entity-backed roles `Bowler`, `TeamCaptain`, `LeagueSecretary` and `LeagueTreasurer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of an existing `Member`. Every endpoint whose authorised roles include an entity-backed role.
4. Path-resource existence, resolved left to right along the URL. Every endpoint with a path parameter. The concrete code is the not-found code of the *first* missing path resource: `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LANE_PAIR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERIES_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DUES_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRIZE_FUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRIZE_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PROTEST_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.
5. Body-reference existence: every identifier carried in the request body resolves to an existing row, checked in the declaration order of the endpoint's input list, with the same family of not-found codes as check 4 and the code belonging to the first unresolvable identifier. An identifier that is absent from the body, carried as `null`, or carried as something that is not a JSON string is not looked up here at all — it is left to check 9, which refuses it with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` if the endpoint requires it and accepts it if the endpoint declares it optional — so only an identifier actually present as a string can produce a 404 from this check, and a present but blank string produces that entity's not-found code rather than a shape error. This applies to league chartering, team entry, membership enrolment, lane-pair registration, fee-remittance drafting, lineup submission, honor-score application and protest filing.
6. `ERR_NOT_LEAGUE_SECRETARY` (403) — when the acting role is `LeagueSecretary`, `X-User-ID` equals the `secretary_member_id` of the league the target resource belongs to. Every mutating endpoint whose authorised roles include `LeagueSecretary`.
7. `ERR_NOT_LEAGUE_TREASURER` (403) — when the acting role is `LeagueTreasurer`, `X-User-ID` equals the `treasurer_member_id` of the league the target resource belongs to. Every mutating endpoint whose authorised roles include `LeagueTreasurer`.
8. `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) — a `Bowler` acts only on their own membership, and a `TeamCaptain` acts only for a team on which the acting member holds a membership of that league. This guards exactly five operations: recording a weekly dues payment, filing an honor-score application, resupplying evidence on one, filing a protest, and submitting a lineup.
9. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) — request shape. All required inputs present; every enum input carrying a declared value; integers actually integers — a JSON boolean is not an integer here, and a string is not an integer either; cents and pin counts not negative; percentages within 0 through 1 inclusive, carrying at most two decimal places and accepted either as a JSON number or as a decimal string spelling the same value; array inputs not empty, and no longer than the maximum the input declares; instants parsing as ISO 8601 with an explicit UTC offset; pagination `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` not negative; every conditionally-required input obeyed **in both directions** (see §2.7 for the full list of conditional pairs); and cross-field bylaw coherence holding. A bounded string's character limit is measured on the value exactly as supplied, while its not-blank test is applied after trimming surrounding whitespace, so a string of nothing but spaces is blank however long it is. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, which is why an endpoint with no required input accepts a bodiless call; a body that is not valid JSON, or that is valid JSON but not a JSON object, is refused here. Every endpoint with an input list.
10. `ERR_CROSS_LEAGUE_REFERENCE` (422) — every entity referenced together by one request belongs to the same league. This applies to membership enrolment, fee-remittance drafting, schedule adoption, lineup submission, honor-score application and protest filing.
11. `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (422) — referenced rows stand in the required relationship to each other. This applies to lineup submission, forfeit declaration, honor-score application and protest filing.
12. `ERR_STALE_LEAGUE_VERSION` (412) — `expected_league_version` equals the league's current `version`. Bylaw adoption only.
13. `ERR_STALE_MATCH_VERSION` (412) — `expected_match_version` equals the match's current `version`. Lineup submission only.
14. `ERR_STALE_PRIZE_FUND_VERSION` (412) — `expected_prize_fund_version` equals the fund's current `version`. Distribution-plan drafting only.
15. `ERR_LEAGUE_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the league's `status` admits the operation. All league-scoped mutations.
16. `ERR_MATCH_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the match's `status` admits the transition. Lineup submission, opening, score entry, certification and forfeit declaration.
17. `ERR_REMITTANCE_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the remittance's `status` admits the transition. Remittance submission and the national decision.
18. `ERR_PRIZE_FUND_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the fund's `status` admits the transition. Plan drafting, plan approval, disbursement and award reversal.
19. `ERR_AWARD_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the application's `status` admits the transition. League verification, evidence resubmission and the association decision.
20. `ERR_PROTEST_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the protest's `status` admits the transition. Hearing opening, league ruling and association ruling.
21. `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the membership's `sanction_status` and `remittance_status` admit the operation. Fee-remittance drafting and dues payment.
22. `ERR_TEAM_NOT_CONFIRMED` (409) — the team a lineup is submitted for, and the opposing team on that match, both have `status` `CONFIRMED`. Lineup submission only.
23. `ERR_BOWLER_NOT_SANCTIONED` (422) — every `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `PREBOWL` lineup slot names a membership whose `sanction_status` is `SANCTIONED`. Lineup submission and honor-score application.
24. `ERR_LANE_PAIR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422) — the match's lane pair has `certification_status` `CERTIFIED` when the match is opened for bowling; and a recertification action is refused on a pair whose `revoked_at` is already set, because `REVOKED` is terminal and no recertification revives it. Match opening and the lane-pair certification action.
25. `ERR_AWARD_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (422) — an honor-score application reaches league verification, and later association certification, only when `lane_certification_verified` is true and `sanctioned_at_game_time` is also true. League verification and the association decision.
26. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — the three duty separations of §2.10. League verification, disbursement, hearing opening and league ruling.
27. `ERR_LANE_PAIR_CONFLICT` (409) — lane-pair occupation windows do not overlap. Schedule adoption only.
28. `ERR_BOWLER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409) — the travel-buffer check of §3.4. Lineup submission only.
29. `ERR_LANE_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` (422) — the lane pair's `certified_through` is at or after the match's `starts_at`. Match opening only.
30. `ERR_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422) — the protest and honor-score filing windows of §3.8 and §3.9. Protest filing and honor-score application.
31. `ERR_PREBOWL_NOT_BEFORE_MATCH` (422) — a pre-bowl slot's `prebowled_at` is strictly earlier than the match's `starts_at`. Lineup submission only.
32. `ERR_ILLEGAL_LINEUP` (422) — lineup shape and the legal-bowler minimum. Lineup submission only.
33. `ERR_VACANCY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422) — the vacancy-plus-absentee ceiling. Lineup submission only.
34. `ERR_DUPLICATE_BOWLER_IN_LINEUP` (422) — no membership twice in one lineup and none on both sides of one match. Lineup submission only.
35. `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422) — substitute eligibility. Lineup submission only.
36. `ERR_SCORE_SET_INVALID` (422) — score-set completeness and range. Score entry only.
37. `ERR_TEAM_COUNT_MISMATCH` (422) — schedule team coverage and distinct sides. Schedule adoption only.
38. `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP` (422) — one membership per member per league, and one live remittance listing per membership. Membership enrolment and fee-remittance drafting.
39. `ERR_AWARD_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (422) — honor-score threshold and application uniqueness. Honor-score application only.
40. `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TEAMS` (422) — the league has at least as many `CONFIRMED` teams as the plan's `place_count`. Plan drafting and plan approval.
41. `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422) — remedy applicability. League ruling and association ruling.
42. `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT` (422) — the hearing-cycle ceiling. League ruling only.
43. `ERR_DUES_ARREARS_BLOCK` (422) — the arrears ceiling on lineup eligibility. Lineup submission only.
44. `ERR_REMITTANCE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422) — the declared remittance total matches the computed one. Remittance submission only.
45. `ERR_DUES_WEEK_ALREADY_PAID` (409) — one dues payment per membership per week. Dues payment only.
46. `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PRIZE_FUND` (422) — the fund can cover the approved awards. Disbursement only.
47. `ERR_DUES_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422) — the tendered amount equals the bylaw weekly dues exactly. Dues payment only.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. Authorisation always outranks state: a request that is both made by the wrong officer and made against a resource in the wrong state returns the authorisation code, not the state code. And **money checks run last**: a dues payment tendered by the wrong actor, for a membership that is not sanctioned, in a week already paid, and in the wrong amount, returns the ownership code — never `ERR_DUES_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

### 2.7. Conditionally Required Inputs and Bylaw Coherence

Several inputs are required under one condition and *forbidden* under its complement, and check 9 of §2.6 enforces both directions. Throughout that shape pass a field present but carrying a JSON `null` counts as absent, and counts as absent in both directions: it does not satisfy a required input, and it does not violate the forbidden half of a conditional pair — sending `claimed_game_number` as `null` on a `SERIES_800_SCRATCH` claim, or `membership_id` as `null` on a `VACANCY` slot, is accepted exactly as omitting the field would be, while sending either as `null` where the pair requires it is refused as a missing input. `claimed_game_number` is required when `award_type` is `PERFECT_GAME_300` or `HIGH_GAME_298_PLUS`, and forbidden when it is `SERIES_800_SCRATCH` or `HANDICAP_SERIES_700`. `prebowled_at` is required on a slot whose `entry_type` is `PREBOWL` and forbidden on a slot of any other `entry_type`. A slot's `membership_id` is required when `entry_type` is `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE`, `PREBOWL` or `ABSENTEE`, and forbidden when `entry_type` is `VACANCY`. On a lane-pair certification action, `certified_through` is required when `action` is `RECERTIFY` and forbidden when `action` is `REVOKE`. On a national decision, `national_receipt_no` is required when `decision` is `ACCEPT` and `rejection_reason` is required when `decision` is `REJECT`.

Bylaw coherence is checked as part of the same shape pass, across fields rather than within one. The elements of `prize_place_weights` must sum to exactly 1.00 and each element must be at least 0.01; `legal_lineup_minimum` must not exceed `lineup_positions`; `max_vacancy_slots_per_lineup` must be strictly less than `lineup_positions`; `weekly_expense_cents` must not exceed `weekly_dues_cents`; and `vacancy_average` must not exceed `handicap_base`. Any of these failing yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and the Conventions That Differ Between Them

Bowling arithmetic and accounting arithmetic round differently, and the difference is load-bearing here.

**Averages truncate.** Every bowler average and every handicap is an integer obtained by discarding the fractional part — truncating toward zero, the traditional bowling convention. Averages and handicaps are never rounded half up, and a quotient of 182.97 yields 182.

**Handicaps floor before they cap, and floor before they sum.** A bowler's per-game handicap is floored to an integer *before* the bylaw ceiling is applied to it, and when several bowlers' handicaps are added into a team handicap, each bowler's handicap is floored first and only the resulting integers are summed. Summing unfloored products and flooring once at the end is a different and incorrect number.

**Money is integer cents throughout.** No monetary field in this system is a decimal; fees, dues, prize bases, reserves, fees withheld and award amounts are all whole cents. Where a percentage is applied to cents, the specification states for each step whether the result floors or rounds half up, and those two behaviours are not interchangeable: the prize reserve floors, while the association administrative fee rounds half up.

**Points carry one decimal place.** Match and standings points are recorded to one decimal place, and only the fractional values .0 and .5 ever arise, because the only fractional award in the system is a half point to each side on a tie.

### 2.9. Cascades, Derived Fields and Compensating Actions

Most of the interesting behaviour of this system is *cascading*: one request changes one row, and that change propagates through counters, derived averages, team figures and league-wide high-water marks. Three rules govern all of it.

First, derived and computed fields are never stale. Counters such as `sanctioned_member_count`, `confirmed_team_count`, `sanctioned_membership_count`, `sanctioned_roster_count`, `certified_games`, `certified_pins`, `dues_paid_cents`, `dues_arrears_weeks`, `certified_week_high_water`, `total_points`, `total_scratch_pins`, `collected_prize_cents`, `paid_award_total_cents` and `undistributed_balance_cents` are recomputed as part of the request that invalidates them, and a read that follows the request observes the new value. The `certification_status` of a lane pair is derived at read time rather than stored.

Second, every row a cascade actually mutates gets its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. A row a cascade merely reads is untouched, and so is a row the cascade recomputes only to find every recomputed field already standing at the value it held — recomputation that changes nothing is not a mutation and moves neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Third, **compensating actions restore exact figures, not approximate ones.** Two invariants are asserted after every operation that could disturb them. The *certified-pin invariant* requires that for every membership, `certified_games` equals the total games recorded across that membership's certified average-bearing slots and `certified_pins` equals the total pins across the same slots; certifying a match and then voiding it under a protest remedy must return `certified_games` and `certified_pins` to the exact integers they held before the certification, and therefore must return `current_average`, `average_status` and `handicap_per_game` to their pre-certification values as well. No drift of even one pin is tolerated. The *prize-money invariant* requires that for every prize fund, `collected_prize_cents` equals `paid_award_total_cents` plus `undistributed_balance_cents`, where `paid_award_total_cents` is the total of that fund's awards whose `status` is `PAID`; disbursing a fund and then reversing every paid award must restore `undistributed_balance_cents` to its exact pre-disbursement value and `paid_award_total_cents` to 0, leaving `collected_prize_cents` untouched. Awards in `PENDING` or `REVERSED` never reduce the undistributed balance, and the reserve and the administrative fee remain inside `undistributed_balance_cents` and are never treated as money paid out.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The league's own officers hold overlapping powers, and three separations keep them from closing a loop alone. A `LeagueSecretary` may not verify an honor-score application whose applicant is the secretary themself — the comparison is between the league's `secretary_member_id` and the `member_id` behind the applying membership. A `LeagueTreasurer` may not disburse a plan they approved, which cannot happen through the secretary route but is checked directly: the disbursing treasurer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the fund's `plan_approved_by_user_id`. And a `LeagueSecretary` may neither open a hearing on, nor rule on, a protest that the secretary themself filed — again comparing `secretary_member_id` against the `member_id` behind `filed_by_membership_id`. All three refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The same principle is expressed structurally elsewhere: the treasurer drafts the distribution plan but can never approve it, and the secretary approves it but can never pay it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Organizational Meeting: Registering Members, Chartering a League, Adopting Bylaws

Everything begins at the national body. An `AssociationOfficer` registers a person by supplying `full_name` (between 1 and 120 characters, not blank), `email` (which must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all registered members, the uniqueness comparison being made on the value exactly as supplied and therefore case-sensitively, and `email` carrying no upper length bound; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` as part of check 9 of §2.6) and `member_class`, which is either `ADULT` or `YOUTH`. The membership class is not cosmetic: it selects the handicap percentage the bowler is entitled to and the fee tier the league will pay for them. Registration deliberately mints nothing. `national_member_number` stays null at this point and `sanctioned_membership_count` starts at 0, because a person becomes a *sanctioned* member of the national body only when a league actually forwards their fee and the national body receipts it. When that first sanctioning happens, the national body assigns the number as the literal prefix `NM-` followed by a zero-padded six-digit sequence — one sequence for the whole service, beginning at `NM-000001` — allocated in ascending `created_at` order of the members reaching their first sanctioning on that request, with the order in which the server inserted those members breaking a `created_at` tie. That allocation order is the members' own order and not necessarily the order their memberships occupy in the accepting remittance's `membership_ids`.

A `LeagueSecretary` charters a league by supplying `name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank), `season_year` (an integer from 2000 through 2100 — the 2026-2027 season is recorded as 2026), `center_name` for the bowling centre hosting the league (required and not blank, with no upper length bound), `declared_team_count` (from 2 through 24, the number of teams the organizational meeting declared it would field), `organizational_meeting_at` as the instant of that meeting, and the two officer bindings `secretary_member_id` and `treasurer_member_id`. The acting secretary must be the very member named in `secretary_member_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_LEAGUE_SECRETARY` even though no league yet exists to bind against; nothing, on the other hand, requires `treasurer_member_id` to name a different member from `secretary_member_id`, and one member may hold both offices. Notably, **no bylaw field is accepted at chartering**: a freshly chartered league stands at `FORMING` with every bylaw null, `version` 1, `total_weeks` 0, `sanctioned_member_count` 0, `confirmed_team_count` 0, `certified_week_high_water` 0 and `bylaws_adopted_at` null. Chartering also silently creates exactly one prize fund for the league, in `ACCUMULATING`, with `basis_variant` `PROJECTED_SEASON_END`, `place_count` 0, every monetary figure on the fund 0 and `plan_drafted_at`, `plan_approved_by_user_id` and `disbursed_at` all null; that fund is never returned by the chartering response and is discoverable only through the prize-fund listing filtered by league.

Bylaw adoption is the act that turns a charter into a working league, and it carries eighteen values in one payload. `handicap_base` (180 through 300) is the scratch score from which handicap is measured. `handicap_percentage` (0.50 through 1.00, two decimals) applies to adult bowlers and `handicap_percentage_youth` (same range and precision) applies to bowlers whose `member_class` is `YOUTH`. `max_handicap_per_game` (0 through 120) is a hard ceiling on any single bowler's per-game handicap. `handicap_average_basis` is either `ENTERING` or `CURRENT` and decides which average feeds the handicap formula. `minimum_games_for_average` (1 through 30) is how many certified games a bowler must have before their average is treated as established. `carryover_policy` is `NONE`, `PRIOR_SEASON_FULL` or `BLENDED` and governs how a prior-season record feeds an average that is not yet established. `vacancy_average` (0 through 300, and never above `handicap_base`) is the average attributed to an empty lineup slot when a team handicap is computed, while `vacancy_score` (0 through 300) is the scratch pin count credited to that slot per game. `absentee_deduction` (0 through 50) is the pin penalty applied to an absent bowler's basis average for each game. `lineup_positions` (2 through 6) is how many positions each team fields per match and is also the roster depth used when a team's roster handicap is computed. `legal_lineup_minimum` (1 through 6, and never above `lineup_positions`) is the minimum number of positions that must be occupied by a sanctioned bowler for a lineup to be legal, and doubles as the sanctioned-roster count at which a team is confirmed. `max_vacancy_slots_per_lineup` (0 through 3, and strictly below `lineup_positions`) caps empty slots in one lineup. `weekly_dues_cents` (0 through 10000) is the weekly dues per bowler, `weekly_expense_cents` (0 through `weekly_dues_cents`) is the non-prize portion of those dues, and the remainder accrues to the prize fund. `max_dues_arrears_weeks` (0 through 10) is how many weeks of unpaid dues a bowler may carry and still appear in a lineup. Finally `prize_place_weights` is an ordered array of between 1 and 12 numbers, element zero being first place, each element between 0.01 and 1.00 with two decimals, and the elements summing to exactly 1.00. The eighteenth value is `expected_league_version` itself, which must be a non-negative integer: a negative or non-integer echo is a shape failure refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the version comparison of §2.5 is ever reached. Percentages and weights are held as exact decimals and rendered back on the league resource as JSON numbers whatever spelling arrived, so a `handicap_percentage` supplied as the string `"0.90"` and one supplied as the number `0.9` both read back as `0.9`, and `prize_place_weights` is echoed as an array of JSON numbers on the same footing.

Adoption from `FORMING` moves the league to `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`, writes every bylaw field onto the league, sets `bylaws_adopted_at` to the current logical-clock instant, and increments the league's `version` by 1. That instant matters far beyond the bylaws themselves: it is the moment from which the fee-remittance late-filing window of §3.3 is measured. Re-adoption is legitimate and is the league's non-linear edge. A league already at `BYLAWS_ADOPTED` may re-adopt at a later meeting, remaining at `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`; a league already at `IN_SEASON` may amend at a special meeting held while the season is under way, remaining at `IN_SEASON`. Both paths require the same optimistic-concurrency echo and the same bylaw-validity checks as the first adoption, both overwrite every bylaw field, both reset `bylaws_adopted_at` to the current logical-clock instant, and both increment `version` by 1.

The recomputation an amendment triggers is where the design earns its keep. Every membership of the league has its `entering_average`, `current_average`, `average_status` and `handicap_per_game` re-derived, and every team of the league has its `team_handicap_per_game` re-derived. A mid-season amendment re-derives all of those *from the pins that are already certified* — no certified pin is added and none is removed — which is exactly how a league can observe the two handicap-basis variants of §3.6 against identical scoring data. One thing an amendment never disturbs: the per-side team handicaps frozen onto matches, `home_team_handicap_per_game` and `away_team_handicap_per_game`, stay at the values captured when each lineup was submitted. The amendment's reach is bounded to the memberships and the teams of the league and stops there. That has one consequence worth stating outright: a team whose `sanctioned_roster_count` already meets a newly *lowered* `legal_lineup_minimum` does move `PROVISIONAL → CONFIRMED` on the amendment, yet the league's own `confirmed_team_count`, `sanctioned_member_count` and `certified_week_high_water` are not recomputed by the amendment at all — they are next refreshed by the sanction cascade of §3.3 or the certification cascade of §3.5, and the amendment neither promotes the league's `status` nor reconsiders any match. Only these three league statuses admit a bylaw payload: `FORMING` for a first adoption, and `BYLAWS_ADOPTED` or `IN_SEASON` for an amendment. Any other league status yields `ERR_LEAGUE_STATE_INVALID`.

Schedule adoption is the secretary's other founding act and requires the league to stand at `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`. The payload carries `total_weeks` — a non-negative integer with no upper bound, which nothing requires to agree with the highest `week_number` among the submitted matches — and `matches`, and each element of `matches` supplies `week_number` (1 through 40), `home_team_id`, `away_team_id`, `lane_pair_id`, `starts_at` and `ends_at`, where `ends_at` must be strictly later than `starts_at`; `starts_at` is the inclusive beginning of the lane-pair occupation and `ends_at` its exclusive end. Every match is created in `DRAFT` with `home_points` 0, `away_points` 0 and both frozen team handicaps 0, and the submitted `total_weeks` is written onto the league — a value the projected prize basis of §3.7 later projects against. The response is the array of created matches, in the order their elements were supplied, carrying `response_type` `array` and HTTP 201. Were a match ever created at a moment when the league already stood at `SANCTIONED` or `IN_SEASON`, it would be created directly in `SCHEDULED` and would never occupy `DRAFT` at all. The schedule must reference exactly `declared_team_count` distinct teams and must name a different home and away team on every match; failing either of those yields `ERR_TEAM_COUNT_MISMATCH`. That every referenced team and every referenced lane pair belongs to *this* league is a separate and earlier requirement: a row that resolves but is owned by another league is refused with `ERR_CROSS_LEAGUE_REFERENCE` under check 10 of §2.6, not with `ERR_TEAM_COUNT_MISMATCH`. Separately, no two matches on the same lane pair that are neither voided nor forfeited may have overlapping occupation windows, where the window runs from `starts_at` inclusive to `ends_at` exclusive — so two matches that merely touch, one ending at the exact instant the next starts, do not conflict. A collision refuses the entire request with `ERR_LANE_PAIR_CONFLICT` and **no match is created at all**.

The season closes when the secretary declares it closed, which requires the league to be `IN_SEASON` and requires every match of the league to have reached `CERTIFIED`, `FORFEITED` or `VOID`; an unsettled match is refused with the same `ERR_LEAGUE_STATE_INVALID` a wrong league status yields, and a league carrying no matches at all satisfies the requirement vacuously and closes without objection. Closing moves the league `IN_SEASON → SEASON_CLOSED`; from that point lineup submission, score entry and match certification are all refused, and the prize distribution workflow of §3.7 becomes available. The league's full status set is `FORMING`, `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`, `SANCTIONED`, `IN_SEASON`, `SEASON_CLOSED` and `PRIZES_DISBURSED`, and none of them is terminal — `SEASON_CLOSED → PRIZES_DISBURSED` on disbursement and `PRIZES_DISBURSED → SEASON_CLOSED` again on the first award reversal.

### 3.2. Teams, Enrolment and the Prior-Season Record

A team is entered by the secretary with just two values: the `league_id` it competes in and a `name` between 1 and 80 characters that must be unique within that league, compared case-sensitively on the value exactly as supplied so that "Alley Cats" and "alley cats" are two different teams; a collision within the league is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Team entry is permitted only while the league is `FORMING` or `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`. A new team stands at `PROVISIONAL` with `sanctioned_roster_count` 0, `team_handicap_per_game` 0, `total_points` 0 and `total_scratch_pins` 0. There are exactly two team statuses, `PROVISIONAL` and `CONFIRMED`, and `CONFIRMED` is terminal: nothing ever moves a team back out of it. A team reaches `CONFIRMED` only by cascade, when its `sanctioned_roster_count` — the number of its roster memberships whose `sanction_status` is `SANCTIONED` — reaches the league's `legal_lineup_minimum`. The league's own teams are readable as a sub-resource list under the league, and that path is resolved left to right: a league that does not exist yields `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_FOUND` before the listing is attempted at all. That listing is ordered by `created_at` ascending and may be narrowed by team `status`.

Enrolment binds a registered member to a league for one season. The secretary supplies `league_id`, `member_id`, an optional `team_id`, a `roster_role` of either `REGULAR` or `SUBSTITUTE`, and optionally the bowler's prior-season record as `prior_season_average` (0 through 300 when present, otherwise null for a bowler with no prior record) and `prior_season_games` (0 through 200, defaulting to 0). `team_id` is nullable because a `SUBSTITUTE` may be carried on no team at all, and it is equally optional for a `REGULAR` — nothing obliges a `REGULAR` enrolment to name a team; when it is supplied it must name a team of the same league, whatever `status` that team itself stands at. Like team entry, enrolment is permitted only while the league is `FORMING` or `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`. A member may hold at most one membership per league, and a second attempt yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP`.

A new membership starts at `sanction_status` `UNSANCTIONED` and `remittance_status` `UNPAID`, with `handicap_per_game` 0, `average_status` `PROVISIONAL`, `certified_games` 0 and `certified_pins` 0. Its `entering_average` is computed immediately, and the rule is a strict three-part test evaluated against the membership's own declared prior-season record and nothing else — no scoring data participates. **If** `prior_season_average` is present, **and** `prior_season_games` is at least 21 games, **and** the league's `carryover_policy` is either `PRIOR_SEASON_FULL` or `BLENDED`, **then** `entering_average` is set to exactly the value of `prior_season_average`, copied without any rounding. In every other case — no prior average, fewer than 21 prior games, or a `carryover_policy` of `NONE` — `entering_average` is null. A bowler enrolled while the league is still `FORMING` with no bylaws adopted therefore has a null `entering_average`, because no carryover policy exists yet to satisfy the third part of the test. `entering_average` is re-derived at exactly three moments: at enrolment, at the instant the membership is sanctioned, and on every bylaw amendment. `current_average` is derived at enrolment as well, by the very rule of §3.6 applied to zero certified games, which makes it exactly equal to `entering_average` whenever that is not null and null otherwise; the enrolment response therefore already carries it, and the membership's `version` is still 1.

### 3.3. Sanctioning: Remitting Fees Upward and the Four-Hop Cascade Back Down

Sanctioning is a money errand with membership consequences, so the treasurer owns the filing and the association owns the decision. The treasurer opens a draft remittance by supplying the `league_id`, an ordered `membership_ids` list of between 1 and 60 membership identifiers with no duplicates — a repeated identifier inside the one array is a shape failure refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP` — and every element a membership of that same league, and a `declared_amount_cents` between 0 and 10000000 — the amount the league declares on the remittance form. The draft prices nothing: it is created at `DRAFT` with `member_count` set to the length of `membership_ids`, `computed_amount_cents` 0, `penalty_cents` 0 and `submitted_at`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `national_receipt_no` and `rejection_reason` all null. Drafting carries no league-status gate of its own — the league's `status` is never consulted, so a draft may be opened while the league still stands at `FORMING`. A membership may be listed only when its `sanction_status` is `UNSANCTIONED` or `PENDING_REMITTANCE` **and** its `remittance_status` is `UNPAID` or `REFUNDED`; a membership outside that combination yields `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_STATE_INVALID`. Separately, a membership may appear on at most one remittance of the league that has not been rejected, and listing it on a second live remittance yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP`.

Submission is where the money is computed, and it is legal from `DRAFT` and from `REJECTED` — the latter being the re-filing loop after the national body sends a remittance back. Submission additionally requires the league to be at `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`, `SANCTIONED` or `IN_SEASON`, and requires every listed membership to be `UNSANCTIONED` at that moment. The submission payload may optionally carry a corrected `declared_amount_cents`, which overwrites the value recorded at drafting — this is the correction path a rejected remittance uses. On submission the server prices the remittance as follows, and the whole computation is in whole cents.

First it establishes how late the filing is. The number of elapsed days is the whole number of complete twenty-four-hour days between the league's `bylaws_adopted_at` and the submission instant, discarding any partial day. A submission instant that falls earlier than `bylaws_adopted_at` — reachable only by winding the logical clock backwards — counts as zero elapsed days rather than as a negative count, and so attracts no penalty. The late-filing grace period is twenty-one days, so a per-member penalty of 1000 cents applies when — and only when — the elapsed whole-day count is **strictly greater than** 21; at exactly 21 elapsed days there is no penalty. Second, it prices each listed membership in the order the identifiers appear in `membership_ids`: a membership whose member's `member_class` is `ADULT` carries a national fee of 2600 cents, and a membership whose member's `member_class` is `YOUTH` carries a national fee of 1200 cents. Third, every listed membership additionally carries a flat local-association fee of 500 cents. `member_count` is set to the number of identifiers in `membership_ids`. `penalty_cents` is `member_count` multiplied by the per-member penalty just established. `computed_amount_cents` is the sum, over every listed membership, of that membership's national fee plus the 500-cent local fee, plus `penalty_cents`. Memberships of the league that are absent from the list are not priced at all. If `declared_amount_cents` does not equal the `computed_amount_cents` just calculated, the submission is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. A corrected `declared_amount_cents` carried on that refused submission has nevertheless already been recorded onto the remittance by the time the comparison fails, and it stays there, while `computed_amount_cents`, `penalty_cents`, `member_count`, `status`, `submitted_at`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `updated_at` and `version` are all left exactly as the refused request found them, and no listed membership moves on either axis. And because a re-filing recomputes the penalty against the *current* clock, a remittance re-filed after the twenty-one-day window carries the late penalty even though the original filing did not.

A successful submission moves the remittance `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` (or `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` on a re-filing), stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical-clock instant, records `submitted_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` for audit purposes only, and advances every listed membership on **both** of its state axes. Those two axes are the subtlest structure in the whole domain, so they are worth spelling out. `sanction_status` tracks national sanctioning and runs `UNSANCTIONED → PENDING_REMITTANCE → SANCTIONED`, reverting to `UNSANCTIONED` when a remittance is rejected. `remittance_status` tracks the money and runs `UNPAID → INVOICED → REMITTED`, or `INVOICED → REFUNDED` on a rejection, and `REFUNDED → INVOICED` again on a re-filing. The two axes advance on the same requests but neither is derivable from the other: a `REFUNDED` row can sit at `UNSANCTIONED`, an `INVOICED` row can sit at `PENDING_REMITTANCE`, and a re-filed remittance moves the money axis `REFUNDED → INVOICED` while the sanction axis repeats `UNSANCTIONED → PENDING_REMITTANCE`. On submission, then, each listed membership moves `UNSANCTIONED → PENDING_REMITTANCE` on the sanction axis and `UNPAID → INVOICED` (or `REFUNDED → INVOICED`) on the money axis, and the fee tier that membership contributes is what the pricing above summed.

The national body's decision is recorded by an `AssociationOfficer` against a remittance that is `SUBMITTED`. Like drafting, and unlike submission, the decision consults no league `status` of its own: the remittance's own `status` is the only state gate it applies. The payload carries `decision`, which is either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`; an `ACCEPT` must supply `national_receipt_no` and a `REJECT` must supply `rejection_reason`. An `ACCEPT` moves the remittance `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and is the head of a **four-hop cascade** that fires in the order the identifiers appear in `membership_ids`:

- **Hop 1 — the memberships.** Every listed membership moves `PENDING_REMITTANCE → SANCTIONED` on the sanction axis and `INVOICED → REMITTED` on the money axis. Each one gains its `handicap_per_game` per §3.6, and has `entering_average`, `current_average` and `average_status` recomputed. Each affected member's `sanctioned_membership_count` increases, the league's `sanctioned_member_count` increases by 1 per membership, and `national_member_number` is minted for any member whose number is still null — the numbers being handed out in the members' own ascending `created_at` order per §3.1, which need not follow `membership_ids` order. A member who both receives a number and sees `sanctioned_membership_count` change is written twice on this hop, once for each, so that member's `version` advances by 2; a member who already held a number and only sees the counter change advances by 1.
- **Hop 2 — the teams.** Caused by hop 1: each roster team named by a listed membership has its `sanctioned_roster_count` and its `team_handicap_per_game` recomputed from the roster rule of §3.6 whether or not its own status moves, and a team whose count has reached the league's `legal_lineup_minimum` moves `PROVISIONAL → CONFIRMED`.
- **Hop 3 — the league.** Caused by hop 2: the league's `confirmed_team_count` increases by 1 for each newly confirmed team, and when that counter equals `declared_team_count` the league itself moves `BYLAWS_ADOPTED → SANCTIONED`. The counter recompute and the status move are two separate writes to the league row, so on the accepting request that both moves a counter and sanctions the league the league's `version` advances by 2, while a request that only moves its counters advances it by 1 and one that moves neither leaves it alone.
- **Hop 4 — the matches.** Caused by hop 3: every match of the league still sitting at `DRAFT` moves to `SCHEDULED`, which is what makes it eligible for lineup submission.

A `REJECT` moves the remittance `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and runs the inverse, but only the inverse of hop 1 — no team and no league counter moves, precisely because none of the listed rows ever reached `SANCTIONED`. Each listed membership falls back `PENDING_REMITTANCE → UNSANCTIONED` on the sanction axis and `INVOICED → REFUNDED` on the money axis, its `handicap_per_game` is forced back to 0, the fees are treated as returned to the league, and the remittance becomes eligible for re-filing. The `national_receipt_no` recorded on an acceptance and the `rejection_reason` recorded on a rejection are both null in the other case.

### 3.4. Certified Lanes and the Legal Lineup

Lane certification is an association act, not a league one, so the `AssociationOfficer` — never the secretary — registers a lane pair by supplying the `league_id` whose matches may occupy it, a `pair_number` between 1 and 60 that must be unique within the league — a collision being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and a `certified_through` instant that is the last instant, inclusive, at which the certification remains valid. Nothing requires that instant to lie in the future at registration: a pair registered with a `certified_through` already behind the logical clock is created successfully and simply reads `EXPIRED` from the outset. Registration sets `last_certified_at` to the current logical-clock instant and leaves `revoked_at` null, and is permitted while the league is `FORMING` or `BYLAWS_ADOPTED`. A pair's `certification_status` is never stored; it is derived on every read by a three-way test in a fixed order: it is `REVOKED` when `revoked_at` is set; otherwise it is `CERTIFIED` when `certified_through` is at or after the current logical-clock instant; otherwise it is `EXPIRED`.

That derivation gives the pair a state machine driven partly by the clock alone. A pair sitting at `CERTIFIED` slides to `EXPIRED` the moment the logical clock advances strictly past its `certified_through`, with no request ever addressed to the pair — and once it has, opening any match assigned to that pair is refused with `ERR_LANE_PAIR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. An officer can move a pair `EXPIRED → CERTIFIED` again by supplying `action` `RECERTIFY` together with a replacement `certified_through` that is strictly later than the current logical-clock instant; that replaces `certified_through` and resets `last_certified_at` to now. An officer can move a pair `CERTIFIED → REVOKED` or `EXPIRED → REVOKED` by supplying `action` `REVOKE`, which sets `revoked_at` to the current logical-clock instant and permanently ends the pair's usefulness: every future opening on that pair is refused. `REVOKED` is terminal in the strongest sense — `revoked_at` is never cleared, and a `RECERTIFY` aimed at a pair whose `revoked_at` is already set is itself refused with `ERR_LANE_PAIR_NOT_CERTIFIED` rather than reviving it. Three things about the certification action are worth stating because they are easy to assume the other way. It carries no league-status gate at all and is legal whatever `status` the pair's league stands at, up to and including `SEASON_CLOSED`. A `RECERTIFY` aimed at a pair that is still `CERTIFIED` is not refused either: it simply replaces `certified_through` and resets `last_certified_at`, the self-loop `CERTIFIED → CERTIFIED`. And a second `REVOKE` aimed at an already-revoked pair is accepted rather than refused — it restamps `revoked_at` to the current instant and bumps the pair's `version` as any mutation does — while a `REVOKE` leaves `certified_through` and `last_certified_at` exactly as they stood.

Lineup submission is the busiest surface in the API and belongs jointly to the `TeamCaptain` acting for their own side and the `LeagueSecretary` acting for either. The payload carries `expected_match_version`, the `team_id` of the side whose lineup this is, and a `slots` array; `expected_match_version` must be a non-negative integer, and a negative or non-integer echo is a shape failure refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the version comparison of §2.5 is ever reached. Each element of `slots` carries a `position`, an `entry_type`, a `membership_id` (null for a `VACANCY` slot, and never supplied on one) and, for a pre-bowled series only, a `prebowled_at` instant. The submittable `entry_type` values are `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE`, `VACANCY`, `ABSENTEE` and `PREBOWL`; the sixth value, `FORFEIT`, is never submittable and exists only on rows the server itself creates when a side forfeits.

Lineup submission is legal while the match sits at `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED` or `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED`, and requires the league to be `SANCTIONED` or `IN_SEASON`. The state outcome depends on what the opposing side has already done. A submission on a `SCHEDULED` match where the other side has no slots yet leaves the match at `SCHEDULED` — the self-loop `SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED`. A submission on a `SCHEDULED` match where the opposing side already has slots for this match completes both sides and advances `SCHEDULED → LINEUPS_SUBMITTED`, at which point the match becomes openable. A match still sitting at `DRAFT` is treated exactly as a `SCHEDULED` one on both counts: a submission leaves it at `DRAFT` while the opposing side has no slots yet, and advances `DRAFT → LINEUPS_SUBMITTED` when the opposing side already has slots for this match. A side that wants to revise its own lineup before the match opens submits again against a `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED` match, which stays at `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED` — the self-loop `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED → LINEUPS_SUBMITTED`. All of these cases carry the same slot preconditions and the same side effects, and every one of them increments the match's `version` by 1.

The `team_id` submitted for must be the match's `home_team_id` or its `away_team_id`, and a `team_id` that is neither yields `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. Both the submitting team and the opposing team on that match must have reached `CONFIRMED`; a scheduled match can perfectly well name a team that never got there, because the league's `declared_team_count` may have been satisfied by other teams, and a lineup on such a match is refused with `ERR_TEAM_NOT_CONFIRMED`. The sanction gate reaches exactly the bowling slots: a `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `PREBOWL` slot naming a membership whose `sanction_status` is not `SANCTIONED` is refused with `ERR_BOWLER_NOT_SANCTIONED`, while an `ABSENTEE` slot's membership is never tested on that axis at all and may perfectly well name an unsanctioned bowler. Nothing else binds a named membership to the submitting side either: on a `REGULAR`, `PREBOWL` or `ABSENTEE` slot neither the membership's own `team_id` nor its `roster_role` is consulted, so a membership of this league carried on no team — or even one carried on the opposing team — may be named, and only the `SUBSTITUTE` eligibility rule and the both-sides duplicate rule below constrain that.

Legality itself is four separate checks, and they are consulted in this order. The lineup must carry exactly `lineup_positions` slots, the `position` values must be exactly 1 through `lineup_positions` with each appearing exactly once — a `position` outside that range carries no bound of its own in the shape pass and is refused by this check rather than as a shape error — and at least `legal_lineup_minimum` of the slots must be `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `PREBOWL`; any of those failing is `ERR_ILLEGAL_LINEUP`. Then the number of `VACANCY` slots plus the number of `ABSENTEE` slots must not exceed `max_vacancy_slots_per_lineup`, failing which is `ERR_VACANCY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Then no membership may appear twice within the one lineup and no membership may appear on both sides of the same match, failing which is `ERR_DUPLICATE_BOWLER_IN_LINEUP`. Then every `SUBSTITUTE` slot must name a `SANCTIONED` membership of the same league whose own `team_id` is either null or different from the opposing team, failing which is `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

Two further gates protect the bowler rather than the lineup. A bowler may not be double-booked: if a named bowler already occupies a lineup slot on another match that is not voided, and that other match's occupation window — widened by a thirty-minute travel buffer on **both** sides — touches or overlaps this match's own unwidened window from `starts_at` to `ends_at`, the submission is refused with `ERR_BOWLER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The comparison is on closed intervals, which has a sharp boundary worth stating plainly: a gap of exactly thirty minutes between the two matches still conflicts, and a gap of thirty-one minutes does not. And a bowler whose `dues_arrears_weeks` exceeds the league's `max_dues_arrears_weeks` may not be named on any slot, which is refused with `ERR_DUES_ARREARS_BLOCK`. A `PREBOWL` slot must carry a `prebowled_at` strictly earlier than the match's `starts_at`; one at or after that instant, or missing altogether, is refused with `ERR_PREBOWL_NOT_BEFORE_MATCH`.

A successful submission does five things. It **deletes** the submitting side's existing slots for that match and replaces them with the newly submitted set — the prior submission is superseded outright, and the resulting rows are discoverable only through the lineup-slot listing filtered by match. It freezes `handicap_per_game_applied` onto every created slot per the lineup rule of §3.6, including the substituted values used for `VACANCY` and `ABSENTEE` slots; every created slot begins with its three game fields null, `games_recorded` 0, `scratch_series` 0 and `certified` false, whatever its `entry_type`. It sets `counts_toward_average` to true on exactly those slots whose `entry_type` is `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `PREBOWL` **and** whose `membership_id` is not null, and false on every other slot. It recomputes the submitting side's frozen team handicap into `home_team_handicap_per_game` or `away_team_handicap_per_game` as appropriate, each of which reads 0 until that side's lineup has been submitted. And it increments the match's `version` by 1 — the value the next submission against that match must echo.

### 3.5. Match Night: Opening, Scoring, Forfeiting and Certifying

Opening a match for bowling is the secretary's act and moves the match `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED → OPEN`. It requires the league to be `SANCTIONED` or `IN_SEASON`, and it requires the assigned lane pair to be genuinely usable: the pair's `revoked_at` must be null and its `certified_through` must be at or after the match's `starts_at`. A revoked or expired pair produces `ERR_LANE_PAIR_NOT_CERTIFIED`, while a pair that is otherwise fine but whose `certified_through` falls earlier than the match's `starts_at` produces `ERR_LANE_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. Opening the league's very first match carries a side effect on the league itself: `SANCTIONED → IN_SEASON`, which is the point from which weekly dues may be recorded. Opening an already-open match yields `ERR_MATCH_STATE_INVALID`.

Score entry moves the match `OPEN → SCORES_ENTERED` and, like certification below, requires the league to be `IN_SEASON`. The payload carries an `entries` array, and each element supplies a `series_entry_id` together with `game1_pins`, `game2_pins` and `game3_pins`. Every slot of both sides whose `entry_type` is `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `ABSENTEE` must appear in `entries`. An `ABSENTEE` slot appears but with its three game fields omitted or null, because the bowler did not roll: it therefore records `games_recorded` 0 and `scratch_series` 0 while still contributing the handicap frozen onto it. Nothing forces that omission, however: an `ABSENTEE` slot that does carry pin values has them recorded and counted into its side's game totals exactly as a bowling slot's would be, because `games_recorded` and `scratch_series` are always simply the count and the sum of the slot's non-null game values. A `VACANCY` slot is never listed in `entries` at all, and listing one is refused with `ERR_SCORE_SET_INVALID`. Each submitted pin value must be an integer between 0 and 300 inclusive, and the two ends of that range fail differently: a negative value is a shape failure refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under check 9 of §2.6, while a value above 300 is refused with `ERR_SCORE_SET_INVALID`. Each `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `PREBOWL` slot must end up carrying all three of its game scores — a series is three games — and a bowling slot missing one of its three scores is refused as `ERR_SCORE_SET_INVALID`. `ERR_SCORE_SET_INVALID` is likewise the code for a `series_entry_id` that names no slot of *this* match, whether it names nothing at all or a slot of another match — score entry is not one of the endpoints check 5 of §2.6 resolves body identifiers for, so an unknown identifier here never produces `ERR_SERIES_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, and only a blank or non-string one is a shape failure — and for the same `series_entry_id` listed twice in one request. For every slot, `games_recorded` is set to the count of its game fields that are not null and `scratch_series` to the sum of those same non-null game values. Nothing is certified by score entry, so no average and no handicap moves yet.

Points are computed at score entry and follow the league's four-point scheme. Three points are decided game by game and the fourth on the total series. For each game in turn — game one, then game two, then game three — each side's game total is the sum, across that side's slots, of that slot's pin count for that game, where a `VACANCY` slot contributes the league's `vacancy_score` instead of any recorded pins, a `FORFEIT` slot contributes 0, and a null pin value counts as 0; to that sum is added that side's frozen per-game team handicap, `home_team_handicap_per_game` for the home side and `away_team_handicap_per_game` for the away side. If the home side's total for that game is greater than the away side's, the home side gains 1 point; if the away side's is greater, the away side gains 1 point; if the two are equal, each side gains half a point. Then each side's series total is the sum of its three game totals as just computed, and the same comparison awards a fourth point: 1 to the side with the greater series total, or half a point to each on a tie. The pins that feed this are only the match's own slots, and the per-game team handicap is only ever the value frozen onto the match at lineup submission — never a live recomputation from the current roster. `home_points` and `away_points` are rendered as JSON numbers carrying that one decimal place, so a clean sweep reads back as `4.0` against `0.0` and a match tied in every game and on the series as `2.0` against `2.0`.

A forfeit is declared by the secretary against one side, naming `forfeiting_team_id`, and is legal from `SCHEDULED`, `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED` or `OPEN`. The surface carries **no league-status gate of its own** — the league's `status` is never consulted, so a side may be forfeited while the league still stands at `SANCTIONED` rather than `IN_SEASON`. The named team must be the match's `home_team_id` or `away_team_id`, and one that is neither yields `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`; that is also the code for a `forfeiting_team_id` naming no team at all, since forfeit declaration is not one of the endpoints check 5 of §2.6 resolves body identifiers for and an unknown identifier therefore never produces `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND` here, only a blank or non-string one being a shape failure. The **preallocation procedure** runs in all three cases, differing only in whether there were slots to replace. From `SCHEDULED` the forfeiting side has no slots at all, so `FORFEIT` rows are *created* for positions 1 through `lineup_positions`. From `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED` or from `OPEN` the forfeiting side's existing slots are *replaced* by `FORFEIT` rows. Either way each `FORFEIT` row carries `membership_id` null, `game1_pins`, `game2_pins` and `game3_pins` at 0, `games_recorded` 0, `scratch_series` 0, `handicap_per_game_applied` 0, `counts_toward_average` false and `certified` false. Also in all three cases: the forfeiting side is written to 0 points and the opponent to the full 4 points with no game-by-game comparison performed, and the forfeiting side's frozen team handicap is recomputed from its new `FORFEIT` rows and therefore becomes 0. Where the opponent had already recorded pins, those slots keep `counts_toward_average` true — `FORFEIT` rows never count toward anybody's average. The match lands at `FORFEITED`, which is terminal. Note that the forfeit surface reaches `FORFEITED` only from `SCHEDULED`, `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED` or `OPEN`; the `CERTIFIED → FORFEITED` edge exists but is reachable only through an upheld protest remedy, as §3.9 describes, and a forfeit declared against a `CERTIFIED` or `VOID` match is refused with `ERR_MATCH_STATE_INVALID` — as is one against a match still at `DRAFT`, one at `SCORES_ENTERED` and one already `FORFEITED`. Declaring a forfeit also runs hops 2 through 4 of the certification cascade below, over the memberships whose slots it just replaced and over the match's two sides: each of those memberships re-derives its averages and handicap, both teams re-derive their figures and absorb the forfeited match into `total_points` and `total_scratch_pins`, and the league re-derives its counters — `certified_week_high_water` included, because a `FORFEITED` match counts as settled — after which every membership of the league has its dues figures refreshed exactly as hop 4 describes.

Certification moves the match `SCORES_ENTERED → CERTIFIED` and requires the league to be `IN_SEASON`. This is the *only* way games become certified games, and it is the head of a four-hop cascade of its own:

- **Hop 1 — the slots.** Every slot of the match has `certified` set to true, and the match's `certified_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant.
- **Hop 2 — the bowlers.** Caused by hop 1: every membership sitting behind a now-certified average-bearing slot recomputes `certified_games`, `certified_pins`, `current_average`, `average_status` and `handicap_per_game` per the rules of §3.6.
- **Hop 3 — the teams.** Caused by hop 2: the affected teams are the match's own two sides together with the roster team of every membership behind a now-certified average-bearing slot — a substitute's own team is therefore re-derived even though it never played this match — and each of them recomputes `sanctioned_roster_count`, recomputes `team_handicap_per_game` from its bowlers' new handicaps, takes the `PROVISIONAL → CONFIRMED` promotion if that count now reaches `legal_lineup_minimum`, and absorbs this match into `total_points` and `total_scratch_pins`.
- **Hop 4 — the league.** Caused by hop 1: the league's `certified_week_high_water` is recomputed, which shifts the inputs of the prize basis in §3.7. `sanctioned_member_count` and `confirmed_team_count` are recomputed in the very same write to the league row, so the whole hop costs the league at most one `version` increment; and once the new high-water mark stands, **every** membership of the league — not only the ones this match touched — has `dues_paid_cents` and `dues_arrears_weeks` refreshed against it, each such membership taking a `version` increment only where one of those two figures actually moves.

Two league-level aggregates are worth defining precisely, because both discriminate between the branches above. `certified_week_high_water` is the greatest week number W such that **every** match of the league whose `week_number` is at or below W has reached `CERTIFIED`, `FORFEITED` or `VOID`; it reads 0 whenever week 1 is not yet fully settled. A team's `total_points` is the sum of that team's points over every match whose status is `CERTIFIED` or `FORFEITED`, recorded to one decimal place and rendered as a JSON number in that same one-decimal form — a `VOID` match contributes nothing. A team's `total_scratch_pins` is the sum of the scratch pins recorded by that team's slots on matches whose status is `CERTIFIED` or `FORFEITED`, and it is the tie-break in the standings.

### 3.6. Averages, Handicaps and the Two Bases the Bylaws Can Choose

Averages are derived from certified games and nothing else. For any one membership, the input set is exactly those lineup slots that name it whose `certified` flag is true **and** whose `counts_toward_average` flag is true — which, by the definition of that flag, means slots whose `entry_type` is `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `PREBOWL` with a non-null `membership_id`. `VACANCY`, `ABSENTEE` and `FORFEIT` slots never participate, and neither do slots on matches that sit at `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `LINEUPS_SUBMITTED`, `OPEN`, `SCORES_ENTERED` or `VOID`, because none of those slots are certified. Over that input set, `certified_games` is the total of the slots' `games_recorded` and `certified_pins` is the total of the slots' `scratch_series`.

Those two integers then select between two variants on the league's minimum-games threshold. When `certified_games` is at least `minimum_games_for_average`, the membership's `average_status` becomes `ESTABLISHED` and `current_average` is `certified_pins` divided by `certified_games` with the fractional part discarded — and the prior-season record is ignored entirely, however generous it was. When `certified_games` is below `minimum_games_for_average`, `average_status` is `PROVISIONAL` and the prior-season record comes into play according to the league's `carryover_policy`, so that the *same* certified pins yield a different average than the established variant would. Under `carryover_policy` `NONE`, `current_average` is `certified_pins` divided by `certified_games` with the fraction discarded when the bowler has at least one certified game, and null when they have none. Under `PRIOR_SEASON_FULL`, `current_average` is the membership's `entering_average` when that is not null, and otherwise falls back to the same truncated quotient when at least one certified game exists, or null when none does. Under `BLENDED`, when `entering_average` is not null, `current_average` is the blend of the two records: multiply `entering_average` by `prior_season_games`, add `certified_pins`, divide that sum by the total of `prior_season_games` plus `certified_games`, and discard the fraction; and when `entering_average` is null, `BLENDED` falls back exactly as `PRIOR_SEASON_FULL` does — the truncated quotient when at least one certified game exists, otherwise null. Averages are recomputed on the certification hop that certifies a slot, on the decertification hops that a void or a protest-driven forfeit runs, on every bylaw amendment, at enrolment, and on every remittance transition that moves a membership's `sanction_status` — submission, acceptance and rejection alike each re-derive `current_average` and `average_status` from the pins then standing, even though none of them adds or removes a certified pin.

A bowler's per-game handicap is derived from an average, but a gate comes first. **If the membership's `sanction_status` is anything other than `SANCTIONED`, `handicap_per_game` is 0** — an unsanctioned bowler carries no handicap, which is why the sanction cascade of §3.3 grants a handicap and a rejection revokes it. For a sanctioned membership, the league's `handicap_average_basis` selects the basis average. Under `ENTERING`, the basis average is the frozen `entering_average`, so games rolled this season never move the handicap at all. Under `CURRENT`, the basis average is the live `current_average` when that is not null, and falls back to `entering_average` when it is. If the selected basis average is null, `handicap_per_game` is 0. Otherwise the percentage is chosen by the bowler's class — `handicap_percentage_youth` when the member's `member_class` is `YOUTH`, and `handicap_percentage` otherwise — and the handicap is computed as follows: subtract the basis average from `handicap_base`, replace a negative result with 0, multiply by the chosen percentage, discard the fraction of that product, and finally take the smaller of that integer and `max_handicap_per_game`. The order of the last two steps is fixed and consequential: **the product is floored first and the ceiling is applied second.**

The two bases are not a stylistic choice; they produce different integers from identical data. Consider a bowler who entered at an average of 180 and whose blended average has since risen to 182, in a league with `handicap_base` 220 and `handicap_percentage` 0.90. On the `ENTERING` basis the handicap is 36; on the `CURRENT` basis it is 34. This is precisely why a mid-season bylaw amendment re-derives every handicap from already-certified pins without adding or removing a single pin. `handicap_per_game` is recomputed whenever the bowler's average is recomputed, on the transition that sanctions a membership, on the transition that reverts a membership out of `PENDING_REMITTANCE` to `UNSANCTIONED`, and on every bylaw amendment.

Team handicaps come in two flavours that must not be confused with one another, and both obey one **pinned rounding order**: each bowler's handicap is floored to an integer first, and only those floored integers are added together. Summing the unfloored products and flooring once at the end is a different number, not a rounding nicety. With averages of 178, 183, 191 and 205 in a league at `handicap_base` 220 and `handicap_percentage` 0.90, flooring per bowler gives 37 plus 33 plus 26 plus 13, which is 109; deferring the flooring to the end would give 110.

The **roster** flavour is what a team's own `team_handicap_per_game` reports. Take every membership whose `team_id` is that team and whose `sanction_status` is `SANCTIONED`; rank them by `handicap_per_game` descending and, where two are equal, by `created_at` ascending; keep the first `lineup_positions` of them, or all of them when there are fewer candidates than that (there is no padding with notional vacancies); and add the kept memberships' `handicap_per_game` values together. A deep roster therefore reports a team handicap that no single lineup need ever match. This value is recomputed on hop 2 of the sanction cascade of §3.3 for every roster team the cascade touches, whether or not that team's own status moves, on hop 3 of match certification, on the decertification hops of a void or protest-driven forfeit, and on bylaw amendment.

The **lineup** flavour is what a match's `home_team_handicap_per_game` and `away_team_handicap_per_game` report, and it is the sum of the `handicap_per_game_applied` values frozen onto exactly that side's slots on exactly that match — including `VACANCY` and `ABSENTEE` substitutes that no roster ranking would ever have selected. Each slot's `handicap_per_game_applied` is frozen at lineup submission by the slot's `entry_type`. A `REGULAR`, `SUBSTITUTE` or `PREBOWL` slot freezes the named membership's own `handicap_per_game`. A `VACANCY` slot freezes the smaller of `max_handicap_per_game` and the floor of the product of `handicap_percentage` with the difference between `handicap_base` and `vacancy_average` (that difference replaced by 0 if it would be negative) — note that a vacancy is always priced at the adult percentage, since no bowler and therefore no `member_class` is attached to it. An `ABSENTEE` slot freezes the smaller of `max_handicap_per_game` and the floor of the product of the bowler's own percentage with the difference between `handicap_base` and the bowler's penalised basis average, where the penalised basis average is the bowler's basis average less `absentee_deduction` (replaced by 0 if that subtraction would go negative), and where the outer difference is likewise replaced by 0 if it would be negative; the basis average and the percentage are selected exactly as they are for a live bowler's handicap, with one deliberate exception: the sanction gate of the live rule is not applied to an absentee freeze at all, so the slot is priced from the averages standing on the named membership whatever its `sanction_status`, and an unsanctioned bowler whose own `handicap_per_game` reads 0 can still freeze a non-zero `handicap_per_game_applied` onto an `ABSENTEE` slot. A `FORFEIT` slot freezes 0.

### 3.7. Weekly Dues, Prize-Fund Accumulation and the Distribution Waterfall

Weekly dues are the prize fund's only source of money. A dues payment may be recorded either by the `LeagueTreasurer` collecting at the desk or by the `Bowler` paying for their own membership, and the payload is just two values: the `week_number` the dues cover, which must be between 1 and the league's `total_weeks` — a `week_number` below 1 or above that bound is a shape failure refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in the shape pass of check 9, so a league whose `total_weeks` still reads 0 admits no dues payment at all — and `amount_cents`, which must be between 0 and 10000 and must equal the league's `weekly_dues_cents` **exactly**. A short or over tender is refused with `ERR_DUES_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, because the prize and expense shares are derived from the bylaws rather than from the tender, and an inexact payment would credit the prize fund with money the league never actually collected. The membership must be `SANCTIONED`; one that is not yields `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_STATE_INVALID`. A membership may be charged at most once per `week_number`, and a second payment for the same week is refused with `ERR_DUES_WEEK_ALREADY_PAID`. Notably, the dues surface carries **no league-status gate of its own** — arrears may still be settled after the season has closed.

Each recorded payment splits deterministically: `prize_share_cents` is `weekly_dues_cents` less `weekly_expense_cents`, the portion that accrues to the prize fund, and `expense_share_cents` is `weekly_expense_cents`, the portion retained for league expenses. Recording a payment increases the membership's `dues_paid_cents` by the tendered `amount_cents` and recomputes its `dues_arrears_weeks`, and increases the league prize fund's `collected_prize_cents` and `undistributed_balance_cents` by the prize share, keeping the money invariant of §2.9 intact. Dues payments are immutable records — there is no update surface, and consistent with §2.4 they carry no `version` and no `updated_at`.

Arrears are measured against settled weeks, not against the calendar. A membership's `dues_arrears_weeks` is the league's `certified_week_high_water` less the number of distinct `week_number` values for which that membership has recorded a dues payment at or below that high-water mark, with a negative result replaced by 0. A bowler who has paid every settled week carries no arrears no matter how many future weeks remain unpaid, and this is the figure the lineup gate of §3.4 compares against `max_dues_arrears_weeks`.

The prize fund itself walks `ACCUMULATING → PLAN_DRAFTED → PLAN_APPROVED → DISBURSED`, with `DISBURSED` reverting to `PLAN_APPROVED` when a paid award is reversed. Drafting the distribution plan is the treasurer's act; it is legal from `ACCUMULATING` and from `PLAN_DRAFTED` (a fresh draft supersedes the prior one outright, overwriting every plan field), requires the league to be `IN_SEASON` or `SEASON_CLOSED`, and requires `place_count` to equal the length of `prize_place_weights`. The payload carries `expected_prize_fund_version`, the `basis_variant` and the `place_count`; `expected_prize_fund_version` must be a non-negative integer, and a negative or non-integer echo is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the version comparison of §2.5 is reached. The `place_count` agreement is itself part of that same shape pass and is therefore refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, ahead of the version echo and ahead of both status gates — and because a league that has adopted no bylaws carries a null `prize_place_weights` which reads here as a list of no elements, a draft attempted against such a league is refused that way whatever `place_count` it asks for, rather than with `ERR_LEAGUE_STATE_INVALID`. Drafting stamps `plan_drafted_at` with the current logical-clock instant, increments the fund's `version` by 1, and creates no award rows at all.

`basis_variant` selects between two genuinely different bases, and this is the fund's dual-variant calculation. Both begin from the same settled figure: the weekly prize share per bowler is `weekly_dues_cents` less `weekly_expense_cents`, and the **settled amount** is the total of `prize_share_cents` across every dues payment belonging to any membership of this league whose `week_number` is at or below the league's `certified_week_high_water`. Dues already collected for a week whose matches are not all settled are excluded from the settled amount under *both* variants. Under `CERTIFIED_TO_DATE`, `plan_basis_cents` is exactly that settled amount and the remaining schedule is ignored entirely; this is the settled-money basis, legal from `IN_SEASON` onward, and it is how the final plan is drafted once the season has closed. Under `PROJECTED_SEASON_END`, the number of remaining weeks is the league's `total_weeks` less its `certified_week_high_water` with a negative result replaced by 0, and `plan_basis_cents` is the settled amount plus the product of those remaining weeks, the league's `sanctioned_member_count` and the weekly prize share per bowler; this is the provisional plan drafted mid-season, and it is strictly larger than the certified basis whenever any week remains. A league with a settled amount of 11200 cents, eight sanctioned members, one remaining week and a 700-cent weekly prize share drafts a basis of 11200 under the certified variant and 16800 under the projected one, from identical dues data.

From `plan_basis_cents` the waterfall runs in a **pinned order of seven steps**, and the order is not negotiable: the reserve tier is read from `plan_basis_cents` itself, not from the net that the reserve reduces, and the administrative fee is a percentage of the post-reserve amount. Reading the tier off the net would be circular, and that reading is forbidden.

1. Choose the reserve percentage: 8 percent when `plan_basis_cents` is at or above the reserve tier threshold of 100000 cents — one thousand dollars — and 5 percent when it is below that threshold.
2. Set `reserve_cents` to the product of `plan_basis_cents` and the chosen reserve percentage, with the fraction discarded.
3. Set `admin_fee_cents` to 3 percent of the amount remaining after the reserve — that is, 3 percent of `plan_basis_cents` less `reserve_cents` — rounded half up to a whole cent.
4. Set `net_distributable_cents` to `plan_basis_cents` less `reserve_cents` less `admin_fee_cents`.
5. Establish the standings that decide which team stands in which place. The candidates are the teams of this league whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`; teams still at `PROVISIONAL` are never candidates, so their points and pins shift no place at all. Rank the candidates by `total_points` descending, then by `total_scratch_pins` descending, then by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. If there are fewer candidates than `place_count`, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TEAMS`. Otherwise, for every whole number *n* from 1 up to and including `place_count`, place *n* belongs to the *n*-th candidate in that ranking.
6. For each place in turn, compute the raw amount as the product of `net_distributable_cents` and that place's weight — the element of `prize_place_weights` at the position one before the place number, so place 1 uses the first element — with the fraction discarded.
7. Compute the remainder as `net_distributable_cents` less the total of all the raw amounts, and allocate that remainder one cent at a time to places in **ascending** place order beginning at place 1, wrapping back to place 1 if the remainder exceeds `place_count`. Each place's `amount_cents` is its raw amount plus the cents allocated to it. Because the remainder is fully allocated, the awards' `amount_cents` values total exactly `net_distributable_cents`.

Approval belongs to the secretary, not the treasurer. It requires the fund to be at `PLAN_DRAFTED` and the league to be at `SEASON_CLOSED`, and it requires at least `place_count` teams of the league to have reached `CONFIRMED` — falling short is again `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TEAMS`. Approval materialises one award per paying place, created in ascending place order, each carrying its `place`, its `weight` copied from the corresponding element of `prize_place_weights` at approval time, and its `amount_cents` from step 7 above, all in status `PENDING`. An award's `weight` is the league's own exact decimal and is rendered back on the award resource as a JSON number on exactly the footing §3.1 describes for the league's weights, so a weight of `0.50` reads back as `0.5`. The approving secretary's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `plan_approved_by_user_id`, and the fund moves `PLAN_DRAFTED → PLAN_APPROVED`. The new award rows are returned by no response body directly and are discoverable through the prize-award listing filtered by fund, ordered by `place` ascending.

Disbursement belongs to the treasurer and requires the fund at `PLAN_APPROVED` and the league at `SEASON_CLOSED`. The acting treasurer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `plan_approved_by_user_id`; equality is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The fund must also be able to cover the plan: the total of the `PENDING` awards' `amount_cents` — which is `net_distributable_cents` — must not exceed `undistributed_balance_cents`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PRIZE_FUND` rather than allowed to drive the balance below zero. On success every `PENDING` award becomes `PAID`, `paid_award_total_cents` is recomputed as the total of the paid amounts and `undistributed_balance_cents` decreases by the same amount, `disbursed_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, the fund moves `PLAN_APPROVED → DISBURSED`, and the league moves `SEASON_CLOSED → PRIZES_DISBURSED`. The fund row is written twice on that one request — once for the status and `disbursed_at`, once for the money recompute — so its `version` advances by 2 wherever the recompute actually moves a figure, and by 1 where the recompute finds every figure already standing exactly where it was.

Reversal is the compensating action, and it belongs to the treasurer. Reversing an award requires the award to be `PAID` and its fund to be `DISBURSED` — either half failing is refused with `ERR_PRIZE_FUND_STATE_INVALID`, the award's own status having no separate code — and requires a `reversal_reason` that is not blank. The response body is the reversed award rather than the fund it adjusted, and the fund row is again written twice, once for its status and `disbursed_at` and once for the money recompute, so the fund's `version` advances by 2 wherever that recompute moves a figure and by 1 where it moves none. The award moves `PAID → REVERSED`, which is terminal for that award; `paid_award_total_cents` drops by exactly the award's `amount_cents` and `undistributed_balance_cents` rises by exactly the same amount, so the money invariant of §2.9 continues to hold. The fund moves `DISBURSED → PLAN_APPROVED` and the league moves `PRIZES_DISBURSED → SEASON_CLOSED` on the **first** reversal, even while other awards of the same fund are still `PAID`, because the plan is no longer fully disbursed; `disbursed_at` is cleared back to null at that point. Reversing every paid award of a fund therefore restores `undistributed_balance_cents` to its exact pre-disbursement value and `paid_award_total_cents` to 0, with `collected_prize_cents` untouched throughout.

### 3.8. Honor Scores: Claiming, Verifying and Certifying an Award

An honor score is a performance the national body puts on permanent record, and the claim originates with the bowler. A `Bowler` files by supplying the `league_id` in which the score was shot, the `membership_id` claiming it, the `series_entry_id` of the slot carrying the score, the `award_type` claimed, optionally the `claimed_game_number` and optionally an `evidence_note` of free text. The acting bowler must be the member behind `membership_id`, and the cited slot's own `membership_id` must equal the application's `membership_id`; a slot belonging to somebody else is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. The claiming membership must be `SANCTIONED`, failing which the claim is refused with `ERR_BOWLER_NOT_SANCTIONED`, and the cited slot must be a certified average-bearing slot — a `VACANCY`, `ABSENTEE` or `FORFEIT` slot can never carry an honor score, and a slot that is not average-bearing or whose `certified` flag is false is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`, the same code the wrong-owner case yields. The filing window is generous but finite: a claim must be filed no later than one hundred and sixty-eight hours — seven days — after the match's `certified_at`, and a later filing is refused with `ERR_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED`; a slot whose match carries no `certified_at` at all offers no window to be inside and is refused the same way.

There are exactly four award types, each with its own arithmetic for `claimed_pins` and its own threshold, and all comparisons are on whole pins. For `PERFECT_GAME_300`, `claimed_pins` is the pin count of the game named by `claimed_game_number`, and the claim is eligible only when that count is exactly 300. For `HIGH_GAME_298_PLUS`, `claimed_pins` is again the named game's pin count, and the claim is eligible when that count is at least 298 and at most 299 — a 300 belongs to the perfect-game category, not this one. For `SERIES_800_SCRATCH`, `claimed_pins` is the slot's `scratch_series`, and the claim is eligible when that is at least 800. For `HANDICAP_SERIES_700`, `claimed_pins` is the slot's `scratch_series` plus three times the slot's `handicap_per_game_applied` — three games' worth of the handicap frozen onto that slot — and the claim is eligible when that total is at least 700. `claimed_game_number` is an integer from 1 through 3 and, per §2.7, is required for the two game categories and forbidden for the two series categories. A claim whose `claimed_pins` falls short of its type's threshold is refused with `ERR_AWARD_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, and so is a second application for the same combination of `series_entry_id` and `award_type` when an existing application for that pair has not been denied.

Filing also captures two evidence flags. `lane_certification_verified` is true when, at this instant, the lane pair of the slot's match has `revoked_at` null and a `certified_through` at or after that match's `starts_at`. `sanctioned_at_game_time` is true when the claiming membership's `sanction_status` is `SANCTIONED` and the cited slot's `certified` flag is true. The application is created at `SUBMITTED` with `round_count` 1.

Both flags are **re-evaluated** against the then-current lane pair and membership when the secretary comes to verify, which is deliberate: a lane pair revoked after the game was bowled leaves an outstanding claim permanently unverifiable. Verification is the secretary's decision and takes a `decision` of either `VERIFY` or `RETURN_FOR_EVIDENCE` plus an optional `evidence_note`. `VERIFY` moves the application `SUBMITTED → LEAGUE_VERIFIED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `verified_by_user_id`, and requires three things at once, tested in this order. First, both evidence flags must hold as re-evaluated now: the match's lane pair still has `revoked_at` null with a `certified_through` at or after the match's `starts_at`, and the claiming membership is still `SANCTIONED` with the cited slot still carrying `certified` true — the match's own `status` is never consulted here, so a slot that kept its certification through a protest-driven forfeit stays verifiable while one decertified by a void does not. When either `lane_certification_verified` or `sanctioned_at_game_time` is false at verification time, the refusal is `ERR_AWARD_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`. Second, the secretary must not be the applicant: the league's `secretary_member_id` must differ from the `member_id` behind the applying membership, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Third, `claimed_pins` recomputed from the slot as it now stands must still reach the award type's threshold. A `VERIFY` that succeeds writes both flags as true; an `evidence_note` sent along with `VERIFY` is accepted and then ignored, leaving the stored note as it was. `RETURN_FOR_EVIDENCE` moves the application `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_EVIDENCE`, requires an `evidence_note` recording what is missing, and hands the claim back to the applicant; that branch re-evaluates nothing and leaves both flags exactly as filing recorded them.

The applicant's loop closes the circle: a `Bowler` who is the member behind the applying membership resupplies a fresh `evidence_note` against an application at `RETURNED_FOR_EVIDENCE`, which moves it `RETURNED_FOR_EVIDENCE → SUBMITTED`, replaces the stored `evidence_note`, increments `round_count` by 1 and clears `verified_by_user_id`. `round_count` therefore counts the number of times the application has entered `SUBMITTED`, including the initial filing, and the loop may repeat.

The association has the last word. An `AssociationOfficer` records a `decision` of `CERTIFY` or `DENY` against an application at `LEAGUE_VERIFIED`, optionally with an `association_ruling_note`. `CERTIFY` requires both `lane_certification_verified` and `sanctioned_at_game_time` to be true — the flags being read exactly as they now stand on the application rather than re-derived from the lane pair and the membership a second time, and either of them being false is refused with `ERR_AWARD_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` — stores the `association_ruling_note` when one was supplied while leaving it null when none was, and moves the application `LEAGUE_VERIFIED → ASSOCIATION_CERTIFIED` — the honor score is now on the national record, and the state is terminal. `DENY` requires an `association_ruling_note` giving the reason and moves the application `LEAGUE_VERIFIED → ASSOCIATION_DENIED`, also terminal; no further transition of any kind is possible from either.

### 3.9. Protests, the Hearing Cycle and Escalation to the Association

A protest is filed by a `Bowler` or a `TeamCaptain` and carries the `league_id` whose bylaws it invokes, an optional `match_id`, the `filed_by_membership_id` of the bowler filing, a `category` and a `protest_note` of between 1 and 1000 characters that is not blank. The filing membership must be a membership of that league whose `sanction_status` is `SANCTIONED`. A `Bowler` may file only for their own membership; a `TeamCaptain` may file for any membership of the same league, except that where the named membership itself carries a `team_id` the acting captain must hold a membership of that very team, failing which the filing is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The four categories are `LINEUP_LEGALITY`, `SCORE_ERROR`, `RULES_INTERPRETATION` and `ELIGIBILITY`; `match_id` is required for `LINEUP_LEGALITY`, `SCORE_ERROR` and `ELIGIBILITY`, and is omitted for a pure `RULES_INTERPRETATION` question that cites no match at all — though it is only optional there and not forbidden, so a `RULES_INTERPRETATION` protest that does cite a match is accepted, records it, is subject to its filing window, and offers it as the match a `VOID_MATCH` remedy would later void. A protest citing a match must be filed no later than seventy-two hours — three days — after that match's `certified_at`; a later filing is refused with `ERR_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and a match belonging to a different league than the protest is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. A cited match whose `certified_at` is still null — one that has never been certified, or one whose stamp a void cleared — imposes no window at all: the filing is accepted and its `deadline_at` recorded as null, exactly as for a protest citing no match. Filing creates the protest at `FILED` with `review_round` 0 and `remedy` `NONE`, stamps `filed_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and sets `deadline_at` to seventy-two hours after the cited match's `certified_at` when a match is cited, or null when none is.

Hearings are opened by the secretary, who may not be the person who filed — the league's `secretary_member_id` must differ from the `member_id` behind `filed_by_membership_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Opening a hearing is legal from `FILED` and from `REMANDED`, moves the protest to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and increments `review_round` by 1. `review_round` therefore counts how many hearings have been opened, and reaching 2 is what unlocks the escalation path for a protest whose category is not `RULES_INTERPRETATION`. None of the four protest surfaces — filing, hearing opening, the league ruling and the association ruling — consults the league's own `status` at all, so a protest may be filed and carried right through its hearing cycle whatever state the league has reached, `SEASON_CLOSED` and `PRIZES_DISBURSED` included.

Ruling on a protest is legal only from `UNDER_REVIEW`, belongs to the same non-filing secretary, and takes a `decision` of `UPHOLD`, `DENY`, `REMAND` or `ESCALATE`, together with an optional `remedy`, a `ruling_note` and an optional `forfeiting_team_id`. A `ruling_note` recording the rationale is required, and required for every one of the four decisions; an omitted `remedy` reads as `NONE`, and a `forfeiting_team_id` is not resolved against the team table here — a blank or non-string value is a shape failure, while a well-formed identifier naming no team is simply a value the remedy test below cannot accept, never `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`. `DENY` moves the protest to `DENIED`, which is terminal, changes no match state, and leaves `remedy` at `NONE` — indeed `NONE` is the only remedy a denial may carry, and any other remedy attached to a denial is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_APPLICABLE`. `REMAND` moves the protest to `REMANDED` to await a further hearing and changes no match state. `ESCALATE` moves the protest to `ESCALATED`, passing jurisdiction to the association so that the league may no longer rule, and is permitted only when the `category` is `RULES_INTERPRETATION` or `review_round` has reached at least 2; an `ESCALATE` meeting neither condition is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_STATE_INVALID`, and that refusal precedes the segregation-of-duties test on the ruling secretary. `UPHOLD` moves the protest to `UPHELD`, which is terminal, and applies the declared remedy. Only `UPHOLD` records a remedy at all, and only `DENY` refuses one: a `remedy` other than `NONE` sent with a `REMAND` or an `ESCALATE` is accepted, applied to nothing and never stored, so the protest's own `remedy` stays at `NONE` on those two branches, and the remedy applicability test is likewise run only for an `UPHOLD`.

The hearing cycle must terminate, and it does so at three rounds. A `REMAND` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT` once `review_round` has already reached 3, because the only exit from `REMANDED` is a further hearing and a fourth hearing would never be legal; the third hearing must therefore end in `UPHOLD`, `DENY` or `ESCALATE`. The full cycle is `FILED → UNDER_REVIEW`, then optionally `UNDER_REVIEW → REMANDED → UNDER_REVIEW` repeating, then out to `UPHELD`, `DENIED` or `ESCALATED`, with `ESCALATED → ASSOCIATION_RULED` as the association's terminal step.

Three remedies exist and each has an applicability test. `NONE` changes nothing. `VOID_MATCH` requires the protested match to be `CERTIFIED`. `FORFEIT_AWARD` requires the protest's `category` to be `LINEUP_LEGALITY`, requires the protested match to be `CERTIFIED`, and requires a `forfeiting_team_id` naming one of that match's two sides — which side is not examined further, so a ruling may in principle award the forfeit against the filer's own team. A remedy that fails its test is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_APPLICABLE`, and so is a `VOID_MATCH` or `FORFEIT_AWARD` on a protest that cites no match at all.

An upheld `VOID_MATCH` remedy runs a **four-hop decertification cascade** that is the exact inverse of certification. Hop 1 moves the match `CERTIFIED → VOID`, clears `certified_at` back to null, writes `home_points` and `away_points` both to 0 in the one-decimal JSON form of §3.5, and records the protest's identifier together with the remedy in `void_reason` — spelled as the protest's `id`, then a colon, then the remedy name, so `VOID_MATCH` on protest `p` reads `p:VOID_MATCH`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, sets `certified` back to false on every slot of the match. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, recomputes `certified_games`, `certified_pins`, `current_average`, `average_status` and `handicap_per_game` on every affected membership — every membership named by any slot of the match, `ABSENTEE` slots included — which lands each one **exactly** on its pre-certification values. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, recomputes `sanctioned_roster_count`, `team_handicap_per_game`, `total_points` and `total_scratch_pins` on every affected team — those memberships' own roster teams together with the match's two sides — and recomputes the league's `certified_week_high_water` alongside its `sanctioned_member_count` and `confirmed_team_count`, after which every membership of the league has its dues figures refreshed against the new mark, exactly as hop 4 of §3.5 describes. The response body of a ruling is the protest, never the match the remedy rewrote. `VOID` is terminal for the match.

An upheld `FORFEIT_AWARD` remedy runs a deliberately *different* set of effects, and the asymmetry is the point: unlike a void, the protesting side's work survives. The match moves `CERTIFIED → FORFEITED` with `forfeiting_team_id` set to the side the ruling named, and its `certified_at` stamp is deliberately *not* cleared — the match keeps the instant it was certified at, which is what keeps the protest and honor-score windows of §3.8 and §3.9 measurable against it. That side's existing slots are deleted and replaced by freshly created `FORFEIT` rows for positions 1 through `lineup_positions`, exactly as the preallocation procedure of §3.5 builds them and scoring 0 in all three games, differing only in that on this branch their `certified` flag is created true. Points are rewritten to 0 for the forfeiting side and the full 4 points for the other side. The forfeiting side's bowlers lose those games from `certified_games` and `certified_pins`, so their averages and handicaps are recomputed, while the protesting side keeps its certified games untouched. And the forfeiting side's frozen team handicap — `home_team_handicap_per_game` or `away_team_handicap_per_game` as appropriate — is recomputed from the new `FORFEIT` rows and therefore becomes 0. The affected teams — the roster teams of the forfeiting side's bowlers together with the match's own two sides — re-derive their figures, and the league's three counters and then every membership's dues figures are refreshed exactly as they are on a void. `FORFEITED` is terminal.

The association rules on an escalated protest by supplying a `remedy`, the `association_ruling` text and, where the remedy needs it, a `forfeiting_team_id`. The ruling is legal only from `ESCALATED`, requires the `association_ruling` text, and moves the protest `ESCALATED → ASSOCIATION_RULED`, which is terminal. The association may impose `NONE` or `VOID_MATCH`, the latter again requiring the protested match to be `CERTIFIED`, and a `VOID_MATCH` imposed by the association runs the very same four-hop decertification cascade that a league upholding would have run. The `ruling_note` recorded by the league and the `association_ruling` recorded by the association are independent fields, and each stays null until its own ruling is made.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every collection is mounted at the root of the URL space; there is no prefix segment, and every path parameter is written `{id}`. Status codes follow one rule with no exceptions: an endpoint that brings a **new** resource into existence answers 201, and an endpoint that modifies, transitions or reads an existing resource answers 200 — the logical-clock setter, which neither creates a resource nor transitions one, falling on the 200 side and answering with an empty `data` object. Refusals carry the HTTP status listed against their error identifier in §5. A trailing slash on any declared path is accepted as the same path. A request this table does not declare is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and only its HTTP status distinguishes the two ways of failing to be declared: a declared path called with a method it does not declare answers HTTP 400, while a path the table does not declare at all answers HTTP 404. Routing is resolved before the header checks of §2.6, so an undeclared request is refused this way whether or not it carried the two headers. The table below is deliberately minimal — it names the method, the path, the purpose and who may call it, and nothing more. Request payloads, response payloads and side effects are specified in the narratives of §2 and §3, and a reader who needs to know what a call *does* must read them there.

List endpoints share the `limit` and `offset` window described in §2.3 and additionally accept optional equality filters drawn from the listed resource's own fields. The league listing filters on `season_year`, `status`, `secretary_member_id` and `treasurer_member_id`. The league-scoped team listing filters on `status`. The membership listing filters on `league_id`, `member_id`, `team_id`, `sanction_status` and `remittance_status`. The fee-remittance listing filters on `league_id` and `status`. The lane-pair listing filters on `league_id` and `certification_status`. The match listing filters on `league_id`, `week_number`, `home_team_id`, `away_team_id`, `lane_pair_id`, `forfeiting_team_id` and `status`. The lineup-slot listing filters on `match_id`, `team_id`, `membership_id`, `entry_type` and `certified`. The dues-payment listing filters on `membership_id` and `week_number`. The prize-fund listing filters on `league_id` and `status`. The prize-award listing filters on `prize_fund_id`, `team_id` and `status`. The honor-score application listing filters on `league_id`, `membership_id`, `series_entry_id`, `award_type` and `status`. The protest listing filters on `league_id`, `match_id`, `filed_by_membership_id`, `category` and `status`. No other query parameter carries meaning on any listing, and one the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than refused. Every filter is exact equality, and string and identifier filters compare case-sensitively; the two integer filters, `season_year` and `week_number`, and the one boolean filter, `certified`, are refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when their value cannot be read as that type — `certified` accepts only `true` or `false`, in any letter case. A filter value that reads cleanly but matches nothing, including a status or category spelling the system never issues, yields an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than an error. `total_count` counts the rows surviving every supplied filter before the window is applied, and the lane-pair listing's `certification_status` filter is matched against the status derived at that moment per §3.4, not against a stored field.
