# Trade Promotion Deductions API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Trade promotion spending is the second-largest line on a consumer packaged goods manufacturer's income statement, and it is almost always the least controlled one. Money is promised to retailers a year in advance in an annual trade fund, planned event by event into temporary price reductions, display placements and feature advertising, accrued week by week as goods ship or as consumers buy, and then reclaimed by the retailer months later in the form of a deduction taken off a remittance cheque. Between the promise and the deduction sit four different books — the budget ledger, the accrual ledger, the accounts-receivable deduction ledger and the post-event analytics — and in most organisations they disagree. The purpose of this API is to make them agree continuously, at the level of a single promotion, and to make every disagreement an explicit, named, machine-readable refusal rather than a spreadsheet reconciliation performed at year end.

This service is the system of record for that reconciliation. It is mounted under `/trade-marketing`, and it holds the annual program envelope, the per-customer and per-brand allocation of that envelope, promotion plans with their tactic lines and retailer commitment paperwork, weekly accrual postings and their compensating reversals, retailer deduction claims with their valuation and settlement, the dispute and recovery process that chases invalid deductions, the post-event measurement of lift, cannibalization and return on investment, and the contract-compliance audit programme run at each retailer. Two invariants are enforced everywhere: money and promoted volume are conserved (whatever a promotion reserves against an allocation is exactly what the allocation reports as unavailable, and whatever an accrual books is exactly what deductions, write-offs and remaining balance add back up to), and nothing is ever silently overwritten (every write is version-stamped, and any write that was planned against a balance the client has not observed is refused rather than applied).

The audience for this document is the engineering team implementing the service. It is written from the trade-marketing business perspective: the domain rules come first and the HTTP surface comes second. Section 2 fixes the global contracts — authentication, envelope shape, pagination, precision, concurrency and the order in which rules are evaluated. Section 3 narrates the business workflows in the order the business performs them, and it is where the field-level and calculation-level detail lives. Sections 4 and 5 are reference indexes only: the endpoint table lists methods, paths and authorised roles but deliberately omits request and response shapes, and the error catalogue lists identifiers and statuses but deliberately omits trigger conditions. Both of those omissions are recovered from Section 3.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request — including every read and including the test-driving clock endpoint — must carry both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. A header that is missing, or that is present but carries the empty string, counts as absent, and the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names defined below is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401) — note that an unrecognised role is an authentication failure, not an authorisation failure. Once the role is recognised, it is checked against the set of roles the target endpoint permits; a recognised role that is not on that list is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- `TradeMarketingDirector` owns the annual trade fund. This role opens programs, registers brands and retail customers, creates and approves budget allocations, requests changes on or approves submitted promotions, locks the program at year end, and is the only role permitted to approve a large write-off.
- `KeyAccountManager` plans promotions at the retailer: it creates promotions and their tactic lines, performs rate maintenance, cancels planned lines, submits plans for review, revises them after changes are requested, withdraws approved promotions, and verifies retailer commitment paperwork.
- `TradeFinanceAnalyst` runs the ledger: it loads weekly volume facts, posts and reverses accruals, values and settles deductions, verifies performance evidence paperwork, drives the activation and aging sweeps, and produces post-event analyses.
- `DeductionSpecialist` owns deduction intake: it books retailer claims, matches them to promotion agreements, opens disputes, drives the retailer round trips, records recoveries, approves small write-offs, and may place a customer on manual credit hold.
- `ComplianceAuditor` audits contract compliance at the customer: it opens the audit, records findings with their recovery claims, and participates in issuing them. It has no authority over money, promotions or deductions.
- `CustomerPortalUser` is the retailer's own portal login. Its only write capability is answering a dispute that has been sent to it.

The five internal roles — `TradeMarketingDirector`, `KeyAccountManager`, `TradeFinanceAnalyst`, `DeductionSpecialist` and `ComplianceAuditor` — carry an **opaque identity**: when `X-User-Role` is any one of these five, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a workflow demands accountability — as `approved_by_user_id` on a promotion, `verified_by_user_id` on a retailer document, `matched_by_user_id` and `validated_by_user_id` on a deduction, `opened_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a dispute, and `auditor_user_id` on a compliance audit — and it compares recorded values against each other for segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

`CustomerPortalUser` is the single exception and is **entity-backed**: when `X-User-Role` is `CustomerPortalUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare that resource's customer foreign key to `X-User-ID`: the promotion's, deduction's, dispute's or allocation's `customer_id`, or the `customer_id` query filter on a list read. A portal user that addresses or filters a resource belonging to any other customer is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This refusal is deliberately evaluated **before** existence is checked, so a portal user cannot probe for other customers' identifiers by comparing a 404 against a 403. The single write a portal user may perform is answering a dispute raised against its own customer's deduction; every other portal capability is a read of its own customer, its own promotions, its own deductions or its own disputes.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A supplied value outside those bounds — a `limit` of 0, a `limit` of 101, a negative `offset` — is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a list endpoint states otherwise, results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Six list endpoints deviate, and their ordering is part of the contract: trade programs, retailer documents across promotions, post-event analyses across promotions and compliance audits across customers are all returned newest first, by `created_at` descending; volume facts are returned by `period_start` ascending; accrual entries are returned by `period_start` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; and disputes are returned by `opened_at` ascending. Tactic lines, whether listed under one promotion or across promotions, are returned by `created_at` ascending — the same order the accrual ledger uses when it decomposes a posting into per-line amounts.

All filters offered by a list endpoint combine conjunctively, and a filter naming an identifier that does not resolve is treated as a not-found condition rather than as an empty page: a list of accrual entries filtered by a `reverses_entry_id` that names no entry is refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND`, and a list of deductions filtered by an unknown `supersedes_deduction_id` is refused with `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, recorded when the row is first written. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations that are side effects of an operation aimed at a different resource.

`VolumeFact` is the single exception. Weekly shipment and consumption facts are an append-only journal: a fact row carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it. There is no update and no delete for a fact; a correction is posted as an additional fact row, never as an edit of an existing one.

The server runs on a **logical clock**, not on wall-clock time. It starts at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to the host's real time, so that every window comparison, every feature cooldown, every dispute aging calculation and every rate-maintenance check is deterministic and reproducible. An operator-facing test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes back the instant it has adopted; all five internal roles may use it. Setting the clock recomputes nothing by itself: aging and window comparisons are evaluated when a resource is read, or when the next sweep capability runs. Wherever the specification says "the logical clock instant", it means the value that capability last established (or the fixed default, if it has never been called). The instants recorded from the logical clock are `opened_at` and `settled_at` on the deduction side, `issued_at` on a compliance audit, `computed_at` on a post-event analysis, and the `run_at` echoed by each sweep.

### 2.5. Money, Volume and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are USD and are carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded **half up**. All ratios — the promotion's `spend_ratio` and the post-event `roi` — are carried to exactly 4 decimal places, rounded half up. Case volumes supplied by clients are integers; the derived volume figures produced by post-event analysis are carried to 2 decimal places because they are averages, not counts. The one input that is allowed finer granularity than money is the per-case allowance rate `rate_per_case`, which may carry up to 3 decimal places; every amount derived from it is still rounded to 2 decimal places.

A monetary field in a request body must be a number that is at or above 0.00 and carries at most 2 decimal places; a rate must carry at most 3; a unit count must be an integer at or above 0; a timestamp must be an RFC 3339 UTC instant ending in `Z`; an enum field must carry one of its declared values; every required field must be present and non-null; and unknown fields are rejected outright. Any of these failures is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

One rounding convention deserves separate emphasis because it changes results by cents and appears in two places. Whenever a total is assembled from several component lines, **each line is rounded to 2 decimal places first and the rounded line amounts are then added**. Summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different — and incorrect — result. This pinned order governs the assembly of a promotion's tactic subtotal from its individual tactic lines, and the assembly of a weekly accrual amount from its per-tactic components.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system are planned against a parent balance that other writers can move, and both therefore require the client to declare which version of that parent it planned against.

Creating a promotion requires `expected_allocation_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `BudgetAllocation` named by `allocation_id`. If that value does not equal the allocation's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_ALLOCATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Several promotions may legitimately be planned against the same allocation cell, and each approval consumes part of `available_amount`, so a create carrying a stale allocation version was planned against a balance that no longer exists. Note that the allocation's `version` advances on every child promotion create as well, which is precisely what makes a second create replaying the same expected version fail.

Validating a deduction requires `expected_promotion_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Promotion` the deduction was matched to. If that value does not equal the promotion's current `version`, the validation is refused with `ERR_STALE_PROMOTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Several deductions may legitimately be validated against one promotion and each consumes part of `accrued_balance`, so a validation carrying a stale promotion version was priced off a balance that has already been spent. The same field and the same rejection code guard rate maintenance on a tactic line: a tactic patch also requires `expected_promotion_version` matching the owning promotion's current `version`, because a patch reprices the promotion's subtotal and can move its allowance tier.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Version numbers are read from a resource and echoed back only on those three writes; everywhere else, the version is an output that lets the client detect that something moved underneath it.

### 2.7. Flat Addressing and Reference Resolution

Every business resource is addressed at one level under `/trade-marketing` and linked to its relatives by explicit foreign keys carried in request bodies or supplied by the single path segment that creates a child. No entity identifier is nested inside another entity's identifier path segment. This keeps reference resolution simple but makes the resolution *order* part of the contract.

Path identifiers are resolved first, left to right, in the order they appear in the URL. The path variables the service uses are `programId`, `brandId`, `customerId`, `allocationId`, `promotionId`, `tacticId`, `documentId`, `factId`, `accrualId`, `deductionId`, `disputeId` and `auditId`, and that is also their resolution precedence when more than one appears. Each has its own refusal: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BRAND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TACTIC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUME_FACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404.

Only after every path identifier resolves are the foreign keys in the request body resolved, in the order the fields appear in that endpoint's input list: `program_id`, then `brand_id`, then `customer_id`, then `allocation_id`, then `promotion_id`, then `deduction_id`, then `reverses_entry_id`, then `supersedes_deduction_id`. A body foreign key that does not resolve produces the same not-found identifier as its path counterpart, so an unresolvable `allocation_id` in a promotion create yields `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` and an unresolvable `supersedes_deduction_id` in a deduction intake yields `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.8. The Order in Which Rules Are Evaluated

A single malformed or ill-timed request commonly violates several rules at once. The response must be deterministic, so all validation rules carry an explicit precedence and **the first rule that fails determines the response; no later rule is evaluated**. The ladder below is the complete precedence order, from first-evaluated to last-evaluated, together with the operations each rung guards. Rungs are numbered sparsely so that the relative order of any two rules is unambiguous.

1. **Rung 1** — both authentication headers present and non-empty, on every endpoint: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Rung 2** — `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles, on every endpoint: `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. **Rung 3** — the caller's role appears in the target endpoint's role list, on every endpoint: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. **Rung 4** — a `CustomerPortalUser` addresses only its own customer's data, on every endpoint whose role list includes `CustomerPortalUser`: `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
5. **Rung 10** — request shape and field-level well-formedness, on every endpoint: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. **Rung 20** — every path identifier resolves, left to right, on every endpoint with path parameters: the not-found family of §2.7.
7. **Rung 21** — every body foreign key resolves, in input-list order, on every endpoint with foreign keys in its body: the same not-found family.
8. **Rung 30** — `expected_allocation_version` matches the referenced allocation's `version`, on promotion create: `ERR_STALE_ALLOCATION_VERSION`.
9. **Rung 31** — `expected_promotion_version` matches the parent promotion's `version`, on deduction validation and on tactic patch: `ERR_STALE_PROMOTION_VERSION`.
10. **Rung 40** — the owning program is `OPEN`, scoped to program-level writes only, namely brand registration, allocation creation and allocation approval: `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. Promotion-level writes are gated by allocation posture at rung 42 instead, so no single request can violate both rungs.
11. **Rung 41** — the funding allocation is `APPROVED`, on promotion create and promotion submit: `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_APPROVED`.
12. **Rung 42** — the funding allocation's `posture` is `OPEN`, on promotion create and promotion approve: `ERR_ALLOCATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. Accrual posting, deduction handling and analysis are deliberately **not** gated by posture — the ledger must keep recording money that has already been spent.
13. **Rung 43** — the promotion's `lifecycle_status` permits the requested transition, on promotion submit, request-changes, revise, approve and withdraw, on tactic create, tactic patch and tactic cancel, on document attach, on accrual post and on analyze: `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID`.
14. **Rung 44** — the tactic line's own `status` is `PLANNED`, on tactic patch and tactic cancel: `ERR_TACTIC_STATE_INVALID`.
15. **Rung 45** — the document is still `SUBMITTED`, on document verify: `ERR_DOCUMENT_ALREADY_VERIFIED`.
16. **Rung 46** — the targeted accrual entry is a reversible posting, on accrual reversal: `ERR_ACCRUAL_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED`.
17. **Rung 47** — the deduction's `status` permits the requested transition, on deduction match, validate, settle and dispute: `ERR_DEDUCTION_STATE_INVALID`.
18. **Rung 48** — the dispute's `status` permits the requested transition, on send-to-retailer, retailer-respond, record-recovery and write-off: `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`.
19. **Rung 49** — the audit's `status` permits the requested transition, on record-finding and issue-findings: `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID`.
20. **Rung 50** — segregation of duties on the opaque acting user, on deduction validation and on dispute write-off: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
21. **Rung 51** — a large write-off carries director authority, on dispute write-off: `ERR_WRITE_OFF_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`.
22. **Rung 52** — document verification is restricted by document class, on document verify: `ERR_DOCUMENT_CLASS_ROLE_MISMATCH`.
23. **Rung 53** — the customer's `trading_status` is `ACTIVE`, on promotion create, promotion approve, deduction settlement and allocation creation: `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD`.
24. **Rung 60** — the brand and the customer named by an allocation belong to the allocation's own program, on allocation creation: `ERR_BRAND_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`.
25. **Rung 61** — the promotion a deduction is matched to belongs to the deduction's own customer, on deduction match: `ERR_DEDUCTION_CUSTOMER_MISMATCH`.
26. **Rung 62** — the promotion a deduction is matched to is a matchable agreement, on deduction match: `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_MATCHABLE`.
27. **Rung 63** — `claim_reference` is unique per customer, on deduction intake: `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_REFERENCE`.
28. **Rung 64** — the customer/brand/fact-type/week tuple is unique, on volume-fact creation: `ERR_VOLUME_FACT_DUPLICATE`.
29. **Rung 65** — a promotion has at most one post-event analysis, on analyze: `ERR_ANALYSIS_ALREADY_EXISTS`.
30. **Rung 66** — at most one accrual posting per promotion week, on accrual post: `ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`.
31. **Rung 67** — the customer's `audit_status` is `CLEAR`, on opening a compliance audit: `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_OPEN`.
32. **Rung 70** — windows and periods are Monday-aligned and correctly ordered, on promotion create, tactic create, tactic patch, volume-fact creation and accrual post: `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_WEEK_ALIGNED`.
33. **Rung 71** — a tactic window is contained in its promotion's window, on tactic create and tactic patch: `ERR_TACTIC_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PROMOTION`.
34. **Rung 72** — an accrual period, after the method's lead-day shift, falls inside the promotion window, on accrual post: `ERR_ACCRUAL_WINDOW_MISMATCH`.
35. **Rung 73** — display exclusivity, on tactic create and tactic patch: `ERR_DISPLAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
36. **Rung 74** — feature cooldown, on tactic create and tactic patch: `ERR_FEATURE_COOLDOWN_CONFLICT`.
37. **Rung 75** — rate maintenance is forward-only, on tactic patch: `ERR_RATE_CHANGE_NOT_FUTURE`.
38. **Rung 80** — the per-day temporary-price-reduction concurrency cap, on tactic create and tactic patch: `ERR_TPR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
39. **Rung 81** — the tactic type carries the right money field, on tactic create and tactic patch: `ERR_TACTIC_TYPE_RATE_MISMATCH`.
40. **Rung 82** — the revision-round cap, on promotion revise: `ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS`.
41. **Rung 83** — the retailer round-trip cap, on send-to-retailer: `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT`.
42. **Rung 84** — a promotion has at least one planned tactic line, on promotion submit: `ERR_PROMOTION_HAS_NO_TACTICS`.
43. **Rung 85** — commitment paperwork covers every display and feature line, on promotion approve: `ERR_COMMITMENT_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`.
44. **Rung 86** — verified performance evidence exists under the evidence-based policy, on deduction validation: `ERR_NO_PERFORMANCE_EVIDENCE`.
45. **Rung 87** — the disputed amount fits the disputable base, on opening a dispute: `ERR_DISPUTED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`.
46. **Rung 88** — the write-off amount fits the disputed amount, on write-off: `ERR_WRITE_OFF_EXCEEDS_OPEN_AMOUNT`.
47. **Rung 89** — the recovered amount fits the disputed amount, on record-recovery: `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_OPEN_AMOUNT`.
48. **Rung 90** — a reversal mirrors its original exactly, on accrual reversal: `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_ENTRY_AMOUNT`.
49. **Rung 95** — allocation dollars suffice, on promotion approve: `ERR_ALLOCATION_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`.
50. **Rung 96** — allocation incremental units suffice, on promotion approve: `ERR_UNITS_EXCEED_UNRESERVED`.
51. **Rung 97** — the program envelope suffices, on allocation creation: `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.
52. **Rung 98** — accrual balance remains under the agreement-cap policy, on deduction validation: `ERR_ACCRUAL_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. Segregation of duties (rung 50) is checked **after** every state precondition, so validating a deduction that is not in the right status reports the status problem, not the duties problem. And the fiscal capacity checks (rungs 95 through 98) are evaluated **last** of all, so a promotion approval that is simultaneously missing its commitment paperwork and over its allocation balance reports the missing paperwork.

### 2.9. Ledger Conservation and Cascade Propagation

Two conservation identities hold at all times, and they are the reason so many operations mutate more than one row.

The **money and units identity** on a budget allocation: `planned_amount` always equals `committed_amount` plus `available_amount`, and `planned_incremental_units` always equals `committed_units` plus `unreserved_units`. Any operation that changes what a promotion has reserved must therefore restate both halves of both pairs on the allocation in the same request.

The **accrual identity** on a promotion: `gross_accrued_amount` always equals `reversed_accrual_amount` plus `consumed_deduction_amount` plus `accrued_balance`. Accruals raise the gross figure, reversals raise the reversed figure, validated deductions and approved write-offs raise the consumed figure, and the balance is whatever is left — which may legitimately be negative when a retailer has deducted more than was ever accrued.

Because the aggregates that enforce these identities live on parents, a single write frequently propagates several hops outward. The deepest chain in the system starts at a deduction validation: consuming accrual moves the promotion's spend figures (hop 1); the recomputed `spend_ratio` can push the promotion's `spend_status` to `OVERSPENT` (hop 2); the first time a promotion reaches `OVERSPENT` the owning allocation's `overspend_event_count` is incremented and its `posture` moves from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` (hop 3); and a `RESTRICTED` allocation contributes penalty points to its customer, which can move that customer's `trading_status` from `ACTIVE` to `CREDIT_HOLD` (hop 4). Every row touched by such a chain has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, including the rows that were only touched as a consequence. A dispute write-off drives a comparable four-hop chain through recognised spend, the post-event analysis, the promotion's `performance_flag` and the allocation's `at_risk_amount`.

Cascades are not symmetric. Reversing an accrual walks the spend axis back down — `OVERSPENT` to `OVERSPEND_WARNING` to `WITHIN_BUDGET` — but it never lowers `overspend_event_count`, so an allocation that was pushed to `RESTRICTED` stays `RESTRICTED` for the rest of the fiscal year. Withdrawing an approved promotion, by contrast, is a genuinely complete compensation: it restores the allocation's `available_amount` and `unreserved_units` to exactly their pre-approval values.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and the Compliance Penalty Ledger

Two decisions in this system are too consequential to be made twice by the same person, and both checks compare **opaque user identifiers, not roles** — so a second operator holding the same role always succeeds where the first is refused.

The operator recorded in a promotion's `approved_by_user_id` may not validate any deduction matched to that promotion. The operator recorded in a dispute's `opened_by_user_id` may not approve that dispute's write-off. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Separately from duties, each customer carries a running compliance score in `compliance_penalty_points`, an integer recomputed whenever an allocation's posture changes, a dispute escalates, audit findings are issued or a deduction is written off. The score is a weighted count of three populations: each of the customer's budget allocations currently in `RESTRICTED` posture contributes 10 points; each `ESCALATED` dispute raised on one of the customer's deductions contributes 5 points; and each compliance audit of that customer currently in `FINDINGS_ISSUED` contributes 10 points. Allocations in `FROZEN` posture contribute nothing — a year-end program lock is not the retailer's fault. The score drives the customer's commercial axis and only ever moves it forward: an `ACTIVE` customer whose score reaches 10 or more is moved to `CREDIT_HOLD` with `credit_hold_reason` set to the text `AUTO: compliance penalty points <points>`, and a customer already on `CREDIT_HOLD` whose score reaches 25 or more is moved to `SUSPENDED`. Both thresholds are inclusive: a score of exactly 10 puts an `ACTIVE` customer on hold, and a score of exactly 25 suspends a customer already on hold.

A customer runs **two independent state axes on one row**. `trading_status` (`ACTIVE`, `CREDIT_HOLD`, `SUSPENDED`) is the commercial axis; `audit_status` (`CLEAR`, `AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS`, `FINDINGS_OPEN`) is the compliance axis. Audit activity moves `audit_status` and never rewrites `trading_status` directly; a credit hold moves `trading_status` and never touches `audit_status`. The only coupling between them is the penalty score, which audit findings feed and which then acts on the commercial axis.

### 2.11. The Fiscal Week: Monday Alignment and Half-Open Windows

The whole system is organised on fiscal weeks that begin on Monday. Every promotion window, every tactic window, every volume-fact period and every accrual period is expressed as an ISO 8601 calendar date that must fall on a Monday, and — where the field is compared against the logical clock or stored as an instant — is interpreted as that Monday at `00:00:00Z`. A date that is not a Monday, or an end that is not strictly after its start, is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_WEEK_ALIGNED` (HTTP 422).

Every window in this specification is **half-open**: the start day is included and the end day is excluded, so a window from one Monday to the Monday four weeks later covers exactly 28 days and exactly 4 fiscal weeks. Two consequences recur throughout Section 3. First, a week count is always the number of days between the two Mondays divided by 7, and it is always a whole positive number. Second, a window that ends on the same Monday another window begins does **not** overlap it, so two such tactic lines may coexist.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Annual Trade Fund: Programs, Brands and Retailers

A fiscal year of trade spending begins when a `TradeMarketingDirector` opens an annual **trade program**. The program declares the `fiscal_year` it covers (a whole number between 2020 and 2100 inclusive), a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters such as "FY2026 National Trade Fund", the `total_budget_amount` that is the entire trade fund envelope in USD (strictly greater than zero, with exactly 2 decimal places), and optionally an `accrual_method_default` of either `SHIPMENT` or `CONSUMPTION`. When `accrual_method_default` is omitted it is `SHIPMENT`. A newly opened program has `status` `OPEN`, an `allocated_amount` of 0.00 and an `unallocated_amount` equal to its `total_budget_amount`.

Those two rollup figures are never client-supplied; they are recomputed whenever a budget allocation is created and whenever the program is read. `allocated_amount` is the sum of `planned_amount` across every budget allocation whose `program_id` names this program, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and it counts allocations in **both** of their approval states — `DRAFT` as well as `APPROVED` — because a budget allocation has no void or cancelled state and so an unapproved allocation still reserves envelope. `unallocated_amount` is the program's `total_budget_amount` minus `allocated_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

At year end the director **locks** the program, moving `status` from `OPEN` to `LOCKED`. `LOCKED` is terminal — there is no unlock. The lock may only be applied to a program that is currently `OPEN`, and it does far more than flip a flag: every budget allocation whose `program_id` names this program is repostured, and because a locked program outranks every other posture consideration, each of those allocations lands in `FROZEN` posture and has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed. The program's own `version` is incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed as well. Once a program is `LOCKED`, three further writes under it are refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED` (HTTP 409): registering a new brand, creating a new budget allocation, and approving an existing one. Programs may be listed with optional `fiscal_year` and `status` filters.

**Brands** are the products the fund promotes, and each is registered under exactly one program by way of a `program_id` in the payload. A brand carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `list_price_per_case` (strictly positive, exactly 2 decimal places) which is the manufacturer's list price per case in USD, a `cogs_per_case` (at or above zero, exactly 2 decimal places) which is the fully loaded cost of goods per case, and a `baseline_units_per_week` (a whole number at or above zero) which is the seasonal planning baseline in cases per week. Cost must be strictly below list price; a payload where `cogs_per_case` is at or above `list_price_per_case` is malformed and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On registration the server derives `unit_margin` as `list_price_per_case` minus `cogs_per_case`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — the figure post-event analysis later multiplies by incremental volume to obtain gross profit. Brands may be listed with an optional `program_id` filter, which is how the brands of one program are found.

**Retail customers** are likewise registered under a program with a `program_id`, a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `channel` of `GROCERY`, `MASS`, `CLUB` or `DOLLAR`, and optionally a `deduction_validation_policy` of `EVIDENCE_BASED` or `AGREEMENT_CAP`, defaulting to `EVIDENCE_BASED` when omitted. The channel drives nothing but reporting filters. The policy flag, by contrast, is one of the most consequential fields in the system: it is the **only** selector of how every future deduction from this retailer is valued, and it is never inferred from the claim itself. A new customer starts with `trading_status` `ACTIVE`, `audit_status` `CLEAR`, `compliance_penalty_points` of 0 and a null `credit_hold_reason`. Two rollups are maintained on the customer row: `open_deduction_amount`, the sum of `claimed_amount` over that customer's deductions whose status is `RECEIVED`, `MATCHED`, `VALIDATED` or `DISPUTED`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; and `write_off_ytd_amount`, the sum of `write_off_amount` over every `WRITTEN_OFF` dispute raised on that customer's deductions, likewise rounded to 2 decimal places. Deductions that have reached `SETTLED` or `RECOVERED` drop out of the open figure. Both are recomputed on deduction intake, match, validation and settlement, and on dispute opening, recovery and write-off. Customers may be listed with optional `program_id`, `channel`, `trading_status` and `audit_status` filters.

A retailer can also be put on hold by hand. A `DeductionSpecialist` or a `TradeMarketingDirector` may place an `ACTIVE` customer on credit hold, supplying a free-text `reason`; the customer's `trading_status` moves `ACTIVE` → `CREDIT_HOLD`, the supplied text is stored as `credit_hold_reason`, `version` is incremented by 1 and `updated_at` refreshed. The compliance axis is deliberately untouched: `audit_status` keeps whatever value it had.

### 3.2. Earmarking the Envelope: Budget Allocations, Posture and the Units Invariant

A **budget allocation** is one cell of the annual plan: a sum of trade dollars earmarked for a specific retailer, a specific brand and a specific kind of event. A `TradeMarketingDirector` or a `TradeFinanceAnalyst` creates one by supplying `program_id`, `customer_id`, `brand_id`, an `event_type` of `SEASONAL`, `EVERYDAY` or `NEW_ITEM`, a `planned_amount` in USD (strictly positive, exactly 2 decimal places), a `baseline_units` figure (a whole number at or above zero) recording the non-promoted case volume the cell assumes for the year, and a `planned_incremental_units` figure (also a whole number at or above zero) recording the incremental case volume the money is expected to buy. That last number is the conserved quantity of the units invariant described in §2.9.

Three checks guard creation beyond the generic ones. The owning program must still be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. The brand named by `brand_id` and the customer named by `customer_id` must both belong to the same program as `program_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BRAND_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). And the program envelope must accommodate the new cell: the program's current `allocated_amount` plus the new `planned_amount` must not exceed the program's `total_budget_amount`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A customer whose `trading_status` is `SUSPENDED` may not receive new allocations at all; that refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 409). A customer merely on `CREDIT_HOLD` may still be allocated to — the hold bites at promotion time and at settlement time, not at planning time.

A newly created allocation has `status` `DRAFT`, `posture` `OPEN` and an `overspend_event_count` of 0, and its ledger is initialised so that `committed_amount` is 0.00, `available_amount` equals `planned_amount`, `committed_units` is 0 and `unreserved_units` equals `planned_incremental_units`; `accrued_amount`, `settled_amount` and `at_risk_amount` all start at 0.00. Creation also recomputes the parent program's `allocated_amount` and `unallocated_amount` and increments the program's `version` by 1.

Approval is the gate that opens a cell for promotion planning. A `TradeMarketingDirector` approves an allocation that is currently `DRAFT` under a program that is currently `OPEN`, moving `status` `DRAFT` → `APPROVED`; the whole ledger rollup is recomputed and the allocation's `version` is incremented by 1 with `updated_at` refreshed. Promotions may be created against a cell only once it is `APPROVED`; a promotion create or submit against a `DRAFT` cell is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409).

`posture` is a **second, independent axis** on the same allocation row, and unlike `status` it is entirely derived. It is recomputed when the owning program is locked, when a child promotion reaches `OVERSPENT`, and whenever the allocation is read, using a strict precedence of `FROZEN` over `RESTRICTED` over `OPEN`: if the owning program's `status` is `LOCKED` the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if the allocation's `overspend_event_count` is 1 or more the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. `overspend_event_count` counts the distinct child promotions that have entered `OVERSPENT` at least once and is **never decremented**. The system-driven posture moves are therefore `OPEN` → `RESTRICTED` (when an overspending child promotion takes the count from 0 up to 1 or more, while the program is still `OPEN`), `OPEN` → `FROZEN` and `RESTRICTED` → `FROZEN` (both when the owning program moves `OPEN` → `LOCKED`). Each of those moves increments the allocation's `version` by 1 and refreshes `updated_at`, and the move into `RESTRICTED` additionally re-scores the allocation's customer, adding 10 penalty points for every allocation of that customer currently in `RESTRICTED`. A `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` posture blocks two operations — creating a promotion under that cell and approving one — with `ERR_ALLOCATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409). It blocks nothing else: accrual posting, deduction handling and post-event analysis all continue, because the ledger must keep recording money that has already been spent.

Five aggregates and two derived figures make up the allocation ledger, all recomputed on promotion approval, promotion withdrawal, accrual posting, accrual reversal, deduction validation, deduction settlement, dispute write-off and any recompute of a post-event analysis. `committed_amount` is the sum of `planned_total_spend` across child promotions whose `lifecycle_status` is `APPROVED`, `LIVE`, `ENDED` or `ANALYZED`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; promotions in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `WITHDRAWN` reserve nothing. `committed_units` is the sum of `committed_units` across that same set of child promotions. `accrued_amount` is the sum, across **all** child promotions regardless of lifecycle, of each promotion's `gross_accrued_amount` minus its `reversed_accrual_amount`, rounded to 2 decimal places. `settled_amount` is the sum of `validated_amount` over deductions whose `status` is `SETTLED` and whose promotion belongs to this allocation, rounded to 2 decimal places. `at_risk_amount` is the sum of the `net_spend` figure from the post-event analysis of every child promotion whose `performance_flag` is `UNDERPERFORMING`, rounded to 2 decimal places; because a promotion has at most one analysis, that reference is unambiguous. From those, `available_amount` is `planned_amount` minus `committed_amount`, rounded to 2 decimal places, and `unreserved_units` is `planned_incremental_units` minus `committed_units`. Allocations may be listed with optional `program_id`, `customer_id`, `brand_id`, `event_type`, `status` and `posture` filters.

### 3.3. Building the Promotion Plan: Tactic Lines and the Off-Invoice Allowance

A `KeyAccountManager` plans the actual events. Planning a promotion means naming the funding cell through `allocation_id`, giving the event a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, and declaring the promoted period as `window_start` and `window_end` — both Monday-aligned ISO dates with the end strictly after the start, and the window half-open so that the end Monday is not itself promoted. The payload may carry an `accrual_method` of `SHIPMENT` or `CONSUMPTION`; when it is omitted the promotion inherits the owning program's `accrual_method_default`. It must carry `expected_allocation_version` as described in §2.6. What it must **not** carry is either `customer_id` or `brand_id`: both are copied from the funding allocation at creation, are never editable afterwards, and are rejected as unknown fields if supplied.

A freshly planned promotion sits in `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT` with `spend_status` `WITHIN_BUDGET`, `performance_flag` `UNRATED` and `revision_round` 0, and its `approved_by_user_id` is null. Creating it increments the funding allocation's `version` by 1 — the parent version advances on every child create, which is exactly why replaying an `expected_allocation_version` that has already been used fails. Beyond the stale-version check, creation requires the allocation to be `APPROVED`, its `posture` to be `OPEN`, and the allocation's customer to be in `trading_status` `ACTIVE`; a customer on `CREDIT_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED` yields `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD`. Promotions may be listed with optional `allocation_id`, `customer_id`, `brand_id`, `lifecycle_status`, `spend_status` and `performance_flag` filters — the last of which, together with `spend_status`, is how the live over-spend monitoring view is assembled.

**Tactic lines** are the substance of the plan, and they may only be added, edited or cancelled while the promotion is `DRAFT`; attempting any of the three in another `lifecycle_status` is refused with `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A line declares a `tactic_type` of `TPR`, `DISPLAY` or `FEATURE`, a `planned_units` figure (a whole number at or above zero) recording the promoted case volume the line expects, and its own `window_start` and `window_end`, again both Monday-aligned with the end strictly after the start. The money field depends on the type and the two are mutually exclusive: a `TPR` line — a per-case temporary price reduction — must carry `rate_per_case`, strictly positive with at most 3 decimal places, and must omit `fixed_fee`; a `DISPLAY` or `FEATURE` line — a fixed-fee placement — must carry `fixed_fee`, strictly positive with exactly 2 decimal places, and must omit `rate_per_case`. Either mistake is refused with `ERR_TACTIC_TYPE_RATE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A tactic window must also be contained in its promotion's window: the tactic's `window_start` must be at or after the promotion's `window_start` and the tactic's `window_end` must be at or before the promotion's `window_end`, or the line is refused with `ERR_TACTIC_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PROMOTION` (HTTP 422).

Two figures are derived on the line itself. `week_count` is the number of days between the line's `window_end` and its `window_start` divided by 7 — always a whole positive number, because both ends are Monday-aligned. `line_planned_spend` is the line's own planned cost: for a `TPR` line it is `planned_units` multiplied by `rate_per_case`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places (and that rounding is load-bearing, because the rate may carry a third decimal); for a `DISPLAY` or `FEATURE` line it is simply `fixed_fee` rounded half up to 2 decimal places. A new line is created in `status` `PLANNED` with `effective_from_period` null.

Every write to a line re-prices the whole promotion, in a strictly pinned four-step order. **Step one:** `tactic_subtotal_amount` is the sum of `line_planned_spend` across the promotion's tactic lines whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` — each line's amount already rounded to 2 decimal places before the addition, never summed raw and rounded once at the end. `CANCELLED` lines are excluded, which is why withdrawal drives the figure to zero. **Step two:** the off-invoice `allowance_rate` is selected from a three-band schedule read against `tactic_subtotal_amount` and **nothing else** — a subtotal below ten thousand dollars earns no allowance at all, a rate of 0.000; a subtotal from ten thousand dollars up to but not including fifty thousand dollars earns 2.0 percent, a rate of 0.020; a subtotal of fifty thousand dollars or more earns 3.5 percent, a rate of 0.035. Both band boundaries are inclusive on the lower bound, so a subtotal of exactly 10000.00 earns 0.020 and a subtotal of exactly 50000.00 earns 0.035. It is tempting to read the allowance as a percentage of the promotion's total spend, and trade-marketing prose often does; that reading is wrong here. The band is chosen from the subtotal, never from `planned_total_spend`, and the evaluation order is subtotal, then band, then allowance, then total — there is no fixed point to iterate. **Step three:** `off_invoice_allowance_amount` is `tactic_subtotal_amount` multiplied by `allowance_rate`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. **Step four:** `planned_total_spend` is `tactic_subtotal_amount` plus `off_invoice_allowance_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `committed_units` is the sum of `planned_units` across the same `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` and `COMPLETED` lines. `planned_total_spend` is the figure that will be committed against the allocation at approval. This whole recomputation runs on tactic creation, on tactic patch, on promotion submit, on promotion approve and on promotion withdrawal.

Adding a line also re-evaluates the promotion's commitment paperwork coverage described in §3.5, because introducing a `DISPLAY` or `FEATURE` line can flip `commitment_verified` from true to false. Each tactic write increments the owning promotion's `version` by 1.

Planning mistakes are removed rather than deleted. A `KeyAccountManager` may cancel a line that is still `PLANNED` on a promotion that is still `DRAFT`, optionally recording a `cancel_reason`; the line moves `PLANNED` → `CANCELLED`, which is terminal, and the row is preserved so the planning history stays auditable. Cancellation drops the line out of `tactic_subtotal_amount`, which can pull `allowance_rate` back down across a band boundary, and out of `committed_units`; it re-evaluates commitment coverage, so cancelling the only `DISPLAY` line can flip `commitment_verified` from false to true; and it releases the line from the three trade-calendar contention sets of §3.4, all of which read only lines in `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED`. Both the line's `version` and the promotion's `version` are incremented by 1. Cancellation is also what makes it possible for a non-`PLANNED` line to sit under a `DRAFT` promotion — the situation in which a second cancel or a patch of that same line is refused with `ERR_TACTIC_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) rather than with the promotion-state code. Tactic lines may be listed under one promotion, or across promotions with an optional `promotion_id` filter, and in either case with optional `tactic_type` and `status` filters; that cross-promotion listing is how the contention sets below are inspected.

### 3.4. Protecting the Trade Calendar and Maintaining Rates

Retailers will not run two of the same placement at once, and the shelf cannot carry unlimited simultaneous price reductions, so three contention rules police the calendar. All three read the same candidate population — tactic lines whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` — and all three are evaluated both when a line is created and when a line is patched. What differs between them is scope and geometry.

**Display exclusivity.** No other `DISPLAY` line on the same `customer_id` **and** the same `brand_id` may have a window that overlaps the requested window. Because windows are half-open, a line ending on a given Monday and a line starting on that same Monday do not overlap, and both are allowed. A genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_DISPLAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

**Feature cooldown.** Feature advertising is scoped more broadly: no other `FEATURE` line on the same `brand_id` — **across all customers**, unlike the display rule — may lie within one week of the requested window. Concretely, the requested window is acceptable only if its `window_start` is at or after the other line's `window_end` plus 7 days, or its `window_end` plus 7 days is at or before the other line's `window_start`. A gap of exactly 7 days passes; a gap of 6 days is refused; and bare adjacency, a gap of zero days, is refused as well even though such windows do not overlap. The refusal is `ERR_FEATURE_COOLDOWN_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

**Temporary-price-reduction capacity.** On any single day, at most two `TPR` lines may cover the same `customer_id` and `brand_id`, counted across promotions rather than within one. Each individual candidate line is perfectly legal on its own; only the collective count on a day fails, and a third overlapping line is refused with `ERR_TPR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

Rate maintenance is the other reason a planned line is touched after it is written. A `KeyAccountManager` may patch a line that is still `PLANNED` on a promotion that is still `DRAFT`, supplying `expected_promotion_version` together with any of `rate_per_case`, `fixed_fee`, `planned_units`, `window_start` and `window_end`. The type/money exclusivity of §3.3, the Monday alignment, the containment in the promotion window and all three contention rules above are re-checked against the patched state. On top of those, rate maintenance is **forward-only**: a patch that changes either `rate_per_case` or `fixed_fee` is accepted only when the line's `window_start` is strictly after the current logical clock instant, so a rate can never be revised for a week that has already begun. A patch that attempts it is refused with `ERR_RATE_CHANGE_NOT_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). When a rate or fee did change, the server stamps `effective_from_period` with the line's `window_start` — the Monday of the first period the new rate applies to — which stays null on a line that has never been repriced. A successful patch recomputes the line's `line_planned_spend` and then the promotion's whole four-step pricing chain, which can move `allowance_rate` across a band boundary in either direction, and increments both the line's `version` and the promotion's `version` by 1.

### 3.5. Retailer Paperwork: Commitment Coverage and Performance Evidence

Trade money is only defensible if the retailer's paperwork exists, and the system distinguishes sharply between the two kinds. A **commitment** document is the signed agreement that a placement will actually run; it gates promotion approval. An **evidence** document is the after-the-fact proof of performance; it supplies the substantiated case count that deduction valuation reads. A `KeyAccountManager` or a `TradeFinanceAnalyst` attaches a document to a promotion by supplying a `doc_class` of `COMMITMENT` or `EVIDENCE`, a `doc_type`, a `covers_tactic_type`, a `received_at` instant recording when the retailer's paperwork arrived (an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits), and optionally `units_verified`.

`doc_type` takes one of five values and its legal pairing with `doc_class` is fixed by the business: `SIGNED_LOI` and `PORTAL_ACK` are the commitment kinds, while `SCAN_DATA`, `DISPLAY_PHOTO` and `AD_TEARSHEET` are the evidence kinds. `covers_tactic_type` declares which tactic lines the paperwork speaks for and takes `TPR`, `DISPLAY`, `FEATURE` or `ALL`, where `ALL` covers every tactic type on the promotion at once. `units_verified` is the number of cases this document substantiates, a whole number at or above zero, defaulting to 0 when omitted; it must be 0 on a `COMMITMENT` document, and a commitment payload carrying a positive count is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Documents attach in any `lifecycle_status` **except** `WITHDRAWN`, deliberately including `ENDED` and `ANALYZED`, because retailer evidence normally arrives well after the event has run; an attach against a withdrawn promotion is refused with `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID`. A new document is created in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `verified_by_user_id` null, and a `SUBMITTED` document counts for absolutely nothing: neither commitment coverage nor deduction valuation looks at it until it is verified.

Verification is class-restricted, and this is a payload-dependent authorisation check rather than a plain role check, because both roles appear on the endpoint. A `COMMITMENT` document may be verified only by a `KeyAccountManager`; an `EVIDENCE` document only by a `TradeFinanceAnalyst`. The wrong pairing is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_CLASS_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The document must still be `SUBMITTED`; `VERIFIED` is terminal, so verifying twice is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_ALREADY_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). A successful verification moves `status` `SUBMITTED` → `VERIFIED`, records the caller's identifier in `verified_by_user_id`, and then branches by class with equal consequence in each direction: for a `COMMITMENT` document the promotion's coverage is recomputed and `commitment_verified` may flip from false to true; for an `EVIDENCE` document the row's `units_verified` begins counting toward the entitlement calculation for that promotion's deductions. Either way, both the document's `version` and the promotion's `version` are incremented by 1 and both `updated_at` values are refreshed.

`commitment_verified` is a derived boolean on the promotion, recomputed on document attach, on document verification and again at approval time. The **required** set is the collection of tactic types among `DISPLAY` and `FEATURE` that appear on the promotion's lines whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED`; `TPR` lines never require commitment paperwork. The **covered** set is built from the promotion's documents whose `doc_class` is `COMMITMENT` and whose `status` is `VERIFIED`: a document whose `covers_tactic_type` is `ALL` contributes all three of `TPR`, `DISPLAY` and `FEATURE`, and any other document contributes exactly the single type it names. `commitment_verified` is true when every member of the required set appears in the covered set. The empty case matters: a promotion carrying only `TPR` lines has an empty required set, and its `commitment_verified` is therefore true with no paperwork at all. Documents may be listed under one promotion with optional `doc_class` and `status` filters, or across promotions with optional `promotion_id`, `doc_class`, `doc_type` and `status` filters — the latter being how the commitment-coverage and evidence-unit input sets are inspected.

### 3.6. Director Review: Submission, Rework, Approval and Withdrawal

Submission opens the review cycle. A `KeyAccountManager` submits a promotion that is currently `DRAFT`, moving `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED`. Two further conditions apply: the funding allocation must still be `APPROVED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_APPROVED`; and the promotion must carry at least one tactic line whose `status` is `PLANNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROMOTION_HAS_NO_TACTICS` (HTTP 422). Submission recomputes the four-step pricing chain so the director reviews current numbers, and increments the promotion's `version` by 1.

From `SUBMITTED` the plan forks two ways, and the two branches have deliberately divergent side-effect sets. On the **rework branch**, a `TradeMarketingDirector` requests changes with a `change_notes` comment; `lifecycle_status` moves `SUBMITTED` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, the reviewer's comment is stored on the promotion's audit trail, the promotion's `version` is incremented by 1 and `updated_at` refreshed — and no budget field anywhere changes. No allocation figure moves, no money is reserved, and no volume is committed. On the **approval branch**, described below, both money and volume are reserved.

Rework closes through a revision loop. A `KeyAccountManager` revises a promotion that is currently `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, moving `lifecycle_status` `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `DRAFT`, which unlocks tactic editing again. Each revise increments `revision_round` by 1, and it is permitted only while `revision_round` is still below 2. The full cycle is therefore `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `DRAFT`, traversable twice; a third attempt to revise is refused with `ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS` (HTTP 409). The promotion's `version` is incremented by 1 on each revise.

Approval is where the plan becomes a financial commitment, and it carries the densest precondition set in the system. A `TradeMarketingDirector` approves a promotion that is currently `SUBMITTED`, provided that: the funding allocation is `APPROVED` and its `posture` is `OPEN`; the allocation's customer is in `trading_status` `ACTIVE`; the promotion's `commitment_verified` is true, meaning every `DISPLAY` or `FEATURE` tactic type on the promotion is covered by a `VERIFIED` `COMMITMENT` document whose `covers_tactic_type` is that type or `ALL` — a failure here is `ERR_COMMITMENT_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422), and a promotion with only `TPR` lines passes it vacuously; the allocation's `available_amount` is at or above the promotion's `planned_total_spend`, since `available_amount` still excludes this not-yet-committed promotion — a failure here is `ERR_ALLOCATION_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422); and the promotion's `committed_units` is at or below the allocation's `unreserved_units` — a failure here is `ERR_UNITS_EXCEED_UNRESERVED` (HTTP 422). Per §2.8 the two fiscal checks are the last rungs evaluated, so a promotion missing both paperwork and money reports the paperwork.

A successful approval moves `lifecycle_status` `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` and records the acting director's identifier in `approved_by_user_id`. That single field is the anchor of the segregation-of-duties rule that will later govern deduction validation, so it is written at approval and never rewritten. The allocation then reserves both quantities at once: `committed_amount` rises by the promotion's `planned_total_spend`, `committed_units` rises by the promotion's `committed_units`, and `available_amount` and `unreserved_units` are recomputed from those. Both the promotion's `version` and the allocation's `version` are incremented by 1 and both `updated_at` values refreshed.

Withdrawal is the exact compensating action for approval. A `KeyAccountManager` withdraws a promotion that is currently `APPROVED`, optionally recording a `withdraw_reason`, and the move `APPROVED` → `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. It is permitted only while the promotion's `gross_accrued_amount` is still 0.00: once a single week has been accrued, the money has begun to move and the compensation is no longer clean, so the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID`. Withdrawal cascades conditionally through the tactic lines: every line whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `CANCELLED`, while `COMPLETED` lines are left untouched. Because those lines have left the `PLANNED`/`ACTIVE`/`COMPLETED` population, the promotion's `committed_units` recomputes to 0 and its `planned_total_spend` recomputes to 0.00; and because the promotion itself has left the `APPROVED`/`LIVE`/`ENDED`/`ANALYZED` population, the allocation drops this promotion's contribution from `committed_amount` and `committed_units`, restoring `available_amount` and `unreserved_units` to exactly their pre-approval values. The promotion's `version`, each cancelled line's `version` and the allocation's `version` are each incremented by 1.

### 3.7. Execution: Volume Facts, Activation and the Weekly Accrual Ledger

Volume is the raw material of every accrual and every measurement, and it arrives as an append-only weekly journal loaded by a `TradeFinanceAnalyst`. Each **volume fact** names a `customer_id` and a `brand_id`, declares a `fact_type` of either `SHIPMENT` — manufacturer shipments, as received on an EDI 856 advance ship notice — or `CONSUMPTION` — retailer point-of-sale, as received on an EDI 852 product activity report — carries a `period_start` giving the Monday of the fiscal week it belongs to, a `units` count of cases shipped or consumed in that week (a whole number at or above zero), and a `source` of `EDI_852`, `EDI_856` or `MANUAL` recording the row's provenance. The combination of `customer_id`, `brand_id`, `fact_type` and `period_start` must be unique across the whole journal; a repeat is refused with `ERR_VOLUME_FACT_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). Recording a fact recomputes nothing: accruals read facts when a period is posted, and post-event analysis reads them when it runs. Facts may be listed with optional `customer_id`, `brand_id`, `fact_type` and `period_start` filters.

Promotions come to life through an **operations checkpoint** that a `TradeFinanceAnalyst` or `TradeMarketingDirector` runs against the logical clock. The run takes no payload and reports back the `run_at` instant it used together with four lists — `promotions_activated`, `promotions_ended`, `tactics_activated` and `tactics_completed`. Promotions are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and within a single run **activation is evaluated before ending**: every `APPROVED` promotion whose `window_start` is at or before the clock date moves `APPROVED` → `LIVE`, and then every `LIVE` promotion whose `window_end` is at or before the clock date moves `LIVE` → `ENDED`. The consequence is that a promotion whose entire window lies in the past traverses `APPROVED` → `LIVE` → `ENDED` in one call. Tactic lines move in step: a line whose window contains the clock date — its `window_start` at or before the clock date and its `window_end` strictly after it — moves `PLANNED` → `ACTIVE`; a line whose `window_end` is at or before the clock date moves `ACTIVE` → `COMPLETED`, or `PLANNED` → `COMPLETED` when its owning promotion made the `LIVE` → `ENDED` move in the same run without the line ever having been activated. The `version` of every promotion and every line the run touches is incremented by 1.

Accruals are posted one week at a time by a `TradeFinanceAnalyst`, against a promotion whose `lifecycle_status` is `LIVE`, `ENDED` or `ANALYZED`; the request carries only the `period_start` of the week being accrued. At most one accrual may exist per promotion and week: a second posting for the same `period_start` is refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409) **even after the first has been reversed**, because the reversal preserves the original for audit rather than erasing it.

Which week the money belongs to, and which facts pay for it, is the promotion's `accrual_method` — and the two bases behave differently in both respects. On the `SHIPMENT` basis the effective period is the requested `period_start` shifted forward by 14 days, and the facts read are those with `fact_type` `SHIPMENT`; the week that funds promoted sell-through is the week the goods left the plant, a fortnight before the consumer saw the price. On the `CONSUMPTION` basis the effective period is the requested `period_start` itself with no shift, and the facts read are those with `fact_type` `CONSUMPTION`. For byte-identical fact data the two bases therefore accrue different amounts in different weeks, and a given promotion is only ever on one basis. The effective period must land inside the promotion's half-open window — at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end` — or the posting is refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_WINDOW_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

The amount itself is assembled as follows. First, `fact_units` is the sum of `units` across volume facts matching the promotion's own `customer_id` and `brand_id`, carrying the `fact_type` the method selected, and whose `period_start` equals the **requested** period exactly — a single week, never a range and never aggregated across weeks. Second, the live tactic lines for the posting are those of the promotion whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` and whose own window contains the effective period, meaning the line's `window_start` is at or before the effective period start and its `window_end` is strictly after it; `CANCELLED` lines contribute nothing. Those lines are taken in `created_at` ascending order — never in identifier order — so the breakdown is deterministic. Third, each line yields its own rounded component: a `TPR` line contributes `fact_units` multiplied by that line's `rate_per_case`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; a `DISPLAY` or `FEATURE` line contributes its `fixed_fee` divided by its own `week_count`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, which amortises the flat placement fee evenly across the weeks the placement runs. Finally, `amount` is the sum of those already-rounded components, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `tactic_line_amounts` is stored as an ordered array of objects each pairing a `tactic_id` with that line's `amount`, in the same `created_at` ascending order.

The posted entry has `entry_type` `ACCRUAL`, `status` `POSTED`, its `fact_units` and `amount` as computed, its `tactic_line_amounts` breakdown, and a `method` copied from the promotion's `accrual_method` at posting time so that a later method change can never rewrite history. Its `reverses_entry_id` and `reversal_reason` are both null. Posting then recomputes the promotion's accrual ledger and spend status, recomputes the allocation's `accrued_amount`, and increments the promotion's `version` and the allocation's `version` by 1 each.

Mistakes in the ledger are corrected by compensation, never by edit. A `TradeFinanceAnalyst` reverses a posted entry by supplying the `amount` being reversed together with a `reversal_reason`. Only an entry whose `entry_type` is `ACCRUAL` and whose `status` is `POSTED` may be reversed: a `REVERSAL` entry cannot itself be reversed and an already-`REVERSED` entry cannot be reversed twice, either attempt being refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED` (HTTP 409). Partial reversals are not supported: the supplied `amount` must be strictly greater than 0.00 and must equal the reversed entry's `amount` exactly, or the request is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_ENTRY_AMOUNT` (HTTP 422). The original entry's `status` moves `POSTED` → `REVERSED`, and a second entry is created carrying `entry_type` `REVERSAL`, `status` `POSTED`, an `amount` equal to the reversed entry's `amount`, a `reverses_entry_id` naming the reversed entry, a `fact_units` of 0 and an empty `tactic_line_amounts` array — and it is that new mirror entry, not the reversed original, that comes back as the response body. `REVERSAL` entries stay `POSTED` forever. The reversal recomputes the promotion's accrual ledger and spend status and the allocation's `accrued_amount`, and increments the reversed entry's `version` by 1 along with the promotion's and the allocation's. Accrual entries may be listed with optional `promotion_id`, `reverses_entry_id`, `entry_type` and `status` filters — the second of which is the reverse lookup from an original to its compensating twin.

Four figures on the promotion make up the accrual ledger, recomputed on accrual posting, accrual reversal, deduction validation, deduction settlement, dispute write-off and dispute recovery. `gross_accrued_amount` is the sum of `amount` over the promotion's entries whose `entry_type` is `ACCRUAL`, **regardless of their status** — a reversed accrual stays in the gross figure and is offset by its twin — rounded to 2 decimal places. `reversed_accrual_amount` is the sum of `amount` over the promotion's entries whose `entry_type` is `REVERSAL`, rounded to 2 decimal places. `consumed_deduction_amount` is the sum of two populations, each rounded to 2 decimal places and then added: `validated_amount` over the promotion's deductions whose `status` is `VALIDATED` or `SETTLED`, plus `write_off_amount` over every `WRITTEN_OFF` dispute raised on one of the promotion's deductions. Deductions sitting in `RECEIVED`, `DISPUTED` or `RECOVERED` contribute nothing. `accrued_balance` is `gross_accrued_amount` minus `reversed_accrual_amount` minus `consumed_deduction_amount`, rounded to 2 decimal places, and it may legitimately be negative when a retailer has deducted more than was ever accrued.

Live over-spend monitoring sits on top of that ledger and is deliberately non-linear. `recognized_spend_amount` is the **greater** of two views — the accrual-book view, `gross_accrued_amount` minus `reversed_accrual_amount`, and the deduction-book view, `consumed_deduction_amount` — rounded to 2 decimal places. It is the greater of the two rather than the sum because a retailer can deduct more than was ever accrued while an accrual can sit un-deducted, and recognised spend must not double count. When the promotion's `planned_total_spend` is 0.00, `spend_ratio` is 0.0000 and `spend_status` is `WITHIN_BUDGET`. Otherwise `spend_ratio` is `recognized_spend_amount` divided by `planned_total_spend`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and `spend_status` follows exact boundaries: a ratio strictly greater than 1.0000 is `OVERSPENT`; a ratio at or above 0.9000 but not exceeding 1.0000 is `OVERSPEND_WARNING`; anything below 0.9000 is `WITHIN_BUDGET`. A ratio of exactly 0.9000 is therefore `OVERSPEND_WARNING`, a ratio of exactly 1.0000 is still `OVERSPEND_WARNING`, and a ratio of 1.0001 is `OVERSPENT`. The recomputation runs after every accrual posting, accrual reversal, deduction validation, dispute write-off, dispute recovery and promotion withdrawal.

The spend axis moves in both directions. Rising spend takes a promotion `WITHIN_BUDGET` → `OVERSPEND_WARNING`, `OVERSPEND_WARNING` → `OVERSPENT`, or — when a single event pushes the ratio past 1.0000 outright — `WITHIN_BUDGET` → `OVERSPENT` directly. A reversal that lowers `recognized_spend_amount` walks it back down, `OVERSPENT` → `OVERSPEND_WARNING` and `OVERSPEND_WARNING` → `WITHIN_BUDGET`. Every one of those moves increments the promotion's `version` by 1 and refreshes `updated_at`. The asymmetry is in the consequence, not the axis: **the first time** a promotion reaches `OVERSPENT`, the owning allocation's `overspend_event_count` is incremented by 1, which drives that allocation's `posture` from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` — and when the promotion later recovers, `overspend_event_count` is *not* decremented, so the allocation stays `RESTRICTED`.

### 3.8. Deduction Intake, Matching, Valuation and Settlement

Months after the event, the retailer simply takes the money. A `DeductionSpecialist` books each chargeback as a **deduction**, supplying the `customer_id` that took it, the retailer's own `claim_reference` (1 to 60 characters), the `claimed_amount` gross-deducted from the remittance (strictly positive, exactly 2 decimal places), the `deduction_date` printed on the remittance advice as an ISO 8601 calendar date, and optionally a `supersedes_deduction_id` naming an earlier claim this corrected re-issue replaces. `claim_reference` must be unique per `customer_id` across every deduction of that customer in every status; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_REFERENCE` (HTTP 409). A new claim is created in `status` `RECEIVED` with `promotion_id`, `validation_basis`, `entitled_amount`, `validated_amount`, `variance_amount`, `matched_by_user_id`, `validated_by_user_id` and `settled_at` all null. Intake immediately recomputes the customer's `open_deduction_amount` and increments the customer's `version` by 1. Deductions may be listed with optional `customer_id`, `promotion_id`, `status` and `supersedes_deduction_id` filters.

**Matching** is the act of finding the agreement the retailer is claiming against. A `DeductionSpecialist` matches a claim that is currently `RECEIVED` by supplying the `promotion_id` of the target agreement, and two coherence conditions apply in this order: the promotion must belong to the deduction's own `customer_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEDUCTION_CUSTOMER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); and the promotion's `lifecycle_status` must be `APPROVED`, `LIVE`, `ENDED` or `ANALYZED`, because a promotion still in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, or one already `WITHDRAWN`, is not a matchable agreement — that refusal is `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_MATCHABLE` (HTTP 409). A successful match moves `status` `RECEIVED` → `MATCHED`, stores the `promotion_id` and records the acting specialist in `matched_by_user_id`, and increments the deduction's `version` by 1.

**Validation** is where the claim is priced, and it is the single densest operation in the system. A `TradeFinanceAnalyst` validates a claim that is currently `MATCHED`, supplying `expected_promotion_version`. Four conditions guard it beyond the state check: the stale-version rejection of §2.6; the segregation-of-duties rule, so the operator whose identifier sits in the matched promotion's `approved_by_user_id` may not validate this claim and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` while any other analyst succeeds; and then one of two policy-specific preconditions. Under the `EVIDENCE_BASED` policy the matched promotion must carry at least one `VERIFIED` `EVIDENCE` document with a `units_verified` greater than zero, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_PERFORMANCE_EVIDENCE` (HTTP 422). Under the `AGREEMENT_CAP` policy that rule does not apply at all; instead the promotion's `accrued_balance` must be strictly greater than 0.00, or the request is refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422) because there is nothing left on the agreement to allow. The same request body therefore succeeds or fails purely on the customer's policy flag.

Valuation itself begins by copying the customer's `deduction_validation_policy` as it stands at that moment into the deduction's `validation_basis`, freezing the basis on the row. Two inputs are then gathered from the matched promotion: the **contract rate**, which is the sum of `rate_per_case` across the promotion's `TPR` lines whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` — `DISPLAY` and `FEATURE` lines contribute no per-case rate; and the **evidence units**, which is the sum of `units_verified` across the promotion's documents whose `doc_class` is `EVIDENCE` and whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, with `SUBMITTED` evidence never counting. The two policies then diverge with equal weight. Under `EVIDENCE_BASED`, `entitled_amount` is built bottom-up from substantiated cases: the evidence units multiplied by the contract rate, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and it is **not** capped by the accrual balance — so a well-evidenced claim can legitimately exceed what was ever accrued and push the promotion over budget. Under `AGREEMENT_CAP`, `entitled_amount` is top-down: it is exactly the matched promotion's `accrued_balance` as it stands at the moment of the request, before this deduction is applied, and evidence is ignored entirely. From identical claim, evidence and accrual data the two bases return different figures, and which one is larger flips depending on whether evidence or accrual is the scarcer resource. From `entitled_amount` two figures follow: `validated_amount` is the lesser of the claim's `claimed_amount` and its `entitled_amount` — the portion that will actually consume promotion accrual — and `variance_amount` is `claimed_amount` minus `validated_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, which is the invalid portion eligible for dispute.

A successful validation moves `status` `MATCHED` → `VALIDATED`, writes `validation_basis`, `entitled_amount`, `validated_amount` and `variance_amount`, and records the acting analyst in `validated_by_user_id`. It then walks the four-hop chain of §2.9: the promotion's `consumed_deduction_amount` rises by `validated_amount` and its `accrued_balance` falls by the same amount; the spend status is recomputed from the new consumed figure and may reach `OVERSPENT`; reaching `OVERSPENT` for the first time increments the allocation's `overspend_event_count` and drives its `posture` `OPEN` → `RESTRICTED`; and the newly `RESTRICTED` allocation adds 10 points to the customer's `compliance_penalty_points`, which can drive that customer's `trading_status` `ACTIVE` → `CREDIT_HOLD`. The allocation's `accrued_amount` is recomputed, and where the promotion already has a post-event analysis that analysis is recomputed in place. The `version` of the deduction, and of each of the promotion, allocation and customer rows the hops actually touched, is incremented by 1.

**Settlement** closes a valid claim. A `TradeFinanceAnalyst` settles a claim that is currently `VALIDATED`, and the customer must be in `trading_status` `ACTIVE`: a retailer on `CREDIT_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED` cannot have deductions settled, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD`. The move `VALIDATED` → `SETTLED` is terminal; `settled_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, the allocation's `settled_amount` and the customer's `open_deduction_amount` are recomputed, and the deduction's, allocation's and customer's `version` values are each incremented by 1. Any of match, validate, settle or dispute attempted from a status that does not permit it — match only from `RECEIVED`, validate only from `MATCHED`, settle only from `VALIDATED`, dispute only from `RECEIVED`, `MATCHED` or `VALIDATED` — is refused with `ERR_DEDUCTION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

### 3.9. Disputing a Claim: the Retailer Round-Trip, Aging and Resolution

Where a claim is wrong, the manufacturer pushes back. A `DeductionSpecialist` opens a **dispute** on a deduction whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `MATCHED` or `VALIDATED`, giving a `reason_code` of `NO_AGREEMENT`, `NO_EVIDENCE`, `RATE_MISMATCH` or `DUPLICATE_CLAIM` and the `disputed_amount` being challenged. That amount must be strictly greater than 0.00 and must not exceed the **disputable base**, which depends on how far the claim has travelled: while the deduction is still `RECEIVED` or `MATCHED` the whole `claimed_amount` is challengeable, but once it has been `VALIDATED` only the `variance_amount` is, because the validated portion has already been conceded. A request past that ceiling is refused with `ERR_DISPUTED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM` (HTTP 422). Opening a dispute moves the deduction's `status` to `DISPUTED` and creates one dispute row in `status` `OPEN`, with `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock, `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting specialist's identifier, `round_count` 0 and `retailer_response`, `recovered_amount`, `write_off_amount` and `resolved_by_user_id` all null. The customer's `open_deduction_amount` is recomputed — a `DISPUTED` claim is still an open claim — and both the deduction's and the dispute's `version` values are incremented by 1. An unmatched claim disputed for `NO_AGREEMENT` never touches any promotion ledger at all, which is the point: there is no agreement to charge it to. Disputes may be listed with optional `deduction_id`, `status` and `reason_code` filters, ordered by `opened_at` ascending.

Negotiation is a bounded loop. A `DeductionSpecialist` sends an `OPEN` dispute to the retailer, optionally attaching `packet_notes`, which moves `status` `OPEN` → `AWAITING_RETAILER` and increments `round_count` by 1. The retailer answers through the portal: the `CustomerPortalUser` whose identifier equals the disputed deduction's own `customer_id` posts a `retailer_response` of `ACCEPT_CHARGEBACK`, `PROVIDE_EVIDENCE` or `REFUSE`, which records that answer and returns `status` `AWAITING_RETAILER` → `OPEN`. That completes one cycle. Because `round_count` is never reset by the return leg, the loop may run at most twice: a send is permitted only while `round_count` is strictly below 2, so a third send on the same dispute is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Sending from any status other than `OPEN`, or responding from any status other than `AWAITING_RETAILER`, is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Time itself escalates a stalled dispute. `aging_days` is the floor of the elapsed seconds between `opened_at` and the current logical-clock instant divided by 86400 — whole days, always truncated down — and it is recomputed on every read, so a dispute's age reflects wherever the clock has been moved to. An **aging sweep**, run by a `TradeFinanceAnalyst` or `DeductionSpecialist` with no payload, scans every dispute whose `status` is `OPEN` or `AWAITING_RETAILER` — `ESCALATED`, `RECOVERED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` disputes are skipped — in `opened_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, never in identifier order. Every candidate whose `aging_days` has reached 90 moves `OPEN` → `ESCALATED` or `AWAITING_RETAILER` → `ESCALATED`; the threshold is inclusive, so 90 days escalates while 89 does not. The sweep reports the `run_at` instant, the count of `disputes_scanned`, and the lists `disputes_escalated` and `customers_penalized`. Each escalation adds 5 points to the disputed deduction's customer via the penalty ledger of §2.10, which can carry that customer `ACTIVE` → `CREDIT_HOLD` or, once already on hold at 25 points or more, `CREDIT_HOLD` → `SUSPENDED`. The `version` of every dispute and every customer the sweep touches is incremented by 1.

A dispute ends one of two ways, from either `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`. **Recovery** is the good outcome: a `DeductionSpecialist` records a `recovered_amount` that is strictly greater than 0.00 and no greater than the dispute's `disputed_amount` — beyond that it is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_OPEN_AMOUNT` (HTTP 422). The dispute moves to the terminal `RECOVERED`, storing `recovered_amount` and the acting user in `resolved_by_user_id`, and the parent deduction's `status` moves `DISPUTED` → `RECOVERED`. Recovered cash is money back, so it lowers `net_spend` on the promotion's post-event analysis where one exists and lifts `roi` accordingly; the customer's `open_deduction_amount` drops because a `RECOVERED` deduction is closed. Both `version` values are incremented by 1.

**Write-off** is the bad outcome, and it is the most heavily guarded action in the system because it destroys money. The request carries a `write_off_amount` and an optional `write_off_reason`; the amount must be strictly greater than 0.00 and no greater than the dispute's `disputed_amount`, or it is refused with `ERR_WRITE_OFF_EXCEEDS_OPEN_AMOUNT` (HTTP 422). Two independent authorisation gates then apply. A `write_off_amount` at or above 500.00 requires a `TradeMarketingDirector`: a `DeductionSpecialist` may approve write-offs strictly below that limit only, and because the comparison is inclusive an amount of exactly 500.00 already needs the director, while 499.99 does not — the refusal is `ERR_WRITE_OFF_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). Separately, and regardless of amount or role, the user recorded in the dispute's `opened_by_user_id` may never approve its write-off; that self-approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A director who did not open the dispute clears both gates.

A successful write-off moves the dispute to the terminal `WRITTEN_OFF`, stores `write_off_amount`, `write_off_reason` and `resolved_by_user_id`, and moves the parent deduction's `status` `DISPUTED` → `WRITTEN_OFF`. It then walks the longest cascade in the service. Written-off money is recognised spend, so hop one adds it to the promotion's `consumed_deduction_amount`, which lowers `accrued_balance`, raises `recognized_spend_amount` and can move `spend_status` toward `OVERSPENT`. Hop two recomputes the promotion's post-event analysis where one exists, whose `net_spend` has just risen and whose `roi` therefore falls. Hop three flips the promotion's `performance_flag` to `UNDERPERFORMING` if the new `roi` has dropped below 1.0000. Hop four raises the owning allocation's `at_risk_amount`, because an underperforming promotion's committed money is now money at risk. The customer's `open_deduction_amount` and `write_off_ytd_amount` are both recomputed, and the `version` of the dispute, the deduction, the promotion and the allocation is each incremented by 1. Attempting recovery or write-off on a dispute that is already `RECOVERED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.10. Measurement and Compliance: Post-Event Analysis and Contract Audits

Once a promotion has run, a `TradeFinanceAnalyst` measures it. Analysis is available only for a promotion whose `lifecycle_status` is `ENDED` or `ANALYZED`, and a promotion may carry at most one analysis: a second attempt satisfies the lifecycle check and is then refused with `ERR_ANALYSIS_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409). The request supplies a `baseline_method` of `TRAILING` or `SEASONAL`, which is pinned onto the row at creation and reused verbatim by every later recompute, so a basis can never be silently switched underneath a published number. A successful analysis moves the promotion `ENDED` → `ANALYZED` and increments its `version` by 1.

Measurement reads **`CONSUMPTION` volume facts only**, for the promotion's own `customer_id` and `brand_id`, in three disjoint half-open week ranges: the trailing window from 28 days before `window_start` up to but excluding `window_start`, the promoted window from `window_start` up to but excluding `window_end`, and the post window from `window_end` up to but excluding `window_end` plus 14 days. `SHIPMENT` facts are never read here, even for a promotion that accrues on the shipment basis — accrual and measurement deliberately disagree about which journal is the truth.

`promo_consumption_units` is the sum of `units` over the promoted-window facts. When **no** `CONSUMPTION` fact at all exists inside the promoted window, `data_status` is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` and the analysis short-circuits: `baseline_weekly_units`, `baseline_units`, `lift_units`, `cannibalization_units`, `net_incremental_units` and `incremental_gross_profit` are all 0.00, `post_period_units` is 0, `roi` is null, and only `net_spend` is still computed. Otherwise `data_status` is `COMPLETE` and the full chain runs.

The baseline is where the two variants part company. Under `TRAILING`, `baseline_weekly_units` is the sum of `units` across the trailing window's facts divided by 4 — the retailer's own recent average across the four whole weeks immediately before the promotion — rounded half up to 2 decimal places, so a soft pre-period flatters the promotion by inflating measured lift. Under `SEASONAL`, `baseline_weekly_units` is the brand's planning constant `baseline_units_per_week` rounded to 2 decimal places, wholly independent of that retailer's recent actuals. From byte-identical facts the two methods report different lift, different cannibalisation and different ROI.

From there: `baseline_units` is `baseline_weekly_units` multiplied by the promotion's week count, rounded to 2 decimal places. `lift_units` is `promo_consumption_units` minus `baseline_units`, rounded to 2 decimal places, and may legitimately be negative when a promotion sold less than the baseline. `post_period_units` is the sum of `units` over the post-window facts. Expected post-period volume is `baseline_weekly_units` multiplied by 2, rounded to 2 decimal places, and `cannibalization_units` is the greater of 0 and that expected figure minus `post_period_units`, rounded to 2 decimal places — a post-promotion trough is pantry-loading clawed back from the lift, while a post-period that beats baseline is clamped to zero rather than credited. `net_incremental_units` is `lift_units` minus `cannibalization_units`, rounded to 2 decimal places, and `incremental_gross_profit` is that figure multiplied by the brand's `unit_margin`, rounded to 2 decimal places.

The denominator is spend net of recoveries. Recovered total is the sum of `recovered_amount` over `RECOVERED` disputes whose deductions are matched to this promotion, rounded to 2 decimal places, and `net_spend` is the promotion's `recognized_spend_amount` minus that total, rounded to 2 decimal places. `roi` is `incremental_gross_profit` divided by `net_spend`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and is null whenever `net_spend` is at or below 0.00 or `data_status` is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` — division by a non-positive denominator is not a rating, it is an absence of one. The promotion's `performance_flag` follows directly: a null `roi` gives `UNRATED`, an `roi` below 1.0000 gives `UNDERPERFORMING`, and an `roi` at or above 1.0000 gives `ON_TRACK`, so an `roi` of exactly 1.0000 is `ON_TRACK`. `computed_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant of the most recent recompute — refreshed by cascaded recomputes from deduction validation, dispute write-off and dispute recovery just as much as by the original analysis call — so a stored analysis is a live figure, not a frozen snapshot. An analysis may be read on its own by `analysisId`, listed for one promotion as the zero-or-one reverse lookup, or listed across promotions newest first with optional `promotion_id`, `baseline_method` and `data_status` filters.

Contract compliance runs on a separate axis. A `ComplianceAuditor` opens an audit against a customer over a scope window given by `scope_start` inclusive and `scope_end` exclusive, where `scope_end` must be strictly after `scope_start`. Only one audit may be open per customer: the customer's `audit_status` must be `CLEAR`, so a retailer already under fieldwork or still carrying issued findings cannot be re-audited, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The audit is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `findings_count` 0, `recovery_claim_amount` 0.00, `issued_at` null and `auditor_user_id` set to the acting auditor; the customer's `audit_status` moves `CLEAR` → `AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS` and its `version` is incremented by 1. Opening an audit never touches the commercial axis: `trading_status` is unaffected.

Findings accumulate one at a time. A `ComplianceAuditor` records a finding with a `finding_code` and a `recovery_amount`, permitted while the audit is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. The first finding moves the audit `SCHEDULED` → `IN_PROGRESS`; each subsequent finding is an `IN_PROGRESS` → `IN_PROGRESS` self-transition. Either way `findings_count` is incremented by 1, the supplied `recovery_amount` is added to `recovery_claim_amount` — the running total of every finding amount recorded on the audit, rounded to 2 decimal places — and the audit's `version` is incremented by 1.

Issuance closes the audit and scores the retailer. A `ComplianceAuditor` or `TradeMarketingDirector` issues findings on an audit that is `IN_PROGRESS` and whose `findings_count` is at least 1 — an audit still `SCHEDULED` has nothing to issue, and the terminal `FINDINGS_ISSUED` cannot be issued twice, both being refused with `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The audit moves `IN_PROGRESS` → `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock; the customer's `audit_status` moves `AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS` → `FINDINGS_OPEN` and 10 penalty points are added through the ledger of §2.10, which can carry `trading_status` `ACTIVE` → `CREDIT_HOLD` on the independent commercial axis and thereby block that retailer's settlements. The audit's and the customer's `version` values are each incremented by 1. Audits may be read individually, listed for one customer, or listed across customers newest first with optional `customer_id` and `status` filters.
