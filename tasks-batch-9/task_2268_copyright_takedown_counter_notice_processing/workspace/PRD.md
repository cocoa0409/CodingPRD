# Copyright Takedown & Counter-Notice Processing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An online service provider that hosts subscriber-uploaded material only keeps its statutory safe harbour if it can prove, on demand, that it did four things correctly and in order: it named an agent to receive copyright complaints and told the world how to reach that agent; it acted expeditiously when a complaint arrived; it gave the accused subscriber a real route back; and it terminated the subscribers who kept infringing. Today those four obligations are tracked in mailboxes, spreadsheets and legal memos, which means the provider cannot answer the only question that matters in litigation — *what did you do, when, and under which published policy?*

This document specifies the API that replaces that patchwork. The service is mounted under `/dmca` and covers the whole chain of custody for a copyright complaint: designated-agent registration with the copyright office and publication of the public contact page, notice intake with statutory element scoring and outright rejection of deficient notices, expeditious disabling of access with subscriber notification, counter-notice receipt and forwarding with business-day restoration timing, strike accounting under a published repeat-infringer policy, account termination with a segregated appeal, abuse investigation and misrepresentation penalties against complainants who file fraudulent takedowns, and a periodic safe-harbour audit that scores the provider's own posture.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **time is explicit**: every deadline, decay curve and business-day window is measured against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so a compliance scenario can be replayed deterministically. Second, **nothing is decided twice by the same hand**: the officer who terminates an account may not decide its appeal, and the investigator who opens an abuse case may not close it. Both commitments exist because the artefacts this API produces are evidentiary.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold for every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, list mechanics, identity and time, the order in which rejections are decided, concurrency control, arithmetic precision, and the cross-cutting compliance gates.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it is acting. Exactly five roles exist: `ComplianceOfficer`, `TakedownAgent`, `AbuseInvestigator`, `RightsHolderRep`, and `Subscriber`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five spellings, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Three of the roles are operator identities that are not backed by any stored record. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition asks for it — as `executed_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a `TerminationCase` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `TakedownAgent`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and no entity field stores it. When `X-User-Role` is `AbuseInvestigator`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it is recorded as `opened_by_user_id` and `substantiated_by_user_id` on a `MisrepresentationCase` but is never matched against an entity's `id`.

The other two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `RightsHolderRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RightsHolder`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `RightsHolder` compare the resource's `rights_holder_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Subscriber`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Account` compare the resource's `account_id` to `X-User-ID`. An entity-backed role that acts on a resource belonging to a different entity is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards exactly ten operations: uploading material, purging material, creating a counter-notice, filing a termination appeal, submitting a notice, withdrawing a notice, registering a reference file, activating a reference file, opening an ingestion batch, and completing an ingestion batch. On the two operations that a `ComplianceOfficer` shares with an entity-backed role — purging material and registering or activating a reference file — the ownership comparison applies only when the acting role is the entity-backed one.

Segregation of duties is enforced on top of role membership. A `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the `executed_by_user_id` recorded on a termination case may not decide that case's appeal; a second officer identity must decide it. An `AbuseInvestigator` whose `X-User-ID` equals a non-null `opened_by_user_id` on a misrepresentation case may neither substantiate nor dismiss that case; a second investigator identity must. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional integer parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` alongside the two request-identification fields.

Rows are always returned in ascending `created_at` order. Ties are broken by `created_at` alone; `id` is never consulted as a tie-breaker, and no business decision anywhere in this API reads `id` for ordering. Filters are applied **before** pagination, so `total_count` is the size of the filtered set rather than the size of the underlying collection, and `has_more` is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows actually returned is less than `total_count`. When several filters are supplied on the same request they intersect.

The available filters are fixed per collection. The provider list filters on `safe_harbor_status`. A provider's designated agents filter on `registration_status`; a provider's policy revisions filter on `status`; a provider's audit records take no filter. The account list filters on `provider_id` and `standing`. An account's content items filter on `availability_status` and `dispute_status`; an account's strikes filter on `status`; an account's termination cases filter on `status`. The rights-holder list filters on `enrollment_status` and `program_tier`; a holder's reference files and a holder's ingestion batches each filter on `status`. The notice list filters on `rights_holder_id`, `ingestion_batch_id` and `status`. The claim list — the only discovery channel for claims created by cascade — filters on `notice_id`, `content_item_id`, `rights_holder_id`, `reference_file_id` and `status`, and the strikes of a single claim filter on `status`. The counter-notice list filters on `claim_id`, `account_id`, `superseded_by_counter_notice_id` and `status`. The misrepresentation-case list filters on `rights_holder_id`, `claim_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored record carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Records also carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly 1 each time the record is mutated. There is one exception: a `SafeHarborAudit` is an immutable evidentiary record and carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely.

All time-bearing behaviour in this system reads a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any call sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. Any role may advance it by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the response echoes back `current_utc`. Advancing the clock is not a passive operation: it re-evaluates strike age, moving strike records through decay and expiry and recomputing each affected account's point total and standing; it promotes forwarded counter-notices whose earliest restoration instant has arrived; and it closes executed termination cases whose appeal window has run out unappealed. Every computed timestamp described in Section 3 — a submission time, a disabling time, a restoration deadline, an accrual time — is taken from this clock.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution

Several reads and actions address a resource through a parent path segment. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that names a resource which does not exist decides the error. A missing provider yields `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing designated agent `ERR_DESIGNATED_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing policy revision `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, a missing account `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing content item `ERR_CONTENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, a missing rights holder `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing notice `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing claim `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, a missing counter-notice `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing strike record `ERR_STRIKE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing termination case `ERR_TERMINATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing misrepresentation case `ERR_MISREPRESENTATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing reference file `ERR_REFERENCE_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing ingestion batch `ERR_INGESTION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing audit record `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`. All fifteen carry HTTP 404.

The same catalogue is reused for foreign keys carried in a request body. When a creation payload names a parent that does not exist — `provider_id`, `account_id`, `rights_holder_id`, `claim_id` or `ingestion_batch_id`, and equally an identifier supplied as a list filter — the matching not-found code from the list above is returned with HTTP 404. This body-level check applies to the creation of designated agents, policy revisions, accounts, content items, reference files, ingestion batches, notices, counter-notices, misrepresentation cases, and safe-harbour audits.

### 2.6. The Rejection Ladder

Because most operations in this domain are guarded by several conditions at once, the order in which those conditions are evaluated is part of the contract: the first condition that fails decides both the error identifier and the HTTP status, and no later condition may mask an earlier one. The ladder is evaluated in the following order.

1. **Header authentication.** Both headers present, role among the five declared names — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Request shape.** Required fields present, enum values in range, `fingerprint_hash` exactly 32 characters drawn from the lowercase hexadecimal alphabet, `claimed_urls` holding between 1 and 25 entries with none of them empty, `limit` between 1 and 100, `offset` not negative, and `limitation_threshold_points` strictly below `termination_threshold_points`. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Path existence**, left to right, per Section 2.5 (404).
5. **Body foreign-key existence**, per Section 2.5 (404).
6. **Resource ownership** for entity-backed roles — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
7. **Registered designated agent.** Before any notice may be validated, the provider must hold at least one designated agent in `REGISTERED`; otherwise `ERR_DESIGNATED_AGENT_NOT_REGISTERED` (409).
8. **Published public contact.** Before an agent registration may be recorded, the provider's public contact information must be published; otherwise `ERR_PUBLIC_CONTACT_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
9. **Safe-harbour eligibility.** Before an automated high-volume ingestion batch is accepted, the provider's `safe_harbor_status` must be `ELIGIBLE`; otherwise `ERR_SAFE_HARBOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (403).
10. **Published repeat-infringer policy.** Before access is disabled by notice processing, and before an account termination is executed, the provider must hold a policy revision in `PUBLISHED`; otherwise `ERR_REPEAT_INFRINGER_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
11. **State legality.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Depending on the resource this is `ERR_INVALID_AGENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COUNTER_NOTICE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CONTENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ACCOUNT_STANDING`, `ERR_INVALID_TERMINATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MISREPRESENTATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE_FILE_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, each with HTTP 409. Every action endpoint is subject to this rung.
12. **Segregation of duties** on appeal decisions, misrepresentation substantiation and misrepresentation dismissal — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
13. **Temporal windows.** Six checks share this rung, each on a different operation: restoring before the claim's due instant gives `ERR_RESTORATION_TOO_EARLY`; a counter-notice arriving after its window gives `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; upholding a disabled claim while its counter-notice window is still open gives `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_WINDOW_OPEN`; recording a notice of court action too late gives `ERR_SUIT_NOTICE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; filing an appeal too late gives `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`; lapsing a registration before its term has run gives `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_EXPIRED`. All six carry HTTP 409.
14. **Referential preconditions.** In this order: a rights holder that is not `ENROLLED` gives `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_NOT_ENROLLED` (409) on reference-file activation, ingestion-batch creation and tier promotion; a `TERMINATED` account gives `ERR_ACCOUNT_TERMINATED` (403) on material upload and counter-notice creation; an upload-restricted account gives `ERR_ACCOUNT_UPLOAD_RESTRICTED` (403) on material upload; a `STANDARD`-tier holder gives `ERR_PROGRAM_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (409) on ingestion-batch creation and on a notice carrying an `ingestion_batch_id`; a promotion to `TRUSTED` with no active reference file gives `ERR_NO_REFERENCE_FILE_ACTIVE` (409); and an abuse score above the applicable ceiling gives `ERR_ABUSE_SCORE_TOO_HIGH` (409) on tier promotion and on enrollment. Because `ERR_ACCOUNT_TERMINATED` is evaluated before `ERR_ACCOUNT_UPLOAD_RESTRICTED`, an upload attempt from a terminated account reports termination rather than restriction, even though both conditions hold.
15. **Suit block.** Restoration is refused while the governing counter-notice is `BLOCKED_BY_SUIT`, with `ERR_RESTORATION_BLOCKED_BY_SUIT` (409).
16. **Uniqueness and interval constraints.** A duplicate `content_url` under the same provider gives `ERR_CONTENT_URL_CONFLICT`; a duplicate `handle` under the same provider gives `ERR_ACCOUNT_HANDLE_CONFLICT`; an occupied dispute interval gives `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_WINDOW`; a second open misrepresentation case on the same holder-and-claim pair gives `ERR_MISREPRESENTATION_CASE_EXISTS`; a second registered designated agent for the same provider gives `ERR_AGENT_ALREADY_REGISTERED`. All five carry HTTP 409.
17. **Actionability.** Processing a validated notice none of whose claimed URLs resolves to a content item in `AVAILABLE` or `RESTORED` gives `ERR_NO_ACTIONABLE_CLAIMS` (HTTP 422). Note that this rung sits *below* the duplicate-interval rung: when URLs do resolve to live material but every one of them is still inside an existing dispute interval from the same complainant, the answer is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_WINDOW`, not `ERR_NO_ACTIONABLE_CLAIMS`.
18. **Quotas.** Exceeding the reference-file allowance gives `ERR_REFERENCE_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (409); exceeding the rolling ingestion allowance gives `ERR_INGESTION_WINDOW_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (409).
19. **Optimistic concurrency**, per Section 2.7 — `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION`, each HTTP 412.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Provider and Claim Versions

Exactly three write operations in this API supersede something a previous writer published, and each of them therefore requires the client to state the parent version it believes it is superseding. The field is non-optional in all three cases.

Recording a copyright-office registration for a designated agent — the request against `POST /dmca/designated-agents/{id}/register` — must carry `expected_provider_version` matching the target provider's current `version`. A registration legitimately supersedes the provider's previously published agent designation, so a writer holding a stale provider view is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and must re-read the provider before re-issuing the registration.

Publishing a repeat-infringer policy revision — the request against `POST /dmca/repeat-infringer-policies/{id}/publish` — must likewise carry `expected_provider_version` matching the provider's current `version`. Publication supersedes whichever revision the provider currently has in `PUBLISHED`, so a writer that does not hold the provider version it intends to supersede is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Creating a counter-notice — the request against `POST /dmca/counter-notices` — must carry `expected_claim_version` matching the target claim's current `version`. A new counter-notice legitimately supersedes an earlier non-terminal counter-notice on the same claim, so the creating writer must present the claim version it observed when it decided to supersede; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Creation itself increments the claim's `version` by 1, which means a second counter-notice submitted against the version the client read before the first one landed is refused.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may be refused for a version mismatch.

### 2.8. Arithmetic Precision and Units

Rounding is always **half up**, and the number of decimal places depends on the quantity. Statutory completeness scores, for both notices and counter-notices, are integers on a scale of 0 to 100 because every element weight is an integer; comparisons against their thresholds are inclusive at the threshold. Account strike points and rights-holder abuse scores are carried to two decimal places, and their threshold comparisons are inclusive. Reference-match confidences are carried to four decimal places, with inclusive threshold comparisons. Safe-harbour components are carried to two decimal places while the overall compliance score is a whole number. Penalties are integers in **cents** throughout — no penalty field is ever expressed in whole currency units — and only the surcharge component is rounded.

Where an evaluation order can change a rounded result, that order is pinned and stated with the calculation in Section 3. Two cases matter especially: strike points are rounded once per strike and then once more over the sum, never by summing unrounded products; and the misrepresentation surcharge tier is selected from the base amount, never re-selected from the running total after the surcharge has been added.

Instants are exact to the millisecond. Business-day arithmetic preserves the UTC time-of-day of its anchor instant. Item counts and day counts are integers.

### 2.9. Parallel State Dimensions on Hosted Material

A piece of hosted material carries **two independent state dimensions**, and neither may be inferred from the other. The access dimension, `availability_status`, is one of `AVAILABLE`, `DISABLED`, `RESTORED` or `PURGED`, with `PURGED` terminal. The dispute dimension, `dispute_status`, is one of `NONE`, `UNDER_NOTICE`, `COUNTER_NOTICED`, `RESTORATION_PENDING` or `RESOLVED`, and has no terminal value. The two dimensions advance on their own transition sets and never gate each other: a purged item can still hold `dispute_status` `RESOLVED`, and a piece of material whose dispute has been resolved may remain `DISABLED` indefinitely.

Provider posture, `safe_harbor_status`, is a third enumeration — `ELIGIBLE`, `AT_RISK` or `FORFEITED` — and is described in Section 2.10. Several other enumerations are cyclic rather than progressive: an account's `standing` runs `ACTIVE` to `LIMITED` to `TERMINATED` and back to `ACTIVE`; a designated agent's `registration_status` runs `REGISTERED` to `LAPSED` to `SUBMITTED` and back to `REGISTERED` on re-designation; a rights holder's `enrollment_status` cycles between `ENROLLED` and `SUSPENDED`; and its `program_tier` walks up from `STANDARD` through `VERIFIED` to `TRUSTED` and steps back down again under abuse pressure.

### 2.10. Safe-Harbour Gates and When Posture Is Recomputed

Safe-harbour posture is not a running tally. It changes only on the operations that touch the provider's own documentation: publishing the public contact information, recording an agent registration, lapsing an agent registration, publishing a policy revision, and running an audit. Notice processing, counter-notice handling, restoration and termination never change posture directly — they change the inputs that the *next* audit will read.

Three **hard gates** sit in front of the score and are evaluated in a fixed order. If `designated_agent_registered` is false the gate failure is `AGENT_NOT_REGISTERED`; otherwise, if `repeat_infringer_policy_published` is false the failure is `POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`; otherwise, if `public_contact_published_at` is null the failure is `CONTACT_NOT_PUBLISHED`; otherwise no gate has failed. When any gate fails, the compliance score is 0, the posture is `FORFEITED`, and no component is computed. Only when all three gates pass does the four-component score decide between `ELIGIBLE` (a score of 85 or more), `AT_RISK` (a score from 60 through 84 inclusive) and `FORFEITED` (a score below 60).

The posture value recorded on the provider is `safe_harbor_status` and the numeric score is `safe_harbor_score`. Both are read-only outputs of the recomputation; `safe_harbor_score` is set to 0 whenever a hard gate has failed.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Maintained Counters

Many operations in this domain touch several aggregates in one request, and the specification treats every one of those secondary effects as mandatory rather than best-effort. When a request mutates a record, that record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1 — this holds for records mutated as a side effect of an operation aimed at a different resource just as much as for the direct target.

A family of counters is maintained by these cascades and must always agree with the records they summarise. On an account, `available_content_count` tracks the number of its content items whose `availability_status` is `AVAILABLE` or `RESTORED`, and `notified_count` tracks how many takedown notifications have been delivered to the subscriber. On a rights holder, `notices_processed`, `withdrawn_count`, `rejected_count` and `misrepresentation_count` count that holder's notices reaching a processed state, its withdrawn notices, its rejected notices, and its substantiated misrepresentation cases respectively. On a reference file, `match_count` counts the claims that selected it. On an ingestion batch, `notices_created` counts the notices carrying that batch's identifier, while `matched_count` and `unmatched_count` are aggregated at completion. On a provider, `designated_agent_registered` is true while at least one of its designated agents sits in `REGISTERED`, `repeat_infringer_policy_published` is true while exactly one of its policy revisions sits in `PUBLISHED`, and `last_audit_at` holds the `created_at` of its most recent audit record.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Provider for Safe Harbour

A `ComplianceOfficer` begins by registering the online service provider itself, supplying its `legal_name` and the `service_domain` it operates. A newly registered provider is deliberately non-compliant: `safe_harbor_status` starts at `FORFEITED` with `safe_harbor_score` 0, `designated_agent_registered` and `repeat_infringer_policy_published` both start false, and `public_contact_url`, `public_contact_published_at` and `last_audit_at` all start null. Nothing about notice handling may proceed until the officer closes those gaps.

Publishing the public contact page is the first of them. The officer supplies the `public_contact_url` at which the designated agent's contact details can be read; the system stores that URL, stamps `public_contact_published_at` from the logical clock, increments the provider's `version` by 1, and recomputes the compliance score and posture — which, on a provider that still lacks a registered agent, leaves the posture at `FORFEITED` because the agent gate fails first.

The designation itself is drafted as a separate record. Drafting takes the `provider_id` the agent is designated for, the `agent_name` of the natural person or entity that will receive notices, the `agent_email` and `agent_phone` at which notices may be sent, and the `mailing_address` to be filed with the copyright office. A freshly drafted designation sits in `registration_status` `DRAFT`, with `copyright_office_receipt`, `submitted_at`, `registered_at` and `expires_at` all null.

Submitting the designation to the copyright office directory moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and requires that `agent_email`, `agent_phone` and `mailing_address` are all non-empty. The transition stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock. The same operation also serves re-designation after a term has run out, moving a designation `LAPSED → SUBMITTED`; on that path `submitted_at` is overwritten with the current clock while `registered_at` and `expires_at` are deliberately retained until the next registration overwrites them. Attempting to submit a designation that is already `SUBMITTED` or `REGISTERED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AGENT_STATE`.

Recording the copyright-office registration moves the designation `SUBMITTED → REGISTERED` and is the most heavily guarded step in this subsection. The officer supplies `expected_provider_version` and may optionally supply the `copyright_office_receipt` identifier returned by the directory. The provider's `public_contact_published_at` must already be non-null, or the request fails with `ERR_PUBLIC_CONTACT_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the provider must not already hold another designation in `REGISTERED`, or the request fails with `ERR_AGENT_ALREADY_REGISTERED`; and the supplied provider version must match, per Section 2.7. On success the transition stamps `registered_at` from the logical clock and sets `expires_at` to `registered_at` advanced by the registration term, which is fixed at 1095 calendar days — three years, and not configurable per provider. It then sets the provider's `designated_agent_registered` to true, increments the provider's `version` by 1, and lets that provider mutation recompute the compliance score, which may advance the posture out of `FORFEITED`. Registering from `DRAFT` or from `LAPSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGENT_STATE`.

Lapsing a registration moves it `REGISTERED → LAPSED` and may only be done once the logical clock has reached or passed `expires_at`; earlier attempts are refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_EXPIRED`, and lapsing anything that is not `REGISTERED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGENT_STATE`. When no other designation of the same provider remains in `REGISTERED`, the lapse clears the provider's `designated_agent_registered`, and that gate flip forces `safe_harbor_status` to `FORFEITED` and `safe_harbor_score` to 0 regardless of what the component score would otherwise have been. The provider's `version` increments by 1.

The third gate is the published repeat-infringer policy, and it doubles as the configuration record for the whole strike regime. Drafting a revision takes the `provider_id` and the `policy_text` that will be published as prose, plus seven optional numeric settings that all have defaults. `strike_expiry_days` — the age at which an accrued strike expires and stops counting — defaults to 365 and must be an integer from 30 through 3650. `decay_step_days`, the age step used by the decay curve, defaults to 90 and must be an integer from 30 through 365. `limitation_threshold_points` defaults to 20 and must be greater than zero and strictly less than `termination_threshold_points`, which defaults to 40 and must be strictly greater than the limitation threshold; a payload that violates that ordering is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `appeal_window_days`, the number of calendar days after a termination execution during which the subscriber may appeal, defaults to 14 and must be an integer from 1 through 90. `counter_notice_window_days`, the number of calendar days after a claim disables material during which a counter-notice is accepted, defaults to 30 and must be an integer from 1 through 180. `dispute_window_days`, the length of the duplicate-claim interval on a single content item, likewise defaults to 30 and must be an integer from 1 through 180. A drafted revision sits in `status` `DRAFT` with `published_at` null.

Publishing a revision moves it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`. The officer supplies `expected_provider_version`, and the revision's limitation threshold must still be strictly below its termination threshold. On success the revision's `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, **any other revision of the same provider that was in `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`**, the provider's `repeat_infringer_policy_published` is set true, the provider's `version` increments by 1, and the resulting provider mutation recomputes the compliance score and may advance the posture. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a revision. Critically, superseding a revision does **not** rewrite window values that were already snapshotted onto existing claims — a claim disabled under the old revision keeps the windows it captured. Publishing something that is already `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`.

Posture itself moves on six declared transitions, all of them driven by the recomputation triggers named in Section 2.10. `FORFEITED → ELIGIBLE` requires all three hard gates to pass and a compliance score of at least 85, and can be produced by publishing a policy revision, recording an agent registration, publishing the public contact, or running an audit; it sets `safe_harbor_score` to the recomputed score and increments the provider's `version` by 1. `FORFEITED → AT_RISK` requires all three gates to pass and a score from 60 through 84 inclusive, and arises from running an audit or publishing a policy revision, setting `safe_harbor_score` to the recomputed score. `ELIGIBLE → AT_RISK` and `AT_RISK → ELIGIBLE` are audit-only transitions, gated on the same score bands respectively — from 60 through 84 inclusive for the downgrade and at least 85 for the upgrade — and each writes the recomputed score into `safe_harbor_score`. Both `ELIGIBLE → FORFEITED` and `AT_RISK → FORFEITED` fire when a hard gate fails or the score falls below 60, and are produced either by lapsing an agent registration or by running an audit; in both cases `safe_harbor_score` is set to 0 when a hard gate has failed, and when the trigger was an audit the audit record additionally captures which gate failed.

### 3.2. Subscriber Accounts and the Material They Host

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a subscriber account against a `provider_id`, giving it a `handle` and an `email`. The handle must be unique within that provider; a collision is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_HANDLE_CONFLICT`. The `email` is the address to which takedown notifications are delivered. A new account starts with `standing` `ACTIVE`, `strike_points` 0, `upload_restricted` false, `notified_count` 0, `available_content_count` 0 and `terminated_at` null.

A `Subscriber` uploads material against its own `account_id`, supplying the `content_url` at which the material lives, a `title`, and a `fingerprint_hash` — a lowercase 32-character hexadecimal content fingerprint that will later be compared against rights holders' reference works. The URL must be unique across all accounts of the same provider; a collision is refused with `ERR_CONTENT_URL_CONFLICT`. A malformed fingerprint — anything other than exactly 32 characters from the lowercase hexadecimal alphabet — is refused as a malformed request. A newly uploaded item enters with `availability_status` `AVAILABLE`, `dispute_status` `NONE`, `disabled_at` and `restored_at` both null, and increments the owning account's `available_content_count` by 1. Uploading is refused outright when the account is `TERMINATED`, with `ERR_ACCOUNT_TERMINATED`, and refused when `upload_restricted` is true because the account's standing is `LIMITED`, with `ERR_ACCOUNT_UPLOAD_RESTRICTED`.

Purging material is the only route to the terminal `PURGED` access state, and it is available on two different footings. A `Subscriber` who owns the item may purge it from `AVAILABLE → PURGED` or from `RESTORED → PURGED`; either path decrements the account's `available_content_count` by 1, because the item had been counted as live. A `ComplianceOfficer` may additionally purge an item that is already `DISABLED → PURGED`, but only when no claim standing against that item is in `COUNTER_NOTICED` or `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` — the provider may not destroy material whose restoration is under active dispute. On that path `available_content_count` is left unchanged, because a disabled item was already excluded from the count. Purging an item that is already `PURGED`, or purging a disabled item that has a counter-noticed or restoration-scheduled claim against it, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTENT_STATE`. Purging touches only the access dimension; the item's `dispute_status` is left exactly as it was, which is how a purged item can carry `RESOLVED`.

Reads let each audience see what it needs. A single content item exposes both state dimensions and is readable by every role including a `RightsHolderRep`. An account's own record exposes its standing and point total; its content items may be filtered by either dimension; its strike records and its termination cases are listable oldest-first. There is in addition a computed strike-accounting read on an account that returns, under the pinned point variant, the `account_id`, the current `strike_points`, the `active_strike_count`, `decayed_strike_count`, `expired_strike_count` and `voided_strike_count` breakdown of its strike records, the `limitation_threshold_points` and `termination_threshold_points` currently in force from the published policy, and the account's `standing`.

### 3.3. Rights Holders, Program Tiers and Reference Files

A rights holder — or the authorised agent acting for one — is registered by a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `legal_name` and a `contact_email`, the latter being the address to which counter-notices will later be forwarded. Two optional allowances may be set at registration: `reference_file_quota`, the maximum number of the holder's reference works that may sit in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` at once, defaulting to 50 and constrained to an integer from 1 through 5000; and `daily_item_quota`, the maximum number of ingestion items accepted in any closed 24-hour window, defaulting to 500 and constrained to an integer from 1 through 100000. A new holder starts at `enrollment_status` `PROSPECT` and `program_tier` `STANDARD`, with `notices_processed`, `withdrawn_count`, `rejected_count`, `misrepresentation_count` and `abuse_score` all 0.

Enrolling the holder in the high-volume program moves `enrollment_status` `PROSPECT → ENROLLED`, and requires a non-empty `contact_email` and an `abuse_score` below 70; an abuse score of 70 or more is refused with `ERR_ABUSE_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`. The same operation also re-admits a suspended holder, `SUSPENDED → ENROLLED`, under the same abuse-score ceiling — but re-admission does not resurrect anything: reference files quarantined during the suspension stay `QUARANTINED` for ever and must be registered afresh to participate in matching again.

Promoting the holder walks `program_tier` up one step at a time, `STANDARD → VERIFIED` and then `VERIFIED → TRUSTED`. Both steps require `enrollment_status` to be `ENROLLED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_NOT_ENROLLED`, and an `abuse_score` below 40, refused otherwise with `ERR_ABUSE_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`. The step to `TRUSTED` carries one extra requirement: the holder must already have at least one reference file in `ACTIVE`, and a promotion attempted without one is refused with `ERR_NO_REFERENCE_FILE_ACTIVE`. Tier is not cosmetic. It sets the weight that future strike records from this holder will carry — 1.0 at `STANDARD`, 1.5 at `VERIFIED`, 2.0 at `TRUSTED` — and reaching `TRUSTED` additionally means every future notice from this holder is scored under the stricter of the two statutory compliance variants described in Section 3.5.

Suspending the holder moves `enrollment_status` `ENROLLED → SUSPENDED`, and either an `AbuseInvestigator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may do it. Suspension is a cascade, not a flag. Every reference file of the holder that sits in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → QUARANTINED`; files still in `PENDING` are left untouched. Each quarantined file then withdraws every non-terminal claim whose `reference_file_id` names it — a claim moves either `DISABLED → WITHDRAWN` or `COUNTER_NOTICED → WITHDRAWN` — which restores the affected content item unless it has been purged, voids the strike record that claim produced, and recomputes the owning account's point total, possibly returning that account's standing from `LIMITED` to `ACTIVE`. When the withdrawn claim had a counter-notice against it, that counter-notice moves to `SUPERSEDED`.

Reference works are registered against a `rights_holder_id` with a `work_title` and a `fingerprint_hash` in the same lowercase 32-character hexadecimal form used for hosted material, and optionally a `registration_number` — the copyright registration identifier, whose mere presence contributes a fixed bonus to match confidence. Either a `RightsHolderRep` acting for the holder or a `ComplianceOfficer` may register one. A new file enters `status` `PENDING` with `activated_at` null and `match_count` 0, and it counts against the allowance immediately: if registering it would push the count of the holder's files in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` above `reference_file_quota`, the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`.

Activating a file moves it `PENDING → ACTIVE`, requires the owning holder to be `ENROLLED`, and requires the stored `fingerprint_hash` to be 32 lowercase hexadecimal characters. Activation stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock and makes the file eligible for confidence-based selection. Activating a file that is already `ACTIVE`, or one that has been `QUARANTINED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE_FILE_STATE`; `QUARANTINED` is terminal.

### 3.4. Automated Ingestion for High-Volume Rights Holders

A `RightsHolderRep` opens an ingestion batch for its own holder, declaring the `rights_holder_id` and the `item_count` of ingestion items the batch will carry, which must be an integer from 1 through 10000. Three preconditions guard the operation, and their relative order is the one given in Section 2.6: the hosting provider's `safe_harbor_status` must be `ELIGIBLE`, refused otherwise with `ERR_SAFE_HARBOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; the holder must be `ENROLLED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_NOT_ENROLLED`; and the holder's `program_tier` must be `VERIFIED` or `TRUSTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_PROGRAM_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`.

The quota that governs automated ingestion is a **closed 24-hour sliding window**, and its arithmetic is worth stating precisely. Take every ingestion batch of the same rights holder whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, `PROCESSING` or `COMPLETED` and whose `accepted_at` falls within the interval that begins 24 hours before the logical clock and ends at the logical clock, both endpoints included — a batch accepted exactly 24 hours before the current instant *is* counted, and batches in `REJECTED` are excluded entirely. Sum their `item_count` values, add the `item_count` requested now, and store the result as the new batch's `rolling_item_total`. The request is refused with `ERR_INGESTION_WINDOW_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` when that rolling total is **strictly greater than** the holder's `daily_item_quota`; a rolling total exactly equal to the quota is accepted, so the quota value itself is attainable. An accepted batch enters `status` `ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at` stamped from the logical clock — that stamp is what anchors the batch inside future windows — and with `notices_created`, `matched_count` and `unmatched_count` all 0.

A batch moves `ACCEPTED → PROCESSING` automatically the first time a notice is created carrying its identifier, and each such notice increments the batch's `notices_created` by 1. The rep closes the batch with a completion action that moves it `PROCESSING → COMPLETED`, permitted only once every notice belonging to the batch has reached `PROCESSED`, `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `CLOSED`; completion then aggregates the batch's outcome, summing each notice's `matched_claim_count` into `matched_count` and each notice's `unmatched_url_count` into `unmatched_count`. A completion attempted on a batch that is not `PROCESSING`, or on one whose notices have not all settled, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`.

An empty batch may be discarded instead. A `ComplianceOfficer` may move a batch `ACCEPTED → REJECTED` provided `notices_created` is still 0; rejecting a batch that already produced notices is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. Rejection matters commercially as well as administratively: a rejected batch's `item_count` leaves the rolling window, so the holder recovers that allowance for subsequent batches. Both `COMPLETED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

### 3.5. Notice Intake and Statutory Element Validation

A takedown notice may be lodged either by the `RightsHolderRep` acting for the complaining party or by a `TakedownAgent` transcribing one received off-channel. Intake takes the `rights_holder_id` of the complaining party, the `submission_channel` — one of `WEB_FORM`, `EMAIL` or `API_BULK` — and `claimed_urls`, an ordered list of between 1 and 25 non-empty allegedly-infringing URLs whose order will later determine the order in which claims are created. An `ingestion_batch_id` may be supplied for automated bulk submissions and is null otherwise; a notice that carries one requires the holder's `program_tier` to be `VERIFIED` or `TRUSTED`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`.

The statutory elements themselves arrive as eight further optional fields, each defaulting to the absent value. `signature_present` is the physical or electronic signature of the complaining party and defaults to false. `work_identification` identifies the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed and defaults to null. `material_identification` identifies the material to be removed and defaults to null. `contact_email` is the complaining party's contact information and defaults to null. `good_faith_statement` is the statement of good-faith belief that the use is not authorised and defaults to false. `accuracy_statement` asserts that the information in the notice is accurate and defaults to false. `authority_statement` asserts authority to act on behalf of the owner and defaults to false. Finally `sworn_under_penalty` records whether the accuracy-and-authority element was sworn under penalty of perjury, defaults to false, and — as explained below — carries no score of its own. A newly received notice sits in `status` `RECEIVED` with `completeness_score` 0, `compliance_variant` `SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLIANCE`, `rejection_reason` null, `matched_claim_count` and `unmatched_url_count` both 0, and `validated_at` and `processed_at` null. Creating the notice also nudges any referenced ingestion batch from `ACCEPTED` to `PROCESSING`.

A `TakedownAgent` then validates the notice, and this single operation branches into two mutually exclusive outcomes with disjoint side-effect sets. Before either branch is reached, the provider must hold at least one designated agent in `REGISTERED` — a provider that has not completed its own registration may not act on complaints at all — and a notice that is not in `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`.

**Scoring.** An element counts as present when a boolean element is true, or when a string element is non-null and still non-empty after trimming surrounding whitespace. Seven elements carry weight, and their weights sum to exactly 100: the signature contributes 8, work identification 18, material identification 20, the contact address 10, the good-faith statement 18, the accuracy statement 8, and the authority statement 18. The `completeness_score` is the sum of the weights of the elements that are present, and is therefore an integer from 0 through 100.

**Variant selection.** The applicable variant is written to `compliance_variant`. It is `STRICT_COMPLIANCE` when the `submission_channel` is `API_BULK` or the complaining holder's `program_tier` is `TRUSTED`; in every other case — a web-form or email submission from a holder at `STANDARD` or `VERIFIED` — it is `SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLIANCE`. Automated bulk channels and the most privileged tier are held to the higher standard precisely because they enjoy the greatest throughput.

**Thresholds and the core gate, in that order.** The evaluation order is pinned: the score is computed first, the variant's threshold is applied second, and the non-waivable core-element gate is applied third. Under `SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLIANCE` the notice clears the threshold when `completeness_score` is at least 92, and `sworn_under_penalty` is ignored entirely — so a notice whose only missing element is the accuracy statement scores exactly 92, meets the threshold, and passes that stage. Under `STRICT_COMPLIANCE` the notice clears the threshold only when `completeness_score` is at least 100 **and** `sworn_under_penalty` is true; the very same payload that clears the substantial threshold at 92 does not clear the strict one. Independently of the score, the core gate walks these five elements in this fixed order — the signature, work identification, material identification, the good-faith statement, and the authority statement — and identifies the first one that is absent. A notice passes validation only when it cleared its variant's threshold **and** no core element is missing; because the gate is applied last, a notice can satisfy its threshold and still be rejected.

**The validated branch.** Moving `RECEIVED → VALIDATED` writes `completeness_score`, `compliance_variant` and `validated_at`, the last taken from the logical clock. Nothing else changes: no claim exists yet and no access has been disabled.

**The rejected branch.** Moving `RECEIVED → REJECTED` writes `completeness_score` and `compliance_variant` just as the validated branch does, and additionally writes `rejection_reason`, whose value is determined in the same pinned order as the checks. When a core element was missing, the reason is the literal text `CORE_ELEMENT_MISSING:` immediately followed by the name of that first missing core element. When no core element was missing, the variant is `STRICT_COMPLIANCE` and `sworn_under_penalty` is false, the reason is `UNSWORN_UNDER_STRICT_VARIANT`. In every remaining case the reason is `SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. On this branch no claim is created and no access is disabled — but the rejection is held against the complainant: the holder's `rejected_count` increments by 1 and the abuse score of Section 3.9 is recomputed, and if that recomputed score reaches the auto-open floor the system opens a misrepresentation case against the holder with `opened_by_user_id` null, provided an eligible claim exists to attach it to. `REJECTED` is terminal for a notice.

A notice may also be withdrawn by the `RightsHolderRep` that owns it, and the withdrawal behaves differently depending on how far the notice had progressed. From `VALIDATED → WITHDRAWN` there is nothing to unwind: the holder's `withdrawn_count` increments by 1 and its abuse score is recomputed. From `PROCESSED → WITHDRAWN` the withdrawal is fully compensating, and is only permitted while **no** child claim has been upheld. Every non-terminal child claim moves either `DISABLED → WITHDRAWN` or `COUNTER_NOTICED → WITHDRAWN`, and a claim withdrawn from `COUNTER_NOTICED` additionally moves its counter-notice to `SUPERSEDED`; each withdrawn claim restores its content item — `availability_status` to `RESTORED` and `dispute_status` to `RESOLVED` — unless that item has been purged, in which case the item is left alone; each restoration increments the owning account's `available_content_count` back by 1; each withdrawn claim voids its strike record, which recomputes the account's `strike_points` and may return that account's standing from `LIMITED` to `ACTIVE`; and finally `withdrawn_count` increments by 1 with the abuse score recomputed. Withdrawing a notice that is already `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`, as is validating a notice that is not `RECEIVED` or processing one that is not `VALIDATED`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

### 3.6. Expeditious Disabling, Claim Creation and Reference Matching

Processing a validated notice is the moment the provider acts, and it is the single most consequential operation in the API. A `TakedownAgent` drives the notice `VALIDATED → PROCESSED`. Two preconditions are checked before any claim is created: the owning provider must hold a policy revision in `PUBLISHED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_REPEAT_INFRINGER_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`; and at least one entry in `claimed_urls` must resolve to a content item whose `availability_status` is `AVAILABLE` or `RESTORED` and whose dispute interval is free.

Resolution matches each entry of `claimed_urls`, in the order the complainant listed them, against the `content_url` of hosted material. An entry that resolves to live material and clears the interval check produces one claim; an entry that resolves to nothing live produces none. If no entry resolves to material in `AVAILABLE` or `RESTORED` at all, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIONABLE_CLAIMS` (HTTP 422). If entries do resolve to live material but every single one of them sits inside an occupied dispute interval, the request is refused earlier, with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_WINDOW`.

**The duplicate-claim interval.** The interval exists to stop a complainant re-disabling the same material over and over. For each existing claim, the occupied interval begins at that claim's `disabled_at` and extends forward by that claim's snapshotted `dispute_window_days`, with the lower bound included and the upper bound **excluded**. The claims considered are those sharing the same content item and the same complaining rights holder — the pair formed by `content_item_id` and `rights_holder_id` — whose `status` is `DISABLED`, `COUNTER_NOTICED`, `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED`, `UPHELD` or `RESTORED`. A claim that reached `RESTORED` deliberately keeps occupying its window as an anti-harassment cooldown; only `WITHDRAWN` releases the interval early. A new claim is refused when the logical clock at processing time falls inside any such interval; because the upper bound is excluded, a clock instant exactly equal to a prior claim's `disabled_at` advanced by its `dispute_window_days` lies outside the interval and the new claim is created.

**Claim creation.** Each resolved URL produces one claim, created in `claimed_urls` order. The claim records the `notice_id` of the notice that produced it, the `content_item_id` of the material it targets, a denormalised `rights_holder_id` for the complainant — which together with the content item forms the interval key just described — and the `matched_url`, which is the `claimed_urls` entry this claim resolved. A claim's `status` begins at `MATCHED`, but that value is transient: the same request that creates the claim disables it, so `MATCHED → DISABLED` completes before the response is returned. The disabling transition snapshots `dispute_window_days` and `counter_notice_window_days` from the provider's currently `PUBLISHED` policy revision onto the claim itself — a later policy revision cannot retroactively lengthen or shorten a window already in flight — and stamps the claim's `disabled_at` from the logical clock, leaving `restoration_due_at` null until a counter-notice is forwarded.

**Reference-file matching.** For each resolved entry the system also tries to attribute the claim to one of the complainant's registered reference works. Only reference files of the notice's own rights holder whose `status` is `ACTIVE` participate; `PENDING` and `QUARANTINED` files are excluded. For each candidate, count the length of the longest common leading run of characters shared by the content item's `fingerprint_hash` and the reference file's `fingerprint_hash`, both being 32 characters long. Divide that length by 32, multiply by 0.80, and add 0.20 when the reference file has a non-null `registration_number` and nothing when it does not; round the result half up to four decimal places to obtain that candidate's confidence. The acceptance floor depends on the holder's `program_tier`: a `TRUSTED` holder needs a confidence of at least 0.90, a `VERIFIED` holder at least 0.95, and a `STANDARD` holder a full 1.0 — the more trusted the holder, the more tolerance its automated matching is granted. Candidates that reach their floor are ranked by confidence descending and then, for equal confidences, by `created_at` ascending; `id` is never consulted. The first candidate after ranking is selected, its identifier is written to the claim's `reference_file_id`, its confidence is written to the claim's `match_confidence`, and that file's `match_count` increments. When no candidate reaches the floor — or the holder has no active reference file at all — the claim is still created on the strength of the URL match alone, with both `reference_file_id` and `match_confidence` left null.

**The cascade on hosted material and its owner.** Each new claim disables its content item. The item's `availability_status` moves `AVAILABLE → DISABLED`, or `RESTORED → DISABLED` when a later notice reaches material that had previously been put back — on that second path the item's `disabled_at` is overwritten with the current clock. Either way the owning account's `available_content_count` decrements by 1. In parallel the item's dispute dimension advances `NONE → UNDER_NOTICE`, or `RESOLVED → UNDER_NOTICE` when a later notice produces a new claim against material whose earlier dispute had already been settled; both paths notify the subscriber at the account's `email` and increment the account's `notified_count` by 1.

**Strike accrual.** Each disabling also accrues one strike record against the owning account. The record captures the `account_id` of the struck subscriber and the `claim_id` of the claim that produced it; `weight` is snapshotted from the complaining holder's `program_tier` at the moment of accrual — 1.0 for `STANDARD`, 1.5 for `VERIFIED`, 2.0 for `TRUSTED` — and `base_points` is the base of 10 points multiplied by that weight. `effective_points` starts equal to `base_points`, because a strike's age is zero at accrual and the decay curve's first factor is 1. The `reason` is `NOTICE_PROCESSED`, `status` is `ACTIVE`, `accrued_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `expires_at` is `accrued_at` advanced by the published policy's `strike_expiry_days`. Every accrual recomputes the account's `strike_points` by the rule in Section 3.7, which may move standing `ACTIVE → LIMITED`, and a recomputed total that also reaches `termination_threshold_points` opens a termination case for that account.

**Finalising the notice.** Processing writes `matched_claim_count`, the number of `claimed_urls` entries that matched live material, and `unmatched_url_count`, the number that matched none, then stamps `processed_at` from the logical clock — that stamp is the endpoint of the response-latency measurement used by the safe-harbour audit. The complainant's `notices_processed` increments by 1. Finally, a processed notice **auto-closes**: as soon as `matched_claim_count` is at least 1 and every one of the notice's child claims has reached one of the terminal claim states `RESTORED`, `UPHELD` or `WITHDRAWN`, the notice transitions `PROCESSED → CLOSED` of its own accord, and no further claim may ever be created against it. Because claims created by cascade are not returned inline anywhere, the claim list — filtered by `notice_id`, `content_item_id`, `rights_holder_id`, `reference_file_id` or `status` — is the discovery channel for them, and the strike records produced by a single claim are listable from that claim.

### 3.7. Counter-Notices, Forwarding and Restoration Timing

A `Subscriber` disputes a disabling by lodging a counter-notice against the specific claim that disabled its material, supplying the `claim_id`, the `expected_claim_version` required by Section 2.7, and the `subscriber_name` of the person submitting it. Five statutory elements follow, all optional at the payload level and all defaulting to the absent value: `contact_email` for the subscriber's contact information, `material_identification` identifying the removed material and its former location, `signature_present` for the subscriber's physical or electronic signature, `good_faith_mistake_statement` for the belief that the material was removed by mistake or misidentification, and the two consents — `consent_to_jurisdiction`, consenting to the jurisdiction of the federal district court, and `consent_to_service_of_process`, consenting to accept service of process from the complainant. The system copies `account_id` from the owner of the claim's content item rather than trusting the payload. A newly received counter-notice sits in `status` `RECEIVED` with `completeness_score` 0, `rejection_reason` null, `superseded_by_counter_notice_id` null, and `forwarded_at`, `restoration_earliest_at`, `restoration_deadline_at` and `suit_notice_at` all null.

Two gates guard receipt. The counter-notice must arrive no later than the claim's `disabled_at` advanced by the claim's snapshotted `counter_notice_window_days`; a later submission is refused with `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And the acting account must not be `TERMINATED`, which is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_TERMINATED`.

Receipt also implements **supersession**. Creating a counter-notice increments the target claim's `version` by 1. If an earlier counter-notice on the same claim is still in `RECEIVED`, that earlier record moves `RECEIVED → SUPERSEDED` and its `superseded_by_counter_notice_id` is set to the identifier of the new one. If the earlier counter-notice had already reached `VALIDATED`, it moves `VALIDATED → SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_counter_notice_id` is set the same way, **and** the claim is returned from `COUNTER_NOTICED` to `DISABLED` so that the newer counter-notice can itself be validated. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

Validation of a counter-notice is again a two-way branch driven by a weighted score with a gate the score cannot compensate for. Six elements carry weight, summing to 100: the signature contributes 20, material identification 20, the good-faith-mistake statement 22, the contact address 26, consent to jurisdiction 6, and consent to service of process 6. Presence is judged exactly as for a notice — a boolean that is true, or a string that is non-null and non-empty after trimming. The `completeness_score` is the sum of the weights of the present elements, an integer from 0 through 100, and the acceptance threshold is 94, inclusive. Separately, the two consents are **non-waivable**: the gate walks `consent_to_jurisdiction` and then `consent_to_service_of_process` and identifies the first one absent. A counter-notice passes only when `completeness_score` is at least 94 and neither consent is missing — which means a counter-notice that omits only consent to jurisdiction scores 94, meets the threshold, and is nonetheless rejected.

On the passing branch the counter-notice moves `RECEIVED → VALIDATED`, and the cascade reaches two other records: the claim moves `DISABLED → COUNTER_NOTICED` and the content item's dispute dimension moves `UNDER_NOTICE → COUNTER_NOTICED`. Access is not yet restored. On the failing branch the counter-notice moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED` and writes `rejection_reason` — the literal text `CONSENT_MISSING:` immediately followed by the name of the first absent consent when a consent was missing, and `SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` otherwise. The claim stays `DISABLED` and access is not restored. `REJECTED` is terminal. Validating a counter-notice that is not in `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COUNTER_NOTICE_STATE`.

Forwarding the counter-notice to the complainant moves it `VALIDATED → FORWARDED` and opens the restoration window. The complaining rights holder must have a non-empty `contact_email` for the forward to be possible. The transition stamps `forwarded_at` from the logical clock and computes the two window bounds in **business days**. A day is a business day when it falls Monday through Friday and its UTC date is not one of the provider's fixed holidays, which are 7 September 2026, 26 and 27 November 2026, 25 December 2026 and 1 January 2027. Counting advances one calendar day at a time from the anchor instant, preserving the anchor's UTC time-of-day exactly, and increments the count only on days that qualify as business days; **the anchor instant itself never counts, even when it falls on a business day**. `restoration_earliest_at` is `forwarded_at` advanced by 10 business days and `restoration_deadline_at` is `forwarded_at` advanced by 14 business days. Forwarding also moves the claim `COUNTER_NOTICED → RESTORATION_SCHEDULED`, copying `restoration_earliest_at` onto the claim's `restoration_due_at`, and moves the content item's dispute dimension `COUNTER_NOTICED → RESTORATION_PENDING`. Forwarding anything that is not `VALIDATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COUNTER_NOTICE_STATE`.

Once the logical clock reaches or passes `restoration_earliest_at`, the counter-notice itself moves `FORWARDED → RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` as the clock advances: restoration has become mandatory rather than merely permitted, and the safe-harbour audit begins counting it as overdue as soon as the clock passes `restoration_deadline_at`.

Restoring access is performed by a `TakedownAgent` on a claim that is in `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED`; a claim in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The logical clock must be at or after the claim's `restoration_due_at`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_RESTORATION_TOO_EARLY`. The governing counter-notice must not be `BLOCKED_BY_SUIT`, and an attempt while it is blocked is refused with `ERR_RESTORATION_BLOCKED_BY_SUIT`. On success the claim moves `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED → RESTORED` and the governing counter-notice moves `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED → RESTORED` with it; the content item's access dimension moves `DISABLED → RESTORED` with `restored_at` stamped from the logical clock and the owning account's `available_content_count` incremented by 1; the item's dispute dimension moves `RESTORATION_PENDING → RESOLVED`; the strike record this claim produced is voided, which recomputes the account's `strike_points`; and if every sibling claim of the same notice has now reached a terminal state, the notice auto-closes. Both `RESTORED` states are terminal.

The complainant's escape hatch is to file suit. Recording the complainant's notice of court action is permitted while the counter-notice is `FORWARDED` or `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` and the logical clock is **strictly before** `restoration_deadline_at`; at or after that instant the record is refused with `ERR_SUIT_NOTICE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and a counter-notice in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COUNTER_NOTICE_STATE`. Either path stamps `suit_notice_at` from the logical clock and moves the counter-notice `FORWARDED → BLOCKED_BY_SUIT` or `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED → BLOCKED_BY_SUIT` respectively; `BLOCKED_BY_SUIT` is terminal, and access stays `DISABLED` throughout. Beyond that, the two paths diverge sharply and both must be implemented.

- When the suit notice arrives while the counter-notice is still `FORWARDED` — that is, before the restoration window has opened — it moves `FORWARDED → BLOCKED_BY_SUIT` and the dispute is finished on the spot. The claim moves `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED → UPHELD`, its `restoration_due_at` is cleared, the content item stays `DISABLED`, and the item's dispute dimension moves `RESTORATION_PENDING → RESOLVED`. No second strike accrues on this path and the existing strike is not voided. When every sibling claim of the notice is terminal, the notice auto-closes.
- When the suit notice arrives after the counter-notice has already moved to `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` — that is, after the restoration window has opened — it moves `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED → BLOCKED_BY_SUIT` and the claim **stays** `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` and the item's dispute dimension stays `RESTORATION_PENDING`. Restoration is blocked rather than cancelled, which is exactly why an attempt to restore that claim now answers `ERR_RESTORATION_BLOCKED_BY_SUIT` at rung 15 of the ladder rather than reporting an illegal state. The claim is finalised separately, by upholding it, which is the only operation that can move a `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` claim once its counter-notice is `BLOCKED_BY_SUIT`.

Upholding a claim likewise has two paths with deliberately different consequences, and both are reachable through the same `TakedownAgent` action.

- From `DISABLED → UPHELD`, the ordinary outcome when no valid dispute ever arrived: the logical clock must be at or after the claim's `disabled_at` advanced by its snapshotted `counter_notice_window_days`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_WINDOW_OPEN`. No counter-notice on the claim may be in `VALIDATED`, `FORWARDED` or `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED`. The content item's dispute dimension moves `UNDER_NOTICE → RESOLVED` while access stays `DISABLED`, and **a second strike record accrues** against the account with `reason` `CLAIM_UPHELD`, recomputing the account's `strike_points`.
- From `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED → UPHELD`, the finalisation path for a claim whose counter-notice is already `BLOCKED_BY_SUIT`: here the counter-notice-window rule does **not** apply and the claim may be upheld at once. The claim's `restoration_due_at` is cleared, the content item stays `DISABLED`, and the item's dispute dimension moves `RESTORATION_PENDING → RESOLVED`. **No second strike accrues on this path, and the first strike is never voided** — the complainant's court filing neither adds to nor subtracts from the subscriber's record.

Either path auto-closes the notice once every sibling claim is terminal, and an uphold attempted on a claim that is neither `DISABLED` nor `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED`-with-a-blocked-counter-notice is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. `UPHELD` is terminal for a claim, as are `RESTORED` and `WITHDRAWN`.

### 3.8. Strike Accounting, Termination and Appeal

An account's `strike_points` is not a stored sum but a decayed recomputation, and its evaluation order is pinned. Only strike records whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `DECAYED` are included; those in `EXPIRED` or `VOIDED` are excluded entirely. For each included strike, compute its age as the number of whole days from its `accrued_at` to the logical clock, discarding any fractional day. Divide that age in days by the published policy's `decay_step_days`, again discarding the fraction, to obtain the strike's decay step. The decay factor is 1.0 at step 0, 0.65 at step 1, and 0.325 at step 2; at any step beyond 2 the factor is 0.0. The strike's contribution is the base of 10 points multiplied by the strike's snapshotted `weight` and then by its decay factor, **rounded half up to two decimal places for that strike alone**. Taking the included strikes in ascending `accrued_at` order, add those per-strike contributions together and round the total half up to two decimal places once more. Rounding per strike before summing is the pinned reading; summing the unrounded products and rounding only once is wrong and will produce a different cent value once several strikes share a fractional factor.

Strike records move under the clock. `ACTIVE → DECAYED` fires when the gap between the logical clock and the strike's `accrued_at` is at least `decay_step_days` and still less than `strike_expiry_days`; the transition recomputes that strike's `effective_points` with the applicable decay factor and recomputes the account's `strike_points`. Both `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and `DECAYED → EXPIRED` fire when the clock reaches or passes the strike's `expires_at`, setting `effective_points` to 0 and recomputing the account's total. Both `ACTIVE → VOIDED` and `DECAYED → VOIDED` fire when the originating claim left `DISABLED` without being upheld — through notice withdrawal or through restoration — or when a termination appeal is decided in the subscriber's favour; voiding likewise sets `effective_points` to 0 and recomputes the total. `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

Standing follows the recomputed total. `ACTIVE → LIMITED` fires as soon as a recomputation puts `strike_points` at or above the published policy's `limitation_threshold_points`, and sets `upload_restricted` to true, which is what blocks further uploads from that account. If the same recomputation also puts the total at or above `termination_threshold_points`, the system opens exactly one termination case for the account, recording the observed total as the case's `trigger_points`, leaving `opened_by_user_id` null because the system rather than a person opened it, and setting `status` to `OPEN`; the step is skipped when the account already has a case in `OPEN` or `EXECUTED`. Standing remains `LIMITED` until that case is actually executed. The reverse transition `LIMITED → ACTIVE` fires whenever a recomputation — after decay, expiry or voiding — puts `strike_points` strictly below `limitation_threshold_points`, and clears `upload_restricted`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` executes the termination, moving the case `OPEN → EXECUTED` and the account `LIMITED → TERMINATED`. Two preconditions apply beyond the case being `OPEN` and belonging to the account: the account's `strike_points` must **still** be at or above `termination_threshold_points` at the moment of execution, so a case whose strikes have decayed in the meantime cannot be executed; and the provider must hold a policy revision in `PUBLISHED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_REPEAT_INFRINGER_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Execution records the acting `X-User-ID` as the case's `executed_by_user_id`, stamps `executed_at` from the logical clock, and sets `appeal_deadline_at` to `executed_at` advanced by the policy's `appeal_window_days`. On the account, `terminated_at` is stamped and `upload_restricted` set true, and **every** content item of the account whose `availability_status` is `AVAILABLE` or `RESTORED` moves to `DISABLED`, each such disabling stamping the item's `disabled_at` and decrementing `available_content_count`. The account's `strike_points` is recomputed. Executing a case that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TERMINATION_STATE`.

The `Subscriber` that owns the account may then appeal, moving the case `EXECUTED → APPEAL_FILED` and stamping `appeal_filed_at` from the logical clock. The clock must be **strictly before** `appeal_deadline_at`; at or after that instant the filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing against a case that is not `EXECUTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TERMINATION_STATE`.

A different `ComplianceOfficer` decides the appeal, supplying `decision` as either `UPHELD` or `VACATED`. Only a case in `APPEAL_FILED` may be decided, and the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `executed_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either outcome records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision`. A `VACATED` decision moves the case `APPEAL_FILED → VACATED` and unwinds the termination completely: every strike record of the account that is `ACTIVE` or `DECAYED` is voided, which recomputes `strike_points` to 0, so the account returns `TERMINATED → ACTIVE` with `terminated_at` cleared and `upload_restricted` false — and because the total is 0 rather than merely reduced, standing settles at `ACTIVE` instead of re-entering `LIMITED`. An `UPHELD` decision moves the case `APPEAL_FILED → CLOSED`, leaves the account `TERMINATED`, and touches no strike record. Both `VACATED` and `CLOSED` are terminal.

The appeal route can also simply run out. As the clock advances, a case moves `EXECUTED → CLOSED` when the logical clock is **strictly after** `appeal_deadline_at` and no appeal was filed. The boundary is deliberately asymmetric: at exactly `appeal_deadline_at` the case is still `EXECUTED`, even though a filing at that same instant already answers `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Once the case is `CLOSED`, both filing and deciding are refused for ever after with `ERR_INVALID_TERMINATION_STATE`.

That leaves one route back for a terminated subscriber whose appeal window lapsed: a `ComplianceOfficer` may reinstate the account. Reinstatement moves the account `TERMINATED → ACTIVE`, clearing `terminated_at` and `upload_restricted`, but only when the account is genuinely `TERMINATED` **and** its recomputed `strike_points` is strictly below `limitation_threshold_points` — which in practice only happens once the strikes have decayed or expired on their own. An account that is not `TERMINATED`, or one whose recomputed total is still at or above the limitation threshold, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ACCOUNT_STANDING`. The termination case itself is left in `EXECUTED` or `CLOSED` and is never reopened by a reinstatement.

### 3.9. Abuse Detection, Misrepresentation Penalties and the Safe-Harbour Audit

The system watches complainants as carefully as it watches subscribers. A rights holder's `abuse_score` measures how much of its notice traffic ends badly, relative to its own volume. Multiply `withdrawn_count` by 1, `rejected_count` by 2 and `misrepresentation_count` by 3, and add those three products together. Divide the sum by the greater of `notices_processed` and a volume floor of 5 — the floor is what stops a holder with two notices and one withdrawal from registering an implausibly extreme figure — multiply by 100, and round half up to two decimal places. The score is recomputed on exactly three events: a notice rejection, a notice withdrawal, and a misrepresentation substantiation.

Two floors act on that score, and both comparisons are inclusive. At **40 or more**, the system opens one misrepresentation case against the holder on its own initiative, selecting the holder's most recent claim whose `status` is `DISABLED`, `COUNTER_NOTICED`, `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` or `UPHELD`, ranked by `disabled_at` descending and then by `created_at` descending, and setting `opened_by_user_id` to null to mark the case as system-opened. If the holder has no eligible claim, no case is opened. The step is also skipped when an `OPEN` case already exists for that same holder-and-claim pair. The same figure of 40 is the promotion ceiling of Section 3.3. At **70 or more**, a substantiation additionally suspends the holder and steps its `program_tier` down one level, and 70 is likewise the enrollment ceiling.

An `AbuseInvestigator` may also open a case directly, supplying the `rights_holder_id` accused of knowing misrepresentation, the `claim_id` of the claim alleged to be a fraudulent takedown, and an `allegation` stating the case. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `opened_by_user_id` and the case enters `status` `OPEN` with `substantiated_by_user_id` null, `affected_claim_count`, `disabled_day_count`, `base_penalty_cents`, `surcharge_cents` and `total_penalty_cents` all 0, and `substantiated_at` null. At most one `OPEN` case may exist per holder-and-claim pair; a second is refused with `ERR_MISREPRESENTATION_CASE_EXISTS`.

Substantiating a case moves it `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED` and is where the penalty is assessed. A second investigator must do it: when `opened_by_user_id` is non-null, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from it, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; a system-opened case with a null `opened_by_user_id` may be substantiated by any investigator. The referenced claim must not be `UPHELD` — a claim the provider itself upheld cannot simultaneously be a fraudulent takedown — and a substantiation against an upheld claim, or against a case that is not `OPEN`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MISREPRESENTATION_STATE`. Substantiation records `substantiated_by_user_id` and stamps `substantiated_at` from the logical clock.

**The penalty.** The penalty base is drawn from the whole notice, not just the one claim named in the case: the input set is every claim of the same takedown notice as the case's claim whose `status` is `DISABLED`, `COUNTER_NOTICED`, `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED`, `RESTORED` or `UPHELD`. `affected_claim_count` is the number of claims in that set. `disabled_day_count` is obtained by taking, for each of those claims, the number of whole days from the claim's `disabled_at` to the moment its content item was restored — or to the logical clock when the item has no restoration instant recorded — discarding any fractional day, and adding those day counts together. `base_penalty_cents` is 25000 cents for each affected claim plus 1500 cents for each disabled day. The surcharge tier is then selected **from the base amount** and never re-selected afterwards: the rate is 0.20 when `base_penalty_cents` is at least 500000, otherwise 0.10 when it is at least 100000, and otherwise zero. `surcharge_cents` is the base multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `total_penalty_cents` is `base_penalty_cents` plus `surcharge_cents`. Re-deriving the tier from the total after the surcharge has been added is the rejected reading and will over-assess any case that straddles a tier floor.

**The substantiation cascade.** Substantiation increments the holder's `misrepresentation_count` by 1, which recomputes `abuse_score`. When that recomputed score is 70 or more, the holder's `program_tier` steps down one level — `TRUSTED → VERIFIED`, or `VERIFIED → STANDARD` — and its `enrollment_status` moves `ENROLLED → SUSPENDED`. Existing strike records keep the `weight` that was snapshotted at accrual; only future strikes use the reduced weight. When the tier falls from `VERIFIED` to `STANDARD`, future notices from the holder revert to `SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLIANCE` scoring unless the submission channel is `API_BULK`, which forces the strict variant regardless of tier. The suspension in turn quarantines every reference file of the holder that is `ACTIVE`, and each quarantined file withdraws the non-terminal claims that had selected it, restoring their content items unless purged, voiding their strike records, and recomputing the affected accounts' `strike_points` — which may return those accounts' standing from `LIMITED` to `ACTIVE`. Dismissing a case instead moves it `OPEN → DISMISSED` under the same segregation-of-duties rule, records no penalty, and leaves `abuse_score` untouched. Both `SUBSTANTIATED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal.

**The safe-harbour audit.** A `ComplianceOfficer` runs an audit against a `provider_id`, and the audit writes an immutable record of what it observed. Before it measures anything, the audit recomputes `account_strike_points` for every account of the provider, so that the repeat-infringer measurement below is taken against fully decayed totals rather than stale ones. The three hard gates of Section 2.10 are then evaluated in their fixed order; when one fails, `compliance_score` is recorded as 0, `resulting_status` as `FORFEITED`, `gate_failure_reason` as the name of the failed gate — `AGENT_NOT_REGISTERED`, `POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED` or `CONTACT_NOT_PUBLISHED` — and the four component fields `response_component`, `restoration_component`, `termination_component` and `contact_component` are all recorded as 0. When no gate fails, `gate_failure_reason` is null and the four components are computed as follows.

`median_response_hours` measures how expeditiously the provider acted. Take every takedown notice of the provider whose `status` is `PROCESSED` or `CLOSED`; for each, express the elapsed time from its `created_at` to its `processed_at` in hours and round half up to two decimal places; sort those latencies ascending. When there are none, the median is 0. When the count is odd, the median is the middle value. When the count is even, the median is the mean of the two middle values, rounded half up to two decimal places. `response_component` is then computed from a 48-hour target: subtract `median_response_hours` from 48, divide by 48, clamp the ratio so that it is never below 0 and never above 1, multiply the clamped ratio by a weight of 40, and round half up to two decimal places. A provider that processes notices instantly earns the full 40; one whose median reaches or exceeds 48 hours earns nothing.

`overdue_restoration_count` counts the counter-notices of the provider whose `status` is `FORWARDED` or `RESTORATION_SCHEDULED` and whose `restoration_deadline_at` is at or before the logical clock — restorations the provider owed and has not delivered, whose claims by construction have not reached `RESTORED`. `restoration_component` starts from a weight of 30 and deducts 6 for each overdue restoration, with a floor of 0: five overdue restorations exhaust the component entirely.

`termination_compliance` is true when no account of the provider holds `strike_points` at or above the applicable `termination_threshold_points` without a termination case in `OPEN` or `EXECUTED` against it — in other words, when the provider has not let a repeat infringer accumulate past the threshold unaddressed. `termination_component` is 20 when that boolean is true and 0 otherwise. `contact_published` is true when the provider's `public_contact_published_at` is non-null, and `contact_component` is 10 when true and 0 otherwise.

`compliance_score` is the sum of the four components, rounded half up to a whole number, and therefore lands between 0 and 100. `resulting_status` is `ELIGIBLE` when that score is at least 85, `AT_RISK` when it is at least 60 but below 85, and `FORFEITED` below 60. The audit then writes `resulting_status` onto the provider's `safe_harbor_status` and the score onto the provider's `safe_harbor_score`, sets the provider's `last_audit_at` to the audit's own `created_at`, and increments the provider's `version` by 1. The audit record itself is never modified afterwards — it carries `id` and `created_at` and nothing else from the common property set — and a provider's audit history is listable, oldest first, so that the posture trail can be reconstructed after the fact.
