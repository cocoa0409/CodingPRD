# Accreditation Body Assessor Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A national accreditation body does not certify products or laboratories itself; it accredits the conformity assessment bodies that do. That work is unusual in that the accreditation body must be simultaneously a rule-maker, a recruiter of scarce technical talent, an auditor, a judge, and a signatory to international mutual-recognition arrangements — and it must keep those functions visibly separated from one another. The programme described here exists because those functions have historically been run on spreadsheets and mailboxes: scheme rules drift between revisions with no record of which revision an in-flight assessment was actually judged against, assessors are assigned to organizations they used to work for, the same person who accepted a corrective action later declares it effective, and the panel that grants accreditation includes the assessor who wrote the findings.

This document specifies the API that closes those gaps. Every accreditation programme is expressed as a versioned **scheme** whose rule set — witnessing floor, competence threshold, closure windows, surveillance cadence — is published, revised and re-published under an explicit revision counter, and every assessment cycle **pins** the revision it was opened against so that a later rule change can never silently re-write the obligations of work already under way. Assessors accumulate competence points from evidence that is first self-declared and then independently verified, and they disclose their relationships with applicant organizations in an append-only declaration log from which cooling-off periods are computed rather than remembered. Assessment work is planned as a day budget and priced from that budget; witnessing of real conformity assessment activity is booked against each assessor's finite annual capacity; findings carry classification-driven deadlines; and the accreditation decision itself is taken by a panel whose members are structurally barred from having assessed the cycle they vote on.

The API is mounted under `/accreditation`, with a single additional test-driving surface under `/_clock`. It is deliberately opinionated about failure: every gate that a real accreditation body would enforce in a committee meeting is expressed here as a named error, evaluated in a defined order, so that a client can always tell which of several simultaneously-broken rules the server objected to first.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This is the very first check the server performs, ahead of any parsing of the body or resolution of any path identifier.

Seven roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `SchemeManager`, `AssessmentManager`, `LeadAssessor`, `TechnicalExpert`, `DecisionPanelMember`, `ApplicantContact`, and `ComplaintOfficer`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those seven strings, or that is one of them but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The authorised roles for each endpoint are listed in the table in §4.

The seven roles divide the work of the accreditation body along segregation-of-duty lines:

- A `SchemeManager` owns scheme rule development and the body's own competence decisions. They create accreditation programmes and drive the rule-revision lifecycle, re-screen assessors and admit them to `QUALIFIED`, suspend, reinstate or withdraw assessors, verify competence evidence and grant qualifications, and suspend, restore or withdraw accredited scopes. They share with an `AssessmentManager` the ability to open a surveillance, reassessment or unannounced cycle against an active scope.
- An `AssessmentManager` runs assessment cycles. They recruit assessors and register their declared evidence, register applicant organizations, propose, confirm and recuse team members, approve assessment plans and return approved plans to draft, close the assessment work of a cycle, and escalate an overdue finding or reopen a closed one. They may not qualify the assessors they assign — that authority belongs to the `SchemeManager` alone. They share the opening of an initial cycle with an `ApplicantContact`, and plan creation and witness-session scheduling with a `LeadAssessor`.
- A `LeadAssessor` leads an assessment team: creating plans, scheduling and recording witnessing, raising findings, and — exclusively — reviewing a submitted corrective action and accepting or rejecting it.
- A `TechnicalExpert` is a technical specialist on the team. They record witnessing outcomes, raise technical findings, and may verify the effectiveness of an accepted corrective action, but they never review a corrective action.
- A `DecisionPanelMember` sits on the independent accreditation decision panel: casting ballots and finalizing decisions, and never having taken part in the assessment of the cycle being decided.
- An `ApplicantContact` is the applicant organization's own contact. They apply for accreditation, submit corrective-action responses for their own findings, and file complaints and appeals.
- A `ComplaintOfficer` handles complaints and appeals independently of the assessment and decision functions, and discharges the body's international mutual-recognition notification obligations.

All seven roles may read the whole registry and all seven may set the logical clock.

**How `X-User-ID` binds to data.** When `X-User-Role` is `LeadAssessor`, `TechnicalExpert` or `DecisionPanelMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Assessor`; ownership and participation checks on any resource that references an assessor compare the resource's assessor reference to `X-User-ID`. This is how the server knows that the person recording a witnessing outcome is the assessor booked for that slot, that the person raising a finding sits on the team, and that the person casting a ballot is not the person who assessed the cycle. When `X-User-Role` is `ApplicantContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ApplicantOrganization`; ownership checks compare the resource's organization reference to `X-User-ID`, resolved through the owning assessment cycle where the resource is a corrective-action response. When `X-User-Role` is `SchemeManager`, `AssessmentManager` or `ComplaintOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as `declared_by_role` records the operator's `X-User-Role` on a disclosure filed on an assessor's behalf) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

An `ApplicantContact` acting on data that belongs to a different applicant organization is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards exactly three operations: opening an assessment cycle, submitting a corrective-action response against a finding, and filing a complaint or appeal. In the response case the organization is not on the finding itself — it is reached through the finding's owning cycle.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer, and must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted, must be an integer, and must be at least 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, or an `offset` below 0, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, oldest row first, unless the endpoint's description in §4 explicitly states otherwise. Where a list endpoint also accepts domain filter parameters, those filters are combined conjunctively: a row is returned only if it matches every supplied filter.

For list responses the `meta` object carries four pagination fields — `limit` and `offset` echoing the values actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before the window was applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching row falls beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, recorded at creation and never changed afterwards.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. Because the specification below refers repeatedly to "bumping the version", note that this is not decoration: a resource's `version` is the observable evidence that a cascade touched it, and two of the write endpoints require the client to prove it has seen the current value (see §2.6).

There is exactly one exception. An `ImpartialityDeclaration` is immutable by design — the disclosure log is append-only, and a relationship is closed by filing a newer disclosure rather than by editing an old one. Declarations therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in a declaration payload.

All date, deadline, cooling-off, recency, expiry and surveillance-due arithmetic in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before any call to the clock-setting capability, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. The clock-setting capability accepts a single `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it; no stored resource is mutated by that call. Where this document says "the current logical-clock date", it means the calendar date of that logical instant in UTC; where it says "the current logical-clock instant", it means the instant itself, rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.

### 2.5. Rule-Revision Pinning: How Scheme Rules Reach a Cycle

An accreditation programme's rule set is not read live by the work it governs. When an assessment cycle is opened, the scheme's `rule_revision` at that moment is copied onto the cycle as `pinned_scheme_rule_revision`, and every downstream gate — the witnessing floor, the competence threshold, the three nonconformity closure windows, the surveillance and reassessment intervals, the complexity multiplier, the unannounced authority flag and the remote-assessment flag — is evaluated against **the scheme revision the cycle pinned**, not against whatever the scheme says today. This is the mechanism that makes an in-flight assessment auditable: the obligations judged at the end of a cycle are the ones that existed at its start.

Pinning has one deliberately sharp consequence. If a `SchemeManager` re-opens a `PUBLISHED` scheme for revision and publishes it again, the scheme's `rule_revision` advances while the cycle's `pinned_scheme_rule_revision` does not, and the cycle becomes stranded: approving a plan for it, and finalizing a decision on it, are both rejected with `ERR_SCHEME_VERSION_SUPERSEDED` (HTTP 422). Those are the only two operations that compare the two numbers. Withdrawing a scheme outright mutates no cycle either, but any in-flight cycle under it then fails at plan approval with `ERR_SCHEME_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 422), which is also the error raised when a cycle or a qualification is opened against a scheme that is not in status `PUBLISHED`.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Cycles and Findings

Two write operations require the client to prove that it is writing against the state it last observed, because in both cases the operation is legitimately repeatable and a state precondition alone cannot distinguish a fresh write from one built on a stale read.

The first is the creation of an assessment plan for a cycle. That request must carry `expected_application_version`, a non-optional integer field whose value is the `AccreditationApplication` `version` the client last read. If it does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new plan legitimately supersedes any `DRAFT` plan of the same cycle — the older plan is `CANCELLED` and records the newer plan's identifier in `superseded_by_plan_id` — so a second creation would otherwise succeed against a stale view in which the team composition, the pinned scheme revision, or an earlier plan still looked the way the client remembered. Successful creation bumps the cycle's `version`, so a client that wants to create two plans in a row must re-read the cycle between them.

The second is the submission of a corrective-action response against a finding. That request must carry `expected_nonconformity_version`, a non-optional integer field whose value is the `Nonconformity` `version` the client last read. If it does not equal the finding's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_NONCONFORMITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A response is legitimately submitted from `OPEN` more than once — after a rejecting review the applicant may resubmit — so only the stale-version check separates a response written against the reviewer's latest feedback from one written against feedback the applicant never saw.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Where this document does not name `expected_application_version` or `expected_nonconformity_version`, the request body must not be expected to carry one.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and the Arithmetic Conventions

Several distinct precision regimes coexist, and mixing them up is the most common source of one-off discrepancies:

- **Competence points** are carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded **half up**. Within the points calculation the only rounding site is the final multiplication by the recency factor; the four weighted factors and their sum are exact integers before that multiplication.
- **The per-scheme complexity multiplier** `complexity_multiplier` is a number in the range 1.0000 to 2.0000 inclusive, expressed with exactly 4 decimal places.
- **Money** is expressed in whole integer cents. Each of the four fee lines is rounded **half up** to whole cents *before* the lines are summed; the sum is never re-rounded and the fee is never computed by summing unrounded lines and rounding once at the end. This per-line rounding is observable whenever `complexity_multiplier` is anything other than 1.0000.
- **Day counts** are whole integers. The witness-day derivation from a scope-line count rounds **up** to the next whole day; a witnessing slot's day count rounds **up**, so any partial day counts as a full witness day; the risk-tiered surveillance interval rounds **down**.
- **Risk scores** are whole integers in the range 0 to 100 inclusive. Where a risk variant carries forward half of a prior score, the halving is left unrounded and the rounding **up** to a whole number is applied once, after the whole carry-forward sum has been assembled — never before.
- **Calendar-month differences** used by cooling-off arithmetic are computed as a year-and-month difference with a day-of-month borrow, never by dividing a day count by 30. Concretely: multiply the difference in years by twelve, add the difference in months, and then subtract one further month if the reference date's day-of-month is less than the end date's day-of-month.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Hierarchical Consistency

This system is built out of cascades: one authorised action routinely mutates three or four other resources, sometimes across aggregate boundaries. Three conventions govern them all.

First, **every resource a cascade touches is genuinely mutated** — its `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed — even when the cascade only changed a derived counter. A client can therefore detect that a scope suspension reached a cycle it was not looking at, simply by observing that the cycle's `version` moved.

Second, **cascades are transitive and are described here hop by hop.** Where §3 says "hop 2" or "hop 3", those hops are not optional elaborations: a cascade that stops after its first hop leaves the registry inconsistent. The escalation of an overdue finding, for example, reaches five hops deep — from the finding, to the organization's risk score, to the originating scope, to the organization, to the organization's other in-flight cycles, and to those cycles' draft plans and scheduled witnessing.

Third, **compensating actions restore conserved totals rather than recomputing them loosely.** Two ledgers are conserved. On an assessor, `annual_witness_capacity_days` always equals `committed_witness_days` plus `available_witness_days`; any operation that books or releases witnessing days must move both derived fields so that identity holds afterwards. On an assessment cycle, `nc_total_count` counts every finding of every classification and status and never moves when a finding merely changes status; only `nc_blocking_open_count`, `nc_closed_count` and `nc_observation_count` redistribute beneath it.

A cascade never re-instates what it previously tore down. Reinstating a suspended assessor does not restore the team assignments or witnessing slots the suspension terminated; restoring a suspended scope does not un-cancel the draft plans the suspension cancelled. Where restoration is required, the resource is created afresh by name.

### 2.9. Path Resolution Order and Validation Precedence

Nested resources are addressed by hierarchical paths, and path identifiers are resolved strictly **left to right in path order**. Each identifier that fails to resolve to a live row produces the not-found error of its own resource type, at HTTP 404: `ERR_SCHEME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WITNESS_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NONCONFORMITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`. Left-to-right resolution matters: when a path names both a scheme and a qualification and neither exists, the scheme's error is the one returned.

Foreign keys supplied **in the request body** are resolved only after every path identifier has resolved, and among themselves they are resolved in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint. A body-supplied foreign key that names no live row produces the same 404 family — `ERR_SCHEME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_FOUND` — for the six endpoints that accept one: opening an initial cycle, proposing a team member, registering a declared qualification, filing an impartiality declaration, filing a complaint or appeal, and assigning a case reviewer.

The complete precedence order the server applies to any request is the following, and it is normative — an earlier rule always masks a later one, so a request that violates several rules reports only the earliest:

1. Header presence (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role validity and endpoint authorisation (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`), and, for the three owner-guarded operations, applicant-organization ownership (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`). These two share a precedence rank.
3. Request-shape validity: on every write, the body must be a JSON object, every required input must be present, every enum-typed value must be a member of its declared set, every count field must be non-negative, and every monetary or points field must be numeric — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). On every list endpoint, pagination validity (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`). These two share a precedence rank.
4. Date-range coherence (`ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, HTTP 422): a plan's `planned_end` must be on or after its `planned_start`; a witnessing slot's `ends_at` must be strictly after its `starts_at`; a declaration's `ended_on`, when supplied, must be on or after its `started_on`. Those three endpoints — plan creation, witness-session scheduling and declaration filing — are the only ones this check guards.
5. Path identifier existence, left to right (the 404 family).
6. Body-supplied foreign-key existence, in declared field order (the 404 family).
7. Stale-version rejection: `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` on plan creation and `ERR_STALE_NONCONFORMITY_VERSION` on response submission. These two share a precedence rank.
8. The target resource's own status: the action must be defined from its current status, and any requested `to_status` must be a declared successor of the current status — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). This governs the seventeen status-driving operations: scheme status change, assessor status change, team-assignment confirm and recuse, plan approve and return-to-draft, witness-session outcome, finding review, effectiveness verification, escalation and reopen, panel vote and finalize, scope status change, and case review, resolve and MRA escalation.
9. The **owning** cycle's status and its freeze flag: the owning `AccreditationApplication` must be in a status that permits the operation and its `suspension_blocked` flag must be false — otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). This guards seven operations: proposing a team member, creating a plan, raising a finding, closing the assessment, scheduling a witnessing session, approving a plan, and finalizing a decision. At the same rank, the owning `Nonconformity` must be in a status that permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_NONCONFORMITY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — which guards corrective-action response submission.
10. Impartiality, independence and segregation-of-duty gates (§2.10).
11. Referential business rules: scheme publication status, pinned-revision currency, qualification currency, scope activity, plan approval and granted-scope-line membership.
12. Temporal business rules: schedule overlap, site buffer, overdue and response windows, and notice periods.
13. Constraint and capacity business rules: witnessing coverage, panel quorum, assessor capacity, duplicate ballots, duplicate active cycles, conditional-grant scope reduction, unannounced authority, remote witnessing permission, MRA eligibility and effectiveness evidence counts.

### 2.10. The Independence Doctrine

Four distinct independence rules operate at rank 10 of the precedence order above, and they are distinct errors because they protect distinct things.

**Cooling-off (`ERR_IMPARTIALITY_CONFLICT`, HTTP 422).** An assessor whose governing disclosure for the applicant organization is still inside its cooling-off window may neither be proposed for that cycle's team nor confirmed onto it.

**Standing restriction (`ERR_ASSESSOR_IMPARTIALITY_RESTRICTED`, HTTP 422).** An assessor whose `impartiality_status` is `RESTRICTED` and who has not since been re-screened may neither be proposed for a team nor confirmed onto one, regardless of which organization the cycle concerns.

**Panel independence (`ERR_PANEL_INDEPENDENCE_BREACH`, HTTP 422).** A panel member who holds a team assignment of *any* status — `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, `RECUSED` or `REJECTED` — on the cycle under decision may not cast a ballot on it. Recusal does not launder participation.

**Segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 422).** Two separate people must perform the two halves of a corrective-action closure: the assessor verifying effectiveness must differ from the assessor who reviewed the response. Symmetrically, the panel member who finalizes a decision record must themselves have cast a ballot on that record — a chair who never voted may not sign the outcome. Both readings live under the same error id, on effectiveness verification and on decision finalization respectively.

A fifth rule guards the appeal function specifically: the reviewer nominated for an appeal must never have sat on the appealed cycle's assessment team nor cast a vote on any of its decision records, or the assignment is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_REVIEWER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 422).

And a sixth guards team membership itself: an assessor acting on a cycle's assessment artefacts must actually be on that team. Raising a finding, recording a witnessing outcome, reviewing a corrective action and verifying effectiveness all require a `CONFIRMED` team assignment on the cycle, and the required `role_type` differs by operation — `LEAD_ASSESSOR` for a corrective-action review, and either `LEAD_ASSESSOR` or `TECHNICAL_ASSESSOR` for an effectiveness verification. A failure of any of those four is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TEAM_MEMBER` (HTTP 422).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Scheme Rule Development: Drafting, Review and Publication

An accreditation programme begins life as a rule set drafted by a `SchemeManager`. Creation requires a `program_code` of 3 to 32 characters that is unique across all accreditation programmes and compared case-sensitively — `ISO17025-LAB` and `iso17025-lab` are two different programme codes — together with a human-readable `name` of 1 to 160 characters and a `standard_ref` naming the international standard the rules align to, one of `ISO_IEC_17025`, `ISO_IEC_17020`, `ISO_IEC_17021_1` or `ISO_IEC_17065`.

Everything else about the rule set is optional at creation and falls back to a default. The per-scheme effort and fee multiplier `complexity_multiplier` defaults to 1.0000 and must lie between 1.0000 and 2.0000 inclusive with exactly four decimal places. The witnessing floor `witness_min_count` — the minimum number of completed witnessing sessions before an assessment cycle may be closed — defaults to 1 and must be between 0 and 10 inclusive. The competence bar `qualification_points_required` defaults to 60.00 and must be between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive with two decimal places. The nominal cadence `surveillance_interval_days` defaults to 365 days — one nominal year between an accreditation grant and the first surveillance due date, before any risk tiering — and must be between 30 and 1095 days inclusive. Its never-risk-adjusted sibling `reassessment_interval_days` defaults to 1460 days, four nominal years, and must be between 365 and 2190 days inclusive. The three closure windows are set independently: `critical_nc_close_days` defaults to 30, `major_nc_close_days` to 60 and `minor_nc_close_days` to 90, and each must be between 1 and 365 days inclusive. Two booleans complete the rule set: `unannounced_authority`, which defaults to false and governs whether the body may run unannounced assessments against active scopes under this programme, and `remote_assessment_allowed`, which defaults to true and governs whether plans and witnessing under this programme may be delivered remotely. A freshly created programme is in `status` `DRAFT` with `rule_revision` 1, `revision_note` null and `published_at` null.

Rule development then proceeds as a loop. From `DRAFT` the `SchemeManager` moves the programme to `UNDER_REVIEW` by supplying a `to_status` of `UNDER_REVIEW`; the only precondition is that the programme is currently `DRAFT`, and the effect is to bump the programme's `version`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` there are two exits. Sending it back to `DRAFT` requires a `revision_note` that is present and non-empty, of 1 to 500 characters; the note is written onto the programme's `revision_note` field, and `rule_revision` is explicitly *not* touched by the return — the revision counter moves only at publication. Alternatively, `UNDER_REVIEW` advances to `PUBLISHED`, which the server accepts only when `witness_min_count`, `qualification_points_required` and all three closure-day fields still satisfy the ranges above. Publication writes `published_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and — this is the subtle part — increments `rule_revision` by 1 **only if `published_at` was already non-null before the transition**. The very first publication of a programme therefore leaves `rule_revision` at 1; the second leaves it at 2, and so on.

A `PUBLISHED` programme has two onward moves, both driven by the same `to_status` field. Re-opening it as `UNDER_REVIEW` starts a fresh revision pass, and mutates nothing but the programme's own `version` — but as §2.5 explains, the next publication will advance `rule_revision` and strand any cycle still pinned to the older number, which surfaces as `ERR_SCHEME_VERSION_SUPERSEDED` when that cycle's plan is approved or its decision finalized. Moving it to `WITHDRAWN`, the terminal state, likewise mutates no cycle at all; in-flight cycles under a withdrawn programme instead fail at plan approval with `ERR_SCHEME_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Any `to_status` that is not a declared successor of the programme's current status — publishing straight from `DRAFT`, for instance, or reviving a `WITHDRAWN` programme — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

The programme registry is readable in full by every role, and the list view can be narrowed by `status` and by `standard_ref`.

### 3.2. Recruiting Assessors and Building Their Competence Record

Recruitment is an `AssessmentManager` responsibility. An assessor is registered with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `email` that is unique across all assessors and must contain exactly one `@` character, and an `employment_type` of either `INTERNAL` — employed by the accreditation body — or `CONTRACT`. An `annual_witness_capacity_days` may be supplied, defaulting to 20 and constrained to between 0 and 250 days; this is the conserved total of the assessor's witness-day ledger, so a new assessor starts with `committed_witness_days` at 0 and `available_witness_days` equal to the capacity just set.

A new assessor occupies two independent state dimensions at once, and this independence is load-bearing rather than incidental. `qualification_status` begins at `APPLICANT` and evolves through `SCREENING`, `QUALIFIED` and `SUSPENDED` toward the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. `impartiality_status` begins at `UNDECLARED` and moves between `SCREENED` and `RESTRICTED` with no terminal state at all. Neither dimension constrains the other: a `RESTRICTED` assessor may still be `QUALIFIED`, and a `SUSPENDED` assessor may still be `SCREENED`. `granted_qualification_count` starts at 0, and `last_screened_on` and `suspension_reason` both start null.

**Declared evidence.** Competence is claimed per programme and per role. Registering a qualification for an assessor requires the `scheme_id` of the programme — which must be in status `PUBLISHED`, or the registration is rejected with `ERR_SCHEME_NOT_PUBLISHED` — a `role_type` of `LEAD_ASSESSOR`, `TECHNICAL_ASSESSOR` or `TECHNICAL_EXPERT`, and three self-declared counts: `years_experience` between 0 and 50, `declared_witness_days` between 0 and 200, and `declared_training_modules` between 0 and 20. An optional `last_witness_on` records the date of the candidate's most recent witnessing activity. A qualification record is unique to the combination of assessor, programme and `role_type`; it is created in `status` `PENDING` with `verified_witness_days` and `verified_training_modules` at 0, `verified_years_experience` null, `verified_points` null, `granted_on`, `expires_on` and `evidence_verified_at` all null. If this is the assessor's very first qualification record and their `qualification_status` is still `APPLICANT`, the same request moves that dimension to `SCREENING` and bumps the assessor's `version`; the `impartiality_status` dimension is untouched.

**How competence points are computed.** Both the provisional and the final points figures come from a single four-factor weighted model, applied twice with different inputs. Experience contributes 4 points for each whole year of experience, capped at 40 points. Witnessing contributes 5 points for each witnessing day, capped at 30 points. Training contributes 10 points for each completed scheme training module, capped at 20 points. Integrity of employment contributes a flat 10 points when the assessor's `employment_type` is `INTERNAL` and a flat 6 points when it is `CONTRACT`. The four contributions are added together to give a raw total, and that raw total is then multiplied by a recency factor and rounded half up to two decimal places. The caps are strictly per factor and never applied to the raw total: the only way to reach 100.00 points is to max out experience, witnessing and training simultaneously *and* be an internal employee.

The two variants differ in exactly two ways — which counts they read, and how they obtain the recency factor.

- The **provisional** variant runs at qualification registration and writes `declared_points`. It reads only the self-declared numbers — the declared years of experience supplied as `years_experience`, plus `declared_witness_days` and `declared_training_modules` — and pins the recency factor to 1.00 regardless of what `last_witness_on` says. It can never move the qualification out of `PENDING`.
- The **verified** variant runs at evidence verification and writes `verified_points`. It reads only the verifier-confirmed numbers `verified_years_experience`, `verified_witness_days` and `verified_training_modules`, and derives the recency factor from the number of whole days between `last_witness_on` and the current logical-clock date: the factor is 1.00 when that gap is at most 365 days, 0.90 when the gap is from 366 to 730 days inclusive, and 0.75 when the gap exceeds 730 days or when `last_witness_on` is null altogether. It is this variant, and only this variant, that decides whether the qualification is granted.

Qualifications already in `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` are never recalculated by either variant.

**Verification and the grant.** A `SchemeManager` records verified evidence by supplying `verified_years_experience`, `verified_witness_days` and `verified_training_modules`, and the server writes all three onto the qualification along with the recomputed `verified_points` and an `evidence_verified_at` stamp at the current logical-clock instant. The qualification is then granted only when two conditions both hold: `verified_points` is at least the programme's `qualification_points_required`, and `verified_witness_days` is at least the witness-day floor for the `role_type` being claimed — 4 days for `LEAD_ASSESSOR`, 2 days for `TECHNICAL_ASSESSOR`, and 0 days for `TECHNICAL_EXPERT`. On a grant, `status` moves `PENDING → GRANTED`, `granted_on` is set to the current logical-clock date, `expires_on` is set to 1460 days — four nominal years — after `granted_on`, the assessor's `granted_qualification_count` increases by 1, and the assessor's `version` is bumped. When either condition fails, the verified figures and `evidence_verified_at` are still written, `status` stays `PENDING`, `granted_on` stays null, and the qualification's own `version` is bumped: **falling short of the bar is not an error**, merely an outcome, and verification may be re-run later with better evidence.

**Admission to qualified standing.** Screening an assessor is a `SchemeManager` operation with no request body that does two things in one pass. On the qualification dimension it moves `SCREENING → QUALIFIED`, but only when the assessor already holds at least one `GRANTED` qualification — `granted_qualification_count` must be 1 or more. On the impartiality dimension it re-evaluates the assessor's standing across every organization they have declared against, as §3.3 describes. Either way it refreshes `last_screened_on` to the current logical-clock date and bumps the assessor's `version`.

**Suspension, reinstatement and withdrawal.** An assessor's standing is driven by a `to_status` together with an optional `reason`. Moving a `QUALIFIED` assessor to `SUSPENDED` requires a `reason`, which is written to `suspension_reason` (1 to 300 characters), and triggers a two-hop cascade: every team assignment of that assessor in `PROPOSED` becomes `REJECTED` and every one in `CONFIRMED` becomes `RECUSED`, each carrying the `recusal_reason` `assessor suspended`; then, for each assignment that just became `RECUSED` while holding `role_type` `LEAD_ASSESSOR` on a cycle in `TEAM_ASSIGNED` with no other `CONFIRMED` lead assessor remaining, that cycle is pushed back from `TEAM_ASSIGNED` to `SUBMITTED`. In the same cascade every witnessing session of that assessor still in `SCHEDULED` becomes `ABORTED` with the `evidence_note` `assessor suspended`, and each such abort returns that session's `day_count` to the assessor's ledger — `committed_witness_days` decreases and `available_witness_days` increases by that day count. The assessor's `version` is bumped.

Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → QUALIFIED` and requires that the assessor still holds at least one `GRANTED` qualification; it clears `suspension_reason` to null and bumps `version`, and it explicitly does **not** restore any team assignment or witnessing session that the suspension terminated — the assessor must be proposed to teams afresh. Withdrawal is reachable from either `QUALIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`, requires a `reason`, and is terminal. From `QUALIFIED` it runs the identical team-assignment and witnessing cascade as suspension; from `SUSPENDED` there is nothing left to tear down. Both withdrawal paths additionally revoke competence: every qualification of that assessor in `PENDING` or `GRANTED` moves to `REVOKED` with its own `version` bumped, and `granted_qualification_count` is set to 0.

**Lazy expiry.** A `GRANTED` qualification is never swept in the background. Expiry is observed lazily at the moment someone tries to confirm a team assignment that relies on it: if the current logical-clock date is strictly later than the qualification's `expires_on`, the qualification is moved `GRANTED → EXPIRED`, the assessor's `granted_qualification_count` is decremented by 1, and the confirmation request that observed the expiry is itself rejected with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_GRANTED`. The state change is durable even though the request failed.

Assessor lists may be filtered by `qualification_status`, `impartiality_status` and `employment_type`; the assessor detail view exposes the full witness-day ledger. Qualification lists may be filtered by `assessor_id`, `scheme_id`, `role_type` and `status`, and the qualification detail view exposes `declared_points` and `verified_points` side by side.

### 3.3. Impartiality Disclosure, Screening and the Conflict Verdict

Impartiality is managed through an append-only disclosure log rather than an editable conflict register. A disclosure names the applicant organization it concerns via `organization_id`, states a `relationship_type`, and records when the relationship began through `started_on`. Two further inputs are optional: `ended_on`, the date the relationship ended, and `detail`, a free-text description of 1 to 500 characters. Leaving `ended_on` null means the relationship is ongoing, and an ongoing relationship never clears a cooling-off period no matter how long ago it started. Where `ended_on` is supplied it must not fall before `started_on`, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. A disclosure may be filed by the assessor themselves — a `LeadAssessor`, `TechnicalExpert` or `DecisionPanelMember` filing their own — or by an `AssessmentManager` acting on the assessor's behalf; whichever it is, the acting `X-User-Role` value is captured on the record as `declared_by_role`.

The `relationship_type` is one of six values, and the server derives two further fields from it. `EMPLOYMENT`, `OWNERSHIP` and `FAMILY` all yield a `severity_class` of `STRUCTURAL` with a `cooling_off_months` of 24. `CONSULTANCY` and `TRAINING_DELIVERY` both yield a `severity_class` of `SERVICE` with a `cooling_off_months` of 12 — one nominal year of separation rather than two. The value `NONE` yields a `severity_class` of `NONE` with a `cooling_off_months` of 0, and exists so that an assessor can positively record that they have no relationship with an organization. Disclosures are never edited or deleted; as §2.4 notes, they carry only `id` and `created_at`.

Because the log is append-only, **only the newest disclosure for a given assessor-and-organization pair counts.** For each such pair the server takes the single row with the greatest `created_at` and evaluates that one; every earlier row for the same pair is superseded and contributes nothing to any decision. This is how an ongoing structural relationship is closed out: not by editing the old row, but by filing a *newer* row for the same pair that carries a non-null `ended_on`.

**Filing a disclosure can restrict an assessor immediately.** When a newly filed disclosure is the newest one for its pair, carries a `severity_class` of `STRUCTURAL`, and has a null `ended_on`, the assessor's `impartiality_status` moves to `RESTRICTED` and the assessor's `version` is bumped. This happens from `UNDECLARED` and equally from `SCREENED` — a screened assessor who discloses a current directorship at an applicant is restricted on the spot. A `RESTRICTED` assessor may neither be proposed for nor confirmed onto any assessment team, for any organization, until re-screened (`ERR_ASSESSOR_IMPARTIALITY_RESTRICTED`).

**Screening clears the restriction.** The screening operation described in §3.2 re-derives the impartiality dimension from the log. It resolves to `SCREENED` when, across every organization the assessor has declared against, no pair's newest disclosure is simultaneously `STRUCTURAL` and open-ended — that is, for every pair the newest row either is not `STRUCTURAL` or carries a non-null `ended_on`. On that outcome the assessor moves `UNDECLARED → SCREENED`, or `RESTRICTED → SCREENED`, or — when the assessor was already `SCREENED` — stays `SCREENED` while `last_screened_on` is refreshed and `version` bumped. If any pair's newest disclosure is still `STRUCTURAL` and open-ended, screening leaves the assessor `RESTRICTED`.

**The conflict verdict.** Standing restriction is a blunt instrument; the per-organization gate is finer, and it is computed fresh at three moments: when an assessor is proposed for a cycle's team, when that proposal is confirmed, and when an assessor is nominated to review a complaint or appeal. The verdict is derived from the newest disclosure of each pair joining the acting assessor to the organization in question, using the current logical-clock date as the reference date:

- A newest disclosure of `relationship_type` `NONE` contributes the verdict `CLEARED`.
- Otherwise, take the cooling-off window implied by the severity class — 24 months for `STRUCTURAL`, 12 months for `SERVICE`. If `ended_on` is null, the pair contributes `BLOCKED`, unconditionally.
- If `ended_on` is set, compute the whole calendar months elapsed from `ended_on` to the reference date using the year-and-month difference with a day-of-month borrow described in §2.7. If that number of months is strictly less than the cooling-off window, the pair contributes `BLOCKED`. If the window has been satisfied and the severity class is `STRUCTURAL`, the pair contributes `CLEARED`. If the window has been satisfied and the severity class is `SERVICE`, the pair contributes `CLEARED_WITH_NOTE` — the relationship no longer blocks, but the fact of it is carried forward on the record.

The aggregate verdict over all pairs takes the most severe contribution: `BLOCKED` if any pair contributed `BLOCKED`, otherwise `CLEARED_WITH_NOTE` if any pair contributed `CLEARED_WITH_NOTE`, otherwise `CLEARED`. Disclosures belonging to other assessors or other organizations are never considered. A `BLOCKED` aggregate rejects the proposal, the confirmation or the reviewer nomination with `ERR_IMPARTIALITY_CONFLICT`.

The disclosure log is readable by every role, filterable by `assessor_id`, `organization_id` and `relationship_type`, and an individual disclosure is addressable by its own identifier — a disclosure identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.4. Applicant Organizations, Opening a Cycle, and Assembling a Conflict-Free Team

A conformity assessment body seeking accreditation is registered by an `AssessmentManager` with a `legal_name` of 1 to 160 characters that must be unique across all applicant organizations, a `country_code` of exactly two upper-case letters naming the registered seat, and a `contact_email` containing exactly one `@` character. Registration puts the organization in `status` `APPLIED` with `risk_score` 0, `active_scope_count` 0, `upheld_complaint_count` 0, `mra_escalation_count` 0 and `suspension_reason` null. As the organization's history accumulates, its status walks between `APPLIED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, `ACCREDITED`, `SUSPENDED` and `WITHDRAWN`, always as a consequence of something happening to a cycle or a scope rather than as a directly requested change. Organization lists may be filtered by `status` and `country_code`.

**Opening an initial cycle.** Either an `AssessmentManager` or the applicant's own `ApplicantContact` opens an assessment cycle, supplying the `organization_id`, the `scheme_id` of the programme applied for, a `cycle_type`, an ordered list of `requested_scope_lines`, and optionally `notice_days`. The `cycle_type` is one of `INITIAL`, `SURVEILLANCE`, `REASSESSMENT` or `UNANNOUNCED`. The `requested_scope_lines` list holds between 1 and 20 unique non-empty strings, each 1 to 80 characters, naming the test, inspection or certification activities the applicant wants accredited; the order supplied is preserved verbatim and matters later, when the panel defines the granted scope. `notice_days` defaults to 30 and must lie between 0 and 120 inclusive; beyond that range check, an `UNANNOUNCED` cycle must carry exactly 0 notice days while every other `cycle_type` must carry at least 14 — a violation of either half is `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_PERIOD`. An `UNANNOUNCED` cycle is additionally only permitted when the pinned scheme revision grants `unannounced_authority`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_UNANNOUNCED_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. The programme must be `PUBLISHED` (`ERR_SCHEME_NOT_PUBLISHED`), and the organization must not already have another cycle for the same programme sitting in a non-terminal status — the terminal statuses being `GRANTED` and `WITHDRAWN` — or the request is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION`.

A newly opened cycle copies the programme's current `rule_revision` into `pinned_scheme_rule_revision` and enters `status` `SUBMITTED` with `decision_round` 1, `nc_total_count`, `nc_blocking_open_count`, `nc_closed_count`, `nc_observation_count` and `completed_witness_session_count` all 0, `planned_effort_days` null and `suspension_blocked` false. For an `INITIAL` cycle `origin_scope_id` is null; cycles spawned from an existing accredited scope carry that scope's identifier there instead (see §3.9). Cycle lists may be filtered by `organization_id`, `scheme_id`, `origin_scope_id`, `cycle_type` and `status`.

**Proposing a team member.** An `AssessmentManager` proposes an assessor onto the cycle's team by supplying an `assessor_id` and the `role_type` the assessor is to occupy. The proposal is screened before it is accepted: the conflict verdict for that assessor against the cycle's applicant organization must not be `BLOCKED` (`ERR_IMPARTIALITY_CONFLICT`), the assessor's `impartiality_status` must not be `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_ASSESSOR_IMPARTIALITY_RESTRICTED`), and the assessor must hold a `GRANTED` qualification for the cycle's programme in the requested `role_type` whose `expires_on` is on or after the current logical-clock date (`ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_GRANTED`). The owning cycle must also be in a status that permits team building and must not be frozen — a `suspension_blocked` cycle rejects the proposal with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`. A surviving proposal is created in `status` `PROPOSED` with `recusal_reason` null and with both `conflict_verdict` and `conflict_checked_at` left null: the verdict is computed at proposal time purely to enforce the gate, and is not persisted until confirmation.

**Confirming a team member.** Confirmation is also an `AssessmentManager` action, carries no request body, and re-runs the whole screen at the later moment. The assignment must still be `PROPOSED`; the cycle must be in `SUBMITTED` or `TEAM_ASSIGNED` and must not be frozen; the assessor's `qualification_status` must be exactly `QUALIFIED`; their `impartiality_status` must not be `RESTRICTED`; a `GRANTED` qualification must exist for the triple of assessor, the cycle's programme and the assignment's `role_type`, with `expires_on` on or after the current logical-clock date — and it is here that the lazy expiry of §3.2 bites; and the conflict verdict against the cycle's organization must not be `BLOCKED`. On success the assignment moves `PROPOSED → CONFIRMED`, and the verdict actually computed — `CLEARED` or `CLEARED_WITH_NOTE` — is written into `conflict_verdict` alongside a `conflict_checked_at` stamp at the current logical-clock instant.

Confirmation of a lead assessor is what starts the cycle moving. When the confirmed assignment's `role_type` is `LEAD_ASSESSOR` and the cycle is still `SUBMITTED`, the cycle advances `SUBMITTED → TEAM_ASSIGNED` with its `version` bumped, and that cycle transition in turn moves the applicant organization from `APPLIED` to `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` with the organization's `version` bumped. Confirming a second lead assessor, or a technical assessor or technical expert, leaves the cycle where it is. The assignment's own `version` is bumped in every case.

**Recusal.** An `AssessmentManager` recuses a `CONFIRMED` team member by supplying a `reason` of 1 to 300 characters, which is written to `recusal_reason`; the cycle must be in `SUBMITTED`, `TEAM_ASSIGNED`, `PLANNED`, `IN_ASSESSMENT` or `NC_OPEN` for the recusal to be accepted. Losing a lead assessor can unwind the cycle: when the recused assignment held `role_type` `LEAD_ASSESSOR`, no other `CONFIRMED` lead assessor remains on the cycle, and the cycle is in `TEAM_ASSIGNED`, the cycle is pushed back `TEAM_ASSIGNED → SUBMITTED` with its `version` bumped. The organization stays `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` — losing a lead assessor is a team-assembly setback, not a withdrawal of the application. `RECUSED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal for an assignment; there is no path back, and re-adding the same assessor means creating a new proposal. Team-assignment lists may be filtered by `application_id`, `assessor_id`, `role_type` and `status`.

### 3.5. Planning the Assessment: Effort Derivation and Fee Construction

Once a cycle has a confirmed team, an `AssessmentManager` or a `LeadAssessor` drafts the assessment plan. The plan declares a `mode` of `ON_SITE`, `REMOTE` or `HYBRID`, an inclusive window bounded by `planned_start` and `planned_end`, and two day counts the planner controls directly: `on_site_days` and `remote_days`, each between 0 and 30 inclusive. The mode constrains those counts — `on_site_days` must be 0 when the `mode` is `REMOTE`, and `remote_days` must be 0 when the `mode` is `ON_SITE` — and `planned_end` must not fall before `planned_start`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The request must also carry `expected_application_version` as described in §2.6, and the cycle must be in a status that permits planning and must not be frozen (`ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`).

Creating a plan when the cycle already has a plan in `DRAFT` is legitimate and is how a planner iterates: the earlier `DRAFT` plan moves to `CANCELLED`, records the new plan's identifier in its own `superseded_by_plan_id`, and has its `version` bumped. The new plan is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `approved_at`, `revision_note` and its own `superseded_by_plan_id` all null, and the cycle's `version` is bumped by the creation.

**Deriving the day budget.** Four derived day counts are written at creation, and the order in which they are computed is normative — each step consumes the output of the one before it.

1. **Witnessing coverage** is never allowed below the programme floor and otherwise scales with the breadth of the application. Divide the number of entries in the cycle's `requested_scope_lines` by three, rounding up to the next whole day, and take whichever is larger: that figure, or the `witness_min_count` of the scheme revision pinned on the cycle. The result is `witness_days_planned`. Three scope lines therefore need one witness day, four scope lines need two, and a programme with a floor of three witness days needs three even for a single-line application.
2. **Core days** is `core_days`, the sum of `on_site_days`, `remote_days` and `witness_days_planned`.
3. **Travel days** is selected from a tier keyed on `core_days` — and on `core_days` alone, never on the total. A `core_days` of 5 or fewer yields `travel_days` of 0; a `core_days` of 6 through 10 inclusive yields 1; a `core_days` above 10 yields 2.
4. **Total** is `total_effort_days`, the sum of `core_days` and `travel_days`.

**Building the fee.** The plan's price is assembled from four separate fee lines, each rounded half up to whole cents *before* any addition takes place. The on-site line `fee_line_on_site_cents` is `on_site_days` multiplied by the standard on-site day rate of `145000` cents per assessor day and then by the programme's `complexity_multiplier`. The remote line `fee_line_remote_cents` is `remote_days` multiplied by the remote day rate of `96500` cents and then by the same multiplier. The witnessing line `fee_line_witness_cents` is `witness_days_planned` multiplied by the witnessing day rate of `132500` cents and then by the same multiplier. The travel line `fee_line_travel_cents` is a flat pass-through of `87500` cents per travel day — **the complexity multiplier does not apply to travel**, because travel is reimbursed at cost rather than priced for effort. `assessment_fee_cents` is then the plain sum of those four already-rounded lines. As §2.7 stresses, rounding each line before summing is not interchangeable with summing and rounding once; the difference is visible whenever `complexity_multiplier` is not exactly 1.0000.

**Approval.** An `AssessmentManager` approves a `DRAFT` plan with no request body, and the approval is where the pinned-revision doctrine is enforced. The plan must be `DRAFT`; the cycle must be in `SUBMITTED` or `TEAM_ASSIGNED` and must not be frozen; the programme must be in `status` `PUBLISHED` (`ERR_SCHEME_NOT_PUBLISHED`); the programme's current `rule_revision` must still equal the cycle's `pinned_scheme_rule_revision` (`ERR_SCHEME_VERSION_SUPERSEDED`); at least one team assignment on the cycle must be `CONFIRMED` with `role_type` `LEAD_ASSESSOR`; a `mode` of `REMOTE` is permitted only when the programme's `remote_assessment_allowed` is true; and a `mode` of `REMOTE` is further permitted only when the programme's `witness_min_count` is 0, since a fully remote assessment cannot discharge a witnessing obligation. Approval recomputes the day breakdown and all four fee lines from scratch, sets `approved_at` to the current logical-clock instant, and bumps the plan's `version`. When the cycle is in `TEAM_ASSIGNED`, the approval also advances it `TEAM_ASSIGNED → PLANNED`, writes the plan's `total_effort_days` into the cycle's `planned_effort_days`, and bumps the cycle's `version`.

**Returning a plan for revision.** An `AssessmentManager` may send an `APPROVED` plan back to `DRAFT` by supplying a `revision_note` of 1 to 500 characters, provided the cycle is in `PLANNED` and **no** witnessing session under the plan has reached `COMPLETED` — once real witnessing evidence exists the plan is locked, and an attempt to return it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The return writes the `revision_note`, clears `approved_at` back to null, and cascades in two directions. Every witnessing session under the plan still in `SCHEDULED` becomes `ABORTED` with the `evidence_note` `plan returned to draft`, and each such abort returns that session's `day_count` to its assessor's ledger — `committed_witness_days` down, `available_witness_days` up by the same amount, so that the capacity identity of §2.8 still holds. In the other direction the cycle moves `PLANNED → TEAM_ASSIGNED`, its `planned_effort_days` is cleared to null, and its `version` is bumped. The plan's own `version` is bumped too. The fee lines computed by the earlier approval are not preserved: the next approval recomputes them from scratch, which matters if the programme's `complexity_multiplier` changed in between.

A plan has two terminal states. It reaches `EXECUTED` when the cycle's assessment work is closed, with its `version` bumped by that transition. It reaches `CANCELLED` either by being superseded, as above, or as collateral of a scope suspension (§3.9). Plan lists may be filtered by `application_id`, `superseded_by_plan_id`, `mode` and `status`, so a planner can retrieve exactly the chain of plans a newer plan replaced.

### 3.6. Witnessing Real Conformity Assessment Activity, and Closing the Assessment

Accreditation is not granted on documentation alone; the body must watch the applicant actually perform the activity it wants accredited. Witnessing slots are booked against an `APPROVED` plan — a plan in any other status rejects the booking with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` — by an `AssessmentManager` or a `LeadAssessor`, who supplies the `assessor_id` of the witnessing assessor, an `activity_type`, a `site_ref` of 1 to 80 characters naming the site or laboratory, a `delivery_mode`, and the slot bounds `starts_at` and `ends_at`. The `activity_type` is one of `TESTING`, `CALIBRATION`, `INSPECTION`, `MANAGEMENT_REVIEW` or `INTERNAL_AUDIT`. The `delivery_mode` is either `ON_SITE` or `REMOTE`, and a `REMOTE` slot is accepted only when the scheme revision pinned on the cycle has `remote_assessment_allowed` true, failing which the booking is rejected with `ERR_REMOTE_WITNESS_NOT_PERMITTED`. The slot is half-open: `starts_at` is inclusive, `ends_at` is exclusive, and `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at` or the booking fails with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The owning cycle must not be frozen.

**How a slot consumes capacity.** The booked cost of a slot is not its wall-clock duration but a whole number of assessor days. Take the exact duration from `starts_at` to `ends_at` in hours, divide by the eight-hour assessment day, and round up: `day_count` is that figure, so any partial day counts as a full witness day and a nine-hour slot costs two days. The slot's `day_count` is then added to the witnessing assessor's `committed_witness_days`, and `available_witness_days` is set to `annual_witness_capacity_days` minus the new `committed_witness_days`. A booking whose `day_count` added to the current `committed_witness_days` would exceed `annual_witness_capacity_days` is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Two scheduling guards.** The first protects the assessor: the requested half-open interval must not intersect the half-open interval of any other witnessing session of the same assessor in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` status, or the booking is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Intervals that merely touch are fine — a slot starting at exactly the instant another slot ends is accepted, because half-open intervals that abut do not overlap.

The second protects the integrity of the witnessing itself, by keeping the same assessment venue from being used for two different applicants in quick succession. For every witnessing session in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` status whose `delivery_mode` is `ON_SITE` and whose `site_ref` equals the requested `site_ref`, but which belongs to a **different** applicant organization, compute the whole-day gap as the calendar date of the requested `starts_at` minus the calendar date of that session's `ends_at`. That gap must be strictly greater than the fourteen-day site buffer; a gap of exactly 14 days is rejected, and so is anything smaller, with `ERR_SITE_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Sessions belonging to the *same* applicant organization are exempt from the buffer entirely — an applicant may be witnessed at its own site as often as the plan requires.

A surviving booking is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `outcome` and `evidence_note` null. Session lists may be filtered by `plan_id`, `assessor_id`, `site_ref`, `delivery_mode` and `status`.

**Recording the result.** Only the assessor booked for the slot may record its result: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the session's own `assessor_id`, and the acting assessor must additionally hold a `CONFIRMED` team assignment on the cycle or the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TEAM_MEMBER`. A `LeadAssessor` or `TechnicalExpert` supplies a `result_status` of either `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED`, optionally an `outcome`, and optionally an `evidence_note`.

Completing a slot requires the plan to still be `APPROVED`, the cycle not to be frozen, and an `outcome` to be present and equal to either `SATISFACTORY` or `DEFICIENT`. The `outcome` and the `evidence_note` — the witness evidence, 1 to 500 characters — are written onto the session, and three cascades follow. The `GRANTED` qualification joining the witnessing assessor to the cycle's programme has its `last_witness_on` refreshed to the current logical-clock date and its `version` bumped, which is how witnessing feeds back into the recency factor of §3.2. The cycle's `completed_witness_session_count` is recomputed. And when the cycle is still in `PLANNED`, the first completion advances it `PLANNED → IN_ASSESSMENT` with its `version` bumped. Note carefully what does **not** happen: a completed witness day is consumed capacity, so `committed_witness_days` is *not* released on completion.

Aborting a slot is the compensating path and is recognised by the absence of an `outcome` — a `result_status` of `ABORTED` must not carry one. The `evidence_note` then records the abort reason, and the ledger is unwound: `committed_witness_days` is decremented by the session's `day_count`, `available_witness_days` is incremented by the same `day_count`, leaving `annual_witness_capacity_days` still equal to the sum of the two, and both the assessor's `version` and the session's `version` are bumped. Sessions are also aborted by the system rather than by a person — on an assessor's suspension or withdrawal, on the return of an approved plan to draft, and on a scope-suspension cascade — and in every one of those cases the `evidence_note` records the cascade reason and the assessor's witness-day ledger is restored by that session's `day_count`. `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` are both terminal.

**Closing the assessment work.** When the on-site and remote work is finished, an `AssessmentManager` closes the assessment. The cycle must be in `IN_ASSESSMENT` and must not be frozen, and the cycle must have an `APPROVED` plan (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`). Two coverage conditions must both hold, and failing either yields `ERR_WITNESS_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`: the cycle's `completed_witness_session_count` must be at least the `witness_min_count` of the pinned scheme revision, **and** the sum of `day_count` over those witnessing sessions of the cycle's `APPROVED` plan whose status is `COMPLETED` must be at least that plan's `witness_days_planned`. The first condition counts sessions; the second counts days; a single long session can satisfy the day total while failing the session count, and several short sessions can satisfy the count while failing the day total.

Closing always moves the cycle's `APPROVED` plan to `EXECUTED` and bumps the cycle's `version`, but it routes the cycle to one of two destinations depending on the finding ledger. When `nc_blocking_open_count` is 1 or more, the cycle moves `IN_ASSESSMENT → NC_OPEN` and waits for corrective action. When `nc_blocking_open_count` is exactly 0, the cycle moves `IN_ASSESSMENT → DECISION_PENDING`, a decision record is created in `status` `VOTING` with `round` equal to the cycle's current `decision_round` and an empty ballot list, and that record's creation recomputes the applicant organization's `risk_score` under the at-decision variant described in §3.7 and bumps the organization's `version`.

### 3.7. Findings, Corrective Action, Effectiveness and Escalation

A finding is raised against a cycle by a `LeadAssessor` or a `TechnicalExpert` who holds a `CONFIRMED` team assignment on it (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TEAM_MEMBER`), while the cycle is in a status that permits findings and is not frozen. The author supplies a `clause_ref` of 1 to 40 characters naming the clause of the international standard the finding is written against, a `classification`, and a `statement` of nonconformity of 1 to 800 characters. The server records `raised_by_assessor_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `raised_on` as the current logical-clock date, and creates the finding in `status` `OPEN` with `response_round` 0, `verified_evidence_count` 0, `reopen_count` 0, and `root_cause`, `action_plan`, `response_submitted_on`, `reviewed_by_assessor_id`, `review_note`, `verified_by_assessor_id`, `effectiveness_outcome`, `closed_on` and `escalated_on` all null.

**The closure deadline.** `due_on` is derived from the `classification` and the closure windows of the scheme revision pinned on the cycle — the live programme's windows are ignored even if they have since changed. A `CRITICAL` finding is due `critical_nc_close_days` calendar days after `raised_on`; a `MAJOR` finding `major_nc_close_days` days after; a `MINOR` finding `minor_nc_close_days` days after. An `OBSERVATION` gets no deadline at all: `due_on` stays null, it never blocks a decision, and it can never be responded to, reviewed, escalated or closed.

**The finding ledger.** Raising a finding increments the cycle's `nc_total_count`, which counts every finding of every classification and status and never moves again. It also increments exactly one of two redistributable counters: `nc_observation_count` when the `classification` is `OBSERVATION`, and `nc_blocking_open_count` otherwise. `nc_blocking_open_count` holds the findings of classification `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR` whose status is `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED` or `ESCALATED`; `nc_closed_count` holds those of the same three classifications whose status is `CLOSED`. Finding lists may be filtered by `application_id`, `raised_by_assessor_id`, `reviewed_by_assessor_id`, `verified_by_assessor_id`, `classification` and `status`.

**The applicant responds.** Only the applicant's own `ApplicantContact` may submit corrective action, and only for its own cycle — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `organization_id` of the finding's owning cycle, or the submission is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The submission carries `expected_nonconformity_version` (see §2.6), a `root_cause` of 1 to 500 characters and an `action_plan` of 1 to 800 characters. The finding must be `OPEN` (`ERR_NONCONFORMITY_STATE_INVALID`), must not be an `OBSERVATION`, and the cycle must be in `NC_OPEN` and not frozen. The current logical-clock date must be on or before the finding's `due_on`; a submission on the due date itself is accepted, a submission the day after is rejected with `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. On success `response_round` increases by 1, `root_cause`, `action_plan` and `response_submitted_on` are written, the finding moves `OPEN → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, and its `version` is bumped. A resubmission after a rejecting review supersedes the earlier response text and increments `response_round` again — the counter records how many attempts were made and is never decremented by a rejection.

**The lead assessor reviews.** Reviewing a submitted corrective action is the `LeadAssessor`'s exclusive act, and requires a `CONFIRMED` team assignment of `role_type` `LEAD_ASSESSOR` on the cycle (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TEAM_MEMBER`). The reviewer supplies an `outcome` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` together with a `review_note` of 1 to 500 characters, and the finding must be in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`. Both branches write `reviewed_by_assessor_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, write the `review_note`, and bump the finding's `version`; they differ only in destination. `ACCEPT` moves the finding `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_ACCEPTED`. `REJECT` sends it back `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → OPEN` while leaving `response_round`, `root_cause` and `action_plan` exactly as submitted, so the applicant can see what was rejected and may resubmit for as long as the due date has not passed.

**Effectiveness verification is deliberately a second pair of eyes.** Verification is performed by a `LeadAssessor` or a `TechnicalExpert` who holds a `CONFIRMED` team assignment on the cycle whose `role_type` is either `LEAD_ASSESSOR` or `TECHNICAL_ASSESSOR` (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TEAM_MEMBER`), on a finding in `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `reviewed_by_assessor_id` — the person who accepted the corrective action may not also declare it effective (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). The verifier supplies an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL` and a `verified_evidence_count`, which must be at least the minimum evidence count for the finding's `classification`: three items for a `CRITICAL` finding, two for a `MAJOR`, and one for a `MINOR`. Too few items is `ERR_EFFECTIVENESS_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`.

A `PASS` writes `verified_by_assessor_id`, `verified_evidence_count`, an `effectiveness_outcome` of `PASS` and a `closed_on` at the current logical-clock date, moving the finding `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED → CLOSED` and bumping its `version`. The ledger then redistributes: the cycle's `nc_blocking_open_count` decreases by 1 and `nc_closed_count` increases by 1 while `nc_total_count` stays put, and the cycle's `version` is bumped. If that closure was the last blocking finding — the cycle is in `NC_OPEN` and `nc_blocking_open_count` has reached 0 — the cycle advances `NC_OPEN → DECISION_PENDING` and a decision record is created in `status` `VOTING` for the cycle's current `decision_round` with an empty ballot list; a record for that round is created only when no record of that round is already `VOTING`. That record's creation in turn recomputes the applicant organization's `risk_score` under the at-decision variant and bumps the organization's `version`.

A `FAIL` writes `verified_by_assessor_id`, `verified_evidence_count` and an `effectiveness_outcome` of `FAIL`, leaves `closed_on` null, moves the finding `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED → OPEN`, and bumps its `version`. Crucially the cycle's `nc_blocking_open_count` is left **unchanged** — the finding never left the blocking set, so nothing has to be added back — and the applicant must submit a further response.

**Escalation of an overdue finding.** An `AssessmentManager` escalates a finding that is in `OPEN` or `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, whose `classification` is `CRITICAL` or `MAJOR`, and for which the current logical-clock date is strictly after `due_on`. An attempt on or before the due date is rejected with `ERR_NOT_YET_OVERDUE`; `MINOR` and `OBSERVATION` findings are never escalable. Escalation writes `escalated_on` at the current logical-clock date, moves the finding to the terminal `ESCALATED`, and bumps its `version`; the finding stays inside `nc_blocking_open_count`, because an escalated finding is more blocking rather than less. Then a five-hop cascade runs, and every hop matters:

1. The applicant organization's `risk_score` is recomputed under the at-decision variant for the escalated cycle.
2. When the cycle's `origin_scope_id` is not null and that accredited scope is `ACTIVE`, the scope moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `escalated nonconformity` and `mra_notified` set to true.
3. That scope suspension moves the applicant organization to `SUSPENDED` and copies the same suspension reason onto the organization's `suspension_reason`.
4. That organization suspension sets `suspension_blocked` to true on **every** other cycle of the organization whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `TEAM_ASSIGNED`, `PLANNED`, `IN_ASSESSMENT` or `NC_OPEN` — every one except the escalated cycle itself.
5. For each of those frozen cycles, every plan in `DRAFT` becomes `CANCELLED`, and every witnessing session in `SCHEDULED` under any plan of those cycles becomes `ABORTED` with the witnessing assessor's `committed_witness_days` restored.

**Reopening a closed finding.** When later evidence contradicts an effectiveness verification, an `AssessmentManager` reopens a `CLOSED` finding by supplying a `reason`, provided the cycle is in `NC_OPEN` or `DECISION_PENDING`. This is a compensating action and it reverses exactly what the closure did: `reopen_count` increases by 1, `closed_on` and `effectiveness_outcome` are cleared to null, the supplied `reason` is written into `review_note`, the finding returns `CLOSED → OPEN`, and its `version` is bumped. The cycle's `nc_blocking_open_count` increases by 1 and `nc_closed_count` decreases by 1, with `nc_total_count` again untouched. And when the cycle had already reached `DECISION_PENDING`, the reopening pulls it back: the cycle moves `DECISION_PENDING → NC_OPEN`, its `decision_round` increases by 1, and the decision record of the previous round that was still `VOTING` is moved to `DEFERRED` — that record is written with an `outcome` of `DEFER` and a `decided_on`, and its ballot list is preserved as an audit record rather than discarded.

**The organization risk score.** Risk is a single weighted model with two variants, and it is recomputed at four moments: whenever a decision record is created, on the escalation of a finding, on the upholding of a complaint or appeal, and when a surveillance, reassessment or unannounced cycle is opened against a scope. The raw score for a cycle sums six weighted terms over the cycle's findings whose `classification` is not `OBSERVATION`: 5 points for each `CRITICAL` finding, 3 for each `MAJOR`, 1 for each `MINOR`, 4 for each finding closed late — that is, whose `closed_on` is strictly later than its `due_on` — 6 for each finding in status `ESCALATED`, and 2 for each qualifying upheld complaint. A single finding may contribute to its classification weight, the late weight and the escalated weight all at once; findings belonging to other organizations are never counted. The complaint term counts `ComplaintAppeal` rows of the organization whose status is `UPHELD` or `ESCALATED_MRA` and whose `resolved_on` falls on or after the current logical-clock date minus 730 days — a two-nominal-year lookback window; cases resolved longer ago than that drop out.

- The **at-decision** variant is the one used inside the cycle that produced the findings, at decision-record creation, at escalation and on an upheld case. It scores only the current cycle's findings, adds no carry-forward and no mutual-recognition term, and takes the smaller of 100 and the raw score.
- The **at-surveillance-planning** variant is used when a follow-on cycle is opened against a scope. It takes half of the granting cycle's raw score — the halving left unrounded — adds the raw score of the surveillance cycle's own findings, adds 3 points for each entry in the organization's `mra_escalation_count`, rounds the resulting sum **up** to a whole number, and then takes the smaller of 100 and that figure. The rounding up happens once, after the whole sum has been assembled, and never on the halved carry-forward term alone.

### 3.8. The Independent Decision Panel

A cycle that reaches `DECISION_PENDING` acquires a decision record, created by the system rather than by any person, in `status` `VOTING` with a `round` equal to the cycle's `decision_round` at that moment, an empty `votes` list, a `vote_count` of 0, and `outcome`, `granted_scope_lines`, `decided_on` and `decided_by_user_id` all null. The record is the panel's working document for exactly one round; a deferred round's record is retained and a new record is created for the next round.

**Casting a ballot.** A `DecisionPanelMember` casts one ballot by supplying a `vote` — one of `GRANT`, `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS`, `REFUSE` or `DEFER` — together with a `rationale`. Four conditions guard the ballot. The record must be in `VOTING`. The acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to an assessor whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED`. The acting assessor must hold no team assignment of any status on the cycle under decision, or the ballot is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_INDEPENDENCE_BREACH` — having been proposed and then recused does not restore independence. And the acting assessor must not already appear as the `voter_assessor_id` of an element of `votes`, or the ballot is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_VOTE`. An accepted ballot appends an object carrying `voter_assessor_id` (the acting assessor's id), the `vote`, the `rationale` and a `cast_at` instant to the append-only `votes` list in cast order, recomputes `vote_count` as the length of that list, and bumps the record's `version`. The record stays in `VOTING`.

**Deriving the panel's outcome.** Finalization resolves the ballot mix to a single outcome, and the order of the tests is normative. Let the number of ballots cast be the record's `vote_count`. Count the `REFUSE` ballots, the `GRANT` ballots and the `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS` ballots. **The refusal test runs first:** if twice the number of `REFUSE` ballots exceeds the total number of ballots — a strict majority to refuse — the outcome is `REFUSE`, and this holds even when grant ballots exist. With four ballots, three of them `REFUSE` and one `GRANT`, the outcome is `REFUSE`. Only if the refusal test fails is the grant test applied: if twice the combined number of `GRANT` and `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS` ballots exceeds the total number of ballots, the outcome is `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS` when at least one `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS` ballot was cast, and `GRANT` otherwise. If neither test produces a strict majority, the outcome is `DEFER`. Only the ballots on this record are counted; ballots on the `DEFERRED` records of earlier rounds are never re-counted.

**Finalizing.** A `DecisionPanelMember` finalizes the record, optionally supplying `granted_scope_lines`. The record must be in `VOTING`, the cycle must be in `DECISION_PENDING` and not frozen, and the record must have at least three ballots — the panel quorum — or the request is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. The finalizing member must themselves have cast a ballot on this record, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The cycle's `pinned_scheme_rule_revision` must still equal the programme's current `rule_revision`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SCHEME_VERSION_SUPERSEDED`. For a `GRANT` or `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS` outcome, every element of `granted_scope_lines` must appear in the cycle's `requested_scope_lines`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_LINE_NOT_REQUESTED`; the supplied order is preserved. And the shape of the grant must match its label: a `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS` outcome must carry a `granted_scope_lines` list strictly shorter than `requested_scope_lines`, while a `GRANT` outcome must carry the full requested list — either mismatch is `ERR_CONDITIONS_REQUIRE_REDUCED_SCOPE`.

A finalization that resolves to `GRANT`, `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS` or `REFUSE` writes `outcome`, `granted_scope_lines`, `decided_on` at the current logical-clock date and `decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, moves the record `VOTING → DECIDED`, and bumps its `version`. From there the cascade differs by outcome:

- On `GRANT` or `GRANT_WITH_CONDITIONS`, the cycle advances `DECISION_PENDING → GRANTED`, which is terminal. An `AccreditationScope` is then created in `status` `ACTIVE`, with `application_id` naming the granting cycle, `organization_id` and `scheme_id` copied from it, `scope_lines` copied from the record's `granted_scope_lines`, `effective_from` at the current logical-clock date, `risk_score_at_grant` frozen from the organization's current `risk_score`, `suspension_reason` and `withdrawn_on` null, `surveillance_risk_score` null, `mra_notified` false, and its two due dates derived as §3.9 describes. The applicant organization then moves to `ACCREDITED` with `active_scope_count` and `risk_score` recomputed and its `version` bumped.
- On `REFUSE`, the cycle moves `DECISION_PENDING → REFUSED` and **no** scope is created. The applicant organization moves back to `APPLIED` when its `active_scope_count` is 0, and otherwise stays `ACCREDITED` because it still holds accreditation elsewhere; either way its `risk_score` is recomputed.

A finalization that resolves to `DEFER` writes an `outcome` of `DEFER`, `decided_on` and `decided_by_user_id`, leaves `granted_scope_lines` null, moves the record `VOTING → DEFERRED`, and bumps its `version`. The cycle then returns `DECISION_PENDING → NC_OPEN` with its `decision_round` increased by 1. This is the panel deferral loop: the next time the cycle's `nc_blocking_open_count` returns to 0, a fresh record is created for the new round, and the earlier record remains as an audit trail. `DECIDED` and `DEFERRED` are both terminal for a record.

`REFUSED` is not quite terminal for a cycle. The one and only path out of it is an upheld appeal (§3.10), which returns the cycle to `DECISION_PENDING`, increases its `decision_round` by 1, and creates a fresh `VOTING` record for that new round. Decision-record lists may be filtered by `application_id`, `status` and `outcome`.

### 3.9. Accredited Scopes, Surveillance Cadence and Reassessment

An accredited scope is the durable output of the whole process, and its most consequential derived value is when the body must come back. **The surveillance cadence is compressed by risk; the reassessment cadence never is.** A risk score of 10 or less is the low band and leaves the programme's nominal `surveillance_interval_days` intact at a factor of 1.00. A risk score above 10 but not above 30 is the medium band and applies a factor of 0.75. A risk score above 30 is the high band and applies a factor of 0.50 — the body returns twice as often. `surveillance_interval_days_effective` is the programme's `surveillance_interval_days` multiplied by the band factor and rounded **down** to a whole number of days, and `next_surveillance_due` is that many days after `effective_from`. `next_reassessment_due`, by contrast, is simply the programme's `reassessment_interval_days` after `effective_from`, with no risk adjustment at any band. At scope creation the risk input is the `risk_score_at_grant` frozen at grant time; on each follow-on cycle it is the `surveillance_risk_score` produced by the at-surveillance-planning variant, and the two interval constants always come from the scheme revision pinned on the granting cycle.

**Opening a follow-on cycle.** A `SchemeManager` or an `AssessmentManager` opens a surveillance, reassessment or unannounced cycle against a live scope, supplying a `cycle_type` and optionally `requested_scope_lines` and `notice_days`. The scope must be in `status` `ACTIVE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The same notice-period rule as an initial cycle applies — an `UNANNOUNCED` cycle must carry exactly 0 `notice_days` and any other `cycle_type` at least 14, on pain of `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_PERIOD` — as does the unannounced-authority gate: an `UNANNOUNCED` cycle requires the pinned scheme revision's `unannounced_authority` to be true, or the request fails with `ERR_UNANNOUNCED_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. And the organization must still have no other non-terminal cycle for the same programme, or the request fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION`.

Opening the cycle creates a new assessment cycle in `SUBMITTED` exactly as §3.4 describes, except that its `origin_scope_id` is set to the scope it was opened against — which is what makes the scope's fate and the cycle's fate interdependent. The same request recomputes the organization's risk under the at-surveillance-planning variant, writes the result onto the scope as `surveillance_risk_score`, and re-derives the scope's `surveillance_interval_days_effective` and `next_surveillance_due` from that fresh figure. A deteriorating organization therefore tightens its own surveillance cadence at the moment the surveillance cycle is opened.

**Suspension.** A `SchemeManager` drives a scope's standing with a `to_status` and an optional `reason`; the same suspension is also reached without human intervention through the escalation cascade of §3.7. Moving an `ACTIVE` scope to `SUSPENDED` writes `suspension_reason` (1 to 300 characters) and sets `mra_notified` to true — a suspension is a notifiable event under the body's mutual-recognition obligations — and then cascades three hops: the applicant organization moves to `SUSPENDED` with the same reason copied onto it and its `active_scope_count` recomputed; `suspension_blocked` is set to true on every cycle of that organization whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `TEAM_ASSIGNED`, `PLANNED`, `IN_ASSESSMENT` or `NC_OPEN`; and for each of those frozen cycles, every plan in `DRAFT` becomes `CANCELLED` while every witnessing session in `SCHEDULED` under any of their plans becomes `ABORTED` with the witnessing assessor's `committed_witness_days` restored. The scope's own `version` is bumped. A frozen cycle rejects every further assessment action with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID` until the freeze is lifted.

**Restoration.** A `SUSPENDED` scope returns to `ACTIVE` only when the reason for suspension has actually been cleared: no finding in status `ESCALATED` may remain on any cycle whose `origin_scope_id` is this scope, nor on the cycle that originally granted it. With that satisfied, `suspension_reason` is cleared to null, the organization's `active_scope_count` is recomputed and the organization moves to `ACCREDITED` when that count is 1 or more, `suspension_blocked` is cleared to false on every cycle of the organization, and the scope's `version` is bumped. Restoration deliberately does not resurrect the plans it cancelled or the sessions it aborted.

**Withdrawal.** Withdrawal is terminal and reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, with an identical cascade in both cases. It writes `withdrawn_on` at the current logical-clock date, sets `mra_notified` to true, and bumps the scope's `version`. Every cycle whose `origin_scope_id` is this scope and whose status is not already `GRANTED`, `REFUSED` or `WITHDRAWN` is itself moved to `WITHDRAWN` with its `version` bumped — that identical transition applies from `SUBMITTED`, `TEAM_ASSIGNED`, `PLANNED`, `IN_ASSESSMENT`, `NC_OPEN` and `DECISION_PENDING` alike. Finally the organization's `active_scope_count` is recomputed, and the organization itself moves to `WITHDRAWN` when that count reaches 0.

Scope lists may be filtered by `application_id`, `organization_id`, `scheme_id` and `status`, and a scope's detail view exposes both due dates.

### 3.10. Complaints, Appeals and Mutual-Recognition Obligations

Two very different cases share one record type, distinguished by `kind`. A `COMPLAINT` concerns an accredited organization's conduct and needs only the `organization_id` it is about. An `APPEAL` contests an accreditation decision and therefore also requires the `application_id` of the decided cycle being appealed; conversely a `COMPLAINT` must either omit `application_id` or send it as null. Both kinds carry a `summary` of 1 to 800 characters — the substance of the complaint or the grounds of appeal — and a `filed_by_name` of 1 to 120 characters naming the natural person or body filing it. Filing is open to a `ComplaintOfficer` and to an `ApplicantContact`, who may file only about its own organization (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`). A case is created in `status` `RECEIVED` with `reviewer_assessor_id`, `resolution_note`, `resolved_on`, `mra_body` and `mra_escalated_on` all null. Case lists may be filtered by `organization_id`, `application_id`, `reviewer_assessor_id`, `kind` and `status`.

**Assigning an independent reviewer.** A `ComplaintOfficer` moves a `RECEIVED` case to `UNDER_REVIEW` by nominating a `reviewer_assessor_id`. The nominated assessor must have `qualification_status` `QUALIFIED`, and their conflict verdict against the case's organization must not be `BLOCKED` (`ERR_IMPARTIALITY_CONFLICT`). For an `APPEAL` there is a further and stricter test: the nominee must hold no team assignment in `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `RECUSED` status on the appealed cycle, and must appear as the `voter_assessor_id` of no ballot on any decision record of that cycle. A nominee who assessed or voted on the cycle being appealed is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_REVIEWER_CONFLICT`. On success `reviewer_assessor_id` is written and the case's `version` is bumped.

**Resolution.** A `ComplaintOfficer` resolves an `UNDER_REVIEW` case by supplying an `outcome` of `UPHELD` or `DISMISSED` together with a `resolution_note` of 1 to 500 characters. Both branches write the note and set `resolved_on` to the current logical-clock date and bump the case's `version`. They then diverge sharply. `DISMISSED` changes no counter on the organization at all. `UPHELD` increments the organization's `upheld_complaint_count` by 1 and recomputes the organization's `risk_score` under the at-decision variant — which is how a substantiated complaint tightens the organization's future surveillance cadence through the 730-day lookback window of §3.7. And when the upheld case is an `APPEAL` whose appealed cycle is in `REFUSED`, that cycle is reopened: it moves `REFUSED → DECISION_PENDING`, its `decision_round` increases by 1, and a fresh decision record is created in `VOTING` for the new round.

**Escalation to the peer body.** The accreditation body's international mutual-recognition arrangement obliges it to notify its peers when accreditation it granted is called into question. A `ComplaintOfficer` discharges that obligation by escalating an `UPHELD` case, supplying the `mra_body` name of the peer body (1 to 120 characters). Escalation is narrowly eligible: the case's `kind` must be `APPEAL`, its `status` must be `UPHELD`, and the organization must hold at least one scope in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status — a case that fails any of those three is rejected with `ERR_MRA_ESCALATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. On success `mra_body` and `mra_escalated_on` at the current logical-clock date are written, the case moves `UPHELD → ESCALATED_MRA` — a terminal state, alongside `DISMISSED` — and the case's `version` is bumped. Two cascades then discharge the obligation: the organization's `mra_escalation_count` increases by 1, which feeds the mutual-recognition term of the at-surveillance-planning risk variant at 3 points per escalation; and `mra_notified` is set to true on every scope of that organization whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are mounted under the `/accreditation` prefix, with the single exception of the clock-setting test surface. A request that creates a new resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; a request that modifies an existing resource, drives a state transition, or reads data returns HTTP 200 with the affected or requested resource in `data`. The clock-setting endpoint returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object.
