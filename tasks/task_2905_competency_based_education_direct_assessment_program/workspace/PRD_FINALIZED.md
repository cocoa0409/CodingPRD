# Competency-Based Education & Direct Assessment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A competency-based university does not sell seat time. It sells a **competency map**: a published inventory of demonstrable skills, each carrying a unit value, each assessed by a rubric, each either mastered or not yet mastered. Students subscribe to a flat-priced period and may attempt any competency they are eligible for as often as the academic-integrity and mentoring guardrails allow. That model is pedagogically clean and administratively hostile: federal financial aid, transfer credit, and satisfactory-academic-progress regulation are all written in credit hours, and a direct-assessment institution has to translate between the two vocabularies without ever letting the translation drift.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that translation. It owns seven concerns that are usually separate systems: the competency map and its prerequisite graph; versioned assessment blueprints whose effective windows tile the timeline of each competency; subscription-term enrollment with unlimited in-period attempts; faculty-mentor caseload assignment with a progress-contact cadence that gates further attempts; independent rubric scoring with inter-rater reliability monitoring and adjudication; academic-integrity casework for unproctored assessments; and the fiscal machinery of aid disbursement, unearned-aid return, and transcript translation. The single hard requirement running through all of it is auditability. Every mastered unit, every dollar of aid, and every credit hour on a transcript must be reconstructible from the records the API exposes, and every compensating action — voiding an attempt, returning aid, superseding a transcript — must restore the ledgers to the cent and to the unit, with no residue left behind.

Two design decisions shape almost every rule that follows. First, the same underlying quantity is deliberately reported through two different arithmetic paths: mastered units become credit hours by truncation per payment period for aid purposes, and by cumulative rounding for transcript purposes, and the two figures are reported side by side without being reconciled. Second, authority is split by segregation of duties across seven roles, so that the person who authors an assessment, the person who mentors the author, the person who scores it, the person who adjudicates its integrity, and the person who releases the money against it are never the same actor.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows it. Where §2 and §3 describe the same rule from different angles, they reinforce one another; they never contradict.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming that principal's role. Both headers must be present and non-empty. A header that is absent, empty, or consists only of whitespace is treated as absent, and the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Header validation is the very first thing the service does, before any payload parsing, path resolution, or business evaluation.

There are exactly seven roles, spelled exactly as follows: `Registrar`, `Student`, `FacultyMentor`, `Evaluator`, `IntegrityOfficer`, `FinancialAidOfficer`, and `PlatformAdmin`. If `X-User-Role` holds any other string, or holds a valid role that is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The authorization table in §4 is the definitive list of which roles may call which operation.

The `Registrar` is the academic records office. It owns the competency map, blueprint publication, enrollment, subscription terms, mentor assignments, and satisfactory-academic-progress checkpoints. The `Student` is the learner: they open and submit their own attempts and read only their own record. The `FacultyMentor` carries a mentee caseload, records progress contacts on a declared cadence, and endorses a mentee for a further attempt after repeated failures. The `Evaluator` is an independent rubric reader who scores attempts, may open an integrity case on a suspicious artifact, and may send an attempt back for revision, but never touches enrollment, aid, or case resolution. The `IntegrityOfficer` investigates and resolves academic-integrity cases for unproctored assessments and applies sanctions, but may not score, may not enrol, may not move money, and may not resolve a case they themselves opened. The `FinancialAidOfficer` schedules, releases, holds, returns, and cancels federal aid against the credit-hour equivalency and may close a term for aid purposes. The `PlatformAdmin` is an operational administrator: it registers mentors and evaluators, changes their availability or certification, drives the logical clock, and reads everything, while holding no academic, integrity, or fiscal authority.

Three of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is a persisted row identifier and ownership checks compare a resource's foreign key against it. When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a student compare that resource's subject student identifier to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `FacultyMentor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Mentor` row; a faculty mentor may only act on a mentor-assignment record whose `mentor_id` equals `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Evaluator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Evaluator` row; an evaluator may only submit a rubric score whose `evaluator_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

The remaining four roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `Registrar`, `IntegrityOfficer`, `FinancialAidOfficer`, or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server may record it for audit — an integrity case records the opening operator in `opened_by_user_id` and the resolving operator in `resolved_by_user_id`, and a satisfactory-academic-progress checkpoint records the acting registrar in `reviewed_by_user_id` — but it performs no existence check on the value.

Ownership enforcement is a distinct failure mode from role authorization. When the role is permitted to call the endpoint but the acting principal does not own the resource in question, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership is enforced on nine operations: reading a single student record, reading a student's progress summary, reading a single assessment attempt, opening an assessment attempt, submitting an attempt, recording a rubric score, recording a mentor progress contact, recording a mentor endorsement, and reading a single transcript record. In each case the comparison is the one described above for that role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10 when the caller omits it, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the ordered result set: its default is 0 and it may not be negative. A `limit` above 100, a `limit` below 1, or a negative `offset` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List endpoints set `response_type` to `array`. Inside `data` they return an object carrying `items` — the array of resource objects for the requested page — together with `total_count`, the number of rows matching the caller's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page. The same `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` values are also present in the `meta` block described in §2.2.

The default sort order for a list endpoint is `created_at` ascending. Several collections deviate from that default because their domain has a more natural reading order, and the deviations are part of the contract: programs and transcript records are returned newest first, by `created_at` descending; competencies are ordered by `code` ascending; assessment blueprints are ordered by `effective_from_utc` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; subscription terms are ordered by `term_start_utc` ascending; mentors are ordered by `current_caseload` ascending then `created_at` ascending, deliberately mirroring the automatic-selection ranking described in §3.4; mentor assignments are ordered by `assigned_at_utc` descending; and evaluator scores are ordered by `reader_ordinal` ascending. Students, evaluators, assessment attempts, integrity cases, satisfactory-academic-progress reviews, and aid disbursements all use the `created_at` ascending default.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification reads a single server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Its value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until an operator sets it the clock reads that instant; the service must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrative capability lets an operator set the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, and a companion read exposes the clock's present value as `current_utc`. Moving the clock backwards is permitted.

Setting the clock is not an inert bookkeeping operation. Every field whose value is derived from the clock is re-evaluated after the clock moves: a mentor assignment's `cadence_status` and `overdue_days` are recomputed against the new instant, and each competency's `effective_blueprint_id` is re-resolved against the blueprint window that now contains the clock. Wherever this document says "the logical clock" or "the current instant", it means the value the clock holds at the moment the request is evaluated.

All instants exchanged with this API are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. An instant that cannot be parsed as such is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. All day-based and hour-based arithmetic in this document uses fixed conversions: 86,400 seconds to a day and 3,600 seconds to an hour.

### 2.5. Flat Collection Topology and Identifier Resolution

The API is deliberately flat. Every entity is a peer collection addressed directly under the root — programs, competencies, assessment blueprints, students, subscription terms, mentors, mentor assignments, evaluators, assessment attempts, evaluator scores, integrity cases, satisfactory-academic-progress reviews, aid disbursements, and transcript records. Relationships are never expressed by nesting one collection inside another in the URL; they are carried as identifier fields in request bodies. The only sub-paths that exist are action sub-paths that operate on the resource named by the preceding `{id}` segment.

Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and each unresolved identifier yields the 404 error belonging to the entity that segment addresses: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPETENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLUEPRINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAP_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_NOT_FOUND`. Because the topology is flat, an action path carries exactly one `{id}` segment, so left-to-right resolution reduces in practice to resolving that one identifier before anything else about the request is considered.

Foreign keys arriving in a request body are subject to the same discipline, one level later in the precedence order. They are resolved in the order the fields are declared for that operation, and each unresolved reference yields the referenced entity's own 404 code — a `program_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, a `student_id` yields `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, a `competency_id` or an unresolvable `prerequisite_competency_id` yields `ERR_COMPETENCY_NOT_FOUND`, an explicit `mentor_id` on an assignment create yields `ERR_MENTOR_NOT_FOUND`, a `term_id` yields `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, an `attempt_id` yields `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND`, and an `evaluator_id` yields `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies to every creation operation in the system: competency creation, blueprint publication, student enrollment, subscription-term opening, mentor-assignment creation, evaluator registration, attempt creation, rubric scoring, integrity-case opening, satisfactory-academic-progress checkpoints, aid scheduling, and transcript creation.

### 2.6. Common Resource Properties and Append-Only Audit Rows

Almost every resource in the system carries four system-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was first written, with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent modification, in the same format. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row, including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade rather than directly by the caller.

There is exactly one exception, and it matters because a client cannot ask for what does not exist. A satisfactory-academic-progress review is an append-only audit event: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, it is never modified after it is written, and no operation exists that would modify it. Every other resource — programs, competencies, assessment blueprints, students, subscription terms, mentors, mentor assignments, evaluators, assessment attempts, evaluator scores, integrity cases, aid disbursements, and transcript records — carries all four properties.

Because `version` is the currency of the concurrency rules in §2.9, the phrase "increments the version" is used throughout §3 with a precise meaning: it adds exactly 1 to that row's `version` and refreshes that row's `updated_at` to the logical clock. A recomputation that leaves every stored field byte-identical is not a mutation and does not increment `version`; §3.8 documents the one checkpoint outcome where this distinction is directly observable.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Monetary Representation

Money never appears as a decimal in this system. Every monetary quantity — `tuition_cents`, `scheduled_amount_cents`, `outstanding_amount_cents`, `disbursed_amount_cents`, `retained_amount_cents`, `returned_amount_cents`, `cancelled_amount_cents`, `unearned_aid_cents`, `school_return_share_cents`, `student_return_share_cents`, `institutional_refund_cents`, `net_institutional_charge_cents`, `aid_scheduled_total_cents`, `aid_retained_total_cents`, and `aid_returned_total_cents` — is an integer number of cents. There is no fractional cent anywhere in the ledger, and no monetary field is ever transported as a string.

Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the method is **round half up**: the value is rounded to the stated number of decimal places, with a tie rounded away from zero. Rounding is applied exactly once, at the point the formula names, never repeatedly during accumulation. The scales are fixed per field and are part of the contract:

- Rubric and mastery percentages carry 2 decimal places: `weighted_score_pct`, `final_score_pct`, `pace_pct`, `irr_agreement_rate_pct`, `mastery_threshold_pct`, `effective_mastery_threshold_pct`, `irr_tolerance_pct`, and `irr_suspend_rate_pct`.
- A term's `earned_pct` carries 4 decimal places, as does the program policy value `aid_earned_pct_full_threshold` it is compared against.
- `units_per_credit_hour` carries 1 decimal place; a competency's `unit_value` carries 2; `critical_dimension_min_ratio` carries 2; a rubric dimension's `weight` carries 4; a submitted dimension `score` carries 2.
- Credit-hour figures split by purpose: `translated_credit_hours`, `cumulative_credit_equivalency`, and `credit_equivalency_variance` carry 1 decimal place, while the per-line `line_credit_hours`, the `line_credit_hour_sum`, and the `rounding_residual_credit_hours` carry 2.
- Every cent amount produced by a formula is rounded half up to a whole cent, that is, to zero decimal places.

Three quantities are produced by truncation rather than rounding, and the distinction is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. A subscription term's `term_days` and `attended_days`, a mentor assignment's `overdue_days`, and a term's `credit_equivalency_earned` are all computed by taking the floor — discarding any fractional remainder — because each answers a "how many whole units have completed" question rather than a "what is the nearest value" question. §3.9 explains why the floor on `credit_equivalency_earned` and the half-up rounding on `translated_credit_hours` are allowed to disagree.

### 2.8. The Program as the Policy Surface

Nearly every threshold in this system is a per-program policy value rather than a global constant, so two programs on the same deployment can run different mastery bars, different aid floors, and different mentoring expectations. Each program is registered with a `code` — globally unique, uppercase, between 3 and 16 characters — and a human-readable `title`, plus the following policy fields. All of them are optional at registration and fall back to the documented default.

- `mastery_threshold_pct` — the minimum final score percent required to master a competency in this program. Default `80.00`. It must be greater than 0 and at most 100.
- `units_per_credit_hour` — how many competency units equate to one federal credit hour. Default `3.0`, and it must be greater than 0.
- `sap_min_units_per_term` — the competency units a student is expected to master per counted subscription term, used as the per-term denominator of the progress-pace calculation. Default `12`, and it must be greater than 0.
- `required_unit_total` — the cumulative mastered units required to graduate. Default `120`, and it must be greater than 0.
- `irr_tolerance_pct` — the maximum absolute gap between two reader scores on one attempt before adjudication becomes necessary. Default `5.00`, and it may not be negative.
- `irr_min_paired_reviews` — the minimum number of paired reviews an evaluator must accumulate before the reliability demotion rule can fire at all. Default `3`, and it must be at least 1.
- `irr_suspend_rate_pct` — the agreement rate below which a certified evaluator is demoted. Default `70.00`, greater than 0 and at most 100.
- `attempt_cooloff_hours` — the hours that must elapse after an attempt is scored below the mastery bar before the same student may open another attempt on the same competency. Default `48`, that is, two full days; it may not be negative.
- `attempts_before_endorsement` — the number of failed non-voided attempts on one competency after which a mentor endorsement becomes a precondition for the next attempt. Default `3`, and it must be at least 1.
- `critical_dimension_min_ratio` — the minimum proportion of its maximum that a rubric dimension flagged critical must reach. Default `0.70`, that is, seventy percent of the dimension's ceiling; greater than 0 and at most 1.
- `cadence_gate_overdue_days` — the mentor-contact overdue days at which new assessment attempts are gated. Default `7`, one full week; it must be at least 1.
- `blueprint_notice_days` — the minimum notice, counted in days from the logical clock, that a superseding blueprint's effective start must observe. Default `14`, two weeks; it may not be negative.
- `aid_earned_pct_full_threshold` — the attended fraction at or above which a closed term is treated as fully earned for aid-return purposes. Default `0.6000`, that is, sixty percent of the period; greater than 0 and at most 1.
- `aid_min_credit_equivalency` — the minimum whole credit-hour equivalency a term must have earned before a scheduled disbursement may be released. Default `1`, and it may not be negative.

Two further program fields are not caller-supplied. `competency_count` is a computed count of the competency rows belonging to this program whose lifecycle status is `ACTIVE`, and it starts at 0. `published_at_utc` records the logical-clock instant at which the program was published and is null while the program is still a draft.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three creation operations in this system legitimately supersede an existing record as part of their normal semantics. Because "supersede" is the correct behaviour rather than an error, the only way to protect a client that is working from an outdated view is to require it to declare which version of the parent it believes it is acting on. Each of these three operations therefore takes a mandatory version field — it is not optional, and omitting it is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Publishing an assessment blueprint requires `expected_competency_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target competency. If that value does not equal the competency's current `version`, the publication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Without this guard a writer holding a stale competency view would silently close the effective window of a published blueprint it had never seen.

Creating a mentor assignment requires `expected_student_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target student. A mismatch against the student's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a new assignment ends the student's current active assignment and frees that mentor's caseload slot, a stale writer would otherwise reassign a mentee whose caseload situation it never observed.

Scheduling an aid disbursement requires `expected_term_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target subscription term. A mismatch against the term's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A term's `credit_equivalency_earned` moves on every mastery inside it, and a new award cancels any award already scheduled on the same term, so a stale term view means the aid officer is scheduling against an equivalency figure that no longer holds.

No other operation in the system takes a version field. An endpoint not named in the three paragraphs above must not accept, require, or check one.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Version Propagation

Consistency in this system is immediate. When a single request causes a chain of consequences, every consequence lands inside that same request, and a caller who reads any affected row immediately afterwards sees the settled result. There is no deferred sweep and no eventual reconciliation.

Two cascades in this specification are **transitive**, meaning each hop is caused by the mutation the previous hop performed rather than by the original request. The first is the mastery cascade: an attempt resolving to mastery awards units to the student, the changed student unit total may in turn trigger graduation, and graduation in turn ends the student's mentoring and closes the student's open term. The second is the integrity-void cascade: voiding an attempt voids its scores, the removal of the attempt's contribution reduces the student's and the term's unit totals, and the reduced term equivalency in turn holds or flags the term's aid. Both are described hop by hop in §3.8 and §3.7 respectively. The important structural point is that a hop fires because of the state the previous hop produced, so an implementation that only applies the first hop will pass no test that reads past it.

Every row a cascade touches is a mutation of that row: its `updated_at` advances to the logical clock and its `version` increments by 1. This includes rows the caller never named — the mentor whose `current_caseload` drops when an assignment ends, the student whose `mastered_unit_total` changes when an attempt resolves, the term whose `attempts_used` changes when an attempt is submitted, and the transcript record that is voided when the unit total moves beneath it.

Derived roll-ups are always recomputed from their source rows and never patched incrementally. A student's `mastered_unit_total`, `mastered_competency_count`, `cumulative_credit_equivalency`, `integrity_strike_count`, `aid_scheduled_total_cents`, `aid_retained_total_cents`, and `aid_returned_total_cents`; a term's `attempts_used`, `units_mastered_in_term`, and `credit_equivalency_earned`; a mentor's `current_caseload`; a competency's `mastered_by_student_count` and `effective_blueprint_id`; a blueprint's `dimension_count` and `attempts_bound_count`; an evaluator's `submitted_score_count`, `paired_review_count`, `agreeing_review_count`, and `irr_agreement_rate_pct`; and a program's `competency_count` are all defined as full recomputations over the rows that feed them. This is what makes every compensating action in §2.12 exact rather than approximate.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

A single request frequently violates more than one rule. The rejection a caller sees is always the violation with the lowest precedence number in the ordering below, and evaluation stops there. The ordering is not merely an implementation convenience — the domain deliberately places academic-integrity and compliance checks ahead of referential and graph checks, those ahead of temporal checks, those ahead of capacity checks, and capacity ahead of fiscal checks, so that the most serious reason a request was refused is the reason reported.

1. Both authentication headers are present and non-empty, treating a blank or whitespace-only value as absent — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles and that role is authorized for the target endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Resource ownership for the three entity-backed roles, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Applies to the nine operations enumerated in §2.1.
4. Every `{id}` path segment resolves to an existing row, left to right, yielding that entity's own 404 code. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
5. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, in declaration order, yielding the referenced entity's own 404 code. Applies to every creation operation listed in §2.5.
6. Request-body shape: every required field is present, every enum field holds a declared value, integers are non-negative, percentages fall within 0.00 to 100.00, ISO 8601 UTC instants parse, a term's `term_end_utc` is strictly after its `term_start_utc`, and the uniqueness constraints hold on a program's `code`, on a competency's `code` within its program, and on the `email` of a student, an evaluator, or a mentor — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every creating or mutating operation.
7. The referenced program is published before a student may enrol in it, before a prerequisite edge may be set on one of its competencies, and before an attempt may be created against one of its competencies — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Applies to student enrollment, setting a competency prerequisite, and attempt creation.
8. The target resource occupies the lifecycle status its operation requires. Publishing needs a draft program, or else `ERR_PROGRAM_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`. Retiring a competency, publishing a blueprint against it, or attempting it needs an active competency, or else `ERR_COMPETENCY_RETIRED`. Activation needs an applicant student, or else `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_APPLICANT`. Reinstatement needs a suspended student, or else `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_SUSPENDED`. Withdrawal and suspension need a student who has not reached a terminal enrollment state, or else `ERR_STUDENT_ALREADY_CLOSED`. Term activation needs a pending term, or else `ERR_TERM_NOT_PENDING`. Recording a contact, recording an endorsement, or ending an assignment needs an active assignment, or else `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Changing an evaluator's standing may not move a suspended evaluator, or else `ERR_EVALUATOR_SUSPENDED`. Issuing a transcript needs a record that has not been voided, or else `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_VOIDED`.
9. The subject student's `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Applies to five operations: opening a subscription term, creating a mentor assignment, opening an assessment attempt, scheduling an aid disbursement, and releasing an aid disbursement.
10. The student holds a subscription term whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose half-open window from `term_start_utc` up to but excluding `term_end_utc` contains the logical clock, and that term is the one the operation is booked against — otherwise `ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`. Applies to attempt creation and aid release.
11. The assessment attempt occupies the status its operation requires: draft to submit, or else `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_DRAFT`; under review or in adjudication to accept a rubric score, and under review or in adjudication to accept a revision request, or else `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`; revision-requested to reopen, or else `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_IN_REVISION`. Any write against an attempt already in the terminal voided state is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_VOIDED`.
12. The integrity case occupies the status its operation requires: open to begin investigating, or else `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_RESOLVED`; under investigation to resolve, where a still-open case yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and an already-resolved case yields `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.
13. The aid disbursement occupies the status its operation requires: scheduled or held to be released or cancelled, and disbursed to be returned. Releasing an award that is already disbursed yields `ERR_AID_ALREADY_DISBURSED`; releasing or cancelling one that is returned or cancelled yields `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`; returning one that is not disbursed yields `ERR_AID_NOT_DISBURSED`.
14. The transcript record is still a draft; an already-issued record yields `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_ALREADY_ISSUED`. Applies to transcript issuance.
15. The subject student's `sap_status` is not already `DISMISSED`, which is terminal — otherwise `ERR_SAP_ALREADY_DISMISSED`. Applies to recording a progress checkpoint.
16. The supplied parent version matches, per §2.9 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Applies to blueprint publication, mentor-assignment creation, and aid scheduling.
17. On submit, `integrity_attestation_accepted` is exactly true and `client_fingerprint` is a non-empty string — otherwise `ERR_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`. Applies to attempt submission.
18. Neither the attempt nor the subject student is under a live integrity hold: no integrity case for the student is open or under investigation, and the attempt's `integrity_hold` flag is false — otherwise `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD_ACTIVE`. Applies to four operations: attempt creation, attempt submission, rubric scoring, and aid release.
19. Segregation of duties. At scoring time the reader must not be linked to the mentor of the student's active mentor assignment, and no existing non-voided score on the attempt may have come from a reader sharing that same mentor link — otherwise `ERR_EVALUATOR_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. At case resolution the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `opened_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_CASE_RESOLVER_IS_OPENER`.
20. The same evaluator has not already scored this attempt, and the attempt has not already collected as many reader scores as its blueprint requires — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVALUATOR_SCORE`. Applies to rubric scoring.
21. The reader's standing permits scoring, and the adjudication slot additionally requires a certified reader; a provisionally certified or suspended reader may not adjudicate — otherwise `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Applies to rubric scoring.
22. No integrity case for the subject student is open or under investigation, because a transcript may not be issued while an integrity matter is unresolved — otherwise `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_INTEGRITY_PENDING`. Applies to transcript issuance.
23. The submitted `dimension_scores` key set equals the bound blueprint's rubric dimension code set exactly, with no missing and no unknown dimension — otherwise `ERR_RUBRIC_DIMENSION_MISMATCH`. Applies to rubric scoring.
24. The blueprint's rubric dimension weights sum to exactly one, every weight is greater than zero, every `max_score` is a positive integer, dimension codes are unique, and at least one dimension exists — otherwise `ERR_RUBRIC_WEIGHTS_INVALID`. Applies to blueprint publication.
25. A prerequisite competency belongs to the same program as the competency that depends on it — otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. Applies to competency creation and to setting a prerequisite edge.
26. Adding the prerequisite edge does not close a cycle in the competency graph, including the self-edge case and any longer cycle reachable by following the chain — otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_CYCLE`. Applies to competency creation and to setting a prerequisite edge.
27. Every competency in the target competency's transitive prerequisite chain is already mastered by this student — otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MASTERED`. Applies to attempt creation.
28. The student has not already mastered this competency — otherwise `ERR_COMPETENCY_ALREADY_MASTERED`. Applies to attempt creation.
29. A blueprint window covers the logical clock for the competency, so a rubric can be pinned — otherwise `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_BLUEPRINT`. Applies to attempt creation.
30. The attempt cool-off window has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_ATTEMPT_COOLOFF_ACTIVE`. Applies to attempt creation.
31. The requested subscription-term window does not overlap any of the student's existing non-cancelled term windows — otherwise `ERR_TERM_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Applies to opening a subscription term.
32. A superseding blueprint's `effective_from_utc` is strictly after the current published blueprint's `effective_from_utc` — otherwise `ERR_BLUEPRINT_WINDOW_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`. Applies to blueprint publication.
33. A superseding blueprint's `effective_from_utc` observes the program's notice period — otherwise `ERR_BLUEPRINT_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`. Applies to blueprint publication.
34. The student's active mentor assignment is not overdue past the program's gate — otherwise `ERR_MENTOR_CADENCE_OVERDUE`. Applies to attempt creation.
35. An explicitly named mentor has spare caseload and is available — otherwise `ERR_MENTOR_CASELOAD_FULL`. Applies to mentor-assignment creation, and only when `mentor_id` is supplied.
36. When `mentor_id` is omitted, automatic selection finds at least one eligible mentor — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MENTOR`. Applies to mentor-assignment creation, and only when `mentor_id` is omitted.
37. When the student's failed non-voided attempts on this competency have reached the program's endorsement threshold, an unconsumed mentor endorsement exists for the student-and-competency pair — otherwise `ERR_MENTOR_ENDORSEMENT_REQUIRED`. Applies to attempt creation.
38. The subject student's `sap_status` is `GOOD_STANDING` or `WARNING` — otherwise `ERR_AID_SAP_INELIGIBLE`. Applies to aid release.
39. The term's `credit_equivalency_earned` is at least the program's `aid_min_credit_equivalency` — otherwise `ERR_AID_CREDIT_EQUIVALENCY_SHORTFALL`. Applies to aid release.

Rule 32 is checked before rule 33 by construction of this ordering, so a blueprint whose window is both out of order and short of notice is reported as a contiguity failure rather than as a notice failure. Similarly, rules 35 and 36 are mutually exclusive branches of the same underlying capacity condition and deliberately report different codes: naming a full mentor explicitly is a conflict, while asking the system to choose from an exhausted pool is an unprocessable request.

### 2.12. Conservation Invariants

Two quantities in this system are protected by exact conservation identities that must hold after every single transition, including the compensating ones. They are stated here once because they constrain a dozen different operations in §3.

**The aid ledger identity.** For every aid disbursement, the scheduled amount always equals the sum of the outstanding, retained, returned, and cancelled amounts. Expressed per lifecycle state: while an award is scheduled or held, `outstanding_amount_cents` equals `scheduled_amount_cents` and the retained, returned, and cancelled buckets are all zero; once disbursed, `outstanding_amount_cents` is zero, `retained_amount_cents` equals `scheduled_amount_cents`, and the returned and cancelled buckets are zero; once returned, `outstanding_amount_cents` is zero, `returned_amount_cents` equals `school_return_share_cents`, `retained_amount_cents` equals `scheduled_amount_cents` less `school_return_share_cents`, and the cancelled bucket is zero; once cancelled, `cancelled_amount_cents` equals `scheduled_amount_cents` and the outstanding, retained, and returned buckets are all zero. Because every amount is an integer number of cents, this identity is exact and admits no rounding residue. The student-level roll-ups `aid_retained_total_cents` and `aid_returned_total_cents` are the sums of the corresponding per-award fields, recomputed from the rows rather than patched. One figure sits deliberately outside this identity: `student_return_share_cents` is the student's own liability to the federal program, is reported for completeness, and is not part of the institution's ledger.

**The mastered-unit identity.** A student's `mastered_unit_total` always equals a full recomputation of the sum of `unit_value` over the competencies for which the student currently holds a non-provisional mastered attempt, and `mastered_competency_count` always equals the count of distinct competencies contributing to that sum. Voiding a mastered attempt is the compensating action for the mastery award and must restore both figures to exactly their pre-mastery values: if mastery moved the total from zero to the competency's `unit_value`, the void moves it back to zero. No residue may remain in the student's total, in the owning term's `units_mastered_in_term` or `credit_equivalency_earned`, or in any transcript record — a transcript is voided outright rather than edited. Because a competency can be mastered at most once per student, `mastered_competency_count` can never exceed the program's `competency_count`, and `cumulative_credit_equivalency` is recomputed from `mastered_unit_total` every time that total moves, in both directions.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Competency Map and its Prerequisite Graph

A program begins life as a draft. When the academic records office registers one, it supplies the `code` and `title` together with whichever policy values from §2.8 it wishes to override, and the program is stored with `status` set to `DRAFT`, `competency_count` at 0, and `published_at_utc` null. Nothing may enrol against a draft program and no attempt may be booked under it; the draft period exists so the records office can lay out the competency map before students can see it.

Publication is the one transition on the program's own axis, moving `status` from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED`. It requires the program to be a draft — a second publication of an already-published program is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_ALREADY_PUBLISHED` — and it requires at least one competency to exist under the program with `status` `ACTIVE`, because a program with an empty map has nothing to assess. On success the program records `published_at_utc` as the logical clock, recomputes `competency_count`, and increments its `version`. `PUBLISHED` is terminal: there is no un-publishing, and from that moment students may be enrolled, prerequisite edges may be adjusted, and attempts may be created. Any of those three operations attempted against a program still in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

A competency is added to the map with a payload naming the owning `program_id`, a `code` that must be unique within that program, a `title`, a positive `unit_value` carrying two decimal places, and a `level` drawn from `FOUNDATION`, `CORE`, or `CAPSTONE`. Two optional inputs shape the competency's behaviour. A `mastery_threshold_pct` supplied here overrides the program's default bar for this competency alone; when it is omitted the stored value is null and the competency inherits. Either way the service publishes the resolved figure as `effective_mastery_threshold_pct`, which equals the competency's own `mastery_threshold_pct` when that is non-null and the owning program's `mastery_threshold_pct` otherwise. A `prerequisite_competency_id` supplied here creates the self-referential edge to another competency in the same map that a student must have mastered first; omitting it leaves the competency at the root of its chain. The new row is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `effective_blueprint_id` null because no rubric has yet been published against it, and `mastered_by_student_count` at 0 — a computed count of the distinct students holding a non-voided mastered attempt on this competency. Creation also increments the owning program's `competency_count` and its `version`.

The prerequisite graph is a directed structure that the service keeps acyclic and program-local. Each competency publishes a computed `prerequisite_depth`: the number of hops from itself to a competency whose own `prerequisite_competency_id` is null, following the chain upward. A root competency therefore has a depth of 0. Two rules protect the graph, both enforced at competency creation and again whenever the edge is later changed. A prerequisite must belong to the same program as the competency depending on it, or the request is refused with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. And the new edge must not close a cycle — neither the degenerate case of a competency naming itself, nor a longer loop reachable by walking the chain transitively — or the request is refused with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_CYCLE`.

The edge can be re-pointed after the fact by the records office, supplying a new `prerequisite_competency_id`; passing null in that field clears the edge entirely and returns the competency to the root of its chain. Re-pointing runs both graph checks again and then recomputes `prerequisite_depth` not only for the competency being edited but for every competency reachable through it, since a change at the base of a chain shifts the depth of everything above it. The competency's `version` increments. This operation is one of the three that require the owning program to be published.

Retirement takes a competency's `status` from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED` and closes it to new assessment. Three preconditions guard it: the competency must currently be `ACTIVE`; no attempt on it may sit in any live status, meaning none may be in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ADJUDICATION`, `INTEGRITY_HOLD`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; and no competency that is still `ACTIVE` may name this one as its prerequisite. A retired competency keeps its history untouched — existing mastered attempts and the units they awarded are never revoked by retirement. Retirement carries three side effects beyond the status change: the owning program's `competency_count` decreases by 1 and its `version` increments; every blueprint of this competency whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` has its `effective_to_utc` set to the logical clock and its `status` changed to `SUPERSEDED`, so the rubric timeline closes at the moment of retirement; and the competency's own `version` increments. Any later attempt to retire, to publish a blueprint against, or to open an attempt on a retired competency is refused with `ERR_COMPETENCY_RETIRED`.

The competency collection can be read in filtered form by owning `program_id`, by `prerequisite_competency_id` to find everything that depends on a given competency, by `status`, and by map `level`.

### 3.2. Assessment Blueprints and the Rubric Window Timeline

Every competency is assessed against a **blueprint**: a versioned rubric that declares how the work is scored, how many independent readers must weigh in, and from when the rubric is in force. Publishing one is a records-office act that names the target `competency_id`, the client's `expected_competency_version` as described in §2.9, the rubric `dimensions`, the `passing_rule`, the `required_evaluator_count`, and the `effective_from_utc` instant at which the new rubric takes over.

The `dimensions` value is an ordered list of between one and eight elements, and the order is significant because accumulation happens in it. Each element carries a `code` that must be unique within the list, a `weight` carrying four decimal places, a `max_score` that must be a positive integer, and a `critical` boolean marking whether the dimension is one a candidate cannot fail regardless of their overall total. The weights must sum to exactly one — no tolerance is granted — every weight must be greater than zero, and the list may be neither empty nor longer than eight elements. A violation of any of those conditions is refused with `ERR_RUBRIC_WEIGHTS_INVALID`. The service publishes `dimension_count` as the length of the list.

Two further inputs decide how the rubric resolves. `passing_rule` is either `WEIGHTED_ONLY`, meaning the weighted total alone decides mastery, or `WEIGHTED_PLUS_ALL_CRITICAL`, meaning the weighted total must clear the bar *and* no critical dimension may have fallen short. `required_evaluator_count` is either 1 or 2, and it is the single input that determines whether an attempt under this rubric is resolved by one reader or by a reader pair with adjudication behind it. The blueprint is created with `status` `PUBLISHED`, `effective_to_utc` null, and `attempts_bound_count` at 0 — a running count of the attempts pinned to this blueprint. It also carries a human-readable `version_label` of the form `BP-` followed by a number, where the number is the one-based ordinal of this blueprint among the blueprints of the same competency when they are ordered by `effective_from_utc` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

The blueprints of one competency form a **contiguous half-open tiling** of that competency's timeline. Each window runs from its `effective_from_utc` inclusive up to its `effective_to_utc` exclusive, and exactly one window — the currently published one — has `effective_to_utc` null and is therefore open-ended. The blueprint in force at some instant is the unique one whose `effective_from_utc` is at or before that instant and whose `effective_to_utc` is either null or strictly after it. Half-openness is load-bearing: at an instant exactly equal to a boundary, the newer window owns the instant and the superseded one does not. When no window contains the instant at all — which happens when the instant precedes the first blueprint's `effective_from_utc` — the resolution yields nothing, and the operation that needed a rubric is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_BLUEPRINT`. Each competency exposes the result of this lookup against the logical clock as its `effective_blueprint_id`; it is a derived cache of the window lookup rather than a declared reference, and it is re-resolved whenever the clock moves.

Publishing a superseding blueprint maintains the tiling in a pinned sequence of steps. First, the service locates the current blueprint of the competency whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose `effective_to_utc` is null. If there is no such blueprint, this is the competency's first rubric: any requested `effective_from_utc` is accepted, no notice period applies, and the new row is stored with `effective_to_utc` null. Otherwise, the requested `effective_from_utc` must be strictly later than the current blueprint's `effective_from_utc`; an equal or earlier instant is refused with `ERR_BLUEPRINT_WINDOW_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`, because it would leave either a gap or an overlap in the tiling. Only then is the notice period examined: the requested `effective_from_utc` must be at or after the logical clock advanced by the program's `blueprint_notice_days`, and a shorter runway is refused with `ERR_BLUEPRINT_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`. The ordering of those two checks is deliberate — a window that is both out of sequence and short of notice is reported as a contiguity failure. Finally the current blueprint's `effective_to_utc` is set to exactly the requested `effective_from_utc` and its `status` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, and the new blueprint is inserted as `PUBLISHED` with `effective_to_utc` null. The superseded blueprint's `version` increments, the competency's `effective_blueprint_id` is refreshed, and the competency's `version` increments.

One consequence deserves emphasis because it is the difference between an auditable institution and an unauditable one: an attempt already bound to a blueprint keeps that binding forever. Superseding a rubric never re-grades anyone. The candidates already in flight are measured against the rubric they were told about, and only attempts created after the new window opens are pinned to the new blueprint. Blueprints can be listed filtered by `competency_id` and by `status`.

### 3.3. Enrollment and the Subscription Term

A student record carries **two independent status axes**, and conflating them is the most common way to get this domain wrong. `enrollment_status` describes the student's relationship with the institution and moves through `APPLICANT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `WITHDRAWN`, and `GRADUATED`, of which `WITHDRAWN` and `GRADUATED` are terminal. `sap_status` describes the student's academic progress and moves through `GOOD_STANDING`, `WARNING`, `PROBATION`, and `DISMISSED`, of which `DISMISSED` is terminal. The two axes advance for different reasons and are never coupled: a reinstated student can be simultaneously `ACTIVE` and on `PROBATION`, and a suspended student's `sap_status` does not change merely because they were suspended. Only the progress-checkpoint operation of §3.8 writes `sap_status`.

Enrollment is created against a published program with a payload naming the `program_id`, the student's `full_name`, and their `email`, which must be globally unique across all students. The new record starts with `enrollment_status` `APPLICANT`, `sap_status` `GOOD_STANDING`, and every derived total at zero: `mastered_unit_total`, `mastered_competency_count`, `cumulative_credit_equivalency`, `integrity_strike_count`, `aid_scheduled_total_cents`, `aid_retained_total_cents`, and `aid_returned_total_cents`. The four lifecycle timestamps `activated_at_utc`, `suspended_at_utc`, `withdrawn_at_utc`, and `graduated_at_utc` all begin null and are each written at most once per occurrence of the corresponding event.

Activation moves `enrollment_status` from `APPLICANT` to `ACTIVE`, requires the student to still be an applicant, requires the owning program to be published, sets `activated_at_utc` to the logical clock, and increments the student's `version`. Suspension moves an active student to `SUSPENDED` with a `reason` in the payload; it sets `suspended_at_utc` to the logical clock, increments `version`, and carries one substantial cascade — every attempt of that student sitting in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `ADJUDICATION` is moved to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and every score on those attempts that was still submitted is voided with `voided_reason` set to `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Suspension deliberately leaves `sap_status` untouched. Reinstatement moves a suspended student back to `ACTIVE`, but only when no integrity case for that student is `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`; it leaves `suspended_at_utc` in place as the historical record and leaves `sap_status` exactly where it was, and increments `version`. The suspend-and-reinstate cycle may repeat any number of times.

Withdrawal is terminal for the enrollment axis and is available from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` with identical consequences. Its payload carries an `effective_utc` instant and a `reason`. The instant must be at or after the student's `activated_at_utc` and at or before the logical clock — a withdrawal cannot be backdated to before the student existed as an active learner, and it cannot be predated into the future. On success `withdrawn_at_utc` is set to the supplied `effective_utc`, not to the clock; every subscription term of the student whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is closed as `CANCELLED` with `closed_effective_utc` set to that same `effective_utc`, which runs the term close-out arithmetic of §3.9 on each of them; every mentor assignment of the student whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is ended, each ending decrementing its mentor's `current_caseload` by 1; every aid disbursement of the student that is scheduled or held is cancelled; and the student's `version` increments. Withdrawing or suspending a student who has already reached `WITHDRAWN` or `GRADUATED` is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The `sap_status` axis simply freezes at whatever value it held.

The commercial container for study is the **subscription term**: a flat-priced period inside which the number of attempts is unlimited. Opening one names the `student_id`, the `term_start_utc` and `term_end_utc` bounds of the period, and the `tuition_cents` charged for the whole period, which may be zero but never negative. The end bound must be strictly after the start bound. The student must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The term is created with `status` `PENDING` and a computed `term_days` equal to the whole number of days between the two bounds, obtained by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86,400 and discarding any remainder. Term windows are half-open, running from `term_start_utc` inclusive to `term_end_utc` exclusive, and the requested window must not intersect any of the student's existing terms other than cancelled ones; because the windows are half-open, one term ending at exactly the instant the next begins is adjacency rather than overlap and is permitted, while any genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_TERM_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

Activating a term moves `status` from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE`, sets `activated_at_utc` to the logical clock, and increments the term's `version`. Three preconditions apply: the term must be pending, or the request is refused with `ERR_TERM_NOT_PENDING`; the student's `enrollment_status` must be `ACTIVE`; and no other term of the same student may already be `ACTIVE`, because a student subscribes to one period at a time. Once active, the term begins accumulating a computed `attempts_used`, the count of attempts booked against it that are not voided. That figure is reported for transparency and is explicitly not a quota — inside the period attempts are unlimited in number, and the only things that throttle a candidate are the cool-off, endorsement, cadence, and integrity gates of §3.5. The term also accumulates `units_mastered_in_term`, the sum of `unit_value` over the distinct competencies mastered on attempts booked to this term, and `credit_equivalency_earned`, the whole credit hours those units are worth for federal aid purposes as computed in §3.9.

Closing a term is a records-office or aid-office act whose payload carries an `outcome` and an `effective_utc`. From `ACTIVE`, the outcome selects one of three terminal states — `COMPLETED`, `LAPSED`, or `CANCELLED` — and in all three the supplied `effective_utc` must be at or after `term_start_utc` and at or before the logical clock. Whichever branch runs, `closed_effective_utc` records the supplied instant, every attempt booked to the term whose status is `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` is voided, and the term's `version` increments. Where the branches diverge is the money, and the divergence is deliberate. `COMPLETED` is the *earned* branch: `earned_pct` is set to `1.0000`, `institutional_refund_cents` to 0, and `net_institutional_charge_cents` to the full `tuition_cents`, regardless of how few days the student actually attended; `attended_days` is still computed and reported. `LAPSED` and `CANCELLED` are the *unearned* branches: both run the full close-out arithmetic of §3.9, writing `attended_days`, `earned_pct`, `institutional_refund_cents`, and `net_institutional_charge_cents`, and both apply the identical aid treatment described there. A `COMPLETED` or `LAPSED` close moves any scheduled award on the term to `HELD` with `hold_reason` `TERM_CLOSED`, while a `CANCELLED` close from `ACTIVE` behaves like `LAPSED` for aid purposes.

There is one further close path, from `PENDING` straight to `CANCELLED`, for a term the student never started, and `CANCELLED` is the only outcome a pending term may be closed with — a `COMPLETED` or `LAPSED` outcome against a pending term is refused with `ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`. It has no attendance to measure, so it does not run the arithmetic at all: `closed_effective_utc` takes the supplied `effective_utc`, `attended_days` is 0, `earned_pct` is `0.0000`, `institutional_refund_cents` equals the whole `tuition_cents`, `net_institutional_charge_cents` is 0, every scheduled or held award on the term is cancelled with `cancelled_amount_cents` equal to `scheduled_amount_cents` and `outstanding_amount_cents` driven to 0, and the term's `version` increments. Until a term is closed, `closed_effective_utc`, `attended_days`, `earned_pct`, `institutional_refund_cents`, and `net_institutional_charge_cents` are all null. After a term is closed, no new attempt may be booked against it. Terms can be listed filtered by `student_id` and by `status`, and students can be listed filtered by `program_id`, by `enrollment_status`, and by `sap_status`.

### 3.4. Faculty Mentoring: Caseload, Contact Cadence and Endorsements

Direct assessment removes the classroom, so the mentor relationship is the only structural contact a student has with the faculty. A mentor is registered with a `full_name`, a globally unique `email`, a `max_caseload` greater than zero that bounds how many concurrently active assignments they may hold, and a `cadence_days` greater than zero that becomes the default contact rhythm for assignments created without an explicit one. A typical faculty appointment sets `max_caseload` to 25. The record starts with `current_caseload` at 0 and `status` `ACTIVE`. Availability can later be set to `ACTIVE`, `ON_LEAVE`, or `INACTIVE`; a mentor who is not `ACTIVE` keeps every assignment they already hold but is excluded from automatic selection.

Assigning a mentor to a mentee is a records-office act. The payload names the `student_id`, carries the `expected_student_version` required by §2.9, and may optionally carry a `mentor_id` and a `cadence_days`. The student must be `ACTIVE`. When `mentor_id` is supplied the pairing is explicit and the resulting record has `selection_mode` `EXPLICIT`; the named mentor must be `ACTIVE` and must have a `current_caseload` strictly below their `max_caseload`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MENTOR_CASELOAD_FULL`. When `mentor_id` is omitted the service chooses, and the record has `selection_mode` `AUTO_SELECTED`.

Automatic selection begins by filtering to the mentors who are eligible, which requires both that `status` is `ACTIVE` and that `current_caseload` is strictly less than `max_caseload` — a mentor exactly at their ceiling is not eligible. The eligible pool is then ranked by four criteria applied in order until one mentor remains: ascending `current_caseload`, so the least-loaded mentor wins; then descending `max_caseload`, preferring the mentor with the most headroom; then ascending `cadence_days`, preferring the most attentive contact rhythm; and finally ascending `created_at` as the deterministic last tie-break. The first mentor after ranking is assigned. When the eligible pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MENTOR`. Note that the two paths report different codes for what is arguably the same capacity shortage — naming a full mentor is treated as a conflict against a specific resource, while asking the service to choose from an exhausted pool is treated as an unprocessable request — and this asymmetry is intentional.

The created assignment stores its own `cadence_days`: the value supplied in the request when one was supplied, and otherwise the `cadence_days` of the mentor who was chosen. It records `assigned_at_utc` as the logical clock, starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `contact_count` at 0, `endorsement_count` at 0, `endorsed_competency_ids` as an empty array, `last_contact_utc` null, and `ended_at_utc` null. Its `next_contact_due_utc` is the assignment instant advanced by `cadence_days` days, its `cadence_status` is `COMPLIANT`, and its `overdue_days` is 0. Creating the assignment also supersedes whatever came before: the student's prior active assignment, if any, is ended and that mentor's `current_caseload` decreases by 1, while the newly chosen mentor's `current_caseload` increases by 1 and their `version` increments. The student's `version` also increments. The invariant behind all of this is caseload-slot conservation — a mentor's `current_caseload` always equals the number of assignments naming them whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, before and after every one of these operations, and it never falls below 0.

The cadence rule is a simple decay curve with a hard boundary. An assignment's `next_contact_due_utc` is its `last_contact_utc` advanced by `cadence_days` days when a contact has been recorded, and its `assigned_at_utc` advanced by `cadence_days` days when none has. Its `cadence_status` is `COMPLIANT` while the logical clock is at or before `next_contact_due_utc` and becomes `OVERDUE` once the clock is strictly past it, so an instant exactly equal to the due instant is still compliant. Its `overdue_days` is 0 while compliant, and otherwise the whole number of days by which the clock has passed the due instant, obtained by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86,400 and discarding the remainder. All three fields are clock-derived, so they are recomputed on every read and whenever the clock is moved.

Recording a progress contact is a self-transition: the assignment stays `ACTIVE` while its cadence clock resets. The payload may carry an optional `note`. Three preconditions apply: the assignment must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; when the caller acts as `FacultyMentor` their `X-User-ID` must equal the assignment's `mentor_id`; and the mentee's `enrollment_status` must be either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, because mentoring a suspended student is precisely how a suspension gets resolved. On success `last_contact_utc` becomes the logical clock, `contact_count` increases by 1, `next_contact_due_utc` is recomputed from the new anchor, `cadence_status` returns to `COMPLIANT`, `overdue_days` returns to 0, and the assignment's `version` increments.

The second self-transition on an active assignment is the **endorsement**, the mechanism by which a mentor unlocks a further attempt for a mentee who has repeatedly fallen short. Its payload names a single `competency_id`. Beyond the assignment being `ACTIVE` and the acting mentor owning it, the endorsed competency must belong to the mentee's own program and must be `ACTIVE`, and it must not already be present in the assignment's `endorsed_competency_ids`. On success the competency is appended to `endorsed_competency_ids`, `endorsement_count` increases by 1, and the assignment's `version` increments. The distinction between those two fields matters: `endorsement_count` is the total number of endorsements ever recorded on the assignment whether or not they were later used, while `endorsed_competency_ids` lists only the endorsements that no attempt has yet consumed, ordered by the instant each endorsement was recorded, oldest first.

Ending an assignment moves `status` from `ACTIVE` to `ENDED`, sets `ended_at_utc` to the logical clock, clears `endorsed_competency_ids` to an empty array so unconsumed endorsements do not survive the pairing that granted them, decrements the mentor's `current_caseload` by 1 and increments that mentor's `version`, and increments the assignment's own `version`. An assignment ends for four different reasons: an explicit records-office decision to end it without replacement; the creation of a superseding assignment for the same student; the graduation cascade of §3.8; and the withdrawal cascade of §3.3. All four apply the identical side-effect set.

Finally, the cadence is not merely advisory — it gates assessment. When a student's active assignment has an `overdue_days` that has reached the program's `cadence_gate_overdue_days`, no new attempt may be opened, and the request is refused with `ERR_MENTOR_CADENCE_OVERDUE`. The boundary is inclusive on the refusing side: at an `overdue_days` of exactly the gate value the attempt is already blocked, while one day less is still allowed. With the default gate of one week, a student whose mentor contact has been overdue for seven days is locked out of new assessment until a contact is recorded. Assignments can be listed filtered by `student_id`, by `mentor_id`, by `status`, and by `cadence_status`; mentors can be listed filtered by `status`.

### 3.5. Opening and Submitting an Assessment Attempt

An attempt is the unit of assessment work. Opening one requires only two inputs — the `student_id` of the candidate and the `competency_id` under assessment — and everything else about the row is decided by the service. Both the candidate themselves and the records office may open one; a candidate acting for themselves is subject to the ownership rule of §2.1.

Because attempts inside a subscription period are unlimited in count, the entire discipline of the model lives in an **eligibility ladder** evaluated in a pinned order. The first rung rejects redundant work: the student must not already have mastered this competency, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMPETENCY_ALREADY_MASTERED`. The second rung enforces the map: every competency in the target's prerequisite chain, walked transitively all the way to its root, must already be mastered by this student, and a single unmastered ancestor anywhere in the chain refuses the attempt with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MASTERED`. The third rung is the **cool-off**: the service finds the student's most recent non-voided attempt on this competency whose status is `SCORED_NOT_MASTERED`, and if one exists, the earliest permissible instant for a new attempt is that attempt's `scored_at_utc` advanced by the program's `attempt_cooloff_hours` — the same value the earlier attempt publishes as its `cooloff_until_utc`. The logical clock must be at or after that instant; the boundary is inclusive, so the attempt becomes available at exactly the cool-off instant rather than a moment later. An earlier clock is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_COOLOFF_ACTIVE`. With the default policy of two full days, a candidate who fell short on Monday morning may retry from Wednesday morning.

The fourth rung is the **endorsement gate**. The service counts the student's non-voided attempts on this competency whose status is `SCORED_NOT_MASTERED`; when that count has reached the program's `attempts_before_endorsement`, an unconsumed mentor endorsement for this student-and-competency pair is required, and without one the attempt is refused with `ERR_MENTOR_ENDORSEMENT_REQUIRED`. Creating the attempt consumes exactly one endorsement — it is removed from the assignment's `endorsed_competency_ids` and the attempt records `endorsement_consumed` as true — so the gate re-arms for the attempt after that. With the default of three, the fourth and every subsequent attempt on a competency the student keeps failing needs a fresh faculty sign-off. The fifth rung applies the two gates documented elsewhere: the mentor cadence gate of §3.4 and the integrity-hold gate of §3.7, in that order, after rungs one through four have all passed.

Voided attempts are invisible to the third and fourth rungs, and this has a consequence worth stating explicitly because it is a real administrative lever rather than an accident. Voiding a failed attempt simultaneously lifts any live cool-off derived from it and lowers the failure count, which can un-require an endorsement that the service was demanding a moment earlier.

Once the ladder is clear, the attempt is created with `status` `DRAFT` and two pinned references. Its `blueprint_id` is resolved by the window lookup of §3.2 against the logical clock and is never re-pinned afterwards. Its `term_id` is the student's `ACTIVE` subscription term whose half-open window contains the logical clock; when the student has no such term the request is refused with `ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The attempt's `attempt_number` is the one-based ordinal of this attempt among the student's non-voided attempts on the same competency, ordered by creation instant. Creation also increments the owning term's `attempts_used` and the pinned blueprint's `attempts_bound_count`, and because each of those is a mutation of the row concerned, the owning term's `version` and the pinned blueprint's `version` each increment as well. A fresh attempt carries `artifact_hash` null, `client_fingerprint` null, `integrity_attestation_accepted` false, `submitted_at_utc` null, `scores_received_count` 0, `final_score_pct` null, `score_variant` null, `is_provisional_score` false, `critical_fail` false, `integrity_hold` false, `held_from_status` null, `revision_count` 0, `scored_at_utc` null, `mastered_at_utc` null, and `cooloff_until_utc` null.

Submission is the candidate's own act and nobody else's. Its payload carries the `artifact_hash` — a client-computed hash of the submitted work — the `integrity_attestation_accepted` flag, and an opaque `client_fingerprint` identifying the device the work was submitted from. Because these assessments are unproctored, the attestation is not decorative: `integrity_attestation_accepted` must be exactly true and `client_fingerprint` must be a non-empty string, or the submission is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`. Beyond that, the attempt must be in `DRAFT` or the submission is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_DRAFT`; the acting candidate's `X-User-ID` must equal the attempt's `student_id`; the attempt's pinned term must still be `ACTIVE` with the logical clock inside its half-open window; and no integrity case on this attempt may be `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`.

A clean submission moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `UNDER_REVIEW`. It stores `artifact_hash`, `client_fingerprint`, and `integrity_attestation_accepted`, sets `submitted_at_utc` to the logical clock, recomputes the owning term's `attempts_used` and increments that term's `version`, and increments the attempt's own `version`.

The same submission may instead land in `INTEGRITY_HOLD`, and which of the two destinations it reaches depends entirely on the **submit-time integrity screen**. The screen fires on either of two signals. The first is a shared device: the submitted `client_fingerprint` already appears on a non-voided attempt belonging to a *different* student that was submitted within the 72 hours — three days — before the logical clock. The second is a duplicated artifact: the submitted `artifact_hash` already appears on a non-voided attempt belonging to a *different* student, with no time limit at all. When only one signal fires, the case is recorded with that signal; when both fire on the same submission, the recorded signal is the duplicated artifact rather than the shared device. On this branch the attempt still stores `artifact_hash`, `client_fingerprint`, and `submitted_at_utc` exactly as the clean branch does, but its `status` becomes `INTEGRITY_HOLD`, its `held_from_status` is set to `UNDER_REVIEW` to record where the review would have resumed, and its `integrity_hold` flag becomes true. Simultaneously the service raises an integrity case against the attempt with `auto_raised` true, `opened_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM`, `status` `OPEN`, `severity` `MEDIUM`, and `attempt_status_at_open` recording `DRAFT`. The attempt's `version` increments. The two destinations share one trigger and have deliberately divergent side-effect sets, so an implementation must evaluate the screen before deciding which state to write.

The revision loop closes the cycle. An attempt sent back for revision sits in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and reopening it returns `status` to `DRAFT`. Reopening requires the attempt to be in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_IN_REVISION`; when the caller acts as the candidate their `X-User-ID` must equal the attempt's `student_id`; and the pinned term must still be `ACTIVE`. Reopening clears `artifact_hash` and `client_fingerprint` back to null, resets `integrity_attestation_accepted` to false, clears `submitted_at_utc`, and increments `version`. Two things it explicitly does not do: it does not change `attempt_number`, and it does not re-pin `blueprint_id` or `term_id` even when a newer blueprint window has opened in the meantime. The full cycle is therefore `DRAFT` → `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REVISION_REQUESTED` → `DRAFT`, repeatable, with the original rubric intact throughout. Attempts can be listed filtered by `student_id`, `competency_id`, `blueprint_id`, `term_id`, and `status`.

### 3.6. Rubric Scoring, Reader Agreement and Evaluator Standing

Evaluators are registered independently of mentors, with a `full_name`, a globally unique `email`, a `certification_level` of either `ASSOCIATE` or `SENIOR`, and an optional `mentor_id`. That last field is the crux of the segregation-of-duties model: when the same human being holds both a faculty-mentor appointment and an evaluator appointment, the evaluator row points at the mentor row, and the service can therefore tell that a proposed reader is the candidate's own coach. An evaluator with no mentor appointment carries `mentor_id` null. A newly registered evaluator begins with `status` `CERTIFIED`, `irr_agreement_rate_pct` 100.00, `submitted_score_count`, `paired_review_count` and `agreeing_review_count` all zero, and `demoted_at_utc` null. Evaluators can be listed filtered by `mentor_id`, `status` and `certification_level`.

A score is recorded against an attempt by naming the `evaluator_id`, supplying `dimension_scores`, and optionally a `narrative`. The `dimension_scores` array carries one element per rubric dimension, each element pairing a `code` with a `score` carried to two decimals. Before any arithmetic happens the service runs a set of gates. The attempt must be in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ADJUDICATION`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, and a voided attempt is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_VOIDED`. No integrity case on the attempt may be `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD_ACTIVE`. The evaluator must not be `SUSPENDED`, and the adjudicating third slot additionally requires standing of exactly `CERTIFIED`; a `PROVISIONAL` or `SUSPENDED` reader in that slot is refused with `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The same evaluator may not score the same attempt twice, and the attempt may not collect more reader scores than the bound blueprint requires, on pain of `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVALUATOR_SCORE`. Then the conflict-of-interest gate: the reader's `mentor_id` must differ from the `mentor_id` of the candidate's own `ACTIVE` mentor assignment, and no existing non-voided score on this attempt may have come from an evaluator sharing that same `mentor_id`; a violation is refused with `ERR_EVALUATOR_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. The second half of that gate matters as much as the first: two readers who are both the same mentor cannot silently constitute the required independent pair. Finally, the submitted set of dimension codes must equal the bound blueprint's dimension code set exactly — no missing dimension and no unknown dimension — or the request is refused with `ERR_RUBRIC_DIMENSION_MISMATCH`, and each raw score must be at least zero and no greater than its dimension's `max_score`.

The **weighted rubric score** is then computed in a pinned order that must be followed exactly. First, for each dimension, divide the submitted score by that dimension's `max_score` to obtain a ratio, and keep that ratio at full precision without rounding it. Second, multiply each ratio by that dimension's `weight` and accumulate the products at full precision, walking the dimensions in the blueprint's declared order. Third, multiply the accumulated total by one hundred and round the result half up to two decimal places; that single figure is `weighted_score_pct`. Summing first and rounding once at the end is the normative rule. Rounding each dimension's contribution to two decimals before adding them is the wrong rule: it drifts by up to one hundredth of a percentage point per dimension and will disagree with the expected result.

The reader's own `critical_fail` flag is decided separately and from the raw ratios, never from the rounded total. It is true when at least one dimension marked `critical` has a ratio strictly below the program's `critical_dimension_min_ratio`. Strictly below is meant literally: a ratio exactly equal to the floor is a pass. Deriving the flag from the ratios rather than the weighted total means a strong overall performance cannot mask a failure on a dimension the program declared indispensable — a candidate can score in the nineties overall and still fail a lightly weighted critical dimension.

Each stored score records a `reader_ordinal`: 1 for the first non-voided score on the attempt, 2 for the second, and 3 for an adjudicating score. A score with ordinal 3 also carries `is_adjudication` true; ordinals 1 and 2 carry it false. Every score records `submitted_at_utc` as the logical clock at recording, begins with `status` `SUBMITTED` and `voided_reason` null, and increments the reader's `submitted_score_count`. Scores can be listed filtered by `attempt_id`, `evaluator_id` and `status`, ordered by `reader_ordinal` ascending.

How the attempt's own `final_score_pct` resolves depends on the blueprint's `required_evaluator_count` and on how many scores have arrived, and there are exactly four variants recorded in `score_variant`.

When the blueprint requires one reader, the sole score resolves the attempt immediately under `SINGLE_READER_FINAL`: `final_score_pct` is that reader's `weighted_score_pct` and `is_provisional_score` is false.

When the blueprint requires two readers and only the first has arrived, the variant is `PROVISIONAL_SINGLE_READER`. The attempt stays in `UNDER_REVIEW`, `final_score_pct` is written as reader one's figure, `is_provisional_score` is set to true, and `scores_received_count` becomes 1 — but no mastery outcome is recorded, no units are awarded, and no cascade fires. This is deliberately a self-transition on `UNDER_REVIEW`: the state does not change, yet the row's visible content and `version` do.

When the second of two required readers arrives, the service compares the absolute difference between the two readers' `weighted_score_pct` figures against the program's `irr_tolerance_pct`. A difference less than or equal to the tolerance is agreement, and the variant is `TWO_READER_MEAN`: add the two figures, divide by two, and round half up to two decimals. The boundary is inclusive, so a gap of exactly the tolerance is agreement. A difference strictly greater than the tolerance is disagreement, and the attempt moves from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `ADJUDICATION`; `final_score_pct` is cleared back to null, `is_provisional_score` is set to false, and `score_variant` is cleared to null. The provisional figure visible a moment earlier therefore vanishes rather than persisting.

An attempt in `ADJUDICATION` waits for a third score from a `CERTIFIED` evaluator who supplied neither of the first two scores. That score takes `reader_ordinal` 3, and the variant becomes `ADJUDICATED`: `final_score_pct` is the adjudicator's `weighted_score_pct` **alone**. It is not the mean of the three scores; averaging all three is the wrong rule and will frequently land on the other side of the mastery threshold from the correct answer. The adjudicator's judgement replaces the disagreeing pair rather than being blended with it.

Whenever the provisional figure is replaced by a final one, the final variant overwrites `final_score_pct`, clears `is_provisional_score` to false, and rewrites `score_variant`. Only a final variant may award units, so the unit total observed after the second score always reflects the final figure and never the provisional one.

Once `is_provisional_score` is false, the mastery outcome is decided. The attempt is mastered when `final_score_pct` is greater than or equal to the competency's effective mastery threshold — the boundary is inclusive, so a score exactly at the threshold is mastery — and, when the blueprint's `passing_rule` is `WEIGHTED_PLUS_ALL_CRITICAL`, additionally when no counted score has `critical_fail` true. Under `WEIGHTED_ONLY` the critical flags are ignored entirely for the outcome. Which scores are counted follows the variant: the sole score under `SINGLE_READER_FINAL`, both reader scores under `TWO_READER_MEAN`, and the adjudicator's score only under `ADJUDICATED`. The attempt's own `critical_fail` is set from that same counted set.

A mastering resolution sets `status` to `SCORED_MASTERED` with both `scored_at_utc` and `mastered_at_utc` at the logical clock, and triggers the mastery cascade described in §3.8. A non-mastering resolution sets `status` to `SCORED_NOT_MASTERED` with `scored_at_utc` at the logical clock, leaves `mastered_at_utc` null, publishes `cooloff_until_utc` as the logical clock advanced by `attempt_cooloff_hours`, and leaves every unit total untouched.

**Inter-rater reliability** is recomputed for both readers of a pair every time a pair completes, whichever way the pair resolves. An evaluator's `paired_review_count` is the number of attempts on which that evaluator submitted a score in reader slot 1 or 2 *and* the other of those two slots on the same attempt also holds a submitted score. Attempts still waiting for their second reader, voided attempts, and adjudicating scores in slot 3 are all excluded from both the numerator and the denominator. The `agreeing_review_count` is the subset of those paired attempts where the absolute difference between the two readers' figures is less than or equal to `irr_tolerance_pct`, again with the boundary counting as agreement. The `irr_agreement_rate_pct` is exactly 100.00 when `paired_review_count` is zero; otherwise it is the agreeing count divided by the paired count, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimals.

Demotion applies to a `CERTIFIED` evaluator when **both** of two conditions hold: `paired_review_count` has reached the program's `irr_min_paired_reviews`, and `irr_agreement_rate_pct` is strictly below the program's `irr_suspend_rate_pct`. Both conditions are required, and the volume condition is the one implementations forget: an evaluator with a rate of 0.00 over only two completed pairs is **not** demoted, because two is below the default minimum of three. Demotion sets `status` to `PROVISIONAL`, sets `demoted_at_utc` to the logical clock, and increments the evaluator's `version`. A `PROVISIONAL` evaluator may still take reader slots 1 and 2; only the adjudicating slot is closed to them.

Voiding an attempt removes its pair from the denominator and the rate is recomputed immediately, so a rate can rise again on its own. A demotion, however, is never automatically undone by a recovering rate. The only way back to `CERTIFIED` is an explicit administrative standing change, which leaves both `irr_agreement_rate_pct` and `demoted_at_utc` untouched — the demotion instant stays on the record as history. Standing can be set to `CERTIFIED`, `PROVISIONAL` or `SUSPENDED` by a platform administrator; attempting to change the standing of an evaluator who is already `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_EVALUATOR_SUSPENDED`, since suspension is terminal. An evaluator may be demoted and restored repeatedly.

Finally, a reader who judges the work unready rather than unsatisfactory can send it back. Requesting revision on an attempt in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ADJUDICATION`, with a `reason`, moves it to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and has one identical side-effect set from either origin: every submitted score on the attempt is voided with `voided_reason` `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `scores_received_count` resets to 0, `final_score_pct` and `score_variant` reset to null, `is_provisional_score` and `critical_fail` reset to false, `revision_count` increments by one, and the reliability figures of every affected evaluator are recomputed — which can itself lift or lower an evaluator's standing basis, since the voided pair leaves the denominator. Revision may not be requested while an integrity case on the attempt is `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`.

### 3.7. Academic Integrity Casework and Sanctions

Because direct assessment is unproctored, integrity casework is a first-class workflow rather than an afterthought. A case is opened against a single attempt by an integrity officer or by an evaluator who noticed something while reading, and its payload names the `attempt_id`, a `signal_type` of `SHARED_DEVICE`, `DUPLICATE_ARTIFACT`, `ATTESTATION_ANOMALY` or `THIRD_PARTY_REPORT`, a triage `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, and an optional `note`. The new case starts with `status` `OPEN`, `sanction` `NONE`, `auto_raised` false, `opened_by_user_id` taken from the acting officer's identity header, and `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolved_at_utc` both null. Its `student_id` is copied from the attempt rather than supplied, and its `attempt_status_at_open` records the attempt's status at the moment the case was raised. Cases opened by the submit-time screen of §3.5 differ only in carrying `auto_raised` true and the literal `SYSTEM` as `opened_by_user_id`. Cases can be listed filtered by `student_id`, `attempt_id`, `status` and `signal_type`.

Opening a case places an immediate hold, and `attempt_status_at_open` is what decides its shape. In every case the attempt's `integrity_hold` flag becomes true. Additionally, when the attempt was in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ADJUDICATION`, it moves to `INTEGRITY_HOLD` and `held_from_status` records exactly which of those three it came from, so the hold can later be lifted back to the right place. When the attempt was already resolved or awaiting revision — `SCORED_MASTERED`, `SCORED_NOT_MASTERED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` — the attempt is merely flagged: `integrity_hold` becomes true, `held_from_status` stays null, and the status is left alone.

The hold has teeth in four places, all reported with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD_ACTIVE`: while any case on an attempt is `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, that attempt cannot be submitted and cannot be scored, no new attempt may be opened for that student, and no aid disbursement for that student may be released. An unresolved integrity matter also blocks transcript issuance, reported separately as `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_INTEGRITY_PENDING`, and blocks reinstatement of a suspended student.

Investigation is the middle step: an `OPEN` case moves to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, the hold stays fully in force, and the case's `version` increments. A case that has already been resolved cannot be investigated, which is refused with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.

Resolution acts only on a case in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` — resolving a case still sitting in `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and resolving an already-terminal case with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. The payload carries an `outcome` and a `sanction`. On success the case's own `status` becomes the supplied `outcome`, so a resolved case reads `SUBSTANTIATED` or `DISMISSED` — the outcome is recorded in that `status` rather than published as a separate case field. Segregation of duties is enforced here as strictly as it is at scoring time: the identity of the resolving officer must differ from the case's `opened_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CASE_RESOLVER_IS_OPENER`. One officer may not both accuse and adjudicate. This also means an auto-raised case, whose opener is the literal `SYSTEM`, can be resolved by any officer.

A `DISMISSED` outcome forces `sanction` to `NONE`, sets `resolved_at_utc` to the logical clock and `resolved_by_user_id` to the acting officer, sets the attempt's `integrity_hold` to false, and — when the attempt is sitting in `INTEGRITY_HOLD` — restores it to whatever `held_from_status` recorded, clearing that field back to null. The student's `integrity_strike_count` is deliberately left unchanged: a dismissal leaves no mark. Restoration to the prior status is conditional on there being no *other* case on the same attempt still `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`; while a second case remains live the attempt stays held. An attempt whose hold began at submission therefore travels `DRAFT` to `INTEGRITY_HOLD` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, rejoining the normal review path exactly where it would have been had the screen not fired.

A `SUBSTANTIATED` outcome accepts a `sanction` of `NONE`, `ATTEMPT_VOID` or `TERM_SUSPENSION`, and its effects are layered. In every substantiated case the sanction, `resolved_at_utc` and `resolved_by_user_id` are recorded and the student's `integrity_strike_count` increments by one. The attempt's `integrity_hold` is cleared, and it is restored to `held_from_status` unless the sanction is `ATTEMPT_VOID`, in which case restoration would be pointless because the attempt is about to be voided. A substantiated case with sanction `NONE` therefore looks like a dismissal to the attempt but not to the student: the strike lands even though nothing else happens.

The strike itself is a second, independent consequence. Because `integrity_strike_count` is the count of the student's substantiated cases, a substantiation that brings it to two suspends the student — `enrollment_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, `suspended_at_utc` is set to the logical clock, and the suspension cascade of §3.3 runs, sweeping the student's in-flight attempts to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and voiding their live scores. This fires for any sanction, including `NONE`, and it is caused by the strike count rather than by the sanction. A first substantiation leaves enrollment alone; the second one costs the student their active standing.

The sanction `TERM_SUSPENSION` adds its own consequences on top: the student's `ACTIVE` subscription term is closed as `CANCELLED`, which runs the full close-out arithmetic of §3.9 on the term itself and applies the same aid treatment as any `CANCELLED` close from `ACTIVE` — a still-`SCHEDULED` award on that term moves to `HELD` with `hold_reason` `TERM_CLOSED`, while an award already `DISBURSED` is left exactly as it stands, because moving money back out is the separate, explicitly requested return operation of §3.9 and no term close performs it — and the student's `enrollment_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` directly, without waiting for a second strike.

The sanction `ATTEMPT_VOID` is the heaviest, because it runs a **four-hop transitive cascade** in which each hop is caused by the previous hop's mutation rather than by the original request. Hop one is the resolution itself, as described above. Hop two voids the attempt: its `status` becomes `VOIDED`, every score on it is voided with `voided_reason` set to `ATTEMPT_VOIDED`, `integrity_hold` becomes false and `held_from_status` returns to null. An attempt may be voided from any non-voided status. Hop three fires **only because hop two removed the attempt's contribution**, and only when the voided attempt was in `SCORED_MASTERED`: the student's `mastered_unit_total` decreases by the competency's `unit_value`, `mastered_competency_count` decreases by one, `cumulative_credit_equivalency` is recomputed, the owning term's `units_mastered_in_term` and `credit_equivalency_earned` are recomputed, every transcript record of that student in `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` is voided with `voided_reason` `UNIT_TOTAL_CHANGED`, and the reliability figures of every affected evaluator are recomputed because the voided pair has left their denominators. Hop four fires **only because hop three changed the term's equivalency**: when the term's recomputed `credit_equivalency_earned` has fallen below the program's `aid_min_credit_equivalency`, every disbursement on that term with `status` `SCHEDULED` moves to `HELD` with `hold_reason` `CREDIT_EQUIVALENCY_SHORTFALL`, and every disbursement already `DISBURSED` has `aid_review_required` set to true — money that has already left cannot be un-sent, so it is flagged for a human instead.

When the voided attempt was in `SCORED_NOT_MASTERED`, hops three and four are no-ops because that attempt contributed no units in the first place. What still happens on any void, including of a `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` attempt swept up by a term close, is renumbering: the `attempt_number` values on the student's remaining non-voided attempts for the same competency are recomputed so they stay a dense one-based sequence in creation order. A void therefore rewrites history in three visible ways at once — the ordinals renumber, any cool-off derived from the voided attempt disappears, and the failure count that drives the endorsement gate drops.

### 3.8. Mastery, Graduation and Satisfactory Academic Progress

Mastery is the only event that moves a student forward, and it fires a **four-hop transitive cascade** in which each hop is caused by the mutation the previous hop made. Hop one is the scoring resolution of §3.6: the attempt reaches `SCORED_MASTERED` with `scored_at_utc` and `mastered_at_utc` both at the logical clock. Hop two fires because the attempt entered that state: the student's `mastered_unit_total` increases by the mastered competency's `unit_value`, `mastered_competency_count` increases by one, `cumulative_credit_equivalency` is recomputed, the owning term's `units_mastered_in_term` and `credit_equivalency_earned` are recomputed, and every transcript record of that student in `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` is voided with `voided_reason` `UNIT_TOTAL_CHANGED` — a transcript is a snapshot and is invalidated rather than edited whenever the underlying total moves. Hop three fires because the unit total changed: when `mastered_unit_total` has reached the program's `required_unit_total`, the student's `enrollment_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `GRADUATED`, `graduated_at_utc` is set to the logical clock, and `sap_status` is forced to `GOOD_STANDING` — graduation is by definition satisfactory progress, and this is the one place other than a progress checkpoint where `sap_status` is written. The graduation boundary is inclusive: exactly reaching the required total graduates the student. Hop four fires because the student graduated: every mentor assignment of that student in `ACTIVE` is ended with `ended_at_utc` at the logical clock, each ending decrementing its mentor's `current_caseload` by one, and every subscription term of that student in `ACTIVE` is closed as `COMPLETED` with `closed_effective_utc` at the logical clock. Reaching the finish line therefore releases the mentor's capacity and closes out the commercial period as fully earned, in one request.

A provisional single-reader score awards nothing at all. Only a final, non-provisional resolution contributes units, so `mastered_unit_total` must always equal a full recomputation over the student's attempts that are currently `SCORED_MASTERED` and non-provisional, and `mastered_competency_count` must always equal the count of distinct competencies among them. These two figures are recomputed rather than incrementally patched, which is what makes the void path of §3.7 exact: mastery takes the total from its old value to the old value plus `unit_value`, a void takes it straight back, and no residue may be left behind in the student's totals, in the owning term's `units_mastered_in_term` or `credit_equivalency_earned`, or in any transcript. Because a competency can be mastered only once per student, `mastered_competency_count` can never exceed the program's `competency_count`. And `cumulative_credit_equivalency` is recomputed from `mastered_unit_total` on every change, in both directions.

The translation from units to credit hours is where this model meets federal regulation, and it is **deliberately computed two different ways that do not reconcile**. Implementations must produce both figures and must not attempt to make them agree.

The **aid variant** governs `credit_equivalency_earned` on a subscription term. It divides that term's `units_mastered_in_term` by the program's `units_per_credit_hour` and takes the floor — discarding any fractional remainder — and it is evaluated strictly per term, never across terms. Aid is certified per payment period, so partial credits earned inside a term are simply lost and are **not** carried forward into the next term. This is why the field is a whole number of credit hours.

The **transcript variant** governs `cumulative_credit_equivalency` on the student and `translated_credit_hours` on a transcript record. It divides the student's lifetime `mastered_unit_total` by `units_per_credit_hour` and rounds half up to one decimal place, evaluated cumulatively over the student's entire record rather than per term.

The consequence is arithmetically unavoidable: summing `credit_equivalency_earned` across a student's terms generally yields a figure **strictly less than** `cumulative_credit_equivalency`, because each term throws away its own remainder while the cumulative figure keeps them all. A student who masters units worth two and two-thirds credit hours in each of three terms earns zero aid credit hours in every one of those terms and eight cumulative transcript credit hours. Both numbers are correct for their own purpose, both are reported side by side, and summing the per-term floors to produce the transcript figure is the wrong rule.

The **progress summary** is a computed read that puts every one of these figures in one place. It reports `student_id`, `enrollment_status`, `sap_status`, `mastered_unit_total`, `mastered_competency_count`, the program's `required_unit_total`, and `cumulative_credit_equivalency`; then `aid_credit_equivalency_sum`, which is the sum of `credit_equivalency_earned` over the student's terms excluding cancelled ones, and `credit_equivalency_variance`, which is `cumulative_credit_equivalency` minus `aid_credit_equivalency_sum` rounded half up to one decimal — the variance is the reconciliation gap itself, published as a first-class number rather than hidden. It also reports the aid ledger roll-ups `aid_scheduled_total_cents`, `aid_retained_total_cents` and `aid_returned_total_cents`; the current mentoring state as `active_mentor_assignment_id`, `cadence_status` and `overdue_days`, which are null, null and 0 respectively when the student has no active assignment; and `open_integrity_case_count`. Every figure in the summary is computed at read time from the underlying rows.

**Satisfactory academic progress** is checked at discrete checkpoints. A review names the `student_id` and the `term_id` of the term at which the checkpoint is taken, and the resulting row is an append-only audit event: it carries `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and no `version`, and it is never updated once written. Pace here is measured in mastered competency units and never in credit hours — a deliberate choice, because the credit-hour figures are lossy in the aid direction and would understate real progress.

The review is computed in five ordered steps. First, `terms_counted` is the number of the student's subscription terms whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED` or `LAPSED` and whose `term_start_utc` is at or before the reviewed term's `term_start_utc`. Pending terms and cancelled terms are excluded from that denominator entirely — a term the student never started, or one that was cancelled, does not raise the bar. Second, `expected_units` is the program's `sap_min_units_per_term` multiplied by `terms_counted`. Third, `cumulative_mastered_units` records the student's `mastered_unit_total` as observed at the checkpoint, and `pace_pct` is exactly 100.00 when `expected_units` is zero; otherwise it is `cumulative_mastered_units` divided by `expected_units`, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimals. Fourth, `pace_band` is assigned from `pace_pct`: `GOOD` at 100.00 or above, `WARN` from 75.00 up to but not including 100.00, `PROB` from 50.00 up to but not including 75.00, and `FAIL` below 50.00. Each lower bound is inclusive and each upper bound exclusive, so a pace of exactly 75.00 is `WARN` and never `PROB`.

The fifth step is the one that carries the real complexity: **the new status depends on both the band and the student's prior `sap_status`**, and the same band produces different outcomes from different starting points. A `GOOD` band returns the student to `GOOD_STANDING` from any prior status. A `WARN` band produces `WARNING` from `GOOD_STANDING`, `PROBATION` from `WARNING`, and `DISMISSED` from `PROBATION`. A `PROB` band produces `PROBATION` from either `GOOD_STANDING` or `WARNING`, and `DISMISSED` from `PROBATION`. A `FAIL` band produces `DISMISSED` from any prior status. Two consequences follow. Replaying an identical pace figure twice escalates rather than repeating: a student stuck at a `WARN` pace goes to `WARNING` at the first checkpoint and `PROBATION` at the second, on exactly the same numbers. And recovery is immediate and complete: one `GOOD` checkpoint returns a student from `PROBATION` all the way to `GOOD_STANDING`, with no intermediate step.

Every review records both sides of that transition as `previous_sap_status` and `new_sap_status`, along with `evaluated_at_utc` at the logical clock and the `reviewed_by_user_id` of the acting records officer. The student's own `sap_status` is set to `new_sap_status` and the student's `version` increments **only when the value actually changed**. A clean checkpoint on a student already in `GOOD_STANDING` who lands in the `GOOD` band is a genuine self-transition: the review row is created and is the only observable outcome, while the student row is left entirely alone, including its `version`.

`DISMISSED` is terminal on this axis. A checkpoint against a student who is already dismissed is refused with `ERR_SAP_ALREADY_DISMISSED` and **no review row is written at all** — the append-only log records checkpoints that happened, not checkpoints that were refused. A review that lands on `DISMISSED` additionally cancels every aid disbursement of that student whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD`, setting each one's `cancelled_amount_cents` to its `scheduled_amount_cents` and driving `outstanding_amount_cents` to zero, and blocks any further disbursement. Reviews can be listed filtered by `student_id` and `term_id`.

### 3.9. Federal Aid, Term Close-Out and Transcript Translation

Aid is scheduled against a subscription term, which is the payment period. The request names the `student_id`, the `term_id`, the `scheduled_amount_cents` — which must be strictly greater than zero — and the `expected_term_version`, because the term's credit-hour equivalency moves on every mastery and an aid officer must not schedule from a stale view. The new award is created with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `outstanding_amount_cents` equal to `scheduled_amount_cents`, and every other ledger bucket at zero: `disbursed_amount_cents`, `cancelled_amount_cents`, `returned_amount_cents` and `retained_amount_cents`. Its `hold_reason`, `credit_equivalency_at_disbursement`, `unearned_aid_cents`, `school_return_share_cents`, `student_return_share_cents`, `disbursed_at_utc` and `returned_at_utc` all begin null, and `aid_review_required` begins false. Scheduling a second award on a term cancels any prior award on that term that was still `SCHEDULED` — one live scheduled award per payment period. Creation increases the student's `aid_scheduled_total_cents` and increments both the student's and the term's `version`. Awards can be listed filtered by `student_id`, `term_id`, `status` and `hold_reason`.

Releasing an award is the point where every other subsystem in this specification gets a vote. The award must be `SCHEDULED` or `HELD`; an award already released is refused with `ERR_AID_ALREADY_DISBURSED`, and one that is `RETURNED` or `CANCELLED` with `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The owning term must be `ACTIVE`, or the release is refused with `ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`. No integrity case of that student may be `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, or the release is refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD_ACTIVE`. The student's `sap_status` must be `GOOD_STANDING` or `WARNING`; a student on `PROBATION` or already `DISMISSED` is refused with `ERR_AID_SAP_INELIGIBLE`. And the term's `credit_equivalency_earned`, computed by the aid floor variant of §3.8, must be at least the program's `aid_min_credit_equivalency`, or the release is refused with `ERR_AID_CREDIT_EQUIVALENCY_SHORTFALL`. That last gate is the whole point of direct assessment aid compliance: money is released against demonstrated competency, not against enrolment.

A successful release moves `status` to `DISBURSED`, sets `disbursed_amount_cents` to `scheduled_amount_cents`, drives `outstanding_amount_cents` to zero, sets `retained_amount_cents` to the full scheduled amount, clears `hold_reason` back to null, sets `disbursed_at_utc` to the logical clock, and captures `credit_equivalency_at_disbursement` as the term's `credit_equivalency_earned` at that exact moment. That captured figure is a compliance snapshot: it is what the institution certified, and it never changes afterwards even when the underlying equivalency later collapses because an attempt was voided. The student's `aid_retained_total_cents` is recomputed and the student's `version` increments.

An award may also be held rather than released. A hold arises from the system rather than from an officer, and `hold_reason` records why, taking one of `CREDIT_EQUIVALENCY_SHORTFALL`, `TERM_CLOSED` or `INTEGRITY_REVIEW`, or null when the award is not held. The two the system produces automatically are `CREDIT_EQUIVALENCY_SHORTFALL`, when the term's `credit_equivalency_earned` has fallen below the program's `aid_min_credit_equivalency`, and `TERM_CLOSED` otherwise — which is what a term close with outcome `COMPLETED` or `LAPSED` produces for any award still scheduled on it. A held award keeps `outstanding_amount_cents` at the full scheduled amount: nothing has moved, the release is merely blocked. A held award may still be released later once the blocking condition clears, and doing so clears `hold_reason` to null and otherwise behaves exactly like releasing a scheduled award.

Cancellation is available on a `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` award, with an optional `reason`, and also happens automatically when a pending term is cancelled, when a student withdraws, or when a progress checkpoint lands on `DISMISSED`. It sets `cancelled_amount_cents` to `scheduled_amount_cents`, drives `outstanding_amount_cents` to zero, clears `hold_reason`, and increments `version`. Cancelling an award that is already `DISBURSED`, `RETURNED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`.

**Term close-out arithmetic** governs both the institutional refund and the aid return, and its evaluation order is pinned: every step consumes only the outputs of the steps above it, and reordering the steps changes the answer.

Step one computes `attended_days` as the whole number of days from `term_start_utc` to `closed_effective_utc`, obtained by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86,400 and discarding the remainder, then clamped at zero so that a close effective before the term even started yields zero attended days rather than a negative count. Step two computes `term_days` the same way from `term_start_utc` to `term_end_utc`; this can never be zero, because the end bound is validated to be strictly after the start bound at creation. Step three computes `earned_pct` as `attended_days` divided by `term_days`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and then capped at 1.0000 so attendance beyond the nominal end cannot earn more than the whole period. Step four applies the program's full-earning threshold: when the step-three value is at or above `aid_earned_pct_full_threshold`, `earned_pct` is set to exactly 1.0000. The comparison uses the step-three value, and the boundary is inclusive. With the default threshold, a student who attended sixty percent or more of the period has earned the whole period, and every remaining step then yields zero.

Step five computes `institutional_refund_cents` as `tuition_cents` multiplied by one minus `earned_pct`, rounded half up to the nearest whole cent — **computed from the gross tuition figure**. Computing it from the net charge instead would be circular, and applying step eight's factor to gross tuition is a different number; neither is the rule. Step six computes `unearned_aid_cents` as the award's `disbursed_amount_cents` multiplied by one minus `earned_pct`, rounded half up to the nearest whole cent. Step seven computes `net_institutional_charge_cents` as `tuition_cents` minus `institutional_refund_cents`. Step eight computes `school_return_share_cents` as the **smaller** of `unearned_aid_cents` and the net institutional charge multiplied by one minus `earned_pct` rounded half up to the nearest cent, and then computes `student_return_share_cents` as `unearned_aid_cents` minus `school_return_share_cents`. Taking the smaller of the two guarantees both shares are non-negative and that they sum to exactly `unearned_aid_cents` with no rounding residue whatsoever — the split is exact by construction rather than by luck.

Returning aid applies that arithmetic to a released award after its term closed short. The award must be `DISBURSED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AID_NOT_DISBURSED`, and the term must be `LAPSED` or `CANCELLED` with an `earned_pct` strictly below 1.0000 — a fully earned term has nothing to return. The return writes `unearned_aid_cents`, `school_return_share_cents` and `student_return_share_cents`, sets `returned_amount_cents` to the school's share, reduces `retained_amount_cents` to `disbursed_amount_cents` minus that same figure, sets `returned_at_utc` to the logical clock, moves `status` to `RETURNED`, recomputes the student's `aid_returned_total_cents` and `aid_retained_total_cents`, and increments both versions. The student's own share is reported as a liability but is not part of the institution's ledger — the school returns its share, and the student owes theirs.

The ledger identity of §2.12 must hold exactly after every one of these transitions, including the compensating return. In `SCHEDULED` and `HELD`, `outstanding_amount_cents` equals `scheduled_amount_cents` and the retained, returned and cancelled buckets are all zero. In `DISBURSED`, outstanding is zero, retained equals the scheduled amount, and returned and cancelled are zero. In `RETURNED`, outstanding is zero, cancelled is zero, `returned_amount_cents` equals `school_return_share_cents`, and `retained_amount_cents` equals the scheduled amount minus that share. In `CANCELLED`, outstanding, retained and returned are all zero and `cancelled_amount_cents` equals the scheduled amount. The student-level roll-ups `aid_retained_total_cents` and `aid_returned_total_cents` are always recomputed by summing the rows, never patched incrementally.

**Transcript translation** is the last piece, and it exists because a receiving institution needs credit hours rather than competency units. Building a transcript names the `student_id` and a `purpose` of `TRANSFER`, `INTERNAL` or `STUDENT_COPY`. The record is created in `DRAFT`, and creating it voids any earlier draft record for the same student with `voided_reason` `SUPERSEDED`.

The `lines` array carries one element per mastered competency, each with a `competency_code`, the `unit_value`, a `line_credit_hours` figure and the `mastered_at_utc` instant. Lines are ordered by `mastered_at_utc` ascending with the mastering attempt's `created_at` ascending as tie-break — ordered by **mastery** instant, not by creation instant, so a competency the student attempted first but mastered later appears later on the transcript. Each line's `line_credit_hours` is that competency's `unit_value` divided by the program's `units_per_credit_hour`, rounded half up to two decimals. The record's `competency_line_count` is the number of lines, `total_mastered_units` is the exact sum of the lines' unit values, and `line_credit_hour_sum` is the sum of the already-rounded per-line figures.

The headline figure, `translated_credit_hours`, is computed from `total_mastered_units` divided by `units_per_credit_hour`, rounded half up to **one** decimal place. It is computed from the summed units and never by adding up the per-line rounded figures; summing the lines is the wrong rule. The gap between the two approaches is not swept under the rug but published as `rounding_residual_credit_hours`, which is `line_credit_hour_sum` minus `translated_credit_hours`, rounded half up to two decimals — the artifact the receiving institution must reconcile, stated explicitly rather than left for them to discover. A transcript for a student with no mastered competencies is perfectly legal: `lines` is the empty array, `competency_line_count` is 0, and every total is zero.

Issuing a draft transcript sets `issued_at_utc` to the logical clock and moves `status` to `ISSUED`. It requires the record to be in `DRAFT` — an already-issued or voided record is refused with `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_ALREADY_ISSUED`, and a voided record specifically with `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_VOIDED` — and it requires that no integrity case of the subject student is `OPEN` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, or it is refused with `ERR_TRANSCRIPT_INTEGRITY_PENDING`. An institution does not certify a record while an integrity question about it is live.

Both `DRAFT` and `ISSUED` records are terminal-bound in one direction only: any change to the student's `mastered_unit_total`, in either direction, voids them with `voided_reason` `UNIT_TOTAL_CHANGED`. An issued transcript is not editable and is not recomputed in place; it is invalidated, and a fresh one must be built. Transcript records can be listed filtered by `student_id`, `status` and `purpose`, ordered by `created_at` descending so the newest attempt at certification is seen first.

Every collection sits at the root of the URL space; there are no nested collection prefixes and no version prefix.
