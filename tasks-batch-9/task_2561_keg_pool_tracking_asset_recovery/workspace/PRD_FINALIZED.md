# Keg Pool Asset Recovery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A stainless keg is the most expensive thing a beverage logistics business owns and the easiest thing it loses. MicroStar operates a shared keg float: rather than each brewery buying its own fleet, brewers lease kegs from pools organised by size, valve type and operating region, pay a price per fill, and are expected to return every shell. In practice a keg leaves the wash line, gets filled, travels to a wholesaler, sits in a retailer's cooler, and either comes back or does not. Between those points the only signal the business has is a laser-etched asset tag read by a handheld scanner, and the handheld is frequently offline when it reads it.

This document specifies the API for the pool operations, asset recovery and service platform that turns that thin stream of tag reads into an auditable account of where every keg is, what it earned, what it cost, and who owes for it when it disappears. The system must do four things at once and do them consistently inside a single request: project each keg's physical custody from its ordered scan stream, price each fill against the brewer's float agreement, measure how long each holder has kept the asset and pay or charge accordingly, and — when a keg does not come home — value the loss against both a replacement-cost and a depreciated-book basis and post the resulting chargeback to the correct party. Around those four functions sit the operational gates that keep a finite fleet from being over-committed: a pool posture that throttles issuance as available inventory thins, a per-agreement float posture that signals replenishment and suspends drawing rights, a cleaning and pressure-test certification cycle that no keg may bypass, and a theft investigation workflow that seizes kegs as evidence and claws back incentives already paid on them.

Three constraints shape the design. First, every derived quantity — pool census, pool posture, agreement posture, brewer chargeback balance, distributor incentive ledger — is recomputed inside the mutating request rather than by a background job, so a caller who reads immediately after writing sees the settled truth. Second, the platform is time-driven but must be reproducible, so all temporal reasoning runs against a settable logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Third, money and asset counts are conserved quantities: for every operation that commits an asset or issues a charge there is a compensating operation that returns the counters to exactly their prior values, and the specification states both halves of each pair explicitly.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Partners and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or blank, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the target operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The authorised roles for each operation are listed in the endpoint table in §4.

The five roles are `PoolOperator`, `Brewer`, `Distributor`, `ServiceTechnician` and `AssetRecoveryAnalyst`.

A `PoolOperator` is MicroStar's own pool operations desk. It owns the fleet, the pools, the float agreements, the issuance pipeline, the logical clock and the settlement side of chargebacks. When `X-User-Role` is `PoolOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `released_by_user_id` on a service cycle) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same is true of `ServiceTechnician` — the wash-line and pressure-test crew, whose `X-User-ID` is recorded on a cycle as `pressure_test_performed_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` — and of `AssetRecoveryAnalyst`, the loss, theft and recovery desk, whose `X-User-ID` is recorded as `created_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a loss assessment and as `opened_by_user_id` on a theft investigation. For these three roles the identifier is an audit and segregation-of-duties token, nothing more.

The two trading-partner roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Brewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Brewer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a brewer compare the resource's brewer-side foreign key to `X-User-ID` — specifically the `brewer_id` of a float agreement being read on its own path, the `brewer_id` of a loss assessment being disputed, the `misdelivered_to_brewer_id` of a reconciliation claim being answered, and the `brewer_id` supplied on a fill scan. No other brewer-side read is ownership-checked: a `Brewer` may read any single order, assessment or claim it can name, and may read every list endpoint open to it, without owning the rows returned. When `X-User-Role` is `Distributor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor` row; ownership checks compare the distributor-side foreign key — the `claimant_distributor_id` of a claim being answered, the `distributor_id` supplied on a distribution or return scan, and the `distributor_id` of an incentive accrual being read. For both entity-backed roles, if `X-User-ID` does not match any existing row of the corresponding entity the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (HTTP 403); if it matches an existing row but not the row the target resource points at, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Two segregation-of-duties rules cut across the roles and both raise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The technician who recorded a keg's pressure test may not be the person who releases that cycle back to float, and the analyst who opened a loss assessment may not be the person who resolves it. A third, related split is keyed on the role rather than the identifier: settling a chargeback is reserved to `PoolOperator` and reversing one is reserved to `AssetRecoveryAnalyst`, even though both roles can reach the chargeback-resolution operation, so no single role can both raise and erase a liability.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the range 1 to 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless an endpoint's own description states otherwise. For list responses, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded at creation from the logical clock. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments as described below.

Only `created_at`, `updated_at` and the envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` carry those three fractional digits. Every other instant the API returns — a scan's `scanned_at` and `recorded_at`, a keg's `dwell_started_at`, `distributor_custody_started_at`, `last_scanned_at` and `last_pressure_test_at`, an order's `dispatched_at`, a service cycle's intake, step and release instants, a claim's `evidence_scanned_at` and `closed_at`, an accrual's `triggering_scanned_at` and `paid_at`, an investigation's `hold_started_at` and `closed_at`, the trailing-window bounds reported by a loss-rate read, and the logical clock's own `current_utc` and `previous_utc` — is rendered to whole seconds, as `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, whenever its millisecond component is zero, and with exactly three fractional digits when it is not; supplied instants are held at millisecond resolution, so a client that sends `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` reads it back as `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. The one exception is a `last_pressure_test_at` that the server defaulted to the importing keg's own `created_at`: being a copy of `created_at`, it renders exactly like its source and therefore always carries three fractional digits, whereas a `last_pressure_test_at` the caller supplied, or one later taken from a service cycle's `pressure_tested_at`, renders in the whole-second form.

Two resource types are append-only records rather than mutable rows and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: a `ScanEvent`, which is an immutable tag read, and a `CommodityIndex` row, which is an immutable price posting. Neither has `updated_at` and neither has `version`; a client must not expect either field on them. Every other resource — `Brewer`, `Distributor`, `KegPool`, `Keg`, `FloatAgreement`, `IssuanceOrder`, `ServiceCenter`, `ServiceCycle`, `LossAssessment`, `ReconciliationClaim`, `ReturnIncentiveAccrual` and `TheftInvestigation` — carries all four common properties.

Version arithmetic follows one rule that is easy to get wrong. A single request advances each affected row's `version` by exactly one, no matter how many cascade links inside that request touch that row. Allocating an issuance order, for example, both writes the agreement's own counters and re-evaluates the posture of every active agreement on the pool; for the allocating agreement those are the same bump, so its `version` moves from 2 to 3, never from 2 to 4. Reads never advance a `version`.

The second half of the rule concerns cascade recomputes that change nothing. A recompute bumps the recomputed row's `version` only if it actually alters at least one stored field of that row. Importing a keg into a pool that already has an active agreement re-evaluates that agreement's `float_posture`, `kegs_at_brewer_count` and `pending_replenishment_kegs`; if all three come out identical to their stored values, the agreement's `version` stays exactly where it was and its `updated_at` is not refreshed. This is what makes a version held by a client survive unrelated fleet imports.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this system — dwell measurement, the trailing ninety-day loss window, pressure-test intervals, intake windows and the misdelivery claim window — is evaluated against a settable **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. Before any client repositions it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must not fall back to real time. A `PoolOperator` may reposition the clock by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports `current_utc` and `previous_utc`. Time may be moved forward or backward, and moving it never rewrites a stored timestamp: it only changes what "now" means for the next business request. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity and re-runs no calculation.

Because operators and handhelds supply instants of their own, the system refuses any supplied instant or date that lies in the future relative to the logical clock, with `ERR_SCAN_TIMESTAMP_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). This applies to a scan's `scanned_at`, a service cycle's `received_at`, an order's `dispatched_at`, an evidence hold's `hold_started_at` and an agreement's `effective_date`.

Elapsed time in days is always computed by taking the difference between two instants in seconds, dividing by 86400 and discarding the remainder — that is, flooring to whole days. A spell of twenty-nine days and twenty-three hours is twenty-nine days, never thirty. Day counts are never rounded half up. Instants themselves are compared at millisecond resolution.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integers in US cents. All rates and shares that are not whole percentages are expressed in basis points, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent, and basis-point values are themselves integers.

Wherever a computation produces a fractional value that must be stored, the value is rounded half up — a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero — to the precision the field declares. Money fields round half up to whole cents. The two percentage fields that carry decimals, `available_pct` on a pool and `on_time_return_pct` and `avg_dwell_days` on a distributor, carry exactly two decimal places, also rounded half up.

One discipline about *when* rounding happens is load-bearing throughout the incentive and exposure calculations: rounding is applied once per row, before any summation, never to an aggregate. A distributor's incentive ledger is the sum of already-rounded per-accrual amounts, and an investigation's stainless exposure is the sum of already-rounded per-keg values. Summing the unrounded values and rounding the total produces a different number and is incorrect.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Shared Parents

Three write operations draw on a parent row that other requests can legitimately move underneath them, and each therefore requires the client to declare which version of that parent it observed. In all three cases the field is mandatory — it is not optional and has no default — and a mismatch against the parent row's current `version` is rejected before any state change is attempted. Operations not named here take no version field at all and must reject one if the concept is invented by a client.

Allocating an issuance order requires `expected_agreement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `FloatAgreement`. Two orders on the same agreement may both legitimately be allocated while float remains, so no state precondition blocks the second allocation; the stale-version check is what forces the operator to re-read the agreement's `kegs_at_brewer_count` and `float_posture` before drawing more float. If the value does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Opening a loss assessment requires `expected_keg_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Keg`. A newer assessment legitimately supersedes a proposed one when fresh evidence arrives, so the second create is not blocked by a state precondition; the stale-version check is what stops an analyst from valuing a keg that has meanwhile been returned, quarantined or attached to a theft investigation. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_KEG_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Responding to a reconciliation claim requires `expected_claim_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `ReconciliationClaim`. Alternating counter-offers are normal product behaviour, so a responder who observed an earlier round would otherwise post an offer against a superseded number; the check pins each response to the round the responder actually saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. Request Admission and the Order of Judgement

A request can fail in many ways at once, and which failure the caller sees is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in a fixed precedence order, and the first check that fails determines the response; no later check may mask an earlier one.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right. When a keg is imported into a pool, the pool identified in the path is resolved before the request body is examined at all; when a nested resource is addressed, each ancestor must exist before its descendant is looked at. A path identifier that does not resolve produces the 404 belonging to that resource type: `ERR_BREWER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KEG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCAN_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`.

The full precedence ladder, from first-judged to last-judged, is as follows.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. The acting role appears in the target operation's authorised-role list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. For the entity-backed roles `Brewer` and `Distributor`, `X-User-ID` names an existing row of that entity — `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (403). This applies to every operation whose authorised roles include `Brewer` or `Distributor`.
4. Request shape: required fields present, JSON types correct, enum values inside their declared domain, integers inside their declared range, ISO 8601 instants parseable, dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and no conditionally forbidden field supplied — for example a `brewer_id` on a scan whose `party_type` is `DISTRIBUTOR`. Failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. On every list operation, `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
6. Every path identifier resolves, left to right — the 404 catalogue above.
7. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, judged in the field order the operation declares. List-valued foreign keys are resolved element by element in the order supplied, and are resolved *within* the scope named earlier in the same body: when a theft investigation is opened, each element of `keg_ids` must be a keg of the named `pool_id`, so a keg that exists but belongs to a different pool is reported as `ERR_KEG_NOT_FOUND` and the whole request is refused with nothing attached. This step guards agreement creation, order creation, scan recording, service-cycle booking, loss-assessment creation, claim creation, investigation creation and investigation status changes, and can raise `ERR_BREWER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KEG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND` (all 404).
8. Entity-backed ownership of the now-known target resource — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403), on every operation whose authorised roles include `Brewer` or `Distributor` and that names an existing resource.
9. The supplied `expected_agreement_version`, `expected_keg_version` or `expected_claim_version` matches its parent's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_KEG_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (412). This guards order allocation, loss-assessment creation and claim response respectively.
10. The target resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared: order `status` for allocate, dispatch and cancel; cycle `stage` for step recording and release; `assessment_status` for dispute and resolve; `chargeback_status` for chargeback resolution; claim `status` for a response; investigation `status` for a status change; keg `custody_state` being `IN_FLOAT` or `IN_SERVICE` for a service-cycle booking; and, on a fill scan, the keg's governing issuance order being `DISPATCHED`. Failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Whether the requested `scan_point` is legal for the keg's current `custody_state` is deliberately **not** judged here — that belongs to step 18 alone.
11. The dimension being moved is not already in one of its terminal states: order `CANCELLED` or `DISPATCHED`, claim `AGREED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, assessment `UPHELD`, `WAIVED` or `SUPERSEDED`, investigation `CLOSED_RECOVERED` or `CLOSED_WRITTEN_OFF`, keg custody `LOST`, `STOLEN` or `SCRAPPED`, agreement `TERMINATED`. Failure is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409).
12. The governing float agreement's `status` is `ACTIVE` — `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on order creation, order allocation and a fill scan.
13. The pool's `posture` is not `FROZEN` — `ERR_POOL_FROZEN` (409), on order creation and order allocation.
14. While the pool's `posture` is `RESTRICTED`, `requested_keg_count` does not exceed the pool's `restricted_max_kegs_per_order` — `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTED` (409), on order creation and order allocation.
15. The governing agreement's `float_posture` is not `SUSPENDED` — `ERR_FLOAT_SUSPENDED` (409), on order allocation and a fill scan.
16. The brewer's `status` is not `SUSPENDED`; `PROBATION` is permitted here and is instead expressed through `float_posture`. Failure is `ERR_BREWER_SUSPENDED` (409), on agreement activation, order creation, a fill scan and loss-assessment resolution.
17. The distributor's `status` is not `SUSPENDED` — `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED` (409), on a distribution or return scan and on an accrual payment.
18. The requested `scan_point` is legal for the keg's current `custody_state` — `ERR_SCAN_SEQUENCE_INVALID` (409), on any scan.
19. A claim response alternates sides, so `last_responder_role` differs from the acting role — `ERR_RESPONDER_OUT_OF_TURN` (409).
20. A keg may only be filled while certified: `service_state` must be `SERVICE_OK` — `ERR_KEG_NOT_FIT_FOR_FILL` (422), on a fill scan.
21. The keg's pressure certification has not lapsed — `ERR_PRESSURE_TEST_OVERDUE` (422), on a fill scan.
22. A cleaning step records a long enough caustic wash — `ERR_CAUSTIC_CYCLE_TOO_SHORT` (422).
23. A pressure-test step records a high enough test pressure — `ERR_PRESSURE_TEST_PSI_TOO_LOW` (422).
24. A quarantined cycle is only retestable, and only releasable, when it was quarantined for a failed pressure test — `ERR_QUARANTINE_RELEASE_BLOCKED` (409), on a pressure-test step and on a release.
25. Certification segregation of duties: the identifier releasing a cycle differs from `pressure_test_performed_by_user_id` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on a release.
26. Money segregation of duties, in two clauses, both `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). On resolution, the identifier resolving an assessment differs from its `created_by_user_id`. On chargeback resolution, the acting role matches the requested action — settlement requires `PoolOperator` and reversal requires `AssetRecoveryAnalyst`. The second clause cannot live at step 2 because it reads the payload's `action`, which is only known to be a valid enum member once step 4 has passed; it is therefore judged here, after the shape checks and after step 10 has confirmed the row's `chargeback_status` is `ISSUED`.
27. A fill scan's `brand_code_scanned` equals the filling brewer's `brand_code` — `ERR_BRAND_MISMATCH` (422).
28. The supplied party identifier matches `party_type`, and the party type is the one the scan point requires — `ERR_SCAN_PARTY_MISMATCH` (422).
29. On a distribution, retail or return scan, `brand_code_scanned` resolves to some existing brewer's `brand_code` — `ERR_BRAND_OWNER_UNKNOWN` (422).
30. A reconciliation claim carries genuine evidence — `ERR_NO_MISDELIVERY_EVIDENCE` (422).
31. A supplied `supersedes_claim_id` names a claim on the same keg whose `status` is `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` — `ERR_CLAIM_SUPERSEDE_MISMATCH` (422).
32. A loss assessment has a chargeable party and a usable stainless price — `ERR_NO_CHARGEABLE_PARTY` or `ERR_NO_COMMODITY_INDEX` (both 422).
33. No supplied instant or date lies in the future — `ERR_SCAN_TIMESTAMP_FUTURE` (422), on a scan, a service-cycle booking, an order dispatch, an investigation status change and an agreement activation.
34. No existing scan on this keg shares the same pair of `scan_point` and `scanned_at` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCAN` (409).
35. `scanned_at` does not fall strictly inside an already-closed custody interval — `ERR_CUSTODY_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (409).
36. `received_at` does not fall inside any existing intake window of the same service centre — `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
37. A reconciliation claim is filed inside the claim window — `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422).
38. Uniqueness of a brewer's `brand_code`, a pool's `pool_code`, a keg's `asset_tag` (globally, across every pool) and a commodity index row's `effective_date` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_BRAND_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_POOL_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_TAG` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_INDEX_DATE` (all 409), on brewer creation, pool creation, keg import and index posting respectively.
39. At most one `ACTIVE` float agreement per brewer-and-pool pair — `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_ACTIVE` (409), on activation.
40. A keg does not carry a second open assessment while an existing one is `DISPUTED` — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_OPEN` (409).
41. A keg does not carry two claims in `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED` at once — `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_OPEN` (409).
42. A counter-offer is inside the round limit — `ERR_CLAIM_ROUND_LIMIT` (409).
43. `requested_keg_count` plus the agreement's `kegs_at_brewer_count` does not exceed its `float_size_kegs` — `ERR_FLOAT_SIZE_EXCEEDED` (422), on order creation.
44. The service centre's `open_cycle_count` is strictly below its `concurrent_cycle_capacity` — `ERR_SERVICE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on a booking.
45. The allocation candidate set is non-empty — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_KEGS` (409), on allocation.
46. When the candidate set is non-empty but smaller than requested, `allow_partial` is true — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FLOAT` (409), on allocation.
47. An agreement may only be terminated when its `kegs_at_brewer_count` is 0 and no order of the agreement sits in `REQUESTED`, `ALLOCATED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` — `ERR_AGREEMENT_FLOAT_OUTSTANDING` (409).
48. Every keg named in an evidence-hold action is already attached to that investigation and is not in a terminal custody state — `ERR_KEG_NOT_ATTACHED` (409).
49. An accrual is only payable from `ACCRUED` — `ERR_ACCRUAL_NOT_PAYABLE` (409).
50. Projected credit exposure stays within the brewer's `credit_limit_cents` — `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402), on order creation and a fill scan.
51. A settlement's `amount_cents` equals the assessment's `net_chargeback_cents` exactly — `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422).

Two adjacencies in this ladder are worth calling out because they are jointly violable and the winner matters. On order creation against a drafted agreement held by an over-limit brewer, the agreement-status check at step 12 wins and the caller sees `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), not the credit failure at step 50. On a fill scan of an uncertified keg presented under the wrong collar, the safety check at step 20 wins and the caller sees `ERR_KEG_NOT_FIT_FOR_FILL` (422), not the brand failure at step 27. Likewise, an analyst attempting a settlement is refused at step 26 with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` before the amount is ever compared at step 51.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities and Cascade Propagation

Three quantities in this system are conserved, meaning that for every operation that moves them there is a compensating operation that returns them to exactly their prior value — not approximately, and with no drift.

The first is **keg count**. At the end of every request that changes any keg's `custody_state`, a pool's `kegs_total` must equal the sum of `kegs_available`, `kegs_circulating`, `kegs_servicing`, `kegs_held` and `kegs_written_off`, as exact integer equality with no tolerance. Every custody movement is a transfer between two of those five buckets; only importing a keg raises `kegs_total`, and writing a keg off moves it into `kegs_written_off` rather than removing it from `kegs_total`. Two compensating pairs must restore the five-counter vector bit for bit: allocating an order and then cancelling it moves kegs out of `IN_FLOAT` and back, leaving only each keg's `version` and `updated_at` changed; and placing kegs on evidence hold and then resuming the investigation moves them out of their buckets and back through each keg's `pre_hold_custody_state`, and does so correctly even when the held kegs were drawn from three different buckets.

The second is **chargeback money**. A brewer's `chargeback_balance_cents` is at all times the sum of `net_chargeback_cents` over that brewer's loss assessments whose `chargeback_status` is `ISSUED` or `SETTLED`. Settling keeps the row inside the sum — settlement records that the brewer paid, it does not erase the liability — while `REVERSED` and `NONE` rows are outside it, and a waived assessment never enters it at all. Upholding an assessment adds its net amount; reversing the resulting chargeback removes the row and restores the exact pre-uphold balance.

The third is **incentive money**. A distributor's `incentive_accrual_cents` is at all times the sum of `incentive_cents` over that distributor's accruals whose `status` is `ACCRUED` or `PAID`. Minting an accrual adds its amount; clawing it back removes it and restores the exact pre-accrual balance. Because a paid row stays inside the sum, clawing back a `PAID` accrual moves the ledger by exactly the same amount as clawing back an `ACCRUED` one. Service history, by contrast, is not money and is not conserved: a clawback never restores `total_returns_count`, `on_time_returns_count`, `on_time_return_pct` or `avg_dwell_days`.

Derived state propagates in a fixed chain, and every link fires inside the same request. A change to any keg's `custody_state` recomputes its pool's five census counters and `available_pct`; the recomputed census recomputes the pool's `posture`; the recomputed posture recomputes `float_posture` on every `ACTIVE` float agreement of that pool. Because pool posture is shared, a keg movement made under one brewer's order can flip a *different* brewer's agreement to `SUSPENDED`. Each row touched by that chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced by one, subject to the single-bump and no-op rules of §2.4.

### 2.10. Two Deliberately Different Interval Rules

The platform reasons about two kinds of time window, and their boundary rules are opposite. They must not be unified.

A keg's history is a contiguous chain of **custody intervals**. Each accepted scan closes the interval that was open and opens the next one, so an interval runs from the instant of the scan that opened it up to, but not including, the instant of the scan that closed it: the interval is half-open with an exclusive end. The currently open interval starts at the keg's `dwell_started_at` and has no end. A new scan whose `scanned_at` is at or after `dwell_started_at` lands in the open interval and is accepted — including the case where `scanned_at` is exactly equal to `dwell_started_at`, because two adjacent intervals touching at a single point do not overlap. A new scan whose `scanned_at` is strictly earlier than `dwell_started_at` falls inside an interval already closed by a party that no longer holds the keg, and is rejected with `ERR_CUSTODY_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

A service centre's **intake window** is a wash-line block, and it is closed at both ends. The window opened by an existing cycle runs from that cycle's `received_at` through that instant plus the centre's `intake_buffer_hours`, endpoints included. A new booking whose `received_at` falls anywhere within any existing window of the same centre, including exactly at either endpoint, is rejected with `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). A `received_at` exactly `intake_buffer_hours` after an existing intake is therefore refused, while one millisecond later is accepted. Windows are evaluated against every cycle the centre has ever received, including released ones, because the wash-line block was consumed regardless of how the cycle later turned out.

### 2.11. Reference Vocabularies

For precision, the enumerated vocabularies used throughout §3 are collected here; the rules that move between their members live in §3.

A brewer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`. A distributor's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and its `cooperation_tier` is `NONE`, `BRONZE`, `SILVER` or `GOLD`. A pool's `posture` is `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. A keg's `custody_state` is one of `IN_FLOAT`, `ISSUED_TO_BREWER`, `FILLED`, `IN_TRADE`, `AT_RETAIL`, `IN_SERVICE`, `QUARANTINED`, `EVIDENCE_HOLD`, `LOST`, `STOLEN` or `SCRAPPED`; its independent `service_state` is `SERVICE_OK`, `SERVICE_DUE`, `SERVICE_OVERDUE` or `SERVICE_FAILED`; and its `dwell_flag` is `WITHIN_LIMIT`, `APPROACHING` or `OVERDUE`. A scan's `scan_point` is `FILL`, `DISTRIBUTION`, `RETAIL` or `RETURN` and its `party_type` is `BREWER`, `DISTRIBUTOR` or `RETAIL_ACCOUNT`. A float agreement's `status` is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `TERMINATED` and its `float_posture` is `HEALTHY`, `TIGHT` or `SUSPENDED`. An issuance order's `status` is `REQUESTED`, `ALLOCATED`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, `DISPATCHED` or `CANCELLED`. A service cycle's `stage` is `RECEIVED`, `CLEANED`, `INSPECTED`, `PRESSURE_TESTED`, `RELEASED` or `QUARANTINED`, its `inspection_result` is `PASS`, `MINOR_DEFECT` or `MAJOR_DEFECT`, its `pressure_test_result` is `PASS` or `FAIL`, and its `quarantine_reason` is `INSPECTION_MAJOR_DEFECT` or `PRESSURE_TEST_FAIL`. A loss assessment's `cause` is `LOST_IN_TRADE`, `DAMAGED_BEYOND_REPAIR`, `NOT_RETURNED_DWELL` or `STOLEN_SUSPECTED`, its `assessment_basis` is `REPLACEMENT_COST` or `DEPRECIATED_BOOK`, its `assessment_status` is `PROPOSED`, `DISPUTED`, `UPHELD`, `WAIVED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and its `chargeback_status` is `NONE`, `ISSUED`, `SETTLED` or `REVERSED`. A reconciliation claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED`, `AGREED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and its `last_responder_role` records either `Brewer` or `Distributor`. An incentive accrual's `accrual_type` is `RETURN_DWELL` or `RECONCILIATION_OFFSET` and its `status` is `ACCRUED`, `PAID` or `CLAWED_BACK`. A theft investigation's `status` is `OPEN`, `EVIDENCE_HOLD`, `CLOSED_RECOVERED` or `CLOSED_WRITTEN_OFF`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding Counterparties and Posting the Stainless Index

Registration is an operations-desk activity. A `PoolOperator` registers a brewing company by supplying its legal `name`, between 1 and 120 characters and not blank, and its `brand_code` — the collar brand code stamped on the kegs that brewer fills, 2 to 8 characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits, and unique across every brewer on the platform. A duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BRAND_CODE`. The operator may also supply `credit_limit_cents`, the maximum outstanding chargeback exposure the business will tolerate before it stops issuing float to this brewer; when omitted it defaults to 40000 cents, and it may never be negative. A newly registered brewer opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `status_reason` unset, `chargeback_balance_cents` at 0, `lifetime_fill_count` at 0, `upheld_loss_count` at 0 and both loss-rate fields, `loss_rate_bp_trailing_90d` and `loss_rate_bp_lifetime`, at 0.

Brewer standing is normally driven by the system, but the desk retains an override. A `PoolOperator` may set a brewer's `status` directly to `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` and may attach a free-text `status_reason`; the reason field otherwise records the name of whichever cascade rule forced the last change. Any such override immediately recomputes `float_posture` on every `ACTIVE` float agreement of that brewer — moving the brewer to `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` forces those agreements to `SUSPENDED`, while returning the brewer to `ACTIVE` releases them.

Wholesalers are registered the same way, with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `region_code` of exactly two uppercase characters used to match distributors to pools. The operator may supply `return_incentive_rate_cents_per_keg`, the base per-keg return incentive before any dwell band or cooperation multiplier is applied; it defaults to 250 cents and must lie between 0 and 2000 inclusive. A new distributor opens with `cooperation_tier` `NONE` and `status` `ACTIVE`, with `total_returns_count`, `on_time_returns_count`, `on_time_return_pct`, `incentive_accrual_cents` and `avg_dwell_days` all at zero and `cooperation_effective_date` unset. The `NONE` tier is deliberately unrewarding: a distributor without a signed cooperation agreement earns nothing on returns, because its cooperation multiplier is 0 basis points.

Signing or revising a **cooperation agreement** is how a distributor enters the incentive scheme. The operator supplies the new `cooperation_tier` and the `effective_date` on which it takes effect, and may optionally revise `return_incentive_rate_cents_per_keg` or the distributor's `status` in the same call. The supplied tier is stored on the distributor and the effective date is stored as `cooperation_effective_date`. This is the only route into `BRONZE` and the only way to move a tier *down*; the ladder from `BRONZE` up through `SILVER` to `GOLD` is climbed automatically, as described in §3.6. Accruals already minted are never re-priced by a later agreement.

Stainless valuation depends on a posted price series. A `PoolOperator` posts a `CommodityIndex` reading by supplying an `effective_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the scrap price `usd_cents_per_kg` as a positive integer of US cents per kilogram, and a `source` label of 2 to 40 characters for audit. Index rows are immutable and `effective_date` is unique across the series, so a correction is posted as a new row under a later date rather than as a second row for the same date; a repeat date is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INDEX_DATE`. Posting a new reading never re-prices a valuation that has already been pinned into a loss assessment or a theft investigation — only future valuations and keg reads pick the new row up.

### 3.2. Pools, Fleet Import and the Posture Gate

A **keg pool** is one asset class. A `PoolOperator` creates it with a `pool_code` of 3 to 16 characters, unique across all pools and refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POOL_CODE` if repeated; a `size_liters` that must be one of 20, 30, 50 or 58; a `stainless_weight_kg` giving the empty stainless mass of one keg in this class, which must be greater than zero, no greater than 60, and carry at most two decimal places; a `replacement_cost_cents` greater than zero, which is the new-keg cost used as the gross basis of replacement-cost loss assessments; and a two-character uppercase `region_code` naming the region the pool serves.

Three optional inputs configure the pool's throttle. `restrict_threshold_pct`, an integer from 0 to 100 defaulting to 25, is the availability percentage at or above which the pool is fully open. `freeze_threshold_pct`, an integer from 0 to 100 defaulting to 10, is the availability percentage below which the pool freezes; it must be strictly less than `restrict_threshold_pct`, and a creation that violates that relationship is refused as an invalid request. `restricted_max_kegs_per_order`, at least 1 and defaulting to 2, caps how many kegs a single order may ask for while the pool is throttled.

A pool maintains a five-bucket census plus a total, all recomputed at the end of any request that changes a keg's `custody_state` in that pool and also at each keg import. `kegs_total` counts every keg row of the pool, written-off kegs included. `kegs_available` counts the kegs whose `custody_state` is `IN_FLOAT`. `kegs_circulating` counts the kegs in `ISSUED_TO_BREWER`, `FILLED`, `IN_TRADE` or `AT_RETAIL`. `kegs_servicing` counts the kegs in `IN_SERVICE` or `QUARANTINED`. `kegs_held` counts the kegs in `EVIDENCE_HOLD`. `kegs_written_off` counts the kegs in `LOST`, `STOLEN` or `SCRAPPED`. Because no keg is ever excluded from `kegs_total`, writing a keg off lowers availability rather than leaving it unchanged.

`available_pct` is 0.00 when `kegs_total` is zero, and otherwise `kegs_available` divided by `kegs_total`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. The pool's `posture` is a pure function of that figure and is never set by an operator. When `kegs_total` is greater than zero and `available_pct` is greater than or equal to `restrict_threshold_pct`, the posture is `OPEN`. When `kegs_total` is greater than zero and `available_pct` is below `restrict_threshold_pct` but greater than or equal to `freeze_threshold_pct`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise — including whenever `kegs_total` is zero — the posture is `FROZEN`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so an `available_pct` exactly equal to `restrict_threshold_pct` reads `OPEN` and one exactly equal to `freeze_threshold_pct` reads `RESTRICTED`. A newly created pool therefore opens with every counter at 0, `available_pct` at 0.00 and `posture` at `FROZEN`: an empty pool cannot issue float.

Posture moves in both directions, always as a recompute rather than as an operator action. Requests that take kegs out of float — an allocation, a maintenance pull onto a service line, an evidence hold — can drive `OPEN → RESTRICTED` and `RESTRICTED → FROZEN` as availability falls through the two thresholds. Requests that return kegs to `IN_FLOAT` — a service release, an order cancellation, an evidence-hold resume — can drive `FROZEN → RESTRICTED` and `RESTRICTED → OPEN` as it rises. A pool can also move straight from `FROZEN` to `OPEN` when a fleet import or a replenishment sweep lifts availability past `restrict_threshold_pct` in one step. Whenever the posture changes, the pool's `version` advances and every `ACTIVE` float agreement on that pool recomputes its own `float_posture`; a pool falling to `FROZEN` therefore suspends the drawing rights of every brewer leasing from it, which in turn blocks their allocations and their fill scans.

Importing a physical keg into a pool is the only operation that raises `kegs_total`. The operator supplies an `asset_tag` of 3 to 24 characters, which is the laser-etched tag read at every touch point. Asset tags are unique **globally**, across every pool and not merely within one: a tag is etched onto the shell, so the same tag may not be reused when a keg is re-homed into a different pool, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_TAG`. The keg's `pool_id` is taken from the pool being imported into and is immutable thereafter.

Because operators migrate existing fleets with real wear and certification history, the import accepts four optional inputs. `initial_custody_state` seeds `custody_state`; only `IN_FLOAT`, `IN_SERVICE` and `QUARANTINED` may be seeded, and when the field is omitted the keg opens in `IN_FLOAT`. `fill_count` seeds the lifetime fill tally, to which every subsequent fill scan adds one. `fills_at_last_pressure_test` records the value `fill_count` held when the keg's last passing pressure test was released, and the pressure-test interval is measured against it. `last_pressure_test_at` records the instant of that last passing test; when the caller omits it the server records the new keg's own `created_at`, treating the factory acceptance test as the certification event, so this field is never null and every certification comparison is well defined.

From the imported counters the server derives `service_state` using exactly the rule the replenishment sweep uses. If `fill_count` minus `fills_at_last_pressure_test` is at least 25 fills, the keg opens `SERVICE_DUE`. If instead its certification is older than two years — that is, more than 730 days have elapsed between `last_pressure_test_at` and the logical clock — it opens `SERVICE_OVERDUE`. Otherwise it opens `SERVICE_OK`. Newly imported kegs open with `dwell_flag` `WITHIN_LIMIT`, with `current_brewer_id`, `current_distributor_id`, `filling_brewer_id`, `issuance_order_id`, `theft_investigation_id`, `filled_brand_code`, `dwell_started_at`, `distributor_custody_started_at`, `last_scanned_at` and `pre_hold_custody_state` all unset, with `cumulative_dwell_days` and `scan_count` at 0, and with `misdelivery_open` and `assessment_open` both false. The import finishes by recomputing the pool census, `available_pct` and `posture`, and then every `ACTIVE` agreement's `float_posture`.

Each keg also carries a `stainless_scrap_value_cents` figure, which is what the shell would fetch as scrap. It is the pool's `stainless_weight_kg` multiplied by the stainless price in force, multiplied by a recovery factor of 65 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. The price in force is taken from the commodity index row with the greatest `effective_date` that is on or before the valuation date, where the valuation date is the date part of the logical clock at the moment of computation; a future-dated index row is never selected, and because `effective_date` is unique the selection is never ambiguous. This value is recomputed afresh every time a keg is read, so a keg read reflects the currently effective index. At import time, if no index row is effective on or before the current logical date, the keg is still imported and its `stainless_scrap_value_cents` simply stays 0 until an index row becomes effective; only opening a loss assessment refuses outright in that situation.

Kegs can be found in two ways. The kegs of one pool can be listed, filtered by `custody_state` and by `service_state` and paged with `limit` and `offset`. The whole fleet can also be listed and filtered by `pool_id`, `current_brewer_id`, `current_distributor_id`, `filling_brewer_id`, `issuance_order_id`, `theft_investigation_id`, `custody_state`, `service_state` and `dwell_flag`; this second listing is the reverse-lookup channel for every custody foreign key a keg carries. Reading a single keg returns both state dimensions, the dwell classification, the custody foreign keys, the tallies `fill_count`, `scan_count` and `cumulative_dwell_days`, the instants `last_scanned_at`, `dwell_started_at`, `distributor_custody_started_at` and `last_pressure_test_at`, the flags `misdelivery_open` and `assessment_open`, and the freshly recomputed `stainless_scrap_value_cents`.

### 3.3. Float Agreements and Replenishment Posture

A **float agreement** is the contract between one brewer and one pool. A `PoolOperator` drafts it by naming the lessee's `brewer_id` and the leased class's `pool_id`, then fixing the commercial terms: `float_size_kegs`, an integer from 1 to 500 that is the ceiling on kegs the brewer may hold simultaneously under this agreement, and `per_fill_rate_cents`, a positive integer that is the base price charged per fill before any dwell surcharge or volume discount. Two optional terms may be supplied: `replenishment_threshold_pct`, an integer from 0 to 100 defaulting to 20, which is the percentage of the float size at or below which the float counts as thin; and `min_fills_per_month`, at least 0 and defaulting to 0, which is recorded for reporting and is enforced by no transition anywhere in the system.

A drafted agreement opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `float_posture` `HEALTHY`, every counter at zero — `kegs_issued_count`, `kegs_at_brewer_count`, `pending_replenishment_kegs`, `billed_fills_count` and `billed_amount_cents` — and `effective_date` and `terminated_reason` unset. A `DRAFT` agreement gates nothing and cannot back an order: any attempt to create or allocate an order against it, or to record a fill under it, is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Activation is an operator step that supplies the `effective_date` on which the agreement legally begins, as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date that may not be in the future. Activation requires the agreement to be in `DRAFT`, requires the brewer's `status` not to be `SUSPENDED` — refused with `ERR_BREWER_SUSPENDED` otherwise — and requires that no other agreement for the same brewer and pool is already `ACTIVE`, refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. On success the agreement moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, records the `effective_date`, computes `float_posture` for the first time, and advances its `version`.

The `status` dimension and the `float_posture` dimension are independent facts about the same agreement. `status` tracks the contract; `float_posture` tracks the operational reality of the float. A `TERMINATED` agreement keeps whatever `float_posture` it last held, frozen, and gates nothing thereafter; an `ACTIVE` agreement can cycle `HEALTHY → TIGHT → SUSPENDED → TIGHT` any number of times without its `status` ever moving. Only `ACTIVE` agreements are recomputed at all: `DRAFT` and `TERMINATED` agreements keep their last posture value and never gate anything.

`float_posture` is recomputed on activation, on every allocation, dispatch or return that affects the agreement, on any change to the brewer's `status`, and on any change to the pool's `posture`. The recompute first refreshes the two census figures. `kegs_at_brewer_count` counts the kegs whose `current_brewer_id` is this agreement's brewer, whose `pool_id` is this agreement's pool, and whose `custody_state` is `ISSUED_TO_BREWER` or `FILLED`. `kegs_issued_count` counts the kegs whose governing order belongs to this agreement and whose order `status` is `ALLOCATED`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` or `DISPATCHED`.

The posture itself is then decided in a pinned order in which the cross-entity gates win over the local ceiling. The agreement is `SUSPENDED` whenever the pool's `posture` is `FROZEN` **or** the brewer's `status` is `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, letting the thin-float ceiling be `float_size_kegs` multiplied by `replenishment_threshold_pct`, divided by 100 and floored to a whole number of kegs, the agreement is `TIGHT` when `kegs_at_brewer_count` is less than or equal to that ceiling, and `HEALTHY` otherwise. Flooring matters: a float of 10 kegs with a 20 percent threshold gives a ceiling of 2, so 2 kegs at the brewer reads `TIGHT` while 3 reads `HEALTHY`.

`pending_replenishment_kegs` is a replenishment *signal*, not a running shortfall. While the posture is `TIGHT` it is the float size minus `kegs_at_brewer_count`, or 0 if that difference would be negative. While the posture is `HEALTHY` or `SUSPENDED` it is 0. Every posture change advances the agreement's `version`, and moving into `SUSPENDED` is what causes later fill scans and allocations under that agreement to be refused with `ERR_FLOAT_SUSPENDED`. The suspension lifts by recompute as soon as the suspending condition clears — the pool leaves `FROZEN` or the brewer returns to `ACTIVE` — at which point the agreement lands on `TIGHT` or `HEALTHY` according to the same ceiling comparison.

Termination closes the contract and requires a `terminated_reason`. From `ACTIVE`, termination is only permitted once all float has come home: `kegs_at_brewer_count` must be 0 and no order of the agreement may sit in `REQUESTED`, `ALLOCATED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`. A premature attempt is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_FLOAT_OUTSTANDING`. A `DRAFT` agreement may be terminated at any time with no such condition. On termination the agreement moves to `TERMINATED`, records the reason, advances its `version`, and leaves any still-open orders exactly as they are — those orders simply can never be allocated or dispatched again. Agreements can be read individually, showing their posture and their billing ledger, and listed with filters on `brewer_id`, `pool_id`, `status` and `float_posture`. The individual read is ownership-checked: a caller acting as `Brewer` must be the lessee the agreement names in `brewer_id`, and a foreign brewer is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` once the agreement has been resolved. The list endpoint carries no such restriction.

### 3.4. Issuance Orders: Request, Allocate, Dispatch, Cancel

Drawing float begins with an **issuance order**. A `PoolOperator` creates one against an `agreement_id`, stating a `requested_keg_count` of at least 1 and optionally setting `allow_partial`, a boolean defaulting to false that decides what happens when the pool cannot fully supply the request. The order records `brewer_id` denormalised from the agreement at creation and never re-derived thereafter, opens in `status` `REQUESTED` with `allocated_keg_count` at 0, `allocated_keg_ids` as an empty array, `allocation_shortfall` at 0 and `per_fill_price_cents_pinned`, `dispatched_at` and `cancel_reason` all unset. Creating an order touches no keg and moves no census counter.

Order creation is nonetheless gated, because the point of the gate is to refuse commitments the business cannot honour. The agreement must be `ACTIVE`; the pool must not be `FROZEN`, refused with `ERR_POOL_FROZEN`; if the pool is `RESTRICTED` the `requested_keg_count` must not exceed the pool's `restricted_max_kegs_per_order`, refused with `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTED`; the brewer must not be `SUSPENDED`; the request must fit the contracted float, so `requested_keg_count` added to the agreement's current `kegs_at_brewer_count` must not exceed `float_size_kegs`, refused with `ERR_FLOAT_SIZE_EXCEEDED`; and finally the brewer's projected exposure must stay inside its credit limit. That fiscal check adds the brewer's `chargeback_balance_cents` to the product of `requested_keg_count` and the agreement's `per_fill_rate_cents`, and refuses with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` when the sum exceeds the brewer's `credit_limit_cents`. Because the fiscal check is judged last, an over-limit brewer holding only a drafted agreement sees `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` rather than the credit failure.

Allocation is where physical kegs are committed, and it is the operation guarded by `expected_agreement_version` as described in §2.7. The candidate set is built from the kegs of the agreement's pool whose `custody_state` is `IN_FLOAT`, whose `service_state` is `SERVICE_OK` or `SERVICE_DUE`, whose `theft_investigation_id` is unset, and whose `assessment_open` flag is false. Two of those filters have consequences the operator can see in the census: a keg that is `SERVICE_OVERDUE`, or attached to a theft investigation, or carrying an open assessment, is still `IN_FLOAT` and still counted in `kegs_available`, yet is not a candidate — so a pool can read `posture` `OPEN` and still fail allocation with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_KEGS`. Kegs that are merely `SERVICE_DUE` *are* candidates, because they remain certified and are only due for a test; they will, however, be refused later at the fill scan.

Candidates are ranked deterministically, ascending, on three keys in this order: first `fill_count`, so the least-filled keg leaves first and wear is spread across the fleet; then `last_pressure_test_at`, so the oldest certification leaves first; then `created_at` as the mandatory final tie-break. A keg's `id` is never an input to the ranking. Because `last_pressure_test_at` defaults to the keg's own `created_at` at import, the second and third keys agree for freshly imported kegs and the import order decides — deterministically.

The selector then takes the smaller of `requested_keg_count` and the number of candidates. If that number is zero, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_KEGS`. If it is greater than zero but less than requested and `allow_partial` is false, the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FLOAT` and nothing changes at all. Otherwise the leading kegs in rank order are taken: `allocated_keg_ids` records their ids in rank order as a reporting convenience that is never an input to any later decision, `allocated_keg_count` records how many were taken, `allocation_shortfall` records `requested_keg_count` minus that number, and `per_fill_price_cents_pinned` copies the agreement's `per_fill_rate_cents` at this moment, so later edits to the agreement do not retro-price this order's fills. The order lands in `ALLOCATED` when the count was fully met and in `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` when it was short — the two branches are otherwise identical, differing only in the non-zero shortfall and the smaller number of keg moves.

Each allocated keg moves `IN_FLOAT → ISSUED_TO_BREWER`, records `current_brewer_id` from the agreement and `issuance_order_id` from the order, sets `dwell_started_at` to the logical clock, sets `dwell_flag` to `WITHIN_LIMIT`, and advances its own `version`. The cascade then fires once, after all kegs have moved: the pool's `kegs_available` falls and `kegs_circulating` rises by the number allocated, `available_pct` and `posture` are recomputed and the pool's `version` advances, and every `ACTIVE` agreement on that pool recomputes `float_posture`, each advancing its own `version`. The allocating agreement additionally refreshes `kegs_issued_count`, `kegs_at_brewer_count` and `pending_replenishment_kegs` — all inside the same single version bump. Allocation also re-checks the gates it shares with order creation: the agreement must be `ACTIVE`, its `float_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, the brewer must not be `SUSPENDED`, the brewer's `chargeback_balance_cents` must be strictly below its `credit_limit_cents`, the pool must not be `FROZEN`, and a `RESTRICTED` pool caps the request at `restricted_max_kegs_per_order`.

Dispatch marks the truck as having left. The operator supplies `dispatched_at`, an instant that must not be in the future — it is an operator-supplied timestamp, deliberately not the logical clock. Dispatch is permitted from `ALLOCATED` and from `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, with identical effects in both cases, and requires the agreement still to be `ACTIVE`. The order moves to `DISPATCHED`, records `dispatched_at`, advances its `version`, and refreshes the agreement's `kegs_issued_count`. Critically, every allocated keg's `dwell_started_at` is re-based to that same supplied `dispatched_at`, so the brewer's dwell clock starts when the truck left rather than when the operator got round to typing it in. `DISPATCHED` is terminal for an order, and only a dispatched order's kegs may be filled.

Cancellation requires a `cancel_reason` and is permitted from `REQUESTED`, `ALLOCATED` and `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`; it is an operator-desk action, so a brewer asks the desk rather than cancelling directly. Cancelling a `REQUESTED` order returns nothing, because nothing was allocated. Cancelling an allocated or partially allocated order returns exactly the kegs named in `allocated_keg_ids` — that is, `allocated_keg_count` kegs, not `requested_keg_count` — each moving `ISSUED_TO_BREWER → IN_FLOAT` while clearing `current_brewer_id`, `issuance_order_id` and `dwell_started_at`, resetting `dwell_flag` to `WITHIN_LIMIT`, and advancing its own `version`. A keg that has already been filled or moved beyond is left untouched, though in practice this cannot arise: filling requires the order to be `DISPATCHED`, and `DISPATCHED` orders cannot be cancelled. `allocated_keg_ids` is left in place as a historical record. The pool census, `available_pct` and `posture` are then recomputed back to exactly their pre-allocation values, and the agreement's counters and `float_posture` are recomputed, with the agreement's `version` advancing.

Orders can be read individually, showing `allocated_keg_ids` and `allocation_shortfall`, and listed with filters on `agreement_id`, `brewer_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. The Scan Stream: Custody Projection and Fill Billing

Every physical fact the platform knows about a keg arrives as a **scan event**. Recording a scan is the single write that drives custody, dwell, fill billing, misdelivery detection and return incentives, and it may be performed by a `Brewer`, by a `Distributor`, or by a `PoolOperator` acting on the fleet's behalf. The caller supplies the `scan_point` at which the tag was read, the `party_type` that says which party identifier is authoritative, the `scanned_at` instant at which the tag was physically read, and the `brand_code_scanned` read off the collar as 2 to 8 uppercase letters and digits. Exactly one party identifier accompanies those four: `brewer_id` when `party_type` is `BREWER`, `distributor_id` when it is `DISTRIBUTOR`, and `retail_account_code` — an opaque retail account label — when it is `RETAIL_ACCOUNT`. Supplying an identifier that the declared `party_type` forbids is already refused as an invalid request during shape checking; a *missing* required identifier, or a `party_type` that the `scan_point` does not allow, is refused with `ERR_SCAN_PARTY_MISMATCH`. The allowed pairings are fixed: a fill is scanned by a brewer, a distribution and a return are scanned by a distributor, and a retail read is scanned by a retail account.

A scan event is immutable once recorded and carries no `updated_at` and no `version`. Alongside the supplied fields the server records `recorded_at`, the logical-clock instant at which the scan reached the server, which is always monotonic and differs from `scanned_at` for late arrivals. It also records four derived facts: `is_late_arrival`, `custody_state_after`, `dwell_days_closed`, and — for fill scans only — `fill_charge_net_cents`, which is null on every other scan point.

Legality of the movement is judged against the keg's current `custody_state`, and this is the only check that judges it: a `FILL` is legal only from `ISSUED_TO_BREWER`, a `DISTRIBUTION` only from `FILLED`, a `RETAIL` only from `IN_TRADE`, and a `RETURN` only from either `IN_TRADE` or `AT_RETAIL`. Anything else is refused with `ERR_SCAN_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. The accepted movements are therefore the closed loop `IN_FLOAT → ISSUED_TO_BREWER → FILLED → IN_TRADE → AT_RETAIL → IN_SERVICE → IN_FLOAT`, with `IN_TRADE → IN_SERVICE` available as a shortcut when a keg is returned without a retail leg.

Two guards protect the integrity of the stream itself. First, an exact-duplicate guard: if the keg already has a scan with the same `scan_point` and the same `scanned_at`, the new scan is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCAN`. Second, and only after that, the interval placement described in §2.10: a `scanned_at` strictly earlier than the keg's `dwell_started_at` falls inside an already-closed interval and is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`, while a `scanned_at` exactly equal to `dwell_started_at` is accepted.

A handheld that was offline when it read a tag produces a **late arrival**, and the projection is built to absorb it without corrupting anything. A scan is flagged `is_late_arrival` when the keg's `last_scanned_at` is already set and this scan's `scanned_at` is strictly earlier than it. The custody projection is nevertheless driven by *arrival* order rather than by `scanned_at` order, because the sequence legality above has already been judged against the projected state: the transition table is applied from the keg's current `custody_state`, the resulting state is stored on the scan as `custody_state_after`, and the keg's `custody_state` becomes that value. The high-water mark never rewinds — `last_scanned_at` becomes whichever is later of its existing value and the new `scanned_at` — so a late arrival cannot make a subsequent genuine scan look late. The spell clock is likewise clamped: `dwell_days_closed` is the difference between this scan's `scanned_at` and the keg's `dwell_started_at`, floored to whole days and floored at 0, so a late arrival contributes zero dwell days rather than a negative number, and `dwell_started_at` becomes whichever is later of its existing value and the new `scanned_at`. Every accepted scan raises the keg's `scan_count` by one, adds the newly closed `dwell_days_closed` into the keg's `cumulative_dwell_days`, and advances the keg's `version`. A late arrival is appended to the stream and never rewrites an earlier row.

A **fill** is the most heavily gated scan in the system, because it both commits an uncertified asset risk and posts money. Before anything is charged, the keg's governing issuance order must be in `DISPATCHED`; the `party_type` must be `BREWER` and the supplied `brewer_id` must equal the keg's `current_brewer_id`; when the caller acts as `Brewer`, `X-User-ID` must equal that same `brewer_id`; the float agreement for that brewer and that keg's pool must be `ACTIVE` and its `float_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`; the brewer's `status` must not be `SUSPENDED`; the keg's `service_state` must be exactly `SERVICE_OK`, so a `SERVICE_DUE`, `SERVICE_OVERDUE` or `SERVICE_FAILED` keg is refused with `ERR_KEG_NOT_FIT_FOR_FILL`; the keg's pressure certification must not have lapsed, meaning no more than 730 days may have elapsed between `last_pressure_test_at` and the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_PRESSURE_TEST_OVERDUE`; and the `brand_code_scanned` must equal the filling brewer's own `brand_code`, otherwise `ERR_BRAND_MISMATCH` — a brewer may not fill a keg under another brand's collar. Where an uncertified keg is also presented under the wrong collar, the certification failure is the one reported.

An accepted fill appends the scan, raises the keg's `fill_count` by one, records `filled_brand_code` from `brand_code_scanned` and `filling_brewer_id` from `brewer_id`, closes the open custody spell in the manner described above, resets `dwell_started_at` to `scanned_at`, and clears `misdelivery_open` — refilling a keg is what settles any unreconciled cross-brand history on it. On the brewer's side, `lifetime_fill_count` rises by one and both `loss_rate_bp_trailing_90d` and `loss_rate_bp_lifetime` are recomputed. On the agreement's side, `billed_fills_count` rises by one, `billed_amount_cents` grows by the fill's net charge, and the agreement's `version` advances. Finally, if after the fill the difference between the keg's `fill_count` and its `fills_at_last_pressure_test` has reached 25 fills, the keg moves `SERVICE_OK → SERVICE_DUE`; it remains allocatable in that condition but will fail the *next* fill's certification check, which is how the pressure-test interval enforces itself.

The **net charge for a fill** is computed in a strictly pinned order, and two of its sub-expressions deliberately read different bases. The base is the agreement's current `per_fill_rate_cents` — taken from the agreement, not from the order's `per_fill_price_cents_pinned`. Next, the raw dwell surcharge: the number of whole days the brewer has held the keg at the moment of the scan, less the thirty-day brewer dwell allowance, floored at zero, multiplied by 30 cents per excess day. That raw surcharge is then capped at 20 percent of the base *alone*, rounded half up to whole cents — never 20 percent of the base plus the raw surcharge, and never a percentage of the post-discount total — and the surcharge actually applied is the smaller of the raw figure and that cap. Adding the base and the capped surcharge gives the pre-discount charge. The volume tier is then chosen using the agreement's already-billed `billed_amount_cents` **plus this fill's pre-discount amount**, so the current fill counts toward its own tier: a basis of at least 9000 cents earns a 300-basis-point discount, a basis of at least 4500 cents earns 150 basis points, and anything less earns none, with both comparisons inclusive so a basis of exactly 9000 earns the higher rate. The discount is that rate applied to the pre-discount charge — so the surcharge is itself discountable and is not added back afterwards — rounded half up to whole cents, and the scan's `fill_charge_net_cents` is the pre-discount charge less that discount. Only after the charge has been computed is the ledger updated: `billed_amount_cents` grows by the net charge, `billed_fills_count` and the keg's `fill_count` each rise by one, and the brewer's `lifetime_fill_count` rises by one. Rejected scans leave the ledger untouched.

To make the pinning unambiguous, consider an agreement with a base of 2200 cents. The brewer's first fill happens 50 brewer-days into the spell: 20 excess days at 30 cents give a raw surcharge of 600, the cap is 20 percent of 2200, which is 440, so the applied surcharge is 440 and the pre-discount charge is 2640. The tier basis is 0 plus 2640, which earns no discount, so the net charge is 2640 and `billed_amount_cents` becomes 2640. The second fill happens at zero brewer-days: no surcharge, a pre-discount charge of 2200, a tier basis of 2640 plus 2200, which is 4840 and earns 150 basis points, a discount of 33 cents, and a net charge of 2167, taking `billed_amount_cents` to 4807. Capping on the base plus the raw surcharge instead of the base alone would have produced 2760 for the first fill, and excluding the current fill from the tier basis would have produced 2200 for the second.

One fiscal gate applies to fills as well as to order creation. A fill is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` when the brewer's `chargeback_balance_cents`, plus the agreement's `billed_amount_cents`, plus this fill's net charge, would exceed the brewer's `credit_limit_cents`.

Scan events are readable individually and listable with filters on `keg_id`, `brewer_id`, `distributor_id`, `scan_point`, `misdelivery_detected` and `is_late_arrival`. That listing is both the reverse-lookup channel for a scan's party references and the evidence search that reconciliation claims are built on.

### 3.6. Trade-Side Custody, Dwell Analysis, Return Incentives and the Sweep

Once filled, a keg passes to the trade. A **distribution** scan is recorded by the wholesaler taking custody: the `party_type` is `DISTRIBUTOR` and the `distributor_id` identifies the wholesaler, whose `status` must not be `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED` — and which, when the caller acts as `Distributor`, must match `X-User-ID`. The keg moves `FILLED → IN_TRADE`, records `current_distributor_id`, and opens a fresh distributor accountability spell by setting `distributor_custody_started_at` to the scan's `scanned_at`. A **retail** scan moves the keg `IN_TRADE → AT_RETAIL` and is recorded against a `retail_account_code`; it closes the current custody spell and resets `dwell_started_at`, but it deliberately does **not** clear `distributor_custody_started_at`, because the wholesaler stays accountable for the shell all the way through the retail leg.

On any distribution, retail or return scan the `brand_code_scanned` must resolve to some existing brewer's `brand_code`, otherwise a misdelivery could not be attributed to an owner and the scan is refused with `ERR_BRAND_OWNER_UNKNOWN`. Misdelivery detection then runs on those same three scan points: when `brand_code_scanned` differs from the keg's stored `filled_brand_code`, the scan records `misdelivery_detected` as true and the keg's `misdelivery_open` flag is raised. Fills never trigger misdelivery detection — they define the brand instead.

A **return** scan closes the trade leg and is the richest cascade in the system. The `party_type` must be `DISTRIBUTOR`, the supplied `distributor_id` must equal the keg's `current_distributor_id`, and that distributor must not be `SUSPENDED`. The keg moves `IN_TRADE → IN_SERVICE` or `AT_RETAIL → IN_SERVICE`, the custody spell closes, `dwell_started_at` resets to `scanned_at`, and `distributor_custody_started_at` is cleared, while `current_distributor_id` is deliberately left in place — the returning wholesaler stays recorded on the shell, and a service release is the only operation that clears that field. Because the accountability spell spanned the retail leg, the days counted against the distributor include the time the keg sat in the retailer's cooler. That closed spell mints a `ReturnIncentiveAccrual` of `accrual_type` `RETURN_DWELL`, which records `distributor_id`, `keg_id`, the `dwell_days` it closed, the band factor `tier_pct_bp`, the tier factor `cooperation_multiplier_bp`, the resulting `incentive_cents`, and `triggering_scanned_at` set to the return scan's `scanned_at`; its `claim_id` is unset, its `status` opens at `ACCRUED`, and `paid_at` and `clawback_reason` are unset. The distributor's `total_returns_count`, `on_time_returns_count`, `on_time_return_pct`, `avg_dwell_days` and `incentive_accrual_cents` are then all recomputed and the distributor's `version` advances; the recomputed on-time percentage may in turn advance the `cooperation_tier` by one step, which re-rates every *later* accrual. Finally the pool's `kegs_circulating` falls by one and `kegs_servicing` rises by one, `posture` is recomputed, and every `ACTIVE` agreement on the pool recomputes `float_posture`. A return scan on a keg that never had a distribution scan — so no accountability spell was ever opened — mints no accrual at all.

**Two dwell clocks** run on the same keg and are never mixed. The brewer, or custody, clock starts at `dwell_started_at` and measures how long the *current* holder has held the keg; it is reset by allocation, by dispatch, by every accepted scan, and by a service release. The distributor, or accountability, clock starts at `distributor_custody_started_at`, is set only by a distribution scan, is not reset by an intervening retail scan, and is cleared by the return scan that closes it; it measures the whole trade-side spell across retail, which is what the return incentive is paid on. Each clock reads zero while its start instant is unset, and otherwise reads the difference between the instant being evaluated and its start instant, floored to whole days and never negative. A scan's `dwell_days_closed` is the brewer clock read at that scan's `scanned_at`, and the keg's `cumulative_dwell_days` is the sum of `dwell_days_closed` across all its scans. A return accrual's `dwell_days` is the distributor clock read at that same instant.

The keg's `dwell_flag` classifies the *open* spell, and — this is important — no scan ever sets it to `APPROACHING` or `OVERDUE`. Only the replenishment sweep writes it. The applicable limit depends on the keg's `custody_state`: a keg in `ISSUED_TO_BREWER` or `FILLED` is measured on the brewer clock against a limit of 30 days with an approaching point at 24 days; a keg in `IN_TRADE` or `AT_RETAIL` is measured on the distributor clock against a limit of 45 days with an approaching point at 36 days. Kegs in `IN_FLOAT`, `IN_SERVICE`, `QUARANTINED`, `EVIDENCE_HOLD`, `LOST`, `STOLEN` or `SCRAPPED` have no dwell limit at all and are forced to `WITHIN_LIMIT`. Given the applicable limit, the open spell reads `OVERDUE` when its whole-day length is **strictly greater** than the limit, `APPROACHING` when it is at or beyond the approaching point but not over the limit, and `WITHIN_LIMIT` otherwise. The strictness matters: exactly 30 brewer-days is not yet overdue, and overdue begins on day 31. The band table used to price return incentives uses a different comparison and must not be unified with this one.

A keg's dwell position can be read on demand. The computed read reports `keg_id`, the keg's `custody_state`, both clocks as `brewer_spell_days` and `distributor_spell_days`, the `limit_days` and `approaching_days` that apply to that custody state, the `dwell_flag` as it *would* be classified right now, the accumulated `cumulative_dwell_days`, and the two start instants `dwell_started_at` and `distributor_custody_started_at`. The read is strictly read-only: the classification it returns is not persisted.

**Pricing a return** starts from the distributor's `return_incentive_rate_cents_per_keg` and applies two independent factors. The dwell band factor is 10000 basis points when the accountability spell closed in 14 days or fewer, 6000 basis points from 15 through 29 days, 2500 basis points from 30 through 44 days, and 0 basis points at 45 days or more — so a keg returned outside the 45-day distributor allowance earns nothing. The cooperation factor is 0 basis points for a `NONE`-tier distributor, 10000 for `BRONZE`, 11500 for `SILVER` and 13000 for `GOLD`, taken from the tier as it stands *at the moment of this scan*. The accrual's `incentive_cents` is the base rate multiplied by the band factor as a proportion of 10000, multiplied by the cooperation factor as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents — and that rounding happens once, on this one row, before any summation. The distributor's `incentive_accrual_cents` is then the sum of the already-rounded `incentive_cents` across its accruals in `ACCRUED` or `PAID`. Rounding the summed raw values instead would give a different, wrong answer: three returns at a base of 250 with a `SILVER` multiplier and dwell of 12, 20 and 35 days round to 288, 173 and 72 cents for a ledger of 533, whereas summing the raw 287.5, 172.5 and 71.875 and rounding the total would give 532.

A `NONE`-tier distributor still accrues rows, each with `incentive_cents` of 0, rather than accruing nothing at all — which matters, because those rows still advance the service history that drives promotion.

**Tier promotion** runs at the end of every return scan that minted an accrual, in a pinned three-step order. First the accrual is valued using the tier as it stands. Then the counters are recomputed *including* the accrual just minted: `total_returns_count` is the number of `RETURN_DWELL` accruals of this distributor in `ACCRUED`, `PAID` or `CLAWED_BACK`; `on_time_returns_count` is the subset of those whose `dwell_days` is 45 or fewer; `on_time_return_pct` is 0.00 when the total is zero and otherwise the on-time count divided by the total, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimals; and `avg_dwell_days` is 0.00 when the set is empty and otherwise the mean of `dwell_days` across that set, rounded half up to two decimals. `RECONCILIATION_OFFSET` accruals are excluded from all four figures. Third, at most **one** step up the ladder is applied and no step may be skipped: a `BRONZE` distributor becomes `SILVER` when its total is at least 3 returns and its on-time percentage is at least 90.00, and a `SILVER` distributor becomes `GOLD` when its total is at least 5 returns and its on-time percentage is at least 96.00, recording `cooperation_effective_date` as the current logical date in either case. Both comparisons are inclusive, so exactly 90.00 promotes to `SILVER` and exactly 96.00 promotes to `GOLD`. A `NONE`-tier distributor never auto-promotes; it must be moved to `BRONZE` by an explicit cooperation agreement first. There is no automatic demotion: a tier once earned is only lowered by an explicit cooperation-agreement write. And because the promotion lands *after* the accrual has been valued, the very return that triggers `BRONZE → SILVER` is itself paid at the `BRONZE` multiplier; the `SILVER` multiplier first appears on the next return.

Accruals are read individually — showing the dwell band and cooperation multiplier that priced them — and listed with filters on `distributor_id`, `keg_id`, `claim_id`, `accrual_type` and `status`. A `PoolOperator` remits an accrual by supplying `paid_at`; the accrual must be in `ACCRUED`, otherwise `ERR_ACCRUAL_NOT_PAYABLE`, and the earning distributor must not be `SUSPENDED`. The accrual moves to `PAID` and records `paid_at`, while the distributor's `incentive_accrual_cents` is deliberately **unchanged**, because paid rows stay inside the ledger sum. A later clawback of a paid row therefore moves the ledger by exactly the same amount as a clawback of an accrued one.

The **replenishment sweep** is the pool's trigger detector. A `PoolOperator` runs it against one pool with no request body. Every keg in the pool has its `dwell_flag` reclassified by the rules above, with kegs that have no dwell limit forced to `WITHIN_LIMIT`; this is the only operation in the entire system that writes `dwell_flag`. The sweep is also the only place a keg moves `SERVICE_DUE → SERVICE_OVERDUE`, which it does when more than 730 days have elapsed between the keg's `last_pressure_test_at` and the logical clock, advancing that keg's `version` and thereby removing it from allocation candidacy. The sweep then recomputes the pool census, `available_pct` and `posture`, and recomputes `kegs_at_brewer_count`, `pending_replenishment_kegs` and `float_posture` on every `ACTIVE` agreement of the pool. It reports `pool_id`, `swept_keg_count`, `approaching_keg_count`, `overdue_keg_count`, `available_pct`, `posture`, `tight_agreement_ids`, `suspended_agreement_ids` and `pending_replenishment_kegs_total`. `swept_keg_count` counts only the kegs that actually had an open dwell spell to advance — that is, those in `ISSUED_TO_BREWER`, `FILLED`, `IN_TRADE` or `AT_RETAIL`; kegs in `IN_FLOAT`, `IN_SERVICE`, `QUARANTINED`, `EVIDENCE_HOLD` or a written-off state are forced to `WITHIN_LIMIT` and are not counted. `approaching_keg_count` and `overdue_keg_count` count the swept kegs whose new flag is `APPROACHING` and `OVERDUE` respectively, `tight_agreement_ids` and `suspended_agreement_ids` list the pool's `ACTIVE` agreements by their recomputed posture, and `pending_replenishment_kegs_total` is the sum of `pending_replenishment_kegs` across them. No keg's `custody_state` changes and no money moves: the sweep detects, it does not remediate.

### 3.7. Cross-Brand Misdelivery and the Reconciliation Negotiation

Kegs get mixed up. A wholesaler that finds another brewery's collar in its yard has to sort it, store it and send it home, and it claims a handling offset from the brewer whose brand was on the shell. That claim is a two-sided negotiation with a bounded number of rounds, and it is the only place in this system where two entity-backed roles write the same row in turn.

A `Distributor` opens a claim by naming the `keg_id`, the `misdelivered_to_brewer_id` it is claiming against, a `claimed_offset_cents` between 1 and 50000, the `evidence_scanned_at` instant it is relying on, and optionally a `supersedes_claim_id`. The claim's own `claimant_distributor_id` is resolved from the acting `X-User-ID` rather than supplied, and `owning_brewer_id` is copied from the keg's `filling_brewer_id`. Evidence is mandatory and is checked strictly: the keg must carry a scan event whose `misdelivery_detected` is true and whose `scanned_at` equals the supplied `evidence_scanned_at` exactly, and the named `misdelivered_to_brewer_id` must be the brewer whose `brand_code` equals *that scan's* `brand_code_scanned`. Naming any other brewer — including the keg's own owning brewer — fails with `ERR_NO_MISDELIVERY_EVIDENCE`, as does citing an instant on which no misdelivery was detected. The claim must also be filed within 30 days of the evidence scan, measured from `evidence_scanned_at` to the logical clock and floored to whole days, so exactly 30 days is still in time and the 31st day fails with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. A keg may not carry two live claims at once: a second claim while one sits in `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_OPEN`. When `supersedes_claim_id` is supplied it must name a claim on the *same* keg whose `status` is `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`; pointing at a claim on a different keg, or at one that is still live or already `AGREED`, is `ERR_CLAIM_SUPERSEDE_MISMATCH`. A new claim opens in `SUBMITTED` with `round_count` at 0, `counter_offset_cents` and `agreed_offset_cents` unset, and `last_responder_role` already recorded as `Distributor`, because filing the claim *is* the distributor's first move.

Every subsequent move is a single response carrying a `response` of `COUNTER`, `AGREE`, `REJECT` or `WITHDRAW`, an `expected_claim_version` that must equal the claim's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` — and, for a counter, a `counter_offset_cents`. Three rules govern who may move. First, ownership: a `Brewer` responding must have an `X-User-ID` equal to `misdelivered_to_brewer_id` and a `Distributor` responding must match `claimant_distributor_id`, or the request is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Second, turn-taking: the claim's `last_responder_role` must differ from the acting role, so the same side can never move twice in a row; a distributor trying to counter its own outstanding counter is `ERR_RESPONDER_OUT_OF_TURN`. Third, terminality: a claim in `AGREED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` accepts no further response and yields `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

A `COUNTER` flips the claim between the two live states and increments `round_count`: from `SUBMITTED` the brewer counters into `COUNTERED`, and from `COUNTERED` the distributor counters back into `SUBMITTED`, each time overwriting `counter_offset_cents` with the new figure and stamping `last_responder_role` with the side that just moved. At most 3 counters may be recorded on one claim; the fourth is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ROUND_LIMIT`, while `AGREE`, `REJECT` and `WITHDRAW` remain available at any round count. The alternation and the cap combine to give a claim a bounded life: file, counter, counter, counter, then agree, reject or withdraw.

An `AGREE` closes the claim in the claimant's favour and is the one response that moves money. It may be recorded by the brewer from `SUBMITTED` or by the distributor from `COUNTERED` — in each case by the side that did not make the last move — and it requires the keg not to be in `LOST`, `STOLEN` or `SCRAPPED`, because a written-off shell cannot be handed back. The claim moves to `AGREED`, `closed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `agreed_offset_cents` is fixed to whichever figure was outstanding: the `counter_offset_cents` when a counter had been made, and the original `claimed_offset_cents` when the brewer simply accepted the opening claim. A `ReturnIncentiveAccrual` of `accrual_type` `RECONCILIATION_OFFSET` is then minted for the claimant with `claim_id` set to this claim, `keg_id` copied from the claim, `incentive_cents` equal to `agreed_offset_cents`, and `status` `ACCRUED`; because no accountability spell priced it, its `dwell_days`, `tier_pct_bp` and `cooperation_multiplier_bp` all read 0 rather than being left unset, and `triggering_scanned_at`, `paid_at` and `clawback_reason` open unset. The distributor's `incentive_accrual_cents` grows by that amount. Crucially, a reconciliation offset is **not** a return: `total_returns_count`, `on_time_returns_count`, `on_time_return_pct`, `avg_dwell_days` and `cooperation_tier` are all left exactly as they were, and no tier promotion is evaluated. Handling somebody else's keg is paid work, not evidence of returning your own kegs promptly. Finally the keg's `misdelivery_open` flag is cleared and the keg's `version` advances.

A `REJECT` — recorded by the brewer, and from `COUNTERED` only when `last_responder_role` is `Distributor` — and a `WITHDRAW` — recorded by the claimant distributor from either live state — both close the claim with `closed_at` stamped and no accrual minted. Both deliberately leave `misdelivery_open` set to true, because the misdelivery itself has not been resolved and the distributor is entitled to file a superseding claim citing the same evidence scan, subject to the 30-day window still being open.

Claims are read individually, and listed with filters on `keg_id`, `claimant_distributor_id`, `owning_brewer_id`, `misdelivered_to_brewer_id`, `supersedes_claim_id` and `status`; the `supersedes_claim_id` filter makes a chain of superseding claims on one keg walkable from either end.

### 3.8. The Service Line: Intake, Cleaning, Inspection, Pressure Test and Release

Every returned keg is cleaned, inspected and pressure tested before it goes back onto the float. A `ServiceCenter` is created with a `name`, a two-letter `region_code`, a `concurrent_cycle_capacity` between 1 and 50, and an `intake_buffer_hours` between 0 and 48 that defaults to 4; its `open_cycle_count` starts at 0 and is thereafter derived as the number of that centre's cycles whose `stage` is `RECEIVED`, `CLEANED`, `INSPECTED`, `PRESSURE_TESTED` or `QUARANTINED`.

Booking a keg onto a line creates a `ServiceCycle` from a `keg_id`, a `service_center_id` and a `received_at`. The keg must be in `IN_FLOAT` — a routine recertification pull off the float — or already in `IN_SERVICE`, having arrived by a return scan or resumed out of an evidence hold and only now being booked onto a line. Every other custody state, including `QUARANTINED`, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a quarantined keg's retest belongs to the cycle it is already attached to, not to a new one. The two variants differ sharply in their effects: booking an `IN_FLOAT` keg moves it to `IN_SERVICE`, bumps its `version`, decrements `kegs_available` and increments `kegs_servicing`, and recomputes `available_pct`, `posture` and every `ACTIVE` agreement's `float_posture`, whereas booking a keg that is already `IN_SERVICE` touches neither the keg nor the census and does not bump the keg's `version` — the return scan already moved it out of `kegs_circulating`.

Two independent resource checks then run on the centre, in a fixed order. The **intake window** comes first: each cycle ever received at a centre reserves the span from its `received_at` through `received_at` plus `intake_buffer_hours`, **closed at both ends**, and a new `received_at` falling anywhere in that span — including on either endpoint — is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A `received_at` exactly `intake_buffer_hours` after an existing intake is therefore rejected, and one millisecond later is accepted. Windows are evaluated against every cycle the centre has ever received, `RELEASED` ones included, because the wash-line block was consumed whatever became of the cycle afterwards. Only if the window is clear does the **aggregate occupancy** check run: the centre's `open_cycle_count` must be strictly below `concurrent_cycle_capacity`, otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This second check is a count, not a pairwise comparison — each cycle is admissible on its own and only the collective breaches the limit — and because `RELEASED` cycles leave the count, releasing one cycle frees exactly one slot. Note that the intake window is the mirror image of the custody-interval rule described earlier: intake spans reject their boundary instant, custody intervals accept it.

The cycle then runs its stages. A `ServiceTechnician` records each step by naming a `step` of `CLEAN`, `INSPECT` or `PRESSURE_TEST` together with an `occurred_at` and the fields that step produces.

- **`CLEAN`** requires `stage` `RECEIVED` and an `occurred_at` at or after the cycle's `received_at` and not after the logical clock. It records `caustic_cycle_minutes`, which must be at least 12 — a shorter caustic wash is refused with `ERR_CAUSTIC_CYCLE_TOO_SHORT` — sets `cleaned_at` and advances the stage to `CLEANED`.
- **`INSPECT`** requires `stage` `CLEANED` and an `occurred_at` at or after `cleaned_at`. It records `inspection_result` and `inspected_at`. A result of `PASS` or `MINOR_DEFECT` advances the stage to `INSPECTED`: a minor defect is cosmetic and does not stop the keg. A result of `MAJOR_DEFECT` diverts the cycle to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` recorded as `INSPECTION_MAJOR_DEFECT`, and moves the keg to custody `QUARANTINED` with `service_state` `SERVICE_FAILED`.
- **`PRESSURE_TEST`** requires `stage` `INSPECTED`, or `stage` `QUARANTINED` with a `quarantine_reason` of `PRESSURE_TEST_FAIL` for the retest loop. The recorded `pressure_test_psi` must be at least 30; a lower reading is an *invalid* test rather than a failed one and is refused with `ERR_PRESSURE_TEST_PSI_TOO_LOW` regardless of the `pressure_test_result` supplied. With an adequate pressure, a `pressure_test_result` of `PASS` advances the stage to `PRESSURE_TESTED`, records `pressure_tested_at` and stamps `pressure_test_performed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, while a `FAIL` diverts to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` `PRESSURE_TEST_FAIL` and sets the keg's `service_state` to `SERVICE_FAILED`.

The quarantine branch is asymmetric by design, and the asymmetry is enforced. A cycle quarantined for `PRESSURE_TEST_FAIL` may be retested, and a passing retest overwrites the failed pressure fields and moves the keg from custody `QUARANTINED` back to `IN_SERVICE`. A cycle quarantined for `INSPECTION_MAJOR_DEFECT` may never be retested and may never be released: a structural defect cannot be argued away by a pressure gauge, and the keg must be scrapped through a damage assessment instead. Both a retest and a release attempt on such a cycle are refused with `ERR_QUARANTINE_RELEASE_BLOCKED`. Every accepted step advances the cycle's `version`.

Release closes the cycle and is deliberately split from certification. The caller supplies a `released_at`; the cycle must be in `PRESSURE_TESTED` with `pressure_test_result` `PASS`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the cycle's `pressure_test_performed_by_user_id`, otherwise the release is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the technician who certified the keg may not also be the one who signs it back into service. Release sets `stage` to `RELEASED`, records `released_at` and `released_by_user_id`, and decrements the centre's `open_cycle_count`. On the keg it is a full reset: custody returns to `IN_FLOAT`, `service_state` becomes `SERVICE_OK`, `fills_at_last_pressure_test` is set to the current `fill_count`, `last_pressure_test_at` is set to the cycle's `pressure_tested_at` rather than to `released_at`, `current_brewer_id`, `current_distributor_id`, `issuance_order_id` and `distributor_custody_started_at` are all cleared, `dwell_started_at` is set to `released_at`, `dwell_flag` returns to `WITHIN_LIMIT`, and the keg's `version` advances. The pool census, `available_pct` and `posture` are recomputed and every `ACTIVE` agreement on the pool recomputes `float_posture`. Because `last_pressure_test_at` takes the test instant and not the release instant, the 730-day recertification clock starts when the gauge was read, so a keg that sat in `PRESSURE_TESTED` for a week has already spent that week of its certification.

Cycles are read individually with every step timestamp and result, and listed with filters on `keg_id`, `service_center_id`, `stage`, `inspection_result` and `pressure_test_result`. A service centre is read individually and reports its live `open_cycle_count`.

### 3.9. Loss Assessment, Two Valuation Bases and the Chargeback Ledger

When a keg does not come back, comes back destroyed, or is presumed stolen, an `AssetRecoveryAnalyst` opens a `LossAssessment` on it. The assessment carries two entirely independent status dimensions: `assessment_status`, which records the adjudication, and `chargeback_status`, which records the money. They are decoupled on purpose — `chargeback_status` can travel from `ISSUED` to `REVERSED` while `assessment_status` stays `UPHELD`, and `assessment_status` can reach `WAIVED` while `chargeback_status` never leaves `NONE`.

Opening an assessment takes a `keg_id`, a `cause`, an `expected_keg_version` that must equal the keg's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_KEG_VERSION` — and optionally an `assessment_basis` and a `theft_investigation_id` when the assessment was raised out of an investigation. The `brewer_id` is not supplied: it is resolved as the keg's `filling_brewer_id` when that is set, and otherwise as its `current_brewer_id`. At least one of the two must be set unless the `cause` is `STOLEN_SUSPECTED`, which charges nobody; a keg with neither is refused with `ERR_NO_CHARGEABLE_PARTY`. A commodity index row effective on or before the valuation date must also exist, otherwise the scrap credit cannot be valued and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_COMMODITY_INDEX` — unlike keg import, which tolerates a missing index. The `created_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, the assessment opens in `assessment_status` `PROPOSED` with `chargeback_status` `NONE`, the keg's `assessment_open` flag is raised, and `dwell_days_at_assessment` records the keg's dwell at creation, taken from whichever clock is open — the brewer clock when both are.

The valuation is **pinned at creation and never recomputed**, which is why the `commodity_index_id` in force is stored on the row: a later index posting cannot re-price a standing assessment, and resolving one does not re-run the arithmetic. Two contractually valid bases exist and they produce different numbers for identical inputs.

- Under **`REPLACEMENT_COST`**, `gross_value_cents` is simply the pool's `replacement_cost_cents`, and `fill_count` is ignored entirely.
- Under **`DEPRECIATED_BOOK`**, the replacement cost is written down 45 basis points for every lifetime fill, with total depreciation capped at 7000 basis points. A candidate book value is the `replacement_cost_cents` multiplied by 10000 less that depreciation, divided by 10000, rounded half up; a floor value is 30 percent of `replacement_cost_cents`, rounded half up; and `gross_value_cents` is the greater of the two.

Basis selection is coerced silently and is never an error. When `assessment_basis` is omitted, or is supplied as `DEPRECIATED_BOOK` on a keg whose `fill_count` is 0, or is supplied as `DEPRECIATED_BOOK` together with a `cause` of `STOLEN_SUSPECTED`, the stored `assessment_basis` becomes `REPLACEMENT_COST` and the gross value follows that variant. A thief takes a keg at market value, not at book value, and an unfilled keg has nothing to depreciate. Once `fill_count` is at least 1 and the cause permits it, `DEPRECIATED_BOOK` always yields a strictly lower gross value than `REPLACEMENT_COST`, so reading the wrong basis is observable in the money.

The shared tail is identical for both bases. `scrap_credit_cents` takes the keg's `stainless_scrap_value_cents` only when the `cause` is `DAMAGED_BEYOND_REPAIR`, because only then is the shell physically in hand; `LOST_IN_TRADE`, `NOT_RETURNED_DWELL` and `STOLEN_SUSPECTED` all credit 0 even though the keg has a scrap value on paper, since nobody can weigh in a keg they cannot find. `assessed_value_cents` is the gross value less the scrap credit, floored at 0. `brewer_liability_pct_bp` is keyed off the cause alone: 6000 basis points for `LOST_IN_TRADE`, 10000 for `DAMAGED_BEYOND_REPAIR`, 10000 for `NOT_RETURNED_DWELL` and 0 for `STOLEN_SUSPECTED`. And `net_chargeback_cents` is the assessed value multiplied by that liability share as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up. Rounding is applied separately to the candidate book value, to the floor value and to the net chargeback; basis points are always integers.

As a worked example, take a pool whose `replacement_cost_cents` is 12000 and a keg with a `fill_count` of 40, assessed under `DEPRECIATED_BOOK` for `LOST_IN_TRADE`. Depreciation is 40 multiplied by 45, which is 1800 basis points, below the 7000 cap. The candidate book value is 12000 multiplied by 8200, divided by 10000, which is 9840. The floor is 30 percent of 12000, which is 3600. The greater is 9840, so `gross_value_cents` is 9840. The cause is not `DAMAGED_BEYOND_REPAIR`, so `scrap_credit_cents` is 0 and `assessed_value_cents` is 9840. Liability is 6000 basis points, so `net_chargeback_cents` is 9840 multiplied by 6000, divided by 10000, which is 5904. The same keg assessed under `REPLACEMENT_COST` would carry a gross value of 12000 and a net chargeback of 7200.

Because `STOLEN_SUSPECTED` carries a liability of 0 basis points, its `net_chargeback_cents` is always 0 and its `chargeback_status` stays `NONE` forever: a suspected theft is booked against the pool, not against the brewer, and recovery runs through a theft investigation instead.

A keg may carry only one live assessment, but the two live states behave differently. A brewer may **dispute** a `PROPOSED` assessment by supplying a `dispute_reason`, with the acting `X-User-ID` required to equal the assessment's `brewer_id`; this moves `assessment_status` to `DISPUTED` and leaves `assessment_open` raised. Opening a second assessment on the same keg while the first is still `PROPOSED` is legitimate — the prior assessment moves to `SUPERSEDED`, its `version` advances, the new one is created in `PROPOSED` and the keg's `assessment_open` stays true — but opening one while an existing assessment is `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_OPEN`. Disputing therefore freezes the valuation in place until an analyst adjudicates it, which is exactly the brewer's remedy against an analyst re-pricing the same loss repeatedly.

An analyst **resolves** an assessment from either `PROPOSED` or `DISPUTED` by supplying a `resolution` of `UPHELD` or `WAIVED` and optional `resolution_notes`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the assessment's `created_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`: the analyst who raised the loss may not be the one who confirms it. An uphold additionally requires the brewer's `status` not to be `SUSPENDED`, which yields `ERR_BREWER_SUSPENDED`. Either resolution records `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolution_notes` and advances the assessment's `version`.

A **waive** is deliberately inert: `chargeback_status` stays `NONE`, no money moves, the keg's `custody_state` and every pool census counter are left exactly as they were, `chargeback_balance_cents` is untouched, and only `assessment_open` is lowered. Waiving is a no-op on the ledger because a `NONE` row was never inside the ledger sum in the first place.

An **uphold** fires the longest cascade in the system, and its links run in a fixed order.

1. The assessment moves to `UPHELD` and, when `net_chargeback_cents` is greater than zero, `chargeback_status` flips from `NONE` to `ISSUED` in the same request. The keg is written off according to the cause: `DAMAGED_BEYOND_REPAIR` scraps it, `LOST_IN_TRADE` and `NOT_RETURNED_DWELL` mark it lost, and `STOLEN_SUSPECTED` leaves custody alone. The keg's `assessment_open` is lowered. The custody move is *scoped*: it happens only when the keg-custody machine declares that edge from the keg's current bucket, meaning a keg becomes lost only from `FILLED`, `IN_TRADE` or `AT_RETAIL`, and scrapped only from `QUARANTINED`. An uphold on a keg in any other bucket — a `NOT_RETURNED_DWELL` uphold on a keg still `ISSUED_TO_BREWER`, say, or a `DAMAGED_BEYOND_REPAIR` uphold on a keg that was never quarantined — still upholds the assessment, still issues the chargeback and still clears `assessment_open`, but leaves `custody_state` and every census counter untouched. There is no error for that combination.
2. Because the chargeback became `ISSUED`, the brewer's `chargeback_balance_cents` is recomputed, `upheld_loss_count` increases by one, both `loss_rate_bp_trailing_90d` and `loss_rate_bp_lifetime` are recomputed, and the brewer's `version` advances.
3. Because the balance was recomputed, the probation gate runs: when `chargeback_balance_cents` is at or above 25000 cents and the brewer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the brewer moves to `PROBATION` with `status_reason` recorded as `CHARGEBACK_BALANCE_THRESHOLD`.
4. Because the brewer entered probation, every `ACTIVE` float agreement of that brewer recomputes `float_posture` to `SUSPENDED`, which blocks that brewer's later allocations and fill scans.

After those four links, the pool census, `posture` and each `ACTIVE` agreement's `float_posture` are recomputed for the written-off keg.

The **loss rate** itself has two variants and only one of them gates anything. The trailing variant counts, inside a window running from 90 days before the logical clock through the clock itself with both ends inclusive, the brewer's `UPHELD` assessments by `created_at` as the numerator and the brewer's fill scans by `scanned_at` as the denominator. The lifetime variant uses the unwindowed `upheld_loss_count` over `lifetime_fill_count`. In both cases, a zero denominator reports a rate of 0 and marks the basis insufficient; otherwise the rate is 10000 multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half up — one upheld loss over three fills is 3333 basis points, not 3334. `WAIVED` and `SUPERSEDED` assessments are excluded from both numerators, and `DISPUTED` ones are excluded until they are upheld. The probation gate reads the **trailing** figure only: a brewer whose lifetime rate is far above the threshold but whose trailing rate is below it stays `ACTIVE`. When the trailing rate reaches 1500 basis points and the brewer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the brewer moves to `PROBATION` with `status_reason` `LOSS_RATE_THRESHOLD`. If one uphold would trip both gates at once, the balance gate claims the transition first, and the loss-rate gate then finds the status is no longer `ACTIVE` and does not overwrite the reason; `LOSS_RATE_THRESHOLD` therefore appears only when the recomputed balance is strictly below 25000 cents while the trailing rate is at or above 1500 basis points. Because both ends of the trailing window move with the clock, a fill as well as a loss can drop out of it, so the trailing rate is not monotonic. The loss rate is also readable on demand for one brewer under a `basis` query parameter that defaults to `TRAILING_90D` and also accepts `LIFETIME`; the read reports `brewer_id`, `basis`, `loss_rate_bp`, `upheld_loss_count`, `fill_count`, `window_start`, `window_end` and `basis_insufficient`, returns unset `window_start` and `window_end` for the lifetime basis, and persists nothing.

The **chargeback ledger** is a conserved quantity: a brewer's `chargeback_balance_cents` is always the sum of `net_chargeback_cents` across that brewer's assessments whose `chargeback_status` is `ISSUED` or `SETTLED`. `SETTLED` stays inside the sum because settling records that the brewer paid, not that the liability never existed; `REVERSED` and `NONE` are outside it, and `SUPERSEDED` and `DISPUTED` rows sit at `NONE` and are therefore excluded.

An issued chargeback is closed by a resolution action carrying an `action` and, for a settlement, an `amount_cents`; optional `resolution_notes` may accompany either. Both `PoolOperator` and `AssetRecoveryAnalyst` reach this operation, but the acting role must match the requested action: `SETTLE` requires `PoolOperator` and `REVERSE` requires `AssetRecoveryAnalyst`, so no single role can both raise and erase a chargeback. A mismatch is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and it is judged *before* the settlement amount is compared — an analyst attempting a settlement with the wrong amount sees the segregation failure, not the amount failure. A `SETTLE` requires `chargeback_status` `ISSUED` and an `amount_cents` exactly equal to `net_chargeback_cents`; partial settlement is not supported and any other figure is `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. It moves the status to `SETTLED`, records `settled_amount_cents`, and leaves `chargeback_balance_cents` unchanged. A `REVERSE` also requires `ISSUED` and moves the status to `REVERSED`, which drops the row out of the sum and restores the balance to exactly its pre-uphold value — bit for bit, with no drift. Both actions advance the assessment's `version` and the brewer's `version`.

A reversal that drops the balance back below 25000 cents lifts the probation it caused, but only that one: the brewer returns to `ACTIVE` with `status_reason` cleared **only** when the current `status` is `PROBATION` *and* the current `status_reason` is `CHARGEBACK_BALANCE_THRESHOLD`, which in turn releases the `SUSPENDED` float posture on that brewer's `ACTIVE` agreements. A probation whose reason is `LOSS_RATE_THRESHOLD`, or any free text an operator set through the brewer-status override, is not lifted by a reversal even though the balance falls: a reversal only undoes the gate it tripped. And the loss counters are money-independent — `upheld_loss_count` and both loss-rate figures are never recomputed by a reversal — so the trailing rate that forced probation still stands afterwards.

Assessments are read individually with their pinned valuation and both status dimensions, and listed with filters on `keg_id`, `brewer_id`, `commodity_index_id`, `theft_investigation_id`, `assessment_status`, `chargeback_status` and `cause`.

### 3.10. Theft Investigation, Evidence Holds and the Write-Off

Systematic keg theft — shells sold for their stainless content — is not a single-keg loss, so it is handled as an investigation over a set of kegs rather than as an assessment on each. An `AssetRecoveryAnalyst` opens one by supplying the `pool_id` under investigation, a narrative `opened_reason` of 1 to 500 characters, the list of `keg_ids` to attach, and optionally a `suspect_distributor_id`. Every element of `keg_ids` must be a keg **of that pool**: a keg that exists but belongs to a different pool is reported as `ERR_KEG_NOT_FOUND` and the whole request is refused, with no partial attach. Elements are resolved in the order supplied, and the pool named earlier in the same body scopes them.

Opening the investigation sets `status` to `OPEN`, stamps `opened_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and writes `theft_investigation_id` onto each named keg. That attachment alone removes the keg from allocation candidacy **without changing its `custody_state`** — a keg under investigation stays where it is in the census and simply stops being issuable. `attached_keg_count` records how many kegs are attached, `held_keg_count` starts at 0, and `stainless_value_at_risk_cents` is computed as the aggregate scrap exposure of the attached kegs: for each attached keg, its pool's `stainless_weight_kg` multiplied by the stainless price in force multiplied by the 65 percent recovery factor, rounded half up **per keg**, and only then summed. Rounding once per keg and summing afterwards is required; summing the raw products and rounding the total would give a different figure. The exposure counts every attached keg regardless of `custody_state`, including kegs already moved to `STOLEN` by an earlier write-off, and because the attached kegs may sit in different pools their stainless weights are not uniform. The `commodity_index_id` in force is pinned onto the investigation each time the exposure is recomputed.

The investigation is then driven by a single status operation carrying an `action` of `EVIDENCE_HOLD`, `RESUME` or `CLOSE`, plus the fields that action needs: `keg_ids` and `hold_started_at` for a hold, and an `outcome` with optional `outcome_notes` for a close. Every action recomputes the pool census, `available_pct`, `posture` and every `ACTIVE` agreement's `float_posture`, and advances the investigation's `version`.

An **evidence hold** requires `status` `OPEN` and a `hold_started_at` not in the future relative to the logical clock. Every keg named in `keg_ids` must already be attached to *this* investigation and must not be in `LOST`, `STOLEN` or `SCRAPPED`; a keg that is unattached or already written off is refused with `ERR_KEG_NOT_ATTACHED`. Each named keg records its current custody state into `pre_hold_custody_state` and then moves to `EVIDENCE_HOLD`, which pulls it out of whichever census bucket it occupied and into `kegs_held`. The investigation moves to `EVIDENCE_HOLD` and recomputes `hold_started_at`, `held_keg_count` and `stainless_value_at_risk_cents`.

A **resume** requires `status` `EVIDENCE_HOLD` and is the exact inverse: each held keg's `custody_state` is restored from its `pre_hold_custody_state`, which returns every pool census counter to its pre-hold value, and the investigation returns to `OPEN` with `held_keg_count` back at 0 and `hold_started_at` cleared. The hold-and-resume pair may repeat any number of times before a terminal close, which is what makes this machine cyclic: evidence surfaces, kegs are impounded, the lead evaporates, the kegs go back into rotation, and the cycle can begin again. A hold may be placed on a keg sitting in `IN_FLOAT`, `FILLED`, `IN_TRADE`, `AT_RETAIL` or `IN_SERVICE`, and a resume returns it to precisely the one it came from — which is exactly why `pre_hold_custody_state` is stored per keg rather than assumed, since a single hold can sweep up kegs from all five buckets at once and each must get its own bucket back.

A **close** may be recorded from either `OPEN` or `EVIDENCE_HOLD`, and the two outcomes diverge completely.

With an `outcome` of `RECOVERED`, every attached keg that is on `EVIDENCE_HOLD` moves to `IN_SERVICE` and detaches by having its `theft_investigation_id` cleared, while an attached keg that was never held only detaches and keeps its `custody_state`. The investigation moves to `CLOSED_RECOVERED` with `closed_at` and `outcome_notes` recorded. No accrual is clawed back: the kegs came home, so the returns that were paid on them were real.

With an `outcome` of `WRITTEN_OFF`, every attached keg that is on `EVIDENCE_HOLD` moves to `STOLEN` — and an evidence hold is the *only* route into `STOLEN`, so a keg can never be written off as stolen without having been formally impounded first — while an attached keg that was never held only detaches. The investigation moves to `CLOSED_WRITTEN_OFF` with `closed_at` and `outcome_notes` recorded. When `suspect_distributor_id` is set, that distributor's accruals in `ACCRUED` **or** `PAID` for the attached kegs all flip to `CLAWED_BACK` with a `clawback_reason` recorded, and `incentive_accrual_cents` is recomputed, which restores it to its exact pre-accrual value: since a `PAID` row never left the ledger sum, clawing back a paid accrual moves the balance by precisely the same amount as clawing back an accrued one. What is *not* restored is the service history — `total_returns_count`, `on_time_returns_count`, `on_time_return_pct` and `avg_dwell_days` all keep the returns, and any `cooperation_tier` promotion those returns earned stands. Service history is not money and is not conserved; a distributor that helped move stolen kegs does not get its statistics rewritten, only its payments reversed.

Investigations are read individually with their held and attached counts and their stainless exposure, and listed with filters on `pool_id`, `suspect_distributor_id`, `commodity_index_id` and `status`.

## 4. API Endpoints

All request and response bodies are JSON and every response follows the envelope described in §2.2. Operations that create a new resource return HTTP 201 with the created row in the `data` field; every other successful operation, including all state transitions, computed reads and list reads, returns HTTP 200. Failures return the status code recorded against their error identifier in §5. The path parameters used below are `brewer_id`, `distributor_id`, `pool_id`, `keg_id`, `scan_event_id`, `agreement_id`, `order_id`, `center_id`, `cycle_id`, `assessment_id`, `claim_id`, `accrual_id` and `investigation_id`; they are resolved left to right before any body field is examined. List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` in addition to the filters named in their descriptions.
