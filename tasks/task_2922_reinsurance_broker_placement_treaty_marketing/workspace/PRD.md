# Reinsurance Treaty Placement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A reinsurance broker sits between an insurer that wants to shed risk and a panel of markets willing to assume it, and almost every problem in that business is a bookkeeping problem dressed up as a negotiation. The cedent's exposure data arrives incomplete and has to be scrubbed and scored before an actuary will put their name to it. The program is then cut into quota-share, excess-of-loss and catastrophe layers, each with its own rate, reinstatements, sublimits and exclusions. Those layers go out to a dozen markets, each of which quotes a line; the lines rarely sum to exactly one hundred per cent, so the broker either keeps marketing or signs everybody down. Money then flows through the broker's own fiduciary account, where the intermediary's compensation must be disclosed to the cedent before anything is bound, and where the person who books a movement must never be the person who releases it.

This document specifies the backend service that carries that workflow. It is a placement and treaty-marketing system for a reinsurance intermediary, covering exposure submission packaging with actuarial support, program structure design, market submission and quote gathering, signing-line allocation across reinsurers, terms negotiation on reinstatements, sublimits, exclusions and commission structures, firm ordering and binding, contract wording certainty and slip-to-contract documentation timelines, brokerage compensation disclosure and fiduciary premium handling, claims advocacy and collection support on disputed cessions, and renewal analytics with market-condition reporting.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating before any detail. First, **every arithmetic result is pinned**: the order in which values are rounded, the order in which lines are allocated, and the point at which a derived figure is frozen are all part of the contract, not implementation latitude — a premium rounded at the wrong step is a wrong premium even when it differs by a single minor unit. Second, **every temporal comparison reads a server-controlled logical clock**, never the host's wall clock, so that deadlines, quote expiries, treaty periods and contract-certainty windows are reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the authority that principal is exercising. If either header is absent, an empty string, or contains only whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (HTTP 401); a whitespace-only header counts as absent, not as a value.

Seven roles exist, and their names must be spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `BrokerPlacementLead`, `BrokerActuary`, `BrokerAccountant`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `CedentUnderwriter`, and `ReinsurerUnderwriter`. The role model is one of **segregation of duties**: placement authority, actuarial sign-off, money movement and independent control are held by four different broker-side roles, and the two market-facing counterparties are external parties who may only touch their own resources.

- `PlatformAdmin` operates the brokerage platform. It seeds the counterparty registry and drives the logical clock, and holds no placement, money or control authority.
- `BrokerPlacementLead` is the placing broker. It structures the program, takes it to market, negotiates, firm-orders, signs down, binds, files contract documentation and runs claims advocacy. It cannot sign off exposure data, cannot move fiduciary money, cannot approve or reverse a transaction, and cannot change a counterparty's security or suspension posture.
- `BrokerActuary` provides broker-side actuarial support: it signs off or rejects the cedent's exposure package and generates renewal analytics. It has no placement, binding or money authority, and — uniquely among the broker-side roles — it may not read the fiduciary ledger.
- `BrokerAccountant` owns fiduciary accounting. It books and settles premium and claim movements, publishes the brokerage compensation disclosure, files collections and closes the placement year. It cannot approve a transaction it booked itself, and it cannot place or bind.
- `ComplianceOfficer` is the independent control function. It owns counterparty suspension and market security posture, the contract-certainty checkpoint and the reversal of a settled movement, and it is the second pair of eyes on every approval. It can never originate a transaction, and it cannot place, bind or sign off.
- `CedentUnderwriter` is the ceding insurer. It prepares and revises its own exposure package, acknowledges or rejects the broker's compensation disclosure, notifies claims and may withdraw a notification.
- `ReinsurerUnderwriter` is the assuming reinsurer. It quotes, requotes and declines against submissions addressed to it, and raises cession disputes on its own signing lines. It never sees another market's quotes or lines.

**Identity semantics.** The five broker-side and platform-side roles — `PlatformAdmin`, `BrokerPlacementLead`, `BrokerActuary`, `BrokerAccountant` and `ComplianceOfficer` — carry **opaque** identities: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is nonetheless recorded on resources where a transition writes it — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `filed_by_user_id` on the broker's submissions and documents, as `actuary_signoff_user_id` on a signed-off exposure package, as `generated_by_user_id` on a renewal report, as `disclosed_by_user_id` on a released disclosure, and as `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a fiduciary movement. The recognised opaque identities are fixed: `admin-1` for `PlatformAdmin`; `lead-1` and `lead-2` for `BrokerPlacementLead`; `act-1` for `BrokerActuary`; `acct-1` and `acct-2` for `BrokerAccountant`; and `compl-1` for `ComplianceOfficer`. A role may therefore hold more than one identity, which matters because the segregation-of-duties check compares identities, not roles.

The two counterparty roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `CedentUnderwriter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Cedent`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Cedent` compare the resource's `cedent_id` — reached directly on a program, or through the parent program of an exposure package, disclosure or advocacy — to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ReinsurerUnderwriter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reinsurer`; ownership checks compare the target resource's `reinsurer_id`, whether it sits on a market submission, a quote or a signing line, to `X-User-ID`.

Two failures follow from this. If `X-User-ID` does not resolve to a known actor — it is neither one of the fixed opaque identities above, nor the `id` of an existing `Cedent` when the role is `CedentUnderwriter`, nor the `id` of an existing `Reinsurer` when the role is `ReinsurerUnderwriter` — the request fails with `ERR_UNKNOWN_USER` (HTTP 401). If the identity resolves but the role is not among those the called endpoint admits, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). And if an entity-backed caller is authorised for the endpoint but reaches for a resource belonging to a different instance of its backing entity, the request fails with `ERR_FOREIGN_RESOURCE` (HTTP 403). That ownership check guards exactly seven write operations: revising an exposure package, quoting against a market submission, requoting, declining a quote, deciding on a brokerage disclosure, withdrawing a notified claim, and raising a cession dispute.

The per-endpoint role lists are given in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` is an integer with a default of 10, a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is an integer with a default of 0 and a minimum of 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer, is below 1 or is above 100, and an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, are rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order. Because the logical clock can be parked on a single instant while several rows are created, identical `created_at` values to the millisecond are reachable, so the tie-break is pinned: rows sharing a `created_at` are returned in the server's monotonically increasing insertion sequence for that collection — that is, in the order in which they were created. The randomly generated `id` never participates in ordering.

Four pagination fields appear in the `meta` object of every list response: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used, `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters **before** `limit` and `offset` are applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows returned in this page is less than `total_count`.

Every query filter declared for a list endpoint is optional, so a bare call with no query string is always legal and simply returns the first page of the unfiltered collection.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

There is no wall clock in this system. Every deadline, expiry, treaty-period and contract-certainty comparison reads a single server-held logical clock, and an administrative test-driving capability sets it to a supplied `current_utc` instant. Before that capability is ever called, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`; that startup default is mandatory and must never fall back to real time. Setting the clock mutates no entity — it simply changes the instant against which every subsequent request evaluates its temporal rules, so a test may park the clock, create several rows, and then jump forward past a deadline.

Instants supplied to the API and returned by it are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, and instants are compared at millisecond resolution. Wherever this document says a comparison is strict, equality fails; wherever it says equality is admitted, the boundary value passes. Those boundaries are load-bearing and are stated individually at each rule.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recording the logical-clock instant at which the row was inserted, in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that resource — including mutations that arrive as a side effect of an operation on a different resource. Twelve of the fourteen resource types behave this way: cedents, reinsurers, programs, exposure submissions, layers, market submissions, quotes, signing lines, brokerage disclosures, fiduciary transactions, claim advocacies and cession disputes.

Two resource types are append-only evidence records and are the exception: a filed contract document and a generated renewal report carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them at all, because neither is ever mutated after it is written.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers in minor currency units, and every field name carrying money ends in `_minor`. There are no fractional minor units anywhere in the system; a computation that produces a fractional amount is rounded to a whole minor unit at the step the relevant rule names, and never earlier or later.

All percentages carry at most four decimal places, and a percentage supplied with more than four decimals is a request-shape violation. Two rounding operations are used and must be distinguished. **Round half up** means round away from zero at the stated precision, so a value exactly halfway between two representable results goes to the larger magnitude. **Floor at four decimals** truncates toward zero at the fourth decimal place and never rounds up; it is used only in signing-down allocation, where truncation followed by an explicit remainder pass is what guarantees the allocated total closes exactly.

Unless a rule says otherwise, monetary results are rounded half up to whole minor units, and derived percentages are rounded half up to the precision the rule names — four decimal places for rates, lines and commission percentages, and two decimal places for scores and renewal analytics. Where a multi-stage calculation is specified, each stage consumes the previous stage's **already rounded** value unless the rule explicitly says the intermediate is carried unrounded; that distinction changes results and is part of the contract.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Two write operations reserve capacity or money against a parent aggregate whose state the client must have observed, and both use optimistic concurrency against the parent's `version`.

Firm-ordering a quote requires a non-optional `expected_layer_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Layer`. If that value does not equal the layer's current `version`, the firm order is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LAYER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This check is the only mechanism by which the system tells a broker that the layer's placed capacity is no longer what they priced their allocation against: oversubscription is legitimate product behaviour, so a second firm order on the same layer would otherwise succeed and simply be resolved later by signing down.

Booking a fiduciary movement on a program requires a non-optional `expected_program_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Program`. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Multiple movements on one program are perfectly legitimate, so without this check the booking would succeed against a `fiduciary_holding_minor` the client never saw; the check forces the accountant to re-read the balance before committing funds.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Every other write operates on whatever the current state is.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Validation Ladder

Path segments are resolved strictly from left to right, and a foreign key supplied in a request body is resolved only after every path segment has resolved. So a request naming a non-existent program and a non-existent layer reports the program failure, and a request naming a valid layer but a non-existent market in its body reports the market failure only because the layer resolved first.

Every path parameter in this system names the identifier of exactly one resource and is always the `id` of that resource: `cedent_id` a cedent, `reinsurer_id` a market, `program_id` a program, `submission_id` an exposure submission, `layer_id` a layer, `market_submission_id` a market submission, `quote_id` a quote, `signing_line_id` a signing line, `disclosure_id` a brokerage disclosure, `document_id` a contract document, `transaction_id` a fiduciary movement, `claim_advocacy_id` an advocacy file, `dispute_id` a cession dispute, and `report_id` a renewal report. Where the same identifier also appears as a body field or a query filter it carries exactly the same meaning.

Checks are evaluated in ascending priority, and **the first violated rule decides the response** — later violations are never reported. The ladder runs authentication, then authorization, then request shape, then existence, then own-state, then cross-entity state, then stale parent version, then regulatory and compliance safety, then temporal windows, then structural constraints, then referential linkage, and finally fiscal capacity. Fiscal checks come last deliberately: in a placement business the broker must be told that the terms themselves are inadmissible — wrong state, suspended market, blown deadline, oversized line — before being told that the market has run out of money for them.

In priority order, the tiers are as follows.

**Priorities 1–2, authentication (all endpoints).** The `X-User-ID` header must be present and non-blank, else `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID`; it must then resolve to a known actor, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_USER`.

**Priority 3, authorization (all endpoints).** The caller's role must appear in the called endpoint's role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

**Priority 4, ownership.** An entity-backed caller may act only on its own resources, else `ERR_FOREIGN_RESOURCE`. This applies to `POST /exposure-submissions/{submission_id}/revise`, `POST /market-submissions/{market_submission_id}/quotes`, `POST /quotes/{quote_id}/requote`, `POST /quotes/{quote_id}/decline`, `POST /brokerage-disclosures/{disclosure_id}/decision`, `POST /claim-advocacies/{claim_advocacy_id}/withdraw` and `POST /claim-advocacies/{claim_advocacy_id}/cession-disputes`.

**Priorities 5–7, request shape, all reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).** On every POST and PATCH endpoint, each input field that has no declared default must be present and non-null (priority 5); enum values must be declared members, numbers must respect their declared range and decimal width, monetary values must be integers in minor units, percentages must carry at most four decimals, datetimes must be ISO-8601 UTC instants with exactly three fractional digits, and arrays must contain only declared members (priority 6). On every collection GET, `limit` must be an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` an integer of at least 0 (priority 7).

**Priorities 8–20, existence, each with its own 404 code.** A `{cedent_id}` path segment or a body `cedent_id` must resolve to an existing cedent, else `ERR_CEDENT_NOT_FOUND` — this guards `GET /cedents/{cedent_id}`, `POST /cedents/{cedent_id}/suspension`, `GET /cedents/{cedent_id}/programs` and `POST /programs` (priority 8). A `{reinsurer_id}` path segment or a body `reinsurer_id` must resolve to an existing market, else `ERR_REINSURER_NOT_FOUND` — guarding `GET /reinsurers/{reinsurer_id}`, `POST /reinsurers/{reinsurer_id}/security-status`, `GET /reinsurers/{reinsurer_id}/signing-lines`, `GET /reinsurers/{reinsurer_id}/market-submissions` and `POST /layers/{layer_id}/market-submissions` (priority 9). A `{program_id}` segment must resolve on every `/programs/{program_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (priority 10). A non-null `prior_program_id` in the body of `POST /programs` must resolve, else `ERR_PRIOR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (priority 11). A `{submission_id}` segment must resolve on every `/exposure-submissions/{submission_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_EXPOSURE_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` (priority 12). A `{layer_id}` segment must resolve on every `/layers/{layer_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_LAYER_NOT_FOUND` (priority 13). A `{market_submission_id}` segment must resolve on every `/market-submissions/{market_submission_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_MARKET_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` (priority 14). A `{quote_id}` segment must resolve on every `/quotes/{quote_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND` (priority 15). A `{signing_line_id}` segment, or a non-null `signing_line_id` in a body, must resolve, else `ERR_SIGNING_LINE_NOT_FOUND` — guarding `POST /signing-lines/{signing_line_id}/release`, `GET /signing-lines/{signing_line_id}`, `POST /programs/{program_id}/fiduciary-transactions` and `POST /claim-advocacies/{claim_advocacy_id}/cession-disputes` (priority 16). A `{disclosure_id}` segment must resolve on every `/brokerage-disclosures/{disclosure_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND` (priority 17). A `{transaction_id}` segment must resolve on every `/fiduciary-transactions/{transaction_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND` (priority 18). A `{claim_advocacy_id}` segment must resolve on every `/claim-advocacies/{claim_advocacy_id}...` endpoint, else `ERR_CLAIM_ADVOCACY_NOT_FOUND` (priority 19). A `{dispute_id}` segment must resolve on `POST /cession-disputes/{dispute_id}/resolve` and `GET /cession-disputes/{dispute_id}`, else `ERR_CESSION_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` (priority 20). Two further path segments, `{document_id}` on `GET /contract-documents/{document_id}` and `{report_id}` on `GET /renewal-reports/{report_id}`, address append-only evidence records directly.

**Priorities 21–31, own-state gates (HTTP 409).** Each entity's own status must admit the requested action: the program's `placement_status` (priority 21, `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`), the program's `documentation_status` against the filed `document_type` (priority 22, `ERR_DOCUMENT_SEQUENCE_INVALID`), the exposure submission's status (priority 23, `ERR_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`), the layer's status (priority 24, `ERR_LAYER_STATE_INVALID`), the market submission's status (priority 25, `ERR_MARKET_SUBMISSION_CLOSED`), the quote's status (priority 26, `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`), the signing line's status (priority 27, `ERR_SIGNING_LINE_STATE_INVALID`), the disclosure's status (priority 28, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`), the fiduciary transaction's status (priority 29, `ERR_TRANSACTION_STATE_INVALID`), the advocacy's status (priority 30, `ERR_ADVOCACY_STATE_INVALID`) and the dispute's status (priority 31, `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`). §3 states, for each action, exactly which statuses admit it.

**Priorities 32–34, cross-entity state (HTTP 409).** The program owning a target layer, market submission or quote must itself be in the placement state the action needs, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`; this guards `POST /programs/{program_id}/layers`, `PATCH /layers/{layer_id}`, `POST /layers/{layer_id}/market-submissions`, `POST /market-submissions/{market_submission_id}/quotes`, `POST /quotes/{quote_id}/firm-order`, `POST /layers/{layer_id}/sign-down` and `POST /signing-lines/{signing_line_id}/release` (priority 32). A loss may be notified only against a bound layer, else `ERR_LAYER_NOT_BOUND` (priority 33). Premium out-payments may settle only once wording is agreed, else `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_NOT_AGREED` (priority 34).

**Priorities 35–36, stale parent version (HTTP 412).** `expected_layer_version` must equal the layer's current `version` on `POST /quotes/{quote_id}/firm-order`, else `ERR_STALE_LAYER_VERSION`; `expected_program_version` must equal the program's current `version` on `POST /programs/{program_id}/fiduciary-transactions`, else `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION`.

**Priorities 37–42, regulatory and compliance safety (HTTP 422).** A suspended market may neither receive a submission nor be firm-ordered, else `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED` (priority 37, guarding `POST /layers/{layer_id}/market-submissions` and `POST /quotes/{quote_id}/firm-order`). Lead status requires lead authorisation, else `ERR_LEAD_NOT_AUTHORISED` (priority 38, guarding `POST /market-submissions/{market_submission_id}/quotes` and `POST /quotes/{quote_id}/requote`). A suspended cedent blocks go-to-market, else `ERR_CEDENT_SUSPENDED` (priority 39). Sign-off requires a sufficient completeness score, else `ERR_SIGNOFF_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (priority 40). Binding requires an acknowledged compensation disclosure, else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (priority 41). Approving a fiduciary movement requires an approver identity distinct from the booker's, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (priority 42).

**Priorities 43–48, temporal windows (HTTP 422).** These are `ERR_TREATY_PERIOD_INVALID` on program creation (priority 43), `ERR_DEADLINE_NOT_IN_FUTURE` on market submission, quoting and requoting (priority 44), `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED` on quoting (priority 45), `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` on countering and firm-ordering (priority 46), `ERR_EVENT_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD` on claim notification (priority 47) and `ERR_CERTAINTY_NOT_BREACHED` on the documentation checkpoint (priority 48).

**Priorities 49–66, structural constraints and referential linkage.** These are `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAME` (priority 49), `ERR_OVERLAPPING_TREATY_PERIOD` (priority 50), `ERR_RENEWAL_PRIOR_MISMATCH` (priority 51), `ERR_LAYER_STRUCTURE_INVALID` (priority 52), `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` (priority 53), `ERR_DUPLICATE_MARKET_SUBMISSION` (priority 54), `ERR_LINE_EXCEEDS_OFFER` (priority 55), `ERR_LINE_EXCEEDS_MAX_LINE` (priority 56), `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (priority 57), `ERR_LAYER_NOT_OVERSUBSCRIBED` (priority 58), `ERR_LEAD_LINE_MISSING` (priority 59), `ERR_DISCLOSURE_PCT_MISMATCH` (priority 60), `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_REFERENCE` (priority 61), `ERR_TRANSACTION_TYPE_LINK_INVALID` (priority 62), `ERR_LINE_LAYER_MISMATCH` (priority 63), `ERR_ALREADY_REVERSED` (priority 64), `ERR_RESOLUTION_AMOUNT_INVALID` (priority 65) and `ERR_COLLECTION_BLOCKED_BY_ARBITRATION` (priority 66). Each condition is stated in the §3 subsection that owns the workflow it guards.

**Priorities 67–72, fiscal capacity.** These are `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (priority 67), `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED` (priority 68), `ERR_DISPUTE_EXCEEDS_CESSION` (priority 69), `ERR_COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_COLLECTIBLE` (priority 70), `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FIDUCIARY_BALANCE` (priority 71) and `ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_TRANSACTIONS` (priority 72).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because own-state (priority 24) precedes cross-entity state (priority 33), a claim notified against a layer that is not bound reports `ERR_LAYER_NOT_BOUND` and never `ERR_LAYER_STATE_INVALID` — the layer-status rule at priority 24 deliberately excludes claim notification from its scope, so exactly one error is possible on that path. And because the stale-version check sits at priority 35, above every safety, temporal, structural and fiscal check, a firm order carrying a stale `expected_layer_version` reports the staleness even when the line would also have been oversized or the market's capacity exhausted.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Many operations in this system change more than the resource they name. A firm order creates a signing line, which re-derives its layer's placement totals, which can promote the program, which in turn lapses every still-open quote and closes every still-open submission on that program — four hops from one call. Releasing a line runs the same chain in reverse. Suspending a market runs it in reverse across every program that market participates in.

Three rules govern all of it. First, cascades are **immediate and complete within the request**: by the time a response is returned, every derived field, every dependent status and every aggregate named in the cascade has been brought up to date, and a subsequent read never sees a half-applied cascade. Second, every entity whose state or derived fields a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, exactly as if it had been written directly — so a cascade is what makes another client's cached `expected_layer_version` or `expected_program_version` stale. Third, a **compound status is always re-derived, never assumed**: a layer's status is a function of the signing lines currently on it, so after any line is created or released the totals are recomputed first and the status follows from the recomputed totals.

Reverts are not perfect mirrors of the forward cascade, and the asymmetries are deliberate. When a program falls back from firm order to in-market because a line was released, quotes that had already lapsed are **not** revived. When a signed-down layer loses a line, the remaining signed lines keep the signed percentages they were allocated — the sign-down is not recomputed. And when a late wording filing lifts a contract-certainty breach, the breach itself is not recorded on the program at all; the only surviving evidence is the timing trail on the filed documents.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties over Fiduciary Money

The intermediary holds client money, so the fiduciary chain is split into four distinct acts performed by distinct authorities: an accountant **books** a movement, a second identity **approves** it, an accountant **settles** it, and only a compliance officer may **reverse** a settled movement. Booking creates the row but moves no money. Approval moves no money either. Only settlement changes the program's balance, and only a compensating reversal row can undo it.

The approval step compares identities rather than roles. A `ComplianceOfficer` may always approve, because that role can never book a movement in the first place. A `BrokerAccountant` may approve only a movement whose `created_by_user_id` differs from the approving `X-User-ID`; approving its own booking is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422). This is why the accounting role holds more than one identity: two accountants can cover each other, but neither can self-approve.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Counterparty Registry and the Treaty Placement File

Nothing can be placed until both sides of the trade exist. A `PlatformAdmin` registers a ceding insurer by supplying its legal `name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters and unique across the cedent register, a five-character uppercase `domicile_code` such as `US-NY`, and a `financial_rating` drawn from `A_PLUS`, `A`, `B_PLUS` and `B` — the credit rating that later feeds renewal analytics. A new cedent opens with `status` set to `ACTIVE` and `suspension_reason` null, and with both of its fiduciary aggregates, `settled_receipts_minor` and `outstanding_reinstatement_premium_minor`, at 0. Registering a name that already exists, compared without regard to letter case, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAME`.

A reinsurance market is registered the same way, by the same authority. Its `name` is likewise 1 to 120 characters and unique across the market register, and it carries a `security_rating` of `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BBB` or `BB`, a `capacity_limit_minor` that is an integer greater than zero and expresses the aggregate the market will expose per aggregation window in minor units, and a `max_line_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 with up to four decimal places, being the largest line the market will write on any single layer. The optional `lead_authorised` flag defaults to false and records whether the market is permitted to quote as lead. A new market opens with `security_status` set to `APPROVED`, `committed_capacity_minor` at 0 and `available_capacity_minor` equal to its `capacity_limit_minor`.

Counterparty posture is a control function, not a placement function. A `ComplianceOfficer` sets a cedent's `status` — either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — together with the `suspension_reason` recorded alongside it, and a suspended cedent can no longer take any program to market, though programs already in market are left entirely alone. The same authority sets a market's `security_status` to `APPROVED`, `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`, supplying a `reason` for the change. The three postures differ in what they permit: an `APPROVED` market may both lead and follow, a `RESTRICTED` market may follow but never lead, and a `SUSPENDED` market may not receive submissions at all and triggers the security cascade described in §3.5.

**Opening the file.** A `BrokerPlacementLead` opens a treaty placement file by supplying the `cedent_id` of the ceding insurer, a `program_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `line_of_business` of `PROPERTY`, `CASUALTY`, `MARINE` or `MOTOR`, an `underwriting_year` between 2020 and 2035 inclusive, an `inception_date` and an `expiry_date`, and the cedent-declared `estimated_premium_income_minor` for the whole program as an integer greater than zero. Four inputs are optional. `prior_program_id` names the expiring program this one renews and is null for a first-time placement. `currency_code` is the three-character uppercase settlement currency and defaults to `USD`. `brokerage_pct` is the brokerage the intermediary retains on each signing line's share of premium; it lies between 0 and 10 inclusive with up to four decimals and defaults to `2.5000`. `signdown_method` selects which allocation variant applies when a layer is oversubscribed, is either `PRO_RATA` or `LEAD_PROTECTED`, and defaults to `PRO_RATA`.

The treaty period is the **half-open** interval running from `inception_date` up to but excluding `expiry_date`. The `expiry_date` must be strictly after the `inception_date`, and the UTC calendar year of the `inception_date` must equal the `underwriting_year`; either failure is reported as `ERR_TREATY_PERIOD_INVALID`. Because the period is half-open, a program incepting at exactly the instant another expires does not overlap it — but if the new period does overlap the period of any other program of the same `cedent_id` and the same `line_of_business` whose `placement_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`, the creation is refused with `ERR_OVERLAPPING_TREATY_PERIOD`. When `prior_program_id` is supplied it must reference a program of the same `cedent_id` and the same `line_of_business` whose `underwriting_year` is exactly one less than this program's; any deviation is `ERR_RENEWAL_PRIOR_MISMATCH`.

A newly opened file starts with `placement_status` at `DRAFT` and `documentation_status` at `NOT_STARTED`. These are **two independent state dimensions on the same resource**: placement runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMISSION_READY`, `IN_MARKET`, `FIRM_ORDER`, `BOUND`, `SETTLED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `SETTLED` and `WITHDRAWN` terminal, while documentation runs `NOT_STARTED`, `SLIP_ISSUED`, `WORDING_DRAFTED`, `WORDING_AGREED`, `EXECUTED` and `CERTAINTY_BREACHED`, with `EXECUTED` terminal. Neither dimension constrains the other except where a rule explicitly says so. At creation the program's `contract_certainty_deadline` is materialised as the `inception_date` advanced by thirty days, and `disclosure_acknowledged` is false. Five money aggregates — `gross_written_premium_minor`, `brokerage_earned_minor`, `net_remittance_minor`, `rounding_residue_minor` and `fiduciary_holding_minor` — all initialise to 0, and `withdrawal_reason` is null.

**Abandoning the file.** The placing broker may withdraw a placement from `DRAFT`, `SUBMISSION_READY`, `IN_MARKET` or `FIRM_ORDER`, supplying a `reason` that is captured as `withdrawal_reason`; from any other placement state the attempt is `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. Withdrawal from `DRAFT` or `SUBMISSION_READY` has nothing to unwind and simply moves `placement_status` to `WITHDRAWN` and increments the program's `version`. Withdrawal from `IN_MARKET` or from `FIRM_ORDER` runs an identical, larger cascade: every layer of the program whose status is not `BOUND` becomes `LAPSED`; every signing line on those layers in `PROVISIONAL` or `SIGNED` becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `PROGRAM_WITHDRAWN`; every quote on those layers in `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED`, `REQUOTED` or `FIRM_ORDER` becomes `LAPSED`; every market submission on those layers in `SENT` or `QUOTED` becomes `CLOSED`; `committed_capacity_minor` is recomputed for every market whose lines were handed back; and only then does `placement_status` become `WITHDRAWN` with the program's `version` incremented.

### 3.2. Exposure Packaging and Actuarial Sign-Off

Before a treaty can be marketed, somebody has to vouch for the numbers the markets will price against. Either the placing broker or the ceding insurer itself may assemble an exposure package on a program, supplying a `package_label` of 1 to 80 characters, an `exposure_records_count` that is an integer of at least zero, a `data_quality_flags_count` recording how many of those records the data-quality scrub flagged — which must be at least zero and no greater than `exposure_records_count` — a `historical_years_supplied` between 0 and 20 inclusive, a `cat_model_version` string of 1 to 40 characters naming the catastrophe model version used for the exposure run, and a `loss_history_completeness_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive with up to four decimals, being the actuary's assessment of how complete the loss history is. An optional `actuarial_report_attached` flag defaults to false and records whether the signed actuarial support report is attached. The package is created in status `DRAFT`, its `program_id` is taken from the program it is created under, and its `actuary_signoff_user_id`, `signed_off_at` and `review_note` all start null.

**Scoring the package.** A `completeness_score` is computed on creation, recomputed whenever the cedent revises the package, and recomputed again when the package is submitted for review, so the score an actuary sees is always current. The score is a weighted sum of four components out of a total of exactly 100 points, and every intermediate ratio is carried unrounded so that the weights cannot drift.

The first component is worth 40 points and rewards clean data: it is the proportion of unflagged records, that is, the number of records supplied less the number flagged, divided by the number supplied. When no records at all were supplied, this proportion is defined to be zero rather than an arithmetic error — a package with an `exposure_records_count` of zero scores nothing on data quality and does not crash the scorer. The second component is worth 25 points and rewards loss history: it is the number of years supplied, capped at a target of five years, divided by five, so five or more years earns the full 25 and four years earns four fifths of it. The third component is worth 25 points and is the `loss_history_completeness_pct` expressed as a proportion, that is, divided by 100. The fourth component is worth a flat 10 points and is earned in full when `actuarial_report_attached` is true and not at all when it is false. The four weighted components are added together and the resulting raw total is rounded half up to two decimal places to give `completeness_score`.

**The review loop.** The placing broker sends a package to the broker actuary, moving it from `DRAFT` to `ACTUARY_REVIEW` and recomputing the score in passing; this requires the package to be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the owning program's `placement_status` still to be `DRAFT`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. A `BrokerActuary` then records a `decision`, optionally accompanied by a `review_note`, against a package in `ACTUARY_REVIEW`; from any other status the decision is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`.

A `decision` of `SIGNED_OFF` requires the package's `completeness_score` to be at least 70.00 — a score of exactly 70.00 passes — and a lower score is refused with `ERR_SIGNOFF_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. Note the ordering: the status gate is checked before the score gate, so signing off a package that is already `SIGNED_OFF` reports the status error and never the score error. On success the package's status becomes `SIGNED_OFF`, `actuary_signoff_user_id` is stamped with the acting actuary's `X-User-ID`, `signed_off_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and `review_note` records whatever note was supplied. Two cascades then fire. Every **other** exposure package of the same program that was itself in `SIGNED_OFF` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, which is that entity's terminal state — only one signed-off package can be the live one. And the program's `placement_status` bubbles up from `DRAFT` to `SUBMISSION_READY`, with the program's `version` incremented. That bubble-up is the crucial ordering fact of the whole placement: it is the actuary's sign-off, not any broker action, that opens the marketing window.

A `decision` of `REJECTED` moves the package to `REJECTED` and records the `review_note`, and deliberately leaves the program's `placement_status` untouched. The ceding insurer then resupplies data on its own rejected package, moving it back to `DRAFT`: this revision accepts any subset of `exposure_records_count`, `data_quality_flags_count`, `historical_years_supplied`, `cat_model_version`, `loss_history_completeness_pct` and `actuarial_report_attached`, overwrites exactly the fields supplied, and recomputes `completeness_score` from the merged result. A revision attempted from any status other than `REJECTED` is `ERR_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`, and a revision attempted by a `CedentUnderwriter` whose `X-User-ID` is not the owning program's `cedent_id` is `ERR_FOREIGN_RESOURCE`. The loop from `DRAFT` through `ACTUARY_REVIEW` to `REJECTED` and back to `DRAFT` may run as many times as the data needs.

### 3.3. Program Structure and the Layer Technical Premium

The placing broker cuts the program into layers while the placement file is still `DRAFT` or `SUBMISSION_READY`; adding a layer from any later placement state is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. Each layer takes a `layer_label` of 1 to 80 characters — something like `Cat XL 10xs20` — and a `layer_type` of `QUOTA_SHARE`, `EXCESS_OF_LOSS` or `CATASTROPHE`. The structural inputs are an `attachment_minor` giving the retention beneath the layer as an integer of at least zero, a `limit_minor` giving the layer limit per occurrence as an integer greater than zero, a `base_rate_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 with up to four decimals, a `subject_premium_base_minor` greater than zero being the premium base the rate applies to, and an `expected_loss_cost_minor` of at least zero carrying the actuarial expected loss cost for the layer.

Six further inputs are optional and carry defaults. `cession_pct` is the quota-share cession percentage, between 0 and 100 inclusive with up to four decimals, defaulting to `0.0000`. `reinstatements_total` is the number of reinstatements purchased, between 0 and 5 inclusive, defaulting to 0. `reinstatement_premium_pct` expresses the reinstatement premium as a percentage of layer premium, pro rata as to amount, between 0 and 200 inclusive with up to four decimals, defaulting to `100.0000`. `sublimit_minor` is a per-event sublimit that must be either zero or no greater than `limit_minor`, and defaults to 0; a zero means there is no sublimit and the full `limit_minor` applies. `ceding_commission_pct` is the provisional ceding commission on quota-share business, between 0 and 60 inclusive with up to four decimals, defaulting to `0.0000`. And `excluded_perils` is the list of peril exclusions agreed on the slip, whose members may only be `TERROR`, `CYBER`, `FLOOD`, `NUCLEAR` and `WAR`, defaulting to an empty list.

**Structural coherence.** A layer must be internally consistent with its own type, and an incoherent structure is refused with `ERR_LAYER_STRUCTURE_INVALID`. A `QUOTA_SHARE` layer requires an `attachment_minor` of exactly zero, a `cession_pct` strictly greater than zero, and a `base_rate_pct` exactly equal to its `cession_pct`. An `EXCESS_OF_LOSS` or `CATASTROPHE` layer requires an `attachment_minor` strictly greater than zero, a `cession_pct` of exactly zero, and a `ceding_commission_pct` of exactly zero — excess layers carry no ceding commission. Separately, a `sublimit_minor` greater than `limit_minor` is refused with `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`. Both checks apply equally to creating a layer and to restructuring one.

A new layer opens with status `OPEN`, with `written_line_total_pct` and `signed_line_total_pct` at `0.0000`, with `limit_eroded_minor`, `reinstatements_used` and `incurred_ceded_minor` at 0, and with `loss_ratio_pct`, `provisional_ceding_commission_minor`, `final_ceding_commission_minor` and `ceding_commission_delta_minor` all null. The layer's own status runs `OPEN`, `IN_MARKET`, `PARTIALLY_PLACED`, `FULLY_PLACED`, `OVERSUBSCRIBED`, `SIGNED_DOWN`, `BOUND` and `LAPSED`, with `BOUND` and `LAPSED` terminal. Restructuring is permitted only while the layer is `OPEN` — from any other layer status the attempt is `ERR_LAYER_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the owning program is `DRAFT` or `SUBMISSION_READY`. A restructure accepts any subset of `layer_label`, `attachment_minor`, `limit_minor`, `base_rate_pct`, `subject_premium_base_minor`, `expected_loss_cost_minor`, `cession_pct`, `reinstatements_total`, `reinstatement_premium_pct`, `sublimit_minor`, `ceding_commission_pct` and `excluded_perils`, overwrites exactly what was supplied, recomputes the technical premium, and increments the layer's `version`.

**The technical premium.** Every layer carries a `quoted_gross_premium_minor`, the broker's own technical price for the layer. It is computed when the layer is created, recomputed on every restructure while the layer is still `OPEN`, and then **frozen for ever** the moment the layer goes to market. Nothing later in the lifecycle — no quote, no signing line, no binding — ever moves it again. It depends only on the layer's own input fields; no sibling layer, quote or signing line participates.

The computation runs in four steps, and the order matters more than the arithmetic. First, the rate is loaded for reinstatements: each purchased reinstatement adds five percentage points of loading, so the loading percentage is five multiplied by `reinstatements_total`, and the loaded rate is `base_rate_pct` multiplied by one plus that loading percentage divided by one hundred. This loaded rate is carried through unrounded — there is no intermediate rounding of the rate. Second, an undiscounted provisional premium is obtained by multiplying `subject_premium_base_minor` by the loaded rate and dividing by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units. That single rounding is the only rounding of the premium before the discount.

Third, a volume discount tier is selected **from the provisional premium**, that is, from the premium before any discount is applied. A provisional premium below 5,000,000 minor units earns no discount at all. From 5,000,000 up to but excluding 25,000,000 minor units it earns a discount of 2.5 per cent. From 25,000,000 up to but excluding 100,000,000 minor units it earns 5 per cent. At 100,000,000 minor units or above it earns 7.5 per cent. The tier is never re-derived from the discounted result: doing so would let a large layer fall back into a cheaper tier and produce a different, wrong premium. Fourth, `quoted_gross_premium_minor` is the provisional premium multiplied by one minus the tier discount percentage divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units.

### 3.4. Marketing the Layers: Submissions, Quotes and Negotiation Rounds

Once the actuary has opened the marketing window, the placing broker releases the program to market, advancing `placement_status` from `SUBMISSION_READY` to `IN_MARKET`. Three conditions must hold. The owning cedent's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; a suspended cedent blocks the release with `ERR_CEDENT_SUSPENDED`. At least one layer of the program must still be `OPEN`, and the program's `documentation_status` must still be `NOT_STARTED` — you cannot re-market a program whose slip has already been issued; either failure, like an attempt from any placement state other than `SUBMISSION_READY`, is `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. On success the program's `version` increments.

**Sending a layer out.** The broker offers a layer to a named market by supplying the `reinsurer_id` of the market and a `response_deadline`, the instant by which that market must respond. The program must be `IN_MARKET` and the layer must be either `OPEN` or `IN_MARKET`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_LAYER_STATE_INVALID` respectively. The target market's `security_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED`. The `response_deadline` must be strictly after the current logical-clock instant, else `ERR_DEADLINE_NOT_IN_FUTURE`. And there must be no other market submission for the same layer and the same market still sitting in `SENT` or `QUOTED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_MARKET_SUBMISSION` — a market that has already declined or whose submission has expired may be approached again.

A market submission is created in status `SENT`, with `layer_id` taken from the layer it was created under, `sent_at` stamped with the current logical-clock instant and `submitted_by_user_id` recording the releasing broker's `X-User-ID`. Its status runs `SENT`, `QUOTED`, `DECLINED`, `CLOSED` and `EXPIRED`, of which `DECLINED`, `CLOSED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. The **first** submission on a layer also carries the layer from `OPEN` to `IN_MARKET`, which is the moment `quoted_gross_premium_minor` is frozen, and increments the layer's `version`. Subsequent submissions on the same layer leave the layer status where it is.

**Quoting.** A `ReinsurerUnderwriter` answers a submission addressed to it with terms: an `offered_line_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 with up to four decimals, being the line the market offers to write on the layer; a `quoted_rate_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 with up to four decimals, being the rate on line the market quotes; a `ceding_commission_pct` between 0 and 60 inclusive with up to four decimals; a `reinstatements_offered` count between 0 and 5 inclusive; a `sublimit_minor` that is an integer of at least zero, where zero means the market insists on no sublimit; and an `expires_at` instant at which the quote lapses if it is not firm-ordered. Two inputs are optional: `is_lead`, defaulting to false, and `additional_exclusions`, defaulting to an empty list and restricted to the members `TERROR`, `CYBER`, `FLOOD`, `NUCLEAR` and `WAR`.

Quoting is gated in several ways. The submission must still be in status `SENT`, else `ERR_MARKET_SUBMISSION_CLOSED`. The acting market's `X-User-ID` must equal the submission's `reinsurer_id`, else `ERR_FOREIGN_RESOURCE`. The current logical clock must be **no later than** the submission's `response_deadline` — a clock landing exactly on the deadline still admits the quote — and a clock past it is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The supplied `expires_at` must be strictly after the current logical clock, else `ERR_DEADLINE_NOT_IN_FUTURE`. And setting `is_lead` to true requires the quoting market to have `lead_authorised` true **and** `security_status` exactly `APPROVED`; a `RESTRICTED` market may follow but never lead, and either failure is `ERR_LEAD_NOT_AUTHORISED`.

A successful quote is created in status `QUOTED` with `negotiation_round` at 0, `last_counter_note` null and `accepted_line_pct` null. Its `market_submission_id` records the submission it answers, and its `layer_id` and `reinsurer_id` are copied from that submission at creation. The submission itself advances from `SENT` to `QUOTED`. A quote's status runs `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED`, `REQUOTED`, `FIRM_ORDER`, `DECLINED`, `LAPSED` and `BOUND`, of which `DECLINED`, `LAPSED` and `BOUND` are terminal.

**The negotiation cycle.** Terms on reinstatements, sublimits, exclusions and commission are settled by a bounded back-and-forth. The placing broker counters a quote that is in `QUOTED` or `REQUOTED`, supplying a `counter_note`; the quote's `negotiation_round` increases by one, the note is stored as `last_counter_note`, and the status becomes `COUNTERED`. Two limits apply. The quote's `negotiation_round` must be **less than three** before a further counter is accepted, so at most three counters are ever possible on one quote and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. And the current logical clock must be strictly before the quote's `expires_at`; a clock landing exactly on `expires_at` means the quote has already lapsed, and the counter is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Countering from any status other than `QUOTED` or `REQUOTED` is `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`.

The market answers a counter by requoting, which is permitted only from `COUNTERED` and only by the market that owns the quote. A requote overwrites `offered_line_pct`, `quoted_rate_pct`, `ceding_commission_pct`, `reinstatements_offered`, `sublimit_minor`, `additional_exclusions` and `expires_at` with the values supplied, leaves `negotiation_round` **unchanged** — only the broker's counter advances the round counter — and moves the status to `REQUOTED`. The same freshness rule applies to the new `expires_at`, which must be strictly after the current logical clock, and the same lead-authorisation rule applies to `is_lead`. The cycle from `COUNTERED` to `REQUOTED` and back to `COUNTERED` may therefore run until the round cap bites.

A market may also walk away. Declining is permitted from `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED` or `REQUOTED`, by the owning market only, with an optional `decline_reason`; the quote's status becomes `DECLINED` and the parent market submission's status becomes `DECLINED` as well, whether it was sitting in `SENT` or in `QUOTED`.

**Ageing the market.** The placing broker may sweep a program that is `IN_MARKET` to bring the paper into line with the clock; sweeping from any other placement state is `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. The sweep expires every market submission on the program's layers that is still in `SENT` and whose `response_deadline` is at or before the current logical-clock instant, moving it to `EXPIRED`, and it lapses every quote on those layers in `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED` or `REQUOTED` whose `expires_at` is at or before that instant, moving it to `LAPSED`. Note that the boundary here is inclusive in the opposite direction from the quoting gate: a deadline exactly equal to the current instant is swept. The sweep deliberately changes **no** layer status and **no** program status — an unplaced layer stays `IN_MARKET` and the broker must decide what to do about it.

### 3.5. Firm Orders, Signing Lines and Aggregate Market Capacity

Firm-ordering is the point at which a quote stops being an indication and becomes a reservation of the market's capital. The placing broker firm-orders against a quote by supplying an `accepted_line_pct` — the line it actually wants from that market — together with the `expected_layer_version` described in §2.7. The operation creates a new resource and answers 201.

The gates on a firm order are numerous, and because the first violated gate decides the response, their **relative order is part of the contract**. The quote's own status is checked first and must be `QUOTED` or `REQUOTED`, else `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`. Then the owning program's `placement_status` must be `IN_MARKET`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. Then `expected_layer_version` must equal the layer's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_LAYER_VERSION` — this sits above every remaining check, so a client working from a stale read is told about the staleness rather than about a downstream consequence of it. Then the quoting market's `security_status` must be either `APPROVED` or `RESTRICTED`, since a `SUSPENDED` market cannot be firm-ordered, else `ERR_MARKET_SUSPENDED`. Then the current logical clock must be strictly before the quote's `expires_at`, else `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Then `accepted_line_pct` must be no greater than the quote's `offered_line_pct` — the broker may firm-order less than a market offered but never more — else `ERR_LINE_EXCEEDS_OFFER`. Then `accepted_line_pct` must be no greater than the market's `max_line_pct`, where exact equality passes, else `ERR_LINE_EXCEEDS_MAX_LINE`. And last of all, the market's capacity gate is tested, else `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Aggregate capacity.** A market's committed capacity is the sum, over its **live** signing lines, of the capital each line ties up. A line is live when its status is `PROVISIONAL`, `SIGNED` or `BOUND`; `RELEASED` lines are excluded entirely, because releasing a line always hands capacity straight back. Each live line's contribution is its layer's `limit_minor` multiplied by the line's `effective_line_pct` and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units, and those per-line amounts are then summed exactly. Because `effective_line_pct` is the `signed_line_pct` when one has been set and the `written_line_pct` otherwise, a `PROVISIONAL` line reserves its full written line right up until signing down shrinks it. A market's `committed_capacity_minor` is that sum and its `available_capacity_minor` is its `capacity_limit_minor` less that sum; both are refreshed whenever any of its lines is created, signed down or released.

That stored pair is the **unwindowed** figure — every live line of the market counts, whatever treaty period its program covers — and it is what a read of the market register reports. The firm-order gate, however, uses a **windowed** figure, and the difference between the two is deliberate. When deciding whether a firm order breaches the market's capacity, only lines whose own program is contemporaneous with the target program count. The aggregation window around the target program runs from thirty days before its `inception_date` to thirty days after its `expiry_date`, and it is **closed at both ends**: a live line counts if and only if its own program's `inception_date` lies inside that window inclusive of both endpoints, so a program incepting exactly thirty days before the target program incepts is inside the window and does consume capacity. The gate itself is then that the windowed committed total, plus this line's capacity — the target layer's `limit_minor` multiplied by `accepted_line_pct`, divided by one hundred and rounded half up to whole minor units — must be **no greater than** the market's `capacity_limit_minor`. Exact equality passes; one minor unit more fails with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**The four-hop firm-order cascade.** A successful firm order records `accepted_line_pct` on the quote and moves the quote to `FIRM_ORDER`. The first hop creates a signing line whose `layer_id`, `quote_id` and `reinsurer_id` identify the layer, the quote and the market, whose `written_line_pct` is copied from the `accepted_line_pct` just accepted and is **never mutated again**, whose `signed_line_pct` is null, and whose status is `PROVISIONAL`. The second hop recomputes the layer's `written_line_total_pct` — the sum of `written_line_pct` over the layer's lines in `PROVISIONAL`, `SIGNED` or `BOUND` — and lets the layer's compound status follow from the recomputed total: a total greater than `0.0000` but less than `100.0000` leaves the layer `PARTIALLY_PLACED`, a total of exactly `100.0000` makes it `FULLY_PLACED`, and a total greater than `100.0000` makes it `OVERSUBSCRIBED`. A layer sitting at `IN_MARKET` can therefore land in `PARTIALLY_PLACED`, or jump straight to `FULLY_PLACED` when a single line fills it exactly, and a `PARTIALLY_PLACED` layer moves to `FULLY_PLACED` or `OVERSUBSCRIBED`. Every one of these layer transitions increments the layer's `version`.

The third hop looks up at the program: when every layer of the program whose status is not `LAPSED` is either `FULLY_PLACED` or `OVERSUBSCRIBED`, the program's `placement_status` advances from `IN_MARKET` to `FIRM_ORDER` and its `version` increments. The fourth hop is caused by that program transition rather than by the original firm order, and it clears the market down: every quote on the program's layers still in `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED` or `REQUOTED` becomes `LAPSED`, and every market submission on those layers still in `SENT` or `QUOTED` becomes `CLOSED`. Alongside all of this, the market's `committed_capacity_minor` and `available_capacity_minor` are recomputed.

**Handing a line back.** The placing broker may release a reserved line before binding, and the release runs the whole cascade in reverse. Release requires the line's status to be `PROVISIONAL` or `SIGNED` — a `BOUND` or already-`RELEASED` line cannot be released, and the attempt is `ERR_SIGNING_LINE_STATE_INVALID` — requires the layer not to be `BOUND`, and requires the owning program to be in `FIRM_ORDER`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. The line's status becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `WITHDRAWN_BY_BROKER`. The first hop lapses the quote that materialised the line, taking it from `FIRM_ORDER` to `LAPSED`. The second hop recomputes the layer's `written_line_total_pct` and `signed_line_total_pct` and reverts the layer's status from the recomputed totals: a `FULLY_PLACED` layer whose written total has fallen below `100.0000` becomes `PARTIALLY_PLACED`; an `OVERSUBSCRIBED` layer becomes `PARTIALLY_PLACED` when its written total falls below `100.0000` and `FULLY_PLACED` when it lands on exactly `100.0000`; and a `SIGNED_DOWN` layer whose signed total has fallen below `100.0000` becomes `PARTIALLY_PLACED`, with the remaining `SIGNED` lines keeping the `signed_line_pct` they were already allocated — the sign-down is emphatically **not** recomputed. The third hop looks up at the program: if, after the releasing hop, at least one layer of the program is now `IN_MARKET` or `PARTIALLY_PLACED`, the program reverts from `FIRM_ORDER` to `IN_MARKET` and its `version` increments. Quotes that had already lapsed are not revived by that revert. Finally the market's `committed_capacity_minor` and `available_capacity_minor` are recomputed and the layer's `version` increments.

**The security cascade.** When a `ComplianceOfficer` moves a market's `security_status` to `SUSPENDED`, the same reverse chain runs across every program that market participates in. Every signing line of the suspended market that is `PROVISIONAL` or `SIGNED` and sits on a layer that is not `BOUND` becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `SECURITY_FAILURE`; that is hop one. Each affected layer then recomputes its `written_line_total_pct` and `signed_line_total_pct` and reverts `FULLY_PLACED` or `OVERSUBSCRIBED` to `PARTIALLY_PLACED`; that is hop two. Each affected program sitting in `FIRM_ORDER` reverts to `IN_MARKET`; that is hop three. Every quote of that market still in `FIRM_ORDER` becomes `LAPSED`, and the market's `committed_capacity_minor` and `available_capacity_minor` are recomputed. Lines on layers that are already `BOUND` are untouched — suspension is prospective, and a bound contract is a contract.

### 3.6. Signing Down, Compensation Disclosure and Binding the Placement

**Signing down.** Markets habitually offer more than the broker needs, so a layer that has attracted more than one hundred per cent of written lines must be resolved down to exactly one hundred before it can be bound. The placing broker signs a layer down; the layer must be `OVERSUBSCRIBED`, meaning its `written_line_total_pct` is **strictly** greater than `100.0000` — a layer sitting on exactly `100.0000` is `FULLY_PLACED` and signing it down is `ERR_LAYER_NOT_OVERSUBSCRIBED` — and the owning program must be in `FIRM_ORDER`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. The allocation considers only the layer's `PROVISIONAL` signing lines, taken in `created_at` ascending order; `RELEASED` lines are excluded, and `SIGNED` or `BOUND` lines cannot exist on an oversubscribed layer at all.

The allocation is a two-stage waterfall, and the stages must not be collapsed. In the first stage each line's raw share is its `written_line_pct` multiplied by one hundred and divided by the written total, computed as an exact rational value, and that raw share is then **floored at four decimal places** — truncated toward zero, never rounded up. Flooring every share necessarily undershoots, so the second stage distributes the residue: the residue is `100.0000` less the sum of the floored shares, and it is handed out in whole steps of `0.0001` percentage points, one step at a time. The order in which lines receive those steps is fully deterministic and uses four keys in strict sequence — the dropped fraction (the raw share less its floored value) descending, then `written_line_pct` descending, then `created_at` ascending, then the market's `name` ascending — and if there are more steps than lines the sequence simply cycles back to the head of the order. The invariant is absolute: the signed total closes on exactly `100.0000`, never `99.9999` and never `100.0001`.

Which lines are scaled depends on the program's `signdown_method`, and the two variants disagree on every follower line. Under `PRO_RATA`, the default, every line is scaled by the same factor of one hundred over the written total; the lead's status buys it nothing at all. Under `LEAD_PROTECTED`, the line whose quote carried `is_lead` true keeps its `written_line_pct` completely untouched, and the followers share only what is left over — each follower's raw share is its `written_line_pct` multiplied by the difference between `100.0000` and the lead's written line, divided by the sum of the followers' written lines — with the floor-and-residue pass running over the followers alone and the residue closing against that reduced target rather than against `100.0000`. Because `LEAD_PROTECTED` depends on there being a lead, that method additionally requires exactly one `PROVISIONAL` line on the layer whose quote is a lead line, else `ERR_LEAD_LINE_MISSING`.

Once the shares are settled, each line's `signed_line_pct` and `effective_line_pct` take its allocated share and its `committed_capacity_minor` becomes the layer's `limit_minor` multiplied by that share and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units. A line whose allocation resolves to `0.0000` is squeezed out of the placement entirely: it becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `SIGNDOWN_ZEROED` and the quote behind it moves from `FIRM_ORDER` to `LAPSED`. Every other `PROVISIONAL` line becomes `SIGNED`. The layer's `signed_line_total_pct` is set to `100.0000`, its status becomes `SIGNED_DOWN`, its `version` increments, and `committed_capacity_minor` and `available_capacity_minor` are recomputed for every market that holds a line on the layer.

**Disclosing the intermediary's compensation.** A broker sitting between a cedent and its markets is paid by both sides, and the cedent is entitled to know how much before it is bound to anything. A `BrokerAccountant` drafts a compensation disclosure against a program, supplying the `brokerage_pct` being disclosed together with an optional `contingent_commission_pct`, which defaults to `0.0000` and must lie between zero and ten inclusive at four decimal places, and an optional `market_service_fee_minor`, a non-negative integer count of minor units that defaults to 0. The disclosure records its `program_id`, starts in `DRAFT`, and leaves `disclosed_by_user_id`, `acknowledged_by_user_id` and `decision_note` null.

Releasing the draft to the cedent requires the disclosure to be in `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATE_INVALID` — requires the program's `placement_status` to be `IN_MARKET` or `FIRM_ORDER`, and requires the disclosed `brokerage_pct` to equal the program's own `brokerage_pct` at the moment of release, else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_PCT_MISMATCH`: the document the cedent sees must be the deal the cedent is actually getting. Release stamps `disclosed_by_user_id` with the acting accountant's identity, moves the status to `DISCLOSED` and increments the disclosure's `version`.

The cedent then decides. The deciding `CedentUnderwriter` must be the program's own cedent, since a `decision` on another cedent's disclosure is `ERR_FOREIGN_RESOURCE`, and the disclosure must be `DISCLOSED`. The `decision` is either `ACKNOWLEDGE` or `REJECT`, and an optional `decision_note` is recorded either way along with `acknowledged_by_user_id`. `ACKNOWLEDGE` moves the disclosure to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, which is terminal, and sets the program's `disclosure_acknowledged` flag to true — the flag that unblocks binding. `REJECT` moves it to `REJECTED` and leaves `disclosure_acknowledged` false, because no disclosure of the program is acknowledged. `REJECTED` is deliberately **not** terminal: the accountant can correct the terms and release the same disclosure again, which takes it from `REJECTED` back to `DISCLOSED`, clearing `acknowledged_by_user_id` and `decision_note` back to null and re-checking the `brokerage_pct` match and the program state. Both decision outcomes increment the disclosure's `version` and the program's `version`.

**Binding.** The placing broker binds the program. Binding requires the program's `placement_status` to be `FIRM_ORDER`, requires `disclosure_acknowledged` to be true — else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` — and requires every layer of the program whose status is not `LAPSED` to be either `FULLY_PLACED` or `SIGNED_DOWN`, else `ERR_LAYER_STATE_INVALID`. A layer left `IN_MARKET`, `PARTIALLY_PLACED` or `OVERSUBSCRIBED` blocks the whole placement.

Binding then converts the whole file. Every layer that is `FULLY_PLACED` or `SIGNED_DOWN` becomes `BOUND` and increments its `version`. On those layers, each `PROVISIONAL` line — a line on a layer that was never signed down, therefore written at exactly the line it was firm-ordered at — has its `signed_line_pct` set equal to its `written_line_pct` and then becomes `BOUND`; each already-`SIGNED` line simply becomes `BOUND`. Every quote on those layers still in `FIRM_ORDER` becomes `BOUND`. The program's `placement_status` becomes `BOUND` and its `version` increments. Binding answers 200 with the program.

**Brokerage and net remittance.** The money on the program is computed at binding, and the order of rounding and summation is pinned because it is observable in the result. The contributing set is only those signing lines that are `BOUND` and sit on a layer that is `BOUND` and belongs to this program, taken in `created_at` ascending order; `PROVISIONAL`, `SIGNED` and `RELEASED` lines and `LAPSED` layers contribute nothing. For each such line, the line's gross premium is its layer's `quoted_gross_premium_minor` multiplied by the line's `signed_line_pct` and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units; the line's brokerage is that line gross multiplied by the program's `brokerage_pct` and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units; and the line's net is the line gross less the line brokerage.

Those per-line figures are then summed exactly. The program's `gross_written_premium_minor` is the sum of `quoted_gross_premium_minor` over the program's `BOUND` layers. Its `brokerage_earned_minor` is the sum of the per-line brokerage amounts — it is **not** the program's gross written premium multiplied by `brokerage_pct` and rounded once, and the two differ by a minor unit or two in ordinary cases, because rounding four lines individually is not the same as rounding their total. Its `net_remittance_minor` is the sum of the per-line net amounts. Its `rounding_residue_minor` is the gross written premium less the sum of `brokerage_earned_minor` and `net_remittance_minor`, and it exists precisely so that the penny the per-line rounding creates or destroys is visible rather than hidden.

**Provisional ceding commission.** Binding also fixes a provisional ceding commission on every `BOUND` layer whose `layer_type` is `QUOTA_SHARE`; excess-of-loss layers have no ceding commission at binding. The provisional figure is the flat variant of the commission settlement: the layer's `quoted_gross_premium_minor` multiplied by its `ceding_commission_pct` and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units, stored as `provisional_ceding_commission_minor`. No loss experience exists yet at binding, so the slide, the pivot loss ratio and both commission clamps described in §3.9 are ignored entirely at this stage.

### 3.7. Contract Certainty and the Slip-to-Contract Trail

Reinsurance is notorious for money moving long before anybody writes down what was agreed. The documentation dimension of a program's state exists to make that gap measurable and, when it grows too wide, to stop the money.

The placing broker files documents against a program, and each filing is append-only: a filed document is never mutated or deleted. A filing supplies a `document_type` and a `wording_reference` of between one and sixty characters — the market wording the document is built on. The system records the document's `program_id`, stamps `issued_at` with the current logical clock, records `filed_by_user_id` as the filing broker's identity, and computes `days_from_inception` as the whole number of days between the program's `inception_date` and `issued_at`: the elapsed seconds divided by eighty-six thousand four hundred, floored, which is negative when the document was filed before inception. A contract document carries only `id` and `created_at` from the common properties; it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because there is nothing about it that can ever change. Filing answers 201 with the document.

The four document types form a chain, and the program's `documentation_status` follows it. A `SLIP` takes the program from `NOT_STARTED` to `SLIP_ISSUED` and requires the program's `placement_status` to be `FIRM_ORDER` or `BOUND` — the slip is the record of a placement that actually exists. A `WORDING_DRAFT` takes `SLIP_ISSUED` to `WORDING_DRAFTED`. An `AGREED_WORDING` takes `WORDING_DRAFTED` to `WORDING_AGREED`. An `EXECUTED_CONTRACT` takes `WORDING_AGREED` to `EXECUTED`, which is terminal, and additionally requires the program's `placement_status` to be `BOUND`: no program is executed on paper before it is bound in the market. Filing a type that does not follow from the program's current documentation state — a wording draft with no slip, an executed contract with no agreed wording, a second slip — is `ERR_DOCUMENT_SEQUENCE_INVALID`.

Filing an `AGREED_WORDING` does more than advance the chain: it releases money that contract uncertainty had frozen. Every fiduciary movement of the program sitting in `HELD` returns to `PENDING`, with its `hold_reason` cleared back to null and its `approved_by_user_id` cleared back to null as well — a released movement must be re-approved from scratch, because the approval it once had was given against a contract that did not yet exist. Each filing increments the program's `version`.

**The compliance checkpoint.** Every program is created with a `contract_certainty_deadline` thirty days after its `inception_date`, and a `ComplianceOfficer` tests it. The checkpoint requires the current logical clock to be **strictly after** the deadline — landing exactly on the deadline instant is not yet a breach — and requires the `documentation_status` to be `NOT_STARTED`, `SLIP_ISSUED` or `WORDING_DRAFTED`, since a program that already has agreed wording, or is already in breach, has nothing to test. A checkpoint that fails either condition is `ERR_CERTAINTY_NOT_BREACHED`.

A breach moves `documentation_status` to `CERTAINTY_BREACHED` from whichever of `NOT_STARTED`, `SLIP_ISSUED` or `WORDING_DRAFTED` it was in, increments the program's `version`, and freezes the outbound money: every fiduciary movement of the program whose `txn_type` is `REINSURER_REMITTANCE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PREMIUM` and whose status is `PENDING` or `APPROVED` becomes `HELD` with its `hold_reason` set to the string `CONTRACT_CERTAINTY_BREACHED`. A movement held out of `APPROVED` **keeps** its `approved_by_user_id` while held; only the release described above clears it. Inbound receipts and collections are not touched — the broker may keep taking the cedent's premium in, it simply may not pay it out.

Late certainty is remediable. `CERTAINTY_BREACHED` is not a terminal state: filing an `AGREED_WORDING` is accepted from `CERTAINTY_BREACHED` exactly as it is from `WORDING_DRAFTED`, moving the program straight to `WORDING_AGREED` and running the same release of every `HELD` movement. Nothing on the program itself remembers that the breach happened — the `documentation_status` field carries no scar — and the only durable evidence is the `days_from_inception` value on the documents in the trail, which is precisely the point of computing it.

### 3.8. Fiduciary Premium Handling

Premium that passes through a broker is not the broker's money. It arrives from the cedent, sits in a fiduciary account, and leaves for the markets, and every movement on that account is booked, approved by a second pair of eyes, and only then settled. The account belongs to the program, and its balance is `fiduciary_holding_minor`.

**Booking.** A `BrokerAccountant` books a movement against a program, supplying a `txn_type`, an `amount_minor` that must be a positive integer count of minor units, and the `expected_program_version` described in §2.7. The amount is always an absolute magnitude; direction is implied entirely by the type. Three types are inflows — `CEDENT_RECEIPT`, the premium the cedent pays in; `CLAIM_COLLECTION`, money recovered from a market; and `REMITTANCE_REVERSAL`, the compensating row described below. Four are outflows — `REINSURER_REMITTANCE`, premium paid out to a market; `BROKERAGE_DEDUCTION`, the broker taking its own commission out of the account; `REINSTATEMENT_PREMIUM`, premium owed for reinstating an eroded limit; and `CEDENT_PAYOUT`, money returned to the cedent.

An optional `signing_line_id` attributes the movement to a particular market's line, and whether it may be supplied is dictated by the type: it is **required** for `REINSURER_REMITTANCE`, `REINSTATEMENT_PREMIUM` and `CLAIM_COLLECTION`, since those three are always owed to or from one identifiable market, and **forbidden** for every other type. Supplying it when it is forbidden, or omitting it when it is required, is `ERR_TRANSACTION_TYPE_LINK_INVALID`; so is booking a `REMITTANCE_REVERSAL` through this endpoint at all, because reversal rows are only ever created by the system. When a link is supplied, the referenced line must sit on a layer belonging to this program, else `ERR_LINE_LAYER_MISMATCH`. A booked movement records `created_by_user_id` as the booking accountant's identity, starts in `PENDING`, leaves `approved_by_user_id`, `value_date`, `hold_reason` and `reverses_transaction_id` null, and increments the program's `version`. No money moves — `fiduciary_holding_minor` is untouched until the movement settles.

**Approval.** A `PENDING` movement is approved by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `BrokerAccountant`; anything other than `PENDING` is `ERR_TRANSACTION_STATE_INVALID`. The accounting role is admitted here only so that one accountant can approve another's work: the approver's identity must differ from `created_by_user_id`, and an attempt to approve one's own booking is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` exactly as described in §2.10. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id` and moves the status to `APPROVED`. Still no money moves.

**Settlement.** A `BrokerAccountant` settles an `APPROVED` movement, and settlement is where the balance actually changes. For the two premium out-payment types, `REINSURER_REMITTANCE` and `REINSTATEMENT_PREMIUM`, the program's `documentation_status` must be `WORDING_AGREED` or `EXECUTED`, else `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_NOT_AGREED` — this is the contract-certainty gate of §3.7 expressed as a payment control. For every outflow type, `amount_minor` must be no greater than the program's current `fiduciary_holding_minor`, since a fiduciary account may never be overdrawn; equality is allowed, and one minor unit more is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FIDUCIARY_BALANCE`. Settlement stamps `value_date` with the current logical clock, moves the status to `SETTLED`, and recomputes the balance and the two cedent aggregates.

**Reversal.** Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may reverse a movement, and only a `SETTLED` one whose `txn_type` is one of the four outflow types — inflows are never reversed through this route — else `ERR_TRANSACTION_STATE_INVALID`. A movement may be reversed once and once only: if any `REMITTANCE_REVERSAL` row already references it, the attempt is `ERR_ALREADY_REVERSED`. Reversal moves the original row from `SETTLED` to `REVERSED`, which is terminal, and appends a **new** movement of type `REMITTANCE_REVERSAL` for the same `amount_minor`, with the original's `signing_line_id` copied across, with `reverses_transaction_id` pointing at the reversed row, already in status `SETTLED` and with `value_date` stamped from the current clock. That newly created compensating row — not the reversed original — is the response body, and the response code is 201 because a resource was created.

**The balance projection.** The ledger is append-only, so `fiduciary_holding_minor` is a projection rather than a running total that is nudged up and down. It considers the program's movements whose status is `SETTLED` **or** `REVERSED`, and is the sum of `amount_minor` over the inflow types in that set less the sum of `amount_minor` over the outflow types in that set. A reversed movement therefore stays in the projection for ever as the outflow it always was, and its compensating inflow cancels it exactly, so the balance returns to precisely the value it held before the reversed movement settled — a delta of the reversed amount and not one unit more. Movements in `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `HELD` are invisible to the balance: booking, approving and holding a movement never move money. Every figure here is an exact integer count of minor units and no rounding occurs anywhere in this projection.

Two aggregates on the cedent are maintained from the same ledger, across **all** of that cedent's programs rather than just the one being settled. `settled_receipts_minor` is the sum of `amount_minor` over the cedent's movements whose `txn_type` is `CEDENT_RECEIPT` and whose status is `SETTLED` — money genuinely received. `outstanding_reinstatement_premium_minor` is the sum of `amount_minor` over the cedent's movements whose `txn_type` is `REINSTATEMENT_PREMIUM` and whose status is `PENDING` or `APPROVED` — reinstatement premium owed but not yet paid, which drops out of the figure the moment it settles.

### 3.9. Claims Advocacy, Dispute Resolution and Placement-Year Closure

When a loss happens, the broker advocates for its client against the markets it placed the risk with. An advocacy file records what the cedent lost, how much of that loss the treaty actually picks up, how much of the cession the markets are arguing about, and how much has been collected.

**Notification.** Either the placing broker or the cedent's own underwriter notifies a loss against a layer, supplying a `claim_reference` of between one and sixty characters, an `event_date` and a positive integer `gross_claim_minor` — the cedent's ground-up gross loss. The `claim_reference` must be unique among the advocacies of the same layer, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_REFERENCE`; the same reference may legitimately appear on a different layer of the same program, because each layer's file is its own. The layer must be `BOUND`, else `ERR_LAYER_NOT_BOUND` — an unbound layer has no contract to claim under. The `event_date` must fall inside the half-open treaty period: an event exactly at the program's `inception_date` is covered, an event exactly at `expiry_date` is not, and anything outside is `ERR_EVENT_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`. And the layer must have aggregate cover left, since a layer whose limit and reinstatements are fully eroded has nothing left to cede and the notification is `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`. A new file starts in `NOTIFIED`, with `disputed_minor`, `collected_minor` and `arbitration_cost_minor` at 0, `advocacy_route` null and `reinstatement_triggered` false, and answers 201.

**The cession waterfall.** How much of the gross loss reaches the layer is decided by a waterfall of caps, and the ceded amount is the smallest of them. The first bucket asks what the treaty is exposed to at all. On a `QUOTA_SHARE` layer the subject amount is the `gross_claim_minor` multiplied by the layer's `cession_pct` and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole minor units — the layer takes its agreed share of every loss. On any excess layer the subject amount is the `gross_claim_minor` less the layer's `attachment_minor`, floored at zero, so a loss that never pierces the attachment point cedes nothing. The second bucket is the per-event cap: the layer's `sublimit_minor` when a sublimit was agreed, meaning it is greater than zero, and otherwise the layer's `limit_minor`. The third bucket is the aggregate cap: the layer's `limit_minor` multiplied by one plus its `reinstatements_total`, less its `limit_eroded_minor`, floored at zero — one limit plus every reinstatement purchased, minus everything already burned. The `ceded_claim_minor` is then the minimum of the subject amount, the per-event cap, the layer's `limit_minor` and the aggregate available amount. The bare `limit_minor` appears as a cap in its own right so that a single event can never cede more than one full limit however many reinstatements remain unused.

**Erosion and reinstatement.** Notification never erodes the layer. `limit_eroded_minor` and `reinstatements_used` move only when an advocacy actually reaches `COLLECTED`, which is why a `NOTIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN` file leaves the layer exactly as it found it. When erosion is charged, the new eroded total is the old `limit_eroded_minor` plus this file's `ceded_claim_minor`, and `reinstatements_used` becomes the new eroded total divided by `limit_minor`, rounded **up** to the next whole number, less one, floored at zero: burning through part of the first limit uses no reinstatement, and crossing into the second limit uses the first. A reinstatement was triggered when that recomputed count is greater than the count before. When one is triggered, the amount reinstated is the new eroded total less the layer's `limit_minor` multiplied by one plus the previous count, and the reinstatement premium owed is the layer's `quoted_gross_premium_minor` multiplied by its `reinstatement_premium_pct`, divided by one hundred, multiplied by the reinstated amount and divided by the layer's `limit_minor`, rounded half up to whole minor units — pro rata to the fraction of the limit put back.

**The dispute overlay.** Markets do not always agree that a loss is covered, and `disputed_minor` is the running total of what remains contested. Each dispute contributes according to its own status: a dispute still `RAISED` contributes its full `disputed_minor`; a dispute `UPHELD`, meaning the market's objection succeeded, also contributes its full `disputed_minor`; a dispute `SETTLED_PARTIAL` contributes its `disputed_minor` less the `settled_minor` the market conceded; and a `WITHDRAWN` dispute contributes nothing at all, because the market conceded in full. The file's `collectible_minor` is then its `ceded_claim_minor` less `disputed_minor`, floored at zero. Separately, `arbitration_cost_minor` is the `ceded_claim_minor` multiplied by one and a half per cent, rounded half up to whole minor units, when the `advocacy_route` is `ARBITRATION`, and 0 otherwise.

**The advocacy lifecycle.** A `NOTIFIED` file goes one of two ways. The placing broker takes it under review, which requires the file to be `NOTIFIED` and the layer to still be `BOUND` and moves it to `UNDER_REVIEW`; or the cedent withdraws it, which requires the file to be `NOTIFIED` and the withdrawing `CedentUnderwriter` to be the program's own cedent, and moves it to `WITHDRAWN`, a terminal state that leaves the layer's erosion and reinstatements untouched because notification never touched them in the first place. Any other file state on either operation is `ERR_ADVOCACY_STATE_INVALID`.

A market raises a dispute against a file, supplying the `signing_line_id` of its own line — which must belong to the advocacy's layer, else `ERR_LINE_LAYER_MISMATCH` — a positive integer `disputed_minor` and a `dispute_reason` drawn from `EXCLUSION_APPLIES`, `LATE_NOTIFICATION`, `AGGREGATION_DISAGREEMENT` and `WORDING_AMBIGUITY`. The sum of `disputed_minor` across the file's disputes that are `RAISED`, `UPHELD` or `SETTLED_PARTIAL` may not exceed the file's `ceded_claim_minor` — the markets cannot collectively dispute more than was ever ceded to them — else `ERR_DISPUTE_EXCEEDS_CESSION`. A dispute starts in `RAISED` with `settled_minor` 0 and `resolution_note` null, and answers 201. Creating the **first** dispute on a file cascades the file from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DISPUTED` and recomputes `disputed_minor` and `collectible_minor`; a second and subsequent dispute recomputes those figures without moving the file, which is already `DISPUTED`.

**The fork.** From `DISPUTED` the broker chooses a `route`, and the two branches diverge in their side effects, not merely in their labels. `NEGOTIATION` sets `advocacy_route` to `NEGOTIATION`, leaves `arbitration_cost_minor` at 0, moves the file to `ADVOCACY_NEGOTIATION`, and leaves collections open throughout. `ARBITRATION` sets `advocacy_route` to `ARBITRATION`, accrues `arbitration_cost_minor` as described above, moves the file to `ADVOCACY_ARBITRATION`, and **freezes collections**: no collection may be booked against a file sitting in `ADVOCACY_ARBITRATION`, and the attempt is `ERR_COLLECTION_BLOCKED_BY_ARBITRATION` until the branch joins.

**Resolution and the join.** The broker resolves each dispute individually, supplying a `resolution`, a `settled_minor` and an optional `resolution_note`. The dispute must be `RAISED`, else `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`, and the amount supplied must match the resolution chosen: `UPHELD` requires `settled_minor` to be exactly 0, since the market conceded nothing; `WITHDRAWN` requires it to equal the dispute's `disputed_minor` exactly, since the market conceded everything; and `SETTLED_PARTIAL` requires it to lie strictly between 0 and `disputed_minor`, since a partial settlement that concedes nothing or everything is one of the other two outcomes. A mismatch is `ERR_RESOLUTION_AMOUNT_INVALID`. Each of the three outcomes is terminal for the dispute, records `settled_minor` and the note, and recomputes the file's `disputed_minor` and `collectible_minor`.

The branches join at a single point. When, after a resolution, **no** dispute of the file remains in `RAISED`, the file cascades from whichever branch it is on — `ADVOCACY_NEGOTIATION` or `ADVOCACY_ARBITRATION`, identically for both — to `RESOLVED`, recomputing `disputed_minor` and `collectible_minor` and refreshing the layer's `incurred_ceded_minor`. While even one dispute is still `RAISED` the file stays on its branch.

**Collections.** A `BrokerAccountant` books a collection received from a market against the file, supplying the market's `signing_line_id` and a positive `amount_minor`. The line must belong to the advocacy's layer, else `ERR_LINE_LAYER_MISMATCH`. The file must not be in `ADVOCACY_ARBITRATION`, as described above. And the file's `collected_minor` plus this amount must be no greater than its `collectible_minor`, else `ERR_COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_COLLECTIBLE`; exact equality is what closes the file. The collection is materialised as a `CLAIM_COLLECTION` fiduciary movement on the advocacy's program in status `PENDING`, linked to the same signing line, and that movement is the 201 response body. The file's `collected_minor` is then recomputed as the sum of `amount_minor` over the `CLAIM_COLLECTION` movements linked to the file's signing lines whose status is `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `SETTLED` — note that a collection counts toward the file the moment it is booked, even though it counts toward the fiduciary balance only once it settles.

When the recomputed `collected_minor` reaches or exceeds `collectible_minor`, the file becomes `COLLECTED`, which is terminal, and this is reachable both from `RESOLVED` and directly from `UNDER_REVIEW` when no dispute was ever raised on the file. The transition runs a three-hop cascade, identically from either origin. The first hop charges the erosion: the layer's `limit_eroded_minor` increases by the file's `ceded_claim_minor` and its `reinstatements_used` is recomputed as described above, with `reinstatement_triggered` set on the file when the count moved. The second hop, only when a reinstatement was consumed, appends a `REINSTATEMENT_PREMIUM` fiduciary movement on the program in status `PENDING` for the reinstatement premium computed above. The third hop refreshes the cedent's `outstanding_reinstatement_premium_minor` from that new movement. The layer's `incurred_ceded_minor` is also refreshed, aggregating the `ceded_claim_minor` of the layer's advocacy files whose status is `RESOLVED` or `COLLECTED` only — files still `NOTIFIED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `DISPUTED`, on either advocacy branch, or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded from incurred experience.

**Closing the placement year.** A `BrokerAccountant` settles the program, which is the accounting close of the treaty year. The program's `placement_status` must be `BOUND`, its `documentation_status` must be `WORDING_AGREED` or `EXECUTED` — a placement whose wording was never agreed cannot be closed — else `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_NOT_AGREED`, and no fiduciary movement of the program may remain in `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `HELD`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_TRANSACTIONS`. Every movement must have either settled or been reversed before the year closes.

Settlement reconciles the ceding commission against realised experience on every `BOUND` layer. Each layer's `incurred_ceded_minor` is set from its `RESOLVED` and `COLLECTED` advocacy files, and its `loss_ratio_pct` is one hundred multiplied by that `incurred_ceded_minor` and divided by its `quoted_gross_premium_minor`, rounded half up to four decimal places. Then, on each `BOUND` layer whose `layer_type` is `QUOTA_SHARE`, the commission slides. The raw commission percentage is the layer's `ceding_commission_pct` plus the difference between the pivot loss ratio of sixty per cent and the realised `loss_ratio_pct`, multiplied by a slide factor of one half: a better-than-pivot loss ratio pays the cedent more commission, a worse one pays less. That raw value is then **clamped first and rounded second** — clamped into the closed range from twenty to thirty-five per cent, and only then rounded half up to four decimal places, because clamping a rounded value and rounding a clamped value differ at the boundary. The layer's `final_ceding_commission_minor` is its `quoted_gross_premium_minor` multiplied by that final percentage, divided by one hundred and rounded half up to whole minor units, and its `ceding_commission_delta_minor` is that final amount less the `provisional_ceding_commission_minor` fixed at binding — positive where the year ran better than expected and negative where it ran worse. The program's `placement_status` becomes `SETTLED`, which is terminal, and its `version` increments.

**Renewal analytics.** A `BrokerActuary` generates a renewal report against a program that is `BOUND` or `SETTLED`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. The report is append-only, carries only `id` and `created_at` from the common properties — no `updated_at` and no `version` — records its `program_id`, the generating actuary's identity as `generated_by_user_id` and the current logical clock as `as_of`, and answers 201. Every figure on it is computed at generation and frozen.

The rate on line of a program is one hundred multiplied by its `gross_written_premium_minor` and divided by the total `limit_minor` of its `BOUND` layers, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is `0.0000` when the program has no bound limit at all. The report's `current_rate_on_line_pct` is that figure for this program and its `prior_rate_on_line_pct` is the same figure for the program named by `prior_program_id`, or `0.0000` when no prior program was named.

Every downstream figure consumes the **already-rounded** rate on line rather than the unrounded ratio; the rounding order is part of the contract. When there is no prior program, or the prior rate on line is `0.0000`, or the prior program has no bound limit, both change figures are `0.00`. Otherwise `rate_change_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the quantity of the current rate on line divided by the prior rate on line less one, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `exposure_change_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the difference between this program's total bound limit and the prior program's total bound limit, divided by the prior program's total bound limit, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `risk_adjusted_rate_change_pct` is then the `rate_change_pct` less the `exposure_change_pct` multiplied by an exposure elasticity of one half, rounded half up to two decimal places — a rate rise that merely follows a matching rise in exposure is not a real rate rise.

The `placement_efficiency_pct` measures signed capacity against everything ever offered, and it is the one aggregate in this system that deliberately reads released rows. Its denominator is the sum of `written_line_pct` over **every** signing line of this program's layers whatever its status, `RELEASED` lines emphatically included, because a line the broker had to hand back was still capacity the broker had to find. Its numerator is the sum of `signed_line_pct` over only those same lines whose status is `BOUND`. The figure is one hundred multiplied by the numerator and divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places, and is `0.00` when the denominator is zero. Finally the `market_condition_index` is six tenths of the `risk_adjusted_rate_change_pct` plus four tenths of the difference between one hundred and the `placement_efficiency_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places: a reading that rises both when rates harden and when capacity gets hard to place.
