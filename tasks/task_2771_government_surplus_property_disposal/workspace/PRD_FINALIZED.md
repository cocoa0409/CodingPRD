# Government Surplus Property Disposal API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state surplus property agency sits between two very different constituencies. On one side are the agencies, motor pools, school districts and hospitals that no longer want a pallet of desktops or a fifteen-year-old dump truck; on the other are the state agencies, cities, counties, eligible nonprofits and members of the public who might want exactly that. Between them sit obligations that do not bend: federal donated property carries a use period that must run its course, data-bearing equipment cannot leave the yard until its storage media have been sanitised and the work certified, controlled articles must be demilitarised, a vehicle cannot change hands without a title, an odometer disclosure and a lien release, and every dollar collected at auction has to find its way back to the agency that gave up the property in the first place — less the agency's own costs, and documented well enough to survive an audit.

This document specifies the API that runs that pipeline end to end. It covers agency turn-in with condition assessment and valuation, the statutory screening ladder that offers property to state agencies, then local governments, then eligible nonprofits, and only then to the public; transfer documentation priced against the recipient's tier with federal donation waivers; auction listing with reserve setting, bidder registration, deposit custody and payment terms; the clearance gates that hold property in custody until sanitisation or demilitarisation is certified; and the proceeds distribution that credits each turning-in agency net of service fees and cost recovery. The system is a system of record first and a marketplace second: nearly every rule below exists because somebody will later ask who valued the item, who certified it, who closed the sale, and who authorised the money.

Two design commitments shape the whole specification. First, **segregation of duties is enforced in the data, not in policy documents** — the technician who certifies a clearance may not be a person who appraised the item, and the finance officer who posts a distribution may not be the officer who closed the lot. Second, **an asset's custody lifecycle and its release-gate lifecycle are independent tracks that advance from different triggers** and only converge at the moment of release. Implementers should expect to reconstruct many rules from the workflow narratives in §3 rather than from a schema listing; the narratives are the specification.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, the logical clock, audit stamps, rounding, concurrency, the two lifecycle axes, cascade semantics, segregation of duties, validation precedence and the two conserved-quantity invariants.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it is acting. There are exactly seven roles, spelled precisely as follows: `ProgramAdmin`, `AgencyOfficer`, `SurplusAssessor`, `DisposalOfficer`, `ClearanceTechnician`, `Bidder` and `FinanceController`. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is missing, or whose value is not one of those seven, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose `X-User-ID` header is missing or empty is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Once the caller is authenticated, the role must appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called, as tabulated in §4; a role calling an endpoint it is not listed for is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` names a real persisted row and ownership checks compare foreign keys against it. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agency`; the value must resolve to an existing `Agency` row, and a header that does not resolve is an `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` failure rather than a not-found. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `Agency` compare the resource's own agency reference to `X-User-ID`: a `TurnIn` is scoped by its `turning_in_agency_id`, a `ScreeningRequest` by its `requesting_agency_id`, a `TransferOrder` by its `recipient_agency_id`, and an `Agency` read by its own `id`. An `AgencyOfficer` reaching a row that belongs to another organisation is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_AGENCY_SCOPE` (HTTP 403); the operations that carry this scope check are agency read, turn-in creation, turn-in submission, single-turn-in read, screening-request filing, screening-request resubmission, single-screening-request read, transfer-order acknowledgement, and single-transfer-order read. When `X-User-Role` is `Bidder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Bidder` row and must resolve to one; ownership checks compare `Bidder.id`, `Bid.bidder_id` and `SaleInvoice.bidder_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_BIDDER_SCOPE` (HTTP 403). That scope check guards single-bidder read, the bidder's own bid listing, deposit recording, bid placement, single-invoice read and invoice payment.

The remaining five roles carry **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `SurplusAssessor`, `DisposalOfficer`, `ClearanceTechnician` or `FinanceController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any row's `id`. The server does, however, record it wherever the audit trail demands: a `SurplusAssessor`'s identifier is stamped as `assessor_user_id` on each `ConditionAssessment`, a `ClearanceTechnician`'s as `technician_user_id` on each `ClearanceCertificate`, a `DisposalOfficer`'s as `awarded_by_user_id` on an awarded `ScreeningCycle`, as `issued_by_user_id` on a `TransferOrder` and as `closed_by_user_id` on a closed `AuctionLot`, and a `FinanceController`'s as `posted_by_user_id` on a posted `ProceedsDistribution`. Those stamped identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties barriers in §2.10 compare against, which is why opaque identity still has to be recorded faithfully.

Role responsibilities are as follows. A `ProgramAdmin` is the agency administrator: it maintains the participant registry, federal donee certifications, auction venues and bidder registrations, and reads everything, but it never appraises property, never certifies a clearance, never awards property and never posts money. An `AgencyOfficer` acts for a single turning-in or requesting organisation and only within that organisation's scope. A `SurplusAssessor` assigns federal condition codes and repair estimates. A `DisposalOfficer` reviews turn-ins, runs the screening ladder, issues and voids transfer documents, lists, opens and closes auction lots, and releases custody. A `ClearanceTechnician` signs the sanitisation and demilitarisation release gates. A `Bidder` acts as its own bidder row: it deposits, bids and pays. A `FinanceController` settles invoices and distributes net proceeds back to the turning-in agencies.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints state otherwise, and both are called out again in §3: the per-asset condition-assessment listing returns the governing assessment first, ordering by `assessed_on` descending and then by `created_at` descending; the per-lot bid listing returns the highest bid first, ordering by `amount_cents` descending.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned window.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host's wall clock. Every deadline, sale window, use period, payment term, cooldown and date comparison is evaluated against a server-side **logical clock**. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to real time under any circumstance. An administrative test-driving capability, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it; the capability is open to all seven roles, performs no state transition and stamps no entity. It returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object.

Where a rule speaks of a *calendar date* rather than an instant — an `acquisition_date`, an `assessed_on`, a `certified_on`, a `needed_by_date`, a `federal_use_period_start_on`, a `federal_use_period_end_on`, a `use_period_end_on`, a `donee_certification_expires_on` or an `end_use_certificate_expires_on` — the value is a `YYYY-MM-DD` date and the comparison is made against the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC. Where a rule speaks of an instant — `opens_at`, `closes_at`, `tier_opened_at`, `tier_deadline_at`, `due_at`, `submitted_at`, `reviewed_at`, `released_at`, `closed_at`, `placed_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `awarded_at`, `posted_at` — the value is a full ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits and the comparison is made against the logical clock instant.

Calendar-day arithmetic on instants is exact: adding a whole number of days means adding that many multiples of 24 hours. Calendar-month arithmetic, used only for federal use periods, adds a number of months to the start date's month while keeping the day-of-month, and clamps to the last day of the target month when that day-of-month does not exist there — so a use period starting on 31 August and running twelve months ends on 31 August, while one starting on 31 August and running eighteen months ends on 28 February in a common year and 29 February in a leap year.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Audit Stamps and Version Counters

Every resource in this system carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row is inserted, taken from the logical clock.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row — and a `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on insert and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade and including recomputation of derived fields. `Agency`, `TurnIn`, `SurplusAsset`, `ScreeningCycle`, `ScreeningRequest`, `TransferOrder`, `SaleVenue`, `AuctionLot`, `Bidder`, `Bid`, `SaleInvoice` and `ProceedsDistribution` all carry the full set.

Two resources are append-only audit records and deliberately do **not** carry those two fields. A `ConditionAssessment` has an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else of the system set: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`. A `ClearanceCertificate` likewise has an `id` and a `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Both are written once and never modified — with the single exception of the `superseded` flag on a `ConditionAssessment`, which the valuation selector maintains and which does not create an `updated_at` or a `version` on that row.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this API is an integer number of **whole cents** and is named with a `_cents` suffix. There are no decimal currency values anywhere in a request or a response body. Rates are the exception: `buyer_premium_pct` and `sales_tax_pct` on a `SaleVenue` are decimal fractions, not percentages — a buyer premium of ten percent is stored as `0.10` — and they preserve the precision they were supplied with.

Whenever a rate is multiplied into an amount, the result is converted back to whole cents by **rounding half up**: a fractional remainder of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude. All such arithmetic is performed in exact decimal arithmetic and never in binary floating point, because the boundary cases are normative rather than incidental. Concretely, an acquisition cost of 3,217,450 cents multiplied by a condition factor of 0.50, an age factor of 0.30 and a functional factor of 1.00 is exactly 482,617.5 cents and must therefore round to 482,618, not 482,617.

Where a formula produces several money lines, the **order of rounding is pinned by the formula that defines it** and is not a free implementation choice. The rules in §3 state, line by line, whether rounding happens once on a whole product or independently on each component before the components are summed; the two conventions disagree by a cent on many perfectly ordinary inputs, and the pinned convention is the correct one. Sums of already-rounded integers are exact and are never rounded again. Ledger recomputation is exact integer addition with no rounding at all.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Protection on Two Write Paths

Exactly two write operations in this API are protected by optimistic concurrency control, and both protect a *parent* row's `version` rather than the row being created.

Recording a condition assessment against an asset — `POST /assets/{id}/condition-assessments` — requires a non-optional `expected_asset_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `SurplusAsset`. If that value does not equal the asset's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The protection is not decorative: a second assessment legitimately supersedes the first, so a writer holding a stale view of the asset would silently overturn a re-appraisal it never saw. The check forces it to re-read first.

Creating a proceeds distribution — `POST /proceeds-distributions` — requires a non-optional `expected_auction_lot_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `AuctionLot`. A mismatch with the lot's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here too a later distribution legitimately supersedes an earlier calculated one for the same lot, so a writer working from a stale lot view would compute a distributable base that no longer reflects the lot's settlement state.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Endpoints not named above must not require, and must ignore, any client-supplied version.

### 2.8. The Two Independent Lifecycle Axes of a Surplus Asset

A `SurplusAsset` row carries two enumerated lifecycle fields at once, and they are genuinely independent state machines rather than two views of one.

`disposition_status` is the **custody and marketing axis**. Its values are `INTAKE`, `ASSESSED`, `SCREENING`, `AWARDED_TRANSFER`, `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE`, `LISTED`, `SOLD`, `RELEASED`, `RETAINED` and `DESTROYED`. It begins at `INTAKE` when the item is added to a turn-in. `RELEASED`, `RETAINED` and `DESTROYED` are terminal. The axis is not linear: a voided transfer walks an asset back from `AWARDED_TRANSFER` to `SCREENING`, an unsold lot walks it back from `LISTED` to `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE` so it can be re-listed, and a debarred winning bidder walks it back from `SOLD` to `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE`.

`clearance_status` is the **release-gate axis**. Its values are `NOT_REQUIRED`, `REQUIRED_PENDING`, `CLEARED` and `BLOCKED`, and it has no terminal state — a cleared asset can be blocked again. It is set at creation and thereafter moves only in response to clearance certificates. Nothing on this axis constrains the disposition axis and nothing on the disposition axis constrains this one: an asset can be `LISTED` and bid on while its clearance is still `REQUIRED_PENDING`, and an asset can be `CLEARED` while it is still sitting in `ASSESSED`. The two axes converge at exactly one point — release of custody — which requires a specific pair of values, and at one other point in the reverse direction: a demilitarisation certificate whose method is destruction drives both axes at once.

Because the axes are independent, a transition on one of them is still a mutation of the same physical row: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by 1. A single operation that moves both axes increments `version` once for that operation's effect on the asset, in line with §2.9.

### 2.9. Cascades, Roll-Ups and Deterministic Ordering

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. Those secondary effects are part of the operation's contract, not best-effort housekeeping: an operation either applies all of its hops or, if a validation fails, applies none of them. Each hop that mutates a row refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1, so a cascade that touches an invoice, a lot, an asset and a bidder bumps four version counters.

Cascades run **downward** (a parent decision resolving its children — a rejected turn-in retaining its assets, a cancelled screening cycle denying its requests, a debarred bidder retracting its bids) and **upward** (a child's terminal state satisfying a parent's completion criterion — a turn-in closing once every one of its assets has reached `RELEASED`, `RETAINED` or `DESTROYED`). Upward closure happens on the server's own initiative, without any client request naming the parent.

Whenever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, the set is processed in `created_at` ascending order. This matters for reproducibility of the audit trail even where the arithmetic itself is order-insensitive, and it is normative wherever §3 names it.

Derived roll-up counters are always **fully recomputed** from their source rows rather than incrementally adjusted, so that a compensating operation restores the previous value exactly rather than leaving an accumulated drift. The recomputed roll-ups are: `TurnIn.asset_count` and `TurnIn.appraised_total_cents`; `ScreeningCycle.open_request_count`; `SaleVenue.active_lot_count`; `AuctionLot.high_bid_cents` and `AuctionLot.bid_count`; `Bidder.winning_lot_count`, `Bidder.deposit_held_cents`, `Bidder.deposit_applied_cents` and `Bidder.deposit_forfeited_cents`; `SaleInvoice.balance_due_cents`; and `Agency.proceeds_account_balance_cents` and `Agency.posted_distribution_count`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two barriers separate incompatible duties, and both are keyed on `X-User-ID` across roles whose identity is opaque.

The first barrier protects appraisal from clearance. The `ClearanceTechnician` filing a clearance certificate against an asset must have an `X-User-ID` that differs from **every** `assessor_user_id` recorded on that asset's condition assessments — not merely the governing one. A technician who also appraised the item, in any assessment, is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

The second barrier protects sale closure from money movement. The `FinanceController` posting a proceeds distribution must have an `X-User-ID` that differs from the `closed_by_user_id` recorded on the `AuctionLot` the distribution settles. The same person closing the lot and posting its proceeds is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

Both barriers are checked early — before the state precondition of the operation they guard — so that a conflicted operator sees the conflict rather than a state complaint. The exact placement is given in §2.12.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Referential Existence

Several collections are addressed as sub-resources: condition assessments and clearance certificates under an asset, bids under an auction lot or under a bidder. For any such path the server resolves identifiers strictly left to right and answers on the **first** identifier that does not resolve. A request for assessments under a non-existent asset therefore returns `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` and never looks at anything to its right. Each entity has its own not-found code: `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURN_IN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUCTION_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALE_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, all answering with HTTP 404.

Once every path identifier resolves, foreign keys carried in the request body are resolved next, in the order in which the fields are declared on the entity being created. The same not-found codes apply. Body-level foreign key resolution is performed by turn-in creation, asset creation, screening-cycle creation, screening-request filing, transfer-order issuance, auction-lot creation, bid placement and proceeds-distribution creation.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence

When several rules could reject the same request, the **first** rule violated in the order below determines the response, and no later rule is evaluated. Implementations must not report a lower-precedence failure while a higher-precedence one is outstanding.

1. `X-User-Role` present and one of the seven roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-ID` present, non-empty, and — for the entity-backed `AgencyOfficer` and `Bidder` roles — resolving to an existing row of the backing entity — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
4. `AgencyOfficer` own-organisation scope — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_AGENCY_SCOPE` (403).
5. `Bidder` own-row scope — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_BIDDER_SCOPE` (403).
6. Request shape: every required input present, non-null and of the declared JSON type, and every string-length and numeric-range constraint satisfied — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
7. Every enum-typed input carries a value from that enum's declared set — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (422).
8. `limit` and `offset` within their permitted ranges on list endpoints — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
9. Conditionally required fields present — otherwise `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED` (422). This rule guards exactly two operations, asset creation and agency registration, and its four conditions are given in §3.2 and §3.1.
10. Dates not in the future relative to the logical clock date — otherwise `ERR_FUTURE_DATE` (422). This rule guards exactly three operations: asset creation, condition-assessment recording and clearance-certificate filing.
11. Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching 404 code from §2.11.
12. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, in entity field-declaration order — otherwise the matching 404 code.
13. Uniqueness: `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGENCY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENUE_CODE` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_BIDDER_EMAIL` (all 409), then the component-graph rule `ERR_ASSET_COMPONENT_CYCLE` (422).
14. Optimistic concurrency: `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (both 412).
15. Segregation of duties: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409), for both barriers of §2.10.
16. Generic state preconditions, including every attempt from a terminal state and every repeat of an already-applied trigger: `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), followed by the specialised state codes `ERR_TURN_IN_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_ASSESSABLE`, `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_REGISTERED`, `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_DISPOSABLE`, `ERR_TURN_IN_NOT_ASSESSED` and `ERR_VENUE_CLOSED`, in that order.
17. Safety, compliance and regulatory rules, in this order: `ERR_FEDERAL_USE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`, `ERR_SANITIZATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_DEMIL_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_END_USE_CERTIFICATE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_CLEARANCE_METHOD_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_APPLICABLE`, `ERR_DONEE_CERTIFICATION_INVALID`.
18. Constraint-satisfaction and temporal rules, in this order: `ERR_TIER_PRIORITY_VIOLATION`, `ERR_ODOMETER_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_LIEN_NOT_RELEASED`, `ERR_REQUEST_CYCLE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TIER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_TIER_WINDOW_OPEN`, `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`, `ERR_ASSET_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.
19. Fiscal rules, in this order: `ERR_BID_BELOW_RESERVE`, `ERR_BID_INCREMENT_NOT_MET`, `ERR_BID_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_BASIS_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_ALREADY_POSTED`.

Three consequences of this ordering are worth spelling out, because they are counter-intuitive and are exercised deliberately. A clearance certificate filed by a conflicted technician against an asset that has no release gate at all reports the segregation-of-duties conflict, not `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_APPLICABLE`, and a certificate with a method outside its type's family reports `ERR_CLEARANCE_METHOD_MISMATCH` before applicability is examined. A bid on a lot that is not open reports `ERR_LOT_NOT_OPEN` even when the bidder is also unregistered, because the lot's state outranks the bidder's registration. And an auction lot whose window is itself malformed still reports a venue-capacity or asset-cooldown conflict first, because `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` sits at the end of the temporal group.

### 2.13. The Two Conserved Quantities

Two invariants hold after every single operation, and both are stated here because several compensating operations exist purely to preserve them.

**Deposit custody.** For every `Bidder`, `deposit_paid_cents` — the cumulative cash the bidder has ever deposited — always equals the sum of `deposit_held_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents`. `deposit_paid_cents` is monotonically non-decreasing: it grows when a deposit is recorded and is never reduced by anything, not by an application to an invoice, not by a forfeiture and not by a void. Money moves only between the other three buckets.

**Proceeds ledger.** The sum of `proceeds_account_balance_cents` across all `Agency` rows always equals the sum of `net_to_agency_cents` across all `ProceedsDistribution` rows whose `status` is `POSTED`. Distributions in `CALCULATED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `REVERSED` contribute nothing. Because the agency balance is recomputed in full from the posted set rather than adjusted by a delta, a post followed by its reversal returns the balance to its exact prior value.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registries: Participating Organisations, Venues and Bidders

Before any property moves, three registries have to be populated, and all three are the `ProgramAdmin`'s responsibility (bidder registration is shared with the `DisposalOfficer`).

An **organisation** — a state agency, a city or county, or an eligible nonprofit — is registered with a legal `name` of 1 to 160 characters, a short operator-facing `agency_code` of 1 to 24 characters, and an `agency_type` of `STATE_AGENCY`, `LOCAL_GOVERNMENT` or `ELIGIBLE_NONPROFIT`. The `agency_type` is not decoration: it is the screening tier the organisation belongs to and the discount band its transfers are priced in. The `agency_code` must be unique across every organisation on file, compared case-sensitively and exactly, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGENCY_CODE`. Registration may optionally carry a `federal_donee_status`, which is one of `NONE`, `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `NONE` when omitted, together with a `donee_certification_expires_on` calendar date. Where `federal_donee_status` is submitted as `CERTIFIED`, the expiry date is mandatory and its absence is rejected with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`; where the donee status is `NONE`, the expiry date is null. A freshly registered organisation starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, a `proceeds_account_balance_cents` of 0 — a figure that never goes below zero — and a `posted_distribution_count` of 0.

Participation can be suspended and reinstated, moving `status` between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` and incrementing the organisation's `version` each time. Suspension is forward-looking only: turn-ins, screening requests and transfer orders already on file are untouched, but any attempt to open a new turn-in for a `SUSPENDED` organisation, to file a screening request naming it, or to issue a transfer order to it, is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`. Suspending an already-suspended organisation, or reinstating an already-active one, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Separately, a donee-certification capability records or withdraws federal surplus donee eligibility, writing `federal_donee_status` and, where applicable, `donee_certification_expires_on`; it is the mechanism by which an organisation becomes eligible to receive federally donated property under §3.3. Organisation listings may be narrowed by `agency_type`, by `status` or by `federal_donee_status`.

A **sale venue** is registered with a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `venue_code` of 1 to 24 characters that must be unique across all venues; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENUE_CODE`. Three commercial parameters come with it. `concurrent_lot_capacity` is the maximum number of lots whose sale windows may overlap at the venue at any instant and must be at least 1. `buyer_premium_pct` is the premium charged to the winning buyer on top of the hammer price, expressed as a decimal fraction between 0 and 0.30 inclusive. `sales_tax_pct` is the tax rate applied to hammer price plus premium, expressed as a decimal fraction between 0 and 0.15 inclusive. A new venue opens with `status` `ACTIVE` and an `active_lot_count` of 0. Closing a venue moves its `status` from `ACTIVE` to `CLOSED`; lots already in `DRAFT` or `OPEN` there keep their own status and continue to run, but no new lot may be created at a closed venue and no draft lot at a closed venue may be opened for bidding — both attempts are refused with `ERR_VENUE_CLOSED`. Venue listings may be narrowed by `status`.

A **bidder** registers with a `legal_name` of 1 to 160 characters, an `email` that must be unique across all bidders when compared case-insensitively — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BIDDER_EMAIL` — and a `bidder_type` of `INDIVIDUAL` or `BUSINESS`. Registration lands in `status` `PENDING` with `end_use_certificate_expires_on` null, a `winning_lot_count` of 0 and all four deposit counters — `deposit_paid_cents`, `deposit_held_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` — at 0. A `ProgramAdmin` or `DisposalOfficer` then drives the registration: `PENDING` becomes `REGISTERED`; a `REGISTERED` bidder may be moved to `SUSPENDED` and back to `REGISTERED`; and either a `REGISTERED` or a `SUSPENDED` bidder may be moved to `DEBARRED`, which is terminal and triggers the unwinding cascade described in §3.7. Suspension leaves existing bids exactly as they stand but blocks new ones with `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_REGISTERED`. A separate capability records the bidder's `end_use_certificate_expires_on`, the expiry of the end-use certificate that restricted property requires before it may be bid on. Bidder listings may be narrowed by `status` or `bidder_type`.

Cash deposits are recorded against a bidder before bidding, and only for a bidder whose `status` is `REGISTERED` — `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` and `DEBARRED` bidders are all refused with `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 403). A deposit carries an `amount_cents` and adds that amount to both `deposit_paid_cents` and `deposit_held_cents`, leaving `deposit_applied_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` untouched, which keeps the deposit-custody identity of §2.13 intact. A `Bidder` may record its own deposits; a `FinanceController` may record anyone's.

### 3.2. Turn-In Intake and the Property Record

Property enters the system through a **turn-in**, opened by an `AgencyOfficer` of the relinquishing organisation or by a `ProgramAdmin` on its behalf. The opening payload names the `turning_in_agency_id` of the organisation giving up custody, a `custody_location` of 1 to 120 characters naming the yard or warehouse where the property physically sits, and optional free-text `remarks`. The turn-in opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `asset_count` and `appraised_total_cents` both 0 and with `submitted_at`, `reviewed_at` and `rejection_reason` all null. Turn-in listings may be narrowed by `turning_in_agency_id` or by `status`.

While the turn-in is still `DRAFT`, items are added to it one at a time. Each item becomes a `SurplusAsset` naming its `turn_in_id`, and the turn-in must be in `DRAFT` at that moment; adding property to a turn-in in any other state is refused with `ERR_TURN_IN_NOT_DRAFT`. An item is described by a `category` — one of `VEHICLE`, `IT_EQUIPMENT`, `FURNITURE`, `HEAVY_EQUIPMENT` or `RESTRICTED_MILITARY` — a `description` of 1 to 200 characters, an `acquisition_cost_cents` that must be strictly greater than zero, and an `acquisition_date` giving the calendar date on which the turning-in agency originally acquired it. That date may not be later than the logical clock's date; a future acquisition date is rejected with `ERR_FUTURE_DATE`.

Four boolean or conditional attributes decide which compliance regimes the item falls under, and all four are supplied at intake. `is_data_bearing` marks an item that contains non-volatile storage and therefore needs data sanitisation before it can leave; it defaults to false. `is_restricted` marks a controlled or restricted article that needs demilitarisation before it can leave; it also defaults to false, and when it is true a `demil_code` of `A`, `B`, `C`, `D` or `Q` is mandatory — its absence is rejected with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`, and for an unrestricted item the `demil_code` is null. `federal_source` marks an item that the turning-in agency itself received as federal surplus donation and which therefore carries a use period; it defaults to false, and when it is true a `federal_use_period_start_on` date is mandatory, again under `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. Vehicles carry their own documentary triple: when `category` is `VEHICLE`, the `vin`, the `odometer_reading` and the `title_number` are all mandatory, and any of the three missing is `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. The `odometer_reading` must be zero or greater. For non-vehicles all three are null. An optional `lien_holder_name` records a lienholder still shown on the title; where it is present, releasing the item will later demand a lien release reference.

Items can be nested. An optional `component_of_asset_id` names another asset that this item is physically installed in — the data-bearing telematics unit inside a fleet truck, for instance — so that the sanitisation obligation attaches to the component while the truck itself moves as a vehicle. The referenced asset must sit on the same `turn_in_id`, must not be the item being created, and must not be one of that item's own ancestors: the component graph must stay acyclic. Any of those three violations is rejected with `ERR_ASSET_COMPONENT_CYCLE` (HTTP 422). Asset listings may be narrowed by `turn_in_id`, by `component_of_asset_id`, by `category`, by `disposition_status` or by `clearance_status`.

Creation initialises both lifecycle axes at once. `disposition_status` begins at `INTAKE` in every case. `clearance_status` begins at `REQUIRED_PENDING` when either `is_data_bearing` or `is_restricted` is true, and at `NOT_REQUIRED` when both are false — in the latter case the release gate stands open from birth and no certificate may ever be filed against the item. Creation also computes `federal_use_period_end_on` for federally sourced items (see §3.3) and leaves it null otherwise, leaves `condition_code` and `fair_market_value_cents` null until the first assessment exists, leaves `released_at`, `odometer_disclosure_reading`, `lien_release_reference` and `title_recipient_name` null until release, and recomputes the parent turn-in's `asset_count`. That recomputation is a mutation of the turn-in row like any other in the sense of §2.5, so adding an item also refreshes the turn-in's `updated_at` and increments the turn-in's `version` by 1 even though the turn-in's own `status` does not move — a `DRAFT` turn-in that has had one item added therefore already stands at `version` 2 before it is submitted.

Submission of the turn-in is the relinquishing organisation's act, and only the officer of that organisation — the one whose `X-User-ID` equals the turn-in's `turning_in_agency_id` — or a `ProgramAdmin` may perform it. The turn-in must be in `DRAFT` and must hold at least one asset; an empty turn-in is refused with `ERR_TURN_IN_NOT_ASSESSED`. Submission moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock and increments the turn-in's `version` by 1. It changes no field on any asset, but it is the gate that opens condition assessment: assessors work on submitted turn-ins. It also freezes the asset set — from this point forward adding property to the turn-in is refused with `ERR_TURN_IN_NOT_DRAFT`.

A `DisposalOfficer` then reviews the submitted turn-in, supplying a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. Acceptance requires that the turn-in be in `SUBMITTED` and that **every** child asset has left `INTAKE` — equivalently, that every child asset has at least one condition assessment on file and therefore a non-null `fair_market_value_cents`. A turn-in with even one unvalued asset cannot be accepted and is refused with `ERR_TURN_IN_NOT_ASSESSED`; this is a compound guard, in which the parent's transition is conditioned on the state of all of its children. Acceptance moves `status` from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `reviewed_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `appraised_total_cents`, and increments the turn-in's `version` by 1. Only after acceptance may the turn-in's assets be pushed into screening or listed for auction.

Rejection requires the turn-in to be in `SUBMITTED` and a `rejection_reason` in the request body. It moves `status` from `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED`, stamps `reviewed_at`, stores the `rejection_reason`, and cascades downward: every child asset whose `disposition_status` is `INTAKE` or `ASSESSED` moves to `RETAINED`, meaning custody returns to the relinquishing agency, and the affected assets are processed in `created_at` ascending order. The turn-in's `version` increments by 1 and so does the `version` of each asset the cascade touched. `REJECTED` is terminal for the turn-in.

The turn-in's other terminal state arrives without anybody asking for it. Whenever an operation leaves every one of an `ACCEPTED` turn-in's assets in a terminal `disposition_status` — that is, every asset is `RELEASED`, `RETAINED` or `DESTROYED` — the turn-in moves from `ACCEPTED` to `CLOSED` on the server's own initiative, its `appraised_total_cents` freezes at its then-current value, and its `version` increments by 1. This upward closure is the final hop of several cascades described later: release of custody, and destruction under a demilitarisation certificate.

The two derived roll-ups on a turn-in are worth stating precisely because they do not mean the same thing. `asset_count` is the number of assets whose `turn_in_id` is this turn-in, counted across every `disposition_status` value without exception. `appraised_total_cents` is the sum of `fair_market_value_cents` over only those child assets whose valuation is non-null, accumulated in `created_at` ascending order, and 0 when none of them is valued yet.
### 3.3. Condition Assessment, Valuation and Federal Use-Period Compliance

A `SurplusAssessor` inspects the property and records a **condition assessment** against a named asset. The assessment carries a federal `condition_code` — one of `C1`, `C2`, `C3`, `C4`, `C5`, `C6` or `C7`, from unused-and-excellent down to scrap — a `functional` boolean recording whether the item powers on and performs its intended function, a `repair_cost_estimate_cents` of zero or more giving the estimated cost to return the item to serviceable condition, an `assessed_on` calendar date of inspection, an optional narrative in `notes`, and the `expected_asset_version` demanded by §2.7. The server stamps `assessor_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and records `asset_id` as the asset addressed in the path. An `assessed_on` later than the logical clock date is rejected with `ERR_FUTURE_DATE`.

Assessment is only admissible while the asset's `disposition_status` is `INTAKE` or `ASSESSED`; an asset anywhere else on the disposition axis is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_ASSESSABLE`. The parent turn-in's state matters too, and it differs between the first assessment and later ones: the first assessment on an asset requires the turn-in to be in `SUBMITTED`, whereas a re-assessment is admissible while the turn-in is in either `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`, so that a valuation can still be corrected after the turn-in has been accepted. An attempt outside those turn-in states is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The first assessment on an asset drives `disposition_status` from `INTAKE` to `ASSESSED`. Every later assessment is a self-transition, `ASSESSED` to `ASSESSED`, and it is a genuine rewrite of the valuation rather than an append: the assessment that had been governing acquires `superseded` equal to true, and the asset's `condition_code` and `fair_market_value_cents` are recomputed from the new governing assessment. Either way the parent turn-in's `appraised_total_cents` is recomputed — a recomputation that, exactly as when an item is added in §3.2, refreshes the turn-in's `updated_at` and increments the turn-in's `version` by 1 — and the asset's `version` increments by 1. Assessments for an asset are listed with the governing assessment first, ordered by `assessed_on` descending and then by `created_at` descending.

**Selecting the governing assessment.** The valuation always works from exactly one assessment, chosen by a deterministic selector over the candidate pool of all condition assessments whose `asset_id` is this asset. Candidates are ranked by `assessed_on` descending — the most recent inspection date wins — and where two share the same inspection date, by `created_at` descending, so the later-recorded of two same-day assessments wins. Because `created_at` is server-stamped per insert with millisecond precision, that ordering is total; the row `id` is never consulted as a tie-break under any circumstance. The top-ranked candidate governs. Every other candidate carries `superseded` equal to true, and the governing one carries `superseded` equal to false. Note carefully that `superseded` is an *output* of the ranking and not a filter on it: an already-superseded assessment stays in the candidate pool for every subsequent run, so a correction recorded with an earlier `assessed_on` cannot dislodge a later one, and deleting nothing ever changes the pool. When the pool is empty the asset's `fair_market_value_cents` and `condition_code` both remain null. Assets whose `disposition_status` is `RETAINED` or `DESTROYED` are never revalued.

**Computing fair market value.** Once the governing assessment is chosen, the asset's `fair_market_value_cents` is derived from the original `acquisition_cost_cents` through three multiplicative factors and one deduction, in this exact order.

The **age factor** reflects straight-line depreciation at ten percent of original value for each full year the agency held the item. Take the number of whole days between the asset's `acquisition_date` and the logical clock's current date, divide by 365 and discard any remainder to obtain the number of full elapsed years. The age factor is one minus ten hundredths for each of those full years, but never less than twenty hundredths — an item held twelve years and an item held twenty years are both floored at the same twenty hundredths of original value.

The **condition factor** is read from the governing assessment's `condition_code` against a fixed schedule: `C1` retains the full original value at one, `C2` retains eighty-five hundredths, `C3` seventy hundredths, `C4` fifty hundredths, `C5` thirty hundredths, `C6` fifteen hundredths, and `C7` five hundredths.

The **functional factor** is one when the governing assessment's `functional` flag is true, and sixty hundredths when it is false.

Multiply `acquisition_cost_cents` by the condition factor, the age factor and the functional factor, and round that single product half up to whole cents. This is the one and only rounding step in the valuation: the three factors are exact decimal literals, the product is evaluated in exact decimal arithmetic, and a product landing exactly on half a cent rounds up. From the resulting whole-cent figure subtract the governing assessment's `repair_cost_estimate_cents`. If the subtraction would go below zero the value is zero instead — repairs can drive an item's market value to nothing but never below it. The result is stored as `fair_market_value_cents`, and the governing assessment's `condition_code` is copied onto the asset's own `condition_code`. Note that the deduction is applied to the *already-rounded* integer, not folded into the product before rounding.

That stored figure is the single valuation from which both disposal channels derive their prices, and they derive different numbers from it: the screening channel discounts it against the recipient's tier and adds a handling fee (§3.5), while the auction channel takes a fraction of it as a reserve (§3.6). The same item therefore carries two different disposal prices depending on which channel claims it.

**Federally donated property.** An item marked `federal_source` came to the turning-in agency as a federal surplus donation and carries a use period during which it must remain in public service. Its `federal_use_period_end_on` is computed at creation by adding a category-dependent number of calendar months to `federal_use_period_start_on`: eighteen months for a `VEHICLE`, a `HEAVY_EQUIPMENT` item or a `RESTRICTED_MILITARY` article, and twelve months for `IT_EQUIPMENT` or `FURNITURE`. The month arithmetic keeps the day-of-month and clamps to the last day of the target month where that day does not exist, as described in §2.4. For an item that is not federally sourced, `federal_use_period_end_on` stays null.

The use period is a hard disposal gate on both channels. An asset may be pushed into tier screening or listed for auction only when it is not federally sourced, or when the logical clock's current date has reached or passed its `federal_use_period_end_on`. Disposal **on** the end date itself is permitted; one day earlier is not, and is refused with `ERR_FEDERAL_USE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`. A recipient organisation that receives federally sourced property inherits its own fresh use period, computed the same way but starting from the calendar date on which it acknowledged the transfer document — that figure is stored as `use_period_end_on` on the transfer order and is described in §3.5.
### 3.4. The Priority Screening Ladder

Surplus property is not simply auctioned. Statute requires that it first be offered, in strict priority order, to other state agencies, then to local governments, then to eligible nonprofits, and only then to the general public. The system models one pass of that ladder as a **screening cycle**, opened by a `DisposalOfficer` for a single asset.

Opening a cycle names the `asset_id`, an `opens_at` instant at which the first tier window opens, and a `tier_window_days` between 1 and 60 giving the length in whole days of *every* tier window in this cycle. The asset must be in `disposition_status` `ASSESSED`, its turn-in must have been `ACCEPTED`, and its `fair_market_value_cents` must be at least two hundred fifty dollars — low-value property is statutorily exempt from tier screening and pushing a below-threshold item into a cycle is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_DISPOSABLE`. The threshold is inclusive on the screening side: an asset valued at exactly twenty-five thousand cents is **not** exempt and must complete screening. Next comes the federal use-period gate of §3.3, refused with `ERR_FEDERAL_USE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`; last comes the window check, in which `opens_at` must be at or after the logical clock, a violation of which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Note the precedence: the disposability complaint precedes the use-period complaint, which in turn precedes the malformed-window complaint.

A new cycle is `OPEN`, its `current_tier` is `STATE_AGENCY`, its `tier_opened_at` equals the supplied `opens_at`, its `tier_advance_count` is 0, its `open_request_count` is 0, and `awarded_by_user_id` and `awarded_at` are null. Its `tier_deadline_at` is derived as the `tier_opened_at` plus `tier_window_days` whole days, where a day is an exact multiple of twenty-four hours. Opening the cycle also moves the asset from `ASSESSED` to `SCREENING` and increments the asset's `version` by 1. Cycles may be listed narrowed by `asset_id`, by `current_tier` or by `status`.

Within an open tier window, an organisation files a **screening request** — its own `AgencyOfficer` files it, or a `ProgramAdmin` files it on the organisation's behalf. The filing names the `screening_cycle_id` it competes in and the `requesting_agency_id` asking for the property, and describes the claim with an `intended_use` of 1 to 200 characters, a `justification` of 1 to 500 characters, and the `needed_by_date` by which the organisation needs the property. Three checks follow existence resolution, in this order: the requesting organisation must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`; the organisation's `agency_type` must equal the cycle's `current_tier`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_TIER_PRIORITY_VIOLATION` — this is what makes the ladder a ladder, and it also means a cycle sitting at `PUBLIC_SALE` admits no screening request at all, since no organisation's type is `PUBLIC_SALE`; and the logical clock must fall inside the closed interval running from `tier_opened_at` through `tier_deadline_at`, both endpoints included, or the filing is refused with `ERR_TIER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Once a cycle has left `OPEN` its tier windows are over and no further request is admitted.

A filed request lands in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `information_request_note`, `denial_reason` all null and `resubmission_count` at 0, and it freezes `tier_at_submission` as a copy of the cycle's `current_tier` at that instant — one of `STATE_AGENCY`, `LOCAL_GOVERNMENT` or `ELIGIBLE_NONPROFIT`. That frozen tier exists for audit and is **never** rewritten afterwards, not even when the request is revised. Filing also recomputes the cycle's `open_request_count`, which is the number of requests on that cycle whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. Requests may be listed narrowed by `screening_cycle_id`, by `requesting_agency_id` or by `status`.

Requests can be sent back for more information and returned to contention, which makes the request lifecycle cyclic. A `DisposalOfficer` moves a `SUBMITTED` request to `INFO_REQUESTED`, recording an `information_request_note`; the parent cycle must still be `OPEN`. A request in `INFO_REQUESTED` is no longer eligible to be awarded, but it still counts inside `open_request_count`, so it continues to hold the tier window open. The requesting organisation's own `AgencyOfficer` — and only that officer, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the request's `requesting_agency_id` — then answers, moving the request from `INFO_REQUESTED` back to `SUBMITTED`. The answer supplies a replacement `justification`, which overwrites the previous one; `resubmission_count` increases by 1, `information_request_note` is cleared back to null, and `tier_at_submission` is left exactly as it was. Resubmission requires the parent cycle to be `OPEN` and requires the logical clock to be at or before the cycle's `tier_deadline_at`; a late answer is refused with `ERR_TIER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

Advancing the ladder is the `DisposalOfficer`'s decision and is gated so that a tier with live interest cannot be skipped. An advance is permitted only when the cycle's `open_request_count` is zero — nobody in this tier wants the property — or when the logical clock has reached or passed `tier_deadline_at`, meaning the window has closed regardless of interest. An advance attempted while the tier still holds open requests inside its window is refused with `ERR_TIER_WINDOW_OPEN`. From `STATE_AGENCY`, `LOCAL_GOVERNMENT` or `ELIGIBLE_NONPROFIT`, the advance keeps the cycle in `OPEN` and moves `current_tier` exactly one step along the fixed order `STATE_AGENCY` → `LOCAL_GOVERNMENT` → `ELIGIBLE_NONPROFIT` → `PUBLIC_SALE`; `tier_opened_at` is reset to the logical clock, `tier_deadline_at` is recomputed from that new opening instant plus the same `tier_window_days`, `tier_advance_count` increases by 1, and the cycle's `version` increments by 1. The cycle therefore visits `OPEN` up to four times, once per tier.

Advancing out of `PUBLIC_SALE` is the ladder's exhaustion. The cycle moves from `OPEN` to `EXHAUSTED`, `tier_advance_count` increases by 1, and — crossing into the other entity — the cycle's asset moves `disposition_status` from `SCREENING` to `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE`. Both the cycle's `version` and the asset's `version` increment by 1. `EXHAUSTED` is terminal for the cycle.

Awarding ends the ladder the other way. A `DisposalOfficer` names one `screening_request_id` to win. The cycle must be `OPEN`; the named request must belong to this very cycle and must be in `status` `SUBMITTED`, and a request from another cycle or in any other status is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_CYCLE_MISMATCH`; and the requesting organisation must still be `ACTIVE`, an award naming a request from a suspended organisation being refused with `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`. The award stamps `awarded_by_user_id` from the officer's `X-User-ID` and `awarded_at` from the logical clock, and moves the cycle from `OPEN` to `AWARDED`. It also resolves every competitor: the named request moves `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED`, and every *other* request on the cycle whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `LOST_AWARD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Crossing into the asset, `disposition_status` moves from `SCREENING` to `AWARDED_TRANSFER`. The cycle's `version`, each denied request's `version`, the approved request's `version` and the asset's `version` all increment by 1.

`APPROVED` and `DENIED` are both terminal for a request, and `denial_reason` takes exactly one of three values across the whole system: `LOST_AWARD` when a competitor won the award, `TRANSFER_VOIDED` when the transfer documenting an approved request was voided (§3.5), and `CYCLE_CANCELLED` when the cycle itself was cancelled because the property was destroyed (§3.8).
### 3.5. Transfer Documentation, Tier Pricing and Voiding

An award entitles the winner to the property, but the property does not move until a **transfer order** documents it. A `DisposalOfficer` issues one, naming the `asset_id`, the `recipient_agency_id` and the `screening_request_id` of the approved request the order documents. All three are supplied explicitly and all three are cross-checked: the `screening_request_id` must name a request whose `status` is `APPROVED`, and the supplied `asset_id` and `recipient_agency_id` must match, respectively, the asset of that request's cycle and the organisation that filed the request. Any divergence is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_CYCLE_MISMATCH`.

Two compliance checks precede that cross-check. The recipient organisation must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`. Then the federal donee rule applies: a transfer to an `ELIGIBLE_NONPROFIT` recipient, and **any** transfer of a `federal_source` asset regardless of recipient type, requires the recipient to hold `federal_donee_status` equal to `CERTIFIED` with a `donee_certification_expires_on` that is on or after the logical clock's current date. A recipient without certification, with a `NONE` or `SUSPENDED` donee status, or with a lapsed expiry date is refused with `ERR_DONEE_CERTIFICATION_INVALID`.

**Pricing the transfer.** A transfer is not a sale at market value; it is a subsidised inter-governmental conveyance, and the subsidy depends on who the recipient is. The pricing runs in four pinned steps, and the order matters because the handling fee looks circular if the steps are reordered.

First, read the recipient's discount factor from its `agency_type`: a `STATE_AGENCY` pays twenty-five hundredths of value, a `LOCAL_GOVERNMENT` pays fifty hundredths, and an `ELIGIBLE_NONPROFIT` pays ten hundredths. Second, multiply the asset's stored `fair_market_value_cents` by that factor and round the product half up to whole cents; the result is stored as `base_transfer_cents`. This is the only rounding in the whole calculation. Third, select the `handling_fee_cents` from a three-band schedule keyed **only** on `base_transfer_cents`: a base below one thousand dollars draws a fifty-dollar fee; a base of one thousand dollars or more but below ten thousand dollars draws a one hundred twenty-five dollar fee; a base of ten thousand dollars or more draws a three hundred dollar fee. The band is chosen from the pre-waiver, pre-fee base figure and the fee is never fed back into the band selection. Fourth, assemble `transfer_price_cents`: it is the `handling_fee_cents` alone when the asset's `federal_source` is true — federally donated property is passed on as a donation and its acquisition value is waived, but the agency's own handling cost is still owed — and it is `base_transfer_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents` when `federal_source` is false. A federally sourced asset therefore still lands in the fee band its (waived) base would have occupied.

A newly issued order sits in `status` `ISSUED` with `issued_by_user_id` stamped from the officer's `X-User-ID`, and with `acknowledged_at`, `use_period_end_on` and `void_reason` all null. Orders may be listed narrowed by `asset_id`, by `recipient_agency_id`, by `screening_request_id` or by `status`.

The recipient's own `AgencyOfficer` — whose `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `recipient_agency_id` — acknowledges receipt of the document, moving `status` from `ISSUED` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamping `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock; the recipient organisation must still be `ACTIVE` at that moment. Acknowledgement increments the order's `version` by 1. Acknowledgement is what starts the recipient's own federal use period, though the resulting `use_period_end_on` is not written until the asset actually leaves custody, as described in §3.8. An acknowledged order reaches `COMPLETED`, its other terminal state, only as a consequence of release.

**Voiding.** An order in either `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` may be voided by a `DisposalOfficer` with a `void_reason`, and the void unwinds the entire award in four hops. Hop one stores the `void_reason` and moves the order to `VOIDED`; when the order had been acknowledged, `acknowledged_at` is retained for audit rather than cleared. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves the documented `ScreeningRequest` from `APPROVED` to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `TRANSFER_VOIDED`. Hop three, caused by hop two, follows from that request no longer being approved: the parent `ScreeningCycle` moves from `AWARDED` back to `OPEN`, with `current_tier` left exactly where it was, `tier_opened_at` reset to the logical clock, `tier_deadline_at` recomputed from that reset opening, and `awarded_by_user_id` and `awarded_at` cleared back to null — the tier gets a fresh full window to find another taker. Hop four, caused by hop three, follows from the cycle being open again: the cycle's asset moves `disposition_status` from `AWARDED_TRANSFER` back to `SCREENING`. Each of the four rows touched — order, request, cycle and asset — has its `version` incremented by 1. `VOIDED` is terminal for the order.

### 3.6. Auction Listing, Deposits and Bidding

Property that nobody claimed through screening, and low-value property that never had to be screened at all, is sold at public auction. A `DisposalOfficer` creates an **auction lot** holding exactly one asset, naming the `asset_id`, the `venue_id` hosting it, an `opens_at` instant at which the sale window begins and a `closes_at` instant at which it ends, and optionally a `reserve_price_cents` override.

Two disposition states admit listing, and they arrive by different routes. An asset in `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE` — one that walked the whole screening ladder to exhaustion, or that came back from an unsold lot or a debarred buyer — is listable outright, moving `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE` → `LISTED`. An asset still in `ASSESSED` is listable only under the de-minimis exemption: its `fair_market_value_cents` must be strictly below two hundred fifty dollars, and its turn-in must have been `ACCEPTED`, in which case it moves `ASSESSED` → `LISTED` without ever entering a screening cycle. The comparison is strict, so an asset valued at exactly twenty-five thousand cents is not exempt and cannot take this path. Any other disposition state, and any above-threshold `ASSESSED` asset that has not completed screening, is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_DISPOSABLE`.

The remaining creation checks run in a fixed order. The venue's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_VENUE_CLOSED`. The federal use-period gate of §3.3 applies here exactly as it does to screening, refused with `ERR_FEDERAL_USE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`. Then two window-conflict rules with deliberately different geometries, checked in this order.

The first is a **venue capacity** rule over half-open windows. Consider the lots at the same venue whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `OPEN`; two windows are treated as intersecting when the new lot's `opens_at` falls strictly before the other's `closes_at` and the other's `opens_at` falls strictly before the new lot's `closes_at`. Windows that merely touch — a new lot opening at the exact instant another closes — do not intersect. If the number of intersecting lots is greater than or equal to the venue's `concurrent_lot_capacity`, the creation is refused with `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`. The second is an **asset cooldown** rule over closed windows with a mandatory buffer, and its capacity is always one rather than a configured number. Consider every lot ever created for the same asset whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `CLOSED_MET`, `CLOSED_NO_SALE` or `SETTLED` — that is, every lot except the cancelled ones. The new lot's `opens_at` must be at or after the latest of those lots' `closes_at` plus a twenty-four hour relist cooldown. Equality passes: opening exactly one day after the previous close is fine, one second earlier is not, and adjacency that the venue rule permits is forbidden here. A violation is refused with `ERR_ASSET_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Finally the window is checked for basic sanity: `opens_at` must be at or after the logical clock and `closes_at` must be strictly later than `opens_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Because that check sits last, a lot whose window is both malformed and conflicting reports the conflict.

Every lot gets a human-readable `lot_number` assembled as the venue's `venue_code`, a hyphen, and the lot's one-based position among all lots at that venue ordered by `created_at` ascending, with the position zero-padded to four digits — the third lot ever created at a venue coded `NORTHYARD` is therefore `NORTHYARD-0003`.

**Setting the reserve.** By default the reserve is derived from the asset's stored valuation: take sixty hundredths of `fair_market_value_cents`, round that product half up to whole cents, and use the larger of that figure and twenty-five dollars. The floor matters for scrap-value property, whose sixty-percent reserve would otherwise be a few cents. The stored `reserve_price_cents` may never be below twenty-five dollars. Where the `DisposalOfficer` supplies a `reserve_price_cents` in the request, that value is stored verbatim without any formula being applied, and the lot records `reserve_source` as `OFFICER_OVERRIDE`; where the officer supplies nothing, the computed figure is stored and `reserve_source` is `COMPUTED`.

**The deposit requirement.** Higher-value lots demand money up front. The lot's `required_deposit_cents` is evaluated exactly once, at lot creation, against whichever `reserve_price_cents` was stored — computed or overridden alike. A reserve below five thousand dollars requires no deposit at all and `required_deposit_cents` is 0. A reserve of five thousand dollars **or more** requires a deposit of ten hundredths of the reserve, rounded half up to whole cents, but never less than five hundred dollars. The comparison is inclusive on the requiring side: a reserve of exactly five thousand dollars does require a deposit.

A new lot sits in `status` `DRAFT` with `high_bid_cents` and `bid_count` at 0 and `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_at` null. Creation also recomputes the hosting venue's `active_lot_count`, which counts that venue's lots in `DRAFT` or `OPEN`, and increments the asset's `version` by 1. Lots may be listed narrowed by `asset_id`, by `venue_id` or by `status`. Opening a draft lot for bidding moves it `DRAFT` → `OPEN`; it requires the venue to still be `ACTIVE`, refusing with `ERR_VENUE_CLOSED` otherwise, and it requires the logical clock to lie inside the sale window, at or after `opens_at` and strictly before `closes_at`, refusing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise.

**Bidding.** A registered `Bidder` places a bid on an open lot, supplying its own `bidder_id` and an `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero. A bidder may only bid as itself. The gates then run in this exact order, and the order is exercised deliberately because several of them can be true at once. The lot must be `OPEN` with the logical clock at or after `opens_at` and strictly before `closes_at`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_OPEN` — this outranks the bidder's own standing, so a bid on a closed lot by an unregistered bidder reports the lot. The bidder's `status` must be `REGISTERED`; `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` and `DEBARRED` are all refused with `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 403). Where the lot's asset has `is_restricted` true, the bidder must hold an unlapsed end-use certificate: `end_use_certificate_expires_on` must be non-null and on or after the logical clock's current date, otherwise `ERR_END_USE_CERTIFICATE_REQUIRED`. The `amount_cents` must be at or above the lot's `reserve_price_cents`, otherwise `ERR_BID_BELOW_RESERVE` (HTTP 422). Where the lot already carries at least one `ACTIVE` bid, the `amount_cents` must also be at or above the lot's current `high_bid_cents` plus a minimum increment of twenty-five dollars, otherwise `ERR_BID_INCREMENT_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). Last, the bidder's `deposit_held_cents` must be at or above the lot's `required_deposit_cents`, otherwise `ERR_BID_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402) — the deposit requirement is re-read from the lot on every bid, and it is satisfied out of held funds without consuming them.

An accepted bid is created in `status` `ACTIVE` with `placed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and it recomputes two roll-ups on the lot: `high_bid_cents` becomes the largest `amount_cents` among the lot's bids whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `WINNING`, and 0 when that set is empty; `bid_count` becomes the number of the lot's bids whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `WINNING` or `OUTBID`, which deliberately excludes retracted ones. A bid's `status` moves among `ACTIVE`, `OUTBID`, `WINNING` and `RETRACTED`, of which `OUTBID` and `RETRACTED` are terminal. Bids may be listed per lot or per bidder, narrowed by `status`, and the per-lot listing returns the highest `amount_cents` first.
### 3.7. Closing a Lot, Invoice Settlement, Default and Debarment

A `DisposalOfficer` closes an open lot once its window has run. Closing requires the lot's `status` to be `OPEN` and the logical clock to be at or after `closes_at`; an early close is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The close then evaluates the **winning-bid candidate set**: the lot's bids whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `amount_cents` is at or above the lot's `reserve_price_cents`. Bids already `OUTBID` or `RETRACTED` are never candidates.

**When the candidate set is non-empty**, the lot closes at `CLOSED_MET` and four hops run. Hop one stamps `closed_by_user_id` from the officer's `X-User-ID` and `closed_at` from the logical clock. Hop two, caused by hop one, runs the winning-bid selector over the candidate set: the highest `amount_cents` wins; where two candidates tie on amount, the earlier `placed_at` wins; where they still tie, the earlier `created_at` wins. The selected bid moves `ACTIVE` → `WINNING`, every other `ACTIVE` bid on the lot moves `ACTIVE` → `OUTBID` processed in `created_at` ascending order, and the winning `Bidder`'s `winning_lot_count` is incremented by 1. Hop three, caused by hop two, follows from a bid having become `WINNING`: a `SaleInvoice` is created for that bidder in `status` `ISSUED`, carrying `auction_lot_id` and `bidder_id`, with `hammer_price_cents` equal to the winning bid's `amount_cents`, its money lines computed as described below, `amount_paid_cents` and `deposit_applied_cents` at 0, `default_reason` and `void_reason` null, and `due_at` set to `closed_at` plus a payment term of seven whole days. Hop four, caused by hop three, follows from an invoice now existing for the lot: the lot's asset moves `disposition_status` `LISTED` → `SOLD`. The close also re-derives `high_bid_cents` and `bid_count` on the lot and recomputes the venue's `active_lot_count`, and increments both the lot's and the asset's `version` by 1.

**When the candidate set is empty** — no bids at all, or every bid below reserve — the lot closes at `CLOSED_NO_SALE`. `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_at` are still stamped, no invoice is created, and the asset moves `disposition_status` `LISTED` → `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE` so it can be listed again once the relist cooldown has elapsed.

**Pricing the invoice.** Three money lines are computed from the winning bid and the hosting venue's rates as read at close time, and the rounding order is pinned. The `buyer_premium_cents` is the `hammer_price_cents` multiplied by the venue's `buyer_premium_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents on its own. The `sales_tax_cents` is the sum of `hammer_price_cents` and `buyer_premium_cents` multiplied by the venue's `sales_tax_pct`, again rounded half up on its own — the tax is charged on the premium-inclusive amount, not on the hammer price alone. The `total_due_cents` is then the exact sum of the three integers with no further rounding at all. Producing the total by rounding a single compound product of the hammer price against both rates together is forbidden: the two orders disagree by a full cent on many inputs, so a hammer price of 301,500 cents yields 354,314 cents under the pinned per-line order and 354,313 cents under the forbidden compound one. Finally, `balance_due_cents` is `total_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`, so a freshly issued invoice has a balance equal to its total.

**Settlement.** The buyer, acting as its own `Bidder`, or a `FinanceController` on the buyer's behalf, applies a payment carrying an `amount_cents` and an optional `use_deposit` flag. The `amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero and must not exceed the invoice's `balance_due_cents` as it stands before the call, and the combined cash plus deposit that this one call would apply must likewise not exceed that balance; either overshoot is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (HTTP 422). Where `use_deposit` is true, the deposit moves first, before the cash is applied: the smaller of the bidder's `deposit_held_cents` and the invoice's `balance_due_cents` moves out of `deposit_held_cents` and into `deposit_applied_cents` on both the `Bidder` and the `SaleInvoice`, and the moved amount counts as payment in its own right. The invoice's `amount_paid_cents` therefore always equals the sum of every cash `amount_cents` received plus the invoice's own `deposit_applied_cents`, and `balance_due_cents` is re-derived as `total_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`. A payment that leaves a balance moves the invoice `ISSUED` → `PARTIALLY_PAID`, or holds it at `PARTIALLY_PAID` on later instalments; a payment that brings `amount_paid_cents` exactly up to `total_due_cents` moves it to `PAID`, and that settlement in turn moves the lot `CLOSED_MET` → `SETTLED`. Each payment increments the `version` of both the invoice and the bidder by 1.

**Default.** A `FinanceController` or a `DisposalOfficer` may declare an unsettled invoice in default, supplying a `default_reason`. The invoice must be `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and the logical clock must be strictly after `due_at`; a default declared on the due instant itself is premature and refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The invoice moves to `DEFAULTED` with `default_reason` stored and `amount_paid_cents` frozen at whatever it had reached. The deposit consequence is a forfeiture, not a refund: the invoice's `deposit_applied_cents` moves out of the bidder's `deposit_applied_cents` and into `deposit_forfeited_cents`, while `deposit_paid_cents` is left untouched. The lot deliberately stays at `CLOSED_MET` rather than reverting, so that a cash-basis distribution of whatever money was actually collected can still be computed.

**Void.** A `FinanceController` or a `DisposalOfficer` may instead void an unsettled invoice with a `void_reason`. The invoice must be `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, and no `ProceedsDistribution` for the same lot may already be `POSTED` — money already paid out to the beneficiary cannot be un-invoiced, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The void stores `void_reason`, returns the invoice's `deposit_applied_cents` to the bidder's `deposit_held_cents`, and zeroes both the invoice's `deposit_applied_cents` and its `amount_paid_cents`; cash refunds are handled outside this API. `PAID`, `DEFAULTED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal for an invoice.

**Debarment.** Debarring a bidder is the widest cascade in the system. A `ProgramAdmin` or a `DisposalOfficer` moves a bidder from `REGISTERED` or from `SUSPENDED` to `DEBARRED`, and five hops follow. Hop one moves the bidder to `DEBARRED`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves every bid belonging to that bidder whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `WINNING` to `RETRACTED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, decrementing that bidder's `winning_lot_count` by 1 for each retracted winner. Hop three, caused by hop two, handles each lot that just lost its winning bid: the lot moves `CLOSED_MET` → `CLOSED_NO_SALE`, its `high_bid_cents` and `bid_count` are re-derived over the bids that remain in `ACTIVE` or `WINNING`, and its `SaleInvoice` in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` recorded as `WINNER_DEBARRED`. Hop four, caused by hop three, follows from those lots having become `CLOSED_NO_SALE`: each affected asset moves `disposition_status` `SOLD` → `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE`, ready to be auctioned again. Hop five, caused by the invoices voided in hop three, returns each voided invoice's `deposit_applied_cents` to that bidder's `deposit_held_cents`, so the debarred bidder's deposit custody identity still balances even as its money is frozen. `DEBARRED` is terminal. The transitions from `PENDING` to `REGISTERED`, between `REGISTERED` and `SUSPENDED`, and a mere suspension all leave existing bids exactly as they stand; only debarment retracts them.

Invoices are never created directly through a request. They may be read individually or listed narrowed by `auction_lot_id`, by `bidder_id` or by `status`, and that listing is the only discovery channel for the invoice the close cascade produces.

### 3.8. Clearance Gates, Demilitarisation and Release of Custody

A `ClearanceTechnician` files a clearance certificate against an asset, supplying the `cert_type` — either `DATA_SANITIZATION` or `DEMILITARIZATION` — the `method` performed, a `serials_verified` boolean recording whether the physical serial numbers matched the record, a `certified_on` calendar date on which the work was done, and optional `notes`. The certificate stores the `asset_id` it addresses and stamps `technician_user_id` from the technician's `X-User-ID`.

Four checks run, in this order. The `certified_on` date may not be in the future relative to the logical clock, refused with `ERR_FUTURE_DATE`. The segregation-of-duties barrier of §2.10 applies next, refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Then the `method` must belong to its `cert_type`'s family: a `DATA_SANITIZATION` certificate admits only `NIST_CLEAR`, `NIST_PURGE` or `DEGAUSS`, a `DEMILITARIZATION` certificate admits only `MUTILATE` or `DESTROY`, and `DESTROY` in particular is admitted only when the asset's `demil_code` is `Q`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_METHOD_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Only then is applicability considered: a certificate may be filed only against an asset whose `clearance_status` is `REQUIRED_PENDING`, `BLOCKED` or `CLEARED`, because an asset at `NOT_REQUIRED` has no release gate to certify, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_APPLICABLE`. Because the family check sits ahead of the applicability check, a badly paired `cert_type` and `method` filed against an unrestricted, non-data-bearing asset reports the method mismatch rather than the inapplicability.

**Which certificates an asset needs.** A `DATA_SANITIZATION` certificate is required when `is_data_bearing` is true; a `DEMILITARIZATION` certificate is required when `is_restricted` is true; an asset with both flags needs both, and only certificates with `serials_verified` true count towards the requirement. Each accepted certificate re-evaluates the clearance axis and leaves `disposition_status` entirely untouched, incrementing the asset's `version` by 1 either way. From `REQUIRED_PENDING`, a verified certificate that completes every required `cert_type` moves the asset to `CLEARED`; a verified certificate that still leaves a required type uncovered leaves the asset at `REQUIRED_PENDING`. A certificate with `serials_verified` false is persisted for audit and moves the asset to `BLOCKED`, whether it arrives at `REQUIRED_PENDING` or at `CLEARED` — a clearance that has already been granted can be revoked by a later failed verification, though only while the asset has not yet reached `RELEASED`. From `BLOCKED`, a verified certificate that completes the required set moves the asset back to `CLEARED`. Certificates against an asset may be listed narrowed by `cert_type`.

**Destruction.** A `DEMILITARIZATION` certificate whose `method` is `DESTROY` — admissible only where `is_restricted` is true and `demil_code` is `Q` — does not merely certify the asset, it ends it. Filed against an asset whose `disposition_status` is `ASSESSED`, `SCREENING`, `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE` or `LISTED`, hop one sets `disposition_status` to `DESTROYED` and `clearance_status` to `CLEARED` simultaneously — the only place in the system where one request moves both axes. What follows depends on where the asset stood. From `ASSESSED` or `SCREENING`, every `ScreeningCycle` for that asset whose `status` is `OPEN` moves to `CANCELLED`, and each of that cycle's requests in `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `CYCLE_CANCELLED`; this always fires from `SCREENING`, because an asset in that state owns an open cycle by construction. From `AUCTION_ELIGIBLE`, every lot for the asset whose `status` is `DRAFT` moves to `CANCELLED`; from `LISTED`, every lot whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `OPEN` does, in each case processed in `created_at` ascending order, with every bid on those cancelled lots whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `WINNING` moving to `RETRACTED` and each affected venue's `active_lot_count` recomputed. In every variant a final hop bubbles upward: when every asset of the parent turn-in has reached a terminal disposition, the turn-in moves `ACCEPTED` → `CLOSED`. The asset's `version` increments by 1.

**Release.** Release is where the two axes finally have to agree, and where the paper trail is checked. A `DisposalOfficer` releases custody, optionally supplying an `odometer_disclosure_reading`, a `lien_release_reference` and a `title_recipient_name`. Two source states admit release, each with its own downstream precondition: an asset at `SOLD` requires the `SaleInvoice` of its lot to be `PAID`, and an asset at `AWARDED_TRANSFER` requires its `TransferOrder` to be `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Both paths additionally require the asset's `clearance_status` to be either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CLEARED`; an asset at `REQUIRED_PENDING` or `BLOCKED` cannot leave the yard no matter how completely its disposition axis has run. Any other disposition state, an unpaid invoice or an unacknowledged order is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Past the state check, four documentation gates run in order. A data-bearing asset that is not `CLEARED` is refused with `ERR_SANITIZATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`. A restricted asset that is not `CLEARED` is refused with `ERR_DEMIL_NOT_CERTIFIED` — so a doubly flagged asset that failed verification reports the sanitisation gate first. A `VEHICLE` requires an `odometer_disclosure_reading` that is present and at or above the asset's recorded `odometer_reading`, together with a non-empty `title_recipient_name`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_ODOMETER_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). An asset carrying a non-null `lien_holder_name` requires a non-empty `lien_release_reference`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_LIEN_NOT_RELEASED`.

A successful release stores `released_at` from the logical clock along with the supplied `odometer_disclosure_reading`, `lien_release_reference` and `title_recipient_name`, and moves the asset to `RELEASED`. From `AWARDED_TRANSFER`, a second hop caused by the asset leaving custody moves the asset's `ACKNOWLEDGED` `TransferOrder` to `COMPLETED` and, where the asset's `federal_source` is true, computes that order's `use_period_end_on` by adding the asset category's use-period months to the calendar date of the order's `acknowledged_at`, with the same day-of-month clamping described in §2.4 — the recipient inherits an obligation of its own, counted from when it accepted the paperwork rather than from when the property moved. A final hop bubbles upward exactly as destruction does: when every asset of the parent turn-in has reached a terminal disposition, the turn-in moves `ACCEPTED` → `CLOSED`. The asset's `version` increments by 1.
### 3.9. Proceeds Distribution and the Agency Proceeds Ledger

Money raised at auction belongs, net of the disposal programme's costs, to the agency that gave up the property. A `FinanceController` computes that figure by creating a proceeds distribution naming the `auction_lot_id`, the `basis` on which it should be computed — `ACCRUAL` or `CASH` — and an `expected_auction_lot_version` for the optimistic-concurrency check of §2.7.

The checks run in a fixed order after the lot has been resolved. The stale-version check comes first, refusing with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION`. Then the requested basis must be available: `ACCRUAL` requires the lot's `status` to be `CLOSED_MET` or `SETTLED`, while `CASH` requires that and additionally requires the lot's non-`VOIDED` `SaleInvoice` to be `PAID` or `DEFAULTED`; an unavailable basis is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_BASIS_UNAVAILABLE`. Last, a lot may carry at most one posted distribution, so creating another one while a distribution for the same `auction_lot_id` is already `POSTED` is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_ALREADY_POSTED`.

The `beneficiary_agency_id` is never supplied by the caller. It is resolved at create time by walking from the lot to its asset, from the asset's `turn_in_id` to that turn-in, and from there to the turn-in's `turning_in_agency_id` — the organisation that relinquished the property, which may be quite different from anyone involved in the sale.

**The distributable base.** The stored `distributable_base_cents` depends on the basis. Under the `ACCRUAL` basis it is the price the lot achieved, the lot's `high_bid_cents`, whether or not the buyer ever paid a cent. Under the `CASH` basis it is the money actually collected, the `amount_paid_cents` of the lot's non-`VOIDED` `SaleInvoice`, which already includes any deposit that was applied against it. For a defaulted invoice the two bases return materially different nets from identical lot data, which is exactly why both are offered: the accrual figure states what was owed, the cash figure states what arrived.

**The service fee.** The programme's fee is marginal and tiered, and each slice is rounded on its own before the slices are added. The first slice is the smaller of the base and five thousand dollars, charged at twelve and a half hundredths. The second slice is the portion of the base above five thousand dollars up to fifty thousand dollars, and is zero when the base does not reach five thousand dollars, charged at eight hundred seventy-five ten-thousandths. The third slice is the portion of the base above fifty thousand dollars, again zero when the base does not reach that far, charged at five hundred twenty-five ten-thousandths. Each of the three products is rounded half up to whole cents independently, and `service_fee_cents` is the exact sum of those three integers. Applying a single blended rate to the whole base, or rounding only once at the end, is not the same computation.

**Cost recovery.** The programme also recovers what it spent making the property safe to sell. The `cost_recovery_cents` is seventy-five dollars for each `DATA_SANITIZATION` certificate plus two hundred twenty-five dollars for each `DEMILITARIZATION` certificate, counted over the asset's clearance certificates whose `serials_verified` is true as they exist at the moment the distribution is created. There is no time window and no de-duplication by `method`: three separate verified sanitisation certificates on one asset recover three times the sanitisation fee, and a certificate filed after the distribution was created does not retroactively change it.

**The net.** The `net_to_agency_cents` is the larger of zero and the base less the service fee less the cost recovery. A cheap asset that cost more to sanitise than it fetched therefore yields zero rather than a debt against the agency, and the shortfall is simply not recovered.

A new distribution lands in `status` `CALCULATED` with `posted_by_user_id`, `posted_at` and `reversal_reason` null. Creating it moves any earlier distribution for the same lot that is still `CALCULATED` to `SUPERSEDED`, so a lot never carries two live drafts, and — because the calculation reads the lot — the create also increments the `AuctionLot`'s `version` by 1, which means a second create against the same `expected_auction_lot_version` will be rejected as stale. Distributions may be read individually or listed narrowed by `auction_lot_id`, by `beneficiary_agency_id`, by `basis` or by `status`.

**Posting.** A `FinanceController` posts a calculated distribution to the beneficiary's ledger. The segregation-of-duties barrier of §2.10 is checked before the state precondition: the poster's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `closed_by_user_id` of the lot the distribution settles, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The distribution must be `CALCULATED`, and no other distribution for the same `auction_lot_id` may be `POSTED`, otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_ALREADY_POSTED`. The post stamps `posted_by_user_id` and `posted_at`, moves the row to `POSTED`, and in a second hop caused by that stamping recomputes the beneficiary agency's `proceeds_account_balance_cents` and `posted_distribution_count`. Both the distribution's and the agency's `version` increment by 1.

**Reversal.** A posted distribution may be reversed by a `FinanceController` with a `reversal_reason`. The row must be `POSTED`, and the reversal is guarded against overdrawing the ledger: where the beneficiary's `proceeds_account_balance_cents` less this row's `net_to_agency_cents` would fall below zero the reversal is refused with `ERR_LEDGER_WOULD_GO_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 409 Conflict). Otherwise the row moves to `REVERSED` with `reversal_reason` stored, and a second hop recomputes the beneficiary's `proceeds_account_balance_cents` and `posted_distribution_count` from the distributions that remain `POSTED`, restoring the pre-post values exactly. Both versions increment by 1. `SUPERSEDED` and `REVERSED` are terminal.

**How the ledger is maintained.** Both posting and reversal recompute the beneficiary's ledger in full rather than applying a delta, which is what makes a post-and-reverse pair provably lossless. The `proceeds_account_balance_cents` of an agency is the sum of `net_to_agency_cents` over every `ProceedsDistribution` whose `beneficiary_agency_id` is that agency and whose `status` is `POSTED`, across all time with no window bounds, accumulated in `created_at` ascending order; rows in `CALCULATED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `REVERSED` are excluded, and `id` is never consulted as an ordering key. The `posted_distribution_count` is the count of that same set. The arithmetic is exact integer cents with no rounding anywhere.
