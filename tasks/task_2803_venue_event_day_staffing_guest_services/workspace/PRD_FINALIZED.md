# Venue Event-Day Staffing & Guest Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A major venue lives or dies on the two hours either side of doors opening. The guest experience department must decide, days ahead of an event, how many ushers, ticket takers, elevator operators and guest services representatives the bowl needs; must fill those positions from a part-time and contracted workforce that bids for shifts rather than being rostered; and must then absorb whatever the event day actually delivers — no-shows, lost children, medical calls, wheelchair escorts that arrive without a booking, and guests who want their money back. Today all of that is coordinated on printed staffing sheets and a radio channel, which means nobody can say afterwards whether the plan was right.

This document specifies the API that replaces those sheets. The system holds the event forecast and the money envelopes attached to it, derives a staffing plan from an attendance basis, opens shift blocks that workers bid on, adjudicates those bids deterministically against credentials and rest rules, tracks attendance and reliability, opens backfill requests automatically when a worker fails to appear, runs the guest-services desk with response deadlines and escalation paths, books accessibility service pools against real capacity, authorizes guest-recovery refunds under role-specific caps with segregation of duties, and finally settles pay and gratuity and produces a recommendation for how the next comparable event should be staffed.

Two constraints shape almost every rule below. First, the system is the record of authority for money: refund budgets, labour cost projections and gratuity distributions are all held in integer cents and must reconcile exactly, with no drift accumulated across approve-and-reverse cycles. Second, the system must be deterministic and reproducible: every selector, ranking and allocation in this specification is fully ordered by business attributes, never by a generated identifier, so that the same inputs always produce the same staffing decision.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold across every endpoint: identity, envelopes, pagination, the clock, common resource properties, arithmetic discipline, concurrency, validation precedence and cascade behaviour.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they act. If `X-User-Role` is absent or is not one of the four role names below, or if `X-User-ID` is absent or empty on a mutating request, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). When the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles the target endpoint admits, the refusal is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

The four recognized roles are `GuestExperienceDirector`, `StaffingCoordinator`, `GuestServicesSupervisor` and `StaffMember`.

A `GuestExperienceDirector` owns the event itself: it creates events, drives the event lifecycle, sets the guest-recovery budget and the gratuity pool, runs payouts, holds the larger refund cap, is the only role that may reverse an authorized refund, and is the only role that may resolve an issue that has been escalated. A `StaffingCoordinator` owns the labour supply side: position families, plans, shift blocks, workers, credentials, bid awards, direct assignments, attendance marking and backfill; it holds no refund authority and cannot move the event lifecycle. A `GuestServicesSupervisor` owns the guest-facing side: issue intake from the text line, kiosks and in-person desks, accessibility pools and bookings, satisfaction responses, and refund authority up to the smaller cap. A `StaffMember` is the worker.

For `GuestExperienceDirector`, `StaffingCoordinator` and `GuestServicesSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule in this document says so — as `reported_by_user_id`, `resolved_by_user_id`, `refund_requested_by_user_id` and `refund_decided_by_user_id` on a guest issue, and as `submitted_by_user_id` on a satisfaction response — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `StaffMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting worker record; ownership checks on any resource that references a worker compare that resource's `staff_member_id` field to `X-User-ID`. A worker filing a bid for somebody else, withdrawing somebody else's bid, or marking attendance on somebody else's assignment is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Three role rules are conditional on request content rather than on the endpoint alone, and each is evaluated as a role check — that is, before any state or business rule on the target row. Reversing a refund authorization is admitted only from `GuestExperienceDirector`; a `GuestServicesSupervisor` sending that decision receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Resolving an issue that sits in `ESCALATED` is admitted only from `GuestExperienceDirector`, even though the same supervisor may resolve the same issue from `IN_PROGRESS`. Marking a no-show or releasing a worker from a shift is admitted only from `StaffingCoordinator`; a `StaffMember` sending either receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, whereas a `StaffMember` sending a check-in against another worker's assignment receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Finally, refund money is subject to segregation of duties. The `X-User-ID` that decides a refund on a guest issue must differ both from the `refund_requested_by_user_id` recorded when the ask was filed and from the `reported_by_user_id` recorded when the issue was first filed. A violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` skips that many records from the front of the result set; when it is omitted it defaults to 0, and a supplied value must not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` (422).

Unless a rule elsewhere in this document names a different ordering, list endpoints return records sorted by `created_at` ascending. Three list endpoints do name a different ordering: the event list is returned with the latest `event_date` first, the position-family list is returned by `code` ascending, and the worker list is returned by `seniority_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the window is applied and `has_more` states whether records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. The Logical Event Clock

Everything in this domain that reads "now" reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time: credential expiry, guest-issue response deadlines, the reopen window, and every `*_at` stamp the system writes. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time.

An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and is available to all four roles. Moving the clock writes no business row: it only changes what subsequent reads and writes compute. In particular, a credential's `status` and a guest issue's `sla_breached` flag are re-derived on their next read rather than being refreshed by the clock move itself.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Mutation Semantics

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits.

All resources except satisfaction responses additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation of the resource — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation for this purpose: when a shift block's `filled_headcount` is re-derived, or a plan's cost aggregates are recomputed, that resource's `updated_at` and `version` move even though no client sent a field for it.

Satisfaction responses are the single exception. They are append-only: they carry `id` and `created_at` only, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent, and no update or delete endpoint exists for them.

### 2.6. Money, Headcount and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents and are represented in JSON as integers, never as decimal strings and never as floating-point currency. Ratios and multipliers — a position family's `attendance_ratio_per_1000`, a tier multiplier, an overtime multiplier — are exact decimal numbers.

Every intermediate value that is not integral is carried as an exact decimal and quantized at the exact step the rule below names, always by rounding half away from zero (round half up). Banker's rounding is never used, and no calculation may be performed in binary floating point. Where a rule rounds twice — once per shift block and once again on a plan-level surcharge — the second rounding consumes the already-rounded output of the first; summing exact products and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer. Two shift blocks each costing 20833.3333 cents round to 20833 each and sum to 41666, whereas rounding the exact sum 41666.6667 once would give 41667.

Headcount arithmetic has its own discipline: a ratio-derived headcount is quantized to four decimal places by rounding half up and only then raised to the next whole number (ceiling). Whole-minute durations — a shift block's length, a worked shift's length — are computed by taking the elapsed seconds between the two instants and truncating toward zero.

No calculation, selector, tie-break or allocation order in this system reads a `id`. Wherever a deterministic fallback key is needed, that key is `created_at` ascending.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Event Record

Exactly one write in this system takes a version guard. `POST /staffing-plans/{id}/activate` requires the non-optional field `expected_event_version`, carrying the `version` of the parent event as the client last observed it — the value returned by reading that event. Activating a plan reprices the whole event-day labour commitment and supersedes whatever plan was active before, so the caller must prove it has seen the current state of the event.

If `expected_event_version` does not equal the event's current `version`, the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` (412) and nothing at all mutates: the plan stays `DRAFT`, any incumbent plan stays `ACTIVE`, and the event's `version` does not move. A successful activation increments the event's `version` by exactly 1, which means a second activation replaying the same `expected_event_version` is refused. The version check sits after authentication, role authorization, request-schema validation and the resolution of path and body foreign keys, and before any check on the plan's or the event's own status.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to other endpoints neither require nor honour one.

### 2.8. Nested and Referential Resolution Order

Path identifiers are resolved left to right. When a path carries an identifier that resolves to nothing, the request is refused with the 404 error naming that entity — `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POSITION_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFFING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POSITION_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BACKFILL_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUEST_ISSUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` — and the first unresolved identifier in the path wins.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after every path identifier, and among themselves in the order the fields are declared on the target entity. Creating a shift block therefore resolves `plan_id` before `position_type_id`, so a request naming both a missing plan and a missing position family reports `ERR_STAFFING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`. The two sub-resource reads in this system — a worker's credentials, and an event's satisfaction responses — follow the same rule: the owning worker or event must resolve before the collection is filtered.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence: Exactly One Error per Request

A rejected request returns exactly one `error_id`: the one belonging to the lowest-numbered rule in the following ladder whose condition holds. Later rules are never consulted once an earlier one has fired, and within a single rung the order stated for that rung is authoritative.

1. Authentication — `X-User-Role` present and one of the four roles, `X-User-ID` present and non-empty on every mutating request (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorization — the acting role is admitted by the endpoint (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`).
3. Request schema — required fields present, JSON types correct, enum values recognized, integers inside their declared ranges (a forecast attendance of at least 0, a requested unit count of at least 1, a satisfaction score from 1 through 5, a headcount override either absent or at least 1), and pagination inside its declared range (`ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`).
4. Interval ordering — an event's `doors_close_at` strictly after its `doors_open_at`, a shift block's `shift_end` strictly after its `shift_start`, a credential's `expires_on` strictly after its `issued_on`, and an accessibility booking's `window_end` strictly after its `window_start` (`ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`). This rung applies to event creation and update, shift-block creation and update, credential creation, and accessibility-booking creation.
5. Path identifier existence, left to right (the entity-specific 404).
6. Body foreign-key existence, in entity declaration order (the entity-specific 404).
7. Resource ownership for worker-scoped writes — filing a bid, withdrawing a bid, and marking attendance while acting as a `StaffMember` (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`).
8. Segregation of duties on a refund decision (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
9. The event version guard on plan activation (`ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION`).
10. The target row's own state admits the requested transition (`ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BID_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BACKFILL_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_ISSUE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ACCESS_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`).
11. Cross-entity state gates, evaluated in this order: first the parent event's status admits the child operation (`ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`), then the referenced worker is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`), then the referenced accessibility pool is `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_AVAILABLE`).
12. Credential compliance (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`). Compliance outranks every capacity, temporal and fiscal check below it — an uncertified worker is reported as uncertified even when the shift block is also full.
13. Uniqueness — no other non-terminal bid by this worker on this shift block (`ERR_DUPLICATE_BID`), and no other non-terminal assignment of this worker on this shift block (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSIGNMENT`).
14. The rest buffer between shifts (`ERR_SHIFT_REST_CONFLICT`). Because this rung sits below uniqueness, a second assignment on the very same shift block reports the duplicate rather than the rest conflict it also implies.
15. The reopen window on a guest issue (`ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_CLOSED`).
16. The reopen ceiling on a guest issue (`ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`). Because this rung sits below the window rule, an issue that is both past its window and at its ceiling reports `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
17. Shift-block capacity (`ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_FULL`).
18. Accessibility pool capacity (`ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`).
19. A historical-basis plan requires a recorded historical turnstile count (`ERR_HISTORICAL_ATTENDANCE_MISSING`).
20. The chosen resolution is permitted for the issue's category (`ERR_RESOLUTION_TYPE_MISMATCH`).
21. A seat-relocation resolution names a zone the event actually staffs (`ERR_RELOCATION_ZONE_UNSTAFFED`).
22. The refund sub-state admits the decision (`ERR_REFUND_STATE_INVALID`).
23. The guest-recovery budget is not exhausted (`ERR_REFUND_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`).
24. Payout phase ordering (`ERR_PAYOUT_PHASE_INVALID`).

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline Across the Staffing Hierarchy

An event owns staffing plans, a plan owns shift blocks, and a shift block owns bids and assignments. When a parent becomes non-operational the system resolves everything below it in one request rather than leaving orphaned commitments, and it does so in a fixed order so that the observable end state is deterministic.

The canonical chain runs four links deep. Cancelling an event voids the event's plans; voiding a plan closes that plan's shift blocks; closing a shift block cancels the assignments still standing on it and system-declines the bids still queued on it. Each link is caused by the link above it, and each affected resource independently has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

Fill state is not stored independently of the assignments that produce it. Outside the two explicit states `CLOSED` and `UNDERSTAFFED_ESCALATED`, a shift block's `status` is a pure function of `filled_headcount` against `required_headcount`: zero filled is `OPEN`, more than zero but fewer than required is `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, and at or above required is `FILLED`. `UNDERSTAFFED_ESCALATED` is sticky — it persists until `filled_headcount` next changes, at which point the ordinary derivation resumes. Wherever a rule below says a slot's status is "re-derived", it means exactly this function is re-applied.

### 2.11. Guest-Recovery Ledger Integrity

The event's guest-recovery money is held in three fields that must always agree. `refund_budget_total_cents` is the ceiling the director set, defaulting to 100000 cents when the event is created without one. `refund_approved_total_cents` is the sum of `refund_approved_cents` across the event's guest issues whose `refund_status` is either `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`; issues whose refund state is `NONE`, `PENDING`, `DENIED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing. `refund_budget_remaining_cents` is `refund_budget_total_cents` less `refund_approved_total_cents`.

Both derived totals are always re-derived from scratch by rescanning the event's issues — never adjusted incrementally — which is what makes an authorization followed by a reversal restore the ledger to its exact pre-authorization value and prevents drift accumulating across repeated approve-and-reverse cycles. The invariant `refund_budget_remaining_cents` plus `refund_approved_total_cents` equals `refund_budget_total_cents` holds after every refund decision and after every event update. Filing a refund ask changes no ledger figure; only a decision does.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Forecasting an Event and Opening a Staffing Plan

Planning begins when a `GuestExperienceDirector` registers an event. The filing carries a public `name` such as "Season Opener", the `event_date` on which the event itself starts, the `doors_open_at` instant at which guests may enter, the `doors_close_at` instant at which the bowl is cleared, and an `expected_attendance` forecast of paid attendance that must not be negative. Three optional figures may accompany it: `historical_actual_attendance`, the turnstile count of the comparable prior event, which is absent by default and must not be negative when supplied; `refund_budget_total_cents`, the guest-recovery ceiling, which defaults to 100000 cents and must not be negative; and `gratuity_pool_cents`, the pooled gratuity collected for contracted tip-eligible positions, which defaults to 0 cents and must not be negative. `doors_close_at` must fall strictly after `doors_open_at`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. Staffing shifts are expected to straddle the doors-open instant, but the system does not enforce that expectation.

A newly registered event opens in `FORECAST` with `version` 1, `refund_approved_total_cents` at 0, `refund_budget_remaining_cents` equal to `refund_budget_total_cents`, and `gratuity_allocated_cents` at 0. The system immediately derives the event's `attendance_tier` from the forecast. Tiering is a floor comparison, inclusive at each floor: a forecast of 40000 or more is `TIER_4` and carries a staffing multiplier of 1.45; a forecast of at least 25000 but below 40000 is `TIER_3` at 1.25; at least 12000 but below 25000 is `TIER_2` at 1.10; anything lower is `TIER_1` at 1.00. Exactly 40000 attendees is therefore `TIER_4` while 39999 is `TIER_3`. The event list may be filtered by `status`.

While the event is still `FORECAST` or has advanced to `PLANNED`, the director may revise it. A revision may change `name`, `doors_open_at`, `doors_close_at`, `expected_attendance`, `historical_actual_attendance`, `refund_budget_total_cents` or `gratuity_pool_cents`; the `event_date` itself is not revisable. Once the event has reached `IN_PROGRESS`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, a revision is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, and it is precisely this refusal that freezes the headcounts of a locked plan. A successful revision recomputes `attendance_tier`, then walks every staffing plan of the event whose status is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` and recomputes that plan's `basis_attendance` and `basis_attendance_tier`, then re-derives every non-closed shift block's `ratio_derived_headcount`, `required_headcount`, `slot_labor_cost_cents` and `status`, then recomputes the plan's four cost and headcount aggregates. It also re-derives `refund_budget_remaining_cents` and increments the event's `version` by 1.

A `StaffingCoordinator` opens a staffing plan against an event by supplying the `event_id` and the `plan_variant` that fixes which attendance number the headcount arithmetic will use. A plan opens in `DRAFT` with `activated_at` absent, with `total_required_headcount`, `base_labor_cost_cents`, `accessibility_surcharge_cents` and `projected_labor_cost_cents` all at 0 because it owns no shift blocks yet, and with a `revision_number` equal to 1 plus the number of plans the event already had at the moment of creation — so a plan that supersedes another is identifiable without reading any identifier.

The two variants differ only in which attendance number becomes the plan's `basis_attendance`, but that difference propagates through every headcount and every cent. Under `FORECAST_BASED`, `basis_attendance` is the event's `expected_attendance`; this basis is always available, so a plan can be opened before any turnstile data exists. Under `HISTORICAL_ACTUAL`, `basis_attendance` is the event's `historical_actual_attendance`, and if that figure is absent the plan cannot be opened at all — the request is refused with `ERR_HISTORICAL_ATTENDANCE_MISSING`. In either case the plan's `basis_attendance_tier` and the multiplier used downstream are re-selected from `basis_attendance` itself and not inherited from the event's own `attendance_tier`. Consider an event forecasting 40000 with a historical count of 25000 and an usher family whose ratio is 1.5 heads per thousand attendees: the forecast-based plan works from 40000 at `TIER_4` and 1.45, giving an usher block 87 heads, while the historical plan works from 25000 at `TIER_3` and 1.25, giving the same block 47. Plans may be listed filtered by `event_id`, `status` and `plan_variant`.

### 3.2. Position Families, Shift Blocks and Plan Costing

Before any plan can be filled out, the coordinator registers the guest-experience **position families** the venue staffs. A family is identified by its `code`, which is one of `USHER`, `TICKET_TAKER`, `ELEVATOR_OPERATOR` or `GUEST_SERVICES_REP`, and carries a `display_name` used on the staffing sheet, a `base_hourly_rate_cents` straight-time rate that must not be negative, an `attendance_ratio_per_1000` headcount ratio that must be strictly greater than zero, a `min_headcount` floor that must not be negative, and a `required_certifications` array drawn from `ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICES`, `EVACUATION_PROCEDURES`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT` and `ELEVATOR_OPERATION`. Two booleans complete the family: `is_tip_eligible`, false unless supplied, states whether assignments on the family share the event gratuity pool, and `is_contracted_position`, also false unless supplied, states whether the family is staffed by contracted workers. Gratuity sharing requires both flags together, as §3.10 sets out. A family may later be retuned in `display_name`, `base_hourly_rate_cents`, `attendance_ratio_per_1000`, `min_headcount`, `required_certifications`, `is_tip_eligible` or `is_contracted_position`, and retuning it changes no shift block, plan or assignment field in that same request — existing blocks are repriced only when the event or the block itself is next revised, which is exactly what makes the frozen numbers of a locked plan observable. Families may be listed filtered by `code` and `is_tip_eligible`.

A **shift block** is one staffing commitment: a position family, in a venue zone, over a half-open time window. The coordinator adds a block to a plan by naming the owning `plan_id`, the `position_type_id` of the family, a `zone_code` label such as `GATE_A`, `TOWER` or `BOWL`, and the `shift_start` and `shift_end` bounds of the window, where `shift_end` must fall strictly after `shift_start`. Optionally the coordinator supplies `required_headcount_override`, an operator figure that replaces the ratio result outright; when it is supplied it must be at least 1, and when it is absent the ratio result stands. A block may only be added while the owning plan is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; on a plan that is `SUPERSEDED`, `LOCKED` or `VOIDED` the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. A new block opens with `filled_headcount` at 0 and `status` `OPEN`.

Sizing a block proceeds in a pinned order. First the owning plan's `basis_attendance` is taken, and the tier and multiplier are selected from that number by the same floor comparison §3.1 describes. Then the raw requirement is computed as the basis attendance divided by 1000, multiplied by the family's `attendance_ratio_per_1000`, multiplied by the tier multiplier — carried as an exact decimal and quantized to four decimal places by rounding half up. That four-decimal figure is then raised to the next whole number, and the whole number is what becomes `ratio_derived_headcount`, except that it is first lifted to the family's `min_headcount` if the ratio result falls below that floor. The order of quantize-then-ceiling matters: 46.87504 quantizes to 46.8750 and rises to 47, and 47.00004 quantizes to 47.0000 and stays at 47 rather than rising to 48. `ratio_derived_headcount` is always recorded even when an override is in force. `required_headcount` is then the `required_headcount_override` when that field carries a value and `ratio_derived_headcount` otherwise; note that the `min_headcount` floor is applied to the ratio result only and never to an override. Alongside the headcount the system records `shift_minutes`, the whole minutes between `shift_start` and `shift_end`.

Costing a plan is a six-step pass over the plan's shift blocks whose `status` is not `CLOSED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, and each step consumes the rounded output of the one before it. Step one prices each block on its own: `slot_labor_cost_cents` is the block's `required_headcount` multiplied by the family's `base_hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by the block's `shift_minutes` divided by 60, rounded half up to whole cents, one block at a time. Step two sums those already-rounded per-block figures into `base_labor_cost_cents`; the exact products are never summed. Step three sums the blocks' `required_headcount` values into `total_required_headcount`, independently of any money. Step four selects an accessibility surcharge rate from that plan-level headcount total and never from an individual block: a total of 300 heads or more draws 900 basis points, at least 150 but fewer than 300 draws 600 basis points, at least 60 but fewer than 150 draws 350 basis points, and anything below 60 draws nothing. Step five computes `accessibility_surcharge_cents` as `base_labor_cost_cents` multiplied by that basis-point rate divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Step six adds the two together into `projected_labor_cost_cents`; the surcharge never re-enters the headcount total or the surcharge-tier selection. Because closed blocks drop out of all four aggregates, superseding a plan — which closes every block it owns — drives all four of them to zero.

This costing pass re-runs whenever the plan's block set or its numbers move: when a block is added, when a block is retimed or resized, when a block is removed, when the plan is activated, and whenever any block's status changes to or from `CLOSED`.

Retiming or resizing a block accepts a new `zone_code`, `shift_start`, `shift_end` or `required_headcount_override`, and is permitted only while the owning plan is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` — on a `LOCKED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED` plan it is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, and on a block already `CLOSED` it is refused with `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`. The revision recomputes `shift_minutes`, `ratio_derived_headcount`, `required_headcount` and `slot_labor_cost_cents`, re-derives the block's `status` from `filled_headcount` against the new `required_headcount` — so shrinking a block can flip it from `PARTIALLY_FILLED` straight to `FILLED` — and recomputes the owning plan's four aggregates. Removing a block entirely is permitted only while the owning plan is `DRAFT`, is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` otherwise, and is refused with `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID` when any non-terminal bid or assignment still references the block; a successful removal recomputes the plan's four aggregates. Blocks may be listed filtered by `plan_id`, `position_type_id`, `status` and `zone_code`.

### 3.3. Activation, Supersession and the Event-Day Lifecycle

Activation is the moment a paper exercise becomes a labour commitment, and it is the one write in this system guarded by the event version described in §2.7. A `StaffingCoordinator` activates a plan that is `DRAFT`; a plan in any other status is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. The parent event must be in `FORECAST` or `PLANNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`. On success the plan moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, its `activated_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, and its four aggregates are recomputed by the costing pass of §3.2.

Activation also settles the incumbent. Any other plan of the same event standing in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, and supersession runs its own cascade: every shift block of the superseded plan whose `status` is not `CLOSED` moves to `CLOSED`; on each such block, every bid whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `DECLINED_BY_SYSTEM` with `decision_reason` recorded as `PLAN_SUPERSEDED`; on each such block, every assignment whose `attendance_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN` moves to `CANCELLED` with `payout_status` driven to `VOIDED`; and the superseded plan's `total_required_headcount`, `base_labor_cost_cents`, `accessibility_surcharge_cents` and `projected_labor_cost_cents` are recomputed over the now-closed block set, which drives all four to zero.

On the event side, activation advances `FORECAST → PLANNED` when the event was still `FORECAST`, and in every case increments the event's `version` by exactly 1 — the same mutation the version guard protects. A second activation against an event already in `PLANNED` leaves the event's `status` at `PLANNED` and still increments its `version` by 1. Revising the event remains permitted while it is `PLANNED`.

The remaining event transitions are driven by a `GuestExperienceDirector` supplying a `target_status` and, optionally, a `reason`. Each of them increments the event's `version` by 1, and any transition the state machine does not declare from the current status is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Advancing `PLANNED → IN_PROGRESS` requires that exactly one plan of the event stands in `ACTIVE`. That plan moves `ACTIVE → LOCKED`, which freezes the block numbers: from that point a revision of the event can no longer recompute any block's `required_headcount`, `ratio_derived_headcount` or `slot_labor_cost_cents`, because revising the event is itself refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` from `IN_PROGRESS` onward. Crossing into `IN_PROGRESS` is also what makes check-in, no-show marking and refund decisions permissible.

Advancing `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED` requires that no assignment on any shift block of the event's locked plan is still standing in `CHECKED_IN`; a worker who has clocked in and not clocked out blocks the close with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`. On success, every assignment on that locked plan whose `attendance_status` is `SCHEDULED` is set to `RELEASED` and its `payout_status` driven to `VOIDED`, and every shift block touched by that release has its `filled_headcount` recomputed and its `status` re-derived. This particular release opens no backfill request and triggers no reliability recomputation. Closing the event is also the precondition that makes the staffing-model recommendation readable.

Cancellation is available from `FORECAST`, from `PLANNED` and from `IN_PROGRESS`, and runs the four-link cascade of §2.10 in full. Link two voids every plan of the event whose status is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; from `IN_PROGRESS` the locked plan is voided as well as any draft plan. Link three, caused by link two, closes every shift block of each voided plan whose `status` is not already `CLOSED`, and drives that plan's four aggregates to zero. Link four, caused by link three, acts on each newly closed block: every assignment whose `attendance_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN` moves to `CANCELLED` with `payout_status` driven to `VOIDED`, and every bid whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `DECLINED_BY_SYSTEM` with `decision_reason` recorded as `EVENT_CANCELLED`. A cancellation from `FORECAST` normally finds nothing below the plan layer, but the same chain applies. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for an event, and `SUPERSEDED`, `LOCKED` and `VOIDED` are terminal for a plan.

One cascade deliberately does not fire. Cancelling an event does **not** touch the event's accessibility bookings. Confirmed bookings keep occupying pool capacity until somebody cancels them explicitly, as §3.9 sets out.

### 3.4. The Part-Time Workforce: Enrolment, Credentials and Standing

Workers are enrolled by a `StaffingCoordinator` with a `full_name`, an `employment_type` of either `PART_TIME_EMPLOYEE` or `CONTRACTED`, a `seniority_date` recording the date of hire, and an `hourly_rate_cents` personal straight-time rate that must not be negative. The optional `in_backfill_pool` flag records whether the worker has opted into the no-show backfill pool and is true unless the enrolment says otherwise. A new worker opens in `ACTIVE` with `reliability_score` and `season_reliability_score` both at 100, `no_show_strikes` and `completed_assignment_count` both at 0, and `last_status_reason` absent.

A worker's `full_name`, `hourly_rate_cents` and `in_backfill_pool` may be updated later. A rate change is forward-looking only: any `base_pay_cents` already computed on a past assignment is not repriced. Workforce standing is never changed through that update path — `status` moves only through the dedicated standing action, which carries a `target_status` and a mandatory `reason`. Sending a `target_status` equal to the worker's current status is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`, and any standing change attempted on a worker already in `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Each successful change records the supplied `reason` in `last_status_reason` and sets `in_backfill_pool` to false when the worker becomes `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` and to true when the worker becomes `ACTIVE`.

Suspension and termination both remove the worker from the floor. Moving `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` or `PROBATION → TERMINATED` runs one identical removal cascade: every assignment of the worker whose `attendance_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `payout_status` driven to `VOIDED`, each affected shift block has its `filled_headcount` decremented and its `status` re-derived, and every bid of the worker whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `DECLINED_BY_SYSTEM` with `decision_reason` recorded as `MEMBER_SUSPENDED`. Moving `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED` runs no such cascade — a suspended worker has already been stripped of standing work — and merely sets `in_backfill_pool` to false.

Reinstatement is deliberately asymmetric. Coming back from `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` carries no reliability requirement at all and simply restores `in_backfill_pool` to true. Coming back from `PROBATION → ACTIVE` additionally requires that the worker's `reliability_score` has recovered to 65 or more; below that the reinstatement is refused. `TERMINATED` is the only terminal standing.

Two reliability figures are maintained for every worker, and they are computed from the same rule over different row sets. The input set is the worker's assignments whose `attendance_status` is either `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`; rows in `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing, which is why releasing a worker from a shift never moves either figure while marking that same shift a no-show moves both. Qualifying rows are ordered by the shift block's `shift_start` descending, with the assignment's own `created_at` descending as the tie-break. For any set of rows the score starts at 100, subtracts 25 for each row in `NO_SHOW`, subtracts a further 8 for each row in `COMPLETED` whose `late_check_in` flag is set, adds 3 for each row in `COMPLETED`, and is then clamped into the range 0 through 100. The clamp is observable: five no-shows produce a raw figure of minus 25 which is stored as 0.

`reliability_score` applies that rule to the three most recent qualifying rows only, so older no-shows fall out of the window and stop hurting the worker. `season_reliability_score` applies the same rule to every qualifying row. The two diverge on identical data: a worker whose three most recent rows are a no-show and two completions, and whose three older rows are two no-shows and a completion, scores 81 on the rolling figure and 34 on the season figure. Only `reliability_score` gates anything — the probation trigger, bid ranking and backfill ranking all read it, while `season_reliability_score` is reported and never consulted. Both are recomputed after every attendance transition into `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`, and only for the worker on that assignment.

Standing can also fall automatically. Whenever a recomputation triggered by a completion or a no-show leaves an `ACTIVE` worker's `reliability_score` below 65, the system moves that worker `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, sets `in_backfill_pool` to false, and records `last_status_reason` as `AUTO_PROBATION_RELIABILITY`. The `season_reliability_score` is not consulted for this gate, which is why the worker in the example above stays `ACTIVE` despite a season figure far below the floor. The demotion carries one further cascade of its own, described in §3.6.

Credentials are recorded per worker. A credential filing names the `staff_member_id`, a `cert_type` drawn from `ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICES`, `EVACUATION_PROCEDURES`, `CODE_OF_CONDUCT` and `ELEVATOR_OPERATION`, the `issued_on` instant on which the credential was awarded, and the `expires_on` instant on which it lapses, which must fall strictly after `issued_on`. A credential's `status` is never stored as sent: it reads `REVOKED` whenever `revoked_at` carries a value, otherwise `EXPIRED` whenever the current logical clock is strictly after `expires_on`, and otherwise `VALID`. Because the comparison is against the logical clock, advancing the clock past an expiry silently makes the credential ineligible on its next read.

A coordinator may revoke a credential with a `reason`, which stamps `revoked_at` with the current logical clock and drives `status` to `REVOKED`. Revocation is forward-looking: no existing assignment or bid is retro-cancelled, and only later eligibility checks change their answer. Credentials may be listed filtered by `staff_member_id`, `cert_type` and `status`, and a worker's own credentials may be read as a sub-resource of that worker with the same `status` filter.

### 3.5. Shift Bidding and Award Adjudication

The venue does not roster its part-time workforce; it publishes shift blocks and lets workers bid. A bid names the `staff_member_id` of the bidding worker and the `position_slot_id` of the block being bid on. A `StaffMember` may file a bid only for their own worker record and is otherwise refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; a `StaffingCoordinator` may file on any worker's behalf. Bidding requires the block's event to be in `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; on an event still in `FORECAST` or already `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` the filing is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`. A bid opens in `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_at` stamped from the current logical clock, `rank_score` absent and `decision_reason` absent. If the same worker already holds a bid on the same block whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED`, the filing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID`; terminal bids are invisible to that check.

Eligibility to actually work a block is one ladder, and both the manual and the batch award path run it identically. The worker's `status` must be `ACTIVE` — `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` are all refused with `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The worker must hold a credential whose `status` is `VALID` for every code listed in the block's position family `required_certifications`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. The worker must not already hold a non-terminal assignment on the same block, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSIGNMENT`. The worker must observe the rest buffer: no other non-terminal assignment of theirs may have a shift window that comes within 30 minutes of the target block's window, where a gap of exactly 30 minutes is accepted and a gap of 29 minutes is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_REST_CONFLICT`. Finally the block must have room — `filled_headcount` strictly below `required_headcount` — or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_FULL`. The ladder is ordered as §2.9 sets out, so a worker who is both uncertified and facing a full block is reported as uncertified.

A coordinator may award a single bid directly. The bid must be `SUBMITTED`; a bid already `AWARDED`, `DECLINED_BY_SYSTEM` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_BID_STATE_INVALID`. The target block must not be `CLOSED` and its owning plan must stand in `ACTIVE`. A successful manual award moves the bid `SUBMITTED → AWARDED`, records `decision_reason` as `MANUAL_AWARD`, creates an assignment whose `source` is `BID_AWARD` and whose `shift_bid_id` points back at the awarded bid, opening in `attendance_status` `SCHEDULED` and `payout_status` `PENDING`, and increments the block's `filled_headcount` by 1 before re-deriving its `status`.

The batch pass adjudicates a whole block in one deterministic sweep and is the preferred route when many workers have bid. Every bid on the block that is `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED` is considered. Eligible bids are ranked by the bidding worker's `reliability_score` descending, then the worker's `seniority_date` ascending so that the earlier hire wins, then the bid's own `created_at` ascending. Ranked bids are awarded in that order until the block's remaining capacity — `required_headcount` less `filled_headcount` — is exhausted, each award producing the same assignment and the same block bookkeeping as a manual award, but with `decision_reason` recorded as `AUTO_AWARD_RERUN` when the bid was promoted from `WAITLISTED`. `rank_score` is stamped on every bid the run ranked, carrying the snapshot of that worker's `reliability_score` at run time.

Bids that survive the eligibility ladder but find no capacity left move `SUBMITTED → WAITLISTED` with `decision_reason` recorded as `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`; no assignment is created for them and the block's `filled_headcount` does not move. Bids that fail the ladder move `SUBMITTED → DECLINED_BY_SYSTEM`, and `decision_reason` records the *first* failing check in the fixed order `MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `CERTIFICATION_MISSING`, then `REST_CONFLICT`. A declined bid does not abort the sweep: the run continues with the remaining bids and returns 200 regardless, listing every bid it touched in ranking order — a run that awards nothing at all is still a success, not an error.

Waitlisting is not a dead end. When capacity is later freed on the block and the batch pass is run again, a bid standing in `WAITLISTED` that still passes the eligibility ladder moves `WAITLISTED → AWARDED`. A waitlisted bid that has become ineligible in the meantime moves `WAITLISTED → DECLINED_BY_SYSTEM` instead, and so does a waitlisted bid whose block is closed or whose worker is suspended or terminated; in those cases `decision_reason` records `PLAN_SUPERSEDED`, `EVENT_CANCELLED`, `MEMBER_SUSPENDED` or the failing eligibility check, according to what caused the decline.

A worker may withdraw their own bid from either `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED`, and only their own — a withdrawal aimed at somebody else's bid is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and a withdrawal aimed at a terminal bid is refused with `ERR_BID_STATE_INVALID`. Withdrawal records `decision_reason` as `WITHDRAWN_BY_MEMBER`. Because the duplicate-bid check considers only non-terminal bids, the same worker may file a fresh bid on the same block afterwards; and because a withdrawn bid is terminal, every later batch pass skips it. `AWARDED`, `DECLINED_BY_SYSTEM` and `WITHDRAWN` are the terminal bid states. Bids may be listed filtered by `staff_member_id`, `position_slot_id` and `status`.

A coordinator may also bypass the bid queue entirely and assign a worker to a block directly, naming the `position_slot_id` and the `staff_member_id`. Direct assignment requires the owning event to be in `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and the owning plan to be in `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` respectively, and it runs the same eligibility ladder in the same order. The resulting assignment carries `source` `MANUAL` with `shift_bid_id` absent, opens in `SCHEDULED` and `PENDING`, and increments the block's `filled_headcount` by 1.

Every one of these fill movements flows back into the block's `status` through the derivation of §2.10. A first award on a block whose `required_headcount` is greater than 1 takes it `OPEN → PARTIALLY_FILLED`; a first award on a block that needs exactly one head takes it `OPEN → FILLED` immediately; a later award that reaches or passes the requirement takes it `PARTIALLY_FILLED → FILLED`, after which further awards are refused with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_FULL`. Conversely, an assignment leaving the filled set takes a block `FILLED → PARTIALLY_FILLED` when at least one head remains and `PARTIALLY_FILLED → OPEN` when none does, and in both directions no other entity is mutated by the block's own re-derivation. A block that had been escalated for understaffing recovers as soon as an assignment lands on it: it moves `UNDERSTAFFED_ESCALATED → PARTIALLY_FILLED` when the new count is above zero but still short of the requirement, and `UNDERSTAFFED_ESCALATED → FILLED` when the requirement is met, and in either case the escalation flag clears and the block stops counting toward the understaffed total that §3.10 reports. Assignments may be listed filtered by `position_slot_id`, `staff_member_id`, `shift_bid_id`, `attendance_status`, `payout_status` and `source`.

### 3.6. Event-Day Attendance, No-Shows and the Backfill Pool

On the day itself an assignment carries two entirely independent state dimensions. `attendance_status` tracks the physical fact of the worker turning up and takes the values `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED`, of which the last four are terminal. `payout_status` tracks the money owed and takes the values `PENDING`, `ACCRUED`, `GRATUITY_ALLOCATED`, `PAID` and `VOIDED`, of which `PAID` and `VOIDED` are terminal. The two advance on their own triggers, and the block-level counter `filled_headcount` reads only the attendance dimension: it is the number of assignments on the block whose `attendance_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`.

All four attendance movements are driven by a single `action` field carrying `CHECK_IN`, `CHECK_OUT`, `NO_SHOW` or `RELEASE`. `CHECK_IN` and `CHECK_OUT` may be sent either by a `StaffingCoordinator` or by the worker who owns the assignment; `NO_SHOW` and `RELEASE` are coordinator-only, and a `StaffMember` sending either is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Any action aimed at a row already in `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, and so is a check-out on a row that has not yet checked in.

Checking in moves the assignment `SCHEDULED → CHECKED_IN` and requires the event to stand exactly in `IN_PROGRESS` — before that the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` — and the block not to be `CLOSED`. The system stamps `check_in_at` from the current logical clock and freezes `late_check_in` at that moment: the flag is set when `check_in_at` falls strictly later than the block's `shift_start` plus a five-minute grace allowance, so a worker arriving exactly at the grace boundary is on time. `payout_status` is untouched by check-in and remains `PENDING`.

Checking out moves the assignment `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED` and is not gated on the event's status at all. The system stamps `checked_out_at` from the current logical clock and sets `worked_minutes` to the whole minutes between `check_in_at` and `checked_out_at`, truncated toward zero. It then prices the shift. The rate used is the **worker's** own `hourly_rate_cents`, never the position family's `base_hourly_rate_cents`. Minutes are split at an eight-hour threshold: the regular portion is the worked minutes or 480, whichever is smaller, and the overtime portion is whatever the worked minutes exceed 480 by, or nothing when they do not exceed it. The regular component is the regular minutes multiplied by the rate divided by 60, rounded half up to whole cents. The overtime component is the overtime minutes multiplied by the rate divided by 60 and then by 1.5, rounded half up to whole cents separately. `base_pay_cents` is the sum of those two already-rounded components — the blended total is never rounded once — and `total_payout_cents` is `base_pay_cents` plus `gratuity_cents`, which is still zero at this point. A worker on 2600 cents an hour who works exactly 480 minutes is paid 20800 with no overtime at all; the same worker at 481 minutes is paid 20800 plus 65, for 20865. In the same request the `payout_status` moves `PENDING → ACCRUED`, freezing `base_pay_cents` and `total_payout_cents` at their computed values, the worker's `completed_assignment_count` is incremented by 1, and both of the worker's reliability figures are recomputed. `filled_headcount` does not move: a completed shift still counts as filled.

Marking a no-show is the most consequential single action in the system, and it fires five links in order. The assignment moves `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`, its `payout_status` is driven `PENDING → VOIDED` and `total_payout_cents` is set to 0. The block's `filled_headcount` is decremented by 1 and its `status` re-derived, and a backfill request is opened against that block in `OPEN` with `triggering_assignment_id` pointing at the assignment that just failed. The worker's `no_show_strikes` is incremented by 1 and both reliability figures are recomputed. If the recomputed rolling `reliability_score` now sits below 65 and the worker was `ACTIVE`, the worker moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` with `in_backfill_pool` set to false and `last_status_reason` recorded as `AUTO_PROBATION_RELIABILITY`. And finally, caused by that demotion, every *other* assignment of the same worker whose `attendance_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose block belongs to a position family whose `required_certifications` include `ELEVATOR_OPERATION` moves to `CANCELLED` with `payout_status` driven to `VOIDED`, and each block touched by those cancellations has its `filled_headcount` decremented and its `status` re-derived. Marking a no-show requires the event to stand exactly in `IN_PROGRESS`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Releasing a worker from a shift is the deliberate counterpart and is far quieter. The assignment moves `SCHEDULED → RELEASED`, its `payout_status` is driven to `VOIDED` and `total_payout_cents` is set to 0, and the block's `filled_headcount` is decremented and its `status` re-derived, restoring the invariant that `filled_headcount` equals the count of assignments in `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`. Release is permitted whatever the event's status, but the block must not be `CLOSED`. Critically, `no_show_strikes`, `reliability_score` and `season_reliability_score` are all left alone: released rows are outside the reliability input set entirely, which is what makes releasing a worker materially different from writing them off as a no-show. One compensation does fire — when the released assignment's `source` is `BACKFILL`, the backfill request that created it reverts `FILLED → OPEN` with `selected_staff_member_id` and `filled_assignment_id` cleared, while its `attempt_count` is deliberately *not* decremented.

Cascaded cancellation, described in §3.3 and §3.4, is the third way an assignment leaves the filled set. From `SCHEDULED` it drives `payout_status` to `VOIDED`, decrements the block's `filled_headcount` and re-derives its `status`, and — unlike a no-show — opens no backfill request at all. From `CHECKED_IN` it likewise drives `payout_status` to `VOIDED` even though `check_in_at` is already stamped, and `worked_minutes` is left unset. The release that fires when an event closes behaves the same way: no backfill request is opened and no reliability recomputation happens.

A **backfill request** is never created by a client; it exists only as the second link of the no-show cascade. It records the `position_slot_id` of the block left short, the `triggering_assignment_id` of the failure that opened it, and an `attempt_count` starting at 0, with `selected_staff_member_id`, `filled_assignment_id` and `cancel_reason` all absent, in status `OPEN`. Those seven fields, together with the common resource properties of §2.5, are the whole of the record: it carries no field naming the cause of the shortfall beyond `triggering_assignment_id`, and `cancel_reason` is written only by the abandonment described below. Requests may be listed filtered by `position_slot_id`, `triggering_assignment_id`, `filled_assignment_id`, `selected_staff_member_id` and `status`.

A coordinator runs the auto-fill pass against an open request. The request must be `OPEN` — on a request already `FILLED`, `UNFILLABLE` or `CANCELLED` the pass is refused with `ERR_BACKFILL_NOT_OPEN` — the block must not be `CLOSED`, its owning plan must stand in `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED`, and the event must be in `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Candidate selection then scans the whole workforce in `created_at` ascending order and keeps a worker only if all of the following hold: their `status` is `ACTIVE`; their `in_backfill_pool` flag is true; they are not the worker whose no-show opened the request; they hold a credential whose `status` is `VALID` for every code in the block's family `required_certifications`; they hold no non-terminal assignment already on that block; and none of their existing non-terminal assignments falls within 30 minutes of the block's window. Here the rest buffer acts as a candidate filter rather than as an error — an excluded worker is simply not a candidate. Surviving candidates are ranked by `reliability_score` descending, then `seniority_date` ascending so the earlier hire wins, then `created_at` ascending, and the top-ranked candidate wins. No identifier is ever consulted, so the winner is reproducible across runs.

A winner moves the request `OPEN → FILLED`: `selected_staff_member_id` and `filled_assignment_id` are recorded, `attempt_count` is incremented by 1, a new assignment is created for the winner with `source` `BACKFILL` in `SCHEDULED` and `PENDING`, and the block's `filled_headcount` is incremented by 1 with its `status` re-derived. An empty candidate pool is not an error: the pass still returns 200, `attempt_count` is still incremented by 1, `selected_staff_member_id` stays absent, the request moves `OPEN → UNFILLABLE`, and the block escalates — `OPEN → UNDERSTAFFED_ESCALATED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED → UNDERSTAFFED_ESCALATED` according to where it stood — so that the shortfall becomes visible in the post-event understaffed count. A coordinator may instead abandon an open request with a `cancel_reason`, moving it `OPEN → CANCELLED` and leaving the block in whatever fill state it already had; later auto-fill attempts against it are then refused with `ERR_BACKFILL_NOT_OPEN`. `FILLED`, `UNFILLABLE` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal request states, though `FILLED` uniquely loops back to `OPEN` through the release compensation above.

### 3.7. Guest Issue Intake, Response Deadlines and Escalation

Guests reach the department through three reporting lines, and an issue records which one was used in its `channel` field: `TEXT_LINE`, `IN_PERSON` or `KIOSK`. A `GuestServicesSupervisor` or a `GuestExperienceDirector` files the issue against an `event_id`, states the `category`, offers a `severity`, writes a free-text `description` of the guest's problem, and may supply a `seat_reference` locator such as `112-R-14`. The `category` is one of `SEAT_RELOCATION`, `CONDUCT_VIOLATION`, `ACCESSIBILITY`, `LOST_FOUND`, `LOST_CHILD`, `MEDICAL` or `OTHER`, and it governs both the permitted resolutions and, in two cases, the severity itself. A new issue opens in `OPEN` with `reported_by_user_id` recorded from `X-User-ID`, `refund_status` at `NONE`, `refund_requested_cents` at 0, `reopen_count` at 0, and `assigned_staff_member_id`, `resolved_by_user_id`, `resolution_type`, `resolved_at`, `relocation_zone_code`, `escalated_from_issue_id`, `refund_requested_by_user_id` and `refund_decided_by_user_id` all absent.

`severity` is computed rather than simply stored. When the `category` is `LOST_CHILD` or `MEDICAL`, or when the issue was created by an escalation and therefore carries an `escalated_from_issue_id`, the severity is forced to `CRITICAL` and whatever the client sent is discarded. Otherwise the client's own value stands, from `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`.

Severity then buys a response allowance. A `CRITICAL` issue is given 5 minutes, a `HIGH` issue 15 minutes, a `MEDIUM` issue 45 minutes and a `LOW` issue 120 minutes, and that allowance is recorded in `sla_minutes`. `sla_clock_started_at` is the issue's `created_at` — which for an escalation child is the escalation instant itself — and `sla_deadline` is `sla_clock_started_at` advanced by `sla_minutes`. The `sla_breached` flag compares a reference instant against that deadline strictly: the reference is `resolved_at` once the issue has been resolved and the current logical clock while it is still unresolved. An issue filed at 12:00:00Z with severity `HIGH` therefore carries a deadline of 12:15:00Z, and resolving at exactly 12:15:00Z leaves `sla_breached` false while resolving at 12:16:00Z sets it true. A lost-child report filed with severity `LOW` stores `CRITICAL` with `sla_minutes` 5, giving it a deadline of 12:05:00Z. Because the unresolved case reads the live clock, reading an issue re-evaluates `sla_breached` on every read until the issue is resolved, at which point the flag freezes.

Handing the issue to a responder is a supervisor action carrying the `staff_member_id` of the intended responder. It moves the issue `OPEN → IN_PROGRESS`, or `REOPENED → IN_PROGRESS` for an issue that had been reopened. The event must stand exactly in `IN_PROGRESS`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`; the responder's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the responder must hold a credential whose `status` is `VALID` for `CODE_OF_CONDUCT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. On success `assigned_staff_member_id` is recorded. Assignment deliberately does *not* restart the response clock: `sla_clock_started_at` and `sla_deadline` are left exactly as they were, and `sla_breached` is merely recomputed against that unchanged deadline. The same holds when a reopened issue is handed out again — the clock is preserved from the original filing, so a reopened issue that was already late stays late.

Escalation is the pressure valve for an issue the department has failed to answer in time. A supervisor may escalate from `OPEN` or from `IN_PROGRESS`, but only while `sla_breached` evaluates true at the current logical clock; escalating an issue that is still inside its allowance is refused with `ERR_ISSUE_STATE_INVALID`. The parent moves to `ESCALATED`, and a brand-new issue is created carrying `escalated_from_issue_id` pointing back at the parent, `severity` `CRITICAL`, `sla_minutes` 5, `sla_clock_started_at` set to the current logical clock, `status` `OPEN`, and the same `event_id`, `category`, `channel`, `seat_reference` and `description` as its parent. It is the child that is returned to the caller, with a 201. Escalating from `IN_PROGRESS` leaves the parent's `assigned_staff_member_id` in place. Once a parent sits in `ESCALATED`, only a `GuestExperienceDirector` may resolve it — a supervisor attempting it receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Issues may be listed filtered by `event_id`, `escalated_from_issue_id`, `assigned_staff_member_id`, `status`, `severity`, `category` and `refund_status`. `CLOSED` is the only terminal issue state; `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_AUTHORIZATION`, `RESOLVED`, `REOPENED` and `ESCALATED` are all live.

### 3.8. Issue Resolution, Refund Authority and the Recovery Ledger

Resolving an issue records a `resolution_type` and moves the issue `IN_PROGRESS → RESOLVED` or `ESCALATED → RESOLVED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_ISSUE_STATE_INVALID`. The chosen resolution must be permitted for the issue's `category`, and the permitted sets are fixed. A `SEAT_RELOCATION` issue admits `SEAT_RELOCATION`, `REFUND`, `COMPLIMENTARY_CREDIT` or `NO_ACTION`. A `CONDUCT_VIOLATION` issue admits `EJECTION` or `NO_ACTION`. An `ACCESSIBILITY` issue admits `SEAT_RELOCATION`, `COMPLIMENTARY_CREDIT` or `NO_ACTION`. A `LOST_FOUND` issue admits `ITEM_RETURNED` or `NO_ACTION`. A `LOST_CHILD` issue admits only `REUNIFICATION`. A `MEDICAL` issue admits `MEDICAL_TRANSPORT` or `NO_ACTION`. An issue categorised `OTHER` admits any of the eight resolutions. Anything outside the permitted set — an ejection recorded against a lost-child report, for instance — is refused with `ERR_RESOLUTION_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

Two resolutions carry extra conditions. A `SEAT_RELOCATION` resolution requires a `relocation_zone_code`, and that zone must match the `zone_code` of at least one shift block that is not `CLOSED` on the event's `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED` plan — a guest may only be moved into a zone the event actually staffs — failing which the request is refused with `ERR_RELOCATION_ZONE_UNSTAFFED`. A `REFUND` resolution requires the issue's `refund_status` to already stand at `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_REFUND_STATE_INVALID`. A successful resolution stamps `resolved_at` from the current logical clock, records `resolved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, freezes `sla_breached` at its computed value, stores `relocation_zone_code` when the resolution was a seat relocation, and makes the issue eligible for the resolved-issue counter of the post-event satisfaction summary.

A resolved issue is not yet final. Within a window of 1440 minutes measured from `resolved_at` — where exactly 1440 minutes is still inside the window — a supervisor may reopen it, moving it `RESOLVED → REOPENED`; past that boundary the reopen is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. An issue may be reopened at most twice: once `reopen_count` has reached 2, a further attempt is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`, and because the window rule is evaluated first, an issue that is both past its window and at its ceiling reports `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Reopening increments `reopen_count` by 1 and clears `resolved_at`, `resolution_type` and `resolved_by_user_id`, but deliberately leaves `refund_approved_cents` and every figure in the event ledger untouched — money already authorized is not clawed back by a reopen.

Closing an issue is the mirror of reopening it. A supervisor may close a `RESOLVED` issue only once strictly more than 1440 minutes have elapsed since `resolved_at`; at or inside that boundary the close is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A closed issue is frozen: reopening it, resolving it again and deciding a refund on it all return `ERR_ISSUE_STATE_INVALID` from then on.

Guest-recovery money moves in two steps by design, because the requester and the decider must be different people. A supervisor first files the ask, supplying `refund_requested_cents`; the system stores that amount, records `refund_requested_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and moves `refund_status` `NONE → PENDING`. No money moves and no ledger figure changes at this point. Note that `refund_status` is a sub-state entirely independent of the issue's own `status`, and takes the values `NONE`, `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `VOIDED`.

A decision is then filed by a different operator, carrying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `VOID`. Reversal is director-only, as §2.1 states. The deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from both `refund_requested_by_user_id` and `reported_by_user_id`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The issue's refund sub-state must admit the decision: an approval requires `refund_status` to be `NONE`, `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, and a reversal requires it to be `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_REFUND_STATE_INVALID`. Before an approval is attempted at all, the event must still have room: the event's `refund_budget_remaining_cents` plus this issue's already-recorded `refund_approved_cents` must be greater than zero, or the approval is refused with `ERR_REFUND_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`.

The authorized amount is then composed in a pinned order. First a role cap: 5000 cents for a `GuestServicesSupervisor` and 50000 cents for a `GuestExperienceDirector`. Second a severity uplift expressed in basis points of that cap — 5000 basis points for a `CRITICAL` issue, 2500 for a `HIGH` issue, and nothing at all for `MEDIUM` or `LOW` — where the uplift amount is the role cap multiplied by the basis points divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents, and added to the role cap to give the effective cap. Third, and only after the uplift has been applied, a hard ceiling of 2500 cents on any issue whose `category` is `SEAT_RELOCATION`: the effective cap becomes the smaller of itself and 2500. So a supervisor deciding a `CRITICAL` issue works to an effective cap of 7500 cents, being 5000 plus half of 5000, but the same supervisor on a `SEAT_RELOCATION` issue is held to 2500 whatever the severity. Fourth, the available budget is the event's `refund_budget_remaining_cents` plus this issue's own already-recorded `refund_approved_cents` — the issue's previous authorization is returned to the pot before it is re-decided. The amount authorized is then the smallest of three figures: the `refund_requested_cents` asked for, the effective cap, and that available budget. It is stored in `refund_approved_cents`, and `refund_status` becomes `APPROVED` when the authorized amount equals the full request and `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` otherwise. `refund_decided_by_user_id` records the deciding operator's `X-User-ID`, and the event's `refund_approved_total_cents` and `refund_budget_remaining_cents` are re-derived by the full rescan of §2.11.

The interesting case is a supervisor whose own cap, rather than the budget, is what held the amount down. When the acting role is `GuestServicesSupervisor` and the effective cap is strictly smaller than the lesser of the requested amount and the available budget, the issue moves `IN_PROGRESS → AWAITING_AUTHORIZATION`: `refund_approved_cents` holds the capped amount, `refund_budget_remaining_cents` is reduced by exactly that amount, and resolution is blocked — an attempt to resolve from `AWAITING_AUTHORIZATION` is refused with `ERR_ISSUE_STATE_INVALID` until a director has acted. When instead the budget was the binding constraint, the issue stays where it is and no authorization step is required.

A director approving from `AWAITING_AUTHORIZATION` finishes the job in one request, moving the issue `AWAITING_AUTHORIZATION → RESOLVED`. The amount is recomputed under the director's own cap and must come out at least as large as the amount already recorded. `refund_approved_cents` is then **replaced** by the director-capped amount rather than added to it, and the event ledger is adjusted by the delta only, so the same money is never counted twice. `refund_status` becomes `APPROVED` when the full request is now covered and stays `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` otherwise, `resolution_type` is set to `REFUND`, and `resolved_at` is stamped from the current logical clock.

The two remaining decisions are simpler. A denial moves `refund_status` to `DENIED`, sets `refund_approved_cents` to 0 and leaves the ledger unchanged, since denied issues never contributed to it. A reversal — available only to a `GuestExperienceDirector` — moves `refund_status` to `VOIDED` and resets `refund_approved_cents` to 0, and because the ledger is always re-derived by rescanning the event's issues, `refund_approved_total_cents` and `refund_budget_remaining_cents` return to exactly their pre-authorization values.

### 3.9. Accessibility Service Pools and Guest Bookings

Alongside its staffing plan, an event advertises pools of accessibility capacity. A `GuestServicesSupervisor` stages each pool against an `event_id`, declaring its `resource_type` — `WHEELCHAIR_ESCORT`, `ASSISTIVE_LISTENING_UNIT` or `COMPANION_SEAT_BLOCK` — the `zone_code` it serves, and a `unit_count` of at least 1. A new pool opens with `status` `AVAILABLE` and `peak_committed_units` at 0. A supervisor may later adjust a pool's `unit_count` or move its `status` between `AVAILABLE`, `MAINTENANCE` and `WITHDRAWN`. Shrinking `unit_count` below the pool's current `peak_committed_units` is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, because that would leave commitments the pool can no longer honour; and taking a pool out of service never auto-cancels bookings that have already been confirmed against it, so a withdrawn pool keeps reporting its committed peak until those bookings are individually dealt with. Pools may be listed filtered by `event_id`, `resource_type`, `status` and `zone_code`.

A booking against a pool carries the `resource_id`, a `guest_reference` locator such as `WILL-CALL-88`, a service window given as `window_start` and `window_end`, a `units_requested` count of at least 1, and an optional `companion_seats_requested` count defaulting to 0. The window must be strictly ordered, `window_start` before `window_end`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. A new booking opens with `status` `REQUESTED` and with `confirmed_at`, `service_started_at`, `completed_at` and `denial_reason` all absent. Bookings may be listed filtered by `resource_id` and `status`.

Every subsequent movement of a booking is a supervisor action naming the desired `target_status`, optionally accompanied by a `denial_reason`. Six movements are permitted and no others; anything else is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.

Confirming a booking moves it `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`. The parent event must stand in `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`; the pool's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`, or it is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_AVAILABLE`; and the pool must have room across the whole of the requested window once this booking's own `units_requested` is counted in, or it is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success `confirmed_at` is stamped from the current logical clock and the pool's `peak_committed_units` is recomputed with this booking included.

Beginning service moves a booking `CONFIRMED → IN_SERVICE` and requires the event to stand exactly in `IN_PROGRESS` and the pool to be `AVAILABLE`; it stamps `service_started_at` and continues to occupy the booking's `units_requested`. Finishing service moves it `IN_SERVICE → COMPLETED` and stamps `completed_at`; from that instant the booking stops counting toward the pool's committed peak, so an overlapping booking that had previously been refused for want of capacity may now be confirmed. Denial moves a booking `REQUESTED → DENIED` and requires a `denial_reason`; since a merely requested booking never held units, nothing is released. Cancellation from `REQUESTED` likewise releases nothing. Cancellation from `CONFIRMED`, by contrast, releases capacity immediately: the pool's `peak_committed_units` over the affected window drops by the cancelled booking's `units_requested`. `COMPLETED`, `DENIED` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal booking states.

Room is judged by peak concurrent occupancy over the window, not by a simple sum. Only bookings on the same pool whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `IN_SERVICE` count as committed; `REQUESTED`, `DENIED`, `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` bookings contribute nothing whatever. To compute the peak, collect every `window_start` and `window_end` of the bookings under consideration into a sorted set of distinct boundary instants. Each boundary except the last opens one elementary slice; for each such slice, add up the `units_requested` of every booking whose `window_start` is at or before that boundary and whose `window_end` is strictly after it. The peak is the largest of those slice totals. A pool's `peak_committed_units` is that figure computed over the committed bookings alone, and the capacity gate for a confirmation is that the same figure, computed over the committed bookings together with the candidate, must be less than or equal to the pool's `unit_count`. Windows are treated as half-open, so two bookings where one ends at exactly the instant the other begins are never concurrent and never compete for the same unit. Because the gate admits equality, a pool of 3 units accepts three fully overlapping single-unit bookings, reaching a peak of exactly 3; a fourth overlapping booking is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

### 3.10. Closing the Books: Payout Runs, Guest Sentiment and Model Refinement

Once the doors have shut and the event has been closed, a `GuestExperienceDirector` settles the labour bill in two ordered runs, each named by a `phase`. The `GRATUITY_ALLOCATION` phase distributes the event's tip pool; it demands that the event's `status` be `CLOSED` and that at least one gratuity-eligible assignment still sit at `payout_status` `ACCRUED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_PAYOUT_PHASE_INVALID`. The `PAYROLL` phase then settles everything; it demands that no gratuity-eligible assignment remain at `ACCRUED` awaiting its share, so attempting payroll before the gratuity run has been made is likewise refused with `ERR_PAYOUT_PHASE_INVALID`. Each run answers with the assignments it touched, listed in exactly the order the allocation ranked them.

Gratuity eligibility is a conjunction of four conditions, and a row that misses any one of them is simply passed over rather than rejected. The assignment's `payout_status` must be `ACCRUED`; the position family behind the assignment's shift block must have `is_tip_eligible` true; that same family must have `is_contracted_position` true; and the worker filling the assignment must have `employment_type` `CONTRACTED`. The third and fourth conditions are separate on purpose: a part-time employee working a tip-eligible, contracted position is *not* eligible, keeps `gratuity_cents` at 0, and simply waits for the payroll phase.

The pool to distribute is the event's `gratuity_pool_cents` less its `gratuity_allocated_cents`, so a second gratuity run over an already-settled event has nothing left to hand out. The eligible rows are ranked by `worked_minutes` descending, then by the worker's `seniority_date` ascending, then by the assignment's `created_at` ascending. The total weight is the sum of the eligible rows' `worked_minutes`. If there are no eligible rows, or if the total weight comes to zero, nothing is allocated at all and `gratuity_allocated_cents` is left exactly where it stood. Otherwise each row's provisional share is the pool multiplied by that row's `worked_minutes`, divided by the total weight, and truncated downward to whole cents. Those truncated shares will fall short of the pool by a remainder, which is the pool less the sum of the truncated shares and is always at least zero and strictly less than the number of eligible rows. That remainder is handed out one cent at a time to the first rows **in ranking order** — deliberately not to the rows with the largest discarded fractions. Consider a pool of 50000 cents over two eligible rows weighted 480 and 300 minutes: the exact shares are 30769.23… and 19230.76…, the truncated shares are 30769 and 19230, and the remainder of 1 cent goes to the 480-minute row, giving 30770 and 19230 even though it was the 300-minute row that had the larger discarded fraction. The cents allocated always add up to the pool to the cent.

Each allocated row then has `gratuity_cents` set to its share, has `total_payout_cents` recomputed as `base_pay_cents` plus `gratuity_cents`, and moves `payout_status` `ACCRUED → GRATUITY_ALLOCATED`; the event's `gratuity_allocated_cents` rises by the whole distributed pool. The payroll phase afterwards moves both `ACCRUED` rows — those that were never gratuity-eligible, whose `gratuity_cents` stays 0 and whose `total_payout_cents` keeps the value frozen at accrual — and `GRATUITY_ALLOCATED` rows to `PAID`, while `VOIDED` rows are skipped entirely and excluded from every phase. A `PAID` row is immutable: no further attendance movement and no further payout movement is accepted on it.

Guest sentiment is gathered separately and is append-only. A `GuestServicesSupervisor` or a `GuestExperienceDirector` files a response against an `event_id`, naming the `zone_code` it came from, a `score` between 1 and 5 inclusive, and an optional free-text `comment`; `submitted_by_user_id` is recorded from `X-User-ID`. A response is the one resource in this system that carries no `updated_at` and no `version` at all — only its `id` and its `created_at` — because it can never be edited, withdrawn or corrected once filed. Responses may be listed for an event, optionally narrowed to a single `zone_code`.

The event's sentiment summary is derived on demand from every response ever filed against the event, with none excluded. `response_count` is how many there are. When there are none, `average_score` and `promoter_share_bp` are both reported as absent rather than as zero, which distinguishes an unsurveyed event from a badly-reviewed one. When there is at least one, `average_score` is the sum of the scores divided by the count, quantized to two decimal places by rounding half up — eight responses summing to 35 give exactly 4.375, which is reported as 4.38 and not 4.37 — and `promoter_share_bp` is the count of responses scoring 4 or better, multiplied by 10000, divided by the total count, and rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. A `zone_breakdown` accompanies the headline figures, one entry per distinct `zone_code` carrying that zone's `zone_code`, its own `response_count` and its own `average_score`, ordered by `zone_code` ascending. Two issue counters complete the picture: `resolved_issue_count` is the number of the event's guest issues whose `status` is `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED`, and `sla_breached_issue_count` is the number whose `sla_breached` flag is true.

The last read of the season feeds the numbers back into next season's ratios. It is available to a `GuestExperienceDirector` or a `StaffingCoordinator`, requires the event's `status` to be `CLOSED` — otherwise it is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` — and works from the event's `LOCKED` plan, considering that plan's shift blocks other than the `CLOSED` ones. It persists nothing whatever; it is a pure read. Its answer reports the `event_id`, an `understaffed_slot_count`, an `average_score`, and a list of `position_recommendations`. The `understaffed_slot_count` is the number of the plan's blocks whose `status` is `UNDERSTAFFED_ESCALATED`, and the `average_score` is the sentiment average described above, absent when the event drew no responses at all.

The blocks under consideration are grouped by `position_type_id` and the groups are emitted ordered by the family's `code` ascending. Within a group, `planned_headcount` is the sum of the blocks' `required_headcount`, `worked_headcount` is the number of assignments on those blocks whose `attendance_status` is `COMPLETED`, and `no_show_count` is the number whose `attendance_status` is `NO_SHOW`. The group's `fill_rate_bp` is `worked_headcount` multiplied by 10000, divided by `planned_headcount`, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. A factor and a `direction` follow from three tests taken in order. When `fill_rate_bp` is strictly below 8000 the factor is 1.10 and the direction is `INCREASE`. Otherwise, when the group had no no-shows at all, the whole plan had no escalated blocks at all, and the event's sentiment average exists and stands at 4.5 or better, the factor is 0.95 and the direction is `DECREASE`. Otherwise the factor is 1.00 and the direction is `HOLD`. Because the first test is strict, a group that worked 4 of 5 planned heads lands on exactly 8000 basis points and is *not* uplifted. The group's `recommended_ratio_per_1000` is then the family's `attendance_ratio_per_1000` multiplied by that factor, quantized to two decimal places by rounding half up: an usher ratio of 1.5 trimmed by 0.95 gives 1.425 and is reported as 1.43, a guest-services ratio of 0.35 trimmed the same way gives 0.3325 and is reported as 0.33, while the same two ratios uplifted by 1.10 give 1.65 and 0.39.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is rooted at the service origin with no version or namespace prefix, and every one of them requires the `X-User-Role` header; mutating requests additionally require `X-User-ID`. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer with 201 and the created resource — including the escalation endpoint, which creates a fresh child issue and returns *that* child rather than the parent it escalated. Endpoints that modify, transition or act upon an existing resource answer with 200 and the updated resource, as do all reads and all computed reads. The single deletion in the surface answers with 204 and an empty data object. Every failure answers with the status listed in §5 for the error identifier concerned, and never with more than one error identifier per request.
