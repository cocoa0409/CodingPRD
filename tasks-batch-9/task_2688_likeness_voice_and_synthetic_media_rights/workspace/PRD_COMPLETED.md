# Likeness Rights Clearance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A talent rights and clearance office sits between three parties who do not trust one another by default: a performer (or, after death, that performer's estate) who owns a face and a voice, a studio or advertiser that wants to synthesise new performances from them, and a set of state legislatures that each answer the question "for how long, and under what labelling" differently. Today that office runs on scanned countersignatures, spreadsheets of union minimums, and an inbox where monitoring alerts about unauthorized synthetic content go to die. The consequences of a lost record are asymmetric and severe: an unlabelled synthetic advertisement is a regulatory exposure, a biometric sample retained one day past its statutory ceiling is a privacy violation, and a replica trained from impounded material is the kind of failure that ends a clearance practice.

This document specifies the Likeness Rights Clearance API, the system of record for that office. It carries a consent grant from drafting through countersignature, suspension, reinstatement and revocation; it holds custody of face scans, voice samples and motion capture with a computed deletion obligation attached to each one; it tracks every digital replica built from that material and every generated performance produced by a replica through an approval workflow the talent — never the licensee — controls; it issues usage licenses with disclosure labelling and removal obligations; it scores suspected unauthorized uses and drives them to takedown or legal escalation; and it pays everybody, in integer cents, through an append-only compensation ledger that can be reconstructed from its own entries.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **money and rights are never silently adjusted** — a royalty accrual that stops being correct is reversed by a new, opposite ledger entry rather than edited, and a rights posture is always recomputed from current facts rather than incrementally patched. Second, **time is explicit**: the server runs on a logical clock that begins at a fixed instant and only moves when a test-driving caller moves it, so every retention deadline, review window, distribution window and post-mortem term is evaluated deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming that user's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-ID` is present but blank or whitespace only, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). These two checks precede every other check in the system.

The five roles are `RightsAdministrator`, `TalentRepresentative`, `EstateRepresentative`, `LicenseeProducer` and `ComplianceOfficer`.

A **`RightsAdministrator`** is clearance-office staff. It owns reference data, talent records, consent drafting, custody of captured samples, replica building and legal escalation. When `X-User-Role` is `RightsAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (as `drafted_by_user_id` on a consent grant, `submitted_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on an estate authority, `escalated_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on an infringement case) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

A **`TalentRepresentative`** acts for a living performer: it countersigns consent, approves replicas and generated performances, and may revoke a revocable grant. When `X-User-Role` is `TalentRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Talent`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Talent` compare the resource's talent foreign key to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is `ERR_NOT_TALENT_REPRESENTATIVE` (HTTP 403).

An **`EstateRepresentative`** acts for a deceased performer's estate and exercises exactly the approval and revocation rights a living performer's representative would. When `X-User-Role` is `EstateRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `EstateAuthority`; ownership checks require that authority to be `VERIFIED` and to name, through its own `talent_id`, the talent behind the target resource. A mismatch — no such authority, or one that is not `VERIFIED`, or one belonging to a different talent — is `ERR_NOT_ESTATE_REPRESENTATIVE` (HTTP 403).

A **`LicenseeProducer`** is the advertiser or studio: it submits and resubmits generated performances, issues usage licenses, attests disclosures, begins distribution and confirms removals. When `X-User-Role` is `LicenseeProducer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `licensee_user_id` on the generated performance that the producer created, copies that value onto every license issued from it, and records the identifier of whichever producer actually called the issuance as that license's `issued_by_user_id`; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to that recorded `licensee_user_id` rather than to any entity's `id`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_LICENSEE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

A **`ComplianceOfficer`** monitors for unauthorized use, verifies synthetic-content disclosure labelling, and triages infringement cases. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `triaged_by_user_id`, `closed_by_user_id` or `disclosure_verified_by_user_id` where the workflow calls for it, but never compares it to an entity's `id`.

Two duties in this office are deliberately segregated, and both are enforced by comparing recorded operator identifiers. The `RightsAdministrator` who verifies an estate authority must not be the same user recorded as that authority's `submitted_by_user_id`, and the `RightsAdministrator` who escalates an infringement case to legal must not be the same user recorded as that case's `triaged_by_user_id`. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, because it records when the request was served rather than the business instant the request was evaluated against; it is the only instant anywhere in a response that does not come from the logical clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- This same envelope also answers the failures that never reach a declared endpoint, always under `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` but not always under that identifier's catalogued HTTP 400: a request for a path the API does not define is answered with HTTP 404, a request using a method the path does not support is answered with HTTP 405, a body that is not parseable JSON is answered with HTTP 400, and an unexpected server-side failure is answered with HTTP 500. A trailing slash is never significant — `/talents` and `/talents/` name the same endpoint.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent, so the default applies; a value that is not an exact decimal integer literal — a fractional form such as `10.0`, a signed or zero-padded form such as `+10` or `010`, or any non-numeric text — is malformed and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` just as an out-of-range value does.

Unless a specific collection says otherwise, lists are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three collections order differently and do so deliberately: state law profiles are returned newest first, by `created_at` descending and then by `id` ascending; union minimum rows are returned by `effective_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, so that a reader sees the schedule history in the order it took effect; and compensation ledger entries are returned by `posted_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, so the ledger reads as the sequence of movements that actually occurred. Because the logical clock stands still until a caller moves it, rows routinely share a `created_at`; every one of these orderings therefore breaks any tie that remains after its stated keys by the sequence in which the server created the rows, the earlier-created row sorting first. The state-law-profile ordering is the one that never needs that fallback, since its `created_at` tie is already broken by `id` ascending as stated.

Collections also accept equality filters, and each filter narrows the result set before pagination. A filter is compared as text against the stored field's own rendered value, and a filter supplied as an empty string is ignored rather than matched against an empty value; a filter naming a value no row carries is not an error and simply yields an empty page. State law profiles filter on `state_code`. Union minimum rows filter on `union_code` and `media_type`. Talents filter on `domicile_state_code`, `life_status` and `rights_posture`. A talent's estate authority claims filter on `status`. Consent grants filter on `talent_id`, `estate_authority_id`, `status` and `exclusivity`. Biometric assets filter on `talent_id`, `consent_grant_id`, `status` and `asset_kind`. Digital replicas filter on `talent_id`, `consent_grant_id`, `status` and `replica_kind`. Generated performances filter on `digital_replica_id`, `licensee_user_id`, `review_status` and `media_type`. Usage licenses filter on `generated_performance_id`, `licensee_user_id`, `distribution_status`, `compliance_status` and `channel`. Compensation entries filter on `talent_id`, `consent_grant_id`, `generated_performance_id`, `usage_license_id`, `reverses_entry_id` and `entry_type`. Infringement cases filter on `talent_id`, `matched_usage_license_id`, `status` and `detection_source`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`, which is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix and no client-supplied component. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at creation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, of the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row — including mutations the row suffers as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource. The counter advances at most once per request: a row that one request mutates more than once, such as a talent whose posture two separate hops of one cascade recompute, still advances its `version` by exactly one for that request, though its `updated_at` is refreshed by each touch. `Talent`, `EstateAuthority`, `ConsentGrant`, `BiometricAsset`, `DigitalReplica`, `GeneratedPerformance`, `UsageLicense` and `InfringementCase` all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

Those two stamps, together with the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`, are the only instants the API writes with a fractional part. Every other ISO 8601 instant returned inside `data` — every logical-clock stamp on a resource, every computed deadline or expiry, every client-supplied instant echoed back, and the `current_utc` and `run_at` an operation report carries — is written at whole-second resolution with no fractional digits at all, exactly as this document writes such instants (for example `2027-08-16T00:00:00Z`). Only an instant that itself carries a non-zero sub-second component, which nothing but driving the logical clock to such an instant can produce, is written with three fractional digits instead, truncated to whole milliseconds.

Three record types are immutable once written and therefore carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`; both fields are absent from their representations entirely. A `StateLawProfile` is a statement of one state's law and is never edited. A `UnionMinimumSchedule` row is a statement of one union's floor as of one effective date and is never edited — a change in the floor is a new row with a later `effective_from`. A `CompensationEntry` is a ledger movement: once appended it is never modified, and a movement that stops being correct is undone by appending an opposing entry. These three carry only `id` and `created_at` alongside their own fields.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Whole-Day Arithmetic

The server evaluates every deadline, window and expiry against a **logical clock**, not against wall-clock time. Before any caller moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that is the fixed startup default, and the server never falls back to real time. A test-driving capability accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and sets the clock to it, returning the new value as `current_utc` and nothing else in `data`; the instant is unconstrained in direction, so the clock may be wound backwards to an instant earlier than its current reading exactly as freely as it is advanced, and it mutates no entity. Both a `RightsAdministrator` and a `ComplianceOfficer` may drive the clock.

Several computations work in whole days rather than instants: the statutory biometric retention ceiling, the deletion grace period, the removal window, the purge execution grace period and the review window are all expressed in days and are added to a base instant by advancing that instant by the stated number of whole days. Post-mortem terms are expressed in whole years and are added by advancing to the same month and day in the later year, with 29 February collapsing to 28 February in a non-leap year. Post-mortem expiry instants are stored at whole-day resolution, as an ISO 8601 UTC instant at `00:00:00Z`. Every client-supplied instant is read as ISO 8601 and normalised to UTC: an explicit offset is honoured and converted, a value carrying no offset at all is read as already being UTC rather than as local time, and a date-only value such as `2027-03-15` is read as that date at `00:00:00Z`.

Boundary inclusivity is specified per rule and is never approximated. A consent term is half-open: it runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_until` exclusive. A distribution window is closed: it runs from `distribution_start` through `distribution_end`, both inclusive. Retention scheduling is inclusive — an asset whose retention deadline is exactly the current logical instant is scheduled for purge. Removal is judged in the licensee's favour: a removal confirmed exactly at the deadline is compliant, and non-compliance is stamped only once the deadline is strictly earlier than the current logical instant.

### 2.6. Money, Shares and the Append-Only Compensation Ledger

All money in this system is held in integer cents, and every monetary field must be sent as a JSON number — a monetary amount sent as a string is malformed and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Wherever a monetary result is derived from multiplication, the multiplicative chain is composed first and rounded exactly once, half up, to a whole number of cents; no intermediate factor is rounded on its own. The single exception is the clearance office's compliance surcharge, which is rounded up to the next whole cent rather than half up, in the compliance fund's favour. Percentage fields — the share percentages on a consent grant and a monitoring vendor's match confidence — must lie within 0 through 1 inclusive as their individual rules state, and are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when they do not; a percentage carrying more decimal places than its own rule allows is rejected the same way. Percentages are echoed back as JSON numbers holding their arithmetic value rather than as fixed-decimal strings, so a share of 0.20 is rendered as `0.2`.

The **compensation ledger** is a single append-only sequence of `CompensationEntry` rows, and it is the only authoritative record of what the office owes. Each entry names the talent it belongs to through `talent_id` and the grant the money moves under through `consent_grant_id`; both are copied from the governing grant rather than supplied by a caller, so the ledger is filterable by talent without a join. An entry raised against a submitted output additionally names that output through `generated_performance_id`; an entry raised at license issuance names the license through `usage_license_id`; and an entry that undoes an earlier entry names that earlier entry through `reverses_entry_id`. Every entry carries an `entry_type`, one of `INITIAL_CREATION`, `UNION_TOPUP`, `PER_USE_PROVISIONAL`, `PER_USE_FINAL` or `REVERSAL`; a signed `amount_cents`, which is negative only on a `REVERSAL`; a machine-generated `memo` explaining why the entry was posted; and `posted_at`, the logical instant at which it was appended, which is always the very same instant as that entry's own `created_at`.

Every entry is split three ways at the moment it is appended. The talent's slice `talent_share_cents` is the entry amount multiplied by the grant's `talent_share_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. The agency's slice `agency_share_cents` is the entry amount multiplied by `agency_share_pct`, rounded half up independently. The office's slice `administrator_share_cents` is the entry amount multiplied by `administrator_share_pct`, rounded half up independently. Because each slice is rounded on its own, the three may not sum to the entry amount; the difference between the entry amount and the sum of the three rounded slices is then added to the talent's slice. That remainder may be negative, and it is never dropped — the talent is the designated beneficiary of the rounding remainder, so the three stored slices always reconstruct `amount_cents` exactly. Consider an entry of 470250 cents split 0.75 / 0.20 / 0.05: the unrounded slices are 352687.5, 94050 and 23512.5, which round to 352688, 94050 and 23513 and sum to 470251; the remainder of negative one cent moves the talent's slice to 352687, and the stored slices are 352687, 94050 and 23513.

A `REVERSAL` entry is never re-derived from the percentages. Its `amount_cents` is the exact negation of the reversed entry's amount, and its three share fields are the exact negations of that entry's three share fields. Reversing an entry of 315000 cents whose slices were 236250, 63000 and 15750 therefore appends an entry of −315000 with slices −236250, −63000 and −15750, so the pair sums to zero in every column. A reversal is written only when there is a live accrual to undo: a performance whose `provisional_compensation_cents` is already zero, or whose provisional entry has already been reversed once, simply has that field cleared to zero and no `REVERSAL` row is appended at all.

Two roll-ups follow the ledger and must equal it at all times. A consent grant's `total_compensation_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every entry whose `consent_grant_id` is that grant, reversals included. A talent's `lifetime_compensation_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every entry whose `talent_id` is that talent, reversals included. Both are recomputed whenever an entry is appended, including entries appended deep inside a cascade. Neither roll-up refresh is by itself a mutation of the row that carries it: appending an entry moves the grant's `total_compensation_cents` and the talent's `lifetime_compensation_cents` in place without advancing either row's `version` or `updated_at`, and those two rows advance their counters only when the same operation independently mutates them — which is why a sweep whose only monetary effect is reversing a lapsed accrual leaves the grant's and the talent's `version` exactly where they were. Ledger entries have no write endpoint of their own; they exist only as consequences of workflow operations, and the collection read is the only channel through which a cascade-created reversal can be discovered.

### 2.7. State Law Profiles and Union Minimum Schedules

Two reference tables supply the external law this office must obey, and both are registered by a `RightsAdministrator` before any talent work can begin.

A `StateLawProfile` states one jurisdiction's rules. Its `state_code` is exactly two uppercase letters and is unique across profiles — a second registration for a `state_code` that already exists is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `postmortem_rights_years` is a whole number from 0 through 100 giving the years of post-mortem likeness rights the state recognises, where 0 means the state recognises none at all. `postmortem_requires_registration` is a boolean: when true, post-mortem rights are unenforceable in that state until the estate supplies a registration number. `biometric_retention_max_days` is the statutory ceiling, from 1 through 3650 days, on retaining a captured biometric or voice payload measured from its capture. `deletion_grace_days`, from 0 through 365, is how long after a consent's effective end the payload may still be held before deletion is due. `removal_window_days`, from 1 through 90, is how long a licensee gets to pull expired synthetic content out of distribution. `disclosure_required_advertising` and `disclosure_required_entertainment` state whether synthetic-content disclosure is mandatory in that state for each of the two distribution channels. Finally `min_disclosure_rank_advertising` and `min_disclosure_rank_entertainment`, each from 1 through 4, give the minimum acceptable disclosure placement for each channel on a fixed four-rung ladder: `METADATA_ONLY` is rank 1, `END_CARD` is rank 2, `PRE_ROLL` is rank 3 and `PERSISTENT_BUG` is rank 4, so a higher rank is a more prominent disclosure.

Profiles are matched to work by state code, not by foreign key: a talent's `domicile_state_code` selects the profile whose `state_code` equals it, and an infringement case's `detected_state_code` selects the profile for the state where a suspected use was observed. Whenever an operation needs a profile that does not exist, it fails with `ERR_STATE_LAW_PROFILE_MISSING` (HTTP 422). That applies to creating a talent, recording a death, drafting a consent grant, registering a biometric asset, issuing a usage license, attesting a disclosure, and opening an infringement case — for a case, both the talent's domicile profile and the detected state's profile must exist. It answers a direct read as well: a read of a state law profile by an `id` no profile carries is reported as `ERR_STATE_LAW_PROFILE_MISSING` (HTTP 422) rather than as a 404 existence failure, and a read of a union minimum row by an unknown `id` is likewise reported as `ERR_UNION_SCHEDULE_MISSING` (HTTP 422), which is why neither reference table appears on the existence rung of §2.10 at all.

A `UnionMinimumSchedule` row states statutory floors for one `union_code` — either `SAG_AFTRA` or `AFM` — for one `media_type` drawn from `ADVERTISING`, `FEATURE_FILM`, `EPISODIC`, `VIDEO_GAME` and `SOCIAL`, effective from one instant `effective_from`. It carries `minimum_initial_cents`, the floor for the initial digital-replica creation payment, and `minimum_per_use_cents`, the floor for each subsequent use payment; both are whole numbers of cents and neither may be negative.

Selecting the governing row is deterministic and is used identically by both compensation calculations. Candidates are the rows whose `union_code` equals the grant's pinned union affiliation and whose `media_type` equals the media type the calculation is pricing. Among those candidates, only rows whose `effective_from` is at or before the reference date qualify, and the qualifying row with the greatest `effective_from` wins; should two qualifying rows share that same `effective_from`, the one with the earlier `created_at` wins, and should they share `created_at` as well — routine under a stationary logical clock — the one registered first wins. The reference date is the grant's `effective_from` for the initial creation payment and for per-use pricing alike. When the grant's pinned union affiliation is absent, no lookup happens and the applicable floor is zero. When the grant does carry a union affiliation and no candidate row qualifies, the operation fails with `ERR_UNION_SCHEDULE_MISSING` (HTTP 422) — this applies to drafting a grant, countersigning it, submitting a generated performance, and issuing a usage license.

### 2.8. Stale-Read Protection at the Two Version Gates

Two writes in this system bind terms that depend on facts the caller read earlier, and in both cases a sequential retry would find nothing obviously wrong and would succeed while quietly using the wrong facts. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent row's `version`.

A `POST /consent-grants` request must carry `expected_talent_version` as a non-optional field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Talent` named by `talent_id`. If that value does not equal the talent's current `version`, the draft is rejected with `ERR_TALENT_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). This matters because a second grant covering the same media type and territory is legitimate product behaviour — a non-exclusive grant may sit alongside an earlier one — so a drafter working from a stale view sees no blocking status field at all. Meanwhile an exclusivity change, a suspension or a death record recorded since that read silently rewrites the terms the drafter believed were in force. Only the advanced `version` reveals the stale read.

A `POST /usage-licenses` request must carry `expected_replica_version` as a non-optional field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `DigitalReplica` behind the performance being licensed. If that value does not equal the replica's current `version`, issuance is rejected with `ERR_REPLICA_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). Issuing a second license over the same replica and an overlapping territory is legitimate up to the declared overlap cap, so a sequential retry would still find the replica `APPROVED_FOR_USE` and would itself succeed; only the advanced `version` reveals that the replica's approval, its source assets or its open license count moved under the licensee's feet.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none may be added. Reading a talent or a replica is how a client obtains the value it must echo back — both single-resource reads expose `version` for exactly this purpose.

### 2.9. The Rights Posture Gate

Each talent carries a derived field `rights_posture`, one of `CLEAR`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is never set by a request. It is recomputed from scratch — never incrementally patched — after every write that changes an infringement case's status, changes a consent grant's status, or changes the talent's `open_removal_obligation_count`, and that includes every hop of every cascade and every phase of the compliance sweep.

The recomputation examines all infringement cases and all consent grants belonging to the talent and applies three clauses in order, the first match winning. If any infringement case of this talent has status `LEGAL_ESCALATED`, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if the talent's `open_removal_obligation_count` is greater than zero, or if any consent grant of this talent has status `SUSPENDED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `CLEAR`. Terminal case statuses — `DISMISSED_LICENSED`, `DISMISSED_NO_ACTION`, `RESOLVED_REMOVED`, `CLOSED_SETTLED` and `CLOSED_LITIGATED` — contribute nothing, and neither do the terminal grant statuses `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`. A talent with no cases, one `ACTIVE` grant and no open removal obligations is `CLEAR`; a talent with one `SUSPENDED` grant is `RESTRICTED`; a talent with one `LEGAL_ESCALATED` case and two open removal obligations is `FROZEN`, because the escalation clause is evaluated first; and when that escalated case is closed as `CLOSED_SETTLED` while two removal obligations are still open, the posture becomes `RESTRICTED` rather than `CLEAR`.

Two counters feed the posture and are themselves derived. `open_removal_obligation_count` is the number of this talent's usage licenses whose `distribution_status` is `REMOVAL_PENDING`, and it never goes below zero. `legal_hold_asset_count` is the number of this talent's biometric assets whose `status` is `LEGAL_HOLD`, and it likewise never goes below zero. The second counter does not enter the posture formula; it exists so that an operator can see at a glance how much material is impounded.

A `FROZEN` posture blocks seven operations, each rejected with `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): drafting a consent grant, countersigning one, approving a replica for use, submitting a generated performance, recording a review decision on one, issuing a usage license, and beginning distribution. Building a replica is deliberately **not** in that list: the operative safety signal there is the legal-hold state of the specific source assets, which is checked by a higher-priority rule of its own. A `RESTRICTED` posture blocks exactly two operations — issuing a usage license and beginning distribution, the two acts that put new synthetic content into the world — each rejected with `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fixed by a global precedence ladder rather than by the order the implementation happens to evaluate things. The ladder, from first-evaluated to last, is as follows.

Authentication comes first: the header check that yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, then the role-authorization check that yields `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Payload well-formedness comes next, yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — every required field present, every enum value inside its declared set, integers not negative, monetary amounts sent as JSON numbers rather than strings, ISO 8601 datetimes parseable, percentage fields within 0 through 1, required arrays non-empty, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` not negative. A field sent as an explicit `null` is treated exactly as an omitted field: a required field sent as `null` is reported here as missing, and an optional field sent as `null` takes its documented default rather than being stored as null. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so a bodyless write simply reports its first missing required field; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object, and a body that is not parseable JSON at all, are themselves rejected on this rung. An integer field may be sent as a JSON number whose fractional part is zero, but never as a string and never with a non-zero fractional part; a monetary or percentage field sent as a string is malformed even when the string holds a valid number. One array escapes this rung: on a replica build, `source_asset_ids` holding more than ten members is malformed here, but an empty list and duplicate members are deliberately deferred to the referential rung's `ERR_NO_SOURCE_ASSETS`, and only a member that is not a non-blank string is caught here. This rung applies to every endpoint that accepts a body or query parameters.

Existence checks follow, in this internal order: `ERR_TALENT_NOT_FOUND` (a talent id from the path or body, as used by the talent read, the talent's estate-authority list and submission, the death record, and consent drafting); `ERR_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_NOT_FOUND` (estate-authority verification, and the `estate_authority_id` supplied on a consent draft); `ERR_CONSENT_GRANT_NOT_FOUND` (the grant read, countersign, suspension and revoke, plus the `consent_grant_id` supplied when registering a biometric asset or building a replica); `ERR_BIOMETRIC_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` (the asset read, the manual purge, and every member of `source_asset_ids` on a replica build); `ERR_DIGITAL_REPLICA_NOT_FOUND` (the replica read, approval for use, and the `digital_replica_id` on a performance submission); `ERR_GENERATED_PERFORMANCE_NOT_FOUND` (the performance read, review, resubmission, and the `generated_performance_id` on a license issuance); `ERR_USAGE_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND` (the license read, disclosure attestation, disclosure verification, go-live, removal confirmation, and the `matched_usage_license_id` optionally supplied when opening a case); `ERR_INFRINGEMENT_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (the case read, triage, escalation and closure); and `ERR_COMPENSATION_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` (the ledger entry read). Each of these rungs governs wherever an identifier of that type is accepted, and all of them carry HTTP 404.

Reference-data availability is next: `ERR_STATE_LAW_PROFILE_MISSING`, then `ERR_UNION_SCHEDULE_MISSING`, both HTTP 422, with the endpoint sets given in §2.7.

Ownership and duty separation follow, in this order: `ERR_NOT_TALENT_REPRESENTATIVE`, then `ERR_NOT_ESTATE_REPRESENTATIVE` (both applying to countersign, revoke, replica approval and performance review), then `ERR_NOT_LICENSEE_OWNER` (applying to resubmission, license issuance, disclosure attestation, go-live and removal confirmation), then `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (applying to estate-authority verification and case escalation).

The two optimistic-concurrency rungs come after ownership and before any state check: `ERR_TALENT_VERSION_STALE` on consent drafting, then `ERR_REPLICA_VERSION_STALE` on license issuance. A stale version is therefore reported ahead of a wrong-state or out-of-scope complaint on the same request, and behind a not-found or an ownership failure.

State-machine admissibility follows, in this internal order: `ERR_INVALID_TALENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSET_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REPLICA_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` — all HTTP 409. Then the posture gates in order: `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_FROZEN`, then `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_RESTRICTED`, then the deceased-talent gate `ERR_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_UNVERIFIED`, which requires that a talent whose `life_status` is `DECEASED` have at least one `VERIFIED` estate authority before any consent, approval or license activity proceeds (that gate applies to consent drafting, countersign, replica approval and performance review).

Safety rules come next, in order: `ERR_REPLICA_SOURCE_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD`, `ERR_ASSET_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD`, `ERR_ASSET_BOUND_TO_ACTIVE_REPLICA`, `ERR_POSTMORTEM_REGISTRATION_MISSING`, `ERR_POSTMORTEM_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_PLACEMENT_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_VERIFIED`, `ERR_TAKEDOWN_MANDATORY`. Then referential scope: `ERR_MEDIA_TYPE_OUT_OF_SCOPE`, `ERR_TERRITORY_OUT_OF_SCOPE`, `ERR_ASSET_CONSENT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_NO_SOURCE_ASSETS`. Then temporal validity: `ERR_CONSENT_TERM_INVALID`, `ERR_WINDOW_EXCEEDS_CONSENT_TERM`, `ERR_OUTSIDE_DISTRIBUTION_WINDOW`. Then the remaining business constraints: `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_REPLICA_OVERLAP_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_REVOCABLE`, `ERR_SEVERITY_BELOW_ESCALATION_FLOOR`. Last of all sits the fiscal rung `ERR_COMPENSATION_BELOW_STATUTORY_FLOOR`, ranked bottom on purpose: a frozen talent, a scope breach or a defective term is always reported ahead of a money defect on the same draft.

Two rungs carve out an exception in favour of a more informative error. When a manual purge targets a biometric asset whose `status` is `LEGAL_HOLD`, the asset-state rung deliberately admits that status and lets the safety rung report `ERR_ASSET_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD` instead of `ERR_INVALID_ASSET_STATE`, so the requester learns the material is impounded rather than merely in an unexpected state; the asset-state rung itself refuses only an already-`PURGED` asset. Likewise, when a resubmission targets a performance already `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` of `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, the performance-state rung defers and the constraint rung reports `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` instead of `ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_STATE`, so the licensee learns why the door is shut.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Cascade Mechanics

Every business resource in this system is its own top-level collection joined to its neighbours by identifiers carried in request bodies; only the estate-authority sub-collection of a talent is addressed through a nested path. Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right: the talent segment is resolved first, and if it names no talent the request fails with `ERR_TALENT_NOT_FOUND` before the sub-collection is consulted or a body-level identifier is examined.

Several operations mutate many rows at once. Three mechanical rules govern all of them. First, hops execute in the stated order, and each hop's input set is the set of rows the previous hop actually changed — a hop that changes nothing stops the chain beneath it. Second, within a hop, candidate rows are processed by `created_at` ascending, so ledger entries appended by a cascade appear in a deterministic sequence. Third, every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly one, including rows the caller never named — a talent whose posture was recomputed, a replica quarantined three hops down, and a license whose removal obligation was opened all advance their own `version`. A recomputation that finds nothing to change is not a touch, however: a talent whose counters, roll-up and posture all come back identical to what they already were, and a replica whose recomputed `open_license_count` is identical, keep their `version` and `updated_at` exactly as they stood. This is what makes the two version gates in §2.8 meaningful: a cascade the client did not initiate is visible to it as an advanced parent version. Requests are also serialised against one another, so a cascade is never observed half-applied: a concurrent reader sees either every hop of it or none of them, and the version bumps a cascade produces become visible together with the response that caused them.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Talent Records, Death and Estate Administration

A talent record is the root of everything else. A `RightsAdministrator` creates one by supplying the performer's `legal_name` (1 to 200 characters, not blank), the `domicile_state_code` of legal domicile (exactly two uppercase letters), and optionally a `union_code`, which is `NONE`, `SAG_AFTRA` or `AFM` and defaults to `NONE`. Creation requires a registered law profile for that domicile state; without one the create is refused. At creation `life_status` is always `LIVING` — a caller cannot open a record already deceased — `date_of_death` and `postmortem_rights_until` are null, `postmortem_registration_number` is null, `rights_posture` is `CLEAR`, and `open_removal_obligation_count`, `legal_hold_asset_count` and `lifetime_compensation_cents` are all zero. Any of these derived values supplied in the payload are ignored rather than honoured.

Recording a death is the event that opens post-mortem administration, and it drives the talent from `LIVING` to `DECEASED`. Only a `RightsAdministrator` may record it, and the payload carries a single field, `date_of_death`, which must be at or before the current logical instant; an instant later than that is malformed rather than a state problem, drawing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` on the payload rung and therefore ahead of any check on the talent itself, including whether the talent exists. The supplied instant is stored exactly as given, time of day and all, even though the post-mortem expiry derived from it is collapsed to whole-day resolution. The record is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TALENT_STATE` if the talent is already `DECEASED`, and with `ERR_STATE_LAW_PROFILE_MISSING` if no law profile exists for the talent's `domicile_state_code` — the domicile is frozen at death precisely because the answer to "how long do the rights last" must stop moving.

The **post-mortem expiry** computation runs at that moment against the domicile profile. If that profile's `postmortem_rights_years` is zero, the state recognises no post-mortem right at all and `postmortem_rights_until` is null. Otherwise `postmortem_rights_until` is the date of death advanced by that number of whole years, landing on the same month and day, stored as an instant at `00:00:00Z`. A talent who died on 15 March 2020 domiciled in a state recognising seventy years therefore has `postmortem_rights_until` of `2090-03-15T00:00:00Z`.

Recording the death then unrolls a four-hop cascade. The first hop stamps `date_of_death`, sets `life_status` to `DECEASED` and computes `postmortem_rights_until` as above. Because of that, the second hop moves every consent grant of this talent whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `grantor_kind` is `TALENT` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, stamping `suspension_reason` as `DEATH_PENDING_ESTATE` — the living person who executed those grants can no longer stand behind them. Because those grants are now suspended, the third hop moves every digital replica whose governing grant is in that set and whose `status` is `BUILT` or `APPROVED_FOR_USE` to `QUARANTINED`, recording `quarantine_reason` as `ESTATE_PENDING` and `held_from_status` as whatever status the replica held immediately before the hop. Because at least one grant is now suspended, the fourth hop recomputes the talent's `rights_posture`, which becomes `RESTRICTED` unless a `LEGAL_ESCALATED` case already forces `FROZEN`. The talent's `version` advances by one, and so does the `version` of every grant and replica the cascade touched. Grants executed by an estate rather than by the living talent are left alone, and no biometric asset is disturbed by a death record.

Administration then passes to the estate. A `RightsAdministrator` files an estate authority claim against the talent, supplying the `executor_name` named in the probate documentation (1 to 200 characters, not blank), a `court_document_ref` pointing at the filed letters testamentary or court order (1 to 120 characters, not blank), and optionally a `registration_number`, the state post-mortem rights registration number (1 to 120 characters, not blank when supplied), which is null when the estate has not registered. A claim may be filed against a talent in either `life_status`: the office routinely lodges the paperwork alongside the death record rather than strictly after it, so no talent-state gate guards this submission and a claim against a `LIVING` talent is accepted. The claim is created with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, and the filer's identifier is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` for later duty-separation enforcement. Claims for a talent are listed in `created_at` ascending order and are filterable by `status`, and a single claim can be read on its own.

Verification is a second `RightsAdministrator`'s act. It is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_STATE` unless the claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, so an already `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` claim cannot be verified again, and it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the verifying user is the same user recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`. On success the claim's `status` becomes `VERIFIED`, `verified_by_user_id` records the verifier and `verified_at` records the logical instant. The talent's `postmortem_registration_number` is then copied from this authority's `registration_number`, so a state that demands registration can be satisfied from the estate paperwork. That copy is unconditional: an authority verified while carrying a null `registration_number` writes null over whatever number an earlier verification had recorded on the talent. Verification also releases what the death record suspended: every consent grant of this talent sitting in `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `DEATH_PENDING_ESTATE` returns to `ACTIVE` with its reason cleared, and every digital replica quarantined with `quarantine_reason` of `ESTATE_PENDING` returns to exactly its `held_from_status`, with both `quarantine_reason` and `held_from_status` cleared, rows processed in `created_at` ascending order. The talent's `rights_posture` is recomputed, and the authority's `version` and the talent's `version` each advance by one. The `status` value `REJECTED` exists on an estate authority to record a claim the office refused to accept.

### 3.2. Drafting a Consent Grant

A consent grant is the document that authorises a digital replica to exist at all, and it is drafted by a `RightsAdministrator` on the talent's behalf before anyone signs anything. The draft names the talent through `talent_id` and declares `grantor_kind` — `TALENT` when the living performer will execute the grant, `ESTATE` when a verified estate will. When `grantor_kind` is `ESTATE`, `estate_authority_id` must name the estate authority that will execute it; when `grantor_kind` is `TALENT`, `estate_authority_id` must be null. Either breach — an `ESTATE` draft that supplies no `estate_authority_id`, or a `TALENT` draft that supplies one — is malformed and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Beyond having to exist, the named authority is otherwise unconstrained at draft time: it need not be `VERIFIED` and need not even belong to this talent, and the talent's own `life_status` does not restrict which `grantor_kind` may be drafted. Every one of those conditions is settled at countersignature instead, which is where the wrong authority or the wrong side of the table is finally refused.

Scope is stated explicitly rather than left to interpretation. `permitted_media_types` is a list of 1 to 5 distinct members drawn from `ADVERTISING`, `FEATURE_FILM`, `EPISODIC`, `VIDEO_GAME` and `SOCIAL`, and every generated performance's media type must later be a member of it. `territory_codes` is a list of 1 to 20 distinct two-letter uppercase territory codes, and every performance and every license territory must later be a member of it. `permitted_use_notes` carries the negotiated specificity clause in 1 to 500 characters of non-blank free text. `exclusivity` is either `EXCLUSIVE` or `NON_EXCLUSIVE`. The term runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_until` exclusive; `effective_until` must be strictly after `effective_from`, and the term must span at least thirty days, or the draft is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_TERM_INVALID`.

Money is stated twice. `initial_creation_fee_cents` is the negotiated fee for building the replica, before any multiplier or union floor, and `per_use_base_fee_cents` is the negotiated base fee for each subsequent use, on the same "before multipliers" basis. Neither may be negative. The three-way split is declared here as well: `talent_share_pct` defaults to 0.75 and must be greater than zero and at most 1 with at most four decimals; `agency_share_pct` defaults to 0.20 and must be at least zero and strictly below 1 with at most four decimals; and the two together may not exceed 1. The residual, `administrator_share_pct`, is computed as one minus `talent_share_pct` minus `agency_share_pct` and is retained by the clearance office. Two booleans complete the draft: `approval_required`, defaulting to true, meaning every generated performance needs an explicit approval decision from the talent or estate representative; and `revocable`, defaulting to true, meaning the grant may later be revoked.

The draft also pins the union affiliation. `union_code_snapshot` is copied from the talent's `union_code` at draft time — null when the talent's affiliation is `NONE` — so that a later change of affiliation cannot retroactively move the floors that govern this grant. Drafting stamps `status` as `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`, records the drafting administrator as `drafted_by_user_id`, leaves `suspension_reason`, `countersigned_at` and `effective_end_actual` null, and initialises `initial_compensation_cents`, `union_topup_cents` and `total_compensation_cents` to zero. The talent's `version` advances by one, which is why a client that drafts two grants in sequence must re-read the talent between them.

Four substantive checks guard the draft beyond the scope and term rules. A talent whose posture is `FROZEN` cannot be the subject of a new grant. A talent whose `life_status` is `DECEASED` must already have a `VERIFIED` estate authority, or the draft is refused with `ERR_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_UNVERIFIED`. For a deceased talent whose domicile profile sets `postmortem_requires_registration` to true, the talent's `postmortem_registration_number` must be non-null, or the draft is refused with `ERR_POSTMORTEM_REGISTRATION_MISSING`; and the domicile-law post-mortem term must still be live — `postmortem_rights_until` must be non-null and at or after the current logical instant — or the draft is refused with `ERR_POSTMORTEM_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`. A state recognising no post-mortem right at all therefore fails this same check, because a null expiry is treated as "nothing to grant".

**Exclusivity conflicts** are evaluated against the talent's other grants. Take the draft's half-open term from `effective_from` to `effective_until` and compare it with the term of every other grant of the same talent that is not in a terminal status — that is, every grant that is not `REVOKED` and not `EXPIRED`. Two grants conflict when their half-open terms overlap and they share at least one member of `permitted_media_types` and at least one member of `territory_codes`. An `EXCLUSIVE` draft may not conflict with any such grant, and a `NON_EXCLUSIVE` draft may not conflict with an existing `EXCLUSIVE` one. Two `NON_EXCLUSIVE` grants may overlap freely. Adjacency is not overlap: one term ending exactly where the next begins is permitted, because the first term excludes its own end instant. A conflict is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The last check on a draft is fiscal, and it is deliberately the last thing reported. When the grant carries a union affiliation, the governing union row is selected as described in §2.7 using the grant's `effective_from` as the reference date and the grant's governing media type, and `per_use_base_fee_cents` must be at least half of that row's `minimum_per_use_cents`. The top-up mechanism absorbs a modest shortfall against the floor, but a negotiated rate below half the floor is not a good-faith rate at all, and the draft is refused with `ERR_COMPENSATION_BELOW_STATUTORY_FLOOR`.

### 3.3. Countersignature and the Initial Creation Payment

Countersignature is the talent's own act — or, post-mortem, the verified estate's — and it takes the grant from `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` to `ACTIVE`. A `TalentRepresentative` may countersign only a grant whose `grantor_kind` is `TALENT`, only while the talent's `life_status` is `LIVING`, and only when its own identifier equals the grant's `talent_id`. An `EstateRepresentative` may countersign only a grant whose `grantor_kind` is `ESTATE`, only when the estate authority named by `estate_authority_id` has `status` of `VERIFIED` and names this grant's talent through its own `talent_id`, and only when its own identifier equals that `estate_authority_id`. Every one of those conditions is an ownership condition settled on the ownership rung, not a state condition: a `TalentRepresentative` that breaches any of its three draws `ERR_NOT_TALENT_REPRESENTATIVE`, and an `EstateRepresentative` that breaches any of its three draws `ERR_NOT_ESTATE_REPRESENTATIVE`. An estate-executed grant offered to the talent side, a talent-executed grant offered to the estate side, and a talent-executed grant whose talent has since died are therefore all reported as ownership failures rather than as `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_TALENT_STATE`. A grant not in `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` draws `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE`; a talent whose posture is `FROZEN` draws `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_FROZEN`; a deceased talent with no `VERIFIED` estate authority draws `ERR_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_UNVERIFIED`; a state that requires registration where the talent has none draws `ERR_POSTMORTEM_REGISTRATION_MISSING`; and a missing union row draws `ERR_UNION_SCHEDULE_MISSING`. On success `countersigned_at` records the logical instant and `status` becomes `ACTIVE`.

Countersignature is also the moment the office owes the talent money, and the **initial creation compensation** is computed then, before any ledger entry is appended. The computation has three steps and its order is observable.

Step one fixes the governing media type deterministically. Each media type has a distinct floor rank: `ADVERTISING` ranks 5, `FEATURE_FILM` ranks 4, `EPISODIC` ranks 3, `VIDEO_GAME` ranks 2 and `SOCIAL` ranks 1. The governing media type is the member of `permitted_media_types` with the greatest rank, and because the ranks are distinct no tie is possible.

Step two builds a multiplicative chain, evaluated left to right in exactly this order. The **breadth factor** starts at 1.00 and adds 0.15 for each permitted media type beyond the first — one media type gives 1.00, two give 1.15, five give 1.60. The **duration factor** depends on the number of whole calendar months from `effective_from` to `effective_until`, counting only complete months: a term of twelve months or fewer gives 1.00, a term of more than twelve but at most thirty-six months gives 1.25, and a longer term gives 1.60. The **exclusivity factor** is 1.35 for an `EXCLUSIVE` grant and 1.00 for a `NON_EXCLUSIVE` one. The negotiated `initial_creation_fee_cents` is multiplied by the breadth factor, then by the duration factor, then by the exclusivity factor, and the fully composed product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents exactly once — never factor by factor.

Step three applies the union floor. The floor is the `minimum_initial_cents` of the union row selected for the grant's affiliation, the governing media type and the grant's `effective_from`; when the grant carries no union affiliation the floor is zero and no lookup happens. `initial_compensation_cents` is then whichever is greater, the rounded amount or the floor, and `union_topup_cents` is the amount by which the floor exceeds the rounded amount, or zero when it does not exceed it. A negotiated fee of 300000 cents for a single-media, twelve-month, non-exclusive grant under a floor of 300000 gives `initial_compensation_cents` of 300000 and `union_topup_cents` of zero — equality meets the floor. The same scope at a negotiated 250000 gives `initial_compensation_cents` of 300000 and `union_topup_cents` of 50000. A fee of 300000 across two media types over thirty-six months, exclusive, composes to 300000 multiplied by 1.15, 1.25 and 1.35, which is 582187.5 and rounds to 582188; the floor of 300000 does not bind, so `union_topup_cents` is zero.

The ledger movements follow. One entry of type `INITIAL_CREATION` is appended for the rounded amount, and only when the union floor strictly exceeds that amount is a second entry of type `UNION_TOPUP` appended for the difference. Each entry is split three ways as described in §2.6. The grant's `initial_compensation_cents`, `union_topup_cents` and `total_compensation_cents` are then recomputed, as is the talent's `lifetime_compensation_cents`, and the `version` of both the grant and the talent advances by one.

### 3.4. Suspension, Reinstatement and Revocation

A grant can be paused without being destroyed. A `RightsAdministrator` submits an `action` of either `SUSPEND` or `REINSTATE`, optionally accompanied by a `suspension_reason`. `SUSPEND` requires the grant to be `ACTIVE` and moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, stamping `suspension_reason` with the supplied reason, which defaults to `COMPLIANCE_HOLD` — the only two reasons in the vocabulary are `DEATH_PENDING_ESTATE` and `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`. The talent's `rights_posture` is recomputed and becomes `RESTRICTED` unless a `LEGAL_ESCALATED` case already forces `FROZEN`. A compliance suspension deliberately does **not** quarantine anything: replicas built under the grant keep their status, in pointed contrast with the death-record cascade, which does quarantine them. Both the grant's `version` and the talent's `version` advance by one.

`REINSTATE` requires the grant to be `SUSPENDED` and moves it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, clearing `suspension_reason` to null. When the suspension arose from a death — `suspension_reason` of `DEATH_PENDING_ESTATE` — two extra conditions apply before the grant may come back. The talent must have an estate authority whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, or the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_UNVERIFIED`; and if the talent's domicile profile sets `postmortem_requires_registration` to true then that authority's `registration_number` must be non-null, or the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_POSTMORTEM_REGISTRATION_MISSING`. Where the talent holds more than one `VERIFIED` authority, the one consulted here — as everywhere the system needs "the" verified authority of a talent — is the earliest created of them. The current logical instant must also be at or before the talent's `postmortem_rights_until`, or the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_POSTMORTEM_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`. On success, every digital replica under this grant sitting in `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` of `ESTATE_PENDING` is restored to exactly its `held_from_status`, with `quarantine_reason` and `held_from_status` cleared, rows processed in `created_at` ascending order. The talent's `rights_posture` is recomputed, and the grant's and talent's `version` each advance by one. A suspension request against a grant in any other status — a terminal grant, or an attempt to reinstate one that is already `ACTIVE` — draws `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE`.

**Revocation** is the deepest cascade in the system, and it belongs to the talent's side of the table: a `TalentRepresentative` or an `EstateRepresentative` requests it with a `revocation_note` — mandatory non-blank free text of no bounded length, validated on the request but not retained on the grant record, so it is absent from the grant's representation. It is admissible from `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and refused from a terminal status with `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE`. A grant whose `revocable` flag is false may never be revoked, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_REVOCABLE`. Ownership follows `grantor_kind`: a grant executed by the talent requires the caller's identifier to equal the grant's `talent_id`, and a grant executed by an estate requires it to equal the grant's `estate_authority_id`, with an `EstateRepresentative` additionally required to hold a `VERIFIED` estate authority naming this grant's talent. Which identifier must match is chosen by `grantor_kind`, but which error is reported on a mismatch is chosen by the caller's own role: a `TalentRepresentative` that fails the comparison draws `ERR_NOT_TALENT_REPRESENTATIVE` and an `EstateRepresentative` that fails it draws `ERR_NOT_ESTATE_REPRESENTATIVE`, even when the grant it aimed at belongs to the other side of the table.

Revoking a grant that never left `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` is simple, because nothing was ever built under it: `status` becomes `REVOKED`, `effective_end_actual` is stamped with the current logical instant, the talent's `rights_posture` is recomputed, and the two versions advance.

Revoking an `ACTIVE` or a `SUSPENDED` grant runs a five-hop cascade, and the two starting states behave identically. Hop one sets `status` to `REVOKED` and `effective_end_actual` to the current logical instant, and clears `suspension_reason` back to null so that a grant revoked out of `SUSPENDED` carries no residual reason. Because the grant has ended, hop two does two things: every biometric asset of this grant whose `status` is `STORED` or `PURGE_SCHEDULED` has its `retention_until` recomputed against the new effective end — and, for an asset already in `PURGE_SCHEDULED`, its `purge_due_at` recomputed with it — and every digital replica of this grant whose `status` is `BUILT` or `APPROVED_FOR_USE` moves to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` of `CONSENT_REVOKED` and `held_from_status` set to the status it just left. Assets already `PURGED` are skipped, and assets in `LEGAL_HOLD` have their deadline recomputed but their destruction stays deferred until the hold releases. Because those replicas are quarantined, hop three moves every generated performance under them whose `review_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` to `WITHDRAWN`; for each withdrawn performance carrying a live provisional accrual — a `provisional_compensation_cents` greater than zero — a `REVERSAL` entry is appended for the exact negation of that accrual, naming the live `PER_USE_PROVISIONAL` entry through `reverses_entry_id`, and `provisional_compensation_cents` is set to zero. Because those performances are withdrawn, hop four moves every usage license on them whose `distribution_status` is `ISSUED` or `LIVE` to `REMOVAL_PENDING`, stamping `removal_reason` as `CONSENT_REVOKED` and computing a fresh `removal_deadline`; each affected replica's `open_license_count` is recomputed from scratch as the number of usage licenses on that replica whose `distribution_status` is `ISSUED`, `LIVE` or `REMOVAL_PENDING`, which means opening a removal obligation leaves the count exactly where it was — only a confirmed removal takes a license out of that set. Because each such license now carries a removal obligation, hop five recomputes the talent's `open_removal_obligation_count` and its `rights_posture`, which becomes `RESTRICTED` unless a `LEGAL_ESCALATED` case already forces `FROZEN`. The grant's `total_compensation_cents` and the talent's `lifetime_compensation_cents` are recomputed from the appended reversals, and the `version` of the grant, the talent and every asset, replica, performance and license the cascade touched advances by one. Within each hop, rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

### 3.5. Biometric Custody, Retention Deadlines and Purge

Captured material is registered by a `RightsAdministrator` under an active grant. The payload record names its subject through `talent_id` and its authority through `consent_grant_id`, declares `asset_kind` as `FACE_SCAN`, `VOICE_SAMPLE` or `MOTION_CAPTURE`, carries a `capture_ref` pointing at the capture session record (1 to 120 characters, not blank), declares `storage_class` as either `ENCRYPTED_VAULT` (the default) or `COLD_ARCHIVE`, and carries a `payload_digest` of the stored payload (1 to 128 characters, non-blank at creation). Registration is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ACTIVE` when the named grant is not `ACTIVE`, and with `ERR_STATE_LAW_PROFILE_MISSING` when the talent's domicile state has no law profile. The two identifiers are recorded exactly as supplied and are never cross-checked against one another at this point: naming a grant that in fact belongs to some other talent is accepted here and only surfaces later, when a replica built over that asset draws `ERR_ASSET_CONSENT_MISMATCH`. A new asset is `STORED`, with `purge_due_at`, `purged_at` and `held_from_status` all null.

The **retention deadline** is stamped at registration and is the whole point of the record. Two obligations compete. The statutory deadline is the asset's `created_at` advanced by the domicile profile's `biometric_retention_max_days`. The contractual deadline is the end of the consent term — `effective_end_actual` when the grant has ended, otherwise `effective_until` — advanced by that profile's `deletion_grace_days`. `retention_until` is whichever of the two comes first, and when the two coincide that shared instant is the answer. An asset created on 1 August 2026 under a profile allowing 365 statutory days and 30 grace days, on a grant whose `effective_until` is 1 August 2027, has a statutory deadline of 1 August 2027 and a contractual deadline of 31 August 2027, so `retention_until` is `2027-08-01T00:00:00Z` and the statute binds. If that grant is then revoked on 15 September 2026, the contractual deadline moves to 15 October 2026 while the statutory deadline stays where it was, so `retention_until` becomes `2026-10-15T00:00:00Z` and the contract binds. For a talent in a state allowing only 180 statutory days and 15 grace days, the same asset and term give a statutory deadline of 28 January 2027 against a contractual deadline of 16 August 2027, so `retention_until` is `2027-01-28T00:00:00Z`.

Retention deadlines are recomputed for every non-purged asset of a grant whenever that grant's effective end moves — at revocation, and at natural expiry — because a shorter term shortens the office's licence to hold the material. A suspension recomputation changes nothing, since suspending a grant does not move its effective end. Whenever an asset is in `PURGE_SCHEDULED` at the moment of recomputation, its `purge_due_at` is recomputed as the new `retention_until` advanced by seven days. Only an asset whose stored value actually moves counts as mutated by the recomputation: one whose recomputed `retention_until`, and where scheduled whose recomputed `purge_due_at`, come back identical to what it already held keeps its `version` and `updated_at` untouched.

Destruction has an automatic path and a manual one. On the automatic path a compliance sweep moves an asset from `STORED` to `PURGE_SCHEDULED` once `retention_until` is at or before the current logical instant — equality schedules the purge — and stamps `purge_due_at` as `retention_until` advanced by the seven-day purge execution grace period. A later sweep then moves an asset from `PURGE_SCHEDULED` to `PURGED` once `purge_due_at` is at or before the current logical instant, clearing `payload_digest` to null so that deletion is externally observable and stamping `purged_at` with the logical instant.

On the manual path a `RightsAdministrator` destroys the sample ahead of schedule, supplying a `purge_note` — mandatory non-blank free text, validated on the request but not retained on the asset record, so it is absent from the asset's representation. It is admissible from `STORED` and from `PURGE_SCHEDULED`, and moves the asset to `PURGED` with `payload_digest` cleared to null, `purged_at` stamped and `purge_due_at` left exactly as it was. An asset already `PURGED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSET_STATE`. An asset in `LEGAL_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_ASSET_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD`, because impounded material may not be destroyed — the state check deliberately admits that status so this more informative refusal is the one the caller sees. And an asset still listed in the `source_asset_ids` of any replica whose `status` is `BUILT` or `APPROVED_FOR_USE` is refused with `ERR_ASSET_BOUND_TO_ACTIVE_REPLICA`, because purging it would orphan a live replica's provenance. `PURGED` is terminal; the asset's `version` advances by one on every one of these moves.

### 3.6. Building a Replica and Winning Approval For Its Use

A digital replica is trained from stored samples by a `RightsAdministrator`. The build names its subject through `talent_id` and its authority through `consent_grant_id`, declares `replica_kind` as `FACE`, `VOICE` or `FULL_PERFORMANCE`, carries a `build_ref` pointing at the model build artifact (1 to 120 characters, not blank), and lists the material it was trained from in `source_asset_ids`, from 1 through 10 distinct biometric asset identifiers. Every member must be an asset of this same talent under this same grant. The only status a member may not hold is `LEGAL_HOLD`; a member sitting in `PURGE_SCHEDULED`, or even one already `PURGED`, is accepted, because `source_asset_ids` is a provenance record and the office is not barred from declaring material whose payload has since gone. The governing grant must be `ACTIVE`, or the build is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Three refusals guard the source list and their relative order matters. If any member is an asset in `LEGAL_HOLD`, the build is refused with `ERR_REPLICA_SOURCE_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD` — training a replica on material impounded for litigation is the gravest failure this office can commit, so that refusal outranks every scope and referential check on the same request, and a payload that mixes impounded material together with material from the wrong grant is reported as the safety failure. Next, if any member belongs to a different `consent_grant_id` or a different `talent_id` than the replica being built, the build is refused with `ERR_ASSET_CONSENT_MISMATCH`, because material captured under one grant may not be reused under another. Finally, an empty `source_asset_ids` or one containing duplicate identifiers is refused with `ERR_NO_SOURCE_ASSETS`. A member naming no asset at all is a not-found failure and is reported earlier still. Notably, the talent's `FROZEN` posture is **not** a gate on building a replica: the legal-hold state of the specific source assets is the operative safety signal here, and the posture gate is deliberately absent from this one operation.

A new replica is `BUILT` with `open_license_count` at zero and `approved_at`, `approved_by_user_id`, `quarantine_reason` and `held_from_status` all null. Nothing may be generated from it yet. Approval for use is the talent's decision, exercised by a `TalentRepresentative` whose identifier equals the replica's `talent_id`, or by an `EstateRepresentative` whose identifier is the `VERIFIED` estate authority of that talent; the request may carry an optional `approval_note`, which must be non-blank when supplied and is validated but not retained, so it never appears in the replica's representation. The replica must be `BUILT`, or the request draws `ERR_INVALID_REPLICA_STATE`; the governing grant must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the talent's posture must not be `FROZEN`, or `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_FROZEN`; and a talent whose `life_status` is `DECEASED` must have a `VERIFIED` estate authority, or `ERR_ESTATE_AUTHORITY_UNVERIFIED`. On success the replica moves `BUILT → APPROVED_FOR_USE`, `approved_at` records the logical instant, `approved_by_user_id` records the approver, and `version` advances by one.

Replicas cycle in and out of impoundment. A cascade from a consent revocation, a death record or a case escalation moves a replica from `BUILT` or from `APPROVED_FOR_USE` to `QUARANTINED`, recording `held_from_status` as the status it left and `quarantine_reason` as `CONSENT_REVOKED`, `ESTATE_PENDING` or `SOURCE_LEGAL_HOLD` according to which cascade fired. A release cascade — estate verification, consent reinstatement, or closure of the escalated case — returns the replica to exactly its `held_from_status`, whether that was `BUILT` or `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, and clears both `quarantine_reason` and `held_from_status`. A replica released back to `APPROVED_FOR_USE` keeps its original `approved_at` and `approved_by_user_id`: no re-approval is needed, because the talent's decision was never withdrawn. `RETIRED` is different. When the governing grant reaches `EXPIRED`, a replica in `BUILT` or in `APPROVED_FOR_USE` moves to `RETIRED`, with `held_from_status` and `quarantine_reason` cleared, and `RETIRED` is terminal — no release path out of it exists.

### 3.7. Generated Performances and the Talent's Approval Right

A `LicenseeProducer` submits a generated performance for approval. The submission names the replica it came from through `digital_replica_id`, identifies the submitting organisation through `licensee_name` (1 to 200 characters, not blank), declares `media_type` as one of the five media values, lists `territory_codes` as 1 to 20 distinct two-letter uppercase codes, describes the specific intended use in `intended_use_description` (1 to 500 characters, not blank), states the reach it projects in `projected_impressions` (not negative), and states the planned first distribution instant in `scheduled_first_use_at`, which is recorded exactly as supplied and echoed back unchanged: it is a declaration of intent only, and is deliberately **not** validated against the governing grant's term, because the window that genuinely has to sit inside that term is bounded later, at license issuance. The submitting producer's identifier is recorded as `licensee_user_id`, and every later ownership check on this performance compares against that recorded value.

The replica must be `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, or the submission draws `ERR_INVALID_REPLICA_STATE`. The governing grant must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The talent's posture must not be `FROZEN`, or `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_FROZEN`. The declared `media_type` must be a member of the grant's `permitted_media_types`, or `ERR_MEDIA_TYPE_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; every declared territory must be a member of the grant's `territory_codes`, or `ERR_TERRITORY_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. A union-covered grant with no applicable schedule row for this media type draws `ERR_UNION_SCHEDULE_MISSING`.

A new submission is `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at zero, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `rejection_reason` and `review_note` all null, and a `review_deadline` set to the current logical instant advanced by the seven-day review window. Whether `approval_required` is set on the grant determines whether that decision is contractually owed at all, but the review workflow itself is always available.

Submission also accrues money, because the talent is owed for a use the moment the licensee asks for one. The **per-use royalty** is computed from four inputs and, like the creation payment, composes its whole chain before rounding once. The **reach factor** is 1.00 when reach is at most one million, 1.40 when reach is above one million and at most ten million, and 1.90 above ten million. The **media multiplier** is 1.50 for `ADVERTISING`, 1.30 for `FEATURE_FILM`, 1.15 for `EPISODIC`, 1.10 for `VIDEO_GAME` and 1.00 for `SOCIAL`. The **territory factor** starts at 1.00 and adds 0.10 for each territory beyond the first — one territory gives 1.00, two give 1.10, ten give 1.90. The grant's `per_use_base_fee_cents` is multiplied by the reach factor, then by the media multiplier, then by the territory factor, and the composed product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents exactly once. The result is then lifted to the applicable union floor: the amount is whichever is greater, the rounded product or the `minimum_per_use_cents` of the union row selected for this grant's affiliation and this performance's `media_type`, with a floor of zero when the grant carries no union affiliation.

On submission the calculation runs in its **provisional** form: reach is the performance's own `projected_impressions` and the territory count is the size of the performance's `territory_codes`. A base fee of 150000 cents for an `ADVERTISING` performance projecting six million impressions in a single territory composes to 150000 multiplied by 1.40, 1.50 and 1.00, which is 315000. The result is written to `provisional_compensation_cents` and posted as a `PER_USE_PROVISIONAL` ledger entry split three ways, an entry that names this performance through `generated_performance_id` and leaves `usage_license_id` null — as does every `REVERSAL` that later undoes it; the grant's `total_compensation_cents` and the talent's `lifetime_compensation_cents` are recomputed, and the talent's `version` advances by one. That accrual is provisional precisely because it is reversed on rejection, on withdrawal and on resubmission.

The talent side then exercises the approval right. A `TalentRepresentative` whose identifier equals the talent behind the performance, or an `EstateRepresentative` holding that talent's `VERIFIED` estate authority, records a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_REVISION` or `REJECT`, with an optional `review_note` of 1 to 500 non-blank characters stored alongside it and, for an outright rejection, a `rejection_reason` that is itself optional — a `REJECT` that supplies none simply leaves `rejection_reason` null. The `review_note` is written on every decision whichever one it is, so a decision that omits it stores null over whatever note an earlier round had recorded. The performance must be `SUBMITTED`, or the decision draws `ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_STATE`; the grant must be `ACTIVE`; the posture must not be `FROZEN`; and a deceased talent must have a `VERIFIED` estate authority. `APPROVE` moves the performance `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, records `reviewed_by_user_id`, and **retains** the provisional accrual — it is reversed only by rejection, withdrawal or resubmission. `REQUEST_REVISION` moves it `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and records `reviewed_by_user_id`, provided `revision_round` is still below the cap of three rounds. `REJECT` moves it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stamping `rejection_reason` from the payload — the reasons in the vocabulary are `OFF_BRAND`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, `QUALITY`, `REVIEW_WINDOW_LAPSED` and `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — appending a `REVERSAL` entry that exactly negates the live `PER_USE_PROVISIONAL` entry, including the exact negation of its three share columns, and setting `provisional_compensation_cents` to zero. A `REQUEST_REVISION` arriving when `revision_round` has already reached three does not loop: it rejects the performance instead, with `rejection_reason` of `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` and the same reversal. On any rejection the grant's `total_compensation_cents` and the talent's `lifetime_compensation_cents` are recomputed and the talent's `version` advances; in every case the performance's own `version` advances by one.

Resubmission belongs to the licensee. The owning `LicenseeProducer` — the one whose identifier equals the performance's `licensee_user_id`, or the request draws `ERR_NOT_LICENSEE_OWNER` — supplies a replacement `intended_use_description` and a replacement `projected_impressions`. The performance must be `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or the request draws `ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_STATE`, and the governing grant must still be `ACTIVE`. Two exhaustion cases are refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` instead: a performance still in `REVISION_REQUESTED` whose `revision_round` has reached three, and a performance already `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` of `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — the second is the reachable form, since the fourth revision request rejects rather than loops. On success the performance moves `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, `revision_round` increases by one, the two supplied fields replace their predecessors, `reviewed_by_user_id` is cleared to null while `review_note` is left standing exactly as the previous round's reviewer wrote it rather than cleared with it, and `review_deadline` is set afresh to the current logical instant advanced by seven days. The stale accrual is not adjusted in place: a `REVERSAL` entry undoes the live `PER_USE_PROVISIONAL` entry, then a fresh `PER_USE_PROVISIONAL` entry is appended for the amount the provisional calculation yields on the new `projected_impressions`, and `provisional_compensation_cents` becomes that new amount. The grant and talent roll-ups are recomputed and the performance's and talent's `version` each advance by one.

Two more paths lead out of the review workflow without a representative acting. If the review window lapses — the performance is still `SUBMITTED` and `review_deadline` is at or before the current logical instant — a compliance sweep moves it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` of `REVIEW_WINDOW_LAPSED`, leaving `reviewed_by_user_id` null and appending the same reversal an explicit rejection would. A lapse is never treated as approval: approval rights exist to protect the talent, so silence is refusal. And if the replica behind the performance is quarantined by a cascade, the performance moves from `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` to `WITHDRAWN`, again with any live provisional accrual reversed and `provisional_compensation_cents` zeroed. Both `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

### 3.8. Issuing a Usage License and Billing the Licensee

An approved performance becomes distributable content only through a usage license, issued by the owning `LicenseeProducer`. The issuance names the output through `generated_performance_id`, declares `channel` as either `ADVERTISING` or `ENTERTAINMENT`, lists `territory_codes` as 1 to 20 distinct two-letter uppercase codes, bounds the distribution window with `distribution_start` and `distribution_end` (both inclusive, with the end at or after the start — an end strictly earlier than the start is malformed rather than a temporal-validity problem, so it draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` on the payload rung, ahead of even the existence check on the performance the request names), states the contracted reach in `actual_impressions` (not negative), and echoes `expected_replica_version` as described in §2.8.

The gates on issuance are dense, because this is the moment synthetic content acquires the right to exist in public. The performance must be `APPROVED`, or issuance draws `ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_STATE`. The caller's identifier must equal the performance's `licensee_user_id`, or `ERR_NOT_LICENSEE_OWNER`. The governing grant must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The talent's posture must be neither `FROZEN` nor `RESTRICTED`, drawing `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_FROZEN` or `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_RESTRICTED` respectively. The channel's mapped media type must be within the grant's `permitted_media_types`, or `ERR_MEDIA_TYPE_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; that mapping is fixed and has exactly two arms — an `ADVERTISING` channel maps to the media type `ADVERTISING` whatever the parent performance declared, and an `ENTERTAINMENT` channel maps to the parent performance's own `media_type` unchanged. Every licensed territory must be a member of the grant's `territory_codes` **and** a member of the parent performance's `territory_codes`, or `ERR_TERRITORY_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. The requested window from `distribution_start` through `distribution_end` must lie entirely inside the grant's half-open term from `effective_from` to `effective_until`, or issuance is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_EXCEEDS_CONSENT_TERM` — a license may not authorise distribution past the day consent lapses. The talent's domicile law profile must exist, and a union-covered grant must have an applicable schedule row for the parent performance's own `media_type` — the channel mapping governs the scope check just described and nothing else, so it never moves which union row the floor is drawn from.

One more constraint caps how much simultaneous exploitation a single replica may carry. At most two usage licenses whose `distribution_status` is `ISSUED` or `LIVE` may hold closed inclusive distribution windows that overlap on the same `digital_replica_id` while sharing at least one territory code. Touching windows count as overlapping — one window ending exactly on the day the next begins is an overlap for this purpose, unlike consent terms, where adjacency is permitted. A third such license is refused with `ERR_REPLICA_OVERLAP_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Issuance stamps `distribution_status` as `ISSUED`, copies `licensee_user_id` from the performance so ownership survives independently of who called, and records the issuing user as `issued_by_user_id`. It also resolves the disclosure obligation from the talent's domicile profile: `disclosure_required` is taken from that profile's `disclosure_required_advertising` when `channel` is `ADVERTISING` and from `disclosure_required_entertainment` when `channel` is `ENTERTAINMENT`. When disclosure is required, `compliance_status` starts at `PENDING_LABEL`; when it is not required, `compliance_status` starts directly at `VERIFIED`, since there is nothing to label and nothing to check. `disclosure_placement`, `disclosure_label_text` and `disclosure_verified_by_user_id` all start null, as do `removal_deadline`, `removal_reason` and `removal_confirmed_at`.

The **final per-use royalty** is computed at issuance by the same formula the provisional accrual used, with two substitutions: reach is the license's `actual_impressions` rather than the performance's projection, and the territory count is the size of the license's `territory_codes`, which may be a strict subset of the performance's declared territories or a different selection within the grant's scope. Nothing else is substituted: the media multiplier and the union floor both keep reading the parent performance's own `media_type` rather than the channel's mapped media type, so an `ADVERTISING`-channel license issued over an `EPISODIC` performance still prices at the 1.15 multiplier and against the `EPISODIC` floor. The same base fee of 150000 cents on an `ADVERTISING` license delivering twelve million impressions across two territories composes to 150000 multiplied by 1.90, 1.50 and 1.10, which is 470250 — 155250 more than the provisional 315000 the same performance accrued. The result is written to `per_use_final_cents` and posted as a `PER_USE_FINAL` ledger entry split three ways, an entry that names the license through `usage_license_id` and leaves `generated_performance_id` null, so the ledger keeps delivery movements filterable apart from the projection movements of the same output. The provisional accrual is **not** reversed at issuance: both amounts coexist in the ledger and both are reflected in the grant's and the talent's roll-ups, because the projection accrual and the delivery accrual are separate obligations.

**Total cost to the licensee** is then assembled in a pinned order. The subtotal is `per_use_final_cents` alone. The surcharge rate is zero when `disclosure_required` is false; otherwise it is 0.0175 for an `ADVERTISING` license and 0.0125 for an `ENTERTAINMENT` one. `compliance_surcharge_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that rate and rounded **up** to the next whole cent — not half up, as every royalty is. `license_total_cents` is the subtotal plus that surcharge. A final royalty of 470250 cents on an `ADVERTISING` license where disclosure is required yields 8229.375, which rounds up to a surcharge of 8230 and a total of 478480; the same royalty on an `ENTERTAINMENT` license yields 5878.125, a surcharge of 5879 and a total of 476129; and the same royalty in a state that does not require entertainment disclosure carries no surcharge at all, so the total is 470250. The surcharge funds disclosure labelling and monitoring, is paid by the licensee to the clearance office, and is deliberately **never** a ledger entry — it never reaches the talent's, the agency's or the administrator's shares. A second license issued under the same performance recomputes its surcharge from its own subtotal alone; the surcharge is never assessed on a running total and never on a previous surcharge.

Issuance finally increments the replica's `open_license_count` by one, recomputes the grant's `total_compensation_cents` and the talent's `lifetime_compensation_cents`, and advances the replica's and the talent's `version` by one each.

### 3.9. Disclosure Labelling, Going Live, and Removal Obligations

A usage license carries two status dimensions on one row, and they advance independently. `distribution_status` tracks where the content stands in the world and moves through `ISSUED`, `LIVE`, `REMOVAL_PENDING` and the terminal `REMOVAL_CONFIRMED`. `compliance_status` tracks the synthetic-content disclosure and moves through `PENDING_LABEL`, `LABELED`, `VERIFIED` and `NON_COMPLIANT`. Only one transition reads across the two dimensions — content may not go live until its disclosure has been verified — and one sweep phase deliberately moves the compliance dimension while the distribution dimension stays exactly where it is, which is what makes the independence observable.

The owning `LicenseeProducer` attests the label, supplying `disclosure_placement` (one of `METADATA_ONLY`, `END_CARD`, `PRE_ROLL` or `PERSISTENT_BUG`) and the label copy in `disclosure_label_text` (mandatory, 1 to 200 characters, not blank). Attestation is admissible when `compliance_status` is `PENDING_LABEL` or `NON_COMPLIANT` — re-attestation after a rejection is the cycle in this dimension — and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_STATE` when the license is already `LABELED` or `VERIFIED`. The attested placement's rank on the four-rung ladder must be at least the domicile profile's minimum rank for this license's `channel`, drawn from `min_disclosure_rank_advertising` for an `ADVERTISING` license and `min_disclosure_rank_entertainment` for an `ENTERTAINMENT` one; a placement below that minimum is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_PLACEMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. A successful attestation moves `compliance_status` from `PENDING_LABEL → LABELED` or from `NON_COMPLIANT → LABELED`, stores both attested fields, resets `disclosure_verified_by_user_id` to null, and advances the license's `version` by one.

A `ComplianceOfficer` then reviews the attestation with a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. The license must be `LABELED`, or the review draws `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_STATE`. `ACCEPT` moves `compliance_status` `LABELED → VERIFIED` and records the reviewing officer as `disclosure_verified_by_user_id`. `REJECT` moves it `LABELED → NON_COMPLIANT` and leaves `disclosure_verified_by_user_id` null, while `disclosure_placement` and `disclosure_label_text` are retained for the audit trail rather than cleared, so a reviewer can see what was claimed. Either way the license's `version` advances by one.

Going live is the owning `LicenseeProducer`'s act and carries the `distribution_reference` — the URL or asset reference where the synthetic content is published, 1 to 300 characters and not blank. It moves `distribution_status` `ISSUED → LIVE` and stores that reference. Four conditions guard it: the license must be `ISSUED`, or `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`; `compliance_status` must be `VERIFIED`, or `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_VERIFIED`; the current logical instant must lie inside the closed window from `distribution_start` through `distribution_end` inclusive, or `ERR_OUTSIDE_DISTRIBUTION_WINDOW`; and the governing grant must still be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The posture gates apply here too: a `FROZEN` talent draws `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_FROZEN` and a `RESTRICTED` one draws `ERR_TALENT_RIGHTS_RESTRICTED`. The license's `version` advances by one.

A removal obligation arises whenever the license stops authorising distribution, and it can arise three ways. A sweep finds the window has closed — `distribution_end` is strictly earlier than the current logical instant — and moves an `ISSUED` or `LIVE` license to `REMOVAL_PENDING` with `removal_reason` of `TERM_EXPIRED`. A consent revocation cascade moves it there with `removal_reason` of `CONSENT_REVOKED`. A case-escalation cascade moves it there with `removal_reason` of `REPLICA_QUARANTINED`. The fourth reason in the vocabulary, `PERFORMANCE_WITHDRAWN`, exists for a removal traced to the withdrawal of the parent output itself. In every case `removal_deadline` is computed as the current logical instant advanced by the domicile profile's `removal_window_days`: an obligation raised on 2 August 2027 for a talent in a state allowing fourteen days gives a deadline of `2027-08-16T00:00:00Z`, and the same obligation for a talent in a state allowing twenty-one days gives `2027-08-23T00:00:00Z`. The talent's `open_removal_obligation_count` increases by one and its `rights_posture` is recomputed, and both the license's and the talent's `version` advance by one.

The owning `LicenseeProducer` discharges the obligation by confirming removal, a request that carries no payload fields of its own. The license must be `REMOVAL_PENDING`, or the request draws `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`, and the caller must be the recorded licensee. Confirmation moves `distribution_status` `REMOVAL_PENDING → REMOVAL_CONFIRMED`, stamps `removal_confirmed_at` with the logical instant, decreases the talent's `open_removal_obligation_count` by one with the posture recomputed, decreases the replica's `open_license_count` by one, and advances the `version` of the license, the talent and the replica. Timeliness is judged in the licensee's favour: a confirmation at or before `removal_deadline` leaves `compliance_status` untouched. But a `NON_COMPLIANT` compliance status already stamped by an earlier overdue sweep is **not** cleared by a late confirmation — the content came down, and the record of it having been overdue stands.

The compensation ledger is readable throughout: a single entry can be read by its identifier, and the collection read — filterable by talent, grant, performance, license, reversed entry and entry type — is the only channel through which entries appended deep inside a cascade, particularly reversals, can be discovered at all.

### 3.10. Unauthorized-Use Monitoring, Takedown and Legal Escalation

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a case when monitoring turns up synthetic content that may not be licensed. The case names the talent whose likeness or voice was detected through `talent_id`, states how the detection arrived in `detection_source` (`MONITORING_FEED` by default, or `SWEEP` for a case the system opened itself, or `MANUAL_REPORT`), records where the content sits in `suspect_url` (1 to 300 characters, not blank), records the `detected_channel` as `ADVERTISING` or `ENTERTAINMENT`, records the two-letter `detected_state_code` of the state where the use was observed, records the observed `detected_view_count` (not negative), and records the monitoring vendor's `match_confidence` between 0 and 1 inclusive with at most three decimals. When triage can already tie the detection to a license, `matched_usage_license_id` names it; it is otherwise null on creation.

Two computations run at the moment the case is persisted. The first is the **severity score**, which decides how hard the office must respond. Confidence dominates it: the score is `match_confidence` weighted at 0.60, plus a saturating reach term weighted at 0.25, plus a posture term weighted at 0.15, with the sum rounded half up to four decimal places. The rounded score is carried as a JSON number holding its arithmetic value, so a score of exactly 0.7500 is rendered as `0.75` rather than as a four-decimal string, and the four-decimal thresholds below are compared against that number. The reach term is `detected_view_count` divided by ten million, capped at 1.0, so anything at or above ten million views contributes the full reach weight. The posture term reads the talent's `rights_posture` **as it stands before this case is persisted** and maps `FROZEN` to 1.0, `RESTRICTED` to 0.6 and `CLEAR` to 0.3. Because the three weights sum to exactly 1.00, the score always lies between 0 and 1. A confidence of 0.925 with six million views against a `CLEAR` talent gives 0.5550 plus 0.1500 plus 0.0450, exactly 0.7500. A confidence of 0.920 on the same facts gives 0.7470. A confidence of 0.500 with four hundred thousand views against a `CLEAR` talent gives 0.3000 plus 0.0100 plus 0.0450, which is 0.3550.

The second computation resolves **enforceability in the state where the use was seen**, which is not necessarily the domicile state and frequently disagrees with it. Using the profile matched by `detected_state_code`, `use_state_rights_until` is null when that state recognises no post-mortem right — and null as well for a talent who has no `date_of_death` to advance, which is every living talent — and is otherwise the talent's `date_of_death` advanced by that state's `postmortem_rights_years`, stored at `00:00:00Z` exactly as the domicile variant is. `enforceable_under_use_state` is true when the talent is `LIVING`, and for a `DECEASED` talent is true only when `use_state_rights_until` is non-null and at or after the current logical instant. Registration is deliberately **not** consulted on this variant, because enforcement in the use state turns on that state's own recognition of the right rather than on the estate's paperwork at home. A talent who died in 2020 domiciled in a state recognising seventy years has live domicile rights, yet a use detected in a state recognising none produces a null `use_state_rights_until` and an `enforceable_under_use_state` of false — the domicile answer says the right lives, the use-state answer says there is nothing to enforce there. The same use detected in a state recognising forty years produces a `use_state_rights_until` of `2060-03-15T00:00:00Z` and an `enforceable_under_use_state` of true. A new case is `DETECTED` with `legal_hold_applied_count` at zero and `triaged_by_user_id`, `escalated_by_user_id`, `closed_by_user_id`, `takedown_issued_at` and `closure_outcome` all null. Both the talent's domicile profile and the detected state's profile must exist, or the case cannot be opened.

Triage is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s fork and takes a `decision` of `DISMISS` or `ISSUE_TAKEDOWN`. The case must be `DETECTED`, or triage draws `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. A `DISMISS` where `matched_usage_license_id` names a license whose `distribution_status` is `ISSUED` or `LIVE` means the detected use was in fact licensed, and the case moves `DETECTED → DISMISSED_LICENSED` with `closure_outcome` recorded as `LICENSED_USE_CONFIRMED`. A `DISMISS` where no license is matched, or where the matched license is no longer distributable, means there is nothing to license and nothing worth pursuing, and the case moves `DETECTED → DISMISSED_NO_ACTION` with `closure_outcome` recorded as `BELOW_ENFORCEMENT_THRESHOLD`. Either way `triaged_by_user_id` records the officer and the case's `version` advances by one.

Dismissal is not always available. A case whose `severity_score` is at or above 0.7500 may not be dismissed at all — the only permitted decision is `ISSUE_TAKEDOWN` — and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TAKEDOWN_MANDATORY`. That threshold is inclusive on the high side, so the worked case that scores exactly 0.7500 is already mandatory. One carve-out keeps such a case from becoming unresolvable: a case whose `enforceable_under_use_state` is false remains dismissible at any severity, because no takedown could issue in the detected state anyway. Conversely, `ISSUE_TAKEDOWN` requires `enforceable_under_use_state` to be true, and a deceased talent whose rights have lapsed in the detected state cannot support one — the attempt is refused with `ERR_POSTMORTEM_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`. A successful takedown moves the case `DETECTED → TAKEDOWN_ISSUED`, stamps `takedown_issued_at` with the logical instant and records `triaged_by_user_id`.

**Escalation to legal** is a `RightsAdministrator`'s act, carries an `escalation_note` — mandatory non-blank free text of no bounded length, validated on the request but not retained on the case record, so it is absent from the case's representation — and is the single most destructive operation in the system. The case must be `TAKEDOWN_ISSUED`, or escalation draws `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Its `severity_score` must be at least 0.5000, or escalation is refused with `ERR_SEVERITY_BELOW_ESCALATION_FLOOR` — the sweep-opened case scoring 0.6900 clears that floor while the 0.3550 case does not. And the escalating administrator must not be the officer recorded as `triaged_by_user_id`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Escalation then unrolls five hops, rows processed in `created_at` ascending order within each hop. Hop one moves the case `TAKEDOWN_ISSUED → LEGAL_ESCALATED` and records `escalated_by_user_id`. Because litigation is now live, hop two impounds evidence: every biometric asset of this case's talent whose `status` is `STORED` or `PURGE_SCHEDULED` moves to `LEGAL_HOLD`, recording `held_from_status` as exactly the status it left so the release can resume the same schedule, and leaving `purge_due_at` untouched; `legal_hold_applied_count` is set to the number of assets moved. Because that material is impounded, hop three moves every digital replica of this talent whose `source_asset_ids` intersects the newly held assets and whose `status` is `BUILT` or `APPROVED_FOR_USE` to `QUARANTINED`, with `quarantine_reason` of `SOURCE_LEGAL_HOLD` and `held_from_status` recorded. Because those replicas are quarantined, hop four moves every generated performance under them whose `review_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` to `WITHDRAWN`, reversing each live provisional accrual and zeroing `provisional_compensation_cents`. Because those performances are withdrawn, hop five moves every usage license on them whose `distribution_status` is `ISSUED` or `LIVE` to `REMOVAL_PENDING`, with `removal_reason` of `REPLICA_QUARANTINED` and a fresh `removal_deadline`. The talent's `legal_hold_asset_count` is recomputed, its `open_removal_obligation_count` increases once per license, and its `rights_posture` becomes `FROZEN`. The case's and the talent's `version` each advance by one, as does the `version` of every asset, replica, performance and license the cascade touched.

Closure takes an `outcome` and may be performed by either a `RightsAdministrator` or a `ComplianceOfficer`. The outcome must match the branch the case sits on, or the request draws `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. From `TAKEDOWN_ISSUED`, an `outcome` of `REMOVED` moves the case `TAKEDOWN_ISSUED → RESOLVED_REMOVED` with `closure_outcome` recorded as `VOLUNTARY_REMOVAL`; no holds were ever applied on that branch, so nothing is released. From `LEGAL_ESCALATED`, an `outcome` of `SETTLED` moves the case to `CLOSED_SETTLED` and an `outcome` of `LITIGATED` moves it to `CLOSED_LITIGATED`, with `closure_outcome` recorded as `SETTLED` or `LITIGATED` respectively; the two behave identically in every other respect. Both escalated closures release what the escalation impounded, but only when no other infringement case of this same talent remains in `LEGAL_ESCALATED`: every biometric asset in `LEGAL_HOLD` returns to exactly its `held_from_status` — `LEGAL_HOLD → STORED` for one held from storage, `LEGAL_HOLD → PURGE_SCHEDULED` for one held mid-schedule — and every digital replica quarantined with `quarantine_reason` of `SOURCE_LEGAL_HOLD` returns to exactly its `held_from_status`, with `held_from_status` and `quarantine_reason` cleared on both. `legal_hold_applied_count` is left as the historical record of what the case once impounded, while the talent's `legal_hold_asset_count` falls back to its pre-escalation value and `rights_posture` is recomputed — typically to `RESTRICTED` while removal obligations remain open, and to `CLEAR` only when none do. Withdrawn performances and licenses in `REMOVAL_PENDING` are **not** restored: those hops are irreversible by design, because content that came out of distribution under a legal cloud does not go back automatically. `closed_by_user_id` records the closer, and the case's and talent's `version` each advance by one.

### 3.11. The Compliance Sweep

The sweep is the office's periodic ops run, invoked by a `RightsAdministrator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` and evaluated entirely against the current logical clock. It takes no input. Its five phases execute in a pinned order, and within each phase rows are processed by `created_at` ascending, so the run is reproducible. Running it twice at the same logical instant changes nothing on the second pass — every phase's precondition is already false for the rows the first pass moved.

Phase one retires expired consent. Every grant whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `effective_until` is at or before the current logical instant moves to `EXPIRED`, with `effective_end_actual` set to `effective_until` rather than to the sweep instant — the term ended when the paper said it would — and with `suspension_reason` cleared to null, since a term that has run out carries no live suspension. Because the grant ended, every biometric asset of that grant that is not already `PURGED` — including one sitting in `LEGAL_HOLD`, exactly as at revocation — has its `retention_until` recomputed against that effective end, with `purge_due_at` recomputed alongside it for an asset in `PURGE_SCHEDULED`, and every digital replica of that grant in `BUILT` or `APPROVED_FOR_USE` moves to `RETIRED`. Consent expiry does **not** itself pull distributed content down; each usage license keeps running until its own window closes in phase two. The talent's `rights_posture` is recomputed and every mutated row advances its `version`.

Phase two opens removal obligations on expired licenses: every license whose `distribution_status` is `ISSUED` or `LIVE` and whose `distribution_end` is strictly earlier than the current logical instant moves to `REMOVAL_PENDING` with `removal_reason` of `TERM_EXPIRED` and a fresh `removal_deadline`. Every license this phase moves increments both `expired_license_count` and `removal_pending_count` — the same population counted twice, once as a window that closed and once as an obligation that opened — and no other phase contributes to `removal_pending_count`.

Phase three punishes overdue removals. Every license whose `distribution_status` is `REMOVAL_PENDING`, whose `removal_deadline` is strictly earlier than the current logical instant, whose `compliance_status` is not already `NON_COMPLIANT`, and whose `removal_confirmed_at` is still null has its `compliance_status` set to `NON_COMPLIANT` while its `distribution_status` stays exactly at `REMOVAL_PENDING` — this is the phase that demonstrates the two dimensions are genuinely independent. A sweep running at exactly the deadline instant does not mark the license non-compliant; a sweep one day later does. Each such license also causes a fresh infringement case to be opened automatically, with `detection_source` of `SWEEP`, a `match_confidence` of 1.000, a `detected_view_count` of zero, `suspect_url` copied from the license's `distribution_reference` — which is null for a license that was never taken live — `detected_state_code` taken from the talent's `domicile_state_code`, `detected_channel` taken from the license's `channel`, and `matched_usage_license_id` naming that license. Its `severity_score` and enforceability are computed exactly as they would be for a manually opened case, with the posture term reading the talent's `rights_posture` as it stood when the sweep began: postures are recomputed only after all five phases have finished, so an obligation phase two opened moments earlier has not yet coloured the score. Because a license already stamped `NON_COMPLIANT` is skipped, a second sweep at any later instant neither re-stamps it nor opens a second case against it.

Phase four closes lapsed reviews: every generated performance still `SUBMITTED` whose `review_deadline` is at or before the current logical instant becomes `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` of `REVIEW_WINDOW_LAPSED`, its provisional accrual reversed and `provisional_compensation_cents` zeroed.

Phase five discharges retention in two ordered steps. First, every asset in `STORED` whose `retention_until` is at or before the current logical instant moves to `PURGE_SCHEDULED` with `purge_due_at` set to `retention_until` advanced by seven days. Then, every asset in `PURGE_SCHEDULED` whose `purge_due_at` is at or before the current logical instant moves to `PURGED`, with `payload_digest` cleared to null and `purged_at` stamped. The second step reads the store as the first step left it, so an asset scheduled in the first step is destroyed by a later run seven days on only while its freshly stamped `purge_due_at` still lies in the future; an asset already more than seven days past its `retention_until` is scheduled and destroyed inside the very same run, counting once in `purge_scheduled_count` and once in `purged_count`. Assets in `LEGAL_HOLD` are skipped by phase five entirely — impounded material is never destroyed on a schedule.

Across all five phases the sweep recomputes `open_removal_obligation_count`, `lifetime_compensation_cents`, each affected grant's `total_compensation_cents` and each affected talent's `rights_posture`, and it advances the `version` of every entity row it mutates by one. The run reports what it did: a `sweep_id`, freshly generated for this run as a UUID with no type prefix and belonging to no persisted row, the `run_at` instant it evaluated against, and the counts `expired_consent_count`, `retired_replica_count`, `expired_license_count`, `removal_pending_count`, `overdue_removal_count`, `auto_rejected_performance_count`, `purge_scheduled_count`, `purged_count` and `opened_case_count`.
