# Home Oxygen & Respiratory Therapy API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Home oxygen is the rare medical service where the clinical record, the physical fleet and the reimbursement ledger must agree with one another every single day. A patient qualifies only if a blood-gas or oximetry study documents a low enough saturation; the certificate of medical necessity that rests on that study must be drafted, submitted, signed by the ordering clinician and independently approved by a compliance auditor before a single concentrator leaves the branch; the concentrator itself must be rated for the prescribed flow, must not be under manufacturer recall, and must be maintained on schedule; and the rental may be reimbursed only for a bounded number of capped-rental months. When any one of those four threads breaks — a study goes stale, a certificate is voided, a recall is published, a smoker is found in the home — the other three have to react immediately and in a defined order.

This document specifies the API for that coordination surface. It is mounted under `/home-oxygen` and serves seven operational roles across a branch network: front-office intake, ordering physicians, documentation compliance, clinical field therapists, delivery drivers, branch operations and the revenue-cycle desk. The system is deliberately built around a *logical clock* rather than wall-clock time, because the entire domain is temporal: study validity windows, recertification due dates, capped-rental month positions, promised arrival windows and travel intervals are all evaluated against a server-held instant that only an operator may advance.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **the branch never guesses**: every derived quantity — a documentation score, a monthly allowable, a cap-month position, a risk score, a cylinder count — is computed by a pinned algorithm with a pinned rounding order, so two operators reading the same data reach the same number. Second, **safety outranks capacity, and capacity outranks money**: when several rules could reject the same request, the rule that protects the patient is reported, not the rule that protects revenue. Implementers should read §2 as a contract and §3 as the domain narrative that gives §2 its meaning.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming that operator's role. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the seven recognised role names, the rejection is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called — as listed in the Authorized Role(s) column of §4 — the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- **`IntakeCoordinator`** — front-office intake. Admits patients, updates their demographic and mobility record, records qualifying studies, drafts, corrects and submits certificates, opens and withdraws branch transfers, and requests travel supply.
- **`Physician`** — the ordering clinician. The only role that may sign a submitted certificate. Otherwise limited to reading branches, patients, qualifying studies and certificates.
- **`ComplianceAuditor`** — documentation compliance. Approves, returns for correction and voids certificates, and publishes manufacturer recall notices.
- **`RespiratoryTherapist`** — clinical field staff. Assigns and releases equipment by role, performs the in-home setup education and safety assessment, files malfunction reports, services quarantined units and records continued-need surveillance reassessments.
- **`DeliveryDriver`** — route execution. Closes or misses non-setup stops on a dispatched route.
- **`BranchManager`** — branch operations. Registers branches and fleet units, retires units, plans, locks, unlocks and dispatches routes, schedules and cancels stops, completes due transfers, discharges patients, decides travel authorizations, drives recall remediation, and advances the logical clock.
- **`BillingSpecialist`** — revenue cycle. Opens rental contracts, posts and reverses capped-rental months, resumes suspended billing and closes contracts.

All seven roles have **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeCoordinator`, `Physician`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `RespiratoryTherapist`, `DeliveryDriver`, `BranchManager` or `BillingSpecialist`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server records those opaque identifiers where the domain calls for an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id`, `signed_by_user_id` and `audited_by_user_id` on a certificate, as `driver_user_id` on a delivery route, as `completed_by_user_id` on a route stop, and as `decided_by_user_id` on a travel authorization — but no ownership check ever resolves `X-User-ID` to an entity primary key.

The one place where `X-User-ID` carries decision weight is **segregation of duties** on certificate approval. A `ComplianceAuditor` approving a certificate must present an `X-User-ID` that differs from *both* the certificate's stored `submitted_by_user_id` and its stored `signed_by_user_id`. If it matches either one, the approval is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The same operator identifier may not both prepare and bless the same compliance document, nor both sign and bless it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and has a minimum of 0 with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer in the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer greater than or equal to 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a list endpoint states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries all four pagination fields: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports how many rows matched the filters before the page window was applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers are never prefixed with a type marker and are never supplied by the client; a client that sends an `id` in a request body has that key ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was first persisted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations caused by a cascade from another entity rather than by a direct request. **`QualifyingTest` is the sole exception.** A qualifying study is an immutable clinical record: once written it is never edited, so a `QualifyingTest` row carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation entirely. Every other resource — `Branch`, `Patient`, `CertificateOfMedicalNecessity`, `RentalContract`, `EquipmentUnit`, `Placement`, `DeliveryRoute`, `RouteStop`, `RecallNotice` and `TravelAuthorization` — carries the full set of four common properties.

Because cascades touch rows the caller never named, version increments propagate widely. A single certificate void, for example, increments the certificate's own `version`, the `version` of every rental contract it suspends, and the patient's `version` through the mirrored certificate status. Implementers should treat "one increment per row actually mutated in this request" as the invariant, not "one increment per request".

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution

Paths are resolved strictly left to right, and each segment's existence check is reported with its own error before any deeper segment is examined. A request naming a rental contract and a posted service month, for example, resolves the contract first and reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) if it is absent; only once the contract is in hand is the month segment matched against that contract's posted ledger, reporting `ERR_POSTED_MONTH_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when no posted element carries it.

The full set of path-segment existence errors is: `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND` for a branch segment, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` for a patient segment, `ERR_QUALIFYING_TEST_NOT_FOUND` for a qualifying-study segment, `ERR_CMN_NOT_FOUND` for a certificate segment, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` for a rental-contract segment, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` for a fleet-unit segment, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a placement segment, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND` for a delivery-route segment, `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_NOT_FOUND` for a route-stop segment, `ERR_RECALL_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND` for a recall-notice segment, `ERR_TRAVEL_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND` for a travel-authorization segment, and `ERR_POSTED_MONTH_NOT_FOUND` for a posted-month segment. All are HTTP 404. The path parameters that carry those segments are named `branch_id`, `patient_id`, `qualifying_test_id`, `cmn_id`, `contract_id`, `equipment_unit_id`, `placement_id`, `route_id`, `stop_id`, `recall_notice_id`, `travel_authorization_id` and `service_month`; the first eleven are opaque resource identifiers as described in §2.4, and the last is a `YYYY-MM` month string matched against the contract's posted ledger rather than a row identifier.

Identifiers supplied in a *request body* rather than in the path are checked later in the ladder (see §2.10) but reuse the same error identifiers: a body-supplied serving or receiving branch that does not resolve reports `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND`, a body-supplied patient reports `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, a body-supplied qualifying study reports `ERR_QUALIFYING_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, a body-supplied certificate reports `ERR_CMN_NOT_FOUND`, a body-supplied contract reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, a body-supplied route reports `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, and a body-supplied predecessor unit reports `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads real wall-clock time. The server holds a single **logical clock**, and its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Before any operator moves it, every date and instant the system computes — a study's expiry, a signature date, a first service month, a placement instant, a recall deadline — derives from that default. Falling back to the host machine's real time is a defect.

Only a `BranchManager` may advance the clock, by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The clock is monotone and forward-only: a supplied instant that is not strictly later than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), and nothing is changed. Each successful advance returns the new `current_utc`, the `previous_utc` it replaced, and two counters describing the sweep it performed on the way: `expired_cmn_count` and `suspended_contract_count`.

The sweep runs *before* the response is returned. Every certificate that is `ACTIVE` and whose recertification due date falls strictly before the new clock's calendar date is expired, and every rental contract hanging off such a certificate whose billing dimension is `ACCRUING` or `CAP_REACHED` is suspended with a certificate-expiry reason. §3.3 describes the sweep's business meaning; the point here is that clock advancement is the only mechanism that can move a certificate to `EXPIRED`, and no business endpoint may move the clock as a side effect.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Certificate and Travel Creation

Two creation operations sit on top of a parent row that other operators mutate frequently, and both are protected by optimistic concurrency control against a stale client read.

Drafting a certificate of medical necessity requires a non-optional `expected_patient_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the parent patient. If that value does not equal the patient's current `version`, the draft is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no certificate row is written. Every certificate write bumps the patient's `version` through the mirrored certificate-status field, so a client that composed its request against an older read is carrying a version the server no longer recognises. Without this check the outcome would be indeterminate: a newly approved certificate legitimately supersedes the patient's prior `ACTIVE` certificate, so a second create composed against the same observed patient state would itself succeed under the product's natural semantics. The version comparison is what makes which request the server honours deterministic.

Requesting travel supply against a rental contract requires a non-optional `expected_contract_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on that contract. A value that does not equal the contract's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no authorization row is written. Billing-month posting, billing resumption and every placement change bump a contract's `version`, so a stale read is common in practice; and because a new authorization legitimately supersedes any prior authorization still in `REQUESTED` on the same contract, the version comparison is again what pins which request wins.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. Certificate submission, signature, approval, return-for-correction and void; contract month posting, reversal, resumption and closure; equipment assignment and release; route locking, unlocking and dispatch; stop creation, completion, missing and cancellation; unit servicing and retirement; recall remediation; transfer and discharge; travel approval and denial — none of them accept an expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding Order and Unit Arithmetic

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in **cents**; there are no fractional-cent values in any persisted field or any response. All scores and factors that are declared as decimals — a certificate's documentation score, a patient's oxygen-safety risk score, a travel altitude factor, a travel effective flow — are carried to exactly **2 decimal places and rounded half up**. All litre quantities, cylinder counts, month positions and minute counts are whole integers.

Rounding *order* is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, and it differs deliberately between calculations:

- The certificate documentation score evaluates each of its six components at unlimited precision and applies **exactly one** half-up rounding to two decimal places, on the final sum. Rounding components first is forbidden and yields a different number.
- The monthly allowable for a rental contract rounds **each equipment line item** half-up to whole cents *first*, then sums the already-rounded line items with exact integer arithmetic. Summing at full precision and rounding once at the end is a rejected order and produces a different total for some equipment combinations.
- The travel supply waterfall rounds at **every step**, and each subsequent step reads the already-rounded value: the altitude factor and the effective flow are rounded half up to two decimals, base litres and reserve litres are floored to whole litres, and the cylinder count is rounded up to a whole cylinder. Carrying full precision forward is a rejected order.
- Each variant of the oxygen-safety risk score applies exactly one half-up rounding to two decimal places, on that variant's own final expression, with no intermediate rounding.
- Whole-day and whole-month arithmetic (study validity windows, recall remediation deadlines, recertification intervals) involves no rounding at all. Timestamps the system computes are emitted as ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits; dates the system computes are emitted as `YYYY-MM-DD`; service months are emitted as `YYYY-MM` and compared lexicographically as strings.

### 2.9. Parallel State Dimensions and Cascade Propagation

Most resources in this system carry a single lifecycle field named `status`. A rental contract is the exception: it carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, `billing_status` and `service_status`. The two evolve independently — a transition on one dimension never reads or writes the other's field unless a declared side effect says so — and every combination of a non-terminal billing state with a non-terminal service state is reachable. A contract may legitimately sit at `CAP_REACHED` on the billing dimension while sitting at `IN_SERVICE` on the service dimension, or at `ACCRUING` while `PICKUP_SCHEDULED`.

Cascades in this system are **transitive**: one hop's effect is the cause of the next hop's precondition, and every hop is mandatory. The two deepest are described in full in §3.6 (a failed in-home safety assessment propagating from stop to contract to placements to fleet units) and §3.7 (a Class I recall propagating from notice to fleet units to placements to contracts). Two aggregate roll-ups run in the other direction: a delivery route's completion is *derived* from its stops, re-evaluated every time a stop reaches a terminal state, and a patient's mirrored certificate status is re-derived from the patient's most-recently-created certificate on every certificate write.

Whenever a cascade mutates a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1, exactly as if the row had been the direct target of the request.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

When more than one rule could reject the same request, exactly one error is reported: the one belonging to the earliest position in the ladder below. The ladder is global — it is not per-endpoint — and it is ordered on a domain principle: authentication and authorization first; then the segregation-of-duties compliance control; then path existence left to right; then request-schema shape; then body foreign-key existence; then optimistic concurrency; then uniqueness; then state preconditions; then clinical safety; then referential coherence between clinical documents; then temporal and interval rules; then capacity and inventory limits; and finally the fiscal capped-rental checks. **Safety precedes capacity, and capacity precedes money**: a branch may not place unsafe equipment in order to protect revenue.

The full ladder, in evaluation order:

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` — both headers present and non-empty, on every endpoint.
2. `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` — `X-User-Role` is one of the seven role names, on every endpoint.
3. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list, on every endpoint.
4. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — on certificate approval only, the acting `X-User-ID` differs from both the stored submitter and the stored signer. This control sits deliberately above path existence because it must fire before any resource is acted on; when the named certificate cannot be located there is no stored submitter or signer to compare against, so the check cannot fire and the missing-certificate result at position 8 surfaces instead.
5. `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND` — the branch path segment resolves, on branch read and branch update.
6. `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` — the patient path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
7. `ERR_QUALIFYING_TEST_NOT_FOUND` — the qualifying-study path segment resolves, on the single-study read.
8. `ERR_CMN_NOT_FOUND` — the certificate path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
9. `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` — the rental-contract path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
10. `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` — the fleet-unit path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
11. `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND` — the placement path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
12. `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND` — the delivery-route path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
13. `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_NOT_FOUND` — the route-stop path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
14. `ERR_RECALL_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND` — the recall-notice path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
15. `ERR_TRAVEL_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND` — the travel-authorization path segment resolves, on every endpoint whose path contains one.
16. `ERR_POSTED_MONTH_NOT_FOUND` — the service-month path segment matches a posted element of the named contract's ledger, on month reversal.
17. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — every field the endpoint declares as required is present, every supplied value matches its declared type, and every numeric value lies inside its declared range. Applies to every creating, updating and deleting endpoint. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored, and system-maintained or computed fields are ignored when supplied.
18. `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` — every supplied enumerated value is a member of that field's declared set. Applies to every creating and updating endpoint that accepts an enumerated field and to every list endpoint that accepts an enumerated filter.
19. `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` — `limit` and `offset` are in range, on every list endpoint.
20. `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND` — a body-supplied `branch_id` or `receiving_branch_id` resolves. Applies to patient admission, fleet-unit registration, route creation, transfer opening and travel-supply requests.
21. `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` — a body-supplied `patient_id` resolves. Applies to qualifying-study recording, certificate drafting and contract opening.
22. `ERR_QUALIFYING_TEST_NOT_FOUND` — a body-supplied `qualifying_test_id` resolves, on certificate drafting.
23. `ERR_CMN_NOT_FOUND` — a body-supplied `cmn_id` resolves, on contract opening.
24. `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` — a body-supplied `contract_id` resolves, on stop creation.
25. `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND` — a body-supplied `route_id` resolves, on stop creation.
26. `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` — a body-supplied `replaces_unit_id` resolves, on fleet-unit registration.
27. `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` — `expected_patient_version` equals the patient's current `version`, on certificate drafting.
28. `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` — `expected_contract_version` equals the contract's current `version`, on travel-supply requests.
29. `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN` — no other patient carries the supplied `mrn`, on patient admission.
30. `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER` — no other fleet unit carries the supplied `serial_number`, on fleet-unit registration.
31. `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — the target row's state field equals one of the origin states declared for the requested transition. This is the generic invalid-transition and terminal-state lockdown guard, and it applies to every action endpoint that drives a lifecycle transition, including repeated triggers of an action already performed.
32. `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — the certificate's revision round is below the ceiling of 3, on return-for-correction.
33. `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ACTIVE` — the governing patient is `ACTIVE`. Applies to six operations: qualifying-study recording, certificate drafting, certificate submission, contract opening, equipment assignment and travel-supply requests.
34. `ERR_CONTRACT_CLOSED` — the contract's `billing_status` is not `CLOSED`. Applies to five operations: month posting, month reversal, billing resumption, equipment assignment and travel-supply requests.
35. `ERR_CONTRACT_BILLING_SUSPENDED` — the contract's `billing_status` is exactly `ACCRUING`, on month posting. A contract in `SUSPENDED` or `CAP_REACHED` cannot accept a new posted month.
36. `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_IN_SERVICE` — the contract's `service_status` is `IN_SERVICE`. Applies to month posting, and to stop creation when the requested `stop_type` is `TANK_EXCHANGE`, `PORTABLE_REFILL`, `PICKUP` or `MAINTENANCE_SWAP`; a `SETUP_DELIVERY` stop is by definition scheduled while the contract is still `PENDING_SETUP`, so this check is not applied to it.
37. `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_DISPATCHED` — the stop's route is `DISPATCHED`. Applies to all three field-closing actions: generic stop completion, setup completion and marking a stop missed.
38. `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_EDITABLE` — the route is in `PLANNING` or `LOCKED`. Applies to stop creation and stop cancellation.
39. `ERR_STOP_TYPE_MISMATCH` — setup completion targets a stop whose `stop_type` is `SETUP_DELIVERY`, and generic completion targets a stop whose `stop_type` is one of `TANK_EXCHANGE`, `PORTABLE_REFILL`, `PICKUP` or `MAINTENANCE_SWAP`. Applies to both completion actions.
40. `ERR_RECALL_NOT_REMEDIATED` — the notice's remediated count has reached its affected count, on recall closure.
41. `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_DUE` — the logical clock's date has reached the pending transfer's effective date, on transfer completion.
42. `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_RETURNED` — the contract's `service_status` is `RETURNED` or `SETUP_FAILED`, on contract closure.
43. `ERR_QUALIFYING_TEST_STALE` — the underlying study is still inside its validity window plus the 15-day grace period, on certificate submission. This check is ordered deliberately *ahead* of the documentation-completeness threshold below it: once a study is past its grace window its currency component scores zero and the remaining five components cap at 80.00, so a stale-study submission would otherwise always surface as an incomplete-documentation failure and this rule could never be observed. Staleness is also the more specific and more actionable finding — the fix is a new study, not a better narrative.
44. `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE` — the certificate's documentation score has reached the pass threshold of 85.00. Applies to two operations: certificate submission and certificate approval.
45. `ERR_TEST_NON_QUALIFYING` — the underlying study's computed group is not `NON_QUALIFYING`, on certificate drafting. This is enforced at draft creation rather than at submission: a non-qualifying study contributes zero group points, and the remaining five documentation components cap at 70.00, so a certificate drafted on one could never clear the threshold anyway. Rejecting at creation keeps the error reachable and stops the unusable draft from ever existing.
46. `ERR_PATIENT_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` — the governing patient's compliance hold is clear. Applies to three operations: equipment assignment, travel-supply requests and travel approval.
47. `ERR_PORTABLE_NOT_INDICATED` — when the requested placement `role` is `PORTABLE`, the contract's certificate prescribes portable oxygen and the patient's mobility profile is `LIMITED_AMBULATORY` or `ACTIVE_AMBULATORY`. Applies to equipment assignment.
48. `ERR_REFILL_REQUIRES_LIQUID_PORTABLE` — when the requested `stop_type` is `PORTABLE_REFILL`, the named contract has at least one placement in `PLACED` whose unit's class is `LIQUID_PORTABLE`. Applies to stop creation.
49. `ERR_NO_RESERVED_EQUIPMENT` — when the requested `stop_type` is `SETUP_DELIVERY`, the named contract has at least one placement in `RESERVED`. Applies to stop creation.
50. `ERR_TEST_PATIENT_MISMATCH` — the study named by `qualifying_test_id` belongs to the patient named by `patient_id`, on certificate drafting.
51. `ERR_CMN_PATIENT_MISMATCH` — the certificate named by `cmn_id` belongs to the patient named by `patient_id`, on contract opening.
52. `ERR_PATIENT_HAS_OPEN_CONTRACT` — no other contract for the same patient has a `billing_status` other than `CLOSED`, on contract opening. At most one open contract per patient exists, which is why every rule in this document that speaks of "the patient's contract" resolves to exactly one row.
53. `ERR_CMN_NOT_ACTIVE` — the contract's linked certificate is `ACTIVE`. Applies to five operations: contract opening, equipment assignment, month posting, billing resumption and travel-supply requests.
54. `ERR_STOP_BRANCH_MISMATCH` — the route named by `route_id` belongs to the same branch that serves the patient of the contract named by `contract_id`, on stop creation.
55. `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_INVALID` — the supplied `receiving_branch_id` differs from the patient's current serving branch. Applies to transfer opening and travel-supply requests.
56. `ERR_REPLACED_UNIT_NOT_REPLACEABLE` — the unit named by `replaces_unit_id` is in `IN_QUARANTINE` or `RETIRED`, on fleet-unit registration.
57. `ERR_CONTRACT_HAS_RECALLED_EQUIPMENT` — no placement on the contract sits in `RECALLED_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, on billing resumption.
58. `ERR_TEST_DATE_IN_FUTURE` — the supplied `tested_at` is at or before the logical clock, on qualifying-study recording.
59. `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT` — `shift_end_utc` is strictly after `shift_start_utc`, and the UTC calendar date of `shift_start_utc` equals the supplied `service_date`. Applies to route creation.
60. `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` — `window_end_utc` is strictly after `window_start_utc`, on stop creation.
61. `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_SHIFT` — the stop's buffered span fits inside the route's shift, on stop creation.
62. `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT` — the stop's buffered span does not overlap any other planned stop's buffered span on the same route, on stop creation.
63. `ERR_INVALID_TRAVEL_INTERVAL` — `return_utc` is strictly after `depart_utc`, on travel-supply requests.
64. `ERR_TRAVEL_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` — the requested travel interval does not intersect any existing requested or approved interval on the same contract, on travel-supply requests.
65. `ERR_TRANSFER_DATE_IN_PAST` — the supplied `transfer_effective_date` is at or after the logical clock's date, on transfer opening.
66. `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` — the supplied `to_iso8601` is strictly after the current logical clock.
67. `ERR_SERVICE_MONTH_BEFORE_START` — the supplied `service_month` is at or after the contract's first service month, compared lexicographically, on month posting.
68. `ERR_SERVICE_MONTH_IN_FUTURE` — the supplied `service_month` is at or before the logical clock's own calendar month, on month posting.
69. `ERR_SERVICE_MONTH_ALREADY_POSTED` — the supplied `service_month` is not already an element of the contract's posted ledger, on month posting.
70. `ERR_ROUTE_HAS_NO_STOPS` — the route has at least one planned stop, on route locking.
71. `ERR_REFILL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — the route's used refill slots do not exceed its branch's daily refill capacity, on route locking.
72. `ERR_ROLE_ALREADY_OCCUPIED` — no placement on the contract already fills the requested `role` in `RESERVED` or `PLACED`, on equipment assignment.
73. `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_EQUIPMENT` — the eligibility filter yields at least one candidate unit, on equipment assignment.
74. `ERR_CYLINDERS_NOT_APPLICABLE` — `cylinders_requested` is zero or omitted when the `stop_type` is `PICKUP` or `PORTABLE_REFILL`, on stop creation.
75. `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_LIQUID_CERTIFIED` — when the supplied `equipment_class` is `LIQUID_RESERVOIR` or `LIQUID_PORTABLE`, the named branch is liquid-certified, on fleet-unit registration.
76. `ERR_PATIENT_HAS_ACTIVE_SERVICE` — no contract for the patient has a `service_status` of `PENDING_SETUP`, `IN_SERVICE` or `PICKUP_SCHEDULED`, on discharge.
77. `ERR_TRAVEL_SUPPLY_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` — the computed authorized cylinder count does not exceed the ceiling of 12, on travel-supply requests.
78. `ERR_CAP_MONTHS_EXHAUSTED` — the computed cap position does not exceed the contract's cap-month limit. Applies to month posting and billing resumption.
79. `ERR_REVERSAL_WOULD_NOT_CLEAR_CAP` — when the contract is `CAP_REACHED`, recomputing the cap position over the ledger without the month being reversed yields a position strictly below the cap-month limit, on month reversal.
80. `ERR_NO_PLACED_EQUIPMENT` — the contract's monthly allowable is greater than zero, on month posting. A contract with no placed equipment has nothing to bill.

### 2.11. Safety Posture and the Compliance Hold

Each patient carries a persisted safety posture consisting of `oxygen_safety_risk_score` (a decimal from 0.00 through 100.00, initialised to 0.00), `risk_variant_applied` (one of `NONE`, `INITIAL_ASSESSMENT` or `ONGOING_SURVEILLANCE`, initialised to `NONE`) and `compliance_hold` (a boolean, initialised to false). The hold is not an operator decision — it is a pure function of the score: `compliance_hold` is true exactly when `oxygen_safety_risk_score` has reached 70.00, and false otherwise. It is re-evaluated every time the score is recomputed.

While the hold is set, three operations are blocked with `ERR_PATIENT_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`: no new equipment may be assigned for the patient's contract, no new travel supply may be requested, and no pending travel authorization may be approved. Everything else — reading, billing, route work already scheduled, transfers, discharge — continues to function. The clinical intent is that a patient whose home is unsafe or whose equipment keeps failing should not receive *more* equipment or be sent travelling until a reassessment brings the score back down.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Branch Network and Fleet Onboarding

Everything in this system hangs off a **branch** — a Lincare service centre with its own fleet, its own drivers and its own service population. A `BranchManager` registers one by supplying a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the US state it operates in. Four operating parameters may be supplied at registration and all four have defaults: `competitive_bid_area` marks whether the branch sits inside a Medicare competitive-bidding area and defaults to false; `liquid_oxygen_certified` marks whether the branch is licensed to fill and deliver liquid oxygen and defaults to false; `refill_capacity_per_day` caps how many portable-refill stops the branch can service on a single route and defaults to 4, accepting any value from 0 through 40; and `tank_exchange_buffer_minutes` sets the trailing turnaround buffer appended to every stop window on the branch's routes, defaulting to 30 minutes and accepting any value from 0 through 120.

Three of those parameters may be revised later by a `BranchManager` — refill capacity, turnaround buffer and liquid certification — because they track licensing and staffing changes rather than the branch's identity. The `name` and `state_code` are fixed at registration. Branches may be listed with filters on `state_code`, `competitive_bid_area` and `liquid_oxygen_certified`.

A branch's fleet is built one physical unit at a time, again by a `BranchManager`. Each unit is registered against an owning `branch_id` with a `serial_number` of 1 to 40 characters that must be globally unique across the whole fleet — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER` — and a `model_code` of 1 to 40 characters. The model code matters far beyond bookkeeping: manufacturer recalls target a model code, not a serial number, so every unit sharing a code shares that code's recall exposure. The unit's `equipment_class` names its physical modality and must be one of `STATIONARY_CONCENTRATOR`, `PORTABLE_CONCENTRATOR`, `LIQUID_RESERVOIR`, `LIQUID_PORTABLE` or `COMPRESSED_CYLINDER`. Registering a unit whose class is `LIQUID_RESERVOIR` or `LIQUID_PORTABLE` at a branch whose liquid certification is false is rejected with `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_LIQUID_CERTIFIED` — an uncertified branch may not hold liquid oxygen at all.

The unit's clinical capability is captured by `max_continuous_lpm`, its rated maximum continuous flow in litres per minute expressed to one decimal place between 0.5 and 15.0, and `max_pulse_setting`, its rated maximum pulse-dose setting from 0 through 9, defaulting to 0 for continuous-only units. Its maintenance schedule is captured by `pm_interval_days`, the preventive-maintenance interval in days between 30 and 1095 and defaulting to 365, together with `next_pm_due_date`, the `YYYY-MM-DD` date on which the next preventive maintenance falls due. Both are supplied at registration; the interval is what the system uses to restamp the due date after service.

Fleet units are also allowed to record their own ancestry. A unit procured specifically to replace a broken or recalled predecessor names that predecessor in `replaces_unit_id`; original stock leaves the field null. A named predecessor must resolve to a real unit and must currently sit in `IN_QUARANTINE` or `RETIRED` — naming a unit that is still healthy and in circulation is rejected with `ERR_REPLACED_UNIT_NOT_REPLACEABLE`. Fleet listings can be filtered by owning branch, by the unit each row replaces, by `model_code`, by `equipment_class` and by lifecycle state, which makes the replacement chain discoverable.

A newly registered unit enters the fleet in `IN_STOCK` with `quarantine_reason` set to `NONE`. Its lifecycle spans `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED`, `DEPLOYED`, `IN_QUARANTINE` and the terminal `RETIRED`. Two further fields describe its quarantine history: `quarantine_reason`, which records why the unit is (or was last) held out of service and takes one of `NONE`, `RECALL`, `MALFUNCTION`, `SAFETY` or `TRANSFER`; and `recall_remediated_at`, the logical-clock instant at which the unit was serviced while a recall notice covering its model code was open or under remediation, null when the unit has never been remediated. A `BranchManager` may permanently retire a unit from either `IN_STOCK` or `IN_QUARANTINE`, moving it to `RETIRED` and incrementing its `version`; retirement from `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED` is not a declared transition and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.2. Intake and Qualifying Studies

An `IntakeCoordinator` admits a patient by supplying an `mrn` — the medical record number, 1 to 40 characters, globally unique across all patients, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN` — a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `branch_id` of the branch that will serve them, a `payer_type` and a `mobility_profile`. The payer class drives documentation strictness downstream and must be one of `MEDICARE_COMPETITIVE_BID`, `MEDICARE_NON_BID`, `MEDICAID` or `COMMERCIAL`. The documented ambulation status must be one of `HOMEBOUND`, `LIMITED_AMBULATORY` or `ACTIVE_AMBULATORY`, and it gates portable equipment: a homebound patient has no documented need to leave the house, so portable systems are not indicated for them. One optional field completes admission — `altitude_feet`, the elevation of the patient's residence from 0 through 15000 feet, defaulting to 0. That elevation is the baseline against which travel destinations are compared, because a patient who already lives at altitude needs less of an adjustment when travelling to altitude.

On admission the patient's derived fields are all initialised: lifecycle `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `current_cmn_status` becomes `NONE`, `oxygen_safety_risk_score` becomes 0.00, `risk_variant_applied` becomes `NONE` and `compliance_hold` becomes false. The patient lifecycle spans `ACTIVE`, `TRANSFER_PENDING` and the terminal `DISCHARGED`; `transfer_to_branch_id` and `transfer_effective_date` are populated only while a transfer is pending and are null otherwise. Patient listings may be filtered by serving branch, by pending receiving branch through `transfer_to_branch_id`, by lifecycle `status`, by `payer_type`, by `mobility_profile` and by `compliance_hold`. Later reassessment may revise `full_name`, `mobility_profile` and `altitude_feet`; the medical record number, the payer class and the serving branch are not editable through that path — the serving branch changes only through a completed transfer.

The field `current_cmn_status` is a **mirror**, not an independent value. It always reflects the status of the patient's most-recently-created certificate of medical necessity, ranked by creation timestamp descending, and reads `NONE` when the patient has no certificate at all. Its domain is therefore the certificate's own state set plus that sentinel: `NONE`, `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `PHYSICIAN_SIGNED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED`, `VOIDED` or `SUPERSEDED`. Every certificate write that changes the newest certificate's status re-derives the mirror and increments the patient's `version`; a write against an *older* certificate leaves the mirror alone.

Qualifying is evidence-driven. An `IntakeCoordinator` records a study against a `patient_id` with a `test_type` of `RESTING_ROOM_AIR`, `EXERCISE`, `NOCTURNAL_OVERNIGHT_OXIMETRY` or `SLEEP_STUDY`, the instant `tested_at` at which the study was performed, and the `ordering_physician_npi` — the ordering physician's National Provider Identifier, exactly ten digits. A study performed in the future is nonsense, so a `tested_at` strictly after the logical clock is rejected with `ERR_TEST_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. Recording a study for a patient who is not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The clinical measurements themselves are all optional at the schema level because different studies produce different evidence: `spo2_percent` is the lowest documented arterial saturation during the study, either null or an integer from 50 through 100, and is null when only a blood gas was drawn; `pao2_mmhg` is the documented arterial oxygen partial pressure, either null or an integer from 20 through 120, and is null when only pulse oximetry was performed; `desat_minutes` is the cumulative number of minutes spent at or below 88 percent saturation, from 0 through 1440 and defaulting to 0, and is consulted only for an overnight oximetry study; and `cor_pulmonale_or_edema` is a boolean defaulting to false that is set when the chart documents cor pulmonale, dependent edema or erythrocythemia — the classic secondary finding that rescues a borderline saturation.

**Qualifying studies are immutable.** There is no update path and no delete path, so the group determination runs exactly once, at the moment the row is created, and its three computed outputs are stamped permanently. The first output is `validity_days`, the number of days the study remains current: a resting-room-air or exercise study is current for 30 days, while an overnight oximetry or sleep study is current for 90 days. Adding that many days to `tested_at` yields `test_validity_expires_at`, emitted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. That window is pinned before the group test is evaluated, so currency and group are independent of one another.

The second output is `qualifying_group`, which takes one of `GROUP_I`, `GROUP_II` or `NON_QUALIFYING`. The more generous group is tested first. A study is a Group I hit when either the saturation is present and is at most 88 percent, or the partial pressure is present and is at most 55 millimetres of mercury. For an overnight oximetry study only, that hit is additionally conditioned on the cumulative desaturation time having reached at least 5 minutes — a single momentary dip does not qualify a patient for nocturnal oxygen. When the Group I test succeeds, the group is `GROUP_I` and no further test is performed. Otherwise the borderline Group II test is evaluated: a study is borderline when either the saturation is present and is exactly 89 percent, or the partial pressure is present and lies inside the inclusive range 56 through 59 millimetres of mercury. A borderline study whose chart also documents cor pulmonale or dependent edema is `GROUP_II`. A study that is neither a Group I hit nor a borderline study with a secondary finding is `NON_QUALIFYING`. Nothing outside the study row itself participates in this determination — not the patient, not any certificate, not even the logical clock. Studies may be listed with filters on `patient_id`, `test_type` and the computed `qualifying_group`.

### 3.3. The Certificate of Medical Necessity: Drafting, Signature, Audit and Expiry

The certificate of medical necessity is the compliance document that authorises a rental. An `IntakeCoordinator` drafts one against a `patient_id` and a `qualifying_test_id`, and the two must agree: a study belonging to a different patient than the one named on the certificate is rejected with `ERR_TEST_PATIENT_MISMATCH`. The named study's computed group must not be `NON_QUALIFYING`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_TEST_NON_QUALIFYING`. Drafting for a patient who is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. As described in §2.7, the draft must also carry `expected_patient_version`.

The prescription itself is captured in five fields. `form_type` distinguishes `INITIAL` for a patient's first certificate, `RECERTIFICATION` for a continued-need renewal, and `REVISED` for a changed prescription. `prescribed_lpm` is the prescribed continuous flow in litres per minute, from 0.5 through 15.0 at one decimal place, and it drives both equipment class selection and travel supply. `hours_per_day` records the documented hours of use per day, from 1 through 24. `portable_prescribed` is a boolean defaulting to false, set when the certificate documents a medical need for a portable system. `length_of_need_months` is the physician-declared length of need in months from 1 through 99, where 99 means lifetime. A sixth field, `narrative_element_count`, records how many of the three required narrative elements — diagnosis, prior therapy trial and expected benefit — the chart actually carries; it accepts 0 through 3 and defaults to 0.

Every certificate is stamped with a **documentation score** in its `documentation_score` field at draft time, recomputed whenever corrections are applied, and recomputed again at submission. Six additive components make up that score, and their maxima sum to exactly 100:

- **Group** — worth up to 30 points. A `GROUP_I` study scores the full 30; a `GROUP_II` study scores 24; a non-qualifying study scores 0.
- **Currency** — worth up to 20 points. When the logical clock is at or before the study's expiry instant, this component is 20. When the clock is past the expiry instant but at or before that instant plus the 15-day grace period, it is 10. Beyond the grace period it is 0.
- **Usage** — worth up to 15 points. Take the documented hours per day, capped at 24, divide by 24, and multiply by 15.
- **Narrative** — worth up to 20 points. Take the narrative element count, divide by 3, and multiply by 20.
- **Portable consistency** — worth 10 points, awarded all-or-nothing. It is 10 when portable oxygen is not prescribed at all, and also 10 when portable oxygen is prescribed and the patient's mobility profile is `LIMITED_AMBULATORY` or `ACTIVE_AMBULATORY`. It is 0 when portable oxygen is prescribed for a `HOMEBOUND` patient.
- **Duration** — worth 5 points, awarded all-or-nothing. It is 5 when the declared length of need has reached 12 months, and 0 below that.

The six components are added together **at unlimited precision** and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once, at the very end. Rounding the individual components first is forbidden because it changes the answer: a `GROUP_I` study inside its validity window, with 7 documented hours per day, one narrative element, consistent portable prescribing and a 6-month length of need, contributes 30 plus 20 plus 4.375 plus 6.66666… plus 10 plus 0, which is 71.04166… and therefore scores 71.04 — whereas rounding the usage component to 4.38 and the narrative component to 6.67 before adding would have produced 71.05. The inputs to the score are the certificate row itself, the single study named by its `qualifying_test_id` at whatever state that row is in, the single patient named by its `patient_id`, and the logical clock. No sibling certificate participates.

The certificate lifecycle begins at `DRAFT` and spans `SUBMITTED`, `PHYSICIAN_SIGNED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, `ACTIVE`, and the three terminal states `EXPIRED`, `VOIDED` and `SUPERSEDED`. Certificates may be listed with filters on `patient_id`, `qualifying_test_id`, `status` and `form_type`, and a single read exposes the documentation score, the revision round and the recertification due date.

Submission by an `IntakeCoordinator` advances the certificate `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is guarded by four preconditions beyond the state itself: the linked study's group must not be `NON_QUALIFYING`; the logical clock must be at or before the study's expiry instant plus the 15-day grace period, or the submission fails with `ERR_QUALIFYING_TEST_STALE`; the freshly recomputed documentation score must have reached the pass threshold of 85.00, or the submission fails with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`; and the patient must be `ACTIVE`. On success the score is recomputed and persisted, `submitted_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, the patient's mirrored certificate status becomes `SUBMITTED` when this is the patient's newest certificate, and the certificate's `version` increments by 1.

Signature is the `Physician`'s only write in this system and it advances `SUBMITTED → PHYSICIAN_SIGNED`. It stamps `signed_by_user_id` with the signing operator's `X-User-ID` and `physician_signature_date` with the logical clock's date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, mirrors `PHYSICIAN_SIGNED` onto the patient when this is the newest certificate, and increments the certificate's `version` by 1. No score threshold is re-checked at signature; the clinician signs what intake prepared.

Approval by a `ComplianceAuditor` advances `PHYSICIAN_SIGNED → ACTIVE` and is the most heavily guarded transition in the document lifecycle. Beyond the origin state it enforces the two segregation-of-duties comparisons of §2.1 and re-checks that the documentation score has reached 85.00 — a score that has drifted below the threshold since submission blocks approval with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. On success the auditor's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `audited_by_user_id`; `initial_date` is stamped with the logical clock's date, marking the first instant this certificate became effective; the recertification due date is computed and persisted; **every other certificate for the same patient that is currently `ACTIVE` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`**; the patient's mirror becomes `ACTIVE` when this is the newest certificate; and the certificate's `version` increments by 1. The supersession cascade is what guarantees the invariant that at most one certificate per patient is `ACTIVE` at a time — an invariant several later rules depend on when they speak of "the patient's active certificate".

The recertification due date is computed from three inputs: the certificate's own `initial_date`, its `length_of_need_months`, and the qualifying group of the study named by its `qualifying_test_id`. A Group I study earns a 12-month recertification interval; a Group II study earns 3 months, because borderline qualification must be revisited far more often. The interval actually applied is the smaller of that group interval and the declared length of need — a physician who declares a 2-month need does not get a 12-month certificate. Adding that number of whole calendar months to the initial date yields `recert_due_date`, emitted as `YYYY-MM-DD`. Month addition keeps the day of the month, and when the target month has fewer days it clamps to the last day of the target month: six months added to 2026-08-31 yields 2027-02-28. Before a certificate first reaches `ACTIVE` the field is null.

Correction is a genuine cycle. A `ComplianceAuditor` may return a certificate for correction from either `SUBMITTED` or `PHYSICIAN_SIGNED`, supplying `correction_notes` as free text. Both branches behave identically and both are equally binding: `revision_round` increments by 1, the supplied notes are stored in `correction_notes`, **both `signed_by_user_id` and `physician_signature_date` are cleared to null** — the physician signature is discarded and must be obtained again even on the branch where a signature existed — the patient's mirror becomes `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` when this is the newest certificate, and the certificate's `version` increments by 1. Return-for-correction is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` once `revision_round` has already reached the ceiling of 3, so the field ranges from 0 through 3. Applying corrections then closes the cycle: an `IntakeCoordinator` may revise `prescribed_lpm`, `hours_per_day`, `portable_prescribed`, `length_of_need_months` and `narrative_element_count` on a certificate in `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, which advances it `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION → DRAFT`, recomputes the documentation score over the edited values, clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, mirrors `DRAFT` onto the patient when this is the newest certificate, and increments the certificate's `version` by 1. The loop `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION → DRAFT` may therefore run at most three times before the ceiling closes it.

Voiding is the auditor's escape hatch, and it is reachable from exactly three states: `ACTIVE`, `DRAFT` and `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`. Each requires a non-empty `void_reason` string, which is stored on the certificate; each mirrors `VOIDED` onto the patient when the voided certificate is the newest one; and each increments the certificate's `version` by 1. The three differ in one respect only: voiding a certificate that was `ACTIVE` additionally **suspends every rental contract hanging off it** whose billing dimension is `ACCRUING` or `CAP_REACHED`, setting each contract's suspension reason to `CMN_VOIDED` and incrementing each contract's `version`; voiding from `DRAFT` or from `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` has no contract to suspend and so performs no cascade. A certificate sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `PHYSICIAN_SIGNED` cannot be voided directly — it must be returned for correction first — which is precisely why the escape hatch from `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` exists for a certificate that has exhausted its three revision rounds.

Expiry is never operator-driven. During the sweep that runs on every logical-clock advance, each certificate that is `ACTIVE` and whose `recert_due_date` falls strictly before the new clock's date moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`. That move suspends every rental contract hanging off the certificate whose billing dimension is `ACCRUING` or `CAP_REACHED`, setting each contract's suspension reason to `CMN_EXPIRED` and incrementing each contract's `version`; it mirrors `EXPIRED` onto the patient when the certificate is the patient's newest; and it increments the certificate's `version` by 1. The count of certificates the sweep expired is reported as `expired_cmn_count` and the count of contracts it suspended as `suspended_contract_count`.

### 3.4. Opening a Capped-Rental Contract and Selecting Equipment

Once a certificate is `ACTIVE`, a `BillingSpecialist` opens the rental contract that will carry the money. The contract names a `patient_id` and the `cmn_id` of the authorising certificate, and the two must agree — a certificate belonging to a different patient is rejected with `ERR_CMN_PATIENT_MISMATCH`. The certificate must be `ACTIVE` at that moment, or the request fails with `ERR_CMN_NOT_ACTIVE`; the patient must be `ACTIVE`, or it fails with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the patient must not already hold a contract whose billing dimension is anything other than `CLOSED`, or it fails with `ERR_PATIENT_HAS_OPEN_CONTRACT`. That last rule is load-bearing: **at most one open contract exists per patient**, which is why every selector elsewhere in this document that says "the patient's contract" resolves to exactly one row.

Two commercial parameters complete the contract. `pricing_basis` selects the reimbursement basis and must be one of `COMPETITIVE_BID_SINGLE_PAYMENT`, `FEE_SCHEDULE_ADJUSTED` or `CASH`; it simultaneously chooses the allowable adjustment factor applied to every equipment line and the variant used to count capped-rental months, so it is the single most consequential field on the row. `cap_month_limit` records how many rental months the payer will reimburse before the cap is reached, accepting 1 through 36 and defaulting to 36.

A newly opened contract is initialised on both dimensions and across the whole ledger: `billing_status` becomes `ACCRUING`, `service_status` becomes `PENDING_SETUP`, `monthly_allowable_cents` becomes 0, `posted_months` becomes an empty array, `posted_month_count` becomes 0, `lifetime_paid_cents` becomes 0, `malfunction_count` becomes 0 and `cumulative_oxygen_gap_minutes` becomes 0. Alongside those, `suspension_reason` sits at `NONE`, `first_service_month` is null until service actually begins, and `last_posted_service_month` is null while the ledger is empty. Contracts may be listed with filters on `patient_id`, `cmn_id`, `billing_status`, `service_status` and `pricing_basis`, and a single read exposes both dimensions together with the posted-month ledger.

**The client never names a piece of equipment.** A `RespiratoryTherapist` requests an assignment by naming only the `role` the equipment is to play in the home — `PRIMARY_STATIONARY`, `PORTABLE` or `BACKUP` — and the server selects the physical unit. That inversion exists because eligibility is a constraint problem no field operator should be solving by hand. Before the selection runs, four guards apply: the contract's billing dimension must not be `CLOSED` (`ERR_CONTRACT_CLOSED`); its certificate must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CMN_NOT_ACTIVE`); the patient must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`) and must not be on compliance hold (`ERR_PATIENT_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`); and the requested role must not already be filled — a contract that already holds a placement in `RESERVED` or `PLACED` for that same role is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_ALREADY_OCCUPIED`. Requesting the `PORTABLE` role carries one extra clinical guard: the contract's certificate must actually prescribe portable oxygen *and* the patient's mobility profile must be `LIMITED_AMBULATORY` or `ACTIVE_AMBULATORY`. A portable request for a `HOMEBOUND` patient, or for a certificate that does not prescribe portable, is rejected with `ERR_PORTABLE_NOT_INDICATED`.

Selection itself works over three inputs: the prescribed flow taken from the certificate the contract names, the branch that serves the contract's patient, and the logical clock's date. It proceeds as an eligibility filter, then a ranking, then a tie-break, with a defined behaviour when nothing survives.

The **eligibility filter** admits a fleet unit only when every one of the following holds: the unit belongs to the serving branch; the unit is in `IN_STOCK` — units in `RESERVED`, `DEPLOYED`, `IN_QUARANTINE` or `RETIRED` never participate at all; the unit's `equipment_class` is one of the classes the requested role permits; the unit's `max_continuous_lpm` is greater than or equal to the prescribed flow, compared at one decimal place; the unit's `next_pm_due_date` is on or after the logical clock's date, so a unit already overdue for maintenance is never sent to a patient; no recall notice exists whose model code matches the unit's `model_code` and whose status is `OPEN` or `IN_REMEDIATION`; and, when the unit's class is `LIQUID_RESERVOIR` or `LIQUID_PORTABLE`, the serving branch is liquid-certified. The permitted classes per role are fixed: `PRIMARY_STATIONARY` admits `STATIONARY_CONCENTRATOR` and `LIQUID_RESERVOIR`; `PORTABLE` admits `PORTABLE_CONCENTRATOR`, `LIQUID_PORTABLE` and `COMPRESSED_CYLINDER`; and `BACKUP` admits `COMPRESSED_CYLINDER` and `STATIONARY_CONCENTRATOR`.

The **ranking** is designed to minimise stranded capacity: surviving candidates are ordered by the difference between the unit's rated maximum continuous flow and the prescribed flow, ascending, so the tightest adequate unit is preferred over an oversized one. Where two candidates leave the same headroom, the **tie-break** prefers the unit whose maintenance is furthest away — that is, the later `next_pm_due_date` first — and where that is still tied, the older row wins, ordering by `created_at` ascending. The first candidate under that complete ordering is the selected unit. When the eligibility filter yields nothing at all, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_EQUIPMENT` and no placement row is created.

On success the server writes a **placement** — the junction that ties one contract to one physical unit for one role. The placement records the `contract_id` it belongs to, the `equipment_unit_id` the selection chose (a field the client may never supply), and the `role` requested. It begins life in `RESERVED`, with `placed_at` and `removed_at` both null, `removal_reason` at `NONE`, `malfunction_count` at 0, `last_symptom` at `NONE` and `last_severity_tier` at `NONE`. The selected unit moves `IN_STOCK → RESERVED` and its `version` increments by 1; the contract's `version` increments by 1 as well. Placement lifecycle spans `RESERVED`, `PLACED` and the two terminal states `REMOVED` and `RECALLED_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, with `removal_reason` drawn from `NONE`, `RELEASED`, `SETUP_FAILED`, `RECALL`, `MALFUNCTION`, `PICKUP` and `TRANSFER`. Because the assignment cascade creates the placement rather than the client, the placement list — filterable by `contract_id`, `equipment_unit_id`, `role` and `status` — is the only discovery channel for a newly created placement, and a single placement read exposes its malfunction counters.

Releasing a reservation is the exact compensating counterpart of assigning one, available to a `RespiratoryTherapist` before setup happens. It requires the placement to be `RESERVED` and the referenced unit to be `RESERVED`. It moves the placement `RESERVED → REMOVED` with `removal_reason` set to `RELEASED` and `removed_at` set to the logical clock, returns the unit `RESERVED → IN_STOCK` in its owning branch's pool, recomputes the contract's monthly allowable, and increments the `version` of the placement, the unit and the contract by 1 each. The invariant that makes this a true compensating pair is that the branch's in-stock count for the unit's model code returns to exactly its pre-assignment value.

The **monthly allowable** is the contract's per-month price before any proration, and it is recomputed at five moments: when the contract enters service, when it returns after a pickup, on either resume-billing transition, when a reserved placement is released, and when a Class I recall pulls placements out of service. It is built from the placements on the contract whose status is exactly `PLACED` — reserved, removed and recalled-out-of-service placements are excluded — each joined to its fleet unit by `equipment_unit_id` regardless of that unit's own current status. No time window applies; every currently-placed placement participates.

For each such placement, the calculation starts from a base amount fixed by the unit's equipment class: 7752 cents for a `STATIONARY_CONCENTRATOR`, 7340 cents for a `PORTABLE_CONCENTRATOR`, 9315 cents for a `LIQUID_RESERVOIR`, 8420 cents for a `LIQUID_PORTABLE`, and 2520 cents for a `COMPRESSED_CYLINDER`. That base is multiplied by the role factor for the placement's role — the full 1.00 for `PRIMARY_STATIONARY`, one quarter at 0.25 for `PORTABLE`, and one tenth at 0.10 for `BACKUP` — then by the basis factor the contract's pricing basis selects: 0.7358 for `COMPETITIVE_BID_SINGLE_PAYMENT`, 0.9050 for `FEE_SCHEDULE_ADJUSTED`, and 1.0000 for `CASH`. The product is then multiplied by the statutory sequestration factor of 0.98, which applies to every line without exception. **Each line item is rounded half up to whole cents at that point**, and only then added into the running total, which is exact integer arithmetic from there on. Summing the lines at full precision and rounding once at the end is a different and explicitly rejected order: a fee-schedule-adjusted contract holding a stationary concentrator in the primary role alongside a portable concentrator in the portable role produces per-line values of 6875 and 1627 for a total of 8502 cents, whereas sum-then-round would have produced 8503. Placement processing order does not affect the total, since the total is a sum. A contract with no placed placement yields a monthly allowable of 0 cents.

### 3.5. Route Planning, Stop Scheduling and Field Execution

Physical work reaches the home through a **delivery route**: one driver, one branch, one service date. A `BranchManager` creates a route by supplying the `branch_id` whose driver will run it, the `service_date` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date, the `driver_user_id` — the opaque operator identifier of the `DeliveryDriver` assigned to the route — and the shift boundaries `shift_start_utc` and `shift_end_utc` as ISO 8601 UTC instants. The shift must be coherent with the service date: the end instant must be strictly after the start instant, and the UTC calendar date of the start instant must equal the supplied service date. A shift that violates either condition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT`. A new route opens in `PLANNING` with `total_o2_liters_committed` at 0, `refill_slots_used` at 0 and `planned_stop_count` at 0. Routes may be listed with filters on `branch_id`, `status` and `service_date`, and the route lifecycle spans `PLANNING`, `LOCKED`, `DISPATCHED` and the terminal `COMPLETED`.

Stops are scheduled onto a route by a `BranchManager` while the route is still editable — that is, while it is in `PLANNING` or `LOCKED`. A stop that is scheduled onto a route already `DISPATCHED` or `COMPLETED` is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_EDITABLE`. Each stop names the `route_id` it belongs to and the `contract_id` being serviced there, and the two must belong to the same branch: a route whose branch differs from the branch serving the contract's patient is rejected with `ERR_STOP_BRANCH_MISMATCH`. The `stop_type` declares what happens at the door and must be one of `SETUP_DELIVERY`, `TANK_EXCHANGE`, `PORTABLE_REFILL`, `PICKUP` or `MAINTENANCE_SWAP`. The promised arrival window is given by `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, with the end instant required to be strictly after the start instant or the stop is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. `service_minutes` records the expected minutes on site and accepts 5 through 240. `cylinders_requested` records how many E-cylinders the driver will drop, accepts 0 through 6, defaults to 0, and is consulted only for setup deliveries, tank exchanges and maintenance swaps — supplying a non-zero cylinder count on a `PICKUP` or a `PORTABLE_REFILL` stop is rejected with `ERR_CYLINDERS_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

Which stop types a contract may receive depends on where the contract sits on its service dimension. A `SETUP_DELIVERY` stop is by definition scheduled while the contract is still `PENDING_SETUP`, so the in-service requirement does not apply to it; but it does require that the contract already holds at least one placement in `RESERVED`, because there is nothing to install otherwise, and a setup stop for a contract with no reserved equipment is rejected with `ERR_NO_RESERVED_EQUIPMENT`. The other four stop types all require the contract's service dimension to be exactly `IN_SERVICE`, failing with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_IN_SERVICE` otherwise. A `PORTABLE_REFILL` stop carries an additional clinical requirement: the contract must hold at least one placement in `PLACED` whose fleet unit is of class `LIQUID_PORTABLE`, since only a liquid portable vessel can be refilled. A refill stop for a contract with no such placed unit is rejected with `ERR_REFILL_REQUIRES_LIQUID_PORTABLE`.

Timing on a route is enforced with the branch's trailing turnaround buffer. The stop's **buffered span** starts at its `window_start_utc` and ends at its `window_end_utc` extended forward by the branch's `tank_exchange_buffer_minutes`. That buffered span must fit inside the route's shift: the window start must be at or after the shift start, and the buffered end must be at or before the shift end, or the stop is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_SHIFT`. The buffered span is treated as **half-open** — it includes its start instant and excludes its buffered end instant — and it must not intersect the buffered half-open span of any other stop on the same route that is still in `PLANNED`. Because the spans are half-open, adjacency does not conflict: a new stop whose `window_start_utc` falls exactly on an existing stop's buffered end instant is accepted. Any genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Each stop is stamped with a **committed litre figure** at creation, in `liters_committed`, and that figure is never revised afterwards. A `PORTABLE_REFILL` stop commits a full liquid portable vessel of 1085 litres regardless of anything else on the stop. A `SETUP_DELIVERY`, `TANK_EXCHANGE` or `MAINTENANCE_SWAP` stop commits 680 litres for each E-cylinder requested — so a tank exchange requesting three cylinders commits 2040 litres, and one requesting none commits nothing. A `PICKUP` stop commits 0 litres, since the driver is collecting rather than delivering. Three route-level roll-ups are re-derived from those per-stop figures whenever a stop is created or cancelled, whenever the route is locked, and whenever the route manifest is read: `total_o2_liters_committed` is the sum of `liters_committed` over the route's stops in `PLANNED`, `COMPLETED` or `MISSED` — cancelled stops are excluded, but a missed stop keeps its litres committed for the day; `refill_slots_used` counts the route's stops whose type is `PORTABLE_REFILL` and whose status is `PLANNED` or `COMPLETED`; and `planned_stop_count` counts the route's stops whose status is exactly `PLANNED`. All three are order-insensitive.

Stops also carry a `sequence_index`, a 1-based position within the route. It is assigned by ordering the route's non-cancelled stops by `window_start_utc` ascending and breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, and it is **recomputed for every remaining non-cancelled stop** on each stop creation and each stop cancellation — inserting an early-morning stop into an existing route renumbers everything after it. A new stop opens in `PLANNED` with `safety_result` at `NOT_ASSESSED`; the in-home assessment fields `smoking_in_home`, `electrical_pass` and `patient_teachback_score` are null until a setup is closed, `education_topics_covered` is an empty array, `safety_findings_count` is 0, and `completed_at` and `completed_by_user_id` are null. Stop lifecycle spans `PLANNED` and the three terminal states `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` and `CANCELLED`. Stops may be listed with filters on `route_id`, `contract_id`, `stop_type` and `status`.

Scheduling a `PICKUP` stop is more than paperwork — it moves the contract's service dimension `IN_SERVICE → PICKUP_SCHEDULED` and increments the contract's `version` by 1, in addition to creating the stop in `PLANNED` and incrementing the route's `version`. That transition requires the target route to be in `PLANNING`.

A `BranchManager` locks a planned route to freeze its stop list, advancing it `PLANNING → LOCKED`. Two capacity guards apply at that moment. The route must carry at least one planned stop, or the lock is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_HAS_NO_STOPS` — an empty route cannot be locked. And the route's used refill slots must not exceed the branch's `refill_capacity_per_day`, or the lock is refused with `ERR_REFILL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a branch configured for the default four refill stops a day can lock a route carrying four but not one carrying five. On success the litre roll-up is re-evaluated and the route's `version` increments by 1. Locking is reversible: the same manager may unlock a route, moving it `LOCKED → PLANNING` and incrementing its `version`, any number of times before dispatch, so a locked route can be reopened for stop edits repeatedly. Dispatch releases a locked route to the driver, moving it `LOCKED → DISPATCHED` and incrementing its `version`. There is no path from `PLANNING` straight to `DISPATCHED`.

Field execution happens only on a dispatched route. All three closing actions — generic completion, setup completion and marking a stop missed — require the owning route to be `DISPATCHED`, failing with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_DISPATCHED` otherwise, and all three require the stop itself to be in `PLANNED`. The two completion actions are also type-segregated: setup completion applies only to a stop whose type is `SETUP_DELIVERY`, and generic completion applies only to a stop whose type is `TANK_EXCHANGE`, `PORTABLE_REFILL`, `PICKUP` or `MAINTENANCE_SWAP`. Calling either on the wrong type is rejected with `ERR_STOP_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

A `DeliveryDriver` closing a non-setup stop moves it `PLANNED → COMPLETED`, stamping `completed_at` with the logical clock and `completed_by_user_id` with the driver's `X-User-ID`, and increments the stop's `version` by 1. When the completed stop is a `PICKUP`, that completion additionally drives the contract's service dimension `PICKUP_SCHEDULED → RETURNED` and, in the same request, moves every placement on the contract that is `PLACED` to `REMOVED` with `removal_reason` set to `PICKUP` and `removed_at` set to the logical clock, returns every fleet unit those placements referenced from `DEPLOYED` back to `IN_STOCK`, and sets the contract's `monthly_allowable_cents` to 0 — there is no equipment in the home, so there is nothing to bill for future months. The contract's `version`, each placement's `version` and each unit's `version` all increment by 1.

The same driver may instead record a stop as missed, moving it `PLANNED → MISSED`. That stamps `completed_at` with the logical clock and `completed_by_user_id` with the driver's `X-User-ID`, decrements the route's `planned_stop_count` by 1, and increments the stop's `version` by 1 — but it deliberately **leaves `total_o2_liters_committed` unchanged**, because the oxygen was loaded onto the truck for the day whether or not the driver got through the door.

Cancellation is the compensating counterpart of stop creation and belongs to the `BranchManager`. It applies to a stop in `PLANNED` on a route that is still in `PLANNING` or `LOCKED`, and moves the stop `PLANNED → CANCELLED`. It decrements the route's `planned_stop_count` by 1, **decreases the route's `total_o2_liters_committed` by exactly this stop's `liters_committed`** so the roll-up returns to its precise pre-creation value, re-derives `refill_slots_used`, and recomputes `sequence_index` for every remaining non-cancelled stop of the route. Both the stop's `version` and the route's `version` increment by 1. When the cancelled stop was a `PICKUP` and no other `PLANNED` pickup stop remains for its contract, the cancellation also reverts the contract's service dimension `PICKUP_SCHEDULED → IN_SERVICE` and increments the contract's `version` by 1 — the pickup scheduling step is fully undone.

Route completion is never requested; it **bubbles up** from the children. At the end of every generic stop completion, every setup completion and every missed-stop recording, the server re-evaluates the owning route: if the route is `DISPATCHED` and every stop belonging to it now sits in `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `CANCELLED`, the route moves `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED`, its `planned_stop_count` is set to 0, and its `version` increments by 1. The parent's state is therefore derived from its children's states and re-derived on every child terminal transition.

For dispatch-day oversight there is a computed manifest read that persists nothing. It re-evaluates the litre roll-up over the route's current stops and returns `route_id`, the route's `status`, `planned_stop_count`, `completed_stop_count`, `missed_stop_count`, `cancelled_stop_count`, `refill_slots_used`, the branch's `refill_capacity_per_day` for comparison, and `total_o2_liters_committed`.

### 3.6. In-Home Setup, Education and the Safety Assessment

Setup is where the clinical, the physical and the safety threads meet. A `RespiratoryTherapist` closes a `SETUP_DELIVERY` stop on a dispatched route by reporting the results of the in-home assessment: `smoking_in_home`, a boolean recording whether anyone in the household smokes; `electrical_pass`, a boolean recording whether the home's electrical inspection passed; `patient_teachback_score`, an integer from 0 through 5 recording how much of the education the patient could repeat back; and, optionally, `education_topics_covered`, an array of topic strings documenting what was taught. All four are persisted onto the stop, which moves `PLANNED → COMPLETED`.

Two derived values are computed from those findings. `safety_result` is `FAIL` exactly when smoking was found in the home, and `PASS` otherwise; it only ever remains at `NOT_ASSESSED` on a setup stop that has not yet been completed. `safety_findings_count` counts how many of three specific predicates hold: that smoking was found in the home, that the electrical inspection did *not* pass, and that the teach-back score is below 3. It therefore ranges from 0 through 3, and a home with a smoker, a failed electrical inspection and a teach-back score of 2 yields 3.

When the assessment passes, the contract enters service. The preconditions are that the contract's service dimension is `PENDING_SETUP`, that the stop is a `SETUP_DELIVERY` in `PLANNED`, that its route is `DISPATCHED`, that the contract holds at least one placement in `RESERVED`, and that no smoking was found — which is what makes the safety result `PASS`. The contract moves `PENDING_SETUP → IN_SERVICE`, and a chain of consequences follows in the same request: the stop is stamped `COMPLETED` with `safety_result` `PASS`, `completed_at` set to the logical clock and `completed_by_user_id` set to the therapist's `X-User-ID`; the stop's safety findings count is computed; the contract's `first_service_month` is stamped with the `YYYY-MM` of the logical clock, permanently marking the month service began; every placement on the contract that is `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → PLACED` with `placed_at` set to the logical clock; every fleet unit those placements reference moves `RESERVED → DEPLOYED`; the contract's monthly allowable is recomputed over the newly placed equipment; the patient's safety posture is recomputed under the initial-assessment variant, with `risk_variant_applied` set to `INITIAL_ASSESSMENT` and the compliance hold re-evaluated; the route is bubbled up to `COMPLETED` if every one of its stops is now terminal; and the `version` of the stop, the contract, each placement, each unit and the patient all increment by 1.

When smoking is found, the setup fails, and it fails as a **four-hop transitive cascade** in which each hop is caused by the hop before it. Hop one records the stop as `COMPLETED` with `safety_result` `FAIL`, `completed_at` set to the logical clock and `completed_by_user_id` set to the therapist's `X-User-ID`. Hop two, *because* hop one recorded a failing safety result, moves the contract's service dimension `PENDING_SETUP → SETUP_FAILED` and — branching off the same hop — moves the contract's billing dimension to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `SETUP_FAILED`. Hop three, *because* hop two moved the contract to `SETUP_FAILED`, moves every placement on the contract that is `RESERVED` to `REMOVED` with `removal_reason` set to `SETUP_FAILED` and `removed_at` set to the logical clock. Hop four, *because* hop three removed those placements, moves every fleet unit they referenced from `RESERVED` to `IN_QUARANTINE` with `quarantine_reason` set to `SAFETY` — the equipment came back off a truck from an unsafe home and does not go straight back into the pool. Alongside the four hops, the contract's `first_service_month` **remains null** and its `monthly_allowable_cents` is set to 0; the patient's safety posture is recomputed under the initial-assessment variant with `risk_variant_applied` set to `INITIAL_ASSESSMENT`; the route is bubbled up to `COMPLETED` if every one of its stops is now terminal; and the `version` of every row the cascade touched increments by 1.

The patient's **oxygen safety risk score** is built from five factors, each expressed on a 0 to 100 scale and each computed identically no matter which variant is being applied. The factors are read from a single reference stop: the setup-delivery stop in `COMPLETED` whose contract belongs to one of this patient's rental contracts, ranked by `completed_at` descending and tie-broken by `created_at` ascending — the most recent completed setup, in other words, and null when the patient has never had one.

- The **smoking factor** is 100 when that reference stop exists and recorded smoking in the home, and 0 otherwise.
- The **electrical factor** is 100 when that reference stop exists and its electrical inspection did not pass, and 0 otherwise.
- The **teach-back factor** is 20 points for every step the patient missed: subtract the recorded teach-back score from 5 and multiply by 20, giving 0 for a perfect score of 5 and 100 for a score of 0. When no reference stop exists the factor is 0.
- The **flow factor** reads the patient's single `ACTIVE` certificate. It is 0 when there is no active certificate at all or the prescribed flow is at most 2.0 litres per minute; 20 when the prescribed flow is above 2.0 and at most 4.0; 40 when it is above 4.0 and at most 6.0; 60 when it is above 6.0 and at most 9.0; and 100 when it exceeds 9.0.
- The **incident factor** sums `malfunction_count` across every rental contract of this patient whose billing dimension is not `CLOSED`, with no time window, then awards 30 points per report and caps the result at 100. One report gives 30, three give 90, four or more give 100.

The **initial-assessment variant** is a weighted average of those five factors: the smoking factor at weight 0.30, the electrical factor at 0.15, the teach-back factor at 0.15, the flow factor at 0.30 and the incident factor at 0.10. The weighted sum is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once, with no intermediate rounding. Because it averages, a single catastrophic factor is diluted by the four benign ones.

The **ongoing-surveillance variant** is deliberately far harsher. It takes the largest of the five factors as its base, counts how many of the five factors are greater than zero, and adds 5 points for each non-zero factor beyond the first. The result is capped at 100.00 and rounded half up to two decimal places, again exactly once. On identical factor values the two variants can diverge dramatically: a factor vector of smoking 0, electrical 100, teach-back 60, flow 60 and incident 30 yields 45.00 under the initial-assessment variant and 100.00 under ongoing surveillance.

Which variant is applied depends entirely on what triggered the recomputation. The initial-assessment variant is applied by the two setup-completion outcomes described above — the move into service and the setup-failure cascade — because both represent the in-home education and safety assessment. The ongoing-surveillance variant is applied when a `RespiratoryTherapist` records a continued-need surveillance reassessment for the patient, and when a malfunction report is filed against one of the patient's placements. Whichever variant runs, its result is written to `oxygen_safety_risk_score`, `risk_variant_applied` is set to name the variant that produced it, and `compliance_hold` is re-evaluated as described in §2.11. A surveillance reassessment does nothing else: it recomputes, restamps the variant, re-evaluates the hold, and increments the patient's `version` by 1.

Alongside the reassessment write there is a computed risk read that persists nothing at all. It returns `patient_id`, the five factors as `factor_smoking`, `factor_electrical`, `factor_teachback`, `factor_flow` and `factor_incident`, **both** scores evaluated over that same factor vector as `initial_assessment_score` and `ongoing_surveillance_score`, and the currently stored posture as `persisted_score`, `persisted_variant` and `compliance_hold`. It is the tool a therapist uses to see what a reassessment *would* do before doing it.

### 3.7. Malfunction Response, Preventive Maintenance and Recall Remediation

Equipment fails in the field, and how hard the system reacts depends on how the failure presented. A `RespiratoryTherapist` files a malfunction report against a placement that is currently `PLACED`, supplying the `symptom` — one of `NO_FLOW`, `ALARM_CONTINUOUS`, `LOW_PURITY`, `POWER_FAILURE` or `LEAK` — and `oxygen_gap_minutes`, the number of whole minutes the patient went without prescribed oxygen. Filing against a placement in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The report is graded into a **severity tier** from the request body alone; no stored row participates in the grading. The tier is `TIER_1` when the symptom is `NO_FLOW` or `LOW_PURITY`, **or** the gap is 60 minutes or more. Failing that, the tier is `TIER_2` when the symptom is `ALARM_CONTINUOUS` or `POWER_FAILURE`, **or** the gap is 15 minutes or more. Otherwise the tier is `TIER_3`. The two clauses of each test are alternatives, so a `LEAK` with a 90-minute gap grades `TIER_1` on the gap alone, while a `NO_FLOW` with a zero-minute gap grades `TIER_1` on the symptom alone; a `LEAK` with a 20-minute gap grades `TIER_2`, and a `LEAK` with a 5-minute gap grades `TIER_3`. Minutes are compared as integers with no rounding.

Every report, whatever its tier, records the same bookkeeping: the placement's `last_symptom` is set to the reported symptom and its `last_severity_tier` to the computed tier, the placement's `malfunction_count` and the owning contract's `malfunction_count` each increase by 1, and the contract's `cumulative_oxygen_gap_minutes` increases by exactly the reported gap. The patient's safety posture is then recomputed under the **ongoing-surveillance** variant, `risk_variant_applied` is set to `ONGOING_SURVEILLANCE`, and `compliance_hold` is re-evaluated — which is how a run of incidents eventually freezes the account. The `version` of the placement, the contract and the patient each increment by 1.

Only `TIER_1` pulls the equipment. On a `TIER_1` report the placement additionally moves `PLACED → REMOVED` with `removal_reason` set to `MALFUNCTION` and `removed_at` set to the logical clock, the fleet unit it referenced moves `DEPLOYED → IN_QUARANTINE` with `quarantine_reason` set to `MALFUNCTION`, and the contract's `monthly_allowable_cents` is recomputed over what is left in the home — which drops the allowable, and may drop it to 0 if that was the only placed unit. `TIER_2` and `TIER_3` reports leave both the placement and the unit exactly where they are, in service; only the counters and the risk score move.

A quarantined unit re-enters the pool through service, which is how both preventive maintenance and recall remediation are recorded. A `RespiratoryTherapist` services a unit that is `IN_QUARANTINE`, moving it `IN_QUARANTINE → IN_STOCK`. Servicing restamps `next_pm_due_date` to the logical clock's date advanced by the unit's own `pm_interval_days` — a unit serviced on the first of a month with the default 180-day interval comes due 180 days later — and resets `quarantine_reason` to `NONE`. If, at that moment, a recall notice exists whose `model_code` equals this unit's `model_code` and whose status is `OPEN` or `IN_REMEDIATION`, the service also stamps the unit's `recall_remediated_at` with the logical clock and increases that notice's `remediated_unit_count`. The unit's `version` increments by 1. A `BranchManager` may instead retire a unit permanently, from either `IN_STOCK` or `IN_QUARANTINE`, moving it to the terminal `RETIRED` and incrementing its `version` by 1. A retired unit is invisible to equipment selection and is excluded from every recall count.

A `ComplianceAuditor` publishes a recall by supplying just two things: the `model_code` being recalled and the `severity`, one of `CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III`. Everything else is derived. The notice opens in `OPEN` with `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, and its `remediation_deadline_utc` is `issued_at` advanced by whole days according to severity: 5 days for `CLASS_I`, 30 days for `CLASS_II` and 90 days for `CLASS_III`. So a `CLASS_I` notice issued at midnight on the first of a month is due at midnight on the sixth, and a `CLASS_III` notice issued at the same instant is due 90 days later; the deadline is rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits.

Three unit counts are stamped onto the notice, and the difference between them matters. `affected_unit_count` counts the fleet units whose `model_code` matches the notice and whose status is `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED`, `DEPLOYED` or `IN_QUARANTINE`; `RETIRED` units are excluded. It is evaluated **before** any recall cascade mutates a single row, and it is **never recomputed afterwards** — it is a permanent snapshot of the exposure at issue time, and it stays put even as the cascade moves every one of those units into quarantine. `auto_quarantined_unit_count` is, for a `CLASS_I` notice, the number of matching units that were `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED` at that same pre-cascade instant — that is, the ones the cascade is about to pull — and is 0 for a `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III` notice, which quarantines nothing. It too is a frozen snapshot. `remediated_unit_count`, by contrast, is a **live recount**, evaluated whenever the notice is read: the number of matching units whose `recall_remediated_at` is not null and is **at or after** the notice's `issued_at`, an inclusive lower bound, so a unit serviced at exactly the issue instant counts as remediated. A unit remediated before the notice was issued does not count.

`CLASS_I` is the only severity that touches the fleet, and it does so as a **four-hop transitive cascade** whose hops are strictly causal. Hop one moves every non-retired fleet unit whose `model_code` matches the notice from `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED` into `IN_QUARANTINE` with `quarantine_reason` set to `RECALL`; units already `IN_QUARANTINE` stay there. Hop two is the recall notice itself recording the snapshots described above — taken, as stated, from the state that existed before hop one. Hop three, *because* hop one quarantined those units, moves every placement in `PLACED` that references one of them from `PLACED → RECALLED_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, setting `removal_reason` to `RECALL` and `removed_at` to the logical clock. Note the distinct terminal state: a recall does not merely `REMOVE` a placement, it marks it recalled out of service, and that distinction is load-bearing later. Hop four, *because* hop three took those placements out of service, moves the billing dimension of each affected contract to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `RECALL`, and recomputes each such contract's `monthly_allowable_cents` over its remaining placed equipment. The `version` of every fleet unit, placement and rental contract the cascade touches increments by 1. A `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III` notice records its snapshots and stops there — no unit is quarantined, no placement is disturbed, no contract is suspended — but such a notice still blocks its model code from being selected for new placements, because equipment selection excludes any unit whose model has an `OPEN` or `IN_REMEDIATION` notice regardless of class.

A recall notice then walks a two-step lifecycle. A `BranchManager` acknowledges it, moving it `OPEN → IN_REMEDIATION` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Later the same manager closes it, moving it `IN_REMEDIATION → CLOSED` and incrementing its `version` by 1 — but only once the live `remediated_unit_count` is greater than or equal to the frozen `affected_unit_count`. Closing a notice whose remediated count still falls short is refused with `ERR_RECALL_NOT_REMEDIATED`. Because the affected count is frozen and the remediated count is live, closure is reached by servicing units one at a time until the live count catches up with the snapshot; retiring a unit does not advance the remediated count, so a notice covering units that are scrapped rather than serviced can only be closed once enough of the remainder have been serviced. There is no path from `OPEN` straight to `CLOSED`.

Recalled equipment also leaves a lasting mark on billing recovery. A contract may not resume billing while any of its placements still sits in `RECALLED_OUT_OF_SERVICE`; the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_HAS_RECALLED_EQUIPMENT`, and the only way forward is to take the recalled placement out of the way and re-equip the patient. Finally, `replaces_unit_id` documents the fleet's replacement lineage: when a new unit is added as the successor to an old one, the unit it names must itself be `IN_QUARANTINE` or `RETIRED`, since a unit still in circulation has not been replaced. Naming a unit in any other status is rejected with `ERR_REPLACED_UNIT_NOT_REPLACEABLE`.

### 3.8. Capped-Rental Billing, the Cap Ceiling and Reversal

A `BillingSpecialist` posts one rental month at a time by naming the `service_month` as a `YYYY-MM` string. Posting is the most heavily guarded action in the system, because it is the one that moves money. The contract's billing dimension must not be `CLOSED`, or the post is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_CLOSED`. It must be exactly `ACCRUING`: a contract sitting in `SUSPENDED` or `CAP_REACHED` cannot accept another month, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_BILLING_SUSPENDED`. The contract's service dimension must be exactly `IN_SERVICE`, or the post is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_IN_SERVICE` — a contract whose equipment has been picked up or whose setup failed has nothing to bill for. The certificate the contract hangs off must be `ACTIVE`, or the post is refused with `ERR_CMN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The month itself must be at or after `first_service_month` under a plain lexicographic comparison of the `YYYY-MM` strings, or the post is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_MONTH_BEFORE_START`; it must be at or before the `YYYY-MM` of the logical clock, or it is refused as a future month with `ERR_SERVICE_MONTH_IN_FUTURE`; and it must not already appear as the `service_month` of an element of `posted_months`, or it is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_MONTH_ALREADY_POSTED`. Finally the contract must actually have something in the home: its `monthly_allowable_cents` must be greater than 0, or the post is refused with `ERR_NO_PLACED_EQUIPMENT`.

The amount posted for a month is the monthly allowable **prorated by days of service in that month**. Let the number of days in the posted service month be its true calendar length — 31 for a 31-day month, 30 for a 30-day month, 28 or 29 for February depending on the year. For every month other than the contract's `first_service_month`, the days of service equal the days in the month, so the month bills in full. For the `first_service_month` alone, the days of service are the days in the month, minus the day-of-month of the logical clock at the instant the service dimension first became `IN_SERVICE`, plus one — an inclusive count of the days from the setup date through the end of the month. A contract that entered service on the 10th of a 31-day month therefore has 22 days of service in that month, and one that entered service on the 1st has the full 31. The posted amount is the monthly allowable multiplied by the days of service, divided by the days in the month, and rounded half up to whole cents. A monthly allowable of 8502 cents over 22 of 31 days gives 6033 cents.

A successful post appends one element to `posted_months`, and that element records four things: the `service_month` it covers, the `amount_cents` computed above, the `days_in_service` used in the proration, and the `posted_at` instant taken from the logical clock. It also increments `posted_month_count` by 1, increases `lifetime_paid_cents` by exactly the appended amount, sets `last_posted_service_month` to the largest `service_month` now present in `posted_months` under lexicographic comparison, and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

Whether that post leaves the contract accruing or exhausts its cap depends on the **cap month position**, and the position is computed one of two entirely different ways. The **service-month basis** counts elapsed calendar months: take twelve times the difference between the candidate month's year and the `first_service_month`'s year, add the difference between their month numbers, and add one. A gap in billing still advances this position, because the count is over the calendar, not over the claims. The **paid-month basis** counts only months already paid: count the elements of `posted_months` whose `service_month` is strictly less than the candidate month under lexicographic comparison, and add one. A skipped month is free under this basis, because a month that was never posted never enters the count.

The contract's `pricing_basis` selects the basis, and nothing else does. A `COMPETITIVE_BID_SINGLE_PAYMENT` contract uses the service-month basis, because competitive-bidding single-payment contracts are paid against the rental period rather than against submitted claims. A `FEE_SCHEDULE_ADJUSTED` or `CASH` contract uses the paid-month basis. The divergence is real and reachable on identical data: a contract with `first_service_month` of `2026-08` and a single posted month of `2026-08`, posting `2026-10`, sits at effective position 3 under the service-month basis but position 2 under the paid-month basis. The `months_remaining` figure is the cap limit minus the effective position, floored at 0 so it never reports a negative allowance.

The boundary is pinned three ways, and the difference between the second and the third is a single month. When the effective position is **strictly below** `cap_month_limit`, the post succeeds and the billing dimension stays `ACCRUING` — the self-loop transition. When the effective position **equals** `cap_month_limit` exactly, the post still succeeds — the month is paid — and the billing dimension then advances `ACCRUING → CAP_REACHED`. When the effective position is **strictly above** `cap_month_limit`, nothing is posted at all and the request is refused with `ERR_CAP_MONTHS_EXHAUSTED`. The cap limit itself is a per-contract field, defaulting to 36 months, and it may never exceed 36. Because whether a given month lands on the self-loop or on the advance to `CAP_REACHED` depends on which basis the pricing selects, two contracts with identical posted history and identical cap limits can be in different billing states after posting the same month.

Reversal is the compensating counterpart of posting, and it is the only way a contract climbs back down from its cap. A `BillingSpecialist` reverses a posted month by naming it in the path; the month must match the `service_month` of an existing element of `posted_months`, or the request is refused with `ERR_POSTED_MONTH_NOT_FOUND`, and the contract must not be `CLOSED`. A reversal from `ACCRUING` is unconditional beyond that and leaves the contract in `ACCRUING` — another self-loop. A reversal from `CAP_REACHED` moves the contract `CAP_REACHED → ACCRUING`, but only when the cap position, **recomputed over `posted_months` with the reversed month removed**, falls strictly below `cap_month_limit`; if removing that month would still leave the contract at or above its cap, the reversal is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_WOULD_NOT_CLEAR_CAP`. That guard has a sharp consequence for competitive-bid contracts: because the service-month basis counts elapsed calendar months and ignores `posted_months` entirely, removing an element cannot change its position, so a `COMPETITIVE_BID_SINGLE_PAYMENT` contract that has reached its cap can never reverse its way back to `ACCRUING`.

Every reversal removes the element whose `service_month` equals the named month, decrements `posted_month_count` by 1, decreases `lifetime_paid_cents` by **exactly the removed element's `amount_cents`**, recomputes `last_posted_service_month` over the remaining elements (leaving it null when none remain), and increments the contract's `version` by 1. Because the decrease uses the stored amount rather than a recomputation, posting a month and then reversing it returns `lifetime_paid_cents` to its exact prior integer value even if the monthly allowable has changed in between — the quantity is conserved.

Suspension is never requested; it always arrives as a cascade, and it can arrive from either `ACCRUING` or `CAP_REACHED`. Five distinct upstream events suspend a contract, and each stamps a matching `suspension_reason`: a certificate voided sets `CMN_VOIDED`, a certificate expired by the clock sweep sets `CMN_EXPIRED`, a `CLASS_I` recall sets `RECALL`, a patient transfer request sets `TRANSFER`, and a failed in-home setup sets `SETUP_FAILED`. The setup-failure route is the one exception to the two source states: it can only be reached from `ACCRUING`, because a contract whose setup has not yet succeeded has never posted a month and so cannot be at its cap. Outside suspension the reason field reads `NONE`.

Resuming is requested, by a `BillingSpecialist`, and it carries four preconditions beyond the contract not being `CLOSED`: the billing dimension must be `SUSPENDED`; the linked certificate must be back to `ACTIVE`, or the resume is refused with `ERR_CMN_NOT_ACTIVE`; the patient must be `ACTIVE`; and no placement on the contract may still sit in `RECALLED_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, or the resume is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_HAS_RECALLED_EQUIPMENT`. Which state the contract resumes into is decided by the same cap arithmetic as posting, evaluated over the current `posted_months`: strictly below the cap limit resumes to `ACCRUING`, exactly at the limit resumes to `CAP_REACHED`, and above the limit is refused with `ERR_CAP_MONTHS_EXHAUSTED`. Either way `suspension_reason` returns to `NONE`, `monthly_allowable_cents` is recomputed over whatever equipment is currently `PLACED` — which is how a contract that lost a unit to a malfunction quarantine comes back at a lower allowable — and the contract's `version` increments by 1.

That recomputation of the cap position at resume time interacts with transfers in a way worth stating plainly. A contract that had reached its cap, was suspended by a transfer request, and then has that suspension lifted will resume into `CAP_REACHED` only if its effective position is still exactly at the limit; a competitive-bid contract whose calendar has moved on will have a position above the limit and cannot resume at all until the cap limit question is settled, while a paid-month-basis contract resumes to `CAP_REACHED` and then rejects its next posted month on the cap check rather than on the billing-state check.

Closing ends the billing dimension permanently. A `BillingSpecialist` may close a contract from `ACCRUING`, from `CAP_REACHED` or from `SUSPENDED`, but only when its service dimension has come to rest in `RETURNED` or `SETUP_FAILED`; a contract still `PENDING_SETUP`, `IN_SERVICE` or `PICKUP_SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_RETURNED`. Closing sets `monthly_allowable_cents` to 0 and increments the contract's `version` by 1. The one exception is the patient-discharge cascade, which closes a `SUSPENDED` contract unconditionally, without regard to the service dimension. `CLOSED` is terminal on the billing dimension: nothing may be posted, reversed or resumed on a closed contract, and no equipment may be assigned to it.

For oversight there is a computed cap-summary read that persists nothing and shows both bases side by side. Its candidate month is the contract's `last_posted_service_month`, or `first_service_month` when no month has been posted. It returns `contract_id`, `candidate_service_month`, `service_month_basis_position`, `paid_month_basis_position`, `variant_applied` naming which basis the contract's pricing selects, `effective_position`, `cap_month_limit`, `months_remaining`, `monthly_allowable_cents`, `lifetime_paid_cents` and `posted_month_count`. It is how a billing specialist sees exactly how far a contract is from its ceiling — and how far it would be under the other basis.

### 3.9. Travel Supply Authorization, Branch Transfer and Discharge

Patients travel, and travelling on oxygen requires a computed supply. An `IntakeCoordinator` opens a travel authorization against a rental contract, supplying `depart_utc` and `return_utc` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, optionally `receiving_branch_id` naming the branch that will service the patient at the destination — null when no hand-off is arranged — optionally `destination_altitude_feet`, an elevation from 0 through 15000 feet that defaults to 0, and the mandatory `expected_contract_version` described in §2.7. The contract must not be `CLOSED` and its certificate must be `ACTIVE`, the patient must be `ACTIVE` and not on compliance hold, and when a receiving branch is named it must differ from the patient's current `branch_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_INVALID`. The return instant must be strictly after the departure instant, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRAVEL_INTERVAL`. And the trip must not collide with another trip: the new authorization's **closed** interval from `depart_utc` through `return_utc` must not intersect the closed interval of any existing authorization on the same contract whose status is `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. Because the intervals are closed, **touching endpoints do conflict** — a new authorization departing at exactly the instant an existing one returns is rejected with `ERR_TRAVEL_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` — and no buffer is applied. A `DENIED` authorization does not block anything.

The supply is then computed as a nine-step waterfall, and the step order is pinned because the dependencies read like a cycle: the reserve percentage is keyed on trip length, the usable hours are keyed on both trip length and the prescribed daily hours, and the handling surcharge is keyed on the cylinder count the reserve produced. Each step is rounded before the next step reads it; carrying full precision forward is a different and explicitly rejected order.

- **Step 0 — trip hours.** Take the elapsed time from `depart_utc` to `return_utc` in whole seconds, divide by 3600, and take the floor. `trip_hours` is therefore whole hours with any partial hour discarded: a trip of 30 hours and 45 minutes yields 30.
- **Step 1 — altitude factor.** The `altitude_gain` is the destination elevation minus the patient's own `altitude_feet`, floored at 0 so a trip to a lower elevation gains nothing. Divide the gain by 1000 and take the ceiling to get the number of thousand-foot steps, multiply by 0.03, add 1.00, round half up to two decimal places, and finally cap the result at 1.30. A patient living at 500 feet travelling to 6500 feet gains 6000 feet, which is six steps, giving 1.18; a gain of 6001 feet is seven steps, giving 1.21; and a gain of 12000 feet computes 1.36 but is capped to 1.30. `altitude_factor` is stored to two decimals.
- **Step 2 — effective flow.** Multiply the certificate's `prescribed_lpm` by the altitude factor and round half up to two decimal places. A prescription of 3.50 litres per minute at a factor of 1.18 gives an `effective_lpm` of 4.13.
- **Step 3 — base litres.** The `usable_hours` are the lesser of the trip hours and the certificate's `hours_per_day` multiplied by the number of calendar days the trip spans, where the day count is the trip hours divided by 24 and rounded up. `base_liters` is then the effective flow multiplied by 60 and by the usable hours, floored to whole litres. A 30-hour trip spans two days, so a patient prescribed 18 hours a day has 36 usable hours available but only 30 trip hours, and the usable hours are 30; a patient prescribed 12 hours a day over the same trip has 24 usable hours, which is the binding figure.
- **Step 4 — reserve.** The reserve percentage is 20 percent when the trip hours are 24 or fewer, 35 percent when they are more than 24 and no more than 72, and 50 percent otherwise. `reserve_liters` is the base litres multiplied by that percentage, floored to whole litres. The tiers are keyed on `trip_hours` from step 0, not on the usable hours from step 3.
- **Step 5 — requirement.** `required_liters` is the base litres plus the reserve litres, exact integer arithmetic.
- **Step 6 — raw cylinders.** `cylinders_raw` is the required litres divided by 680 litres per E-cylinder and rounded **up** to a whole cylinder. A requirement of 681 litres needs two cylinders.
- **Step 7 — handling surcharge.** `handling_surcharge_cylinders` is 1 when `cylinders_raw` is 6 or more, and 0 otherwise. This step reads `cylinders_raw`, the count **before** the surcharge, and never the final authorized count — were it to read the final count, the surcharge would feed the threshold that produced it and the value would be undefined. A raw count of 5 therefore attracts no surcharge even though adding one would have crossed the threshold.
- **Step 8 — authorized cylinders.** `supply_cylinders_authorized` is `cylinders_raw` plus the surcharge. If that total exceeds 12 cylinders the request is refused with `ERR_TRAVEL_SUPPLY_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` and **no authorization row is created at all** — a raw count of 12 becomes 13 with the surcharge and is refused, while a raw count of 11 becomes 12 and is accepted exactly at the ceiling.

Every intermediate figure is persisted on the authorization — `trip_hours`, `altitude_factor`, `effective_lpm`, `base_liters`, `reserve_liters`, `required_liters`, `cylinders_raw`, `handling_surcharge_cylinders` and `supply_cylinders_authorized` — so the arithmetic can be audited step by step, and the single-authorization read returns all of them. The row opens in `REQUESTED`, and creating it increments the parent contract's `version` by 1. Authorizations may be listed with filters on `contract_id`, `receiving_branch_id` and `status`.

A `BranchManager` then decides. Approval moves the authorization `REQUESTED → APPROVED` and stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the manager's `X-User-ID`, but only when the linked contract's billing dimension is not `SUSPENDED`, its service dimension is exactly `IN_SERVICE`, and the patient is not on compliance hold — an authorization for a patient whose risk posture has crossed the hold threshold is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` even though the request itself was accepted earlier, because the posture may have moved in between. Denial moves the authorization `REQUESTED → DENIED`, stamps `decided_by_user_id` the same way, and accepts an optional `denial_reason` recording why. Both `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are terminal; there is no path between them and no way back to `REQUESTED`.

Transferring a patient between branches is a three-step cycle. An `IntakeCoordinator` opens it by supplying `receiving_branch_id`, which must resolve to an existing branch and must differ from the patient's current `branch_id`, and `transfer_effective_date`, which must be at or after the logical clock's date or the request is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_DATE_IN_PAST`. The patient moves `ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PENDING`, `transfer_to_branch_id` and `transfer_effective_date` are stamped from the body, every rental contract of the patient whose billing dimension is `ACCRUING` or `CAP_REACHED` is suspended with `suspension_reason` set to `TRANSFER`, and the patient's `version` increments by 1. Note that `branch_id` itself does **not** move at this point — the patient is still served by the origin branch until the transfer is effected.

The cycle then closes one of two ways, and the difference between them is exactly one field. A `BranchManager` completes the transfer, moving the patient `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE`, but only once the logical clock's date has reached `transfer_effective_date`; completing early is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_DUE`. Completion **sets `branch_id` to `transfer_to_branch_id`**, then clears both `transfer_to_branch_id` and `transfer_effective_date` to null, and resumes every contract of the patient that is `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `TRANSFER` by returning it to `ACCRUING` with the reason reset to `NONE`. An `IntakeCoordinator` may instead cancel the pending transfer, which also moves the patient `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE`, also clears both transfer fields to null, and also resumes the transfer-suspended contracts to `ACCRUING` with the reason reset — but **leaves `branch_id` unchanged**, returning the patient's branch assignment and both transfer fields to their exact pre-request values. Both paths increment the patient's `version` by 1. Both paths resume billing on the `ACCRUING` branch unconditionally, even for a contract that was `CAP_REACHED` when the transfer opened; such a contract's next posted month is therefore rejected by the cap check rather than by the billing-state check.

If a transfer is opened for a patient with no receiving branch arranged, the transfer also pulls the equipment: every placement on the patient's contracts that is `PLACED` moves `PLACED → REMOVED` with `removal_reason` set to `TRANSFER` and `removed_at` set to the logical clock. Because the patient transfer request requires a `receiving_branch_id`, a normal branch-to-branch transfer leaves the placements in the home untouched and only the billing dimension moves.

Discharge ends the relationship. A `BranchManager` discharges an `ACTIVE` patient, moving them to the terminal `DISCHARGED`, but only once no rental contract of that patient still has a live service dimension: any contract in `PENDING_SETUP`, `IN_SERVICE` or `PICKUP_SCHEDULED` blocks the discharge with `ERR_PATIENT_HAS_ACTIVE_SERVICE`, so the equipment must be picked up — or the setup must have failed — before the patient can be discharged. On success, every rental contract of the patient whose billing dimension is not already `CLOSED` is closed regardless of its service dimension; every certificate of the patient in `ACTIVE` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `PATIENT_DISCHARGED`; and the patient's `current_cmn_status` mirror is re-derived **after** that void, from the status of the patient's most-recently-created certificate ranked by `created_at` descending — so a discharged patient whose newest certificate was the active one reports `VOIDED` on the mirror. The patient's `version` increments by 1. `DISCHARGED` is terminal: no qualifying study may be recorded, no certificate drafted or submitted, no contract opened, no equipment assigned and no travel requested for a discharged patient, each such attempt being refused with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.
