# Equine Therapy & Therapeutic Riding API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A PATH-style therapeutic riding centre runs on four scarce resources that nobody can substitute for one another: a small string of horses that can only carry so much weight for so many minutes a week, a roster of volunteers who lead and sidewalk, a handful of certified instructors whose credentials expire on their own schedule, and restricted money that arrives with a funder's name attached to it. Every ride is the intersection of all four. This document specifies the API that runs that intersection: rider intake and functional assessment, horse-rider matching and session scheduling, volunteer leader/sidewalker crewing, instructor certification, scholarship and grant funding with per-charge billing, therapeutic goal tracking, safety incident management, and the period reporting that funders and accreditors read.

The centre's operational pain is that most of these constraints only become visible at the moment a ride is about to happen — a physician release that quietly expired last Tuesday, a horse that has already carried its weekly minutes, an instructor whose certification slipped into a grace window, a grant whose restriction excludes this particular rider's programme. The API therefore refuses to treat any of these as background data. Every write is evaluated against a single, totally ordered validation ladder, and five safety and compliance gates sit inside that ladder at fixed positions so that a rejection message always names the *most serious* reason a request failed, not merely the first one an implementation happened to notice.

The second design commitment is auditability. Money is never destroyed, only re-partitioned: every charge is exactly covered by allocation lines drawn from an ordered funding waterfall, and reversing a charge walks the same path backwards, restoring each source ledger to the cent. A reporting period, once finalized, never moves again — late-arriving changes land in a separate amendment ledger so that the frozen numbers a funder already received remain exactly the numbers that were sent. Readers of this document should treat the ladder in §2.7, the gate ordering in §2.8, and the funding waterfall in §3.8 as the three specifications from which most of the system's observable behaviour follows.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same error is returned when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the five roles the centre recognises. Those five roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in the header, are `ProgramAdmin`, `Instructor`, `VolunteerCoordinator`, `IntakeCoordinator` and `BillingClerk`. When the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). §4 lists the authorised roles for each endpoint.

`ProgramAdmin` is the centre operations lead. The role owns the barn, the certification register, the money register and the reporting period; it is the only role permitted to drive the logical clock, declare condition policies, register horses and place or release horse holds, register instructors and manage their continuing education and certification renewals, register grants, revoke a physician release, grant a rider medical clearance, review or close a safety incident, create or finalize a period report, and run the goal staleness sweep. `Instructor` is a PATH-certified instructor who books and runs sessions, files assessments, opens and finalizes progress notes and files incidents. `VolunteerCoordinator` owns the volunteer roster, the qualification matrix and session crewing, and is the only role that may release a crew assignment, record annual re-training, or read the volunteer service-hours ledger; the coordinator may never book, confirm, complete or cancel a session. `IntakeCoordinator` owns rider intake, physician releases, therapeutic goals and scholarship awards, and may cancel a session, but may not touch the barn, the certification register or incident review. `BillingClerk` owns the money register: the clerk reverses charges and reads every funding artefact, and — this is the segregation of duties the centre's auditors require — may never create, complete or cancel the session whose charge they reverse.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Instructor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Instructor` row; ownership checks compare a resource's instructor foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Two such checks exist. Completing a session requires `X-User-ID` to equal that session's `instructor_id`, and finalizing a progress note requires `X-User-ID` to equal that note's `author_instructor_id`; in both cases another `Instructor` attempting the operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), which is the same code an unauthorised role receives. Drafting a progress note likewise records the caller: `author_instructor_id` is taken from `X-User-ID` and is never accepted from the request body. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `VolunteerCoordinator`, `IntakeCoordinator` or `BillingClerk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may log it but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and in particular a `ProgramAdmin` performing a session completion or a note finalization is not subject to the two ownership checks above, which bind `Instructor` callers only.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a list accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when it is not supplied; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest accepted value is 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and its smallest accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A supplied `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, a supplied `offset` below 0, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

List responses always order rows by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning, and Optimistic Concurrency

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recording when the row was written. All resources except `ConditionPolicy` additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. `version` begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. `ConditionPolicy` rows are catalogue entries that are written once and never mutated: they expose only `id` and `created_at`, and carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Where a workflow below says an operation "bumps the version" of a related entity, it means that entity's own `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed, even though the caller addressed a different resource.

Three write endpoints are governed by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* entity's version, and no others. Adding a crew assignment to a session — the create under `POST /sessions/{session_id}/crew-assignments` — requires a non-optional `expected_session_version` field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Session`. Filing an assessment under `POST /riders/{rider_id}/assessments` and filing a physician release under `POST /riders/{rider_id}/medical-releases` each require a non-optional `expected_rider_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Rider`. In all three cases, if the supplied value does not equal the parent entity's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is persisted. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all, and supplying one to them has no effect.

The reason each rule exists is worth stating, because in each case the write would otherwise be perfectly legitimate on its own terms and so cannot be resolved by a state precondition. Adding a *second, different* volunteer to a session is a normal thing to do, so only a stale `expected_session_version` can tell the coordinator that the session's crew composition, its `schedule_status`, or the rider's crew requirement moved underneath the view they were working from. Filing an assessment supersedes the rider's prior recorded assessment and rewrites the rider's independence level and crew requirement, so only a stale `expected_rider_version` distinguishes a writer who saw the superseding assessment from one who did not. Filing a release supersedes the rider's prior active release and re-points which release the rider is riding under, so a stale `expected_rider_version` means the client's view of which condition codes are currently cleared is out of date.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Dates, and Instant Rendering

The server keeps a **logical clock**. Every "now" in this specification — every derived status evaluation, every deadline comparison, every system-stamped timestamp — reads that logical clock, and the server must never consult real wall-clock time for any business decision. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. A `ProgramAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting endpoint, which answers with `current_utc`. Because `to_iso8601` is a caller-supplied instant, `current_utc` echoes back exactly the string that was supplied, character for character; before the first such call it reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, with no fractional digits added. Moving the clock persists nothing else, but every derived status in the system — release status, instructor certification status, volunteer qualification status, volunteer status, rider assessment status, grant status and scholarship award status — is re-evaluated against the new instant on the next read.

Three rendering conventions govern every datetime the API emits, and they must not be conflated.

Timestamps the **server itself mints** are rendered as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ` — an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. This covers `created_at`, `updated_at`, `reported_at`, `finalized_at`, `evaluated_at`, `superseded_at` and `revoked_at`.

Instants **supplied by the caller** are stored and echoed back byte for byte, exactly as received. The server must not normalise them, must not add or strip fractional digits, and must not convert a supplied offset to UTC. This covers `scheduled_start`, `occurred_at`, `to_iso8601` and `release_at`, and it also covers `certification_expires_at` at the moment an instructor is registered. If a caller supplies `2026-06-01T14:00:00Z`, every later read of that field returns `2026-06-01T14:00:00Z` and not `2026-06-01T14:00:00.000Z`.

Instants the server **derives by arithmetic** on a caller-supplied date or instant are rendered in the server-minted format with three fractional digits. This covers `scheduled_end`, `expires_at` (on both releases and volunteer qualifications), `next_due_at`, `assessment_next_due_at`, `stale_after`, `report_due_at`, `hold_release_at` when the incident cascade computes it, `retraining_due_at`, `suspended_until`, and `certification_expires_at` after a renewal has recomputed it. Note the consequence for `hold_release_at`: when a `ProgramAdmin` places a manual horse hold, `hold_release_at` is the caller-supplied `release_at` echoed byte for byte, but when the incident cascade places a rest hold, `hold_release_at` is derived and therefore carries three fractional digits.

Date-only fields are rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD` and are likewise stored and echoed byte for byte. The date-only fields are `signed_on`, `performed_on`, `completed_on`, `renewed_on`, `signed_off_on`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `on_date`, `date_of_birth`, `last_retraining_on` and the horse's `current_week_start_date`. When a date-only value takes part in instant arithmetic it is first promoted to that calendar date at `00:00:00.000Z`.

### 2.6. Numbers, Money, and the Minutes-to-Hours Conversion

All money in this system is held in whole cents as integers. Wherever a monetary amount is produced by multiplying by a percentage, the result is rounded to whole cents using round-half-up. Weights in pounds are rounded to 2 decimal places, round-half-up. Service hours are rounded to 2 decimal places, round-half-up.

Minutes become hours by exactly one route: dividing by 60 and rounding the quotient to 2 decimal places, round-half-up. Nothing in this system may convert minutes to hours by any other route, and the *order* of that conversion relative to summation is pinned wherever an aggregate is taken: each session's hours are rounded first and the rounded per-session figures are then summed, never the other way round. The difference is observable. Three fifty-minute sessions contribute 0.83 hours each and therefore 2.49 hours in total; summing the minutes first and converting once would give 2.5, and that answer is wrong.


### 2.7. The Ordered Validation Ladder

Every request is evaluated against one ladder of 44 rungs, in ascending rung number, and the **first** rung whose condition fails determines the response. There are no independent or unordered checks anywhere in this API: whenever two rungs would both reject a request, the lower-numbered rung's error is the one returned, and the higher-numbered rung is never reached. A rung whose scope does not include the endpoint being called is skipped without effect. Rungs 14 through 18 are the five global write gates described in §2.8.

1. Both the `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` headers are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the five roles in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The role is among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. Ownership and segregation of duties: completing a session requires `X-User-ID` to equal that session's `instructor_id`, and finalizing a progress note requires `X-User-ID` to equal that note's `author_instructor_id`. Neither may be performed by a different `Instructor` — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to session completion and progress-note finalization only.
5. Pagination parameters, when supplied, are integers: `limit` at least 1 and at most 100, `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every list-returning read.
6. The request body is a JSON object and every field the endpoint requires is present and not null — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every POST and PATCH.
7. Field types, numeric ranges and string formats hold — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every POST and PATCH. Specifically: `weight_lb` is greater than 0 and at most 400; `duration_minutes` is at least 30 and at most 90; `balance_score` and `trunk_control_score` are each at least 0 and at most 10; `baseline_rating` and `target_rating` are each at least 1 and at most 5, and `target_rating` is strictly greater than `baseline_rating`; every `rating` inside `goal_ratings` is at least 1 and at most 5; `cap_pct` is greater than 0 and at most 0.90; `period_cap_cents` is greater than 0; `awarded_amount_cents` is greater than 0; `payer_rate_cents` is at least 0; `body_weight_lb` is greater than 0; `tack_weight_lb` is at least 0; `training_hours` is at least 0; `hours` is greater than 0; `mounted_minutes` is at least 0 and at most the session's `duration_minutes`; `period_end` is the same date as or later than `period_start`; every date-only field matches the `YYYY-MM-DD` shape; and every instant field parses as ISO 8601.
8. Enum membership on request-body fields, as a closed list — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). Applies to every POST and PATCH. §2.9 states exactly which fields route here and which do not.
9. A `{rider_id}` path segment resolves to an existing rider — otherwise `ERR_RIDER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every path containing `{rider_id}`.
10. A `{session_id}` path segment resolves to an existing session — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every path containing `{session_id}`.
11. A `{horse_id}` path segment resolves to an existing horse — otherwise `ERR_HORSE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every path containing `{horse_id}`.
12. Every other path identifier resolves, checked left to right along the URL — otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). The identifiers in this class are `{release_id}`, `{assessment_id}`, `{volunteer_id}`, `{qualification_id}`, `{instructor_id}`, `{assignment_id}`, `{goal_id}`, `{note_id}`, `{grant_id}`, `{award_id}`, `{charge_id}`, `{allocation_id}`, `{incident_id}` and `{report_id}`.
13. Foreign keys carried in the request body resolve, checked in this field order: `rider_id`, `horse_id`, `instructor_id`, `supervising_instructor_id`, `volunteer_id`, `medical_release_id`, `preferred_grant_id`, and then every `goal_id` inside `goal_ratings` in the order the client supplied them. An unresolvable `rider_id` yields `ERR_RIDER_NOT_FOUND` (404), an unresolvable `horse_id` yields `ERR_HORSE_NOT_FOUND` (404), and every other unresolvable body identifier — including `supervisor_instructor_id` on instructor registration — yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to session creation, crew assignment creation, rider medical clearance, session completion, progress-note drafting, incident filing and instructor registration.
14. **Gate 1 — incident hold.** No subject the write touches carries a hold placed by an incident whose `status` is `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_HOLD_ACTIVE` (409). A subject is held when the rider's `medical_hold_incident_id` is set, when the horse's `status` is `REST_HOLD` with `hold_reason` equal to `INCIDENT_REST_HOLD`, when the named volunteer's `status` is `SUSPENDED`, or when the effective instructor's `certification_status` is `SUSPENDED`. Applies to session creation, session confirmation, session completion and crew assignment creation.
15. **Gate 2 — finalized reporting period.** Session creation and session completion are rejected when the session date falls inside the closed period of a period report whose `status` is `FINALIZED`; period-report creation is rejected when the requested period overlaps a finalized report's period; period-report finalization is rejected when the target report is already `FINALIZED` — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_FINALIZED` (409). Applies to session creation, session completion, period-report creation and period-report finalization.
16. **Gate 3 — horse operational hold.** The horse's `status` is `AVAILABLE` — otherwise `ERR_HORSE_ON_HOLD` (409). Applies to session creation, session confirmation and session completion.
17. **Gate 4 — instructor certification.** Evaluated on the instructor of record and, when one is named, additionally on the supervising instructor; both must pass. Session creation and session confirmation require `CURRENT`; session completion accepts `CURRENT` or `GRACE`. `LAPSED` and `SUSPENDED` are rejected everywhere — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_LAPSED` (409). Applies to session creation, session confirmation and session completion.
18. **Gate 5 — physician release.** The rider's most recent medical release has derived status `ACTIVE` at the logical clock. A rider with no release at all is rejected with `ERR_RELEASE_MISSING` (422); a rider whose most recent release is `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_RELEASE_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to session creation, session confirmation, session completion and rider activation.
19. The target resource is in a from-state that the requested transition declares — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action endpoint. This rung is what rejects a second confirmation of a confirmed session, a cancellation of an already-cancelled session, a no-show recorded before the scheduled start or against a session that is not `CONFIRMED`, a reversal of an already-reversed charge, a sign-off on a qualification that is already `QUALIFIED`, a review of an incident already `UNDER_REVIEW`, a close of an incident still `OPEN`, a finalization of a note that is no longer `DRAFT`, a discontinuation of an already-discontinued goal, a reopening of a goal that is not `MET`, a rating of a `DISCONTINUED` goal, a horse hold release attempted before `hold_release_at` has been reached, and a withdrawal of a rider who still holds sessions in `CREW_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED`.
20. The rider's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_RIDER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to session creation and session confirmation.
21. The rider's `assessment_status` is neither `OVERDUE` nor `NEVER_ASSESSED` — otherwise `ERR_REASSESSMENT_OVERDUE` (422). Applies to session creation and session confirmation.
22. Every code in the rider's `condition_codes` has a condition-policy row for the session's `activity_type` — otherwise `ERR_CONDITION_POLICY_MISSING` (422). Applies to session creation and session confirmation.
23. No code in the rider's `condition_codes` is contraindicated for this activity — otherwise `ERR_CONDITION_CONTRAINDICATED` (422). Applies to session creation and session confirmation.
24. Every precaution-tier code of the rider for this activity appears in the active release's `cleared_condition_codes` — otherwise `ERR_PRECAUTION_NOT_CLEARED` (422). Applies to session creation and session confirmation.
25. Supervision pairing is correct: an instructor of record whose `certification_level` is `IN_TRAINING` must be accompanied by a `supervising_instructor_id` whose tier ordinal is strictly greater than the trainee's, and a `supervising_instructor_id` must be omitted or null when the instructor of record is not `IN_TRAINING` — otherwise `ERR_SUPERVISOR_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to session creation.
26. The effective instructor's tier ordinal is at least the required ordinal — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to session creation and session confirmation.
27. The session's `effective_load_lb`, taken in the `current_declared` variant, is at most the horse's `max_load_lb`; equality passes — otherwise `ERR_HORSE_LOAD_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to session creation.
28. The new session's half-open time interval does not intersect the half-open interval of another live session of the same horse — otherwise `ERR_HORSE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to session creation.
29. The gap between the new session and every adjacent live session of the same horse is at least 15 minutes — otherwise `ERR_HORSE_REST_INTERVAL` (409). Applies to session creation.
30. The horse's committed session count for the day, including the new session, is at most 3, and the horse's committed minutes for the day, including the new session's `duration_minutes`, are at most 150 — otherwise `ERR_HORSE_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to session creation.
31. The horse's committed minutes for the week, including the new session's `duration_minutes`, are at most 240 — otherwise `ERR_HORSE_WEEKLY_MINUTES_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to session creation.
32. The new session's half-open interval does not intersect the half-open interval of another live session naming the same instructor in either the `instructor_id` or the `supervising_instructor_id` slot — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to session creation.
33. The requested `crew_role` is one the rider actually needs and is not already filled — otherwise `ERR_CREW_ROLE_NOT_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to crew assignment creation.
34. The same volunteer is not already assigned to this session in any role — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREW_ASSIGNMENT` (409). Applies to crew assignment creation.
35. The volunteer's closed session interval neither touches nor overlaps the closed interval of another live session they are already assigned to — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to crew assignment creation.
36. The volunteer's `status` is `ACTIVE`; `SUSPENDED` and `INACTIVE` are both rejected — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to crew assignment creation.
37. The volunteer's `age_years` is at least the minimum age for the requested `crew_role` — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_UNDER_AGE` (422). Applies to crew assignment creation and to qualification sign-off.
38. The volunteer holds a qualification for exactly this `crew_role` whose derived status is `QUALIFIED`, and a sign-off is attempted only once the role's minimum training hours have been logged — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (422). Applies to crew assignment creation and to qualification sign-off.
39. For a `SIDEWALKER` assignment, `hold_type` is present and equals the rider's `sidewalker_hold_type`; for a `LEADER` assignment, `hold_type` is omitted or null — otherwise `ERR_HOLD_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to crew assignment creation.
40. The session's `crew_satisfied` is true, with exact counts on both limbs — otherwise `ERR_CREW_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to session confirmation.
41. A supplied `preferred_grant_id` passes the grant eligibility filter for this rider and session date — otherwise `ERR_GRANT_RESTRICTION_VIOLATION` (422). Applies to session completion.
42. For a session-fee charge on a rider whose `funding_mode` is `FUNDED_ONLY`, the waterfall leaves nothing uncovered before the private-pay line — otherwise `ERR_FUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (422). Applies to session completion.
43. The instructor's `ce_hours_accrued` is at least 20 — otherwise `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to certification renewal.
44. The supplied `expected_session_version` or `expected_rider_version` equals the stored parent version — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to crew assignment creation, assessment filing and medical-release filing. This rung is deliberately last: every other rung must already pass before a stale version can be observed, so a request that is both stale and otherwise invalid always reports the other fault.

### 2.8. The Five Global Write Gates and Their Precedence

Five checks in the ladder are **global write gates**: they express the centre's safety, compliance and audit commitments, and they are ordered against one another so that the most serious reason a write was refused is always the reason reported. The gates occupy rungs 14 through 18 in exactly this order, and each gate pre-empts every gate below it as well as every ordinary rung that follows:

1. **Incident hold gate** (rung 14, `ERR_INCIDENT_HOLD_ACTIVE`) — an open or under-review incident has a hold on the rider, horse, volunteer or instructor the write touches.
2. **Finalized period gate** (rung 15, `ERR_REPORT_FINALIZED`) — the write would create or newly complete billable work inside a finalized reporting period, or would create a report overlapping one.
3. **Horse operational hold gate** (rung 16, `ERR_HORSE_ON_HOLD`) — the horse is on a vet, farrier or rest hold.
4. **Instructor certification gate** (rung 17, `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_LAPSED`) — the effective instructor's certification is lapsed or suspended, or is merely in grace on an endpoint that demands a current certificate.
5. **Medical release gate** (rung 18, `ERR_RELEASE_MISSING` or `ERR_RELEASE_EXPIRED`) — the rider is not riding under an active physician release.

Two consequences deserve to be spelled out because implementations get them wrong. First, all five gates sit **above** the state rung: confirming a session that is already `CONFIRMED` while the horse sits on a farrier hold reports `ERR_HORSE_ON_HOLD`, not `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because rung 16 is reached before rung 19. Second, the gates sit above every domain rule that follows them, so a session creation that would also breach the horse's weekly minutes but whose rider is on an incident hold reports `ERR_INCIDENT_HOLD_ACTIVE`. Each gate is individually observable with every earlier gate passing: gate 2 is seen with no incident open, gate 3 with no incident and no finalized period, gate 4 with the horse available on top of that, and gate 5 with the instructor current on top of all of it.

### 2.9. Enumerations and Enum Error Routing

`ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 400) has a **closed** routing list. A request-**body** field whose name appears in the following list, carrying a value outside that field's declared member set, returns `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`: `household_income_band`, `demographic_group`, `program_code`, `funding_mode`, `activity_type`, `assessment_type`, `tier`, `min_instructor_tier`, `certification_level`, `crew_role`, `hold_type`, `incident_type`, `cancellation_reason`, `domain`, `restricted_program_code`, `restricted_demographic` and `variant`. **No other field routes here.** Every other invalid value returns something else: an out-of-range value supplied as a *query parameter* — including a `status`, `schedule_status`, `billing_status`, `assessment_status`, `certification_status`, `charge_type`, `source_type`, `severity`, `program_code`, `activity_type`, `crew_role`, `certification_level`, `domain` or `variant` used as a list filter or a computed-read selector — returns `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) from rung 7; an unknown status filter returns `ERR_VALIDATION`; and a malformed or unresolvable identifier returns the relevant not-found error from rungs 9 through 13. The distinction is by *position*, not by field name: `activity_type` in the body of a condition-policy creation routes to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, while `activity_type` in the query string of a condition-policy listing routes to `ERR_VALIDATION`.

The member sets themselves are as follows. A rider's `household_income_band` is one of `BAND_A`, `BAND_B`, `BAND_C`, `BAND_D`; `demographic_group` is one of `VETERAN`, `YOUTH`, `ADULT`, `SENIOR`; `program_code` and a session's `activity_type` are each one of `THERAPEUTIC_RIDING`, `HIPPOTHERAPY`, `EQUINE_LEARNING`, `VAULTING`; `funding_mode` is `PRIVATE_PAY_ALLOWED` or `FUNDED_ONLY`. A grant's `restricted_program_code` is null or one of the four programme codes, and its `restricted_demographic` is null or one of the four demographic groups. An assessment's `assessment_type` is `INTAKE` or `ANNUAL_REASSESSMENT`. A condition policy's `tier` is one of `CONTRAINDICATION`, `PRECAUTION`, `CLEAR`, and its `min_instructor_tier`, like an instructor's `certification_level`, is one of `IN_TRAINING`, `REGISTERED`, `ADVANCED`, `MASTER`. A goal's `domain` is one of `PHYSICAL`, `COGNITIVE`, `SOCIAL_EMOTIONAL`, `BEHAVIORAL`, `EDUCATIONAL`. An incident's `incident_type` is one of `FALL`, `LOOSE_HORSE`, `NEAR_MISS`, `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`, `MEDICAL_EVENT`.

Three enum fields have a member set that is narrower than the set of values the same name can take elsewhere, and the narrowing is part of the contract. On a crew assignment, `crew_role` accepts only `LEADER` or `SIDEWALKER` — `BARN` is a qualification role and is never a session crew role, so supplying it there is an `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`; on a volunteer qualification, `crew_role` accepts `LEADER`, `SIDEWALKER` or `BARN`. On a manual horse hold, `hold_type` accepts only `VET_HOLD` or `FARRIER_HOLD`; on a crew assignment, `hold_type` accepts one of `NO_CONTACT`, `ARM_OVER_THIGH`, `THIGH_HOLD`, `OVER_THE_THIGH`, `ANKLE_HOLD`. On a session cancellation, the `cancellation_reason` a caller may supply is one of `RIDER_REQUEST`, `WEATHER`, `HORSE_PULLED` or `PROGRAM_CLOSURE` — and only those four; the *stored* `cancellation_reason` on a session is drawn from a different and wider set, `EARLY_RIDER`, `LATE_RIDER`, `WEATHER`, `HORSE_PULLED`, `PROGRAM_CLOSURE`, `RIDER_HOLD` or `NO_SHOW`, because the stored value is derived rather than accepted (§3.6). Supplying `EARLY_RIDER`, `LATE_RIDER`, `RIDER_HOLD` or `NO_SHOW` as the input `cancellation_reason` is therefore an `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

The `variant` selector on the three computed reads is likewise field-specific: the session eligibility read accepts `current_declared`, `at_assessment`, `at_booking_instant` or `at_ride_instant` and defaults to `current_declared` when omitted; the horse workload read accepts `committed` or `delivered` and defaults to `committed` when omitted; the volunteer service-hours read accepts `scheduled` or `delivered` and has **no** default — `variant` is a required input there, and omitting it is an `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).


### 2.10. Nested Paths, Child Ownership, and Response Projection

Several resources are addressed through their parent: a rider's releases, assessments, goals and scholarship awards; a session's crew assignments, progress notes and incidents. Path parameters on such URLs are validated **left to right**, so a request naming a rider that does not exist and a release that does not exist reports the rider failure first. When a child is created through a parent path, the parent link is taken from the path and never from the body: `rider_id` on a medical release, an assessment, a goal and a scholarship award, `session_id` on a crew assignment, a progress note, a charge and an incident, and `volunteer_id` on a volunteer qualification are all set by the server from the URL. A body field attempting to set them is ignored. `charge_id` on a funding allocation and `grant_id` or `scholarship_award_id` on the grant and scholarship lines are likewise server-set.

Every mutating operation returns the resource the caller addressed, not the resources it cascaded into. Completing a session returns the `Session`; the `Charge` and `FundingAllocation` rows it created are read back through their own list and read endpoints. Closing an incident returns the `Incident`, not the rider, horse, volunteer or instructor whose holds it lifted. The one thing a mutating response must always show is the addressed resource's *post-mutation* state, including its incremented `version` and every field recomputed as a consequence of the write — a response that projects a field from a pre-mutation snapshot is wrong.

Reads recompute. Every derived field is evaluated against the logical clock at the moment of the read, so reading a rider, a release, an instructor, a volunteer, a qualification, a grant or a scholarship award after the clock has moved returns different derived values from identical stored data, with no write in between.

### 2.11. Status Axes and Their Evaluation Precedence

Several entities carry more than one status axis, and the axes evolve independently. A `Rider` has both a lifecycle `status` and a parallel `assessment_status`; a `Session` has both a `schedule_status` and a `billing_status`. Neither axis constrains the other directly: a session can be `COMPLETED` on the schedule axis while still `CHARGED` on the billing axis, and a rider can be `ACTIVE` on the lifecycle axis while `OVERDUE` on the assessment axis.

Four derived statuses are computed by a **precedence chain** that must be evaluated in the stated order, because more than one clause can be true at once.

A medical release's `status` is `REVOKED` if `revoked_at` is set; otherwise `SUPERSEDED` if `superseded_at` is set; otherwise `EXPIRED` if the logical clock has reached `expires_at`; otherwise `ACTIVE`. A volunteer's `status` is `SUSPENDED` if `suspended_until` is set and the logical clock is before it; otherwise `INACTIVE` if the logical clock has reached `retraining_due_at`; otherwise `ACTIVE`. A volunteer qualification's `status` is `SUSPENDED` if its owning volunteer is `SUSPENDED`; otherwise `IN_TRAINING` if `signed_off_on` is null; otherwise `EXPIRED` if the logical clock has reached `expires_at`; otherwise `QUALIFIED`. An instructor's `certification_status` is `SUSPENDED` if `suspended_until` is set and the logical clock is before it; otherwise `CURRENT` if the logical clock is before `certification_expires_at`; otherwise `GRACE` if the logical clock is before 60 days after `certification_expires_at`; otherwise `LAPSED`.

Three more derived statuses follow simpler rules. A scholarship award is `SUPERSEDED` if `superseded_at` is set; otherwise `EXHAUSTED` if `period_used_cents` has reached `period_cap_cents`; otherwise `ACTIVE`. A grant is `EXHAUSTED` when `remaining_amount_cents` is exactly 0 and `OPEN` otherwise. A charge is `REVERSED` once `reversed_at` is set; otherwise `FUNDED` when `balance_due_cents` is 0; otherwise `PENDING`.

Because these statuses are derived rather than stored, they are deliberately **not monotone**: an instructor in `LAPSED` returns to `CURRENT` the instant a renewal moves `certification_expires_at`, a qualification returns from `EXPIRED` to `QUALIFIED` when re-training re-signs it, a grant returns from `EXHAUSTED` to `OPEN` when an allocation is released, and a rider's `assessment_status` jumps from `OVERDUE` straight back to `CURRENT` the moment a reassessment is filed. Nothing is persisted for these transitions, so no downstream rule can hold a stale copy of them. The one status that is deliberately *persisted* and therefore one-directional is a goal's `STALE` (§3.7).

### 2.12. Conservation of Money and of Capacity

Five invariants hold at every observable instant, and each names a forward action and the compensating action that undoes it. They are the acceptance criteria for the cascades in §3.8 and §3.9.

A charge's gross `amount_cents` always equals the sum of `amount_cents` across that charge's allocation lines whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`, for as long as the charge is `PENDING` or `FUNDED`; once the charge is `REVERSED`, its `allocated_cents` and `balance_due_cents` are both 0 and every one of its allocation lines is `RELEASED`. A grant's `awarded_amount_cents` always equals its `remaining_amount_cents` plus the sum of `amount_cents` across allocated lines that name it, so reversing a charge returns `remaining_amount_cents` to exactly its pre-charge value and returns the grant to `OPEN`. A scholarship award's `period_used_cents` always equals the sum of `amount_cents` across allocated lines that name it, and never exceeds `period_cap_cents`; reversal restores it exactly and returns the award to `ACTIVE`. A horse's `current_week_committed_minutes` always equals the sum of `duration_minutes` over that horse's sessions in `CREW_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` whose `scheduled_start` falls in the current week, so cancelling a session returns exactly that session's `duration_minutes` of daily and weekly capacity — no more and no less, which means a booking rejected for weekly overflow succeeds after one cancellation of equal duration. Finally, for a finalized period report, the frozen `total_charged_cents` plus `amendment_net_cents` always equals what a live recomputation of the same closed period would produce at the current instant; the frozen aggregate never moves and the whole delta lands in the amendment ledger.


## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Rider Intake, Assessment, and the Independence Ladder

A rider enters the programme when an `IntakeCoordinator` (or a `ProgramAdmin`) registers them with a `full_name`, a `date_of_birth`, a declared `weight_lb`, a `household_income_band`, a `demographic_group`, the `program_code` they are enrolling in, and an array of `condition_codes` carrying their diagnosis or condition codes. Two funding facts may be supplied at intake and are otherwise defaulted: `funding_mode` defaults to `PRIVATE_PAY_ALLOWED`, and `payer_rate_cents` — the per-charge insurance or payer contribution ceiling in cents — defaults to 0. The new rider lands in `INTAKE` on the lifecycle axis and `NEVER_ASSESSED` on the assessment axis, with `independence_level`, `required_sidewalker_count`, `requires_leader`, `sidewalker_hold_type` and `assessment_next_due_at` all null, `backriding_required` false, `goals_met_count` 0, and `medical_hold_incident_id`, `withdraw_reason` and `active_release_id` all null. `weight_lb` must be greater than 0 and at most 400 pounds.

Intake facts that can change over a rider's involvement are mutable: an update accepts any of `weight_lb`, `household_income_band`, `condition_codes`, `funding_mode` and `payer_rate_cents`, bumps the rider's `version`, and — because the load and admissibility calculations read the rider live — silently changes what every one of that rider's live sessions reports for `effective_load_lb` and `precaution_tier` on the next read. Changing `weight_lb` is precisely what makes the two load variants of §3.3 diverge. Riders can be listed and filtered by `status`, `program_code`, `assessment_status`, `medical_hold_incident_id` and `active_release_id`, the last two of which are the channel by which a caller finds every rider currently held by a given incident or riding under a given release.

Before a rider may be scheduled at all, an `Instructor` or `ProgramAdmin` must file a functional **assessment**. The filing carries an `assessment_type` — `INTAKE` for the first assessment and `ANNUAL_REASSESSMENT` for each one after it — a date-only `performed_on`, a `balance_score` and a `trunk_control_score` each between 0 and 10 inclusive, the `rider_weight_lb` measured in the therapy room on the day, a `backriding_required` flag, and the `expected_rider_version` demanded by §2.4. The two scores add together into `combined_score`, and `combined_score` places the rider on a four-rung independence ladder: 17 or more is `LEVEL_4`, 12 through 16 is `LEVEL_3`, 7 through 11 is `LEVEL_2`, and 6 or less is `LEVEL_1`. Because each score runs from 0 to 10, `combined_score` runs from 0 to 20 and every band is reachable.

The independence level immediately fixes the crew the rider needs, through a single fixed matrix. A `LEVEL_1` rider needs 2 sidewalkers and a leader, with the sidewalkers using the `OVER_THE_THIGH` hold. A `LEVEL_2` rider needs 2 sidewalkers and a leader, using `THIGH_HOLD`. A `LEVEL_3` rider needs 1 sidewalker and a leader, using `ARM_OVER_THIGH`. A `LEVEL_4` rider needs no sidewalkers and no leader, and their nominal hold is `NO_CONTACT`. Those three values become the assessment's own `required_sidewalker_count`, `requires_leader` and `sidewalker_hold_type`, and are copied onto the rider along with `independence_level` and `backriding_required`. A fifth hold value, `ANKLE_HOLD`, is a legal value for a crew assignment's `hold_type` but is never produced by this matrix.

Filing an assessment also stamps the assessment's `next_due_at` at 365 days after `performed_on` (with `performed_on` promoted to midnight UTC first), copies that instant onto the rider as `assessment_next_due_at`, and moves the rider's previous assessment from `RECORDED` to `SUPERSEDED`; only the newest assessment for a rider is ever `RECORDED`. The rider's `version` increments by 1 on every filing. Assessments can be listed by `rider_id` and by `status`, and read individually.

The rider's `assessment_status` is then derived on every read from the logical clock. A rider with no recorded assessment is `NEVER_ASSESSED`. A rider whose `assessment_next_due_at` has been reached is `OVERDUE`. A rider inside the final 30 days before `assessment_next_due_at` is `DUE`. Anything earlier is `CURRENT`. Both `NEVER_ASSESSED` and `OVERDUE` block booking and confirmation with `ERR_REASSESSMENT_OVERDUE`, while `DUE` is a warning state that blocks nothing. This axis is derived and therefore reversible in both directions: filing an annual reassessment moves a rider from `OVERDUE` or `DUE` straight back to `CURRENT`, and moving the clock forward moves them back out again.

Activation is the gate between intake and riding. An `IntakeCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` moves a rider `INTAKE → ACTIVE` only when the rider is still in `INTAKE`, at least one assessment for them is `RECORDED`, and `active_release_id` is not null — that is, a physician release with derived status `ACTIVE` exists at the logical clock. A rider with no release at all is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_MISSING` and one whose newest release has lapsed with `ERR_RELEASE_EXPIRED`. Activation increments the rider's `version`.

Withdrawal is permanent. A rider in `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE` or `MEDICAL_HOLD` may be moved to `WITHDRAWN` with a supplied `reason`, which is stored as `withdraw_reason`, provided the rider holds no session in `CREW_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED`; a rider with live sessions is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` until those sessions are cancelled. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal on the lifecycle axis. The return path from `MEDICAL_HOLD` is different and is described in §3.9: a `ProgramAdmin` grants medical clearance by naming a `medical_release_id` together with a `note`, and the rider moves `MEDICAL_HOLD → ACTIVE` only when `medical_hold_incident_id` has already been cleared and the named release belongs to this rider and is `ACTIVE`. Both transitions bump the rider's `version`.

### 3.2. Physician Releases and Condition Admissibility

A **medical release** is a physician's signature on a date. Filing one carries the date-only `signed_on`, the `physician_name`, an array of `cleared_condition_codes` naming the condition codes that physician has explicitly cleared, and the rider's `expected_rider_version`. The server computes `expires_at` as 365 days after `signed_on` promoted to midnight UTC. Filing a release for a rider who already has an `ACTIVE` one stamps the older release's `superseded_at` with the logical clock, re-points the rider's `active_release_id` at the new release, and increments the rider's `version`. A `ProgramAdmin` may instead revoke a release outright with a supplied `reason`, which stamps `revoked_at` with the logical clock, re-derives `active_release_id`, and increments the release's own `version`; `revoked_at` is null on every release that has not been revoked, and `superseded_at` is null on every release that has not been superseded. Releases are listed by `rider_id` and by `status`, and read individually, with `status` derived at the logical clock by the precedence chain in §2.11.

Validity is always evaluated **at an instant**, and which instant is chosen matters. A release is `REVOKED` at an instant if it was revoked at or before it, `SUPERSEDED` if it was superseded at or before it, `EXPIRED` once that instant has reached `expires_at`, and `ACTIVE` otherwise. Note that expiry is inclusive of the boundary: a ride at exactly `expires_at` is riding on an expired release. Every write gate evaluates the rider's most recent release — most recent by `created_at`, newest first — at the logical clock of the call. The eligibility read described in §3.6 can instead evaluate it at the session's `scheduled_start`, and the two answers genuinely differ: a session booked a week before a release expires but scheduled for two weeks after it reports an `ACTIVE` release at the booking instant and an `EXPIRED` one at the ride instant, is accepted when it is booked, and is then refused at completion once the clock has moved.

Admissibility of a diagnosis is not a property of the code alone but of the **pair** of code and activity. A `ProgramAdmin` declares one such pair at a time as a **condition policy**, supplying the `condition_code`, the `activity_type` it governs, the `tier`, and optionally a `min_instructor_tier` that defaults to `REGISTERED`. `CONTRAINDICATION` bars participation outright. `PRECAUTION` permits participation only with a physician clearance and may additionally escalate the instructor tier required to run the pair. `CLEAR` imposes nothing. Condition policies are listed and filtered by `condition_code` and `activity_type`. Because the pair is what is governed, the same rider can be perfectly admissible for `THERAPEUTIC_RIDING` and barred from `VAULTING` on the same day.

Evaluating a rider against a session's activity produces the session's `precaution_tier`. The evaluation starts from the set of codes the rider's currently `ACTIVE` release has cleared — an empty set when there is no active release — and then walks every code in the rider's `condition_codes`. A code with no condition-policy row for this activity stops the evaluation with `ERR_CONDITION_POLICY_MISSING`. A code whose policy `tier` is `CONTRAINDICATION` stops it immediately at `CONTRAINDICATION`, which is the worst possible outcome and is reported as `ERR_CONDITION_CONTRAINDICATED`; a physician clearance never lifts a contraindication, which is why the cleared set is consulted only for precaution-tier codes. A code whose policy `tier` is `PRECAUTION` and which is *not* in the cleared set raises the running worst tier to `PRECAUTION`, reported as `ERR_PRECAUTION_NOT_CLEARED`. When no code raises anything, `precaution_tier` is `CLEAR`.

The rider's precaution-tier codes also set the session's `required_instructor_tier`, and here the clearing works the other way round. Every condition-policy row for this rider and this activity whose `tier` is `PRECAUTION` contributes its `min_instructor_tier`, **including rows whose code the release has cleared** — clearing removes the participation bar, not the instructor-tier requirement. The highest contribution wins, and the result is floored at `REGISTERED`, so `required_instructor_tier` is never below `REGISTERED` even for a rider with no precautions at all. Tiers are compared by ordinal, where `IN_TRAINING` is 0, `REGISTERED` is 1, `ADVANCED` is 2 and `MASTER` is 3, and the effective instructor's ordinal must be at least the required ordinal — equality passes. The order of the two evaluations is pinned: the precaution-tier evaluation runs first and may reject on its own account, and the instructor-tier evaluation runs only if it passed, which is exactly why rungs 22, 23 and 24 sit above rung 26 in the ladder.


### 3.3. The Barn: Load, Workload, and Operational Holds

A `ProgramAdmin` registers a horse with a `name`, a `body_weight_lb` greater than 0, and a `tack_weight_lb` of at least 0 representing the saddle and equipment the horse carries on every session. From `body_weight_lb` the server immediately derives `max_load_lb` as one fifth of the horse's body weight — twenty per cent — rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. A new horse is `AVAILABLE`, with `hold_release_at` and `hold_reason` both null.

**Effective load** is what the horse actually carries: a rider weight, plus the horse's own `tack_weight_lb`, plus 140.0 pounds when the rider's `backriding_required` flag is true and 0 pounds otherwise, with the total rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Booking compares that figure against `max_load_lb` and permits it when it is at most `max_load_lb` — a load exactly equal to the ceiling passes, and only a strictly greater load is refused with `ERR_HORSE_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. Which rider weight enters the sum is the interesting part, because two are stored. The `current_declared` reading uses the rider's `weight_lb`, the value the most recent intake update wrote, and this is the reading the booking gate uses and the reading the session's own `effective_load_lb` field always reports. The `at_assessment` reading instead uses `rider_weight_lb` from the rider's newest `RECORDED` assessment — the weight measured in the therapy room. The two agree until somebody updates the rider's declared weight, and diverge from that moment on; the eligibility read of §3.6 exposes both.

Horses are also rationed by **time**, and the ledger has two readings of its own. A horse's committed workload counts every session of that horse whose `schedule_status` is `CREW_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, taking each session's booked `duration_minutes`. Its delivered workload counts only sessions in `COMPLETED`, taking each session's actually-ridden `mounted_minutes`. Sessions in `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` are excluded from **both** readings, which is exactly what makes a cancellation return capacity to the horse. The two readings diverge for every session that is booked but not yet ridden, and for every session ridden short of its booking.

Both readings are taken over a day window and a week window. The day window runs from midnight UTC on the date in question up to, but not including, midnight the following day. The week window starts on the Monday on or before that date, at midnight UTC, and runs seven days forward, again excluding its own end. A horse's own record carries these live counters at the logical clock date: `current_week_start_date` is the Monday on or before today, `current_week_committed_minutes` and `current_day_committed_minutes` carry the committed reading for the current week and the current day, and `current_day_session_count` counts the horse's sessions in `CREW_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` whose `scheduled_start` falls on the logical clock date. A dedicated computed read exposes the same ledger for any `on_date` a caller asks about, in either the `committed` or the `delivered` variant, answering with the `horse_id`, the echoed `on_date`, the `week_start_date` for that date, the `variant` used, the `day_session_count`, the `day_minutes`, the `week_minutes`, and the headroom figures `day_remaining_minutes` (150 minus `day_minutes`) and `week_remaining_minutes` (240 minus `week_minutes`).

Three capacity ceilings apply at booking, all evaluated against the committed reading *after* the new session is notionally added. A horse may carry at most 3 sessions on a calendar date, and at most 150 committed minutes on that date; breaching either limb is `ERR_HORSE_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A horse may carry at most 240 committed minutes in the Monday-based week containing the session date; breaching it is `ERR_HORSE_WEEKLY_MINUTES_EXCEEDED`. Every one of these comparisons permits landing exactly on the ceiling and refuses only the value that exceeds it, so a day filled to precisely 150 minutes is legal and the next minute is not. With sessions running between 30 and 90 minutes, a day reaches exactly 150 with a 90 and a 60, and a week reaches exactly 240 with 150 on Monday and 90 on Tuesday, so both ceilings are reachable and crossable inside the three-sessions-a-day limit.

Two more scheduling rules protect the same horse. A session occupies a **half-open** interval from its `scheduled_start` up to but not including its `scheduled_end`; two such intervals conflict when each starts before the other ends, and a conflict against another live session of the same horse — again meaning `CREW_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` — is `ERR_HORSE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. A session that starts exactly when another ends does *not* overlap. Where no overlap was found, the horse must still be given a **rest interval**: the gap between the end of the earlier interval and the start of the later one must be at least 15 minutes for every neighbouring live session of that horse, and a gap of exactly 15 minutes is allowed. A shorter gap is `ERR_HORSE_REST_INTERVAL`. Together these two rules make the horse's occupancy a buffer-extended half-open interval, and they are evaluated in that order: overlap first, rest interval only if there was none.

Horses go out of service in three ways. A `ProgramAdmin` may place a manual hold by naming a `hold_type` of `VET_HOLD` or `FARRIER_HOLD` together with a `release_at` instant; the horse's `status` becomes that hold type, `hold_release_at` takes the supplied `release_at` verbatim, and `hold_reason` is recorded as the literal string `MANUAL`. The third hold, `REST_HOLD`, is never requested by anybody — it is placed by the incident cascade of §3.9, which sets `hold_release_at` to three days after the incident's `occurred_at` and writes `hold_reason` as the literal string `INCIDENT_REST_HOLD`. Any hold moves the horse off `AVAILABLE` and increments its `version`, and any of the three holds blocks booking, confirmation and completion with `ERR_HORSE_ON_HOLD`.

Coming back is the mirror image. A `ProgramAdmin` releasing a hold moves the horse from `VET_HOLD`, `FARRIER_HOLD` or `REST_HOLD` back to `AVAILABLE`, clearing both `hold_release_at` and `hold_reason` and incrementing `version`, but only once the logical clock has reached `hold_release_at`; an attempt before that instant is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The single exception is an incident rest hold, which a `ProgramAdmin` may lift early by closing the incident that placed it — that is the only path that returns a horse carrying `INCIDENT_REST_HOLD` to `AVAILABLE` before `hold_release_at` has been reached.

### 3.4. Volunteers, Qualifications, and Session Crewing

Volunteers are registered by a `VolunteerCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` with a `full_name`, a `date_of_birth` and a `last_retraining_on` date recording the most recent annual re-training. From these the server derives `age_years` — the whole completed years between `date_of_birth` and the logical clock date, meaning the difference in years reduced by one when the clock's month and day fall strictly before the birth month and day — and `retraining_due_at`, which is 365 days after `last_retraining_on` promoted to midnight UTC. `suspended_until` and `suspension_reason` start null, `open_assignment_count` starts at 0 and thereafter counts the volunteer's `ASSIGNED` crew assignments on sessions that are `CREW_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED`.

A volunteer's `status` follows the precedence chain of §2.11 and cycles in both directions. A suspension placed by an incident makes them `SUSPENDED` until `suspended_until` passes. Reaching `retraining_due_at` makes them `INACTIVE`. Recording the annual re-training with a `completed_on` date writes that date into `last_retraining_on`, re-derives `retraining_due_at`, increments the volunteer's `version`, and returns them `INACTIVE → ACTIVE`. **Open question for the implementer:** the re-training transition is declared out of the `INACTIVE` state, yet a volunteer's qualifications expire on their own 365-day clock anchored on each sign-off date and can therefore lapse while the volunteer is still `ACTIVE`. This PRD does not resolve whether re-training recorded against an `ACTIVE` volunteer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` because the transition's from-state is `INACTIVE`, or is accepted as an ordinary refresh because the operation's stated purpose includes renewing expired qualifications; both readings are consistent with the rest of this document and the implementer must pick one and apply it uniformly.

Competence is tracked per role. A qualification is opened for a `crew_role` of `LEADER`, `SIDEWALKER` or `BARN` with the `training_hours` logged so far, and starts `IN_TRAINING` with `signed_off_on` and `expires_at` null. A `VolunteerCoordinator` signs it off by supplying `signed_off_on`, which moves it `IN_TRAINING → QUALIFIED` and sets `expires_at` to 365 days after `signed_off_on` promoted to midnight UTC, incrementing the qualification's `version`. Sign-off is refused unless the logged `training_hours` have reached the role minimum — 12 hours to lead, 6 to sidewalk, 3 for barn work, all inclusive thresholds — which is `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_QUALIFIED`, and unless the volunteer has reached the role's minimum age, which is `ERR_VOLUNTEER_UNDER_AGE`. Those ages are 16 to lead, 14 to sidewalk and 12 for barn work, and the comparison is inclusive, so a volunteer may sidewalk on their fourteenth birthday. A qualification whose `expires_at` the clock has reached is `EXPIRED`; re-training re-signs **every** expired qualification of that volunteer with `signed_off_on` set to the re-training's `completed_on` and `expires_at` 365 days after it, incrementing each affected qualification's `version`. A qualification also reads as `SUSPENDED` for as long as its owning volunteer is suspended, and returns to `QUALIFIED` when the volunteer's suspension is lifted and its own `expires_at` has not yet been reached. Qualifications are listed by `volunteer_id`, `crew_role` and `status`, and read individually.

Crewing a session is where all of this is enforced at once. A `VolunteerCoordinator`, `ProgramAdmin` or `Instructor` assigns a volunteer to a session by naming the `volunteer_id`, the `crew_role` — only `LEADER` or `SIDEWALKER` here, never `BARN` — the `expected_session_version` demanded by §2.4, and, for a sidewalker, the `hold_type`. The assignment lands `ASSIGNED` with `release_reason` null and increments the session's `version`. Seven checks guard it and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.7, which is worth restating because the rejections are easy to confuse: the requested role must be one the rider actually needs and not already filled, otherwise `ERR_CREW_ROLE_NOT_REQUIRED`; the same volunteer must not already hold an `ASSIGNED` assignment on this session in any role, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREW_ASSIGNMENT`; the volunteer must not already be crewing an overlapping session, otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`; the volunteer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED`; the volunteer must meet the role's minimum age, otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_UNDER_AGE`; the volunteer must hold a `QUALIFIED` qualification for **exactly** this role, otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_QUALIFIED`; and the hold must match, otherwise `ERR_HOLD_TYPE_MISMATCH`. A sidewalker qualification never authorises leading. A sidewalker assignment must carry a `hold_type` equal to the rider's `sidewalker_hold_type`, and a leader assignment must carry none at all — supplying one is the same mismatch error.

The volunteer overlap rule is deliberately stricter than the horse and instructor rules and this is the one place the three diverge. A volunteer's occupancy of a session is a **closed** interval from `scheduled_start` through `scheduled_end` inclusive, with no buffer, and two closed intervals conflict when each begins at or before the other ends. A volunteer whose eleven o'clock session ends exactly as another begins therefore *does* conflict, where the same pair of sessions would be perfectly legal for the horse or the instructor. The rule considers only sessions in `CREW_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED` on which that volunteer already holds an `ASSIGNED` assignment.

Whether a session's crew is complete is the `crew_satisfied` flag, and it demands **exact** equality on both limbs: the count of `ASSIGNED` sidewalker assignments must equal the rider's `required_sidewalker_count`, and the count of `ASSIGNED` leader assignments must equal 1 when the rider's `requires_leader` is true and 0 when it is false. Over-assignment fails just as under-assignment does. Released assignments never count. For a `LEVEL_4` rider the requirement is zero on both limbs, so the **empty** crew satisfies it and such a session confirms with no crew assignments at all.

Releasing a volunteer is a `VolunteerCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` operation carrying a `reason`. The assignment moves `ASSIGNED → RELEASED`, `release_reason` records the supplied reason, the session's `crew_satisfied` is re-derived, the volunteer's `open_assignment_count` drops, and the assignment's `version` increments. If the release drops a `CONFIRMED` session below its crew requirement, that session reverts `CONFIRMED → CREW_PENDING` and its own `version` increments — the reversible edge of the schedule axis, reached without any operator touching the session itself. Crew assignments are listed by `session_id`, `volunteer_id` and `status`, and read individually.

Volunteer service hours are a computed read over a **closed** date range, and the caller must state which reading they want: `period_start`, `period_end` and `variant` are all required. The `scheduled` reading counts the volunteer's `ASSIGNED` assignments on sessions in `CREW_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` and takes each session's `duration_minutes`; the `delivered` reading counts only `COMPLETED` sessions and takes each session's `mounted_minutes`. In both, a session is in range when its `scheduled_start` date falls between `period_start` and `period_end` inclusive. Each qualifying session's minutes are converted to hours and rounded to 2 decimal places **first**, the rounded figures are then summed, and the sum is rounded to 2 decimal places again. The answer carries the `volunteer_id`, the echoed `period_start`, `period_end` and `variant`, the resulting `service_hours`, and a `session_count` giving the number of qualifying assignments.


### 3.5. Instructors, Certification, and Supervision

A `ProgramAdmin` registers an instructor with a `full_name`, a PATH `certification_level` of `IN_TRAINING`, `REGISTERED`, `ADVANCED` or `MASTER`, and a `certification_expires_at` instant marking the end of the current certification cycle, which is caller-supplied and therefore echoed back byte for byte. An instructor whose `certification_level` is `IN_TRAINING` is required to name a `supervisor_instructor_id` pointing at another instructor; an instructor at any other level must omit it or send it as null. **Open question for the implementer:** the ladder assigns no rung to this pairing rule *at registration time* — rung 25 governs the equivalent pairing on a session's `instructor_id` and `supervising_instructor_id`, and its scope is session creation only — so this PRD does not resolve whether a trainee registered without a supervisor is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) as a missing required field or with `ERR_SUPERVISOR_REQUIRED` (422) as a supervision fault; a `supervisor_instructor_id` that names nobody is unambiguously `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404) either way. New instructors start with `ce_hours_accrued` at 0 and `suspended_until` null.

`certification_status` is derived on every read by the precedence chain of §2.11: suspended first, then current while the clock is before `certification_expires_at`, then a 60-day grace window, then lapsed. The consequences of the middle state are deliberately asymmetric. An instructor in `GRACE` may still **complete** a session that is already confirmed — the ride happened and the record must be closed — but may not **book** a new session and may not **confirm** one, and both refusals are `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_LAPSED`. Once the clock passes 60 days beyond `certification_expires_at` the instructor is `LAPSED` and every instructor-bearing write fails with the same error. Instructors are listed and filtered by `certification_status`, `certification_level` and `supervisor_instructor_id`, the last of which is how a caller finds every trainee reporting to a given supervisor, and read individually.

Renewal has two halves. Continuing education is accrued first: a `ProgramAdmin` logs `hours` (which must be greater than 0) together with the `completed_on` date they were earned, and the hours are added to the instructor's running `ce_hours_accrued`, incrementing the instructor's `version`. Then, once at least 20 hours have accrued, the same role may renew a certification that is in `GRACE` or `LAPSED` by supplying a `renewed_on` date: `certification_expires_at` is recomputed as 730 days — two years — after `renewed_on`, `ce_hours_accrued` is reset to 0, and `version` increments, returning the instructor to `CURRENT`. Renewing with fewer than 20 accrued hours is `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`, and attempting to renew a certification that is already `CURRENT` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Note that `certification_expires_at` arrives caller-supplied at registration and is echoed verbatim, but after a renewal it is a derived instant and is rendered with three fractional digits per §2.5.

Supervision matters at booking time. When a session names a `supervising_instructor_id`, that supervisor is the **effective instructor** for the purpose of the tier comparison in §3.2; when no supervisor is named, the instructor of record is. Certification, however, is checked on *both*: an instructor of record in `GRACE` blocks a booking even if the supervisor is `CURRENT`, and a supervisor in `LAPSED` blocks it even if the instructor of record is impeccable. The pairing itself must also be well-formed — a trainee of record with no supervisor, a supervisor whose tier ordinal is not strictly greater than the trainee's, or a supervisor named alongside a non-trainee instructor of record, are all `ERR_SUPERVISOR_REQUIRED`.

### 3.6. Booking Through Delivery: the Session Lifecycle

Booking a horse-rider match is the densest operation in the system. An `Instructor` or `ProgramAdmin` supplies the `rider_id`, the `horse_id`, the `instructor_id` of the instructor of record, the `activity_type`, the caller-supplied `scheduled_start`, and a `duration_minutes` between 30 and 90 inclusive, optionally naming a `supervising_instructor_id`. The server derives `scheduled_end` as `scheduled_start` plus `duration_minutes`, rendered as a server-derived instant with three fractional digits even though `scheduled_start` itself is echoed verbatim. The new session lands in `CREW_PENDING` on the schedule axis and `UNBILLED` on the billing axis, with `mounted_minutes` null, `service_hours` 0, `cancellation_reason` null and `crew_satisfied` false. It also derives `quoted_fee_cents`, `effective_load_lb`, `precaution_tier` and `required_instructor_tier`, and it raises the horse's committed day and week minutes by `duration_minutes`.

The quoted fee is `duration_minutes` multiplied by the per-minute rate of the `activity_type`, in whole cents, with no rounding needed because both operands are integers. `THERAPEUTIC_RIDING` bills 150 cents a minute — so an hour of therapeutic riding quotes 9000 cents, ninety dollars. `HIPPOTHERAPY` bills 260 cents a minute, `EQUINE_LEARNING` 120, and `VAULTING` 180.

Everything in §2.7 rungs 14 through 32 is evaluated at booking, in that order, and it is worth naming the sequence once in business terms: incident holds, then the finalized-period freeze, then the horse's operational hold, then instructor certification, then the physician release, then the target's own state, then the rider being `ACTIVE`, then the reassessment clock, then condition-policy coverage, contraindication and precaution clearance, then supervision pairing, then the instructor tier, then the horse's load ceiling, then the horse's overlap and rest interval, then the horse's daily and weekly ceilings, then the instructor's own overlap. A booking that violates several of these reports only the earliest.

A session's whole admissibility picture is also available as a computed read, without attempting a write. It answers with the `session_id`, the `variant` used, an `admissible` boolean, a `blocking_gate`, the `precaution_tier`, the `required_instructor_tier`, the `instructor_certification_status`, the `release_status`, the `effective_load_lb`, the `horse_max_load_lb`, the `crew_satisfied` flag, the rider's `required_sidewalker_count`, `requires_leader` and `sidewalker_hold_type`, and the `quoted_fee_cents`. The `variant` parameter selects one axis and leaves the other at its default. `current_declared`, the default, and `at_assessment` select which rider weight feeds `effective_load_lb`, as described in §3.3, and leave the clock axis at the logical clock. `at_booking_instant` and `at_ride_instant` select the instant at which `release_status` and `instructor_certification_status` are evaluated — the logical clock of the call, or the session's `scheduled_start` respectively — and leave the weight axis at `current_declared`. An instructor whose certificate lapses between booking and ride therefore reports `CURRENT` at the booking instant and `GRACE` or `LAPSED` at the ride instant from identical stored data. **Open question for the implementer:** this PRD does not fix the string vocabulary of `blocking_gate`. Two readings are equally consistent with the rest of this document — that it carries the error identifier of the first check that would refuse the write (for instance `ERR_HORSE_ON_HOLD`), or that it carries the name of the gate itself — and nothing else in the specification depends on which is chosen; what is fixed is that `blocking_gate` is populated exactly when `admissible` is false and is null when `admissible` is true.

Confirmation moves a session `CREW_PENDING → CONFIRMED`. It takes no request body. Beyond the gates, it requires `crew_satisfied` to be true — otherwise `ERR_CREW_INCOMPLETE` — and it re-runs the rider, reassessment, condition, tier and certification checks, because any of them may have decayed since the booking. A confirmed session can fall back to `CREW_PENDING` when a crew release drops it below requirement, as described in §3.4, and can be confirmed again once the crew is rebuilt.

Completion is the only operation that creates money, and it is bound to the instructor who taught. The session's own instructor supplies `mounted_minutes` — at least 0 and at most the booked `duration_minutes` — and may optionally name a `preferred_grant_id`. The session moves `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`, `mounted_minutes` is stored, and `service_hours` is recomputed as the mounted minutes divided by 60 and rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. A `SESSION_FEE` charge is created whose gross amount is billed on the minutes **actually ridden**, not the minutes booked: a 60-minute `THERAPEUTIC_RIDING` booking ridden for 45 minutes quotes 9000 cents on the session and bills 6750 cents on the charge, from identical stored data. The funding waterfall of §3.8 then runs, and the session's `billing_status` becomes `FUNDED` when the resulting charge's `balance_due_cents` is 0 and `CHARGED` otherwise. Completion is additionally refused when a finalized report already covers the session date, when the instructor's certification is neither `CURRENT` nor `GRACE`, when the rider's release is not `ACTIVE` at the logical clock, and — for a `FUNDED_ONLY` rider — when the waterfall would leave a remainder.

Cancellation is available to an `Instructor`, a `ProgramAdmin` or an `IntakeCoordinator` from either `CREW_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED`. The caller supplies a `cancellation_reason` drawn from `RIDER_REQUEST`, `WEATHER`, `HORSE_PULLED` or `PROGRAM_CLOSURE`, and the value **stored** on the session is derived rather than copied. `WEATHER`, `HORSE_PULLED` and `PROGRAM_CLOSURE` are stored as themselves and are never chargeable. `RIDER_REQUEST` is resolved against the notice window: the hours of notice are the time from the logical clock to the session's `scheduled_start`, and 24 hours or more of notice stores `EARLY_RIDER` while anything less stores `LATE_RIDER`. The boundary is inclusive on the free side — a cancellation exactly 24.0 hours ahead is `EARLY_RIDER` and free — and a cancellation made when the clock is already past `scheduled_start` yields negative notice and therefore lands on `LATE_RIDER`. Whichever reason is derived, the session moves to `CANCELLED`, every `ASSIGNED` crew assignment on it becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to the literal string `SESSION_CANCELLED`, the horse's committed day and week minutes drop by `duration_minutes`, and the session's `version` increments. Only the derived class `LATE_RIDER` creates a charge: a `LATE_CANCELLATION_FEE` for half the quoted fee, rounded half-up to whole cents, which then runs the waterfall. No other derived class creates any charge at all.

A no-show is recorded by an `Instructor` or `ProgramAdmin` against a `CONFIRMED` session once the logical clock has reached `scheduled_start`; attempting it earlier, or against a session in any other state, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The session moves `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW`, its stored `cancellation_reason` becomes `NO_SHOW`, every `ASSIGNED` crew assignment is `RELEASED` with the same `SESSION_CANCELLED` reason, and a `NO_SHOW_FEE` charge is created for the full quoted fee — 100 per cent of it, rounded half-up to whole cents — which then runs the waterfall with grant and scholarship money excluded. Both fee charges are computed from the **quoted** fee, the one based on booked `duration_minutes`, never from mounted minutes.

Sessions are read individually with both status axes and every derived field re-evaluated, and are listed and filtered by `rider_id`, `horse_id`, `instructor_id`, `supervising_instructor_id`, `schedule_status`, `billing_status` and `on_date`.


### 3.7. Goals, Progress Notes, and the Staleness Sweep

Therapeutic progress is tracked as measurable goals. An `Instructor`, `ProgramAdmin` or `IntakeCoordinator` opens one against a rider by naming the `domain` — one of `PHYSICAL`, `COGNITIVE`, `SOCIAL_EMOTIONAL`, `BEHAVIORAL` or `EDUCATIONAL` — a free-text `objective` stating what is being measured, a `baseline_rating` recorded at creation, and a `target_rating` that counts as a hit. Both ratings run from 1 to 5 inclusive and `target_rating` must be strictly greater than `baseline_rating`. A new goal is `ACTIVE`, with `current_rating` and `last_rated_at` null, `consecutive_target_hits` 0, `discontinue_reason` null, and `stale_after` set to 90 days after the goal's `created_at`. Goals are listed and filtered by `rider_id`, `status` and `domain`, and read individually.

Two operator actions move a goal directly. Discontinuing it, with a supplied `reason` stored as `discontinue_reason`, is available from `ACTIVE`, `MET` or `STALE` and lands in `DISCONTINUED`, which is terminal; discontinuing an already-discontinued goal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Reopening is available from `MET` only — reopening a goal in any other state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and returns it to `ACTIVE` while resetting `consecutive_target_hits` to 0. Reopening also carries a required `reason` in the request body; the field must be present, and no field on the goal preserves it. Both actions bump the goal's `version` and both re-derive the rider's `goals_met_count`, which is simply the number of that rider's goals whose `status` is `MET`.

Progress is recorded against a delivered session. Only an `Instructor` may draft a **progress note**, supplying a free-text `narrative` and a `goal_ratings` array whose entries each carry a `goal_id` and an integer `rating` between 1 and 5 inclusive. The server copies the session's `rider_id` onto the note and sets `author_instructor_id` from `X-User-ID`; the note lands in `DRAFT` with `finalized_at` null and `recorded_service_hours` 0. Every `goal_id` in the array must belong to this session's rider and must not name a goal in `DISCONTINUED`, which is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. **Open question for the implementer:** finalization plainly requires the parent session to be `COMPLETED`, but this PRD does not resolve whether *drafting* a note against a session that has not yet been completed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at draft time or is permitted and simply cannot be finalized until the ride is closed out; the state rung's scope over creation endpoints is not pinned, and the implementer must choose one reading and hold to it. Notes are listed and filtered by `session_id`, `rider_id`, `author_instructor_id` and `status`, and read individually.

Finalizing a note takes no request body and may be performed only by the note's own author. It stamps `finalized_at` with the logical clock and copies the parent session's `service_hours` into `recorded_service_hours`, freezing the hours the note is evidence for. It then applies every rating in `goal_ratings`, **in the order the client supplied them** — the array order is the processing order, and it is observable whenever two entries name the same goal. For each entry, the goal's `current_rating` becomes the supplied `rating` and its `last_rated_at` becomes the logical clock. A rating at or above the goal's `target_rating` increases `consecutive_target_hits` by 1; a rating below it resets `consecutive_target_hits` to 0. A goal that was `STALE` returns to `ACTIVE` on being rated at all, regardless of the value. A goal whose `consecutive_target_hits` reaches 2 moves to `MET`. The rider's `goals_met_count` is re-derived afterwards, and each touched goal's `version` increments along with the note's own.

Where the note lands depends on the reporting calendar, and both branches are real destinations. When no period report covering the session date is `FINALIZED`, the note moves `DRAFT → FINALIZED`. When one is, the note moves `DRAFT → AMENDED` instead: the goal side effects fire identically, `finalized_at` is stamped identically, the covering report's `amendment_count` increases by 1, its `amendment_net_cents` is unchanged because a note carries no money, and the report's frozen aggregates are not touched. Note the asymmetry with the booking and completion gates — a finalized period *blocks* new and newly-completed sessions with `ERR_REPORT_FINALIZED`, but it does not block a note; it re-routes it.

A goal that nobody rates goes **stale**. Its `stale_after` is 90 days after `last_rated_at` when it has ever been rated, and 90 days after `created_at` when it has not. A `ProgramAdmin` runs a periodic sweep that moves every goal whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `stale_after` the logical clock has reached — the boundary is inclusive, so a goal exactly at `stale_after` is swept — into `STALE`, incrementing each swept goal's `version`. The sweep answers with a `swept_count`, the array of `stale_goal_ids` in `created_at` ascending order, and the `evaluated_at` instant it ran at. The sweep is deliberately **one-directional**: it only ever moves goals from `ACTIVE` to `STALE`, it never moves one back, and it never touches goals in `MET`, `STALE` or `DISCONTINUED`. Because `STALE` is a persisted state that only an explicit new rating can leave, a goal can never silently drift back to `ACTIVE` when the clock moves.


### 3.8. Funding, Charges, Reversal, and Period Reporting

Restricted money arrives as **grants**. A `ProgramAdmin` registers one with a `grant_name`, a `funder_name`, an `awarded_amount_cents` greater than 0, and a fiscal period given as the date-only `period_start` and `period_end`, both inclusive. Two optional restrictions may be attached: `restricted_program_code`, which when set limits the grant to riders whose `program_code` equals it, and `restricted_demographic`, which when set limits it to riders whose `demographic_group` equals it. Both default to null, meaning unrestricted. A new grant has `allocated_amount_cents` 0 and `remaining_amount_cents` equal to `awarded_amount_cents`, and reads as `OPEN`; it reads as `EXHAUSTED` exactly when `remaining_amount_cents` is 0. `allocated_amount_cents` is always the sum of `amount_cents` over that grant's allocation lines whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`, and `remaining_amount_cents` is `awarded_amount_cents` less that sum. Grants are read individually with their live ledger.

Rider-specific money arrives as **scholarship awards**. An `IntakeCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` awards one with a `cap_pct` greater than 0 and at most 0.90 — the percentage-of-fee ceiling — an absolute `period_cap_cents` greater than 0, and an inclusive `period_start` and `period_end`. A new award has `period_used_cents` 0, `superseded_at` null, and reads as `ACTIVE`; filing a later award for the same rider whose period overlaps an existing `ACTIVE` one stamps the older award's `superseded_at` with the logical clock. An award reads as `EXHAUSTED` once `period_used_cents` has reached `period_cap_cents`. Awards are listed by `rider_id` and `status`, and read individually with their live period ledger.

Every charge is settled by a **funding waterfall** that runs in one pinned order and produces one allocation line per source that contributed, plus a private-pay line that is always produced. The charge's gross `amount_cents` is the starting figure; call the amount still unfunded the remainder, which begins equal to the gross. Which sources are permitted depends on `charge_type`: a `SESSION_FEE` may draw on all five, in the order grant, scholarship, sliding scale, payer, private pay; a `LATE_CANCELLATION_FEE` and a `NO_SHOW_FEE` may draw only on sliding scale, payer and private pay, with the grant and scholarship steps skipped entirely. A charge whose `charge_type` is not `SESSION_FEE` therefore carries `scholarship_excluded` true, and a `SESSION_FEE` carries it false. Each line records its `source_type`, its `sequence_index` — 1 for grant, 2 for scholarship, 3 for sliding scale, 4 for payer, 5 for private pay — and its `amount_cents`, and lines are created in ascending `sequence_index`, with the grant lines in the candidate order below. Each line's amount is rounded to whole cents **at line level** before it is subtracted from the remainder; there is never a sum-then-round step, and every draw is additionally capped at the remainder so the lines can never over-allocate.

**Step 1, grant.** The candidate set is every grant that is `OPEN`, whose fiscal period contains the session date (the calendar date of the session's `scheduled_start`, with both period bounds inclusive), whose `restricted_program_code` is either null or equal to the rider's `program_code`, and whose `restricted_demographic` is either null or equal to the rider's `demographic_group`. Candidates are ranked by `period_end` ascending, then by `remaining_amount_cents` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. Walking that ranking while a remainder survives, each grant contributes the smaller of its own `remaining_amount_cents` and the current remainder, and a positive contribution emits a line naming the grant through `grant_id`. A grant whose restrictions do not match is silently skipped: an empty candidate set contributes 0 and the waterfall simply proceeds, never an error.

**Step 2, scholarship.** The award used is the rider's award whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose period contains the session date, taking the first row when ranked by `period_end` ascending then `created_at` ascending. Its contribution is the smallest of three figures: the percentage cap, which is `cap_pct` of the **gross** charge amount rounded half-up to whole cents; the period room, which is `period_cap_cents` less `period_used_cents`; and the current remainder. A positive contribution emits a line naming the award through `scholarship_award_id`.

**Step 3, sliding scale.** The rider's `household_income_band` sets a discount percentage — `BAND_A` 60 per cent, `BAND_B` 40 per cent, `BAND_C` 20 per cent and `BAND_D` nothing at all — and the contribution is that percentage of the **current remainder**, rounded half-up to whole cents, capped at the remainder.

**Step 4, payer.** The contribution is the smaller of the rider's `payer_rate_cents` ceiling and the current remainder.

**Step 5, private pay.** This line is **always** emitted, even when its `amount_cents` is 0, and it carries whatever remainder survived the first four steps. That same figure becomes the charge's `balance_due_cents`, and `allocated_cents` becomes the sum of every emitted line, which by construction equals the gross. Before the line is written, one refusal applies: when the charge is a `SESSION_FEE`, the rider's `funding_mode` is `FUNDED_ONLY`, and a remainder survives, the whole request is rejected with `ERR_FUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and nothing at all is persisted — the session stays `CONFIRMED` and no charge or allocation line exists. That refusal is scoped to session fees: a cancellation or no-show fee always lands on private pay regardless of `funding_mode`.

Two consequences of this ordering are deliberate and should not be smoothed away. The scholarship cap is a percentage of the **gross** while the sliding-scale discount is a percentage of the **remainder**, so a rider holding a scholarship arrives at step 3 with a smaller base and receives a smaller absolute sliding-scale discount than an otherwise identical rider without one. And because grant and scholarship money cannot touch a cancellation or no-show fee, the same rider pays substantially **more** out of pocket for a no-show fee than for the delivered session it replaced — the scholarship that shielded the session fee is unavailable, and the sliding-scale base is no longer shrunken.

A completing instructor may name a `preferred_grant_id`. It is validated against exactly the step-1 eligibility filter for this rider and this session date — `OPEN` status, a period covering the date, and both restrictions satisfied — and a grant that fails any limb is rejected with `ERR_GRANT_RESTRICTION_VIOLATION`. **Open question for the implementer:** this PRD does not resolve what a *passing* `preferred_grant_id` does to the allocation. Two readings are available — that the field is purely a caller assertion, validated and then discarded, leaving the candidate ranking above untouched; or that the named grant is promoted to the head of the step-1 candidate order while the remaining candidates keep their declared ranking — and the specification pins only the rejection, not the effect.

Charges themselves are not created directly by anybody: they arise from completion, from a late rider cancellation, and from a no-show, as described in §3.6. A charge carries its `charge_type`, the gross `amount_cents`, the live `allocated_cents` and `balance_due_cents`, the `scholarship_excluded` flag, its derived `status`, and `reversed_at` and `reversal_reason` which are null until it is reversed. Charges are listed by `session_id`, `status` and `charge_type` and read individually with their live allocation ledger; allocation lines are listed by `charge_id`, `grant_id`, `scholarship_award_id`, `source_type` and `status`, and read individually.

**Reversal** is a `BillingClerk` or `ProgramAdmin` operation carrying a `reason`, and it is a five-hop cascade in which each hop is caused by the previous one rather than by the original request. The charge must be `PENDING` or `FUNDED`; reversing an already-reversed charge is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Hop one moves the charge to `REVERSED`, stamps `reversed_at` with the logical clock, stores `reversal_reason`, zeroes both `allocated_cents` and `balance_due_cents`, and increments the charge's `version`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves every one of that charge's allocation lines from `ALLOCATED` to `RELEASED`, incrementing each line's `version`. Hop three, caused by hop two, restores each released line's source ledger: a grant line reduces the grant's `allocated_amount_cents` and raises its `remaining_amount_cents` by the line's `amount_cents`, flipping the grant from `EXHAUSTED` back to `OPEN` once anything remains; a scholarship line reduces the award's `period_used_cents` by the same amount, flipping the award from `EXHAUSTED` back to `ACTIVE` once usage falls below `period_cap_cents`. Hop four, caused by hop three, moves the parent session's `billing_status` from `CHARGED` or `FUNDED` to `REVERSED` — but only once every charge of that session is `REVERSED` and every touched source ledger has been restored — and increments the session's `version`. Hop five, caused by hop four, reaches the period report covering the session date, and its behaviour differs by that report's own state, which is the subject of the rest of this section.

**Period reports** are the centre's accountability artefact. A `ProgramAdmin` creates one over an inclusive `period_start` and `period_end`, and it lands in `DRAFT`. A report's session set is every session whose `scheduled_start` date falls inside that closed range. From that set the report derives `total_service_hours`, which sums the per-session `service_hours` of the `COMPLETED` sessions — each already rounded to 2 decimal places — and rounds the sum to 2 decimal places again; `rider_count`, the number of distinct riders with at least one `COMPLETED` session in the range; `veteran_rider_count`, the subset of those riders whose `demographic_group` is `VETERAN`; and `volunteer_hours`, the delivered reading of volunteer service hours across the same range, summed over all volunteers. From the charges belonging to those sessions it derives `grant_utilization_cents`, `scholarship_spend_cents` and `private_pay_cents` — the sums of `amount_cents` over `ALLOCATED` lines whose `source_type` is `GRANT`, `SCHOLARSHIP` and `PRIVATE_PAY` respectively — and `total_charged_cents`, the sum of `amount_cents` over in-period charges whose `status` is not `REVERSED`. While the report is `DRAFT`, `amendment_count` and `amendment_net_cents` are both 0 and every read recomputes every aggregate from live data, so a charge reversed after the report was created simply disappears from `total_charged_cents`.

Finalizing a report recomputes every aggregate once from live data, freezes all of them, moves the report `DRAFT → FINALIZED`, stamps `finalized_at` with the logical clock and increments the report's `version`. From that instant the report is immutable and the finalized-period gate begins refusing new bookings and new completions whose session date falls inside the period, and refusing the creation of any report whose period overlaps this one, all with `ERR_REPORT_FINALIZED`; finalizing an already-finalized report is the same error. Late-arriving money does not disturb the frozen numbers: when hop five of a reversal reaches a `FINALIZED` report, `amendment_count` increases by 1 and `amendment_net_cents` decreases by the reversed charge's gross `amount_cents`, while every aggregate — `total_charged_cents` included — stays exactly where finalization left it. When hop five reaches a `DRAFT` report there is no amendment ledger to write to; the report simply recomputes on its next read. Reading the same period the two ways therefore yields two different, both-correct answers, and the relationship that ties them together is that the frozen `total_charged_cents` plus `amendment_net_cents` always equals what a live recomputation would produce right now.


### 3.9. Safety Incidents, Holds, and the Return to Service

Safety reporting is never suppressed. An `Instructor` or `ProgramAdmin` files an incident against a session with an `incident_type`, an `injury_reported` boolean, the caller-supplied `occurred_at` instant, a free-text `narrative`, and optionally an `implicated_volunteer_id` naming the volunteer involved — null when nobody is. The incident lands in `OPEN` with `reviewer_notes` and `resolution` null, and its `reported_at` stamped at the logical clock.

`severity` is a pure table lookup on the pair of `incident_type` and `injury_reported`, and all ten pairs are enumerated. A `FALL` with an injury is `SEV_1` and without one is `SEV_2`. A `MEDICAL_EVENT` is `SEV_1` whether or not an injury was reported. A `LOOSE_HORSE` with an injury is `SEV_1` and without one is `SEV_2`. An `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE` with an injury is `SEV_2` and without one is `SEV_3`. A `NEAR_MISS` is `SEV_3` either way.

Severity sets the reporting deadline. `report_due_at` is 24 hours after `occurred_at` for a `SEV_1`, 48 hours for a `SEV_2` and 72 hours for a `SEV_3`, and `reported_late` is true when `reported_at` is strictly after `report_due_at` — reporting exactly at the deadline is on time. A late report is **recorded and never rejected**: the centre would rather have a late incident report than none.

Severity also decides which holds land, and the four flags on the incident record what happened. For a `SEV_1`, `rider_hold_applied`, `horse_hold_applied` and `instructor_suspension_applied` are all true, and `volunteer_suspension_applied` is true when — and only when — an `implicated_volunteer_id` was supplied. For a `SEV_2`, `horse_hold_applied` is true and `volunteer_suspension_applied` is true when a volunteer was implicated, while `rider_hold_applied` and `instructor_suspension_applied` are false. For a `SEV_3` all four are false and no hold of any kind is placed.

Each hold takes a different shape. A rider hold moves the rider `ACTIVE → MEDICAL_HOLD` and sets `medical_hold_incident_id` to the new incident's `id`. A horse hold moves the horse `AVAILABLE → REST_HOLD` with `hold_reason` the literal `INCIDENT_REST_HOLD` and `hold_release_at` three days after the incident's `occurred_at`. A volunteer suspension writes `suspension_reason` as the literal `INCIDENT_SEV_1` or `INCIDENT_SEV_2` according to severity, and sets `suspended_until` to 30 days after `occurred_at` for a `SEV_1` and to the open-ended sentinel instant `9999-12-31T00:00:00.000Z` for a `SEV_2`, meaning the `SEV_2` suspension can only ever be lifted by closing the incident. An instructor suspension likewise sets `suspended_until` to `9999-12-31T00:00:00.000Z`, open-ended by construction. Every entity a hold lands on has its `version` incremented. **Open question for the implementer:** where a session names both an instructor of record and a supervising instructor, this PRD does not resolve whether a `SEV_1` instructor suspension attaches to the instructor of record alone or to both instructors on the session; the severity matrix names the effect without naming its subject when supervision is in play, and the implementer must pick one reading.

The rider hold is the head of a four-hop cascade, and each hop is caused by the one before it rather than by the filing itself. Hop one is the incident's creation and the rider's move to `MEDICAL_HOLD`. Hop two, caused by hop one, cancels every session of that rider whose `schedule_status` is `CREW_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `scheduled_start` is still in the future relative to the logical clock, storing `RIDER_HOLD` as each session's `cancellation_reason`; **no fee is created for any of them, whatever the notice window would otherwise have implied**, and the incident's `cancelled_session_count` records how many were cancelled. Hop three, caused by hop two, releases every `ASSIGNED` crew assignment on each of those cancelled sessions, writing `SESSION_CANCELLED` as each `release_reason`. Hop four, caused by hop three, re-derives every affected volunteer's `open_assignment_count` downward.

While an incident is `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, every hold it placed is the highest-precedence refusal in the system: any booking, confirmation, completion or crew assignment touching a held rider, horse, volunteer or instructor is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_HOLD_ACTIVE` before any other check is reached. This is also why a volunteer suspended by a live incident is reported through the incident gate rather than through `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED`; that latter error is what a caller sees for a volunteer who has gone `INACTIVE` by reaching `retraining_due_at`, or for one whose suspension outlived a closed incident.

Review is a `ProgramAdmin` operation carrying `reviewer_notes`, moving the incident `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW` and incrementing its `version`. It changes nothing else: every hold placed at creation remains in force through review, which is deliberate.

Closing is where holds come off, selectively. A `ProgramAdmin` supplies a `resolution` together with three booleans — `release_rider_hold`, `release_horse_hold` and `release_volunteer_suspension` — and the incident moves `UNDER_REVIEW → CLOSED`; closing an incident still in `OPEN` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When `release_rider_hold` is true, the rider's `medical_hold_incident_id` is cleared — but the rider **stays** in `MEDICAL_HOLD`. Closing the incident alone never returns a rider to riding; a fresh physician clearance is always required, and only then may a `ProgramAdmin` move the rider `MEDICAL_HOLD → ACTIVE` by naming a `medical_release_id` belonging to that rider whose derived status is `ACTIVE`, together with a `note`. When `release_horse_hold` is true and the horse is carrying an `INCIDENT_REST_HOLD`, the horse returns to `AVAILABLE` with `hold_release_at` and `hold_reason` cleared, and this is the only route that lifts an incident rest hold early. When `release_volunteer_suspension` is true, the implicated volunteer's `suspended_until` and `suspension_reason` are cleared and they return to `ACTIVE`. The suspended instructor is released unconditionally on close — no flag governs it — with `suspended_until` set back to null. Every entity whose hold was lifted has its `version` incremented, as does the incident.

Incidents are listed and filtered by `session_id`, `implicated_volunteer_id`, `status` and `severity`, and read individually.
