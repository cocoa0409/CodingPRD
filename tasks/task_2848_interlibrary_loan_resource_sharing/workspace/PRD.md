# Interlibrary Loan & Resource Sharing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An academic library's resource-sharing unit lives between two pressures. On one side sit patrons who need a monograph or a journal article that the library does not hold; on the other sit dozens of consortium partner libraries, each with its own fee schedule, turnaround promise, renewal policy and reciprocity terms, and each of which is simultaneously a lender to us and a borrower from us. Between the two lies a body of rules that no spreadsheet survives: copyright compliance limits on how many free article fills a single journal may supply in a calendar year, patron standing and unpaid-balance gates, hold-shelf and electronic-access expiry windows, recall handling, purchase-on-demand escalation for requests the rota cannot fill, and a monthly borrowing-versus-lending settlement across the whole consortium.

This document specifies the API for that unit. The service takes a request in, checks it against local holdings and licensed electronic access before it ever touches a partner, walks a ranked rota of candidate lenders one round at a time, tracks the physical parcel and the resulting loan through pickup, renewal, recall, return, damage and loss, delivers article scans through a time- and count-limited access window, escalates dead ends to acquisitions, notifies the patron at every lifecycle event, and reconciles the money with each partner month by month. Every endpoint is mounted under the `/ill` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described in §4.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **all money is integer cents** — there are no fractional currency values anywhere in the system, and every percentage or rate is applied with one explicitly pinned rounding step. Second, **nothing happens on a wall clock**. The server maintains a logical UTC clock that every deadline, expiry, window and due-date rule reads, and every time-driven state transition in the domain fires inside one explicit operational sweep rather than in a background scheduler. This makes the entire lifecycle — including a fourteen-day transit failure or a thirty-day electronic-access expiry — reproducible from a fixed starting instant.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — including list reads, the operational sweep and the logical-clock control endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before every other check in the system; a request that fails it never reaches path resolution, body validation or business rules.

The six roles are `Patron`, `IllBorrowingStaff`, `PartnerAgent`, `AcquisitionsLibrarian`, `ConsortiumAccountant` and `LibraryAdmin`. Once authentication succeeds, the acting role must appear in the authorized-role list published for the endpoint in §4; if it does not, the response is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the six roles are backed by a persisted entity, which is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Patron`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patron` row. A `Patron` may act only on their own account and on resources that trace back to it: an `IllRequest` whose `patron_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and any `LoanRecord` or `ElectronicDelivery` whose parent request carries that `patron_id`. Addressing another patron's request, loan or delivery is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Partner` row. A `PartnerAgent` may act only on a `SupplyOffer` whose `partner_id` equals `X-User-ID`, on a `Shipment` or `LoanRecord` whose parent request has `committed_partner_id` equal to `X-User-ID`, and on a `PartnerInvoice` whose `partner_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Reaching into another partner's offer, parcel, loan or invoice is likewise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

The remaining four roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `IllBorrowingStaff`, `AcquisitionsLibrarian`, `ConsortiumAccountant` or `LibraryAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server does record it where a workflow demands an audit counterparty — as `intake_user_id` on an `IllRequest`, as `offered_by_user_id` on a `SupplyOffer`, as `approved_by_user_id` on a `CopyrightClearance`, and as `escalated_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a `PurchaseOnDemandOrder` — but it performs no ownership comparison for these roles.

Those recorded operator identifiers exist because the unit enforces **segregation of duties** on two decisions. The `AcquisitionsLibrarian` who decides a purchase-on-demand proposal must not be the user who escalated it, and the `IllBorrowingStaff` member who records a paid-royalty copyright clearance must not be the user who took the request in. In the first case `X-User-ID` is compared against the order's `escalated_by_user_id`; in the second against the request's `intake_user_id`. An equal comparison in either case is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). This check sits immediately after ownership and before any existence or state check, so a self-decision is reported as a duties violation rather than being masked by a later rung of the ladder.

Role responsibilities are otherwise divided as follows. `IllBorrowingStaff` runs the borrowing desk: intake, royalty clearances, rota rounds, escalation, parcel receipt, hold-shelf pickup, return and loss declaration, plus the copyright-usage read. `PartnerAgent` acts for a lending library: accepting or declining offers, shipping, confirming what came back, recalling an item, delivering a scan, and disputing or reinstating an invoice. `AcquisitionsLibrarian` decides and receives purchases. `ConsortiumAccountant` generates, issues and settles partner invoices and applies patron charge settlements. `LibraryAdmin` owns partner master data, reciprocity terms, patron standing and migrated infraction history, and may waive a patron's charges.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many rows come back; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. The `offset` parameter says how many rows to skip and defaults to 0; negative offsets are not accepted. A `limit` outside the range 1 to 100 inclusive, or an `offset` below 0, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in this domain — `Patron`, `Partner`, `ReciprocityAgreement`, `Rota`, `RotaEntry`, `BibItem`, `IllRequest`, `SupplyOffer`, `CopyrightClearance`, `Shipment`, `LoanRecord`, `ElectronicDelivery`, `PurchaseOnDemandOrder`, `PartnerInvoice`, `LendingCharge` and `PatronNotification` — carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`).

All of those resources except one also carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a patron's counters or risk scores are recalculated, or when a partner's balance figures move because a request was committed or cancelled, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented.

The single exception is `PatronNotification`. Notices are append-only records of something that already happened; a notification row carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it. There is no endpoint that updates or deletes a notification.

### 2.5. Money, Counts and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary field in this specification is an integer number of cents and is transported as a JSON integer, never as a string and never as a decimal. Field names ending in `_cents` are always whole cents; field names ending in `_days`, `_items`, `_count`, `_hours`, `_months` or `_year` are whole integers of their stated unit.

Only two rules in the whole domain introduce a fraction, and each resolves it with a single rounding step, rounding half up (a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude). The first is the departmental subsidy on a patron charge, where a percentage rate is applied to a gross amount and rounded once. The second is the consortium surcharge on an invoice line, where the surcharge is rounded once **per line** and only then summed — summing the raw line amounts first and rounding the total once is a different and incorrect answer. Everywhere else the arithmetic is exact integer work: fee ranking, royalty fees, overdue fines, the settlement waterfall, standing scores and window counting all stay in whole cents or whole counts, and no intermediate value is ever rounded.

Elapsed-time arithmetic is whole-day arithmetic on UTC instants. Where a rule converts an interval into days it divides the elapsed seconds by 86,400 and takes the floor, so a partial day never counts as a whole one. Where a rule adds days to an instant it preserves the time-of-day component of that instant; a loan received at 14:30 on one day and given a 21-day period is due at 14:30 on the twenty-first day after it. Where a rule converts months to years it takes the ceiling, as §3.3 describes for embargoes.

### 2.6. Snapshots, Cascades and Derived Counters

This domain distinguishes sharply between **live master data** and **snapshots taken at a decision point**. A partner's published terms — its fee, its turnaround, its loan period, its renewal ceiling and its renewal permission — are live master data that an administrator may change at any time. The moment a lender commits to a request, the terms that matter to that request are copied onto the request and later onto the loan, and from then on they are frozen: updating the partner afterwards changes nothing about a commitment or a loan that was already snapshotted. The same applies to a rota entry's quoted figures, which are copied onto the offer when the offer is opened, and to the replacement cost of a borrowed item, which is copied onto the loan at receipt.

Several operations mutate more than one row. A lender's acceptance prices the request and moves the committed partner's balance figures; a damage confirmation writes the parcel, the loan, the request, the patron's counters and possibly the patron's account standing; a cancellation reaches into clearances, offers, purchase proposals, the request's money fields and the partner's balances. Each affected row is a separate mutation with its own `updated_at` refresh and `version` increment. Where a workflow narrative in §3 lists a cascade, every element of that cascade is required, not optional.

One balance figure is a conserved quantity and deserves explicit statement. A partner's `borrow_payable_cents` is the sum of the committed fees of that partner's live borrow requests. Committing a request adds that request's committed fee; cancelling the same request must return `borrow_payable_cents` to exactly the value it held before the commitment, with no drift in either direction. The same conservation applies to `borrowed_from_count` and, through it, to `net_borrow_imbalance_items`.

Derived counters are never client-supplied. Every field described in §3 as a count, a total, a score or a balance is computed by the server from the rows that feed it, and a client-supplied value for such a field in a request body is ignored.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Partners and Rotas

Three write operations in this domain can legitimately supersede a row that another writer may already have replaced. Each of them is therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the **parent** row it depends on, and each requires the client to declare the parent version it last observed. The version field is a required input on these three operations; it is not optional and it may not be omitted.

- Creating a reciprocity agreement requires `expected_partner_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Partner` named by `partner_id`. If it does not equal that partner's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because an agreement create may supersede a prior agreement and must then run a window-overlap test over the partner's remaining active agreements: a writer holding a stale partner version would compute that test against a picture of the agreement set that no longer exists.
- Generating a draft settlement invoice for a partner also requires `expected_partner_version` against that partner's current `version`, and a mismatch is likewise `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Draft regeneration supersedes any existing draft for the same settlement month, so a writer working from a stale partner version would publish totals derived from a borrowing-versus-lending balance that has since moved.
- Creating a rota entry requires `expected_rota_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Rota` named by `rota_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROTA_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new entry for a partner already quoted on that rota supersedes the previous quote, so a writer holding a stale rota version would re-quote on top of a candidate set it has not seen and would silently displace a newer quote.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Both agreement creation and invoice generation increment the parent `Partner`'s `version` by 1 as part of their own success, and rota-entry creation increments the parent `Rota`'s `version` by 1, which is what makes a second write with the same declared version fail.

### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder

When more than one rule could reject the same request, the outcome is not a matter of implementation taste: the checks are evaluated in one fixed order and the **first** failure decides the response. The ladder below lists every rung with its rung number; a lower number is evaluated earlier. Rules with different numbers are never evaluated in the other order, and a request that violates several rules always reports the lowest-numbered violation. The trigger conditions are stated in full in §3 near the workflows they guard; what follows fixes the ordering, the error identifier and the scope.

1. **10 —** authentication headers present and role recognized: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. **20 —** acting role authorized for the endpoint: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. **25 —** entity-backed role owns the addressed resource: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403), on every endpoint reachable by `Patron` or `PartnerAgent`.
4. **28 —** segregation of duties: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on exactly two endpoints — the purchase-on-demand decision and the royalty-clearance create.
5. **30 —** every identifier in the URL path names an existing row, resolved left to right: 404 with the code for the first missing row — `ERR_PATRON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BIB_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROTA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROTA_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LENDING_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint whose path carries an identifier.
6. **32 —** every foreign key supplied in the request body names an existing row, checked in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint in §3: 404 with `ERR_PATRON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BIB_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROTA_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Applies to every write endpoint that carries a foreign key in its body.
7. **35 —** the body is well formed: required fields present, enum values within their declared sets, integers non-negative where this document says so, `period_month` matching a four-digit year, a hyphen and a two-digit month between `01` and `12`, `effective_to` strictly later than `effective_from`, `issn` present when `item_type` is `ARTICLE`, `pages_requested` present when a `COPY` is requested for an `ARTICLE`, `delivery_mode` equal to `ELECTRONIC` whenever `service_type` is `COPY`, pagination `limit` between 1 and 100 and `offset` at least 0. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint.
8. **38 —** the request's `service_type` equals the named rota's `material_type`: `ERR_ROTA_MATERIAL_MISMATCH` (422), on request intake.
9. **40 —** the request's `status` permits the operation: `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE` (409), on request cancellation, royalty-clearance create, a rota round, purchase escalation and electronic delivery.
10. **41 —** the offer is still `OFFERED`: `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN` (409), on offer acceptance, offer decline and lender shipping.
11. **42 —** the parcel is `IN_TRANSIT` and its `direction` matches the operation, `INBOUND` for borrower receipt and `RETURN` for lender confirmation: `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409), on both parcel operations.
12. **43 —** the loan's `custody_status` permits the operation, `AWAITING_PICKUP` for pickup and `WITH_PATRON` for renewal, return and loss declaration: `ERR_LOAN_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE` (409), on all four loan operations.
13. **44 —** the electronic delivery is not `EXPIRED`: `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409), on a download. An `EXHAUSTED` delivery passes this rung and is stopped later at rung 73.
14. **45 —** the purchase order's `status` permits the operation, `PROPOSED` for a decision and `APPROVED` for receipt: `ERR_POD_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` (409).
15. **46 —** the invoice's `status` permits the operation, `DRAFT` to issue, `ISSUED` to pay or dispute, `DISPUTED` to reinstate: `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE` (409).
16. **47 —** an agreement named in `supersedes_agreement_id` is still `ACTIVE`: `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on agreement creation.
17. **48 —** `expected_partner_version` equals the partner's current `version`: `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (412), on agreement creation and draft-invoice generation.
18. **49 —** `expected_rota_version` equals the rota's current `version`: `ERR_STALE_ROTA_VERSION` (412), on rota-entry creation.
19. **50 —** an article request being sourced holds a valid copyright clearance or falls outside the recency window: `ERR_COPYRIGHT_LIMIT_REACHED` (422), on a rota round where `copyright_required` is true and the request has no active clearance. Reaching the free-fill limit at intake is not an error — the request parks on hold instead.
20. **51 —** a royalty clearance is only recordable against a request that is on copyright hold and not already covered: `ERR_COPYRIGHT_CLEARANCE_NOT_REQUIRED` (422), on the royalty-clearance create.
21. **52 —** the declared `royalty_cents` equals the computed royalty fee: `ERR_ROYALTY_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422), on the royalty-clearance create.
22. **55 —** the work is not already on our own shelf: `ERR_HELD_LOCALLY` (422), on request intake.
23. **56 —** licensed electronic access does not already cover the work: `ERR_LICENSED_ACCESS_AVAILABLE` (422), on request intake.
24. **58 —** the patron's `status` is `ACTIVE`: `ERR_PATRON_BLOCKED` (403), on request intake.
25. **59 —** the patron's `open_request_count` is below `max_open_requests`: `ERR_PATRON_REQUEST_CAP_REACHED` (409), on request intake.
26. **60 —** the patron's provisional standing score is below the block threshold: `ERR_PATRON_STANDING_BLOCKED` (403), on request intake.
27. **62 —** `needed_by` is strictly later than the logical clock: `ERR_NEEDED_BY_IN_PAST` (422), on request intake.
28. **63 —** the new agreement's window does not intersect another active window on the same partner: `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), on agreement creation.
29. **64 —** the prospective loan window does not push the patron above `max_concurrent_loans`: `ERR_PATRON_LOAN_WINDOW_CAPACITY` (409), on inbound parcel receipt.
30. **65 —** the loan is not past a recall deadline: `ERR_RECALL_OVERDUE` (409), on renewal.
31. **66 —** the logical clock is strictly before the delivery's `expires_at`: `ERR_DELIVERY_EXPIRED` (409), on a download.
32. **67 —** the invoice's settlement period has closed: `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (409), on invoice issue.
33. **68 —** the hold shelf has not expired: `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), on pickup.
34. **70 —** the loan's `recall_status` is `NONE`: `ERR_RENEWAL_BLOCKED_BY_RECALL` (409), on renewal.
35. **71 —** the loan's `renewals_allowed` snapshot is true: `ERR_RENEWALS_NOT_ALLOWED_BY_LENDER` (422), on renewal.
36. **72 —** the renewal budget and the total-loan ceiling both leave room: `ERR_RENEWAL_CAP_REACHED` (409), on renewal.
37. **73 —** `download_count` is below `max_downloads`: `ERR_DELIVERY_DOWNLOAD_CAP_REACHED` (409), on a download.
38. **74 —** the request's `attempt_count` is below the rota's `max_attempts`: `ERR_ROTA_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (409), on a rota round.
39. **76 —** the accepting partner is `ACTIVE` and supports the request's `service_type`: `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422), on offer acceptance.
40. **77 —** the work is not already held locally or already on order: `ERR_ALREADY_HELD_LOCALLY` (422), on purchase escalation and on a purchase decision of `APPROVE`.
41. **78 —** the rota produced at least one eligible candidate: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LENDER` (422), on a rota round.
42. **80 —** the patron's `unpaid_balance_cents` is at most `unpaid_balance_cap_cents`: `ERR_PATRON_BALANCE_EXCEEDED` (402), on request intake. Note that this fiscal gate is evaluated **after** all six earlier intake gates, so a blocked patron whose balance is also over the cap is reported as blocked, not as over-balance.
43. **81 —** a payment does not exceed the loan's total remaining charge: `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` (422), on a charge settlement.
44. **82 —** a declared `damage_fee_cents` does not exceed the loan's `replacement_cost_cents`: `ERR_DAMAGE_FEE_EXCEEDS_REPLACEMENT` (422), on a lender confirmation whose condition is `DAMAGED`.

Note the consequences of this ordering on the renewal path in particular: the custody check at rung 43 precedes the recall-deadline check at rung 65, which precedes the open-recall check at rung 70, the lender-policy check at rung 71 and the cap check at rung 72. A renewal attempted on a loan that is both breached and beyond its renewal cap reports `ERR_RECALL_OVERDUE`, never `ERR_RENEWAL_CAP_REACHED`. Likewise on the download path: an expired-and-exhausted delivery reports `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_AVAILABLE` from rung 44, an available-but-past-`expires_at` delivery reports `ERR_DELIVERY_EXPIRED` from rung 66, and only a live delivery with no downloads left reaches `ERR_DELIVERY_DOWNLOAD_CAP_REACHED` at rung 73.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Retrieval

Many paths address a child through its parent, for example a request's clearances, a request's parcels, a rota's entries or a partner's invoices. For any such path, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names nothing produces its own 404 code immediately — the server does not look further right. A clearance list requested under a request id that does not exist answers `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; an invoice list under a non-existent partner answers `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`. The same precedence applies to writes: the parent must exist before any body field of the child is examined, and body foreign keys are only resolved after every path identifier has resolved.

Several child rows in this domain are created only as a side effect of some other operation and are therefore reachable only through their parent's list endpoint. The clearances written automatically at intake, the return parcel created when an item is returned or a hold is abandoned, and the loan created when an inbound parcel is received all fall into this group, and §4 lists the list endpoint that discovers each of them.

### 2.10. The Logical Clock and Time-Driven Change

The server keeps one logical UTC clock. Its value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until a client moves it, every rule that reads "the clock" reads that instant; the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied instant, named `to_iso8601` in the request body, and answers with the new value in a `current_utc` field. The clock only ever moves forward: an instant strictly earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Moving the clock mutates no entity and fires no transition of its own.

Everything that "happens with the passage of time" — agreements expiring, offers timing out, recalls being breached, holds being abandoned, electronic access expiring, parcels being written off in transit, exhausted rotas being closed or escalated, and patron standing being re-scored — happens inside the nightly operational sweep described in §3.9, and nowhere else.

### 2.11. Filtered Reads and Reverse Reachability

Every list endpoint accepts the pagination parameters from §2.3 plus a small set of domain filters, and every filter narrows the result set by exact match on the named field. Patrons list by `status` and `external_id`; partners by `status` and `consortium_code`; reciprocity agreements by `partner_id`, by `supersedes_agreement_id` and by `status`; rota entries by `status`, whether listed under their rota or under the partner they quote; bibliographic records by `issn`, `item_type` and `local_holding_status`; requests by `patron_id`, `bib_item_id`, `rota_id`, `committed_partner_id`, `status` and `service_type`; clearances, purchase orders and electronic deliveries under a request by `status`; notifications under a request by `kind`; supply offers by `request_id`, `partner_id` and `status`; a request's parcels by `direction` and `status`; a request's loans by `custody_status`; a partner's lending charges by `status`; and a partner's invoices by `period_month` and `status`. Filters combine conjunctively, and a filter naming a value outside its enum is a malformed request.

The copyright-usage read is not a list but a computed report: it takes an `issn` and a `calendar_year` and answers with those two echoed back plus `counted_fill_count` (how many free fills that journal-year pair has consumed), `free_fills_remaining` (how many remain), `royalty_paid_count` and `pre_window_exempt_count`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Patron Accounts, Standing and Migrated History

Everything the unit does starts from a patron account. Registering one is open to `LibraryAdmin` and `IllBorrowingStaff` and requires an `external_id` — the campus-unique identifier, between 1 and 32 characters long and unique across all patron rows — together with a `full_name`, an `email` that notices are sent to, a `patron_type` of `FACULTY`, `GRAD`, `UNDERGRAD` or `STAFF` (recorded for reporting only and never used as a gate), and the owning academic `department` code. Three ceilings may be supplied at registration and default when they are not: `max_open_requests` defaults to 5 and must be at least 1, `max_concurrent_loans` defaults to 3 and must be at least 1, and `unpaid_balance_cap_cents` defaults to 5000 cents — fifty dollars — and must be at least 0. A new account opens with `status` set to `ACTIVE`, every derived counter at 0 and both risk scores at 0.

A patron's `status` is the standing gate that intake reads. It takes one of three values: `ACTIVE`, `BLOCKED` or `WITHDRAWN`. `BLOCKED` is written either by an administrator amending the account or automatically by the standing cascade described below; `WITHDRAWN` is only ever written by an administrator. Crucially, the automatic cascade only ever moves the account **into** `BLOCKED` — the sole route back to `ACTIVE` is a `LibraryAdmin` amending the account, which is also the only way to produce `WITHDRAWN`. That amendment accepts any of `status`, `email`, `max_open_requests`, `max_concurrent_loans` and `unpaid_balance_cap_cents`, increments the patron's `version` by 1, and re-derives both `provisional_risk_score` and `final_risk_score` afterwards.

Accounts migrated from the previous circulation system arrive with borrowing history that no `LoanRecord` in this system can explain, so a `LibraryAdmin` may load it directly. Each such load names a `kind` — one of `LOST`, `DAMAGED`, `LATE_RETURN`, `UNCLAIMED` or `UNPAID_CHARGE` — and carries either a `count`, which defaults to 1 when omitted, or an `unpaid_amount_cents` figure. A `LOST`, `DAMAGED`, `LATE_RETURN` or `UNCLAIMED` load adds its `count` to the matching migrated counter: `historical_lost_count`, `historical_damaged_count`, `historical_late_return_count` or `historical_unclaimed_count` respectively. An `UNPAID_CHARGE` load adds its `unpaid_amount_cents` to `historical_unpaid_cents`. Each load then re-derives the live counters and both risk scores, increments the patron's `version` by 1, and answers with the updated patron rather than with a history row of its own.

The live counters sit on top of the migrated ones. A patron's `lost_count` is `historical_lost_count` plus the number of that patron's loans whose `billing_status` is `LOST_BILLED`. `damaged_count` is `historical_damaged_count` plus the number of that patron's loans whose `billing_status` is `DAMAGE_BILLED`. `late_return_count` is `historical_late_return_count` plus the number of that patron's loans whose `overdue_fine_cents` is greater than 0. `unclaimed_count` is `historical_unclaimed_count` plus the number of that patron's requests whose `status` is `UNCLAIMED`. `unpaid_balance_cents` is `historical_unpaid_cents` plus the sum of `outstanding_cents` over that patron's loans whose `billing_status` is `FINE_ACCRUED`, `DAMAGE_BILLED` or `LOST_BILLED`. `pending_charge_cents` is the sum of `net_patron_charge_cents` over that patron's requests whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED` or `UNFILLED`. And `open_request_count` counts that patron's requests whose `status` is none of `CLOSED`, `CLOSED_WITH_DAMAGE`, `CLOSED_LOST`, `CANCELLED`, `UNFILLED`, `FILLED_BY_PURCHASE` or `UNCLAIMED` — in other words, the requests still consuming desk attention.

Standing is expressed as a score out of one hundred, and the same weighted base is shared by two variants that deliberately disagree. The base is the sum of five terms: 25 points for each entry in `lost_count`, 12 points for each entry in `damaged_count`, 6 points for each entry in `late_return_count`, 8 points for each entry in `unclaimed_count`, and one further point for each whole 1000 cents — each whole ten dollars — of `unpaid_balance_cents`, taking the floor of that division so that a balance of 1999 cents contributes exactly one point.

The **provisional** variant is evaluated during intake, before the new request row is created, and equals the base clamped into the range 0 to 100 inclusive. It is stored on the patron as `provisional_risk_score`, and intake is refused with `ERR_PATRON_STANDING_BLOCKED` when it reaches 40 or more. The **final** variant is evaluated by the operational sweep, after every billing side effect of the night has landed, and takes the base, adds 6 points for each of that patron's loans that is still in custody `WITH_PATRON` with the logical clock strictly past its `due_at`, subtracts 4 points for each of that patron's loans in custody `RETURNED_TO_LENDER` whose `overdue_fine_cents` is exactly 0, and clamps the result into 0 to 100 inclusive after those two adjustments have been applied. It is stored as `final_risk_score`, and a patron whose `final_risk_score` reaches 40 or more has their `status` set to `BLOCKED`. Because the in-flight and on-time terms exist in one variant and not the other, the two scores legitimately differ for identical stored data whenever the patron is holding an overdue item or has returned items on time.

The same final variant, and the same threshold of 40, is also applied inline whenever a damage or loss billing event lands during the day rather than during the sweep — the recomputation and the possible move to `BLOCKED` are part of those cascades, as §3.7 sets out.

### 3.2. The Partner Network, Reciprocity Terms and Lender Rotas

Consortium partners are master data owned by `LibraryAdmin`. Registering one requires a `code` that is unique across all partners and between 2 and 16 characters long, a `name`, the `consortium_code` the partner settles through, a `base_lending_fee_cents` figure of at least 0, an `avg_turnaround_days` figure of at least 0, three capability flags — `ships_physical` for physical lending, `supplies_electronic` for article scans, and `allows_renewals` for whether renewals are entertained at all — a `loan_period_days` value between 1 and 120, and a `max_renewals` ceiling of at least 0. A `status` of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `WITHDRAWN` may be supplied and defaults to `ACTIVE`. The partner's published fee and published turnaround serve only as fallbacks: they are the figures used when a rota quote for that partner omits an explicit fee or turnaround of its own.

Five balance figures hang off each partner and are maintained by the server. `borrowed_from_count` is the number of requests committed to that partner whose `status` is neither `CANCELLED` nor `UNFILLED`. `lent_to_count` is the number of lending charges recorded against that partner. `net_borrow_imbalance_items` is `borrowed_from_count` minus `lent_to_count` and may legitimately be negative. `borrow_payable_cents` is the sum of `committed_fee_cents` over that partner's committed requests, again excluding those that are `CANCELLED` or `UNFILLED`. `lend_receivable_cents` is the sum of `charge_cents` over that partner's lending charges whose `status` is still `OPEN`.

An administrator may amend a partner's `status`, `base_lending_fee_cents`, `avg_turnaround_days`, `ships_physical`, `supplies_electronic`, `allows_renewals`, `loan_period_days` or `max_renewals`. Each amendment increments the partner's `version` by 1 and takes effect for future decisions only: no figure already snapshotted onto a request or a loan is rewritten by it.

**Reciprocity agreements** govern how a partner's quoted price is discounted and how the two libraries settle. Creating one is a `LibraryAdmin` action against a named `partner_id` and declares a `fee_waiver_mode` of `NONE`, `FULL` or `CAPPED`, a `settlement_mode` of `GROSS` or `NET`, a `turnaround_guarantee_days` figure (recorded for reporting only and never enforced), and the agreement's occupation window as `effective_from`, which is inclusive, and `effective_to`, which is exclusive and must be strictly later than `effective_from`. Two figures are optional: `capped_waiver_cents`, at least 0 and defaulting to 0, which names the cents deducted from a quote when `fee_waiver_mode` is `CAPPED` and is ignored under the other two modes; and `balance_tolerance_items`, at least 0 and defaulting to 3, above which a lopsided borrowing balance starts costing the partner points in the ranking. A new agreement opens with `status` `ACTIVE`; the other two values it can hold are `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED`.

A create may also name `supersedes_agreement_id`, the identifier of an existing agreement on the same partner that this one replaces. The named agreement must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise the create is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and an identifier naming nothing at all is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. The ordering here matters: the named agreement is flipped from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED` **first**, and only then does the window-overlap test run, with the just-superseded agreement excluded from the comparison set. That is what makes a straight replacement of an overlapping agreement legal.

The overlap test itself treats each agreement's window as half-open. A new agreement intersects an existing `ACTIVE` agreement on the same partner when the new window's `effective_from` is strictly before the existing window's `effective_to` **and** the existing window's `effective_from` is strictly before the new window's `effective_to`; any such intersection is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Adjacency is therefore permitted: an agreement beginning at exactly the instant another one ends does not intersect it. Both the create and the supersede flip run under the optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.7, and the create increments the parent partner's `version` by 1.

Where several agreements could apply to a partner at some instant, exactly one is selected: among that partner's agreements whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose window contains the logical clock — meaning `effective_from` is at or before the clock and `effective_to` is strictly after it — the one with the latest `effective_from` wins, and if two share that instant the one with the earlier `created_at` wins. When no agreement qualifies, the partner is treated as having no agreement at all.

**Rotas** are the ordered pools of candidate lenders. A rota carries a `name`, a `material_type` of `LOAN` or `COPY` fixing the kind of request it may source, and a `selection_policy` of `LANDED_COST` or `MANUAL_RANK` choosing how candidates are ranked. Three further settings default: `max_attempts`, the ceiling on how many rota rounds a request may spend, defaults to 3 and must be between 1 and 10; `auto_escalate_pod`, which decides whether an exhausted request is escalated to a purchase proposal instead of being closed unfilled, defaults to false; and `offer_response_hours`, the window a lender has to answer, defaults to 48 hours — two days — and must be between 1 and 720.

Each partner's place on a rota is a rota entry naming its `rota_id` and `partner_id` and carrying a `manual_rank` of at least 1, a `quoted_fee_cents` of at least 0, a `quoted_turnaround_days` figure of at least 0 in whole days, and an `is_enabled` flag that defaults to true. Setting `is_enabled` to false removes the entry from consideration without deleting it. An entry opens with `status` `ACTIVE`; when a later create re-quotes the same rota-and-partner pair, the older entry is flipped to `SUPERSEDED` and the new one takes its place, which is exactly why entry creation is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the parent rota and increments that rota's `version` by 1. An entry's `is_enabled` and `manual_rank` may also be changed in place without re-quoting; that amendment increments the **entry's** own `version` by 1 and leaves the rota's `version` untouched.

### 3.3. Bibliographic Records and Request Intake

Before a request can be taken in, the work itself must be described. A bibliographic record carries a `title` and an `item_type` of `BOOK`, `ARTICLE`, `CHAPTER`, `THESIS` or `MEDIA`, plus a `publication_year` between 1500 and 2100 that feeds two different rules — the copyright recency window of §3.4 and the licensed-embargo test below. Identifiers depend on the form: `issn` names the host journal and is required when `item_type` is `ARTICLE` and otherwise null, `isbn` names a monograph and is null for articles, and `journal_title` is null unless the item is an article. Four further facts about our own holdings default when omitted: `local_holding_status` starts at `NONE` and may also be `HELD_AVAILABLE`, `HELD_CHECKED_OUT` or `ON_ORDER`; `licensed_access_status` starts at `NONE` and may also be `LICENSED_FULL` or `LICENSED_EMBARGOED`; `embargo_months` starts at 0, must be at least 0 and is only consulted under `LICENSED_EMBARGOED`; and `list_price_cents` starts at 0, must be at least 0, and is later snapshotted onto a purchase proposal and used as a borrowed item's replacement cost when it is non-zero.

Holdings facts change, so a `LibraryAdmin` or `AcquisitionsLibrarian` may amend a record's `local_holding_status`, `licensed_access_status`, `embargo_months` or `list_price_cents`; each amendment increments that record's `version` by 1. Two of those values are also written automatically: an approved purchase moves the record to `ON_ORDER`, and a received purchase moves it to `HELD_AVAILABLE`.

Taking a request in is open to `IllBorrowingStaff` and to a `Patron` acting on their own account. The payload names the `patron_id` on whose behalf the request is made, the `bib_item_id` for the work, and the `rota_id` of the pool that will be walked to find a lender. It declares a `service_type` of `LOAN`, meaning a physical item will be shipped, or `COPY`, meaning an article scan will be delivered electronically, and this value must equal the named rota's `material_type` — a mismatch is refused with `ERR_ROTA_MATERIAL_MISMATCH`. It declares a `delivery_mode` of `PICKUP`, `ELECTRONIC` or `MAIL`, which must be `ELECTRONIC` whenever `service_type` is `COPY`. It declares `needed_by`, the instant after which the item is useless to the patron, which must be strictly later than the logical clock — an instant at or before the clock is `ERR_NEEDED_BY_IN_PAST`. And it declares `max_supply_fee_cents`, at least 0, the ceiling above which a candidate lender's price disqualifies it. One optional figure completes the payload: `pages_requested`, between 1 and 200, which is required when a `COPY` is requested for an `ARTICLE` and null otherwise.

Six substantive gates then run, in the order §2.8 pins. First, the work must not already be sitting on our own shelf: a `local_holding_status` of `HELD_AVAILABLE` is refused with `ERR_HELD_LOCALLY`, because the patron should borrow it locally instead. Second, licensed electronic access must not already cover it. That second test reads the record's licensed-access state: a record at `NONE` is not covered; a record at `LICENSED_FULL` is covered outright; and a record at `LICENSED_EMBARGOED` is covered when its `publication_year` is at or before the current calendar year of the logical clock minus the embargo expressed in whole years, where the embargo in years is `embargo_months` divided by 12 and rounded **up** to a whole year — so an embargo of anywhere from 1 to 12 months costs one year, 13 to 24 months costs two, and so on. When the work turns out to be covered, intake is refused with `ERR_LICENSED_ACCESS_AVAILABLE`.

The third, fourth and fifth gates concern the patron: an account whose `status` is `BLOCKED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_PATRON_BLOCKED`; an account whose `open_request_count` has already reached `max_open_requests` is refused with `ERR_PATRON_REQUEST_CAP_REACHED`; and an account whose provisional standing score reaches the block threshold of 40 is refused with `ERR_PATRON_STANDING_BLOCKED`. The sixth gate is the `needed_by` test above. Only after all six have passed does the fiscal gate run: a patron whose `unpaid_balance_cents` strictly exceeds `unpaid_balance_cap_cents` is refused with `ERR_PATRON_BALANCE_EXCEEDED`, carrying HTTP 402 rather than 403 because the remedy is money rather than permission. A balance exactly equal to the cap is acceptable.

A request that survives intake is created with `intake_user_id` set from `X-User-ID` — the audit counterparty that §3.4's segregation rule later compares against — and with `copyright_required` derived on the spot: it is true when the `service_type` is `COPY`, the work's `item_type` is `ARTICLE`, and the work has a non-null `issn`; otherwise it is false. The request's own lifecycle then branches immediately, **inside the same call**. Its nominal initial state is `SUBMITTED`, but `SUBMITTED` is a transient value that no API response ever exposes: by the time intake answers, the request has already moved either `SUBMITTED → SOURCING`, meaning it is ready for the rota, or `SUBMITTED → COPYRIGHT_HOLD`, meaning a copyright decision is owed first. §3.4 fixes which branch is taken. On the sourcing branch a notice of kind `REQUEST_SUBMITTED` is emitted; on the hold branch the notice is of kind `COPYRIGHT_HOLD`. Either way the patron's `open_request_count`, `pending_charge_cents` and `provisional_risk_score` are re-derived afterwards.

Across its life a request's `status` moves within a fixed set of eighteen values: `SUBMITTED`, `COPYRIGHT_HOLD`, `SOURCING`, `OFFER_PENDING`, `SUPPLY_COMMITTED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `ON_HOLD_SHELF`, `WITH_PATRON`, `RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED_ELECTRONIC`, `ESCALATED_POD`, `CLOSED`, `CLOSED_WITH_DAMAGE`, `CLOSED_LOST`, `UNCLAIMED`, `CANCELLED`, `UNFILLED` and `FILLED_BY_PURCHASE`. The last seven of those are terminal: once a request is `CLOSED`, `CLOSED_WITH_DAMAGE`, `CLOSED_LOST`, `UNCLAIMED`, `CANCELLED`, `UNFILLED` or `FILLED_BY_PURCHASE`, nothing moves it again, and every operation that demands a live request refuses with `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`. Alongside `status`, a request carries `unfilled_reason`, which is `NONE` while sourcing is viable, becomes `ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` when the rota budget runs out, and becomes `TRANSIT_LOST` when an inbound parcel is written off; it is reset to `NONE` if a rejected purchase proposal sends the request back to the rota. Two timestamps close the picture: `fulfilled_at` records the instant the item actually reached the borrower, whether by parcel receipt or by electronic delivery, and is the key that decides which settlement period a borrow line falls into; `closed_at` records the instant the request reached a terminal state. Both are null until earned.

Patrons are told what happens to their requests. Every notice is an append-only record naming the `request_id` it concerns, a `kind`, a `channel` that is always `EMAIL` in this release, a rendered `subject` line, and a `sent_at` instant taken from the logical clock. Thirteen kinds exist, one per notifiable event: `REQUEST_SUBMITTED`, `COPYRIGHT_HOLD`, `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, `ELECTRONIC_DELIVERY_READY`, `RECALL_ISSUED`, `UNCLAIMED`, `DAMAGE_BILLED`, `LOST_BILLED`, `REQUEST_UNFILLED`, `POD_APPROVED`, `PURCHASE_RECEIVED`, `DELIVERY_EXPIRED` and `REQUEST_CANCELLED`. Each is emitted exactly where the workflow narratives below say so.

### 3.4. Copyright Compliance for Article Copies

United States interlibrary loan practice allows a borrowing library a limited number of free article fills from any single journal in any single calendar year; beyond that, a royalty must be paid. This system tracks that allowance with a counter keyed on the pair of `issn` and `calendar_year` — and on nothing else. It is not keyed on the patron, and it is not keyed on the bibliographic record, so two separate records that share an `issn` share one allowance.

Only recent material counts against the allowance. A work counts toward the limit when its `publication_year` is strictly greater than the current calendar year of the logical clock minus five; anything published at or before that boundary year lies outside the five-year recency window and never consumes the allowance. The counter itself is the number of clearance rows sharing the given `issn` and `calendar_year` whose `method` is `CONTU_FREE` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; voided rows do not count, and rows recorded under the other two methods do not count either. The free-fill allowance per journal-year pair is five.

At intake, when a request's `copyright_required` is true, exactly one of three outcomes follows. If the work does **not** count toward the limit, a clearance is created with `method` `PRE_WINDOW_EXEMPT` and `sequence_in_year` 0, and the request proceeds to `SOURCING`. If the work does count and the counter for its journal-year pair is still below five, a clearance is created with `method` `CONTU_FREE` and `sequence_in_year` set to the counter's new value — one for the first such fill of the year, two for the second, and so on up to five — and the request again proceeds to `SOURCING`. Otherwise the allowance is spent: **no clearance row is created at all**, and the request parks at `COPYRIGHT_HOLD`. Reaching the limit is not an error at intake; it is a routing decision. A request whose `copyright_required` is false skips all of this and goes straight to `SOURCING` with no clearance row.

Every clearance records the `request_id` it covers, the `issn` and `calendar_year` it was counted under (the calendar year being the UTC year of the logical clock at the moment the clearance was written), its `method`, its `sequence_in_year`, a `royalty_cents` figure that is non-zero only under `ROYALTY_PAID`, the `pages_covered` copied from the request's `pages_requested`, an `approved_by_user_id` that is null for both automatic methods, and a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `VOIDED`. Because the two automatic methods are written by a cascade rather than by a client, the only way to see them is to list the clearances of their request.

A request sitting at `COPYRIGHT_HOLD` moves forward only when an `IllBorrowingStaff` member records a paid-royalty clearance, which drives the transition `COPYRIGHT_HOLD → SOURCING`. Three conditions guard it. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the request's `intake_user_id`, or the attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the person who took the money decision cannot also be the person who took the request. The request must actually be on hold and not already covered: recording a royalty against a request in any other state, or against one that already holds a `CONTU_FREE` or `PRE_WINDOW_EXEMPT` clearance, is `ERR_COPYRIGHT_CLEARANCE_NOT_REQUIRED`. And the `royalty_cents` declared in the payload must equal the fee the system computes, otherwise `ERR_ROYALTY_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

That fee is the larger of two amounts: a floor of 1500 cents, and a base of 800 cents plus 65 cents for each page in the request's `pages_requested`. For a twenty-page article the base term is 800 plus 65 multiplied by 20, which is 2100 cents; that exceeds the floor, so the fee is 2100 cents. For a five-page article the base term is 800 plus 325, or 1125 cents, which is below the floor, so the fee is 1500 cents.

On success a clearance is created with `method` `ROYALTY_PAID`, `sequence_in_year` 0 — a paid fill never consumes a free slot — and `approved_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The declared amount is copied onto the request's own `royalty_cents`, and the request's `gross_charge_cents`, `subsidy_cents` and `net_patron_charge_cents` are re-derived by the pricing rule of §3.5, after which the patron's `pending_charge_cents` is re-derived too.

The allowance is a pool, not a ledger of consumed slots, so it can be returned. Cancelling a request voids every `ACTIVE` clearance it holds, moving each `status` to `VOIDED`, and because the counter only counts `ACTIVE` rows, the freed slot becomes available to the next article request for that journal and year. Staff can see the state of the pool before they take a request in: the copyright-usage report answers, for one `issn` and one `calendar_year`, the `counted_fill_count` consumed so far, the `free_fills_remaining` out of five, and for context the `royalty_paid_count` and `pre_window_exempt_count` recorded under the other two methods.

One later consequence deserves stating here. If a request somehow reaches a rota round while `copyright_required` is true and it holds no `ACTIVE` clearance of any method, the round is refused with `ERR_COPYRIGHT_LIMIT_REACHED` — the compliance rule is enforced at the point where we would otherwise ask a lender to supply a copy we are not cleared to receive.

### 3.5. Sourcing the Rota: Candidate Ranking, Offers and Commitment Pricing

A request at `SOURCING` is moved forward one rota round at a time by `IllBorrowingStaff`. Each round advances the request `SOURCING → OFFER_PENDING` and opens exactly one offer to exactly one partner: the system never fans out to several lenders at once, and it never falls back to a partner the ranking did not choose.

Three things are checked before a round begins. The request must be at `SOURCING`; its `attempt_count`, the number of rota rounds already spent, must be strictly below the rota's `max_attempts`, or the round is refused with `ERR_ROTA_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`; and, for an article copy whose `copyright_required` is true, an `ACTIVE` clearance must exist, as §3.4 explains.

The round then computes how much time is left. **Days available** is the elapsed interval from the logical clock to the request's `needed_by`, divided into whole days by taking the floor, so a deadline eleven hours away yields zero days available. On every successful round this figure is snapshotted onto the request as `days_available_at_source`, replacing whatever the previous round left there.

Eligibility comes next, and a candidate must satisfy every clause. It must be a rota entry belonging to the request's rota whose own `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `is_enabled` flag is true. Its partner's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. For a `LOAN` request the partner's `ships_physical` must be true; for a `COPY` request the partner's `supplies_electronic` must be true. The candidate's effective fee, defined in a moment, must be at or below the request's `max_supply_fee_cents`. Finally the partner must not already have answered on this request: any partner holding an offer on this request whose `status` is `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `ACCEPTED` is excluded from every later round, which is what stops the rota loop from asking the same library twice.

The **effective fee** is the entry's quote after the reciprocity waiver of the agreement selected for that partner by the rule in §3.2. When no agreement applies, or the applicable agreement's `fee_waiver_mode` is `NONE`, the effective fee is the entry's `quoted_fee_cents` unchanged. Under `FULL` it is 0. Under `CAPPED` it is `quoted_fee_cents` minus that agreement's `capped_waiver_cents`, with any negative result raised to 0.

Two penalties then convert price into a comparable landed cost. **Lateness** is the candidate's `quoted_turnaround_days` minus the days available, with a negative result raised to 0 — a partner promising four days against six days of runway is not late at all, while the same partner against two days of runway is two days late. Each late day costs 250 cents. **Imbalance** looks at the borrowing relationship: the tolerance is the applicable agreement's `balance_tolerance_items` when an agreement applies and 3 items when none does, and a partner whose `net_borrow_imbalance_items` is strictly greater than that tolerance draws a flat penalty of 1500 cents. The landed cost of a candidate is therefore its effective fee, plus 250 cents multiplied by its late days, plus the imbalance penalty where it applies. No rounding occurs anywhere in this rule; every term is already whole cents.

Ranking is a total order with no ties left unresolved, and which order applies depends on the rota's `selection_policy`. Under `LANDED_COST` candidates are sorted by landed cost ascending, then by `quoted_turnaround_days` ascending, then by `manual_rank` ascending, then by the entry's `created_at` ascending. Under `MANUAL_RANK` they are sorted by `manual_rank` ascending, then by landed cost ascending, then by the entry's `created_at` ascending. The first candidate under the applicable order wins the round.

If the candidate set turns out to be empty, the round fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LENDER` and **nothing at all is written**: `attempt_count` is not incremented, no offer row is created, and the request stays at `SOURCING`. On success the opposite holds: `attempt_count` is incremented by 1, `days_available_at_source` is snapshotted, and an offer is created for the winning partner. That offer records its `request_id` and `partner_id`, an `attempt_seq` equal to the request's `attempt_count` after the increment (so the first round produces sequence 1), the winner's `quoted_fee_cents` and `quoted_turnaround_days` snapshotted at offer time, the `effective_fee_cents` after the waiver, the `landed_cost_cents` that won the ranking, an `imbalance_penalty_cents` of 1500 when the imbalance penalty applied to that partner at offer time and 0 otherwise, an `offered_at` instant taken from the logical clock, an `offered_by_user_id` recording the staff member who ran the round, a `decline_reason` of `NONE`, and a `respond_by` deadline equal to `offered_at` plus the rota's `offer_response_hours` hours. The offer opens at `status` `OFFERED`; its other four values are `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED`, and any operation demanding an open offer refuses an offer in one of those four with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN`.

The lender answers. A `PartnerAgent` may only answer for its own partner, and declining drives `OFFERED → DECLINED` with a `decline_reason` of `NOT_HELD`, `IN_USE`, `POLICY` or `COST` recorded from the payload, sending the request back `OFFER_PENDING → SOURCING`. This is the rota-round loop, and the essential point is that **`attempt_count` is not reset by it** — the rota's `max_attempts` is what eventually terminates the loop. The declining partner is permanently out of the running for this request.

Acceptance drives `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and the request `OFFER_PENDING → SUPPLY_COMMITTED`. Beyond the offer being open, the accepting partner must still be `ACTIVE` and must still support the request's `service_type`, otherwise the acceptance is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` — a partner suspended between the offer and the answer cannot commit. On success the request takes its commitment snapshot: `committed_partner_id` is set to the accepting partner, `committed_fee_cents` is set to the offer's `effective_fee_cents`, and `committed_loan_period_days` is set from that partner's current `loan_period_days`. Then the partner's `borrowed_from_count`, `net_borrow_imbalance_items` and `borrow_payable_cents` are re-derived, and the request is priced.

Pricing a request runs in a pinned six-step order, and the step order is the whole rule. **First**, the base is the request's `committed_fee_cents`. **Second**, the rush surcharge: when the request's `days_available_at_source` is at most 3 days, `rush_surcharge_cents` is 1200 cents, and otherwise it is 0 — a request accepted with three days of runway is a rush, one with four days is not. **Third**, the royalty term is the `royalty_cents` of the request's `ACTIVE` `ROYALTY_PAID` clearance if it has one, and 0 otherwise. **Fourth**, `gross_charge_cents` is the sum of those three: base plus rush plus royalty. **Fifth**, the departmental subsidy rate is looked up **from the gross figure, never from the net**: a gross at or below 2000 cents subsidises at 0.80, a gross from 2001 through 6000 cents at 0.60, a gross from 6001 through 12000 cents at 0.40, and a gross strictly above 12000 cents at 0.25. **Sixth**, `subsidy_cents` is the gross multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents, and then capped at 4000 cents — the cap is applied to the rounded product, never before the tier lookup. **Seventh and last**, `net_patron_charge_cents` is `gross_charge_cents` minus `subsidy_cents`. Keying the tier on the net figure, or applying the cap before the tier is chosen, produces a different answer for the same data and is wrong. The patron's `pending_charge_cents` is re-derived once the request's money fields have settled.

The same pricing rule runs again, unchanged, whenever a paid-royalty clearance is recorded on a request that was already priced or is priced later, because the royalty term feeds the gross and therefore the tier.

### 3.6. Physical Fulfilment: Shipping, the Hold Shelf, Pickup, Renewals and Recalls

For a `LOAN` request, the committed lender hands a parcel to a courier, which advances the request `SUPPLY_COMMITTED → IN_TRANSIT`. The lender supplies the `carrier` name and the courier's `tracking_code`, and may declare an `insured_value_cents` figure of at least 0 that defaults to 0. The resulting parcel is created with `direction` `INBOUND`, `status` `IN_TRANSIT` and `shipped_at` taken from the logical clock. A parcel's `direction` is either `INBOUND`, meaning lender to borrower, or `RETURN`, meaning borrower to lender, and its `status` runs over `IN_TRANSIT`, `RECEIVED`, `CONFIRMED_OK`, `CONFIRMED_DAMAGED`, `CONFIRMED_LOST` and `LOST_IN_TRANSIT`. Shipping is only possible while the underlying offer is still open and the request's `service_type` is `LOAN`.

Receiving the inbound parcel is an `IllBorrowingStaff` action and is the busiest single operation in the domain. The parcel must be `INBOUND` and `IN_TRANSIT`, otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Before anything is written, the patron's loan-window capacity is tested. Each loan occupies the **closed** interval from its `received_at` to its `due_at`, and the counted set is that patron's loans whose `custody_status` is `AWAITING_PICKUP` or `WITH_PATRON` — loans at `RETURN_PREPARED`, `UNCLAIMED_RETURN_PREPARED`, `RETURNED_TO_LENDER` or `DECLARED_LOST` are excluded, because the item is no longer in the patron's hands. The prospective window runs from the logical clock to the clock plus the request's `committed_loan_period_days` days. Two windows overlap when the first starts at or before the second ends and the second starts at or before the first ends; because the intervals are closed, two windows that merely touch at a single endpoint **do** overlap. Let the overlap count be the number of counted loans whose window overlaps the prospective window; receipt is refused with `ERR_PATRON_LOAN_WINDOW_CAPACITY` when that count plus one would exceed the patron's `max_concurrent_loans`. This is an aggregate limit — each existing loan is individually legal, and only their collective overlap at the prospective window breaches it.

On success the parcel moves `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED` with `received_at` set to the clock, and a loan record is created. The loan's own `received_at` is the clock; its `original_due_at` and its current `due_at` are both the clock plus the request's `committed_loan_period_days` days, and `original_due_at` is never rewritten afterwards; its `pickup_expires_at` is the clock plus 7 days, giving the patron one week on the hold shelf; its `max_renewals` and `renewals_allowed` are snapshotted from the committed partner's `max_renewals` and `allows_renewals`; its `replacement_cost_cents` is the work's `list_price_cents` when that is non-zero and 7500 cents otherwise; and its `inbound_tracking_code` is copied from the parcel's `tracking_code` so the loan can be reconciled against the courier record. The loan opens on all three of its state dimensions at once: `custody_status` `AWAITING_PICKUP`, `billing_status` `NO_CHARGE`, `recall_status` `NONE`. The request advances `IN_TRANSIT → ON_HOLD_SHELF`, its `fulfilled_at` is set to the clock, and a notice of kind `READY_FOR_PICKUP` is emitted. Because the loan is created by this cascade rather than by a client, listing a request's loans is how it is discovered.

A loan carries **three independent state dimensions on one row**, and they advance separately. `custody_status` tracks physical possession over `AWAITING_PICKUP`, `WITH_PATRON`, `RETURN_PREPARED`, `UNCLAIMED_RETURN_PREPARED`, `RETURNED_TO_LENDER` and `DECLARED_LOST`, of which the last two are terminal. `billing_status` tracks money over `NO_CHARGE`, `FINE_ACCRUED`, `DAMAGE_BILLED`, `LOST_BILLED`, `CHARGE_SETTLED` and `CHARGE_WAIVED`, of which the last two are terminal. `recall_status` tracks the lender's recall over `NONE`, `RECALL_ISSUED`, `RECALL_HONORED` and `RECALL_BREACHED`, of which the last two are terminal. A loan may perfectly well sit at custody `RETURNED_TO_LENDER` while its billing is still `DAMAGE_BILLED`; the dimensions never gate one another except where a rule below says so explicitly.

Pickup is an `IllBorrowingStaff` action that drives custody `AWAITING_PICKUP → WITH_PATRON` and the request `ON_HOLD_SHELF → WITH_PATRON`, stamping `picked_up_at` from the clock. It requires the loan to be at custody `AWAITING_PICKUP`, the request's inbound parcel to be at `RECEIVED`, and the logical clock to be strictly before `pickup_expires_at`; a clock that has reached the hold-shelf deadline but has not yet been swept is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

Renewals extend the loan and may be requested by the borrowing patron or by desk staff. The gates are evaluated in exactly the order §2.8 pins: custody must be `WITH_PATRON`; the loan must not be past a recall deadline, meaning `recall_status` must not be `RECALL_BREACHED` and, where it is `RECALL_ISSUED`, the clock must be at or before `recall_due_at`, otherwise `ERR_RECALL_OVERDUE`; `recall_status` must then be `NONE` outright, so a recall that has been issued, honoured or breached locks renewals out with `ERR_RENEWAL_BLOCKED_BY_RECALL`; the loan's `renewals_allowed` snapshot must be true, otherwise `ERR_RENEWALS_NOT_ALLOWED_BY_LENDER`; and `renewal_count` must be strictly below `max_renewals`, otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_CAP_REACHED`. The projected new due date is the earlier of the current `due_at` plus 14 days — a fortnight per renewal — and the hard ceiling of `received_at` plus 84 days, twelve weeks from receipt. If that projection is not strictly later than the current `due_at`, the renewal buys nothing and is likewise refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_CAP_REACHED`. On success `due_at` becomes the projection, `renewal_count` is incremented by 1, and `original_due_at` stays exactly where it was.

A lender may recall its item early. Recalling drives `recall_status` `NONE → RECALL_ISSUED` and is permitted only while custody is `AWAITING_PICKUP` or `WITH_PATRON`. The payload may carry `grace_days`, which defaults to 7 days when omitted; `recall_due_at` becomes the logical clock plus that many days, and the loan's `due_at` is pulled in to the earlier of its current value and `recall_due_at`, so a recall never pushes a due date outward. A notice of kind `RECALL_ISSUED` is emitted. While the recall is open the item is due on the recall deadline, renewals are locked out, and the sweep will mark the recall breached if the deadline passes with the item still out.

### 3.7. Returns, Damage, Loss and the Settlement Waterfall

Returning an item is an `IllBorrowingStaff` action and requires custody `WITH_PATRON`. It drives custody `WITH_PATRON → RETURN_PREPARED`, stamps `return_prepared_at` from the logical clock, resolves the recall dimension, computes the overdue fine, creates the return parcel with `direction` `RETURN`, `status` `IN_TRANSIT` and `shipped_at` from the clock, and advances the request `WITH_PATRON → RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`.

Three things happen in one call, and their order matters. The recall dimension is resolved first: a loan at `recall_status` `RECALL_ISSUED` moves to `RECALL_HONORED` when the clock is at or before `recall_due_at` and to `RECALL_BREACHED` when it is later. The **overdue fine** is then computed from that resolved dimension. The end instant of the computation is `return_prepared_at` on the return path and the logical clock on the loss-declaration path. Overdue days are the elapsed interval from the loan's current `due_at` — the value already shifted by any renewal extension or recall pull-in, never `original_due_at` — to the end instant, divided into whole days by taking the floor and raised to 0 if negative. Billable days are the overdue days minus the 2-day grace period, again raised to 0, so a return three days late bills one day. The raw fine is 150 cents multiplied by the billable days. The multiplier is 3 when the recall dimension resolves to `RECALL_BREACHED` at this same call and 1 otherwise. The fine is the raw amount multiplied by that multiplier, and only then capped at 6000 cents: **the cap is applied after the multiplier, never before it**, so a raw fine of 3000 cents on a breached recall yields 6000 cents rather than 3000. `overdue_fine_cents` is written exactly once in the loan's lifetime, by whichever of the two paths fires first, and `billing_status` moves `NO_CHARGE → FINE_ACCRUED` when the written figure is greater than 0. The patron's `late_return_count`, `unpaid_balance_cents` and `final_risk_score` are then re-derived.

Declaring an item lost is also an `IllBorrowingStaff` action from custody `WITH_PATRON`, and it short-circuits the return path entirely. Custody goes to `DECLARED_LOST` and `billing_status` to `LOST_BILLED`; `lost_replacement_cents` is set from the loan's `replacement_cost_cents` snapshot, `lender_processing_fee_cents` is set to 2500 cents, and `overdue_fine_cents` is written from the fine rule with the clock as the end instant. The request goes to `CLOSED_LOST` with `closed_at` stamped, a notice of kind `LOST_BILLED` is emitted, and the patron's `lost_count`, `unpaid_balance_cents`, `open_request_count` and `final_risk_score` are re-derived, with the account driven to `BLOCKED` if the recomputed final score reaches 40.

The lender then confirms what arrived. The parcel must be `RETURN` and `IN_TRANSIT`, and the `condition` reported is `OK`, `DAMAGED` or `LOST`. On the `DAMAGED` branch the declared `damage_fee_cents` must be at or below the loan's `replacement_cost_cents` snapshot, otherwise the confirmation is refused with `ERR_DAMAGE_FEE_EXCEEDS_REPLACEMENT` — a library may not bill more for a scratch than the book is worth. This single call is the deepest cascade in the system, and on the `DAMAGED` branch it runs five levels down: the parcel moves to `CONFIRMED_DAMAGED` with `confirmed_at` stamped; the loan's custody moves to `RETURNED_TO_LENDER` and its `billing_status` to `DAMAGE_BILLED` with `damage_fee_cents` recorded, `lender_processing_fee_cents` set to 2500 cents, and `total_charge_cents` and `outstanding_cents` recomputed; the request moves to `CLOSED_WITH_DAMAGE` with `closed_at` stamped; a notice of kind `DAMAGE_BILLED` is emitted; and the patron's `damaged_count`, `lost_count`, `unpaid_balance_cents`, `open_request_count` and `final_risk_score` are all re-derived, with the account driven to `BLOCKED` when that recomputed `final_risk_score` reaches 40. A damage confirmation on a fifth loan can therefore be the call that blocks the patron.

The `OK` branch moves the parcel to `CONFIRMED_OK`, the loan's custody to `RETURNED_TO_LENDER`, the request to `CLOSED`, and re-derives the patron's `open_request_count`; no money is touched. The `LOST` branch moves the parcel to `CONFIRMED_LOST`, the loan's custody to `DECLARED_LOST` and its billing to `LOST_BILLED` with `lost_replacement_cents` from the `replacement_cost_cents` snapshot and the 2500-cent processing fee, the request to `CLOSED_LOST`, and emits a notice of kind `LOST_BILLED`, with the same patron recomputation and the same `BLOCKED` outcome as the damage branch.

Two subtleties govern the confirmation. First, the **request status is only advanced when the request is currently at `RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`**. A parcel returned because the hold shelf expired belongs to a request already terminal at `UNCLAIMED`, and confirming it moves the loan's custody `UNCLAIMED_RETURN_PREPARED → RETURNED_TO_LENDER` while leaving the request exactly where it is. Second, an overdue fine already written survives everything: `billing_status` moves `FINE_ACCRUED → DAMAGE_BILLED` or `FINE_ACCRUED → LOST_BILLED` with the fine still counted inside `total_charge_cents`, on top of which the new charge is added.

A loan's money is four buckets, and `total_charge_cents` is always their sum: `overdue_fine_cents` plus `damage_fee_cents` plus `lost_replacement_cents` plus `lender_processing_fee_cents`. Against each sits a paid figure — `paid_overdue_cents`, `paid_damage_cents`, `paid_lost_cents` and `paid_processing_cents` — whose sum is `total_paid_cents`, and `outstanding_cents` is `total_charge_cents` minus `total_paid_cents`. The processing fee is 2500 cents once a damage or loss charge exists on the loan and 0 otherwise; it is never charged for a plain overdue return.

Settlement carries an `action` of either `PAY` or `WAIVE`, and the `ConsortiumAccountant` and `LibraryAdmin` roles may both reach it: `PAY` is the accountant's routine action and `WAIVE` the administrator's discretionary one. A `PAY` carries `payment_cents` and allocates it across the four buckets in a **pinned waterfall order — overdue first, then damage, then replacement, then the processing fee**. The remaining amount in a bucket is its charge minus its paid figure, and the total remaining is the sum of the four. If `payment_cents` exceeds that total remaining, the call fails with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` and **nothing whatever is written**: no bucket is partially filled, no field changes, and the caller must re-send a correct amount. Otherwise the payment walks the buckets in order, applying to each the smaller of what is left of the payment and what that bucket still owes, then carrying the remainder to the next bucket. A payment of 4000 cents against a 1000-cent fine, a 3000-cent damage fee and a 2500-cent processing fee therefore fills the fine bucket, fills 3000 of the damage bucket, and leaves the processing bucket untouched at 0. There is no rounding and no remainder to distribute. Once the allocation is done, `total_paid_cents` and `outstanding_cents` are recomputed and `billing_status` moves to `CHARGE_SETTLED` exactly when `outstanding_cents` has reached 0.

A `WAIVE` is the opposite: it is permitted from `FINE_ACCRUED`, `DAMAGE_BILLED` or `LOST_BILLED`, it forces `outstanding_cents` to 0 **without touching any paid bucket** — the four paid figures and `total_paid_cents` stay exactly as they were, so the audit trail still shows that nothing was collected — and it drives `billing_status` to `CHARGE_WAIVED`. Both `CHARGE_SETTLED` and `CHARGE_WAIVED` are terminal. Either way the patron's `unpaid_balance_cents` and `final_risk_score` are re-derived afterwards, and note that a waived or settled loan drops out of the `unpaid_balance_cents` sum entirely, because that sum covers only loans at `FINE_ACCRUED`, `DAMAGE_BILLED` or `LOST_BILLED`. Clearing a patron's debt never un-blocks the account by itself: a `BLOCKED` patron returns to `ACTIVE` only when a `LibraryAdmin` updates the account's `status` directly.

### 3.8. Electronic Delivery, Purchase on Demand and Cancellation

A `COPY` request is fulfilled by a scan rather than a parcel. The committed lender delivers it, which drives the request `SUPPLY_COMMITTED → DELIVERED_ELECTRONIC`, and the delivering partner's `supplies_electronic` must be true. The lender supplies an opaque `file_token` handle for the scanned file and a `pages_delivered` count between 1 and 200 inclusive. The delivery row records `delivered_at` from the logical clock, an `expires_at` of `delivered_at` plus 30 days, a `max_downloads` of 3, a `download_count` of 0 and a `status` of `AVAILABLE`; the request's `fulfilled_at` is set to the clock and a notice of kind `ELECTRONIC_DELIVERY_READY` is emitted.

The patron then downloads the file, and the access gate has two independent limbs checked in the order §2.8 pins. The delivery's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`, so an already `EXPIRED` or `EXHAUSTED` row is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The logical clock must then be strictly before `expires_at`, otherwise the download is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_EXPIRED` — a window whose deadline has passed but which has not yet been swept is closed to the patron even though its stored `status` still reads `AVAILABLE`. Finally `download_count` must be strictly below `max_downloads`, otherwise the refusal is `ERR_DELIVERY_DOWNLOAD_CAP_REACHED`. A permitted download increments `download_count` by 1, and the download that brings the count level with `max_downloads` also drives `status` `AVAILABLE → EXHAUSTED` in the same call, so the third download succeeds and the fourth is refused. `EXPIRED` and `EXHAUSTED` are both terminal, and the difference between them is which limb closed the window.

When the rota cannot fill a request, the desk escalates it to acquisitions, driving `SOURCING → ESCALATED_POD`. Escalation is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_HELD_LOCALLY` when the work's `local_holding_status` is `HELD_AVAILABLE` or `ON_ORDER` — there is no point buying a book that is already on the shelf or already on order. The order records the request, a `vendor` supplied by the caller, a `list_price_cents` snapshotted from the work's `list_price_cents` at escalation, and an `escalated_by_user_id` holding the escalating `X-User-ID`, or the literal string `system` when the operational sweep escalated automatically. It opens at `status` `PROPOSED` with `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_note`, `decided_at` and `received_at` all null.

An `AcquisitionsLibrarian` decides the proposal with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional free-text `decision_note`. The order must be at `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_POD_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, and the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `escalated_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either way `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` are recorded. Approving drives `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, sets the work's `local_holding_status` to `ON_ORDER` and emits a notice of kind `POD_APPROVED`; approval is additionally refused with `ERR_ALREADY_HELD_LOCALLY` when the work's `local_holding_status` has become `HELD_AVAILABLE` since the escalation. Rejecting drives `PROPOSED → REJECTED` and is the system's **second cycle**: the request returns `ESCALATED_POD → SOURCING`, and it does so with a **fresh budget** — `attempt_count` is reset to 0 and `unfilled_reason` is reset to `NONE`, so a request that had exhausted its rota rounds may walk the whole rota again. Receiving an approved purchase requires `status` `APPROVED`, drives `APPROVED → RECEIVED` with `received_at` stamped, sets the work's `local_holding_status` to `HELD_AVAILABLE`, advances the request `ESCALATED_POD → FILLED_BY_PURCHASE` with `closed_at` stamped, emits a notice of kind `PURCHASE_RECEIVED`, and re-derives the patron's `open_request_count`. The patron's original request is thus closed by a purchase rather than by a loan.

Cancellation is available to the requesting patron and to desk staff, and it may be exercised from `COPYRIGHT_HOLD`, `SOURCING`, `OFFER_PENDING`, `SUPPLY_COMMITTED` or `ESCALATED_POD`; from anywhere else it is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`, which in particular means a request whose parcel is already in transit cannot be cancelled. The cascade unwinds every commitment the request had made. Every `ACTIVE` clearance of the request is voided, which releases its slot back into the (`issn`, `calendar_year`) allowance as §3.4 describes. Every `OFFERED` offer moves to `CANCELLED` and every `PROPOSED` purchase order moves to `CANCELLED`. The request's `committed_partner_id` is cleared to null and its `committed_fee_cents`, `rush_surcharge_cents`, `royalty_cents`, `gross_charge_cents`, `subsidy_cents` and `net_patron_charge_cents` are all zeroed, so a cancelled request contributes nothing to the patron's `pending_charge_cents` and nothing to any invoice period. `closed_at` is stamped, a notice of kind `REQUEST_CANCELLED` is emitted, and the previously committed partner's `borrowed_from_count`, `net_borrow_imbalance_items` and `borrow_payable_cents` are re-derived so the consortium balance no longer reflects the abandoned borrowing.

### 3.9. Consortium Settlement and the Nightly Operational Sweep

Every partner is simultaneously a lender to us and a borrower from us, and the settlement machinery keeps both sides of that relationship on one ledger. The borrowing side is the request itself: a fulfilled, non-cancelled request committed to a partner is a borrow line worth its `committed_fee_cents`. The lending side is recorded explicitly by the accountant or the desk as a lending charge, carrying the `partner_id` of the partner that borrowed from us, the `item_title` of the item we lent, the `service_type` (`LOAN` or `COPY`) of what we supplied, a `charge_cents` amount of at least 0 before any surcharge, and an `incurred_at` instant which is what decides the settlement period the line falls into. A lending charge opens at `status` `OPEN` and moves to `INVOICED` when an invoice containing it is issued; creating one re-derives the partner's `lent_to_count`, `net_borrow_imbalance_items` and `lend_receivable_cents`.

An invoice is drafted for one partner and one `period_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, which must match a four-digit year, a hyphen and a month from `01` through `12`. The period window is **half-open**: `period_start` is the first instant of that month in UTC and `period_end` is the first instant of the following month, and a line qualifies when its instant is at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. A request fulfilled at midnight on the first of the next month belongs to the next period, not this one. Drafting is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the parent partner as §2.7 requires, and it supersedes rather than duplicates: any existing invoice for the same partner and the same `period_month` that is still at `DRAFT` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the new draft replaces it.

The borrow lines of a draft are the requests whose `committed_partner_id` is this partner, whose `status` is neither `CANCELLED` nor `UNFILLED`, whose `fulfilled_at` is not null, and whose `fulfilled_at` falls inside the half-open window. The lend lines are that partner's lending charges whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `incurred_at` falls inside the same window. `borrow_line_count` and `lend_line_count` record how many of each were found.

Each line is then surcharged by the 7.5 percent consortium administration levy: the surcharged value of a line is the line amount plus that amount multiplied by 0.075, rounded half up to whole cents. **The rounding order is pinned: round each line, then sum the rounded values.** Summing the raw amounts and rounding the single total gives a different answer and is wrong — three lines of 1000 cents each surcharge to 1075 cents apiece for a total of 3225 cents, whereas rounding a 3225-cent total once happens to agree, but three lines of 333 cents each surcharge to 358 cents apiece for 1074 cents, where the sum-then-round route would give 1074.675 rounded to 1075. `payable_total_cents` is the sum of the surcharged borrow lines and `receivable_total_cents` the sum of the surcharged lend lines.

Which variant produced the amounts is recorded in `settlement_variant`, taken from the `settlement_mode` of the partner's applicable active agreement selected by §3.2's rule, defaulting to `GROSS` when no agreement applies. `gross_amount_cents` is **always** `payable_total_cents`, identically in both variants. Under the `GROSS` variant `net_amount_cents` is also `payable_total_cents`: the lend side is still reported in `receivable_total_cents` for information, but it is not netted off, so the borrower pays its full borrow total and the lending side is invoiced separately. Under the `NET` variant `net_amount_cents` is `payable_total_cents` minus `receivable_total_cents`, which may well be negative. `direction` follows from that figure: `PAYABLE` when `net_amount_cents` is at or above 0, meaning we owe the partner, and `RECEIVABLE` when it is negative, meaning the partner owes us. A `GROSS` invoice is therefore always `PAYABLE`, while a `NET` invoice flips when we lent more than we borrowed.

An invoice opens at `DRAFT` and is issued by the accountant, which requires that the settlement period be closed — the logical clock must have reached `period_end`, and an attempt to issue a month that is still running is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. Issuing stamps `issued_at` from the clock, moves every `OPEN` lending charge line inside the period to `INVOICED`, and re-derives the partner's `lend_receivable_cents`. An issued invoice is then settled with an `action` of `PAY`, `DISPUTE` or `REINSTATE`. Paying drives `ISSUED → PAID` with `settled_at` stamped, and `PAID` is terminal. Disputing drives `ISSUED → DISPUTED` and requires a `dispute_reason`, which is recorded on the row; either the accountant or the partner's own agent may raise it. Reinstating drives `DISPUTED → ISSUED`, clears `dispute_reason` back to null, and pointedly **does not rewrite `issued_at`** — the original issue instant survives the round trip, which is what makes the `ISSUED → DISPUTED → ISSUED` cycle auditable and repeatable. Any of the three actions attempted from the wrong state is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE`, and `SUPERSEDED` is the other terminal state, reachable only by being replaced with a fresh draft.

Everything in this domain that is driven by the passage of time happens inside the operational sweep and nowhere else. The sweep may be run by desk staff or by an administrator, and it performs **eight steps in exactly this pinned order**, each step observing the effects of the steps before it. **Step 1** expires reciprocity agreements: every agreement at `ACTIVE` whose `effective_to` the clock has reached moves to `EXPIRED`. **Step 2** expires supply offers: every offer at `OFFERED` whose `respond_by` the clock has reached moves to `EXPIRED`, and each parent request cascades from `OFFER_PENDING` back to `SOURCING` without any refund of its rota budget. **Step 3** breaches recalls: every loan at `recall_status` `RECALL_ISSUED` whose `recall_due_at` the clock has passed moves to `RECALL_BREACHED`, which is what arms the triple fine multiplier for a later return. **Step 4** abandons hold-shelf items: every loan at custody `AWAITING_PICKUP` whose `pickup_expires_at` the clock has reached moves to `UNCLAIMED_RETURN_PREPARED` with `return_prepared_at` stamped, a return parcel is created, the request is closed as `UNCLAIMED` with `closed_at` stamped, a notice of kind `UNCLAIMED` is emitted, and the patron's `unclaimed_count`, `open_request_count` and `final_risk_score` are re-derived. **Step 5** expires electronic access: every delivery at `AVAILABLE` whose `expires_at` the clock has reached moves to `EXPIRED`, its request closes as `CLOSED` with `closed_at` stamped, a notice of kind `DELIVERY_EXPIRED` is emitted, and the patron's `open_request_count` is re-derived. **Step 6** writes off transit failures: every inbound parcel still at `IN_TRANSIT` whose `shipped_at` is 14 days or more behind the clock moves to `LOST_IN_TRANSIT`, and its request closes as `UNFILLED` with `unfilled_reason` `TRANSIT_LOST`, `closed_at` stamped and a notice of kind `REQUEST_UNFILLED`. **Step 7** disposes of exhausted rotas: for every request at `SOURCING` whose `attempt_count` has reached its rota's `max_attempts`, the request is escalated to `ESCALATED_POD` with a `PROPOSED` purchase order whose `escalated_by_user_id` is the literal string `system` when the rota's `auto_escalate_pod` is true, and is otherwise closed as `UNFILLED` with `unfilled_reason` `ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, `closed_at` stamped and a notice of kind `REQUEST_UNFILLED`. **Step 8** re-derives standing: every patron's `final_risk_score` is recomputed with the final-standing variant of the standing score, and any patron whose score reaches 40 is driven to `BLOCKED`.

Two properties of the sweep are as important as its steps. Within each step, rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order, tie-broken by the ascending `created_at` of the parent request, so the outcome of a sweep over many due rows is fully determined. And the sweep is **idempotent**: running it a second time without moving the logical clock performs no further writes and returns all-zero counters. Its response is not an entity but a summary object carrying `ran_at`, the clock instant at which it ran, together with `agreements_expired`, `offers_expired`, `recalls_breached`, `pickups_abandoned`, `deliveries_expired`, `shipments_lost`, `requests_escalated`, `requests_unfilled` and `patrons_blocked`, each counting the rows that step actually changed on this run.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

The table below is the complete surface of the API. Every path is mounted exactly as written, and the only path outside the `/ill` prefix is the logical-clock control endpoint. Creation endpoints answer HTTP 201 and modification endpoints answer HTTP 200; both return the created or updated resource in the `data` field of the envelope described in §2.2. Three endpoints answer 200 with something other than the resource named in their own path: recording migrated infraction history returns the updated patron, running the operational sweep returns its counter summary object, and setting the logical clock returns an object carrying `current_utc`. Endpoints that open a subordinate resource answer 201 with that subordinate resource — running a rota round returns the new supply offer, receiving an inbound parcel returns the newly created loan, and escalating a request returns the new purchase-on-demand order.
