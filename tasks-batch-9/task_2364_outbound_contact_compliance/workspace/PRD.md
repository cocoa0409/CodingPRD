# Outbound Contact Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every outbound dial and every outbound text a contact centre places is a legal act. Somebody must be able to prove, years later, that the consumer had given a valid consent of the right kind for the right channel, that the number was scrubbed against the national, state and internal do-not-call registries with data that was still fresh, that the call landed inside the recipient's own local calling window and not on a holiday or during a declared emergency, that the dialer was not abandoning more calls than the regulator permits, and that the moment the consumer said "stop" the instruction propagated everywhere within ten business days. Today that proof is assembled by hand from spreadsheets, dialer exports and email threads, and the gaps only surface when a complaint or a lawsuit arrives.

This document specifies the Outbound Contact Compliance API: a single system of record that refuses to place a non-compliant contact attempt in the first place, rather than reporting on it afterwards. The service holds consumer contact records with their recipient time-zone offsets, consent records captured per channel with their evidence handles, revocation requests with their propagation clocks, the three do-not-call list families, dated calling-window policies per state, dialer configurations with abandonment ceilings and answering-machine-detection rules, agent scripts with their disclosure blocks and recording-consent posture, campaigns that bind all of the above together, the individual attempts themselves, and the complaint file with its risk scoring and litigation pattern monitoring.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, every gate that can refuse an attempt is evaluated in a fixed, documented order, so that the same non-compliant request always produces the same diagnosis — an auditor reading a rejection knows exactly which control fired. Second, compliance state is never allowed to drift: honouring a revocation does not merely mark a row, it revokes consents, puts the consumer on the internal suppression list, invalidates the scrubs that were computed before the suppression existed, and suspends the campaigns those scrubs were feeding — all inside the request that recorded the revocation. Every resource in the domain is a peer collection mounted under `/compliance`, cross-referenced by identifier in request bodies rather than by nesting; the two exceptions are the reverse-lookup reads of a contact's revocation history and a campaign's scrub history.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else in the request, including body parsing. Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear on the endpoint being called; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The authorised roles for each endpoint are listed in §4.

The five roles are `Consumer`, `CampaignManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `DialerOperator` and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `Consumer` is the consumer whose number is being contacted. They may capture consent for their own record, revoke it by any reasonable method, file a complaint, and read their own compliance history. When `X-User-Role` is `Consumer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contact`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Contact` compare the resource's `contact_id` field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a `Consumer` captures a consent record or logs a revocation request, the payload `contact_id` must equal `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_CONTACT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership rule is scoped to exactly those two write operations — complaint intake by a `Consumer` carries no such restriction.

The other four roles are operator roles whose identifiers are not backed by any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `CampaignManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `DialerOperator` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `approved_by_user_id` on an `AgentScript` or a `DialerProfile`, and as `launch_certified_by_user_id` on a `Campaign` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. A `CampaignManager` builds campaigns, drafts scripts and dialer profiles, queues and runs scrubs, and closes campaigns, but may never approve a script, certify a launch or adjudicate a complaint. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns the compliance controls: script and dialer-profile approval, launch certification, campaign suspension and resumption, revocation and complaint adjudication, attempt voiding and suppression-list maintenance. A `DialerOperator` runs the dialer: requesting attempts, recording dispositions, running reassigned-number checks, and logging revocations or complaints heard on a call. A `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform: importing contacts, publishing state calling-window policies, declaring emergency restrictions, and driving the logical clock and the synthetic reassigned-number database.

Segregation of duties is enforced *within* the `ComplianceOfficer` role by user identifier. The officer whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `approved_by_user_id` when a script was approved is refused the launch certification of any campaign pinned to that script, with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Putting a campaign on the wire therefore requires two distinct `ComplianceOfficer` identities.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many items come back; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. A `limit` outside that inclusive range is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The `offset` parameter says how many items to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two reverse-lookup reads override that default: a contact's revocation history is returned newest `received_at` first, and a campaign's scrub history is returned newest `requested_at` first.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` says whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and Versioning

All eleven resource families in this domain — contacts, consent records, revocation requests, suppression entries, scrub jobs, campaigns, dialer profiles, agent scripts, calling-window policies, contact attempts and complaints — carry the same four system-managed properties, and none of them omits any of the four:

- `id` (string): a server-minted unique identifier formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the row was first created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

A mutation, for these purposes, includes a mutation the client did not directly request. When honouring a revocation rewrites a contact's consent census, when a scrub completion copies its eligible-target count onto a campaign, when an abandonment breach locks a dialer profile, or when a cascade voids somebody else's pending attempt, each touched row independently refreshes its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version`. Derived and computed fields are stored on the row and returned on every read; recomputing them counts as a mutation of the row that owns them.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this domain reads wall-clock time. Every timestamp the server writes, every calling window it evaluates, every propagation deadline, cooldown horizon, list-refresh test, scrub expiry and reassigned-number currency test reads a single logical clock. Before any client has set that clock it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock endpoint; the call mutates no entity and every subsequent request observes the new instant.

A second administrative facility, also restricted to `PlatformAdmin`, seeds the synthetic reassigned-number database that number-status checks query. Each seeded record pairs a `phone_number` with the `reassigned_on` date on which that number was reassigned to a different subscriber. A number with no seeded record is not an error — it is the safe-harbour outcome described in §3.1.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Protection on Consent Capture and Scrub Queueing

Two write operations in this domain would otherwise succeed against a parent row the client has not actually observed, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency precondition. In each case the version field is a required input, not an optional one.

A `POST /compliance/consent-records` request must carry `expected_contact_version` holding the `version` of the target `Contact` as the client last read it. Capturing a new consent for an existing contact-and-channel pair legitimately supersedes the prior `ACTIVE` record, so the write would succeed on identical state and the client would never notice that the contact had meanwhile been suppressed or re-tiered. If `expected_contact_version` does not equal the contact's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_CONTACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), forcing the capturing client to re-read the contact's suppression status and consent census first.

A `POST /compliance/scrub-jobs` request must carry `expected_campaign_version` holding the `version` of the target `Campaign` as the client last read it. A newly queued job legitimately supersedes the campaign's prior completed job once it executes, so this write likewise cannot be resolved by a state precondition alone: a client holding an out-of-date view of the campaign — its script pinning, its target states or its scrub status having changed — must re-read before scrubbing again. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The same field and the same rejection apply to `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch`, where `expected_campaign_version` is likewise a required input and a mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this domain accepts a version field, and none applies a version check.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Percentage measures — the two abandonment rates, the rolling compliance rate, the per-day rates stored in a campaign's rate history, and the configured abandonment ceiling — are carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half up. A percentage whose denominator is zero is reported as `0.00` rather than being left absent or null.

Risk scores are likewise carried to 2 decimal places, rounded half up, after the whole weighted sum has been formed; the individual weighted terms are not rounded on the way. Statutory exposure is an integer count of cents and needs no rounding, because every factor entering it is an integer. Line ceilings and minute values are integers, and the point at which a fractional line count is truncated downwards is stated exactly once, in §3.6 — truncating earlier or later produces a different number.

One rate in this domain is rounded twice, in a fixed order: the rolling compliance rate first rounds each contributing day's rate to 2 decimal places and only then rounds the mean of those already-rounded day values to 2 decimal places. Pooling the days' numerators and denominators and dividing once is a different computation and is not what this specification asks for.

### 2.8. The Compliance Gate Ladder: Evaluation Order

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one diagnosis is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failing check ends the request. Text order in §3 carries no authority; this ladder does.

1. Header presence and role validity, on every endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The acting role appearing on the endpoint, on every endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Consumer ownership of the payload `contact_id`, on `POST /compliance/consent-records` and `POST /compliance/revocation-requests` — `ERR_NOT_CONTACT_OWNER`.
4. Request well-formedness on every endpoint — body parses, every required field present, every enum value a member of its declared set, every number inside its declared range, phone numbers in E.164 form, timestamps and dates parseable, `limit` within 1 to 100 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. Existence of the referenced rows, in this order of resource families. The `Contact` named by a path identifier or a payload `contact_id`, on `GET /compliance/contacts/{id}`, `POST /compliance/contacts/{id}/number-status-check`, `POST /compliance/consent-records`, `POST /compliance/revocation-requests`, `POST /compliance/contact-attempts`, `POST /compliance/complaints` and `GET /compliance/contacts/{id}/revocation-requests` — `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `Campaign` named by a path identifier or a payload `campaign_id`, on `GET /compliance/campaigns/{id}`, `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch`, `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/suspend`, `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/resume`, `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/close`, `GET /compliance/campaigns/{id}/scrub-jobs`, `POST /compliance/scrub-jobs`, `POST /compliance/contact-attempts` and `POST /compliance/complaints` — `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `ConsentRecord` named by a path identifier on `GET /compliance/consent-records/{id}`, `POST /compliance/consent-records/{id}/suspend` and `POST /compliance/consent-records/{id}/reinstate` — `ERR_CONSENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `RevocationRequest` on `POST /compliance/revocation-requests/{id}/validate`, `POST /compliance/revocation-requests/{id}/reject` and `POST /compliance/revocation-requests/{id}/propagate` — `ERR_REVOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `SuppressionEntry` on `POST /compliance/suppression-entries/{id}/remove` — `ERR_SUPPRESSION_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `ScrubJob` on `POST /compliance/scrub-jobs/{id}/execute` — `ERR_SCRUB_JOB_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `DialerProfile` named by a path identifier or a payload `dialer_profile_id`, on `GET /compliance/dialer-profiles/{id}`, `POST /compliance/dialer-profiles/{id}/approve` and `POST /compliance/campaigns` — `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `AgentScript` named by a path identifier, by a payload `agent_script_id`, or by a payload `disclosure_script_id`, on `GET /compliance/agent-scripts/{id}`, `POST /compliance/agent-scripts/{id}/submit-review`, `POST /compliance/agent-scripts/{id}/approve`, `POST /compliance/agent-scripts/{id}/request-changes`, `POST /compliance/campaigns` and `POST /compliance/consent-records` — `ERR_AGENT_SCRIPT_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `CallingWindowPolicy` named by a path identifier or a payload `supersedes_policy_id`, on `GET /compliance/calling-window-policies/{id}`, `POST /compliance/calling-window-policies/{id}/declare-emergency` and `POST /compliance/calling-window-policies` — `ERR_CALLING_WINDOW_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `ContactAttempt` on `GET /compliance/contact-attempts/{id}`, `POST /compliance/contact-attempts/{id}/outcome` and `POST /compliance/contact-attempts/{id}/void` — `ERR_CONTACT_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND`. Then the `Complaint` on `GET /compliance/complaints/{id}`, `POST /compliance/complaints/{id}/substantiate` and `POST /compliance/complaints/{id}/dismiss` — `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`.
6. The stale-version preconditions of §2.6: `expected_contact_version` on `POST /compliance/consent-records` — `ERR_STALE_CONTACT_VERSION`; then `expected_campaign_version` on `POST /compliance/scrub-jobs` and `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch` — `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION`.
7. Entity-state admissibility, one resource family at a time. The campaign's status admitting the trigger, on `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch`, `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/suspend`, `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/resume`, `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/close`, `POST /compliance/scrub-jobs`, `POST /compliance/scrub-jobs/{id}/execute` and `POST /compliance/contact-attempts` — `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. Then the consent record's status, on suspend and reinstate — `ERR_CONSENT_STATE_INVALID`. Then the addressed revocation dimension, on validate, reject and propagate — `ERR_REVOCATION_STATE_INVALID`. Then the scrub job being `PENDING`, on execute — `ERR_SCRUB_JOB_STATE_INVALID`. Then the script's status, on submit-review, approve and request-changes — `ERR_SCRIPT_STATE_INVALID`. Then the profile's status, on profile approval — `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_STATE_INVALID`. Then the attempt's status, on outcome and void — `ERR_ATTEMPT_STATE_INVALID`. Then the complaint being `OPEN`, on substantiate and dismiss — `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. Then the suppression entry being `ACTIVE`, on remove — `ERR_SUPPRESSION_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`. Then the policy being `ACTIVE` with no emergency already in force, on declare-emergency — `ERR_POLICY_STATE_INVALID`.
8. The channel-uniqueness guard on consent reinstatement — `ERR_CONSENT_CHANNEL_CONFLICT` — followed by the reason-specific resume guard on campaign resumption — `ERR_RESUME_BLOCKED`.
9. Availability of a governing calling-window policy, on `POST /compliance/contact-attempts` and `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch` — `ERR_CALLING_WINDOW_POLICY_MISSING`.
10. The attempt-time safety block, in this exact order on `POST /compliance/contact-attempts`: suppression-list coverage — `ERR_SUPPRESSED_NUMBER`; existence of a consent basis — `ERR_NO_CONSENT_ON_RECORD`; the express-written requirement for marketing — `ERR_NO_EXPRESS_WRITTEN_CONSENT`; reassigned-number check currency — `ERR_RND_CHECK_REQUIRED`; the reassigned verdict itself — `ERR_REASSIGNED_NUMBER`.
11. Recording-consent adequacy of the pinned script, on `POST /compliance/contact-attempts` and `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch` — `ERR_RECORDING_CONSENT_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` — then the script's state coverage, on the same two endpoints — `ERR_SCRIPT_STATE_NOT_COVERED`.
12. The launch-readiness block: the script being approved, on `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch` — `ERR_SCRIPT_NOT_APPROVED`; the script's channel matching the campaign's, on `POST /compliance/campaigns` and `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch` — `ERR_SCRIPT_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`; the profile having been approved at least once, on launch — `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_NOT_APPROVED`; the profile not being locked, on `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch` and `POST /compliance/contact-attempts` — `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_LOCKED`; the newest scrub being complete, on the same two endpoints — `ERR_SCRUB_NOT_CURRENT`; at least one eligible target remaining, on launch — `ERR_EMPTY_TARGET_SET`; and segregation of duties, on launch — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
13. The temporal block: no emergency restriction in force, on `POST /compliance/contact-attempts` and `POST /compliance/campaigns/{id}/launch` — `ERR_EMERGENCY_RESTRICTION`; the recipient's local date not being a holiday, on attempts — `ERR_HOLIDAY_RESTRICTION`; the recipient's local time falling inside the effective window, on attempts — `ERR_OUTSIDE_CALLING_WINDOW`; the scrub not having expired, on attempts and launch — `ERR_SCRUB_EXPIRED`.
14. The remaining business constraints, in this order: phone-number uniqueness on contact import — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PHONE_NUMBER`; policy-period overlap on policy publication — `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; duplicate pending revocation on revocation intake — `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVOCATION_PENDING`; method-and-channel plausibility on revocation intake — `ERR_METHOD_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`; channel agreement on attempt creation — `ERR_ATTEMPT_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`; the mandatory abandonment message on outcome recording — `ERR_ABANDON_MESSAGE_REQUIRED`; the outcome classification agreeing with the connect timing on outcome recording — `ERR_ABANDON_CLASSIFICATION_INVALID`; answering-machine detection being enabled on outcome recording — `ERR_AMD_NOT_ENABLED`; and the waiver requirement on profile approval — `ERR_ABANDONMENT_LIMIT_WAIVER_REQUIRED`.
15. The contact cooldown on attempt creation — `ERR_CONTACT_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`.
16. Instantaneous pacing capacity on attempt creation — `ERR_PACING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
17. The consumer's frequency cap on attempt creation — `ERR_ATTEMPT_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`.

The consequences of this ordering are deliberate and worth naming. A suppressed consumer with no consent on record is reported as `ERR_SUPPRESSED_NUMBER`, never as `ERR_NO_CONSENT_ON_RECORD`. A stale reassigned-number check on a number that has in fact been reassigned is reported as `ERR_RND_CHECK_REQUIRED`, because currency is tested before the verdict is read. A launch certified by the very officer who approved the script, against a campaign whose scrub has also expired, is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, because the whole launch-readiness block precedes the temporal block. An attempt outside the calling window to a consumer whose budget is exhausted is reported as `ERR_OUTSIDE_CALLING_WINDOW`.

### 2.9. Nested Reads and Path Resolution

Only two paths in this domain nest one resource under another, and both are read-only reverse lookups: a contact's revocation history and a campaign's scrub history. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so a request for the revocation history of a contact that does not exist is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND` before any revocation row is examined, and a request for the scrub history of a campaign that does not exist is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`. Neither read invents a 404 for an empty result: a contact with no revocations and a campaign with no scrubs both return an empty array with a `total_count` of 0.

Cross-references carried in request bodies are resolved in the family order of §2.8 step 5, which matters when a single payload names several rows. Creating a campaign whose `dialer_profile_id` and `agent_script_id` are both unknown is reported as `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`; capturing a consent whose `contact_id` and `disclosure_script_id` are both unknown is reported as `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`; logging a complaint whose `contact_id` and `campaign_id` are both unknown is reported as `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Cascades and the Two Conserved Quantities

Compliance state in this domain is propagated immediately and synchronously. Every consequence of an operation — however many hops away — is applied inside the request that triggered it, and every read that follows sees the settled result. There is no deferred reconciliation, no eventual convergence and no background sweep.

Two quantities are conserved across every possible sequence of operations, and they are the sharpest available test of a correct implementation.

The first is the consumer's attempt budget. For any contact, `attempts_charged` counts that consumer's contact attempts whose `voided_at` is still null, `attempt_budget_remaining` is `attempt_budget_total` minus that count, and the two always sum back to `attempt_budget_total`. Creating an attempt charges exactly one; voiding an attempt — whether an officer voided it by hand or a campaign suspension voided it as a cascade — credits exactly one back. A create-then-void round trip therefore returns the consumer to the numbers they started with, and `attempt_budget_remaining` is never negative, because a create is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` while the remainder is already zero.

The second is the consent census. For any contact, `total_consent_count` always equals the sum of `active_consent_count`, `suspended_consent_count`, `revoked_consent_count` and `superseded_consent_count`. No consent row is ever deleted, so the total only grows — by exactly one per captured record — while every status movement merely shuffles the four buckets. Revoke-then-reinstate and suspend-then-reinstate both restore the original bucket distribution exactly.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Consumers, Wireless Identification and the Frequency Cap

A `PlatformAdmin` imports a consumer as a **Contact**. The import carries the consumer's `phone_number` in E.164 form — a leading plus sign, a first digit between 1 and 9, and then between seven and fourteen further digits — together with `state_code`, exactly two uppercase letters naming the US state whose calling-window policy will gate every attempt to this consumer. No two contacts may hold the same `phone_number`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PHONE_NUMBER`. Two more fields describe the recipient's clock: `utc_offset_minutes`, a fixed integer offset of the consumer's local time from UTC that must lie between −720 and 840 inclusive, and `time_zone_label`, the IANA label shown to agents. The label is display only. It never enters this or any other calculation, and there is no daylight-saving arithmetic anywhere in this domain — `utc_offset_minutes` alone determines recipient-local time.

Two optional import fields shape how aggressively the consumer may be worked. `phone_type` records the carrier classification captured at import: one of `WIRELESS`, `LANDLINE` or `UNKNOWN`, defaulting to `UNKNOWN` when omitted. `UNKNOWN` is not a free pass — the reassigned-number gate treats it exactly as it treats `WIRELESS`, so only an explicitly `LANDLINE` classification escapes that gate. `attempt_budget_total` is the lifetime permitted-attempt allowance for this consumer and must lie between 1 and 20 inclusive, defaulting to 3.

A freshly imported contact is fully determined: `attempts_charged` is 0, `attempt_budget_remaining` equals `attempt_budget_total`, `internal_dnc_status` is `CLEAR`, `risk_tier` is `LOW`, all five census counters — `active_consent_count`, `suspended_consent_count`, `revoked_consent_count`, `superseded_consent_count` and `total_consent_count` — are 0, `overdue_propagation_count` is 0, `rnd_result` is `NOT_CHECKED`, `rnd_checked_at` and `rnd_claimed_consent_date` are null, and `rnd_safe_harbor_eligible` is false. The record is returned with a 201.

Three of a contact's fields are conclusions rather than inputs. `internal_dnc_status` is `SUPPRESSED` when at least one of the consumer's consent records is in `REVOKED` status, or when their `risk_tier` is `CRITICAL`; in every other situation it is `CLEAR`. Its only two values are `CLEAR` and `SUPPRESSED`. `risk_tier` is derived from the consumer's substantiated complaints as described in §3.9 and takes one of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`. `attempt_budget_remaining` never falls below zero.

Before a wireless number may be dialled or texted, somebody must have asked the reassigned-number database whether the subscriber has changed. A `DialerOperator`, a `CampaignManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` runs that check by submitting the `claimed_consent_date` — the calendar date, in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, on which the business claims the consent was obtained. The database is consulted for the contact's `phone_number`, and the verdict lands in `rnd_result`, whose four values are `NOT_CHECKED`, `NO_MATCH`, `REASSIGNED` and `NO_DATA`. If a seeded record exists for that number and its `reassigned_on` date falls strictly after the submitted claimed date, the result is `REASSIGNED` — the number changed hands after the consent was supposedly given. If a record exists but its `reassigned_on` date falls on or before the claimed date, the result is `NO_MATCH`: the reassignment predates the consent and is irrelevant. If the database holds no record for the number at all, the result is `NO_DATA`, and it is precisely this outcome that confers the statutory safe harbour: `rnd_safe_harbor_eligible` is true when and only when `rnd_result` is `NO_DATA`, and false for every other value. The check also stamps `rnd_checked_at` with the current logical clock instant and stores the submitted date in `rnd_claimed_consent_date`, then increments the contact's `version` and returns the updated contact with a 200.

A check does not last forever. It is current while the clock is strictly earlier than 31 days after `rnd_checked_at`; at exactly that instant, and at any instant after it, the check is stale and counts as never having happened. Only the most recent check of a contact is ever consulted.

Reading a contact returns its live suppression status, risk tier, consent census, attempt-budget numbers and reassigned-number stamps, and is open to all five roles. A `ComplianceOfficer`, a `DialerOperator` or the `Consumer` themselves may also list every revocation request received for that consumer, newest `received_at` first, narrowing the list by `intake_status`, by `propagation_status`, and paging it with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Capturing Consent, Superseding It, and the Census

Consent is captured one channel at a time. Either the consumer themselves — for their own contact record only — or a `ComplianceOfficer` submits a payload naming the `contact_id`, the `channel` the consent covers (`VOICE` or `SMS`), and the legal basis in `consent_type`: `EXPRESS_WRITTEN`, `EXPRESS_ORAL` or `ESTABLISHED_BUSINESS_RELATIONSHIP`. How the consent was collected goes in `capture_method`, one of `WEB_FORM`, `PAPER_FORM`, `IVR_RECORDING`, `SMS_DOUBLE_OPT_IN` or `AGENT_ATTESTATION`. The disclosure the consumer actually saw is pinned by two fields: `disclosure_script_id`, naming the agent script whose disclosure text was presented at capture, and `disclosure_text_version`, an integer of at least 1 recording which version of that text it was. That version number is pinned and never rewritten, even when the script it came from is revised afterwards. `signature_evidence` carries the stored evidence handle — a signature image identifier, an IVR recording identifier or a double-opt-in token — and is required whenever `consent_type` is `EXPRESS_WRITTEN`. The payload also carries `expected_contact_version`, as §2.6 requires.

Capture stamps `captured_at` with the current logical clock instant, sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, and returns the new record with a 201. Its `suspended_reason` and `revoked_at` are null and its `reinstatement_count` is 0. If the consumer already had an `ACTIVE` record for that same contact-and-channel pair, that older record moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and the contact's `active_consent_count` and `superseded_consent_count` are recomputed. In every case the contact's census is recomputed with `total_consent_count` growing by one, and the contact's `version` increments.

A consent record's four statuses are `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED` and `SUPERSEDED`, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal — it accepts no further transition of any kind. A `ComplianceOfficer` may suspend an `ACTIVE` record pending investigation without destroying it, supplying a free-text `reason` that is stored as `suspended_reason`; the record moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, the contact's `active_consent_count` and `suspended_consent_count` are recomputed while `total_consent_count` stays put, and the contact's `version` increments. Suspending anything that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Reinstatement is the compensating move, and it serves both `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and `REVOKED → ACTIVE`. The officer supplies a `reinstatement_note`. Because a consumer may hold at most one live consent per channel, reinstatement is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_CHANNEL_CONFLICT` when another `ACTIVE` record already covers the same contact-and-channel pair. Both branches increment the record's `reinstatement_count` by one and recompute the contact's census with the total unchanged, and both increment the contact's `version`: the branch out of `SUSPENDED` clears `suspended_reason` back to null and rebalances the active and suspended counters, while the branch out of `REVOKED` clears `revoked_at` back to null and rebalances the active and revoked counters. The branch out of `REVOKED` carries one extra consequence: when the consumer is left with no `REVOKED` consent record at all *and* their `risk_tier` is not `CRITICAL`, their `internal_dnc_status` returns to `CLEAR` and every `ACTIVE` internal-list suppression entry standing against their `phone_number` moves `ACTIVE → REMOVED` with `removal_reason` set to `CONSENT_REINSTATED`. A consumer whose tier is `CRITICAL` stays suppressed no matter how many consents are reinstated. Reinstating a record that is already `ACTIVE`, or any operation at all on a `SUPERSEDED` record, is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_STATE_INVALID`.

When the dialer needs a legal basis for an attempt, the server picks one rather than letting the caller nominate it. The candidates are the consent records of that consumer whose `channel` equals the requested channel and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; suspended, revoked and superseded rows are invisible to this selection even when they are the newest rows for the channel. If there are no candidates the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_CONSENT_ON_RECORD`. Otherwise the candidates are ordered by `captured_at` descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first one is chosen and recorded on the attempt as `consent_record_id`. Row identifiers never participate in that ranking. One further test then applies: when the campaign's `campaign_purpose` is `MARKETING`, the chosen record's `consent_type` must be `EXPRESS_WRITTEN`, and an attempt relying on oral consent or an established business relationship for a marketing campaign is refused with `ERR_NO_EXPRESS_WRITTEN_CONSENT`. Informational and debt-collection purposes accept any of the three bases.

Reading a single consent record returns its status, its revocation stamp and its reinstatement count. Listing consent records may be narrowed by `contact_id`, by the `disclosure_script_id` the records were captured under, by `channel` and by `status`, with `limit` and `offset` for paging.

### 3.3. Honouring a Revocation: Two Dimensions and a Ten-Business-Day Clock

A consumer may withdraw consent by any reasonable method, and the system's job is to accept the withdrawal, act on it immediately, and prove that it reached every downstream system within the statutory window. A `Consumer` (for their own record), a `DialerOperator` who heard it on a call, or a `ComplianceOfficer` logs the withdrawal as a **RevocationRequest**. The payload names the `contact_id`, the `channel_received` on which the withdrawal arrived — one of `VOICE`, `SMS`, `EMAIL`, `WEB` or `MAIL` — the `method` the consumer used, and the `scope` of what they asked to stop, which is `ALL_CHANNELS`, `VOICE` or `SMS`. An optional `received_note` captures the consumer's verbatim wording.

The five accepted methods each belong to exactly one arrival channel, and the pairing is checked at intake: `REPLY_STOP` is reasonable only on `SMS`, `VERBAL_REQUEST` only on `VOICE`, `EMAIL_REQUEST` only on `EMAIL`, `WEB_PORTAL` only on `WEB`, and `POSTAL_MAIL` only on `MAIL`. Any other combination is refused with `ERR_METHOD_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`. A consumer may also have only one unadjudicated withdrawal outstanding at a time: if another revocation request for the same consumer is still sitting in `intake_status` `RECEIVED`, the new intake is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVOCATION_PENDING`.

A single revocation row carries **two independent state dimensions**, and they advance under different triggers. The intake dimension, `intake_status`, starts at `RECEIVED` and moves to either `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED`; `REJECTED` is terminal. The propagation dimension, `propagation_status`, starts at `NOT_STARTED`, is advanced to `IN_PROGRESS` by validation, and from there reaches either `PROPAGATED` or `OVERDUE`, both of which are terminal for that dimension. On intake, `received_at` is stamped with the current clock instant, `intake_status` is `RECEIVED`, `propagation_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `validated_at`, `propagated_at`, `rejection_reason` and `target_systems` are all null, `consent_records_revoked_count` is 0, and the row comes back with a 201.

Intake also fixes the deadline. `propagation_deadline_at` is ten business days after `received_at`, counted as follows. A cursor begins at `received_at` and keeps its time of day unchanged throughout. Ten days remain to be counted; the cursor advances by one whole day at a time, and each day the cursor lands on reduces the remaining count by one only if that day's UTC date falls Monday through Friday and is not listed in the governing policy's `holiday_dates`. When the remaining count reaches zero, the cursor's instant is `propagation_deadline_at`. The governing policy is the single active calling-window policy selected for the consumer's `state_code` at the moment of intake, using the selection rule of §3.5; when no policy covers that state, only Saturdays and Sundays are skipped and no holidays apply.

Validation is where the compliance machinery earns its keep. A `ComplianceOfficer` accepts the withdrawal as genuine, supplying a `validation_note`, and the request's `intake_status` moves `RECEIVED → VALIDATED` with `validated_at` stamped at the current clock instant, while the propagation dimension moves `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS` in the same request. Attempting to validate a row whose `intake_status` is not `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_REVOCATION_STATE_INVALID`. Four hops of consequence then run, in order.

The first hop revokes consent. Every consent record of that consumer whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `channel` is covered by the request's `scope` — every channel when the scope is `ALL_CHANNELS`, otherwise only the channel the scope names — moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped at the current clock instant. Rows moving `ACTIVE → REVOKED` and rows moving `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` are treated identically here; each moved row is counted, and the running total is stored on the revocation request as `consent_records_revoked_count`. The second hop, caused by the first, puts the consumer on the internal do-not-call list: `internal_dnc_status` becomes `SUPPRESSED`, the consent census counters are recomputed, the contact's `version` increments, and an `ACTIVE` internal-list suppression entry is created for the consumer's `phone_number` if no such entry already exists. The third hop, caused by the second, invalidates the scrubs that were computed before this suppression existed: every scrub job whose status is `COMPLETED`, whose `expires_at` is still later than the current clock instant, whose `list_types` includes `INTERNAL`, and whose campaign's `target_state_codes` contains the consumer's `state_code`, moves `COMPLETED → INVALIDATED` with `invalidation_reason` set to `NEW_INTERNAL_SUPPRESSION`. The fourth hop, caused by the third, stops the traffic: every `ACTIVE` campaign whose `latest_scrub_status` has just become `INVALIDATED` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `SCRUB_STALE`, voiding each of its pending attempts and crediting the affected consumers' attempt budgets as §3.8 describes, and incrementing its own `version`.

Rejection is the other branch of the intake dimension and it is deliberately inert. The officer supplies a `rejection_reason` of `NOT_CONSUMER`, `UNVERIFIABLE_IDENTITY` or `DUPLICATE`; `intake_status` moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED`, the reason is stored, and `propagation_status` stays exactly where it was at `NOT_STARTED`, because the two dimensions move under different triggers and a rejected intake never propagates. No consent record is touched, so the consent census is untouched as well. Rejecting a row whose `intake_status` is not `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_REVOCATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Recording propagation closes the file. A `ComplianceOfficer` submits `target_systems`, the list of downstream systems the withdrawal was pushed to, which is stored on the row; `propagated_at` is stamped at the current clock instant. The call requires `propagation_status` to be `IN_PROGRESS` and `intake_status` to be `VALIDATED`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_REVOCATION_STATE_INVALID`. Which terminal state it lands in depends on one inclusive comparison: if the current clock instant is at or before `propagation_deadline_at`, the dimension moves `IN_PROGRESS → PROPAGATED` and nothing else changes; if the current clock instant is later than `propagation_deadline_at`, it moves `IN_PROGRESS → OVERDUE` and, in addition, the consumer's `overdue_propagation_count` increases by one and the contact's `version` increments. Propagating at exactly the deadline instant is on time; one second later is late.

### 3.4. Do-Not-Call Lists and Scrub Execution

Three list families feed suppression, and one row shape serves all three. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin` adds a **SuppressionEntry** naming its `list_type` — `NATIONAL`, `STATE` or `INTERNAL` — the `phone_number` in E.164 form, the `source` the row came from (`FTC_DNC_DOWNLOAD`, `STATE_REGISTRY`, `CONSUMER_REQUEST` or `LITIGATION_HOLD`), and `source_refreshed_at`, the instant the source data behind the row was last refreshed. `state_code` is required when `list_type` is `STATE` — naming the state registry the row came from, two uppercase letters — and is ignored for the other two list types. The entry is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `removed_at` and `removal_reason` null, and returns a 201.

Adding an internal-list entry is not inert. When the `phone_number` on a new `ACTIVE` internal entry belongs to an existing contact, the same two hops that close §3.3 run here as well: the current completed scrub jobs that requested `INTERNAL` and cover that consumer's state move to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidation_reason` `NEW_INTERNAL_SUPPRESSION`, and their `ACTIVE` campaigns move to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `SCRUB_STALE`, voiding pending attempts and crediting budgets.

Removal is the only transition a suppression entry has: `ACTIVE → REMOVED`, driven either by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying a `removal_reason` of `CONSENT_REINSTATED`, `REGISTRY_DELISTING` or `DATA_ERROR`, or by the reinstatement cascade of §3.2, which supplies `CONSENT_REINSTATED` itself. Either way `removed_at` is stamped at the current clock instant. Removing an entry that is already `REMOVED` is refused with `ERR_SUPPRESSION_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`. A removed internal entry stops suppressing attempts from that moment on, but scrub jobs it has already invalidated stay `INVALIDATED` — undoing the suppression does not resurrect the scrub. Entries may be listed and narrowed by `list_type`, `phone_number`, `state_code` and `status`, with `limit` and `offset`.

Scrubbing itself is a two-step affair, because a scrub is a dated piece of evidence and not a live query. A `CampaignManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` queues a **ScrubJob** naming the `campaign_id` whose target set is to be scrubbed and the `list_types` to scrub against — a non-empty subset of `NATIONAL`, `STATE` and `INTERNAL` with no duplicates — plus the `expected_campaign_version` of §2.6. The campaign must be in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_LAUNCH`; queueing a scrub against an `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` campaign is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. The job is created with `status` `PENDING` and `requested_at` stamped at the current clock instant, `completed_at`, `expires_at`, `failure_reason` and `invalidation_reason` all null and the three counts at 0, and the owning campaign's `version` increments. The job comes back with a 201.

Executing a queued job is one trigger with two divergent outcomes, and which one it takes turns entirely on list currency. The job must still be `PENDING` — a `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `INVALIDATED` job cannot be executed again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SCRUB_JOB_STATE_INVALID` — and the owning campaign must still be in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_LAUNCH`. The currency test covers the two external list families only: for each of `NATIONAL` and `STATE` that this job requested, at least one `ACTIVE` suppression entry must exist whose `source_refreshed_at` is at or after 31 days before the current clock instant, and for `STATE` that entry must additionally carry a `state_code` that appears in the campaign's `target_state_codes`. A job requesting only `INTERNAL` faces no currency test at all.

When any requested external list fails that test, the job moves `PENDING → FAILED`: `failure_reason` is set to `STALE_OR_EMPTY_LIST`, `completed_at` and `expires_at` stay null, the campaign's `latest_scrub_status` becomes `FAILED`, the campaign's own `status` is left exactly where it was, and the campaign's `version` increments.

When every requested external list is current, the job moves `PENDING → COMPLETED` and resolves the target set. `contacts_scrubbed_count` is the number of contacts whose `state_code` appears in the campaign's `target_state_codes`. Of those, a contact counts as suppressed when at least one suppression entry exists whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `list_type` is among the job's `list_types`, whose `phone_number` equals the contact's `phone_number`, and — for a `STATE` entry only — whose `state_code` equals the contact's `state_code`; `contacts_suppressed_count` is the number of such contacts. `eligible_target_count` is `contacts_scrubbed_count` less `contacts_suppressed_count`, and can never be negative because the suppressed contacts are a subset of the examined ones. Removed entries do not suppress, and an internal entry is simply ignored by a job that did not request `INTERNAL`. The job's `completed_at` is stamped at the current clock instant and `expires_at` is set to 31 days after `completed_at`. Every other scrub job of the same campaign that was sitting in `COMPLETED` moves `COMPLETED → SUPERSEDED`. Finally the campaign is updated: its `latest_scrub_status` becomes `COMPLETED`, its own `eligible_target_count` is overwritten with this job's, its `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes — and if it was in `DRAFT` it moves `DRAFT → PENDING_LAUNCH`, becoming launchable only because a child job completed. A campaign already in `PENDING_LAUNCH` stays in `PENDING_LAUNCH`, with only those two derived fields refreshed. Either branch returns the job with a 200.

A campaign's `latest_scrub_status` always reflects the newest job of that campaign ordered by `requested_at` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and takes one of `PENDING`, `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `INVALIDATED`. Its `eligible_target_count` reflects the newest `COMPLETED` job, and is 0 when there is none. A completed scrub is current while the clock is strictly earlier than its `expires_at`; at exactly `expires_at` the scrub has already expired. Superseded, invalidated, failed and pending jobs never satisfy the currency test at all. Reading a job returns its counts, its validity window and its failure or invalidation reason; a campaign's jobs may be listed newest `requested_at` first, narrowed by `status` and paged with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.5. State Calling Windows, Holiday Calendars and Emergency Restrictions

A **CallingWindowPolicy** encodes one state's rules for one period of time. A `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` publishes it with the `state_code` it governs, the earliest permitted recipient local time in `window_start_local` and the exclusive end of the permitted window in `window_end_local`, both as `HH:MM` on a 24-hour clock with `window_end_local` strictly later than `window_start_local`. The `recording_consent_regime` records that state's recording law as either `ONE_PARTY` or `ALL_PARTY`. `effective_from` and `effective_to` bound the period the policy is in force, with `effective_to` strictly after `effective_from`. `holiday_dates` is an optional list of recipient-local calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form on which no attempt may be placed and which are also skipped when counting the business days of a revocation propagation deadline; it defaults to an empty list. `supersedes_policy_id` optionally names the currently active policy for that same state which this one replaces, and is null for a state's first policy.

On publication the server derives `window_start_minutes` and `window_end_minutes` by parsing the two `HH:MM` inputs into minutes after local midnight, sets `status` to `ACTIVE` and `emergency_status` to `NONE`, leaves `emergency_declared_at` and `emergency_note` null, and returns a 201. Periods for one state may not collide: the new policy's effective period must not overlap that of an `ACTIVE` policy for the same `state_code` unless `supersedes_policy_id` names precisely that policy, and `window_start_local` must be strictly before `window_end_local`; a violation of either is refused with `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. When `supersedes_policy_id` is supplied, the named policy must be `ACTIVE`, must govern the same `state_code`, and the new policy's `effective_from` must fall at or after the named policy's `effective_from`; the named policy then moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `effective_to` is truncated to the new policy's `effective_from`, so that the two periods sit exactly adjacent with no gap and no overlap. A `SUPERSEDED` policy is permanently out of the running.

Selecting the governing policy is a single documented rule, used at attempt time, at launch time and at revocation intake alike. The candidates are the policies whose `state_code` equals the relevant state, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose effective period contains the current clock instant on a half-open basis — `effective_from` is inside the period, `effective_to` is outside it. If there are no candidates, the operation is refused with `ERR_CALLING_WINDOW_POLICY_MISSING`. Otherwise the candidates are ordered by `effective_from` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first is chosen; an attempt records it as `calling_window_policy_id`. Because superseded policies are excluded, a superseding policy takes over at exactly its own `effective_from` instant.

Emergencies are declared on the policy, not on the campaign. Only a `PlatformAdmin` may declare one, supplying an `emergency_note`. The policy must be `ACTIVE` and its `emergency_status` must currently be `NONE`; a superseded policy, or one already under an emergency, refuses the call with `ERR_POLICY_STATE_INVALID`. The declaration is a self-loop on `status`, `ACTIVE → ACTIVE` — the policy stays `ACTIVE` — with the change landing on the independent `emergency_status` field, which moves `NONE → ACTIVE`, alongside `emergency_declared_at` stamped at the current clock instant and the note stored. It then reaches straight into the campaign layer: every `ACTIVE` campaign whose `target_state_codes` contains this policy's `state_code` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `EMERGENCY_RESTRICTION`, voiding each of those campaigns' pending attempts with the budget credits of §3.8 and incrementing each campaign's `version`.

Policies may be read individually — returning the derived minute bounds and the emergency status — or listed, narrowed by `state_code`, by the `supersedes_policy_id` they name, by `status` and by `emergency_status`, and paged with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.6. Two Approval Tracks: Agent Scripts and Dialer Configuration

Nothing goes on the wire without an approved script and an approved dialer configuration, and the two travel independent approval tracks with different shapes.

An **AgentScript** is drafted by a `CampaignManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `name` shown to agents, the `channel` it is written for (`VOICE` or `SMS`), a `recording_consent_mode` of `ONE_PARTY` or `ALL_PARTY` recording the recording-consent posture the script satisfies, and `covered_state_codes`, a non-empty list of two-uppercase-letter state codes whose disclosure requirements the script satisfies. Its substance lives in `disclosure_blocks`, an ordered list of at least one block, each block an object carrying a `block_type` — one of `IDENTIFICATION`, `PURPOSE`, `OPT_OUT` or `RECORDING_NOTICE` — and its `text`. The list must contain one `IDENTIFICATION` block and one `OPT_OUT` block; a draft that identifies nobody or offers no way out is malformed. A new script is created with `status` `DRAFT` and `review_cycle_count` 0, with `change_note`, `approved_by_user_id` and `approved_at` null, and returns a 201.

The script's three statuses are `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` and `APPROVED`, and its review loop is genuinely a loop. Submitting a script for review moves it `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` and clears any `change_note` back to null. From `IN_REVIEW`, a `ComplianceOfficer` may send it back with a `change_note`, which moves it `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, stores the note and increments `review_cycle_count` by one — that counter is the record of how many revision rounds a script went through. Alternatively the officer approves it with an `approval_note`, moving it `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`, recording their own `X-User-ID` in `approved_by_user_id` and stamping `approved_at` with the current clock instant. `APPROVED` is terminal. Submitting a script that is not `DRAFT`, or approving or requesting changes on a script that is not `IN_REVIEW`, is refused with `ERR_SCRIPT_STATE_INVALID`. The `approved_by_user_id` written here is the segregation-of-duties key of §2.1: that officer is barred from certifying the launch of any campaign pinned to this script. Reading a script returns its approval stamp and its review-history counter.

A **DialerProfile** is drafted by a `DialerOperator` or a `CampaignManager`. Its `mode` is `PREVIEW`, `POWER` or `PREDICTIVE`, and `agents_available` — the staffed agent count that feeds the pacing calculation — must lie between 1 and 500 inclusive. Four optional fields carry defaults: `abandonment_limit_pct`, the operator-configured abandonment ceiling compared against the regulatory rate, which must lie between 0.01 and 100.00 inclusive at two decimal places and defaults to 3.00; `amd_enabled`, defaulting to false, which decides whether answering-machine detection runs at all; `amd_abandon_on_machine`, defaulting to false, recording whether a detected machine is dropped without an agent — this flag is kept for audit and never changes any rate; and `abandon_message_seconds`, the length in seconds of the recorded abandonment message played on abandoned calls, which must lie between 1 and 10 inclusive and defaults to 2. A new profile is created with `status` `DRAFT`, `waiver_reference` and `locked_reason` and `approved_by_user_id` null, `max_lines_last_computed` equal to `agents_available`, and `requires_waiver` set to true exactly when `abandonment_limit_pct` is strictly greater than the regulatory ceiling of 3.00 percent and false otherwise. It returns a 201.

The profile's three statuses are `DRAFT`, `APPROVED` and `LOCKED`, and none of them is terminal. A `ComplianceOfficer` approves a profile from either `DRAFT` or `LOCKED`, so both `DRAFT → APPROVED` and `LOCKED → APPROVED` are admissible and behave identically; approving one that is already `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_STATE_INVALID`. A profile whose `requires_waiver` is true — that is, one configured above the 3.00 percent regulatory ceiling — may only be approved when the payload supplies a non-empty `waiver_reference` documenting the exception; omitting it, or supplying an empty string, is refused with `ERR_ABANDONMENT_LIMIT_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. Approval moves the profile to `APPROVED`, records the approving officer's `X-User-ID` in `approved_by_user_id`, clears `locked_reason` back to null, stores the supplied `waiver_reference` (which stays null when `requires_waiver` is false), and recomputes `max_lines_last_computed` treating the campaign's regulatory abandonment rate as `0.00`, since at approval time the profile may have no campaign at all. The reverse move, `APPROVED → LOCKED`, is never requested by a client: it is the second hop of the abandonment cascade in §3.8, sets `locked_reason` to `ABANDONMENT_BREACH`, and is undone only by a fresh approval — which is exactly what makes a campaign suspended for a locked profile resumable again.

Pacing capacity is computed from the profile in five ordered steps, each consuming the previous step's clamped output. First, the headroom is the profile's `abandonment_limit_pct` less the persisted `regulatory_abandonment_rate_pct` of the campaign being paced, taken as `0.00` when there is no such campaign. Second, that headroom is clamped into the range from `0.00` to a cap of 2.00 percentage points — negative headroom becomes zero, headroom above two points becomes two. Third, the multiplier is one plus the clamped headroom multiplied by a gain of 0.25 lines per percentage point. Fourth, the line ceiling is the greater of `agents_available` itself and the product of `agents_available` and the multiplier truncated downwards to a whole number — the truncation happens at this step and nowhere else, because truncating before the clamp or clamping after a different truncation yields a different answer. Fifth and last, a profile in `PREVIEW` mode never over-dials: its line ceiling is simply `agents_available`, regardless of everything computed before. The internal abandonment measure never enters this calculation. The result is stored on the profile as `max_lines_last_computed` whenever an approval, an outcome or a void recomputes it, and is copied onto each new attempt as `max_lines_snapshot`, evaluated from the state persisted before that attempt was accepted. Reading a profile returns its waiver requirement, its lock reason and its last computed line ceiling.

### 3.7. Assembling, Certifying and Recovering a Campaign

A `CampaignManager` creates a **Campaign** as the object that binds consent, lists, windows, scripts and dialer configuration together. The payload carries an operator-facing `name`, the `channel` this campaign dials or texts (`VOICE` or `SMS`), the `campaign_purpose` that decides which consent basis the attempt gate will demand (`MARKETING`, `INFORMATIONAL` or `DEBT_COLLECTION`), a non-empty `target_state_codes` list of two-uppercase-letter states, and the `dialer_profile_id` and `agent_script_id` it pins. The pinned script's `channel` must equal the campaign's `channel`, at creation and again at launch; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_SCRIPT_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`. A new campaign has `status` `DRAFT`, `latest_scrub_status` and `measurement_date` and `suspension_reason` and `launch_certified_by_user_id` and `pinned_script_version` all null, `eligible_target_count` 0, `live_answer_count_today` and `abandoned_count_today` and `machine_count_today` and `no_answer_count_today` all 0, `permitted_attempt_count` and `voided_attempt_count` 0, `regulatory_abandonment_rate_pct` and `internal_abandonment_rate_pct` and `rolling_compliance_rate_pct` all `0.00`, and an empty `daily_rate_history`. It returns a 201.

The campaign's five statuses are `DRAFT`, `PENDING_LAUNCH`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal. The move `DRAFT → PENDING_LAUNCH` is never requested directly; it bubbles up from a completed scrub, as §3.4 describes.

Certification is the gate between `PENDING_LAUNCH` and `ACTIVE`, and it is a `ComplianceOfficer`'s act. The officer supplies a `certification_note` and the `expected_campaign_version` of §2.6, and the campaign must currently be in `PENDING_LAUNCH`; certifying a `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` campaign is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. Everything the campaign depends on is then re-verified. A governing `ACTIVE` calling-window policy covering the current clock instant must exist for *every* state in `target_state_codes`, or the launch is refused with `ERR_CALLING_WINDOW_POLICY_MISSING`. Where any of those policies has an `ALL_PARTY` `recording_consent_regime`, the pinned script's `recording_consent_mode` must also be `ALL_PARTY`, or the launch is refused with `ERR_RECORDING_CONSENT_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`. The pinned script's `covered_state_codes` must include every one of the campaign's target states, or the launch is refused with `ERR_SCRIPT_STATE_NOT_COVERED`. The script must be `APPROVED` — a `DRAFT` or `IN_REVIEW` script yields `ERR_SCRIPT_NOT_APPROVED` — and its channel must still match. The pinned dialer profile must have been approved at least once, meaning its status is no longer `DRAFT`; a still-`DRAFT` profile yields `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_NOT_APPROVED`, while a profile sitting in `LOCKED` yields `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_LOCKED`. The campaign's newest scrub job must be in `COMPLETED` — a newest job that is `PENDING`, `FAILED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `INVALIDATED` yields `ERR_SCRUB_NOT_CURRENT` — and the campaign's `eligible_target_count` must be at least 1, since a campaign whose every target was suppressed has nobody to call and yields `ERR_EMPTY_TARGET_SET`. The certifying officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the pinned script's `approved_by_user_id`, or the launch is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. No targeted state's policy may have `emergency_status` `ACTIVE`, or the launch is refused with `ERR_EMERGENCY_RESTRICTION`. And that completed scrub must not have expired: the clock must still be strictly before its `expires_at`, or the launch is refused with `ERR_SCRUB_EXPIRED`. The relative order in which these refusals are reported is fixed by §2.8.

A campaign that survives all of that moves `PENDING_LAUNCH → ACTIVE`. Its `launch_certified_by_user_id` records the certifying officer's `X-User-ID`. Its `pinned_script_version` captures the script's `version` at this instant, and it is a genuine pin: later edits to that script never change the number recorded here. Measurement starts from zero — `measurement_date` becomes the UTC calendar date of the current clock instant, all four of today's counters are set to 0, and both `regulatory_abandonment_rate_pct` and `internal_abandonment_rate_pct` are set to `0.00`. The campaign's `version` increments and the updated campaign comes back with a 200.

A campaign can leave `ACTIVE` for five distinct reasons, and `suspension_reason` names which: `MANUAL` when a `ComplianceOfficer` suspends it by hand with a `suspension_note`; `ABANDONMENT_BREACH` when its own abandonment rate crosses the profile's ceiling; `PROFILE_LOCKED` when the profile it points at was locked because a *sibling* campaign breached — that is, any campaign on the same profile other than the one whose breach caused the lock; `SCRUB_STALE` when its newest scrub was invalidated by a new internal suppression, which also forces its `latest_scrub_status` to `INVALIDATED`; and `EMERGENCY_RESTRICTION` when an emergency is declared for a state it targets. In `DRAFT`, `PENDING_LAUNCH`, `ACTIVE` and `CLOSED`, `suspension_reason` is null. Every one of the five suspension paths does the same housekeeping: each of the campaign's contact attempts still sitting at `PENDING` is voided with `void_reason` `CAMPAIGN_SUSPENDED`, each such void credits its consumer's attempt budget as §2.10 requires, and the campaign's `version` increments. Suspending a campaign that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`.

Resumption is `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, driven by a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `resume_note`, and the guard it must pass depends on why the campaign went down — in every case on the state of some *other* entity. A campaign suspended for `SCRUB_STALE` may resume only once its newest scrub job is `COMPLETED` and the clock is still strictly before that job's `expires_at`, which in practice means a fresh scrub must be queued and executed. A campaign suspended for `PROFILE_LOCKED` or for `ABANDONMENT_BREACH` may resume only once its dialer profile is back in `APPROVED`. A campaign suspended for `ABANDONMENT_BREACH` must additionally wait for the breached measurement day to close: the UTC date of the current clock instant must differ from the campaign's `measurement_date`. A campaign suspended for `EMERGENCY_RESTRICTION` may resume only once every `ACTIVE` policy for every state in `target_state_codes` has `emergency_status` back to `NONE`. A campaign suspended `MANUAL`ly carries no extra guard. Where the applicable guard still fails, resumption is refused with `ERR_RESUME_BLOCKED`; where it holds, `suspension_reason` is cleared back to null and the campaign's `version` increments. Resuming a campaign that is not `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`.

Closure is a `CampaignManager`'s act, takes a `closure_note`, and is admissible from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_LAUNCH`, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`; every one of those four moves — `DRAFT → CLOSED`, `PENDING_LAUNCH → CLOSED`, `ACTIVE → CLOSED` and `SUSPENDED → CLOSED` — lands in `CLOSED` and increments the campaign's `version`. Closing from `ACTIVE` additionally voids the campaign's `PENDING` attempts with `void_reason` `CAMPAIGN_SUSPENDED` and credits those consumers' attempt budgets, exactly as a suspension would. Closing from `SUSPENDED` additionally clears `suspension_reason` back to null. Closing from `DRAFT` or from `PENDING_LAUNCH` has no side effect beyond the status change. `CLOSED` accepts nothing further, and any trigger against a closed campaign is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`.

Reading a campaign returns both abandonment measures, the rolling seven-day compliance rate — recomputed as part of serving the read — its scrub status and its suspension reason. Campaigns may be listed and narrowed by the `agent_script_id` or `dialer_profile_id` they reference, by `status` and by `channel`, with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.8. Placing an Attempt, Recording its Disposition, and the Breach Cascade

A **ContactAttempt** is created only by a `DialerOperator`, and the payload is deliberately thin: the `campaign_id` placing the attempt, the `contact_id` being contacted, and the `channel` used. The campaign must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`, and the attempt's `channel` must equal the campaign's own `channel`, or it is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`. Everything else about the attempt is derived by the server from the gates it must pass, and the full order in which those gates fire is fixed by §2.8.

Suppression is checked first among the safety gates. The consumer's number must not be covered by any `ACTIVE` suppression entry — a `NATIONAL` or `INTERNAL` entry matching the number, or a `STATE` entry matching both the number and the consumer's own `state_code` — and the consumer's `internal_dnc_status` must be `CLEAR`; either condition failing is refused with `ERR_SUPPRESSED_NUMBER`. Next comes the consent basis and the marketing test of §3.2, which produce `consent_record_id` or the refusals `ERR_NO_CONSENT_ON_RECORD` and `ERR_NO_EXPRESS_WRITTEN_CONSENT`.

Then the reassigned-number gate, which applies when the consumer's `phone_type` is `WIRELESS` or `UNKNOWN` and is skipped entirely for a `LANDLINE`. A check must exist and still be current — the clock strictly earlier than 31 days after `rnd_checked_at` — or the attempt is refused with `ERR_RND_CHECK_REQUIRED`; a consumer never checked at all, whose `rnd_result` is still `NOT_CHECKED`, fails here too. Only then is the verdict read: an `rnd_result` of `REASSIGNED` refuses the attempt with `ERR_REASSIGNED_NUMBER`, because a reassigned number may not be dialled at all, safe harbour or no safe harbour. Both `NO_MATCH` and `NO_DATA` pass this gate, and `rnd_safe_harbor_eligible` records which of the two it was.

Recording-consent adequacy and script coverage come next, applying the same two rules the launch gate applies, now to the single recipient state rather than to all target states: when the governing policy's `recording_consent_regime` is `ALL_PARTY`, the campaign's pinned script's `recording_consent_mode` must also be `ALL_PARTY` or the attempt is refused with `ERR_RECORDING_CONSENT_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`; and the pinned script's `covered_state_codes` must list the recipient's `state_code` or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SCRIPT_STATE_NOT_COVERED`. The dialer profile must not be `LOCKED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_DIALER_PROFILE_LOCKED`. The campaign's newest scrub job must be `COMPLETED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SCRUB_NOT_CURRENT`, and that scrub must not have expired — the clock strictly before its `expires_at` — or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SCRUB_EXPIRED`.

The temporal gates work in the recipient's own local time, and the arithmetic is exact. Take the hour and minute of the current clock instant in UTC, express them as minutes past UTC midnight, add the consumer's `utc_offset_minutes`, and reduce the sum modulo 1440 minutes per day — adding a further 1440 first if the sum came out negative, so that the result always lands between 0 and 1439. That value is recorded on the attempt as `recipient_local_minutes`, and its zero-padded `HH:MM` rendering is recorded as `recipient_local_time`. The recipient's local calendar date is the date reached by adding `utc_offset_minutes` to the current clock instant, which may well differ from the UTC date. If that local date appears in the governing policy's `holiday_dates`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_HOLIDAY_RESTRICTION`. Separately, if any `ACTIVE` policy for a state this campaign targets has `emergency_status` `ACTIVE`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_EMERGENCY_RESTRICTION`.

The permitted window itself is built in five steps from the governing policy and the selected consent. The base window runs from the policy's `window_start_minutes` to its `window_end_minutes`. Anything weaker than express written consent loses the first hour of the day: when the selected consent record's `consent_type` is anything other than `EXPRESS_WRITTEN`, a consent extension of 60 minutes applies, and when it is `EXPRESS_WRITTEN` the extension is zero. Texts are held to a window narrowed at *both* ends: when the attempt's `channel` is `SMS` a channel shift of 30 minutes applies, and for `VOICE` it is zero. The federal floor and ceiling are then applied after both of those adjustments: `effective_window_start_minutes` is the greater of the base start plus the consent extension and the federal floor of eight o'clock in the recipient's local morning — 480 minutes past local midnight — plus the channel shift; `effective_window_end_minutes` is the lesser of the base end minus the channel shift and the federal ceiling of nine o'clock in the recipient's local evening, 1260 minutes past local midnight. Both bounds are recorded on the attempt. Admission is half-open: the attempt is permitted when `recipient_local_minutes` is greater than or equal to `effective_window_start_minutes` and strictly less than `effective_window_end_minutes`. A recipient-local time exactly at the start is inside the window; a recipient-local time exactly at the end is outside it. Failing this test refuses the attempt with `ERR_OUTSIDE_CALLING_WINDOW`.

Three throttles close the ladder. The contact cooldown looks at that consumer's most recent attempt whose `voided_at` is null — across every campaign and both channels, the one with the greatest `requested_at`, ties broken by the greatest `created_at`. If such an attempt exists and the current clock instant falls at or before 30 minutes after its `requested_at`, the new attempt is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. The guard interval is closed at both ends: an attempt exactly 30 minutes after the previous one is still refused, one second later is permitted. Voiding an attempt removes it from the cooldown basis entirely. Pacing capacity comes next: the campaign's current count of attempts still sitting at `PENDING` must be strictly below the line ceiling computed for its dialer profile per §3.6, and an attempt that would exceed that instantaneous capacity is refused with `ERR_PACING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Finally the frequency cap: the consumer's `attempt_budget_remaining` must be at least 1, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`.

An accepted attempt is created with `requested_at` stamped at the current clock instant and `outcome` `PENDING`. It records the `consent_record_id` and `calling_window_policy_id` the server selected, the four window and local-time fields already described, `cooldown_until` set to 30 minutes after the current clock instant — the closed interval from `requested_at` through `cooldown_until` is what blocks the next attempt to this consumer — and `max_lines_snapshot`, the line ceiling as computed from state persisted before this attempt. Its `agent_connect_seconds` and `outcome_recorded_at` and `voided_at` and `void_reason` are null, and `abandon_message_played` is false. The consumer's `attempts_charged` increases by one, `attempt_budget_remaining` decreases by one and the contact's `version` increments; the campaign's `permitted_attempt_count` increases by one. The attempt comes back with a 201.

Recording a disposition is the `DialerOperator`'s next act, and it takes the `outcome` plus, where relevant, `agent_connect_seconds` and `abandon_message_played`. The attempt must still be `PENDING`, or the call is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_STATE_INVALID`. The six values `outcome` may hold are `PENDING`, `CONNECTED_LIVE`, `ABANDONED`, `MACHINE_ANSWERED`, `NO_ANSWER` and `VOIDED`, of which four are recordable dispositions and each has its own admission rule and its own counter.

`PENDING → CONNECTED_LIVE` requires `agent_connect_seconds` to be present and no greater than the two-second abandonment threshold; a live-connect claim above two seconds is refused with `ERR_ABANDON_CLASSIFICATION_INVALID`. The supplied value is stored, `outcome_recorded_at` is stamped, the campaign's `live_answer_count_today` increases by one, both abandonment measures are recomputed, `rolling_compliance_rate_pct` is recomputed, and the dialer profile's `max_lines_last_computed` is refreshed.

`PENDING → ABANDONED` requires `abandon_message_played` to be true, because the recorded abandonment message is mandatory; an abandonment recorded without it is refused with `ERR_ABANDON_MESSAGE_REQUIRED`. It further requires `agent_connect_seconds` to be either absent or strictly greater than two seconds, since a call an agent picked up within two seconds was not abandoned; violating that is refused with `ERR_ABANDON_CLASSIFICATION_INVALID`, and the message check is evaluated before the classification check. `outcome_recorded_at` is stamped and `abandon_message_played` stored as true; the campaign's `live_answer_count_today` and `abandoned_count_today` each increase by one, and both abandonment measures and `rolling_compliance_rate_pct` are recomputed. This is the only disposition that can trigger the abandonment cascade.

`PENDING → MACHINE_ANSWERED` requires the campaign's dialer profile to have `amd_enabled` true, since a machine answer can only be asserted where answering-machine detection actually runs; otherwise the call is refused with `ERR_AMD_NOT_ENABLED`. `outcome_recorded_at` is stamped and the campaign's `machine_count_today` increases by one. `live_answer_count_today` is deliberately untouched, which is why a machine answer leaves `regulatory_abandonment_rate_pct` exactly where it was while moving `internal_abandonment_rate_pct`.

`PENDING → NO_ANSWER` carries no additional precondition beyond the attempt being `PENDING`. `outcome_recorded_at` is stamped and the campaign's `no_answer_count_today` increases by one; neither abandonment measure moves.

Before any of those counters is touched, the measurement day is rolled over if it has changed. When the UTC calendar date of the current clock instant differs from the campaign's `measurement_date`, the closing day's figures are appended to `daily_rate_history` as an entry carrying that `date`, its `live_answer_count`, its `abandoned_count` and its regulatory `rate_pct`; the history is kept ordered by `date` ascending. All four of today's counters then reset to 0 and `measurement_date` becomes the new UTC date — and only then is the current disposition counted.

The two abandonment measures are two variants of one computation over the same day's data. Only attempts of this campaign whose `voided_at` is null and whose disposition was recorded on `measurement_date` contribute; attempts still at `PENDING` contribute to neither measure. `regulatory_abandonment_rate_pct` is `0.00` when `live_answer_count_today` is zero, and otherwise 100 multiplied by `abandoned_count_today`, divided by `live_answer_count_today`, rounded half up to two decimal places — a denominator of live answers only, meaning connected-live and abandoned dispositions, with machine answers excluded entirely. This is the regulatory measure, and it is the only one ever compared against the profile's `abandonment_limit_pct`, therefore the only one that can suspend a campaign. `internal_abandonment_rate_pct` is `0.00` when `live_answer_count_today` plus `machine_count_today` is zero, and otherwise 100 multiplied by `abandoned_count_today`, divided by the sum of `live_answer_count_today` and `machine_count_today`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because its denominator additionally includes machine answers, for identical data it is always less than or equal to the regulatory measure, and it diverges from it the moment a single machine answer exists. It is an internal quality measure reported on every campaign read and used as a gate nowhere.

A breach is declared only when two conditions hold together, both evaluated after the current disposition has been applied: `live_answer_count_today` is at least 2 — the minimum measured volume below which no rate is meaningful — and `regulatory_abandonment_rate_pct` is *strictly* greater than the profile's `abandonment_limit_pct`. The comparison is strict, so a rate exactly equal to the configured limit does not breach. When it does breach, three hops follow in order. The campaign moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `ABANDONMENT_BREACH`, voiding its `PENDING` attempts with `void_reason` `CAMPAIGN_SUSPENDED` and crediting those consumers' attempt budgets, and its `version` increments. The dialer profile it points at then moves `APPROVED → LOCKED` with `locked_reason` `ABANDONMENT_BREACH` and its own `version` increments. And every *other* `ACTIVE` campaign pointing at that same profile then moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `PROFILE_LOCKED`, likewise voiding its `PENDING` attempts, crediting those consumers' budgets and incrementing its own `version`. The sibling campaigns are suspended because the profile was locked, not because they abandoned anything themselves.

The rolling compliance rate is a seven-day view of the regulatory measure, and its two-stage rounding matters. The window is the seven UTC calendar dates ending at the campaign's `measurement_date` inclusive — that date and the six before it. For each date in the window, the day's live answers and abandoned answers are taken from `daily_rate_history`, or from today's counters when the date is `measurement_date` itself. A date with at least one live answer contributes one sample: 100 multiplied by that date's abandoned count, divided by that date's live-answer count, rounded half up to two decimal places. A date with zero live answers is skipped entirely and contributes no sample at all — it does not contribute a `0.00`. When no date in the window contributes a sample, `rolling_compliance_rate_pct` is `0.00`; otherwise it is the sum of the contributed samples divided by the number of contributed samples, rounded half up to two decimal places. Each day is rounded first and the mean of those rounded values is rounded second; pooling the days' abandoned counts and live-answer counts and dividing once is a different number and is not what this rate means.

Voiding an attempt is a `ComplianceOfficer`'s compliance correction and is admissible from every one of the five recordable states, so that `PENDING → VOIDED`, `CONNECTED_LIVE → VOIDED`, `ABANDONED → VOIDED`, `MACHINE_ANSWERED → VOIDED` and `NO_ANSWER → VOIDED` are all available so long as `voided_at` is still null; voiding an already-voided attempt is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_STATE_INVALID`. The officer supplies a `void_reason` of `MISDIAL`, `CAMPAIGN_SUSPENDED` or `COMPLIANCE_CORRECTION`, while the cascade paths of §3.3, §3.5 and §3.7 supply `CAMPAIGN_SUSPENDED` themselves. In every case `voided_at` is stamped at the current clock instant, the reason is stored, the attempt's `outcome` becomes `VOIDED`, the consumer's `attempts_charged` decreases by one and `attempt_budget_remaining` increases by one with the contact's `version` incrementing, and the campaign's `permitted_attempt_count` decreases by one while `voided_attempt_count` increases by one. What differs between the five is which campaign counter is given back, and each gives back exactly what it had taken: voiding from `PENDING` reverses no disposition counter, but the campaign's counters and both abandonment measures for `measurement_date` are recomputed; voiding from `CONNECTED_LIVE` decreases `live_answer_count_today` by one and recomputes both measures; voiding from `ABANDONED` decreases both `live_answer_count_today` and `abandoned_count_today` by one and recomputes both measures; voiding from `MACHINE_ANSWERED` decreases `machine_count_today` by one, which moves the internal measure while leaving the regulatory measure exactly where it was; and voiding from `NO_ANSWER` decreases `no_answer_count_today` by one. A suspension that has already been applied is never reversed by a later void, however favourably the recomputed rate turns out. `VOIDED` is terminal.

Reading an attempt returns the consent basis and the policy it was authorised under, the window it was measured against and its outcome. Attempts may be listed and narrowed by `campaign_id`, by `contact_id`, by the `consent_record_id` relied on, by the `calling_window_policy_id` applied, and by `outcome`, with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.9. Complaints, Risk Tiering and Litigation Pattern Monitoring

A **Complaint** may be logged by a `ComplianceOfficer`, by a `DialerOperator` who took it on a call, or by the `Consumer` themselves. The intake names the `contact_id` of the complaining consumer and the `campaign_id` named in the complaint, which is null when the complaint is unattributed. `source` records where it arrived from — `DIRECT`, `STATE_AG`, `FTC` or `LITIGATION` — and `alleged_violation_type` records what the consumer alleges: `NO_CONSENT`, `DNC_VIOLATION`, `TIME_WINDOW`, `ABANDONMENT` or `RECORDING_CONSENT`. The consumer's own account goes verbatim into `narrative`, and `litigation_flag` records whether counsel has been engaged, defaulting to false. Intake stamps `received_at` at the current clock instant, sets `status` to `OPEN`, and returns a 201 with `adjudicated_risk_score`, `verified_attempt_count`, `exposure_cents`, `willful`, `finding_note` and `dismissal_note` all null.

Risk scoring is one weighted formula with two variants, and both variants share the same two weight tables. The source weight is 1.0 for `DIRECT`, 1.25 for `STATE_AG`, 1.5 for `FTC` and 2.0 for `LITIGATION`. The violation weight is 1.5 for `NO_CONSENT`, 1.25 for `DNC_VIOLATION`, 1.0 for `TIME_WINDOW`, 0.75 for `ABANDONMENT` and 1.1 for `RECORDING_CONSENT`. Both variants begin with the source weight multiplied by 10 source points plus the violation weight multiplied by 8 violation points.

The provisional variant runs at intake, when nothing has been investigated yet, and lands in `provisional_risk_score`. To those two weighted terms it adds an attempt contribution: the lesser of the consumer's `attempts_charged` and a cap of 5 attempts, multiplied by 2 provisional points per attempt. `litigation_flag` contributes nothing to this variant. The whole sum is then rounded half up to two decimal places.

The adjudicated variant runs at substantiation and lands in `adjudicated_risk_score`. Its attempt contribution is uncapped and worth more: `verified_attempt_count` multiplied by 4 adjudicated points per attempt, where `verified_attempt_count` is the number of contact attempts of the complained-about pairing — the complaint's `contact_id` together with its `campaign_id` — whose `voided_at` is null and whose `outcome` is anything other than `PENDING`. To that it adds a flat litigation bonus of 15 points when the complaint's `litigation_flag` is true, and nothing when it is false. The whole sum is rounded half up to two decimal places. Only this variant feeds the consumer's risk tier. A complaint that is dismissed keeps its provisional score and never receives an adjudicated one.

Substantiation is a `ComplianceOfficer`'s finding and moves the complaint `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED`. The officer supplies `willful`, the willfulness finding, and `finding_note`, the investigator's account; both are stored. `verified_attempt_count`, `adjudicated_risk_score` and `exposure_cents` are all computed. Statutory exposure is the plainest calculation in the domain: `exposure_cents` is `verified_attempt_count` multiplied by a statutory base of 50000 cents per attempt, multiplied by 3 when the supplied `willful` is true and by 1 when it is false. Every factor is an integer, so the result is an exact integer count of cents.

The consumer's `risk_tier` is then recomputed from scratch across every complaint of that consumer whose status is `SUBSTANTIATED`, taking the peak — the greatest — `adjudicated_risk_score` among them. Where there is no substantiated complaint at all the tier is `LOW`. Otherwise the thresholds are inclusive at each step: a peak of at least 55 makes the tier `CRITICAL`, otherwise a peak of at least 40 makes it `HIGH`, otherwise a peak of at least 20 makes it `MEDIUM`, and anything below 20 leaves it `LOW`. A peak of exactly 55.00 is `CRITICAL`; a peak of 54.99 is `HIGH`. Open and dismissed complaints are ignored by this computation entirely, so dismissing a complaint can never raise a tier. The contact's `version` increments.

Landing on `CRITICAL` is what turns a scoring exercise into an operational freeze. When the recomputed `risk_tier` is `CRITICAL`, the consumer's `internal_dnc_status` becomes `SUPPRESSED`, an `ACTIVE` internal-list suppression entry is created for their `phone_number` if none already exists, and every one of their `ACTIVE` consent records moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `CRITICAL risk tier`. A tier of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH` carries none of those consequences.

Dismissal is the parallel branch and moves the complaint `OPEN → DISMISSED` on a `dismissal_note`, which is stored. It produces none of the substantiation side effects: `adjudicated_risk_score`, `verified_attempt_count`, `exposure_cents` and `willful` all stay null, and the consumer's `risk_tier` is not recomputed at all. Substantiating or dismissing a complaint that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`; both `SUBSTANTIATED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal.

Litigation monitoring rests on `pattern_cluster_size`, which answers "how many live complaints of this kind, against this campaign, arrived within the same 30-day neighbourhood?". A complaint's cluster consists of the complaints sharing its `campaign_id` and its `alleged_violation_type`, whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, and whose `received_at` lies within 30 days of the anchor complaint's `received_at` on either side — the comparison is inclusive, so exactly 30 days apart is inside the cluster. `pattern_cluster_size` is the number of complaints in that cluster, counting the anchor itself when the anchor qualifies, and it is 1 for a lone qualifying complaint. Whenever a member joins or leaves — at intake, at substantiation and at dismissal — every member of the affected cluster has its own `pattern_cluster_size` rewritten, so the sizes across a cluster stay mutually consistent. Because a dismissed complaint leaves the cluster, dismissal shrinks every remaining member's count.

Reading a complaint returns both risk scores, its verified attempt count, its statutory exposure and its cluster size. Complaints may be listed and narrowed by `contact_id`, by `campaign_id`, by `status`, by `litigation_flag` and by `alleged_violation_type`, with `limit` and `offset`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — importing a contact, capturing a consent, logging a revocation, adding a suppression entry, queueing a scrub, creating a campaign, drafting a dialer profile or an agent script, publishing a calling-window policy, requesting an attempt, intaking a complaint, or seeding a reassigned-number record — return `201 Created`. Every operation that modifies an existing resource, including every state transition and every administrative capability, returns `200 OK`. All error responses carry the status code listed in §5.
