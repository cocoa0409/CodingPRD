# Hvac Filter Subscription Resident Benefits API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Air-filter neglect is the quietest and most expensive failure mode in single-family and multi-family rental management. A clogged filter costs the resident nothing to ignore and costs the property owner a compressor. The program described in this document industrialises the fix: at move-in the physical filter is measured, mapped to a catalog SKU, and a subscription ships the right filters to the resident's door on a cadence derived from the equipment and the household — while a bundle of resident benefits (credit reporting, renters insurance, a damage waiver and an air-quality guarantee) rides along on the same enrolment so the property manager can market the program as a resident amenity rather than a maintenance tax.

Three operational realities drive most of the complexity in the requirements that follow. First, the money is split: some properties are invoiced centrally for every subscription they sponsor, others push the charge to the resident, and the program extends a bounded amount of credit to each property manager, so every charge-posting operation has to be checked against that ceiling before it commits. Second, shipping a filter is not the same as installing a filter. The program therefore treats installation compliance as an evidence problem, scoring either HVAC blower telemetry or the resident's own renewal-survey answers, opening an investigable case when the evidence is suspicious, and forfeiting the insurance-like riders when non-installation is confirmed. Third, residents move — sometimes to another unit at the same property, sometimes across the portfolio — and a mid-lease address change has to remap the SKU, prorate two subscriptions against the same pivot date, and post exactly one net money movement rather than two legs that could drift apart.

This document is the complete specification for the backend service. It fixes the transport contract (envelope, pagination, identity headers, error catalogue), the arithmetic (every fee, credit, score and threshold), and the lifecycle rules (eleven state machines across nine mutable record types, three transitive cascades, and two compensating-action pairs whose conserved quantities must return to their exact pre-operation values). Where a rule looks like it could reasonably be implemented two ways, this document pins the one way that is correct.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the four recognised role names, or names a role that the target endpoint does not authorise, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The four roles are `ProgramOperator`, `PropertyManager`, `Resident` and `ClaimsAdjuster`, spelled exactly that way.

`ProgramOperator` is program staff. The role holds the full operational surface — catalog registration and retirement, the property lifecycle, telemetry ingestion, shipment fulfilment, non-installation investigation, claim disbursal and the logical clock — but is deliberately barred from triaging or adjudicating claims. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example there is no operator field on any record for this role) but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership narrowing applies to an operator's reads or writes.

`ClaimsAdjuster` is the independent adjudicator of the two insurance-like riders. When `X-User-Role` is `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does record it — as `triaged_by_user_id` on a `Claim` when that adjuster performs intake, and as `decided_by_user_id` when an adjuster records the decision — but it never compares either value to an entity's `id`. It does compare the two to each other: the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.10 forbids the individual who triaged a claim from deciding it.

`PropertyManager` is the operator of exactly one managed property. When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Property`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Property` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. In practice a unit resolves to its property through `property_id`, and a subscription (with every shipment, claim, entitlement, ledger row, survey, case and transfer hanging off it) resolves through the subscription's own `property_id`. A manager acting on a resource that resolves to a different property is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

`Resident` is the enrolled household member. When `X-User-Role` is `Resident`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Resident`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Resident` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — that is, the target must resolve to that resident through the subscription's `resident_id`. A resident reaching for another household's records is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Residents may answer renewal surveys, file, resubmit and withdraw their own claims, cancel their own subscription, and read their own records; they may not score evidence, confirm a case, adjudicate, or move money.

The ownership narrowing described for `PropertyManager` and `Resident` applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes either of those two roles, and it is evaluated after existence checks but before any business rule — see the ladder in §2.10.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and must be at least 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); the same error covers a filter query parameter whose value is not a recognised member of the enumeration it filters on.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned page.

Filter parameters are additive and case-sensitive against the enumeration values defined in this document. Property listing filters on `status`, `billing_mode` and `benefit_tier`. Unit listing under a property filters on `status`. Runtime-reading listing filters on `source`. Subscription listing filters on `resident_id`, `unit_id`, `property_id`, `assigned_sku_id`, `predecessor_subscription_id`, `status` and `installation_posture`. Shipment listing filters on `subscription_id`, `sku_id` and `status`. Entitlement listing under a subscription filters on `benefit_code` and on `status`, where the `status` parameter is matched against the entitlement's `state` value. Ledger listing under a subscription filters on `entry_type`. Claim listing filters on `subscription_id`, `claim_type` and `status`. Case listing under a subscription filters on `status` and `detection_method`. Transfer listing filters on `subscription_id`, `to_unit_id`, `remapped_sku_id`, `successor_subscription_id` and `status`. Renewal-survey listing under a subscription takes no filter beyond pagination.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Auditing Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC notation with exactly three fractional-second digits. Most record types additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical-clock instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. Three record types are append-only observations and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — for them both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the payload entirely: `RuntimeReading` (an ingested telemetry window), `RenewalSurvey` (a resident's answered survey) and `LedgerEntry` (a money row). Every other record type — properties, units, filter SKUs, residents, subscriptions, shipments, non-installation cases, claims, benefit entitlements and subscription transfers — carries all four common fields.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and before any operator advances it, every date computation, deadline, coverage window and staleness comparison reads that default value. The server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An operator-only test-driving capability repoints the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc`; it mutates no record. Every "logical clock instant" and "logical clock date" referred to elsewhere in this document means the current value of that clock, and the logical-clock date is the UTC calendar date of that instant.

Instant-valued input fields must be ISO 8601 UTC strings ending in `Z`, with an optional fractional-second part of one to six digits; `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` are both accepted and denote the same instant. Date-only input fields must match the `YYYY-MM-DD` form. A malformed value in either family, a missing required field, a wrong JSON type, an unrecognised enumeration member, or an integer outside its declared range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.5. Nested Reads and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

A handful of collections are reachable only as sub-resources of their owner — the units of a property, the telemetry windows of a unit, and a subscription's shipments, entitlements, ledger rows, claims, cases and renewal surveys. These nested paths exist purely as reverse lookups over a foreign key; they do not imply that the child is stored inside the parent. Every write, by contrast, targets a top-level collection and names its parents by id in the request body or in a single path segment.

When a path carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first unresolvable segment determines the response. Only after every path segment resolves are foreign keys in the request body resolved, in the order they are declared for that endpoint. Each missing referent has its own error: `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SKU_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. A `to_unit_id` naming no unit is an `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` like any other unit reference.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and Precision Discipline

All money in this system is expressed in whole cents as signed integers. There are no floating-point money fields and no currency codes. Where a formula divides, the division is carried in exact arithmetic and the result is rounded exactly where this document says it is rounded, using **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two cents rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude cent. Two disciplines matter more than the rounding mode itself and are restated at each formula: *how many* roundings a calculation performs, and *at which step* each one occurs. Rounding a sum is not the same as summing roundings, and this specification pins which of the two is correct every time the distinction arises.

Cadence arithmetic is integer-valued in days; where the cadence formula snaps a value to a grid it always snaps downward (never to nearest, never upward), and where it clamps a value it does so before the snap. Evidence scores are integers in the range 0 through 100 inclusive, produced by one rounding at the very end of an otherwise exact rational computation. Percentages that appear in tiered lookups — the waiver-relief tier and the non-installation haircut — are exact fractions applied inside a rounding call, never pre-rounded on their own.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Superseding Writes

Two write operations in this system legitimately discard something the client may not have observed, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the parent record's `version`.

The first is opening a mid-lease address change. The request must carry `expected_subscription_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Subscription`. If that value does not equal the subscription's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because creating a transfer supersedes any earlier transfer on the same subscription that is still in `REQUESTED` or `SKU_REMAPPED`; a create issued from a stale view would silently discard an address change the client never saw, and the version check makes that discard explicit instead.

The second is recording a measured filter size on a unit. The request must carry `expected_unit_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Unit`. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a capture overwrites the unit's prior `actual_measured_size_code` and re-runs filter mapping and pricing for every live subscription on the unit; written from a stale view it would silently replace a measurement the client never observed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. An expected-version field supplied to any endpoint other than these two is not part of that endpoint's contract and carries no meaning.

### 2.8. The Two Independent Dimensions of a Subscription

A subscription record carries two state fields that evolve independently of one another on the same row. The commercial dimension, `status`, begins at `PENDING_SETUP` and moves through `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `TRANSFER_PENDING` to one of the two terminal values `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED`. The compliance dimension, `installation_posture`, begins at `UNVERIFIED` and moves among `COMPLIANT`, `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED`, `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED` and `REMEDIATED`, and has no terminal value at all.

Neither dimension constrains the other's transition set. A subscription may legitimately be `ACTIVE` and `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED` at the same time, or `SUSPENDED` and `COMPLIANT`. Compliance never gates a commercial transition: a confirmed non-installation does not suspend or cancel billing. What it does gate is claim settlement arithmetic and rider entitlement, described in §3.5 through §3.7.

Both dimensions contain cycles, and the cycles are load-bearing. On the commercial side, `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` closes when a suspended property is reactivated, and `ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE` closes when a requested transfer is rejected. On the compliance side, `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED → NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED → REMEDIATED → COMPLIANT` is the full remediation loop, and `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED → COMPLIANT` is the short-circuit taken when a suspicion is cleared without escalation. A claim adds a third cycle of its own, `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, bounded as described in §3.7.

Because both dimensions live on one row, any transition on either of them increments the same `version` counter and refreshes the same `updated_at`. A request that names a transition the current value of the relevant dimension does not permit is rejected with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Compensating Actions

Three operations in this system are the head of a transitive cascade: each link is caused by the previous link rather than by the original request, and every link must land.

**Cascade A — confirmed non-installation, five links deep.** Confirming an investigable case escalates the case to `ESCALATED_TO_PM`; that escalation drives the subscription's `installation_posture` from `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED` to `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED`; that confirmation forfeits the subscription's damage-waiver and air-quality-guarantee entitlements; each forfeiture posts a credit ledger row; and each ledger row re-derives the owning property's outstanding balance.

**Cascade B — property suspension, three links deep.** Suspending a property drives every `ACTIVE` subscription at that property to `SUSPENDED`; each of those suspensions cancels that subscription's still-`SCHEDULED` shipments and suspends its renters-insurance and air-quality-guarantee entitlements.

**Cascade C — transfer completion, two links deep with a fork.** Completing a transfer mints a successor subscription on the destination unit and, as a second consequence of the same completion, cancels the outgoing subscription — which itself cancels that subscription's scheduled shipments and expires its entitlements.

Two operation pairs are **compensating**, meaning the second operation must restore the exact observable value the first one disturbed. Reactivating a suspended property must leave each affected subscription's count of `ACTIVE` entitlements, and the exact set of `benefit_code` values among them, identical to their pre-suspend values; this works because the suspend cascade marks exactly the rows it suspended with `suspended_by_property_event` set to true and the reactivate cascade restores exactly those rows, clearing the flag back to false. Rows that were already `FORFEITED` before the suspension are terminal and must not reappear among the `ACTIVE` rows — that reappearance is precisely the drift this invariant exists to detect. Likewise, reversing a claim payment must return the owning property's outstanding balance to its exact pre-payout value, because the payout row and the reversal row sum to exactly zero.

At every observable instant, the total number of entitlement rows on a subscription equals the sum of the rows in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `FORFEITED` and `EXPIRED`. Rows are never deleted when a benefit leaves the entitled set; they are transitioned to a terminal value instead.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

Validation is a strictly ordered ladder. Rules are evaluated in the order below, and **the first rule that fails determines the response**; no rule below it is evaluated. This ordering is observable and is part of the contract — a request that violates several rules at once must surface the earliest violation.

1. Both identity headers present, and `X-User-Role` one of the four role names — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every resource the request references exists, path segments resolved left to right first and body foreign keys afterwards in declaration order — otherwise the matching 404 from §2.5. Applies to every endpoint.
4. Ownership scope for the two entity-backed roles, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `PropertyManager` or `Resident`.
5. Request body shape: required fields present, enumeration members recognised, integers inside their declared ranges, date-only and instant strings well formed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
6. List query parameters valid, as described in §2.3 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
7. The optimistic-concurrency check of §2.7 — `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (412). Applies to exactly two endpoints: opening a subscription transfer and recording a filter-size capture.
8. The property reached from the target resource is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to exactly three operations: enrolling a subscription, activating a subscription, and completing a transfer. Adding a unit to a property is deliberately **not** covered, because a property must already hold at least one unit before it can be activated at all.
9. The subscription's `status` permits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to seven operations: activating a subscription, cancelling a subscription, scheduling a shipment, recording a renewal survey, running a non-installation evaluation, opening a transfer, and filing a claim.
10. The shipment's `status` permits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to all five shipment actions: ship, deliver, request-return, return and write-off, including any attempt from a terminal status.
11. The claim's `status` permits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to all seven claim actions: triage, request-info, resubmit, decide, pay, reverse-payment and withdraw.
12. The case's `status` permits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to all four case actions: send-notice, confirm, remediate and close-unsubstantiated.
13. The transfer's `status` permits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to all three transfer actions: remap-sku, complete and reject.
14. Segregation of duties: the acting adjuster's `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `triaged_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422). Applies to the decide action only.
15. The case's stored `evidence_score` is greater than or equal to the case's stored `confirm_threshold` — otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_BELOW_CONFIRM_THRESHOLD` (422). Applies to the confirm action only.
16. The requested detection method has evidence: at least two telemetry readings inside the evaluation window for runtime analytics, at least one survey for the renewal survey method — otherwise `ERR_NO_EVIDENCE_FOR_METHOD` (422). Applies to the non-installation evaluation only.
17. The entitlement whose `benefit_code` equals the claim's `claim_type` exists on the claim's subscription and is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_BENEFIT_NOT_ENTITLED` (422). Applies to two operations: filing a claim, and an approving decision, where it is re-evaluated against the state at the decision instant.
18. The claim's `incident_date` falls inside the coverage window — otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_COVERAGE_WINDOW` (422). Applies to filing a claim only.
19. The subscription's assigned filter SKU is still `ACTIVE` in the catalog — otherwise `ERR_SKU_DISCONTINUED` (422). Applies to two operations: activating a subscription and scheduling a shipment.
20. Filter-SKU selection yields at least one candidate for the target unit — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SKU` (422). Applies to three operations: enrolling a subscription, recording a filter-size capture, and remapping a transfer's SKU.
21. No other subscription pairs the same unit with the same resident while live — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SUBSCRIPTION` (409). Applies to enrolling a subscription only.
22. The requested lease interval clears every live occupancy on the unit by more than the turnover buffer — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to two operations: enrolling a subscription and opening a transfer.
23. The requested `scheduled_ship_date` falls outside the previous cadence window — otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to scheduling a shipment only.
24. The claim's `info_request_round` is still below the round cap — otherwise `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT` (422). Applies to the request-info action only.
25. The transfer's `effective_date` sits inside the permitted range — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_DATE_INVALID` (422). Applies to opening a transfer only.
26. The transfer destination is a different unit and is `AVAILABLE` — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID` (422). Applies to two operations: opening a transfer and remapping a transfer's SKU.
27. The cancellation `effective_date` sits inside the permitted range — otherwise `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_INVALID` (422). Applies to cancelling a subscription only, and is skipped entirely when the subscription is `PENDING_SETUP`, because that case voids rather than settles.
28. The telemetry window is non-degenerate — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RUNTIME_WINDOW` (422). Applies to ingesting a runtime reading only.
29. The claim's `requested_amount_cents` does not exceed the hard cap — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP` (422). Applies to filing a claim only.
30. The property's credit ceiling absorbs the charge this request would post — otherwise `ERR_PROPERTY_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402). Applies to two operations: activating a subscription and completing a transfer.

The exact substantive condition behind each of rules 8 through 30 is stated in the workflow subsection of §3 that the rule guards; this ladder fixes only the order and the code.

### 2.11. The Property Ledger and the Credit Ceiling

Every money movement in the program is an append-only ledger row hanging off a subscription. Ledger rows are never updated and never deleted; a mistake is corrected by posting a compensating row. Each row carries a signed `amount_cents` in which a **positive** value is a charge that raises what the billed party owes the program and a **negative** value is a credit that lowers it, an `entry_type` naming what produced it, a `billed_party` snapshotting the subscription's `billing_party` as it stood at insert time, a `period_start` and `period_end` bracketing the billing period the row covers (both equal to the row's own date for one-off rows), a `memo` carrying a short human-readable label, and a `source_ref_id`.

`source_ref_id` is polymorphic: the record type it points at is determined by `entry_type`. An `UNRETURNED_SHIPMENT_FEE` row posted by a shipment write-off points at that `Shipment`'s id. `CLAIM_PAYOUT` and `CLAIM_PAYOUT_REVERSAL` rows point at the `Claim`'s id. A `WAIVER_REVERSAL` row points at the forfeited `BenefitEntitlement`'s id. A `TRANSFER_ADJUSTMENT` row points at the `SubscriptionTransfer`'s id. `ENROLLMENT_FEE`, `MONTHLY_FEE` and `PRORATION_CREDIT` rows point at the `Subscription`'s id.

A property's `outstanding_balance_cents` is a derived field, re-derived on every single ledger insert. It is the sum of `amount_cents` across every ledger row whose `billed_party` is `PROPERTY_MANAGER` and whose subscription's `property_id` names that property. There is no time window: the ledger is append-only and nothing is ever removed, so every row ever inserted contributes. Rows whose `billed_party` is `RESIDENT` never enter a property's balance. Because integer addition is order-independent the balance needs no tie-break, but for reproducible listings ledger rows are returned ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `period_start` ascending.

The derived balance is what the credit ceiling is checked against. Each property declares a `credit_limit_cents`, the maximum outstanding balance the program will extend to it, and two operations must prove the ceiling absorbs the charge they intend to post before they commit anything: subscription activation and transfer completion. The check applies to property-manager-billed subscriptions at a property whose `billing_mode` is `PM_BILLED`: the property's current `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the charge the request would post must be less than or equal to `credit_limit_cents`, and a request that would push it past the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) with no partial commitment.

A property also exposes `unit_count`, the number of units at that property whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `OCCUPIED`. Units that have gone `OUT_OF_SERVICE` do not count.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Filter Catalog and Its Retirement

Nothing can be shipped until the catalog exists. A `ProgramOperator` registers each purchasable filter as a catalog entry carrying a `sku_code` of one to thirty-two characters that must be globally unique across the catalog, a `size_code` written as three dimension groups of one or two digits each separated by the letter x (for example `20x25x1`), a `thickness_inches` value that must be exactly one of 1, 2, 4 or 5, a `merv_rating` of at least 6 and at most 16, a `unit_cost_cents` greater than zero representing what the program pays for one filter, a `ship_weight_oz` greater than zero giving the per-filter shipping weight in ounces, and a `pack_quantity` of at least 1 and at most 6 describing how many filters come in one sealed pack. Because packs are sealed, a shipment can only ever contain a whole multiple of the pack quantity. A newly registered SKU is `ACTIVE` with `version` 1.

Retiring a SKU moves it `ACTIVE → DISCONTINUED` and is the only transition the catalog machine has; `DISCONTINUED` is terminal. Retirement deliberately does **not** re-point the subscriptions already mapped to that SKU — their `assigned_sku_id` keeps naming the retired entry. The stale link is surfaced later, at the moment it would matter: an attempt to activate such a subscription, or to schedule another shipment on it, is rejected with `ERR_SKU_DISCONTINUED`. From the retirement onward the SKU is also excluded from every future selection candidate set, so re-measuring the unit or transferring the subscription will map it onto a live SKU instead.

### 3.2. Properties, Units and the Move-In Measurement

A property enters the program in `ONBOARDING`. Enrolling one records a `name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `billing_mode` of either `PM_BILLED` or `RESIDENT_PAID` declaring who the program invoices by default, a `benefit_tier` of `BASIC`, `PLUS` or `PREMIUM` naming the resident-benefit package the property bought, the two rider flags `damage_waiver_enabled` and `aqg_enabled`, and a `credit_limit_cents` of zero or more. The new record starts with `outstanding_balance_cents` at 0, `unit_count` at 0, `suspend_reason` null and `version` 1.

Rental units are added under the property by a `ProgramOperator` or by the property's own `PropertyManager`. Each unit records a `unit_label` of one to twenty-four characters that must be unique among that property's units, an `hvac_system_type` of `SINGLE_STAGE`, `TWO_STAGE`, `VARIABLE_SPEED` or `HEAT_PUMP`, a `filter_slot_count` of at least 1 and at most 6 giving the number of slots that must be re-filtered on every shipment, a `return_grille_count` of at least 1 and at most 6 that is documentation for the field crew and never an input to any fee, and a `nominal_size_code` in the same three-group dimension form as the catalog, read off the filter already sitting in the rack. A new unit is `AVAILABLE`, its `actual_measured_size_code` is null, its `size_capture_count` is 0 and its `version` is 1; the owning property's `unit_count` rises by one.

Only once a property holds at least one unit may it be activated: the `ONBOARDING → ACTIVE` transition requires the property to be `ONBOARDING` and its `unit_count` to be at least 1, and it clears `suspend_reason` back to null and bumps the property's `version`. This is why the property-active guard of §2.10 rule 8 deliberately does not cover unit creation — requiring an active property to add a unit would make activation unreachable.

Suspending an active property records the `suspend_reason` text supplied with the request and is the head of cascade B: every subscription at that property that is `ACTIVE` goes `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, and each of those suspensions in turn cancels that subscription's `SCHEDULED` shipments and suspends the subset of its benefit entitlements described in §3.6. Reactivation is the compensating action: the property returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspend_reason` set back to null, every subscription at the property that is `SUSPENDED` returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, and each such subscription restores exactly the entitlement rows carrying `suspended_by_property_event` true. Reactivation resurrects nothing else — shipments cancelled by the suspension stay `CANCELLED`, and entitlements that were already `FORFEITED` stay forfeited.

Offboarding ends the relationship. It runs only from `SUSPENDED`, and only when no subscription at the property is in `PENDING_SETUP`, `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFER_PENDING`; a subscription that is merely `SUSPENDED` does not block it. On success the property becomes `OFFBOARDED` — terminal — and every unit at the property whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `OCCUPIED` is driven to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, which is likewise terminal for a unit.

Unit occupancy is bookkeeping the system maintains for itself rather than something a caller sets. A unit moves `AVAILABLE → OCCUPIED` when a subscription referencing it becomes `ACTIVE`, and returns `OCCUPIED → AVAILABLE` when the last live subscription on it reaches `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` — precisely, when no subscription with that `unit_id` remains in `PENDING_SETUP`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TRANSFER_PENDING`. Every one of these transitions increments the unit's `version`.

The move-in measurement is the one write on a unit that is concurrency-guarded. A crew member measures the rack physically and submits the measured `actual_measured_size_code` together with the `expected_unit_version` described in §2.7. On acceptance the unit's `actual_measured_size_code` is overwritten, `size_capture_count` rises by one, and the unit's `version` increments. The capture then re-derives, for every subscription on that unit whose `status` is `PENDING_SETUP`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, the whole pricing chain of §3.3 in order: the mapped SKU, then the cadence, then the filters per shipment, then the monthly fee. If no catalog entry can satisfy the newly measured size for one of those subscriptions, the whole capture is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SKU` and nothing is written.

Telemetry arrives separately. A `ProgramOperator` ingests one HVAC window at a time against a unit, supplying `window_start` as the inclusive left edge, `window_end` as the exclusive right edge, `runtime_minutes` of blower operation observed in that window (zero or more), a `static_pressure_delta_pa` greater than zero giving the pressure drop across the filter rack in pascals, and a `source` of `SMART_THERMOSTAT`, `PRESSURE_SENSOR` or `GATEWAY_BACKFILL`. Rising pressure across successive windows is the physical signature of a filter that was never changed. A window whose `window_end` is not strictly after its `window_start`, or whose `static_pressure_delta_pa` is not greater than zero, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RUNTIME_WINDOW`. Accepted readings are immutable observations: they carry no `version`, they transition nothing, and they are never modified.

Residents are registered independently of any unit, with a `full_name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters, an `email` that must contain the `@` character and must be globally unique across residents, a `household_size` of at least 1 and at most 12, and the three household flags `has_pets` (shedding pets in the home), `smoker` (a smoker in the household) and `allergy_flag` (the household needs elevated filtration). A new resident is a `PROSPECT`. They become `RESIDING` the moment a subscription referencing them becomes `ACTIVE`, and they leave as `MOVED_OUT` — terminal — when a subscription referencing them is cancelled, but only under the condition stated in §3.9. Each of those two transitions increments the resident's `version`.

### 3.3. Enrolment: Mapping the SKU, Setting the Cadence, Pricing the Subscription

Enrolment is where almost all of the program's arithmetic happens. A `ProgramOperator` or the property's `PropertyManager` enrols a household on a unit by supplying the `resident_id` of the enrolled resident, the `unit_id` of the unit being filtered, a `billing_party` of either `PROPERTY_MANAGER` or `RESIDENT` naming who is invoiced for this particular subscription's fees, an `enrollment_channel` of `MOVE_IN_PACKET`, `RESIDENT_PORTAL` or `PM_BULK_UPLOAD` recording how the enrolment was captured, and the lease window as `lease_start_date` and `lease_end_date`, both date-only, with the end strictly later than the start. The subscription's `property_id` is not supplied — it is denormalised from the unit's own `property_id` — and the owning property must be `ACTIVE` at that moment or the enrolment is rejected under §2.10 rule 8.

Two occupancy guards run before any arithmetic. First, no other subscription may pair the same `unit_id` with the same `resident_id` while still in `PENDING_SETUP`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TRANSFER_PENDING`; if one does, the enrolment is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SUBSCRIPTION`. Second, the requested lease interval must not collide with any live occupancy of the same unit, and the collision test carries a mandatory three-day turnover buffer. Treat both the requested interval and each existing interval as closed at both ends — the lease occupies its start date and its end date. For every existing subscription on that unit whose status is `PENDING_SETUP`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TRANSFER_PENDING`, the requested interval is legal only if either the requested `lease_start_date` falls more than three days after that subscription's `lease_end_date`, or the requested `lease_end_date` falls more than three days before that subscription's `lease_start_date`. A gap of exactly three days still conflicts: the earliest legal start against an existing lease is the fourth day after that lease's `lease_end_date`. A collision is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`.

**Selecting the filter.** The program maps the unit onto exactly one catalog entry, and the selection is a pure function of the unit, the resident and the catalog. The size it searches for is the unit's `actual_measured_size_code` whenever a move-in measurement has been captured, and the `nominal_size_code` read off the old filter only while no measurement exists. The filtration floor it demands is a `merv_rating` of at least 11 when the resident's `allergy_flag` is set, and at least 8 otherwise. From those two inputs the candidate set is every catalog entry whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `size_code` matches the searched size exactly, whose `merv_rating` is greater than or equal to that floor, and whose `pack_quantity` is less than or equal to the unit's `filter_slot_count` plus a slack allowance of two packs. Candidates are then ranked cheapest-adequate-filtration-first: the lowest `merv_rating` wins, ties among equal ratings go to the lowest `unit_cost_cents`, and any remaining tie goes to the entry with the earliest `created_at`. Creation time is the final tie-break — a candidate's `id` is never consulted. The winner becomes the subscription's `assigned_sku_id`. When the candidate set comes out empty the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SKU` and nothing is created.

**Setting the shipping cadence.** The subscription's `ship_cadence_days` is derived in a fixed order from the equipment, the chosen filter's thickness and the household's load. Start from the base interval implied by the unit's `hvac_system_type`: ninety days for `SINGLE_STAGE`, seventy-five for `TWO_STAGE`, and sixty for both `VARIABLE_SPEED` and `HEAT_PUMP`. Add a thickness bonus taken from the selected SKU: thirty days when its `thickness_inches` is 4 or more, otherwise fifteen days when its `thickness_inches` is 2 or more, otherwise nothing. Then count household load points, which accumulate rather than replace one another: one point when `household_size` is at least 4, one further point when `household_size` is at least 6 (so a household of six or more earns two points from size alone), two points when `has_pets` is true, one point when `smoker` is true, and one point when `allergy_flag` is true. Each load point shortens the interval by ten days, so subtract ten days for every point from the base-plus-bonus figure. Clamp the result next, never below thirty days and never above one hundred and twenty days. Finally snap the clamped value **down** onto the fifteen-day logistics grid — take the largest multiple of fifteen that does not exceed it, discarding any remainder, never rounding to nearest and never rounding up — and re-apply the thirty-day floor to that snapped value. The order matters: clamp first, snap second, floor again third.

**Sizing each shipment.** Because packs are sealed, `filters_per_shipment` is the unit's `filter_slot_count` rounded up to a whole number of packs and then multiplied back into filters. Divide the slot count by the selected SKU's `pack_quantity`, round that quotient **up** to the next whole pack whenever it is not already whole, and multiply the whole pack count by `pack_quantity` again. A three-slot unit on a two-per-pack SKU therefore ships four filters, not three.

**Deciding the benefit bundle.** The subscription's `entitled_benefit_codes` is a set assembled from the property's purchase and then narrowed. Begin with the tier the property bought: `BASIC` grants `CREDIT_REPORTING` alone; `PLUS` grants `CREDIT_REPORTING` and `RENTERS_INSURANCE`; `PREMIUM` grants `CREDIT_REPORTING`, `RENTERS_INSURANCE` and `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE`. Add `DAMAGE_WAIVER` when the property's `damage_waiver_enabled` flag is set, and add `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE` when its `aqg_enabled` flag is set — adding a code already present changes nothing. Then apply the payer interaction: remove `RENTERS_INSURANCE` when this subscription's `billing_party` is `RESIDENT` while the property's `billing_mode` is `PM_BILLED`, because the insurance rider only rides along when the payer matches the property's default billing mode. Finally, and only when this set is being recomputed for a subscription whose `installation_posture` has become `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED`, remove both `DAMAGE_WAIVER` and `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE`. The surviving codes are stored sorted in ascending lexicographic order, and one entitlement row is created per surviving code in `PENDING` — the rows mirror the set, and a row whose code later leaves the set is never deleted but transitioned to a terminal value instead.

**Pricing the subscription.** The `monthly_fee_cents` figure is built from three annual components that are each divided by twelve and rounded independently, and only then added. Let the shipment size be the subscription's `filters_per_shipment`. The number of shipments per year is three hundred and sixty-five divided by the subscription's `ship_cadence_days`, kept as an exact fraction throughout and never rounded to a whole number of shipments. The per-shipment weight is the selected SKU's `ship_weight_oz` multiplied by the shipment size; the weight surcharge is the amount by which that per-shipment weight exceeds a free allowance of thirty-two ounces — zero when it does not exceed it — multiplied by six cents per excess ounce. The **annual filter component** is the SKU's `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by the shipment size, multiplied by shipments per year, and then multiplied by one plus the thirty-five percent program markup. The **annual logistics component** is a handling charge of four hundred and ninety-five cents plus the weight surcharge, multiplied by shipments per year. The **annual benefit component** is the sum, over the codes currently in `entitled_benefit_codes`, of that benefit's annual price in cents: three thousand six hundred for `CREDIT_REPORTING`, fifteen thousand six hundred for `RENTERS_INSURANCE`, seven thousand two hundred for `DAMAGE_WAIVER` and four thousand eight hundred for `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE`. Now divide each of the three annual components by twelve and round each quotient half up to whole cents **separately** — three independent roundings — and add the three rounded monthly figures with plain integer addition. Summing the three annual components first and rounding once at the end is a different number for most inputs and is not what this system computes.

The whole chain is re-derived, in the order SKU selection, then cadence, then filters per shipment, then benefit set, then monthly fee, whenever any of its inputs changes for a live subscription: on a move-in measurement, on a transfer's SKU remap and on the successor subscription a completed transfer mints, and the monthly fee alone is re-derived whenever the entitled benefit set changes after a confirmed non-installation.

A freshly enrolled subscription is `PENDING_SETUP` on the commercial dimension and `UNVERIFIED` on the compliance dimension, with `version` 1. Its `predecessor_subscription_id` is null unless a transfer minted it, its `next_ship_date` is null because nothing is scheduled yet, its `shipment_sequence_counter` is 0, its `activated_at`, `cancel_effective_date`, `cancel_reason`, `latest_detection_method` and `latest_evidence_score` are all null, and the five frozen settlement figures `gross_prorated_credit_cents`, `waiver_credit_pct`, `unreturned_fee_gross_cents`, `unreturned_fee_net_cents` and `net_settlement_cents` all start at 0. Enrolment does **not** move the resident out of `PROSPECT`: that happens only at activation.

### 3.4. Activation and the Start of Billing

Activation is the commitment point, and it is the most heavily guarded operation in the system. A `ProgramOperator` or the property's `PropertyManager` activates a subscription with an empty body — no date is accepted, because the activation instant is read from the logical clock. The transition `PENDING_SETUP → ACTIVE` requires four things: the subscription is `PENDING_SETUP`; the property reached through `property_id` is `ACTIVE`; the catalog entry named by `assigned_sku_id` is still `ACTIVE`, since a retired SKU surfaces here as `ERR_SKU_DISCONTINUED`; and, when this subscription's `billing_party` is `PROPERTY_MANAGER`, the property's credit ceiling absorbs the two charges activation will post — that is, the property's current `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the twenty-five-hundred-cent enrolment fee plus the first month's fee must be less than or equal to its `credit_limit_cents`, and a shortfall is `ERR_PROPERTY_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The first month is prorated from the activation day forward, and the activation day itself is billable. Take the logical-clock date as the activation date, count the number of days in the calendar month containing it, and count the billable days as that month length minus the activation day-of-month plus one — activating on the eighteenth of a thirty-one-day month gives fourteen billable days. The first month's fee is the subscription's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by billable days, divided by the month length, and rounded half up to whole cents in a single rounding at the end.

Everything activation touches, it touches in one atomic step. The subscription's `status` becomes `ACTIVE` and its `activated_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant. The resident referenced by `resident_id` moves `PROSPECT → RESIDING`. The unit referenced by `unit_id` moves `AVAILABLE → OCCUPIED`. Every benefit entitlement on the subscription that is `PENDING` moves `PENDING → ACTIVE` with its `effective_from` stamped at the same logical-clock instant. The first shipment is created for the subscription in `SCHEDULED` with `sequence_number` 1, a `scheduled_ship_date` equal to the logical-clock date, a `quantity` equal to the subscription's `filters_per_shipment` and a `sku_id` snapshotting `assigned_sku_id`, and the subscription's `shipment_sequence_counter` rises to 1. Two ledger rows are posted against the subscription, both charges: an `ENROLLMENT_FEE` row of positive two thousand five hundred cents, and a `MONTHLY_FEE` row of positive the first month's prorated fee, each with `source_ref_id` naming the subscription itself. The property's `outstanding_balance_cents` is then re-derived, and the subscription's `version` increments.

The subscription's `next_ship_date` is a derived convenience field maintained on every event that adds, cancels or advances a shipment. It is the `scheduled_ship_date` of the shipment with the greatest `scheduled_ship_date` among that subscription's shipments whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `SHIPPED`, and it is null when no such shipment exists. After activation it therefore equals the first shipment's date; after a property suspension cancels that shipment it falls back to null.

### 3.5. Fulfilment: Scheduling, Shipping, Delivery and the Return Fork

Subsequent shipments are scheduled by a `ProgramOperator` against a live subscription. The request carries the target `scheduled_ship_date` and may optionally carry a `quantity` and a `tracking_code`. When `quantity` is omitted it defaults to the subscription's `filters_per_shipment`; when it is supplied it must equal `filters_per_shipment` exactly, and any other value fails shape validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The new shipment is `SCHEDULED` with `version` 1, a `sequence_number` one greater than the highest `sequence_number` already used on that subscription — equivalently, the subscription's `shipment_sequence_counter` is advanced by one and its new value is stamped onto the shipment — and a `sku_id` copied from the subscription's current `assigned_sku_id` — that copy is a snapshot and is never re-pointed afterwards even if the subscription is later remapped onto a different catalog entry. Scheduling also re-checks that the assigned SKU is still `ACTIVE`, rejecting with `ERR_SKU_DISCONTINUED` otherwise.

Consecutive shipments may not crowd each other. Each shipment occupies a cadence window that begins on its `scheduled_ship_date` and runs for the subscription's `ship_cadence_days`, and the window is **half-open with no buffer**: it includes its first day and excludes the day exactly `ship_cadence_days` later. To test a new date, find the subscription's latest existing shipment — the one with the greatest `scheduled_ship_date` among all shipments whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_REQUESTED`, `RETURNED` or `UNRETURNED_WRITTEN_OFF`, breaking ties by the greatest `created_at` — and reject the request with `ERR_SHIPMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when the requested date is on or after that shipment's `scheduled_ship_date` and strictly before that date advanced by the cadence. Adjacency is explicitly legal: a date exactly `ship_cadence_days` after the previous shipment's date is accepted, leaving a zero-day gap between consecutive windows.

Dispatch moves a shipment `SCHEDULED → SHIPPED`. It requires the shipment to be `SCHEDULED` **and** the owning subscription to be `ACTIVE`, stores the `tracking_code` the carrier assigned, and stamps `shipped_at` with the logical-clock instant. Delivery confirmation moves it `SHIPPED → DELIVERED` and records the carrier-reported `delivered_at`, which must not precede the shipment's `shipped_at`; an earlier instant fails shape validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every shipment mutation increments that shipment's `version`.

Delivery is where the machine forks. Opening a return moves the shipment `DELIVERED → RETURN_REQUESTED` and records the `return_reason` supplied with the request, and from `RETURN_REQUESTED` exactly two arms lead out, with deliberately divergent consequences. On the **first arm**, the filters came back: the shipment moves `RETURN_REQUESTED → RETURNED`, records the `returned_at` instant the returned filters were scanned back in — which must not precede `delivered_at` — leaves `unreturned_fee_cents` at 0, and posts **no** ledger row at all. On the **second arm**, the filters never came back: the shipment moves `RETURN_REQUESTED → UNRETURNED_WRITTEN_OFF`, and its `unreturned_fee_cents` is set to the lesser of two figures — the `unit_cost_cents` of the catalog entry named by the shipment's own `sku_id` snapshot multiplied by the shipment's `quantity`, and a per-shipment cap of two thousand five hundred cents. Note that the fee reads the shipment's snapshotted `sku_id`, not the subscription's current `assigned_sku_id`. That fee is then posted as an `UNRETURNED_SHIPMENT_FEE` ledger row of positive that amount with `source_ref_id` naming the shipment, and the property's `outstanding_balance_cents` is re-derived. `RETURNED`, `UNRETURNED_WRITTEN_OFF` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal for a shipment; any action attempted from a terminal status is rejected with `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Three shipment transitions are driven by the subscription rather than by a fulfilment operator. When the owning subscription enters `SUSPENDED`, `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED`, every shipment of that subscription still in `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. When the owning subscription enters `CANCELLED`, every shipment in flight is recalled: a `SHIPPED` shipment moves `SHIPPED → RETURN_REQUESTED` and a `DELIVERED` shipment moves `DELIVERED → RETURN_REQUESTED`. Each of these system transitions increments the affected shipment's `version` like any other mutation.

### 3.6. Installation Compliance: Evidence, Cases, Escalation and the Benefit Bundle

Shipping a filter is not installing a filter, and the program treats the difference as an evidence problem with two independent sources.

The first source is the resident's own word. A `Resident` — or a `ProgramOperator` acting on their behalf — answers a renewal survey against a live subscription, reporting `installed_last_shipment` as a plain yes-or-no on whether the most recently delivered filters actually went into the rack, a `filters_on_hand_count` of at least 0 and at most 24 giving how many uninstalled filters are still sitting in the closet, and optionally a `self_reported_change_date` naming the resident's last remembered filter change; that date may be omitted or null. An accepted survey is stamped with `submitted_at` from the logical clock and is thereafter immutable: it carries no `version`, and it transitions nothing on its own.

The second source is the telemetry described in §3.2, where a filter that was never changed shows up as a steadily rising pressure drop.

**Scoring an evaluation.** A `ProgramOperator` runs an evaluation against a subscription by naming a `detection_method` of either `RUNTIME_ANALYTICS` or `RENEWAL_SURVEY` and an `evaluated_through_date` bounding the evidence period under review. The method chosen selects one of two genuinely different scoring variants: they read different evidence bearing on the same physical question, they produce different integer scores, and they carry different confirmation thresholds. In both variants the result is an `evidence_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive, where a higher score means stronger evidence that the filters were never installed.

Under `RUNTIME_ANALYTICS`, the evidence set is the telemetry of the subscription's own unit taken after the last delivery. Identify the subscription's most recently delivered shipment — the one with the greatest `delivered_at` among shipments whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_REQUESTED`, `RETURNED` or `UNRETURNED_WRITTEN_OFF`, breaking ties by the greatest `created_at` — and collect every reading on that unit whose `window_start` is at or after that shipment's `delivered_at`, ordered by `window_start` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. At least two readings are required; with fewer the evaluation is rejected with `ERR_NO_EVIDENCE_FOR_METHOD`. From the first and last readings of that ordered list, compute the observation span as the exact number of days — fractional days allowed — from the first reading's `window_start` to the last reading's `window_end`. Sum `runtime_minutes` across every reading in the set. The pressure drift is the last reading's `static_pressure_delta_pa` minus the first reading's, divided by the first reading's value. The **drift term** is that drift fraction divided by a saturation point of twenty-five percent, then clamped into the range zero through one. The **runtime term** is the summed runtime minutes divided by an expected eight hours — four hundred and eighty minutes — of blower operation per day of the observation span, capped at one. Weight the drift term by six tenths and the runtime term by four tenths, add them, clamp the sum into the range zero through one, multiply by one hundred, and round half up once to a whole number. Under this method a score of 40 or more is suspicious and a score of 60 or more can be confirmed.

Under `RENEWAL_SURVEY`, the evidence set is exactly one row: the subscription's renewal survey with the greatest `submitted_at`, breaking ties by the greatest `created_at`. At least one survey is required; with none the evaluation is rejected with `ERR_NO_EVIDENCE_FOR_METHOD`. Compute how many filters the household should still be holding: sum the `quantity` of every shipment of this subscription whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_REQUESTED`, `RETURNED` or `UNRETURNED_WRITTEN_OFF`, then subtract the subscription's `filters_per_shipment` from that total and treat any negative result as zero — the most recent shipment's worth is legitimately still uninstalled. The score starts at zero and accumulates three independent contributions. Seventy points are added when the survey's `installed_last_shipment` is false. The excess on hand is the survey's `filters_on_hand_count` minus the expected holding computed above, treated as zero when negative, and ten points are added per excess filter counting at most three of them — so the excess contribution never exceeds thirty points. Fifteen points are added when the survey's `self_reported_change_date` is null, or when it falls strictly earlier than the logical-clock date moved back by twice the subscription's `ship_cadence_days`. The total is then capped at one hundred. Under this method a score of 55 or more is suspicious and a score of 80 or more can be confirmed — a score that would confirm under runtime analytics may be merely suspicious here.

**Materialising the case.** Every evaluation writes a case row, whatever the score, so the audit trail is complete. The case records the `subscription_id` it investigates, the `detection_method` that produced it, the computed `evidence_score`, and the two thresholds of that method frozen onto the row at creation as `suspect_threshold` and `confirm_threshold`, and it is stamped with `opened_at` from the logical clock. Its `notice_sent_at`, `remediation_deadline` and `remediation_note` are null. The evaluation also writes the score onto the subscription as `latest_evidence_score` and the method as `latest_detection_method`, overwriting whatever the previous evaluation left there, and increments the subscription's `version`.

The score then decides both the case's opening state and the subscription's compliance posture. When the score is greater than or equal to the method's suspect threshold, the case opens in `OPEN` and the subscription's `installation_posture` advances to `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED` — from `UNVERIFIED → NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED` if it had never been checked, or `COMPLIANT → NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED` if a previous evaluation had cleared it. When the score is strictly below the suspect threshold, the case is written **directly** in the terminal state `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED` — it never occupies `OPEN` and accepts no action of any kind — and the posture moves `UNVERIFIED → COMPLIANT` or, closing the remediation loop, `REMEDIATED → COMPLIANT`.

**Working an open case.** Sending the resident notice moves the case `OPEN → NOTICE_SENT`, stamps `notice_sent_at` with the logical-clock instant, and sets `remediation_deadline` to the date of that notice advanced by a twenty-one-day grace period. A suspicion can be dropped at either point: closing a case as unsubstantiated runs from `OPEN` or from `NOTICE_SENT`, moves the case to `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED`, and short-circuits the posture `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED → COMPLIANT`.

**Confirming — cascade A.** Confirmation runs only from `NOTICE_SENT` and only when the case's stored `evidence_score` is greater than or equal to its own frozen `confirm_threshold`; a score below the frozen threshold is rejected with `ERR_EVIDENCE_BELOW_CONFIRM_THRESHOLD`. Because the threshold was frozen at creation, a later change to the constants cannot retroactively make an old case confirmable. On success the case moves `NOTICE_SENT → ESCALATED_TO_PM`, and that escalation drives the subscription's posture `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED → NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED`. That confirmation forfeits riders: every entitlement on the subscription whose `state` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `benefit_code` is `DAMAGE_WAIVER` or `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE` moves to `FORFEITED` with `forfeit_reason` set to `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED` and `terminated_at` stamped from the logical clock; a row forfeited out of `SUSPENDED` additionally has its `suspended_by_property_event` cleared to false so that a later property reactivation cannot resurrect it. `CREDIT_REPORTING` and `RENTERS_INSURANCE` rows are untouched.

Each forfeited row then produces its own credit. Work the forfeited rows in ascending `benefit_code` order so the ledger insert order is deterministic. For each, take that benefit's annual price in cents from the table in §3.3, divide it by twelve to get the monthly value, multiply by the days remaining in the calendar month containing the confirmation date — counted as the month length minus the confirmation day-of-month plus one, so the confirmation day itself is refunded — divide by that month length, and round half up to whole cents. Each row is rounded on its own; a single rounding over the summed rows is not what this system computes. The resulting figure is posted as a `WAIVER_REVERSAL` ledger row whose `amount_cents` is the **negative** of it (it is a credit), whose `billed_party` snapshots the subscription's `billing_party`, and whose `source_ref_id` names the forfeited entitlement row. Each such insert re-derives the property's `outstanding_balance_cents`. Finally the subscription's `entitled_benefit_codes` is recomputed — which is where the `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED` removal rule of §3.3 bites — and `monthly_fee_cents` is recomputed from the surviving set, so the resident's or manager's go-forward bill drops by the riders they lost.

**Remediating.** Once escalated, the property manager can install the filters and say so: remediation runs only from `ESCALATED_TO_PM`, records the `remediation_note` supplied with the request, moves the case `ESCALATED_TO_PM → REMEDIATED`, and moves the posture `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED → REMEDIATED`. Remediation deliberately does **not** un-forfeit anything — forfeiture is permanent for that subscription, and the closest the subscription can get to whole again is a subsequent clean evaluation moving `REMEDIATED → COMPLIANT`. Both `REMEDIATED` and `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED` are terminal for a case, and any action attempted from a state the case machine does not permit is rejected with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`.

**The entitlement lifecycle in full.** An entitlement row is created `PENDING` at enrolment, one per code in the subscription's `entitled_benefit_codes`, and it exposes its `benefit_code`, its `state`, the `suspended_by_property_event` marker, its `effective_from` and `terminated_at` instants, and its `forfeit_reason`. It becomes `ACTIVE` with `effective_from` stamped when the subscription activates. A property suspension suspends only the two operationally-suspendable riders — a row moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_by_property_event` set true when its `benefit_code` is `RENTERS_INSURANCE` or `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE`, while `CREDIT_REPORTING` and `DAMAGE_WAIVER` rows stay `ACTIVE` — and a property reactivation moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with the marker cleared to false for exactly the rows carrying it. A confirmed non-installation forfeits as described above. Cancellation expires: a row that is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `EXPIRED` with `terminated_at` stamped and `forfeit_reason` set to `SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED` for an ordinary cancellation or `TRANSFER_SUPERSEDED` when the cancellation was driven by a completed transfer, while voiding a never-activated subscription moves each still-`PENDING` row to `EXPIRED` with `forfeit_reason` `SUBSCRIPTION_VOIDED`. `FORFEITED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal, and `forfeit_reason` stays null on any row that reached neither.

### 3.7. Claims: Filing, the Review Cycle, Adjudication and Payment

Two of the bundled benefits behave like insurance and can be claimed against. A `Resident`, their `PropertyManager` or a `ProgramOperator` files a claim naming the `subscription_id` it is filed against, a `claim_type` of either `DAMAGE_WAIVER` or `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE`, the `incident_date` on which the loss occurred, a `requested_amount_cents` greater than zero, and a `description` of one to five hundred characters narrating the incident. A new claim is `SUBMITTED` with `info_request_round` 0, `version` 1, and `provisional_amount_cents`, `approved_amount_cents`, `triaged_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `paid_at` and `reversal_reason` all null.

Filing is gated four ways. A subscription that was never activated cannot back a claim, so filing against one whose `status` is `PENDING_SETUP` or `VOIDED` is rejected with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_INVALID`. The entitlement backing the claim must be live: the subscription must own an entitlement row whose `benefit_code` equals the requested `claim_type` and whose `state` is `ACTIVE` at that moment, and a row that is absent, `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED`, `FORFEITED` or `EXPIRED` yields `ERR_BENEFIT_NOT_ENTITLED`. The incident must fall inside the coverage window, which has three edges at once: the `incident_date` must be on or after the calendar date of the subscription's `activated_at`, must not be later than the logical-clock date, and must not be more than sixty days before the logical-clock date; any of the three failing is `ERR_OUTSIDE_COVERAGE_WINDOW`. Finally the ask is capped: a `requested_amount_cents` above one hundred thousand cents is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP`.

**The settled base.** Both settlement figures a claim carries are computed from the same shared base, and the base depends on the claim type. For a `DAMAGE_WAIVER` claim, subtract a five-thousand-cent deductible from `requested_amount_cents`, treat a negative result as zero, and then cap the remainder at a per-incident ceiling of thirty thousand cents; the base is whichever of those two is smaller. For an `AIR_QUALITY_GUARANTEE` claim the base is a refund-of-fees calculation instead: count the whole days from the calendar date of the subscription's `activated_at` to the claim's `incident_date`, divide by thirty and discard the remainder to get elapsed months, treat a negative count as zero, cap the month count at twelve, multiply the subscription's `monthly_fee_cents` by that capped month count to get the entitled amount, and take the base as the lesser of `requested_amount_cents` and that entitled amount.

**Two settlement figures, taken at two different moments.** This is the sharpest arithmetic distinction in the claim workflow. At intake, a `ClaimsAdjuster` triages the claim, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, recording their `X-User-ID` as the claim's `triaged_by_user_id`, and freezing `provisional_amount_cents`. The provisional figure reads the subscription's `monthly_fee_cents` exactly as it stands at the triage instant, and it applies **no** non-installation reduction whatever the subscription's `installation_posture` happens to be — it is the shared base and nothing more. At adjudication, the approving path recomputes from scratch: it re-reads `monthly_fee_cents` and `installation_posture` at the decision instant, and applies a fifty-percent reduction to the base when the posture is `NON_INSTALL_SUSPECTED`, or no reduction otherwise, rounding the reduced figure half up to whole cents in the single rounding this calculation performs. `approved_amount_cents` therefore differs from `provisional_amount_cents` for a claim triaged while the subscription was `COMPLIANT` and decided after a suspicion was raised — that divergence is the intended behaviour, not a defect. A posture of `NON_INSTALL_CONFIRMED` never reaches this arithmetic at all, because the matching entitlement is already `FORFEITED` by then and the re-check described below rejects the approval first.

**The revision loop.** From `UNDER_REVIEW` an adjuster may send the claim back for more evidence, supplying the `question` to be answered. That moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and increments `info_request_round` by one. The loop is bounded: a request-info attempt on a claim whose `info_request_round` has already reached two is rejected with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT`, so at most two round trips are possible. The filer — the resident, their property manager, or an operator — closes each round by resubmitting with an updated `description`, which moves the claim `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and leaves `info_request_round` where it was. Resubmission explicitly does **not** recompute `provisional_amount_cents`: the triage-time freeze survives every iteration of the loop.

**Deciding.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` adjudicates by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY` together with a `decision_note`, and the acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `decided_by_user_id` either way. Two guards run first. Segregation of duties bars the individual who triaged the claim from deciding it: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the claim's `triaged_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the role is authorised, the person is not. Then, on the approving path only, entitlement is re-checked against the state at the decision instant: the entitlement row whose `benefit_code` equals the `claim_type` must still be `ACTIVE`, and a row that has since been forfeited or expired yields `ERR_BENEFIT_NOT_ENTITLED`. An approval moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and stores `approved_amount_cents` from the decision-time variant above. A denial moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, sets `approved_amount_cents` to 0, and posts no ledger row; `DENIED` is terminal.

**Paying, and unpaying.** A `ProgramOperator` disburses an approved claim, which requires the claim to be `APPROVED` with an `approved_amount_cents` greater than zero. The claim moves `APPROVED → PAID`, `paid_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and a `CLAIM_PAYOUT` ledger row is posted whose `amount_cents` is the **negative** of `approved_amount_cents` — money leaving the program is a credit to the billed party — with `billed_party` snapshotting the subscription's `billing_party` and `source_ref_id` naming the claim. The property's `outstanding_balance_cents` is then re-derived. Reversal is the compensating action: it runs only from `PAID`, records the `reversal_reason` supplied with the request, moves the claim `PAID → PAYMENT_REVERSED`, and posts a `CLAIM_PAYOUT_REVERSAL` row of exactly **positive** `approved_amount_cents`, carrying the same `billed_party` as the matching payout row and the same `source_ref_id`. `approved_amount_cents` is deliberately left unchanged so the two ledger legs sum to exactly zero and the property's balance returns to its exact pre-payout value with no drift. `PAYMENT_REVERSED` is terminal.

**Walking away.** The filer may abandon a claim that has not yet been decided, supplying a `withdraw_reason`. Withdrawal runs from `SUBMITTED`, from `UNDER_REVIEW` or from `INFO_REQUESTED`, and in all three cases moves the claim to the terminal state `WITHDRAWN` with no money movement and no settlement recomputation. Any claim action attempted from a state the claim machine does not permit — including all four terminal states — is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.8. Mid-Lease Address Change and Subscription Transfer

Residents move inside a portfolio, and when they do the filter subscription has to move with them: a different rack, possibly a different size, certainly a different cadence and price. The program models this as a transfer record that walks its own short workflow and, on completion, replaces one subscription with another rather than editing the first one in place.

Opening a transfer is the concurrency-guarded write of §2.7. A `ProgramOperator` or the property's `PropertyManager` supplies the `subscription_id` being moved, the `to_unit_id` of the destination unit, the `effective_date` on which the move takes effect — which is also the proration pivot for both legs — and the `expected_subscription_version`. The outgoing subscription must be `ACTIVE`. The destination must be a genuinely different unit and must be `AVAILABLE`: a `to_unit_id` equal to the subscription's current `unit_id`, or a destination unit in any status other than `AVAILABLE`, is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID`. The pivot date must be usable: an `effective_date` earlier than the logical-clock date, or falling outside the closed interval from the subscription's `lease_start_date` to its `lease_end_date`, is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_DATE_INVALID`. And the destination unit's occupancy must accommodate the incoming lease: the same closed-interval-plus-three-day-turnover-buffer test described in §3.3 is applied to the destination unit's live subscriptions, with a collision rejected as `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`.

On acceptance the transfer record is created in `REQUESTED` with `version` 1, its `remapped_sku_id`, `successor_subscription_id` and `rejection_reason` null and its three proration figures at 0, and the outgoing subscription moves `ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PENDING`. Opening a transfer also supersedes every earlier transfer on the same subscription that is still open: each one in `REQUESTED` or in `SKU_REMAPPED` is moved to the terminal state `SUPERSEDED`. This supersession is exactly why the version guard exists — without it, a client working from a stale view would silently discard an address change it had never seen.

Remapping is the next step and belongs to a `ProgramOperator`. It requires the transfer to be `REQUESTED`, the destination unit to still be `AVAILABLE`, and the selection rules of §3.3 to yield at least one candidate when run against the destination unit's own `hvac_system_type`, `filter_slot_count` and effective size — an empty candidate set is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SKU`. The winner is stored as the transfer's `remapped_sku_id` and the transfer moves `REQUESTED → SKU_REMAPPED`.

**Completion — cascade C.** Completion requires the transfer to be `SKU_REMAPPED`, the outgoing subscription to be `TRANSFER_PENDING`, and the property owning the destination unit to be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE`); the destination property's credit ceiling must also absorb the charge the completion will post, otherwise `ERR_PROPERTY_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The successor subscription is priced before any money is computed: run the whole chain of §3.3 against the destination unit and its property, in the order SKU selection (already fixed as `remapped_sku_id`), then cadence, then filters per shipment, then benefit set, then monthly fee.

The proration then runs in three pinned steps against the calendar month containing the transfer's `effective_date`. Count the days in that month, and count the remaining days as that month length minus one less than the `effective_date` day-of-month — so the pivot day itself is part of the remainder. Step one credits the outgoing subscription: multiply the **outgoing** subscription's `monthly_fee_cents` by the remaining days, divide by the month length, and round half up to whole cents; that is `proration_credit_cents`. Step two charges the successor for the same remainder at its own price: multiply the **successor's** freshly computed `monthly_fee_cents` by the same remaining days, divide by the same month length, and round half up; that is `proration_charge_cents`. Step three is a plain integer subtraction of the credit from the charge, giving the signed `net_adjustment_cents`. Both roundings are independent and both happen before the subtraction — the difference is never computed on unrounded values, and the two legs are never posted to the ledger separately.

Completion then executes as one atomic step. The transfer moves `SKU_REMAPPED → COMPLETED` — terminal — with the three proration figures frozen onto it. A successor subscription is minted on the destination unit in `status` `ACTIVE` and `installation_posture` `UNVERIFIED`, with `predecessor_subscription_id` pointing back at the outgoing subscription, `assigned_sku_id` set to `remapped_sku_id`, `property_id` denormalised afresh from the destination unit, and its own recomputed `ship_cadence_days`, `filters_per_shipment`, `entitled_benefit_codes` and `monthly_fee_cents`; the transfer's `successor_subscription_id` records it. As a second consequence of the same completion the outgoing subscription moves `TRANSFER_PENDING → CANCELLED` with its `cancel_effective_date` set to the transfer's `effective_date` and its `cancel_reason` overwritten with the literal string `TRANSFER_SUPERSEDED` — whatever text the caller may have supplied earlier is replaced. That cancellation cancels the outgoing subscription's still-`SCHEDULED` shipments and expires its entitlements with `forfeit_reason` `TRANSFER_SUPERSEDED`, but it deliberately does **not** run the cancellation settlement of §3.9: the transfer proration replaces it entirely, and no proration-credit or unreturned-fee row is posted for the outgoing leg. Exactly one signed ledger row is posted, a `TRANSFER_ADJUSTMENT` carrying `net_adjustment_cents` with `source_ref_id` naming the transfer, booked against the successor subscription, after which the property's `outstanding_balance_cents` is re-derived. Because the successor is immediately `ACTIVE`, the resident stays `RESIDING` — this is the conditional part of the resident cascade described in §3.9 — and the destination unit moves `AVAILABLE → OCCUPIED` while the origin unit returns to `AVAILABLE` if no live subscription remains on it.

Rejection closes the commercial cycle instead. Running from either `REQUESTED` or `SKU_REMAPPED`, it records the `rejection_reason` supplied with the request, moves the transfer to the terminal state `REJECTED`, and returns the outgoing subscription `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE` with no fee change, no entitlement change, no proration and no ledger row of any kind. Any transfer action attempted from a state the transfer machine does not permit — including all three terminal states `COMPLETED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` — is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. Cancellation, Settlement and Winding Down

Cancellation has two entirely different shapes depending on whether billing ever started, and the request looks the same in both: an `effective_date` and a `cancel_reason`. A `Resident`, a `PropertyManager` or a `ProgramOperator` may cancel.

**Never activated.** A subscription still in `PENDING_SETUP` was never billed, so there is nothing to settle. It moves `PENDING_SETUP → VOIDED`, records the supplied `cancel_effective_date` and `cancel_reason`, moves each of its still-`PENDING` entitlement rows to `EXPIRED` with `forfeit_reason` `SUBSCRIPTION_VOIDED`, and runs no settlement and posts no ledger row whatever. It leaves the resident's `status` and the unit's `status` untouched — the resident stays a `PROSPECT` and the unit stays `AVAILABLE`, because neither was ever advanced. The effective-date range check is skipped entirely on this path.

**Already billing.** A subscription in `ACTIVE` or in `SUSPENDED` settles. Both source states produce the identical side-effect set. First the date is checked: the `effective_date` must be on or after the logical-clock date and must not be later than the subscription's `lease_end_date`, and a violation is `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_INVALID`. Then the settlement is computed — and it is computed **before** any shipment transition is applied, over the shipment statuses as they stand at the moment of the request.

The settlement is a five-step waterfall, evaluated exactly once in the order one through five with no iteration. **Step one** prorates the unused remainder of the current billing month. Take the calendar month containing the `effective_date`, whose first day is the settlement's `period_start`, and count its length. The used days are one less than the `effective_date` day-of-month, because service is treated as consumed through the prior day; the unused days are the month length minus the used days. Multiply the subscription's `monthly_fee_cents` by the unused days, divide by the month length, and round half up to whole cents: that is `gross_prorated_credit_cents`. **Step two** measures the gross unreturned exposure. Collect every shipment of this subscription whose `status` is `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED` or `RETURN_REQUESTED` — those three and no others, so `SCHEDULED`, `CANCELLED`, `RETURNED` and `UNRETURNED_WRITTEN_OFF` shipments are all excluded — and for each one compute the lesser of the `unit_cost_cents` of the catalog entry named by that shipment's own `sku_id` multiplied by its `quantity`, and the two-thousand-five-hundred-cent per-shipment cap. Sum those capped figures to get `unreturned_fee_gross_cents`; the sum is order-independent. **Step three** picks the waiver-relief tier, and it is keyed **only** on step one's output. When `gross_prorated_credit_cents` is below one thousand cents the relief `waiver_credit_pct` is zero; from one thousand cents up to but not including five thousand it is twenty-five percent; from five thousand up to but not including ten thousand it is fifty percent; at ten thousand cents or above it is seventy-five percent. Keying the tier on the net settlement instead would be a genuine circularity and is forbidden. **Step four** relieves the fee: multiply `unreturned_fee_gross_cents` by one minus the relief percentage and round half up once to whole cents, giving `unreturned_fee_net_cents`. **Step five** is a plain integer subtraction: `net_settlement_cents` is `gross_prorated_credit_cents` minus `unreturned_fee_net_cents`, and it may legitimately come out negative, meaning the resident or manager owes the program rather than the reverse. Exactly two roundings occur in the whole waterfall, at step one and at step four.

All five figures are frozen onto the subscription as `gross_prorated_credit_cents`, `waiver_credit_pct`, `unreturned_fee_gross_cents`, `unreturned_fee_net_cents` and `net_settlement_cents`, and the subscription moves to `CANCELLED` with its `cancel_effective_date` and `cancel_reason` recorded. Only then do the shipments move: every shipment still `SCHEDULED` is cancelled, and every shipment in `SHIPPED` or `DELIVERED` is recalled to `RETURN_REQUESTED`. Every entitlement row still in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `EXPIRED` with `forfeit_reason` `SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED`. Two ledger rows may be posted, both with `source_ref_id` naming the subscription: a `PRORATION_CREDIT` row whose `amount_cents` is the **negative** of `gross_prorated_credit_cents`, always; and an `UNRETURNED_SHIPMENT_FEE` row of **positive** `unreturned_fee_net_cents` carrying a `memo` reading `cancellation unreturned reconciliation`, posted only when that net figure is greater than zero. The property's `outstanding_balance_cents` is then re-derived and the subscription's `version` increments.

Two occupancy cascades close out the record, and both are conditional. The resident moves `RESIDING → MOVED_OUT` only when no other subscription of that resident remains in `PENDING_SETUP`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TRANSFER_PENDING`; this is precisely why a cancellation produced by a completed transfer leaves the resident `RESIDING`, since the successor subscription is already `ACTIVE`. The unit moves `OCCUPIED → AVAILABLE` only when no subscription in those same four statuses references it any longer.

**Previewing a settlement.** Because the waterfall's ordering is subtle, the program exposes a read-only computed preview against the current logical clock. It takes the hypothetical `effective_date` and returns the whole intermediate chain so the evaluation order is observable: the `subscription_id`, the `used_days` and `unused_days` of step one, the resulting `gross_prorated_credit_cents`, the `exposed_shipment_count` of shipments that entered step two, the `unreturned_fee_gross_cents` that step produced, the `waiver_credit_pct` tier step three selected — reported here as a decimal string, one of `0.00`, `0.25`, `0.50` or `0.75` — then `unreturned_fee_net_cents` and finally `net_settlement_cents`. The preview mutates nothing: no state changes, no ledger rows, no frozen fields, no version bumps.

**Winding down a property.** The property lifecycle described in §3.2 is the endgame for everything above. Suspension freezes billing-adjacent activity across the whole property without destroying it, reactivation restores exactly what suspension took, and offboarding — permitted only from `SUSPENDED` and only when no subscription at the property is `PENDING_SETUP`, `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFER_PENDING` — retires the units. A property's ledger history survives offboarding untouched: rows are never deleted, so its `outstanding_balance_cents` remains the exact sum of everything ever posted against it.
