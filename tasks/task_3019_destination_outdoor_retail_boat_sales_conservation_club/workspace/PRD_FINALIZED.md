# Destination Outdoor Retail API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A destination outdoor retail store is not a shop with a checkout lane; it is a place people come to spend an afternoon. The aquariums, the museum gallery, the catch-and-release fishing pond, the archery lanes and simulators, the taxidermy gallery and the calendar of seminars and kids workshops all exist to hold visitors on the floor, and every one of them consumes a shared, finite resource — the store's daily dwell-minute budget. This API is the operational backbone for that model. It governs who may occupy which venue and for how long, how that occupation is priced in dwell minutes, and how the store's admission posture is derived from the sum of those commitments. When the budget runs dry or a venue is over-occupied, the whole store does not simply stop; it moves into a restricted or frozen posture that gates every subsequent venue decision.

Running alongside the attraction business is a genuine boat dealership operating inside the retail floor, and a state-licensed permit counter that acts as an agent of the wildlife agency. Both are compliance businesses wearing retail clothes. A boat sale carries four independent state dimensions that advance on different clocks — the commercial stage of the deal, the funding of the contract, the progress of the state title work, and the completion of the rigging work order — and a rejected title can drag a delivered deal backwards into a contracted stage so it can be re-filed. The permit counter sells seasonal licences and immediately incurs an obligation to remit the collected fees to the state; the remittance must be tamper-evident, its totals must be reproducible after a correction, and the clerk who sold a permit may never be the administrator who seals it into a batch.

The club programme threads through both. Members accrue points on settlements, on event attendance and on manual adjustments; those points drive a tier ladder, a redeemable balance net of a tier-retention floor, and a conservation donation match that is capped for the calendar year. Club credit cards are opened through a segregated underwriting and decision process, and an active card changes the economics of a trade-in, the salary of a boat deal, and the stakes of a no-show. This document specifies the roles, the response contract, the cross-cutting store-wide principles, and the workflow rules for every one of those processes.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply everywhere: they are the invariants that every endpoint honours regardless of which entity it touches. Where a rule is specific to a workflow, it appears in §3 instead; where a rule is specific to a single request contract, it appears in §4.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent on an endpoint whose authorized roles do not include `Anonymous`, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present but the declared `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the endpoint authorizes, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Only a small set of read paths and two enrolment paths admit `Anonymous`, so the overwhelming majority of traffic must be authenticated.

The platform recognizes eighteen roles, spelled exactly as follows: `Anonymous`, `Member`, `AttractionHost`, `EventsCoordinator`, `DealDeskClerk`, `ServiceWriter`, `ServiceTechnician`, `ReceivingManager`, `PermitClerk`, `RemitAdmin`, `AgencyOperator`, `TitleClerk`, `CreditUnderwriter`, `CreditOfficer`, `ClubCardSpecialist`, `FinanceAdmin`, `StoreManager`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

Exactly one role is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's `member_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Member` may therefore read and amend only their own member record, their own boat deals, their own intakes, their own fulfilments, their own registrations and their own club card applications.

Every other role — `Anonymous`, `AttractionHost`, `EventsCoordinator`, `DealDeskClerk`, `ServiceWriter`, `ServiceTechnician`, `ReceivingManager`, `PermitClerk`, `RemitAdmin`, `AgencyOperator`, `TitleClerk`, `CreditUnderwriter`, `CreditOfficer`, `ClubCardSpecialist`, `FinanceAdmin`, `StoreManager` and `PlatformAdmin` — has an opaque operator identity. For these roles, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The server does record it where a workflow assigns operator provenance: the issuing `agent_user_id` of a permit sale, the `issuer_admin_user_id` stamped at sale time, the `remitter_admin_user_id` stamped when a remittance batch is sealed, the `underwriter_user_id` and `decision_user_id` carried on a club card application, and the `rm_cross_check_code` presented when an oversize load is released. Several of those recorded identities are subsequently compared to one another by the segregation-of-duties rules in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paged with two query parameters. `limit` caps how many records are returned and defaults to 10 when it is not supplied; it must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of leading records to skip and defaults to 0 when it is not supplied. Both parameters are optional on every list path, and the paged collections include the store's members, attractions, waivers, sessions, boat units, appraisals, title filings, boat deals, permit sales, remit batches, club card applications, point events, service orders, service intakes, event offerings, event registrations, bay bookings and order fulfilments.

Unless a listing is described otherwise, records come back sorted by their creation timestamp in ascending order. The response `meta` object for a list carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`; `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before paging was applied, and `has_more` reports whether further records remain past the returned window.
### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource in this API — stores, members, attractions, sessions, boat units, appraisals, service orders, title filings, boat deals, permit sales, remit batches, club card applications, point events, event offerings, event registrations, bay bookings, order fulfilments, service intakes and liability waivers — carries the same four system-managed fields.

- `id` is a server-minted UUID. It identifies the resource and is never accepted from a client on creation. No type prefix, no entity abbreviation and no human-readable fragment is embedded in it.
- `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written.
- `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with the same precision, refreshed on every mutation of the row — including mutations the client did not request directly, such as a derived-field recomputation, a cascade from a parent resource, or a checkpoint sweep performed by an automated capability.
- `version` is an integer that starts at its initial value and increments by one on each mutation of the row.

There are no exceptions to this convention in this system: every one of the nineteen entities is a full common-field resource, so `updated_at` and `version` are present everywhere. A derived field that is recomputed in place is a mutation like any other — rewriting `Store.dwell_committed_minutes`, `Store.attraction_posture`, `Member.points_balance` or a batch's live totals moves that row's `updated_at` and advances its `version`. This matters operationally: the version counters are the substrate for the stale-version rejections described in §2.10, so an implementation that recomputes a derived field silently without advancing `version` will fail the concurrency contract.

Every entity also exposes its own `status` enumeration (or, for `BoatDeal`, its four dimension fields) plus whatever business fields the workflow defines. Foreign references are always carried as explicit identifier fields on the child row — `store_id`, `member_id`, `boat_unit_id`, `attraction_id`, `service_order_id`, `event_offering_id`, `boat_deal_id`, `deal_id`, `booking_id`, `waiver_id`, `permit_sale_id`, `trade_in_appraisal_id`, `title_filing_id`, `pre_service_order_id`, `attraction_id` on offerings, `boat_deal_id` on point events — and readers may follow each of those in both directions from the flat read routes catalogued in §4.

### 2.5. The Store Clock and the Ops-Run Checkpoint

Time in this system is a logical clock, not the wall clock. The server's clock starts at exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` on startup and holds that instant until an authorised `PlatformAdmin` moves it. A single platform-level capability accepts a target instant in the `to_iso8601` field and repositions the clock there; it is the only way the clock advances. Every temporal predicate in the system — waiver expiry, venue slot windows, no-show escalation, intake abandonment, lead-time projection, dwell posture evaluation — is evaluated against this logical instant, never against the host machine's time. If the clock is never moved, the store reads as permanently sitting at the first of September 2026.

A second platform capability is the ops-run checkpoint, scoped to one store and executable by a `PlatformAdmin` or that store's `StoreManager`. The checkpoint is a single sweep that advances the store's `permit_remit_epoch` counter by one, stamps `last_ops_run_at`, expires overdue waivers, promotes waitlisted venue sessions and waitlisted event registrations where capacity has freed, marks no-shows for slots that ended with no check-in, completes events whose end instant has passed, abandons intakes whose pickup window has elapsed, and re-derives the store's admission posture. Because the sweep is the only thing that moves `permit_remit_epoch`, that counter is a monotone measure of how many checkpoints a store has passed through, and it is exposed alongside `last_ops_run_at` on the store read.

Two postures can be derived from the same inputs and can legitimately disagree. The live posture is recomputed on every read and on every mutation, over the current row set. The checkpoint posture is derived during the sweep and deliberately ignores venue sessions whose occupancy slot lies in the future, so a store whose only over-capacity venue is booked for a slot later in the day reads as `OPEN` under the checkpoint derivation and as `FROZEN` under the live derivation. Both are correct readings of their respective variants; the live posture is the one that gates request handling.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution and the Priority Order of Failure

The API is mounted under `/stores`, and many of its routes nest a store-scoped child beneath the store segment. Requests against nested routes are validated left to right, one path identifier at a time, and the request fails on the first identifier that does not resolve. A request naming a `store_id` that matches no store row fails with `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before any other check runs. This holds for every endpoint whose path begins with the store segment.

Once the store resolves, every non-null identifier supplied in the request body or in a path position must resolve to a row of the type that position declares. A reference to a missing attraction, member, waiver, boat unit, appraisal, title filing, boat deal, permit sale, remit batch, club card application, point event, service order, service intake, event offering, event registration, bay booking or order fulfilment fails with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A `session_id`, `order_id`, `filing_id`, `batch_id`, `application_id`, `registration_id`, `fulfillment_id`, `intake_id`, `appraisal_id`, `offering_id`, `point_event_id`, `sale_id`, `deal_id`, `boat_unit_id`, `attraction_id`, `member_id`, `waiver_id`, `booking_id` or `bay_no` that appears in a path is governed by this rule exactly as a body reference is.

Ownership is checked next, and it is a distinct failure from absence. When a child identifier appears in a nested path beneath a store segment, that child must actually belong to the store named in the same path. A session cancelled by naming a `session_id` whose `attraction_id` resolves to a venue belonging to a different store is rejected with `ERR_PARENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 404), not with a permission error and not with a not-found error for the child itself. The same applies to a bay, a work order, an offering, a filing, a sale or a batch referenced under a store that does not own it.

Bay numbers carry an additional range gate. A load bay is addressed by `bay_no`, and a booking is accepted only when `bay_no` falls between 1 and the owning store's `load_bays_total`, inclusive. A bay number outside that closed interval — zero, negative, or one past the last bay — is rejected with `ERR_BAY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), which is deliberately a distinct error from a missing store or a mismatched parent.

The left-to-right rule applies to read routes as well as write routes. Because the flat canonical read routes are addressed as `/stores/<segment>/{id}` — for instance the member read, the attraction read, the session read, the boat-unit read, the appraisal read, the filing read, the deal read, the sale read, the batch read, the application read, the point-event read, the order read, the intake read, the offering read, the registration read, the booking read and the fulfilment read — a reader that requests a nested listing such as the sessions on a venue, the fulfilments presented under a waiver, the deals that applied an appraisal, the service intakes verified against a permit sale, the work orders attached to a deal, the event offerings hosted at a venue, or the bookings on one load bay will have each of the identifiers on that path resolved in the same left-to-right order.

### 2.7. The Attraction Posture Gate and the Dwell Budget

Each store owns one shared dwell-minute budget: `dwell_capacity_minutes` is the budget a `StoreManager` declares when the store is created, and it is consumed by every venue session and every published event the store hosts. Consumed minutes are tracked as `dwell_committed_minutes`, defined as the sum of `dwell_charge_minutes` across the store's attraction sessions whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, plus the `dwell_charge_minutes` of every event offering whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`. What remains is `dwell_free_minutes`, the capacity minus the committed minutes. Sessions in `REQUESTED`, `WAITLIST_FALLBACK`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` consume nothing, and neither do draft, cancelled or completed offerings.

From those figures the store derives a single admission posture held in `attraction_posture`, taking one of three values: `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. The derivation is a pure function of the enumerated inputs, so identical inputs always produce an identical posture and no tie-break rule is needed. Two subsidiary counts feed it. The first is the number of the store's venues where `in_use_units` has grown past `capacity_units`. The second is the number of the store's venues that require a waiver and that hold at least one `CONFIRMED` session whose referenced waiver has an `expires_on` instant earlier than the store clock's current instant.

A store is `FROZEN` when its free minutes have fallen below zero, or when at least one of its venues is over capacity. It is `RESTRICTED` when free minutes have fallen below thirty minutes without crossing zero, or when at least one waiver-requiring venue is holding a confirmed session under an expired waiver — and it is `RESTRICTED` in that case even when the budget is healthy. In every remaining case the store reads `OPEN`. Thirty minutes is the critical band: a store with thirty or more free minutes and no expired-waiver venue is open, a store with twenty-nine free minutes is restricted.

Posture is not advisory. A `FROZEN` store blocks any operation that moves an attraction session toward `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS` with `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). A `RESTRICTED` store blocks confirmation of a new session, blocks the check-in that would move a confirmed party to `IN_PROGRESS`, and blocks the promotion of a `WAITLIST_FALLBACK` session out of the waitlist, in all three cases with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409). Contracting a boat deal is also refused while the store is `FROZEN`, and this is the gate that ties the dealership floor to the attraction floor. The posture is re-derived after essentially every event that changes committed minutes: a session confirming, checking in, cancelling or completing; a session being marked a no-show; an event publishing or cancelling; and the ops-run checkpoint. When a store that had gone `RESTRICTED` releases enough capacity it returns to `OPEN` on the next derivation, and when a sibling session on a store that has just turned `RESTRICTED` is still sitting in `REQUESTED`, it is rejected with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` as part of the same recomputation.

The store exposes a computed dwell read that reports `dwell_capacity_minutes`, `dwell_committed_minutes`, `dwell_free_minutes`, `attraction_posture`, the over-capacity venue count and the expired-waiver venue count without mutating any row.

### 2.8. Monetary Precision and Rounding Discipline

Money is stored in whole cents and never as a floating-point dollar amount. Amount fields carry the `_cents` suffix throughout the system: `unit_price_cents`, `motor_price_cents`, `trailer_price_cents`, `offer_amount_cents`, `accepted_credit_cents`, `deposit_cents`, `project_charge_cents`, `contract_price_cents`, `total_fee_cents`, `balance_due_cents`, `funded_amount_cents`, `settlement_variance_cents`, `fee_cents`, `gross_fee_cents`, `interpretative_gross_cents`, `remit_variance_cents`, `remit_ledger_cents`, `credit_limit_cents`, `donation_match_cents` and `conservation_match_cents`. Points, minutes and days are whole integers: `points_delta`, `points_awarded`, `points_balance`, `points_lifetime_earned`, `redeemable_points`, `no_show_strikes`, `party_size`, `seats`, `specimen_quantity`, `land_access_units`, `term_days`, `quoted_lead_days`, `promised_lead_days`, `actual_lead_days`, `overrun_minutes`, `dwell_charge_minutes`, `dwell_committed_minutes`, `dwell_free_minutes`, `dwell_cost_minutes`, `dwell_capacity_minutes`, `capacity_units`, `in_use_units`, `confirmed_count`, `registration_cap`, `load_bays_total`, `lane_no`, `bay_no` and `created_seq`.

Every monetary result is expressed to exactly two decimal places and is produced by rounding half up. Where a computation passes through more than one rounding gate, the gates are ordered and each intermediate result is rounded at the point the computation specifies; rounding is never deferred from one gate to a later one, and reordering two gates can change the final cent. The full set of money and minute calculations, each with its own pinned evaluation order, is specified in §2.8.1 and developed in the workflows of §3. The one integer-arithmetic exception is a fractional add-on in the permit-fee calculation, which is carried at full precision through an integer base and rounded exactly once at the end.

#### 2.8.1. Calculation Rules and Their Evaluation Order

Sixteen named calculations produce the derived fields in this system. Each one has a fixed evaluation order, and each one is triggered by a specific lifecycle event rather than by a read.

**`boat_deal_contract_price`** computes `contract_price_cents` for a deal. It runs when a deal is created, on the `QUOTED → CONTRACTED` transition, and on every subsequent mutation of the deal. Its inputs are the deal's hull's `unit_price_cents`, `motor_price_cents` and `trailer_price_cents`; the `unit_condition` of that hull; the club card status of the purchasing member; and, when an accepted appraisal exists for that same member, that appraisal's `accepted_credit_cents`. The price begins as the hull price plus the motor price plus the trailer price plus a fixed rigging fee of ninety-five thousand cents. An accessory discount is then applied: the lesser of the motor-plus-trailer sum and five hundred thousand cents is taken as the accessory base, and that base is multiplied by five percent when the unit condition is `NEW` and by two and a half percent for `USED` and `DEMO` units, with the discount rounded half up before it is subtracted. A tier credit is computed next, and the ordering here is pinned: the negotiated base is multiplied by the member's credit rate and rounded half up first, and only then is the result capped by taking the lesser of it and the accepted appraisal's credit. The member's credit rate is zero for card status `NONE`, one percent for `PENDING`, five percent for `ACTIVE` and zero for `SUSPENDED`. Finally the accessory discount and the trade credit are both subtracted from the base, and the result is floored by taking the greater of that difference and one cent, so a contract price is never zero and never negative. The eligible appraisal is one whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `member_id` equals the deal's `member_id`; eligible boat units are those whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `DEAL_PENDING`, and units already `SOLD` are excluded from eligibility.

This calculation is dual-variant, and which variant is in force is a function of the deal's commercial stage alone — the presence or absence of a funding record never selects it. The provisional quote variant applies while the deal's commercial stage is `QUOTED`, and only there; under it the tier credit is computed at half the member's credit rate and the rigging fee is excluded from the base, so the same hull, motor and trade produce a lower quoted price. The contracted settlement variant applies from the moment the deal leaves `QUOTED` — that is, while the deal's commercial stage is `CONTRACTED`, `DELIVERED` or `SETTLED`, whether or not any funding record exists; under it the full credit rate and the rigging fee both apply, producing a higher `contract_price_cents` for identical inputs, so contracting a deal raises its stored price against the quote it carried a moment earlier.

**`deal_fee_and_tax_relay`** computes `total_fee_cents`. It runs on the deal's contract, funding and settlement transitions, and it is always evaluated after `boat_deal_contract_price` has settled — the relay consumes the settled price as its fee base and never iterates back into it, so there is no circular fixed point. The relay adds a documentation fee of fifty-nine thousand nine hundred cents, a title fee of forty-five hundred cents when the deal's filing is a `BOAT_TITLE` filing and twenty-five hundred cents for any other filing kind, and sales tax at seven hundred twenty-five basis points. The tax base is the fee base plus the documentation fee and deliberately excludes the title fee. Tax is computed by multiplying the whole taxable base by the tax rate and rounding half up exactly once — never by rounding each component separately and summing. Under the contract-price basis, used while the deal's commercial stage is `QUOTED` or `CONTRACTED`, the fee base is the contract price. Under the delivered-settlement basis, used while the stage is `DELIVERED` or `SETTLED`, an additional delivery-prep charge of twenty-five thousand cents is folded into the fee base, which raises both the taxed amount and the total fee.

**`delivery_settlement_variance`** computes `settlement_variance_cents`. It runs on the contract-to-delivered transition and on the delivered-to-contracted revert. The provisional side is the quote-variant contract price — the price that excludes the rigging fee and halves the tier credit. The final side is the contracted-variant contract price plus the relayed fees and tax computed on the delivered-settlement basis. The stored value is the final side minus the provisional side, a signed delta reported separately from the deal's balance. On the delivered-to-contracted revert the field is reset to zero and both sides are recomputed from the pre-delivery values.

**`deal_points_award`** computes `points_awarded` for a deal that has reached `SETTLED`. It selects a rate band from the award price: eight basis points when the price is below five million cents, twelve basis points when it is at least five million but below fifteen million cents, and eighteen basis points at or above fifteen million cents. Band membership is inclusive of the lower bound and exclusive of the upper bound, and the last band is unbounded above. The rate band applies to the whole price rather than marginally. Points are computed by multiplying the price by the selected band rate and dividing by one hundred thousand, rounding half up exactly once on that total; summing separately rounded per-band pieces is forbidden. When the member's card status is `ACTIVE` at the instant of settlement the rounded total is then doubled by multiplying it by two and rounding half up again. The result is finally capped by taking the lesser of it and twenty thousand points. The award price is the deal's `contract_price_cents` under the default settlement path, and the undiscounted hull-plus-motor-plus-trailer list value — exclusive of the rigging fee, the accessory discount and any trade credit — under the `points_on_msrp_value` variant, which applies when the deal was contracted while the member's card was `ACTIVE`. The list-value path is strictly greater than the contract price whenever any trade credit or accessory discount applied.

**`trade_in_credit`** computes `accepted_credit_cents` when an appraisal moves to `ACCEPTED`. It waterfall through two ordered arcs. The first arc is a rebate on the offer: when the tendering member's card status is `ACTIVE` the offer is multiplied by three hundred basis points; for every other card status the rebate rate is zero. The second arc is a title penalty: when the trade unit carries no `prior_title_no` the penalty rate is zero, and when it does carry one the offer is multiplied by two hundred basis points. Each arc is rounded half up on its own before it is combined. The credited value is the offer amount, plus the rounded rebate, minus the rounded penalty. The ordering is pinned: the rebate is rounded before it is added and the penalty is rounded before it is subtracted, so a prior-title trade tendered by an active-card member credits the offer plus three percent minus two percent with each percentage rounded independently.

**`permit_fee`** computes `fee_cents` for a permit sale, at creation and again on any batch correction that supplies a revised fee. It is a hybrid piecewise calculation: an integer-tiered base, a fractional land-access add-on, and exactly one rounding gate at the end. The term length is first bucketed — at most seven days lands in the first bucket, at most thirty in the second, at most three hundred sixty-five in the third, and anything longer in the fourth. The base is the sale's type-and-residency fee scaled by that bucket's multiplier: the first bucket is at par, the second at one hundred twenty-five percent, the third at par, and the fourth at one hundred fifty percent. A fishing resident permit pays thirty-two hundred cents, a non-resident eight thousand four hundred, a senior fifteen hundred and a youth eight hundred; a hunting resident permit pays forty-five hundred, a non-resident twelve thousand, a senior twenty-two hundred and a youth nine hundred; an archery-range resident permit pays twenty-one hundred, a non-resident thirty-nine hundred, a senior eleven hundred and a youth six hundred; and a boat registration pays sixty-five hundred cents for every residency class. Each public-land access unit adds a further one and a quarter cents. The base is carried as a whole number of ten-thousandths of a cent, the add-on stays fractional, and the two are summed and then divided by ten thousand and rounded half up exactly once. That single gate is what makes the ordering observable.

This calculation is also dual-variant. Under the exact-rational variant, used while the containing remittance batch is `OPEN` or `CORRECTED`, the fractional add-on is carried at full precision and rounded once at the end. Under the per-unit-rounded variant, used once a batch has been `SEALED` and then `CORRECTED` and the agency supplies a `revised_fee_cents`, the add-on is rounded per unit before summation; a fishing, resident, seven-day sale with three land-access units prices at thirty-two hundred three cents under the per-unit variant against thirty-two hundred four under the exact-rational variant.

**`attraction_dwell_charge`** computes `dwell_charge_minutes` when a session is confirmed or parked in the waitlist. The venue's `dwell_cost_minutes` is the per-guest rate. A band multiplier is selected from the party size, applied per band to the whole party rather than marginally: three when the party is ten or more, two when it is at least five but under ten, and one below five. A `POND_CLINIC` session with a party of fewer than three is pinned to a multiplier of one, because clinics never qualify for the group rate. The base is the per-guest rate multiplied by that band multiplier. A surcharge of twenty minutes is added when the slot's end instant is more than ninety minutes after its start instant, and nothing is added otherwise. The charge is the base plus the surcharge, in whole minutes, rounded half up if a fractional per-guest rate straddles a band. A session parked in the waitlist pins the value it would have had at its original request instant and does not recompute when it is later promoted.

**`event_dwell_charge`** computes an offering's `dwell_charge_minutes` at publish. A free demo takes the flat branch and is charged fifteen minutes regardless of any registration volume; the seat arithmetic is skipped entirely. Every other offering takes the capped two-phase branch: half the registration cap, rounded up, is charged at the base rate of half a minute per seat, and the remaining overflow half is charged at double that rate. The two halves are summed and the sum is rounded half up exactly once — rounding each half separately is forbidden. A cap of thirty therefore costs twenty-three minutes.

**`intake_project_charge`** computes `project_charge_cents` at each stage gate of a service intake. It is a strict function of the stage and the intake kind, evaluated in a fixed order. The base depends on the kind: forty-five thousand cents for taxidermy, twelve thousand for outfitting and eight thousand for boat service. A stage uplift is added — nothing at `INTAKE_QUOTED` or `PERMIT_VERIFIED`, fifteen thousand cents at `MATERIALS_ORDERED`, and twenty-five thousand cents at `CURING` and at `READY_FOR_PICKUP`. A specimen uplift is then added; the per-specimen rate is zero before `CURING` and thirty-five hundred cents at or after `CURING`, multiplied by the intake's `specimen_quantity`. The charge is the base plus the stage uplift plus the specimen uplift, and the result is finally clamped by taking the greater of it and the intake's `deposit_cents`, so a project charge can never fall below the deposit already collected. A six-specimen taxidermy intake that quotes forty-five thousand cents before its permit is verified later carries sixty-six thousand cents once it has entered curing.

**`attraction_posture`** derives `attraction_posture` from the store's committed minutes, its over-capacity venue count and its expired-waiver venue count, exactly as §2.7 describes. It is evaluated on every attraction session transition, on every event publish and at the ops-run checkpoint. It reads in integer minutes and performs no rounding. Its live and checkpoint variants differ in whether sessions with a future `slot_start_utc` are considered, as §2.5 describes.

**`no_show_strike`** computes `no_show_strikes` for a member at one attraction. It is evaluated at the ops-run checkpoint for a confirmed session whose slot ended with no check-in. The member carries an epoch instant in `strike_epoch_started_at`, and only no-show sessions whose `slot_start_utc` is at or after that epoch are counted, so completed sessions are excluded from the count even though they do move the epoch. The raw count is reduced by a decay of half a strike for every full ninety-day quarter that has elapsed between the epoch and the store clock's current instant; the decay is continuous within a quarter at half a strike per quarter. The effective count is the raw count minus the decay, floored to a whole number and floored again at zero so it can never go negative. A member whose epoch predates their three no-shows by two full quarters reads an effective count of two and is admitted. The stored `no_show_strikes` is that effective value. When the effective count reaches three, the member's next session request at that attraction is rejected. Two reset events restart the curve, and they are evaluated before the decay because they set the epoch: any session the member completes at that attraction resets `strike_epoch_started_at` to that session's start instant, and the activation of the member's club card resets it to the activation instant. A freshly enrolled member carries no epoch at all until one of those two events fires, so every no-show they accrue counts. Because the activation reset moves the epoch forward, no-shows recorded before an activation fall outside the window and count as zero regardless of how many there were.

**`club_points_apply`** replays the member's whole point ledger in chronological order and yields `points_balance` and `points_lifetime_earned`. The events are ordered by `created_at` ascending with `created_seq` ascending as the tie-break, and that ordering is mandatory because the replay is order-dependent. The replay walks the ledger accumulating two running totals. For `AWARD` and `ADJUST` events the event's `points_delta` is added to both the balance and the lifetime total. For `REDEEM` events the absolute value of the event's `points_delta` is subtracted from the balance and the lifetime total is untouched. For `REVERSAL` events the event's signed delta is added to the balance and the absolute value of that delta is subtracted from the lifetime total, because a reversal of an award is recorded as a negative movement. After each event both running totals are clamped at zero by taking the greater of the running total and zero, so the balance and the lifetime earned can never go negative at any intermediate step. The stored `points_balance` is the final balance and the stored `points_lifetime_earned` is the final lifetime total. Swapping two events that share a `created_at` but differ in `created_seq` changes the intermediate values and therefore can change where the clamp bites, which is why the tie-break is part of the contract. Redemption events whose originating award was itself reversed are excluded from the balance replay but still occupy their position in the ordering.

**`tier_threshold_eval`** runs after every point replay and derives `club_tier` and `redeemable_points`. It reads the member's `points_lifetime_earned` and compares it against three thresholds with inclusive lower bounds: lifetime of at least sixty thousand points reads `PATRON`, otherwise at least twenty-five thousand reads `GOLD`, otherwise at least eight thousand reads `SILVER`, and anything lower reads `BASE`. The comparison is inclusive, so exactly twenty-five thousand lifetime points is `GOLD` and twenty-four thousand nine hundred ninety-nine is `SILVER`. Downgrades are applied only against the member's current lifetime value, never against a historical maximum. `redeemable_points` is then computed as the member's `points_balance` minus a tier retention floor, clamped at zero: the floor is zero points at `BASE`, five hundred at `SILVER`, fifteen hundred at `GOLD` and four thousand at `PATRON`. A `GOLD` member holding two thousand points therefore has five hundred redeemable points, and a `BASE` member holding one hundred has one hundred. The tier ladder and its retention floors are re-derived whenever the member's card is activated, and the retention floor is the reason a higher tier does not simply mean more spendable points.

**`conservation_match_accrue`** computes the donation match a redemption generates and adds it to the member's annual accrual. It runs on every redemption event and again at the ops-run checkpoint. Points convert to redeemed value at twenty points to the dollar, so one point is worth five cents; the matchable value is the absolute point movement scaled to cents, and the proposed grant is that value multiplied by the sponsoring application's `donation_match_bps` rate in basis points, rounded half up. A cap gate then applies, and its boundary is deliberately strict: the grant is permitted when the proposed amount is at or below the remaining headroom, and only a proposed amount strictly greater than the headroom is clipped, to the headroom or to zero, whichever is greater. Because the gate is strictly greater than, reaching exactly the cap is permitted. The granted amount is written to the event's `donation_match_cents` and added to the member's `conservation_match_cents`, which accrues for the calendar year and is capped at five hundred cents. Only redemption events count toward the match base, and only for members whose `donation_opt_in` is true and whose club card is `ACTIVE`; awards, adjustments and reversals are excluded.

**`lead_time_actual`** computes `actual_lead_days` and `is_late` for a work order. It runs when an order moves to `COMPLETED` and again at the ops-run checkpoint. Actual lead time is the whole number of calendar days between the order's `started_at_utc` and its `completed_at_utc`, obtained by dividing the elapsed seconds by the seconds in a day and flooring. The order is late when its actual lead time is strictly greater than its `promised_lead_days`, so finishing at exactly the promised number of days is on time and finishing one day past it is late. For an order still `IN_PROGRESS` at a checkpoint the projection substitutes the store clock for the completion instant, which is why the same order can read late under the projection and on time once it actually completes.

**`permit_remit_snapshot`** computes the three monetary totals on a remittance batch. It runs when the batch is sealed and again on every subsequent correction. It reads the store's permit sales whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `VOIDED`, `REMITTED` or `CORRECTED` and whose `created_at` falls inside the batch's window, where the window is half-open: a sale created exactly at `window_start_utc` is included and a sale created exactly at `window_end_utc` is excluded. `gross_fee_cents` is the sum of those sales' `fee_cents` at the instant of sealing, and it never moves again. `interpretative_gross_cents` is the same sum recomputed live from the sales' current fees, so it tracks a correction. `remit_variance_cents` is the live total minus the frozen total, a signed delta written from the institution's perspective. The two sums are computed independently and neither is ever derived from the other, so the variance becomes non-zero exactly when a correction lands.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three pairs of operator identities must be distinct, and each pair is enforced as a hard rejection rather than a warning. The first guards the permit remittance: the operator sealing a batch, identified by the `X-User-ID` of the sealing request, must differ from the `issuer_admin_user_id` recorded on every sale inside the batch, because the clerk who sold a permit may not also certify the remittance of its fee. The second and third guard the club credit card: the `underwriter_user_id` recorded when an application enters underwriting must be non-empty and must differ from the `decision_user_id` presented when the decision is recorded, and that same distinctness is re-checked at decision time. All of these violations are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) — a permission failure, not a validation failure. The permit-counter form of the rule is enforced at seal time, and the club-card form is enforced both when the application moves into underwriting and when the decision is recorded; the `underwriter_user_id` is frozen at the moment of underwriting so that a later decision call cannot quietly present the same operator under a different field.

A fourth distinctness rule guards the loading dock. The receiving-manager cross-check recorded as `rm_cross_check_code` when an oversize load or a boat delivery orientation is released must be non-empty and must differ from the `member_id` receiving the goods, which is the value the member's own `X-User-ID` would carry. A release presented with an empty cross-check code, or with a code equal to the receiving member's identity, is rejected with `ERR_RECEIVING_CROSS_CHECK_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write paths in this system require the client to present the version it last observed, and both reject a mismatch rather than merging.

Creating an order fulfilment against a load bay booking requires the request to carry `expected_booking_version`, the client's last-observed `version` of the `LoadingBayBooking` the fulfilment is being created against. If the booking's current `version` has advanced past the presented value, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The stale expected version is the only signal the server needs: a booking that changed since the client last read it may already have been completed, checked in or cancelled, and the fulfilment record would otherwise claim a bay slot the client never saw. Creating a fulfilment against a booking also advances that booking's `version` by one, which is precisely what makes the guard meaningful for the next writer.

Creating a permit sale into a remittance batch requires the request to carry `expected_remit_batch_version`, the client's last-observed `version` of the target `PermitRemitBatch`. If the batch's current `version` has advanced past the presented value, the create is rejected with the same `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is the seal: a batch that changed since the client last read it may already have been sealed, and the seal already froze the gross fee snapshot over a set of sales that did not include the one now being added. A successful sale creation advances the containing batch's `version` by one.

No other write path in this system takes a version field, including the deal transitions. The deal mutation routes carry `expected_deal_version` — the contract, funding, refund and settle capabilities each require the client's last-observed `version` of the `BoatDeal`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every deal mutation advances the deal's `version`, so a client that has not re-read a deal since contracting it cannot fund, refund or settle it.

These are optimistic-concurrency guards framed in stale-version terms: the rejection is decided purely by comparing the presented expected version with the current stored version of the parent entity, and a client that has re-read the parent since its last write always passes.

### 2.11. Validation Order and Deterministic Failure

The system evaluates forty-one validation rules, and where more than one rule could fail on a single request the winner is fixed by priority rather than by chance. Lower priority numbers are evaluated first. Authentication and authorisation are checked before anything else: missing headers yield `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other rule, and an unauthorised role yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403) next. Existence and path resolution follow: a missing store yields `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a missing referenced row yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a child that does not belong to the named store yields `ERR_PARENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 404), and a bay number outside the store's bay range yields `ERR_BAY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). State checks then run: a target entity not in any source state the requested transition declares yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and a target entity already in a terminal state yields `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409).

After the state layer come the posture gates and then the safety rules, which outrank every fiscal and capacity rule. Repairing conflicts inside and between those bands, the following four orderings are pinned and must be honoured literally. A `p9` safety rejection for a missing waiver beats a `p18` fiscal rejection for the dwell budget, so a session request that is both waiver-less and over-budget fails as `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) and never mentions the budget. A `p7` terminal-state rejection beats a `p9` missing-waiver rejection, so re-submitting against a finished session fails as `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409) rather than as a waiver problem. A `p11` permit-verification rejection beats a `p17` venue-capacity rejection, so a taxidermy intake presented without a lawful-take permit fails as `ERR_PERMIT_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) even if the venue is also full. And a `p13` venue-overlap rejection beats a `p17` registration-cap rejection. Within a single priority band the ordering between the sibling rules is not observable — the two `p9` rules guard different endpoints, as do the `p16`, `p17`, `p19`, `p20` and `p22` pairs — so an implementation may evaluate them in either order.

The remaining bands are: capacity and referential rules at `p15` through `p17`; the dwell-budget gate at `p18`; the remittance and segregation fiscal rules at `p19`; the deal funding and settlement fiscal rules at `p20`; the credit-limit floor at `p21`; the term-length, deposit and immutability rules at `p22` and `p23`; the general request-shape rule at `p24`, which rejects absent required fields, invalid enum members and negative numeric fields with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — the one exception to the non-negativity rule being `PointEvent.points_delta`, which is signed by design because redemptions and reversals carry negative movements; and the stale-version check at `p25`, which is the last rule evaluated and which rejects a mismatched expected version with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Destination Floor: Venues, Sessions and the Shared Dwell Budget

A `StoreManager` stands up a destination store with a `name`, the two-letter `state_code` whose wildlife agency will receive the store's permit remittances and title filings, a `dealer_license_no`, a `dwell_capacity_minutes` budget and a `load_bays_total`. The store opens with its admission posture already `OPEN`, its committed minutes and free minutes both zero, and its remittance ledger and permit-remit epoch both zero. From that point the store's floor plan is assembled venue by venue. A `StoreManager` opens an attraction by declaring its `attraction_type`, its `capacity_units` — the number of guests who may occupy it at once — and its `dwell_cost_minutes`, the minutes each guest consumes per session. The venue's `requires_waiver` flag is not supplied by the caller; the server derives it from the type. The `AQUARIUM`, `MUSEUM` and `TAXIDERMY_GALLERY` types never require a waiver. The `FISHING_POND`, `ARCHERY_RANGE` and `SIMULATOR` types always do. A venue is created `PLANNED` and must be opened by its manager before it will accept sessions; opening it moves `status` from `PLANNED` to `OPEN` and immediately triggers a posture re-derivation for the store. The venue status enumeration is `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `CLOSED` — a store may narrow a venue to `RESTRICTED` for staffing reasons or close it outright with `CLOSED`, and only an `OPEN` venue will confirm a new session.

Venue sessions are the unit of floor occupancy. A session request carries the `attraction_id` of the venue, the `member_id` of the occupying club member, a `session_kind`, the `party_size`, the inclusive `slot_start_utc` and exclusive `slot_end_utc` of the occupancy interval, and — where the venue demands one — a `waiver_id`. The `session_kind` is one of `GENERAL_ADMISSION`, `ARCHERY_LANE`, `SIMULATOR` or `POND_CLINIC`. Every session is created in `REQUESTED` and is then resolved immediately by the server without a second client call: it confirms, it parks, or it fails. There is no client-driven confirm step for a venue session.

Confirmation is the normal path, and it requires the entire allocation precondition set to hold at the moment of the request. The venue itself must be `OPEN`, and the store's `attraction_posture` must be `OPEN` rather than `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. The posture gate is consulted before the venue's capacity and overlap measures: a store that has gone `FROZEN` refuses the request with `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) before any occupancy arithmetic runs, and a store that has gone `RESTRICTED` refuses a request that would otherwise confirm with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409). The charged party must fit, and the measure applied depends on whether the requested interval collides with a party already on the venue. When it collides with a `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS` session on the same venue, the two parties are judged against the venue's shared capacity — they may share the venue only while their combined size stays within its `capacity_units`, and a collision that would breach that shared ceiling is reported as an overlap (developed below) — and because the occupancy interval is half-open, a party whose slot begins exactly when another's ends is adjacent rather than overlapping and is permitted. When the requested interval collides with no existing party, the measure is the venue occupancy aggregate: the venue's current `in_use_units` plus the requested `party_size` may not exceed its `capacity_units`. When the venue requires a waiver, a `LiabilityWaiver` must exist for that member with an `activity_scope` matching the venue, must be in status `SIGNED`, and must have an `expires_on` instant at or after the store clock's current instant; §3.2 develops the waiver rules in full. Lane discipline applies to the staffed venues: an `ARCHERY_LANE` session may claim a `lane_no` no higher than twelve, and a `SIMULATOR` session may claim no higher than six. When the session is a breakout attached to an event, the parent offering's registration capacity must accommodate the party: the offering's `confirmed_count` plus the session's `party_size` must stay at or below its `registration_cap`, unless that cap is zero, which signals an uncapped free demo.

A request that overshoots the venue occupancy aggregate is not discarded — it parks. When the requested interval collides with no existing party on the venue, the venue's `in_use_units` plus the party size would exceed its `capacity_units`, and the store's posture is still `OPEN` rather than `FROZEN`, the session enters `WAITLIST_FALLBACK`, consumes no capacity and no dwell minutes at that instant, and waits. A request that instead collides with a confirmed party and would breach the shared capacity is never parked; it is refused outright as an overlap, as the next paragraph describes. The ops-run checkpoint re-evaluates these parked sessions, and the ordering among them is deliberate: a parked session is promoted from `WAITLIST_FALLBACK` to `CONFIRMED` during a checkpoint only when capacity has freed, the store is `OPEN` rather than merely unfrozen, and no earlier-created `WAITLIST_FALLBACK` session for the same venue on an overlapping interval is still waiting — the oldest claim on a contested slot wins. A promoted session adds its `party_size` to the venue's `in_use_units`, and its `dwell_charge_minutes` is pinned to the value it would have had at the original request instant rather than recomputed at promotion. A parked session that cannot be saved is cancelled instead: when the store's posture has become `FROZEN` at the checkpoint, or the session's `slot_start_utc` has already passed the store clock, it moves from `WAITLIST_FALLBACK` to `CANCELLED` with no side effects at all.

Committing capacity is a two-sided mutation. Confirming a session adds its `party_size` to the venue's `in_use_units`, computes its `dwell_charge_minutes` through the dwell-charge calculation, and then re-derives the store aggregate — the committed minutes move up, the free minutes move down, and the posture is re-evaluated and may tip from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED`. Releasing capacity runs the same machinery in reverse: cancelling a `CONFIRMED` session subtracts its `party_size` from the venue's `in_use_units`, recomputes the committed and free minute totals, and re-evaluates the posture, so a store that had gone `RESTRICTED` returns to `OPEN` once enough sessions release. Completions do the same. An attraction host checks a confirmed party in when its `slot_start_utc` has arrived, moving the session from `CONFIRMED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, and later completes it, moving `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED`. Completion is where the overrun is recorded: `overrun_minutes` is the store clock's current instant minus the session's `slot_end_utc`, expressed in whole minutes and clamped at zero, so finishing early records zero overrun rather than a negative figure. Both transitions release the party's capacity and re-derive the store aggregate.

Two boundary rules catch malformed and impossible bookings before any of that logic runs. A session whose `slot_end_utc` is not strictly after its `slot_start_utc` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422), with equality rejected at the boundary — a zero-length occupancy is not a valid session. And a lane number that exceeds its venue's ceiling is rejected with `ERR_LANE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) rather than silently clamped. Capacity shortfalls that are not waitlistable surface as errors rather than parking: a fresh, non-overlapping request whose party would push the venue past its `capacity_units` is refused with `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) — the outcome when the store's posture is `RESTRICTED` — while a confirmation attempt made while the store is `RESTRICTED` without such a shortfall surfaces as `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409). A request made while the store is `FROZEN` surfaces as `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409), decided before the venue's capacity and overlap measures run. An overlapping-interval collision that would breach the shared capacity is reported with `ERR_SESSION_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Session listings may be filtered by `attraction_id`, `member_id`, `waiver_id`, `store_id` or `status`.

### 3.2. Conduct on the Floor: Waivers, No-Shows and Strike Decay

Liability waivers are signed once and presented many times. A waiver is recorded against a `member_id` with an `activity_scope` and the `signed_at_utc` supplied by the signing party, plus an optional `waiver_term_days` that defaults to three hundred sixty-five days when it is not supplied. The scope is one of `CATCH_AND_RELEASE_POND`, `ARCHERY_RANGE`, `SIMULATOR` or `BOAT_DELIVERY_ORIENTATION`; pond, archery and simulator scopes cover the three venues that require waivers, and the boat-delivery scope covers the orientation briefing that accompanies a boat handover. At creation the server computes `expires_on` as the signature instant plus the term in whole days; the field is derived and is not accepted from the caller. A new waiver for a member and scope is not additive: the moment a fresh `SIGNED` waiver is written for a member and scope that already has one, the older row moves from `SIGNED` to `SUPERSEDED`, so exactly one live waiver per member per scope is ever valid. Expiry is a checkpoint concern: during an ops-run sweep, any `SIGNED` waiver whose `expires_on` has fallen before the store clock's current instant moves to `EXPIRED`. Waivers are listed with optional filters on `member_id`, `activity_scope` and `store_id`, and the sessions and the fulfilments presented under a given waiver can each be listed in their own right.

Two gates consume waivers, and both fail closed. A venue that requires a waiver will not confirm a session presented without one: a request that names no `waiver_id`, or names one whose scope does not match the venue, is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A boat-delivery orientation release presented without a waiver of the boat-delivery scope fails the same way, with the same error, even though the release is a different workflow entirely. Even when a waiver is present, it must be live: a waiver that is not `SIGNED`, or whose `expires_on` instant has arrived at or passed the store clock's current instant, is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). The boundary is inclusive at the expiry side, so a waiver expiring exactly on the current instant is no longer live; this same liveness test guards both the venue session and the boat-delivery orientation release. The expiry check is evaluated against the logical clock, so an operation performed while the clock sits at the store's startup instant of the first of September 2026 will accept a waiver that a later checkpoint would expire.

No-show conduct is tracked per member per attraction and decays on a slow curve. A `CONFIRMED` session that reaches a checkpoint with its `slot_end_utc` already behind the store clock and no check-in on record is marked `NO_SHOW`: the party's capacity is released, and the member's `no_show_strikes` at that venue increments. The same outcome awaits a session that was never checked in but had already been marked `IN_PROGRESS` and left open — a completed-but-abandoned occupancy also lands on `NO_SHOW` and also increments the strike count. Overrunning a completed session is punished as well: when a session completes with `overrun_minutes` strictly greater than thirty, an additional strike is added to every other session of the same member at the same attraction. The count that matters at request time is the effective count produced by the strike calculation in §2.8.1 — the raw no-show count since the member's `strike_epoch_started_at`, reduced by half a strike for each elapsed ninety-day quarter, floored and clamped at zero. When that effective count reaches three, the member's next session request at that attraction is rejected with `ERR_NO_SHOW_STRIKE_LIMIT` (HTTP 422). The member is not permanently barred: the epoch moves. Completing any session at the attraction resets `strike_epoch_started_at` to that session's start instant, and activating the member's club card resets it to the activation instant, which pushes earlier no-shows out of the counting window so they are never counted again. A freshly enrolled member has no epoch recorded at all until one of those two events fires.

### 3.3. The Store Calendar: Offerings, Registrations and Workshop Materials

The store calendar is run by an `EventsCoordinator`, and it is the second consumer of the store's dwell budget. An offering is created with an `offering_kind` of `SEMINAR`, `KIDS_WORKSHOP` or `FREE_DEMO`, a `title` for the printed calendar, an inclusive `starts_at_utc` and exclusive `ends_at_utc`, a `registration_cap`, and — when the event is hosted in a venue rather than on the open store floor — an `attraction_id`. Offerings begin in `DRAFT`. Publishing one is gated on four conditions. The cap must be positive for a seminar or a kids workshop and must be exactly zero for a free demo, so the cap field doubles as the signal for whether the event is capped at all. The end instant must be strictly after the start instant, with equality rejected as `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). When an `attraction_id` is supplied, the venue must exist, and its `status` must be `PLANNED` or `OPEN` — a venue that has been closed for the season cannot host a new event. And a published offering may not share a venue slot with another published offering: a `PUBLISHED` offering on the same `attraction_id` whose interval overlaps the new one is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_VENUE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), with zero tolerance for adjacency, so an event that begins exactly when another ends is still a conflict in the calendar even though the same adjacency is fine for a venue session.

Publishing commits dwell minutes, and the commit is where the budget gate bites. Publication computes the offering's `dwell_charge_minutes` through the event dwell-charge calculation — fifteen flat minutes for a free demo, and for a capped event the sum of half the cap at the per-seat base rate plus the overflow half at double rate, rounded once. The charge is then added to the store's `dwell_committed_minutes` and the free minutes recomputed. If that recomputation would leave the free minutes below zero, the publication is refused outright with `ERR_DWELL_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) and no field on the offering changes at all — the event stays `DRAFT` and the budget is untouched. A successful publication also re-evaluates the store's posture, which may tip from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` as the event's minutes land.

Registration is where the cap is enforced, and it is enforced late by design. A member registers with a `member_id` and a desired number of `seats`; the registration is created in `REGISTERED` and consumes no seat inventory at creation, because capacity is only spent at confirmation. An `EventsCoordinator` then confirms the seats, which is permitted only while the offering is `PUBLISHED` and only while the offering's current `confirmed_count` plus the requested `seats` stays at or below the `registration_cap` when that cap is positive. The same cap governs the registration itself: a registration whose `seats` would carry the offering's confirmed count past its `registration_cap` while that cap is positive is refused at creation with `ERR_REGISTRATION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409), so the ceiling is enforced on both the create and the confirm path. A confirmation that would breach the cap is not silently trimmed either; it is refused with the same error. A confirmation attempt made when no seat inventory remains is not rejected either — when the cap is positive and the confirmed count plus the requested seats would exceed it, the registration is instead parked and demoted from `REGISTERED` to `WAITLISTED` automatically, consuming no seats. A successful confirmation adds the seats to the offering's `confirmed_count`.

Kids workshops carry a materials constraint that reaches into the service department. When the materials a `KIDS_WORKSHOP` requires exceed the store's available workshop-material stock, the confirmation does not simply fail: the server synthesises an `OUTFITTING` work order to cover the shortfall, and the registration is parked in `WAITLISTED` instead of confirming, so the workshop's materials are brought in through the same service pipeline that handles any other order. The check is relative to the store's declared stock: neither the offering nor the store publishes a workshop-material quantity as a required field, so a store that has declared no such stock has nothing the workshop's seats can exceed and the shortfall branch is simply never reached for it. The registration can be promoted later. During an ops-run checkpoint, a `WAITLISTED` registration moves to `CONFIRMED` when the offering's confirmed count plus its seats now fits under the cap and no earlier-created `WAITLISTED` registration for the same offering is still waiting; a promotion adds the seats to the confirmed count.

Cancellation flows in both directions. A registrant or a coordinator may cancel a registration from `REGISTERED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED`. When the prior status was `CONFIRMED` the seats are given back to the offering; and when the erstwhile registration covered at least two seats and had been confirmed, the member is credited two hundred fifty points as a consolation, recorded as a manual adjustment against their ledger rather than as a settlement award. When an entire offering is cancelled, the cascade is total: every registration still in `REGISTERED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `CANCELLED`, each of those registrations grants its member the same two hundred fifty points where it covered two or more seats, the offering's confirmed count is rewound to zero, and the offering's dwell charge is released back to the store budget. An offering that reaches its end instant is instead completed by the checkpoint: confirmed registrations whose `attendance_marked` flag was set true at check-in move to `ATTENDED`, confirmed registrations whose flag was left false move to `CANCELLED`, the offering's dwell charge is released, and each attended registration that covered at least one seat awards its member one hundred points through an award event. Attendance is thus captured by a flag supplied when the member is checked in at the event, and the difference between a marked and an unmarked confirmed registration is the difference between an award and a cancellation.

### 3.4. The Boat Dealership: Contracting, Funding and Four Independent Dimensions

A boat deal is the most stateful object in the system. It carries four independent dimensions that advance on separate clocks, plus money fields recomputed on every mutation. The commercial `deal_stage` runs `QUOTED`, `CONTRACTED`, `DELIVERED`, `SETTLED` and is cyclic — a delivered deal can be dragged back to `CONTRACTED`. The `funding_status` dimension runs `UNFUNDED`, `PENDING`, `FUNDED`, `REFUNDED`. The `title_status` dimension runs `NOT_STARTED`, `FILED`, `ISSUED`, `REJECTED`. The `rigging_status` dimension runs `NOT_STARTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETE`. All four start at their initial values when a deal is quoted: `deal_stage` `QUOTED`, `funding_status` `UNFUNDED`, `title_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `rigging_status` `NOT_STARTED`. Every deal mutation recomputes the money and advances the deal's `version`.

A `DealDeskClerk` quotes a deal by naming the `member_id` of the purchasing club member, the `boat_unit_id` of the hull under contract, an optional `trade_in_appraisal_id` where the member is tendering a trade, and an optional `deposit_cents` applied at contracting. Quoting computes the provisional contract price under the quote variant — the rigging fee excluded and the tier credit halved — and leaves `title_filing_id` null, because no filing exists while the deal is `QUOTED`. Boat units themselves are received into inventory with a `stock_no` that must be unique within the store, a `model_name`, a `hull_id_no` that must also be unique within the store, a `unit_price_cents`, a `unit_condition` of `NEW`, `USED` or `DEMO`, and optional `motor_price_cents` and `trailer_price_cents` that default to zero when the unit is a bare hull or is rigged without a trailer. A used unit also carries its `prior_title_no` from the previous certificate of title; a new unit carries none. Units enter inventory `AVAILABLE`, and a clerk may reprice or re-describe a unit before it is committed by amending its `model_name`, `unit_price_cents`, `motor_price_cents` or `trailer_price_cents` — which re-prices every deal already referencing that unit. Duplicate inventory identifiers are refused: a `stock_no` already present in the store fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOCK_NO` (HTTP 409) and a repeated `hull_id_no` fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_HIN` (HTTP 409).

Contracting is the pivot of the commercial side, and it is gated four ways. The hull must be `AVAILABLE`. When a `trade_in_appraisal_id` is present, that appraisal must be `ACCEPTED` and must belong to the same member who is buying — a trade tendered by one member cannot fund another member's deal. The buyer's `club_card_status` must be `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `NONE`, which is to say a suspended card does not block a purchase and a declined application does not either; only the states outside that set are refused. And the store must not be `FROZEN`. Contracting then does a great deal at once. The hull moves from `AVAILABLE` to `DEAL_PENDING`, so the unit is held but not yet sold and can still be repriced. The `deal_stage` moves from `QUOTED` to `CONTRACTED`. The title work begins automatically: the server creates a `TitleFiling` for the hull with a filing kind of `BOAT_TITLE` and a `state_code` equal to the store's own, and stamps the new filing's identifier into the deal's `title_filing_id` while moving the deal's `title_status` from `NOT_STARTED` to `FILED`. The contract price is recomputed under the contracted variant — the rigging fee restored and the full tier credit applied — and that contracted reading then stands for the rest of the deal's life, because contracting is the transition that leaves `QUOTED` and no later deal mutation, delivery included, returns it to the quote variant; the fee and tax relay then runs on the settled price, and finally `balance_due_cents` is recomputed as the contract price plus the total fee minus the funded amount minus the deposit, with the settlement variance excluded from the balance by design. The store's `version` is advanced by one because the deal's contracting touched the store aggregate. Contracting carries `expected_deal_version` and is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) if the deal has moved since the client last read it.

Funding is a `FinanceAdmin` concern and is deliberately asymmetric. Recording a funding amount in the `amount_cents` field below the deal's current balance is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 422) at the exact boundary: an amount equal to the balance is accepted, an amount one cent below it is not. A shortfall-recorded amount that does satisfy the other preconditions nevertheless advances the funding dimension to `PENDING` — a partial payment is recorded but does not fund the deal. Specifically, when the funding dimension is `UNFUNDED`, the deal's commercial stage is `CONTRACTED` or `DELIVERED`, and the amount is strictly below the balance due, the dimension moves from `UNFUNDED` to `PENDING` and nothing else changes. When the funding dimension is `UNFUNDED` and the stage is `CONTRACTED` or `DELIVERED` and the amount is at or above the balance due, the dimension moves straight from `UNFUNDED` to `FUNDED`, the recorded `funded_amount_cents` is set to the amount presented, and the balance is recomputed down to zero. When the dimension is already `PENDING` and the recording amount is at or above the current balance due, the dimension moves from `PENDING` to `FUNDED`, `funded_amount_cents` is set, and the balance is recomputed. A refund is available to a `FinanceAdmin` while the dimension is `FUNDED` and the deal has not yet settled: it moves the dimension from `FUNDED` to `REFUNDED`, zeroes `funded_amount_cents`, and recomputes the balance, which now stands unbacked again. The gate is the funding dimension rather than a terminal-stage marker: a refund is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) whenever the deal's `funding_status` is not `FUNDED`, and a `SETTLED` deal — outside the stage the refund transition declares — is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Delivery is automatic, and it waits on the other three dimensions. A deal moves from `CONTRACTED` to `DELIVERED` only when `funding_status` is `FUNDED`, `title_status` is `ISSUED` and `rigging_status` is `COMPLETE` — all three simultaneously. The server does not require a client to ask for the delivery; the transition fires on the next call made against the deal once the last of the three lands, because a deal is never in a terminal state and there is therefore always a further step to take. Delivery flips the hull from `DEAL_PENDING` to `SOLD`, which in turn obliges every work order attached to that hull to proceed toward delivery, recomputes `settlement_variance_cents` as the final basis minus the provisional basis, and advances the store's version. Settlement is the final commercial act, restricted to a `FinanceAdmin`, and it requires the deal to be `DELIVERED` with a balance due at or below zero — a zero balance settles and a one-cent balance does not, and the refusal is `ERR_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 422). Settlement moves the stage from `DELIVERED` to `SETTLED`, creates an award event carrying the points computed by the settlement award calculation, replays the member's point ledger and re-derives their tier and redeemable balance, and evaluates the conservation donation match. Because the settlement's points increment can carry the member's lifetime total across a tier boundary, a settlement that lifts a member with no club card past a threshold also attaches a pending club card application to them, which is how the dealership feeds the club programme. Both funding and settlement carry `expected_deal_version`.

### 3.5. Trade-Ins, Title Work and the Cyclic Revert

Trade units enter the dealership through appraisals. A `DealDeskClerk` opens an appraisal against a `member_id`, the `boat_unit_id` of the traded unit, and an `offer_amount_cents`; the appraisal is created `PENDING` with its `accepted_credit_cents` still zero and its `appraised_at_utc` stamped by the server. A pending appraisal may be revised — its `offer_amount_cents` and its description may be amended freely — but its `appraised_at_utc` is immutable from the moment it is written: any write that attempts to rewrite that server-stamped instant is rejected with `ERR_TYPE_IMMUTABLE` (HTTP 422) and nothing else on the row changes, even if the same request also carried a legitimate revision to the offer. An appraisal is accepted by a clerk, which moves it from `PENDING` to `ACCEPTED` and computes its `accepted_credit_cents` through the trade-in credit calculation: the offer, plus a three percent rebate when the tendering member's card is `ACTIVE` and nothing otherwise, minus a two percent penalty when the traded unit carries a `prior_title_no` and nothing otherwise, with each arc rounded independently before combination. The credit is what a deal can then apply. Acceptance also supersedes any earlier accepted appraisal for the same `boat_unit_id`, since a unit can only be traded once. An appraisal may equally be declined, with a `decline_reason` recorded, moving it to `DECLINED` and leaving the member's club card status untouched. Appraisals are listed with filters on `boat_unit_id`, `member_id`, `store_id` and `status`, and the deals that consumed a given appraisal can be listed in their own right with a filter on `deal_stage`.

Title work runs alongside the deal on its own dimension. A filing is opened against a `boat_unit_id` with a `filing_kind` of `BOAT_TITLE`, `MOTOR_TITLE`, `TRAILER_REGISTRATION` or `LIEN_RELEASE`, and with a `state_code` that must equal the originating store's own `state_code`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TITLE_STATE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The same error guards a second rule: a `LIEN_RELEASE` filing may not be opened against a unit that has no `prior_title_no`, because a brand-new hull carries no lien to release. Filings begin `DRAFT`. A `TitleClerk` submits a draft to the agency, stamping `filed_at_utc` and moving the filing from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`. The agency then either issues or rejects. Issuance records the agency-issued `agency_reference`, stamps `acknowledged_at_utc`, moves the filing from `SUBMITTED` to `ISSUED` and, when the filing is the deal's `BOAT_TITLE` filing, advances the deal's `title_status` from `FILED` to `ISSUED`; if the deal's commercial stage is `CONTRACTED` and its funding dimension is `FUNDED` and its rigging dimension is `COMPLETE`, that same issuance is what completes the trio and the deal is auto-delivered on the next call against it. Issuance is performed by a `TitleClerk` or an `AgencyOperator`. The state-code and lien-release rules are checked both when a filing is opened and when it is issued.

Rejection is the event that makes this state machine non-linear. An agency may reject a filing that is `SUBMITTED` or `ISSUED` — the `rejection_reason` is recorded and the filing moves to `REJECTED`. When the rejected filing is the unit's `BOAT_TITLE` filing and the deal has already been delivered, the deal is dragged backwards on the cyclic edge: `deal_stage` moves from `DELIVERED` back to `CONTRACTED`, and a great deal unwinds with it. The deal's `title_status` moves from `ISSUED` to `REJECTED` and then immediately back to `NOT_STARTED`, so a fresh filing is permitted and the deal is not stranded in a terminal-looking title state. The hull's `status` moves from `SOLD` back to `DEAL_PENDING`, which re-opens the unit's rigging work: the work order whose `boat_unit_id` is this unit, whose `order_kind` is `RIGGING` and whose `status` is `DELIVERED` is returned to `COMPLETED`. Because that order has left `DELIVERED`, the deal's rigging dimension drops with it, from `COMPLETE` back to `SCHEDULED`. The settlement variance is reset to zero. The store's version advances. The commercial consequence is plain: a rejected title forces the deal back one stage rather than forward, and the buyer's money and the dealer's rigging work are both held against a deal that the state will not yet accept.

### 3.6. Rigging, Service Work Orders and the Delivery Handover

Work orders are the service department's unit of labour and they are the machinery that completes the rigging dimension of a deal. A `ServiceWriter` or `ServiceTechnician` opens an order with an `order_kind` of `RIGGING`, `OVERSIZE_LOADING`, `TAXIDERMY`, `OUTFITTING` or `POND_MAINTENANCE`, a `boat_unit_id` for the unit being worked, a customer-promised `promised_lead_days` in whole days, and an optional `deal_id` linking the order to a boat deal — the `boat_deal_id` field is the alias column the deal-side order listing reads, and counter service work runs with the deal link left null. Orders begin `INTAKE`. At most one `RIGGING` order may be open at a time for the same deal: a second non-terminal rigging order for a deal that already has one is refused with `ERR_RIGGING_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409).

An order advances through `INTAKE`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `COMPLETED`, and is handed over by moving from `COMPLETED` to `DELIVERED`; `CANCELLED` is reachable from the two earliest states. Scheduling an intake order is blocked while a linked fulfilment is not ready: the scheduling call requires every order fulfilment whose `pre_service_order_id` points at this order to already be in `READY_FOR_RELEASE`, and a bypass attempt is refused with `ERR_FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409) — a fulfilment may never be stepped over. Scheduling also requires that no other `RIGGING` order for the same hull is currently `IN_PROGRESS`. When the scheduled order is a rigging order linked to a deal, the deal's `rigging_status` advances from `NOT_STARTED` to `SCHEDULED` in the same operation. Starting work is a technician's act and requires the store not to be `FROZEN`, because a frozen store is not operating; it stamps `started_at_utc` and moves the order to `IN_PROGRESS`. An order that is already `IN_PROGRESS` is completed by a technician, which stamps `completed_at_utc` and evaluates the lead time immediately: `actual_lead_days` is the whole days floored from start to completion, and `is_late` is true when that figure is strictly greater than `promised_lead_days`. A late completion does not merely flag the order — the store's overdue projection for its in-progress work advances by the difference between the actual and promised lead times, and the completion instants of the other in-progress orders are preserved while that projection moves.

Handing over a completed order is what frees everything downstream. Delivering a completed order moves it to `DELIVERED`, and when the order is a rigging order linked to a deal, the deal's `rigging_status` advances from `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETE` — which may be the last of the three preconditions the deal was waiting on, in which case the deal is auto-delivered against the next call. Delivery of an `OVERSIZE_LOADING` order additionally requires the linked `LoadingBayBooking` to have been checked in: such an order may only be handed over once its booking is `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`, and a hand-over attempted before the check-in is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). And delivery unblocks the fulfilment: the order fulfilment whose `pre_service_order_id` is this order now has at least one satisfied precondition, so a later release call on it will be accepted. Cancelling an order is possible from `INTAKE` or `SCHEDULED` only; when the intake collected a deposit, the cancellation refunds it as points — a manual adjustment of the deposit divided by ten and floored, so a five-thousand-cent deposit yields five hundred points — rather than as cash.

Service intakes are the long-lead counter: taxidermy mounts, outfitting projects and boat service drop-offs, each with a `quoted_lead_days` turnaround and a `deposit_cents` collected up front. An intake is taken against a `member_id`, a `service_order_id` for the order that will fulfil it, an `intake_kind` of `TAXIDERMY`, `OUTFITTING` or `BOAT_SERVICE`, a `specimen_quantity` counting the specimens or garments received, and an optional `species_code` and `permit_sale_id`. It begins `INTAKE_QUOTED` with its project charge computed at that stage. The intake then walks `INTAKE_QUOTED`, `PERMIT_VERIFIED`, `MATERIALS_ORDERED`, `CURING`, `READY_FOR_PICKUP` and on to `RELEASED` or `ABANDONED`, and its `project_charge_cents` is recomputed at every gate through the intake charge calculation, which adds the stage uplift, adds a specimen uplift of thirty-five hundred cents per specimen only once curing has begun, and clamps the total up to at least the deposit already collected so the charge can never fall below what the member has paid.

The permit gate comes first. Advancing a taxidermy intake past `INTAKE_QUOTED` requires a `permit_sale_id` referencing a permit sale whose `permit_type` is `HUNTING` or `ARCHERY_RANGE` and whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `REMITTED` — a lawful-take permit, because a mount may not be made of an unlawfully taken animal. An intake of any other kind presented with a non-null `permit_sale_id` is equally refused, since non-hunting intakes have no permit to present. The refusal is `ERR_PERMIT_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), and it is enforced both when the intake is created and when the permit verification step is taken, so a taxidermy intake cannot be taken into the shop without its permit at either gate. Verification also extends the linked work order's promised lead time where the recomputed project charge is positive, and re-evaluates that order's lateness against the new promise. Advancing to `MATERIALS_ORDERED` requires the intake's `deposit_cents` to be at least five thousand cents inclusive; a deposit below that floor is refused with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_DEPOSIT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Advancing into `CURING` additionally requires that a taxidermy intake's `specimen_quantity` be at most twenty, and entering curing pins the linked order's `promised_lead_days` to the quoted lead time plus the twenty-one-day curing extension. Marking a project ready moves the intake to `READY_FOR_PICKUP` and completes the linked work order, stamping its completion instant. Releasing it to the member requires the linked order to be `COMPLETED` and moves the intake to `RELEASED`. An intake left uncollected is abandoned by the checkpoint once ninety days have passed since the linked order's completion: the intake moves from `READY_FOR_PICKUP` to `ABANDONED`, the deposit is forfeited rather than refunded, and the project charge remains payable. Intake descriptions — `species_code`, `specimen_quantity` and `quoted_lead_days` — may be revised before the gates advance, but `specimen_quantity` freezes once `MATERIALS_ORDERED` has been reached: a later write to it is rejected with `ERR_TYPE_IMMUTABLE` (HTTP 422), the same error the appraisal timestamp raises. Intakes may be listed filtered by `service_order_id`, `permit_sale_id`, `member_id`, `store_id` or `status`, and the intakes verified against a given permit sale can be listed directly.

Oversize merchandise and boat handover share the loading dock. A `ReceivingManager` reserves a bay with a `bay_no`, a `member_id`, the `service_order_id` of the oversize handling order, and the inclusive `booked_start_utc` and exclusive `booked_end_utc` of the reservation. The bay must exist inside the store's `load_bays_total`, and the reservation must not collide: another booking on the same store and same bay number whose interval overlaps the requested interval widened by a fifteen-minute turnover buffer at each end is refused with `ERR_BAY_BOOKING_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) — adjacency without the buffer is permitted, so a back-to-back slot is legal only if the fifteen minutes between the end of one and the start of the next are respected. Bookings begin `RESERVED`. A booking whose end instant is not strictly after its start instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). When the load arrives, the manager checks the booking in, moving it from `RESERVED` to `CHECKED_IN`, and that check-in is what lets the bay's linked `OVERSIZE_LOADING` work order move from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS`. Bookings are listed with filters on `store_id`, `bay_no`, `member_id`, `service_order_id` and `status`, and the bookings on a single bay can be listed directly by bay number.

The release record closes the loop. A `ReceivingManager` creates an order fulfilment for an `OVERSIZE_CARRYOUT` or a `BOAT_DELIVERY_ORIENTATION` with a `member_id`, a `pre_service_order_id` naming the rigging or handling order that must complete first, a `booking_id` naming the bay reservation the handover consumes, an optional `waiver_id` for the boat orientation, and a required `expected_booking_version` carrying the client's last-observed version of that booking. The fulfilment is created `READY_FOR_RELEASE`, and the create advances the booking's version by one. Creating a fulfilment against a booking whose version has advanced past the presented `expected_booking_version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because a booking that changed since the client last read it may already have been completed or cancelled and the fulfilment would otherwise claim a bay slot the client never saw. Release is gated four ways. The pre-service order must actually be `DELIVERED`; a release attempted while it is not is refused with `ERR_FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409). A boat delivery orientation must present a `waiver_id` for the boat-delivery scope, and failing to do so is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), just as a waiver-requiring venue session would be. An oversize carry-out must have its booking already `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`. And the `rm_cross_check_code` supplied must be non-empty and must differ from the receiving member's identity, or the release is refused with `ERR_RECEIVING_CROSS_CHECK_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A successful release stamps `fulfilled_at_utc`, moves the fulfilment from `READY_FOR_RELEASE` to `RELEASED`, and — when the service order the fulfilment consumed is now delivered — closes the booking from `CHECKED_IN` to `COMPLETED`. A pending fulfilment may instead be cancelled by a manager or a `PlatformAdmin`, which moves it to `CANCELLED` and cascades the referenced booking to `CANCELLED` as well, whether it was `RESERVED` or `CHECKED_IN`. Fulfilments are listed with filters on `pre_service_order_id`, `booking_id`, `member_id`, `waiver_id`, `store_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. The Permit Counter and the State Remittance Ledger

The service counter sells seasonal licences as an agent of the state, and every sale it makes creates a remittance obligation. A `PermitClerk` records a sale against a `member_id` with a `permit_type` of `FISHING`, `HUNTING`, `ARCHERY_RANGE` or `BOAT_REGISTRATION`, a `residency_class` of `RESIDENT`, `NON_RESIDENT`, `SENIOR` or `YOUTH`, a `term_days` length, and an optional `land_access_units` add-on that defaults to zero. The sale's `fee_cents` is computed by the permit-fee calculation, whose term bucketing and single rounding gate are specified in §2.8.1; the pricing inputs are taken from the request and never re-derived from the permit's issue date or any stored date. Two boundary rules guard the request shape. The `term_days` must be at least one, so a zero-day term is rejected at the boundary, and `land_access_units` may not be negative; either violation yields `ERR_INVALID_TERM_DAYS` (HTTP 422). A sale is created in `ISSUED` with its lifetime fee already computed.

Each sale belongs to a remittance batch, and the sale create carries both the `permit_remit_batch_id` of the batch it is joining and the `expected_remit_batch_version` the client last observed for that batch. A create whose presented version has been overtaken by the batch's current version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the batch may have been sealed in the interim and the seal already froze a gross fee snapshot over a set of sales that did not include this one. A successful create increments the containing batch's version by one and re-derives the batch's live totals, so the next writer's expectation is immediately out of date unless they re-read. The clerk's identity is captured on the sale twice and for two different purposes: the `agent_user_id` supplied on the create is the opaque operator identifier of the issuing clerk, and the server independently stamps `issuer_admin_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` at sale time. The second stamp is the one the segregation rules read later. The clerk who issues a sale must not be the operator who seals that sale's batch.

A remittance batch is a window over the store's permit sales, opened by a `RemitAdmin` with an inclusive `window_start_utc` and an exclusive `window_end_utc`. A batch is created `OPEN` with its three monetary totals at zero. Sealing is the tamper-evident act. It requires at least one eligible sale in the window — a sale whose `store_id` is the batch's store, whose `status` is `ISSUED`, and whose `created_at` falls inside the half-open window — and a batch presented with an empty window is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_REMIT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Sealing also enforces the segregation rule: the `X-User-ID` of the sealing operator, recorded as `remitter_admin_user_id` on the batch and stamped onto every sale in it, must differ from the `issuer_admin_user_id` of every sale inside the batch. A seal attempted by an administrator who recorded one of the batch's own sales is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). When those gates pass, the seal takes the frozen snapshot: `gross_fee_cents` is the sum of the sales' `fee_cents` at that instant and never moves again, `interpretative_gross_cents` is the same sum recomputed live, and `remit_variance_cents` is the live total minus the frozen total. The batch moves from `OPEN` to `SEALED`, and the store's `remit_ledger_cents` gains the frozen gross fee, so the store's ledger is the accumulated sum of every sealed batch's frozen total.

From `SEALED` the batch is transmitted to the agency, moving to `TRANSMITTED`, and the agency then either acknowledges it or corrects it. Acknowledgement records the agency's `agency_ack_no`, which must be a non-empty string, moves the batch from `TRANSMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`, and — the part that matters most — cascades every sale in the batch: each sale in status `ISSUED` or `CORRECTED` moves to `REMITTED`. The store's `permit_remit_epoch` counter increments by one, which is the same counter the ops-run checkpoint advances and which therefore marks the batch acknowledgement as a ledger-relevant event at store scope. Acknowledgement is the terminal state for a clean batch.

Correction is the other branch, and it is a self-loop by design. The agency may reject a transmitted batch with a revised fee for a named sale, presenting the `sale_id` and a `revised_fee_cents`. The correction is permitted while the batch is `TRANSMITTED`, and at least one sale in the batch must have a revised fee of at least zero — the revision is priced through the per-unit-rounded variant of the permit fee rather than the exact-rational one. The batch moves from `TRANSMITTED` to `CORRECTED`, and for each sale whose revised fee was supplied the sale's `fee_cents` is overwritten with the revised figure and the sale moves from `REMITTED` to `CORRECTED`. The live total is recomputed over the corrected fees while the frozen total stays exactly where the seal left it, and the variance is recomputed as the difference. Further correction rounds are possible: a batch that is already `CORRECTED` accepts another correction, provided the referenced `sale_id` genuinely belongs to that batch, and the named sale's fee is overwritten again while its status stays `CORRECTED`. A batch may therefore take several correction rounds, one sale per call, each round pushing the variance further from the frozen snapshot. When a corrected batch is finally acknowledged, it moves to `ACCEPTED` and each of its `CORRECTED` sales moves to `REMITTED`, with each sale's fee frozen at its corrected lifetime value; a batch that was corrected and then accepted is the only path by which a corrected sale is ever settled.

Individual sales have their own state life. An `ISSUED` sale may be voided by a permit clerk or a platform administrator, moving it to `VOIDED`, but only while it does not belong to a batch that has been `SEALED`, `TRANSMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` — once a batch is sealed the sale inside it is out of the clerk's hands, because the snapshot is already taken. A sale has one further wrinkle: when the void happens after the sale's batch was already corrected, the void recomputes the sale's `fee_cents` through the permit-fee calculation and recomputes the parent batch's live total and variance, so a void is not merely a status flip in that case. Voiding is reachable only from `ISSUED`; a sale that is already `REMITTED` or `CORRECTED` cannot be voided. The two automatic sale transitions belong to the batch: a sale in `ISSUED` moves to `REMITTED` when its batch is acknowledged, and a sale in `REMITTED` moves to `CORRECTED` when its batch is corrected, with the recomputed fee feeding the batch's live total for comparison against the frozen snapshot. Sales are listed with filters on `permit_remit_batch_id`, `store_id`, `member_id` and `status`, and batches with filters on `store_id` and `status`.

### 3.8. The Conservation Club: Points, Tiers, the Donation Match and Club Cards

Enrolling at the club desk is open to a walk-in. A member is created against a store with an `email` that must be unique within that store and a `full_name`, plus an optional `donation_opt_in` flag that defaults to false. A duplicate email in the same store is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_EMAIL` (HTTP 409). A new member opens with their club card status `NONE`, their tier `BASE`, and their point balances at zero. Their profile may be amended afterwards — the `full_name` and the `donation_opt_in` flag are mutable, and a change to the opt-in flag recomputes their redeemable balance — and this self-service amendment is the one operation the `Member` role holds exclusively, which is why the member read and the member write both resolve ownership against `X-User-ID`.

Points arrive on the ledger as events. A point event is posted against a `member_id` with an `event_kind` of `AWARD`, `REDEEM`, `REVERSAL` or `ADJUST`, a signed `points_delta`, and an optional `boat_deal_id` linking the event to the deal that produced it — awards earned at settlement carry that link, redemptions and adjustments do not. The delta is signed by design: a redemption or a reversal carries a negative movement, and the general non-negativity rule that guards every other numeric field explicitly exempts this one. Because the delta is signed, a negative value here is a valid request and not an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Each event also carries a server-assigned `created_seq` monotone sequence number, which exists solely so that two events sharing a `created_at` can still be replayed in a deterministic order. A redemption also produces a `donation_match_cents` value. Posting any event replays the member's entire ledger through the point replay, re-derives their tier and redeemable balance, and evaluates the conservation match on redemptions. Point events are listed with filters on `member_id`, `boat_deal_id`, `store_id` and `event_kind`.

The replay is the single source of truth for both balances and it is order-dependent. Its full rule set is given in §2.8.1: awards and adjustments add to both the balance and the lifetime total, redemptions subtract from the balance alone, reversals add their signed delta back to the balance and subtract their magnitude from the lifetime total, and both running totals are clamped at zero after every single event rather than only at the end. That per-event clamp is what makes the ordering observable, because an intermediate dip below zero is absorbed at the moment it happens and cannot be recovered by a later award. The tier ladder is then read off the lifetime total with inclusive lower bounds — sixty thousand points for `PATRON`, twenty-five thousand for `GOLD`, eight thousand for `SILVER` — and the redeemable balance is the raw balance less the tier's retention floor of four thousand, fifteen hundred, five hundred or zero points, clamped at zero. A member with exactly twenty-five thousand lifetime points is `GOLD`, not `SILVER`; a member whose lifetime total has fallen back below a threshold they once held is downgraded, because the tier is evaluated against the current lifetime value and never against a historical maximum. Tier changes also follow from card activation and from a declined application.

The conservation match ties redemptions to a donation. When a member who has opted in and holds an active club card redeems points, the redemption's magnitude is converted to redeemed value at twenty points to the dollar, the value is multiplied by the sponsoring application's `donation_match_bps` rate, and the proposed grant is added to the member's `conservation_match_cents` for the calendar year — up to a ceiling of five hundred cents. The ceiling gate is strict in one direction only: a proposed grant at or below the remaining headroom is granted in full, and only a grant strictly greater than the headroom is clipped down to the headroom, never below zero. A member who is already at the cap and redeems again receives additional match only if the cap has headroom, which it does not, so the second redemption in a year produces a smaller match than the first and the third produces none. Awards, adjustments and reversals generate no match at all, and neither do redemptions by members who never opted in or who hold no active card.

Club credit cards are opened through a segregated process. A `StoreManager` or a `ClubCardSpecialist` opens an application against a `member_id` with a `card_product` of `CLASSIC`, `REWARDS` or `PATRON`, a `donation_match_bps` rate that will be read by the conservation match, and the `underwriter_user_id` of the operator who will underwrite it. The application begins `PENDING` with its credit limit at zero. Underwriting is a `CreditUnderwriter`'s exclusive act: it moves the application from `PENDING` to `UNDERWRITING` and requires the supplied `underwriter_user_id` to be non-empty and to differ from the `decision_user_id` that a later decision will present. The underwriter's identity is frozen at that point, so the officer who decides cannot later present the same operator in the deciding field. The decision is a `CreditOfficer`'s exclusive act and is taken against an application in `UNDERWRITING` with a `decision_user_id` that differs from the recorded `underwriter_user_id` — a decision taken in the same name is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

An approval and a decline are specified with equal weight. An approval carries a `decision`, a `decision_reason`, and a `credit_limit_cents` that must be at least fifty thousand cents inclusive; a limit below that floor is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_BELOW_FLOOR` (HTTP 422). The required `decision_reason` is validated before that floor, so an approval presented without one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than the fiscal floor. An approval also requires that the applicant's `club_tier` be `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `PATRON`, or that the card product be `CLASSIC` — the entry-level card is open to any member, and a `REWARDS` or `PATRON` product is reserved for members who have earned a tier. The stored value of a qualifying approval's `credit_limit_cents` is the limit as approved. A qualifying approval moves the application from `UNDERWRITING` to `APPROVED` and moves the member's `club_card_status` from `NONE` to `PENDING`. A decline requires a non-empty `decision_reason`, moves the application from `UNDERWRITING` to `DECLINED`, leaves the member's card status exactly where it was, and re-derives the member's tier — a downgrade fires only where the lifetime total has genuinely fallen below the current tier's threshold, so a decline never demotes a member whose points still support their tier. A declined application is terminal and does not block anything downstream, which is precisely why contracting a boat deal tolerates a card status of `NONE`.

Activation is the last step and it changes the member's economics. A `StoreManager` activates an approved application, moving it from `APPROVED` to `ACTIVATED` and requiring the member's card status to be either `NONE` or `PENDING`. The member's `club_card_status` moves to `ACTIVE`, the point replay and tier evaluation re-run, and — the part with reach well beyond the club desk — the strike epoch resets. An active card is worth real money elsewhere in the store: it raises the credit rate applied to a boat deal's contract price from nothing to five percent, it unlocks the three percent trade-in rebate and the award doubling at settlement, it makes the member's redemptions eligible for the conservation match, and it clears their no-show history. Applications are listed with filters on `member_id`, `store_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. The Clock, the Ops-Run Sweep and Test Driving

Because this system's time is a logical clock rather than the wall clock, a platform administrator holds the single capability that advances it: a request carrying a `to_iso8601` target instant repositions the store clock to that moment, and it is the only mechanism by which the clock ever moves. The server starts at exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` and holds that instant indefinitely until the first such call. Every deadline in the system is evaluated against it, which means a test-driving session can sit at the first of September 2026 and observe a waiver that a later session sees expire, a no-show that has not yet accrued and a lead time that has not yet run late. The clock capability carries no store scope because it is platform-wide.

The ops-run checkpoint is the second automated lever and it belongs to one store. It is a single sweep, executable by a `PlatformAdmin` or by that store's own `StoreManager`, and it returns a summary of what it did: the store's `permit_remit_epoch` after the sweep, the number of sessions promoted out of the waitlist, the number of registrations promoted out of the waitlist, the number of waivers expired, and the store's `attraction_posture` after the sweep. Internally it advances `permit_remit_epoch` by one, stamps `last_ops_run_at`, expires every `SIGNED` waiver whose `expires_on` has fallen behind the clock, promotes parked sessions and parked registrations where the waiting-order rule allows, marks no-shows for confirmed or in-progress occupancy whose slot ended unchecked, completes events whose end instant has passed, abandons stale intakes and re-derives the store's posture. The sweep is also the only place where the projected variants of the lead-time and posture calculations are exercised, since an order still in progress and a store whose contested venue is booked for a later slot both look different under the projected reading than under the actual one. Nothing in the sweep requires a client to name the affected rows; the store is the only argument.
