# Bank-Owned Life Insurance Funding API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A bank's benefits finance office buys life insurance on its own employees for one reason: the cash value of those contracts is the informal funding vehicle that stands behind the nonqualified deferred compensation promises the bank has already made to its executives. That single sentence hides an enormous amount of institutional discipline. Before a dollar of premium moves, the office must satisfy itself that the issuing carrier is financially sound, that the product's charge structure is what the carrier disclosed, that the employee has been told about the coverage and has signed a notice-and-consent form, that the bank has an insurable interest in that employee, and that the aggregate premium does not push the institution past the concentration limits its regulators expect it to observe. After the policy is issued the discipline does not relax — every carrier statement is a data point on crediting rates and mortality-and-expense charges, every death benefit has to be split between the bank and the insured's family under the terms of the endorsement, and every exchange or surrender has to be modelled for tax consequences before anyone commits to it.

This document specifies the API that the benefits finance office, the treasury function, the risk function, the claims administrators and the board secretary all work through. The system is deliberately built as a compliance-and-audit platform rather than a bookkeeping convenience: nothing advances on a single operator's signature, capacity is a scarce quantity that must be reserved and released with exact conservation, and every derived figure — a financial strength score, a headroom amount, a taxable gain, a survivor entitlement — is recomputed by the server from persisted inputs rather than accepted from a client. Where the bank's own capital is the denominator, the server always reads the latest filed capital snapshot rather than trusting a number in a request body.

Two design consequences run through every section that follows. First, the platform is **segregation-of-duties native**: the operator who prepares a diligence file cannot decide it, the operator who submits a purchase request cannot approve it, and the operator who files a death claim cannot adjudicate it. Second, the platform is **cascade-heavy**: posting a routine carrier statement can, in a single request, revalue a policy, re-rate a carrier's concentration posture, put a product on watch, close that product to new business, flag the carrier's watch list and send every one of that carrier's in-flight diligence reviews back to the analyst for more information. Implementers should read the workflow sections with that fan-out in mind — the interesting behaviour of this system lives in the secondary and tertiary effects, not in the primary state change.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting under. If either header is absent or present but empty — a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty — the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. If both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared roles, or is a declared role that does not appear on the called endpoint's authorized-role list, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `BenefitsAnalyst`, `RiskOfficer`, `TreasuryController`, `ClaimsAdministrator`, `BoardSecretary` and `CarrierLiaison`. A `PlatformAdmin` is the operations administrator: it onboards carriers and products and drives the logical clock. A `BenefitsAnalyst` prepares diligence files, employee records, consent documentation and purchase requests, and issues policies once a request is approved. A `RiskOfficer` decides diligence reviews, clears and approves purchases, manages carrier authorization posture, adjudicates death claims, reviews dispositions and compiles the annual board assessment. A `TreasuryController` files capital snapshots, reserves capacity, posts carrier statements, runs the deferred compensation ledger and executes dispositions. A `ClaimsAdministrator` files claims, records beneficiary splits and distributes proceeds. A `BoardSecretary` accepts or returns the annual risk assessment on the board's behalf. A `CarrierLiaison` is a carrier-side counterparty with read-only reach into its own book of business.

Identity semantics differ between the internal roles and the carrier-side role, and the difference matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `BenefitsAnalyst`, `RiskOfficer`, `TreasuryController`, `ClaimsAdministrator` or `BoardSecretary`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow requires an audit trail — as `recorded_by_user_id` on a filed capital snapshot or a posted valuation, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a diligence review, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a purchase request, and as `filed_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a death benefit claim — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CarrierLiaison`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting carrier itself; on every endpoint whose authorized-role list includes `CarrierLiaison`, the server compares the `carrier_id` of each resource named in the path or in the request body against `X-User-ID`, and any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). A `CarrierLiaison` reading a carrier record other than its own, listing product types or purchase requests belonging to another carrier, or reading a policy or a statement period issued by another carrier, all fail that way. Where the carrier identifier is the path segment itself — reading a carrier — the comparison is made against the raw path value before the carrier is looked up, so a liaison naming another carrier's identifier sees the scope error even if that identifier does not exist; where the scoped resource instead carries `carrier_id` as one of its own fields — a product type, a policy, a statement period — the resource is resolved first, so an unknown identifier reports its own not-found error and the scope check then runs against the loaded `carrier_id`. On the four scoped list endpoints a `carrier_id` filter naming another carrier is a scope violation, while omitting that filter silently narrows the result set to the acting carrier's own rows rather than returning the whole table; listing statement periods as a liaison likewise returns only postings against that carrier's own policies.

The three authorization checks are ordered. Header presence is checked first, then role eligibility for the endpoint, then — only for endpoints that admit `CarrierLiaison` at all — the carrier scoping check. This ordering means a missing header always surfaces as `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rather than as a scope error, and a `CarrierLiaison` calling an endpoint it is not listed on surfaces as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than as `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it is taken from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of section 2.5, which governs only the persisted fields and comparisons described there.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped, as is a `limit` or `offset` whose value cannot be parsed as an integer at all.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a workflow section states otherwise. Because the logical clock only moves when a `PlatformAdmin` moves it, rows minted at the same clock instant share a `created_at`; those ties are broken by the server's creation sequence, so the effective order is always creation order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any rows remain beyond the current page.

Filter parameters on list endpoints are additive: supplying `carrier_id` and `status` together narrows to rows matching both. Filters never change the ordering rule, and `total_count` always reflects the filtered population rather than the whole table. An enum-valued filter carrying a value outside that enum's declared members, or a boolean filter such as `watch_list_flag` carrying anything other than `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every resource the platform persists carries a server-minted `id` in canonical UUID form. Identifiers never carry a type prefix, and clients must never supply one on a create — an `id` present in a create body is ignored, and clients must treat identifiers as opaque strings. Alongside `id`, most resources carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits. Client-supplied date and instant fields, by contrast — `as_of_date` on a capital snapshot or a statement period, `coverage_start_date` and `coverage_end_date`, `requested_effective_date`, `issue_date`, `period_start_date` and `date_of_death` — are echoed back exactly as they were supplied rather than normalised to that rendering, and each may be given either as a calendar date such as `2026-08-01` or as a full ISO 8601 UTC instant; a date-only value is interpreted as midnight UTC in every comparison the server makes against it. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly or arrived as a cascade from an operation on a different resource.

Two resource families are deliberately narrower. A filed capital snapshot and a posted carrier statement period are both append-only records of something that happened, and neither is ever edited after the fact. Both therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and no operation in this specification mutates either of them. Every other resource — carriers, product types, diligence reviews, employees, consent records, purchase requests, policies, deferred compensation plans, participant liabilities, death benefit claims, beneficiary allocations, disposition analyses and risk assessments — carries the full quartet.

The relationship between `updated_at` and `version` is strict: any request that changes a resource's persisted state bumps `version` by 1 and refreshes `updated_at` to the current logical clock instant. A request that is rejected changes nothing and bumps nothing. Where a workflow paragraph says a cascade "increments the carrier's version", it means precisely one increment per affected carrier per request, not one increment per field touched.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every date and instant comparison in this specification — consent coverage windows, notification waiting periods, valuation period overlap, the instant a review was submitted or a claim distributed — is made against the server's **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. Before the logical clock is set for the first time it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the platform's test-driving clock capability; the response reports exactly two fields, the resulting `current_utc` together with `expired_consent_count`, the number of consent records the move swept into expiry. The move is accepted whether it advances the clock or rewinds it.

Moving the clock is not a passive operation. Each move sweeps the consent population: every consent record currently in `EXECUTED` whose `coverage_end_date` now lies before the new clock value is moved to `EXPIRED`, and for every employee left with no remaining `EXECUTED` consent record the employee's `insurable_interest_status` is reset to `NOT_ESTABLISHED`. Each swept consent record has its own `version` incremented by 1, as does each employee whose insurable interest posture changed.

### 2.6. Monetary Precision, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities are USD amounts carried to exactly 2 decimal places. Wherever this document specifies a rounding, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero in the positive direction. A request body containing a USD amount with more than 2 decimal places is a schema violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Amounts are rendered in JSON as numbers rather than as strings, so a whole-dollar figure serialises without its trailing cents digits.

Ratios in this domain are expressed in **basis points**, where one basis point is one ten-thousandth of the reference quantity. Every basis-point field is an integer, and every basis-point figure the server derives is produced by taking the untruncated ratio, multiplying by ten thousand, and rounding half up to a whole number. Basis-point results are allowed to be negative where the underlying arithmetic permits it — a mortality-and-expense drift below the disclosed level and a net credited yield on a period whose charges exceeded its credited interest are both legitimately negative integers.

Two rounding orders in this system are pinned, and getting them wrong produces answers that differ by cents. First, when a deferred compensation plan's projected-benefit liability is computed, each participant's discounted amount is rounded to 2 decimal places **before** the participant amounts are summed; the sum of unrounded values is never rounded at the end. Second, when a death benefit is split among beneficiaries, each beneficiary's share is rounded to 2 decimal places first, the residual between the entitlement and the sum of rounded shares is computed, and that single signed residual is then applied to exactly one allocation. Likewise, when net proceeds of a disposition are computed, the surrender charge and the tax liability are each rounded to 2 decimal places before either is subtracted from the gross amount — never rounded after the subtraction.

### 2.7. Capital Snapshots: the Shared Denominator

Nearly every limit in this specification is a fraction of the bank's regulatory capital, so the platform keeps an append-only series of capital snapshots. A `TreasuryController` files one by supplying its `as_of_date`, the `tier1_capital_usd` measured on that date, and the `total_assets_usd` of the consolidated balance sheet; both amounts must be strictly greater than zero, and the server stamps `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. When any calculation needs a capital denominator, it uses **the latest snapshot**, defined as the snapshot with the greatest `as_of_date`, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending.

Filing a snapshot is itself a re-rating event. Because carrier concentration is measured against `tier1_capital_usd`, a new latest snapshot causes the server to recompute `concentration_bps` for every carrier on file and to re-apply the authorization hysteresis described in the carrier workflow — a carrier whose concentration crosses the restriction entry threshold under the new denominator moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `RESTRICTED` in the same request, and one whose concentration falls to the release threshold or below moves from `RESTRICTED` back to `AUTHORIZED`. Each carrier whose posture or concentration figure changed has its `version` incremented by 1.

If no capital snapshot has ever been filed, any operation that needs a denominator cannot proceed. Reserving capacity for a purchase request, reading the what-if capacity projection for a request, submitting a diligence review for decision, and compiling an annual risk assessment all fail with `ERR_NO_CAPITAL_POSITION` (HTTP 422) when the snapshot series is empty. Those four operations are the complete set guarded by that error. Any other cascade that would recompute a carrier's `concentration_bps` against the missing denominator simply leaves the stored `concentration_bps` untouched and proceeds with the rest of its work.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Five decision points in this system are guarded by a four-eyes rule: the operator taking the decision must not be the operator who prepared the item being decided. Approving a diligence review and declining a diligence review both require that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the review's `submitted_by_user_id`. Approving a purchase request requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the request's `submitted_by_user_id`. Approving a death benefit claim and denying a death benefit claim both require that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the claim's `filed_by_user_id`. Those five operations are the complete set guarded by this rule, and a violation on any of them is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Withdrawal is the mirror image of the same principle. A purchase request may only be withdrawn by the operator who submitted it, so on that operation the acting `X-User-ID` must **equal** the request's `submitted_by_user_id`. A mismatch there is not treated as a four-eyes violation: it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), not with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Rejection, by contrast, is a risk-side action and imposes no identity relationship at all.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Carriers and Policies

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because both size themselves against an aggregate figure carried on a parent resource that other writers can move.

Reserving capacity for a purchase request requires the non-optional input field `expected_carrier_version`, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on the carrier the request names. If that value does not equal the carrier's current `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is not a redundant guard: a second purchase request sitting in `DILIGENCE_CLEARED` against the same carrier is a perfectly legitimate thing to reserve, and no state field on either the request or the carrier forbids it — but the reservation is sized against the carrier's `reserved_premium_usd`, which an earlier reservation has already moved. The stale `expected_carrier_version` is exactly what the platform rejects, forcing the controller to re-read the carrier before committing more of its capacity.

Creating a disposition analysis requires the non-optional input field `expected_policy_version`, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on the policy the analysis addresses. A mismatch against the policy's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the reason is semantic rather than defensive: creating an analysis supersedes every earlier analysis on that policy still sitting in `DRAFT` or `REVIEWED`, so a second create is legitimate and no state precondition rejects it. What the platform will not accept is an author whose view of the policy predates the supersede that an earlier create already performed.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field. Approving, rejecting, committing, executing, distributing and every other action operate on the resource's current state without an echoed version, and supplying an unexpected version field to them is simply an undeclared body key. Undeclared body keys are ignored rather than rejected on every endpoint.

### 2.10. Cascades, Conservation Invariants and Version Propagation

Because carrier exposure, plan funding and capacity are all aggregates maintained on parent resources, this platform recomputes rather than remembers. A carrier carries `reserved_premium_usd`, the sum of `reserved_premium_usd` over its purchase requests sitting in `CAPACITY_RESERVED`, `NOTICE_FILED`, `CONCURRENCE_PENDING` or `APPROVED`; `in_force_premium_usd`, the sum of `single_premium_usd` over its policies in `IN_FORCE`, `PENDING_EXCHANGE` or `CLAIM_PENDING`; and `in_force_cash_value_usd`, the sum of `cash_surrender_value_usd` over that same set of policies. Whenever an operation changes a member of one of those sets, the aggregate is refreshed in the same request and the carrier's `version` is incremented by 1.

Two conservation invariants must hold exactly, and they are the sharpest tests of a correct implementation. The **reserved-premium invariant**: every dollar added to a carrier's `reserved_premium_usd` at reservation time is later removed exactly once, whether by rejection, by withdrawal, by carrier termination or by issuance moving it into `in_force_premium_usd`. Reserving and then withdrawing must leave the carrier's `reserved_premium_usd` at precisely its pre-reservation value. The **cost-basis invariant**: committing a disposition analysis and then abandoning it must leave the policy's `cost_basis_usd` and `cash_surrender_value_usd` at precisely their pre-commit values.

Cascades in this system run to three hops in two places, and both are specified in full in the workflow sections. A posted carrier statement can revalue a policy, re-rate a carrier, close a product, flag the carrier's watch list and reopen every in-flight diligence review of that carrier. A distributed death claim or an executed surrender can zero a policy's cash value, re-rate the carrier, unmatch every deferred compensation liability funded by that policy, and freeze the affected plan when its recomputed funding posture reaches `CRITICAL`. Each distinct resource touched by a cascade receives exactly one `version` increment for the request, and each has its `updated_at` refreshed.

### 2.11. Validation Order and Nested Path Resolution

The platform evaluates every request through a fixed ladder of checks, and the ladder is normative because it determines which error a request carrying several defects reports. The order is: header presence, then role eligibility, then carrier scoping for endpoints that admit `CarrierLiaison`, then request-schema validity, then existence of every identifier in the URL path, then existence of every foreign key in the request body, then the terminal-state check, then the transition-legality check, then the optimistic-concurrency check, then the four-eyes check, then the consent and insurable-interest family, then the diligence-decision family, then the referential-consistency family, then the temporal family, then the exposure-constraint family, and finally the fiscal-capacity family.

Schema validation, sitting fourth, covers all of the following in one pass: required fields present, values of the declared type, enum-valued fields carrying only declared members, USD amounts non-negative and carrying at most 2 decimal places, basis-point values integral, datetimes well-formed ISO 8601 UTC, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0. A string field that is present but blank or whitespace-only fails the same way, while an integer field accepts an integer-valued JSON float such as `500.0` and rejects only a genuinely fractional one. Every one of those failures reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the error reported is the one belonging to the entity that segment addresses: a missing carrier reports `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing product type `ERR_PRODUCT_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing employee `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing diligence review `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, a missing purchase request `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, a missing policy `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, a missing death benefit claim `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, a missing deferred compensation plan `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing disposition analysis `ERR_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`. A consent record, a posted statement period, a beneficiary allocation, a participant liability, a capital snapshot or a risk assessment that cannot be found all report `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. Every one of these is HTTP 404, as is a request whose path matches no declared endpoint at all; a method that an otherwise existing path does not declare answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 405.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, using the same per-entity error identifiers; where the referenced entity has no dedicated identifier the generic `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` applies. Two state guards then apply to every action that advances a state machine: an attempt to advance a resource already sitting in a terminal state for the dimension the action drives reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` (HTTP 409), and an attempt to take a transition that the resource's current state does not offer reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). The transition-legality check also guards three creates: issuing a policy, filing a death claim, and adding a participant liability to a plan that is `FROZEN` or `TERMINATED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Carrier Onboarding, Due Diligence and Authorization Posture

A `PlatformAdmin` brings a new insurance carrier onto the platform by supplying its registered `legal_name`, the `naic_code` it uses on regulatory filings, the financial strength rating `am_best_rating` supplied at onboarding, its `statutory_surplus_usd` (which must be strictly greater than zero), and the two-letter `domicile_state` of its domicile. The rating is one of `A_PLUS_PLUS`, `A_PLUS`, `A`, `A_MINUS`, `B_PLUS_PLUS`, `B_PLUS` or `B`. A newly onboarded carrier begins life in `PENDING_DILIGENCE` with `watch_list_flag` false, with `reserved_premium_usd`, `in_force_premium_usd` and `in_force_cash_value_usd` all at 0.00, with `concentration_bps` and `regulatory_notice_count` at 0, and with `last_approved_review_year` unset. Carriers can be listed with optional filters on `status` and `watch_list_flag`, and read individually — the individual read is how a controller obtains the carrier `version` it will have to echo before reserving capacity.

Nothing may be placed with a carrier in `PENDING_DILIGENCE`. Authorization comes only from an approved due diligence file, which a `BenefitsAnalyst` opens by naming the `carrier_id` under diligence and the `review_year` the cycle covers. A fresh file sits in `DRAFT` with `info_request_count` at 0, with `rating_points`, `surplus_points`, `concentration_points` and `financial_strength_score` all at 0 until the first submission scores it, and with both `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` unset. Nothing forbids two files on the same carrier for the same `review_year`. Diligence files can be listed with optional filters on `carrier_id`, `status` and `review_year`.

Submitting the file for decision advances it `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, and the same action re-advances a file that has been sent back, `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. Both paths carry identical preconditions and identical effects: the carrier must currently be in `PENDING_DILIGENCE`, `AUTHORIZED` or `RESTRICTED` (a `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` carrier cannot have a file submitted against it, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`), and at least one capital snapshot must exist, failing which the submission reports `ERR_NO_CAPITAL_POSITION`. On success the server recomputes `rating_points`, `surplus_points`, `concentration_points` and `financial_strength_score` from scratch, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`, and increments the file's `version` by 1.

**The financial strength score.** `rating_points` is a straight lookup on the carrier's `am_best_rating`: a rating of `A_PLUS_PLUS` scores 100 points, `A_PLUS` scores 90, `A` scores 80, `A_MINUS` scores 70, `B_PLUS_PLUS` scores 55, `B_PLUS` scores 40, and `B` scores 20. `surplus_points` rewards balance-sheet size in half-billion-dollar steps: divide the carrier's `statutory_surplus_usd` by 500,000,000, discard any fractional part of that quotient, multiply the resulting whole number of steps by 10, and cap the product at 100 — so a carrier with two and a half billion dollars of surplus earns 50 points, and any carrier above five billion earns the full 100. `concentration_points` penalises existing exposure: take 100, subtract the carrier's current `concentration_bps` divided by 20, and if the result would be below zero use zero instead; this component keeps one decimal place. The three components are then weighted — half the weight on `rating_points`, three tenths on `surplus_points`, one fifth on `concentration_points` — and the weighted total is rounded half up to a whole number to give `financial_strength_score`. One override applies afterwards: if the carrier's `watch_list_flag` is true, the score is capped at 60, meaning the recorded score becomes the lower of 60 and the value just computed. The score is always read from the carrier as it stands at submission time, including whatever `concentration_bps` the carrier currently carries.

A `RiskOfficer` who wants more from the analyst before deciding sends the file back, advancing `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and incrementing `info_request_count` by 1. That operator-driven loop is capped at two rounds: if `info_request_count` has already reached 2, the request is refused with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). The cycle `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` may therefore run at most twice before the officer must either approve or decline.

Approval advances the file `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`. Two checks guard it, and they are evaluated in that order: first the four-eyes rule, so that the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id` or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; then the score threshold, requiring `financial_strength_score` to be at least 65, with a score of 64 or below refused as `ERR_FINANCIAL_STRENGTH_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). Because the four-eyes check is evaluated first, an operator approving their own submission of a sub-threshold file sees the segregation error rather than the score error. On success the server records the acting `X-User-ID` as `decided_by_user_id`, increments the file's `version` by 1, and — this is the cascade that makes the carrier usable — drives the carrier `PENDING_DILIGENCE → AUTHORIZED`, sets the carrier's `last_approved_review_year` to the file's `review_year`, and increments the carrier's `version` by 1. A carrier that is already `AUTHORIZED` or `RESTRICTED` when the file is approved keeps that posture untouched, but still takes the `last_approved_review_year` write and the same single version increment.

Declining is the symmetric decision and reaches `DECLINED` from either `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. It too requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, failing which it reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; it too records `decided_by_user_id` and increments the file's `version` by 1. Unlike approval it touches nothing on the carrier at all — a declined file leaves the carrier exactly where it was, which for a first-time carrier means `PENDING_DILIGENCE`. Both `APPROVED` and `DECLINED` are terminal for the diligence dimension, and a further action against a file in either state reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`.

**Concentration posture and its hysteresis band.** An authorized carrier is continuously re-rated on how much of the bank's capital its contracts represent. `concentration_bps` expresses the carrier's `in_force_cash_value_usd` as basis points of the latest snapshot's `tier1_capital_usd`, and the platform applies a deliberately asymmetric two-threshold rule to it. A carrier in `AUTHORIZED` moves to `RESTRICTED` as soon as its recomputed `concentration_bps` reaches 1500 or more — that is, once its cash value reaches fifteen percent of tier-1 capital. A carrier in `RESTRICTED` returns to `AUTHORIZED` only once its recomputed `concentration_bps` has fallen to 1200 or below, twelve percent of tier-1 capital. Between those two figures the carrier stays where it is: a `RESTRICTED` carrier whose recomputed concentration is strictly greater than 1200 takes an explicit `RESTRICTED → RESTRICTED` self-loop, refreshing the stored `concentration_bps` and incrementing the carrier's `version` by 1 without releasing the restriction. Any value from 1201 through 1499 therefore leaves a restricted carrier restricted, and the entry and release evaluations run wherever exposure changes: on a posted carrier statement, on policy issuance, on a filed capital snapshot, on a distributed death claim, and on an executed surrender or exchange.

Carrier posture is also under direct risk control. A `RiskOfficer` may suspend a carrier from either `AUTHORIZED` or `RESTRICTED`, moving it to `SUSPENDED` and incrementing its `version` by 1; suspension needs no further precondition beyond the carrier being in one of those two states. Reinstatement takes a carrier `SUSPENDED → AUTHORIZED`, but only when two conditions hold together: the carrier's `concentration_bps` is 1200 or below, and its `watch_list_flag` is false. A suspended carrier that is still concentrated, or that still has a product closed to new business behind it, cannot be reinstated and the attempt is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Termination is the end of the relationship and only available from `SUSPENDED`. It requires that no policy naming this carrier remains in `IN_FORCE`, `PENDING_EXCHANGE` or `CLAIM_PENDING` — live exposure must be disposed of first, and an attempt against a carrier that still has any is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. When it succeeds the cascade is broad: every product type of that carrier currently in `OPEN` or `ON_WATCH` is driven to `CLOSED_TO_NEW`; every purchase request naming that carrier and sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `DILIGENCE_CLEARED`, `CAPACITY_RESERVED`, `NOTICE_FILED`, `CONCURRENCE_PENDING` or `APPROVED` is driven to `REJECTED`, and each such request's `reserved_premium_usd` is subtracted from the carrier's `reserved_premium_usd` and then zeroed on the request itself; and the carrier's `version` is incremented by 1, as is the `version` of every product type and purchase request the cascade touched. `TERMINATED` is terminal for a carrier.

### 3.2. Insurable Interest and Notice-and-Consent Documentation

No policy can be placed on an employee the bank has not registered and cannot lawfully insure. A `BenefitsAnalyst` registers a prospective insured by supplying `full_name`, the payroll identifier `employee_number`, the base `annual_salary_usd` (strictly greater than zero), and optionally `officer_flag`, which defaults to false and is set true only for designated executive officers. A new employee record starts with `insurable_interest_status` at `NOT_ESTABLISHED` and `employment_status` at `ACTIVE`. The analyst can also record a later change of employment posture by supplying a new `employment_status` value drawn from `ACTIVE`, `TERMINATED` and `DECEASED`; that write is unconditional — any of the three values may be recorded from any current value, including returning a `DECEASED` employee to `ACTIVE` — and it sets the field and increments the employee's `version` by 1. Reading an employee returns the current insurable interest posture alongside the identifying fields.

Insurable interest is never asserted by a client. It is derived, and the derivation has exactly two qualifying paths: the employee's `annual_salary_usd` is at least 250,000.00, **or** the employee's `officer_flag` is true. If either holds, the derived value is `ESTABLISHED`; if neither holds, it is `NOT_ESTABLISHED`. The derivation runs whenever a consent record for that employee is executed, and it is re-checked when a purchase request naming that employee is submitted.

A consent record documents one closed coverage interval. The analyst creates it in `DRAFT` by supplying the `employee_id` giving notice and consent, the inclusive `coverage_start_date` and inclusive `coverage_end_date` of the interval, the `consent_form_reference` identifying the signed document, and the `notice_and_consent_version` tag of the template used; `executed_at` starts unset. The platform does not require `coverage_start_date` to fall on or before `coverage_end_date`. Consent records can be listed with optional filters on `employee_id` and `status`.

**Consent windows must not crowd each other.** On creation the platform compares the proposed interval against every other consent record of the same employee that is currently in `DRAFT` or `EXECUTED` — records in `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` are ignored entirely and never conflict. Both intervals are treated as closed at both ends, and a symmetric buffer of 30 whole UTC days is applied on **both** sides. Two windows conflict when the new window's `coverage_start_date` falls on or before the existing window's `coverage_end_date` advanced by 30 days, and at the same time the existing window's `coverage_start_date` falls on or before the new window's `coverage_end_date` advanced by 30 days. Because both comparisons are inclusive, a gap of exactly 30 days between two windows still conflicts; a gap of 31 days is accepted. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

Executing the consent records the employee's signature and advances the record `DRAFT → EXECUTED`. Two preconditions apply. The employee must currently be in `employment_status` `ACTIVE`, and an attempt against a `TERMINATED` or `DECEASED` employee is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The logical clock must also be on or before the record's `coverage_end_date`; signing a window that has already run out is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). On success the server stamps `executed_at` with the current logical clock instant, re-derives the employee's `insurable_interest_status` by the salary-or-officer test above, increments the employee's `version` by 1, and increments the consent record's `version` by 1.

Consent can be withdrawn. Revoking takes an `EXECUTED` record to `REVOKED` and increments its `version` by 1. Revocation also asks whether the employee has any other consent record still in `EXECUTED`: if none remains, the employee's `insurable_interest_status` is reset to `NOT_ESTABLISHED` and the employee's `version` is incremented by 1; if another executed record survives, the employee's posture is left alone. Expiry through the logical clock sweep behaves identically on that point — a swept record moves `EXECUTED → EXPIRED`, and only an employee left with no remaining `EXECUTED` record has its posture reset. `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal for a consent record, and any further action on a record in either state reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`.

### 3.3. Purchase Requests from Drafting through Capacity Reservation

A purchase request is the vehicle that carries a proposed single-premium placement through five separate gates before a policy exists. A `BenefitsAnalyst` drafts one by naming the `carrier_id` the premium would be placed with, the `product_type_id` the policy would be issued under, the `employee_id` of the proposed insured, the `single_premium_usd` the bank would pay, the `face_amount_usd` of death benefit requested, and the `requested_effective_date` desired for the contract. Both amounts must be strictly greater than zero. A drafted request sits in `DRAFT` with `reserved_premium_usd` at 0.00, `base_ratio_bps`, `carrier_concentration_bps` and `effective_limit_pct` at 0, `aggregate_limit_usd` and `headroom_usd` at 0.00, `notice_required` false, and `notice_filed_at`, `notice_waiting_ends_at`, `concurrence_requested_at`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` all unset. Even at draft time the product must belong to the carrier named on the same request; a product whose own `carrier_id` differs from the request's `carrier_id` is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CARRIER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Requests can be listed with optional filters on `carrier_id`, `product_type_id`, `employee_id` and `status`, and read individually to inspect the capacity figures frozen onto them.

Submission advances the request `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is where the consent and insurable-interest evidence is tested. Three business checks apply, all at the same priority band and all evaluated after the state guards. First, an `EXECUTED` consent record must exist for the request's employee whose closed coverage interval contains the `requested_effective_date` — that is, whose `coverage_start_date` is on or before the requested effective date and whose `coverage_end_date` is on or after it. With no such record the submission fails with `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING` (HTTP 422) — unless at least one covering record of that employee is in `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED`, in which case the more specific `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED` is reported instead; a covering record still sitting in `DRAFT` earns no such substitution and yields `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`. Second, where covering `EXECUTED` records do exist, their windows must not all have run out under the current clock: `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED` is reported only when **every** covering executed record has a `coverage_end_date` lying strictly before the logical clock, so a single still-current covering record carries the submission through. Third, the employee's `insurable_interest_status` must read `ESTABLISHED`, which as described above requires salary of at least 250,000.00 or an `officer_flag` of true; otherwise the submission fails with `ERR_INSURABLE_INTEREST_NOT_ESTABLISHED` (HTTP 422). The product-belongs-to-carrier consistency check is re-applied here too, again reporting `ERR_PRODUCT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`.

Submission also settles the regulatory notification question once and for all. The server sets `notice_required` to true when the request's `single_premium_usd` is **strictly greater than** 25,000,000.00, and to false otherwise; a premium of exactly 25,000,000.00 does not require notification. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`, and the request's `version` is incremented by 1.

Diligence clearance is the risk function's confirmation that the counterparty and the product are both acceptable, and it advances the request `SUBMITTED → DILIGENCE_CLEARED`. A `RiskOfficer` performing it must find three things true, and they are evaluated in a fixed order that follows the ladder of §2.11: the diligence-decision check first, then the product check, then the carrier-posture check. Among that carrier's diligence files in `APPROVED`, the one with the greatest `review_year` — with ties on that year resolved to the earliest-created of them — must carry a `financial_strength_score` of at least 65; if the carrier has no approved file at all, or the most recent approved year's score falls below 65, the clearance fails with `ERR_DILIGENCE_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 422). The product type must be in `OPEN` or `ON_WATCH`; a product in `CLOSED_TO_NEW` fails with `ERR_PRODUCT_CLOSED_TO_NEW` (HTTP 422). The carrier must currently be `AUTHORIZED` — a carrier in any other posture, including `RESTRICTED`, blocks clearance, and that block is reported as `ERR_CARRIER_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422) rather than as a transition error. A carrier whose posture blocks clearance while it also has no qualifying approved diligence file therefore reports `ERR_DILIGENCE_NOT_CLEARED`. On success only the request's `version` moves, incremented by 1.

**Reserving capacity** is the request's first material claim on the bank's balance sheet and belongs to the `TreasuryController`. It advances the request `DILIGENCE_CLEARED → CAPACITY_RESERVED` and takes exactly one input field, `expected_carrier_version`, which must equal the named carrier's current `version` or the reservation is refused with `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Beyond the optimistic-concurrency guard, three business constraints apply in a fixed order. The product type must not be `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, reported as `ERR_PRODUCT_CLOSED_TO_NEW`. The carrier must not be `RESTRICTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, since a reservation adds exposure, and any of those postures is reported as `ERR_CARRIER_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422). Then the single-carrier concentration test is applied, reported as `ERR_CARRIER_CONCENTRATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), and only after it passes is the aggregate capital headroom test applied, reported as `ERR_CAPITAL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The concentration test therefore always wins over the headroom test when a request would breach both.

**How capacity is measured.** The capacity computation has a pinned evaluation order, and the order is normative because the aggregate limit depends on a tier that is itself chosen from utilisation of the *base* limit. It is executed exactly once per request; there is no second pass, and the tier chosen is never re-derived from the limit it produced.

The computation proceeds in eight steps. First, read `tier1_capital_usd` from the latest capital snapshot — greatest `as_of_date`, ties broken by `created_at` ascending — and if no snapshot exists at all stop and report `ERR_NO_CAPITAL_POSITION`. Second, compute the **base limit** as tier-1 capital multiplied by twenty-five hundredths, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; this is the figure reported as `base_limit_usd`. Third, compute `committed_usd`, the bank's existing commitment, as the sum of `reserved_premium_usd` across **every** carrier on the platform plus the sum of `single_premium_usd` across **every** policy whose status is `IN_FORCE`, `PENDING_EXCHANGE` or `CLAIM_PENDING`; policies in `EXCHANGED`, `SURRENDERED` or `PAID_OUT` are excluded from that sum. Fourth, compute `prospective_usd` by adding this request's own `single_premium_usd` to `committed_usd` — the request's premium is always included even though the request is not yet reserved. Fifth, compute `base_ratio_bps` by dividing `prospective_usd` by the base limit, multiplying by ten thousand, and rounding half up to a whole number. Sixth, select `effective_limit_pct` from that ratio: twenty-five hundredths when `base_ratio_bps` is 6000 or less, twenty-two hundredths when `base_ratio_bps` is greater than 6000 and at most 8500, and twenty hundredths when `base_ratio_bps` is greater than 8500. In other words the bank's aggregate allowance tightens from a quarter of tier-1 capital to twenty-two percent once prospective commitments pass sixty percent of the base allowance, and to twenty percent once they pass eighty-five percent of it. Seventh, compute `aggregate_limit_usd` as tier-1 capital multiplied by the selected `effective_limit_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Eighth, compute `headroom_usd` as `aggregate_limit_usd` minus `prospective_usd`; this value is allowed to be negative, and a negative result is exactly what the aggregate gate rejects, requiring `headroom_usd` to be zero or greater.

Single-carrier concentration is computed alongside, from the same tier-1 denominator: add the carrier's current `reserved_premium_usd`, the carrier's current `in_force_premium_usd`, and this request's `single_premium_usd`, divide by tier-1 capital, multiply by ten thousand, and round half up to a whole number to obtain `carrier_concentration_bps`. The ceiling it is measured against depends on the product's `account_type`: a product whose account type is `SEPARATE` carries a tighter ceiling of 1000 basis points — ten percent of tier-1 capital — while products whose account type is `GENERAL` or `HYBRID` carry a ceiling of 1500 basis points, fifteen percent. That ceiling is the figure reported as `single_carrier_limit_bps`, and the gate requires `carrier_concentration_bps` to be less than or equal to it; a strictly greater value is the concentration breach.

A `RiskOfficer` or a `TreasuryController` can run the whole computation read-only as a what-if projection against any request, without reserving anything and without mutating any resource. The projection returns `tier1_capital_usd`, `base_limit_usd`, `committed_usd`, `prospective_usd`, `base_ratio_bps`, `effective_limit_pct`, `aggregate_limit_usd`, `headroom_usd`, `carrier_concentration_bps` and `single_carrier_limit_bps` — the complete working of the calculation, so that a controller can see which gate would bite before attempting the reservation. Like the reservation itself, the projection reports `ERR_NO_CAPITAL_POSITION` when no snapshot exists.

When the reservation succeeds, the figures are **frozen onto the request**: `base_ratio_bps`, `effective_limit_pct`, `aggregate_limit_usd`, `headroom_usd` and `carrier_concentration_bps` are all written as computed, and `reserved_premium_usd` on the request is set to the request's `single_premium_usd`. The same premium is added to the carrier's `reserved_premium_usd`, the carrier's `version` is incremented by 1, and the request's `version` is incremented by 1. From that moment the request is holding a piece of the bank's capacity, and every path out of the reservation must give it back exactly once.

Two paths give it back before issuance. A `RiskOfficer` may reject a request from `SUBMITTED`, `DILIGENCE_CLEARED`, `CAPACITY_RESERVED`, `NOTICE_FILED`, `CONCURRENCE_PENDING` or `APPROVED`, driving it to `REJECTED`; the request's `reserved_premium_usd` is subtracted from the carrier's `reserved_premium_usd`, the carrier's `version` is incremented by 1, the request's `reserved_premium_usd` is set to 0.00, and the request's `version` is incremented by 1. A `BenefitsAnalyst` may withdraw a request from `SUBMITTED`, `DILIGENCE_CLEARED`, `CAPACITY_RESERVED`, `NOTICE_FILED` or `CONCURRENCE_PENDING` — note that withdrawal, unlike rejection, is **not** available from `APPROVED` — driving it to `WITHDRAWN` with exactly the same premium release, the same carrier `version` increment and the same zeroing. Withdrawal additionally requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equal the request's `submitted_by_user_id`, because it is the compensating action for the submitter's own work; a different analyst attempting it is refused. For a request that was never reserved the release arithmetic subtracts 0.00 and the carrier's totals are unchanged, but the carrier's `version` still moves. `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `ISSUED` are all terminal for a purchase request.

### 3.4. Regulatory Notification, Concurrence, Approval and Issuance

Once capacity is reserved the workflow forks, and which branch a request takes was decided at submission time by the `notice_required` flag. This is the one genuine branch in the purchase lifecycle, and the two branches are not interchangeable.

**Branch A — the notification branch.** A request whose `notice_required` is true must have its regulatory notification filed before anything else happens. A `TreasuryController` files it, advancing the request `CAPACITY_RESERVED → NOTICE_FILED`; the action is available only when `notice_required` is true, and attempting it on a request that does not require notification is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Filing stamps `notice_filed_at` with the current logical clock instant and sets `notice_waiting_ends_at` to that instant advanced by 30 whole UTC days. It also increments the carrier's `regulatory_notice_count` by 1 — that counter is the running tally of how many purchase requests against this carrier have ever reached `NOTICE_FILED` — and increments the carrier's `version` by 1. The request's own `version` is incremented by 1.

**Branch B — the no-notification branch.** A request whose `notice_required` is false skips notification entirely. A `BenefitsAnalyst` asks the risk function for concurrence directly from `CAPACITY_RESERVED`, advancing the request `CAPACITY_RESERVED → CONCURRENCE_PENDING`; that path is available only when `notice_required` is false, and attempting it on a request that does require notification is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Branch B stamps `concurrence_requested_at` with the current logical clock instant, leaves both `notice_filed_at` and `notice_waiting_ends_at` unset, and deliberately does **not** touch the carrier's `regulatory_notice_count` at all.

**The join.** The same concurrence request also runs from `NOTICE_FILED → CONCURRENCE_PENDING`, and on that path the precondition is that `notice_filed_at` has been stamped. It likewise stamps `concurrence_requested_at` and increments the request's `version` by 1. Both branches therefore converge on `CONCURRENCE_PENDING`, but they arrive carrying different data: a branch-A request has `notice_filed_at` and `notice_waiting_ends_at` populated, a branch-B request has both unset.

That divergence matters at approval. A `RiskOfficer` approves a request from `CONCURRENCE_PENDING`, advancing it to `APPROVED`, and two guards apply in order. The four-eyes rule comes first: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the request's `submitted_by_user_id`, or the approval fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The waiting-period rule comes second and applies **only** when `notice_filed_at` is populated — that is, only on branch A: the logical clock must have reached `notice_waiting_ends_at`, and an earlier clock value fails with `ERR_NOTICE_WAITING_PERIOD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). The comparison is inclusive, so a clock reading exactly equal to `notice_waiting_ends_at` passes. A branch-B request has no waiting period to serve and can be approved as soon as concurrence is requested. On success the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `approved_by_user_id` and the request's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Issuance.** With an approved request in hand a `BenefitsAnalyst` records the contract the carrier actually issued, supplying the `purchase_request_id` of the approved request together with the carrier-assigned `policy_number` and the `issue_date` on which the carrier issued the contract. The request must be in `APPROVED` and no policy may already reference it; because `ISSUED` is terminal for a request, a second issuance attempt against the same request reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`. The carrier must be `AUTHORIZED`, and a carrier that has since become `RESTRICTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` blocks issuance with `ERR_CARRIER_RESTRICTED`; the product type must also not have become `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, reported as `ERR_PRODUCT_CLOSED_TO_NEW`.

Issuance creates the policy and carries a five-part cascade. The new policy copies `carrier_id`, `product_type_id` and `employee_id` from the request, copies `single_premium_usd` and `face_amount_usd` from it as well, sets `cost_basis_usd` equal to that premium because the premium paid *is* the tax cost basis, sets `cash_surrender_value_usd` equal to that same premium as the opening cash value, sets `policy_year` to 1, leaves `exchanged_from_policy_id` unset since this is an original purchase rather than an exchange, and enters service in `IN_FORCE`. The purchase request advances `APPROVED → ISSUED` and its `reserved_premium_usd` is set to 0.00, with its `version` incremented by 1. On the carrier, the premium is **moved** rather than duplicated: it is subtracted from `reserved_premium_usd` and added to `in_force_premium_usd`, it is also added to `in_force_cash_value_usd`, `concentration_bps` is recomputed against the latest snapshot's `tier1_capital_usd`, the authorization hysteresis is applied so that a carrier crossing 1500 basis points moves `AUTHORIZED → RESTRICTED`, and the carrier's `version` is incremented by 1. Issuance responds with HTTP 201 and the created policy.

Policies can be listed with optional filters on `carrier_id`, `product_type_id`, `employee_id`, `purchase_request_id`, `exchanged_from_policy_id` and `status`, and each can be read individually — the individual read is where a controller obtains the policy `version` it must echo when creating a disposition analysis. `EXCHANGED`, `SURRENDERED` and `PAID_OUT` are the terminal policy states.

### 3.5. Product Registration and Carrier Statement Monitoring

A `PlatformAdmin` registers each carrier product that is available for placement, supplying the issuing `carrier_id`, the carrier's `product_name`, the `account_type` of the contract, its `mec_status`, the `disclosed_me_charge_bps` the carrier has disclosed as its mortality-and-expense charge in basis points of prior cash value per valuation period, and the `guaranteed_min_crediting_bps` representing the contractual minimum gross crediting rate in basis points. `account_type` is one of `GENERAL`, `SEPARATE` or `HYBRID`, and as noted in the capacity discussion a separate-account product carries the tighter single-carrier ceiling. `mec_status` is either `MEC` or `NON_MEC`, and it is the switch that selects the cost-recovery variant used in disposition analysis. Both basis-point inputs must be zero or greater. A registered product opens in `OPEN` with `consecutive_low_yield_count`, `latest_me_charge_bps` and `latest_me_drift_bps` all at 0. Products can be listed with optional filters on `carrier_id`, `status` and `account_type`, and read individually.

Every quarter or statement cycle the carrier reports what it credited and what it charged, and a `TreasuryController` posts that statement period against a specific policy. The posting names the `policy_id`, the inclusive `period_start_date` at which the interval opens and the exclusive `as_of_date` at which it closes — which is also the statement date — the `credited_interest_usd` the carrier credited for the period, the `mortality_expense_charge_usd` it assessed, the `gross_crediting_rate_bps` it declared, and optionally the `market_value_adjustment_usd` for the period, which defaults to 0.00. Credited interest, the charge and the declared gross rate must all be zero or greater; the market value adjustment is the single monetary input in this specification permitted to be negative, since a market value adjustment can legitimately run either way. The server stamps `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and the posting responds with HTTP 201.

**Statement periods may not overlap.** Statement intervals are half-open — the `period_start_date` is included, the `as_of_date` is excluded — and no buffer is applied. Two periods on the same policy conflict when the new period's `period_start_date` falls strictly before the existing period's `as_of_date` and, at the same time, the existing period's `period_start_date` falls strictly before the new period's `as_of_date`. Because both comparisons are strict, a new period that starts exactly where an existing period ended is **accepted** — perfect adjacency with a zero gap is the normal case for consecutive statements. This is precisely the opposite of the buffered closed-interval treatment given to consent windows, where a zero gap and even a thirty-day gap both conflict. Every statement period on the policy participates in the comparison; postings are append-only and carry no status to filter on. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_VALUATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

The posting's own derived figures come first. `prior_cash_value_usd` is captured as the policy's `cash_surrender_value_usd` immediately before this posting. `cash_value_usd` is the prior cash value plus the credited interest, minus the mortality-and-expense charge, plus the market value adjustment, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `net_yield_bps` is the credited interest minus the mortality-and-expense charge, divided by the prior cash value, multiplied by ten thousand and rounded half up to a whole number — a figure that is legitimately negative when the period's charge exceeded its credited interest. `me_charge_bps` is the mortality-and-expense charge divided by the prior cash value, multiplied by ten thousand and rounded half up to a whole number. `me_drift_bps` is `me_charge_bps` minus the product's `disclosed_me_charge_bps`, and it too may be negative when the carrier charged less than it disclosed. Where the prior cash value is exactly 0.00 no division is attempted: `net_yield_bps` and `me_charge_bps` are both reported as 0, so `me_drift_bps` becomes the negative of the product's `disclosed_me_charge_bps`, and because 0 sits below the 150-basis-point floor such a posting still advances the low-yield streak. The computed `cash_value_usd` is not floored at zero.

**The posting cascade runs in a pinned order, and every step is mandatory.** First the statement period row is persisted. Second, the policy's `cash_surrender_value_usd` is set to the computed `cash_value_usd` and the policy's `version` is incremented by 1. Third, the carrier's `in_force_cash_value_usd` is recomputed and `concentration_bps` re-derived against the latest snapshot's `tier1_capital_usd`, with the hysteresis applied — `AUTHORIZED → RESTRICTED` once concentration reaches 1500 basis points or more, `RESTRICTED → AUTHORIZED` once it falls to 1200 or below, and the `RESTRICTED → RESTRICTED` self-loop for anything strictly above 1200 that has not yet reached the release level — and the carrier's `version` is incremented by 1. Fourth, the product's `latest_me_charge_bps` and `latest_me_drift_bps` are set from whichever posting on that product now carries the greatest `as_of_date` — normally this posting, but a statement stamped earlier than one already on file leaves the incumbent figures standing — and if that latest posting's `me_drift_bps` is **strictly greater than** 25 a product in `OPEN` moves `OPEN → ON_WATCH`; a drift of exactly 25 does not trip the watch, and this step leaves a product already `ON_WATCH` or `CLOSED_TO_NEW` where it is. Fifth, the product's `consecutive_low_yield_count` is incremented by 1 when the posting's `net_yield_bps` is **below** 150 basis points, and reset to 0 otherwise. Sixth, once that count reaches 2 or more, the product is driven to `CLOSED_TO_NEW` — from `OPEN` or from `ON_WATCH`, with identical treatment on both paths — and the owning carrier's `watch_list_flag` is set true with the carrier's `version` incremented by 1. Seventh, a carrier whose `watch_list_flag` has just turned from false to true drives **every** diligence review of that carrier currently in `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED`, incrementing each such review's `info_request_count` by 1 and its `version` by 1. Each product type the cascade mutates has its own `version` incremented by 1.

Two subtleties in that cascade deserve emphasis. The low-yield streak counter belongs to the **product**, not to the policy: it spans every policy issued under that product, and it is a running tally that each posting advances or resets as it arrives, on that posting's own `net_yield_bps`, irrespective of where its `as_of_date` falls among the postings already on file. The `as_of_date` ordering, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, governs only which posting supplies the product's `latest_me_charge_bps` and `latest_me_drift_bps`. And the cascaded information request is **not** subject to the two-round cap that governs an operator-driven information request: the cascade increments `info_request_count` regardless of how high it already stands, precisely so that a carrier deterioration always reopens the file.

An `ON_WATCH` product can be brought back. A `RiskOfficer` clears the watch, advancing the product `ON_WATCH → OPEN`, but only when the product's `latest_me_drift_bps` is 25 or below; a product still drifting above tolerance cannot be cleared and the attempt is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Clearing the watch also resets `consecutive_low_yield_count` to 0 and increments the product's `version` by 1. `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, by contrast, is terminal — a product closed to new business is never reopened. The carrier's `watch_list_flag` follows directly from that: it reads true once any product type of that carrier has reached `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, and since no product ever leaves `CLOSED_TO_NEW` the flag does not clear afterwards. That is why a carrier with a closed product behind it can be suspended but never reinstated, and why its diligence files can never score above the watch-list cap of 60. Statement periods can be listed with an optional `policy_id` filter and read individually.

### 3.6. Deferred Compensation Liability Matching and Funding Posture

The whole purpose of the insurance book is to stand behind promises the bank has made under its nonqualified deferred compensation arrangements, so the platform keeps those promises on its own ledger and measures how well the cash values cover them. A `TreasuryController` creates a plan by supplying the `plan_name` and the `plan_type`, which is one of `SERP`, `DEFERRED_BONUS` or `DIRECTOR_FEE`, and optionally the `discount_rate_bps` used by the projected-benefit view, which defaults to 500 basis points — five percent a year — and must be strictly greater than zero. A new plan opens in `ACTIVE` with `accrual_liability_usd`, `pbo_liability_usd` and `matched_cash_value_usd` all at 0.00, with `funding_posture` at `FUNDED` and `funded_ratio_bps` at 10000, which is the convention for a plan with no liabilities at all.

Participants are added one liability at a time. The controller supplies the `nqdc_plan_id` the liability belongs to, the `employee_id` of the participant, the `accrued_to_date_usd` accrued under the accrual basis (zero or greater), the total `projected_benefit_usd` payable at the payout date (strictly greater than zero), the whole number of `years_to_payout` from valuation to payout (zero or greater), and optionally the `matched_policy_id` of the policy whose cash value funds this liability — that field may be omitted or left unset, in which case the liability is simply unmatched. A liability opens in `ACTIVE`. A plan in `FROZEN` or `TERMINATED` accepts no new participant liabilities at all, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; that is exactly what freezing a plan is for. Liabilities can be listed with optional filters on `nqdc_plan_id`, `employee_id`, `matched_policy_id` and `status`, and read individually.

**Two liability bases, computed the same way from different numerators.** For each participant liability the accrual figure is simply the recorded `accrued_to_date_usd`. The projected figure, `discounted_benefit_usd`, discounts the participant's `projected_benefit_usd` back to today: divide the projected benefit by the quantity one plus the plan's `discount_rate_bps` expressed as a fraction of ten thousand, raised to the power of that participant's `years_to_payout`, and round the result half up to 2 decimal places. A participant zero years from payout is therefore discounted by nothing and their projected figure equals their projected benefit. The rounding order is pinned: **each participant's discounted amount is rounded first, and the rounded amounts are then summed.**

At plan level, `accrual_liability_usd` is the sum of `accrued_to_date_usd` over the plan's `ACTIVE` liabilities and `pbo_liability_usd` is the sum of the rounded per-participant `discounted_benefit_usd` values over the same set. `matched_cash_value_usd` is the sum of `cash_surrender_value_usd` over the policies named by those `ACTIVE` liabilities that actually carry a `matched_policy_id`, counting only policies whose status is `IN_FORCE`, `PENDING_EXCHANGE` or `CLAIM_PENDING` — a policy that has been exchanged, surrendered or paid out contributes nothing even if a liability still names it. Liabilities in `SETTLED` are excluded from both liability sums and from the matched cash value.

The funded ratio and the posture then follow. `funded_ratio_bps` is the matched cash value divided by the liability for the basis in question, multiplied by ten thousand and rounded half up to a whole number; when the basis liability is exactly 0.00 the ratio is reported as 10000 rather than being left undefined. `funding_posture` is `FUNDED` when `funded_ratio_bps` is 10000 or more — full coverage — `UNDERFUNDED` when it is at least 8000 but below 10000, and `CRITICAL` when it falls below 8000, meaning less than eighty percent of the liability is covered.

**Which basis is persisted, and which is merely exposed.** The `funded_ratio_bps` and `funding_posture` stored on the plan are **always** computed on the accrual basis. A read of the plan returns that accrual-basis posture. The funding summary read, however, accepts an optional `basis` query parameter and will report on either view: with `basis` omitted or set to `ACCRUAL` it reports the accrual view, and with `basis` set to `PBO` it reports the projected-benefit view; any other value of `basis` is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The summary is a pure read and persists nothing, not even when the view it computes differs from the posture stored on the plan. Either way the summary returns the `basis` it used, both `accrual_liability_usd` and `pbo_liability_usd`, the `matched_cash_value_usd`, the `basis_liability_usd` for the selected basis, the resulting `funded_ratio_bps` and `funding_posture`, and the `participant_count` of `ACTIVE` liabilities. Because the accrual basis uses undiscounted accruals while the projected basis uses the full projected benefit discounted back, the accrual view is systematically the more favourable of the two — the very same participants can read `UNDERFUNDED` on the accrual basis and `CRITICAL` on the projected basis. The annual risk assessment counts plans on the **projected** basis, not on the accrual posture stored on the plan.

The plan's stored totals are recomputed whenever the underlying population changes: when a participant liability is created, when a liability is settled, when the controller explicitly asks for a revaluation, and as the cascaded recompute inside a distributed death claim or an executed surrender. An explicit revaluation recomputes `accrual_liability_usd`, `pbo_liability_usd`, `matched_cash_value_usd`, `funded_ratio_bps` and `funding_posture`, drives the plan `ACTIVE → FROZEN` when the recomputed accrual-basis posture is `CRITICAL`, and increments the plan's `version` by 1. Revaluation is available from `ACTIVE` and from `FROZEN` alike — a `FROZEN` plan is simply remeasured without any status change — while a `TERMINATED` plan reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`. Creating and settling a liability recompute the same figures but never freeze the plan, however critical the recomputed posture. Settling a liability sets its `status` to `SETTLED`, increments that liability's `version` by 1, recomputes the plan's totals and accrual-basis posture, and increments the plan's `version` by 1.

Plan posture is also under direct control. Freezing takes a plan `ACTIVE → FROZEN` on the controller's own initiative and increments its `version` by 1. Unfreezing takes it `FROZEN → ACTIVE`, but only after a fresh recomputation shows the accrual-basis posture to be `FUNDED` or `UNDERFUNDED`; a plan still recomputing as `CRITICAL` cannot be unfrozen and the attempt is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Termination is available only from `FROZEN` and only when every participant liability of the plan is already `SETTLED` — a plan carrying no participant liabilities at all satisfies that test trivially; otherwise the attempt is likewise refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The cycle `ACTIVE → FROZEN → ACTIVE` may therefore run any number of times, but `TERMINATED` is terminal, as is `SETTLED` for an individual liability.

### 3.7. Death Benefit Claims, Adjudication and the Beneficiary Split

When an insured employee dies, a `ClaimsAdministrator` files a claim against the policy by naming the `policy_id`, the recorded `date_of_death`, the `proof_reference` identifying the proof-of-death document, and optionally the `terminal_dividend_usd` the carrier will add to the face amount, which defaults to 0.00 and must be zero or greater. Filing is only possible against a policy in `IN_FORCE`. The filing itself is a three-resource write: the claim is created with `adjudication_status` `FILED`, `distribution_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `allocated_bps_total` at 0, `adjudicated_by_user_id` unset and `filed_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`; the policy advances `IN_FORCE → CLAIM_PENDING` with its `version` incremented by 1; and the insured employee's `employment_status` is set to `DECEASED` with the employee's `version` incremented by 1.

A claim carries **two independent state dimensions**, and understanding that they are independent is essential. `adjudication_status` tracks whether the claim is payable and moves through `FILED`, `UNDER_ADJUDICATION`, and then to `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `VOIDED`, with `DENIED` and `VOIDED` terminal. `distribution_status` tracks whether the money has been split and paid and moves through `NOT_STARTED`, `ALLOCATED` and `DISTRIBUTED`, with `DISTRIBUTED` terminal. The dimensions advance separately: a claim can be `APPROVED` on the adjudication dimension while its distribution dimension is still `NOT_STARTED` or has reached `ALLOCATED`, and a distribution-dimension revert leaves `adjudication_status` untouched at `APPROVED`. Claims can be listed with optional filters on `policy_id`, `adjudication_status` and `distribution_status`, and read individually with both dimensions and the split amounts.

The administrator opens adjudication, moving the claim `FILED → UNDER_ADJUDICATION` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Decision then passes to the risk function. A `RiskOfficer` approves the claim, advancing `UNDER_ADJUDICATION → APPROVED`, but only if the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `filed_by_user_id`; the same operator both filing and approving is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval is where the money is worked out: `total_death_benefit_usd`, `survivor_entitlement_usd` and `bank_portion_usd` are all computed, `adjudicated_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1. Denial is the mirror decision, advancing `UNDER_ADJUDICATION → DENIED` under the identical four-eyes guard reporting the identical `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, stamping the same `adjudicated_by_user_id`, incrementing the claim's `version` by 1, and additionally reversing what filing did to the rest of the world: the policy returns `CLAIM_PENDING → IN_FORCE` with its `version` incremented by 1, and the employee's `employment_status` returns to `ACTIVE` with the employee's `version` incremented by 1. That reversal is applied only while the policy is still sitting in `CLAIM_PENDING`; a policy that has since moved elsewhere, and its employee with it, are left untouched and take no version increment.

**Splitting the benefit.** The total benefit is the policy's `face_amount_usd` plus the claim's `terminal_dividend_usd`. Against that total two ceilings are computed and the lower one wins. The first is a proportional cap: sixty hundredths of the total death benefit, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The second is a salary multiple: the insured employee's `annual_salary_usd` multiplied by two, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `survivor_entitlement_usd` is the lesser of those two figures, and `bank_portion_usd` is the total death benefit minus the survivor entitlement — so a highly paid employee under a modest face amount is capped by the proportional test, while a modestly paid employee under a large face amount is capped by the salary multiple.

Family shares are recorded one at a time. The administrator adds a beneficiary by naming the `claim_id` being split, the `beneficiary_name`, the `relationship` of that beneficiary to the insured — one of `SPOUSE`, `CHILD`, `TRUST` or `ESTATE` — and the `allocation_pct_bps` share in basis points, which must be at least 1 and at most 10000. Each allocation opens in `ACTIVE` with `gross_amount_usd` at 0.00, its `allocation_pct_bps` is added to the claim's `allocated_bps_total`, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1. The create imposes no precondition on either of the claim's state dimensions — a share may be added to a claim still in `FILED` just as readily as to an `APPROVED` one — so the running-total ceiling below is its only business gate. The running total of `ACTIVE` allocations may never exceed 10000 basis points on a create, and an addition that would push it past that is rejected with `ERR_BENEFICIARY_TOTAL_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Allocations in `VOIDED` contribute neither basis points nor amounts. Allocations can be listed with optional filters on `claim_id` and `status`, and read individually.

Sealing the split advances the claim's distribution dimension `NOT_STARTED → ALLOCATED`. Two guards apply: the claim's `adjudication_status` must already be `APPROVED`, failing which the seal is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and the `ACTIVE` allocations must sum to **exactly** 10000 basis points, with any other total refused as `ERR_BENEFICIARY_TOTAL_INVALID`. This single guard on `adjudication_status` is the only coupling between the two dimensions on the way up. Sealing computes `gross_amount_usd` on every `ACTIVE` allocation, and the rounding order is pinned: first, walking the `ACTIVE` allocations in order of `created_at` ascending, each allocation's share is the `survivor_entitlement_usd` multiplied by that allocation's `allocation_pct_bps` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Then the residual is computed as `survivor_entitlement_usd` minus the sum of those rounded shares — a value that may be positive or negative. That single signed residual is added to exactly one allocation: the `ACTIVE` allocation carrying the greatest `allocation_pct_bps`, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by `id`. Summing the unrounded shares and rounding the total at the end would produce different cents and is not the order used. After every allocation mutation the conservation identity must hold exactly: the `total_death_benefit_usd` equals `bank_portion_usd` plus the sum of `gross_amount_usd` over the claim's `ACTIVE` allocations.

A sealed split can be unwound one beneficiary at a time. Voiding an allocation sets its `status` to `VOIDED` and its `gross_amount_usd` to 0.00, reduces the claim's `allocated_bps_total` by the voided `allocation_pct_bps`, reverts the claim's distribution dimension `ALLOCATED → NOT_STARTED`, increments the allocation's `version` by 1 and the claim's `version` by 1. The claim's `distribution_status` must be `ALLOCATED` for this to be available, and an attempt against a claim whose distribution dimension is elsewhere is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Note what the revert leaves alone: `adjudication_status` stays at `APPROVED`, so the administrator can correct a share and re-seal without re-adjudicating.

Voiding the **claim** is the broader reversal and is available from `FILED`, `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` or `APPROVED` on the adjudication dimension, driving it to `VOIDED`. It requires only that the claim's `distribution_status` is not yet `DISTRIBUTED` — once money has gone out the claim can no longer be voided, and the attempt is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Voiding reverses everything filing did and everything allocation did: the policy returns `CLAIM_PENDING → IN_FORCE`, the employee's `employment_status` returns to `ACTIVE`, every `ACTIVE` beneficiary allocation is set to `VOIDED` with its `gross_amount_usd` zeroed, the claim's `allocated_bps_total` is reset to 0, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1 along with one increment on each other resource the reversal touched. Two limits on that breadth: the policy and employee reversal is applied only while the policy is still in `CLAIM_PENDING`, and the distribution dimension itself is not rewound — a claim voided out of a sealed split keeps `distribution_status` at `ALLOCATED`.

**Distribution** is the end of the line and the largest cascade in the system. It advances the claim's distribution dimension `ALLOCATED → DISTRIBUTED`, requires the claim's `adjudication_status` to be `APPROVED` and its `distribution_status` to be `ALLOCATED` — both enforced as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and increments the claim's `version` by 1. Its first hop drives the policy `CLAIM_PENDING → PAID_OUT` and sets the policy's `cash_surrender_value_usd` to 0.00, incrementing the policy's `version` by 1. Its second hop removes that policy's premium and cash value from the carrier's `in_force_premium_usd` and `in_force_cash_value_usd`, recomputes the carrier's `concentration_bps` and re-applies the authorization hysteresis — which is where a paid-out policy can release a `RESTRICTED` carrier back to `AUTHORIZED` if concentration has fallen to 1200 basis points or below — and increments the carrier's `version` by 1. Its third hop reaches the deferred compensation ledger: every participant liability whose `matched_policy_id` was this policy has that field cleared, and that unmatch recomputes the owning plan's `accrual_liability_usd`, `pbo_liability_usd`, `matched_cash_value_usd`, `funded_ratio_bps` and `funding_posture`. If the recomputed accrual-basis posture is `CRITICAL`, the plan is driven `ACTIVE → FROZEN` in the same request. Every liability, plan, policy and carrier the cascade touched receives exactly one `version` increment.

### 3.8. Disposition Analysis, Tax Consequences and Execution

Nothing leaves the insurance book without being modelled first. A `TreasuryController` opens a disposition analysis on a policy by naming the `policy_id`, the `disposition_type` under consideration — one of `FULL_SURRENDER`, `PARTIAL_SURRENDER` or `EXCHANGE_1035` — the `expected_policy_version` echoing the policy `version` the controller last read, and, depending on the disposition, either a `replacement_carrier_id` or a `requested_withdrawal_usd`. The replacement carrier is the receiving carrier of a section 1035 exchange and must be supplied **exactly when** `disposition_type` is `EXCHANGE_1035` and left unset otherwise; supplying it for a surrender, or omitting it for an exchange, is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`. The withdrawal amount applies only to a partial surrender, must be zero or greater, defaults to 0.00, and must be 0.00 for the other two dispositions — a non-zero withdrawal on a `FULL_SURRENDER` or an `EXCHANGE_1035` is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because it sits in the schema band it surfaces ahead of even the policy's own existence check. For a partial surrender the requested withdrawal must not exceed the policy's `cash_surrender_value_usd`, and an excessive request is rejected with `ERR_SURRENDER_EXCEEDS_CASH_VALUE` (HTTP 422). The `expected_policy_version` guard is evaluated once the policy and any named replacement carrier have been resolved but before both the replacement-carrier consistency test and that withdrawal-versus-cash-value test.

Creating an analysis is deliberately a superseding operation: every other analysis on the same policy still sitting in `DRAFT` or `REVIEWED` is set to `REJECTED` in the same request. That is why the create is guarded by `expected_policy_version` rather than by a state precondition — a second create is a legitimate act of replacing an earlier plan, and what the platform refuses is an author working from a view of the policy that predates a supersede that already happened. The create responds with HTTP 201, opens the new analysis in `DRAFT` with `resulting_policy_id` unset, and increments the policy's `version` by 1. Analyses can be listed with optional filters on `policy_id`, `replacement_carrier_id`, `resulting_policy_id` and `status`, and read individually.

**The tax model.** Two variants exist, and which one applies is decided by the product, not by the operator. `cost_recovery_method` is set to `LIFO_INCOME_FIRST` when the policy's product carries `mec_status` `MEC`, and to `FIFO_BASIS_FIRST` when it carries `NON_MEC`. The surrender charge tier is read from the policy's `policy_year`: a first-year contract carries 600 basis points, a second-year contract 500, a third 400, a fourth 300, a fifth 200, a sixth 100, and a contract in its seventh year or any later year carries no surrender charge at all — years beyond the seventh are treated as the seventh. No operation in this specification advances `policy_year`: it is set to 1 at issuance and to 1 again on an exchange successor, so in practice every analysis reads the first-year tier of 600 basis points. Two further constants apply to the tax figure itself: the federal rate is 2100 basis points and the state rate is 600 basis points, so a taxable gain is taxed at a combined twenty-seven percent.

The gross amount and the taxable gain then depend on the disposition. For a `FULL_SURRENDER` the gross amount is the policy's whole `cash_surrender_value_usd`, and the `taxable_gain_usd` is that cash value minus the policy's `cost_basis_usd`, or zero if the basis exceeds the cash value. For an `EXCHANGE_1035` the gross amount is likewise the whole cash value, but the `taxable_gain_usd` is 0.00 — that tax deferral is the entire point of the exchange. For a `PARTIAL_SURRENDER` the gross amount is the `requested_withdrawal_usd`, and here the two cost-recovery variants diverge. Under `FIFO_BASIS_FIRST` the withdrawal recovers cost basis first, so the `taxable_gain_usd` is the withdrawal minus the policy's `cost_basis_usd`, or zero if the basis is the larger — meaning a withdrawal that stays below basis is entirely tax free. Under `LIFO_INCOME_FIRST` the withdrawal distributes income first, so the `taxable_gain_usd` is the lesser of the withdrawal and the inside build-up, where the inside build-up is the cash value minus the cost basis floored at zero — meaning the very same withdrawal on identical figures is fully taxable up to the gain in the contract.

From those two quantities the remaining figures follow, with a pinned rounding order. `surrender_charge_usd` is the gross amount multiplied by the tier's basis points divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `tax_liability_usd` is the `taxable_gain_usd` multiplied by the combined federal and state basis points divided by ten thousand, also rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `net_proceeds_usd` is then the gross amount minus the rounded surrender charge minus the rounded tax liability — each of the two deductions is rounded **before** the subtraction, never after.

The analysis then walks a five-state workflow. A `RiskOfficer` reviews a `DRAFT` analysis, advancing it `DRAFT → REVIEWED`; the policy must still be `IN_FORCE`, and the review recomputes `cost_recovery_method`, `taxable_gain_usd`, `surrender_charge_usd`, `tax_liability_usd` and `net_proceeds_usd` against the policy's current values, so a statement posted between create and review is reflected in the reviewed figures. The same officer may instead reject a `REVIEWED` analysis, advancing it `REVIEWED → REJECTED`, which is terminal and touches nothing else.

Committing is the treasury controller's decision to proceed and advances the analysis `REVIEWED → COMMITTED`. It requires the policy to be `IN_FORCE` and drives it `IN_FORCE → PENDING_EXCHANGE`, incrementing the policy's `version` by 1 and the analysis's `version` by 1. A commitment can be walked back: abandoning advances the analysis `COMMITTED → ABANDONED` and drives the policy `PENDING_EXCHANGE → IN_FORCE`, restoring the policy's `cost_basis_usd` and `cash_surrender_value_usd` to precisely their pre-commit values. Commit followed by abandon must therefore be exactly cost-basis neutral, and abandonment requires the policy to be in `PENDING_EXCHANGE`.

**Executing an exchange.** From `COMMITTED`, with the policy in `PENDING_EXCHANGE` and `disposition_type` equal to `EXCHANGE_1035`, the controller executes the exchange by supplying the successor contract's `policy_number` and `issue_date`. The `replacement_carrier_id` must be set and that carrier must be `AUTHORIZED`; a replacement carrier that is `RESTRICTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` blocks execution with `ERR_CARRIER_RESTRICTED`. Execution responds with HTTP 201 and the successor policy. The successor is created with `exchanged_from_policy_id` pointing at the predecessor, `single_premium_usd` and `cash_surrender_value_usd` both equal to the analysis's `net_proceeds_usd` — the net transferred cash value — `cost_basis_usd` carried over from the predecessor **unchanged**, which is exactly what makes the exchange tax deferred rather than tax free, `policy_year` reset to 1, and status `IN_FORCE`. The successor also copies the predecessor's `product_type_id` and `face_amount_usd` — it is not re-registered under a product belonging to the replacement carrier, so its product type still names the old carrier — and it carries no `purchase_request_id`, since no purchase request stands behind it. The replacement carrier's posture is the only eligibility test at execution; no product-status check applies. The analysis advances `COMMITTED → EXECUTED` with `resulting_policy_id` set to the successor, and the predecessor advances `PENDING_EXCHANGE → EXCHANGED`. Both carriers are then re-rated: the predecessor's premium and cash value are removed from the old carrier's `in_force_premium_usd` and `in_force_cash_value_usd`, the successor's figures are added to the replacement carrier's totals, `concentration_bps` is recomputed on both with the hysteresis applied on each, and both carriers' `version` values are incremented by 1.

**Executing a surrender.** From `COMMITTED`, with the policy in `PENDING_EXCHANGE` and `disposition_type` equal to `FULL_SURRENDER` or `PARTIAL_SURRENDER`, the controller executes the surrender. The analysis advances `COMMITTED → EXECUTED` and the policy advances `PENDING_EXCHANGE → SURRENDERED`. On a `FULL_SURRENDER` the policy's `cash_surrender_value_usd` is set to 0.00 and the policy is removed from the carrier's in-force totals entirely; on a `PARTIAL_SURRENDER` the policy's `cash_surrender_value_usd` is reduced by the `requested_withdrawal_usd` instead, floored at 0.00, and its `cost_basis_usd` is left alone. Both dispositions nevertheless drive the policy to `SURRENDERED`, which is not a live status, so on either path the policy leaves the carrier's `in_force_premium_usd` and `in_force_cash_value_usd` entirely; the partial path differs only in the residual cash value it leaves on the policy record. Either way the carrier's `concentration_bps` is recomputed with the hysteresis applied — the release path back to `AUTHORIZED` at 1200 basis points or below is live here — and the carrier's `version` is incremented by 1. The surrender then runs the same third hop as a distributed claim: every participant liability whose `matched_policy_id` was this policy has that field cleared, the owning plan's totals, `funded_ratio_bps` and `funding_posture` are recomputed, and a plan whose recomputed accrual-basis posture is `CRITICAL` is driven `ACTIVE → FROZEN`. `EXECUTED`, `ABANDONED` and `REJECTED` are the terminal analysis states.

### 3.9. Annual Risk Assessment and Board Reporting

Once a year the risk function compiles a single document that tells the board how much risk the bank-owned life insurance programme is carrying. A `RiskOfficer` compiles it by naming the `capital_position_id` of the capital snapshot the assessment is measured against and the `assessment_year` the report covers. No two assessments may carry the same `assessment_year` whatever status the incumbent has reached, and a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_YEAR_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409); compilation also needs a capital snapshot to exist at all, failing which it reports `ERR_NO_CAPITAL_POSITION`. Compilation responds with HTTP 201, opens the assessment in `COMPILED`, stamps `compiled_at` with the current logical clock instant, and leaves `board_note` unset. Assessments can be listed with optional filters on `capital_position_id`, `status` and `assessment_year`, and read individually.

**The six counts.** `restricted_carrier_count` counts the carriers whose `concentration_bps` is **strictly greater** than 1500 and whose `status` is not `TERMINATED` — note that this count uses a strict comparison against the restriction entry threshold, so a carrier sitting at exactly 1500 basis points is restricted but is not counted here. `watch_list_carrier_count` counts the carriers whose `watch_list_flag` is true and whose `status` is not `TERMINATED`. `on_watch_product_count` counts the product types in `ON_WATCH` and `closed_product_count` counts those in `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, both regardless of the posture of the carrier behind them. `critical_plan_count` and `underfunded_plan_count` count the deferred compensation plans whose `status` is not `TERMINATED` and whose funding posture on the **projected-benefit basis** is `CRITICAL` and `UNDERFUNDED` respectively — deliberately not the accrual-basis posture persisted on the plan, because the board is entitled to the less flattering of the two views.

**The weighted total.** `risk_points` is the sum of six weighted terms: each restricted carrier contributes 20 points, each watch-list carrier 15, each `ON_WATCH` product 5, each `CLOSED_TO_NEW` product 10, each critical plan 25, and each underfunded plan 10. All counts and the point total are whole numbers and no rounding is involved. `risk_rating` is then `SEVERE` when `risk_points` is 60 or more, `HIGH` when it is at least 35 but below 60, `MODERATE` when it is at least 15 but below 35, and `LOW` when it is below 15. The capital snapshot the assessment names is recorded as `capital_position_id` alone: the assessment does not copy `tier1_capital_usd` onto itself, and that figure contributes nothing to `risk_points`.

Submission to the board advances the assessment to `BOARD_SUBMITTED` and is available from either `COMPILED` or `RETURNED`. The two entry paths differ in one important respect: submitting from `COMPILED` carries the figures forward as compiled, while submitting from `RETURNED` **recompiles** every one of the six counts, `risk_points` and `risk_rating` before submitting, so that a board that sent the report back for rework receives figures measured against the world as it now stands rather than as it stood at the original compilation. Either way the assessment's `version` is incremented by 1.

The `BoardSecretary` closes the loop. Acceptance advances the assessment `BOARD_SUBMITTED → BOARD_ACCEPTED` and stores the supplied `board_note`, which is the note the secretary records on the board's behalf and is a required non-empty string on both the acceptance and the return; the assessment's `version` is incremented by 1 and `BOARD_ACCEPTED` is terminal. A return advances it `BOARD_SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, likewise storing the supplied `board_note` and incrementing the `version` by 1, and hands the report back to the risk officer. The cycle `BOARD_SUBMITTED → RETURNED → BOARD_SUBMITTED` may run as many times as the board requires, with a full recompilation on every re-submission.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The API is mounted at the server root, so every path below is absolute. Creation of a new persisted resource responds with HTTP 201 and the created object; every other successful operation, including every state-advancing action and every read, responds with HTTP 200. The two exceptions worth calling out are that filing a capital snapshot, posting a statement period, adding a participant liability, adding a beneficiary allocation and executing a section 1035 exchange all create resources and therefore all answer 201, while an action such as approving, committing or distributing answers 200 with the updated resource.
