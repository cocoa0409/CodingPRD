# Plant Breeding Variety Trials API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A seed company's research and development organisation is, at heart, a machine for converting scarce field plots and scarce cash into a small number of commercial varieties. Every season a breeding programme must decide which germplasm gets sown in a winter nursery in the southern hemisphere, which lines earn a place in a replicated multi-location yield trial, which candidates survive the stage gate into pre-commercial increase, and which get discarded. Those decisions are made against noisy phenotypic data, a fixed plot budget, a fixed trialing budget, and — for traited varieties — a regulatory and grower-stewardship envelope that can be withdrawn at any time. Today that decision record lives in a scatter of spreadsheets, nursery notebooks and email approvals, which makes the same question ("how many plots has this programme actually committed, and to what?") produce a different answer depending on who is asked.

This document specifies the Plant Breeding Variety Trials API, the system of record for that pipeline. It governs breeding objective definition by market, crossing block planning and progeny registration, nursery occupancy and generation advancement, phenotyping with replicated plot designs, multi-location trial networks with environment classification and Finlay–Wilkinson stability analysis, marker-assisted backcrossing for trait integration, stewardship agreements for traited varieties, stage-gate advancement decisions with explicit resource allocation, and pre-commercial seed increase with launch volume planning. The API is deliberately conservative: every plot and every gram of parent seed is a conserved quantity, and the server maintains the counters that prove conservation rather than trusting a client to compute them.

Two properties of the design deserve emphasis up front, because they shape almost every rule that follows. First, the system is **posture-driven**: trial sites, breeding lines and programmes all carry derived postures and statuses that gate what may be done next, and those postures are re-derived by the server after every relevant write rather than being set by a client. Second, the system is **deterministic**: all arithmetic is exact decimal arithmetic, all cascade orderings are pinned, and the two operations that can legitimately supersede a previously written record require the client to prove it has observed the record's current state before writing.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere in the API. Endpoints are declared at the API root: there is no version segment and no tenant segment, so every path in §4 is absolute and complete.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Seven roles are recognised, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `PipelineDirector`, `ProgramLead`, `BreedingScientist`, `TrialAgronomist`, `DataAnalyst`, `RegulatoryOfficer`, and `SupplyPlanner`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those seven strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are peers rather than a hierarchy: each owns a disjoint slice of the pipeline. `PipelineDirector` owns the R&D portfolio — registering and closing programmes, recording every stage-gate advancement decision, and closing analysed series — with global scope rather than being tied to one programme. `ProgramLead` runs a single breeding programme. `BreedingScientist` owns germplasm and nursery operations: nursery blocks, crossing blocks, pollination, progeny registration, generation-advancement plantings and marker-assisted backcrossing. `TrialAgronomist` owns the multi-location trial network: sites, entries, series planting, phenotyping, and per-entry data closure. `DataAnalyst` owns statistical analysis and pipeline reporting. `RegulatoryOfficer` owns stewardship and grower-agreement compliance for traited varieties. `SupplyPlanner` owns pre-commercial seed increase and launch volume planning. All seven roles may set the logical clock described in §2.5.

Identity semantics differ between `ProgramLead` and the other six roles, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramLead`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BreedingProgram`; ownership checks compare the owning programme of every resource named in the path and in the body — the `program_id` of an objective, line, trial series or crossing block, or the programme behind the line that stands behind an agreement, a seed increase lot, a nursery planting, a trial entry or a backcross project — against `X-User-ID`, and any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PipelineDirector`, `BreedingScientist`, `TrialAgronomist`, `DataAnalyst`, `RegulatoryOfficer` or `SupplyPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — as `decided_by_user_id` on a decision recorded by a `PipelineDirector`, as `recorded_by_user_id` on an observation captured by a `TrialAgronomist`, as `run_by_user_id` on an analysis executed by a `DataAnalyst`, and as `submitted_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on an agreement handled by a `RegulatoryOfficer` — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. One consequence of the opaque form matters for compliance: because a `RegulatoryOfficer` identifier is not an entity key, the segregation-of-duties rule in §3.9 compares two recorded header values directly.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records and defaults to 0; negative offsets are not accepted. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two orderings do differ and are stated where they belong: the `entry_means` and `environment_index` arrays inside a series analysis are ordered by their own metrics (§3.7).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Record Versioning

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp. Client-supplied identifiers are never honoured for `id`.

Most resources — `BreedingProgram`, `BreedingObjective`, `NurseryBlock`, `CrossingBlock`, `BreedingLine`, `NurseryPlanting`, `TrialSite`, `TrialSeries`, `TrialEntry`, `TrialSeriesAnalysis`, `AdvancementDecision`, `StewardshipAgreement`, `SeedIncreaseLot` and `BackcrossProject` — additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation of the record, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade. A change to a derived counter is a mutation for this purpose: when a nursery harvest credits seed to a line, or a withdrawal releases plots back to a programme, the affected record's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed.

`PhenotypeObservation` is the single exception. A phenotype observation is an immutable measurement: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation.

### 2.5. The Agronomic Calendar and the Logical Clock

Time in this system is agronomic, so two wire formats coexist and each field uses exactly one of them. Calendar fields are **date-only**, written `YYYY-MM-DD` with no time part and no zone: `sow_date`, `expected_harvest_date`, `occupancy_end_date` and `actual_harvest_date` on a nursery planting; `season_start_date` and `season_end_date` on a trial site; `planting_window_start` and `planting_window_end` on a trial entry; and `effective_from` and `effective_to` on a stewardship agreement. Instants are written `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` — UTC, second precision, no fractional seconds — and only three fields use that shape: the common `created_at` and `updated_at`, and the logical clock's `current_utc`. A date-only value that is not exactly ten characters in `YYYY-MM-DD` form is a malformed request and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

All date comparisons read a server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real system time. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it; that call is available to all seven roles, and setting the clock does not by itself recompute any entity — derived values are re-derived when the operations that own them run. Whenever a date-only field is compared against the clock, the comparison uses the clock's UTC calendar date.

### 2.6. Decimal Discipline: Exact Arithmetic and Pinned Rounding Order

Every arithmetic rule in this specification is performed on exact decimals, never on binary floating point. This is not a stylistic preference: several of the pinned intermediate values in this domain land exactly on a half — a raw plot mean of 72.455 must become 72.46 — and binary floating point silently produces the other answer. Unless a rule names a different rounding place, rounding is **half up** at the decimal place the rule names.

The specific precisions are: monetary values (`plot_cost_usd`, `total_budget_usd`, `plot_cost_subtotal_usd`, `management_fee_usd`, `total_trial_cost_usd`) at 2 decimal places, half up; `plot_mean_yield_kg` at 3 decimal places, half up; `yield_kg_per_ha`, `adjusted_mean_kg_per_ha`, `index_kg_per_ha`, `deviation_mse`, each component of the advancement index and `decision_index_value` at 2 decimal places, half up; `stability_regression_b` at 4 decimal places, half up; `recurrent_genome_pct` at 3 decimal places, half up. `planned_units_bags` and `allocated_seed_g` are integers produced by rounding upward to the next whole number, and all seed masses, plot counts and generation numbers are integers with no rounding at all.

Rounding **order** is as important as rounding place, and where an order is pinned this document says so explicitly at the point of use. Two cases recur: a per-hectare yield is derived from the already-rounded three-decimal plot mean rather than from the raw sum, and a stability regression is computed from already-rounded two-decimal yields and environment indices rather than from re-derived ones. Ratios used to classify a posture — the site utilisation of §3.5 — are compared as exact decimal ratios, so a boundary value lands on the side the rule names rather than one place away from it.

### 2.7. Validation Order and the First-Failure Rule

A single request can violate several rules at once, and the response must be predictable, so the checks below are evaluated **strictly in the order listed** and the **first** one that fails decides the response. A later check never masks an earlier one: if a request both cites a closed programme and carries a stale version, the stale-version rejection wins because it sits earlier in the ladder. Each numbered step names the rule, the error it raises, and the operations it guards; the workflow narrative in §3 repeats the domain reasoning behind the interesting ones.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven role names — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list in §4 — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** The body and query string parse; required fields are present; enum members are recognised; numbers are within their declared ranges; date-only fields are `YYYY-MM-DD`; `limit` is between 1 and 100 and `offset` is at least 0. This step also settles the cross-field rules that need no other entity to decide: `granted_plots` must be 0 unless `decision` is `ADVANCE`; `donor_line_id` must differ from `recurrent_line_id`; `market_allocations` must be non-empty with every `demand_kg` greater than 0; `approved_markets` must be non-empty; `yield_weight` plus `disease_weight` plus `quality_weight` must equal exactly 1.00; and `expected_harvest_date` must be strictly later than `sow_date`. Any failure here is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the path resolves, validated left to right, with the first unresolved identifier deciding the code: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NURSERY_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CROSSING_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLANTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_SERIES_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEED_LOT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_BACKCROSS_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint that takes a path identifier.
5. **Programme scope.** When the acting role is `ProgramLead`, `X-User-ID` must equal the owning `BreedingProgram` identifier of every resource named in the path and in the body — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `ProgramLead`.
6. **Body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves, validated in the order the endpoint's input list declares them, raising the matching 404 from the set `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NURSERY_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_SERIES_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to crossing block planning, direct breeding line registration, progeny registration, nursery planting creation, trial series creation, trial entry creation, advancement decision recording, seed increase lot reservation, and backcross project creation.
7. **Line staleness.** `expected_line_version` equals the target `BreedingLine`'s current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_LINE_VERSION` (412). Applies to advancement decision recording.
8. **Series staleness.** `expected_series_version` equals the target `TrialSeries`' current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` (412). Applies to series analysis creation.
9. **Programme open.** The `BreedingProgram` behind the resource being created or mutated has `status` `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED` (409). Applies to every write that creates or mutates a resource owned by a programme.
10. **Planting state.** The nursery planting is in the state its transition requires — `SCHEDULED` to be marked as sown, `PLANTED` to be harvested — otherwise `ERR_PLANTING_INVALID_STATE` (409).
11. **Series state.** The trial series is `PLANNING` for entry creation and for planting, `DATA_COMPLETE` or `ANALYZED` for analysis creation, and `ANALYZED` for closure; a `CLOSED` series accepts no mutation at all. Violations raise `ERR_SERIES_INVALID_STATE` (409).
12. **Entry state.** The trial entry is `PLANTED` for finalisation and for invalidation, and either `ENTERED` or `PLANTED` for withdrawal and for recording an observation — otherwise `ERR_ENTRY_INVALID_STATE` (409).
13. **Agreement state.** The stewardship agreement is `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` for submission, `SUBMITTED` for review, and `APPROVED` for suspension — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409).
14. **Lot state.** The seed increase lot is `RESERVED` to start production, `IN_PRODUCTION` to be completed, and one of `RESERVED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `ON_HOLD` to be cancelled — otherwise `ERR_LOT_INVALID_STATE` (409).
15. **Project active.** The backcross project has `status` `ACTIVE` before another marker cycle runs — otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
16. **Line not discarded.** Every `BreedingLine` the mutation references has `pipeline_stage` other than `DISCARDED` — otherwise `ERR_LINE_DISCARDED` (409). This guards eight operations: nursery planting creation, marking a planting as sown, trial entry creation, stewardship agreement drafting, agreement submission, seed increase lot reservation, backcross project creation, and running a marker cycle.
17. **Crossing block pollinated.** The crossing block named in the path has `status` `POLLINATED` before a progeny line may be registered against it — otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_POLLINATED` (409).
18. **Cited series analysed.** The `TrialSeries` an advancement decision cites has `status` `ANALYZED` — otherwise `ERR_SERIES_INVALID_STATE` (409).
19. **Stewardship approved.** The line behind a seed increase lot has an `APPROVED` stewardship agreement, meaning its `stewardship_status` is `APPROVED`; both `NOT_REQUIRED` and `PENDING` fail with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_NOT_APPROVED` (422). Applies to seed increase lot reservation and to starting production on a lot.
20. **Stewardship not suspended.** The line's `stewardship_status` is not `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to seed increase lot reservation, to starting production on a lot, and to an `ADVANCE` decision leaving `PRE_COMMERCIAL`.
21. **Segregation of duties.** `X-User-ID` differs from the agreement's `approved_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to agreement suspension.
22. **Market covered.** `target_market` appears in the `approved_markets` array of the line's `APPROVED` stewardship agreement — otherwise `ERR_MARKET_NOT_IN_AGREEMENT` (422). Applies to seed increase lot reservation.
23. **Objective belongs to the programme.** The referenced `BreedingObjective`'s `program_id` equals the line's `program_id` — otherwise `ERR_OBJECTIVE_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to direct breeding line registration and to progeny registration.
24. **Parents belong to the programme.** `female_parent_line_id` and `male_parent_line_id` both belong to the same `program_id` as the new line, and a backcross `donor_line_id` belongs to the recurrent line's programme — otherwise `ERR_PARENT_LINE_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to progeny registration, direct breeding line registration, and backcross project creation.
25. **Site not frozen.** The target `TrialSite`'s `capacity_posture` is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_SITE_CAPACITY_FROZEN` (409). Applies to trial entry creation.
26. **Restricted-site stage gate.** When the target site's `capacity_posture` is `RESTRICTED`, only a series whose `stage` is `STAGE_2` or `STAGE_3` may add an entry — otherwise `ERR_SITE_RESTRICTED_STAGE` (409). Applies to trial entry creation.
27. **Block occupancy.** The new planting's occupancy interval does not intersect the interval of any other planting on the same nursery block, as specified in §3.3 — otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to nursery planting creation.
28. **Nursery season.** The `sow_date` month is admissible for the block's `nursery_type` — otherwise `ERR_NURSERY_SEASON_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to nursery planting creation.
29. **Sowing not in the past.** `sow_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's current date — otherwise `ERR_SOW_DATE_IN_PAST` (422). Applies to nursery planting creation.
30. **Entry window inside the season.** `planting_window_start` is on or after the site's `season_start_date`, `planting_window_end` is on or before the site's `season_end_date`, and `planting_window_end` is on or after `planting_window_start` — otherwise `ERR_ENTRY_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_SEASON` (422). Applies to trial entry creation.
31. **Agreement dates.** `effective_from` is on or after the logical clock's current date and `effective_to` is strictly later than `effective_from` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_DATES_INVALID` (422). Applies to stewardship agreement drafting.
32. **No duplicate entry.** No existing `TrialEntry` whose `status` is `ENTERED`, `PLANTED` or `HARVESTED` already carries the same combination of `trial_series_id`, `line_id` and `trial_site_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRIAL_ENTRY` (409). Applies to trial entry creation.
33. **Replication divisibility.** The entry's `plot_count` is an exact multiple of the series' `replication_count` — otherwise `ERR_PLOT_COUNT_NOT_DIVISIBLE` (422). Applies to trial entry creation.
34. **Observation uniqueness.** No `PhenotypeObservation` already exists with the same combination of `trial_entry_id`, `trait_code` and `rep_number`, and `rep_number` lies between 1 and the series' `replication_count` inclusive — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBSERVATION` (409). Applies to observation recording.
35. **Sufficient replication.** The entry's `valid_rep_count` is at least 3 — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REPLICATION` (422). Applies to entry finalisation.
36. **Sufficient locations.** The series' `location_count` is at least its `min_locations` and its `entry_count` is at least 2 — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOCATIONS` (422). Applies to series planting.
37. **Non-empty inclusion set.** For the `BALANCED_CORE` variant the core site set is non-empty, and for either variant at least one `HARVESTED` entry exists — otherwise `ERR_EMPTY_BALANCED_CORE` (422). Applies to series analysis creation.
38. **Trial data exists.** The decision's line has at least one `TrialEntry` with `status` `HARVESTED` in the cited series — otherwise `ERR_NO_TRIAL_DATA` (422). Applies to advancement decision recording.
39. **Line ready.** For an `ADVANCE` decision leaving `NURSERY`, the line's `advancement_readiness` is `READY` — otherwise `ERR_LINE_NOT_READY` (422). Applies to advancement decision recording.
40. **Index clears the bar.** For an `ADVANCE` decision, the CUMULATIVE advancement index is at least the objective's `min_advancement_index` — otherwise `ERR_INDEX_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (422). Applies to advancement decision recording.
41. **No agreement already live.** The line has no `StewardshipAgreement` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to stewardship agreement drafting.
42. **Code uniqueness.** `block_code` is unique across all nursery blocks, `site_code` is unique across all trial sites, and `designation` is unique within its `program_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). Applies to nursery block registration, trial site registration, direct breeding line registration, and progeny registration.
43. **Block plot capacity.** The planting's `plot_count` does not exceed the nursery block's physical `plot_count` — otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_PLOT_COUNT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to nursery planting creation.
44. **Site plot capacity.** The post-insertion daily peak at the site does not exceed the site's `total_plot_capacity` — otherwise `ERR_SITE_PLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to trial entry creation.
45. **Programme plot budget.** The plots the request commits — the entry's `plot_count`, the planting's `plot_count`, or the decision's `granted_plots` — do not exceed the programme's `plot_budget_remaining`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_PLOT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to trial entry creation, nursery planting creation, and advancement decision recording.
46. **Programme cash budget.** The series' `total_trial_cost_usd` does not exceed the programme's `total_budget_usd` less its `committed_budget_usd` — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_USD_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to series planting.
47. **Parent seed on hand.** The lot's `allocated_seed_g` does not exceed the line's `seed_inventory_g` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEED_INVENTORY` (409). Applies to seed increase lot reservation.

### 2.8. Nested Resource Retrieval and Path Resolution

Several resources are addressed underneath their container, because containment here is real ownership: objectives, trial series listings and the pipeline report hang off a programme; entries and analyses hang off a trial series; observations hang off a trial entry; stewardship agreements and seed increase lot listings hang off a breeding line. Where a relationship is a reference rather than containment — a line's parents, a junction entry's site, a backcross project's donor — the related resource is named in the body or in a query filter, never in the path.

When a path carries more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first one that does not resolve decides the 404 code, before any body-level check runs. A request for an observation under a trial entry that does not exist fails with `ERR_TRIAL_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` even if the body is also malformed in a way that would otherwise be reported, because path resolution sits at step 4 of the ladder in §2.7 while body foreign keys sit at step 6. Path resolution also precedes every state and business check, so a mutation aimed at a non-existent series never reports a state conflict.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two writes in this system legitimately supersede an existing record, and both therefore require the client to prove it has observed the current state of the record it is about to reorganise. Outside these two, no endpoint accepts a version field and no endpoint performs a version check.

The first is **recording an advancement decision**. The request must carry `expected_line_version` as a required field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `BreedingLine`. If that value does not equal the line's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LINE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is structural: a new decision for the same combination of `line_id` and `trial_series_id` marks the previous decision superseded and releases its granted plots, so a writer holding a stale view of the line would silently overturn a decision it never read.

The second is **creating a trial series analysis**. The request must carry `expected_series_version` as a required field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `TrialSeries`. If that value does not equal the series' current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Again the reason is structural: a new analysis of the same `variant` supersedes the prior analysis row, and a writer whose view of the series predates a data change — a late finalisation, a withdrawal, or another analyst's run — would otherwise publish results computed over a different inclusion set than the one the series now has.

Both checks sit early in the ladder of §2.7, at steps 7 and 8, ahead of every state and business rule. A stale write is therefore reported as a staleness failure rather than as whatever downstream conflict it would have caused.

### 2.10. Cascading Consistency and Server-Derived State

Almost every write in this API has secondary consequences, and the specification treats those consequences as part of the operation rather than as eventual housekeeping. Consistency is immediate: by the time a response is returned, every counter, posture and parent status the write affects has already been re-derived, and a subsequent read observes the new values. Where a cascade touches several sibling records, the records are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and where one cascade must complete before another begins, this document pins the order explicitly at the point of use — the discard cascade in §3.8 is the clearest example.

Derived fields are never accepted from a client. A programme's plot and cash commitments, a nursery block's occupancy, a site's peak occupancy and posture, a series' entry and location census, an entry's replication census and yield rollup, a line's readiness, seed ledger and provisional index — all of these are computed by the server from the records that justify them, and a value supplied for such a field in a request body is ignored. Two state dimensions on a `BreedingLine` illustrate how independent the derived machinery is: `pipeline_stage` is driven only by advancement decisions and `stewardship_status` only by stewardship agreement transitions. Neither gates the other's transitions, so the combinations `STAGE_2` with `SUSPENDED`, `PRE_COMMERCIAL` with `NOT_REQUIRED`, and `STAGE_1` with `APPROVED` are all legal simultaneous states. The single coupling is terminal: once `pipeline_stage` is `DISCARDED`, both dimensions are frozen and `stewardship_status` keeps whatever value it held at that moment.

Any cascade step that changes a record mutates that record in the sense of §2.4 — it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` — including cascades a client did not directly request. When a discarded line withdraws one of its trial entries, the entry's `version` advances even though the client wrote to the decision endpoint; when a suspended agreement puts a lot on hold, the lot and the line both advance.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing a Breeding Programme and Its Market Objectives

Everything in this system hangs off a **breeding programme**. A `PipelineDirector` registers one by supplying a `name` between 1 and 120 characters, a `crop_code` drawn from `MAIZE`, `SOYBEAN`, `WHEAT` and `CANOLA`, a `market_zone` label naming the commercial zone the programme serves (`US_MIDWEST`, for instance), a `total_plot_budget` of at least 1 field plot, and a `total_budget_usd` greater than zero carrying two decimal places. The programme opens with `status` `ACTIVE`; the only other value that field ever takes is `CLOSED`. At registration `committed_plot_count`, `committed_budget_usd`, `active_line_count` and `pre_commercial_units_bags` are all zero, and `plot_budget_remaining` equals `total_plot_budget` because nothing has been committed yet.

The two budgets are the programme's hard limits, and the server maintains the commitment counters that measure them. `committed_plot_count` is a conserved quantity summed over exactly three sources and no others: the `plot_count` of every trial entry whose series belongs to this programme and whose `status` is `ENTERED`, `PLANTED` or `HARVESTED`; the `plot_count` of every nursery planting whose line belongs to this programme and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PLANTED`; and the `granted_plots` of every advancement decision whose line belongs to this programme, whose `decision` is `ADVANCE`, whose `is_superseded` is false, and whose line's `pipeline_stage` is not `DISCARDED`. `plot_budget_remaining` is `total_plot_budget` less `committed_plot_count`. On the cash side, `committed_budget_usd` is the sum of `total_trial_cost_usd` over the programme's trial series whose `status` is `PLANTED`, `DATA_COMPLETE`, `ANALYZED` or `CLOSED`. Two census counters complete the picture: `active_line_count` counts the programme's breeding lines whose `pipeline_stage` is neither `DISCARDED` nor `COMMERCIAL`, and `pre_commercial_units_bags` sums `planned_units_bags` over the seed increase lots of the programme's lines whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `COMPLETED`, excluding lots that are `ON_HOLD` or `CANCELLED`.

Closing a programme is a director's prerogative and flips `status` to `CLOSED`. From that moment every write that would create or mutate a resource owned by the programme is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED`; reads continue to work, because a closed programme still has to be auditable.

A read-only **pipeline report** serves the portfolio review meeting, deriving its whole payload live from the programme's lines, entries, plantings, decisions and lots rather than from stored snapshots. Its body reports `program_id`, the programme's `status`, a `lines_by_stage` object keyed by pipeline stage with the count of lines currently at each stage, `active_line_count`, `discarded_line_count` for the lines whose `pipeline_stage` is `DISCARDED`, `committed_plot_count`, `plot_budget_remaining`, `total_plot_budget`, `committed_budget_usd`, `budget_remaining_usd` (the programme's `total_budget_usd` less its `committed_budget_usd`), `pre_commercial_units_bags`, and two stewardship census figures: `stewardship_approved_line_count` for lines whose `stewardship_status` is `APPROVED` and `stewardship_suspended_line_count` for lines whose `stewardship_status` is `SUSPENDED`.

Within a programme, a `ProgramLead` or a `PipelineDirector` defines one or more **breeding objectives**, each one the specification of what a variety must achieve to satisfy a particular market. An objective is created under its programme, so its `program_id` is taken from the path rather than from the body, and it carries a `market_segment` label (`MX_NORTH`, say), a `target_yield_kg_per_ha` greater than zero expressing the yield the segment demands in kilograms per hectare, and a `min_generation_number` of at least 2 fixing the earliest selfing generation that may enter a yield trial. Disease expectations are given as `required_disease_resistances`, an array of trait codes each drawn from `GLS`, `NCLB`, `SCLB`, `RUST`, `SDS` and `PHYTOPHTHORA` — the array may be empty — together with a `min_resistance_score` between 1 and 9 that fixes the score at which a line counts as resistant. Quality expectations are given as `quality_trait_specs`, a map from spec code to numeric threshold whose recognised keys are `TEST_WEIGHT_MIN`, `PROTEIN_MIN`, `OIL_MIN` and `MOISTURE_MAX`, and which may also be empty.

The objective also fixes how the three evidence streams are weighed against each other. `yield_weight`, `disease_weight` and `quality_weight` each lie between 0 and 1 inclusive with two decimal places, and the three must sum to exactly 1.00 — a body whose weights sum to 0.99 or 1.01 is rejected at the request-shape step with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Finally `min_advancement_index` is the bar a line's cumulative advancement index must reach before the committee may advance it against this objective. Objectives can be listed under their programme with `limit` and `offset`, and read individually; the programme's trial series can likewise be listed with optional `stage` and `status` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Germplasm Origination: Crossing Blocks, Progeny Lines and Introductions

New genetics enter the pipeline in one of two ways, and both produce a `BreedingLine`. The planned route runs through a **crossing block**: a `BreedingScientist` or a `ProgramLead` plans one by naming the owning `program_id`, the `nursery_block_id` of the physical nursery block where the crosses will be made, a `season_code` labelling the nursery season (`2026W`, for example), and a `planned_cross_count` of at least one. The block opens in `status` `PLANNED` with `realized_cross_count` at 0 and `progeny_line_count` at 0.

Pollination is the scientist's report of what actually happened in the field. It moves the block from `PLANNED` to `POLLINATED` and stores a `realized_cross_count` that must be at least 1 and no greater than `planned_cross_count`; the block must still be `PLANNED` and its programme must be `ACTIVE`. Only a `POLLINATED` block accepts progeny: registering a progeny line against a block that is still `PLANNED`, or one that has already reached `CLOSED`, is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_POLLINATED`.

Registering a progeny line records the breeder's germplasm decisions: an `objective_id` naming the objective the line is bred against, a `designation` the breeder will use to refer to it, a `generation_number` of at least 2 (2 meaning F2, 3 meaning F3, and so on), and the `female_parent_line_id` and `male_parent_line_id` of the cross. Three optional fields may seed the line's data: `seed_inventory_g` for the unallocated seed on hand in grams, `disease_resistance_scores` as a map from disease trait code to an integer score between 1 and 9, and `quality_trait_values` as a map from quality trait code — `TEST_WEIGHT`, `PROTEIN`, `OIL` or `MOISTURE` — to the measured numeric value. Both maps default to empty and `seed_inventory_g` defaults to 0. The new line takes its `program_id` from the block's programme and its `origin_block_id` from the block in the path, opens at `pipeline_stage` `NURSERY` and `stewardship_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, has `cumulative_seed_produced_g` set to whatever `seed_inventory_g` it was created with, and increments the programme's `active_line_count` by one. Immediately after the progeny is created the block re-derives `progeny_line_count`, which counts the lines whose `origin_block_id` is this block and whose `pipeline_stage` is not `DISCARDED`; when that count equals `realized_cross_count`, the block auto-transitions `POLLINATED → CLOSED` without any client request, and subsequent progeny registrations against it fail with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_POLLINATED`.

The second route is direct registration by a `BreedingScientist` or a `ProgramLead`, used for introductions, check varieties and externally sourced germplasm. Here the body supplies `program_id` and `objective_id` explicitly along with `designation` and `generation_number`, and may optionally supply `female_parent_line_id`, `male_parent_line_id`, `seed_inventory_g`, `disease_resistance_scores` and `quality_trait_values`. A directly registered line has `origin_block_id` null, opens at `pipeline_stage` `NURSERY` with `stewardship_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, sets `cumulative_seed_produced_g` to its opening `seed_inventory_g`, and increments the programme's `active_line_count`. Parent references are self-references into the same table, so the germplasm graph is a pedigree in which any line may be cited as the female or male parent of later lines; a line with neither parent set is a founder.

Both routes derive `advancement_readiness` immediately and identically from the submitted `generation_number` measured against the objective's `min_generation_number`: the field reads `READY` when the line's `generation_number` is greater than or equal to that minimum, and `NOT_READY` otherwise. A line registered at or above the objective's minimum generation is therefore `READY` from the moment it exists, and one registered below it starts `NOT_READY` and must earn readiness in the nursery.

Three referential rules protect the pedigree, and each has its own code. A `designation` must be unique within its `program_id`, and reusing one draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The referenced objective must belong to the same programme as the line, or the request fails with `ERR_OBJECTIVE_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. And both parent lines must belong to that same programme, or the request fails with `ERR_PARENT_LINE_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. Crossing blocks can be listed with optional `program_id`, `nursery_block_id`, `status` and `season_code` filters plus `limit` and `offset`; breeding lines can be listed with optional `program_id`, `objective_id`, `origin_block_id`, `female_parent_line_id`, `male_parent_line_id`, `pipeline_stage` and `stewardship_status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, which is how the pedigree is walked in either direction. A line's full state — both dimensions, its seed ledger and its derived indices — is available on an individual read, and `pipeline_stage` always holds one of `NURSERY`, `STAGE_1`, `STAGE_2`, `STAGE_3`, `PRE_COMMERCIAL`, `COMMERCIAL` or `DISCARDED` while `stewardship_status` always holds one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`.

### 3.3. Nursery Rotation, Occupancy and Generation Advancement

Generations are advanced in nurseries, and nursery ground is a scarce, physically exclusive resource. A `BreedingScientist` registers a **nursery block** with a `block_code` that must be unique across all blocks, a `nursery_type` of either `WINTER` or `SUMMER`, a `location_code` naming the physical location (`CL_QUILLOTA`, for instance), a `plot_count` of at least 1 describing how many plots the block physically holds, and an optional `fallow_buffer_days` of at least 0 that defaults to 14 when omitted. The distinction between the two nursery types is seasonal rather than cosmetic: a `WINTER` block is a counter-season off-shore nursery and admits sowing only in October, November, December, January or February, while a `SUMMER` block is a home-season nursery and admits sowing only in April, May, June, July or August. A sowing month outside the block's admissible set is refused with `ERR_NURSERY_SEASON_MISMATCH`.

A block's `occupancy_status` is derived, never set. It reads `AVAILABLE` unless the logical clock's calendar date falls inside the occupancy interval of a planting on this block whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `PLANTED`, `HARVESTED` or `FAILED`. Inside such an interval it reads `OCCUPIED` when the clock date is on or after that planting's `sow_date` and strictly before its `expected_harvest_date`, and `FALLOW` when the clock date is on or after `expected_harvest_date` and strictly before `occupancy_end_date`. When the clock date falls inside the intervals of several plantings at once, the planting with the smallest `sow_date` decides the reported value, and a tie on `sow_date` is broken by the earlier `created_at`.

Scheduling a **nursery planting** commits both ground and budget. The scientist supplies the `nursery_block_id`, the `line_id` of the line being advanced, a `sow_date`, an `expected_harvest_date` that must be strictly later than the `sow_date`, and a `plot_count` of at least 1. The planting is created in `status` `SCHEDULED`; the server computes `occupancy_end_date` as the `expected_harvest_date` advanced by the block's `fallow_buffer_days` days, which is the exclusive end of the block's occupancy interval and therefore the first date on which the block may be re-sown. Creation adds the planting's `plot_count` to the programme's `committed_plot_count` and recomputes `plot_budget_remaining`. Four rules guard the request beyond the shape checks: the requested `plot_count` may not exceed the block's physical `plot_count`, or `ERR_BLOCK_PLOT_COUNT_EXCEEDED` is returned; the `sow_date` may not be earlier than the logical clock's current date, or `ERR_SOW_DATE_IN_PAST` is returned; the committed plots may not exceed the programme's `plot_budget_remaining`, or `ERR_PROGRAM_PLOT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` is returned; and the block must actually be free for the whole interval.

Freedom of the block is judged on half-open intervals. The new planting occupies the block from its `sow_date` up to but not including its `occupancy_end_date`, and that interval must not intersect the equivalent interval of any other planting on the same block whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `PLANTED`, `HARVESTED` or `FAILED`. Because the intervals are half-open, touching endpoints do not conflict: a new planting whose `sow_date` equals an existing planting's `occupancy_end_date` is admitted, since the fallow period has just expired. Any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`. Note that a failed planting still holds its block through the fallow buffer — a crop failure does not free the ground early.

Marking a scheduled planting as sown moves it `SCHEDULED → PLANTED` and re-derives the block's `occupancy_status`, which becomes `OCCUPIED`; the planting must be `SCHEDULED` and the line it advances must not be `DISCARDED`. Harvest is where the planting forks, and the two branches differ materially. The scientist submits an `actual_harvest_date`, which must be on or after the `sow_date`, and a `harvested_seed_g` figure in whole grams. Both branches bank the seed: `seed_inventory_g` and `cumulative_seed_produced_g` on the line each rise by `harvested_seed_g`, and the block's `occupancy_status` becomes `FALLOW`, and the programme's `committed_plot_count` falls by the planting's `plot_count` with `plot_budget_remaining` recomputed, because the planting has left the set of scheduled-or-planted commitments.

The branches diverge on generation. When `harvested_seed_g` is at least 250 grams — the minimum viable seed quantity for the next selfing cycle, so exactly 250 grams succeeds — the planting becomes `HARVESTED`, the line's `generation_number` rises by 1, its `nursery_cycles_completed` rises by 1, and the planting's `generation_advanced_to` records the new `generation_number`. The line's `version` advances and its `advancement_readiness` is then re-derived against the objective's `min_generation_number`, which is how a line flips `NOT_READY → READY`. When `harvested_seed_g` is below 250 grams the planting becomes `FAILED` instead: the seed is still banked exactly as above, but `generation_advanced_to` stays null, `generation_number` is not incremented and `nursery_cycles_completed` is not incremented, so `advancement_readiness` cannot flip on this branch. `nursery_cycles_completed` is by definition the number of this line's plantings that reached `HARVESTED`, which is why a failure leaves it untouched. Plantings can be listed with optional `nursery_block_id`, `line_id` and `status` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.4. Trait Integration Through Marker-Assisted Backcrossing

Converting an elite line to carry a disease-resistance trait is a separate, parallel activity from yield advancement. A `BreedingScientist` opens a **backcross project** by naming the `recurrent_line_id` of the elite parent that will receive the trait, the `donor_line_id` of the line supplying it — which must be a different line from the recurrent parent, and must belong to the recurrent line's programme — a `target_trait_code` drawn from `GLS`, `NCLB`, `SCLB`, `RUST`, `SDS` and `PHYTOPHTHORA`, and a `marker_assisted` boolean stating whether molecular markers are screened at each cycle. The project opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `bc_generation` at 1 and `target_marker_present` false, and the server immediately computes the project's `recurrent_genome_pct`.

Genome recovery follows the classical backcrossing expectation, plus a bonus that only a marker-assisted programme with a confirmed marker earns. The theoretical part is 100 multiplied by the quantity one minus one half raised to the power of the current `bc_generation` plus one. A bonus of 2.5 percentage points is added when, and only when, both the project's `marker_assisted` flag is true and its `target_marker_present` is true; otherwise no bonus is added. That sum is rounded half up to three decimal places and then capped so that `recurrent_genome_pct` never exceeds 99.900. The capped, rounded value is the one every downstream comparison uses.

A **marker cycle** reports the outcome of one more backcross generation: the scientist supplies `target_marker_present` for the cycle just completed, the project's `bc_generation` rises by 1, the reported flag is stored, and `recurrent_genome_pct` is recomputed from the new generation count. The project must be `ACTIVE`, or the cycle is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the recurrent line must not be `DISCARDED`. If the recomputed `recurrent_genome_pct` is below 99.0, or if `target_marker_present` is false, the project stays where it is — the transition is a self-loop `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`, and there is no cap on how many cycles a project may run.

Conversion happens when the recomputed `recurrent_genome_pct` reaches 99.0 or more **and** `target_marker_present` is true. Then the project moves `ACTIVE → CONVERTED`, and the conversion reaches across into the germplasm: the recurrent line's `disease_resistance_scores` map gains an entry for the project's `target_trait_code` with the score 8, overwriting any lower score already recorded there, and the line's `version` advances. Because the trait map changed, the recurrent line's `advancement_index_provisional` is recomputed immediately as described in §3.8. A converted project runs no further cycles. Projects can be listed with optional `recurrent_line_id`, `donor_line_id`, `status` and `target_trait_code` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and a project's third and final resting state, `TERMINATED`, is reached only through the discard cascade of §3.8, which writes no trait score to the recurrent line.

### 3.5. Trial Sites, Capacity Posture and Entry Admission

Yield testing happens across a network of locations, and each location is registered by a `TrialAgronomist` as a **trial site**. A site carries a `site_code` unique across all sites, an `environment_class` drawn from `HIGH_YIELD_IRRIGATED`, `RAINFED_TEMPERATE`, `DROUGHT_STRESS` and `DISEASE_NURSERY`, a `total_plot_capacity` of at least 1 describing how many plots the site can host on any single day, a `plot_cost_usd` greater than zero with two decimals giving the cost of one plot at that location, and a season window defined by `season_start_date` and a `season_end_date` that must be strictly later than the start. A new site opens with `peak_committed_plot_count` at 0, `available_plot_count` equal to `total_plot_capacity`, and `capacity_posture` `OPEN`.

Site occupancy is measured as a daily peak rather than a season total, because plots are reused between windows. The server considers every trial entry at the site whose `status` is `ENTERED`, `PLANTED` or `HARVESTED`, and sweeps every date from the site's `season_start_date` through its `season_end_date` inclusive. For each date it sums the `plot_count` of every such entry whose `planting_window_start` is on or before that date and whose `planting_window_end` is on or after it — both endpoints occupy the site, so two entries that share a single boundary date do compete for plots. `peak_committed_plot_count` is the largest of those daily sums, or 0 when the site has no active entries at all, and `available_plot_count` is `total_plot_capacity` less that peak. It never goes negative, because admission refuses any entry that would push the peak beyond capacity.

The site's **capacity posture** is derived from utilisation, defined as `peak_committed_plot_count` divided by `total_plot_capacity` and compared as an exact decimal ratio rather than as a floating-point approximation. `capacity_posture` reads `FROZEN` when utilisation is 1.00 or greater, `RESTRICTED` when utilisation is 0.85 or greater but below 1.00, and `OPEN` otherwise; a site sitting exactly at 0.85 is therefore `RESTRICTED` and a site sitting exactly at 1.00 is `FROZEN`. Posture, peak and availability are all re-derived together whenever the entry population at the site changes: on entry creation, on entry withdrawal, on entry invalidation, and on the withdrawal cascade that a `DISCARD` decision triggers. Because withdrawn and invalidated entries release their plots immediately, a busy site can relax back down the ladder `FROZEN → RESTRICTED → OPEN` as entries drop out.

A **trial series** is the network itself — one staged experiment replicated across locations. A `ProgramLead` or a `TrialAgronomist` opens one by naming the owning `program_id`, the `stage` it serves (`STAGE_1`, `STAGE_2` or `STAGE_3`), the `season_year` of harvest, the `plot_design` used at every location (`RCBD`, `AUGMENTED` or `ALPHA_LATTICE`), a `replication_count` of at least 2 giving the replications per entry per location, and a `min_locations` of at least 1 fixing how many distinct sites the network needs before it may be planted — a value of 1 is legal and describes a single-location screening nursery series. The series opens in `status` `PLANNING` with `entry_count` and `location_count` at 0 and all three cost fields — `plot_cost_subtotal_usd`, `management_fee_usd` and `total_trial_cost_usd` — null.

Adding a **trial entry** places one line at one site: it is the junction that joins a series, a breeding line and a location. The agronomist supplies the `line_id`, the `trial_site_id`, a `plot_count` of at least 1, and a `planting_window_start` and `planting_window_end` bounding the dates on which the entry occupies plots, the end date inclusive. The entry is created in `status` `ENTERED` with `valid_rep_count` at 0 and both `plot_mean_yield_kg` and `yield_kg_per_ha` null. Creation then updates three neighbours: the series' `entry_count` — the count of its entries whose `status` is `ENTERED`, `PLANTED` or `HARVESTED` — and its `location_count`, the number of distinct `trial_site_id` values over those same entries; the site's peak, availability and posture; and the programme's `committed_plot_count`, which rises by the entry's `plot_count` with `plot_budget_remaining` recomputed. Entries may only be added while the series is `PLANNING`, and a series that has reached `CLOSED` accepts no mutation whatever.

Entry admission is the most heavily guarded operation in the system, and the order in which its checks run is fixed so that the reported reason is stable. The posture gates come first: a site whose `capacity_posture` is `FROZEN` refuses the entry outright with `ERR_SITE_CAPACITY_FROZEN`, and a site whose posture is `RESTRICTED` admits an entry only from a series whose `stage` is `STAGE_2` or `STAGE_3`, refusing an early-stage series with `ERR_SITE_RESTRICTED_STAGE` — scarce ground at a nearly full location is reserved for advanced material. Next the planting window must sit inside the site's season: `planting_window_start` on or after `season_start_date`, `planting_window_end` on or before `season_end_date`, and `planting_window_end` on or after `planting_window_start`, or `ERR_ENTRY_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_SEASON` is returned. Then the same line may not already be entered at the same site in the same series — any existing entry with that same combination of `trial_series_id`, `line_id` and `trial_site_id` in `status` `ENTERED`, `PLANTED` or `HARVESTED` makes the request a duplicate and yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRIAL_ENTRY`. Then `plot_count` must be an exact multiple of the series' `replication_count`, since every replication needs the same plot allotment, or `ERR_PLOT_COUNT_NOT_DIVISIBLE` is returned. Only then does the admission test proper run: the server performs the daily sweep as it would stand **after** inserting the new entry, and if the resulting peak exceeds the site's `total_plot_capacity` the entry is refused with `ERR_SITE_PLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The last gate is fiscal: the entry's `plot_count` must fit inside the programme's `plot_budget_remaining`, or `ERR_PROGRAM_PLOT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` is returned.

Entries can be listed with optional `trial_series_id`, `line_id`, `trial_site_id` and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and each entry is individually readable together with its replication census and yield rollup.

### 3.6. Committing a Series to the Ground: Costing, Planting and Data Closure

Planting a series is the irreversible act that turns a plan into a field commitment, and it prices the trial at the same moment. The agronomist may plant only a series still in `PLANNING` whose programme is `ACTIVE`, whose `location_count` is at least its `min_locations`, and whose `entry_count` is at least 2; a network that is short of locations or has fewer than two entries is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOCATIONS`.

Costing runs in a pinned order over the entries that will actually go into the ground — that is, the entries of this series whose `status` is `ENTERED` at the moment of the call. Entries already withdrawn are excluded and never re-enter the cost. First, `plot_cost_subtotal_usd` is the sum, over those entries, of each entry's `plot_count` multiplied by the `plot_cost_usd` of the site hosting it, rounded half up to two decimals. Second, a management tier is selected from that subtotal **and from nothing else**: the rate is 0.12 when `plot_cost_subtotal_usd` is strictly below 10000.00, 0.09 when it is strictly below 50000.00, and 0.07 otherwise. The comparisons are strict, so a subtotal of exactly 10000.00 selects the 0.09 rate and a subtotal of exactly 50000.00 selects 0.07. The dependency here looks circular — the total is the subtotal plus the fee, while the fee depends on a tier — and it is resolved by fixing the tier input to the pre-fee subtotal, never to the total. Third, `management_fee_usd` is `plot_cost_subtotal_usd` multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half up to two decimals. Fourth, `total_trial_cost_usd` is `plot_cost_subtotal_usd` plus `management_fee_usd`, rounded half up to two decimals.

Before anything is written, that total is tested against the programme's uncommitted cash: `total_trial_cost_usd` must be no greater than `total_budget_usd` less `committed_budget_usd`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_USD_EXCEEDED`. When the test passes, the series moves `PLANNING → PLANTED`, the three cost fields are stored, the programme's `committed_budget_usd` rises by `total_trial_cost_usd`, and every entry of the series still in `status` `ENTERED` is cascaded `ENTERED → PLANTED` in `created_at` ascending order. Observations may be recorded against an entry once it is planted.

Phenotyping is per plot and per replication. A `TrialAgronomist` records a **phenotype observation** on an entry by supplying a `trait_code` drawn from `YIELD_KG_PLOT`, `TEST_WEIGHT`, `PROTEIN_PCT`, `OIL_PCT`, `MOISTURE_PCT` and `DISEASE_SEVERITY`, a `rep_number` identifying the replication, a `value` of at least 0 carrying up to three decimals, an optional `qc_status` of either `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` that defaults to `ACCEPTED`, and an optional `is_outlier` boolean that defaults to false and lets an analyst exclude a plot from every rollup. The server stamps `recorded_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Observations may be recorded while the entry is `ENTERED` or `PLANTED`, `rep_number` must lie between 1 and the series' `replication_count` inclusive, and the combination of `trial_entry_id`, `trait_code` and `rep_number` must be unique — a repeat, or a `rep_number` outside the legal range, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBSERVATION`. Every recorded observation re-derives the entry's `valid_rep_count`, which counts only this entry's observations whose `trait_code` is `YIELD_KG_PLOT`, whose `qc_status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `is_outlier` is false. Observations on one entry can be listed with optional `trait_code` and `qc_status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`.

Finalising an entry closes data collection on it and computes its yield. The entry must be `PLANTED`, and its `valid_rep_count` must be at least 3 — fewer valid replications cannot support a mean, so the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REPLICATION`. The rollup uses exactly the inclusion set that defines `valid_rep_count`: `ACCEPTED`, non-outlier `YIELD_KG_PLOT` observations of this entry, with rejected rows and outlier-flagged rows excluded from both the count and the sum. `plot_mean_yield_kg` is the sum of those observation `value` figures divided by `valid_rep_count`, rounded half up to three decimals. Then — and the order matters — that already-rounded three-decimal mean is scaled to a per-hectare figure and rounded again: `yield_kg_per_ha` is `plot_mean_yield_kg` multiplied by 800, rounded half up to two decimals. The factor of 800 is the number of 12.5-square-metre plots in one hectare of 10,000 square metres. The entry then moves `PLANTED → HARVESTED`.

Two other endings exist for an entry, and they differ in exactly one respect. **Withdrawal** applies to an entry that is `ENTERED` or `PLANTED`, whose series is not `CLOSED`: the agronomist supplies a `withdrawal_reason`, the entry moves to `WITHDRAWN`, the programme's `committed_plot_count` falls by the entry's `plot_count` with `plot_budget_remaining` recomputed, the site's `peak_committed_plot_count`, `available_plot_count` and `capacity_posture` are re-derived over the remaining active entries, and the series' `entry_count` falls by one with `location_count` re-derived — the plots were never used, so the location no longer counts. **Invalidation** applies to an entry that is `PLANTED` and whose plot data failed quality control: the agronomist again supplies a `withdrawal_reason`, the entry moves `PLANTED → INVALIDATED`, both `plot_mean_yield_kg` and `yield_kg_per_ha` are set back to null, the programme's `committed_plot_count` falls by the entry's `plot_count` exactly as on a withdrawal, and the site's peak, availability and posture are re-derived. What does **not** change on invalidation is the series census: `entry_count` and `location_count` are left alone, because the entry was actually sown and the location was genuinely used. An invalidated entry is excluded from every analysis inclusion set.

All three endings share one consequence. After a finalisation, a withdrawal or an invalidation, the server re-evaluates the parent series: when the series is `PLANTED`, no entry of the series is left in `status` `PLANTED`, and at least one entry of the series has reached `HARVESTED`, the series auto-transitions `PLANTED → DATA_COMPLETE` and its `version` advances. This is a derived, compound transition — no client requests it — and it is the gate that makes the series analysable. A series where every entry was withdrawn, leaving nothing `HARVESTED`, stays `PLANTED`.

### 3.7. Adjusted Means, the Environment Index and Stability Analysis

Once a series is `DATA_COMPLETE`, a `DataAnalyst` runs a **series analysis**. The request carries just two fields: a `variant` naming the inclusion rule to apply, either `UNBALANCED_INCLUSIVE` or `BALANCED_CORE`, and `expected_series_version` carrying the analyst's last observed `version` of the series. A series may be analysed while it is `DATA_COMPLETE` or already `ANALYZED` — re-running an analysis is normal practice — but a mismatch on `expected_series_version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` before anything is computed, because the inclusion set may have shifted under the analyst's feet.

The candidate pool is always the same: the entries of this series whose `status` is `HARVESTED`. Entries still `ENTERED` or `PLANTED`, and entries that ended `WITHDRAWN` or `INVALIDATED`, contribute nothing. The `variant` then narrows the pool. Under `UNBALANCED_INCLUSIVE` the included set is the whole candidate pool: every harvested entry at every location, including locations where some lines of the series were never tested — a view under which a line tested mainly at a stress site is dragged down relative to its peers. Under `BALANCED_CORE` the server first identifies the core locations: a site qualifies as core when **every** breeding line that has at least one harvested entry anywhere in this series also has a harvested entry at that site. The included set is then the candidate pool restricted to those core locations. If the core set turns out to be empty, or if under either variant there is no harvested entry at all, the analysis is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_BALANCED_CORE`.

Three quantities are then computed over the included set. For each breeding line with at least one included entry, `adjusted_mean_kg_per_ha` is a replication-weighted mean: the sum, over that line's included entries, of each entry's `yield_kg_per_ha` multiplied by its `valid_rep_count`, divided by the sum of those same `valid_rep_count` values, rounded half up to two decimals. For each included location, `index_kg_per_ha` is an **unweighted** site mean: the sum of `yield_kg_per_ha` over the included entries at that site divided by the number of those entries, rounded half up to two decimals. This index is the environment's productivity signal, and it is the x-axis of the stability regression.

Stability follows the Finlay–Wilkinson approach, fitted per line across that line's own included locations, where each point pairs the site's `index_kg_per_ha` as the independent value with the entry's `yield_kg_per_ha` as the dependent value. Both inputs are the already-rounded two-decimal stored figures, never re-derived ones. Let n be the number of included entries the line has. When n is below 2 no line can be fitted, so `stability_regression_b` and `deviation_mse` are both null and `stability_class` is `UNCLASSIFIED`. Otherwise the regression coefficient is the sum over the line's points of the product of (the site index minus the mean site index) and (the entry yield minus the mean entry yield), divided by the sum over those points of the square of (the site index minus the mean site index); `stability_regression_b` is that coefficient rounded half up to four decimals. When n is 3 or more, `deviation_mse` is the sum over the points of the squared residual — the entry yield minus the value the fitted line predicts at that site, that prediction being the mean entry yield plus the unrounded coefficient multiplied by (the site index minus the mean site index) — divided by n minus 2, rounded half up to two decimals; when n is exactly 2 there are no degrees of freedom left and `deviation_mse` is null. Classification reads the rounded four-decimal `stability_regression_b`: `STABLE` when it lies between 0.80 and 1.20 inclusive, `RESPONSIVE` when it is greater than 1.20, and `BUFFERED` in every other case, meaning a coefficient below 0.80.

The analysis record publishes those results in two arrays. `entry_means` holds one object per included line, each carrying `line_id`, `adjusted_mean_kg_per_ha`, `included_location_count` for the number of distinct locations at which that line has an included entry, `stability_regression_b`, `deviation_mse` and `stability_class`; the array is ordered by `adjusted_mean_kg_per_ha` descending, with ties broken by the line's `created_at` ascending. `environment_index` holds one object per included location, each carrying `trial_site_id`, that site's `environment_class` and its `index_kg_per_ha`; the array is ordered by `index_kg_per_ha` descending, with ties broken by the site's `created_at` ascending. At the top level the record also reports `included_location_count` for the number of distinct locations in the variant's inclusion set, `included_line_count` for the number of distinct lines with at least one included entry, an `is_superseded` flag that starts false, and `run_by_user_id` stamped with the analyst's `X-User-ID`.

Publishing an analysis has three further consequences. Every earlier analysis of this series with the **same** `variant` has its `is_superseded` set to true, so at most one analysis of each variant is ever current — analyses of the other variant are untouched. The series moves to `ANALYZED` (from `DATA_COMPLETE`, or remaining there if it was already `ANALYZED`) and its `version` increments by one, which is why a second analyst working from a stale read is turned away. And for every line that appears in `entry_means`, the line's `advancement_index_provisional` is recomputed, processed in `entry_means` order. Analyses of a series can be listed with optional `variant` and `is_superseded` filters plus `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.8. Stage Gates: The Advancement Index, Committee Decisions and Series Closure

The **advancement index** is the single number the stage-gate committee argues over, and it exists in two variants that deliberately disagree. Both are built from the same three components, each scored out of roughly one hundred and each measured against the line's own `BreedingObjective`.

The **yield component** starts from the line's adjusted mean in the cited series. The source figure is the line's `adjusted_mean_kg_per_ha` taken from the non-superseded `BALANCED_CORE` analysis of that series if one exists; failing that, from the non-superseded `UNBALANCED_INCLUSIVE` analysis; and if neither exists the yield component is MISSING. The stored two-decimal value is used as it stands. The component is 100 multiplied by that source figure divided by the objective's `target_yield_kg_per_ha`, rounded half up to two decimals, and then capped at 120 — a line cannot bank more than a twenty-percent overshoot of its market's yield target.

The **disease component** is MISSING when the line's `disease_resistance_scores` map is empty. Otherwise, if the objective's `required_disease_resistances` array is empty the component is exactly 100.00, since nothing was demanded. If the array is non-empty, the server counts the required trait codes that are both present in the line's `disease_resistance_scores` map and recorded there with a score at or above the objective's `min_resistance_score`; the component is 100 multiplied by that matched count divided by the number of required trait codes, rounded half up to two decimals.

The **quality component** is MISSING when the line's `quality_trait_values` map is empty. Otherwise the server counts the misses across the objective's `quality_trait_specs`. A spec whose code ends in `_MIN` — `TEST_WEIGHT_MIN`, `PROTEIN_MIN`, `OIL_MIN` — passes when the line's measured value for the corresponding trait (`TEST_WEIGHT`, `PROTEIN`, `OIL`) is at or above the spec's threshold. `MOISTURE_MAX` passes when the line's measured `MOISTURE` value is at or below its threshold. A spec whose corresponding trait is absent from `quality_trait_values` counts as a miss. Each miss costs 15 points off a perfect hundred, and the component is the larger of 0 and 100 less 15 times the miss count — so four or more misses floor the component at zero rather than driving it negative.

The weighted base is then the objective's `yield_weight` multiplied by the yield component, plus its `disease_weight` multiplied by the disease component, plus its `quality_weight` multiplied by the quality component, rounded half up to two decimals. What happens next is what separates the two variants. The **PROVISIONAL** variant is optimistic: every MISSING component is imputed at 60, no stage-confidence factor is applied, and the result is the weighted base itself. It is written to `BreedingLine.advancement_index_provisional`, stays null until the line has at least one of the three components available, and is recomputed whenever a series analysis includes the line, whenever the line's `disease_resistance_scores` change — as on a backcross conversion — and whenever its `quality_trait_values` change. The **CUMULATIVE** variant is pessimistic and is the one the gate actually enforces: every MISSING component is forced to 0.00, and the weighted base is multiplied by a stage-confidence factor that depends on the line's current `pipeline_stage` — 0.90 at `NURSERY`, 0.95 at `STAGE_1`, and 1.00 at `STAGE_2`, `STAGE_3` and `PRE_COMMERCIAL` — with the product rounded half up to two decimals. Because the base is rounded to two decimals **before** that multiplication, and the product rounded again, an early-stage line with missing data scores strictly lower on the cumulative view than on the provisional one. Only the line's own trait maps and the single winning analysis row of the cited series feed either variant; entries of other series never contribute.

An **advancement decision** is recorded by a `PipelineDirector` and is the only thing that moves `pipeline_stage`. The body names the `line_id`, the `trial_series_id` of the analysed series the decision rests on, the `decision` itself — `ADVANCE`, `HOLD` or `DISCARD` — a `granted_plots` count of at least 0 allocating plots to the line for its next stage, the `expected_line_version` demanded by §2.9, and an optional `rationale` committee note. The server computes the CUMULATIVE index at decision time and freezes it as `decision_index_value`, records `from_stage` as the line's `pipeline_stage` immediately before the decision and `to_stage` as its stage immediately after, stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and sets `is_superseded` false. `granted_plots` must be 0 unless the `decision` is `ADVANCE`. In every branch, any earlier decision for the same combination of `line_id` and `trial_series_id` has `is_superseded` set to true and its `granted_plots` released from the programme's `committed_plot_count`, the line's `version` increments by one and its `updated_at` is refreshed, and the cited series must be `ANALYZED` — a decision resting on unanalysed data is refused with `ERR_SERIES_INVALID_STATE`. The line must also have at least one entry in `status` `HARVESTED` in the cited series, or `ERR_NO_TRIAL_DATA` is returned. Decisions can be listed with optional `line_id`, `trial_series_id`, `decision` and `is_superseded` filters plus `limit` and `offset`.

An `ADVANCE` decision walks the line one rung up a fixed ladder — `NURSERY → STAGE_1`, `STAGE_1 → STAGE_2`, `STAGE_2 → STAGE_3`, `STAGE_3 → PRE_COMMERCIAL`, `PRE_COMMERCIAL → COMMERCIAL` — and the cited series must be the one that generated the evidence for that rung: `STAGE_1` for the first two rungs, `STAGE_2` for the third, and `STAGE_3` for the fourth and the fifth. A decision citing a series at the wrong stage for the line's position cannot carry the advance and is refused with `ERR_SERIES_INVALID_STATE`. Every rung additionally requires that the frozen CUMULATIVE index reach the objective's `min_advancement_index`, failing which the decision is refused with `ERR_INDEX_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, and that `granted_plots` fit inside the programme's `plot_budget_remaining`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_PLOT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The first rung out of `NURSERY` also requires `advancement_readiness` to read `READY`, so a line that has not yet reached the objective's `min_generation_number` is turned away with `ERR_LINE_NOT_READY`. On success the stage moves, `granted_plots` is added to the programme's `committed_plot_count` with `plot_budget_remaining` recomputed, and the line's `advancement_readiness` is reset to `NOT_READY` for the next stage.

The last rung is the commercial launch gate and carries two extra conditions: the line's `stewardship_status` must be `APPROVED`, and its `planned_launch_units_bags` must be at least 1, so no variety becomes `COMMERCIAL` without both a live stewardship package and a real launch volume behind it. A line whose `stewardship_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` or `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_NOT_APPROVED`, and one whose stewardship has been withdrawn — `stewardship_status` `SUSPENDED` — is refused with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_SUSPENDED`. When the line does reach `COMMERCIAL`, the programme's `active_line_count` falls by one, because a commercial variety has left the active pipeline.

A `HOLD` decision is the branch that keeps a line in the pipeline without allocating anything to it. The line's `pipeline_stage` is unchanged, so `from_stage` and `to_stage` both record the current stage; `granted_plots` must be 0; the cited series must be `ANALYZED`; the line must not already be `DISCARDED`; and the supersession, plot release and version bump described above all still apply. A hold is therefore also the way to withdraw a previously granted plot allocation for a line at a given series.

A `DISCARD` decision terminates the line, and its cascade is the largest single side-effect set in the system. `granted_plots` must be 0, the cited series must be `ANALYZED`, and the line must not already be `DISCARDED`. The cascade runs in a pinned order. First the line itself: `pipeline_stage` becomes `DISCARDED`, its `version` increments, and `stewardship_status` is frozen at whatever value it held. Then the trial entries: every entry of this line whose `status` is `ENTERED` or `PLANTED` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` set to `LINE_DISCARDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each entry's `version` incrementing; each such withdrawal re-derives that entry's site — `peak_committed_plot_count`, `available_plot_count` and `capacity_posture`, which may relax `FROZEN → RESTRICTED → OPEN` — and drops the withdrawn `plot_count` from the programme's `committed_plot_count` with `plot_budget_remaining` recomputed, while the programme's `active_line_count` falls by one for the discarded line. Only when the entry cascade has run to completion do the seed lots follow: every seed increase lot of this line whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `ON_HOLD` becomes `CANCELLED` with `hold_reason` set to `LINE_DISCARDED`, again in `created_at` ascending order, and each cancellation credits that lot's `allocated_seed_g` back to the line's `seed_inventory_g` and re-derives `reserved_seed_g` and `planned_launch_units_bags`, after which the programme's `pre_commercial_units_bags` is recomputed. Only when the seed lots have all been cancelled do the backcross projects follow: every backcross project whose `recurrent_line_id` is this line and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `TERMINATED`, with no trait score written to the recurrent line. Finally, any non-superseded `ADVANCE` decision on this line has `is_superseded` set to true and its `granted_plots` released from the programme's `committed_plot_count`.

Closing a series is the director's sign-off that the stage gate has been fully worked. A series may be closed only while it is `ANALYZED`, and only when every breeding line holding a `HARVESTED` entry in that series has a non-superseded advancement decision for it; a series with an undecided line, or one not yet `ANALYZED`, is refused with `ERR_SERIES_INVALID_STATE`. Closure moves the series to `CLOSED` and releases nothing — the plots stayed in the ground — but from then on the series accepts no further entries, no further analyses and no other mutation.

### 3.9. Regulatory Stewardship, Pre-Commercial Seed Increase and Launch Volume

A traited variety cannot be sold on agronomic merit alone; it needs a regulatory dossier and a grower stewardship package, and either can be withdrawn after approval. A `RegulatoryOfficer` drafts a **stewardship agreement** against a breeding line by supplying `approved_markets` as a non-empty array of market codes the traited line may be sold into, a `regulatory_dossier_ref` giving the dossier reference number, a `grower_agreement_ref` naming the grower stewardship agreement template, and an effective window of `effective_from` and `effective_to`. The window is checked against the logical clock: `effective_from` must be on or after the clock's current date and `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_DATES_INVALID`. The line must not be `DISCARDED`, and it must not already hold an agreement whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` — a second live package on the same line is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. Drafting creates the agreement in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `requested_changes` null, and both `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null, and it moves the line's second state dimension from `stewardship_status` `NOT_REQUIRED` to `PENDING`, incrementing the line's `version`.

Review is a cycle, not a straight line. Submitting an agreement moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, leaving `revision_round` at 0 on that first pass; the line must not be `DISCARDED`. A `RegulatoryOfficer` or a `PipelineDirector` then reviews it with an `outcome` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`. A `REQUEST_CHANGES` outcome requires `requested_changes` to be a non-empty string, moves the agreement `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, stores that text, and leaves the line's `stewardship_status` at `PENDING`. Re-submitting from there is the cycle edge `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`: it advances `revision_round` by one, clears `requested_changes` back to null, and refreshes `submitted_by_user_id`. The counter therefore records how many revision rounds the package has actually been through, advancing as the agreement re-enters review rather than at the moment changes are requested, and there is no cap on how many cycles an agreement may run. An `APPROVE` outcome moves the agreement `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the approver's `X-User-ID`, and flips the line's `stewardship_status` to `APPROVED` with the line's `version` incrementing.

Suspension is the compliance emergency brake, available to a `RegulatoryOfficer` or a `PipelineDirector` on an agreement that is `APPROVED`. The request carries a `suspension_reason`. Segregation of duties applies: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the agreement's recorded `approved_by_user_id`, so the officer who approved a package may not be the one who suspends it, and a self-suspension is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A successful suspension cascades in a pinned order. The agreement moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` and stores `suspension_reason`. Because of that, the line's `stewardship_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and its `version` increments. Because of that, every seed increase lot of the line whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` moves to `ON_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `STEWARDSHIP_SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each lot keeps its `allocated_seed_g` debited from the line so the seed ledger does not drift. And because those lots have left the planned set, the line's `planned_launch_units_bags` and the programme's `pre_commercial_units_bags` are both recomputed. `SUSPENDED` is where an agreement's lifecycle ends. Agreements can be listed under their line with an optional `status` filter plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually together with their `revision_round` and reviewer identities.

Pre-commercial supply is the `SupplyPlanner`'s domain. Reserving a **seed increase lot** names the `line_id` being increased, a `target_market` code the launch volume is destined for, a `market_allocations` array of at least one object each carrying a `market_code` and a `demand_kg` figure greater than zero, and a `launch_year` for the commercial launch. Two regulatory gates precede any arithmetic: the line's `stewardship_status` must be `APPROVED`, so both `NOT_REQUIRED` and `PENDING` fail with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_NOT_APPROVED`, and a line whose stewardship has been withdrawn fails with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_SUSPENDED`. The `target_market` must additionally appear in the `approved_markets` array of the line's `APPROVED` agreement, or the reservation is refused with `ERR_MARKET_NOT_IN_AGREEMENT`.

Volume planning then runs in a pinned rounding order that matters commercially. For each element of `market_allocations` the server inflates that market's `demand_kg` by a seed loss allowance of eight percent — multiplying it by one plus 0.08 — divides the result by the 25-kilogram commercial bag size, and rounds **that quotient up** to the next whole bag. `planned_units_bags` is the sum of those per-allocation bag counts. Rounding up per allocation and only then summing is the required order: summing the demand first and rounding once produces a smaller, wrong bag count. Next the parent seed requirement: `allocated_seed_g` is `planned_units_bags` multiplied by 25 kilograms, converted to grams by multiplying by 1000, divided by the field multiplication factor of 40 — one gram of parent seed yields forty grams in the multiplication field — and rounded up once at the end to a whole gram. The fiscal gate is last: `allocated_seed_g` may not exceed the line's `seed_inventory_g`, or the reservation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEED_INVENTORY`. On success the lot is created in `status` `RESERVED` with `hold_reason` null, `allocated_seed_g` is debited from the line's `seed_inventory_g`, and the line's `reserved_seed_g` and `planned_launch_units_bags` together with the programme's `pre_commercial_units_bags` are all re-derived.

The line's seed ledger is a conserved system, and its three derived fields are defined precisely so that conservation is checkable at any moment. `reserved_seed_g` is the sum of `allocated_seed_g` over the line's lots whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `ON_HOLD`, excluding `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` lots. `consumed_seed_g` is the sum of `allocated_seed_g` over the line's lots whose `status` is `COMPLETED` — parent seed physically sown out in a finished increase. `cumulative_seed_produced_g` is the conserved total: the `seed_inventory_g` the line was created with plus every gram ever credited to it, and it never decreases. Together these close the invariant that the line's `seed_inventory_g` plus its `reserved_seed_g` plus its `consumed_seed_g` equals its `cumulative_seed_produced_g` at every observable moment. `planned_launch_units_bags`, meanwhile, sums `planned_units_bags` over the line's lots whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `COMPLETED`, excluding lots that are `ON_HOLD` or `CANCELLED` — which is exactly why a suspension can drag a line back below the launch-volume condition of the commercial gate.

A lot's own lifecycle has four moves. **Starting production** takes a `RESERVED` lot to `IN_PRODUCTION`, and it re-checks the stewardship gate at that moment — the line's `stewardship_status` must still be `APPROVED`, with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_NOT_APPROVED` and `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_SUSPENDED` guarding it exactly as at reservation — while `allocated_seed_g` stays debited. **Completing** takes an `IN_PRODUCTION` lot to `COMPLETED`: the allocated parent seed moves out of `reserved_seed_g` and into `consumed_seed_g`, staying out of `seed_inventory_g` permanently, and the multiplied harvest is credited — both the line's `seed_inventory_g` and its `cumulative_seed_produced_g` rise by `allocated_seed_g` multiplied by the field multiplication factor of 40 — so the seed-mass invariant still closes on the new, larger total. **Going on hold** is never requested directly; `RESERVED` and `IN_PRODUCTION` lots reach `ON_HOLD` only through the stewardship suspension cascade described above, and the seed stays debited while they sit there. **Cancelling** applies to a lot that is `RESERVED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `ON_HOLD`, accepts an optional `hold_reason`, and is exactly compensating: the lot becomes `CANCELLED`, the line's `seed_inventory_g` is credited back by the lot's `allocated_seed_g` while `reserved_seed_g` and `planned_launch_units_bags` are re-derived, the line's `version` increments, and the programme's `pre_commercial_units_bags` is recomputed — leaving no drift in the ledger. Lots can be listed under their line with optional `status` and `launch_year` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually with their bag plan.
