# Used Furniture Consignment Gallery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A used-furniture consignment gallery earns its margin on two things it can never fully control: what a sofa is actually worth once a mover puts hands on it, and how long that sofa is willing to sit on a showroom floor before somebody buys it. Consignors send photographs and a declared replacement value; appraisers grade from those photographs and quote a ticket price; a truck and a crew then climb whatever stairs the building actually has, and the piece arrives in a condition that may or may not match its photographs. From that moment the gallery is running a clock: a declining price schedule steps the ticket down week by week, floor space is finite and must be reclaimed, unsold merchandise has to be donated, returned or discounted before it becomes dead inventory, and every one of those events moves money in or out of a consignor's payout ledger.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that business end to end. It covers photographic acceptance screening with condition grading, consignment agreements pinned to a declining price schedule, pickup routing with stair and elevator surcharge quoting followed by settlement against what the crew actually did, floor placement with tagging and periodic shrinkage reconciliation, proceeds splitting by merchandise category and sale price band, disposition election deadlines on unsold pieces, buyer delivery scheduling with damage claim negotiation, and a consignor payout ledger whose dormant balances eventually escheat. The service is the single system of record: there is no second ledger, no spreadsheet of surcharges, and no manual override of a price the schedule has already produced.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every monetary figure is an integer number of cents and every derived figure is reproducible** — given the same stored inputs, two independent runs of a calculation must land on the same cent, which is why the rounding points in this document are pinned rather than left to implementation taste. Second, **the system refuses rather than guesses**: a piece whose grade is unknown is not placed, a payout computed against a balance the caller has not observed is not paid, and an operator who counted an inventory variance is not permitted to approve the money that variance releases.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. Both are mandatory on every endpoint, including read endpoints. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the presented role does not appear in the authorized-role list for the target endpoint given in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `Consignor`, `Buyer`, `IntakeAppraiser`, `FloorOperator`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `GalleryManager` and `SystemOperator`.

- A **`Consignor`** is the owner of the consigned pieces. When `X-User-Role` is `Consignor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consignor` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Consignor` compare that resource's `consignor_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Consignor` may elect the disposition of a piece only when that piece's `consignor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and their reads of consignor records, ledger entries, agreements, items, item-level ledger entries, pickup jobs, sales and damage claims are restricted to rows whose `consignor_id` resolves to `X-User-ID`.
- A **`Buyer`** is the purchaser of a piece. When `X-User-Role` is `Buyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Buyer` row; a `Buyer` may file a damage claim only against a delivery order whose underlying sale carries a `buyer_id` equal to `X-User-ID`, and their reads of buyer records, sales, delivery orders and damage claims are restricted to their own rows. Resubmitting or withdrawing a damage claim is a `Buyer`-only capability.
- An **`IntakeAppraiser`** screens submitted pieces from photographs, assigns the provisional condition grade, and opens consignor records for walk-ins. When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeAppraiser`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `screened_by_user_id` on the `ConsignmentItem` it screens, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- A **`FloorOperator`** runs the physical floor: receiving inspection, placement and tagging, missing/recovered spot checks, releases out of the building, shrinkage counts and register sales. When `X-User-Role` is `FloorOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `received_by_user_id` on an inspected item, as `sold_by_user_id` on a sale it rings, and as `counted_by_user_id` on a shrinkage count it submits, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- A **`LogisticsCoordinator`** owns the moving fleet and the white-glove crews: quoting and settling pickups, scheduling deliveries and recording their outcomes. When `X-User-Role` is `LogisticsCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `scheduled_by_user_id` on both the `PickupJob` and the `DeliveryOrder` it creates, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- A **`GalleryManager`** holds commercial and financial authority: agreements, price schedules, write-off approvals, sale reversals, claim adjudication, buyer blocks, zone closures and consignor payouts. When `X-User-Role` is `GalleryManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `approved_by_user_id` on an activated agreement and on an approved shrinkage audit, and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on an adjudicated claim, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- A **`SystemOperator`** drives the logical clock and the two nightly epochs and holds no commercial authority whatsoever: it cannot create, price, place or move any piece. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may log it but does not compare it to any entity's `id` and does not persist it on any row.

Ownership scoping and role membership are separate gates. A `Consignor` presenting a syntactically valid header pair but asking for another consignor's ledger is refused; a role that is simply not listed for an endpoint never reaches the ownership check at all.


### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest accepted value is 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0, and its smallest accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint description in §4 states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The two ledger read endpoints are the documented exception: consignor ledger entries and item ledger entries are both returned in `effective_at` ascending order. A schedule's markdown steps are returned in `sequence` ascending order, and a zone's audit history is returned oldest first, newest last.

List responses carry the pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit all four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a request body is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock described in §2.5 at the instant the row was inserted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation that actually changes a stored field. Two resource kinds are deliberately immutable once written and therefore carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`: a `PriceScheduleStep`, which is appended and never edited, and a `PayoutLedgerEntry`, which is minted and never amended. For those two kinds only `id` and `created_at` are present.

A mutation that changes nothing does not bump `version`. This matters most inside the nightly epochs, where thousands of rows are visited but only some change: during the markdown epoch an item's `version` is incremented only when one of its fields actually changed, and during the escheatment epoch a consignor's `version` is incremented only when its dormancy posture or its balance actually changed.

Derived fields are recomputed and re-persisted whenever any of their inputs change, so a read always sees a consistent row rather than a stale aggregate. `ConsignmentAgreement.item_count`, `PriceSchedule.step_count`, `PickupJob.item_count`, `PickupJob.total_volume_cuft`, `ConsignmentItem.lot_component_count`, `ConsignmentItem.lot_ready`, `FloorZone.occupied_sqft`, `FloorZone.utilization_state`, `Consignor.unclaimed_balance_cents` and `Consignor.lifetime_credits_cents` all behave this way.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Two Nightly Epochs

Every deadline, dormancy count and floor-tenure count in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any client has set it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time. A `SystemOperator` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; time may only move forward, so a request whose `current_utc` is earlier than the current clock reading is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). A request that sets the clock to exactly its present value is accepted. Setting the clock mutates no entity and implies no epoch run.

Two batch epochs exist, both `SystemOperator`-only, both idempotent with respect to a clock that has not moved, and both driven entirely by the clock rather than by any client-supplied cursor. The **markdown epoch** steps placed merchandise down its schedule, flags pieces that have gone unsold, converts lapsed disposition elections into donations, and expires agreements whose term has run out. The **escheatment epoch** advances every consignor's dormancy posture and escheats balances that have gone stale. Neither epoch takes a request body. Each processes its population in `created_at` ascending order — items for the markdown epoch, consignors for the escheatment epoch — so replaying the same state twice produces the same ledger.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and the Pinned Order of Arithmetic

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no floating-point currency fields, no fractional cents, and no currency codes. Percentages and factors are the only non-integer values that appear, and they exist purely as multiplicands.

Wherever a monetary result is produced by multiplication, the product is rounded **half up** to a whole cent: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero. Where a calculation multiplies twice, the rounding points are pinned and are **not** collapsible: each factor is applied to an already-rounded integer number of cents, and the intermediate result is rounded before the next factor is applied. Collapsing two pinned multiplications into a single expression produces a different — and wrong — answer for some inputs, so the sequence described in the relevant workflow must be followed literally.

Two further conventions hold throughout. First, **floors and caps are applied after rounding, never before**: a stepped price is rounded and then compared with its floor, and a surcharge is rounded and then compared with its cap. Second, wherever a total is split into two shares, one share is computed and rounded and the **other is the exact remainder** — so the two shares always re-add to the total to the cent. This applies to the consignor and gallery shares of a sale price and to the gallery and consignor buckets of an adjudicated damage claim.

Day counts are whole days obtained by dividing an elapsed number of seconds by 86,400 and discarding any remainder, so a partial day never advances a count. Square footage is an exact integer sum and is never rounded; the utilization ratio derived from it is compared without rounding.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Payouts and Write-Offs

Two operations compute money from a figure the caller must have read beforehand, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control. In each case the guard field is a mandatory, non-optional element of the request body: omitting it is a schema violation answered with `ERR_VALIDATION`, not a bypass.

A **payout disbursement** on a consignor — the `POST /consignors/{id}/payouts` operation — requires `expected_consignor_version` in its body, carrying the `version` of that `Consignor` as the client last observed it. The disbursement pays out the whole stored balance, so it must be computed against the balance the client actually saw. If `Consignor.version` has advanced since that read — because another writer booked a sale credit, a pickup surcharge debit or a damage chargeback that legitimately supersedes the earlier balance — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONSIGNOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no ledger entry is minted.

A **single-item shrinkage write-off** — the `POST /consignment-items/{id}/write-off` operation — requires `expected_item_version` in its body, carrying the `version` of that `ConsignmentItem` as the client last observed it. The shrinkage credit is a function of the item's `current_price_cents` at approval time, so if `ConsignmentItem.version` has advanced since the manager's read — because a markdown run stepped the price down, or because a recovery cleared the missing flag — the write-off is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) instead of crediting a superseded price.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field. Batch write-off approval on a shrinkage audit, sale reversal, delivery completion and claim adjudication all operate without one.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Three pairs of steps in this business must be performed by two different people, and the service enforces that by comparing `X-User-ID` against a user id recorded earlier on the resource. The rule is checked **before any resource state is inspected**, which means a stale or already-finished resource still answers with the segregation error rather than a state error.

The three barred pairings are: the `FloorOperator` recorded as `ShrinkageAudit.counted_by_user_id` may not be the `GalleryManager` `X-User-ID` that approves that audit's write-offs; the `LogisticsCoordinator` recorded as `DeliveryOrder.scheduled_by_user_id` may not be the `X-User-ID` that adjudicates a damage claim filed against that delivery order; and the `IntakeAppraiser` recorded as `ConsignmentItem.screened_by_user_id` may not be the `X-User-ID` that performs that item's receiving inspection. Any of the three, when violated, is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks run in the strict order below; the first violated check answers the request and no later check is consulted. Implementers should read this ladder as a rejection sequence rather than a set of independent guards, because several of these errors are only reachable when an earlier one has passed.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization.** The presented role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Segregation of duties**, as described in §2.8, on exactly three endpoints: shrinkage-audit write-off approval, damage-claim adjudication, and item receiving inspection — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
4. **Path existence.** Every `{id}` segment addressed in the URL resolves to a stored row, on every path-addressed endpoint. When a path carries more than one identifier they are validated strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable segment is the one reported. The reported code is selected by which segment failed: `ERR_CONSIGNOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PICKUP_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404.
5. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, validated in the order the keys appear in that endpoint's input list. This applies to agreement creation, item intake, price-schedule-step append, pickup-job creation, pickup-job item attachment, item placement, sale creation, delivery-order creation, damage-claim filing and shrinkage-audit submission. The same 404 family is used, selected by which body key failed: an unresolvable `consignor_id` answers `ERR_CONSIGNOR_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `price_schedule_id` or `schedule_id` answers `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `agreement_id` answers `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `item_id`, `parent_lot_item_id` or member of `counted_item_ids` answers `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `truck_id` answers `ERR_TRUCK_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `crew_id` answers `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `placement_zone_id` or `floor_zone_id` answers `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `buyer_id` answers `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `sale_id` answers `ERR_SALE_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `delivery_order_id` answers `ERR_DELIVERY_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. Because the path is checked first, a path id and a body foreign key are never both reported.
6. **Schema conformance.** On every write endpoint, the body carries all required fields with correct types, every enum value is in range, and every numeric bound holds — `declared_value_cents` at least 1000, `crew_size` between 2 and 6 inclusive, `footprint_sqft` at least 1, `volume_cuft` at least 1, `floor_number` at least 1, each photo sub-score between 0 and 10 inclusive, `markdown_pct` between 0.00 and 0.90 inclusive, `min_price_cents` at least 0, `start_day_offset` at least 0, `sequence` at least 1, `term_days` at least 1, `unsold_trigger_day` at least 1, `election_window_days` at least 1, `capacity_sqft` at least 1, `capacity_volume_cuft` at least 1, `max_concurrent_jobs` at least 1, `claim_amount_cents` at least 1 and `sale_price_cents` at least 1. A violation is `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).
7. `expected_consignor_version` equals the stored `Consignor.version`, on payout disbursement — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CONSIGNOR_VERSION` (412).
8. `expected_item_version` equals the stored `ConsignmentItem.version`, on single-item write-off — otherwise `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (412).
9. The item's `lifecycle_state` is a legal from-state for the requested transition, on every item action endpoint — screening, receiving, placement, disposition election, inventory flagging, write-off and release — plus pickup-job item attachment and sale creation. Otherwise `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID` (409).
10. The item's `inventory_state` is a legal from-state for the requested transition, on item placement, item write-off, item inventory flagging, sale creation and delivery-order creation. In particular a piece in `SHRINKAGE_MISSING` or `WRITTEN_OFF_INV` cannot be sold, placed or delivered. Otherwise `ERR_ITEM_CUSTODY_INVALID` (409).
11. The consignor's `escheat_state` is not `ESCHEATED`, on payout disbursement, agreement creation and item intake — otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNOR_ESCHEATED` (409).
12. The consignor's `status` is `ACTIVE`, on agreement creation, item intake and item placement — otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNOR_SUSPENDED` (409).
13. The buyer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, on sale creation — otherwise `ERR_BUYER_BLOCKED` (409).
14. The governing agreement's `status` is `ACTIVE`, on item intake and item placement — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
15. The sale's `status` is `COMPLETED`, meaning neither `SETTLED` nor `REVERSED`, on sale reversal and delivery-order creation — otherwise `ERR_SALE_ALREADY_REVERSED` (409).
16. The claim's `status` is the from-state the requested action needs — `SUBMITTED` for an information request and for adjudication, `INFO_REQUESTED` for a resubmission — on all three of those endpoints. Otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (409).
17. `resubmission_count` is below 2, on damage-claim information request and on damage-claim resubmission — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_RESUBMIT_LIMIT` (409).
18. `election_round` is below 2, on a disposition election whose `election` is `DISCOUNT` — otherwise `ERR_ELECTION_ROUND_EXHAUSTED` (409).
19. The logical clock is strictly before `election_deadline_at`, on disposition election — otherwise `ERR_ELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (409).
20. `claim_window_ends_at` is not null and the logical clock is strictly before it, on damage-claim filing — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
21. The requested truck window clears the turnaround buffer against every other non-terminal job on the same truck, on pickup-job creation — otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
22. Adding the order would not exceed the crew's concurrent-job ceiling, on delivery-order creation — otherwise `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
23. `window_start` is strictly before `window_end`, on pickup-job creation and delivery-order creation — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID` (422).
24. A new markdown step is appended in sequence at a strictly increasing day offset, on price-schedule-step creation — otherwise `ERR_SCHEDULE_STEP_OVERLAP` (422).
25. The requested clock value is at or after the current logical clock, on the clock-setting endpoint — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422).
26. `photo_count` is at least 3 and all three appraiser sub-scores are supplied, on photographic screening — otherwise `ERR_PHOTOS_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
27. The item's grade is not `REJECTED`, on pickup-job item attachment and on item placement — otherwise `ERR_GRADE_REJECTED` (422).
28. A lot parent has `lot_ready` true, on item placement — otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_READY` (409).
29. The supplied `tag_code` is not already held by another item, on item placement — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAG_CODE` (409).
30. The elected disposition is available for this item, on disposition election — otherwise `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
31. `parent_lot_item_id` names a legal lot parent, on item intake — otherwise `ERR_LOT_NESTING_FORBIDDEN` (422).
32. The target zone's `status` is `OPEN`, on item placement and on shrinkage-audit submission — otherwise `ERR_ZONE_CLOSED` (409).
33. The job's `total_volume_cuft` including the piece being attached does not exceed the truck's `capacity_volume_cuft`, on pickup-job item attachment — otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
34. The zone's `occupied_sqft` plus the piece's `footprint_sqft` does not exceed the zone's `capacity_sqft`, on item placement — otherwise `ERR_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
35. `counted_item_ids` contains no duplicates and every id in it is currently placed in the zone being counted, on shrinkage-audit submission — otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_COUNT_INVALID` (422).
36. `sale_price_cents` clears the register floor, on sale creation — otherwise `ERR_SALE_BELOW_FLOOR` (422).
37. `claim_amount_cents` does not exceed the parent sale's `sale_price_cents`, on damage-claim filing and on damage-claim adjudication — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_SALE` (422).
38. `unclaimed_balance_cents` is strictly greater than 0, on payout disbursement — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE` (409).
39. `unclaimed_balance_cents` is at least the payout minimum, on payout disbursement — otherwise `ERR_PAYOUT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (409). The zero-or-negative test at position 38 deliberately runs first: were the order reversed, every negative balance would answer `ERR_PAYOUT_BELOW_MINIMUM` and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE` would be unreachable.

Four further checks are endpoint-local, meaning no rule between positions 19 and 39 above applies to the endpoint that carries them, so their absolute position in the ladder is never observable — they simply run after positions 1 through 6 on their own endpoints. They are: the agreement is in `DRAFT` when activation is requested, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409); the pickup job is in `SCHEDULED` when item attachment, completion or cancellation is requested, otherwise `ERR_PICKUP_JOB_STATE_INVALID` (409); the shrinkage audit is in `COUNTED` when write-off approval is requested, otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` (409) — and because this one sits after position 3, the operator who counted the audit still receives `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when the audit has already been reconciled; and the referenced price schedule is `ACTIVE` on agreement creation and agreement activation while a retirement request targets a schedule that is not already `RETIRED`, otherwise `ERR_SCHEDULE_RETIRED` (409). Finally, email uniqueness is checked after schema validation at position 6 on consignor registration and on buyer registration, answering `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL` (409); those two endpoints trigger none of positions 7 through 39, so their relative position is likewise unobservable.

### 2.10. Floor Zone Occupancy and the Utilization Band

A `FloorZone` publishes two derived figures that the rest of the system reads rather than recomputes ad hoc. `occupied_sqft` is the exact integer sum of `footprint_sqft` over every `ConsignmentItem` whose `placement_zone_id` names that zone **and** whose `inventory_state` is either `IN_CUSTODY` or `SHRINKAGE_MISSING`. Pieces in `WRITTEN_OFF_INV` and pieces in `RELEASED` are excluded, which is precisely why writing off a missing piece frees square feet and why recovering a written-off piece consumes them again — a piece that is merely mislaid keeps occupying its footage, because the gallery cannot rent that space to anything else until the loss is booked.

`utilization_state` is the band that ratio falls into. Dividing `occupied_sqft` by `capacity_sqft` without rounding, the zone is `FULL` when the ratio is at least 1.00, `TIGHT` when the ratio is at least 0.75 but below 1.00, and `NORMAL` otherwise. Both figures are recomputed on every event that changes a zone's membership: a placement, a release, a write-off, a recovery and a clearance relocation. A relocation touches two zones and both are recomputed — the zone the piece left and the zone it arrived in.

### 2.11. The Consignor Payout Ledger

Every movement of consignor money is a `PayoutLedgerEntry`, and the ledger is append-only: entries are never edited, never deleted and never merged. Each entry names its owner through `consignor_id`, names the piece it is attributable to through `item_id` (null for account-level entries such as a pickup surcharge or a disbursement), records `amount_cents` as a **signed** integer where credits to the consignor are positive and debits negative, records the resulting `balance_after_cents` as the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` immediately after that entry was applied, carries a human-readable `memo` recording the entry's provenance, and stamps `effective_at` with the logical-clock instant at which it was applied.

`entry_type` discriminates the nine mechanisms that can mint an entry: `SALE_CREDIT`, `SALE_REVERSAL_DEBIT`, `SHRINKAGE_CREDIT`, `SHRINKAGE_RECOVERY_DEBIT`, `PICKUP_SURCHARGE_DEBIT`, `DAMAGE_ALLOCATION_DEBIT`, `DISPOSAL_FEE_DEBIT`, `PAYOUT_DISBURSEMENT_DEBIT` and `ESCHEATMENT_DEBIT`. Three reporting columns exist only on a `PAYOUT_DISBURSEMENT_DEBIT` and are null on every other `entry_type`: `withholding_cents`, `processing_fee_cents` and `net_payout_cents`.

Two consignor-level aggregates are derived from that ledger and re-persisted whenever it grows. `unclaimed_balance_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every entry belonging to the consignor, and it may legitimately go negative when surcharge and chargeback debits exceed credits. `lifetime_credits_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` restricted to entries whose amount is positive, so it never decreases.

Balance movement drives two consignor-level postures. Whenever a recomputed `unclaimed_balance_cents` falls below negative 50,000 cents, the consignor's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED`; whenever a recomputed balance is greater than or equal to zero while the consignor is `SUSPENDED`, the `status` returns to `ACTIVE`. Separately, `last_activity_at` records the logical-clock instant of the most recent consignor-facing ledger event and is refreshed by every minted entry with exactly one deliberate exception: an `ESCHEATMENT_DEBIT` does **not** refresh it, so escheating a balance cannot restart the dormancy curve that produced the escheatment.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Records: Consignors, Buyers, Schedules and Capacity

Registering a **consignor** opens the account that everything else hangs from. Either a `GalleryManager` or an `IntakeAppraiser` may do it, since appraisers routinely open records for walk-ins at the loading dock. The submitted payload carries a `display_name`, an `email`, an `address_line`, the `floor_number` of the pickup residence where 1 means ground floor, and a `has_elevator` flag stating whether the building has a working elevator. The `email` must not already belong to another consignor, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL`. A `payout_method` may be supplied and must be one of `ACH`, `CHECK` or `STORE_CREDIT`; when it is omitted the account defaults to `ACH`. A freshly registered consignor opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `escheat_state` `NONE`, `unclaimed_balance_cents` of 0, `lifetime_credits_cents` of 0, and `last_activity_at` stamped with the current logical clock so the dormancy curve begins from registration rather than from the first sale.

Address facts drift, and only a `GalleryManager` may correct them. A correction may revise any of `address_line`, `floor_number`, `has_elevator` or `payout_method`, and it increments the consignor's `version` by 1. It does **not** retroactively requote anything: pickup jobs already created keep the `declared_stair_flights` and `declared_elevator` snapshots they took at creation, because the crew was dispatched against the facts as they stood then. Listing consignors is a manager and system-operator capability and accepts optional `status` and `escheat_state` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

A **buyer** record is opened by a `GalleryManager` or a `FloorOperator` at the register and carries the same shape of address facts — `display_name`, `email`, `address_line`, `floor_number` and `has_elevator` — because those facts price the white-glove delivery rather than the pickup. A buyer's `email` is likewise unique across buyers, with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL` on collision, and a new buyer opens with `status` `ACTIVE`. A manager may later move a buyer to `BLOCKED`, which bumps the buyer's `version` by 1 and bars future purchases while leaving every existing `Sale` and `DeliveryOrder` completely untouched; attempting to block a buyer who is already `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_BUYER_BLOCKED`. Buyer listings accept an optional `status` filter.

The **declining price schedule** is the gallery's merchandising policy expressed as data. A `GalleryManager` creates the shell with a `name`, an `unsold_trigger_day` naming the floor tenure at or beyond which a piece is flagged as unsold, and an `election_window_days` count granting the consignor a window to elect a disposition once flagged. A new schedule opens `ACTIVE` with `step_count` of 0, and its `step_count` is always the number of markdown steps attached to it. Steps are appended one at a time, never edited: each carries the `schedule_id` it belongs to, a 1-based `sequence`, a `start_day_offset`, a `markdown_pct` between 0.00 and 0.90 inclusive naming the fraction to subtract from the ticket price while that step is in force, and a `min_price_cents` of at least 0 that acts as an absolute cents floor under the marked-down price. A step governs the half-open day window beginning at its own `start_day_offset` and ending immediately before the next step's `start_day_offset`. Appends must be strictly ordered: the submitted `sequence` must equal the schedule's current `step_count` plus 1, the `start_day_offset` must be strictly greater than the previous step's `start_day_offset`, and the very first step must sit at offset 0. Any violation of those three conditions is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_STEP_OVERLAP`. A successful append recomputes `step_count` and increments the schedule's `version` by 1. A schedule's steps are readable in `sequence` ascending order by managers, floor operators and the system operator.

Retiring a schedule closes it to new business without disturbing old business: its `status` becomes `RETIRED`, its `version` increments by 1, no new agreement may reference it, and every `ACTIVE` agreement that already references it keeps marking its items down exactly as before. Retiring a schedule that is already `RETIRED`, or referencing a `RETIRED` schedule when drafting or activating an agreement, is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_RETIRED`. Schedule listings accept optional `status`, `limit` and `offset`.

Physical capacity comes in three flavours, all registered before the work that consumes them. A **truck** is registered by a `LogisticsCoordinator` with a `name` call sign and a `capacity_volume_cuft` ceiling on the summed cubic feet of one pickup load; it opens `AVAILABLE` and may later read `MAINTENANCE`. A **white-glove crew** carries a `name` and a `max_concurrent_jobs` ceiling on how many non-terminal delivery windows may overlap at once; it opens `AVAILABLE` and may later read `OFF_DUTY`. A **floor zone** is created by a `FloorOperator` or a `GalleryManager` with a `name`, a `capacity_sqft` of usable square feet, and a `zone_type` that must be one of `SHOWROOM_FRONT`, `SHOWROOM_REAR`, `WAREHOUSE` or `CLEARANCE`; a new zone opens with `occupied_sqft` of 0, `utilization_state` `NORMAL` and `status` `OPEN`. A manager may close a zone, which sets `status` to `CLOSED` and increments the zone's `version` by 1 while leaving `occupied_sqft` untouched and every piece already inside it exactly where it stands — a closed zone refuses new placements and new counts, it does not evacuate. Closing an already-closed zone is refused with `ERR_ZONE_CLOSED`. Zone listings accept optional `zone_type` and `status` filters; truck and crew listings accept an optional `status` filter.

### 3.2. Agreements and the Intake of a Piece

A **consignment agreement** binds one consignor to one declining price schedule for a fixed term, and only a `GalleryManager` may draft or activate one. Drafting requires the `consignor_id` of the consignor being bound, the `price_schedule_id` of the schedule to pin, and a `term_days` length of at least 1 day. A drafted agreement opens in `status` `DRAFT` with an `item_count` of 0, and both `effective_at` and `expires_at` stay null until activation. Drafting is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNOR_ESCHEATED` when the consignor's `escheat_state` is `ESCHEATED`, with `ERR_CONSIGNOR_SUSPENDED` when the consignor's `status` is `SUSPENDED`, and with `ERR_SCHEDULE_RETIRED` when the named schedule is not `ACTIVE`.

Activation moves the agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and starts the clock running. It requires no body. The agreement must be in `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`; the bound consignor's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; and the referenced schedule must still be `ACTIVE` with a `step_count` of at least 1, since an agreement pinned to a schedule with no markdown steps could never price anything. On success, `effective_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, `expires_at` is computed as `effective_at` advanced by `term_days` whole days — that is, by `term_days` multiplied by 86,400 seconds — `approved_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the agreement's `version` increments by 1. Agreement listings accept optional `consignor_id`, `price_schedule_id` and `status` filters, so a manager can pull every agreement pinned to a schedule about to be retired.

An agreement leaves `ACTIVE` in exactly two ways, both automatic. The markdown epoch moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` for any agreement whose `expires_at` is at or before the current clock; from that point no new piece may be taken in under it and no piece under it may be placed on a floor, but pieces already on the floor keep being marked down normally — an expired agreement gates intake and placement, nothing else. The escheatment epoch moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` for every agreement of a consignor that reaches `escheat_state` `ESCHEATED` in that run, closing the agreement out; intake and placement under it are refused thereafter.

**Intake** of a piece is an `IntakeAppraiser`-only capability. The payload names the `agreement_id` the piece is consigned under, a short `title`, a `category` that must be one of `SOFA`, `DINING`, `CASEGOOD`, `RUG`, `ART` or `OUTDOOR`, a `declared_value_cents` of at least 1000 representing the consignor's declared replacement value, a `photo_count` of at least 0 stating how many intake photographs were supplied, a `volume_cuft` of at least 1 for the cubic feet the piece will consume on the truck, and a `footprint_sqft` of at least 1 for the square feet it will consume in a zone while placed. The consignor is **never** taken from the request body: `consignor_id` is copied from the named agreement at intake, so a piece can never be attributed to a consignor who did not sign for it. A new piece opens with `lifecycle_state` `INTAKE_SUBMITTED` and `inventory_state` `PENDING_CUSTODY`, and the owning agreement's `item_count` is recomputed. Intake is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` when the named agreement is `DRAFT`, `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`, with `ERR_CONSIGNOR_SUSPENDED` when the copied consignor is `SUSPENDED`, and with `ERR_CONSIGNOR_ESCHEATED` when that consignor has been escheated.

Pieces that sell as a set are modelled as a **lot**. A component piece is created with `parent_lot_item_id` naming the lot parent it belongs to; standalone pieces and lot parents themselves leave that field null. Lots are exactly one level deep and never cross agreements: the named parent must itself not be a component, meaning its own `parent_lot_item_id` must be null, and it must belong to the same agreement as the component being created. Either violation is refused with `ERR_LOT_NESTING_FORBIDDEN`. Supplying `parent_lot_item_id` recomputes the parent's `lot_component_count`, which is simply the number of pieces pointing at it, and re-evaluates the parent's `lot_ready` flag. `lot_ready` is true when the piece has no components at all — a `lot_component_count` of zero — and otherwise true only when every one of its components has reached `inventory_state` `IN_CUSTODY`; a parent with even one component still in `PENDING_CUSTODY` is not ready. Item listings accept a generous filter set for exactly this reason: optional `consignor_id`, `agreement_id`, `parent_lot_item_id`, `pickup_job_id`, `placement_zone_id`, `lifecycle_state` and `inventory_state`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.3. Photographic Screening, Condition Grading and the Opening Ticket Price

Screening is where the gallery decides whether it wants the piece at all, and it is an `IntakeAppraiser`-only step performed while the furniture is still in the consignor's living room. The appraiser submits a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` together with three condition sub-scores drawn from the photographs, each on a 0-to-10 scale: `photo_surface_score` for surface condition, `photo_structure_score` for structural soundness, and `photo_completeness_score` for completeness of parts. The piece must be in `lifecycle_state` `INTAKE_SUBMITTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. It must also carry at least 3 intake photographs and all three sub-scores must be present; fewer than three photographs, or a missing sub-score, is refused with `ERR_PHOTOS_INSUFFICIENT`.

The three sub-scores collapse into a single `photo_score` by weighted average, with surface condition carrying the most weight. Multiply the surface sub-score by 45 hundredths, the structure sub-score by 30 hundredths and the completeness sub-score by 25 hundredths, add the three products together, and round that sum half up to two decimal places — the rounded figure is `photo_score`, and the weights sum to exactly one so the score stays inside the same 0-to-10 range as its inputs.

The condition grade is read off a ladder applied to the **rounded** `photo_score`, never to the raw weighted sum, and the ladder is walked from the top: a `photo_score` below 3.00 grades `REJECTED`; otherwise a score of 8.50 or higher grades `A`; otherwise a score of 6.50 or higher grades `B`; otherwise a score of 4.50 or higher grades `C`; otherwise the piece grades `D`. Every boundary comparison is inclusive, so a score landing exactly on a boundary takes the **higher** grade — a `photo_score` of exactly 6.50 is a `B`, not a `C`. The result is written to `provisional_condition_grade`.

The opening ticket price follows from the grade and the merchandise category, and it is computed in **two separately rounded steps** that must not be collapsed into one. First, multiply `declared_value_cents` by the grade factor and round the product half up to a whole number of cents: the grade factors are 85 hundredths for `A`, 70 hundredths for `B`, 55 hundredths for `C` and 40 hundredths for `D`. Second, take that already-rounded integer number of cents, multiply it by the category factor, and round that product half up to a whole number of cents. The category factors are 95 hundredths for `SOFA`, 105 hundredths for `DINING`, 100 hundredths for `CASEGOOD`, 110 hundredths for `RUG`, 120 hundredths for `ART` and 85 hundredths for `OUTDOOR` — art carries a premium over its declared value while outdoor furniture is discounted against it. A grade of `REJECTED` yields no price at all: there is no grade factor for it, and the price stays null.

When the derived grade is `REJECTED`, or when the appraiser's `decision` is `REJECT`, the piece moves `INTAKE_SUBMITTED → SCREEN_REJECTED`, which is terminal on the merchandising dimension. The `photo_score` and `provisional_condition_grade` are still recorded for the record, `provisional_list_price_cents` stays null, `screened_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and `inventory_state` remains `PENDING_CUSTODY` forever — a rejected piece is never picked up and never enters the building. When the `decision` is `ACCEPT` and the derived grade is anything other than `REJECTED`, the piece moves `INTAKE_SUBMITTED → SCREEN_ACCEPTED`. The computed price is written to `provisional_list_price_cents` and **also** seeds `list_price_cents`, while the derived grade seeds `condition_grade` as well as `provisional_condition_grade`, so the piece can be quoted and scheduled onto a truck before anyone has physically touched it. `current_price_cents` is written from the same figure at this point. Screening records `screened_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and increments the piece's `version` by 1.

A **lot parent** is priced differently from a standalone piece. Any piece whose `lot_component_count` is zero is priced by the two-step grade-and-category calculation above. A piece that has components is priced as a bundle instead: collect every component whose `lifecycle_state` is not `SCREEN_REJECTED`, sum their `list_price_cents` values — the components' prices are already rounded integers, and addition is order-independent, though components are walked in `created_at` ascending order for deterministic replay — multiply that sum by 90 hundredths, and round the single product half up to a whole number of cents. The bundle therefore always tickets below the sum of its parts, which is the point of selling a dining set as a set. A lot parent's `condition_grade` is not graded independently: it is the lowest grade among its components, ranked with `A` highest, then `B`, then `C`, then `D` lowest, so one badly scuffed chair drags the whole set's grade down.

Both grade columns and both price columns are readable on a single item, by design. `provisional_condition_grade` and `provisional_list_price_cents` are frozen at their screening values forever, while `condition_grade` and `list_price_cents` are the effective figures that later steps overwrite — keeping the divergence between what the photographs promised and what arrived permanently observable on the row.

### 3.4. Pickup Routing, Surcharge Quoting and the Transfer of Custody

Emptying a consignor's residence is a `LogisticsCoordinator` capability from end to end. Reserving a truck window requires the `consignor_id` whose residence is being emptied, the `truck_id` being reserved, a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the reserved window, and a `crew_size` of between 2 and 6 movers inclusive. An optional `item_ids` array may list screened pieces to load onto the job immediately; each listed piece must be in `lifecycle_state` `SCREEN_ACCEPTED` with no `pickup_job_id` yet assigned, and each moves `SCREEN_ACCEPTED → SCHEDULED_PICKUP` with its `pickup_job_id` pointing at the new job. A new job opens in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `scheduled_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`.

The reserved window must be coherent and it must not crowd another job on the same truck. `window_start` must be strictly before `window_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID`. Beyond that, a truck needs a **45-minute turnaround gap** between consecutive jobs to unload and reposition, so the requested window is compared against every other non-terminal job already reserved on the same truck. The request is refused with `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT` exactly when both of the following hold against some other job: the requested start is earlier than that job's end advanced by 45 minutes, **and** that job's start is earlier than the requested end advanced by 45 minutes. A window that begins exactly 45 minutes after another job ends therefore clears, while one that begins 44 minutes after does not.

Access facts are snapshotted onto the job at creation rather than read live, because the crew is dispatched against what the consignor declared. `declared_elevator` is a copy of the consignor's `has_elevator`, and `declared_stair_flights` is the consignor's `floor_number` reduced by 1 but never below zero — a ground-floor residence declares zero flights — except that a consignor who declared an elevator declares zero flights regardless of `floor_number`.

The **surcharge** is built up from four components and then adjusted twice. Start from a base of 12,000 cents for showing up at all. Add a volume fee: divide the job's `total_volume_cuft` by 10 and round **up** to the next whole block, then charge 2,500 cents per block, so a 41-cubic-foot load pays for five blocks. Add an access fee: when an elevator is in play the access fee is a flat 3,000 cents and no stair charge applies at all; otherwise charge 4,500 cents per flight for up to 6 flights — flights beyond the sixth are not charged at that per-flight rate — and when the flight count is strictly greater than 6, add a further 8,000 cents extreme-climb premium on top of the six charged flights. Add a bulky-item fee of 5,500 cents for each attached piece whose `category` is `SOFA` or `DINING` **and** whose `volume_cuft` is at least 60; pieces that meet only one of those two conditions attract nothing. Those four terms sum to a subtotal, which is then multiplied by a crew multiplier and rounded half up to a whole number of cents: a crew of 2 or fewer multiplies by 1.00, a crew of exactly 3 multiplies by 1.25, and a crew of 4 or more multiplies by 1.45. Finally the surcharge is capped at 35 hundredths of the summed `declared_value_cents` of every piece attached to the job, that cap being itself rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and the surcharge charged is the smaller of the two rounded integers — the gallery will not bill a mover's day out at more than a third of what the furniture is worth.

That calculation runs in two variants against the same formula. The **quoted** variant runs at scheduling and again after every item attachment; it reads `declared_stair_flights`, `declared_elevator` and the quoted `crew_size`, writes the result to `quoted_surcharge_cents`, and mints no ledger entry whatsoever — it is a forward-looking estimate only. The **final** variant runs at completion; it reads what actually happened and writes `final_surcharge_cents`, together with `surcharge_variance_cents` computed as `final_surcharge_cents` minus `quoted_surcharge_cents`. `surcharge_variance_cents` is null until completion. It is the final figure that is billed — never the variance, and never the quote.

Attaching one more piece to an already-scheduled job requires only the `item_id` of the piece. The job must still be in `status` `SCHEDULED`, or the attach is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_JOB_STATE_INVALID`. The piece must be in `SCREEN_ACCEPTED` with no `pickup_job_id`, or the attach is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, and a piece whose `condition_grade` is `REJECTED` may never be scheduled for pickup at all — that attempt is refused with `ERR_GRADE_REJECTED`. The truck must also have room: if the job's recomputed `total_volume_cuft` including the piece being attached would exceed the truck's `capacity_volume_cuft`, the attach is refused with `ERR_TRUCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the piece moves `SCREEN_ACCEPTED → SCHEDULED_PICKUP`, the job's `item_count` and `total_volume_cuft` are recomputed, the quoted variant rewrites `quoted_surcharge_cents` against the enlarged load, and the job's `version` increments by 1.

**Completion** settles the job from what the crew actually did. The coordinator supplies `actual_stair_flights` of at least 0, an `actual_elevator_used` flag, and an `actual_crew_size` of between 2 and 6 inclusive. The job must be in `status` `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_PICKUP_JOB_STATE_INVALID`, and must have at least one piece attached. The final variant of the surcharge calculation runs and writes `final_surcharge_cents` and `surcharge_variance_cents` in either branch of what follows.

The completion request then forks on crew adequacy, and the two outcomes have entirely disjoint consequences. When `actual_crew_size` is strictly less than the quoted `crew_size` reduced by 1 — a crew that showed up two or more movers short — the job moves `SCHEDULED → FAILED`: no piece advances on either state dimension, no ledger entry of any kind is minted, and the truck window is released so it stops participating in overlap checks. Otherwise the job moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, and a four-link cascade fires. First, every attached piece advances `SCHEDULED_PICKUP → RECEIVED` on the merchandising dimension and `PENDING_CUSTODY → IN_CUSTODY` on the custody dimension, which is what makes it eligible for receiving inspection; any lot parent above those pieces has its `lot_ready` recomputed, since custody of a component is exactly what that flag tracks. Second, because the job reached `COMPLETED` with a settled surcharge, exactly one `PICKUP_SURCHARGE_DEBIT` ledger entry is minted for the job's consignor, carrying `amount_cents` equal to the negation of `final_surcharge_cents` and an `item_id` of null, since the surcharge is a job-level charge rather than a piece-level one. Third, because that entry exists, the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` is recomputed and `last_activity_at` is stamped with the clock. Fourth, because the recomputed balance may now be deeply negative, if it has fallen below negative 50,000 cents the consignor's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED`. The job's `version` increments by 1.

**Cancellation** unwinds a scheduled job cleanly. The job must be in `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_PICKUP_JOB_STATE_INVALID`. Its `status` becomes `CANCELLED`, every attached piece reverts `SCHEDULED_PICKUP → SCREEN_ACCEPTED` with its `pickup_job_id` cleared to null so it can be re-scheduled onto another truck, the truck window stops blocking overlap checks for future reservations, and the job's `version` increments by 1. `quoted_surcharge_cents` is retained on the cancelled row for audit but is never billed — no `PICKUP_SURCHARGE_DEBIT` is ever minted for a job that did not complete. Pickup-job listings accept optional `consignor_id`, `truck_id` and `status` filters, and a single job read exposes both the quote and, once completed, the settled surcharge and the variance between them.

### 3.5. Receiving Inspection, Grade Divergence and the Dispute Election

Once a piece is physically in the building a `FloorOperator` inspects it and re-grades it, and this is the one step where the photographs are put to the test. The inspection submits the same three sub-scores — `photo_surface_score`, `photo_structure_score` and `photo_completeness_score` — this time drawn from hands-on examination. The piece must be in `lifecycle_state` `RECEIVED` and in `inventory_state` `IN_CUSTODY`. The operator performing the inspection may not be the appraiser who screened the piece: when `X-User-ID` equals the piece's `screened_by_user_id`, the inspection is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` before any state is even examined.

The weighted score and the grade ladder are computed exactly as at screening, but this variant writes different columns. `photo_score` is overwritten, `condition_grade` is overwritten with the newly derived grade, and `list_price_cents` is recomputed by the same two-step grade-and-category calculation using the new grade — while `provisional_condition_grade` and `provisional_list_price_cents` are left **frozen** at their screening values, which is what makes the divergence permanently visible. `current_price_cents` is refreshed from the recomputed ticket price, `received_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the piece's `version` increments by 1.

Where the piece goes next depends on **how far** the grade fell. Grades occupy a rank ladder on which `A` is 4, `B` is 3, `C` is 2, `D` is 1 and `REJECTED` is 0, and the downgrade distance is the rank of `provisional_condition_grade` minus the rank of the newly received grade. A distance of at most one grade — including no downgrade at all, and including an upgrade — moves the piece `RECEIVED → AWAITING_PLACEMENT` and the gallery simply absorbs the difference. A distance of two or more grades moves the piece `RECEIVED → RECEIVING_DISPUTE` instead, because the consignor is entitled to be told that the piece they described is not the piece that arrived. A `B` graded down to `D`, or an `A` graded down to `C`, are two-grade downgrades and land in dispute; a `D` graded down to `REJECTED` is only a one-grade step and lands in `AWAITING_PLACEMENT` with `condition_grade` `REJECTED` and `list_price_cents` null. Receiving does not refuse a rejected piece — it will be refused later, when someone tries to put it on a floor, with `ERR_GRADE_REJECTED`.

A dispute additionally stamps `election_deadline_at` with the current clock advanced by the schedule's `election_window_days` whole days, that is by `election_window_days` multiplied by 86,400 seconds, so the consignor has a bounded window to accept the downgraded piece or recall it.

Whenever a receiving inspection changes the `list_price_cents` of a piece that is a lot **component**, a five-link cascade runs upward, and it runs identically whether the inspection landed in `AWAITING_PLACEMENT` or in `RECEIVING_DISPUTE`. First, this component's `condition_grade` and `list_price_cents` are written. Second, because the component's price changed and its `parent_lot_item_id` is not null, the parent's `list_price_cents` is recomputed by the bundle calculation — re-summing the non-rejected components and re-applying the 90-hundredths bundle factor — and the parent's `condition_grade` is re-derived as the lowest grade among its components. Third, because the parent's ticket price changed and the parent is sitting on a floor in `ON_FLOOR` or `MARKED_DOWN`, the parent's `current_price_cents` is recomputed by the declining-schedule calculation described in §3.7. Fourth, because that recomputed post-markdown figure may now be small, if the parent's `current_price_cents` has fallen below 50,000 cents while the parent occupies a zone whose `zone_type` is not `CLEARANCE`, the clearance relocation selector runs on the parent and updates its `placement_zone_id`. The comparison is made against the post-markdown figure, never against the raw bundle price, which is why the markdown recomputation must run before the threshold test. Fifth, because the parent's `placement_zone_id` changed, both the zone it left and the zone it arrived in have their `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` recomputed.

From `RECEIVING_DISPUTE` the consignor has two exits, and only the consignor who owns the piece — or a `GalleryManager` taking the election over the telephone — may take either. Both require the logical clock to be strictly before `election_deadline_at`; once the clock has reached or passed that instant the election is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. Electing `DISCOUNT` accepts the downgraded piece: the piece moves `RECEIVING_DISPUTE → AWAITING_PLACEMENT`, `disposition_election` is set to `DISCOUNT`, `election_deadline_at` is cleared to null, `list_price_cents` is kept at its downgraded value, and — importantly — `election_round` is left **unchanged**, because this discount is a concession on a grading dispute rather than one of the two unsold-inventory discount rounds described in §3.7. Electing `RETURN` recalls the piece: it moves `RECEIVING_DISPUTE → RETURN_SCHEDULED`, `disposition_election` is set to `RETURN`, and `election_deadline_at` is cleared to null. `DONATE` is not an exit from a receiving dispute.

### 3.6. Placement, Tagging and Clearance Relocation

Putting a piece on a sales floor is the moment its commercial life begins, and it is a `FloorOperator` capability. The request names the `placement_zone_id` of the target zone and the `tag_code` to be printed and attached to the piece. Placement is gated by a long list of conditions, each with its own refusal. The piece must be in `lifecycle_state` `AWAITING_PLACEMENT`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, and in `inventory_state` `IN_CUSTODY`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_CUSTODY_INVALID` — a piece that is `SHRINKAGE_MISSING` or `WRITTEN_OFF_INV` obviously cannot be placed, because nobody knows where it is. The owning agreement must be in `status` `ACTIVE` with the logical clock still strictly before its `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the owning consignor must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNOR_SUSPENDED`. A piece whose `condition_grade` is `REJECTED` may never reach a floor and is refused with `ERR_GRADE_REJECTED`. A lot parent must have `lot_ready` true — every one of its components in `IN_CUSTODY` — otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_READY`, since selling three chairs of a four-chair set as a set is not a thing the gallery does. The target zone must have `status` `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_ZONE_CLOSED`, and it must have room: the zone's `occupied_sqft` plus the piece's `footprint_sqft` must not exceed the zone's `capacity_sqft`, otherwise `ERR_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Finally the `tag_code` must be free — no other piece whose `inventory_state` is anything other than `WRITTEN_OFF_INV` may already hold it — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAG_CODE`. Tag codes of written-off pieces are deliberately recyclable, because those tags have physically left the building.

On success the piece moves `AWAITING_PLACEMENT → ON_FLOOR`, its `placement_zone_id` and `tag_code` are written, and its `version` increments by 1. `listed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock **only on the first placement** — that is, only when `listed_at` was still null — because `listed_at` is the origin of the piece's floor tenure and re-placing a recovered piece must not reset the clock it has already burned. The declining-schedule calculation then runs against the piece's tenure: on a first placement `days_on_floor` is 0, so the step in force is the sequence-1 step sitting at day offset 0, `markdown_sequence` becomes 1, and `current_price_cents` becomes `list_price_cents` reduced by that step's `markdown_pct` and then raised to that step's `min_price_cents` if the reduction landed below it.

Cheap merchandise does not belong in the front showroom, so placement also carries a **clearance test**. If the resulting `current_price_cents` is below 50,000 cents and the chosen zone's `zone_type` is not `CLEARANCE`, the clearance relocation selector runs immediately, and every zone the piece touches has its `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` recomputed afterwards.

The selector is deterministic. Consider every `FloorZone` whose `zone_type` is `CLEARANCE`, whose `status` is `OPEN`, and whose remaining room — `capacity_sqft` less `occupied_sqft` — is at least the piece's `footprint_sqft`; `occupied_sqft` is read as currently stored, which includes the effect of any release already performed earlier in the same cascade. Rank the survivors by remaining room descending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending; a row's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. The first-ranked zone wins. The piece is released from its former zone — that zone's `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` being recomputed — then `placement_zone_id` is set to the winning zone and the winner's `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` are recomputed in turn, and `clearance_relocation_pending` is set to false. When no clearance zone qualifies, nothing moves: the piece stays exactly where it is and `clearance_relocation_pending` is set to **true**, recording that a relocation was owed but could not be honoured for want of space.

### 3.7. The Markdown Epoch, the Unsold Flag and the Disposition Election Loop

The markdown epoch is the beating heart of the gallery's pricing policy. It visits every piece whose `listed_at` is not null and whose owning agreement is in `status` `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`, in `created_at` ascending order, and it increments a piece's `version` only when one of that piece's fields actually changed.

For each such piece the epoch first recomputes floor tenure. `days_on_floor` is the number of whole days between `listed_at` and the current logical clock: subtract `listed_at` from the clock, divide the elapsed seconds by 86,400, and discard the remainder, so a piece placed nineteen hours ago has been on the floor for zero days. When `listed_at` is null, `days_on_floor` is 0. The epoch then walks the markdown steps of the schedule pinned to the piece's agreement in `sequence` ascending order and selects the **last** step whose `start_day_offset` is less than or equal to `days_on_floor`; because every schedule carries a sequence-1 step at day offset 0, that selection always succeeds. `markdown_sequence` is set to the selected step's `sequence`. The stepped price is `list_price_cents` multiplied by one minus the step's `markdown_pct`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `current_price_cents` is then the **larger** of that stepped figure and the step's `min_price_cents` — the floor is a hard floor, so it can raise the stepped price but never lower it, and the comparison happens strictly after rounding.

That is the **posted** pricing variant, in force whenever the piece's `disposition_election` is anything other than `DISCOUNT`. A piece carrying a `DISCOUNT` election is priced by the **discount-elected** variant instead, which layers a further ten-percent cut onto the same step before the floor is applied: multiply `list_price_cents` by one minus the step's `markdown_pct`, then multiply that by one minus ten hundredths, round the result half up to a whole number of cents, and only then raise it to the step's `min_price_cents` if it fell below. Identical stored data therefore yields a strictly different `current_price_cents` under the two variants, and which variant applies is read from `disposition_election` alone.

State movement follows the step in force. A piece in `ON_FLOOR` whose governing step has a `sequence` of 2 or higher — that is, a step later than the sequence-1 step it was placed under — moves `ON_FLOOR → MARKED_DOWN` as its `markdown_sequence` and `current_price_cents` advance. A piece already in `MARKED_DOWN` whose governing step `sequence` is strictly greater than its stored `markdown_sequence` takes the self-loop `MARKED_DOWN → MARKED_DOWN`, and it may advance through more than one step in a single run if the clock jumped far enough. Both edges re-evaluate the clearance test identically: when the freshly computed `current_price_cents` is below 50,000 cents and the piece's current zone has a `zone_type` other than `CLEARANCE`, the clearance relocation selector runs and both the source and the destination zones have their `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` recomputed.

The same epoch decides when a piece has simply failed to sell. Each piece carries an effective trigger day, `unsold_trigger_day_effective`, computed as the schedule's `unsold_trigger_day` plus 30 days for each discount round the consignor has already taken — that is, plus 30 multiplied by `election_round`. When a piece in `ON_FLOOR` or `MARKED_DOWN` has a `days_on_floor` greater than or equal to its `unsold_trigger_day_effective`, it moves to `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION` and `election_deadline_at` is stamped with the current clock advanced by the schedule's `election_window_days` whole days, that is by `election_window_days` multiplied by 86,400 seconds. A flagged piece keeps its `placement_zone_id` and keeps consuming floor space — the gallery is not going to warehouse it while the consignor makes up their mind.

From `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION` the consignor — or a `GalleryManager` recording an election taken over the telephone — chooses one of three `election` values, and every one of them requires the logical clock to be strictly before `election_deadline_at`, on pain of `ERR_ELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. Not every election is available for every piece: `DONATE` is refused for a piece whose `category` is `ART`, and refused for any piece whose `current_price_cents` still exceeds 200,000 cents, because a valuable piece is not charity; `RETURN` is refused for a piece whose `inventory_state` is `WRITTEN_OFF_INV`, since the gallery cannot hand back something it has already conceded is lost. Either refusal answers `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

Electing `DISCOUNT` puts the piece back to work and is the one election that loops. The piece moves `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION → MARKED_DOWN`, `election_round` is incremented by 1 — which immediately pushes `unsold_trigger_day_effective` out by another 30 days and buys the piece a further stretch of floor time — `disposition_election` is set to `DISCOUNT`, `election_deadline_at` is cleared to null, and the pricing calculation is re-run in the discount-elected variant so the extra ten-percent cut takes effect against the step in force. The clearance test is then re-evaluated against the new `current_price_cents` exactly as during the epoch. That loop is bounded: `election_round` must be strictly below 2 when a `DISCOUNT` election is attempted, so a piece can be discounted back onto the floor at most twice, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_ROUND_EXHAUSTED`. The unsold loop therefore terminates only through a donation, a return, or a sale.

Electing `DONATE` moves the piece `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION → DONATION_SCHEDULED`, and electing `RETURN` moves it `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION → RETURN_SCHEDULED`; both set `disposition_election` to the elected value and clear `election_deadline_at` to null, and both increment the piece's `version` by 1. Consignors who say nothing at all are decided for them: when the markdown epoch finds a piece in `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION` whose `election_deadline_at` is at or before the current clock with no election recorded, the epoch itself moves the piece `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION → DONATION_SCHEDULED`, sets `disposition_election` to `DONATE` by default, and clears `election_deadline_at`. This is the only path in the system that elects on a consignor's behalf, and the `memo` on the eventual disposal-fee ledger entry records that the election lapsed rather than that it was made.

**Releasing** a piece out of the building closes the merchandising story, and a `FloorOperator` performs it with no request body at all. A piece in `RETURN_SCHEDULED` moves `RETURN_SCHEDULED → RETURNED`; a piece in `DONATION_SCHEDULED` moves `DONATION_SCHEDULED → DONATED`; the target state always matches the recorded `disposition_election`, and any other `lifecycle_state` is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. In both cases `inventory_state` becomes `RELEASED`, `placement_zone_id` is cleared to null, the former zone's `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` are recomputed, and the piece's `version` increments by 1. Both cases also cost the consignor a handling fee booked as a single `DISPOSAL_FEE_DEBIT` ledger entry, after which `unclaimed_balance_cents` is recomputed: a return costs 4,000 cents of return handling, and a donation costs 2,500 cents of donation handling — the amounts are entered as negatives, since they are debits against the consignor.

### 3.8. Selling the Piece, Splitting the Proceeds, Delivering It and Adjudicating Damage

A sale is rung either by the `FloorOperator` at the register or by a `GalleryManager`, and it names the `item_id` of the piece sold, the `buyer_id` of the purchaser of record, and the `sale_price_cents` actually charged. The piece must be in `lifecycle_state` `ON_FLOOR` or `MARKED_DOWN`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, and in `inventory_state` `IN_CUSTODY`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_CUSTODY_INVALID` — a piece flagged `SHRINKAGE_MISSING` cannot be sold, however willing the walk-in customer. The buyer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_BUYER_BLOCKED`. And the register has a floor: `sale_price_cents` must be at least the piece's `current_price_cents` multiplied by 80 hundredths and rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The gallery may haggle a little at the counter but not below the consignor's negotiated floor, and a price below it is refused with `ERR_SALE_BELOW_FLOOR`.

The **split** determines how much of that price the consignor sees, and it is assembled from three ingredients before being clamped. Start from the category base share: 50 hundredths for `SOFA`, 55 hundredths for `DINING`, 55 hundredths for `CASEGOOD`, 60 hundredths for `RUG`, 65 hundredths for `ART` and 45 hundredths for `OUTDOOR`. Add a price-band bonus read off `sale_price_cents`: nothing at all below 50,000 cents, 5 hundredths for a price of at least 50,000 cents but below 150,000 cents, and 10 hundredths for a price of 150,000 cents or more — big tickets earn the consignor a better share because the gallery's handling cost does not scale with them. Then subtract a tenure penalty of 5 hundredths when the piece's `markdown_sequence` at the moment of sale is 3 or higher, since a piece that needed three markdown steps to move consumed floor space the gallery could have sold twice. The resulting fraction is clamped into the closed range from 40 hundredths to 70 hundredths — both bounds are real and reachable — and the clamped value is what is stored as `split_pct_applied`.

`consignor_share_cents` is then `sale_price_cents` multiplied by `split_pct_applied`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and `gallery_share_cents` is the exact remainder of `sale_price_cents` after the consignor's share is taken out. The gallery never computes its own rounded product, so the two shares always re-add to `sale_price_cents` to the cent. `markdown_sequence_at_sale` records a copy of the piece's `markdown_sequence` at the instant of sale, preserving the input that drove the tenure penalty; `refund_cents` opens at 0 and accumulates only through damage adjudication.

Ringing a sale fires a four-link cascade. The `Sale` row is created with `status` `COMPLETED`, `sold_at` stamped from the clock and `sold_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`, and the piece moves to `SOLD` on the merchandising dimension. Its `placement_zone_id` is deliberately **retained** until the piece physically leaves on a delivery, so a sold-but-undelivered piece keeps counting toward its zone's `occupied_sqft`. Second, because the sale exists, one `SALE_CREDIT` ledger entry of positive `consignor_share_cents` is minted against the consignor. Third, because that entry exists, the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` and `lifetime_credits_cents` are recomputed and `last_activity_at` is stamped with the clock. Fourth, because those figures moved: a consignor whose `status` was `SUSPENDED` and whose recomputed balance is now greater than or equal to zero returns to `ACTIVE`, and because `last_activity_at` moved, `escheat_state` returns to `NONE`.

A manager may **reverse** a completed sale, which requires no body. The sale's `status` must be `COMPLETED` — neither `SETTLED` nor already `REVERSED` — otherwise `ERR_SALE_ALREADY_REVERSED`, and the piece must still be in `inventory_state` `IN_CUSTODY`. On reversal the sale's `status` becomes `REVERSED`, `reversed_at` is stamped from the clock, and the sale's `version` increments by 1. Exactly one `SALE_REVERSAL_DEBIT` entry is minted, carrying the negation of `consignor_share_cents`, so the sale's two ledger entries for that piece sum to exactly zero and the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` returns precisely to its pre-sale value; `last_activity_at` is stamped again. The piece goes back on the floor in the zone it never left, that zone's `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` being recomputed, keeping its `markdown_sequence` and `current_price_cents`, and the pricing calculation is re-run against it. Its `lifecycle_state` mirrors the step in force: it returns to `ON_FLOOR` when `markdown_sequence` is 1, and to `MARKED_DOWN` when `markdown_sequence` is 2 or higher. Any delivery order still in `SCHEDULED` against that sale is moved `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, which releases the crew window so it stops participating in concurrency checks.

**White-glove delivery** is scheduled by a `LogisticsCoordinator` against the `sale_id` being delivered, the `crew_id` reserved, and a `window_start` and `window_end`. Delivery windows are **half-open**: `window_start` is inclusive and `window_end` is exclusive, which is why two orders that merely touch at an instant do not conflict. `window_start` must be strictly before `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID`. The underlying sale must be in `status` `COMPLETED`, otherwise `ERR_SALE_ALREADY_REVERSED`, and the sold piece must still be in `inventory_state` `IN_CUSTODY`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_CUSTODY_INVALID`. Crews are finite: count the crew's non-terminal orders whose half-open windows contain any instant of the requested window, and if adding this order would push that count above the crew's `max_concurrent_jobs`, the request is refused with `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

The buyer's access facts are snapshotted onto the order at creation, exactly as the consignor's are onto a pickup job: `elevator_used` is a copy of the buyer's `has_elevator`, and `stair_flights` is the buyer's `floor_number` reduced by 1 but never below zero, or zero outright when the buyer declared an elevator. The buyer-paid `delivery_fee_cents` is then the sum of three integer terms and involves no rounding at all, because every term is already whole cents. Begin with a base white-glove charge of 7,500 cents. Add an access charge: a flat 3,000 cents when `elevator_used` is true; otherwise 4,500 cents per flight for up to 6 flights, plus a further 8,000 cents when `stair_flights` is strictly greater than 6. Add an assembly charge of 4,000 cents when the sold piece's `category` is `DINING` or `CASEGOOD`, and nothing otherwise — those are the categories that arrive in pieces. A new order opens in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `scheduled_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`, which thereafter bars that same user id from adjudicating any damage claim filed against this order.

Recording the delivery **outcome** is a single capability that forks on an `outcome` of either `DELIVERED` or `REFUSED`, and the two branches share nothing but the timestamp. Both require the order to be in `status` `SCHEDULED` and the sale to be in `status` `COMPLETED`, and both stamp `outcome_recorded_at` with the current clock and increment the order's `version` by 1. On `DELIVERED` the order moves `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED`: `claim_window_ends_at` is set to the current clock advanced by five whole days, that is by 5 multiplied by 86,400 seconds; the sale moves `COMPLETED → SETTLED`; the piece moves `SOLD → SETTLED` on the merchandising dimension and `IN_CUSTODY → RELEASED` on the custody dimension; and `placement_zone_id` is finally cleared to null with the former zone's `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` recomputed, freeing the square feet the sold piece had still been holding. `DELIVERED` additionally requires the piece to be in `IN_CUSTODY` at that moment.

On `REFUSED` the order moves `SCHEDULED → REFUSED` and nothing settles: `claim_window_ends_at` stays **null**, which is the mechanism by which a refused delivery can never carry a damage claim. The sale moves `COMPLETED → REVERSED`, a `SALE_REVERSAL_DEBIT` of the negated `consignor_share_cents` is minted, the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the piece goes back onto the floor in its former zone — to `ON_FLOOR` when its `markdown_sequence` is 1 and to `MARKED_DOWN` when it is 2 or higher. A third, purely cascaded outcome exists: an order sitting in `SCHEDULED` when its parent sale is reversed directly moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and its crew window is released.

A **damage claim** may be filed by the `Buyer` who bought the piece, or by the `LogisticsCoordinator` for a claim phoned in to the crew. It names the `delivery_order_id` the damage is attributed to, a `claim_amount_cents` of at least 1 that the buyer asks to be made whole for, and a `description` narrating the damage; a `fault_party` may optionally be supplied at filing. The window is tight: `claim_window_ends_at` must be non-null and the logical clock must be strictly before it, otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` — which is also what a claim against a still-`SCHEDULED` or a `REFUSED` delivery receives, since neither ever opened a window. `claim_amount_cents` must not exceed the parent sale's `sale_price_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_SALE`. A filed claim opens in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `resubmission_count` at 0 and has no ledger effect whatsoever.

Claims negotiate before they resolve. A `GalleryManager` may send a `SUBMITTED` claim back for more detail by supplying an `info_request_note`, which moves it `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED`, stores the note, leaves `resubmission_count` unchanged and does **not** extend the claim clock. The `Buyer` then responds with a replacement `description` and an optional `withdraw` flag. With `withdraw` false or absent, the claim moves `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, `resubmission_count` is incremented by 1, and the stored `description` is replaced by the resubmitted narrative. With `withdraw` true, the claim moves `INFO_REQUESTED → WITHDRAWN`, `resubmission_count` is left unchanged, no ledger entry is minted, and the delivery order's claim window is unaffected. Either way the claim's `version` increments by 1. The negotiation cycle is bounded at two passes: both the information request and the resubmission require `resubmission_count` to be strictly below 2, and an attempt beyond that is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_RESUBMIT_LIMIT`. Requesting information on a claim that is not `SUBMITTED`, resubmitting one that is not `INFO_REQUESTED`, or adjudicating one that is not `SUBMITTED`, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

**Adjudication** is a `GalleryManager` decision carrying an `approve` boolean and a `fault_party` that must be one of `GALLERY`, `CONSIGNOR`, `BUYER` or `UNDETERMINED`. The claim must be in `SUBMITTED`, the `claim_amount_cents` must not exceed the parent sale's `sale_price_cents`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the `scheduled_by_user_id` of the delivery order the claim is filed against — the coordinator who booked the delivery does not get to rule on whether it went badly.

When `approve` is false the claim moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, all three money columns — `adjudicated_amount_cents`, `gallery_absorbed_cents` and `consignor_debited_cents` — are written as 0, and no ledger entry is minted. When `approve` is true the claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and the allocation runs. `adjudicated_amount_cents` is the smaller of `claim_amount_cents` and the parent sale's `sale_price_cents`. If `fault_party` is `GALLERY` or `BUYER`, the gallery eats the whole thing: `gallery_absorbed_cents` is the full `adjudicated_amount_cents` and `consignor_debited_cents` is 0. Otherwise the gallery's own deductible fills **first**: the gallery bucket is the smaller of `adjudicated_amount_cents` and 5,000 cents, and the remainder is `adjudicated_amount_cents` less that bucket. When `fault_party` is `CONSIGNOR` the whole remainder becomes `consignor_debited_cents`; when it is `UNDETERMINED` the remainder is split evenly, so `consignor_debited_cents` is the remainder multiplied by 50 hundredths and rounded half up to a whole number of cents. In both of those branches `gallery_absorbed_cents` is the exact remainder of `adjudicated_amount_cents` after the consignor's debit, so the two buckets always re-add to `adjudicated_amount_cents` to the cent.

An approval then moves money in two places. The parent sale's `refund_cents` is increased by `adjudicated_amount_cents`, accumulating across successive approved claims on the same sale. And when `consignor_debited_cents` is strictly greater than zero, exactly one `DAMAGE_ALLOCATION_DEBIT` ledger entry of the negated `consignor_debited_cents` is minted, the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` is recomputed, `last_activity_at` is stamped with the clock, and if the recomputed balance has fallen below negative 50,000 cents the consignor's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED`. A `consignor_debited_cents` of exactly zero mints nothing. `adjudicated_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` and the claim's `version` increments by 1. Claim listings accept optional `delivery_order_id` and `status` filters; sale listings accept optional `item_id`, `buyer_id` and `status`; delivery-order listings accept optional `sale_id`, `crew_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Shrinkage, Write-Offs, Payouts and the Escheatment of Dormant Proceeds

Furniture goes missing. A `FloorOperator` who notices a gap during a spot check flags the piece by submitting a `flag` of `MISSING`, and a piece that later turns up is flagged `RECOVERED`; those are the only two accepted values. Flagging a piece `MISSING` requires it to be in `inventory_state` `IN_CUSTODY` and requires its `lifecycle_state` to be none of `SOLD`, `SETTLED`, `RETURNED`, `DONATED` or `SCREEN_REJECTED`. The flag moves the custody dimension `IN_CUSTODY → SHRINKAGE_MISSING` and leaves `lifecycle_state` deliberately **untouched** — a piece that is `MARKED_DOWN` keeps being marked down by the epoch while it is missing, because the two state dimensions of a piece evolve independently — and the piece keeps counting toward its zone's `occupied_sqft` until the loss is actually booked.

Recovery has two shapes depending on whether money had already changed hands. A `RECOVERED` flag on a piece in `SHRINKAGE_MISSING` simply moves it `SHRINKAGE_MISSING → IN_CUSTODY`, mints **no** ledger entry at all — no shrinkage credit was ever booked for a piece that was merely mislaid — and leaves `placement_zone_id` unchanged, so the zone's `occupied_sqft` does not move either. A `RECOVERED` flag on a piece in `WRITTEN_OFF_INV` is a different matter: the custody dimension moves `WRITTEN_OFF_INV → IN_CUSTODY`, the merchandising dimension moves `WRITTEN_OFF → AWAITING_PLACEMENT` so the piece must be placed and tagged again from scratch, `tag_code` is cleared to null to force that re-tagging, one `SHRINKAGE_RECOVERY_DEBIT` ledger entry equal to the exact negation of that piece's earlier `SHRINKAGE_CREDIT` is minted to unwind the credit, the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the zone occupancy figures are recomputed because the recovered piece consumes square feet again. Either flag increments the piece's `version` by 1, and a flag whose custody from-state does not match is refused with `ERR_ITEM_CUSTODY_INVALID`. Note that `WRITTEN_OFF` is **not** a terminal merchandising state precisely because of this path — a recovered piece re-enters the merchandising lifecycle at `AWAITING_PLACEMENT`.

A **shrinkage audit** is the systematic version of the same discovery. A `FloorOperator` submits the `floor_zone_id` of the zone being counted together with `counted_item_ids`, the array of item ids physically found in it. The zone's `status` must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_ZONE_CLOSED`; a closed zone is not counted. `counted_item_ids` must contain no duplicates and every id in it must be a stored piece currently placed in the zone being counted, otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_COUNT_INVALID`; an id naming no stored piece at all is instead answered by `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` at the body-existence stage.

The audit derives four figures. `expected_item_count` is the number of pieces whose `placement_zone_id` names the counted zone and whose `inventory_state` is either `IN_CUSTODY` or `SHRINKAGE_MISSING`. `counted_item_count` is the number of distinct entries in `counted_item_ids` that belong to that expected set. `variance_count` is `expected_item_count` less `counted_item_count`. And `missing_item_ids` is the list of expected ids absent from `counted_item_ids`, ordered by the pieces' `created_at` ascending — that ordering is load-bearing, because it is the order in which the write-offs are later processed. `counted_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. Every expected piece absent from the count is immediately moved to `inventory_state` `SHRINKAGE_MISSING` with its `lifecycle_state` untouched.

What the audit does next forks on whether anything is actually missing. When `variance_count` is 0, the audit is `RECONCILED` at the moment of creation: `write_off_total_cents` is 0, `approved_by_user_id` stays null because nobody needed to approve anything, and no ledger entry is minted. When `variance_count` is greater than 0, the audit opens in `status` `COUNTED` and waits for a manager. A zone carries at most one non-terminal audit, so submitting a fresh count on a zone that already has an unreconciled one moves the older audit `COUNTED → ABANDONED`; the superseded audit books nothing at all, and the pieces on its `missing_item_ids` keep the `SHRINKAGE_MISSING` custody state the older count gave them.

Approving a count's write-offs is a `GalleryManager` capability requiring no body, and it is where the shrinkage credit is finally paid. The audit must be in `status` `COUNTED`, otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` — but the segregation check runs first, so the very operator recorded as `counted_by_user_id` receives `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when the audit has already been reconciled. The credit for one piece is that piece's `current_price_cents` multiplied by 60 hundredths and rounded half up to a whole number of cents; the gallery does not make a consignor whole for a lost piece, it settles at a fraction of the marked price. The batch is walked in `missing_item_ids` order — the pieces' `created_at` ascending — and each piece produces its **own** `SHRINKAGE_CREDIT` ledger entry of that positive per-piece amount; a single merged entry for the batch is never minted. `write_off_total_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-piece credits, never a rounding of the summed prices. Each piece in the batch moves to `inventory_state` `WRITTEN_OFF_INV` and `lifecycle_state` `WRITTEN_OFF` with its `placement_zone_id` cleared to null, and each owning consignor has `unclaimed_balance_cents` and `lifetime_credits_cents` recomputed and `last_activity_at` stamped with the clock. The audit's `status` becomes `RECONCILED`, `approved_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, its `version` increments by 1, and the zone's `occupied_sqft` and `utilization_state` are recomputed exactly **once** after the whole batch rather than once per piece. A zone's audit history is readable oldest-first with an optional `status` filter.

The same credit can also be paid one piece at a time. A `GalleryManager` writing off a single piece supplies `expected_item_version` and nothing else. The piece's `lifecycle_state` must be one of `AWAITING_PLACEMENT`, `ON_FLOOR`, `MARKED_DOWN` or `UNSOLD_PENDING_ELECTION`, and its `inventory_state` must be `SHRINKAGE_MISSING` — you cannot write off a piece nobody has reported missing. On success the custody dimension moves `SHRINKAGE_MISSING → WRITTEN_OFF_INV` and the merchandising dimension moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`, `placement_zone_id` is cleared to null and the released zone's occupancy figures are recomputed, one `SHRINKAGE_CREDIT` entry of the same sixty-percent-of-current-price amount is minted, the consignor's `unclaimed_balance_cents` and `lifetime_credits_cents` are recomputed with `last_activity_at` stamped from the clock, and the piece's `version` increments by 1.

**Payouts** empty the consignor's ledger, and only a `GalleryManager` may issue one. The request carries `expected_consignor_version` and nothing else, because there is no partial payout: the whole stored balance goes out at once. An escheated consignor is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNOR_ESCHEATED`. The balance must be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE`, and it must then be at least the 2,000-cent payout minimum, otherwise `ERR_PAYOUT_BELOW_MINIMUM`; a consignor may still be paid out while their `escheat_state` is `DORMANT`, since dormancy is a warning rather than a freeze.

The waterfall runs against the stored `unclaimed_balance_cents` as the available amount. The withholding tier is keyed on that **pre-withholding** available balance and is never recomputed against any later figure in the waterfall: nothing is withheld when the available amount is below 100,000 cents; 2 hundredths of it are withheld when the amount is at least 100,000 cents but below 500,000 cents; and 3 hundredths are withheld at 500,000 cents or above. `withholding_cents` is the available amount multiplied by that fraction, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `processing_fee_cents` is read from the consignor's `payout_method`: 250 cents for `CHECK`, and nothing at all for `ACH` or `STORE_CREDIT`. `net_payout_cents` is then the available amount less `withholding_cents` less `processing_fee_cents`, by exact subtraction with no further rounding. Exactly one `PAYOUT_DISBURSEMENT_DEBIT` entry is minted for the negation of the whole available amount — not the net figure — carrying `withholding_cents`, `processing_fee_cents` and `net_payout_cents` as reporting fields on that same entry rather than as separate ledger rows. The recomputed `unclaimed_balance_cents` is therefore exactly 0, `last_activity_at` is stamped from the clock, a `DORMANT` `escheat_state` returns to `NONE`, a `SUSPENDED` consignor whose recomputed balance is greater than or equal to zero returns to `ACTIVE`, and the consignor's `version` increments by 1.

Money nobody ever claims eventually leaves the gallery's books, and the **escheatment epoch** is what moves it. Walking every consignor in `created_at` ascending order, the epoch computes dormancy as the number of whole days between `last_activity_at` and the current logical clock — elapsed seconds divided by 86,400 with the remainder discarded. A consignor whose `unclaimed_balance_cents` is zero or negative has nothing to escheat and their `escheat_state` stays `NONE` regardless of how long they have been quiet. Otherwise the curve has three bands, tested in this order. At 545 dormant days or more the consignor becomes `ESCHEATED`: one `ESCHEATMENT_DEBIT` entry for the negation of the whole `unclaimed_balance_cents` is minted, driving the recomputed balance to exactly 0, and `last_activity_at` is deliberately **not** refreshed by that entry, so escheating cannot reset the very curve that produced it. At 365 dormant days or more, but fewer than 545, the consignor becomes `DORMANT` with no ledger effect and no change to the balance. Below 365 days the consignor is `NONE` — renewed activity resets the curve entirely, and indeed the `DORMANT → NONE` return happens the moment any ledger entry other than an `ESCHEATMENT_DEBIT` is minted for them, because that entry stamps `last_activity_at` with the clock and restarts the count from zero. A consignor sitting in `NONE` with a positive balance and 545 dormant days escheats in a single run without passing through `DORMANT` first.

Escheatment cascades into the consignor's commercial standing: every one of their `ACTIVE` agreements is moved `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` in the same run, and every subsequent payout request, agreement draft and intake attempt is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNOR_ESCHEATED`. Only rows whose `escheat_state` or balance actually changed have their `version` incremented. The ledger itself remains fully readable throughout: a consignor's own entries are readable in `effective_at` ascending order with an optional `entry_type` filter, and the entries attributable to one piece are readable the same way, so both a consignor and an auditor can reconstruct exactly how a balance reached the figure that was escheated.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are mounted at the service root; there is no shared resource prefix. Creating a new resource answers **201 Created** and returns the created row in `data`. Every other successful operation — reads, list reads, state transitions, corrections, and both nightly epochs — answers **200 OK**, and where a resource was modified the updated row is returned in `data`. The two operations that mutate nothing observable in a resource body, namely setting the logical clock and running either epoch, answer 200 OK with an empty object in `data`. There are no 204 responses and no endpoint returns a bare body outside the envelope defined in §2.2.
