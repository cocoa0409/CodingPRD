# Childrens Product Testing & Certification API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Juvenile-product manufacturers live under a regulatory regime that is unusually unforgiving: a cot, a high chair, a plush toy or a teething ring must be shown — before it ships, and repeatedly for as long as it keeps shipping — to sit within numeric limits for lead in paint, total lead content, phthalate plasticisers, mechanical hazards such as small parts, and flammability. Proving that is not a single laboratory report. It is a chain of artefacts: the correct set of rules identified for the product's category and its actual bill of materials, an accredited third-party laboratory for each individual test method, a defensible sampling plan for both initial certification and periodic verification, a children's product certificate carrying every legally required content element and accompanying every outbound shipment, a permanent tracking label bearing the production batch identifier on both the product and its packaging, and — where the certifier leans on a supplier rather than testing the finished good — signed reliance documentation for each component part certificate. Compliance teams today assemble this chain in spreadsheets, and the chain breaks quietly: a laboratory's accreditation lapses mid-test, a supplier switches a coating without telling anyone, a batch ships against a certificate that was withdrawn last week.

This API is the system of record for that chain. It holds the regulatory reference library, the register of suppliers and laboratories with their accreditation scopes, every product with its derived rule set and its evidence, every sampling plan with its unit ledger, every test order with its price and result, every certificate with its two independent status dimensions, every production batch with its tracking-label state, and every noncompliance case with its retest rounds and corrective action plans. Crucially, it is not a passive filing cabinet. When a laboratory accreditation is suspended, the orders resting on it are invalidated, their sample units returned and their fees credited, and the affected sampling plans fall due for resampling. When a test fails, a noncompliance case opens, the product is suspended, its issued certificates are withdrawn, and the batches those certificates accompanied are stopped in distribution. When a supplier changes a material, the reliance the certifier placed on that supplier's certificate is voided and the evidence gap reopens. Those propagations are the product.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **segregation of duties is enforced by user identity, not merely by role** — the person who ordered the evidence may not be the person who issues the certificate that cites it, and the person whose failing report opened a case may not be the person who closes it. Second, **every deadline is read from a server-controlled logical clock**, so that coverage windows, periodic due dates, attestation expiries and traceback cutoffs are deterministic and testable rather than dependent on wall time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. Both must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must name one of the six roles the system recognises. A request that omits either header, supplies an empty value, or names a role outside the recognised set is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the target operation is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The authorisation check is per-operation; §4 lists the authorised roles for every endpoint.

The six roles are `ComplianceAdministrator`, `TestCoordinator`, `CertificationOfficer`, `DistributionManager`, `LaboratoryUser`, and `SupplierUser`.

A `ComplianceAdministrator` owns the regulatory reference data — rules, categories and the mappings between them — together with the party register of suppliers, laboratories and accreditation scopes, product registration, bill-of-materials entry, product archival, the logical clock, and the immutable material-change log. This role deliberately cannot book tests, cannot record reliance decisions, cannot issue certificates and cannot move inventory. A `TestCoordinator` scopes products onto an evidence path, builds and maintains sampling plans, books third-party testing, cancels orders and orders retest rounds; it cannot accredit or bar laboratories, cannot report results, cannot issue certificates and cannot release inventory. A `CertificationOfficer` decides whether to rely on a supplier's component certificate, drafts and issues children's product certificates, voids unissued drafts, files corrective action plans and closes noncompliance cases; it cannot book or report tests and cannot move inventory. A `DistributionManager` records production batches and their tracking labels, furnishes and recalls certificate accompaniment for shipments, releases and rescinds releases, and issues the formal stop-distribution instruction; it cannot certify and cannot order or report tests.

Four of the six roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAdministrator`, `TestCoordinator`, `CertificationOfficer` or `DistributionManager`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `ordered_by_user_id` on a test order booked by a coordinator, as `reported_by_user_id` and `opened_by_user_id` where a report filing opens a case, as `reliance_recorded_by_user_id` on a component certificate, as `issued_by_user_id` on a certificate, as `closed_by_user_id` on a closed case, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on a material change event — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `LaboratoryUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Laboratory`; ownership checks on any resource that references a laboratory compare the resource's laboratory foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, taking an order into testing or filing its report requires that the order's `laboratory_id` equal `X-User-ID`; any other order is refused with `ERR_LAB_NOT_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier`; ownership checks compare a resource's supplier foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Submitting a component part certificate requires that the part's `supplier_id` equal `X-User-ID`; any other part is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_PART_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Beyond role membership, two duty separations are enforced against `X-User-ID` itself and both raise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). At certificate issuance, the issuing `X-User-ID` must differ from the `ordered_by_user_id` of every test order counted as evidence for the certificate's cited rules. At case closure, the closing `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `opened_by_user_id`. The same identity that reported a failing result therefore cannot record reliance documentation or issue a certificate for that product, nor close the case its report opened.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and must not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry four pagination fields inside `meta`: the echoed `limit`, the echoed `offset`, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. One endpoint states otherwise: the material change event log is returned newest first, ordered by `occurred_at` descending and then by `created_at` descending, because an audit reader always wants the most recent change at the top.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix and no client control; a client never supplies an `id`. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first persisted. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. There is one deliberate exception: a material change event is append-only evidence, so a `MaterialChangeEvent` row carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no operation that updates it. Every other entity in the system — regulatory rules, product categories, category-rule mappings, suppliers, laboratories, laboratory accreditations, products, component parts, component part certificates, sampling plans, test orders, children's product certificates, production batches and noncompliance cases — carries all four common properties.

The three-fractional-digit rendering belongs to those two audit stamps alone (and, per section 2.2, to `api_request_timestamp`). Every **other** datetime-valued field a response carries — a mapping's `effective_from`, a laboratory's `influence_attestation_valid_through`, an accreditation's `valid_from` and `valid_through`, a component part's `last_material_change_at`, a component part certificate's `coverage_from` and `coverage_through`, a sampling plan's `activated_at`, `last_verified_at` and `periodic_due_at`, a test order's `window_start`, `window_end` and `reported_at`, a certificate's `issued_at`, a production batch's `produced_on` and `released_at`, and a material change event's `occurred_at` — is rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with a trailing `Z` at whole-second precision, and gains a three-digit fractional part only when the instant it holds actually carries a non-zero sub-second component; in that case the fraction is that component truncated toward zero to whole milliseconds rather than rounded. Whichever family a field belongs to, a datetime field holding no value renders as JSON null. An instant a client supplied is echoed back as the same instant under this rule, never re-stamped from the clock and never widened to a precision it did not carry.

Time in this system is a **logical clock** held by the server. Every deadline, coverage-window boundary, due date, expiry check and traceback cutoff reads that clock and never the machine's wall time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until an administrator moves it, that is the instant every rule sees. A `ComplianceAdministrator` may advance or rewind the clock to any supplied instant by submitting a `to_iso8601` value; the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`, rendered under the same whole-second instant rule as the non-audit datetime fields above — so a clock parked on the startup instant reads back exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and a fractional part appears only when the administrator set an instant that carried one. Moving the clock mutates no entity row — state machines observe the new instant only when a subsequent request arrives and re-evaluates its own preconditions against it. Fields the server stamps from the logical clock rather than from the request include a plan's `activated_at` and `last_verified_at`, an order's `reported_at`, a batch's `released_at`, a certificate's `issued_at`, a part's `last_material_change_at`, and an event's `occurred_at`.

### 2.5. Reference Resolution and Nested Path Validation

Paths in this API are shallow — at most one level of nesting below a collection — but many operations reference several entities at once, some through the URL and some through the request body. The path parameters that identify a resource are `rule_id` for a regulatory rule, `category_id` for a product category, `mapping_id` for a category-to-rule mapping, `supplier_id` for a supplier, `laboratory_id` for a laboratory, `accreditation_id` for a laboratory accreditation, `product_id` for a product, `part_id` for a component part, `part_certificate_id` for a component part certificate, `plan_id` for a sampling plan, `order_id` for a test order, `certificate_id` for a children's product certificate, `batch_id` for a production batch, `case_id` for a noncompliance case and `event_id` for a material change event. Resolution is strictly ordered: path segments are validated left to right, and only then are the foreign-key fields of the request body validated in the order the endpoint declares them. The response reports the **leftmost** unresolved reference, and each entity has exactly one not-found code, used identically whether the reference arrived in the path or in the body.

A missing regulatory rule reference — whether a rule path segment or a body `rule_id` — yields `ERR_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. A missing category, whether a path segment or a body `category_id`, yields `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`. A missing mapping yields `ERR_MAPPING_NOT_FOUND`. A missing supplier, path or body `supplier_id`, yields `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`. A missing laboratory, path or body `laboratory_id`, yields `ERR_LABORATORY_NOT_FOUND`. A missing accreditation yields `ERR_ACCREDITATION_NOT_FOUND`. A missing product, path or body `product_id`, yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`. A missing component part — a part path segment or a body `component_part_id` — yields `ERR_COMPONENT_PART_NOT_FOUND`, and the same code is used when the referenced part exists but belongs to a different product than the one named in the path. A missing component part certificate yields `ERR_PART_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`. A missing sampling plan, path segment or body `sampling_plan_id`, yields `ERR_SAMPLING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, and the same code covers a plan that exists but belongs to a different product than the order being booked. A missing test order yields `ERR_TEST_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. A missing children's product certificate, path segment or body `certificate_id`, yields `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, and likewise when the certificate exists but belongs to a different product than the batch being recorded. A missing production batch yields `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`. A missing noncompliance case yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`. A missing material change event yields `ERR_MATERIAL_CHANGE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`. Every one of these carries HTTP 404.

Existence resolution happens in two passes at adjacent priorities: the first pass covers every reference reachable from a path parameter or from a body foreign key on any endpoint that has one; the second pass re-walks body foreign keys in declared field order and applies to the eight creation operations that accept them — mapping creation, accreditation creation, product registration, component part creation, component part certificate submission, test order booking, production batch recording, and material change event recording.

### 2.6. Stale-Snapshot Protection at the Certificate and Corrective-Action Desks

Two write operations sit where a second writer's unobserved change would silently destroy meaning, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency against the **parent** entity's `version`.

Drafting a children's product certificate against a product requires `expected_product_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the `version` of the `Product` the client last read. If that value does not equal the product's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new draft certificate legitimately supersedes the product's currently issued certificate and freezes a snapshot of the product's required rule set; a writer holding an out-of-date view of the product would pin the wrong predecessor and the wrong cited rule snapshot. The value actually supplied is retained on the certificate as `expected_product_version_seen`, so the audit file records which product snapshot the certifier worked from.

Recording a corrective action plan on a noncompliance case requires `expected_case_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the `version` of the `NoncomplianceCase` the client last read. If that value does not equal the case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each recorded plan legitimately replaces the previous one and advances `corrective_action_revision`, so a writer working from an out-of-date view of the case would overwrite a plan it never saw.

No other operation in the system takes a version field. Endpoints outside these two neither require nor accept `expected_product_version` or `expected_case_version`.

### 2.7. Arithmetic, Rounding and Evaluation Order

Money in this system is expressed in whole cents as integers; there are no fractional-cent amounts anywhere in a stored field or a response. Wherever a percentage, a basis-point rate or a multiplier produces a fractional amount, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Basis points are always divided by 10000 to obtain a rate, so a surcharge of 250 basis points is a rate of two and a half percent.

Two rounding disciplines are pinned and must not be reorganised. First, laboratory line pricing rounds the **per-sample** amount to whole cents once, and only then multiplies by the sample count; the alternative order — multiplying the exact fractional per-sample amount by the sample count and rounding the product — is explicitly not used, and multiplication by the sample count is exact integer arithmetic. Second, the order invoice applies rounding half up independently at two points: once when the expedite fee is derived from the subtotal, and once when the reliance discount is derived from the total that already contains that expedite fee. The steps run exactly once each and strictly in that order, so although the discount tier appears to depend on a figure that itself depends on the subtotal, the value is well defined.

Sample units, production units and concurrency counts are integers throughout, with no rounding of any kind once computed. Sample sizing rounds its volume component up to the next whole number on the forward-looking variant and down to the preceding whole number on the chronological variant, and applies no other rounding. Score and percentage fields are integers: the risk-weighted gap score rounds its percentage half up before penalties are added and clamps once afterwards, while certificate content completeness takes the floor of its percentage. Threshold comparisons preserve their stated inclusivity exactly: where a rule says a value at or below a limit passes, a value exactly equal to the limit passes; where a periodic due instant is reached, an instant exactly equal to the due instant is due; where a traceback cutoff applies, a batch produced exactly at the cutoff instant is inside the scope.

### 2.8. Conservation Ledgers and Their Compensating Actions

Three quantities in this system are conserved, and each has a compensating action that restores the ledger exactly.

**Sample units.** A sampling plan's `units_committed` is the sum of `sample_count` over its test orders whose `order_state` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TESTING`, `REPORTED_PASS` or `REPORTED_FAIL`; orders in `CANCELLED` or `INVALIDATED` release their units and drop out of the sum. Its `units_available` is `units_authorized` minus `units_committed`. The invariant is that `units_committed` plus `units_available` equals `units_authorized` at all times, and it must hold identically before an order is booked and after that same order is cancelled — cancellation returns exactly the units the booking took, with no drift.

**Distribution units.** Every production batch's `quantity` sits in exactly one of the product's four counters at any instant: `units_held`, `units_released`, `units_stopped` or `units_destroyed`. The invariant is that the sum of those four counters equals the total `quantity` produced across every batch of the product, with no status filter applied to that total. It must hold identically before a batch is released and after the release is rescinded, since rescission moves the same quantity back to the counter it came from.

**Laboratory fees.** A laboratory's outstanding balance is the sum of `invoice_total_cents` over its test orders in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TESTING`, `REPORTED_PASS` or `REPORTED_FAIL`. The invariant is that `billed_fee_cents_total` minus `credited_fee_cents_total` equals that outstanding balance. Every order is billed at creation, and cancellation or invalidation credits back the exact `invoice_total_cents` that was billed, so the difference returns to its pre-billing value with zero drift.

The on-demand compliance summary read for a product recomputes both unit ledgers and reports whether they balance, alongside the freshly derived rule set and score. It is a pure read: it mutates no row and bumps no `version`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Version Propagation

Several operations in this system reach well past the row they name. The rule is that every entity a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1 — a cascade that moves a product, its certificate and three batches bumps five versions, not one. Where a cascade recomputes a derived field, the recomputation happens after the state change that caused it, so the derived value reflects the post-transition world.

Four named cascades run through the domain, and each is described in full where its business narrative lives:

- The **accreditation suspension cascade** starts when an accreditation scope is suspended or expired, invalidates the affected orders at that laboratory for that test method, returns their sample units and credits their fees, and pushes the affected sampling plans into resampling.
- The **failed test stop-distribution cascade** starts when a laboratory reports a failing measurement, opens a noncompliance case, suspends the product, withdraws the product's issued certificates, and stops the batches those certificates accompanied.
- The **material change resample cascade** starts when a material change is recorded against a component part, supersedes that part's reliance eligibility, voids the relied-upon certificates on it, pushes the product's active sampling plans into resampling, and restricts the product's compliance posture.
- The **noncompliance closure cascade** returns stopped batches to hold, moves their units back, and lifts the product out of remediation.

Once a product reaches `ARCHIVED` its `certification_state` is terminal, and every write that names or references that product — or any of its children — is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_ARCHIVED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Laboratory Integrity and Undue-Influence Prevention

Third-party testing only means something if the laboratory is genuinely third-party, genuinely accredited for the exact method, and genuinely free of pressure from the party being certified. Four gates enforce that, and each is checked at both assignment time and start-of-testing time where the scaffold's workflow reaches both.

A laboratory whose `operational_state` is `BARRED` may not be assigned new work and may not take an order into testing; attempts are refused with `ERR_LAB_BARRED` (HTTP 422). This gate applies to booking a test order, to starting testing, and to submitting a component part certificate that names the barred laboratory as the source of the underlying data.

A laboratory must hold a standing undue-influence prevention attestation before work is assigned to it. Its `influence_attestation_state` must be `CURRENT` and its `influence_attestation_valid_through` must be at or after the logical clock instant. An attestation that is `MISSING`, that has been marked `EXPIRED`, or whose validity instant is at or before the logical clock, blocks the operation with `ERR_UNDUE_INFLUENCE_ATTESTATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422). This gate applies to booking a test order and to starting testing.

A `MANUFACTURER_OWNED` laboratory may never be handed a retest round. When an operation would create a test order flagged as a retest, the laboratory's `independence_class` must be `INDEPENDENT` or `FIREWALLED_GOVERNMENT`; a manufacturer-owned laboratory in that position is refused with `ERR_MANUFACTURER_OWNED_LAB_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422).

Finally, accreditation is scoped to a single test method, not to a laboratory in general. Exactly one accreditation record must exist for the target laboratory whose `test_method_code` equals the test method of the rule under test, whose `accreditation_state` is `ACCREDITED`, and whose closed coverage interval — from `valid_from` through `valid_through`, both endpoints inclusive — contains the logical clock instant. Absence of such a record is refused with `ERR_LAB_METHOD_NOT_ACCREDITED` (HTTP 422). This gate applies to booking a test order and to submitting a component part certificate; the same coverage condition is additionally re-verified as a precondition when the laboratory takes an order into testing.

### 2.11. The Order in Which Refusals Are Decided

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is decided by the **first** violated rule in the strict ordering below, and no later rule may mask an earlier one. The ordering is normative: a request that both names an archived product and exceeds the testing budget reports the archived product, because the archival guard sits earlier. Priority numbers below are evaluation ranks, not values a client sends.

1. Authentication — both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming one of the six roles → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Role authorisation — the caller's role appears among the roles authorised for the target operation → `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Laboratory assignment ownership — when the caller is a `LaboratoryUser`, `X-User-ID` equals the `laboratory_id` of the test order named in the path → `ERR_LAB_NOT_ASSIGNED` (403). Applies to taking an order into testing and to filing its report.
4. Supplier part ownership — when the caller is a `SupplierUser`, `X-User-ID` equals the `supplier_id` of the component part named in the path → `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_PART_OWNER` (403). Applies to submitting a component part certificate.
5. Request shape — every required field present, every enum value inside its declared set, every regex-constrained string matching its pattern, every numeric field inside its declared range, and on list endpoints `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive with `offset` at least 0 → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
6. Path and body reference existence — every resource named by a path segment or by a request-body foreign key exists, validated left to right across the path and then in declared body-field order, reporting the leftmost missing reference with that entity's own not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter or a body foreign key.
7. Body foreign-key existence, second pass — every foreign key supplied in the body resolves, in declared field order, using the same per-entity not-found codes (404). Applies to mapping creation, accreditation creation, product registration, component part creation, component part certificate submission, test order booking, production batch recording, and material change event recording.
8. Product archival — the product named or referenced by the request is not in `ARCHIVED` → `ERR_PRODUCT_ARCHIVED` (409). Applies to every state-changing operation that names or references a product.
9. Product certification state — the product is in a state the operation allows: `DRAFT` for submission for certification; one of `SCOPED`, `EVIDENCE_RELIANCE`, `FULL_TEST_PROGRAM`, `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, `CERTIFIED` or `REMEDIATION` for booking a test order; `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` for certificate issuance; `CERTIFIED` for archival → `ERR_PRODUCT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to those four operations: submission for certification, archival, test order booking, and certificate issuance.
10. Test order state — `SCHEDULED` for taking into testing, `IN_TESTING` for filing a report, and either `SCHEDULED` or `IN_TESTING` for cancellation → `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to those three operations.
11. Sampling plan state — `DRAFT` for activation, either `ACTIVE` or `RESAMPLE_DUE` for periodic review, and either `ACTIVE` or `RESAMPLE_DUE` to accept a new test order → `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to plan activation, periodic review, and test order booking.
12. Certificate axis state — `issuance_status` `DRAFT` for issuing and for voiding, `issuance_status` `ISSUED` for furnishing, `accompaniment_status` `FURNISHED` for recalling accompaniment, and on batch release the referenced certificate must be `ISSUED` on the issuance axis **and** `FURNISHED` on the accompaniment axis simultaneously → `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to issuing, voiding, furnishing, recalling accompaniment, and batch release.
13. Production batch state — `HELD` for release, `RELEASED` for rescinding a release, and either `HELD` or `STOP_DISTRIBUTION` for tracking-label edits (a `DESTROYED` batch accepts no label edit) → `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to release, rescind-release, and tracking-label update.
14. Noncompliance case state — `OPEN` for issuing stop-distribution, either `STOP_DISTRIBUTION_ISSUED` or `RETEST_FAILED` for ordering a retest, either `RETEST_PASSED` or `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED` for recording a corrective action, and `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED` for closure → `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to those four case operations.
15. Component part certificate state — `reliance_state` is `SUBMITTED` when reliance documentation is recorded; a row already in `RELIED_UPON`, `REJECTED` or `RELIANCE_VOID` is refused → `ERR_PART_CERTIFICATE_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to recording reliance documentation.
16. Product stale-snapshot guard — the submitted `expected_product_version` equals the product's current `version` → `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (412). Applies to drafting a children's product certificate.
17. Case stale-snapshot guard — the submitted `expected_case_version` equals the case's current `version` → `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (412). Applies to recording a corrective action plan.
18. Laboratory not barred → `ERR_LAB_BARRED` (422). Applies to test order booking, taking an order into testing, and component part certificate submission.
19. Undue-influence attestation current and unexpired → `ERR_UNDUE_INFLUENCE_ATTESTATION_MISSING` (422). Applies to test order booking and taking an order into testing.
20. Retest independence — a `MANUFACTURER_OWNED` laboratory may not receive a retest order → `ERR_MANUFACTURER_OWNED_LAB_FORBIDDEN` (422). Applies to ordering a retest round.
21. Method accreditation — exactly one `ACCREDITED` accreditation row covers the laboratory, the rule's test method and the logical clock → `ERR_LAB_METHOD_NOT_ACCREDITED` (422). Applies to test order booking and component part certificate submission.
22. Reliance agreement on file — when the reliance decision is to rely, the part's supplier has `reliance_agreement_state` `ON_FILE` → `ERR_RELIANCE_AGREEMENT_MISSING` (422). Applies to recording reliance documentation.
23. Certificate content complete — `content_completeness_pct` equals 100, meaning all seven required-content slots are satisfied → `ERR_CERTIFICATE_CONTENT_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to certificate issuance.
24. Product posture cleared — the owning product's `compliance_posture` is `CLEARED` → `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_NOT_CLEARED` (422). Applies to certificate issuance and to batch release.
25. Tracking label compliant — the batch's `tracking_label_compliant` is true → `ERR_TRACKING_LABEL_NONCOMPLIANT` (422). Applies to batch release and to case closure, where every stopped batch of the product must be label-compliant before it may be reinstated.
26. Segregation of duties — at issuance, `X-User-ID` differs from `ordered_by_user_id` on every test order counted as evidence for the certificate's cited rules; at case closure, `X-User-ID` differs from the case's `opened_by_user_id` → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to certificate issuance and case closure.
27. Interval well-formedness — `window_end` is strictly after `window_start` and `window_start` is at or after the logical clock; `coverage_through` is strictly after `coverage_from`; `valid_through` is strictly after `valid_from` → `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (422). Applies to test order booking, component part certificate submission, and accreditation creation.
28. Laboratory slot capacity — peak concurrency of half-open testing windows at the laboratory does not exceed its `concurrent_slot_capacity` → `ERR_LAB_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to test order booking and to ordering a retest round.
29. Component coverage separation — no colliding closed coverage interval, buffer included, exists for the same part-and-rule pair → `ERR_COMPONENT_COVERAGE_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to component part certificate submission.
30. Periodic review due — on periodic review of an `ACTIVE` plan the logical clock is at or after `periodic_due_at`; an instant exactly equal to it satisfies the check → `ERR_PERIODIC_REVIEW_NOT_DUE` (409). Applies to periodic review.
31. Batch inside certificate coverage — `produced_on` is at or after the referenced certificate's `issued_at` when that certificate is `ISSUED`, or at or after its `created_at` while it is still `DRAFT`; and `produced_on` falls inside the calendar month named by the certificate's `manufacture_date_month` → `ERR_BATCH_OUTSIDE_CERTIFICATE_COVERAGE` (422). Applies to production batch recording.
32. Rule applicability — the submitted `rule_id` is a member of the product's derived `required_rule_ids` → `ERR_RULE_NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_PRODUCT` (422). Applies to test order booking and to recording reliance documentation.
33. Component reliance permitted — when the reliance decision is to rely, the cited rule's `component_reliance_allowed` is true → `ERR_COMPONENT_RELIANCE_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Applies to recording reliance documentation.
34. Evidence path agreement — reliance may only be recorded while the product's `evidence_path` is `COMPONENT_RELIANCE`, and a non-retest product-level test order may not be booked for a rule already fully covered by relied-upon component certificates on a `COMPONENT_RELIANCE` product → `ERR_EVIDENCE_PATH_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to test order booking and to recording reliance documentation.
35. Mapping uniqueness — no `ACTIVE` mapping already exists for the submitted category-and-rule pair → `ERR_MAPPING_DUPLICATE` (409). Applies to mapping creation.
36. Material change subject coherence — when `subject_type` is `COMPONENT_PART`, `component_part_id` is supplied and that part belongs to the submitted `product_id`; when `subject_type` is `PRODUCT`, `component_part_id` is omitted or null → `ERR_PART_SUBJECT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to material change event recording.
37. Bill of materials non-empty — the product has at least one component part before it may be submitted for certification → `ERR_NO_COMPONENT_PARTS` (422). Applies to submission for certification.
38. Derived rule set non-empty — rule derivation returns at least one rule → `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_RULES` (422). Applies to submission for certification.
39. Sample units available — the requested `sample_count` does not exceed the plan's `units_available` → `ERR_SAMPLE_UNITS_EXHAUSTED` (422). Applies to test order booking and to ordering a retest round.
40. Sampling floor met — on activation, `units_authorized` is at least the product category's `sampling_floor_units` → `ERR_SAMPLING_FLOOR_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to plan activation.
41. Batch code uniqueness — the submitted `batch_code` is unique among the product's batches → `ERR_BATCH_CODE_DUPLICATE` (409). Applies to production batch recording.
42. Testing budget — the sum of `invoice_total_cents` over the product's orders in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TESTING`, `REPORTED_PASS` or `REPORTED_FAIL`, plus the candidate order's own `invoice_total_cents`, does not exceed the product's `testing_budget_cents` → `ERR_PRODUCT_TESTING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (402). Applies to test order booking and to ordering a retest round. This is the last check in the entire ordering, so a budget refusal always means every other rule passed.
43. Accreditation state — the accreditation being suspended is in `ACCREDITED`; both `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal, so a second suspension of the same row is refused → `ERR_ACCREDITATION_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to accreditation suspension only, and sits after the fiscal check because it guards a single operation that no fiscal rule touches.

Note the practical consequences of this ordering on the two operations that carry the most rules. When booking a test order, laboratory integrity (barred status, then attestation, then method accreditation) is decided **before** window well-formedness, slot capacity, rule applicability, evidence-path agreement, unit availability and budget — so a barred laboratory surfaces as `ERR_LAB_BARRED` even if the window is also malformed and the budget is also exhausted. When issuing a certificate, product state comes first, then the certificate's own axis state, then content completeness, then product posture, and only then segregation of duties — so an incomplete certificate reports `ERR_CERTIFICATE_CONTENT_INCOMPLETE` rather than the self-issuance conflict that would also have applied.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Regulatory Reference Library

Nothing can be certified until the regulatory library exists, and only a `ComplianceAdministrator` may populate it. A **regulatory rule** is registered with a `rule_code` — an uppercase snake-case identifier of between 3 and 40 characters that must be unique across all rules — and a `hazard_class` drawn from `LEAD_PAINT`, `LEAD_CONTENT`, `PHTHALATES`, `MECHANICAL_HAZARD` or `FLAMMABILITY`. Each rule names exactly one `test_method_code`, an uppercase alphanumeric string that may contain dashes, again between 3 and 40 characters; this is the single method a laboratory must hold accreditation for in order to run the rule, and it is the join key that makes accreditation meaningful rather than decorative. The rule carries its numeric ceiling or floor as `limit_value`, which must be at least 0, expressed in a `limit_unit` of `PPM`, `PERCENT_BY_WEIGHT`, `NEWTON` or `MILLIMETRE`, together with a `limit_comparator` of either `AT_OR_BELOW` or `AT_OR_ABOVE` that defaults to `AT_OR_BELOW` when the registrant omits it.

Three further attributes on a rule drive downstream behaviour and are easy to overlook. `periodic_interval_months` — an integer of at least 1 and at most 60 — states how often the rule demands re-verification; the strictest such interval across a product's rules is what sets that product's periodic clock, so a rule demanding quarterly verification carries the value 3 and will dominate a rule demanding annual verification. `base_method_fee_cents` is the laboratory's base charge, in whole cents, for one sample under this method, and must be at least 0. `component_reliance_allowed` — a boolean defaulting to true — declares whether a supplier's component part certificate may stand in for finished-product testing of this rule; a rule that must be tested on the assembled product sets it false. Finally, `severity_weight`, an integer between 1 and 10 inclusive, is the weight this rule contributes when the system measures how far a product's evidence falls short.

**Product categories** describe the juvenile-product families the rules attach to. A category carries a unique uppercase snake-case `category_code` of 3 to 40 characters, a human-readable `display_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `intended_age_max_months` between 0 and 168 inclusive marking the top of the intended-user age range, and a `sampling_floor_units` of at least 1 — the absolute minimum number of sample units any sampling plan for a product in this category may authorise. The boolean `durable_infant_product`, defaulting to false, marks cots, high chairs, carriers and similar durable infant or toddler goods; setting it true doubles computed sample sizes, as §3.5 describes.

Rules reach products through a many-to-many junction. A **category-rule mapping** names a `category_id` and a `rule_id`, an `effective_from` instant from which the mapping participates in rule identification with the boundary treated as inclusive, and an `applicability_condition` — the predicate the system evaluates against a product's actual bill of materials to decide whether the rule is genuinely required. The five conditions are `ALWAYS`, which always applies; `IF_PAINTED_SURFACE`, which applies when the product has at least one part of material class `PAINTED_COATING`; `IF_PLASTICIZED_PART`, which applies when at least one part is `PLASTICIZED_PLASTIC`; `IF_SMALL_PART_PRESENT`, which applies when at least one part fits the small-parts cylinder when detached; and `IF_ACCESSIBLE_METAL`, which applies when at least one part is of material class `ACCESSIBLE_METAL` **and** is also marked accessible to a child. Note the asymmetry: only the metal condition compounds accessibility with material class.

Each mapping tracks its own `mapping_state`, which is `ACTIVE` on creation and may move to `RETIRED`. Attempting to create a second `ACTIVE` mapping for a category-and-rule pair that already has one is refused with `ERR_MAPPING_DUPLICATE`. Retiring a mapping moves it from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED`, increments the mapping's `version` by 1, and re-derives the required rule set for every product of that category whose `certification_state` is still `DRAFT` — and for no other product. Products that have already been scoped keep the rule snapshot they were scoped against, so a mid-programme retirement never silently narrows an in-flight certification. Retirement also frees the category-and-rule pair, so a fresh `ACTIVE` mapping for the same pair may be created afterwards. Rules may be listed with an optional `hazard_class` filter, and mappings may be listed filtered by `category_id`, `rule_id` or `mapping_state`.

### 3.2. The Party Register: Laboratories, Accreditation Scopes and Suppliers

Registering a **laboratory** records its `lab_name` of 1 to 160 characters, the `accreditation_body_code` of the body that assessed it as an uppercase alphanumeric string of 2 to 20 characters, its `independence_class` — one of `INDEPENDENT`, `FIREWALLED_GOVERNMENT` or `MANUFACTURER_OWNED` — and its `concurrent_slot_capacity`, an integer between 1 and 10 inclusive giving how many of its scheduled or in-testing orders may have overlapping testing windows at any single instant. A newly registered laboratory begins with `operational_state` `ACTIVE`, `influence_attestation_state` `MISSING`, a null `influence_attestation_valid_through`, and both fee ledger counters — `billed_fee_cents_total` and `credited_fee_cents_total` — at 0.

Before any work may be assigned, an administrator records the laboratory's standing undue-influence prevention attestation by supplying an `attestation_valid_through` instant. Doing so sets `influence_attestation_state` to `CURRENT`, writes the supplied instant into `influence_attestation_valid_through`, and increments the laboratory's `version` by 1. The same administrator may bar or reinstate a laboratory by submitting a new `operational_state` value, which is stored directly and likewise increments `version` by 1. Both operations are recorded against the laboratory itself and neither touches any order.

**Accreditation scopes** are the finest-grained integrity artefact in the system. Each scope names the `rule_id` whose method it covers, and at creation the system copies that rule's `test_method_code` onto the accreditation record's own `test_method_code` — the scope key every later lookup uses. The scope carries a closed coverage interval from `valid_from` through `valid_through`, both endpoints inclusive, and `valid_through` must be strictly after `valid_from` or the request is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. An optional `fee_surcharge_basis_points`, an integer between 0 and 5000 inclusive that defaults to 0, records a premium this laboratory charges when testing under this scope. A new scope begins in `accreditation_state` `ACCREDITED` with a null `suspension_reason`. Scopes may be listed filtered by `laboratory_id`, `rule_id` and `accreditation_state` — this list is the verification channel a coordinator uses to answer "is this laboratory accredited for this method right now".

Suspending a scope is the head of the accreditation suspension cascade and requires a `suspension_reason`. Ordinarily the scope moves `ACCREDITED → SUSPENDED` with the submitted reason stored. There is one variant: when the submitted reason is the literal `COVERAGE_ELAPSED` and the logical clock is already strictly past the scope's `valid_through`, the scope moves `ACCREDITED → EXPIRED` instead. Expiry is recorded through the same operator action precisely so that it remains reachable from an empty database with nothing but the logical clock to move. Both `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal, and a second suspension attempt against a row already in either is refused with `ERR_ACCREDITATION_STATE_INVALID`.

The cascade that follows a suspension or expiry has four consequences, all of them mandatory. First, the accreditation's own `version` increments by 1. Second, every test order at the same laboratory whose rule shares the suspended `test_method_code` and whose `order_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_TESTING` moves to `INVALIDATED`. Third, each invalidated order returns its `sample_count` from its plan's `units_committed` to that plan's `units_available` and, where the order had already reached `IN_TESTING`, its `invoice_total_cents` is added to the laboratory's `credited_fee_cents_total`; each affected plan and the laboratory each have `version` incremented by 1. Fourth, each invalidated order's sampling plan, if it is `ACTIVE`, moves `ACTIVE → RESAMPLE_DUE` with its `resample_trigger` set to `RELIANCE_VOID`, which in turn recomputes the owning product's risk-weighted gap score and pulls its `compliance_posture` down to `RESTRICTED`.

**Suppliers** complete the register. A supplier is recorded with a `legal_name` of 1 to 160 characters and a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the ISO country of its manufacturing site. Its `reliance_agreement_state` may be supplied at registration and otherwise defaults to `NOT_ON_FILE`; only the value `ON_FILE` — meaning a signed component-part reliance agreement is held — permits the certifier to lean on that supplier's certificates. The derived `certificates_issued_count` starts at 0 and tracks how many of the supplier's component part certificates are in `SUBMITTED` or `RELIED_UPON`: it rises by 1 when the supplier submits a certificate, and falls by 1 when a certificate is rejected or when a previously relied-upon certificate is voided.

### 3.3. Product Registration, the Bill of Materials and Rule Identification

A children's product enters the system in `DRAFT`. Registration records the `category_id` that will drive rule identification, a `model_name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `manufacture_site_code` of 2 to 12 uppercase alphanumeric characters that will later be printed inside the tracking label, and `projected_annual_units` of at least 1 — the manufacturer's forward-looking volume forecast, which is the sole volume input to initial sample sizing. An optional `testing_budget_cents` sets the ceiling on how much outstanding laboratory invoicing this product may carry across its non-terminal orders; when omitted it defaults to 2,000,000 cents, which is to say twenty thousand dollars, and it must be at least 0. At registration the product's `certification_state` is `DRAFT`, its `compliance_posture` is `UNSCOPED`, and its `required_rule_ids` is the empty array — rule derivation deliberately does not run at registration, only at scoping.

The **bill of materials** is built one component part at a time, each part naming the `supplier_id` that manufactures it, a `part_name` of 1 to 120 characters as it appears in the bill of materials, and a `material_class` of `PAINTED_COATING`, `PLASTICIZED_PLASTIC`, `ACCESSIBLE_METAL`, `TEXTILE` or `RIGID_UNPAINTED_PLASTIC`. Two booleans complete the part: `accessible_to_child`, defaulting to true, states whether a child can reach the part during reasonably foreseeable use, and `small_part_when_detached`, defaulting to false, states whether the part fits the small-parts cylinder once detached. A new part's `reliance_status` is `PENDING` and its `last_material_change_at` is null. Adding a part increments the owning product's `version` by 1, because the bill of materials is part of the product's compliance identity. Parts may be listed filtered by `product_id`, `supplier_id` and `reliance_status`.

**Rule identification** is the derivation at the heart of the domain, and it runs at two moments: when the product is submitted for certification, and again on every component part addition while the product's `certification_state` is still `DRAFT` or `SCOPED`. It proceeds as follows. First, gather the candidate mappings: every category-rule mapping whose `category_id` equals the product's `category_id`, whose `mapping_state` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock — the lower bound is inclusive, so a mapping effective exactly now counts. Second, gather the parts: every component part of the product, with no filtering on `reliance_status` whatsoever, so a superseded part still contributes its material class to the predicates. Third, walk the candidate mappings in a fixed order — descending `severity_weight` of the mapped rule first, and among rules of equal weight ascending `rule_code`; because `rule_code` is unique, this ordering never ties and no identifier is ever consulted as a fallback. For each mapping in that order, evaluate its `applicability_condition` against the gathered parts using the five predicates of §3.1, and when the condition holds and the rule is not already in the result, append the rule to the result. The finished ordered list becomes the product's `required_rule_ids`. An `ACTIVE` mapping whose condition no part satisfies contributes nothing, which means a product can legitimately end up with an empty derived set even though its category has mappings.

Submitting a product for certification is the fork point of the product lifecycle, performed by a `TestCoordinator`, who supplies the `evidence_path` the programme will follow — either `COMPONENT_RELIANCE` or `FULL_PRODUCT_TEST`. Three preconditions must hold: the product's `certification_state` is `DRAFT`, at least one component part exists for it, and rule derivation yields a non-empty rule set. An empty bill of materials is refused with `ERR_NO_COMPONENT_PARTS`; a bill of materials that produces no applicable rules is refused with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_RULES`; a product already past `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_STATE_INVALID`. On success, the submitted value is written to `evidence_path`, `required_rule_ids` is rewritten from the fresh derivation, the risk-weighted gap score and compliance posture are recomputed, the product advances `DRAFT → SCOPED`, and `version` increments by 1. Because a freshly scoped product has no evidence at all, its gap score is non-zero and its posture moves from `UNSCOPED` to `RESTRICTED`.

The **risk-weighted gap score** is an integer from 0 to 100 that answers "how much of what this product must prove is still unproven", and it is recomputed on product scoping, on every test order report filing, on every reliance recording or voiding of a component certificate, on every opening or closing of a noncompliance case, and on every sampling plan movement into or out of resampling. It is built as follows. Let the total weight be the sum of `severity_weight` across every rule in `required_rule_ids`. A rule counts as covered according to the product's `evidence_path`: on `FULL_PRODUCT_TEST`, a rule is covered when a test order exists for this product and that rule whose `order_state` is `REPORTED_PASS`; on `COMPONENT_RELIANCE`, a rule is covered when a component part certificate exists for that rule in `reliance_state` `RELIED_UPON` attached to a part of this product. Sum the `severity_weight` of the covered rules to get the covered weight; the uncovered weight is the total weight minus the covered weight. The base figure is 100 whenever the total weight is 0, and otherwise 100 multiplied by the uncovered weight, divided by the total weight, rounded half up to a whole number. Two penalties are then added: 15 points for each noncompliance case on this product whose `case_state` is `OPEN`, `STOP_DISTRIBUTION_ISSUED`, `RETEST_ORDERED` or `RETEST_FAILED`, and 10 points for each sampling plan on this product whose `plan_state` is `RESAMPLE_DUE`. The final `risk_weighted_gap_score` is the base plus both penalties, clamped once into the range 0 through 100 — the clamp is applied after the penalties, never before. A score of 0 means every required rule is covered by current evidence and no penalty applies.

The **compliance posture** is recomputed immediately after every gap score evaluation, and also on product scoping and on archival. It is a five-branch decision in which the first matching branch wins, and the branch order is normative. A product whose `certification_state` is `DRAFT` or `ARCHIVED` is `UNSCOPED`. Otherwise, a product with at least one noncompliance case in `OPEN`, `STOP_DISTRIBUTION_ISSUED`, `RETEST_FAILED` or `ESCALATED_RECALL` is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, a product with at least one sampling plan in `LAPSED` is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, a product whose `risk_weighted_gap_score` is exactly 0 is `CLEARED`. Otherwise the product is `RESTRICTED`. The ordering matters in a way implementers routinely get wrong: a product holding a lapsed plan **and** a zero gap score is `SUSPENDED`, not `CLEARED`, because the lapsed-plan branch is evaluated first. Note also which case states are excluded from the posture's suspension branch — a case sitting in `RETEST_ORDERED`, `RETEST_PASSED`, `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED` or `CLOSED` does not by itself suspend the product, even though `RETEST_ORDERED` does still contribute a 15-point penalty to the gap score.

An administrator may **archive** a certified product. Archival requires `certification_state` `CERTIFIED` and requires that no production batch of the product sits in `RELEASED` or `STOP_DISTRIBUTION` — goods in the field or under a stop order cannot be walked away from. On success the product moves `CERTIFIED → ARCHIVED`, its `compliance_posture` becomes `UNSCOPED`, every sampling plan of the product whose `plan_state` is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `RESAMPLE_DUE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the product's `version` increments by 1. `ARCHIVED` is terminal, and every subsequent write against the product or its children raises `ERR_PRODUCT_ARCHIVED`.

Alongside the plain product read, a computed **compliance summary** read recomputes rule derivation, the gap score, the posture and both unit ledgers on demand without mutating anything. It reports `product_id`, the derived `required_rule_ids`, the split of those rules into `covered_rule_ids` and `uncovered_rule_ids`, the recomputed `risk_weighted_gap_score` and `compliance_posture`, the `open_case_count` and `resample_due_plan_count` that fed the penalties, the four distribution counters `units_held`, `units_released`, `units_stopped` and `units_destroyed`, the `units_total_produced` they must sum to, and a `ledger_balanced` boolean stating whether they do. Products may be listed filtered by `category_id`, `certification_state` and `compliance_posture`.

### 3.4. The Two Evidence Paths: Component Reliance versus Full Product Testing

Immediately after a product reaches `SCOPED`, the system itself moves it onto one of two mutually exclusive branches, chosen by the `evidence_path` the coordinator declared. Both moves are automatic — no separate operator action drives them — and each increments the product's `version` by 1 in its own right, so a submission that scopes and then forks advances `version` twice.

On the **component reliance branch**, taken when `evidence_path` is `COMPONENT_RELIANCE`, the product advances `SCOPED → EVIDENCE_RELIANCE`. This branch has a precondition of its own: every rule in the product's `required_rule_ids` must have `component_reliance_allowed` set to true, because a product cannot lean entirely on suppliers if even one of its rules demands finished-product testing. The branch's distinguishing side effect is that every component part of the product whose `reliance_status` is `PENDING` becomes `VALID`, making the parts eligible to carry reliance; no laboratory fee is billed anywhere on this branch, since the evidence arrives from the suppliers. A part added to the bill of materials after this point starts at `PENDING` again and reaches `VALID` when every rule in the product's `required_rule_ids` that applies to that part has a relied-upon certificate on it.

On the **full product testing branch**, taken when `evidence_path` is `FULL_PRODUCT_TEST`, the product advances `SCOPED → FULL_TEST_PROGRAM`. Its distinguishing side effect is precisely the opposite: every component part is left at `reliance_status` `PENDING`, so no component certificate can ever be counted as evidence for this product and finished-product test orders are the only admissible evidence. This branch imposes no `component_reliance_allowed` precondition, because it never relies on a component certificate in the first place.

**Component part certificates** are what a supplier submits, and only a `SupplierUser` acting for the owning supplier may submit one. The submission names the `laboratory_id` of the third-party laboratory that produced the underlying data, the `rule_id` the part is certified against, the `measured_value` the supplier attests to expressed in that rule's `limit_unit` and at least 0, and a closed coverage interval running from `coverage_from` through `coverage_through` with both endpoints inclusive. `coverage_through` must be strictly after `coverage_from`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. On acceptance the system copies the part's `supplier_id` onto the certificate, sets `reliance_state` to `SUBMITTED`, leaves `reliance_documentation_ref` and `reliance_recorded_by_user_id` null, and increments the supplier's `certificates_issued_count` by 1. The named laboratory must not be barred and must hold a current accreditation for the rule's test method, as §2.10 requires.

Coverage intervals for the same part-and-rule pair are kept strictly apart, with a mandatory quarantine buffer of one week — seven days — between consecutive intervals. The check considers only existing certificates for the same `component_part_id` and the same `rule_id` whose `reliance_state` is `SUBMITTED` or `RELIED_UPON`; rows in `REJECTED` or `RELIANCE_VOID` free their interval and are ignored. A candidate collides with such an existing certificate when the candidate's `coverage_from` is at or before the existing `coverage_through` extended forward by seven days, **and** the existing `coverage_from` pulled back by seven days is at or before the candidate's `coverage_through`. Capacity here is exactly one — there is no aggregate allowance, adjacency is forbidden, and any gap shorter than the buffer is a collision, so a candidate beginning exactly at an existing interval's `coverage_through` collides. Any collision is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`. Certificates may be listed filtered by `component_part_id`, `supplier_id`, `laboratory_id`, `rule_id` and `reliance_state`.

The reliance decision belongs to the `CertificationOfficer`, who records a `reliance_decision` of either `RELY` or `REJECT` together with a `reliance_documentation_ref` identifying the reliance documentation package. In both branches the certificate must currently be in `reliance_state` `SUBMITTED`, and in both branches the submitted `reliance_documentation_ref` and the acting `X-User-ID` as `reliance_recorded_by_user_id` are stored on the certificate and the certificate's `version` increments by 1. Beyond that the two branches diverge sharply, and each branch's full set of consequences is spelled out below.

Choosing `RELY` moves the certificate `SUBMITTED → RELIED_UPON`, and requires five things beyond the state check: the owning supplier's `reliance_agreement_state` is `ON_FILE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RELIANCE_AGREEMENT_MISSING`; the component part's `reliance_status` is `VALID`; the cited rule's `component_reliance_allowed` is true, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_RELIANCE_NOT_ALLOWED`; the certificate's `rule_id` is a member of the product's `required_rule_ids`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RULE_NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_PRODUCT`; and the product's `evidence_path` is `COMPONENT_RELIANCE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_PATH_MISMATCH`. Its consequences are: the supplier's `certificates_issued_count` is left exactly as it is, because a relied-upon row was already inside the counted set; the parent part moves `PENDING → VALID` once every rule in `required_rule_ids` that applies to that part holds a relied-upon certificate; the product's risk-weighted gap score and compliance posture are recomputed; and when the recomputed gap score is 0 while the product sits in `EVIDENCE_RELIANCE`, the product advances `EVIDENCE_RELIANCE → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` with `compliance_posture` set to `CLEARED`. Both the certificate's `version` and the product's `version` increment by 1.

Choosing `REJECT` moves the certificate `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. Its consequences are the mirror image: the supplier's `certificates_issued_count` is decremented by 1, because a rejected row leaves the counted set, and the supplier's `version` increments by 1; the component part is left at `reliance_status` `PENDING`; and the product's gap score, posture, `certification_state` and `version` are all left untouched, because a rejection adds no evidence. `REJECTED` is terminal — a rejected certificate cannot be reconsidered, and a second decision against it is refused with `ERR_PART_CERTIFICATE_STATE_INVALID`.

A third, system-driven edge exists on this machine. A relied-upon certificate moves `RELIED_UPON → RELIANCE_VOID` when its component part is superseded by a material change, as §3.10 describes; that edge decrements the supplier's `certificates_issued_count` by 1, increments both the certificate's and the supplier's `version` by 1, and pushes the product's active sampling plans into resampling. `RELIANCE_VOID` is terminal.

### 3.5. Sampling Plans and the Periodic Verification Cycle

A sampling plan is the coordinator's authorisation to consume physical units for testing, and every plan declares a `plan_type` of either `INITIAL` or `PERIODIC`. An initial plan covers a certification that has not yet produced anything, so its volume input is the product's forward-looking `projected_annual_units`. A periodic plan trues up against production that has already happened, so its volume input is `baseline_units_produced` — the units actually made during the trailing interval, supplied on the request, defaulting to 0, required to be at least 0, and expected whenever `plan_type` is `PERIODIC`. The plan also carries `resample_grace_days`, an integer between 0 and 365 inclusive that defaults to 30, giving how long an unserviced resampling obligation may sit before the plan lapses.

**Sample sizing** runs at plan creation and again on every entry into resampling, and its two variants differ in three coordinated ways. Begin by taking the rule count — the number of entries in the product's `required_rule_ids`. For the `INITIAL` variant, divide the product's `projected_annual_units` by 2,500 and round the quotient **up** to the next whole number to obtain the volume component, then add a base of 3 and multiply the sum by the rule count. For the `PERIODIC` variant, divide the plan's `baseline_units_produced` by 5,000 and round the quotient **down** to the preceding whole number to obtain the volume component, then add a base of 1 and multiply the sum by the rule count. Because the periodic variant uses a divisor twice as large, a base a third the size, and rounds down where the initial variant rounds up, identical volume figures always yield a strictly smaller periodic authorisation than initial authorisation. Next, if the product's category is flagged `durable_infant_product`, double the running figure. Finally, take the greater of that figure and the category's `sampling_floor_units`, then take the lesser of that result and the hard ceiling of 40 sample units; the outcome is written to `units_authorized`. Every step is integer arithmetic and no rounding occurs beyond the single ceiling or floor on the volume component. At creation, `units_committed` is 0 and `units_available` equals `units_authorized`; whenever `units_authorized` is recomputed, `units_available` is refreshed as `units_authorized` minus `units_committed`. A new plan begins in `plan_state` `DRAFT` with `resample_trigger` `NONE`, with `activated_at`, `last_verified_at` and `periodic_due_at` all null.

Activating a plan requires two things beyond the plan being in an activatable state: the owning product's `certification_state` must be one of `SCOPED`, `EVIDENCE_RELIANCE`, `FULL_TEST_PROGRAM`, `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, `CERTIFIED` or `REMEDIATION`, and the plan's `units_authorized` must be at least the product category's `sampling_floor_units` — a plan sized below the category floor is refused with `ERR_SAMPLING_FLOOR_NOT_MET`. Activation is accepted from `DRAFT`, moving the plan `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, and from `RESAMPLE_DUE`, moving it `RESAMPLE_DUE → ACTIVE` after resampling has been arranged; any other `plan_state` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Because rank 9 of the refusal ordering in section 2.11 does not extend to plan activation, this operation decides the plan's own `plan_state` (rank 11) immediately after the archival guard and **before** the owning product's `certification_state` requirement, which in turn is decided before the sampling floor: activating a `SUPERSEDED` plan on a product still in `DRAFT` reports `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. The operations rank 9 does cover — submission for certification, archival, test order booking and certificate issuance — are unaffected and still decide the product's `certification_state` in its rank-9 position, ahead of any plan or certificate axis state. Activation stamps both `activated_at` and `last_verified_at` with the logical clock instant, recomputes `periodic_due_at`, resets `resample_trigger` to `NONE` on the resampling re-entry edge, and increments the plan's `version` by 1. It also supersedes competition: any other plan on the same product with the same `plan_type` whose `plan_state` is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `RESAMPLE_DUE` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its own `version` incremented by 1. Superseding cancels no orders — existing test orders keep their committed units recorded against the superseded plan, so the unit ledger of the superseded plan remains internally consistent.

The **periodic due instant** is derived from the strictest interval the product's rules demand. Take every rule in the product's `required_rule_ids` and find the smallest `periodic_interval_months` among them; when the required set is empty, `periodic_due_at` is null. Otherwise, add that number of calendar months to the plan's `last_verified_at`, clamping the day of month to the last day of the target month where the source day does not exist there, and preserving the time of day exactly. All arithmetic is calendar-month arithmetic on the UTC instant — no interval is ever expressed in days. The baseline `last_verified_at` is the plan's `activated_at` until the plan's first passing test order resets it, and every subsequent passing order under the plan resets it again and recomputes `periodic_due_at`.

Running a **periodic review** on an `ACTIVE` plan requires that the logical clock be at or after `periodic_due_at`; an instant exactly equal to it is due, and an instant strictly before it is refused with `ERR_PERIODIC_REVIEW_NOT_DUE`. When due, the plan moves `ACTIVE → RESAMPLE_DUE`, its `resample_trigger` is set to `PERIODIC_INTERVAL`, its `units_authorized` is recomputed by the sizing rule with `units_available` refreshed accordingly, the owning product's `compliance_posture` is set to `RESTRICTED`, and both the plan's and the product's `version` increment by 1. Running the same review against a plan already in `RESAMPLE_DUE` is how a lapse is recognised: once the logical clock is strictly past `periodic_due_at` plus the plan's `resample_grace_days` days, the plan moves `RESAMPLE_DUE → LAPSED` and the owning product's `compliance_posture` is set to `SUSPENDED`, with both versions incremented by 1. `LAPSED` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal.

The cycle closes automatically. When a test order booked under a `RESAMPLE_DUE` plan reaches `REPORTED_PASS`, the plan moves `RESAMPLE_DUE → ACTIVE`, its `resample_trigger` returns to `NONE`, `last_verified_at` is stamped with the logical clock and `periodic_due_at` recomputed, and its `version` increments by 1. A plan may traverse `ACTIVE → RESAMPLE_DUE → ACTIVE` any number of times over its life. Two other triggers put a plan into resampling without a periodic deadline being reached: a voided reliance sets `resample_trigger` to `RELIANCE_VOID`, and a material change against the product sets it to `MATERIAL_CHANGE`. Plans may be listed filtered by `product_id`, `plan_type` and `plan_state`.

### 3.6. Booking and Executing Third-Party Testing

Booking a test order is the single most rule-dense operation in the system. A `TestCoordinator` names the `product_id` under test, the `sampling_plan_id` whose authorised units the order will consume, the `laboratory_id` that will perform the work, the `rule_id` under test, a `sample_count` of at least 1, and a testing window running from `window_start` to `window_end`. The window is **half-open**: it includes `window_start` and excludes `window_end`. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start` and `window_start` must be at or after the logical clock — a window that ends at or before it starts, or that begins in the past, is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. An optional `expedite_requested` boolean, defaulting to false, asks for expedited turnaround and feeds the pricing waterfall. On creation the order's `order_state` is `SCHEDULED`, its `ordered_by_user_id` is the acting `X-User-ID`, `is_retest` is false and `retest_case_id` is null; `measured_value`, `result`, `reported_at` and `reported_by_user_id` are all null. The order's `rule_id` must be a member of the product's `required_rule_ids`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_RULE_NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_PRODUCT`.

Evidence sources must not be mixed carelessly. A non-retest product-level order may not be booked for a rule that is **already** fully covered by relied-upon component certificates on a product whose `evidence_path` is `COMPONENT_RELIANCE`, because that rule's evidence already comes from the supplier; such a booking is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_PATH_MISMATCH`. The same product may still order product-level testing for a rule its suppliers have not covered, and retest orders created through the noncompliance workflow are exempt from this restriction entirely.

**Laboratory slot capacity** is aggregate, not per-order. Consider only orders at the same laboratory whose `order_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_TESTING`; orders in `CANCELLED`, `INVALIDATED`, `REPORTED_PASS` or `REPORTED_FAIL` have released their window and are excluded. Form the set of candidate breakpoints: the candidate order's own `window_start`, together with the `window_start` of every such existing order that falls at or after the candidate's `window_start` and strictly before the candidate's `window_end`. Sort those breakpoints ascending. At each breakpoint, the concurrency is 1 — the candidate always contributes exactly one — plus the number of existing orders whose `window_start` is at or before that instant and whose `window_end` is strictly after it. Peak concurrency is the largest such count across all breakpoints. Only when peak concurrency **exceeds** the laboratory's `concurrent_slot_capacity` is the booking refused, with `ERR_LAB_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Adjacency is therefore permitted: an existing order ending exactly when the candidate begins does not overlap it, because the existing window excludes its own end instant.

Pricing runs in two stages, the line fee first and the invoice total second. The **line fee** starts from the accreditation that authorises the work: the single accreditation row for this laboratory and this rule's test method that is in `ACCREDITED` and whose closed coverage interval contains the logical clock — the accreditation gate of §2.10 guarantees exactly one such row exists. Take the rule's `base_method_fee_cents`, multiply it by one plus the accreditation's `fee_surcharge_basis_points` divided by 10000, then multiply by the laboratory's independence multiplier: 1.00 for an `INDEPENDENT` laboratory, 1.05 for a `FIREWALLED_GOVERNMENT` laboratory, and 1.20 for a `MANUFACTURER_OWNED` laboratory. Round that per-sample amount half up to whole cents **at this point and only at this point**, then multiply the rounded per-sample amount by the order's `sample_count` as exact integer arithmetic. The result is written to `line_fee_cents`.

The **invoice total** then runs a five-step waterfall, exactly once each and strictly in this order. Step one takes the subtotal as the order's `line_fee_cents`. Step two determines the expedite rate: it is zero basis points when `expedite_requested` is false; otherwise it is 1500 basis points when the subtotal is at or below 50,000 cents, 1000 basis points when the subtotal is at or below 150,000 cents, and 600 basis points for any larger subtotal — the rate falls as the order grows, because expedited handling of a large job costs proportionally less. The expedite fee is the subtotal multiplied by that rate divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Step three adds the expedite fee to the subtotal to give the total before discount. Step four determines the reliance discount, which exists only for products on the component reliance path: the rate is zero basis points whenever the product's `evidence_path` is anything other than `COMPONENT_RELIANCE`; otherwise it is 1200 basis points when the total before discount is at or above 120,000 cents, 800 basis points when the total before discount is at or above 60,000 cents, and zero basis points below that. The discount is the total before discount multiplied by that rate divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Step five subtracts the discount from the total before discount, and the result is written to `invoice_total_cents`. Note that the discount tier is keyed on a figure that already contains the expedite fee; the evaluation order above is what makes the value well defined despite the apparent circularity.

Two ledger movements complete the booking. The order's `sample_count` is added to the plan's `units_committed` and subtracted from its `units_available`; a `sample_count` exceeding the plan's current `units_available` is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_UNITS_EXHAUSTED`. And the order's `invoice_total_cents` is added to the laboratory's `billed_fee_cents_total` at creation — every order is billed the moment it is booked, which is why a `SCHEDULED` order already counts toward the laboratory's outstanding balance. Before either movement is applied, the fiscal ceiling is checked: the sum of `invoice_total_cents` over the product's orders in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TESTING`, `REPORTED_PASS` or `REPORTED_FAIL`, plus the candidate's own `invoice_total_cents`, must not exceed the product's `testing_budget_cents`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_TESTING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

When the assigned laboratory takes the order up, the order advances `SCHEDULED → IN_TESTING` and its `version` increments by 1. This is a re-verification point, not a formality: the laboratory's `operational_state` must still be `ACTIVE`, its `influence_attestation_state` must still be `CURRENT`, an `ACCREDITED` accreditation for the rule's test method whose coverage interval contains the logical clock must still exist, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `laboratory_id`. A laboratory barred, un-attested or de-accredited between booking and start-of-testing is stopped here. No further billing occurs at this point, since the fee was taken at booking.

Filing the report is where the machine branches. The laboratory submits a single `measured_value` — one representative measurement per order, with no aggregation across samples — and the system stores it together with `reported_at` from the logical clock and `reported_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. The pass/fail decision compares the submitted value with the rule's `limit_value` according to the rule's `limit_comparator`: under `AT_OR_BELOW` the result is `PASS` when the measured value is less than or equal to the limit, and `FAIL` otherwise; under `AT_OR_ABOVE` the result is `PASS` when the measured value is greater than or equal to the limit, and `FAIL` otherwise. The comparison is exact, with no rounding and no tolerance, and a measured value exactly equal to `limit_value` passes under **both** comparators. The decision is written to `result`.

On a pass, the order advances `IN_TESTING → REPORTED_PASS`. Its plan's `last_verified_at` is stamped with the logical clock and `periodic_due_at` recomputed; if the plan was in `RESAMPLE_DUE` it returns to `ACTIVE` with `resample_trigger` reset to `NONE`. The product's `risk_weighted_gap_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed, and when the product sits in `FULL_TEST_PROGRAM` and the recomputed score is 0, the product advances `FULL_TEST_PROGRAM → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` with `compliance_posture` set to `CLEARED` — the join point at which both evidence branches converge, each having had its own branch-specific evidence kind counted. When the passing order was a retest, the servicing noncompliance case advances `RETEST_ORDERED → RETEST_PASSED` and its `active_retest_order_id` is cleared to null. The plan's `version` and the product's `version` each increment by 1, as does the order's own.

On a fail, the order advances `IN_TESTING → REPORTED_FAIL` and the four-hop stop-distribution cascade fires. Hop one: when `is_retest` is false, a noncompliance case is opened in `OPEN` with its `product_id`, `triggering_test_order_id` and `failed_rule_id` copied from this order and its `opened_by_user_id` set to the reporting `X-User-ID`; when `is_retest` is true, no new case is created and instead the servicing case advances `RETEST_ORDERED → RETEST_FAILED` with `active_retest_order_id` cleared to null. Hop two: the product moves `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` or `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → SUSPENDED`, whichever applies, provided the failing order's `rule_id` is a member of the product's `required_rule_ids`; its `compliance_posture` becomes `SUSPENDED` and its `risk_weighted_gap_score` is recomputed. Hop three: every children's product certificate of that product whose `issuance_status` is `ISSUED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Hop four: every production batch referencing a withdrawn certificate whose `distribution_state` is `RELEASED` moves `RELEASED → STOP_DISTRIBUTION`, moving its `quantity` out of the product's `units_released` and into `units_stopped`. Every entity each hop touches has its own `version` incremented by 1. Contrast this with the pass branch, which resets the periodic clock instead of opening anything — the two branches share only the storing of `measured_value`, `result`, `reported_at` and `reported_by_user_id`.

Cancelling an order is the compensating action and is available while the order is in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_TESTING`, moving it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` or `IN_TESTING → CANCELLED` respectively. Cancellation returns the order's `sample_count` from the plan's `units_committed` to its `units_available`, restoring the sample-unit invariant, and adds the order's `invoice_total_cents` to the laboratory's `credited_fee_cents_total`, restoring the fee invariant to exactly its pre-booking value. The plan's `version`, the laboratory's `version` and the order's own `version` each increment by 1. Orders may also reach `INVALIDATED` without operator action, through the accreditation suspension cascade of §3.2, moving `SCHEDULED → INVALIDATED` or `IN_TESTING → INVALIDATED` with the same unit return and fee credit. All four of `REPORTED_PASS`, `REPORTED_FAIL`, `CANCELLED` and `INVALIDATED` are terminal. Orders may be listed filtered by `product_id`, `sampling_plan_id`, `laboratory_id`, `rule_id`, `order_state` and `is_retest`.

### 3.7. The Children's Product Certificate: Content, Issuance and Accompaniment

A children's product certificate is the artefact regulators ask for, and it carries **two independent status dimensions on one row**. The first, `issuance_status`, tracks the legal issuance lifecycle through `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, and then one of `SUPERSEDED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `VOIDED`. The second, `accompaniment_status`, tracks whether the certificate travels with outbound shipments, moving through `NOT_FURNISHED`, `FURNISHED` and `RECALLED_FROM_SHIPMENTS`. The two axes advance independently: no movement on one constrains the other except where a precondition explicitly says so, and there is exactly one such coupling, named below.

A `CertificationOfficer` drafts a certificate against a product whose evidence is complete, supplying five required-content elements plus the stale-snapshot guard of §2.6. The content elements are `certifier_legal_name`, the legal name of the certifying party, 1 to 160 characters; `certifier_contact_email`, the recordkeeper's contact, 5 to 160 characters and required to contain an at-sign; `manufacture_place`, the place of manufacture, 1 to 120 characters; `manufacture_date_month`, the date of manufacture written as a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month between 01 and 12; and `testing_place`, the place where the supporting testing occurred, 1 to 120 characters. Drafting is permitted only while the product's `certification_state` is `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`; any other state is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_STATE_INVALID`.

The draft is created with `issuance_status` `DRAFT` and `accompaniment_status` `NOT_FURNISHED`, with `issued_by_user_id` and `issued_at` both null. Three derived fields are populated at that moment. `supersedes_certificate_id` is set to the `id` of the product's currently issued certificate, or null when the product has none — this is the self-referential link that makes certificate history traceable. `cited_rule_ids` is set to a snapshot of the product's `required_rule_ids`. `cited_evidence_count` is set to the number of distinct evidence rows backing those cited rules at that moment, counting passing test orders and relied-upon component part certificates together. The submitted `expected_product_version` is retained as `expected_product_version_seen`, and the product's own `version` increments by 1 because drafting a certificate against it is a compliance-relevant event.

**Content completeness** is then computed as a percentage over exactly seven required-content slots, and the certificate's `content_completeness_pct` is the count of satisfied slots multiplied by 100, divided by 7, with the result floored to a whole number. The seven slots are: `certifier_legal_name` is non-empty after trimming; `certifier_contact_email` contains an at-sign; `manufacture_place` is non-empty after trimming; `manufacture_date_month` matches the four-digit-year, hyphen, two-digit-month-between-01-and-12 pattern; `testing_place` is non-empty after trimming; `cited_rule_ids` is non-empty and equals the product's `required_rule_ids` when compared as sets; and `cited_evidence_count` is at least as large as the number of entries in `cited_rule_ids`. All seven satisfied yields exactly 100; six of seven yields 85. The figure is recomputed when issuance is attempted, so a rule set that has drifted since drafting — a new part having added a rule the snapshot does not cite — will be caught at issuance rather than sailing through.

Issuing a certificate moves it `DRAFT → ISSUED` on the issuance axis and requires five things: `issuance_status` is `DRAFT`; `content_completeness_pct` equals 100, or the request is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_CONTENT_INCOMPLETE`; the product's `compliance_posture` is `CLEARED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_NOT_CLEARED`; the product's `certification_state` is `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`; and the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `ordered_by_user_id` of every test order cited as evidence by this certificate, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success, `issued_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID` and `issued_at` to the logical clock instant; the predecessor named by `supersedes_certificate_id`, when non-null, moves `ISSUED → SUPERSEDED`; and the product advances `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → CERTIFIED` with `compliance_posture` `CLEARED`. Both the certificate's `version` and the product's `version` increment by 1, and the predecessor's own `version` increments by 1 as well. Issuance leaves `accompaniment_status` untouched at `NOT_FURNISHED` — the newly issued certificate does not automatically accompany anything.

When a certificate is superseded this way, one cross-axis consequence follows: if that predecessor's `accompaniment_status` was `FURNISHED` at that moment, it is set to `RECALLED_FROM_SHIPMENTS`, because an obsolete certificate must stop travelling with goods. A certificate that was never issued may instead be **voided**, moving `DRAFT → VOIDED` with its `version` incremented by 1 and no effect whatsoever on the product, which stays in `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`. And a certificate may be **withdrawn** without operator action, moving `ISSUED → WITHDRAWN` as hop three of the failed-test cascade when its product is suspended; that hop in turn stops the released batches the certificate accompanied. `SUPERSEDED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `VOIDED` are all terminal on the issuance axis.

On the accompaniment axis, a `DistributionManager` records that an issued certificate now travels with shipments, moving it `NOT_FURNISHED → FURNISHED`. This is the single cross-axis coupling in the whole machine: furnishing requires the issuance axis to have reached `ISSUED`, and attempting it on a draft is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STATE_INVALID`. The same manager may later withdraw the certificate from shipments, moving it `FURNISHED → RECALLED_FROM_SHIPMENTS`, which blocks further batch releases against it; `issuance_status` is explicitly left unchanged by that move, so an `ISSUED` certificate stays `ISSUED`. `RECALLED_FROM_SHIPMENTS` is terminal on the accompaniment axis, and both accompaniment moves increment the certificate's `version` by 1. Certificates may be listed filtered by `product_id`, `supersedes_certificate_id`, `issuance_status` and `accompaniment_status`.

### 3.8. Production Batches, Tracking Labels and Distribution Control

Physical goods enter the system as production batches, recorded by a `DistributionManager` against the certificate that must accompany their shipments. A batch names the `certificate_id` it ships under, a `batch_code` identifying the production batch, a `produced_on` instant marking production completion, and a `quantity` of at least 1 unit. The `batch_code` must match a strict format: 3 to 10 uppercase letters or digits, then a hyphen, then exactly 6 digits — a code failing that pattern is a request-shape violation. The code must also be unique among the batches of the same product; a repeat is refused with `ERR_BATCH_CODE_DUPLICATE`. Three optional boolean label flags, each defaulting to false, are recorded at the same time: `label_on_product`, whether the permanent tracking label is affixed to the product itself; `label_on_packaging`, whether the label is printed on the packaging; and `label_batch_code_printed`, whether the `batch_code` itself appears in the label text. A new batch begins in `distribution_state` `HELD` with `released_at` null and `release_rescind_count` at 0, and its `quantity` is added to the product's `units_held`.

Batches must fall inside the coverage their certificate asserts. The `produced_on` instant must be at or after the certificate's `issued_at` when that certificate is `ISSUED`, or at or after the certificate's `created_at` while it is still `DRAFT`; and `produced_on` must fall inside the calendar month named by the certificate's `manufacture_date_month`. A batch failing either condition is refused with `ERR_BATCH_OUTSIDE_CERTIFICATE_COVERAGE`.

**Tracking-label compliance** is a strict conjunction of five conditions, all of which must hold: `label_on_product` is true, `label_on_packaging` is true, `label_batch_code_printed` is true, the `batch_code` matches the required format, and the owning product's `manufacture_site_code` is non-empty. The outcome is written to `tracking_label_compliant`. A label present on the product alone, or on the packaging alone, is not compliant — the regulation demands both surfaces, so partial labelling earns no partial credit. The manager may revise the three label flags at any time while the batch is in `HELD` or `STOP_DISTRIBUTION`, submitting all three of `label_on_product`, `label_on_packaging` and `label_batch_code_printed`; the flags are stored, `tracking_label_compliant` is recomputed, and the batch's `version` increments by 1. A `DESTROYED` batch accepts no label edit and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID`.

Releasing a held batch for distribution is the moment every upstream control converges, and it demands four things at once beyond the batch being `HELD`: `tracking_label_compliant` is true, or the release is refused with `ERR_TRACKING_LABEL_NONCOMPLIANT`; the referenced certificate's `issuance_status` is `ISSUED` **and** its `accompaniment_status` is `FURNISHED`, both axes satisfied simultaneously, or the release is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STATE_INVALID`; and the owning product's `compliance_posture` is `CLEARED`, or the release is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_NOT_CLEARED`. On success the batch moves `HELD → RELEASED`, `released_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the batch's `quantity` moves from the product's `units_held` to its `units_released`; both the batch's and the product's `version` increment by 1.

Release is reversible. Rescinding a release moves the batch `RELEASED → HELD`, moves the same `quantity` from the product's `units_released` back to `units_held`, clears `released_at` to null, increments `release_rescind_count` by 1, and increments both the batch's and the product's `version` by 1. Because the movement is exact and symmetric, the distribution-unit invariant of §2.8 holds identically before the release and after the rescission.

Three further destinations exist, none of them reached by a batch-level operation. A batch moves into `STOP_DISTRIBUTION` from either `HELD` or `RELEASED` — from `HELD` when it falls inside a noncompliance case's corrective scope, and from `RELEASED` either for the same reason or as hop four of the failed-test cascade when the certificate it ships under is withdrawn. Either movement transfers its `quantity` from `units_held` or `units_released` into `units_stopped`, incrementing the batch's and product's `version` by 1. A stopped batch returns `STOP_DISTRIBUTION → HELD` when its case is closed, provided its `tracking_label_compliant` is true, moving its `quantity` back from `units_stopped` to `units_held`; a batch may be stopped and reinstated repeatedly over its life. And a batch moves to `DESTROYED` from `HELD`, `RELEASED` or `STOP_DISTRIBUTION` when a case escalates to recall, moving its `quantity` from whichever counter held it into `units_destroyed`. `DESTROYED` is terminal — a destroyed batch can never be recovered or corrected. Batches may be listed filtered by `product_id`, `certificate_id` and `distribution_state`.

### 3.9. Noncompliance Response: Stop-Distribution, Retest Rounds and Corrective Action

A noncompliance case is never created by a user. It is opened by the system the instant a test report is filed as a failure, as described in §3.6, carrying `product_id`, `triggering_test_order_id` (the failing order), `failed_rule_id` (copied from that order's `rule_id`) and `opened_by_user_id` (the actor who filed the failing report). A new case begins in `case_state` `OPEN`, with `retest_round_count` 0, `active_retest_order_id` null, `scope_variant` `BATCH_SCOPED`, `scope_units` 0, `corrective_action_ref` null, `corrective_action_revision` 0 and `closed_by_user_id` null. Each of the four case operations demands a specific `case_state` and refuses any other with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`: `OPEN` to issue the stop-distribution instruction, either `STOP_DISTRIBUTION_ISSUED` or `RETEST_FAILED` to order a retest, either `RETEST_PASSED` or `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED` to file a corrective action, and `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED` to close. `CLOSED` and `ESCALATED_RECALL` are both terminal. A case whose product has no production batch in `HELD` or `RELEASED` has nothing to stop, and the stop-distribution instruction against it is likewise refused with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`.

**Issuing the stop-distribution instruction** is the `DistributionManager`'s act, and it takes one input — `scope_variant`, either `BATCH_SCOPED` or `LOT_TRACEBACK`. The choice of variant is the single most consequential decision in the whole response, because it decides how far back the recall reaches. The case moves `OPEN → STOP_DISTRIBUTION_ISSUED`, the submitted `scope_variant` is stored, `scope_units` is recomputed, every batch inside the resulting scope moves to `STOP_DISTRIBUTION`, the product's `units_held`, `units_released` and `units_stopped` are recomputed, and both the case's and the product's `version` increment by 1.

The **corrective scope** is computed in two variants that differ only in where the chronological cutoff falls. Under `BATCH_SCOPED`, the operator asserts the defect is confined to the material the failing order sampled, and the cutoff is the failing order's own `window_start`. Under `LOT_TRACEBACK`, the operator cannot bound the defect and traces back to the last proof of conformity: the cutoff is the greatest `reported_at` among the product's test orders for the same `failed_rule_id` whose `order_state` is `REPORTED_PASS` and whose `reported_at` precedes the failing order's `reported_at`; when no such earlier passing order exists, the cutoff falls back to the product's own `created_at` — never to an identifier and never to the beginning of time. In both variants the in-scope set is every production batch of the case's product whose `distribution_state` is anything other than `DESTROYED` and whose `produced_on` is at or after the cutoff. The lower bound is inclusive: a batch produced at exactly the cutoff instant is inside the scope. `scope_units` is the sum of those batches' `quantity` values. Destroyed batches are excluded because a destroyed batch can no longer be recovered or corrected. For identical batch data, `LOT_TRACEBACK` always returns a unit count greater than or equal to `BATCH_SCOPED`, because its cutoff can only be earlier or the same.

**Ordering a retest** is the `TestCoordinator`'s act and takes `sampling_plan_id`, `laboratory_id`, `sample_count`, `window_start`, `window_end` and an optional `expedite_requested`. Before anything else, one regulatory bar applies here that applies nowhere else: a laboratory whose `independence_class` is `MANUFACTURER_OWNED` may never be assigned a retest order, so the target laboratory's `independence_class` must be `INDEPENDENT` or `FIREWALLED_GOVERNMENT` or the request is refused with `ERR_MANUFACTURER_OWNED_LAB_FORBIDDEN`. A manufacturer may test its own routine production in its own laboratory, but once its product has failed, an independent eye is mandatory. The referenced sampling plan must also hold `units_available` at least equal to the submitted `sample_count`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_UNITS_EXHAUSTED`.

The retest endpoint is at once the cycle edge and the loop-termination edge of the case machine, and which of the two it takes is decided by a single comparison against `retest_round_count` **as it stands before this call increments it**. When `retest_round_count` is at or above the maximum of 3 retest rounds, the case escalates; otherwise it orders another round. A case whose `retest_round_count` is exactly 3 escalates. A case at 2 orders one more retest and lands at 3.

On the ordering path the case moves `STOP_DISTRIBUTION_ISSUED → RETEST_ORDERED` on the first round, or `RETEST_FAILED → RETEST_ORDERED` on every subsequent round. A test order is created in `SCHEDULED` with `is_retest` true, `retest_case_id` set to this case and `rule_id` set to the case's `failed_rule_id` — the retest re-tests exactly the rule that failed and nothing else — priced through the line-fee and invoice-total calculations of §3.6, with its `invoice_total_cents` added to the laboratory's `billed_fee_cents_total`. The case's `active_retest_order_id` is set to the new order's id, `retest_round_count` increments by 1, and the `sample_count` is committed on the sampling plan by adding it to `units_committed` and subtracting it from `units_available`. On the first round only, the product moves `SUSPENDED → REMEDIATION`; across later loop iterations the product stays in `REMEDIATION`. The case's `version` and the sampling plan's `version` increment by 1 each, and on the first round the product's `version` increments by 1 as well.

The loop closes through report filing, not through a case endpoint. When the active retest order enters `REPORTED_FAIL`, the case moves `RETEST_ORDERED → RETEST_FAILED`, clearing `active_retest_order_id` to null and incrementing the case's `version` by 1; no second case is opened, because the failure belongs to the case already in flight. The report is matched to the case by requiring that the reporting order's id equal the case's `active_retest_order_id`. When the active retest order enters `REPORTED_PASS` instead, the case moves `RETEST_ORDERED → RETEST_PASSED` under the same matching requirement, clearing `active_retest_order_id` and incrementing `version` by 1.

On the escalation path — `RETEST_FAILED → ESCALATED_RECALL`, taken by the same retest endpoint once the round threshold is reached — no test order is created at all. Instead the case's `scope_variant` is forced to `LOT_TRACEBACK` regardless of what it previously held, `scope_units` is recomputed under that widest variant, every production batch of the product in `HELD`, `RELEASED` or `STOP_DISTRIBUTION` moves to `DESTROYED`, the product's `units_held`, `units_released`, `units_stopped` and `units_destroyed` are all recomputed, and both the case's and the product's `version` increment by 1. Three consecutive failed retests exhaust the operator's credibility, so the system stops asking for evidence and destroys the goods.

**Filing a corrective action** is the `CertificationOfficer`'s act and takes `expected_case_version` and `corrective_action_ref`. The submitted `expected_case_version` must equal the case's stored `version` or the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, as set out in §2.6. From `RETEST_PASSED` the case moves to `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED`, storing `corrective_action_ref` and incrementing `corrective_action_revision` by 1. From `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED` the same call self-loops on `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED`, replacing `corrective_action_ref` with the newly submitted reference — superseding the prior plan — and again incrementing `corrective_action_revision` by 1. Either way the case's `version` increments by 1, which is precisely why the version guard matters: a second officer holding a stale read of a case that has already been revised once cannot silently overwrite the revision they never saw.

**Closing the case** is also the `CertificationOfficer`'s act and takes no input. Two preconditions beyond the `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED` state apply. First, the closing `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `opened_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`: the party who discovered the failure may not be the party who declares it resolved. Second, every production batch of the product still in `STOP_DISTRIBUTION` must have `tracking_label_compliant` true, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRACKING_LABEL_NONCOMPLIANT` — stopped goods may not return to the held pool carrying defective labels. On success the case moves `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_APPROVED → CLOSED`, `closed_by_user_id` is stamped from the header, every batch of the product in `STOP_DISTRIBUTION` moves back to `HELD` with its `quantity` moving from the product's `units_stopped` to its `units_held`, and the product moves `REMEDIATION → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` once no other case on that product remains open. The product's `risk_weighted_gap_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed — the closure removes this case from the open-case penalty count — and both the case's and the product's `version` increment by 1. Cases may be listed filtered by `product_id`, `triggering_test_order_id`, `failed_rule_id`, `active_retest_order_id` and `case_state`.

### 3.10. The Material-Change Log and the Resample Cascade

Certification is a statement about a specific design built from specific parts. When either changes, the evidence behind the certificate stops describing the goods being shipped, and the system must notice. The material-change log is how it notices. A `ComplianceAdministrator` records an event against a product or against one of its component parts, submitting `product_id`, a `subject_type` of either `PRODUCT` or `COMPONENT_PART`, a `change_kind` drawn from `SUPPLIER_SWITCH`, `MATERIAL_SUBSTITUTION`, `COATING_CHANGE`, `DESIGN_GEOMETRY_CHANGE` or `PACKAGING_ONLY`, a `description` of 1 to 400 characters narrating the change for the audit file, and — only when the subject is a part — a `component_part_id`.

The polymorphic subject discriminator is enforced strictly in both directions. When `subject_type` is `COMPONENT_PART`, `component_part_id` must be supplied and the named part's `product_id` must equal the submitted `product_id`; when `subject_type` is `PRODUCT`, `component_part_id` must be omitted or null. Either violation — a part event with no part, a part belonging to a different product, or a product event that nonetheless names a part — is refused with `ERR_PART_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`. On acceptance the event's `occurred_at` is stamped with the logical clock and `recorded_by_user_id` with the `X-User-ID` header value.

**Whether the change forces resampling** turns on `change_kind` alone and on nothing else — not on the subject type, not on the product's state, not on the narrative. `triggers_resample` is false when `change_kind` is `PACKAGING_ONLY`, and true for all four other kinds. `PACKAGING_ONLY` is the single non-triggering kind, on the reasoning that a new carton does not change what the child touches.

Recording the event always increments the product's `material_change_count` by 1, whichever way the resample decision falls. Beyond that, everything depends on `triggers_resample`.

When `triggers_resample` is true, the cascade runs in five hops. Hop 1 is the event record itself. Hop 2 applies only when `subject_type` is `COMPONENT_PART`: the named part's `last_material_change_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the part moves out of its current reliance state — `VALID → SUPERSEDED` for a part that was carrying reliance, or `PENDING → SUPERSEDED` for a part that had not yet earned it. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a component part: a superseded part is never restored, and continued reliance requires a fresh part record with fresh evidence. Hop 3 applies only on the `VALID → SUPERSEDED` edge: every component-part certificate of that part whose `reliance_state` is `RELIED_UPON` moves to `RELIANCE_VOID`. No certificate cascade fires from the `PENDING → SUPERSEDED` edge, for the plain reason that a `PENDING` part can hold no relied-upon certificate — the branch is silent, not suppressed. Hop 4 applies regardless of subject type: every `ACTIVE` sampling plan of the product moves to `RESAMPLE_DUE`, with `resample_trigger` set to `RELIANCE_VOID` when hop 3 actually voided a relied-upon certificate, and to `MATERIAL_CHANGE` otherwise. Hop 5, also regardless of subject type, recomputes the product's `risk_weighted_gap_score` and sets its `compliance_posture` to `RESTRICTED` — the design has moved out from under its evidence, so distribution is constrained until the resample closes the gap. Every entity touched along the way increments its own `version` by 1, including the product and, on the part branch, the part and each voided certificate.

When `triggers_resample` is false — that is, on a `PACKAGING_ONLY` change — the contrast is exact and deliberate: no component part row, no component-part certificate, no sampling plan and no product posture field is touched at all. The event is written, `material_change_count` increments, and nothing else in the system moves. A packaging-only note is a bookkeeping entry, not a compliance event.

The material-change log is append-only, and it is the one collection in this system that departs from the common audit shape. An event row carries `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and, critically, no `version` — because it is never modified there is no optimistic-concurrency surface to protect, and no update or delete endpoint exists for it. Events are read individually and listed newest-first, ordered by `occurred_at` descending and then by `created_at` descending as the tie-break, which is the sole departure from the ascending-`created_at` ordering described in §2.3. The list may be filtered by `product_id`, `component_part_id`, `subject_type`, `change_kind` and `triggers_resample`.
