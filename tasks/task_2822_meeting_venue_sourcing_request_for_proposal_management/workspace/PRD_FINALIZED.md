# Meeting Venue Sourcing & RFP Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Corporate meeting planning is a procurement discipline disguised as an events discipline. A single user conference commits hundreds of room nights, a six-figure food-and-beverage minimum and an audiovisual estimate that nobody reads carefully, and it does so through a document trail — a requirement brief, a destination shortlist, a solicitation to bidders, a stack of proposals in incompatible formats, a negotiation thread, and finally a contract whose attrition and cancellation language decides whether an unexpected travel freeze costs the company nothing or costs it a quarter of the room revenue. The teams doing this work today reconcile that trail in spreadsheets. Bids arrive quoting gross rates when the benchmark is net, resort fees hide outside the service-charge base, and the "cheapest" proposal on a summary tab turns out to be the most expensive once taxes compound on top of a service charge. Nobody can say, mid-cycle, how much of the meeting's budget is actually still uncommitted.

This document specifies the Meeting Venue Sourcing & RFP Management API, the system of record for that entire trail. It carries a meeting from requirement definition through destination shortlisting and site inspection, into a standardized solicitation whose responses are forced into one comparable shape, through a total-cost model that prices every bid on the same evaluation order, through negotiation rounds that leave an auditable counter-by-counter history, and finally into award, internal approval routing, contract execution, a dated deposit schedule and a post-event savings report. Every venue is scored against a published rate benchmark for its destination tier and the meeting's program category, so preferred-supplier-program compliance is a computed verdict rather than an opinion.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **money is conserved**: a meeting's budget ceiling is always exactly the sum of what remains, what is committed and what has been lost to cancellation liability, and every forward operation has a compensating reverse operation that restores that identity to the cent. Second, **authority is separated**: the planner who awards a contract can never approve it, no single approver can clear two controls on the same contract, and a supplier's representative can act only on that supplier's own bids and contract. Everything else in this document — the state machines, the cost model, the validation ladder — exists to make those two commitments enforceable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and versioning, arithmetic precision, the logical clock, path resolution, the order in which validations fire, optimistic concurrency, and the conserved quantities the system must never let drift.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. Both are mandatory on every endpoint, including read endpoints and the logical-clock endpoint. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognized role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called (see the authorization column of the endpoint table in section 4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- `SourcingManager` — the corporate meeting planner. Defines and locks meeting requirements, builds and prunes the destination shortlist, plans and closes out site inspections, drafts, issues and closes solicitation rounds, evaluates bids, raises negotiation counters, awards an event and supersedes a prior award, routes a contract for internal approval, planner-signs it, terminates an executed contract and files the post-event savings report.
- `VenueRep` — the supplier-side representative of exactly one venue. Submits, declines, withdraws and revises that venue's own bids, and countersigns that venue's own contract.
- `ContractApprover` — the internal approver who clears the `MANAGER`, `VP` and `LEGAL` control steps on awarded contracts.
- `FinanceController` — the treasury role that generates a contract's deposit schedule and records deposit payments.
- `ProgramAdmin` — the preferred-supplier-program owner. Registers venues, moves program standings, publishes rate benchmarks and clears the `PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE` control step.

Four of these five roles are opaque operator identities, and one is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `SourcingManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it — as `sourcing_owner_user_id` on the meeting event the planner opens, as `awarded_by_user_id` on the contract the planner awards, as `initiated_by_user_id` on a negotiation counter, as `evaluated_by_user_id` on a bid evaluation and as `reviewer_user_id` on a supplier performance review — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `ContractApprover` (recorded as `approver_user_id` on the approval step it decides), for `FinanceController` (recorded nowhere on any entity) and for `ProgramAdmin` (recorded as `approver_user_id` when it clears a compliance step and as `reviewer_user_id` when it captures a performance review). None of those four identifiers is ever matched against a stored row.

`VenueRep` is different. When `X-User-Role` is `VenueRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Venue`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Venue` compare the resource's `venue_id` foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `VenueRep` submitting, declining, withdrawing or revising a bid must present an `X-User-ID` equal to that bid's `venue_id`, and a `VenueRep` countersigning a contract must present an `X-User-ID` equal to that contract's `venue_id`. A mismatch on any of those five operations is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Read access for a `VenueRep` is not narrowed by this rule — the listing and retrieval endpoints on which the role is authorized behave identically for every caller.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip before the first returned item; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Both must be integers. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list endpoints return their items sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Four collections carry an explicit ordering that supersedes that default: a bid's cost-model snapshots are returned oldest first, a bid's negotiation counters are returned in ascending `round_seq`, a contract's approval steps are returned in ascending `step_seq`, and a contract's deposit installments are returned in ascending `installment_seq`. The event cost-comparison collection is ordered by contracted-basis total ascending, with ties broken by the bid's `created_at` ascending.

List responses carry the pagination state in the envelope's `meta` object as `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set and `has_more` indicates whether any items remain beyond the returned page.

Collections also accept optional equality filters, and supplying an unrecognized filter value for an enum-typed filter is a request-shape error. Rate benchmarks may be narrowed by `destination_tier` and by `program_type`. Venues may be narrowed by `destination_tier`, by `city` and by `preferred_supplier_status`. Supplier performance reviews may be narrowed by `venue_id`, by `event_id` and by `review_source`. Meeting events may be narrowed by `status`, by `program_type` and by `awarded_proposal_id`. Shortlist entries may be narrowed by `event_id`, by `venue_id` and by `status`. Site inspections may be narrowed by `shortlist_entry_id`, by `venue_id`, by `inspector_user_id` and by `status`. Solicitation rounds may be narrowed by `event_id`, by `previous_rfp_id` and by `status`. Bids may be narrowed by `rfp_id`, by `venue_id` and by `status`, and this bid collection is the only discovery channel for the `INVITED` bids minted when a solicitation is issued. A bid's cost-model snapshots may be narrowed by `basis` and its counters by `resolution`. A contract's approval steps may be narrowed by `decision` and its deposit installments by `status`. Contracts may be narrowed by `event_id`, by `proposal_id`, by `venue_id`, by `approval_state` and by `signature_state`. Post-event reports may be narrowed by `event_id` and by `contract_id`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource the system persists carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded domain meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; they never derive one from a natural key such as `event_code`.

Most resources also carry a standard trio of audit fields. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format recording the most recent mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

Two resource types are append-only records and deliberately carry a reduced set: a published **rate benchmark** and a **bid evaluation** snapshot both expose only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, because neither is ever mutated after it is written. Every other resource type in the system — meeting events, venues, shortlist entries, site inspections, solicitation rounds, bids, negotiation counters, contracts, approval steps, deposit installments, post-event reports and supplier performance reviews — carries all four of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

A mutation for versioning purposes includes recomputation of a derived field. When issuing a solicitation refreshes a meeting event's status, when a bid submission moves that event's `best_total_cost_usd`, when awarding moves its `committed_spend_usd`, or when a contract execution moves a venue's `blocked_room_nights`, the affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by one — even though the client's request was addressed at a different resource. Where a single request touches several rows, each touched row advances its own `version` independently; there is no shared or global version counter.

### 2.5. Monetary Amounts, Percentages and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are US dollar values carried to exactly 2 decimal places, and every rounding step uses the round-half-up convention. Where a calculation in section 3 describes a sequence of rounding steps, each step rounds at the point it is described; the system never defers rounding to the end of a chain that was specified as rounding intermediate results, because doing so produces a different number of cents. Monetary values appear in response bodies as JSON numbers, never as strings.

Percentage-typed fields are carried as decimal fractions, not as whole numbers: a service charge of twenty-two percent is `0.22`, and a commission of ten percent is `0.10`. Each percentage field has its own declared range, stated where the field is introduced in section 3, and a value outside that range is a request-shape error. One derived percentage departs from the two-decimal rule: a bid's `benchmark_variance_pct` is carried to 4 decimal places, rounded half up, and the compliance tier comparisons are made against that already-rounded value.

Counts of nights and rooms are exact integers. Where a formula calls for rounding a night count down, it takes the largest integer not greater than the value; where it calls for rounding up, it takes the smallest integer not less than the value. Those two operations are never replaced by half-up rounding.

Request bodies are checked for shape before any business rule runs: every required field must be present and of its declared type, every enum-typed field must carry a declared member, every money field must be non-negative and carry at most two decimal places, and every percentage field must fall inside its declared range. Any failure here is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every deadline comparison, inspection-window check, cancellation-tier selection, termination-window check and post-event-conclusion check reads a single server-held **logical current UTC instant**. On startup that instant is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and before any client has moved it the server reports and reasons about that default value; it must not fall back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance.

An operator in any of the five roles may advance or rewind the logical clock by supplying a replacement instant as `current_utc`. The call replaces the clock value used by every subsequent request and stores nothing on any entity; it returns HTTP 200 with an empty data object. Wherever section 3 says "the current logical clock", it means the value most recently set through this capability, or the startup default if it has never been set.

Timestamps the system stamps from the logical clock — `issued_at`, `closed_at`, `submitted_at`, `evaluated_at`, `resolved_at`, `decided_at`, `executed_at`, `terminated_at` and `paid_at` — are all ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits.

### 2.7. Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Many endpoints address a resource, and some address a child through its parent. Identifiers embedded in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error: the request is rejected immediately with the 404 that belongs to that segment's resource type, without attempting to resolve anything to its right and without running any business rule. The path variables and their error codes are: `event_id` yields `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`; `venue_id` yields `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`; `entry_id` yields `ERR_SHORTLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`; `inspection_id` yields `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`; `rfp_id` yields `ERR_RFP_NOT_FOUND`; `proposal_id` yields `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`; `contract_id` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; `step_id` yields `ERR_APPROVAL_STEP_NOT_FOUND`; `installment_id` yields `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `benchmark_id` yields `ERR_BENCHMARK_NOT_FOUND`; `report_id` yields `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`; and `review_id` yields `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. Where a sub-collection is mounted under a bid or a contract the parent variable is spelled simply `id`, and it resolves to that parent's own not-found code — a bid's snapshot or counter sub-collections yield `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, and a contract's approval-step or deposit-installment sub-collections yield `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved in the same spirit, immediately after every path segment has resolved and before any state or business rule runs. A `venue_id` that names no venue when adding a shortlist entry yields `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`; a `previous_rfp_id` that names no solicitation round when drafting a round yields `ERR_RFP_NOT_FOUND`; a `proposal_id` that names no bid when awarding yields `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`; a `counter_id` that names no negotiation counter when revising a bid yields `ERR_COUNTER_NOT_FOUND`; and an `event_id` that names no meeting event when capturing a performance review yields `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence Across the Sourcing Pipeline

Most operations in this system are guarded by several rules at once, and when more than one would fail the caller must see a predictable answer. Checks are therefore evaluated in a fixed global order, and the **first** failing check determines the response; no later check may mask an earlier one. The bands below are listed from highest precedence to lowest, and within a band the listed checks apply only to the operations named.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization.** The declared role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Supplier ownership.** For a `VenueRep`, `X-User-ID` equals the `venue_id` of the bid or contract the path addresses. This applies to exactly five operations: submitting a bid, declining a bid, withdrawing a bid, revising a bid and countersigning a contract — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`.
4. **Path resolution.** Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, on every path-addressed endpoint — otherwise the segment's own 404 code as listed in section 2.7.
5. **Body foreign-key resolution.** Every foreign key in the request body resolves, on the five operations listed in section 2.7 — otherwise that key's own 404 code.
6. **Request shape.** Required fields present and correctly typed, enum members recognized, money non-negative with at most two decimals, percentages inside range, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. **Pagination bounds.** `limit` and `offset` inside their ranges, on every list endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
8. **Date-range sanity.** `end_date` strictly after `start_date` when opening a meeting event — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. And `window_end` strictly after `window_start` when planning a site inspection — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_WINDOW`, evaluated after the date-range check.
9. **Uniqueness.** In this order: `event_code` unused by another meeting event when opening one (`ERR_EVENT_CODE_DUPLICATE`); no benchmark yet published for the destination-tier and program-type pair when publishing one (`ERR_BENCHMARK_DUPLICATE`); no shortlist entry yet existing for the event-and-venue pair and `rank_seq` unused inside the event when adding an entry (`ERR_VENUE_ALREADY_SHORTLISTED`); no solicitation round yet existing for the event-and-round-number pair when drafting a round (`ERR_RFP_ROUND_DUPLICATE`).
10. **Lifecycle state.** In this order: the meeting event's `status` admits the operation (`ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`); the shortlist entry's `status` admits it (`ERR_SHORTLIST_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`); the solicitation round's `status` admits it (`ERR_RFP_STATE_INVALID`); the bid's `status` admits it (`ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`); the site inspection's `status` admits it (`ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`); the contract's two state dimensions, or the addressed approval step's or deposit installment's own state, admit it (`ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`); an award against an event that already has a contract requires that contract not to be executed (`ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_EXECUTED`); and a deposit schedule may be generated at most once per contract (`ERR_DEPOSIT_ALREADY_SCHEDULED`).
11. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied expected version matches the parent's current `version`, on the two operations that carry one — otherwise `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PROPOSAL_VERSION` as described in section 2.9.
12. **Referential business rules.** In this order: the bid's parent solicitation round is `ISSUED`, on bid submission and on countering a bid term (`ERR_RFP_NOT_OPEN`); the referenced shortlist entry is `SHORTLISTED`, on planning a site inspection (`ERR_SHORTLIST_ENTRY_NOT_SHORTLISTED`); the referenced counter belongs to the addressed bid and is still `OPEN`, on revising a bid (`ERR_COUNTER_NOT_OPEN`); the round-chaining rule holds, on drafting a solicitation round (`ERR_PREVIOUS_ROUND_NOT_CLOSED`); and the review-source rule holds, on capturing a performance review (`ERR_REVIEW_EVENT_INVALID`).
13. **Approval-chain integrity.** On deciding an approval step, in this order: every lower `step_seq` on the same contract is already `APPROVED` (`ERR_APPROVAL_STEP_OUT_OF_ORDER`); `X-User-Role` equals the step's `required_role` (`ERR_APPROVAL_LEVEL_ROLE_MISMATCH`); `X-User-ID` is not the contract's `awarded_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); and `X-User-ID` is not already recorded as `approver_user_id` on another step of the same contract (`ERR_DUPLICATE_APPROVER`).
14. **Supplier-program safety.** The venue's `preferred_supplier_status` is not `SUSPENDED`, on four operations — adding a shortlist entry, promoting a candidate onto the shortlist, planning a site inspection and awarding an event (`ERR_VENUE_SUSPENDED`). Then, when the meeting event restricts sourcing to preferred suppliers, the entry's `program_status_at_shortlist` snapshot is `IN_PROGRAM`, on two operations — promoting a candidate onto the shortlist and awarding an event (`ERR_PREFERRED_PROGRAM_REQUIRED`). Then the awarded bid's `compliance_status` is not `NON_COMPLIANT`, on awarding an event (`ERR_RATE_BENCHMARK_NON_COMPLIANT`).
15. **Benchmark availability and capacity fit.** A rate benchmark exists for the venue's destination tier and the event's program type, on two operations — submitting a bid and filing the post-event report (`ERR_BENCHMARK_NOT_CONFIGURED`). Then the venue's `largest_space_sqft` is at least the event's `largest_meeting_space_sqft`, on adding a shortlist entry (`ERR_VENUE_SPACE_INSUFFICIENT`).
16. **Solicitation readiness.** On issuing a solicitation round, in this order: at least one entry of the event is `SHORTLISTED`, `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED` or `INSPECTED` (`ERR_NO_SHORTLISTED_VENUES`); and every such eligible entry flagged `inspection_required` has reached `INSPECTED` (`ERR_INSPECTION_INCOMPLETE`). Then, on awarding an event, the bid carries at least one cost-model snapshot taken on the `CONTRACTED_BASIS` (`ERR_BID_EVALUATION_REQUIRED`).
17. **Temporal windows.** In this order: `response_due_at` lies strictly after the current logical clock and strictly before the event's `start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z`, on drafting and on issuing a solicitation round (`ERR_RESPONSE_DUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`); the current logical clock is at or before the round's `response_due_at`, on submitting a bid (`ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`); the inspection window does not intersect the event's own date interval, on planning a site inspection (`ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAPS_EVENT`); the buffered occupation interval does not intersect another live inspection's on the same venue, also on planning a site inspection (`ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`); the current logical clock is strictly after the event's `end_date` at `23:59:59.999Z`, on filing the post-event report (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_CONCLUDED`); and the current logical clock is strictly before the event's `start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z`, on terminating a contract (`ERR_TERMINATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`).
18. **Room-block capacity.** The contract's `peak_rooms_per_night` plus the summed `peak_rooms_per_night` of the venue's executed contracts whose event date ranges overlap this one is at most the venue's `guest_rooms_total`, on countersigning a contract (`ERR_VENUE_ROOM_BLOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`). Then no post-event report already exists for the event, on filing one (`ERR_POST_EVENT_REPORT_EXISTS`).
19. **Fiscal rules.** In this order: the awarded bid's `total_cost_contracted_usd` is at most the event's `budget_ceiling_usd`, on awarding an event (`ERR_BUDGET_CEILING_EXCEEDED`); every lower `installment_seq` on the same contract is `PAID`, on paying a deposit installment (`ERR_DEPOSIT_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`); `paid_amount_usd` equals the installment's `amount_usd` to the cent, also on paying an installment (`ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`); and `actual_room_nights` is at most the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights`, on filing the post-event report (`ERR_ACTUALS_EXCEED_CONTRACT`).

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are the source of most disagreement. Awarding an event whose `status` is `CONTRACTED` passes the lifecycle-state band deliberately, so that the more specific `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_EXECUTED` — and not the generic `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` — is what the caller sees; an event in `CONTRACTED` always has an executed contract, so every award naming a bid on such an event lands there. Symmetrically, filing a post-event report against an event whose `status` is already `COMPLETED` passes the lifecycle-state band so that `ERR_POST_EVENT_REPORT_EXISTS` is what the caller sees.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Events and Bids

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency, because in both the domain deliberately permits a legitimate repeat and the system must distinguish a legitimate repeat from a caller acting on a view it has not refreshed.

The first is **awarding a meeting event**. The award request must carry `expected_event_version` as a non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `MeetingEvent`. If that value does not equal the event's current `version`, the award is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because the product intentionally allows a later award to supersede an earlier one while the prior contract has not yet reached `EXECUTED`; a second award carrying a fresh version therefore succeeds and legitimately replaces the first, and only the stale view is refused.

The second is **countering a bid term**. The counter request must carry `expected_proposal_version` as a non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Proposal`. If that value does not equal the bid's current `version`, the counter is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPOSAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Raising several counters against one bid across a negotiation is the normal pattern, so a counter carrying a fresh version succeeds; only the stale view is refused.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. In particular, promoting a shortlist entry, issuing or closing a solicitation round, submitting, revising or withdrawing a bid, routing or signing or countersigning or terminating a contract, deciding an approval step, paying an installment and filing a post-event report all take no expected-version input and perform no version comparison.

### 2.10. Cascading Propagation and the System's Conserved Quantities

This system is a graph, not a tree: a venue and a meeting event are peers joined both through shortlist entries and through solicitation rounds and bids, a solicitation round points back at the closed round it supersedes, and a contract references a meeting event, a bid and a venue at once. A single client request therefore routinely mutates rows in several aggregates. When it does, the server performs every propagation itself in the same request — there is no deferred reconciliation, no background sweep and no eventual convergence. Every rollup field is correct the instant the mutating request returns, and each touched row independently refreshes its `updated_at` and advances its `version`.

Two quantities are **conserved** and must never drift.

The first is **money, per meeting event**. At every observable instant, and for every meeting event, the sum of `budget_remaining_usd`, `committed_spend_usd` and `cancellation_liability_usd` equals `budget_ceiling_usd` exactly. The system maintains this by deriving `budget_remaining_usd` as the event's `budget_ceiling_usd` less its `committed_spend_usd` less its `cancellation_liability_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and re-asserting that derivation after every operation that moves either of the other two terms. An event is created with `committed_spend_usd` and `cancellation_liability_usd` both at `0.00`, so the identity holds from creation. Awarding adds the new contract's `contracted_total_usd` to `committed_spend_usd`. Superseding an award, or an approval rejection that voids the contract, subtracts the voided contract's `contracted_total_usd` again. Terminating an executed contract subtracts that contract's `contracted_total_usd` from `committed_spend_usd` and adds its `cancellation_fee_usd` to `cancellation_liability_usd`. Each of those forward moves has a compensating reverse move, and after any forward-and-compensating pair the identity must still hold to the cent with no accumulated drift.

The second is **room-night inventory, per venue**. At every observable instant a venue's `blocked_room_nights` equals the sum of `contracted_paid_room_nights` over that venue's contracts whose `signature_state` is `EXECUTED`. Reaching `EXECUTED` increases the venue's `blocked_room_nights` by the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights`; terminating that contract decreases it by the same amount, restoring the identity. These are exact integer nights with no rounding anywhere.

A contract's `peak_rooms_per_night` is derived from the contracted block and the meeting's length: it is the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights` divided by the parent meeting event's `nights_span`, rounded **up** to the next whole room. That figure is what the capacity guard at countersignature tests. The guard collects the venue's other contracts whose `signature_state` is `EXECUTED` and whose meeting event's date range overlaps this event's on a fully-closed basis — that is, the other event's `start_date` is on or before this event's `end_date` and the other event's `end_date` is on or after this event's `start_date` — sums their `peak_rooms_per_night`, adds this contract's `peak_rooms_per_night`, and requires the result to be at most the venue's `guest_rooms_total`. The comparison is inclusive, so a request that lands exactly on `guest_rooms_total` is allowed; only exceeding it is refused. The guard is evaluated before the `blocked_room_nights` increment, never after.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties in Contract Approval

Approval authority on a contract is deliberately fragmented, and three independent rules enforce that fragmentation. First, a step may only be decided by a caller whose `X-User-Role` equals the step's own `required_role` — a `ProgramAdmin` clears the `PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE` control and a `ContractApprover` clears the `MANAGER`, `VP` and `LEGAL` controls, and no substitution is permitted in either direction. Second, the planner who awarded the contract may never decide any of its steps: the `X-User-ID` recorded as the contract's `awarded_by_user_id` is barred from every step of that contract, regardless of what role that person also holds. Third, no two steps of one contract may be **approved** by the same `X-User-ID`: once an identifier appears as `approver_user_id` on any step of a contract, it is barred from approving any other step of the same contract. That third rule is asymmetric between the two decisions and deliberately so — it gates an `APPROVED` decision but not a `REJECTED` one, because a rejection terminates the chain instead of adding another independent sign-off. Together these mean a contract requiring four controls needs four distinct approving identities, none of which is the awarding planner's.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Supplier Master and the Rate Benchmark Programme

Nothing can be sourced until the preferred-supplier programme has described the market. A `ProgramAdmin` builds the supplier master by registering each venue with its `name`, its `city`, its `country` as an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code, and its `destination_tier` — one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, the axis along which the programme benchmarks rates. Capacity is described by two integers: `guest_rooms_total`, the count of sellable guest rooms, which must be at least 1 and which later caps the peak rooms per night this venue may block; and `largest_space_sqft`, the largest contiguous function space in square feet, which must be at least 0. Registration may optionally set the venue's `preferred_supplier_status`, one of `PREFERRED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `NONE` or `SUSPENDED`; when the field is omitted the venue is registered as `NONE`. A freshly registered venue has `performance_score` null, `performance_score_simple_mean` null, `performance_review_count` at 0 and `blocked_room_nights` at 0.

Programme standing is not static, and moving it is its own operation. A `ProgramAdmin` may promote a venue to `PREFERRED`, put it on `CONDITIONAL`, drop it to `NONE` or suspend it outright, and the move writes the new `preferred_supplier_status` and advances the venue's `version` by 1. Two consequences of a standing change are deliberately asymmetric and must not be conflated. Shortlist entries that already exist are **not** rewritten: each entry's `program_status_at_shortlist` snapshot keeps the value it captured at entry creation, so suspending a venue does not retroactively push its existing entries out of programme. Approval routing, by contrast, reads the venue's standing **live**, so a standing change alters which control steps are generated for any contract routed after the change — but never for a route already generated.

Rate benchmarking is the second half of the programme. A `ProgramAdmin` publishes one benchmark per pair of `destination_tier` and `program_type`, where `program_type` is one of `BOARD_MEETING`, `SALES_KICKOFF`, `TRAINING`, `USER_CONFERENCE` or `INCENTIVE`. The benchmark carries `benchmark_adr_usd`, the programme-negotiated benchmark average daily rate, which must be strictly greater than 0; `benchmark_service_charge_pct`, the benchmark service-charge fraction, which must be at least 0 and at most 1; and `benchmark_tax_pct`, the benchmark occupancy-tax fraction, likewise at least 0 and at most 1. The tier-and-programme pair is unique: publishing a second benchmark for a pair that already has one is rejected with `ERR_BENCHMARK_DUPLICATE`. A published benchmark is immutable — it carries only `id` and `created_at`, has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no operation that edits or retires one. Because the pair is unique, the candidate set for any lookup is always exactly zero or one row, and where a workflow needs a benchmark and finds none it fails loudly with `ERR_BENCHMARK_NOT_CONFIGURED` rather than substituting a default rate.

### 3.2. Defining, Locking and Shortlisting a Meeting Requirement

A `SourcingManager` opens a meeting sourcing file by describing the meeting. The planner supplies `event_code`, a planner-assigned sourcing code between 3 and 32 characters that must be globally unique across meeting events — a collision is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_CODE_DUPLICATE`. Alongside it come `name`, the human-readable meeting name; `program_type`, the programme category from the five values listed above, which together with the eventual venue's tier selects the rate benchmark; `start_date`, the first arrival date; and `end_date`, the departure date. Both dates are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings interpreted in UTC, and `end_date` must be strictly after `start_date` — an equal or inverted pair is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. From that pair the server computes `nights_span`, the number of whole days from `start_date` to `end_date`, which is the room-block night count the deposit and capacity arithmetic later divides by.

The requirement's size and budget arrive in four more inputs. `peak_attendees` is the peak concurrent attendee count and must be at least 1. `requested_room_nights` is the total room nights the planner intends to contract and must be at least 1. `largest_meeting_space_sqft` is the largest single general-session space required in square feet and must be at least 0. `budget_ceiling_usd` is the hard ceiling on the contracted total cost; it must be strictly greater than 0, carries two decimal places, and is enforced at award rather than at creation. Finally, the optional `preferred_program_only` flag defaults to `false`; when it is `true`, only venues whose entry snapshot says they are in programme may be shortlisted or awarded.

Creation seeds the whole rollup surface so that the money identity of section 2.10 holds from the very first instant. The new event's `status` is `DRAFT`, `sourcing_owner_user_id` is set to the calling planner's `X-User-ID`, `budget_remaining_usd` equals `budget_ceiling_usd`, and `committed_spend_usd` and `cancellation_liability_usd` are both `0.00`. `shortlisted_venue_count` is 0, while `best_total_cost_usd`, `awarded_proposal_id` and `savings_recognized_usd` are all null — there is no live bid, no award and no closeout report yet.

Locking the requirement definition moves the event `DRAFT → REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED` and is the gate that opens shortlisting; before it, no shortlist entry may be created at all. The transition requires the event to be in `DRAFT` — any other status is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` — and re-asserts that `peak_attendees` is at least 1, that `requested_room_nights` is at least 1 and that `budget_ceiling_usd` is strictly greater than 0. Locking mutates no child rows whatsoever; its entire effect is that the requirement fields become immutable inputs which every downstream calculation may safely treat as frozen. The event's `version` advances by 1.

Shortlisting begins once the requirement is locked and stays open while the event is in `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED`, `SOURCING` or `BID_REVIEW` — those three statuses, and no others, admit a new entry. The planner adds a candidate by naming a `venue_id` and an explicit `rank_seq`, the planner-assigned shortlist ordering in which 1 is the highest preference; `rank_seq` must be at least 1 and must be unused within the event. The optional `inspection_required` flag defaults to `false`; when `true`, the entry cannot be invited into a solicitation until it has a completed site inspection. Three guards fire on creation, in the order given in section 2.8: a repeat of the same event-and-venue pair, or a `rank_seq` already taken inside the event, is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_ALREADY_SHORTLISTED`; a venue whose `preferred_supplier_status` is `SUSPENDED` is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_SUSPENDED`; and a venue whose `largest_space_sqft` is smaller than the event's `largest_meeting_space_sqft` is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_SPACE_INSUFFICIENT`.

The new entry lands in `CANDIDATE` with `elimination_reason` null and `inspection_count` at 0, and it captures a snapshot that is never re-derived. `program_status_at_shortlist` is set to `IN_PROGRAM` when the venue's `preferred_supplier_status` at that moment is `PREFERRED` or `CONDITIONAL`, and to `OUT_OF_PROGRAM` when it is anything else. That snapshot — not the venue's live standing — is what the preferred-programme restriction is later tested against. Creating an entry also advances the parent meeting event's `version` by 1.

Promotion is the deliberate act of committing a candidate to the solicitation, moving the entry `CANDIDATE → SHORTLISTED`. It requires the entry to be in `CANDIDATE`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_SHORTLIST_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`; it requires the parent event to be in `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED`, `SOURCING` or `BID_REVIEW`; it re-checks that the venue's `preferred_supplier_status` is not `SUSPENDED`, rejecting with `ERR_VENUE_SUSPENDED` if it has been suspended since the entry was added; and when the event's `preferred_program_only` is `true` it requires the entry's `program_status_at_shortlist` to be `IN_PROGRAM`, rejecting with `ERR_PREFERRED_PROGRAM_REQUIRED` otherwise. On success the parent event's `shortlisted_venue_count` increases by 1 and the event's `version` advances by 1. That count is maintained as the number of the event's entries whose `status` is one of `SHORTLISTED`, `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`, `INSPECTED`, `RFP_ISSUED` or `AWARDED` — entries still in `CANDIDATE` and entries already `ELIMINATED` are excluded.

Elimination drops a venue from consideration and records why. The caller supplies `elimination_reason`, which is stored on the entry, and the entry moves to `ELIMINATED`. Elimination is permitted from any status other than `AWARDED` and `ELIMINATED`; attempting it on an entry already in one of those two terminal states is rejected with `ERR_SHORTLIST_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`. The side effects depend on where the entry was, and all five source states are specified:

- From `CANDIDATE`, the entry moves `CANDIDATE → ELIMINATED` with `elimination_reason` stored. The entry was never counted, so `shortlisted_venue_count` does not change.
- From `SHORTLISTED`, the entry moves `SHORTLISTED → ELIMINATED` with `elimination_reason` stored, and the parent event's `shortlisted_venue_count` decreases by 1.
- From `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`, the entry moves `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED → ELIMINATED` with `elimination_reason` stored, every site inspection on the entry whose `status` is `PLANNED` moves `PLANNED → CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `SHORTLIST_ELIMINATED`, and the parent event's `shortlisted_venue_count` decreases by 1.
- From `INSPECTED`, the entry moves `INSPECTED → ELIMINATED` with `elimination_reason` stored, and the parent event's `shortlisted_venue_count` decreases by 1.
- From `RFP_ISSUED`, the entry moves `RFP_ISSUED → ELIMINATED` with `elimination_reason` stored, the parent event's `shortlisted_venue_count` decreases by 1, and every site inspection on the entry whose `status` is `PLANNED` moves `PLANNED → CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `SHORTLIST_ELIMINATED`.

In every case the parent meeting event's `version` advances by 1. Both `AWARDED` and `ELIMINATED` are terminal for a shortlist entry: there is no operation that revives an entry out of either.

### 3.3. Site Inspection Planning and Venue Occupation Windows

A shortlisted venue is often visited before it is invited to bid, and the system treats an inspection as an exclusive occupation of the venue's time rather than as a calendar note. A `SourcingManager` plans an inspection by naming the `shortlist_entry_id` of the entry being inspected, the `inspector_user_id` of the inspecting planner — an opaque operator identifier that is stored as supplied and never matched against any entity — and a concrete window bounded by `window_start` and `window_end`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`; an equal or inverted window is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_WINDOW`. The optional `travel_buffer_minutes` field is the number of minutes of travel buffer appended after `window_end` to form the venue's occupation interval; it defaults to 90 minutes — an hour and a half — when omitted, and must be at least 0 and at most 720 minutes, that is, no more than twelve hours.

From those inputs the server derives `occupied_until` as `window_end` advanced by `travel_buffer_minutes`, and treats the half-open interval from `window_start` up to but not including `occupied_until` as the venue's buffered occupation interval. The inspection also copies `venue_id` from the parent shortlist entry at creation and never re-derives it, so a later change on the entry cannot move an inspection to a different venue.

Three business guards then apply, in the order given in section 2.8. The referenced entry must be exactly in `SHORTLISTED` — an entry still in `CANDIDATE`, already in `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`, already `INSPECTED`, already `RFP_ISSUED`, `AWARDED` or `ELIMINATED` is rejected with `ERR_SHORTLIST_ENTRY_NOT_SHORTLISTED`. The venue's `preferred_supplier_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_VENUE_SUSPENDED`. And two temporal exclusions hold: the inspection window must not intersect the parent meeting event's own fully-closed date interval, which runs from the event's `start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z` through its `end_date` at `23:59:59.999Z` inclusive — an intersection is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAPS_EVENT`, because a site visit cannot be scheduled while the meeting itself is in the building — and the new buffered occupation interval must not intersect the buffered occupation interval of any other inspection on the same `venue_id` whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `COMPLETED`, an intersection being rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because both intervals are half-open at the top, an inspection whose `window_start` falls exactly on another inspection's `occupied_until` does not conflict; abutting is permitted, overlapping is not. Inspections whose `status` is `CANCELLED` release their interval entirely and never participate in the conflict test.

A successful plan creates the inspection in `PLANNED` with `findings_score` null and `cancel_reason` null, moves the parent entry `SHORTLISTED → INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`, increases that entry's `inspection_count` by 1 and advances the entry's `version` by 1. The `inspection_count` field is maintained as the number of inspections on the entry whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `COMPLETED`.

Completion records the visit's outcome. It requires the inspection to be in `PLANNED` **and** the current logical clock to be at or after the inspection's `window_end` — a completion attempted while the clock is still short of `window_end` is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, the same code an attempt on an already-completed or already-cancelled inspection receives. The caller supplies `findings_score`, the inspector's score from 1 through 5 inclusive, which is persisted on the inspection. The inspection moves `PLANNED → COMPLETED` and the parent entry moves `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED → INSPECTED`, with the entry's `version` advancing by 1.

Cancellation is the revert path, and it exists so that a scrubbed visit does not cost the venue its place on the shortlist. It requires the inspection to be in `PLANNED`, rejecting anything else with `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, and the caller supplies `cancel_reason`, which is persisted. The inspection moves `PLANNED → CANCELLED`, its buffered occupation interval from `window_start` up to `occupied_until` is released so that a later inspection may reuse that time, and the parent entry's `inspection_count` decreases by 1. When that decrement brings `inspection_count` to 0 and the entry is still in `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`, the entry reverts `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED → SHORTLISTED`, ready to be re-planned; when other planned or completed inspections remain on the entry, the entry's status is left where it is. Either way the entry's `version` advances by 1. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal for an inspection.

### 3.4. Solicitation Rounds and the Negotiation Round Cycle

A solicitation round is the planner's formal request for proposals, and an event may run several of them: round 1 is the initial solicitation and every round after it is a best-and-final round that supersedes a closed predecessor. A `SourcingManager` drafts a round against an event that is in `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED` or in `BID_REVIEW` — those two statuses and no others — supplying `round_number`, which must be at least 1 and must be unused within the event, and `response_due_at`, the deadline by which venues must respond. `standard_response_version` names the standardized response format version bidders must answer in and is either `V1` or `V2`; forcing every bid into one declared format is what makes the later comparison meaningful. The optional `instructions` field carries free-text instructions to bidders and is null when omitted. The optional `previous_rfp_id` chains this round to the closed round it supersedes.

Three guards govern the draft. A `round_number` already used by another round of the same event is rejected with `ERR_RFP_ROUND_DUPLICATE`. The chaining rule is strict in both directions: when `round_number` is greater than 1, `previous_rfp_id` must be supplied, must reference a round belonging to the same event, and that referenced round's `status` must be `CLOSED`; when `round_number` is exactly 1, `previous_rfp_id` must be absent. Any violation of either direction — an omitted chain on a later round, a chain to another event's round, a chain to a round that is not yet `CLOSED`, or a chain supplied on round 1 — is rejected with `ERR_PREVIOUS_ROUND_NOT_CLOSED`. And `response_due_at` must lie strictly after the current logical clock and strictly before the event's `start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z`; a deadline at or before the clock, or at or after the arrival date, is rejected with `ERR_RESPONSE_DUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. A drafted round lands in `DRAFT` with `invited_count` at 0, `submitted_count` at 0, `issued_at` null and `closed_at` null.

Issuing the round is where the solicitation actually reaches the market, and it is the single most consequential fan-out in the system. It requires the round to be in `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_RFP_STATE_INVALID` — the parent event to be in `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED` or `BID_REVIEW`, at least one entry of the event to be in `SHORTLISTED`, `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED` or `INSPECTED` (otherwise `ERR_NO_SHORTLISTED_VENUES`, because a solicitation to an empty invitee set is meaningless), every one of those eligible entries flagged `inspection_required` to have reached `INSPECTED` (otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_INCOMPLETE`), and `response_due_at` to still lie strictly after the current logical clock (otherwise `ERR_RESPONSE_DUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, since a round drafted days ago may have gone stale). Note carefully that the eligible set is defined by the three statuses above and is **not** narrowed by `inspection_required`: an entry flagged as requiring inspection does not quietly drop out of the invitee list, it blocks the entire issue until it has been inspected.

On success, `issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the round moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`. One bid is created in `INVITED` for each eligible entry, processed in ascending `rank_seq` order so the highest-preference venue's bid is created first, each copying `venue_id` from its source entry; `invited_count` is set to the number of bids created. These freshly minted `INVITED` bids are not returned by the issue response and are discoverable only through the bid collection. Each source entry then moves to `RFP_ISSUED` and its `version` advances by 1, and all three eligible source states are admitted: an entry in `SHORTLISTED` moves `SHORTLISTED → RFP_ISSUED`; an entry in `INSPECTED` moves `INSPECTED → RFP_ISSUED`; and an entry in `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED` moves `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED → RFP_ISSUED` only when its `inspection_required` is `false`, which is exactly the case the `ERR_INSPECTION_INCOMPLETE` guard leaves reachable. Finally the parent event advances: `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED → SOURCING` when this is round 1, or `BID_REVIEW → SOURCING` when `round_number` is greater than 1, and the event's `version` advances by 1.

Closing the round ends the response period. It requires the round to be in `ISSUED`, rejecting anything else with `ERR_RFP_STATE_INVALID`. `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the round moves `ISSUED → CLOSED`, and every bid of that round still sitting in `INVITED` moves `INVITED → EXPIRED` — a venue that never answered is expired rather than declined, and expiry mutates nothing else. The parent event moves `SOURCING → BID_REVIEW`, its `best_total_cost_usd` is recomputed, and its `version` advances by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal for a round; there is no reopening, and a further negotiation round is a new round chained to this one.

Those two edges are what make the event's lifecycle cyclic. Issuing round 1 drives `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED → SOURCING`; closing it drives `SOURCING → BID_REVIEW`; issuing round 2 drives `BID_REVIEW → SOURCING` again; closing round 2 returns the event to `BID_REVIEW`. A planner may repeat that cycle as many times as the negotiation needs, and the event's `AWARDED`, `CONTRACTED`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` statuses lie outside it.

Two counters on the round are maintained continuously. `invited_count` is the number of bids created by issuing this round. `submitted_count` is the number of this round's bids whose `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `COUNTERED`, `REVISED` or `AWARDED`. It rises by 1 when a venue submits and falls by 1 when a venue withdraws, and bids in `INVITED`, `DECLINED`, `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` are not counted.

The event's `best_total_cost_usd` is the running answer to "what is the cheapest live bid on this meeting". It is recomputed whenever a bid enters or leaves the live set — on submission, on revision, on withdrawal — and on closing a round. The live set is every bid belonging to **any** solicitation round of the event, not only the newest round, whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `COUNTERED` or `REVISED`; a bid from round 1 that was never rejected, withdrawn or expired therefore still competes against round 2's bids. When that live set is empty, `best_total_cost_usd` is null. Otherwise it is the `total_cost_contracted_usd` of the live bid with the smallest `total_cost_contracted_usd`, with ties broken in favour of the bid with the earlier `created_at` and never by comparing `id` values; the stored two-decimal value is carried across unchanged with no further rounding. The same recomputation pass refreshes `shortlisted_venue_count` from the entry-status rule given in section 3.2.

### 3.5. Bid Submission, the Total Cost Model and Rate Benchmarking

A bid begins life as an `INVITED` shell minted by the issue, and the venue fills it in. A `VenueRep` acting for that bid's own venue submits the response in the standardized format, and the submission carries the whole commercial offer in one payload: `room_rate_usd`, the quoted average daily rate; `rate_type`, either `NET` or `GROSS`, declaring whether that rate is net of travel-agency commission or gross of it; `commission_pct`, the commission fraction deducted when the rate is gross, which must be at least 0 and at most `0.20`; `room_nights_offered`, the total room nights the venue will hold including complimentary nights; `comp_room_ratio`, an integer meaning one complimentary room night per that many offered room nights, which must be at least 0 and where 0 disables comps entirely; `resort_fee_usd_per_night`, the mandatory resort or facility fee charged per paid room night; `service_charge_pct`, the service-charge fraction; `tax_pct`, the occupancy or sales tax fraction; `av_estimate_usd`, the quoted audiovisual estimate before the volume-tier adjustment; `fnb_minimum_usd`, the food-and-beverage minimum the venue requires; and `concession_credit_usd`, the concession credit offered before capping.

The same payload carries the risk terms that the later contract negotiation turns on. `attrition_allowance_pct` is the fraction of the contracted block the group may release without damages, at least 0 and at most `0.50`. `cancellation_schedule` is an ordered array of objects, each with an integer `days_before_arrival_min` and a numeric `fee_pct`, describing the tiered cancellation ladder; the array always contains a tier whose `days_before_arrival_min` is 0, so a cancellation at any distance from arrival always has a governing tier. `force_majeure_scope` declares the breadth of the force majeure clause offered and is one of `NARROW`, `STANDARD` or `BROAD`. `rebooking_credit_pct` is the fraction of any cancellation fee the venue will return as rebooking credit, at least 0 and at most 1.

Four preconditions gate the submission beyond the ownership check of section 2.1. The bid must be in `INVITED`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`. The bid's parent solicitation round must be in `ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_RFP_NOT_OPEN`. The current logical clock must be at or before the round's `response_due_at`; the deadline is **inclusive**, so a submission landing exactly on `response_due_at` is accepted and only a strictly later one is rejected with `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. And a rate benchmark must exist for the pairing of the bidding venue's `destination_tier` with the meeting event's `program_type`, otherwise `ERR_BENCHMARK_NOT_CONFIGURED`.

On acceptance the bid moves `INVITED → SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock as the instant of the first submission, and every quoted term is persisted. The server then computes `net_adr_usd`, `comp_room_nights`, `paid_room_nights` and `total_cost_contracted_usd` from the contracted-basis total cost model below, computes `benchmark_variance_pct` and `compliance_status` from the rate-benchmark rules below, increases the parent round's `submitted_count` by 1, recomputes the event's `best_total_cost_usd` and advances the event's `version` by 1.

A venue that does not wish to bid declines instead. Declining requires the bid to be in `INVITED` and the caller to own the venue, moves the bid `INVITED → DECLINED`, and deliberately leaves the matching shortlist entry untouched so that a later negotiation round may still invite the same venue.

**The total cost model.** Every bid is priced by one model with a pinned evaluation order, so that two bids differing only in where they hide their money still compare correctly. The model has two variants that differ **only** in how many room nights they charge for and whether they book projected attrition damages; every other step is identical. The shared preamble runs first:

1. `comp_room_nights` is 0 when `comp_room_ratio` is 0; otherwise it is `room_nights_offered` divided by `comp_room_ratio`, rounded **down** to a whole night.
2. `paid_room_nights` is `room_nights_offered` less `comp_room_nights`.
3. `net_adr_usd` is the commission-adjusted daily rate. When `rate_type` is `GROSS`, it is `room_rate_usd` multiplied by one minus `commission_pct`, rounded half up to two decimals. When `rate_type` is `NET`, it is `room_rate_usd` itself, rounded half up to two decimals.
4. `av_adjusted_usd` applies a volume discount to the audiovisual estimate in three tiers. When `av_estimate_usd` is at most 10,000.00 US dollars, the estimate is carried at full value — multiplied by 1.00. When it exceeds that first tier ceiling but is at most 25,000.00 US dollars, it is multiplied by 0.95, a five percent volume discount. When it exceeds 25,000.00 US dollars, it is multiplied by 0.90, a ten percent volume discount. In every tier the product is rounded half up to two decimals, and the tier boundaries are inclusive at the top, so an estimate of exactly 10,000.00 takes the full-value tier and one of exactly 25,000.00 takes the five-percent tier.

Next the variant decides the night count and the damages term. Under the **`CONTRACTED_BASIS`** variant, the nights charged are the full contracted block — `paid_room_nights` — and the attrition damages term is exactly `0.00`. Under the **`ACTUALIZED_BASIS`** variant, the model assumes the group will shrink by twelve percent and prices that downside: the projected paid nights are `paid_room_nights` multiplied by one minus 0.12, rounded **up** to a whole night; the allowance floor is `paid_room_nights` multiplied by one minus the bid's `attrition_allowance_pct`, rounded **down** to a whole night; the shortfall is the allowance floor less the projected paid nights, or 0 when that difference is negative, whichever is larger; and the damages term is the shortfall multiplied by `net_adr_usd` multiplied by 0.80 — eighty percent of the room revenue the released nights would have produced — rounded half up to two decimals. The nights charged under this variant are the projected paid nights.

The remaining steps then run in exactly this pinned order, using the chosen night count wherever "the nights charged" appears, and rounding half up to two decimals at each numbered step:

5. `room_revenue_usd` is `net_adr_usd` multiplied by the nights charged.
6. `resort_fee_total_usd` is `resort_fee_usd_per_night` multiplied by the nights charged.
7. `fnb_usd` is the bid's `fnb_minimum_usd`, carried into the model unchanged.
8. `service_charge_usd` is `service_charge_pct` applied to the sum of `room_revenue_usd`, `fnb_usd` and `av_adjusted_usd`. Resort fees are deliberately **outside** this base.
9. `tax_usd` is `tax_pct` applied to the sum of `room_revenue_usd`, `resort_fee_total_usd`, `fnb_usd`, `av_adjusted_usd` and `service_charge_usd`. Resort fees are **inside** this base, and tax compounds on top of the service charge rather than beside it.
10. The gross figure is the sum of `room_revenue_usd`, `resort_fee_total_usd`, `fnb_usd`, `av_adjusted_usd`, `service_charge_usd` and `tax_usd`.
11. The concession cap is `room_revenue_usd` multiplied by `0.08`, rounded half up to two decimals. `concession_applied_usd` is the smaller of the venue's offered `concession_credit_usd` and that cap, so a generous concession is honoured only up to eight cents on every dollar of room revenue.
12. The variant's total is the gross figure, less `concession_applied_usd`, plus the attrition damages term, rounded half up to two decimals.

Every input to this model is read from the single bid row being priced. Nothing is read from the parent solicitation round and nothing from sibling bids. The contracted-basis output is written to the bid's `total_cost_contracted_usd` on every submission and on every revision, and that figure — never the actualized one — is the only total the budget ceiling is tested against and the only total the volume-rebate tier reads.

**Rate benchmark compliance.** Alongside the cost model, every submission and every revision recomputes the bid's standing against the published benchmark. The system finds the single benchmark whose `destination_tier` equals the bidding venue's `destination_tier` and whose `program_type` equals the meeting event's `program_type`; because that pair is unique the candidate set holds exactly zero or one row, and an empty set fails the submission with `ERR_BENCHMARK_NOT_CONFIGURED` rather than falling back to any default rate. With the benchmark in hand, `benchmark_variance_pct` is the signed fraction by which the bid's `net_adr_usd` exceeds the benchmark's `benchmark_adr_usd`: the difference between `net_adr_usd` and `benchmark_adr_usd`, divided by `benchmark_adr_usd`, rounded half up to **four** decimal places. A bid priced under benchmark therefore carries a negative variance.

`compliance_status` is then decided from that rounded variance and the venue's live `preferred_supplier_status`, and the three branches are mutually exclusive:

- When the venue's `preferred_supplier_status` is `SUSPENDED`, the verdict is `NON_COMPLIANT` regardless of variance.
- When the venue's `preferred_supplier_status` is `PREFERRED` or `CONDITIONAL`, a variance at or below `0.0000` earns `COMPLIANT`; a variance above `0.0000` but at or below the maximum programme premium of `0.1000` — ten percent over benchmark — earns `COMPLIANT_WITH_VARIANCE`; and a variance above `0.1000` earns `NON_COMPLIANT`. Both comparisons are inclusive against the already-rounded four-decimal value, so a variance of exactly `0.1000` is `COMPLIANT_WITH_VARIANCE` and not `NON_COMPLIANT`.
- When the venue's `preferred_supplier_status` is `NONE`, the best available verdict is one grade lower: a variance at or below `0.0000` earns `COMPLIANT_WITH_VARIANCE`, and any variance above `0.0000` earns `NON_COMPLIANT`. An out-of-programme venue can never reach `COMPLIANT`.

Before a bid has ever been submitted its `compliance_status` is `NOT_EVALUATED`, which is the field's initial value and is never re-entered afterwards.

### 3.6. Bid Comparison, Evaluation Snapshots and Term Counters

Comparing bids is a repeated, auditable act rather than a single verdict, so the planner's cost modelling is captured as immutable snapshots. A `SourcingManager` evaluates a bid by naming the `basis` on which to price it — either `CONTRACTED_BASIS` or `ACTUALIZED_BASIS` — and the system runs that variant of the total cost model and appends a **bid evaluation** row recording every component. The row carries `proposal_id`, the `basis` used, `evaluated_by_user_id` set to the calling planner's `X-User-ID`, `paid_room_nights_used` (which is `paid_room_nights` under the contracted basis and the projected paid nights under the actualized basis), `room_revenue_usd`, `resort_fee_total_usd`, `av_adjusted_usd`, `fnb_usd`, `service_charge_usd`, `tax_usd`, `concession_applied_usd`, `attrition_damages_usd` (always `0.00` under the contracted basis), `total_cost_usd` for that basis, `benchmark_adr_usd` copied from the benchmark in force at evaluation time, and `evaluated_at` stamped from the logical clock. The row is created with HTTP 201 and is never mutated afterwards; it carries `id` and `created_at` only.

Evaluation is permitted while the bid is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISED`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`. All three source states are admitted and each behaves as follows: a bid in `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; a bid in `REVISED` moves `REVISED → UNDER_REVIEW`, which is the edge that closes the negotiation cycle; and a bid already in `UNDER_REVIEW` stays in `UNDER_REVIEW`, which is how the planner re-prices the same bid on the other basis. In all three cases a new snapshot row is appended and earlier rows are left exactly as written, the bid's `total_cost_modeled_usd` and `last_evaluation_basis` are overwritten from the newest row, and the bid's `version` advances by 1. The bid's `total_cost_contracted_usd` is **not** touched by evaluation; it changes only on submission and revision. Before any evaluation exists, both `total_cost_modeled_usd` and `last_evaluation_basis` are null.

Alongside the snapshots, the planner can read a side-by-side comparison of the event's live bids. Each comparison row reports the bid's `proposal_id` and `venue_id`, the `venue_name` of the bidding venue, the bid's current `status`, its `total_cost_contracted_usd`, its `total_cost_actualized_usd` — the actualized-basis total for the same bid — the `variant_delta_usd` obtained by subtracting the actualized total from the contracted total, the bid's `benchmark_variance_pct`, its `compliance_status`, and a boolean `within_budget` that is true exactly when the bid's `total_cost_contracted_usd` is at most the event's `budget_ceiling_usd`. This comparison recomputes both variants of the cost model on the fly and writes no snapshot rows; it is a pure read that leaves the bid's `total_cost_modeled_usd` and `last_evaluation_basis` alone.

Negotiation is conducted one term at a time. A `SourcingManager` raises a counter against a bid that is `UNDER_REVIEW`, naming the `term_category` being countered — one of `RATE`, `CONCESSION`, `ATTRITION`, `CANCELLATION`, `FORCE_MAJEURE` or `REBOOKING_CREDIT` — together with the target value. Numeric categories carry their target in `proposed_numeric_value` and leave `proposed_text_value` null: those are `RATE`, `CONCESSION`, `ATTRITION` and `REBOOKING_CREDIT`. Text categories carry their target in `proposed_text_value` and leave `proposed_numeric_value` null: those are `CANCELLATION` and `FORCE_MAJEURE`. An optional `planner_note` carries free-text negotiation context and is null when omitted. The request must also carry `expected_proposal_version` exactly as described in section 2.9; a value that does not equal the bid's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPOSAL_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Beyond the state check the bid's parent solicitation round must still be in `ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_RFP_NOT_OPEN` — a planner cannot open new negotiation on a closed round.

A successful counter is created with HTTP 201 in `resolution` `OPEN`, with `resolved_at` null, with `initiated_by_user_id` set to the calling planner's `X-User-ID`, and with `round_seq` set to the bid's `negotiation_round_count` plus 1, giving a 1-based sequence in creation order within the bid. The bid then moves `UNDER_REVIEW → COUNTERED`, its `negotiation_round_count` increases by 1, and its `version` advances by 1.

The venue answers a counter by revising the bid. A `VenueRep` acting for the bid's own venue names the `counter_id` being answered and a boolean `accepted`, and may restate any of the affected commercial terms through nine optional fields: `revised_room_rate_usd`, `revised_concession_credit_usd`, `revised_attrition_allowance_pct`, `revised_cancellation_schedule`, `revised_force_majeure_scope`, `revised_rebooking_credit_pct`, `revised_av_estimate_usd` and `revised_fnb_minimum_usd`. Each supplied revised value overwrites the corresponding quoted term on the bid — a supplied `revised_room_rate_usd` becomes the new `room_rate_usd`, a supplied `revised_fnb_minimum_usd` becomes the new `fnb_minimum_usd`, and so on for each pair — while omitted fields leave their quoted term untouched. Revision requires the bid to be in `COUNTERED`, otherwise `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the referenced counter to belong to this same bid and to still be in `OPEN`; a counter belonging to a different bid or already resolved is rejected with `ERR_COUNTER_NOT_OPEN`, while a `counter_id` naming nothing at all yields `ERR_COUNTER_NOT_FOUND`.

On success the referenced counter's `resolution` becomes `ACCEPTED` when `accepted` is `true` and `DECLINED_BY_VENUE` when it is `false`, and its `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The contracted-basis cost model is then re-run over the revised terms, refreshing `net_adr_usd`, `comp_room_nights`, `paid_room_nights` and `total_cost_contracted_usd`, and the benchmark rules are re-run, refreshing `benchmark_variance_pct` and `compliance_status`. The bid moves `COUNTERED → REVISED`, its `version` advances by 1, and the event's `best_total_cost_usd` is recomputed. From `REVISED` the planner evaluates again, which returns the bid to `UNDER_REVIEW` and closes the loop; the cycle `UNDER_REVIEW → COUNTERED → REVISED → UNDER_REVIEW` may run as many times as the negotiation requires, and each pass leaves one more counter row and one more evaluation row behind as the audit trail.

Withdrawal is the venue's exit from a live negotiation. A `VenueRep` acting for the bid's own venue may withdraw from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `COUNTERED` or `REVISED`, and all four source states behave identically in their primary effect: the bid moves to `WITHDRAWN`, the parent round's `submitted_count` decreases by 1, and the event's `best_total_cost_usd` is recomputed with the event's `version` advancing by 1. Withdrawal from `COUNTERED` carries one extra consequence worth stating explicitly: every counter on the bid whose `resolution` is `OPEN` **stays** `OPEN`. Counters are historical record and are never retro-resolved by anything that happens to the bid afterwards. A bid in any other status — `INVITED`, `DECLINED`, `AWARDED`, `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` — cannot be withdrawn and is rejected with `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`. The five statuses `AWARDED`, `REJECTED`, `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` are terminal for a bid.

### 3.7. Award, the Volume Rebate and the Award Cascade

Awarding is the pivot of the whole pipeline: it converts one bid into a **contract** and simultaneously closes out every competing artefact the event has accumulated. A `SourcingManager` awards by naming the `proposal_id` of the winning bid, supplying `expected_event_version` as described in section 2.9, and optionally an `award_note`. The award succeeds only from an event in `BID_REVIEW` or `AWARDED`, and both source states are legitimate: `BID_REVIEW → AWARDED` is the first award, and `AWARDED → AWARDED` is a supersede, where the planner replaces one award with another. An event in any other lifecycle state is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, and an event already in `CONTRACTED` is rejected with the more specific `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_EXECUTED` as described in section 2.8. The named bid must belong to this event, and its `status` must be exactly `UNDER_REVIEW`; the bid moves `UNDER_REVIEW → AWARDED`. Any other bid status — including `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED` and `REVISED` — is rejected with `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`, which means the planner must evaluate a freshly submitted or freshly revised bid before it can be awarded. The response is HTTP 201 and its body is the newly created contract.

Five further guards protect the award, applied in the order of section 2.8. The winning venue's `preferred_supplier_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_VENUE_SUSPENDED` — a supplier suspended after being shortlisted cannot be awarded. When the event's `preferred_program_only` is `true`, the winning bid's shortlist entry must carry `program_status_at_shortlist` of `IN_PROGRAM`, otherwise `ERR_PREFERRED_PROGRAM_REQUIRED`, and note that this reads the snapshot taken at shortlisting rather than the venue's live status. The winning bid's `compliance_status` must not be `NON_COMPLIANT`, otherwise `ERR_RATE_BENCHMARK_NON_COMPLIANT`; `COMPLIANT` and `ABOVE_BENCHMARK` are both awardable. The winning bid must have at least one bid evaluation whose `basis` is `CONTRACTED_BASIS`, otherwise `ERR_BID_EVALUATION_REQUIRED` — the planner must have priced the bid on the contracted basis before committing to it, and an actualized-basis evaluation alone does not satisfy this. Finally the winning bid's `total_cost_contracted_usd` must be at most the event's `budget_ceiling_usd`, otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_CEILING_EXCEEDED`; equality is allowed, so a bid priced at exactly the ceiling is awardable. This ceiling test uses the pre-rebate figure, not the post-rebate `contracted_total_usd`, so a bid that only fits the budget once the volume rebate is deducted is still refused.

The award price is not simply the bid's quoted total. A **volume rebate** is applied at award time, and the tiering is driven by the pre-rebate figure. First, `pre_rebate_total_usd` is snapshotted from the winning bid's `total_cost_contracted_usd`. The rebate percentage is then chosen from that pre-rebate figure: a `pre_rebate_total_usd` below `150000.00` earns `volume_rebate_pct` of `0.000`; a `pre_rebate_total_usd` at or above `150000.00` but below `400000.00` earns `0.020`; and a `pre_rebate_total_usd` at or above `400000.00` earns `0.035`. The tier minimums are inclusive, so a pre-rebate total of exactly `150000.00` earns `0.020` and a pre-rebate total of exactly `400000.00` earns `0.035`. Then `volume_rebate_usd` is `pre_rebate_total_usd` multiplied by `volume_rebate_pct`, rounded to 2 decimal places, and `contracted_total_usd` is `pre_rebate_total_usd` minus `volume_rebate_usd`, rounded to 2 decimal places. The tier is chosen once, from the pre-rebate total, and is never re-derived from `contracted_total_usd`: a pre-rebate total of `152000.00` sits in the `0.020` tier and yields a `contracted_total_usd` of `148960.00`, which is below `150000.00`, and that does not demote the contract to the `0.000` tier.

Every commercial term the parties agreed on is frozen onto the contract at award time, so that later revision of the bid — which is impossible once it is `AWARDED`, but which the model nevertheless guards against — could not disturb what was signed. The contract's `contracted_room_rate_usd` is the bid's `net_adr_usd`; its `contracted_paid_room_nights` is the bid's `paid_room_nights`; its `contracted_room_revenue_usd` is `contracted_room_rate_usd` multiplied by `contracted_paid_room_nights`, rounded to 2 decimal places. The contract also copies `attrition_allowance_pct`, `cancellation_schedule`, `force_majeure_scope`, `rebooking_credit_pct`, `service_charge_pct`, `tax_pct`, `resort_fee_usd_per_night`, `fnb_minimum_usd` and `concession_applied_usd` from the winning bid, copies `peak_rooms_per_night` as computed in section 2.10, records `awarded_by_user_id` as the awarding planner's `X-User-ID`, and records `venue_id` and `proposal_id` so the contract points back at both the supplier and the bid it was struck from.

A new contract begins with `approval_state` `PENDING_ROUTING` and `signature_state` `UNSIGNED` — the two parallel dimensions described below — and with its money and signature fields empty: `deposit_total_usd` null, `deposit_paid_usd` `0.00`, `deposit_outstanding_usd` null, `approval_step_count` `0`, and `planner_signatory_name`, `venue_signatory_name`, `executed_at`, `terminated_at`, `cancellation_fee_usd`, `rebooking_credit_usd` and `termination_reason` all null.

The award then cascades outward through four levels, each level acting on the artefacts produced by the level above.

1. **The event.** The event moves to `AWARDED`, its `awarded_proposal_id` is set to the winning bid, its `committed_spend_usd` is set to the contract's `contracted_total_usd`, its `budget_remaining_usd` is recomputed from the money identity in section 2.10, and its `version` advances by 1.
2. **The losing bids.** Every other bid on the same solicitation round whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `COUNTERED` or `REVISED` moves to `REJECTED`. Bids in `INVITED`, `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` are left exactly as they are: only live competing bids are rejected.
3. **The losing shortlist entries.** For each bid rejected at level 2, that bid's shortlist entry moves to `ELIMINATED` with `elimination_reason` set to `NOT_SELECTED`. Entries whose bids were untouched at level 2 keep their own status.
4. **The losing site inspections.** For each shortlist entry eliminated at level 3, every site inspection on that entry whose `status` is `PLANNED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `SHORTLIST_ELIMINATED`. Inspections already in `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` are left untouched, and each cancelled inspection releases its venue occupation window as described in section 3.3.

Superseding an award adds a fifth strand of unwinding, applied to the artefacts of the award being replaced. The previously awarded bid moves from `AWARDED` to `REJECTED`. Its contract moves to `approval_state` `CANCELLED` and `signature_state` `VOIDED`, which are the terminal states on the two dimensions. Both approval source states are cancelled the same way: a superseded contract still in `PENDING_ROUTING` moves `PENDING_ROUTING → CANCELLED`, and one already in `IN_APPROVAL` moves `IN_APPROVAL → CANCELLED`. Only a contract whose `signature_state` is not yet `EXECUTED` can be superseded, which is exactly what `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_EXECUTED` protects. Every approval step on that superseded contract whose `decision` is still `PENDING` moves to `SKIPPED`, and every deposit installment on it whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`. The event's `awarded_proposal_id` and `committed_spend_usd` then re-point at the new contract rather than accumulating both, so the money identity continues to hold across the supersede. A superseded contract can never be routed, signed, countersigned or executed again; `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED` are dead ends.

### 3.8. Approval Routing, Signature and Contract Execution

A contract carries two independent state dimensions. `approval_state` tracks internal authorisation and runs `PENDING_ROUTING → IN_APPROVAL → APPROVED`, with `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` as its other terminal states. `signature_state` tracks the signing ceremony and runs `UNSIGNED → PLANNER_SIGNED → COUNTERSIGNED → EXECUTED`, with `TERMINATED` and `VOIDED` as its other terminal states. The two dimensions advance independently, and `EXECUTED` is the fork-join where they meet: it is reachable from either side, depending on which dimension finishes last. Whenever a contract is described as being in `EXECUTED` below, that means `signature_state` is `EXECUTED`.

**Routing.** A `SourcingManager` routes a contract for approval, which generates its whole approval chain in one shot. Routing requires `approval_state` to be `PENDING_ROUTING` — any other value is `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID` — and requires `signature_state` to be neither `TERMINATED` nor `VOIDED`, since a voided or terminated contract has nothing left to authorise. The chain is assembled from four possible levels, in this fixed order:

1. `PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE` is included, and always placed first when present, when the venue's `preferred_supplier_status` is `NONE` or `CONDITIONAL`. This status is read **live** from the venue at routing time; the snapshot taken onto the shortlist entry when the venue was shortlisted is deliberately not consulted here, so a venue promoted to `PREFERRED` after shortlisting routes without a compliance step, and a venue demoted to `CONDITIONAL` after shortlisting gains one.
2. `MANAGER` is always included.
3. `VP` is included when `contracted_total_usd` is at or above `250000.00`.
4. `LEGAL` is included when `contracted_total_usd` is at or above `500000.00`, or when the contract's `force_majeure_scope` is `NARROW`. Either condition alone is sufficient, so a narrow force majeure clause pulls legal review onto even a small contract.

Both threshold comparisons are inclusive, so a `contracted_total_usd` of exactly `250000.00` adds the `VP` step and exactly `500000.00` adds the `LEGAL` step. `step_seq` is then assigned `1` through `N` in exactly that list order. Each step records its `approval_level` and the `required_role` that may decide it: `ProgramAdmin` for `PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE`, and `ContractApprover` for `MANAGER`, `VP` and `LEGAL`. Each step is created with `decision` `PENDING` and with `approver_user_id`, `decision_note` and `decided_at` all null. The contract's `approval_step_count` is set to the number of steps generated, the contract moves `approval_state` `PENDING_ROUTING → IN_APPROVAL`, and its `version` advances by 1.

**Deciding a step.** An approver decides a step by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and an optional `decision_note`. The step must be `PENDING` with its parent contract in `IN_APPROVAL`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. Four further guards then apply in the order given in section 2.8: every step of the same contract with a lower `step_seq` must already be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_APPROVAL_STEP_OUT_OF_ORDER`; the caller's `X-User-Role` must equal the step's `required_role`, otherwise `ERR_APPROVAL_LEVEL_ROLE_MISMATCH`; the caller's `X-User-ID` must not be the contract's `awarded_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the caller's `X-User-ID` must not already appear as the `approver_user_id` of another step on the same contract, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPROVER`. The last of these applies only when the decision is `APPROVED`: a rejection may be cast by someone who has already approved an earlier step on the same contract, because a rejection ends the chain rather than accumulating independent sign-offs. Both decisions persist `approver_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and stamp `decided_at` from the logical clock.

An `APPROVED` decision on any step other than the highest `step_seq` simply moves that step `PENDING → APPROVED` and leaves the contract in `IN_APPROVAL`, with the contract's `version` advancing by 1. An `APPROVED` decision on the highest `step_seq` additionally moves the contract `approval_state` `IN_APPROVAL → APPROVED`. If at that moment `signature_state` is already `COUNTERSIGNED`, the contract immediately joins to `EXECUTED` in the same request, firing exactly the execution effects listed below. If it is not, the approval dimension is finished and the join waits for the countersignature.

A `REJECTED` decision cascades in three levels.

1. The step moves `PENDING → REJECTED` and the contract's `approval_state` moves `IN_APPROVAL → REJECTED`.
2. Every remaining step on the contract whose `decision` is still `PENDING` moves to `SKIPPED`, and the contract's `signature_state` moves to `VOIDED` — reachable from `UNSIGNED`, from `PLANNER_SIGNED` and from `COUNTERSIGNED` alike, so a rejection voids the contract no matter how far the signing ceremony had progressed. Every deposit installment on the contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`.
3. Because the contract is voided, the awarded bid returns to `REJECTED`, the event's `awarded_proposal_id` is cleared to null, its `committed_spend_usd` returns to `0.00` with `budget_remaining_usd` recomputed, and the event moves `AWARDED → BID_REVIEW` so the planner can award a different bid.

A `SKIPPED` step keeps `approver_user_id` and `decided_at` null: nobody decided it. Steps also become `SKIPPED` through the award-supersede path described in section 3.7, where the cause is `approval_state` becoming `CANCELLED` rather than `REJECTED`.

**Signing.** A `SourcingManager` signs by supplying `planner_signatory_name`, which is persisted on the contract. Signing requires `signature_state` to be `UNSIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`, and requires `approval_state` to be neither `REJECTED` nor `CANCELLED`. It deliberately does **not** require `approval_state` to be `APPROVED`: the planner is free to sign while approvals are still in flight, or even before routing, and the contract moves `UNSIGNED → PLANNER_SIGNED` regardless. That independence is the point of the two dimensions.

**Countersigning.** A `VenueRep` acting for the contract's own venue countersigns by supplying `venue_signatory_name`, which is persisted. Countersigning requires `signature_state` to be `PLANNER_SIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`, requires `approval_state` to be neither `REJECTED` nor `CANCELLED`, and is the single point at which the venue's room-block capacity is checked, as described in section 2.10: the contract's `peak_rooms_per_night` plus the summed `peak_rooms_per_night` of the venue's already-executed contracts whose event date ranges overlap this event's must be at most the venue's `guest_rooms_total`, otherwise `ERR_VENUE_ROOM_BLOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the contract moves `PLANNER_SIGNED → COUNTERSIGNED`, and if `approval_state` is already `APPROVED` the contract immediately joins to `EXECUTED` in the same request.

**Execution.** However the join is reached — countersignature arriving last, or the final `APPROVED` step arriving last — execution has exactly one set of effects. `executed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The venue's `blocked_room_nights` increases by the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights`, restoring the room-night identity of section 2.10. The awarded shortlist entry moves `RFP_ISSUED → AWARDED`, which is its second terminal state alongside `ELIMINATED`. The event moves `AWARDED → CONTRACTED`. The event's `cancellation_liability_usd` stays `0.00`: liability is recognised only when a termination actually charges a fee. Each of these effects fires once, in the request that completes the join, and the contract's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.9. Deposits, Termination and Post-Event Closeout

**The deposit schedule.** Once a contract is `EXECUTED`, a `FinanceController` generates its deposit schedule. This is the only place in the system where finance acts alone, and it may happen at most once per contract: the contract's `deposit_total_usd` must still be null, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_ALREADY_SCHEDULED`, and `signature_state` must be `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. The deposit is 30 percent of the money actually committed: `deposit_total_usd` is `contracted_total_usd` multiplied by `0.30`, rounded to 2 decimal places.

That total is then split across exactly three installments carrying `weight_pct` values of `0.34`, `0.33` and `0.33` at `installment_seq` `1`, `2` and `3`. The rounding order is pinned and must be followed exactly. First, each installment's `amount_usd` is computed independently as `deposit_total_usd` multiplied by that installment's `weight_pct`, rounded to 2 decimal places. Second, the residual is computed as `deposit_total_usd` minus the sum of those three rounded amounts, rounded to 2 decimal places. Third, that residual is added onto installment 3's `amount_usd` and the result rounded again to 2 decimal places. The residual always lands on installment 3, never on installment 1 or 2. Summing the exact unrounded shares and rounding once at the end is a different algorithm and produces a different cent split; it is wrong. What this order guarantees is that the sum of the three `amount_usd` values equals `deposit_total_usd` to the cent, exactly.

Each installment's `due_date` is derived from the event's `start_date` by subtracting a fixed offset: 180 days for `installment_seq` 1, 90 days for `installment_seq` 2 and 30 days for `installment_seq` 3, each rendered as a calendar date. Each installment is created with `status` `SCHEDULED` and with `paid_amount_usd`, `paid_at` and `payment_reference` null. The contract's `deposit_paid_usd` is set to `0.00` and its `deposit_outstanding_usd` to the sum of the three amounts — which is to say, to `deposit_total_usd` — and its `version` advances by 1. Installments are listed in ascending `installment_seq`.

**Paying an installment.** A `FinanceController` records payment by supplying `paid_amount_usd` and an optional `payment_reference`. Three guards apply in ladder order. The installment must be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID` — a `PAID` or `CANCELLED` installment cannot be paid again. Every installment on the same contract with a lower `installment_seq` must already be `PAID`, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`; deposits are consumed strictly in order. And `paid_amount_usd` must equal the installment's `amount_usd` to the cent, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; partial payments and overpayments are both refused, which is what keeps the deposit arithmetic exact. On success the installment moves `SCHEDULED → PAID`, `paid_amount_usd` and `payment_reference` are persisted, `paid_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the contract's `deposit_paid_usd` increases by the amount paid and its `deposit_outstanding_usd` decreases by the same amount. `PAID` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal states of an installment.

**Termination.** A `SourcingManager` terminates an executed contract by supplying a `termination_reason`, which is persisted. Termination requires `signature_state` to be `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the current logical clock to be strictly before the event's `start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z`, otherwise `ERR_TERMINATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A group that has already arrived cannot cancel; the window closes at the first instant of the arrival day.

The cancellation fee is drawn from the `cancellation_schedule` the parties negotiated. First, `days_to_arrival` is the whole number of days between the current clock and the event's `start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z`, computed by taking the difference in seconds, dividing by 86400 and flooring. Then the applicable tier is the one with the largest `days_before_arrival_min` that is at or below `days_to_arrival`. The comparison is inclusive, so being exactly 181 days out selects a tier whose `days_before_arrival_min` is 181 rather than falling to the next tier down. Because every `cancellation_schedule` contains a tier with `days_before_arrival_min` of `0`, some tier always applies. Then `cancellation_fee_usd` is the contract's `contracted_room_revenue_usd` multiplied by that tier's `fee_pct`, rounded to 2 decimal places. The fee base is `contracted_room_revenue_usd` and nothing else: F&B, audiovisual, resort fees, service charge and tax are never in the base, however large they are. Finally `rebooking_credit_usd` is `cancellation_fee_usd` multiplied by the contract's `rebooking_credit_pct`, rounded to 2 decimal places — the credit is a share of the fee charged, not of the contract value.

Termination then unwinds the commitment. `terminated_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the contract moves `signature_state` `EXECUTED → TERMINATED`. Every deposit installment on the contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and the contract's `deposit_outstanding_usd` becomes `0.00`; installments already `PAID` are left untouched, so `deposit_paid_usd` does not change. The venue's `blocked_room_nights` decreases by the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights`, restoring the room-night identity of section 2.10. The event's `committed_spend_usd` returns to `0.00`, its `cancellation_liability_usd` increases by the `cancellation_fee_usd`, its `budget_remaining_usd` is recomputed so the money identity of section 2.10 still holds, and the event moves `CONTRACTED → CANCELLED`. The liability line is why the identity is a three-way split rather than two: cancelled money is neither committed nor available.

**The post-event report.** After the meeting has happened, a `SourcingManager` files the post-event report, which is created with HTTP 201 and closes the event. The body carries `actual_room_nights`, `actual_fnb_usd` and `actual_av_usd`, each of which must be at least zero, and an optional `notes`. Five guards apply in ladder order: the event must be in `CONTRACTED`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`; the current logical clock must be strictly after the event's `end_date` at `23:59:59.999Z`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_CONCLUDED`, since actuals cannot be reported while the meeting is still running; no report may already exist for the event, otherwise `ERR_POST_EVENT_REPORT_EXISTS`, and this is the error an event in `COMPLETED` reports as described in section 2.8; a rate benchmark must exist for the venue's `destination_tier` paired with the event's `program_type`, otherwise `ERR_BENCHMARK_NOT_CONFIGURED`, because the savings figures are measured against it; and `actual_room_nights` must be at most the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights`, otherwise `ERR_ACTUALS_EXCEED_CONTRACT`, since the group cannot consume more paid nights than were contracted. The report's `contract_id` is set to the event's executed contract, and every input for the reconciliation comes from that contract's snapshots rather than from the awarded bid, which is never re-read.

The reconciliation runs in this order. `attrition_allowed_nights` is the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights` multiplied by one minus its `attrition_allowance_pct`, floored to a whole number, so a fractional allowance never rounds up in the group's favour. `attrition_shortfall_nights` is `attrition_allowed_nights` minus `actual_room_nights`, floored at zero, so a group that met or beat its allowance owes nothing. `attrition_charge_usd` is `attrition_shortfall_nights` multiplied by the contract's `contracted_room_rate_usd` multiplied by `0.80`, rounded to 2 decimal places — damages are 80 percent of the lost room revenue, not 100 percent. `actual_room_cost_usd` is `contracted_room_rate_usd` multiplied by `actual_room_nights`, rounded to 2 decimal places. The realized resort fee is the contract's `resort_fee_usd_per_night` multiplied by `actual_room_nights`, rounded to 2 decimal places; it is an intermediate used by the two lines below and is not stored as a field of the report. `realized_service_charge_usd` is the sum of `actual_room_cost_usd`, `actual_fnb_usd` and `actual_av_usd`, multiplied by the contract's `service_charge_pct`, rounded to 2 decimal places — the realized resort fee is outside the service-charge base, exactly as it was at bid time. `realized_tax_usd` is the sum of `actual_room_cost_usd`, the realized resort fee, `actual_fnb_usd`, `actual_av_usd` and `realized_service_charge_usd`, multiplied by the contract's `tax_pct`, rounded to 2 decimal places — the resort fee is inside the tax base, and tax applies on top of the service charge. `realized_total_usd` is the sum of `actual_room_cost_usd`, the realized resort fee, `actual_fnb_usd`, `actual_av_usd`, `realized_service_charge_usd`, `realized_tax_usd` and `attrition_charge_usd`, minus the contract's `concession_applied_usd`, rounded to 2 decimal places.

Two savings figures close the file, and they answer different questions. `benchmark_room_cost_usd` is the benchmark's `benchmark_adr_usd` multiplied by `actual_room_nights`, rounded to 2 decimal places, and `savings_vs_benchmark_usd` is `benchmark_room_cost_usd` minus `actual_room_cost_usd`, rounded to 2 decimal places: this is what the negotiation earned against the market rate, and it is a rooms-only comparison. `savings_vs_budget_usd` is the event's `budget_ceiling_usd` minus `realized_total_usd`, rounded to 2 decimal places: this is what the programme returned against its own plan, across every line of spend. Either figure may be negative.

Filing the report then moves the event `CONTRACTED → COMPLETED`, sets its `savings_recognized_usd` to the report's `savings_vs_budget_usd`, sets its `committed_spend_usd` to `realized_total_usd` with `budget_remaining_usd` recomputed so the money identity still holds against actuals rather than against the contracted estimate, decreases the venue's `blocked_room_nights` by the contract's `contracted_paid_room_nights` since the rooms have now been consumed, and advances the event's `version` by 1. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal states of an event.

**Supplier performance.** Finally, a `SourcingManager` scores a venue. The body carries `review_source`, which is `EVENT` for a scorecard tied to a completed event in this system or `EXTERNAL` for an imported programme-history scorecard, four integer scores `service_score`, `value_score`, `av_quality_score` and `responsiveness_score` each between 1 and 5 inclusive, an optional `event_id` and an optional `comment`. The `reviewer_user_id` is recorded from the caller's `X-User-ID`. The two sources have mirror-image requirements: `review_source` of `EVENT` requires `event_id` to name an event whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose awarded contract's `venue_id` equals the venue being reviewed, while `review_source` of `EXTERNAL` requires `event_id` to be absent. Either requirement failing yields `ERR_REVIEW_EVENT_INVALID`.

Each review's `composite_score` is `0.35` times `service_score` plus `0.25` times `value_score` plus `0.20` times `av_quality_score` plus `0.20` times `responsiveness_score`, rounded to 2 decimal places. The venue's two score fields are then both recomputed over **every** review of that venue, regardless of `review_source`: there is no status filter, because reviews are never retracted. For both variants the reviews are first ranked newest-first, ordered by `created_at` descending and then by `composite_score` descending as the tie-break; `id` is never a decision input. The `performance_score` field takes the recency-weighted variant, `RECENCY_WEIGHTED`: each review at rank `r`, counting from 1 for the newest, is weighted by `0.7` raised to the power `r - 1`, and `performance_score` is the sum of weight times `composite_score` divided by the sum of the weights, the division happening before the single rounding to 2 decimal places. The `performance_score_simple_mean` field takes the unweighted variant, `SIMPLE_MEAN`: the sum of every `composite_score` divided by the number of reviews, rounded to 2 decimal places. The two diverge whenever the reviews are not all equal, and the recency-weighted figure is the one the sourcing pipeline reads while the simple mean serves preferred-supplier-programme eligibility audits. Both are null when the venue has zero reviews. Capturing a review also increases the venue's `performance_review_count` by 1 and advances its `version` by 1.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served under the paths shown and returns the standard envelope of section 2.2. Endpoints that create a new persistent resource — publishing a rate benchmark, registering a venue, capturing a performance review, creating an event, adding a shortlist entry, scheduling a site inspection, opening a solicitation round, evaluating a bid, raising a counter, awarding an event and filing a post-event report — respond with HTTP 201 and return the newly created resource in `data`.
