# Custody RIA Advisor Transition API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Registered investment advisers who custody client assets at a third-party custodian live or die by the completeness of their paperwork. This document specifies the back-office platform that a custodian offers to those advisers: a single API through which an advisory firm is onboarded, granted a custody agreement, issued the trading and technology entitlements it is entitled to, migrates an entire book of business away from a prior custodian, manages model portfolios and block trades, is billed an advisory fee on the balances actually custodied, admits alternative-asset valuations into that fee basis, and moves client money under verified standing instructions. Every one of those operations is gated by a regulatory posture that the platform derives continuously rather than stores by decree, so a firm whose custody evidence is deficient does not merely receive a warning — its entitlement set, its data feeds and its ability to trade all contract accordingly.

The platform exists because these workflows are inseparable from one another in practice. A block trade cannot be allocated to an account that is frozen; an account cannot be activated before the firm's agreement is countersigned; an invoice cannot be settled before the household's fee basis reconciles against the sum of its accounts; and an examination bundle cannot be sealed until every custody-rule requirement has an accepted, in-period evidence row behind it. The design therefore treats a small number of conserved quantities — the firm's agreement authority, its evidence rollup, the household fee basis, the block's executed notional, the cost basis carried across a transfer — as invariants that the platform recomputes and, where regulation demands it, actively enforces.

What follows is written for the engineer who must build the service. Section 2 fixes the cross-cutting contracts: authentication and role identity, the response envelope, pagination, the posture derivation, segregation of duties, household aggregation, monetary precision, the audit trail, nested-path resolution, optimistic concurrency, evidence and examination, and the invariant/configuration surface. Section 3 narrates the business logic in the order an adviser actually encounters it, scattering the parameters, thresholds and rejection codes across the workflows that need them. Section 4 and Section 5 are lookup tables only.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal; `X-User-Role` names their operational capacity. A request that omits either header, or that presents an `X-User-Role` value which is not one of the seven roles enumerated below, is rejected with `ERR_AUTHENTICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 401). A request that presents a well-formed role which is not among the roles the target operation authorizes is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authentication is evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before any other validation the platform performs.

The platform recognizes exactly these roles, spelled as shown:

- `PlatformAdmin` — the custody-platform operator who provisions firms, the logical clock, the singleton system configuration and regulatory admin actions.
- `AdvisorOperationsLead` — the adviser firm's own operations staff: drafts agreements, opens households and accounts, runs onboarding batches, trades to model, and seeds fee runs.
- `AdvisorSupportAnalyst` — read-mostly adviser staff who service client inquiries and cannot originate any state change whatsoever.
- `CustodyOperationsOfficer` — the custodian's own officer: countersigns agreements, grants entitlements, activates and closes accounts, completes movements, and runs invariant sweeps.
- `ComplianceReviewer` — the independent compliance function: accepts or rejects evidence, freezes firms and accounts, revokes entitlements and feeds, reopens mismatched billing runs, and seals examination bundles. This role is segregated from every originating role.
- `InvestmentCommitteeChair` — the adviser's investment committee chair, who owns model approval and activation and cannot trade or bill.
- `Client` — the end client of the adviser firm, acting on their own household's accounts; verifies standing instructions and authorizes or rejects money movements.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role. For six of the seven roles the identifier is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `AdvisorOperationsLead`, `AdvisorSupportAnalyst`, `CustodyOperationsOfficer`, `ComplianceReviewer`, or `InvestmentCommitteeChair`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for an actor stamp — for example as `drafted_by_user_id`, `executed_by_user_id`, `accepted_by_user_id` or `acknowledged_by_user_id` on the relevant row — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

The `Client` role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `Client`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Household`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Household` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a client may only read or act on rows whose `account_id` belongs to the household named by `X-User-ID`; every other household's rows resolve to `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` rather than to a permission error, so the platform does not disclose the existence of another client's data.

Rejections across the whole surface are evaluated in a fixed category order, and knowing it tells a caller which of several violated conditions will actually be reported. Authentication is examined first, then the caller's authorization for the specific operation. Existence follows — whether the firm named in the path or body exists, whether the other path parameters and body foreign keys resolve, and whether each child row is owned by the parent named ahead of it in the same path. Only then is the target row's state examined, followed by the business-safety family (posture, entitlement, agreement authority, segregation of duties, outstanding regulator actions, and the stale-version refusals), then business constraints, then the temporal constraints, then cross-firm reference integrity, and finally the fiscal floors. Because the order is fixed, a request that violates more than one family always reports the earliest family in this list, so for example a seeding attempt against a zero-basis household that is also below the minimum threshold reports the zero-basis error rather than the minimum error.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, and the platform accepts no value below 1 or above 100. An out-of-range value is not refused but clamped — a `limit` below 1 is read as 1 and one above 100 as 100 — and a `limit` that is absent, empty or not a whole number falls back to the default of 10. `offset` skips a number of items from the front of the result set; it defaults to 0, and a negative or non-numeric `offset` is likewise normalized to 0 rather than refused. Unless an endpoint documents otherwise, every list response is ordered by the resource's `created_at` timestamp in ascending order — oldest first — so that repeated paged reads over a stable data set are deterministic. Several list endpoints additionally filter on one or more optional query parameters named after the fields they select on — a firm's `firm_posture`, an account's `household_id`, `firm_id` or `account_status`, a run's `billing_run_status`, and the like — each applied only when the parameter is supplied, so an omitted filter is not an error and an unrecognized filter value simply matches nothing. A filter parameter the PRD's descriptions do not name is still honored where the implementation applies it, and a filter the implementation does not apply is ignored rather than refused.

Paginated responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`. `total_count` reports the number of matching rows in the full result set, not the number on the current page, and `has_more` is true exactly when further rows remain beyond the current page. The list endpoints that take the pagination parameters are the ones the Endpoint Definitions table describes as taking `limit`/`offset` — the collection lists such as firms, households, accounts, transfers, models, block orders, billing runs, credits, evidence, feeds and batches, and the nested lists of those same kinds; a small number of nested reads that return a whole owned collection in one shot — a transfer's lots, a model's target lines, an agreement's successors, the entitlements claiming an agreement, the credits a run consumed, a firm's invariant rows and its admin-action rows — are returned unpaginated and omit the four pagination fields from `meta` entirely.

### 2.4. Custody Agreement Authority and the Derivation of Firm Posture

The platform does not let an operator set a firm's operating posture directly. `firm_posture` on an `AdvisorFirm` is a derived aggregate over three input families: the firm's current executed `CustodyAgreement`, the rollup of the firm's `CustodyRulesEvidence` rows, and the firm's `AdvisorFirmEntitlement` set. It is re-derived after every child mutation that touches any of those families, and it gates virtually every write in the system.

The derivation is a pinned, first-match evaluation over the firm's child rows, and the order matters because a firm can satisfy several conditions at once:

0. If the firm has an `AdminActionLog` row whose `admin_action_kind` is `REGULATORY_FREEZE` and whose `admin_action_status` is `ACTIVE`, the posture is `FROZEN` with `posture_reason_code` set to `REGULATORY_FREEZE`. This rule is checked first and outranks everything below it.
1. Otherwise, if the firm has zero `CustodyAgreement` rows whose `agreement_status` is `EXECUTED`, the posture is `SUSPENDED` with `posture_reason_code` `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`.
2. Otherwise, if any `CustodyRulesEvidence` row has `requirement_status` `DEFICIENT` and `critical` true, the posture is `LIMITED` with `posture_reason_code` `CUSTODY_EVIDENCE_DEFICIENT`.
3. Otherwise, if any `AdvisorFirmEntitlement` row has `entitlement_status` `REVOKED`, the posture is `LIMITED` with `posture_reason_code` `ENTITLEMENT_REVOKED`.
4. Otherwise, if an `AdminActionLog` row of the firm has `admin_action_status` `UNACKNOWLEDGED`, the posture is `LIMITED` with `posture_reason_code` `ADMIN_ACTION_UNACKNOWLEDGED`.
5. Otherwise, if a `CustodyAgreement` row of the firm has `agreement_status` `TERMINATED`, the posture is `SUSPENDED` with `posture_reason_code` `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`.
6. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN` and `posture_reason_code` is null.

The permitted values of `firm_posture` are exactly `OPEN`, `LIMITED`, `SUSPENDED` and `FROZEN`; `FROZEN` is terminal. A newly registered firm therefore starts life `SUSPENDED`, because it has no executed agreement yet — this is the intended cold-start state, not an error.

Alongside the posture, the platform maintains `supervision_evidence_status` on `AdvisorFirm` as the aggregate rollup of the firm's custody evidence. Its value is `CLEAR` when every critical evidence row is in `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`, `DEFICIENT` when any critical evidence row is `DEFICIENT`, and `UNKNOWN` when the firm has no critical evidence rows at all. Legal values are `CLEAR`, `DEFICIENT` and `UNKNOWN`. The two derived values are complementary: the posture is the operative gate, and the evidence rollup is the narrative explanation a reviewer reads.

Because posture is derived rather than asserted, a whole family of rejections is expressed in terms of it. Any write endpoint outside the two exempt capabilities — acknowledging an admin action and moving the logical clock — is refused with `ERR_FIRM_FROZEN` (HTTP 403) while the firm is `FROZEN`; the invariant-evaluation sweep is the one further write that is exempt from this guard as well, so it may always be run against a frozen firm. Trading-path actions additionally require the posture to be exactly `OPEN`, and are refused with `ERR_FIRM_NOT_OPEN_FOR_TRADING` (HTTP 409) otherwise. Actions that depend on the existence of live agreement authority are refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409) when the firm has no executed agreement or when the agreement a request names is not the firm's current one.

### 2.5. Segregation of Duties and Two-Party Controls

The platform enforces a small number of two-party controls, all of which compare the acting `X-User-ID` against a user identifier already stamped on the row. A request that would have one officer perform both halves of a control is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The controls are: countersigning a custody agreement, where the executing officer must differ from `drafted_by_user_id`; accepting custody evidence, where the accepting officer must differ from the submitter recorded in `drafted_by_user_id`; and rejecting custody evidence and marking it deficient, where the rejecting officer must likewise differ from `drafted_by_user_id`. The values themselves are never validated against a user directory — only the fact that they differ is inspected.

A second family of controls concerns outstanding regulator actions. While the firm has an `AdminActionLog` row whose `admin_action_status` is `UNACKNOWLEDGED`, the two most consequential two-party operations — countersigning the firm's agreement and accepting an evidence package — are refused with `ERR_ADMIN_ACTION_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409). Acknowledging the admin action records `acknowledged_note` and `acknowledged_by_user_id`, sets `admin_action_status` to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and lets the posture derivation fall through to whatever its lower rules then dictate.

### 2.6. Households as the Billing Aggregation Unit

`Household` is the unit at which advisory fees are aggregated and at which credit balances are held. A household carries `household_name` for display, `household_status` with the permitted values `ACTIVE`, `BILLING_HELD` and `CLOSED` — a newly opened household beginning at `ACTIVE` — and three derived monetary or counter fields: `household_fee_basis_cents`, `household_annual_fee_cents`, and `billing_period_number`, which holds the index of the last settled billing period and starts at 0 before the first settlement. Accounts are attached to a household through the account's `household_id`, and an account may be moved from one household to another; because the fee basis of both the source and the destination household changes when that happens, the platform re-derives both.

Not every account inside a household counts toward the fee basis. An account's `billable` flag is derived from `account_type` at creation — it is true when the type is `INDIVIDUAL`, `JOINT`, `TRUST`, `IRA`, `ROTH_IRA`, or `ROTH_IRA`-class retirement types including `ROTH_IRA`, and false for `ENTITY` accounts, which are custody-only and never billable — and it is fixed for the life of the row. The permitted values of `account_type` are `INDIVIDUAL`, `JOINT`, `TRUST`, `IRA`, `ROTH_IRA` and `ENTITY`. Lifecycle state matters too, and is re-examined on every derivation: only accounts whose `account_status` is `OPEN` or `FROZEN` are included; `PENDING` and `CLOSED` accounts are excluded, and a `CLOSED` account drops out of the basis the moment it closes.

Household aggregation is at the client's election, recorded per account in `householding_consent`, whose permitted values are `OP_IN`, `OP_OUT` and `NONE`. The intent of the field is to let a client choose whether the fee ladder is run once against the whole household or restarted for each account; the exact consequences are specified with the fee calculation in §3.4.

### 2.7. Cent-Exact Arithmetic and Rounding Discipline

All money on this platform is an integer number of cents. Fields whose names end in `_cents` — `market_value_cents`, `account_fee_basis_cents`, `household_fee_basis_cents`, `household_annual_fee_cents`, `carryover_cents`, `sent_cost_basis_cents`, `basis_appraisal_cents`, `allocated_cost_cents`, `fee_rebate_cents`, `credit_cents`, `gross_fee_cents`, `credit_applied_cents`, `net_fee_cents`, `stated_invoice_cents`, `fee_basis_snapshot_cents`, `accepted_value_cents`, `stated_value_cents`, `valuation_floor_cents`, `periodic_limit_cents`, `consumed_period_cents`, `amount_cents`, `withheld_cents`, `net_disbursed_cents`, `expected_total_cents` and `observed_total_cents` — are whole cents, never fractional, and never strings.

Two non-cent integer scales appear alongside them. Quantities are carried in micro-shares: `quantity_micros` and `share_micros` count shares in units of one millionth, so one share is 1000000. Prices are carried in micros of one cent: `valuation_price_micros`, `execution_price_micros`, and `average_price_micros` count in units where one US cent is 10000 micros. Converting a micro-share quantity at a micro-cent price into cents therefore divides by ten billion, and the constant `MICROS_TO_CENTS_DIVISOR` is 10000000000 for exactly that reason. The related constants `MICROS_PER_SHARE` (1000000) and `MICROS_PER_CENT` (10000) are used wherever a formula has to move between the two scales.

Rounding is specified per calculation, and the platform uses the round-half-up rule wherever a calculation is specified to round at all. Monetary results are stated to two decimal places — that is, to whole cents — and the rounding method is round half up, not truncation, not banker's rounding, and not floor. Some intermediate steps are deliberately floored rather than rounded; those steps are called out explicitly in the calculation prose in §3, and the distinction is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

### 2.8. Common Resource Properties and the Audit Trail

Every one of the platform's twenty-three entities carries the same four system-managed properties, and clients may rely on them uniformly:

- `id` — a unique, server-minted identifier for the resource. It is a UUID. It carries no type prefix and no entity-name segment; two resources of different entities are distinguished by which collection the client asked for, never by the shape of the identifier.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits (for example `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z`), stamped when the row was first created.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the platform performs on its own initiative rather than at a client's request.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 0 and increments by one on every modification to the row. It is the token clients echo back for optimistic concurrency control, and it is the token the platform refreshes whenever a cascade mutates a row.

All twenty-three entities — `AdvisorFirm`, `CustodyAgreement`, `AdvisorFirmEntitlement`, `Household`, `HouseholdAccount`, `AssetTransfer`, `TransferLot`, `ModelPortfolio`, `ModelTarget`, `BlockOrder`, `BlockAllocation`, `FeeBillingRun`, `FeeCredit`, `AlternativeAssetHolding`, `MoneyMovementInstruction`, `StandingInstruction`, `CustodyRulesEvidence`, `DataFeedProvision`, `OnboardingBatch`, `AdminActionLog`, `SystemConfig`, `DatabaseInvariant` and `ExaminationBundle` — carry the full set of four. No entity in this domain omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Two derived fields are worth naming here because they are recomputed rather than written by clients and are easy to overlook. `household_fee_basis_total_cents` on the firm is the sum of `household_fee_basis_cents` across every household of the firm, and it is the firm-level mirror of the household-level basis. `billed_period_count` on an account counts the settled billing periods in which the account was included in a fee basis. Both are maintained as by-products of the fee pipeline described in §3.4.

Several server-minted tamper-evidence tokens exist purely to make the audit trail verifiable later: `countersignature_hash` on an executed agreement, `settlement_evidence_hash` on a settled billing run, `evidence_document_hash` on accepted evidence, and `sealed_evidence_hash` on a sealed examination bundle. Each is minted at the instant of the transition that names it, is opaque to clients, and reappears in the examination bundle.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Resolution

Many of the platform's read operations address a descendant directly through its ancestry: a transfer's lots, an account's alternatives, an account's standing instructions and money movements, a model's target lines and block orders, an agreement's entitlements and successors, a firm's custody evidence, examination bundles, data feeds and onboarding batches. For every such request the platform resolves path parameters strictly left to right, and the first identifier that cannot be resolved stops the request.

The failure taxonomy mirrors the resolution order. A path or body reference that names an `AdvisorFirm` which does not exist yields `ERR_FIRM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A path parameter or body foreign key that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); a required request field that is absent or blank — a firm's `legal_name` or `crd_number`, an account's `account_number`, and the like — is deliberately reported in this same not-found family rather than as a distinct validation error. A child row that exists but is not owned by the parent named earlier in the same path yields `ERR_PARENT_CHILD_MISMATCH` (HTTP 404) — the `TransferLot` does not belong to that `AssetTransfer`, the `CustodyAgreement` does not belong to that firm, and so on. Two server-enforced uniqueness rules are likewise reported through `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`: a second firm whose `legal_name` or `crd_number` collides with a live peer's, and a second non-`CLOSED` account whose `account_number` collides within the same firm. This ownership rule extends across firms: a foreign key written in a request body that points at a row owned by a different firm is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), which is deliberately a different status from the not-found family because the row does exist and the caller simply is not entitled to reference it. A body reference that cannot be tied to the named parent or firm is refused at the point it is evaluated rather than silently ignored.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency and Conditional Requests

Three write operations are guarded by an explicit optimistic-concurrency contract in addition to the platform-wide `If-Match` mechanism. For each, the request body must carry a non-optional version field named after the parent entity it protects, and the value must equal that parent's current `version` at the moment the server evaluates the request. A mismatch means the client has been reading a state that has since been superseded, and the write is refused rather than applied.

- Requesting a model rebalance must carry `expected_model_portfolio_version`, matching the target `ModelPortfolio`'s current `version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_MODEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The version legitimately advances on any rebalance entry, including the approval round trip, so a client holding a pre-round view of a model is acting on a superseded state.
- Running a block allocation must carry `expected_block_order_version`, matching the target `BlockOrder`'s current `version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The version advances whenever the block is legitimately superseded — for example by a re-execution that reprices the fill — so the client's expected allocation basis is stale.
- Relinking an account to a different household must carry `expected_household_version`, matching the destination `Household`'s current `version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The household version advances whenever another account is legitimately relinked into or out of it, which supersedes the client's view of the membership, so the relink has to be re-decided against fresh membership state.

Endpoints that are not named in the three rules above do not take any expected-version field. Separately, and independently of the three named rules, the platform honors a generic conditional-request contract on every `PUT` and every state-changing `POST`: such a request must carry an `If-Match` header, and its absence is refused with `ERR_IF_MATCH_REQUIRED` (HTTP 428), while a header whose value does not match the target row's current `version` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_IF_MATCH` (HTTP 412). The header is not required on the operations that create a new, addressable resource — registering a firm, drafting an agreement, requesting an entitlement, opening a household or account, opening an alternative holding, creating a standing instruction or a money movement or a block or a batch, issuing a fee credit, opening an examination bundle, raising evidence, requesting a data feed, issuing an admin action, or creating the singleton configuration row — even though several of those are `POST`s, since there is no existing row whose version could be echoed. On the state-changing verbs that do address an existing row the contract is enforced, and the check is evaluated after that row has been resolved — a request naming a row that does not exist reports the not-found family first — but ahead of the row's state examination, so a missing or mismatched token on a row that is also in the wrong state reports the conditional-request error rather than a state error. Endpoints that accept it additionally document an optional `if_match` request field alongside their body fields, which carries the same token for clients that prefer to send it in the body rather than in the header; when a body field is present it takes the place of the header for that request. The two write routes that enable the contract only when a token is actually supplied — countersigning a firm's pending agreement and terminating a firm's current agreement, both of which address the acting row through the firm named in their path rather than as a standalone path parameter — are the sole further exceptions: for those two a request carrying no token at all is not refused with `ERR_IF_MATCH_REQUIRED` and proceeds on the row identified by the path, while a token that is supplied and does not match the row's current `version` is still refused with `ERR_INVALID_IF_MATCH`. Reads never consult it, and a handful of state-changing routes are declared exempt outright: the invariant sweep, the data-feed pull (which is a read-like page delivery), and the driver-only clock set are not guarded by the conditional-request contract.

### 2.11. Custody Rule Evidence and the Examination Bundle

Custody rule compliance is modeled as a set of evidence requirements raised against a firm. Each `CustodyRulesEvidence` row names a `requirement_code`, carries a `critical` boolean, and moves through the lifecycle `PENDING` → `SUBMITTED` → `ACCEPTED` or `DEFICIENT` or `WAIVED`, with `LAPSED` reachable from `ACCEPTED` and `DEFICIENT` looping back to `SUBMITTED`. The permitted `requirement_code` values are `QUALIFIED_CUSTODIAN`, `SURPRISE_EXAMINATION`, `CLIENT_NOTIFICATION`, `TWO_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION`, `FEE_DISCLOSURE`, `ANNUAL_ATTESTATION`, `STANDING_INSTRUCTION_AUTHORIZATION` and `FEE_CREDIT_DISCLOSURE`; the permitted `requirement_status` values are `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DEFICIENT`, `WAIVED` and `LAPSED`; the terminal states are `WAIVED` and `LAPSED`.

A `critical` evidence row is one whose `DEFICIENT` verdict suspends the firm's posture — that is precisely the meaning of the flag, and it is what links this subsystem to §2.4's second rule. Non-critical rows may instead be waived, and only non-critical rows may be waived at all.

Correction rounds are counted, and the count is bounded. `evidence_round_number` starts at 0 and increments on every transition from `SUBMITTED` to `DEFICIENT`; a row sitting at 4 may not be resubmitted, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Accepted evidence is not permanent either: a row can lapse once a full annual window has elapsed since the firm's agreement was executed.

`ExaminationBundle` is the assembled evidence artifact for a half-open period. Its `bundle_status` takes the values `ASSEMBLING`, `GENERATED`, `INCOMPLETE` and `SEALED`; a bundle is opened `ASSEMBLING`, becomes `GENERATED` when nothing is missing or `INCOMPLETE` when something is, and may only be sealed from `GENERATED` — sealing anything else is refused with `ERR_BUNDLE_NOT_SEALABLE` (HTTP 409). When assembly finds gaps it reports them in `missing_evidence_codes`, a `generated_at` timestamp is stamped, and the assembly inputs and outputs are specified in full in §3.8.

### 2.12. Database Invariants and the Platform Configuration Surface

The platform maintains firm-scoped database invariants: rows of type `DatabaseInvariant` whose `invariant_code` is one of `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_CONSISTENCY`, `TRANSFER_BASIS_CONSERVATION`, `HOUSEHOLD_FEE_BASIS_RECONCILIATION` or `CASCADE_ECONOMIC_EFFECT`. Each row reports an `invariant_status` of either `HOLDING` or `BROKEN`, the quantities it compared in `expected_total_cents` and `observed_total_cents`, a `breach_detail_code` populated when the status is `BROKEN`, and a `last_evaluated_at` timestamp refreshed by the most recent evaluation sweep. Boolean invariants record 0 in both totals.

Whether a broken invariant merely reports or actually blocks writes is a platform-wide switch, held on the singleton `SystemConfig` row whose `config_key` is always `CUSTODY_PLATFORM_DEFAULTS`. Its `database_invariant_enforcement` field takes the values `WARN` and `ENFORCED` and defaults to `WARN`; under `ENFORCED`, a firm-scoped `BROKEN` invariant causes the guarded writes to be refused with `ERR_INVARIANT_CHAIN_BROKEN` (HTTP 409). Same as `platform_fee_enabled`, the enforcement value is advisory until a configuration row exists: with no row provisioned the platform reads the enforcement setting as `WARN`. The same row carries `platform_fee_enabled`, a boolean defaulting to true, which determines whether the platform-fee component contributes to the advisory fee ladder described in §3.4; the configuration row itself may be created once and is idempotent thereafter, a second creation call re-reporting the existing singleton rather than minting a rival. The configuration endpoints do not extend the `WARN`/`ENFORCED` value set or range-check it — an out-of-set value is stored as supplied and is simply never equal to `ENFORCED`.

The platform also runs on a logical clock rather than wall-clock time, because several of its rules are temporal. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read exactly that value until an operator advances it; it never silently falls back to the host's wall clock. Advancing the clock recomputes every derived temporal quantity in the system — `valuation_age_days`, `round_trip_days`, and every other window that is measured against "now".

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows the order in which an advisory firm actually experiences the platform: it registers, papers its custody relationship, is granted the entitlements it needs, migrates its book, trades, is billed, admits illiquid holdings, moves client money, and finally defends itself in an examination. Each subsection carries only the rules that belong to that stage, so a threshold met in one stage is often only explained in another. Cross-references to §2 point at the contracts that are common to all stages.

### 3.1. Registration, Agreement Drafting and Countersignature

A `PlatformAdmin` registers an adviser firm by supplying its `legal_name`, its `crd_number` and its `home_state_code`, the two-letter code of the state of its principal office. Both `legal_name` and `crd_number` are required and must be unique among the firm's peers — no `ARCHIVED` firm state exists in this domain, so every previously registered firm is a peer — and a collision on either field is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The registration response is a `201 Created` carrying the firm, whose `custody_agreement_status` begins at `PENDING`, whose `entitlement_count` begins at 0, and whose `household_fee_basis_total_cents` begins at 0; `home_state_code` is carried through from the request and is not itself validated. No custody-evidence rows and no database-invariant rows are created as a side effect of registration; those are raised deliberately later. Because the firm has no executed agreement at this point, the posture derivation of §2.4 lands on rule 1 and the firm is born `SUSPENDED` with `posture_reason_code` `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`, and it is that derived pair — not the value the caller supplied — that the registration response reports as `firm_posture` and `posture_reason_code`. That is expected, and it is also why a freshly registered firm cannot yet activate an account or provision a feed.

Agreements are drafted by an `AdvisorOperationsLead`, who supplies the `adviser_crd_number` the firm attests. A newly drafted `CustodyAgreement` begins at `agreement_status` `DRAFT`; the permitted values are `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `EXECUTED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `TERMINATED`, and `TERMINATED` is terminal. Two live drafts are permitted for the same firm at once — the interesting decision is not how many drafts exist but which of them is submitted.

Submission moves a `DRAFT` agreement to `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, and it carries three preconditions. The row must actually be in `DRAFT`; a submission against any other non-terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and a row that has left `DRAFT` for a countersigned or superseded state — `EXECUTED` or `SUPERSEDED` — or reached `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409) instead, terminal-set membership being examined ahead of the from-state mismatch. The firm's `crd_number` must equal the agreement's `adviser_crd_number` — a mismatch is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). And no sibling `CustodyAgreement` of the same firm may currently hold `agreement_status` of either `PENDING_SIGNATURE` or `EXECUTED`; submitting a second concurrent authority is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409) because the firm would otherwise have two competing authorities. On success the request's `X-User-ID` is recorded into `drafted_by_user_id`, which is the segregation-of-duties stamp that §2.5 compares against later. Submission does not disturb existing entitlements; the derived `payload_integrity_verified` of every entitlement of the firm is re-stamped to true exactly when its `requested_custody_agreement_id` equals the firm's current executed agreement and false otherwise, so an entitlement that was granted against a different executed agreement keeps its `entitlement_status` of `GRANTED` but has `payload_integrity_verified` recomputed to false, which is the platform's way of marking it as claimed against stale authority without revoking it prematurely.

A submitted agreement can be walked back. The drafter may revert it from `PENDING_SIGNATURE` to `DRAFT`, which clears `drafted_by_user_id` and nulls the submission stamp; the only precondition is that the row currently sits in `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, and a revert of an agreement that has already been countersigned, superseded or terminated is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409), while any other non-`PENDING_SIGNATURE` state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Countersignature is the custodian's act, performed by a `CustodyOperationsOfficer`. Executing the firm's pending agreement has three preconditions: the firm must have an agreement sitting in `PENDING_SIGNATURE`; the firm's `crd_number` must equal that agreement's `adviser_crd_number`; and the executing officer must differ from `drafted_by_user_id` (a same-officer attempt is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION`, HTTP 403, per §2.5). A firm with nothing in `PENDING_SIGNATURE` to execute is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) when it already holds a countersigned agreement — an executed authority cannot be countersigned a second time — and with `ERR_CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409) when it holds none at all. This operation is also blocked while the firm has an unacknowledged regulator action, in which case it is refused with `ERR_ADMIN_ACTION_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409). On success the agreement moves to `EXECUTED`, `executed_by_user_id` records the acting officer, `signed_at` is stamped, `countersignature_hash` is minted as an opaque tamper-evidence token, and the firm's own `custody_agreement_status` is set to `EXECUTED` with `agreement_executed_at` stamped to the same instant. Executing an agreement therefore restores a `SUSPENDED` firm to `OPEN` provided the other prerequisites of the posture derivation are satisfied.

Superession is the interesting part. When a newer agreement for the same firm is executed while an older one is `EXECUTED` and a third sat in `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, the older agreement moves to `SUPERSEDED` and records the `superseded_by_agreement_id` of the agreement that displaced it; the successor's own status is what the firm reports. That backward link is not cosmetic — it is what makes the old authority stop authorizing, because every `AdvisorFirmEntitlement` of the firm whose `requested_custody_agreement_id` is not the newly current agreement is cascaded from `GRANTED` to `REVOKED` at that moment. The revocation then bubbles one more hop: if the revoked entitlement's `entitlement_code` was `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` or `ALTERNATIVE_ASSETS`, every non-`ARCHIVED` `ModelPortfolio` of the firm has its `model_status` re-derived and is forced out of `ACTIVE` — one that was `ACTIVE` being moved to `REBALANCING`, while one that was `APPROVED` keeps its status but is flagged — and any subsequent entitlement-gated model write (model approval, model activation, or a rebalance entered from `APPROVED`) is refused with `ERR_MODEL_MANAGEMENT_ENTITLEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). A firm that re-papers its custody relationship therefore loses its model authority unless it re-grants it.

Termination is the final agreement transition and the most constrained. It is only available on the firm's current agreement, only from `EXECUTED`, and only when the firm has zero `HouseholdAccount` rows whose `account_status` is `OPEN` — an attempt while any account is still open is refused with `ERR_OPEN_ACCOUNTS_BLOCK_TERMINATION` (HTTP 409). On success the agreement moves to `TERMINATED`, the firm's `custody_agreement_status` becomes `TERMINATED`, and three cascades fire in one transaction: the firm drops to `SUSPENDED` with `posture_reason_code` `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_TERMINATED` — that reason is stamped explicitly over the ordinary first-match derivation, which on a firm left without an executed agreement would otherwise report `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`; every `AdvisorFirmEntitlement` of the firm currently in `GRANTED` transitions to `SUSPENDED`, while an entitlement still sitting in `REQUESTED` is left as it is; every non-terminal `BlockOrder` of the firm sitting in `SUBMITTED` transitions to `UNALLOCATED` with `unallocated_share_micros` set to the full `executed_share_micros`; and every `DataFeedProvision` of the firm in `ACTIVE` transitions to `THROTTLED` with `throttle_reason_code` `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`. A prior superseded agreement is not terminated by this operation — it is already inert.

### 3.2. Entitlements, Trading Authority and the Data Feed Surface

An entitlement is the platform's unit of granted authority, and it is always claimed against a specific agreement. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` requests one by supplying `firm_id`, an `entitlement_code`, and the `requested_custody_agreement_id` the firm claims authorizes it. The permitted codes are `TRADING_AUTHORIZATION`, `MONEY_MOVEMENT`, `MODEL_MANAGEMENT`, `BILLING`, `ALTERNATIVE_ASSETS`, `DATA_FEED` and `API_ACCESS`. A new entitlement begins at `entitlement_status` `REQUESTED`; the permitted values are `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED`, `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED`, and `REVOKED` is terminal for the machine even though the row itself can be reopened.

Granting is the custodian's act. A `CustodyOperationsOfficer` grants or re-grants an entitlement, and the operation is available from either `REQUESTED` or `SUSPENDED`, which makes `GRANTED` ↔ `SUSPENDED` a genuine cycle rather than a one-way edge — the posture derivation can suspend and restore the same entitlement repeatedly, in any order. Four preconditions apply. The firm's posture must be `OPEN` or `LIMITED`; a `SUSPENDED` or `FROZEN` firm's grant attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The firm must hold an `EXECUTED` agreement, and the entitlement's `requested_custody_agreement_id` must name the firm's current executed agreement — a claim against a superseded authority is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_AGREEMENT_SUPERSEDED` (HTTP 409), and a firm with no executed agreement at all is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED`. Finally, no sibling `AdvisorFirmEntitlement` of the same firm may already hold the same `entitlement_code` in `GRANTED`; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409), and the same duplicate check guards the initial request of an entitlement as well as the grant. On success `effective_from` is stamped with the current instant, the derived `payload_integrity_verified` becomes true, and the firm's `entitlement_count` increases by one. Granting a `BILLING` entitlement has an extra consequence worth noting: every household of the firm re-derives its `household_status`, and a household whose `householding_consent` is `OP_OUT` and which holds more than one billable account transitions from `ACTIVE` to `BILLING_HELD`.

Revocation is reserved to a `ComplianceReviewer` and — on the explicit path — is available only from `SUSPENDED`; a live `GRANTED` entitlement cannot be revoked directly, it must first be suspended by the posture machinery. Revoking nulls `effective_from` and, for `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` and `ALTERNATIVE_ASSETS`, forces every non-`ARCHIVED` model of the firm to a status where its next write fails with `ERR_MODEL_MANAGEMENT_ENTITLEMENT_REQUIRED`. The same two-code bubble also fires when an entitlement is cascaded from `GRANTED` to `REVOKED` because its agreement was superseded, and when an entitlement is cascaded from `GRANTED` to `SUSPENDED` because the firm's posture dropped; in the suspension case the model is forced out of `ACTIVE` but the entitlement remains restorable.

Reopening closes the cycle. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` returns a `REVOKED` entitlement to `REQUESTED`, which re-points `requested_custody_agreement_id` at the firm's current executed agreement and sets `payload_integrity_verified` back to true. Two preconditions govern it: the row must be `REVOKED`, and the firm must hold an executed agreement — with no executed agreement the attempt is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). Note that reopening does not grant anything; the entitlement must be granted again from `REQUESTED`, which is what re-runs the duplicate and current-agreement checks.

Entitlements are enforced, not merely recorded. An operation that needs a named entitlement whose `entitlement_status` is anything other than `GRANTED` — that is, `REQUESTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` — is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 403). The gated operations are: model approval, model rebalancing and model activation, all of which require `MODEL_MANAGEMENT`; block submission, which requires both `TRADING_AUTHORIZATION` and `MODEL_MANAGEMENT`; billing-run seeding, which requires `BILLING`; submitting an alternative valuation, which requires `ALTERNATIVE_ASSETS`; feed provisioning, which requires `DATA_FEED`; and account activation, which requires either `TRADING_AUTHORIZATION` or `BILLING`.

Data feeds are the technology-integration half of the entitlement surface. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` requests a feed by naming `firm_id`, a `feed_type` and the `delivery_endpoint_host` it should be delivered to; the permitted `feed_type` values are `POSITIONS`, `TRANSACTIONS`, `BALANCES`, `FEES` and `ALTERNATIVES`. A new `DataFeedProvision` begins at `REQUESTED`, and the permitted `provision_status` values are `REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE`, `THROTTLED` and `REVOKED`, with `REVOKED` terminal.

Turning a feed on requires the row to be `REQUESTED`, the firm to hold a granted `DATA_FEED` entitlement, and the firm's posture to be exactly `OPEN` — a posture that is merely `LIMITED` is refused with `ERR_FIRM_NOT_OPEN_FOR_TRADING` (HTTP 409). On success `provision_status` becomes `ACTIVE` and `throttle_reason_code` is cleared to null. Throttling is not a client action: whenever the firm's posture is re-derived to `LIMITED`, `SUSPENDED` or `FROZEN`, every feed of the firm then `ACTIVE` moves to `THROTTLED` and `throttle_reason_code` is set to the posture reason code binding on the firm at that moment — `CUSTODY_EVIDENCE_DEFICIENT`, `ENTITLEMENT_REVOKED`, `ADMIN_ACTION_UNACKNOWLEDGED`, `REGULATORY_FREEZE`, `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED` or `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`, as the case may be. A broken invariant chain does not by itself throttle a feed. Restoration runs the other way: a `THROTTLED` feed returns to `ACTIVE`, with `throttle_reason_code` cleared, whenever the firm's posture is next re-derived to `OPEN`; the invariant sweep is not itself what restores a feed, so a firm that stays `LIMITED` keeps its feeds throttled straight through a successful sweep. Revocation is available to a `ComplianceReviewer` from both `THROTTLED` and `ACTIVE`.

Pulling a page from a feed is where the throttle becomes visible. The request carries `limit` and `offset` and is refused with `ERR_FEED_THROTTLED` (HTTP 409) if the feed's `provision_status` is `THROTTLED`. A successful pull delivers one page of records, increments `provisioned_records_count` by the number of records in that page, records the page size for the current call in `delivered_count` — which is 0 before the first pull — and stamps `last_delivered_at`.

### 3.3. Book Transition: Onboarding Batches, Asset Transfers and Basis Carryover

Migrating a book of business is the platform's bulk operation, and it is modeled as an `OnboardingBatch`. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` creates one by naming `firm_id`, a `batch_kind`, an `items` collection and an optional `chunk_size`; the permitted kinds are `ACCOUNTS`, `TRANSFERS`, `LOTS` and `EVIDENCE`. The kind selects which materially different row shape the batch describes: `ACCOUNTS` materializes `HouseholdAccount` rows, `TRANSFERS` materializes `AssetTransfer` rows together with their lots, `LOTS` appends `TransferLot` rows to an existing transfer, and `EVIDENCE` materializes `CustodyRulesEvidence` rows. A batch begins at `batch_status` `CREATED`, and the permitted values are `CREATED`, `DISPATCHED` and `COMPLETED`; a dispatch moves a batch straight from `CREATED` to `COMPLETED` in one step, so `DISPATCHED` is a permitted value the platform never actually enters. Creation reports `requested_item_count`, the number of items submitted, and does not itself reject a `batch_kind` outside the permitted four — that is reported at dispatch as an unreachable area.

`chunk_size` governs how many items are materialized per dispatch chunk; when it is omitted the platform materializes the whole batch in one chunk. Dispatch is idempotent by design, and this is the property clients should rely on: a repeated dispatch skips rows whose natural key already exists rather than duplicating them, so `dispatched_item_count` is stable across retries and `skipped_duplicate_count` reports how many items the most recent dispatch skipped. The natural key is the `account_number` for an `ACCOUNTS` batch, the `requirement_code` for an `EVIDENCE` batch, the `symbol` within the target transfer for a `LOTS` batch, and the `delivering_firm_name`/`method` pair for a `TRANSFERS` batch; an item whose key collides with a row already materialized is skipped, while an item introducing a new key is materialized even if it shares every other field with an earlier row. A dispatch against a batch that is already `COMPLETED` succeeds as a further no-op: it re-reports the batch as it stands with its stored counts unchanged rather than being refused, and it does not re-run the reachability check below. A dispatch is refused — and refused with the same `ERR_ONBOARDING_BATCH_NOT_DISPATCHABLE` (HTTP 409) that signals an unreachable area — when the area named by the batch kind is not reachable: for an `ACCOUNTS` batch the household must be non-`CLOSED`; for a `TRANSFERS` batch the receiving account must be `OPEN` or `FROZEN`; for a `LOTS` batch the named `transfer_id` must already exist; and for an `EVIDENCE` batch the firm must already have a database row of the kind's entity. An item skipped because its natural key already exists is not an error at all — it is reported through `skipped_duplicate_count` — so the error identifier is reserved for a genuinely unreachable area.

A single account can also be opened on its own rather than through a batch. The creating `AdvisorOperationsLead` supplies the `household_id`, the servicing `firm_id`, an `account_number`, an `account_type` and the client's `householding_consent`. The household must belong to the named firm, or the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). `account_number` is required and must be unique among the firm's non-`CLOSED` accounts, a collision being refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. The permitted `account_type` values are `INDIVIDUAL`, `JOINT`, `TRUST`, `IRA`, `ROTH_IRA` and `ENTITY`, and a value outside that set is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) rather than accepted. `householding_consent` defaults to `NONE` when omitted; the permitted values are `OP_IN`, `OP_OUT` and `NONE`, and the value is not itself validated at creation. A new account begins at `account_status` `PENDING`; the permitted values are `PENDING`, `OPEN`, `FROZEN` and `CLOSED`, and `CLOSED` is terminal. A `PENDING` account is not billable-basis-bearing yet — only `OPEN` or `FROZEN` accounts count toward the fee basis of §3.4, so a freshly created account contributes nothing until it is activated. Its `model_portfolio_id` is null until a model is assigned, and `last_listed_cents` and `last_listed_at` are unset until the account is first listed.

Activation is the custodian's decision and is gated twice over. A `CustodyOperationsOfficer` moves a `PENDING` account to `OPEN` when the firm's posture is `OPEN` or `LIMITED`, when the firm holds an executed agreement, and when at least one of the firm's entitlements with code `TRADING_AUTHORIZATION` or `BILLING` is `GRANTED`. If the firm holds entitlements but the one that applies is not granted, the refusal is `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED`; if the account's firm has no executed agreement, the refusal is `ERR_CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED`. A newly opened account triggers the owning household to recompute `household_fee_basis_cents`.

Freezing and unfreezing belong to a `ComplianceReviewer`. An `OPEN` account can be frozen, and a `FROZEN` one can be unfrozen when the firm's posture is `OPEN` or `LIMITED` — a firm that is `SUSPENDED` or `FROZEN` blocks the unfreeze with `ERR_FIRM_NOT_OPEN_FOR_TRADING` (HTTP 409), while the freeze itself imposes no posture condition beyond the firm not being `FROZEN`. Freezing has consequences that reach well beyond the account: every `BlockAllocation` of that account whose `allocation_status` is `PENDING` is excluded from the next allocation run, and — one hop further — the parent `BlockOrder` recomputes `unallocated_share_micros` as its executed total less the shares already assigned to non-`PENDING` allocations. A block that had reached `ALLOCATED` or `UNALLOCATED` is re-labelled from that refreshed residual, entering `UNALLOCATED` when it becomes non-zero and `ALLOCATED` when it returns to zero; a block still sitting in `EXECUTED` has its residual refreshed to the full executed quantity but keeps the `EXECUTED` status, since it has not yet been through an allocation run. Unfreezing an account does not itself recompute any block; it is what makes an `UNALLOCATED` block recoverable, because the previously excluded accounts become eligible again and a fresh allocation run then re-derives the residual from the now-larger pool of eligible allocations. Both directions recompute the household's `household_fee_basis_cents`, since the account remains in the fee basis while frozen but drops out once closed.

Closing is only permitted from `OPEN` or `FROZEN` and is guarded by in-flight work. From `OPEN`, the account must have zero `AssetTransfer` rows in `REQUESTED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED` or `BASIS_REVIEW`, and zero `MoneyMovementInstruction` rows in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` or `AUTHORIZED`. From `FROZEN`, the movement condition is dropped — only transfers block the close — and the firm's posture may additionally be `SUSPENDED`. Closing sets `account_status` to `CLOSED`, drops the account out of the household fee basis, and revokes every `StandingInstruction` of the account that is in `VERIFIED`.

Listing is a separate, parallel dimension of an account's state that advances while the lifecycle dimension stands still. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` may run a listing against an account in `PENDING`, `OPEN` or `FROZEN`; the lifecycle state is unchanged by the operation. Listing recomputes the account's presented value and writes the traditional sleeve into `last_listed_cents` and the run's instant into `last_listed_at`. The presented total is the traditional sleeve — each position's `quantity_micros` multiplied by its `valuation_price_micros` and divided by ten billion, summed across the account's positions and rounded half up to whole cents at the position level — plus the alternative sleeve, which is the sum of `accepted_value_cents` over the account's alternative holdings that are both `ACCEPTED` and not stale. Note the deliberate asymmetry: `last_listed_cents` records the traditional sleeve only and excludes the alternative sleeve, whereas the fee basis described in §3.4 includes the accepted alternative sleeve, so the two derivations differ by exactly the accepted alternative value. Listing also refreshes the firm-level reconciliation described in §3.8, and it is cooldown-guarded: a listing requested inside the account's one-hour listing window is refused with `ERR_LISTING_COOLDOWN` (HTTP 409). The window is the half-open interval from the account's previous `last_listed_at` up to but not including one hour after it, and the request acknowledges which calendar slot it is claiming by way of the account's conditional-request token.

Two accounts can be moved into the same household too: relinking reassigns an account's `household_id` to a `target_household_id` and re-derives the fee basis and annual fee of both the old and the new household, which is why §2.10 requires the destination household's `expected_household_version` alongside the account's own conditional token.

Transfers are the per-asset half of the migration. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` opens one by naming the `receiving_account_id`, the receiving `firm_id`, the `delivering_firm_name` and a `method`; the permitted methods are `ACAT`, `DIRECT_ROLLOVER` and `DTC_NON_ACAT`, though the value is not validated against that set at creation. The receiving account must belong to the named firm, or the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A new `AssetTransfer` begins at `transfer_status` `REQUESTED`, and the permitted values are `REQUESTED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED`, `REJECTED`, `BASIS_REVIEW` and `CREDITED`, with `CREDITED` and `REJECTED` terminal; its `carryover_cents` begins at 0 and `cost_basis_complete` at false. Incoming positions are described as `TransferLot` rows added against the transfer — each new lot written with `transfer_id` set to the transfer that owns it, and each carrying a `symbol`, a `quantity_micros`, the delivering firm's reported `sent_cost_basis_cents` (null when none was reported), the original `lot_inventory_date`, and the custodian's `valuation_price_micros` for the lot; a new lot begins at `lot_status` `PENDING`. The account's `market_value_cents` is the sum over the lots applied to it — those whose `lot_status` is `APPLIED` and whose parent transfer receives into the account — of the quantity at the valuation price, divided by ten billion; lots still `PENDING` or `STAMPED`, and lots on a transfer that was rejected, contribute nothing.

Submitting a transfer to the delivering firm requires the row to be `REQUESTED`, the receiving account to be `OPEN` or `FROZEN` — anything else is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409) — and the firm's posture to be `OPEN` or `LIMITED`. Recording receipt is a `CustodyOperationsOfficer` action available from `SUBMITTED`; it forks the transfer, and the fork is the heart of the model. On receipt every lot is stamped with its `basis_confidence_grade` and its `suspected_double_basis` flag, the receiving account recomputes `market_value_cents` from its positions, and the owning household recomputes `household_fee_basis_cents`.

If every lot arrived with a non-null `sent_cost_basis_cents`, the transfer proceeds straight to `CREDITED`: `carryover_cents` and `sent_basis_confidence` take the carryover results described below, `cost_basis_complete` takes the value the grading rule dictates — true only when the transfer carries at least one lot and every one of its lots graded `HIGH`, so a transfer whose lots all reported a basis but graded `MEDIUM` or `LOW` credits with `cost_basis_complete` false — `credited_at` is stamped, and each lot's `basis_appraisal_cents` is set to its own reported cost basis. A transfer received with no lots at all also credits immediately, with `carryover_cents` 0, `sent_basis_confidence` `NONE`, and `cost_basis_complete` false. If instead at least one lot arrived with a null `sent_cost_basis_cents`, the transfer detours to `BASIS_REVIEW`, with `carryover_cents` forced to 0 and `cost_basis_complete` forced to false, and the unbased lots stay out of the receiving account's fee basis until a basis is accepted. This detour is triggered by the lot state established by the receipt hop rather than by the original receive request, which is what makes the fork real.

From `BASIS_REVIEW` an `AdvisorOperationsLead` may accept the completed basis package: the row must be in `BASIS_REVIEW`, every lot must now carry a non-null `sent_cost_basis_cents` — failing that, the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and the firm must hold a `QUALIFIED_CUSTODIAN` evidence row in `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED` — without that evidence the attempt is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). Acceptance credits the transfer with the same grading rule as the straight-through path — `cost_basis_complete` true only when every lot grades `HIGH` — sets `carryover_cents` and `sent_basis_confidence` to the carryover results, and stamps `credited_at`, then recomputes the receiving account's `market_value_cents` and `account_fee_basis_cents` and, one hop further, the owning household's `household_annual_fee_cents`. A `CustodyOperationsOfficer` may instead reject the package, which requires `BASIS_REVIEW` and a non-empty `rejected_reason_code`; rejection moves the transfer to `REJECTED`, forces `carryover_cents` to 0, discards each of the transfer's lots to `DISCARDED` with `basis_appraisal_cents` nulled so they leave the receiving account's positions, and recomputes the household's fee basis. The same `rejected_reason_code` requirement governs rejecting a transfer earlier, from `REQUESTED`, which simply moves it to `REJECTED` without touching any lots — the free-form reason is recorded on the row either way, and a blank reason is refused.

Lots have their own small linear pipeline layered underneath the transfer's. A lot begins at `PENDING` and is stamped to `STAMPED` by the receipt hop, when its `basis_confidence_grade` and `suspected_double_basis` are written. It is then driven to `APPLIED` when the parent transfer reaches `CREDITED`, at which point its `basis_appraisal_cents` receives the accepted basis and the receiving account's position set gains the lot for fee-basis purposes; the parent reaching `CREDITED` and a non-null `sent_cost_basis_cents` are both required. If the parent instead reaches `REJECTED`, the lot is discarded to `DISCARDED` with `basis_appraisal_cents` nulled. `APPLIED` and `DISCARDED` are the lot's terminal states.

Cost-basis carryover is graded, and the grading rule is chronological. The transfer's lots are processed in ascending `lot_inventory_date`, with `symbol` ascending as the tie-break. The transfer as a whole is flagged as suspected double basis when its `method` is `ACAT` and an earlier `AssetTransfer` into the same `receiving_account_id` already has `transfer_status` `CREDITED` and shares at least one symbol with this transfer; that single flag is then applied to every lot of the transfer.

Each lot is then graded in the following order. A lot with a null `sent_cost_basis_cents` grades `NONE`. Otherwise, if the transfer is suspected of double basis, the lot grades `LOW`. Otherwise, if the lot's `lot_inventory_date` precedes the instant at which the transfer was submitted, the lot grades `HIGH`; any other lot with a non-null reported basis grades `MEDIUM`. The permitted grades are `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW` and `NONE`, and the same four values are the permitted set for the transfer-level `sent_basis_confidence`. The transfer's `carryover_cents` is the sum of `sent_cost_basis_cents` over its `HIGH`-graded lots only, and `cost_basis_complete` is true exactly when every received position was stamped with a `HIGH`-confidence basis.

Two exceptions keep the grading honest. A firm-to-firm delivery — an `ACAT` or `DTC_NON_ACAT` transfer — grades every lot and carries only the `HIGH`-graded basis, leaving `MEDIUM`, `LOW` and `NONE` lots uncosted. A `DIRECT_ROLLOVER`, by contrast, is graded under the same single rule — a null-basis lot `NONE`, every non-null-basis lot `HIGH` or `MEDIUM` since rollovers are exempt from the double-basis suspicion and so never downgraded to `LOW` — but its `carryover_cents` is special: the rollover carries every non-null reported basis regardless of the grade that lot received, not just the `HIGH`-graded ones. Lots are graded regardless of size, including `quantity_micros` of 0, which contributes nothing to the carried total but still participates in grading; a rejected transfer contributes nothing at all and its lots are discarded.

One unit note deserves emphasis because it is a common source of double counting. Carryover operates on a transfer's inbound lots, where each `sent_cost_basis_cents` is a whole-cent amount for the entire lot, whereas alternative-asset valuation operates on a single holding's stated value. The two chains are nested rather than overlapping: a holding that arrives as a transfer lot keeps its carried basis until it is classified as an alternative asset, at which point the haircut chain described in §3.6 takes over and the carried basis is excluded from the newly created `AlternativeAssetHolding`, so the same cents are never counted twice in the fee basis.

### 3.4. Model Portfolios, Target Weights and the Trading Path

A model portfolio is how an adviser expresses an investment strategy before executing it. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` creates one by supplying `firm_id` and a `model_name`; it begins at `model_status` `DRAFT`, and the permitted values are `DRAFT`, `APPROVED`, `REBALANCING`, `ACTIVE` and `ARCHIVED`, with `ARCHIVED` terminal. `approval_round_number` starts at 0 and increments by one on each approval; `drift_approved_at` is unset until the first approval; and `target_weight_total_bps` begins at 0 and is thereafter the derived sum of the model's target lines.

Target lines are the model's substance. Adding one supplies a `symbol` and a `target_weight_bps` weight, and `symbol` must be unique within the model — a repeat symbol is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Replacing a line's weight is also possible, and both operations recompute `target_weight_total_bps`. The invariant that governs the whole model is that the target weights must sum to exactly 10000 basis points at approval time; an operation that checks it is refused with `ERR_MODEL_WEIGHTS_INVALID` (HTTP 422) when the sum differs from 10000 by any amount. The sum is checked in exactly three places: approving the model, and adding a target line to a model that is already `APPROVED`. Replacing a line's weight never checks the sum, and adding or replacing a target line on a `DRAFT` model never checks it either, so a draft may legitimately sit at any total until it is approved.

Approval is the investment committee chair's act and produces a genuinely cyclic machine. An `InvestmentCommitteeChair` approves a model from either `DRAFT` or `REBALANCING`, and in both cases the sum must be exactly 10000; the approval requires the firm to hold a `GRANTED` `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` entitlement — refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 403) when none is live — and a posture of `OPEN` or `LIMITED`. On success `model_status` becomes `APPROVED`, `drift_approved_at` is stamped, `approval_round_number` increases by one, and any prior entitlement-blocked flag on the model is cleared. Because rebalancing returns a model to `APPROVED` through the same path, each traversal of the loop bumps the round counter, so a model that has been approved, rebalanced and approved again is observably on round 2. Attempting to approve an `ACTIVE` model is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and a re-approval attempted on an already-`ARCHIVED` model with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Rebalancing is available from both `APPROVED` and `ACTIVE`, and it is the operation guarded by the optimistic-concurrency contract of §2.10: the request must carry `expected_model_portfolio_version` matching the model's current `version`, and a stale value is refused with `ERR_STALE_MODEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Entering rebalancing from `APPROVED` additionally requires a `GRANTED` `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` entitlement; the `ACTIVE` path does not restate that requirement. Both paths set `model_status` to `REBALANCING` and submit the model's draft blocks.

Activation is what puts a model to work. From `APPROVED`, an `InvestmentCommitteeChair` activates it provided the firm holds a `GRANTED` `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` entitlement and at least one of the firm's accounts is already assigned to this model through its `model_portfolio_id` — activating a model nothing points at is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Both checks precede the posture gate, so a `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` entitlement that is not live is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 403), and any of the trading-path posture requirements being unmet is refused with `ERR_FIRM_NOT_OPEN_FOR_TRADING` (HTTP 409). On success the model becomes `ACTIVE`, every account of the firm assigned to this model that is still `PENDING` is opened, and the owning households of every assigned account — whether newly opened or not — recompute both `household_fee_basis_cents` and `household_annual_fee_cents`.

Archiving is available from `DRAFT` freely, and from `APPROVED` only when no account of the firm is assigned to the model. A model in any other non-`ARCHIVED` state — `REBALANCING` or `ACTIVE` — cannot be archived and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, while re-archiving a model already `ARCHIVED` is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. A model is also forced toward archival by the entitlement machinery: when a `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` or `ALTERNATIVE_ASSETS` entitlement is revoked or suspended — because an agreement was superseded or the firm's posture dropped — every non-`ARCHIVED` model of the firm is driven out of `ACTIVE`, a model that was `ACTIVE` being moved to `REBALANCING` and a model that was `APPROVED` keeping its status but being flagged, and the next entitlement-gated model write in the firm (approval, activation, or a rebalance entered from `APPROVED`) fails with `ERR_MODEL_MANAGEMENT_ENTITLEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409) so long as any of the firm's models still carries that flag.

Block orders are how a model's intent reaches the desk. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` drafts one by naming the `firm_id`, the `model_id` it implements, the `symbol`, the `side` (`BUY` or `SELL`), the `executed_share_micros` actually filled at the desk, and the single `execution_price_micros`. A block begins at `block_status` `DRAFT`, and the permitted values are `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `EXECUTED`, `ALLOCATED`, `SETTLED` and `UNALLOCATED`, with `SETTLED` terminal; its `total_requested_share_micros` begins at 0 and `unallocated_share_micros` begins null, unset until an allocation or freeze recomputes it. The model named must belong to the same firm, or the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Allocations are the pre-trade intentions recorded against a block; each one is written with `block_order_id` naming the block that owns it. Adding one supplies the destination `account_id`, the `requested_share_micros` the account asked for, and a client-supplied `allocation_rank` that is unique within the block and serves as the deterministic tie-break for leftover shares — lower rank wins; a duplicate rank is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_CONSISTENT` (HTTP 422), and the account must belong to the block's firm or the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. A new allocation begins at `allocation_status` `PENDING` with `share_micros`, `allocated_cost_cents` and `average_price_micros` all null. The block's derived `total_requested_share_micros` is the sum of its allocations' requested quantities and is recomputed as allocations are added, counting every allocation regardless of the state of the account it targets.

Submitting a block is gated on a tight set of conditions, examined in this order: the row must be `DRAFT`; every allocation must target an account whose `account_status` is `OPEN`; the parent model must still be `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`; the firm must hold `GRANTED` `TRADING_AUTHORIZATION` and `GRANTED` `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` entitlements; the firm's posture must be exactly `OPEN`; and the requested total must be at least the executed total with no duplicated `allocation_rank` within the block. The order matters because several can fail at once. Otherwise-eligible accounts aside, an allocation pointing at a non-`OPEN` account is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409) ahead of the model check, a block whose parent model has left `APPROVED` and `ACTIVE` behind is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409) ahead of the entitlement checks, and a missing `TRADING_AUTHORIZATION` or `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` entitlement is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 403) ahead of the requested-total consistency check. If requested shares sum to less than the executed total, or if an `allocation_rank` is duplicated within the block, the submission is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_CONSISTENT` (HTTP 422). The requested total consulted here counts every allocation regardless of the state of its target account, so a block whose requested sum is pulled below the executed total only because some targets are frozen is refused as inconsistent rather than as ineligible.

Executing a submitted block records the desk fill and moves it to `EXECUTED`. Execution is also where the whole-order posture gate applies: a posture that is not exactly `OPEN` at execution time is refused with `ERR_FIRM_NOT_OPEN_FOR_TRADING` (HTTP 409). A block that is still `SUBMITTED` when the firm's posture drops to `SUSPENDED` or `FROZEN` is cascaded to `UNALLOCATED` with `unallocated_share_micros` set to the full `executed_share_micros`, which is the mechanism by which a firm walking into trouble stops trading mid-flight.

Allocation is the arithmetic core of the trading path, and it is pinned. It is available from `EXECUTED` and from `UNALLOCATED` as well, so a block driven to `UNALLOCATED` because none of its targets was eligible can be allocated again once those accounts become eligible; it requires `expected_block_order_version` to match the block's current `version` per §2.10 — a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — and the block must carry at least one allocation row and a non-zero executed quantity, failing which it is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ALLOCATION_TARGET` (HTTP 409). A block whose allocations all target non-`OPEN` accounts is not refused on that ground alone: no allocation is eligible, so the arithmetic simply leaves the full residual unassigned. The arithmetic proceeds in this order. First, the eligible allocations' `requested_share_micros` are summed to form the requested total, counting only allocations whose account is `OPEN`; allocations targeting an account that is not `OPEN` are excluded from this sum and receive no shares at all, keeping `allocation_status` `PENDING` and `share_micros` null. Second, each included allocation's exact entitlement is its requested quantity multiplied by the executed total and divided by the requested total; its base quantity is that exact figure floored to a whole micro-share, and its remainder is the fractional part left over. Third, the residual — the executed total minus the sum of the base quantities — is handed out one micro-share at a time in descending remainder order, ties broken by ascending `allocation_rank`; the units advance down that order rather than piling onto the same allocation while another is still available, so an allocation receives a second residual unit only if more units remain than there are included allocations. Each allocation's `share_micros` is its base quantity plus one if it won a residual unit. Fourth, each allocation's `allocated_cost_cents` is its share quantity multiplied by the execution price and divided by ten billion, rounded half up to whole cents. Fifth, each allocation's `average_price_micros` is its allocated cost multiplied by ten billion and divided by its share quantity, rounded half up to whole micros. The permitted `allocation_status` values are `PENDING`, `ALLOCATED`, `REBATED` and `SETTLED`; filled allocations become `ALLOCATED`. The block's `unallocated_share_micros` is the executed total minus the shares actually assigned, and the block becomes `ALLOCATED` when that residual is zero and `UNALLOCATED` otherwise. For a `SELL` block the direction of the allocation reverses but the arithmetic is identical. A block whose executed quantity is zero never reaches allocation at all.

Allocation has two deliberately different costing orders, and the difference is observable. The pinned production order rounds each allocation's cost to the cent independently, so the sum of the allocations' allocated costs equals the block notional with no residual cent left over. An indicative preview — a read-only capability — instead reports the block-level notional rounded once, which is the figure a round-then-split variant would start from and which for a three-way split sits one cent below the production order's independently-rounded sum whenever more than one allocation rounds up. The preview writes no allocation rows and mints no cents of its own; it only records the indicative figure on the block as `preview_notional_cents` — null until a preview has actually been run — and deliberately leaves the block's `version` and `updated_at` untouched, so a preview never invalidates a subsequent allocation run. The preview accepts its own `executed_share_micros` and `execution_price_micros` in the body, falling back to the block's stored fill when either is omitted, so that alternative fills can be modelled.

A settled block is not automatically a correct one, so a compensating invariant runs on both allocation and settlement. The block notional is the executed quantity at the execution price divided by ten billion, rounded half up to whole cents. The allocated total is the sum of the allocations' `allocated_cost_cents`. If the allocated total exceeds the block notional, the positive drift is distributed back as execution credits of one cent at a time, in descending `allocated_cost_cents` order with ascending `allocation_rank` breaking ties, with each credited allocation's `fee_rebate_cents` increased, its `allocation_status` set to `REBATED`, and its `average_price_micros` recomputed after the credit. The invariant the platform guarantees afterwards is that the sum of allocated costs minus the sum of the rebates equals the block notional exactly. This applies to both `BUY` and `SELL` blocks.

Where the drift lands depends on how far the block has progressed. Because the settlement endpoint is entered only from `ALLOCATED`, every drift the platform actually observes at settlement lands on the allocations themselves: the positive cent-at-a-time rebate is applied to the allocations, each credited allocation taking `fee_rebate_cents` and the `REBATED` status. The alternative household-level path — crediting the drift to the owning household as a `FeeCredit` instead, leaving `allocated_cost_cents` untouched — is retained as a defensive branch but is not reachable through the declared API, since no operation both leaves a block `SETTLED` and re-opens it to allocation. Where the household path did apply it would first perform a block-level reset, zeroing `fee_rebate_cents` and returning allocation statuses from `REBATED` to `ALLOCATED`, so that the block-level and household-level paths are provably exclusive and drift is never compensated twice; when that path does fire it credits the whole drift as a single `FeeCredit` on the household owning the largest allocation, with the block notional's drift never split across more than one household.

Settlement is the custodian's confirmation. It requires the block to be `ALLOCATED` and `unallocated_share_micros` to be exactly 0; a block with an outstanding residual is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). Settlement moves the block to `SETTLED`, moves every allocation to `SETTLED`, recomputes the receiving accounts' `market_value_cents`, and — one hop further — has each owning household recompute `household_fee_basis_cents`. It also re-runs the compensating invariant so that any over-allocation credit reaches the affected allocations.

### 3.5. Alternative Asset Custody and Valuation Acceptance

Illiquid interests enter the platform as `AlternativeAssetHolding` rows attached to a custody account. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` opens one by naming the `account_id`, the `asset_name`, a `unit_count`, the `stated_value_cents` the adviser supplied, a `valuation_floor_cents` below which the accepted value may never fall, and the `valuation_as_of_date` the stated value represents. A new holding begins at `asset_acceptance_status` `PENDING`, and the permitted values are `PENDING`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED`, with `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` terminal. `stated_value_cents` is never used directly for billing — its only role is as the input to the haircut chain.

Submitting a valuation for review moves the holding to `UNDER_REVIEW`. It requires the row to be in `PENDING` and the firm to hold a `GRANTED` `ALTERNATIVE_ASSETS` entitlement; a request missing that entitlement is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 403). Submission recomputes `valuation_age_days` as the number of whole days between the current instant and `valuation_as_of_date`, and refreshes `stale_valuation`. The submitted as-of date must not land on a calendar day already occupied by an accepted valuation for the same `asset_name` on the same account; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VALUATION_PERIOD` (HTTP 409).

Acceptance is a `ComplianceReviewer`'s decision and runs the haircut calculation. Four gates must pass together, and they are examined in the order given here so that a request failing more than one reports the earliest. First the valuation age must be at most 270 days — a holding older than that may not be admitted to the fee basis at all, and is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_STALE` (HTTP 422). Next, the account may not host another holding in `UNDER_REVIEW` under a different `asset_name`, because the binding to an accepted value could not otherwise be resolved; the ambiguity is refused with `ERR_AMBIGUOUS_VALUATION_BINDING` (HTTP 409). Then the stated value must be at least the holding's `valuation_floor_cents`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_BELOW_FLOOR` (HTTP 422). And the account may not host another holding with the same `asset_name` in `UNDER_REVIEW`, which is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ALTERNATIVE_HOLDING` (HTTP 409).

When the gates pass, the accepted value is the holding's stated value reduced by the applicable haircut percentage, then clamped so that it is never below `valuation_floor_cents` and never above `stated_value_cents`. The haircut percentage is not taken from the request; it is selected by the valuation's age. Every valuation that reaches acceptance has already passed the 270-day gate, so the 2500-basis-point haircut — 25.00 per cent — is the only percentage the platform ever applies; the alternative 3500-basis-point band exists in the catalogue of constants but is not reachable through the acceptance path, a valuation whose stated value is within the floor suffering the 2500-point reduction rather than any lesser one. The haircut actually applied is reported in basis points on the holding as `haircut_pct`. The product is rounded half up to whole cents. `accepted_value_cents` receives the clamped result, and the derived `fee_basis_eligible` becomes true.

A holding older than the 270-day threshold is never admitted: `accepted_value_cents` stays null and `stale_valuation` becomes true, which removes the holding from the fee-basis aggregation even if a later call re-supplies a value. `stale_valuation` is exactly the boolean that says the holding's valuation age exceeds 270 days, and it is the flag that excludes an otherwise-accepted holding from the basis. Acceptance has two downstream effects in the same transaction: the holding's account gains the sum of `accepted_value_cents` over its eligible holdings, and the owning household recomputes `household_fee_basis_cents`. Rejecting a submitted valuation moves the holding to `REJECTED`, nulls `accepted_value_cents` and sets `fee_basis_eligible` false, with the same account and household recomputation following. Neither operation validates any reason code — the rejection records no reason on the holding at all.

### 3.6. Fee Basis Aggregation and the Advisory Fee Ladder

The platform's billing model rests on one aggregate quantity, the household fee basis, which is rebuilt from the accounts beneath it. An account contributes to the household basis only when its `account_status` is `OPEN` or `FROZEN`, when its `billable` flag is true, and when the firm's posture is `OPEN` or `LIMITED`. Accounts in `PENDING` or `CLOSED` are excluded; `ENTITY` accounts are excluded by their `billable` flag; a position with a quantity of zero contributes nothing; and a holding with `stale_valuation` true contributes nothing even when it is `ACCEPTED`.

For each included account the platform derives three sub-totals. The traditional value is the sum over the account's positions of quantity multiplied by valuation price and divided by ten billion, rounded half up. The traditional basis is the sum over the account's positions of the greater of that position's market value and its cost basis, rounded half up; a position's cost basis is the lot's accepted `basis_appraisal_cents` once it has one, and otherwise the delivering firm's reported `sent_cost_basis_cents`, treated as zero when neither figure exists. The alternative basis is the sum of `accepted_value_cents` over the account's alternative holdings that are `ACCEPTED` and not stale. The account's `account_fee_basis_cents` is its traditional basis plus its alternative basis, and the household's `household_fee_basis_cents` is the sum of `account_fee_basis_cents` over its included accounts. Because every per-position value is already rounded before it is summed, the account total is an integer sum of rounded values rather than a rounding of an integer sum.

The basis has two variants, and the drift between them is itself meaningful. The authoritative variant rebuilds the household basis from every included account on any account or position mutation, or on any read of the basis, and it is the only variant the platform actually applies — every derivation of `household_fee_basis_cents` recomputes the traditional sleeve from the account's positions rather than substituting a stored snapshot. The listed-snapshot variant, under which `last_listed_cents` would stand in for the account's traditional sleeve whenever the account had been listed since its last position mutation, is described here because `last_listed_cents` is maintained for exactly that purpose, but it is not selected at runtime: the two coincide only while no position has moved since the listing, and a position that changes after a listing is reflected in the rebuilt basis immediately, leaving `last_listed_cents` as the stale historical figure the run's listing captured. Any drift a reconciliation is designed to detect is therefore between the stored listing snapshot and the authoritative rebuild, not between two basis figures held side by side.

The advisory fee itself is computed by a pinned ladder that must be evaluated in a fixed order. Steps one through three are performed on an annual basis, and no rounding happens until the fourth step; the platform's full precision is carried through the ladder and only collapsed at the end.

The founding requirement is that the household's `household_fee_basis_cents` must be positive; a run against a household with no included billable accounts is refused with `ERR_FEE_BASIS_ZERO` (HTTP 409).

The first step is the attributable tier fee. The platform walks the attributable tier ladder in ascending order of its lower bound. For each tier defined by a lower bound in cents, an upper bound in cents, and a rate in basis points, the tier's amount is the smaller of the household basis and the tier's upper bound, minus the tier's lower bound; the topmost tier has no upper bound, so its amount is simply the household basis minus its lower bound. When that amount is positive, the platform adds to the running attributable annual fee the tier's amount multiplied by its rate and divided by one hundred, with the result floored to whole cents. The attributable ladder's bands and rates are: the first 250000000 cents of basis at 110 basis points; the next band, from 250000001 through 500000000 cents, at 90 basis points; the band from 500000001 through 1000000000 cents at 70 basis points; and everything above 1000000000 cents at 55 basis points.

The second step is the platform fee, and unlike the attributable ladder it selects a single tier rather than accumulating across bands. The platform finds the one platform-tier band whose range contains the household basis — the topmost band having no upper bound — takes that band's rate, and computes the household basis multiplied by that rate divided by one hundred, floored to whole cents. The platform ladder's bands and rates are: up to 250000000 cents at 8 basis points; from 250000001 through 500000000 cents at 6 basis points; from 500000001 through 1000000000 cents at 4 basis points; and everything above 1000000000 cents at 3 basis points. Whether the platform component is included at all is governed by §2.12's `platform_fee_enabled` switch on the platform configuration row.

The third step sums the two components into the annual fee. The fourth step pro-rates it to the billing period: the annual fee is multiplied by the period's `period_days` and divided by 365, where `period_days` is `period_end_date` minus `period_start_date` in whole days over a half-open interval. The fifth step rounds that product half up to whole cents, and this is the only rounding the ladder performs — the result is the run's `gross_fee_cents`. The sixth step subtracts the credits applied, giving `net_fee_cents` as the gross fee minus `credit_applied_cents`.

Credit consumption is pinned as strictly as the ladder. The platform walks the household's `FeeCredit` rows whose `credit_status` is `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` in ascending `earned_on_date` order, breaking ties on ascending `created_at` as the deterministic fallback for credits stamped at the same instant; the credit's identifier is never consulted as a tie-break. Each credit is consumed only down to the remaining headroom, which is `gross_fee_cents` minus `credit_applied_cents` accumulated so far. A credit fully consumed becomes `APPLIED`; one only partly consumed becomes `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` and keeps its remainder; a credit reached after the headroom is exhausted stays `AVAILABLE` and is left untouched. A consequence worth stating plainly is that `credit_applied_cents` never exceeds `gross_fee_cents`. The permitted `credit_status` values are `AVAILABLE`, `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` and `APPLIED`.

Both fee variants are computed off the same ladder, and which one applies is a matter of client consent. The consent test is read over the household's accounts that are not `PENDING` or `CLOSED` — accounts still in formation or already gone from the household do not participate — with the whole membership considered only when every account in the household is `PENDING` or `CLOSED`, and an empty household falling back to the aggregated variant against its zero basis. When at least one of those considered accounts has `householding_consent` of `OP_IN` or `NONE`, the ladder runs once against the single aggregate `household_fee_basis_cents`; this aggregate figure is the value stored on `household_annual_fee_cents`. When every one of those considered accounts has `householding_consent` of `OP_OUT`, the same ladder runs independently for each of them against that account's own `account_fee_basis_cents`, and the per-account annual fees are summed. For identical data the per-account variant yields a strictly larger annual fee than the aggregated variant whenever the household holds more than one account, because the ladder restarts at its lowest band for every account.

Issuing a credit is a `CustodyOperationsOfficer` action that names the `credit_cents` amount. A new credit begins at `credit_status` `AVAILABLE` with `earned_on_date` stamped to the current instant, and it carries a null `applied_to_billing_run_id` until a run consumes it. A credit is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_FEE` (HTTP 422) when issuing it would push the household's outstanding balance — the total across its credits in `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` — strictly above the household's `household_annual_fee_cents`; the comparison is against the annual fee as it currently stands rather than a recomputation on the spot.

### 3.7. Billing Runs: Seeding, Remittance and the Reconciliation Loop

A billing run is the platform's invoice for one household over one period, and it is created already settled. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` seeds one by supplying `firm_id`, `household_id`, the `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, a `fee_variant_used` of either `HOUSEHOLD_AGGREGATED` or `PER_ACCOUNT`, and the `stated_invoice_cents` the adviser is remitting. The household must belong to the named firm, or the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). `fee_variant_used` and `stated_invoice_cents` are both optional on the request and neither is validated: when `fee_variant_used` is omitted the run records the variant the aggregation actually selected, and when `stated_invoice_cents` is omitted it is left null and no remittance comparison is ever made against it. The permitted `billing_run_status` values are `SETTLED`, `INVOICE_MISMATCH`, `REOPENED` and `VOIDED`, and `VOIDED` is terminal — note that `SETTLED` is the creation state, not the end state.

Six conditions govern seeding, and the platform evaluates them in a fixed relative order because more than one can fail at once. The household's own lifecycle is examined first: a household whose `household_status` is `BILLING_HELD` cannot seed a new run at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_BILLING_HELD` (HTTP 409). The account being billed comes next: a household that holds accounts but none of them `OPEN` or `FROZEN` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409); a household holding no accounts at all has no account row to fail this check, so it falls through to the fiscal floors. The period itself must be well formed — `period_end_date` must fall strictly after `period_start_date`, failing which the run is refused with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_INVALID` (HTTP 422); the same error covers a period longer than 400 days. Two fiscal floors then apply. The household basis must be strictly greater than zero, and a zero basis is refused with `ERR_FEE_BASIS_ZERO` (HTTP 409). Finally the basis must reach an inclusive minimum of 250000000 cents before a run may be seeded; a basis of exactly 250000000 cents is accepted and one of 249999999 cents is refused with `ERR_FEE_BASIS_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 409). A household whose basis is zero fails both the zero test and the minimum test, and the zero test is the earlier of the two.

Seeding runs the aggregation and the ladder described in §3.6, consumes the household's available credits under the pinned selector, records the household basis it captured in `fee_basis_snapshot_cents`, sets the run's `billing_run_status` to `SETTLED`, and mints `settlement_evidence_hash` as an opaque evidence token. It also advances the household's `billing_period_number` by one and increments `billed_period_count` by one on each of the household's included billable accounts — that is, each account in `OPEN` or `FROZEN` with `billable` true — so the counter records how many settled periods the account took part in. The run is billed against a `BILLING` entitlement, so a firm whose billing authority is not granted is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 403) before the household and fiscal checks are reached. Seeding is also an invariant-guarded write: when the firm's configuration has enforcement turned on and a firm-scoped invariant is `BROKEN`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVARIANT_CHAIN_BROKEN` (HTTP 409).

Remittance is where an adviser's own arithmetic is tested against the platform's. The run must be `SETTLED`. Submitting a stated invoice for a settled run compares `stated_invoice_cents` against the run's recomputed `net_fee_cents`, the recomputation re-running the current aggregation, ladder and credit selector. This operation never rejects on a mismatch: it records the stated amount and the freshly recomputed `gross_fee_cents`, `credit_applied_cents` and `net_fee_cents` on the run, and, when the stated amount differs from the recomputed net fee, transitions the run from `SETTLED` to `INVOICE_MISMATCH` and holds the household's billing by moving `household_status` from `ACTIVE` to `BILLING_HELD`. The consequence ripples one hop further, because a household left `BILLING_HELD` cannot seed a new run. A remittance that does match leaves the run `SETTLED`.

Reopening is the compliance function's corrective lever, reserved to a `ComplianceReviewer`. From `INVOICE_MISMATCH` the run moves to `REOPENED`; the consumed credits are released, with every `FeeCredit` the run consumed returning to `credit_status` `AVAILABLE` and the run's `credit_applied_cents` and `net_fee_cents` released with them; and the household returns from `BILLING_HELD` to `ACTIVE`. The reopening reviewer's identity is not compared against any other party — the invoking role is simply the compliance role, and no segregation check applies to this operation.

Re-settling a reopened run is an `AdvisorOperationsLead` action available only from `REOPENED`. It sets the run back to `SETTLED`, re-runs the aggregation and the ladder from the current credit selector — recomputing `gross_fee_cents`, `credit_applied_cents` and `net_fee_cents` and refreshing `fee_basis_snapshot_cents` to the household's current basis — and mints a fresh `settlement_evidence_hash`. The mismatch guard applies here too: if the run's `stated_invoice_cents` still differs from the freshly recomputed `net_fee_cents`, the re-settle is refused with `ERR_FEE_INVOICE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). This is the operation that actually raises that error; the remittance step merely recorded the disagreement.

Voiding is the other terminal path, reserved to a `ComplianceReviewer` and available from `SETTLED`. It sets the run to `VOIDED`, returns every `FeeCredit` the run had consumed to `AVAILABLE`, and releases `credit_applied_cents` to 0 with `net_fee_cents` reset to `gross_fee_cents`. A run already `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; a run in any other non-`SETTLED` state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Household billing state and run state move together throughout the loop: a mismatch holds the household, a reopen releases it, and a run left `REOPENED` is a run whose credits are back in the pool awaiting the next settlement attempt.

### 3.8. Client Standing Instructions and Money Movement

Money leaves a custody account only under a verified standing instruction, and the platform models the instruction and the movement as two separate rows with distinct state machines. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` creates an instruction against an account by supplying an `instruction_type`, a `payee_name` and a `periodic_limit_cents`. The permitted `instruction_type` values are `THIRD_PARTY_DISBURSEMENT`, `WIRE` and `JOURNAL`. A third-party disbursement requires a payee that differs from the household's primary; the `payee_name` is the field that carries it. The `periodic_limit_cents` is not a per-movement cap but the maximum aggregate movement permitted across all of the account's instructions within a billing period, which is why the consumed total is tracked on the instruction row rather than recomputed per movement.

A new instruction begins at `verification_status` `PENDING`, and the permitted values are `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED`, with `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` terminal; `round_trip_days` begins null and `consumed_period_cents` begins at 0. Creation mints a `verification_token`, an opaque server-issued secret returned in the creation response and consumed exactly once when the client verifies.

Verification is the client's own act, performed under the `Client` role, and it is the point at which the platform ties the instruction to the household that owns the account: the acting `X-User-ID` must match the household's primary client user identifier for the account, or the request resolves no resource at all per §2.1. The row must be `PENDING`, and the request must present the `verification_token` that was issued at creation — a call whose token does not match that issued value is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), the same not-found answer the ownership rule gives, so the platform never confirms that the token was merely wrong. On success the instruction becomes `VERIFIED` and `verified_at` is stamped with the current instant.

Two things can end a verified instruction. Expiry is an operational check performed by a `CustodyOperationsOfficer` against a `VERIFIED` instruction, and it fires only once the round trip has reached its ceiling: `round_trip_days` is the number of whole days between the current instant and `verified_at`, and the instruction may be expired once that figure has reached 7 — an instruction whose round trip has not yet reached seven days is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Expiry moves the instruction to `EXPIRED`. Revocation is a `ComplianceReviewer` action available from `VERIFIED` and moves the instruction to `REVOKED`. Both paths share a cascade: every `MoneyMovementInstruction` of that account currently sitting in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` is transitioned to `REJECTED`, so an instruction that dies takes its in-flight movements with it. An instruction is also revoked as a side effect of its account closing.

Movements are drafted by an `AdvisorOperationsLead` against a named instruction. The creation request supplies the `account_id`, the `standing_instruction_id` that authorizes the movement, the `amount_cents`, a `movement_type`, and an `authorization_note`. The account must be the one the instruction was raised against, or the request is refused with `ERR_PARENT_CHILD_MISMATCH` (HTTP 404). The permitted `movement_type` values are `ACH_DISBURSEMENT`, `WIRE`, `JOURNAL` and `CHECK`, though the value is not itself validated at creation. A new movement begins at `movement_status` `DRAFT` and must satisfy the standing instruction's periodic limit — an `amount_cents` that exceeds the instruction's `periodic_limit_cents` less its already-consumed total is refused at creation with `ERR_STANDING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409), so the same limit is enforced both here and again at authorization; the permitted statuses are `DRAFT`, `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, `AUTHORIZED`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED`, with `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` terminal. `authorization_note` is captured as supplied and may be left absent at creation; it is only required by the authorization sequence below.

Submission for authorization is available from `DRAFT` and requires the firm's posture to be `OPEN` or `LIMITED` and the account's `account_status` to be `OPEN`. A movement against a frozen or otherwise ineligible account is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409), and a posture that is not open enough for the money-movement path is refused with `ERR_FIRM_NOT_OPEN_FOR_TRADING` (HTTP 409). Submission moves the movement to `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`.

Authorization is the client's act and is where the verification rules actually bite. The platform evaluates a fixed sequence of checks and the first one that fires determines both the error and — where the sequence does not reject — the withholding decision; a later rule never overrides an earlier one's withholding outcome.

1. If the `authorization_note` is blank, the movement is rejected with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOTE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).
2. If the movement's `amount_cents` exceeds the linked instruction's `periodic_limit_cents` minus its already-consumed total, it is rejected with `ERR_STANDING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
3. If the linked instruction's `verification_status` is anything other than `VERIFIED`, the movement is rejected with `ERR_STANDING_INSTRUCTION_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409).
4. If the instruction's `round_trip_days` has reached 7 or more, the movement is rejected with `ERR_STANDING_INSTRUCTION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409).
5. Otherwise the withholding decision is made. If the account's `account_type` is `IRA` or `ROTH_IRA`, the movement's `jurisdiction_withholding_required` is set to true and `withheld_cents` is the movement amount multiplied by 1000 basis points and divided by one hundred, floored to whole cents, and capped so that it never exceeds the movement amount itself; for every other account type, `withheld_cents` is 0.

Two notes on that sequence are worth keeping in mind. The amount-versus-limit check compares against the instruction's remaining headroom, not its raw limit, so consumed movements reduce the room available to later ones. And the amount-versus-limit check is evaluated before the verification checks, so a movement on an unverified instruction that also breaches the limit reports the limit breach as the limiting rule for the request as a whole, even though the instruction-verification rule would also have fired. The residual is deliberate and is pinned in that order — account eligibility first, then the note and limit constraints, then the verification and expiry rules — and the platform's behaviour on a request that fails several of these at once follows it exactly.

On successful authorization the movement's `movement_status` becomes `AUTHORIZED`, `authorized_at` is stamped, the linked instruction's `consumed_period_cents` increases by the movement's `amount_cents`, and the derived `net_disbursed_cents` is set to the movement amount minus the withheld amount. Because the consumption is booked at authorization, completing the movement later does not add to it again — the completion step leaves the consumed total unchanged. Authorization also recomputes the account's `market_value_cents` as the movement posts and, one hop further, the owning household's `household_fee_basis_cents`.

The remaining transitions are simple but terminal in different ways. A `Client` may reject a movement from `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, which moves it to `REJECTED`. An `AdvisorOperationsLead` may cancel a movement from `DRAFT`, which moves it to `CANCELLED`. A `CustodyOperationsOfficer` may complete an authorized movement, moving it from `AUTHORIZED` to `COMPLETED`.

A read-only replacement view of the withholding logic also exists — a per-movement summary probe on an account, `GET /ria/accounts/{id}/money-movements/summary` — and it deliberately does not agree with the authorization path in every case. This probe reports, per movement on the account, the withdrawal amount each restriction would independently demand — the jurisdiction amount, the entitlement-level amount, and the limit breach — and surfaces the single largest of them as `probe_withheld_cents` alongside a `restriction_demands` breakdown, ordering them by magnitude rather than by the write path's first-rule-wins sequence; the movements it walks are the account's movements in the usual oldest-first order. On an account whose authorization chain is not fully propagated, the probe reports the entitlement-level withdrawal — which is 0 — rather than the jurisdiction withholding, because it re-reads the posture chain before it reads the movement row; for a fully propagated chain it agrees with the persisted `withheld_cents` and `net_disbursed_cents`. Clients should treat the probe as a diagnostic of the maximum exposure and the authorization endpoint as the operative decision.

A separate custodian research lookup, `POST /ria/lookup/lot-inventory`, answers a narrower question and is unrelated to the withholding chain: given an `account_id`, a `symbol` and a `lookback_days` window, it returns a card carrying `account_id`, `symbol`, `lookback_days` and `highest_shares_held_micros` — the largest `quantity_micros` held in that symbol across the account's applied positions — or 0 when the account holds none. The `lookback_days` value is echoed back as supplied and does not itself restrict the search.

### 3.9. Custody Evidence, Examination and Invariant Enforcement

Custody-rule evidence is raised against a firm by an `AdvisorOperationsLead`, who names the `requirement_code` and sets the `critical` flag. A new evidence row begins at `requirement_status` `PENDING`, and the raise immediately re-runs the posture derivation. The permitted `requirement_code` values are `QUALIFIED_CUSTODIAN`, `SURPRISE_EXAMINATION`, `CLIENT_NOTIFICATION`, `TWO_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION`, `FEE_DISCLOSURE`, `ANNUAL_ATTESTATION`, `STANDING_INSTRUCTION_AUTHORIZATION` and `FEE_CREDIT_DISCLOSURE`; the permitted `requirement_status` values are `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DEFICIENT`, `WAIVED` and `LAPSED`, with `WAIVED` and `LAPSED` terminal.

Submission is available from `PENDING` and requires the firm's posture to be `OPEN`, `LIMITED` or `SUSPENDED` — a frozen firm cannot submit evidence at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_FIRM_FROZEN` (HTTP 403). Submission sets the status to `SUBMITTED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` into `drafted_by_user_id`, stamps `submitted_at`, and re-rolls the firm's `supervision_evidence_status`.

Acceptance belongs to a `ComplianceReviewer` and is available from `SUBMITTED`. The accepting officer must differ from `drafted_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403); and the operation is blocked while the firm has an unacknowledged regulator action, in which case it is refused with `ERR_ADMIN_ACTION_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409). On success the status becomes `ACCEPTED`, `accepted_by_user_id` records the officer, and `evidence_document_hash` is minted as the document hash the examination bundle will later require. The evidence rollup is recomputed, and the posture derivation runs one more hop: a firm sitting in `LIMITED` whose critical evidence has all been accepted returns to `OPEN`.

A firm-level regulatory freeze is imposed by a `ComplianceReviewer` from any live posture, issued with a `freeze_reason_code`; it drives `firm_posture` to `FROZEN`, cancels the firm's `DRAFT` and `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` money movements to `CANCELLED`, reopens its settled billing runs, suspends its `GRANTED` entitlements, and throttles its active data feeds. An already-`AUTHORIZED` movement is not cancellable and is left alone. Once frozen, only the acknowledgement of an admin action and the movement of the logical clock remain open to the firm.

Rejection is the other reviewer verdict, also available from `SUBMITTED` and also requiring the acting officer to differ from the submitter — the same `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` guard applies. It is issued with a `deficiency_reason_code`, recorded on the row as supplied though not itself required or validated, and it moves the status to `DEFICIENT` and increments `evidence_round_number` by one. The consequences fan out over three hops: the firm's `supervision_evidence_status` becomes `DEFICIENT`; the posture derivation runs, dropping an `OPEN` firm with a critical deficient row to `LIMITED`; and that `LIMITED` posture in turn suspends the firm's `MODEL_MANAGEMENT` and `ALTERNATIVE_ASSETS` entitlements, which is where the evidence chain finally reaches the investment side of the business.

Resubmission is the correction loop and is available from `DEFICIENT`, provided the row's `evidence_round_number` is strictly less than 4. An attempt on a row that has already consumed its four rounds is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Resubmission sets the status back to `SUBMITTED` and re-stamps `drafted_by_user_id` with the resubmitting officer — which matters, because the next acceptance or rejection will compare against that new value. The fourth round is the ceiling and a fifth attempt is refused; the cycle is closed by the submit step, not by a separate one.

Waiving is only available to a `ComplianceReviewer` against a `SUBMITTED` row, and only when the row's `critical` flag is false; a critical requirement cannot be waived at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Waiver moves the row to `WAIVED`.

Lapsing is the temporal counterpart, available to a `ComplianceReviewer` from `ACCEPTED` and conditioned on the firm's `agreement_executed_at` being non-null and at least 365 days in the past. A firm whose agreement was executed more recently than that cannot have its accepted evidence lapsed, and — like a firm whose agreement has never been executed — the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Lapsing moves the row to `LAPSED`, recomputes the rollup, and re-runs the posture derivation.

Examination bundles turn evidence into a defensible artifact. A `ComplianceReviewer` opens one for a firm by supplying a `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, and it begins at `bundle_status` `ASSEMBLING`; the permitted values are `ASSEMBLING`, `GENERATED`, `INCOMPLETE` and `SEALED`. Generation runs the assembly rule and refreshes `missing_evidence_codes`, sets `generated_at`, and leaves the bundle `GENERATED` when nothing is missing or `INCOMPLETE` when something is.

Assembly walks eight required codes: the six custody-rule codes `QUALIFIED_CUSTODIAN`, `SURPRISE_EXAMINATION`, `CLIENT_NOTIFICATION`, `TWO_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION`, `FEE_DISCLOSURE` and `ANNUAL_ATTESTATION`, plus the two movement-evidence codes `STANDING_INSTRUCTION_AUTHORIZATION` and `FEE_CREDIT_DISCLOSURE`. For each required code the platform looks for `CustodyRulesEvidence` rows of the firm whose `requirement_code` matches, whose `requirement_status` is `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`, and whose `submitted_at` falls at or after `period_start_date` and strictly before `period_end_date`. If no such row exists, that code is recorded as missing. The missing list is then sorted ascending in stable lexicographic order and reported as `missing_evidence_codes`. Monetary movements are counted toward the movement-evidence codes when their authorization instant falls in the same half-open interval, so the interval's start is inclusive and its end is exclusive. Sealing is permitted only from `GENERATED` — an `INCOMPLETE` or still-`ASSEMBLING` bundle is refused with `ERR_BUNDLE_NOT_SEALABLE` (HTTP 409) — and sealing mints `sealed_evidence_hash`, the seal the independent examiner verifies.

Enforcement of the firm's conserved quantities runs through the database invariants. An invariant sweep recomputes every invariant of a firm and stores the results; it is also the operation that provisions any of the four invariant codes the firm does not yet have, so a firm's invariant set grows to the full four as it is used. The four codes are `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_CONSISTENCY`, `TRANSFER_BASIS_CONSERVATION`, `HOUSEHOLD_FEE_BASIS_RECONCILIATION` and `CASCADE_ECONOMIC_EFFECT`, each reporting `invariant_status` of `HOLDING` or `BROKEN`, the compared quantities in `expected_total_cents` and `observed_total_cents`, a `breach_detail_code` when broken, and a refreshed `last_evaluated_at`.

The reconciliation invariant is the one with the most moving parts, and it compares three derivations of the same quantity. The first basis is the firm's own `household_fee_basis_total_cents`. The second is the sum of `household_fee_basis_cents` across every household of the firm, including `BILLING_HELD` and `CLOSED` ones. The third is the sum of `account_fee_basis_cents` across the firm's included accounts, applying the account-inclusion filter described in §3.6 before summing. The drift between the firm-level and household-level figures is the first basis minus the second; the drift between the household-level and account-level figures is the second minus the third. The invariant is `BROKEN` exactly when either drift is non-zero and the platform configuration has `database_invariant_enforcement` set to `ENFORCED`; the tolerance is zero cents, so the comparison is exact equality rather than an approximate one. When it is broken, `observed_total_cents` records the account-level sum, `expected_total_cents` records the firm-level total, and `breach_detail_code` is `HOUSEHOLD_FEE_BASIS_TOTAL_DRIFT` when the firm-level and account-level totals differ, or `HOUSEHOLD_FEE_BASIS_STALE_ROLLUP` when those two agree and it is only the household-level rollup that has drifted. Each of the four invariant codes is evaluated on every sweep, and a firm holds one `DatabaseInvariant` row per code it has been provisioned with — the sweep itself creates whichever of the four the firm is still missing, so a firm's invariant set grows to the full four the first time it is swept. The reconciliation endpoint recomputes the firm's `household_fee_basis_total_cents` from its households before it evaluates, and it reports every invariant row of the firm. A sweep is itself guarded: when enforcement is on and an invariant is already broken, the sweep is refused with `ERR_INVARIANT_CHAIN_BROKEN` (HTTP 409), and so are the two other guarded writes named in §3.7 and §3.2 — seeding a billing run and provisioning a data feed.

The other three invariants are simpler and are all compared under the same zero-tolerance, enforcement-gated rule. `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_CONSISTENCY` is boolean: it holds when the firm has an agreement in `EXECUTED` and its own `custody_agreement_status` is likewise `EXECUTED`, and records 0 in both `expected_total_cents` and `observed_total_cents`; when it breaks its `breach_detail_code` is `CUSTODY_AGREEMENT_INCONSISTENT`. `TRANSFER_BASIS_CONSERVATION` sums, over every `TransferLot` of the firm whose `lot_status` is `APPLIED`, the delivering firm's reported `sent_cost_basis_cents` into `expected_total_cents` and the accepted `basis_appraisal_cents` into `observed_total_cents`, breaking with `TRANSFER_BASIS_DRIFT` when the two diverge. `CASCADE_ECONOMIC_EFFECT` sums the `fee_rebate_cents` granted across the firm's block allocations, records 0 in both totals, and can only break — with `CASCADE_DRIFT` — if that sum were negative, which the one-cent credit path never produces, so in practice it holds whenever it is evaluated. The reconciliation endpoint refreshes the `last_evaluated_at` of every invariant of the firm on each sweep.

Regulator actions arrive through the admin-action surface. A `PlatformAdmin` issues one against a firm by naming the `firm_id`, an `admin_action_kind` and a free-form `admin_action_detail`. The permitted kinds are `REGULATORY_FREEZE` and `DEFICIENCY_NOTICE`, and the permitted statuses are `UNACKNOWLEDGED`, `ACTIVE` and `ACKNOWLEDGED`. A `REGULATORY_FREEZE` is created directly in `ACTIVE` and, through the posture derivation's level-0 rule, forces the firm `FROZEN` regardless of any other condition the firm satisfies. A `DEFICIENCY_NOTICE` is created `UNACKNOWLEDGED` and, through the level-4 rule, forces the firm `LIMITED` until it is acknowledged. Acknowledgement is performed by an `AdvisorOperationsLead` or a `CustodyOperationsOfficer`, requires an `acknowledged_note`, records `acknowledged_by_user_id`, sets the status to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and clears both the unacknowledged limitation and the active freeze so that the posture falls through to whatever the lower rules then dictate. Acknowledging an admin action is one of the two write capabilities the frozen posture does not block.

Finally, the platform's clock is an operator capability rather than an ambient fact. A `PlatformAdmin` may set the logical current instant by supplying `current_utc`; the capability has no persisted entity behind it and exists purely so that the platform's temporal rules can be exercised deterministically. It begins at exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` and stays there until moved. Moving it recomputes every derived temporal quantity in the system — the valuation ages, the instruction round-trip days, and every other window measured against the present.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Two status-code conventions govern the whole surface. An operation that creates a new, addressable resource returns `201 Created`; operations that modify an existing resource, or that add a new item to a collection already owned by an existing resource, return `200 OK`. Reads return `200 OK`. Every `PUT` and every state-changing `POST` additionally expects the conditional-request header described in §2.10, and the three operations guarded by an explicit optimistic-concurrency contract (§2.10) expect `expected_model_portfolio_version`, `expected_block_order_version` or `expected_household_version` in the request body as well.
