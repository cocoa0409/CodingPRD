# State Auto-IRA Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The state has enacted a facilitated retirement savings mandate: every private employer above a small-employer floor must either sponsor a qualified retirement plan of its own or connect its payroll to the state-run automatic individual retirement account programme. This service is the system of record for that mandate. It tracks which employers are covered and by which phased deadline, accepts and adjudicates qualified-plan exemption claims, auto-enrols employees at a statutory default deferral rate, receives payroll deductions batch by batch, deposits them into individual Roth accounts, and follows the money all the way through investment allocation, annual contribution-limit correction, portability between employers and institutions, and eventual account closure.

Two forces shape almost every rule in this document. The first is the **non-fiduciary boundary**. The programme only works politically if an employer's participation is purely ministerial: it reports who it employs, registers, withholds exactly the amount the programme computes, and reports separations. It never chooses a contribution rate, a fund, an opt-out, or a correction method on a saver's behalf, and the API is deliberately shaped so that an employer that tries is refused with a distinct, auditable error rather than a generic permission failure. The second is **enforcement with due process**. Employers that miss a registration deadline or sit on withheld employee money become delinquent, are assessed a penalty computed under two competing statutory formulas, receive a cure period, and may ultimately be referred to an outside agency — but an officer may never dispose of a penalty they themselves raised, and an abatement must unwind the referral it produced.

Money correctness is the third, quieter constraint. Every monetary figure in the system is an integer number of cents and every rate is expressed in basis points, because a retirement account that drifts by a cent per posting is a recordkeeping defect that compounds for forty years. Contribution ceilings are per person and per calendar year, not per employer, so a saver working two covered jobs can breach the limit even though neither employer did anything wrong; the programme therefore credits contributions in full, detects the excess afterwards, and drives a formal correction case with net-income-attributable arithmetic. Because tests and audits must be reproducible, the service runs on a settable logical clock rather than on wall-clock time, and every deadline, window, seasoning period and rolling-frequency check reads that clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock setter must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the principal's role. If `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request — including whether the addressed resource exists — is examined. If the role in `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the endpoint admits, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), again before existence is checked, so a caller with the wrong role cannot use 404-versus-403 responses to probe which identifiers exist.

There are exactly five roles, spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramAdministrator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Recordkeeper`, `Employer`, and `Participant`.

A `ProgramAdministrator` is state programme staff. They configure the statutory parameter set and the fund menu, create participant records, run the batch jobs, decide exemption certifications, and act on a saver's behalf in the service centre. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `assessed_by_user_id` on a penalty assessment when an administrator-visible flow writes it) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` is enforcement staff: they revoke exemptions, raise penalty assessments, abate or affirm them, and refer matters to outside agencies. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `assessed_by_user_id` when the officer raises an assessment, as `disposed_by_user_id` when an officer disposes of one, and as `referred_by_user_id` when an officer refers one out; those recorded values are compared against each other for segregation of duties, never against an entity `id`.

A `Recordkeeper` is the programme's financial recordkeeper and the only role that moves money: it posts and reverses remittance batches, credits investment earnings, executes excess corrections, settles or returns portability transfers, and finalises account closures. When `X-User-Role` is `Recordkeeper`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`.

An `Employer` principal is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Employer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Employer record; ownership checks on any resource that references an employer compare that resource's employer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `Employer` principal may only address its own employer row, its own exemption certifications, its own payroll remittances and their lines, its own penalty assessments and the referrals raised from them, and enrolments whose employer is itself. An attempt to address another employer's subtree is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE` (HTTP 403). Balances are none of an employer's business: the participant-account, excess-contribution-case and portability-transfer collections do not admit the `Employer` role at all, so an employer reaching for them is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

A `Participant` principal is likewise entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Participant record; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's participant foreign key to `X-User-ID`. A `Participant` owns every decision about their own money — deferral rate, escalation preference, opt-out and permissible withdrawal, fund allocation, excess-correction method, portability and account closure — but may read or write only their own participant record, enrolments, accounts, elections, excess cases and transfers; touching another saver's resources yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`. Every enforcement operation and every money-moving recordkeeper operation is closed to `Participant` with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Ownership is always evaluated *after* existence, so an ownership failure can never reveal the identifier space of another tenant. Where the system itself acts rather than a human — a cascade or a batch sweep — it records the literal string `SYSTEM` in the user-identifier fields instead of a caller's `X-User-ID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching record remains beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

Each listing also exposes filters that narrow the matched set before pagination. Program years may be filtered by `calendar_year`. The fund menu may be filtered by `category`, by `status`, and by `default_eligible`. Employer-scoped certification listings accept `status`; employer-scoped penalty listings accept `status`, `violation_type` and `origin`. Participant listings accept `state_resident` and `catch_up_eligible`. Enrolment listings accept `employer_id`, `participant_id`, `enrollment_status` and `rate_source`. Account listings accept `participant_id`, `rolled_over_from_account_id`, `account_status`, `account_type` and `posture`. Election listings accept `status` and `source`. Excess-case listings accept `status` and `calendar_year`. Remittance listings accept `employer_id` and `status`; remittance-line listings accept `status`. Referral listings accept `status` and `agency`. Transfer listings accept `participant_id`, `source_account_id`, `destination_account_id`, `transfer_kind` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two configuration resources are exceptions: a program year and an investment option are immutable once created, so each of them carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resource shapes entirely. Everything else — employers, exemption certifications, participants, enrolments, participant accounts, investment elections, payroll remittances, remittance lines, excess-contribution cases, penalty assessments, enforcement referrals and portability transfers — carries the full set.

Because a mutation to a child can change a parent's derived figures, a `version` bump is not confined to the row named in the request. Where a workflow below states that an operation adjusts an employer's or an account's derived totals, that parent's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed as part of the same operation. This matters directly: the two optimistically-concurrent endpoints described in §2.7 compare a client-supplied version against exactly these parent counters.

Time is not read from the host. The service maintains a **logical clock** whose value at start-up is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time — before any clock call is made, the clock reads that instant. A `ProgramAdministrator` may move the clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying `current_utc`; the response echoes back the instant now in effect as `current_utc`, rendered in the same ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits that `created_at` uses rather than replayed as the caller's literal input string. Every date-derived computation in the system reads the logical clock: `age_at_year_end`, remittance lateness, every deadline and window, posture derivation, and all three batch jobs. Each batch job also accepts an optional date override — `as_of_date` on the auto-enrolment and compliance-sweep batches, `effective_date` on the annual-escalation batch — which, when supplied, is the date the batch evaluates its windows against instead of the clock's own date.

### 2.5. Statutory Parameters: the Program Year

All statutory dials live on a single immutable configuration row per calendar year, and any operation that needs those dials reads the row whose `calendar_year` equals the calendar year of the current logical clock. If no such row exists, the operation is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_YEAR_NOT_CONFIGURED` (HTTP 409). Creating a second row for a `calendar_year` that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_YEAR` (HTTP 409). `calendar_year` must be an integer from 2000 through 2100 inclusive.

A minimal configuration call supplies only three values: `calendar_year`, `ira_annual_limit_cents` (the annual IRA contribution ceiling in cents for a saver under 50, which must be at least 1), and `default_contribution_rate_bp` (the auto-enrolment default deferral rate in basis points of gross wages, which must be from 100 through 1500 inclusive). Every remaining dial has a default that is materialised onto the stored row when the caller omits it, so a stored program year always exposes a concrete value for each one:

- `catch_up_limit_cents` — additional annual room for a saver whose `age_at_year_end` is at least 50; defaults to 100000 cents, that is one thousand dollars.
- `escalation_increment_bp` — basis points that each annual escalation run adds to a deferral rate; defaults to 100, one full percentage point.
- `escalation_cap_bp` — the ceiling automatic escalation never exceeds; defaults to 800, eight percent of wages.
- `escalation_seasoning_days` — the minimum number of days that must separate a saver's first contribution from an escalation's effective date before that enrolment may be escalated; defaults to 180, roughly two calendar quarters.
- `opt_out_window_days` — the length in days of both the pre-activation opt-out window and the post-first-contribution permissible-withdrawal window; defaults to 30.
- `remittance_due_days` — days after `pay_date` by which a payroll batch must reach the programme; defaults to 7, one week.
- `notice_period_days` — days after a penalty's `notice_date` before the initial statutory per-employee tier applies; defaults to 90.
- `sustained_notice_days` — days after `notice_date` before the sustained statutory per-employee tier is added on top; defaults to 180.
- `register_penalty_initial_cents_per_employee` — the initial statutory penalty per eligible employee; defaults to 25000 cents, two hundred and fifty dollars a head.
- `register_penalty_sustained_cents_per_employee` — the additional sustained statutory penalty per eligible employee; defaults to 50000 cents, five hundred dollars a head.
- `remit_penalty_monthly_rate_bp` — basis points of unremitted deductions charged per whole thirty-day block of lateness; defaults to 150, one and a half percent a month.
- `remit_penalty_floor_cents` — the minimum graduated remittance penalty; defaults to 10000 cents, one hundred dollars.
- `remit_penalty_cap_bp` — the ceiling on the graduated remittance penalty expressed as basis points of the unremitted amount; defaults to 5000, half of the shortfall.
- `nia_daily_rate_bp` — the deemed daily earnings rate in basis points used by the daily-prorata net-income-attributable variant; defaults to 2.
- `correction_deadline_month_day` — a month-and-day string in `MM-DD` form, located in the year after `calendar_year`, by which an excess contribution must be corrected; defaults to `"04-15"`, the ordinary individual tax filing date.

### 2.6. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer count of cents, and every rate, allocation weight and proportional charge is an integer count of basis points, where 10000 basis points is the whole. There are no floating-point amounts anywhere in the contract. Whenever a rule multiplies an amount by a basis-point rate, the result is divided by 10000 and rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** — a fractional remainder of exactly one half rounds away from zero, upward in magnitude. Intermediate values are never rounded early: each calculation below names the exact points at which rounding occurs, and rounding happens only at those points, never on a value that is about to be fed into a further multiplication.

Headcounts are whole integers. Deadlines, coverage bounds, pay-period bounds, effective dates and settlement dates are ISO calendar dates with no time component, and comparisons between them are date comparisons; instants such as `registered_at`, `decided_at`, `revoked_at`, `opted_out_at`, `submitted_at`, `posted_at`, `reversed_at`, `superseded_at`, `completed_at`, `closed_at` and `referred_at` are full ISO 8601 UTC timestamps taken from the logical clock. Where a derived total is defined to be non-negative — a saver's remaining contribution room, for instance — it is floored at 0 rather than allowed to go negative.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Employer and Account Aggregates

Two write endpoints are optimistically concurrent, because each rewrites derived state that a client must have read first in order to compose a sensible request. On both, the version field is a required input, not an optional one.

A `POST /participant-accounts/{id}/investment-elections` request must carry `expected_account_version`, holding the `version` of the addressed participant account as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the account's current `version` — because a remittance posting, an excess correction, an earnings credit, a permissible withdrawal or a portability debit has advanced the account since the client read it — the election is refused with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and the client must re-read the account and resubmit against the fresh version. A matching value succeeds and advances the account's `version` by one again. The reason this endpoint is guarded is that an allocation is a statement about a *balance*: reallocating stale money silently misplaces whatever arrived in between.

A `POST /employers/{id}/employee-count-recounts` request must carry `expected_employer_version`, holding the `version` of the addressed employer as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the employer's current `version`, the recount is refused with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A recount rewrites `wave_tier` and `registration_deadline`, and an employer that was registered, penalised or exempted since the client last read it must not have its mandate deadline recomputed against a superseded baseline. A matching value succeeds and advances the employer's `version` by one.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field. Writes elsewhere are last-write-wins on the row they address, with the derived-figure recomputations described in each workflow.

### 2.8. Nested Addressing and Path Resolution

Several resources are addressed through a parent: certifications, enrolments, payroll remittances and penalty assessments hang off an employer; investment elections, excess-contribution cases, earnings postings and closure steps hang off a participant account; remittance lines hang off a remittance batch; enforcement referrals hang off a penalty assessment. Path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the response. Each addressed collection has its own not-found identifier so that the failing hop is unambiguous: `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXEMPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCESS_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PENALTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FUND_OPTION_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.

Identifiers that arrive in the *body* rather than the path are resolved after the body's shape has been validated, so a malformed payload reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a spurious 404. Body-borne references are resolved on three operations: creating an enrolment under an employer resolves `participant_id`; adding a line to a remittance batch resolves `enrollment_id`; requesting a portability transfer resolves `participant_id`, `source_account_id` and, when present, `destination_account_id`. An unresolvable body reference reports the not-found identifier of the collection it named — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the ascending order below and the first violation wins; no later check can mask an earlier one.

1. **Identity present.** On every endpoint except the logical-clock setter, a missing or empty `X-User-ID` yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role permitted.** On every endpoint with a non-empty role list, a caller whose role is not on that list yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Path target exists.** On every endpoint with an identifier segment in its path, an unknown identifier yields that collection's not-found error.
4. **Caller owns the target.** On every endpoint admitting the `Employer` or `Participant` role, an entity-backed principal that is not the owner of the addressed resource — the employer of the enrolment or remittance, or the participant of the account, enrolment, excess case or transfer — yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`.
5. **Request shape.** On every endpoint with a body or query parameters, a missing required field, a wrong type, an unknown enum member, a numeric field outside its declared range, a non-ISO-8601 date, a `limit` outside 1 to 100 or a negative `offset` yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This step also enforces the conditional pairings: `target_year` is required when `category` is `TARGET_DATE` and must be absent otherwise; `destination_account_id` is required when `destination_type` is `PROGRAM_ACCOUNT` and forbidden otherwise; `external_institution_name` is required when `destination_type` is `EXTERNAL_IRA` and must be null otherwise; `pay_period_end` must be on or after `pay_period_start`; `pay_date` must be on or after `pay_period_end`; and `attestation_accepted` must be true.
6. **Body references resolve.** As described in §2.8, on enrolment creation, remittance-line creation and portability-transfer creation.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** (priority 15). On the two endpoints of §2.7, a mismatched `expected_account_version` yields `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` and a mismatched `expected_employer_version` yields `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYER_VERSION`. Both sit ahead of every state and business check, so a client holding a stale read is told to re-read rather than handed a business error it cannot act on.
8. **Lifecycle state gates** (priority 20). Registration requires an employer in `PENDING_REGISTRATION` whose `wave_tier` is not `NOT_COVERED`, else `ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`. A certification decision requires `SUBMITTED` and a revocation requires `APPROVED`, else `ERR_EXEMPTION_STATE_INVALID` — which also makes the decision operation non-idempotent, since deciding an already-decided certification is refused. Enrolment triggers are gated by `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`: opt-out requires `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, re-enrolment requires `OPTED_OUT`, a rate election requires `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, and an employment update requires any status other than `TERMINATED`. Enrolment creation, re-enrolment and remittance-batch creation all require the employer's `registration_status` to be exactly `REGISTERED`, else `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_REGISTERED`. Remittance triggers are gated by `ERR_REMITTANCE_STATE_INVALID`: lines may be added only while the batch is `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, submission requires `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, posting and rejection require `SUBMITTED`, and reversal requires `POSTED`. A participant account whose `account_status` is `CLOSED` refuses every write — portability requests, investment elections, closure requests and earnings postings — with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. Excess-case triggers are gated by `ERR_EXCESS_CASE_STATE_INVALID`: method election requires `DETECTED` and completion requires `METHOD_ELECTED`. Penalty triggers are gated by `ERR_PENALTY_STATE_INVALID`: a disposition requires `ASSESSED` or `AFFIRMED`, and creating a referral requires `AFFIRMED`. Settling or returning a transfer requires status `REQUESTED`, else `ERR_TRANSFER_STATE_INVALID`. Closure completion requires `CLOSURE_PENDING`, else `ERR_CLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`.
9. **Derived posture gate** (priority 22). A portability request or a closure request against an account whose derived `posture` is `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKED` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED`. This sits strictly *after* the closed-account gate, so a `CLOSED` account — whose posture is also `LOCKED` — always reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED` and never `ERR_ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED`. Investment elections are deliberately not posture-gated.
10. **Segregation of duties and the fiduciary boundary** (priority 25). A penalty disposition whose `X-User-ID` equals the assessment's `assessed_by_user_id` yields `ERR_SELF_DISPOSITION_FORBIDDEN`; because this sits after the state gate, an already-abated assessment reports its state error even to the original assessor. An `Employer` principal attempting a rate election or an opt-out yields `ERR_FIDUCIARY_BOUNDARY_VIOLATION`.
11. **Enforcement hold** (priority 45). Filing an exemption certification or restating an employee count while the employer has at least one enforcement referral in status `OPEN` yields `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_HOLD`. This deliberately outranks the temporal checks, so an employer under referral is told about the hold rather than about a stale plan date.
12. **Referential and cross-aggregate coherence** (priority 50). An investment election naming a fund code that matches no option, or one whose option status is not `OPEN`, yields `ERR_FUND_NOT_AVAILABLE`. A remittance line whose enrolment belongs to a different employer, or is `OPTED_OUT` or `TERMINATED`, or has no open account, yields `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_DEDUCTIBLE`. A portability transfer whose source account does not belong to the named participant, whose destination equals its source, whose destination belongs to a different participant, or whose destination account is not open, yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNER_MISMATCH`. An auto-enrolment run that can find no default fund for a saver it is about to activate yields `ERR_NO_DEFAULT_FUND_AVAILABLE`.
13. **Occupancy, uniqueness and frequency** (priority 55). A new remittance batch whose pay period collides with a live batch of the same employer yields `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A duplicate live enrolment for an employer-and-participant pair yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`. A certification whose coverage interval collides with a live certification of the same employer yields `ERR_EXEMPTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A second indirect rollover inside the rolling annual window yields `ERR_ROLLOVER_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`.
14. **Temporal windows** (priority 58). `ERR_EXEMPTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` on an already-elapsed coverage interval; `ERR_OPT_OUT_WINDOW_CLOSED` on a late pre-activation opt-out; `ERR_PERMISSIBLE_WITHDRAWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` on a withdrawal requested outside its window; `ERR_CORRECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` on a method election or completion after the correction deadline; `ERR_ROLLOVER_WINDOW_EXPIRED` on a late indirect settlement; `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_ACTIVE` on an affirmation raised while the cure period still runs.
15. **Arithmetic coherence** (priority 65). `ERR_ALLOCATION_TOTAL_INVALID` when election weights do not reconcile — checked after fund availability, so an election naming a terminated fund reports the fund problem rather than an arithmetic one. `ERR_DEDUCTION_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` when a line's withheld amount is not exactly the programme-computed amount. `ERR_REMITTANCE_TOTAL_MISMATCH` when a batch's lines do not reconcile to its declared total at submission, or the batch has no lines. `ERR_RATE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` when a participant-elected deferral rate falls outside the permitted band.
16. **Fiscal sufficiency, last** (priority 70). `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ACCOUNT_BALANCE` (HTTP 402) when a portability amount, or a refund-style correction amount, exceeds the account's balance; `ERR_RESIDUAL_BALANCE_PRESENT` when closure completion is attempted on an account that still holds money. Ranking money last means every structural, temporal and coherence problem is reported before the question of whether the account can afford the request — so a closed account presented with an oversized request reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`, never the balance error.

### 2.10. The Non-Fiduciary Boundary

The statute protects employers from fiduciary liability by keeping their role ministerial, and the API encodes that boundary rather than merely documenting it. An `Employer` principal may create its own employer record, complete its registration and acknowledge non-fiduciary status, restate its headcount, file a qualified-plan exemption certification, enrol employees at the programme default, open payroll batches, add lines to them, submit them, and report a separation date. It may read its own subtree. That is the whole of its authority.

Three participant-facing decisions are closed to it in two different ways, and the difference is deliberate. A rate election and an opt-out *do* admit the `Employer` role onto the route, precisely so that an employer's attempt is refused by the named, auditable `ERR_FIDUCIARY_BOUNDARY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) rather than by an indistinguishable role check — the programme wants a record that an employer reached for a saver's decision. Investment elections and excess-correction method elections, by contrast, do not list the `Employer` role at all, so an employer attempt there is stopped one step earlier by the role check and reports `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Implementations must reproduce both outcomes exactly as described.

The boundary is also arithmetic. An employer does not decide how much to withhold: for each line it reports gross wages, and the programme derives the amount that should have been withheld from the enrolment's current deferral rate. A line whose withheld amount differs from that derived figure by even one cent is refused with `ERR_DEDUCTION_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. This is what makes the employer a pass-through rather than a plan sponsor.

### 2.11. Fund-Level Conservation and the Allocation Split

Each participant account carries a fund-level breakdown in `fund_balances`, an array of objects each pairing a `fund_code` with a `balance_cents`. That breakdown is **re-split from scratch on every single change to the account's `balance_cents`** — a contribution posting, a reversal, an earnings posting, a correction debit, a portability debit or credit, or a permissible withdrawal. It is never updated incrementally, because incremental updates let rounding error accumulate across events.

The split works from the account's currently active investment election. Order that election's allocation entries by allocation weight in basis points descending, and where two entries carry the same weight, by fund code ascending. For every entry except the last in that order, the fund's balance is the account balance multiplied by the entry's weight in basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. The last entry in the order does not get its own multiplication: it absorbs the remainder, taking the account balance minus the sum of all the balances already assigned. Consequently the fund balances always sum to exactly the account's `balance_cents`, with no drift, ever. Rounding each fund independently is a defect, not a simplification: it can produce a fund total one cent above the account balance. A balance of zero produces every fund at zero, and an account with no active election has an empty `fund_balances` array.

The same conservation discipline governs the two money invariants the programme audits. Contribution room is conserved: whatever a posting adds to a saver's year-to-date contributions, a reversal of that posting subtracts exactly, leaving no drift. Programme cash is conserved: money that leaves an account into an in-flight transfer is held in that transfer's in-transit leg and is either delivered or fully restored, never partially lost.

### 2.12. Account Posture: a Derived Gate over the Account's Children

Alongside its lifecycle `account_status`, every participant account exposes a second, purely derived dimension called `posture`, recomputed after every write that could change any of its inputs. The derivation is a single pass, evaluated in this order:

- If `account_status` is `CLOSURE_PENDING` or `CLOSED`, the posture is `LOCKED`.
- Otherwise, if any excess-contribution case of this account is in status `DETECTED`, `METHOD_ELECTED` or `EXPIRED`, **or** any portability transfer naming this account as its source is still in status `REQUESTED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
- Otherwise the posture is `UNRESTRICTED`.

Because posture never depends on posture, that single pass always reaches a stable answer. The same recomputation refreshes `uncorrected_excess_cents`, which is the sum of the detected excess amounts over this account's cases whose status is `EXPIRED` — money the saver failed to correct in time.

Posture gates money movement, not investment freedom. `LOCKED` refuses new portability transfers and new closure requests. `RESTRICTED` also refuses new portability transfers and new closure requests, but it still permits investment elections, because a saver must be able to move out of a losing fund while an excess correction is pending. Contribution postings and correction executions are never posture-gated; they proceed regardless of posture.

### 2.13. Cascades, Propagation and the Batch Jobs

Many of the workflows in §3 are cascades: a single request changes one row, and the consequences propagate deterministically into siblings and parents within the same request. Three propagation habits hold everywhere. First, when an operation changes a child in a way that alters a parent's derived figures, the parent's derived figures are recomputed and the parent's `updated_at` and `version` move as part of the same operation — an employer's unremitted total and compliance posture, an account's balances, headroom, fund split and posture, a batch's line count and reconciliation totals. Second, cascade-created rows are real, first-class resources: they appear in the corresponding listing even though no creation response ever returned them, which is why the employer-scoped certification and penalty listings, the account-scoped excess-case listing and the assessment-scoped referral listing all exist. Third, cascade-created rows record the literal `SYSTEM` in their actor fields.

Three administrative batch jobs drive time-dependent behaviour, and none of them is a mere convenience: they are the only way certain transitions ever happen. The **auto-enrolment run** activates notified enrolments whose opt-out window has closed, opening accounts and seeding default investment elections, and reports `activated_count`, `accounts_opened_count` and `skipped_count`. The **annual escalation run** raises seasoned deferral rates, reporting `escalated_count`, `capped_count` and `skipped_count`. The **compliance sweep** re-derives employer delinquency, mints sweep-origin penalty assessments, expires overdue excess cases with excise tax, and lapses overdue indirect rollovers, reporting `delinquent_employer_count`, `assessments_created_count`, `expired_case_count` and `failed_transfer_count`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Covered Employer Onto the Programme

An employer enters the system as a record carrying its `ein` (its employer identification number, unique across the programme and written in the two-digits-hyphen-seven-digits form), its `legal_name`, the `worksite_state` of its primary worksite as two uppercase letters, and its `employee_count` — the most recently reported eligible-employee headcount, which may be zero but never negative. Either a `ProgramAdministrator` or the employer itself may create the record. A second record for an `ein` that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EIN`.

Creation immediately derives the employer's place in the phased mandate. The headcount is matched against four descending bands, and the band fixes both `wave_tier` and `registration_deadline`: a headcount of at least 100 lands in `WAVE_1` with a deadline of 30 June 2026; a headcount from 50 through 99 inclusive lands in `WAVE_2` with a deadline of 31 December 2026; a headcount from 5 through 49 inclusive lands in `WAVE_3` with a deadline of 30 June 2027; and a headcount of 4 or fewer lands in `NOT_COVERED`, whose `registration_deadline` is null because there is nothing to register for. Deadlines are plain calendar dates with no time component.

An employer carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and confusing them is the most common misreading of this domain. `registration_status` is the mandate dimension: `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `REGISTERED`, `EXEMPT_CERTIFIED` or `EXEMPT_SMALL`. `compliance_status` is the enforcement dimension: `COMPLIANT`, `DELINQUENT`, `PENALIZED` or `REFERRED`. Neither dimension constrains the other — an employer can be `REGISTERED` and `REFERRED` at the same time, or `EXEMPT_CERTIFIED` and `DELINQUENT`. On creation, an employer whose tier resolves to `NOT_COVERED` is placed directly in `EXEMPT_SMALL` rather than in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`; every covered employer starts in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`. Either way the enforcement dimension starts at `COMPLIANT`.

Completing registration is the employer's own act and no one else's: only the `Employer` principal that owns the record may do it, the record must still be in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, and its `wave_tier` must be something other than `NOT_COVERED` — otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_STATE_INVALID`. The payload must carry `non_fiduciary_acknowledged` as true, the employer's acknowledgement that its role is ministerial, together with the `payroll_contact_email` at which the programme will reach its payroll department; a payload that does not affirm the acknowledgement is not a registration. On success the mandate dimension moves `PENDING_REGISTRATION → REGISTERED`, `non_fiduciary_acknowledged` is stored as true, `payroll_contact_email` is stored, `registered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the employer's `version` increments by one. Before registration both `payroll_contact_email` and `registered_at` are null.

Headcounts change, and a restatement is a first-class operation rather than a general-purpose edit. Either a `ProgramAdministrator` or the owning employer may submit a recount carrying a new `employee_count`, the `effective_date` on which the new count took effect, and `expected_employer_version` as described in §2.7. The recount re-derives `wave_tier` and `registration_deadline` from the same four bands, records `last_recount_effective_date` as the supplied effective date (it is null for an employer that has never been recounted), and increments the employer's `version`. Crossing the small-employer boundary moves the mandate dimension in whichever direction the new count points: a recount that raises the headcount above 4 moves `EXEMPT_SMALL → PENDING_REGISTRATION` and materialises a concrete deadline, while a recount that drops it to 4 or fewer resolves the tier to `NOT_COVERED`, returns the employer to `EXEMPT_SMALL` and nulls the deadline again. A recount is refused with `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_HOLD` while any referral raised against the employer remains `OPEN` — an employer whose file sits with an outside agency does not get to restate the facts underneath it.

### 3.2. Qualified-Plan Exemptions: Claiming, Deciding and Losing One

An employer that already sponsors its own retirement plan claims an exemption by filing a certification, which either the employer itself or a `ProgramAdministrator` may submit against the employer's own record; the certification's owning employer is taken from the address, never from the payload. The filing declares `qualified_plan_type` — one of `401K`, `403B`, `SIMPLE_IRA`, `SEP_IRA` or `DEFINED_BENEFIT` — the `plan_sponsor_ein` of the entity sponsoring that plan, which for a controlled group may legitimately differ from the employer's own identification number, the `plan_effective_date` on which the qualified plan took effect, and `attestation_accepted`, the employer's attestation under penalty of perjury, which must be true.

Filing derives a three-year coverage interval. `coverage_period_start` equals `plan_effective_date` and is the inclusive lower bound; `coverage_period_end` equals `plan_effective_date` plus three years and is the **exclusive** upper bound. A new certification is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when that half-open interval intersects the interval of another certification of the same employer whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`. Half-openness matters here: a certification whose start falls exactly on a prior certification's end is accepted, because the prior coverage has already lapsed on that day. Certifications in `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` release their interval entirely. Separately, a certification whose derived `coverage_period_end` is on or before the current clock date is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` — a plan whose three-year window has already elapsed cannot support a present exemption, and the employer must re-certify with a current effective date. If the employer is under an `OPEN` referral, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_HOLD` is reported instead of either of those, since the enforcement interlock outranks both the overlap and the window check. A newly filed certification is created in status `SUBMITTED`, with `decision_note`, `revocation_reason`, `decided_at` and `revoked_at` all null.

Only a `ProgramAdministrator` decides a certification, supplying `decision` as either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and optionally a `decision_note`. Both outcomes stamp `decided_at` from the logical clock and store the note. A rejection moves the certification `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and leaves the employer's `registration_status` exactly as it was. An approval moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and requires that the clock date be inside the coverage interval — on or after `coverage_period_start` and strictly before `coverage_period_end` — and that the employer have no enrolment in status `ACTIVE`, since an employer cannot simultaneously claim an exemption and be running programme payroll deductions for live savers. Approval then drives the employer's mandate dimension to `EXEMPT_CERTIFIED`, whether it was sitting in `PENDING_REGISTRATION` or in `REGISTERED`, and increments the employer's `version`. Approval also has one enforcement side effect: if the employer's `compliance_status` was `DELINQUENT` purely because it had not registered by its deadline, that delinquency is premised on a mandate that no longer applies, so the enforcement dimension clears back to `COMPLIANT`. Deciding an already-decided certification is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_STATE_INVALID`.

Revocation is the head of the deepest cascade in this domain, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may pull the trigger. The certification must be `APPROVED` and the employer's mandate dimension must be `EXEMPT_CERTIFIED`; the request supplies `revocation_reason`. In one request, the following happens in order. The certification moves `APPROVED → REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped and `revocation_reason` stored. The employer's `registration_status` falls back to `PENDING_REGISTRATION`. If the clock date is later than the employer's `registration_deadline`, the employer's `compliance_status` becomes `DELINQUENT`, because the mandate it was excused from has been overdue all along. A penalty assessment is then minted automatically with `violation_type` `FAILURE_TO_REGISTER`, `origin` `SYSTEM_CASCADE`, `notice_date` set to the employer's `registration_deadline` and `assessed_by_user_id` set to the literal `SYSTEM`, which drives `compliance_status` on to `PENALIZED`. Finally, if — and only if — the employer already has a separate assessment in status `AFFIRMED`, an enforcement referral is minted **on that pre-existing affirmed assessment**, not on the assessment just created, which is still inside its own cure period and cannot yet be affirmed; that referral carries `agency` `STATE_LABOR_COMMISSIONER` and `referred_by_user_id` `SYSTEM`, and pushes `compliance_status` to `REFERRED`. The employer's `version` increments once for the whole cascade. Revoking a certification that is not `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.3. The Saver's Enrolment: Notice, Opt-Out, Activation and Rate

A saver first exists as a participant record created by a `ProgramAdministrator`, carrying `full_name`, `date_of_birth`, `email`, `ssn_last4` (exactly four digits of the taxpayer identifier), and `state_resident` indicating whether the saver resides in the sponsoring state. Two contact fields, `mailing_address` and `phone`, may be supplied at creation but are otherwise null until a service update fills them in. Three figures on the record are derived rather than supplied: `age_at_year_end`, the saver's whole years of age on 31 December of the calendar year in which the logical clock currently sits; `catch_up_eligible`, true exactly when `age_at_year_end` is at least 50; and `indirect_rollover_count`, the number of this saver's portability transfers whose kind is `INDIRECT_60_DAY` and whose status is `SETTLED`. Later, either the saver or a `ProgramAdministrator` may run a service-centre contact update supplying any of `email`, `mailing_address` and `phone`; identity and birth date are immutable, and the update increments the participant's `version`.

Enrolling an employee is the employer's act, performed against its own record and available also to a `ProgramAdministrator`. The payload carries only facts the employer legitimately owns: the `participant_id` of the saver, the `hire_date` at this employer, and `annual_compensation_cents`, the annualised compensation as the employer reports it. That compensation figure is reference data only and is never used to compute a deduction. The employer supplies no rate at all: the new enrolment's `contribution_rate_bp` is seeded from the program year's `default_contribution_rate_bp` with `rate_source` set to `DEFAULT`. The enrolment is created in status `NOTIFIED`, and its `opt_out_deadline` is the date of creation plus the program year's `opt_out_window_days` — thirty days of notice under the default configuration. The owning employer is taken from the address. An enrolment attempt against an employer whose `registration_status` is anything other than `REGISTERED` is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_REGISTERED`: an employer sitting in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `EXEMPT_CERTIFIED` or `EXEMPT_SMALL` cannot enrol anyone. An attempt that duplicates a live pairing — an enrolment already exists for this employer and this saver in status `NOTIFIED`, `ACTIVE` or `OPTED_OUT` — is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`; re-hiring someone whose prior enrolment is `TERMINATED` is allowed and creates a fresh row, whereas someone sitting in `OPTED_OUT` must be re-enrolled rather than duplicated.

Activation is never a request; it is the work of the auto-enrolment batch. For each enrolment in `NOTIFIED` whose `opt_out_deadline` has arrived — the batch's evaluation date is on or after that deadline — and whose employer is still `REGISTERED`, the enrolment moves `NOTIFIED → ACTIVE`. If the saver has no participant account yet, the batch opens one of `account_type` `ROTH_IRA` with a zero balance and an `UNRESTRICTED` posture, and seeds a system-chosen investment election as described in §3.4. The batch's response reports how many enrolments it activated, how many accounts it opened, and how many candidates it skipped.

Opting out is the saver's decision, exercisable by the `Participant` who owns the enrolment or by a `ProgramAdministrator` acting in the service centre; an `Employer` principal that reaches for it is refused with `ERR_FIDUCIARY_BOUNDARY_VIOLATION`. The request carries an `opt_out_reason_code` of `NOT_INTERESTED`, `HAS_OTHER_PLAN`, `AFFORDABILITY` or `OTHER`, and optionally `request_permissible_withdrawal`. Two starting points are legal. From `NOTIFIED`, the opt-out is a pre-activation refusal and is only available while the clock date is strictly before `opt_out_deadline`; on or after that date the request is refused with `ERR_OPT_OUT_WINDOW_CLOSED` and the saver must instead wait for activation and opt out of an `ACTIVE` enrolment. A `NOTIFIED → OPTED_OUT` opt-out opens no account and moves no money. From `ACTIVE`, the opt-out is always available to the saver. Either way `opted_out_at` is stamped from the clock and `opt_out_reason_code` is stored, and the enrolment's `contribution_rate_bp` is retained as audit history until a re-enrolment resets it.

A saver who has already had money withheld may also ask for it back. When `request_permissible_withdrawal` is true, the withdrawal is available only inside the statutory window that opens at the saver's first contribution: `first_contribution_date` must be non-null and the clock date must be no later than `first_contribution_date` plus the program year's `opt_out_window_days`. Outside that window the request is refused with `ERR_PERMISSIBLE_WITHDRAWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` — note that the refusal is of the *withdrawal*, so a saver past the window may still opt out by simply not asking for one. Inside the window, `permissible_withdrawal_cents` — which is zero on every enrolment that has never paid one — is set to the enrolment's `lifetime_deducted_cents` — the sum of the withheld amounts across every posted line of this enrolment — and that same number of cents is debited from the account's `balance_cents` and from its `contribution_basis_cents`, with the fund-level split recomputed and the account's `version` incremented.

Re-enrolment reverses an opt-out, and the saver or a `ProgramAdministrator` may request it with an optional `reenroll_note`. The enrolment must be in `OPTED_OUT`, the employer must be back in — or still in — `REGISTERED`, else `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_REGISTERED`, and `employment_end_date` must be null, since a separated employee has nothing to re-enrol into. On success the enrolment moves `OPTED_OUT → ACTIVE`, and the rate is deliberately **not** resumed: `contribution_rate_bp` is reset to the program year's `default_contribution_rate_bp` with `rate_source` back to `DEFAULT`, `escalation_opt_out` is cleared to false, `reenrollment_count` increments by one, and `opted_out_at` and `opt_out_reason_code` are cleared.

A saver may also elect their own deferral rate at any point while the enrolment is `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, supplying `contribution_rate_bp` and optionally `escalation_opt_out`. The elected rate must be at least 100 basis points and no more than 1500 basis points — one percent to fifteen percent of gross wages — and a value outside that band is refused with `ERR_RATE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`; a saver who wants to contribute nothing expresses that as an opt-out, not as a rate of zero. A successful election overwrites the rate and sets `rate_source` to `PARTICIPANT_ELECTED`, and stores `escalation_opt_out` when it was supplied. Here too an `Employer` principal is refused with `ERR_FIDUCIARY_BOUNDARY_VIOLATION`.

Automatic escalation is the programme's nudge, applied once a year by the annual-escalation batch, which may be given an explicit `effective_date` to evaluate against. The batch considers **only** enrolments that satisfy all four conditions: the status is `ACTIVE`; `escalation_opt_out` is false; `first_contribution_date` is non-null; and the number of whole days from `first_contribution_date` to the effective date is at least the program year's `escalation_seasoning_days`, six months of seasoning under the default configuration. For each qualifying enrolment the batch computes a candidate rate as the current `contribution_rate_bp` plus `escalation_increment_bp`, but no higher than `escalation_cap_bp` — the smaller of those two numbers wins. If the candidate rate is strictly greater than the current rate, the enrolment's `contribution_rate_bp` becomes the candidate and `rate_source` becomes `ESCALATED`. If it is not — the enrolment already sits at or above the cap — the enrolment is left completely untouched and is reported as skipped rather than escalated. The cap clamps, it does not reject: an enrolment at 750 basis points with a 100-basis-point increment and an 800-basis-point cap lands on exactly 800, never on 850. The batch reports how many enrolments it escalated, how many it clamped at the cap, and how many it skipped.

Separation is reported by the employer alone, supplying an `employment_end_date`. That single field terminates the enrolment: from either `ACTIVE` or `OPTED_OUT` the status becomes `TERMINATED`, and `TERMINATED` is terminal — no trigger ever moves an enrolment out of it, and any trigger attempted against one is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`. From that point no remittance line may reference the enrolment. The saver's account, however, is untouched: it remains open and fully portable, because the money belongs to the person, not to the job.

### 3.4. The Account and Where Its Money Sits

The fund menu is programme configuration, curated by a `ProgramAdministrator` and immutable once published: the programme adds a replacement option rather than editing a live one. Each option carries a `fund_code` — a short stable code of 4 to 12 uppercase alphanumeric characters, unique across the menu, and the token by which allocations name it — a human-readable `name`, a `category` of `TARGET_DATE`, `CAPITAL_PRESERVATION`, `EQUITY_INDEX` or `BOND_INDEX`, an `expense_ratio_bp` from 0 through 500 basis points, and `default_eligible`, the flag that says whether the automatic default selector is allowed to pick this option. `target_year`, the glide-path landing year, is required when and only when `category` is `TARGET_DATE`, must fall between 2020 and 2085 inclusive, and must be null for every other category; violating that pairing in either direction is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An option's `status` may be supplied at creation as `OPEN`, `CLOSED_TO_NEW` or `TERMINATED` and defaults to `OPEN`, which lets the programme seed a legacy option that still holds balances but can no longer be elected into. A duplicate `fund_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FUND_CODE`.

When the auto-enrolment batch opens an account it must also decide where the money goes, and it does so **without consulting the saver's preferences at all** — that indifference is precisely what keeps the employer non-fiduciary. The selector first computes the saver's retirement year as the year component of `date_of_birth` plus 65. It then builds a first candidate pool from every menu option whose `category` is `TARGET_DATE`, whose `status` is `OPEN`, whose `default_eligible` is true, and whose `target_year` is greater than or equal to that retirement year. From that pool it picks the option with the smallest `target_year`; where two tie, the one with the smaller `expense_ratio_bp`; where those also tie, the one created earliest. The resulting election records `selection_basis` as `TARGET_DATE_GLIDE_PATH`. If the first pool is empty, the selector falls back to a second pool of every option whose `category` is `CAPITAL_PRESERVATION`, whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `default_eligible` is true, picking the smallest `expense_ratio_bp` and breaking ties on the earliest creation; that election records `selection_basis` as `CAPITAL_PRESERVATION_FALLBACK`. If both pools are empty the batch cannot invest an account it would be opening, so it fails with `ERR_NO_DEFAULT_FUND_AVAILABLE`, creates no partial account, and leaves the enrolment in `NOTIFIED`. A successful default election holds exactly one allocation entry at the full 10000 basis points and records `source` as `SYSTEM_DEFAULT`.

The account itself is the individual retirement account. It records the `participant_id` of its owner and an `account_type` of either `ROTH_IRA` — which is what the auto-enrolment batch opens — or `TRADITIONAL_IRA`, which only ever comes into existence through a recharacterisation correction. `rolled_over_from_account_id` is null for an ordinary account and points at a source account when this account received a settled programme-to-programme portability transfer or was opened by a recharacterisation. Its lifecycle lives in `account_status`, which starts at `OPEN`; its derived gate lives in `posture`, described in §2.12. Its money lives in `balance_cents`, the total cents held, and `contribution_basis_cents`, the portion of that total attributable to contributions rather than to earnings, which is what drives the pro-rata basis split on any distribution. `ytd_contribution_cents` holds contributions allocated to this account from remittances whose pay date falls inside the current program year, net of completed excess corrections; `remaining_headroom_cents` holds the saver's remaining annual room and never drops below zero; `uncorrected_excess_cents` holds excess money that was never corrected in time; `fund_balances` holds the fund-level split; and `active_election_id` names the account's currently governing investment election, discoverable through the account's election history listing, and is null until the auto-enrolment batch seeds the first default. Three closure fields — `closure_reason`, `residual_disposition` and `closed_at` — stay null while the account is `OPEN`.

A saver changes funds by creating a new investment election on the account, which either the `Participant` who owns it or a `ProgramAdministrator` may do; the `Employer` role is not admitted to this operation at all, so an employer attempt is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. The election supplies `allocations`, an array of objects each pairing a `fund_code` with an `allocation_bp` weight, together with `expected_account_version` as §2.7 requires. Every named `fund_code` must match a menu option whose `status` is `OPEN`; a code matching nothing, or matching an option that is `CLOSED_TO_NEW` or `TERMINATED`, is refused with `ERR_FUND_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The weights must then sum to exactly 10000, every individual `allocation_bp` must be strictly greater than zero, and no `fund_code` may appear twice; any of those three failures is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_TOTAL_INVALID`, and because fund availability is checked first, an election naming a terminated fund reports the fund problem rather than the arithmetic one. On success the account's previously active election moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped from the clock — that stamp is an exclusive upper bound on the old election's effectivity window, and, as §3.6 explains, it is also the signal that steers net-income-attributable arithmetic onto its deemed-rate variant. The new election is created `ACTIVE` with `source` `PARTICIPANT_ELECTED`, its `effective_date` set to the clock's date as the inclusive lower bound of its own window, its `selection_basis` null because no automatic selector chose it, and the account's `active_election_id` repointed at it. The fund split is then recomputed from the new weights and the account's `version` increments. An election against an account whose `account_status` is `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`; an election against a `RESTRICTED` account is deliberately permitted, so a pending excess case can never trap a saver in a fund they want to leave.

Investment earnings are the only way a balance grows without a contribution, and only a `Recordkeeper` may credit them. The posting supplies `earnings_cents`, which may be negative to record a loss, and the `posting_date` on which the earnings are recognised. The account's `balance_cents` is adjusted by that amount while `contribution_basis_cents` is left exactly as it was — earnings create no new basis — the fund split is recomputed from the new balance, and the account's `version` increments. Earnings postings against a `CLOSED` account are refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. Because earnings drift a balance away from the sum of its contributions, they are what makes the correction arithmetic of §3.6 non-trivial.

### 3.5. Payroll Deduction: From Draft Batch to Posted Contributions

Every pay cycle, a registered employer opens a remittance batch for one pay period. The batch declares `pay_period_start` and `pay_period_end`, the inclusive first and last days of the period, `pay_date`, the day wages were actually paid and deductions withheld, and `total_deducted_cents`, the employer's declared total withheld for that period, which must be at least 1. Request shape requires `pay_period_end` to be on or after `pay_period_start` and `pay_date` to be on or after `pay_period_end`. The owning employer comes from the address, and only that `Employer` principal may open the batch — a batch against an employer that is not `REGISTERED` is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_REGISTERED`. The batch is created in status `DRAFT`, with `line_count`, `total_allocated_cents`, `total_excess_cents` and `days_late` at their initial values and every timestamp and reason field null.

Pay periods occupy calendar space exclusively. A new batch whose `[pay_period_start, pay_period_end]` interval — **closed on both ends** — intersects the interval of another batch of the same employer whose status is anything other than `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because both ends are inclusive, two periods that share a single boundary day collide, while a period beginning the day after another ends is accepted. A reversed batch releases its interval so a corrected replacement can occupy the same period.

Lines are added to a batch one employee at a time while the batch is in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`; adding a line to a batch in any other status is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_STATE_INVALID`. Each line names its `enrollment_id`, reports `gross_wages_cents` for the period (which may be zero but never negative), and declares `deducted_cents`, the cents actually withheld, which must be at least 1. Each line also carries the `remittance_id` of the batch it belongs to. The programme independently derives `expected_deduction_cents` at line-creation time as the line's `gross_wages_cents` multiplied by the enrolment's current `contribution_rate_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to a whole cent; if `deducted_cents` does not equal that figure exactly, the line is refused with `ERR_DEDUCTION_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. The line must also be a coherent target: an enrolment belonging to a different employer than the batch's, an enrolment in status `OPTED_OUT` or `TERMINATED`, or an enrolment whose saver has no open account, is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_DEDUCTIBLE`. A created line starts in status `PENDING` with `allocated_cents` and `excess_cents` at zero, and the parent batch's `line_count` increments.

Submitting the batch is the employer's handover to the programme and may carry an optional `submission_note`. The batch must be in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`; it must have at least one line; the sum of its lines' `deducted_cents` must equal its declared `total_deducted_cents`, failing which the submission is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_TOTAL_MISMATCH`; and its pay period must still not overlap another live batch of the employer. On success the batch moves to `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` is stamped, any prior `rejection_reason` is cleared, and lateness is frozen: `days_late` becomes the number of whole days by which the submission clock date exceeds `pay_date` plus the program year's `remittance_due_days`, floored at zero so an on-time batch records zero. The comparison is strict, so a batch submitted exactly on `pay_date` plus `remittance_due_days` is on time. Submission then recomputes the employer's `unremitted_deduction_cents` and re-derives its `compliance_status`, and an employer whose unremitted total has become positive moves `COMPLIANT → DELINQUENT`.

The programme may hand a batch back. A `Recordkeeper` or a `ProgramAdministrator` may reject a `SUBMITTED` batch with a `rejection_reason`, moving it to `REJECTED` while leaving every line in `PENDING` so the employer can amend and resubmit. Resubmission from `REJECTED` re-runs the reconciliation check and re-freezes `days_late` against the new submission clock, which is how a repair cycle accumulates lateness rather than escaping it.

Posting is the deepest money cascade in the system, and only a `Recordkeeper` performs it, supplying a `posting_reference` from the recordkeeping platform. The batch must be `SUBMITTED`, and every enrolment referenced by its lines must be in status `ACTIVE`. On success the batch moves `SUBMITTED → POSTED`, `posted_at` and `posting_reference` are stored, and every line moves `PENDING → POSTED` with `allocated_cents` set equal to its `deducted_cents` — the programme credits contributions in full and corrects any excess afterwards rather than refusing money at the door. Each affected account has its `balance_cents`, `contribution_basis_cents` and `ytd_contribution_cents` increased by the amounts credited to it, its `remaining_headroom_cents` recomputed, its fund split re-derived, and its `version` incremented. The batch's `total_allocated_cents` and `total_excess_cents` are recomputed as the sums of `allocated_cents` and `excess_cents` over its lines that are in `POSTED`. On the enrolment side, `first_contribution_date` is filled in with the pay date of the earliest posted line for that enrolment — it stays null until a first posting — and `lifetime_deducted_cents` accumulates the enrolment's posted withheld amounts. The employer's `unremitted_deduction_cents` and `compliance_status` are recomputed, since the money has now arrived.

Contribution ceilings are the reason posting has to compute anything at all. A saver's annual ceiling is the program year's `ira_annual_limit_cents`, plus the program year's `catch_up_limit_cents` when the saver's `age_at_year_end` is at least 50, and nothing extra otherwise. `ytd_contribution_cents` aggregates `allocated_cents` over **every** posted line that credits this saver, across all of their employers, whose parent batch's `pay_date` falls inside the program year's `calendar_year`; that cross-employer aggregation is what makes the limit a per-person rule rather than a per-employer one. Lines in status `PENDING`, `REVERSED` or `RETURNED` contribute nothing to the aggregate, and every completed excess correction subtracts its detected excess amount from it. The saver's `remaining_headroom_cents` is then the ceiling minus that aggregate, floored at zero.

At posting time the lines of a batch are evaluated one at a time in ascending order of pay date and, within the same pay date, of line identifier. For each line, the room available before that line is the ceiling minus the year-to-date total accumulated so far, floored at zero; the line's `excess_cents` is its `allocated_cents` minus that room, likewise floored at zero. The full `allocated_cents` is still credited to the account regardless; the excess is merely recorded. For every account that crossed its ceiling during the posting, a single excess-contribution case is opened in status `DETECTED`, capturing the account's `account_id`, the program year's `calendar_year`, the `detected_excess_cents`, the `detected_at_pay_date` taken from the pay date of the batch whose posting produced the excess, the `opening_fmv_cents` recorded as the account's `balance_cents` immediately *before* this posting, the `period_contributions_cents` begun with the deposit that carried the excess, the `computation_period_start` set to the clock's date at detection, and the `deadline_date` described in §3.6. Every affected account's posture is then re-derived, which turns it `RESTRICTED` for as long as the case is unresolved.

A posting can be undone, but only as an exact compensation and only by a `Recordkeeper`, who supplies a `reversal_reason`. The batch must be `POSTED`, and no excess-contribution case that this batch produced may already be in status `COMPLETED` — once a correction has actually moved money, the posting behind it can no longer be unwound. On success the batch moves `POSTED → REVERSED` with `reversed_at` and `reversal_reason` stored; every posted line moves to `REVERSED` with its `allocated_cents` and `excess_cents` zeroed and the batch's `total_allocated_cents` and `total_excess_cents` recomputed to zero; each affected account has its `balance_cents`, `contribution_basis_cents` and `ytd_contribution_cents` decremented by exactly the amounts the posting added, with `remaining_headroom_cents` recomputed, the fund split re-derived and its `version` incremented. Every excess-contribution case that this batch opened becomes `VOIDED`, whether it was still in `DETECTED` or had already reached `METHOD_ELECTED`; a voided case keeps its `detected_excess_cents`, and its elected `correction_method` where one was chosen, purely as audit history, and no longer restricts anything. The affected accounts' postures are re-derived accordingly. Contribution room ends up exactly where it started, with no drift, and the batch's pay period is freed for a replacement batch.

### 3.6. Correcting an Excess Contribution

An excess-contribution case is the programme's due-process wrapper around money that should not be in a retirement account. Its `deadline_date` is built by taking the program year's `correction_deadline_month_day` — a month and day such as the default `"04-15"` — and placing it in the year after the case's `calendar_year`, giving the saver until the ordinary tax filing date of the following spring. `period_distributions_cents` starts at zero and accumulates any cents distributed out of the account during the computation period. `closing_fmv_cents`, both net-income figures, `nia_method_applied`, `applied_nia_cents`, `corrected_amount_cents`, `settlement_reference` and `completed_at` are all null while the case is unresolved, and `excise_tax_cents` sits at zero.

The saver chooses the correction path. Either the owning `Participant` or a `ProgramAdministrator` may elect a `correction_method` of `REFUND_TO_PARTICIPANT`, `RECHARACTERIZE_TO_TRADITIONAL` or `CARRY_FORWARD` while the case is in `DETECTED` and the clock date is on or before `deadline_date`; a later attempt is refused with `ERR_CORRECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`, and an attempt against a case in any status other than `DETECTED` is refused with `ERR_EXCESS_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The `Employer` role is not admitted to this operation at all, so an employer attempt is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. A successful election moves the case `DETECTED → METHOD_ELECTED` and records the chosen method.

Executing the correction is a `Recordkeeper` act, supplying a `settlement_reference`. The case must be in `METHOD_ELECTED` and the clock date must still be on or before `deadline_date`. Completion first captures `closing_fmv_cents` as the account's `balance_cents` at that moment, before any correction debit, and `period_contributions_cents` as the cents contributed into the account from `computation_period_start` through completion, always including the deposit that carried the excess.

**Both** net-income-attributable variants are then computed and **both** are persisted on the case, so the divergence between them is always inspectable. The adjusted-balance variant works from two fair-market snapshots: the adjusted opening balance is `opening_fmv_cents` plus `period_contributions_cents` minus `period_distributions_cents`; the adjusted closing balance is `closing_fmv_cents`; and `nia_adjusted_balance_cents` is `detected_excess_cents` multiplied by the difference of the closing balance minus the adjusted opening balance, divided by that adjusted opening balance, rounded half up to a whole cent. Where the closing balance is below the adjusted opening balance the difference is negative and the resulting net income is negative, which correctly reduces the amount to be corrected. The adjusted opening balance can never be zero, because the period contributions always include the deposit that carried the excess. The deemed-rate variant instead ignores balances: the number of whole days from `computation_period_start` to the completion clock date is multiplied by the program year's `nia_daily_rate_bp` and by `detected_excess_cents`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole cent, giving `nia_daily_prorata_cents`.

Which of the two is actually applied is fully deterministic and is decided by whether the account's investment mix moved during the computation period. If the account has any investment election whose `superseded_at` is non-null and falls on or after `computation_period_start` and strictly before the completion instant, then no single opening-and-closing pair fairly represents the excess and `nia_method_applied` is set to `DAILY_PRORATA`; otherwise `nia_method_applied` is set to `ADJUSTED_BALANCE`. `applied_nia_cents` takes the value of whichever variant field the applied method names, and `corrected_amount_cents` is `detected_excess_cents` plus `applied_nia_cents`.

From there the three elected methods diverge, and each has its own complete set of effects. Under `REFUND_TO_PARTICIPANT`, the account's `balance_cents` and `contribution_basis_cents` are both debited by `corrected_amount_cents`, and its `ytd_contribution_cents` is reduced by `detected_excess_cents` — the excess itself, not the excess plus earnings. This branch alone has a solvency precondition: the account's `balance_cents` must be at least `detected_excess_cents` plus `applied_nia_cents`, and a refund larger than the balance is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ACCOUNT_BALANCE` (HTTP 402). Under `RECHARACTERIZE_TO_TRADITIONAL`, a second participant account of `account_type` `TRADITIONAL_IRA` is opened for the same saver with its `rolled_over_from_account_id` pointing at the Roth account, and is credited `corrected_amount_cents`; the Roth account is debited that same amount, and the Roth account's `ytd_contribution_cents` is reduced by `detected_excess_cents`. Under `CARRY_FORWARD`, no money moves at all: the balance is untouched, `ytd_contribution_cents` is reduced by `detected_excess_cents`, and the amount is counted against the following program year instead. In every branch the case moves `METHOD_ELECTED → COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped and `settlement_reference` stored, the fund-level split is recomputed on every account whose balance changed — both sides in the recharacterisation case — `remaining_headroom_cents` is recomputed, posture is re-derived (which typically returns the account to `UNRESTRICTED`), and the account's `version` increments.

A case that nobody resolves does not simply linger. When the compliance sweep runs and finds a case in `DETECTED` or `METHOD_ELECTED` whose `deadline_date` is behind the clock date, the case moves to `EXPIRED` and the statutory excise tax is booked: `excise_tax_cents` becomes `detected_excess_cents` multiplied by 600 basis points — six percent — divided by 10000 and rounded half up to a whole cent. The elected `correction_method`, where one was elected, is retained as audit history. The account's `uncorrected_excess_cents` is then recomputed as the sum of `detected_excess_cents` across its `EXPIRED` cases, and its posture remains `RESTRICTED`, because uncorrected excess money is exactly the situation in which the programme does not want new money movement. `COMPLETED`, `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal: no trigger moves a case out of any of them.

### 3.7. Portability: Moving Retirement Savings

A saver may move money out of one of their own accounts, either to an outside institution or to another of their own programme accounts, and either the owning `Participant` or a `ProgramAdministrator` may request it. The request names the `participant_id` of the owner, the `source_account_id` to be debited, a `destination_type` of `EXTERNAL_IRA` or `PROGRAM_ACCOUNT`, a `transfer_kind` of `DIRECT_ROLLOVER`, `TRUSTEE_TO_TRUSTEE` or `INDIRECT_60_DAY`, and the gross `amount_cents` to move, which must be at least 1. When the destination is a programme account, `destination_account_id` is required and `external_institution_name` must be absent; when the destination is external, `external_institution_name` is required and `destination_account_id` must be absent. Portability moves money within one person's own savings and is never a transfer between two people: a source account that does not belong to the named participant, a destination equal to the source, a destination belonging to a different participant, or a destination account that is not open, is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNER_MISMATCH`.

Indirect rollovers are the only kind the programme rations, because the tax rules ration them. When `transfer_kind` is `INDIRECT_60_DAY`, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLLOVER_FREQUENCY_LIMIT` if the saver already has an indirect transfer — counted across every one of that person's accounts, not per account — in status `SETTLED` or `REQUESTED` whose settlement date, or whose request date when it has not settled, falls inside the trailing window that runs from 364 days before the request date through the request date itself, both ends included. That is 365 calendar days counting the request date. Concretely, a prior rollover settled on 1 May 2026 blocks a request on 30 April 2027, because that window opens on 1 May 2026, and permits one on 1 May 2027, because by then the window opens on 2 May 2026. `DIRECT_ROLLOVER` and `TRUSTEE_TO_TRUSTEE` movements are unlimited.

The five money figures are pinned at request time, against the source account's balances **before** the debit, and they must be derived in exactly this order, because a different order gives a different answer. First, the fee tier is keyed on the **gross** `amount_cents` and never on any net figure: a gross amount of 500000 cents or less charges 40 basis points; from 500001 through 950000 cents inclusive charges 50 basis points; 950001 cents or more charges 60 basis points. `fee_cents` is the gross amount multiplied by that tier rate, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Second, `basis_portion_cents` is the gross amount multiplied by the source account's pre-debit `contribution_basis_cents`, divided by the source account's pre-debit `balance_cents`, rounded half up. Third, `taxable_cents` is the gross amount minus `basis_portion_cents`. Fourth, `withholding_cents` is the mandatory ten-percent withholding — 1000 basis points — applied to `taxable_cents`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up, but only when `transfer_kind` is `INDIRECT_60_DAY`; for the other two kinds it is zero. Fifth, `net_cents` is the gross amount minus `fee_cents` minus `withholding_cents`. Keying the fee tier on a post-withholding figure is the classic error this ordering exists to prevent: a gross indirect rollover of 1000000 cents has 50000 cents withheld, and treating the remaining 950000 as the tier key drops it into the 50-basis-point band instead of the 60-basis-point band, understating the fee and overstating the net proceeds by 1250 cents.

On acceptance the transfer is created in status `REQUESTED`, `in_transit_cents` is set to the gross `amount_cents` — the in-flight leg that keeps programme cash conserved — `requested_on` is set to the clock's date, and `settle_by_date` is set to `requested_on` plus 60 days for an `INDIRECT_60_DAY` transfer and to null for the other kinds. The source account is debited immediately: `balance_cents` falls by the gross `amount_cents` and `contribution_basis_cents` falls by `basis_portion_cents`, the fund split is recomputed, the account's posture is re-derived to `RESTRICTED` for as long as the transfer is in flight, and the account's `version` increments. `is_taxable_distribution` starts false, and `settled_on`, `settlement_reference` and `return_reason` start null. The request is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED` when the source account is `CLOSED`, with `ERR_ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED` when its posture is `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKED`, and with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ACCOUNT_BALANCE` when the gross `amount_cents` exceeds the source `balance_cents`.

Three endings are possible, and each is available only while the transfer is still `REQUESTED`; attempting to settle or return anything else is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_STATE_INVALID`. A `Recordkeeper` settles the transfer by supplying the `settled_on` date the recordkeeping platform reports and a `settlement_reference`. For an `INDIRECT_60_DAY` transfer, `settled_on` must be on or before `settle_by_date`, and a later date is refused with `ERR_ROLLOVER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Settlement moves the transfer `REQUESTED → SETTLED`, zeroes `in_transit_cents`, and stores both supplied values. When the destination is a programme account, that account's `balance_cents` increases by `net_cents`, its `contribution_basis_cents` increases by `basis_portion_cents`, its `rolled_over_from_account_id` is set to the `source_account_id`, and its fund split is recomputed. When the destination is external, the money leaves the programme altogether and programme cash falls by the net proceeds together with the fee and the withholding. An `INDIRECT_60_DAY` settlement sets `is_taxable_distribution` to true. The source account's posture is re-derived, and the `version` of every touched account increments.

Alternatively the receiving institution sends the money back, and a `Recordkeeper` records that with a `return_reason`. A return is a **full compensation**: the transfer moves `REQUESTED → RETURNED`, `in_transit_cents` is zeroed, the source account's `balance_cents` increases by the whole gross `amount_cents` and its `contribution_basis_cents` by `basis_portion_cents`, the `fee_cents` charge is reversed, the fund split is recomputed, the posture is re-derived and the account's `version` increments. Programme cash is restored with zero drift — the saver is not charged for a transfer that never happened.

The third ending is a lapse rather than an act. When the compliance sweep finds an `INDIRECT_60_DAY` transfer still in `REQUESTED` whose `settle_by_date` is behind the clock date, the transfer moves `REQUESTED → FAILED_TAXABLE`, `in_transit_cents` is zeroed and `is_taxable_distribution` is set to true. No money returns to the source account: the distribution is deemed taxable and stays out. `SETTLED`, `RETURNED` and `FAILED_TAXABLE` are all terminal.

### 3.8. Enforcement: Delinquency, Penalties, Referral and Abatement

An employer's enforcement posture is never set by hand; it is derived. First the programme computes `unremitted_deduction_cents`, the sum of `total_deducted_cents` over that employer's remittance batches whose status is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` and whose `pay_date` plus the program year's `remittance_due_days` is strictly earlier than the current clock date. Batches in `POSTED`, `REJECTED` or `REVERSED` contribute nothing: posted money has arrived, rejected money was never accepted, and reversed money has been compensated. Because the day comparison is strict, a batch still inside its grace period contributes nothing either.

`compliance_status` is then re-derived from the highest applicable condition downwards. If any enforcement referral raised on any of this employer's assessments is in status `OPEN`, the employer is `REFERRED`. Otherwise, if any of its penalty assessments is in status `ASSESSED` or `AFFIRMED`, it is `PENALIZED`. Otherwise, if `unremitted_deduction_cents` is greater than zero, it is `DELINQUENT`. Otherwise, if `registration_status` is `PENDING_REGISTRATION` and `registration_deadline` is non-null and the clock date is later than that deadline, it is again `DELINQUENT`. Otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. This derivation runs whenever an input to it moves — on remittance submission and posting, on penalty creation and disposition, on referral creation, on the exemption cascade, and on every compliance sweep.

A `ComplianceOfficer` assesses a penalty against an employer by naming a `violation_type` of `FAILURE_TO_REGISTER`, `FAILURE_TO_REMIT` or `FAILURE_TO_ENROLL`, the `notice_date` on which the employer was noticed, and optionally `cure_days`, the number of days after that notice within which the employer may cure, defaulting to 30. The assessment records `cure_deadline` as `notice_date` plus `cure_days`, snapshots `employee_count_at_assessment` from the employer's current `employee_count`, records `origin` as `MANUAL`, sets `assessed_by_user_id` to the calling officer's `X-User-ID`, opens in status `ASSESSED`, and drives the employer's `compliance_status` to `PENALIZED` while incrementing its `version`. `disposed_by_user_id`, `disposition_note` and `last_accrual_date` are null at that point.

Two competing statutory amounts are **always** computed and **always** stored. The per-employee statutory figure counts how long the violation has stood: the number of whole days from `notice_date` to the assessment clock date is compared against two thresholds, and the tiers are **additive rather than replacing**. Starting from zero, if that day count is at least the program year's `notice_period_days`, the figure gains `employee_count_at_assessment` multiplied by `register_penalty_initial_cents_per_employee`; then, if the day count is also at least `sustained_notice_days`, the figure gains a further `employee_count_at_assessment` multiplied by `register_penalty_sustained_cents_per_employee`. At 200 days under the default dials, a five-employee firm therefore owes five times 25000 cents plus five times 50000 cents, that is 375000 cents in total, not merely five times 50000. That figure is stored as `statutory_amount_cents`.

The graduated figure is proportional to money actually withheld and not passed on. `shortfall_basis_cents` snapshots the employer's `unremitted_deduction_cents` at the moment the assessment is created, or at the moment a sweep last re-accrued it. `months_late` is computed over the overdue batches feeding that shortfall: for each such batch, the days late are measured from `pay_date` plus `remittance_due_days` to the **assessment clock date**, not from the frozen lateness stored on the batch at submission, so lateness keeps accruing for as long as the money is withheld; the largest of those day counts is divided by 30 and rounded **up** to the next whole number of 30-day blocks. A raw amount is then `shortfall_basis_cents` multiplied by `remit_penalty_monthly_rate_bp` multiplied by `months_late`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. A cap is `shortfall_basis_cents` multiplied by `remit_penalty_cap_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. The final graduated amount takes the larger of the raw amount and `remit_penalty_floor_cents`, and then takes the smaller of that and the cap — **the floor is applied before the cap**, so a tiny shortfall whose floor exceeds its own cap settles at the cap rather than at the floor. That figure is stored as `graduated_amount_cents`. When `shortfall_basis_cents` is zero the graduated figure evaluates to zero, because the floor does not apply to a shortfall that does not exist, and a registration-only violation is therefore never inflated by it.

`assessed_cents` — the amount actually owed — is then pinned by `violation_type`. A `FAILURE_TO_REGISTER` violation owes `statutory_amount_cents`. A `FAILURE_TO_ENROLL` violation likewise owes `statutory_amount_cents`. A `FAILURE_TO_REMIT` violation owes whichever of `statutory_amount_cents` and `graduated_amount_cents` is larger.

Disposition is where segregation of duties bites. A `ComplianceOfficer` disposes of an assessment by supplying `disposition` as either `AFFIRM` or `ABATE`, with an optional `disposition_note`, and the disposing officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the assessment's `assessed_by_user_id`; an officer disposing of their own assessment is refused with `ERR_SELF_DISPOSITION_FORBIDDEN`. Affirmation is available only from `ASSESSED`, and only once the cure period has genuinely run out: the clock date must be later than `cure_deadline`, and an affirmation on or before that date is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`. Affirmation moves the assessment `ASSESSED → AFFIRMED`, stores `disposed_by_user_id` and `disposition_note`, and freezes `assessed_cents` at its computed value. Abatement, by contrast, remains available throughout the cure period and from either `ASSESSED` or `AFFIRMED`, moving the assessment to `ABATED`. `ABATED` is terminal, and a disposition attempted against an already-abated assessment is refused with `ERR_PENALTY_STATE_INVALID` — notably even for the original assessor, because the state gate is evaluated before the segregation-of-duties check.

Abatement carries a cascade of its own. Every enforcement referral of that assessment that is still `OPEN` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_reason` recorded. The employer's `compliance_status` is then re-derived by the rules above, which is what lifts an employer out of `REFERRED`: if another assessment of that employer remains in `ASSESSED` or `AFFIRMED`, the employer falls back to `PENALIZED`; if none does and `unremitted_deduction_cents` is zero, it returns all the way to `COMPLIANT`. The employer's `version` increments.

Referral is the programme's escalation to an outside authority, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may raise one, and only against an assessment in status `AFFIRMED`; an assessment still inside its cure period cannot be referred, because it cannot yet be affirmed, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_PENALTY_STATE_INVALID`. The referral names an `agency` of `STATE_LABOR_COMMISSIONER`, `STATE_TAX_AUTHORITY` or `ATTORNEY_GENERAL` and a free-text `referral_basis` giving the statutory ground. It also carries the `penalty_id` of the assessment it was raised from. It is created in status `OPEN`, with `referred_at` stamped from the clock, `referred_by_user_id` set to the raising officer's `X-User-ID`, and `employer_ein_snapshot` denormalised from the employer's `ein` at that instant and never re-derived afterwards, so the referral remains an accurate record of what was sent even if the employer later restates its identifiers. Creating the referral pushes the employer's `compliance_status` to `REFERRED`, increments the employer's `version`, and places the employer under the enforcement hold of §2.9 — from that point it may neither file a new exemption certification nor restate its headcount until the referral is withdrawn. `withdrawn_reason` is null while the referral stands.

The compliance sweep is the batch that makes enforcement advance with time rather than only with requests, and either a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `ProgramAdministrator` may run it, optionally against a supplied `as_of_date`. In one pass the sweep recomputes every employer's `unremitted_deduction_cents` and re-derives every `compliance_status`, moving employers `COMPLIANT → DELINQUENT` where the derivation now says so. For employers sitting past their registration deadline it mints a penalty assessment with `origin` `SWEEP`, `assessed_by_user_id` set to the literal `SYSTEM` and `notice_date` set to the employer's `registration_deadline`, which drives those employers on to `PENALIZED`; where such an assessment is re-accrued on a later sweep, `last_accrual_date` records the date of that sweep and the shortfall figures are recomputed against it. The same pass expires overdue excess-contribution cases with their excise tax as described in §3.6, and lapses overdue indirect rollovers into `FAILED_TAXABLE` as described in §3.7, re-deriving the posture of every account it touched.

### 3.9. Closing an Account

Closure is deliberately a two-step affair, because the first step must not be able to strand money. The saver, or a `ProgramAdministrator` on their behalf, requests closure of an account that is in `account_status` `OPEN`, supplying a `closure_reason` of `PARTICIPANT_REQUEST`, `ROLLOVER_OUT`, `DECEASED` or `PROGRAM_ACTION`, and a `residual_disposition` of either `ROLLOVER_OUT` or `CASH_DISTRIBUTION` saying how any remaining balance is to be handled. The account's derived posture must be `UNRESTRICTED` and no portability transfer sourced from the account may still be in status `REQUESTED`; a request against a `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKED` account is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED`, and a request against a `CLOSED` account with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. On success the account moves `OPEN → CLOSURE_PENDING`, both instructions are recorded, the posture is immediately re-derived to `LOCKED`, and the account's `version` increments. From that moment no new transfer and no further closure request will be accepted, which is what forces the residual balance to be drained through an ordinary portability transfer or distribution rather than through the closure itself.

Finalising closure belongs to the `Recordkeeper`, who may attach an optional `completion_note`. The account must be in `CLOSURE_PENDING`, else `ERR_CLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`; its `balance_cents` must be exactly zero, else `ERR_RESIDUAL_BALANCE_PRESENT`; and no excess-contribution case of the account may remain in `DETECTED` or `METHOD_ELECTED`, since an unresolved correction cannot be executed against a closed account. On success the account moves `CLOSURE_PENDING → CLOSED`, `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the account's currently `ACTIVE` investment election becomes `SUPERSEDED` and `active_election_id` is cleared to null, and the account's `version` increments. `CLOSED` is terminal: the account is excluded from every subsequent contribution posting, and every write against it is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`.
