# AIS Inspection & Decontamination API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Aquatic invasive species — zebra and quagga mussels, Eurasian watermilfoil, New Zealand mudsnails — move between water bodies almost exclusively on trailered boats. A single ballasted wakeboat that leaves an infested reservoir with two litres of untreated water in its ballast tanks can seed a clean lake three hundred miles away in an afternoon. The state's watercraft inspection program exists to break that pathway, and it does so with a physical apparatus: staffed inspection stations at high-risk lake access points and on the interstate highway corridors that feed them, hot-water pressure decontamination units, numbered tamper-evident seals, and a workforce whose certification is audited hour by credited hour. This document specifies the backend service that runs that apparatus.

The service is a compliance-and-audit system before it is anything else. Every consequential act — a boater refusing inspection, a technician holding a nozzle on a bilge for ninety seconds at 3,200 PSI, a verifier signing off a documented dry period, a steward accepting a live-organism detection into the official state record — must be reconstructible after the fact from the stored rows alone, with the acting principal, the frozen inputs, and the derived numbers all preserved. Because so much of the program is *temporal* — dry times measured in days, quarantine holds measured in hours, seal reciprocity measured against the moment a partner state issued the seal, inspector certifications that lapse after a year — the service runs on an explicit logical clock rather than wall time, so that field scenarios and regression tests can be driven deterministically.

Three properties are non-negotiable in the design that follows. First, **segregation of duties**: the technician who performs a treatment may not sign its dry verification, the inspector who seals a vessel may not verify its release, and the inspector who files a detection may not acknowledge it into the state database. Second, **conservation**: physical seals, allocated campaign money, station fee ledgers, and enforcement strikes are all conserved quantities, and every forward action that moves one has a named compensating action that moves it back exactly. Third, **frozen inputs**: a risk band captured at the moment a decontamination order is written governs that order's dry-time obligation forever, no matter how the underlying inspection is later re-screened. Engineers reading this specification should treat those three properties as the tie-breakers whenever a rule appears ambiguous.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. Both must be present and non-empty; a header whose value consists only of whitespace is treated exactly as if it were absent, and the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same 401 with the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` identifier is returned when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven roles the program recognises. Once the role is recognised but is not listed among the roles permitted on the addressed operation, the rejection becomes `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `ProgramAdmin`, `Inspector`, `DeconTechnician`, `ReleaseVerifier`, `EnforcementOfficer`, `StateDataSteward`, and `TrainingOfficer`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the state program office. It builds the inventory of water bodies, sites and operates stations, enrolls inspectors, negotiates interstate reciprocity, plans and launches education campaigns, and controls the logical clock. An `Inspector` is a field inspector at a station: opening inspections, performing risk screening including reciprocity seal checks, dispositioning inspections, ordering decontamination, sealing vessels under quarantine, requesting their release, and filing detection reports. A `DeconTechnician` is a certified decontamination technician who performs the hot-water pressure treatment itself. A `ReleaseVerifier` is the independent verifier who signs off documented dry time on treated vessels and verifies quarantine releases. An `EnforcementOfficer` is the enforcement liaison who receives refusal and quarantine-failure referrals, issues citations with penalties, and dismisses unfounded referrals. A `StateDataSteward` is the state database custodian who accepts or rejects submitted detection reports — the only path in the entire system that mutates a water body's infestation posture. A `TrainingOfficer` is the training coordinator who records completed inspector training modules, which is likewise the only path that raises an inspector's certification level.

Three of those roles are **entity-backed**, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Inspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Inspector` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `DeconTechnician` and when `X-User-Role` is `ReleaseVerifier`: in both cases `X-User-ID` is the `id` of an `Inspector` row, because certification level and endorsement have to be checkable, and because segregation of duties is enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against the inspector foreign keys already recorded on the target resource. On any operation whose permitted roles include `Inspector`, `DeconTechnician`, or `ReleaseVerifier`, an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no `Inspector` row is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

The remaining four roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `EnforcementOfficer`, `StateDataSteward`, or `TrainingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id` and never resolves it to a row. Consequently an `EnforcementOfficer` is not subject to any ownership test, and a `StateDataSteward` acknowledging a detection report is subject to the segregation-of-duties comparison only in the sense that its opaque identifier must differ from the inspector identifier already stored on the report.

Ownership is checked separately from authorization. For inspector-acted operations on an already-existing resource, `X-User-ID` must equal the owning foreign key on that resource: `inspector_id` on the inspection record for both screening and disposition, `reported_by_inspector_id` on the detection report for submission, and `issued_by_inspector_id` on the quarantine order for the release request. A mismatch on any of those four operations is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing operation is paginated with two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a listing operation states otherwise, results are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size for this response), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond this page). Filter query parameters narrow the matching set before `total_count` is computed, so a filtered list reports the size of the filtered set, not of the whole collection.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the service persists is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a request body is one of the ignored unknown fields described in §2.11. Alongside `id`, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation that changes the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. The `version` counter is the substrate of the stale-view protection described in §2.7, and it is deliberately bumped by *cascade* writes as well as by direct ones: if a station's seal counters move because a quarantine order was issued elsewhere, that station's `version` advances and its `updated_at` refreshes just as though the station had been edited directly.

`TrainingRecord` is the single exception. A training record is an immutable audit row: once a module completion is logged it is never edited, so a `TrainingRecord` carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and there is no operation anywhere in the API that updates one.

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

Dry times, quarantine hold periods, seal ages, certification expiries and campaign windows are all measured against a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance. A `ProgramAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Setting the clock is inert with respect to stored data: advancing it mutates no entity by itself.

Instead, every elapsed-time and expiry rule is evaluated **lazily**, on the next request that touches the affected entity. The most consequential instance of laziness is inspector certification expiry, described in §3.2: an inspector whose certification lapsed six months of logical time ago still reads as `ACTIVE` until some request references them, at which point the expiry is applied, persisted, and the referencing request fails. Engineers should read "the current logical time" throughout this document as "the value of the logical clock at the moment the request is processed".

### 2.6. Nested Paths and Referential Integrity

Two resources are addressed as sub-resources of a station or an inspector — a station's hours-of-operation windows and an inspector's training records. Where a path contains more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that resolves to nothing determines the error. A request against the hours of a station that does not exist therefore fails with `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) regardless of anything else in the request, and a request against the training records of a non-existent inspector fails with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

The same discipline extends from paths to bodies. Every foreign key supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing row before any business rule is evaluated: `station_id`, `watercraft_id`, `prior_water_body_id`, `home_station_id`, `last_water_body_id`, `target_water_body_id`, `inspection_record_id`, `downstream_of_water_body_id` and `water_body_id` are all checked this way, and on screening, when a seal number is presented, `presented_seal_state_code` must resolve to a reciprocity agreement. The identifier reported names the *kind* of thing that was missing: `ERR_WATER_BODY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WATERCRAFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECON_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUARANTINE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETECTION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RECIPROCITY_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` — each HTTP 404.

### 2.7. Stale-View Protection on Vessel and Record Writes

Two write operations in this API are protected by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases the product's own semantics permit a second, sequential call from the same state — so no state precondition can distinguish a writer holding a fresh view of the parent from one holding a stale view. In each case the client must echo back the `version` it last observed on the *parent* entity, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is a hard rejection.

A `POST /inspection-records/{id}/screening` request must carry `expected_watercraft_version`, a non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Watercraft` being screened. If that value does not equal the vessel's current `version`, the screening is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WATERCRAFT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. This matters more than it might appear, because a vessel's `version` advances along several paths the screening client never sees: an enforcement referral booking a compliance strike advances it, a verified decontamination clearing the mandatory-decon flag advances it, and an approved quarantine release clearing the hold advances it. A client that read the vessel before any of those cascades therefore holds a stale view even if it has never issued a screening of its own.

A `POST /decon-orders` request must carry `expected_inspection_record_version`, a non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `InspectionRecord` named by `inspection_record_id`. If that value does not equal the record's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_RECORD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A record awaiting decontamination may legitimately carry more than one decontamination order — a fresh order re-books the vessel for a new treatment cycle and supersedes the earlier one — so a second sequential create observes exactly the same inspection status as the first and would otherwise succeed. The stale-version check is what pins which view of the record the new order was written against.

No other operation in this API accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in the two paragraphs above take neither `expected_watercraft_version` nor `expected_inspection_record_version`, and supplying one there has no effect.

### 2.8. Numeric Discipline: Money, Points, Rates and Hours

All money in this system is held in **whole cents** as integers — inspection fees, decontamination charges, program surcharges, civil penalties and campaign budgets alike. There are no fractional cents anywhere; wherever a percentage is applied to a cent amount, the product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents.

Risk scores and screening point terms are whole numbers. Where a formula divides to produce a points term, the quotient is taken toward negative infinity (floored) to a whole number before it is used, and the final score is rounded half up to a whole number exactly once, at the point the formula specifies, then clamped into its allowed range.

Two quantities are held to two decimal places, rounded half up: an inspector's credited training hours total, and a decontamination treatment's effectiveness percentage. Compliance rates in campaign evaluation are held to four decimal places, rounded half up, and the effectiveness scores derived from them are held to two decimal places, rounded half up, clamped into the closed interval from 0.00 to 100.00.

Dry-time obligations, quarantine hold durations, seal ages and certification validity are all whole numbers of hours. Where a duration is derived by division — the standing-time credit, the seal age — the quotient is floored to whole units before use. Every threshold comparison in this document is stated with explicit inclusivity, and the pattern is consistent: a value exactly equal to a minimum satisfies that minimum, and a value exactly equal to a tier boundary lands in the higher tier.

### 2.9. Certification Gates and Segregation of Duties

Two orthogonal guards sit in front of the field operations. The first is **standing**: any inspector referenced by a request — the acting principal, and any inspector named by an identifier in the body — must be in `ACTIVE` standing at the moment the request is processed, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). This guard applies to opening an inspection, screening it, dispositioning it, creating a decontamination order, recording a treatment, recording a dry verification, issuing a quarantine order, verifying a quarantine release, and submitting a detection report.

The second is **certification**. A `DeconTechnician` recording a treatment must hold certification level `DECON_CERTIFIED` and must have the decontamination endorsement on file; a `ReleaseVerifier` signing a dry verification or verifying a quarantine release must hold certification level `LEVEL_2` or `DECON_CERTIFIED`. Failing either test yields `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 403), and the guard applies to exactly three operations: recording a treatment, recording a dry verification, and verifying a quarantine release.

Segregation of duties is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against inspector identifiers already stored on the target row, and it covers three pairs. The technician who performed a decontamination treatment may not sign that order's dry verification. The inspector who issued a quarantine order may not verify that order's release. The principal who filed a detection report may not acknowledge it into the state database. Any of those three collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and the guard applies to exactly those three operations: dry verification, quarantine release verification, and state acknowledgement.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

Many operations in this system mutate more than one row. The service is immediately consistent: by the time a response is returned, every cascaded write has landed, every derived counter has been recomputed, and the `version` and `updated_at` of every touched row have advanced. Cascades are described in full in the workflow narratives of §3; what matters globally is that they are not optional, not deferred, and not partial.

Four quantities in this program are **conserved**, each with a named forward action and a named compensating action that reverses it exactly.

Physical seals are conserved at each station. For any station whose lifecycle has ever left the planning stage, `seal_stock_count` plus `seals_in_circulation_count` plus `seals_retired_count` always equals `seal_stock_initial_count`. Issuing a quarantine order moves one seal from stock into circulation; approving that quarantine's release — or reporting the seal breached — moves it from circulation into retired. The three counters only ever move between one another; no path adds to or subtracts from the total, so the sum is unchanged by the forward action, by the compensating action, and by any number of rejected-release loops in between.

Campaign money is conserved across stations. The sum of `campaign_allocated_cents` over all stations always equals the sum of `budget_cents` over all campaigns that are running or evaluated, and for each such campaign the allocated amounts recorded in its allocation array always sum to exactly its `budget_cents`. Launching a campaign credits each candidate station; cancelling a running campaign debits each station by exactly the amount that was recorded for it and empties the array. Evaluating a campaign deliberately leaves both sides untouched, so evaluation cannot drift the ledger.

The station fee ledger is conserved against its records. For every station, `collected_fees_cents` equals the sum over that station's inspection records of `total_fee_cents` minus `fee_refunded_cents`, and `refunded_fees_cents` equals the sum of `fee_refunded_cents` over the same records. Recording a treatment books the decontamination fee delta onto the ledger; abandoning a treated vessel reverses the treatment-attributable portion exactly, so after an abandonment the ledger equals what it would have been had the record been disposed with no treatment at all.

Enforcement strikes are conserved against referrals. For every vessel, `compliance_strikes` equals the count of that vessel's inspection records whose enforcement standing is `REFERRED` or `CITED`; `watchlist_status` is `WATCHLISTED` exactly when `compliance_strikes` has reached the watchlist threshold; and `mandatory_decon_flag` is true only while the vessel is `WATCHLISTED` and no decontamination order has reached verification since the flag was set. Referring a record books a strike; dismissing that referral removes it and re-derives both the watchlist status and the mandatory-decon flag from the new count, leaving the vessel exactly as it was before the referral.

### 2.11. Order of Rejection

When a single request would fail several checks at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The checks are evaluated in the fixed sequence below, and the first check that fails ends the request; nothing after it runs and nothing is written. Engineers should treat this ordering as part of the contract, not as an implementation detail — a great many of the program's rules are only observable through *which* of two plausible errors comes back.

1. Both authentication headers are present and non-empty, whitespace-only counting as absent — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every operation.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every operation.
3. The named role appears among the roles permitted on the addressed operation — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every operation.
4. For the entity-backed roles `Inspector`, `DeconTechnician` and `ReleaseVerifier`, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing inspector — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every operation whose permitted roles include one of those three.
5. On every collection listing, `limit` is an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
6. On every creating or modifying operation, the body is structurally sound — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Every required field is present; every supplied value matches its declared type and, for enumerated fields, is one of the declared members; and every numeric and ordering constraint holds. Specifically: `hours` is greater than 0, `standing_water_ml` is at least 0, `decon_bay_count` is at least 0, `seal_stock_initial_count` is at least 0, `hull_length_ft` is greater than 0, `budget_cents` is greater than 0, `impressions` is at least 0, `max_seal_age_hours` is greater than 0, `water_temp_f` is greater than 0, `pressure_psi` is greater than 0, `dwell_seconds` is greater than 0, `closes_at` is strictly later than `opens_at`, and `end_at` is strictly later than `start_at`. Fields the service does not recognise, and fields it derives for itself, are silently ignored when a client supplies them rather than being rejected.
7. Every resource named in the path exists, resolved left to right — otherwise the matching 404 from the family listed in §2.6.
8. Every foreign key supplied in the body exists — otherwise the matching 404 from the same family.
9. A station's siting is coherent with its type — otherwise `ERR_STATION_SITING_INVALID` (422). Applies to station creation.
10. A declared drainage parent does not create a cycle — otherwise `ERR_WATER_BODY_CYCLE` (422). Applies to water body creation and to water body correction.
11. A vessel's registration number and an agreement's partner state code are each unused — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (409). Applies to vessel registration and to agreement creation.
12. A quarantine order's seal number is unused across all quarantine orders ever written, terminal ones included — otherwise `ERR_SEAL_NUMBER_DUPLICATE` (409). Applies to quarantine issuance.
13. The acting inspector owns the targeted row — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to screening, disposition, detection-report submission and the quarantine release request.
14. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, including the dimension-specific transition sets on an inspection record — otherwise the applicable 409 from this family: `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE` when a station transition is not declared from the station's current status, including any transition attempted out of the terminal closed status; `ERR_STATION_NOT_OPEN` when an inspection is opened at a station that is not open; `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTOR_STATE` when an inspector transition is not declared from the inspector's current standing; `ERR_RECORD_NOT_SCREENED` when a disposition is requested on a record still in `INITIATED`; `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_DISPOSED` when screening or disposition is requested on a record whose inspection standing has already left `SCREENED`; `ERR_RECORD_NOT_AWAITING_DECON` when a decontamination order or a quarantine order is created for a record not in `DECON_REQUIRED`; `ERR_INVALID_ENFORCEMENT_STATE` when the requested enforcement action is not declared from the record's current enforcement standing, such as citing a record still at `NONE` or dismissing one already `CITED`; `ERR_DECON_NOT_TREATED` when dry verification is requested on an order in `ORDERED` or `RETREAT_REQUIRED`; `ERR_DECON_ALREADY_TERMINAL` when treatment or verification is requested on an order already in `VERIFIED` or `ABANDONED`, which is why those two 409s never both apply to one call; `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_SEALED` when a release request or a breach report targets an order in a state that does not permit it; `ERR_QUARANTINE_RELEASE_NOT_REQUESTED` when release verification targets an order not in `RELEASE_REQUESTED`; `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE` when submission targets a report that is neither `DRAFT` nor `REJECTED`, or acknowledgement targets a report that is not `SUBMITTED`; and `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE` when a campaign transition is not declared from its current status, evaluation of a campaign that is not running included.
15. The echoed parent version matches — otherwise `ERR_STALE_WATERCRAFT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_RECORD_VERSION` (412), as described in §2.7.
16. Every inspector referenced by the request is re-evaluated against the logical clock and is then in `ACTIVE` standing — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
17. The treating or verifying inspector holds the required certification — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (403).
18. The treating station is equipped for hot water and has at least one decontamination bay — otherwise `ERR_STATION_NOT_HOT_WATER_CAPABLE` (409). Applies to treatment.
19. The treatment measurements meet the band minimums — otherwise `ERR_TREATMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422). Applies to treatment.
20. The documented dry period has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_DRY_TIME_NOT_ELAPSED` (409). Applies to dry verification.
21. The quarantine hold period has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_HOLD_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (409). Applies to the release request.
22. A verified decontamination exists where one is required before release — otherwise `ERR_DECON_REQUIRED_BEFORE_RELEASE` (409). Applies to release verification.
23. A verified decontamination exists where one is required before a pass — otherwise `ERR_DECON_REQUIRED_BEFORE_PASS` (409). Applies to disposition.
24. The acting `X-User-ID` is not on both halves of a segregated pair — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
25. The order's attempt count is below the treatment cap — otherwise `ERR_DECON_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to treatment.
26. A new hours window does not overlap an existing one at the same station — otherwise `ERR_HOURS_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
27. The vessel is not already under an unexpired hold — otherwise `ERR_WATERCRAFT_ALREADY_QUARANTINED` (409). Applies to quarantine issuance.
28. A decontamination bay is free — otherwise `ERR_DECON_BAY_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Applies to treatment.
29. The record warrants quarantine — otherwise `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_WARRANTED` (409). Applies to quarantine issuance.
30. The arrival instant falls inside a declared operating window — otherwise `ERR_STATION_OUTSIDE_OPERATING_HOURS` (409). Applies to opening an inspection.
31. A presented seal's number carries the agreement's accepted prefix, on an agreement that is active and honours this program's seals — otherwise `ERR_SEAL_PREFIX_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to screening.
32. A presented seal is not older than the agreement permits — otherwise `ERR_SEAL_RECIPROCITY_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to screening.
33. A campaign's window has closed — otherwise `ERR_EVALUATION_WINDOW_NOT_ENDED` (409). Applies to campaign evaluation.
34. A declared prior water body is consistent with the vessel's recorded history — otherwise `ERR_PRIOR_WATER_BODY_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to screening.
35. At least one station qualifies for the campaign — otherwise `ERR_NO_STATIONS_FOR_CAMPAIGN` (422). Applies to launching a campaign.
36. The campaign's compliance denominator is non-empty — otherwise `ERR_NO_INSPECTIONS_IN_WINDOW` (422). Applies to campaign evaluation.
37. The issuing station has at least one seal on the shelf — otherwise `ERR_STATION_SEAL_STOCK_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to quarantine issuance.
38. The campaign budget is within the program cap and large enough — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to campaign creation and to launching a campaign.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Because the stale-version check sits at step 15 and the inspector-standing check at step 16, a screening request that carries a stale vessel version *and* is issued by a lapsed inspector reports the stale version, not the lapsed certification. And because the state-transition check sits at step 14, ahead of every safety gate, a dry verification aimed at an order that is still `ORDERED` reports `ERR_DECON_NOT_TREATED` rather than complaining that the dry time has not elapsed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Foundations: Water Bodies, Station Siting and Operating Hours

Before a single boat can be inspected, the program office has to describe the water it is protecting. Registering a water body captures its public `name`, the two-letter `state_code` of the state it sits in — exactly two uppercase letters — and its physical `water_body_class`, one of `LAKE`, `RESERVOIR`, `RIVER` or `CANAL`. Two program judgements are recorded at the same time and may be left to their defaults: `risk_tier`, one of `HIGH`, `MODERATE` or `LOW`, defaulting to `MODERATE`; and `infestation_status`, one of `CLEAN`, `SUSPECT` or `CONFIRMED_INFESTED`, defaulting to `CLEAN`. Both detection counters, `detection_count` and `live_detection_count`, start at 0 and are never written by a client.

Water bodies form a drainage graph. A registration may name `downstream_of_water_body_id`, the identifier of the water body immediately upstream of the one being registered; the field is nullable and defaults to null for a headwater. Following that chain from the new or corrected water body must never arrive back at the same water body, and a water body may not name itself; either shape is rejected with `ERR_WATER_BODY_CYCLE`. Correction after a survey is deliberately narrow: only `downstream_of_water_body_id` and `risk_tier` may be changed once the row exists, the drainage cycle check is re-run from the corrected water body, and the correction leaves `detection_count`, `live_detection_count` and `infestation_status` untouched while advancing the water body's `version` by 1. Infestation posture is not correctable by hand at all — the detection-report acknowledgement described in §3.8 is its single writer. Listings of the inventory may be narrowed by `downstream_of_water_body_id`, `risk_tier`, `infestation_status` and `state_code`.

**Stations** come in two siting classes, and the class dictates which locating field is required. `station_type` is either `WATERBODY_ACCESS` — a checkpoint at a lake or reservoir access point — or `HIGHWAY_CORRIDOR`, a checkpoint on a trunk road. A `WATERBODY_ACCESS` station must supply a non-null `water_body_id` and must leave `corridor_route_code` null; a `HIGHWAY_CORRIDOR` station must supply a non-null `corridor_route_code` and must leave `water_body_id` null. Any other combination — an access station missing its water body or carrying a route code, a corridor station missing its route code or carrying a water body — is rejected with `ERR_STATION_SITING_INVALID`. Beyond `name` and the siting pair, station creation accepts four operational declarations, all optional: `mandatory_inspection`, false unless stated, marking a station where stopping is compulsory rather than voluntary; `decon_bay_count`, 0 unless stated, the number of physical decontamination bays; `hot_water_capable`, false unless stated, true when the site has a hot-water pressure unit; and `seal_stock_initial_count`, 0 unless stated, the number of numbered seals physically delivered at commissioning.

A station's `status` is one of `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED`, and it is never supplied by a client. A newly sited station opens in `PLANNED` status with its ledgers empty. `seal_stock_count` is set equal to `seal_stock_initial_count` — the same initialisation is applied again at the moment the station first leaves `PLANNED`, so the two are always consistent — while `seals_in_circulation_count`, `seals_retired_count`, `certified_inspector_count`, `collected_fees_cents`, `refunded_fees_cents` and `campaign_allocated_cents` all start at 0. The derived flag `decon_service_available` starts false, because no certified inspector is posted yet; it is true exactly when the station's `certified_inspector_count` has reached two *and* `hot_water_capable` is true, and it is recomputed every time either input moves.

Operating hours are declared one window at a time, each window naming the station in `station_id` and carrying an inclusive `opens_at` and an exclusive `closes_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, plus an optional `staffing_level` of `SINGLE` or `DOUBLE` that defaults to `SINGLE`. `closes_at` must be strictly later than `opens_at`. Windows at the same station must not overlap: a new window collides with an existing one when the new window's `opens_at` falls strictly before the existing window's `closes_at` *and* the existing window's `opens_at` falls strictly before the new window's `closes_at`, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_HOURS_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Windows that merely touch at an endpoint — one closing at the exact instant the next opens — are accepted, which is how a station declares back-to-back shifts.

A station's lifecycle is driven by a `ProgramAdmin` supplying a `target_status` and, optionally, a `reason` for the audit trail. `PLANNED → OPEN` opens the station for the season and is permitted only when at least one hours window has already been declared for it; an attempt to open a station with no declared hours is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE`. Opening also re-initialises the seal stock, recomputes `decon_service_available`, and advances the station's `version` by 1. From `OPEN` the station may go to `SUSPENDED`, and from `SUSPENDED` back to `OPEN`, each transition advancing `version` by 1; the pair forms the operating cycle `OPEN → SUSPENDED → OPEN` and a station may be suspended and resumed any number of times. Suspension blocks the opening of new inspections at that station but leaves in-flight records and their decontamination orders entirely alone.

Closure is permanent. A station may be closed from `PLANNED` with no further condition, and from either `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED` provided no inspection record at that station is still live — that is, provided no record there sits in `INITIATED`, `SCREENED` or `DECON_REQUIRED`. Each closure sets the status to `CLOSED` and advances `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal: any subsequent status call against a closed station, whatever `target_status` it names, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE`, no field changes, and the station's `version` is *not* bumped. Station listings may be narrowed by `water_body_id`, `station_type`, `status` and `hot_water_capable`.

### 3.2. The Inspection Workforce: Training, Certification and Standing

Enrolling an inspector is a `ProgramAdmin` act and needs very little: the inspector's legal `full_name`, the `home_station_id` of the station whose staffing they will count toward, and — taken from the hiring packet, defaulting to false — `decon_endorsement`, true when the packet includes the hot-water decontamination endorsement. An inspector's `status` is one of `PROBATIONARY`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED`, and `certification_level` is one of `TRAINEE`, `LEVEL_1`, `LEVEL_2` or `DECON_CERTIFIED`. Every derived attribute starts at its floor: `training_hours_total` at 0, `certification_level` at `TRAINEE`, `certification_expires_at` at null, and standing at `PROBATIONARY`. Inspector listings may be narrowed by `home_station_id`, `certification_level`, `status` and `decon_endorsement`.

Certification is earned module by module. A `TrainingOfficer` logs a completed curriculum module against an inspector, naming the trainee in `inspector_id` and supplying the state curriculum's `module_code` (non-empty, at most 32 characters), the credited `hours` for the module (greater than zero, at most two decimal places), a `passed` flag, and optionally the `completed_at` instant the module finished. Training records are immutable audit rows — as noted in §2.4 they carry no `version` and no `updated_at`, and no operation updates one.

Each logged module recomputes the inspector's credited total and, from it, their level. `training_hours_total` is the sum of `hours` over that inspector's training records whose `passed` is true, rounded half up to two decimal places; modules with `passed` false credit zero hours and are excluded from the sum entirely. `certification_level` then follows from that total by three comparisons: below 8 credited hours the level is `TRAINEE`; from 8 credited hours up to but not including 40, the level is `LEVEL_1`; from 40 credited hours upward the level is `LEVEL_2` when the inspector has no decontamination endorsement, and `DECON_CERTIFIED` when they do. The comparisons are inclusive at the lower bound, so a total of exactly 8.00 hours already reaches `LEVEL_1`, and a total of exactly 40.00 hours already reaches `LEVEL_2` — or `DECON_CERTIFIED` for an endorsed inspector.

Promotion out of `TRAINEE` is what ends probation. When a credited module lifts a `PROBATIONARY` inspector's `certification_level` above `TRAINEE`, the service moves them `PROBATIONARY → ACTIVE` in the same request, stamps `certification_expires_at` at the current logical time plus 8,760 hours — one 365-day year — and advances the inspector's `version` by 1. If the level lands at `LEVEL_2` or `DECON_CERTIFIED`, the promotion also flows through to the home station: `certified_inspector_count` — the number of that station's inspectors who are simultaneously in `ACTIVE` standing and at level `LEVEL_2` or `DECON_CERTIFIED` — is recomputed, and `decon_service_available` is recomputed from the new count.

Standing is administered by a `ProgramAdmin` supplying a `target_status` and an optional `reason`. An inspector in either `PROBATIONARY` or `ACTIVE` standing may be moved to `SUSPENDED`; when the suspended inspector held `LEVEL_2` or `DECON_CERTIFIED`, the home station's `certified_inspector_count` is decremented and `decon_service_available` recomputed. Reinstatement `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` requires two things: the inspector's `certification_level` must not be `TRAINEE`, and the current logical time must be strictly before their `certification_expires_at`. Reinstatement re-runs the station staffing cascade and advances the inspector's `version` by 1, closing the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` loop. An inspector transition that is not declared from the inspector's current standing is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTOR_STATE`. The inspector machine has no terminal state at all.

Certifications lapse, and they lapse **lazily**. There is no sweep and no background job: the `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` transition fires the next time the inspector is referenced, either as the acting principal or as the subject of a treatment or verification, and only when the current logical time has reached or passed their `certification_expires_at`. When it fires, the inspector's standing is set to `EXPIRED`, the home station's `certified_inspector_count` is decremented if they held `LEVEL_2` or `DECON_CERTIFIED`, `decon_service_available` is recomputed, and the inspector's `version` advances by 1 — and *then* the triggering request itself is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. This evaluation happens ahead of the request's own state preconditions, which is why the expiry persists even though the request that surfaced it failed. A client that retries the same request immediately will get the same rejection, but the inspector row now honestly reads `EXPIRED`.

There are two ways back from `EXPIRED`, and they are deliberately asymmetric. The administrative route, `EXPIRED → ACTIVE` by a `ProgramAdmin`, requires only that the inspector's `certification_level` is not `TRAINEE` — it carries no requirement that the certification still be unexpired, since the entire point of the renewal is that it has already lapsed — and it re-stamps `certification_expires_at` at the current logical time plus 8,760 hours, re-runs the station staffing cascade, and advances `version` by 1. The training route reaches the same edge from the other side: logging a further passed module against an `EXPIRED` inspector whose credited total keeps their level above `TRAINEE` also moves them `EXPIRED → ACTIVE`, likewise re-stamping `certification_expires_at` at the current logical time plus 8,760 hours, likewise re-running the station staffing cascade, and likewise advancing `version` by 1. Both routes are declared, so neither is an accident of implementation.

### 3.3. Vessels, Home States and Interstate Seal Reciprocity

The watercraft registry is the program's memory of individual boats, and either a `ProgramAdmin` or an `Inspector` may add to it. A vessel is registered with its state `registration_number` (non-empty, at most 32 characters, and unique across the whole registry — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`), the two-letter `home_state_code` of the state it is registered in, its hull class in `vessel_type`, and its `hull_length_ft` in feet, which must be greater than zero. `vessel_type` is one of `OPEN_HULL`, `PONTOON_MULTI_CHAMBER`, `BALLASTED_WAKEBOAT` or `PERSONAL_WATERCRAFT`; the two middle-water-carrying classes, `PONTOON_MULTI_CHAMBER` and `BALLASTED_WAKEBOAT`, are the ones that attract the elevated vessel factor in §3.4, because multi-chamber pontoons and ballast tanks hold water that a visual inspection cannot see.

Two optional declarations are taken from the owner at registration. `last_water_body_id` names the water body the vessel was most recently launched in, and `last_removed_at` records the instant it was last pulled from the water; both default to null, and `last_removed_at` is the baseline from which the standing-time dry credit is measured. Everything else on a vessel is derived by the program and never supplied by a client: `compliance_strikes` starts at 0, `watchlist_status` starts `CLEAR` (its only other value being `WATCHLISTED`), `mandatory_decon_flag` starts false, and both `last_decon_verified_at` and `quarantine_hold_until` start null. Vessel listings may be narrowed by `last_water_body_id`, `watchlist_status`, `vessel_type` and `registration_number`.

Interstate coordination is expressed as **reciprocity agreements**, one per partner state, negotiated by the program office. An agreement is created with the partner's two-letter `partner_state_code`, unique across agreements and likewise guarded by `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`, and the `accepted_seal_prefix` (non-empty, at most 8 characters) that a seal number from that state must begin with before this program will look at it. Three further terms are optional: `honors_our_seals`, true unless stated otherwise, recording that the partner reciprocates by honouring seals this program issues; `max_seal_age_hours`, defaulting to 72 hours — three days — and required to be greater than zero, the oldest a partner seal may be and still be recognised at screening; and `fee_waiver`, false unless stated, true when an honoured partner seal also waives this program's base inspection fee. An agreement's `status` begins `ACTIVE`, and a `ProgramAdmin` may move it between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` by supplying a `target_status` with an optional `reason`. Only an `ACTIVE` agreement is consulted at screening; suspending an agreement stops honouring newly presented seals from that state, but inspection records that were already granted the credit keep it. Agreement listings may be narrowed by `partner_state_code` and `status`.

The **seal decision** runs at the very start of every screening, before any scoring, and its outcome is recorded on the inspection record. When the boater presents nothing — `presented_seal_number` is null — the decision is trivially negative: `reciprocity_seal_honored` is set false, `reciprocity_credit_points` is set to 0, `honored_agreement_id` is set null, and the decision stops there without consulting any agreement. When a seal *is* presented, exactly one agreement is consulted: the one whose `partner_state_code` equals the record's `presented_seal_state_code`. Because partner state codes are unique, that selector is total and needs no ranking or tie-break. If no agreement exists for that state code, the screening is rejected outright with `ERR_RECIPROCITY_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

If the matched agreement is `SUSPENDED`, the seal is simply not honoured — this is not an error. The record's `reciprocity_seal_honored` is set false, `reciprocity_credit_points` is set to 0, `honored_agreement_id` is set null, and the decision stops, leaving the screening to proceed with no reciprocity credit. Otherwise two tests follow. First, `presented_seal_number` must begin with the agreement's `accepted_seal_prefix`, on an agreement that is active and whose `honors_our_seals` is true; a number that does not carry the prefix, or an agreement that does not reciprocate, is rejected with `ERR_SEAL_PREFIX_MISMATCH`. Second, the seal must not be too old: the seal's age in whole hours is the elapsed time from `presented_seal_issued_at` to the record's `arrived_at`, floored to a whole number of hours — the arrival instant, deliberately, and never the current logical time — and the screening is rejected with `ERR_SEAL_RECIPROCITY_EXPIRED` only when that age is strictly greater than the agreement's `max_seal_age_hours`. An age exactly equal to `max_seal_age_hours` is honoured.

A seal that survives both tests is honoured. The record's `reciprocity_seal_honored` is set true, `honored_agreement_id` is set to the matched agreement, and `reciprocity_credit_points` is set to 20 — the fixed credit an out-of-state inspection buys a boater at this program's stations. That 20-point credit is subtracted in the scoring described next, and, if the honouring agreement also carries `fee_waiver`, it additionally zeroes the base inspection fee described in §3.5.

### 3.4. Arrival, Screening and the Risk Score

An inspection begins when a boater pulls into a lane. The `Inspector` on duty opens a record naming the `station_id` where the boat has stopped, the `watercraft_id` of the vessel presented, and the `arrived_at` instant the boater reached the station. The acting inspector is not supplied in the body — `inspector_id` is copied from `X-User-ID`, and the acting inspector must be in `ACTIVE` standing or the request fails with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. Two site conditions apply. The station's status must be `OPEN`, not merely planned or suspended, or the request is rejected with `ERR_STATION_NOT_OPEN`. And `arrived_at` must fall inside at least one of the station's declared operating windows, evaluated as the half-open interval from `opens_at` up to but not including `closes_at`: an arrival at exactly `opens_at` is inside the window, an arrival at exactly `closes_at` is not, and a station that has declared no windows at all rejects every arrival. A miss here is `ERR_STATION_OUTSIDE_OPERATING_HOURS`.

A freshly opened record is deliberately empty of judgement. `inspection_status` — one of `INITIATED`, `SCREENED`, `PASSED`, `DECON_REQUIRED`, `DECON_COMPLETED`, `DECON_FAILED_ABANDONED` or `REFUSED` — starts `INITIATED`, and `enforcement_status` — one of `NONE`, `REFERRED`, `CITED` or `DISMISSED` — starts `NONE`; these are the two independent dimensions described below. No scoring and no fee calculation runs at this point. `risk_score`, `advisory_risk_score`, `dry_credit_points`, `reciprocity_credit_points`, `inspection_fee_cents`, `decon_fee_cents`, `surcharge_cents`, `total_fee_cents`, `fee_refunded_cents` and `penalty_cents` all sit at their declared defaults of 0; `screening_completed_at`, `honored_agreement_id`, `referral_reason` and `agency_code` all sit at null; `reciprocity_seal_honored`, `decon_mandatory` and `quarantine_warranted` all sit at false; and `risk_band` sits at `LOW`. Record listings may be narrowed by `station_id`, `inspector_id`, `watercraft_id`, `prior_water_body_id`, `honored_agreement_id`, `inspection_status`, `enforcement_status` and `risk_band`.

**Screening** is the substantive act, and it is the owning inspector's alone: `X-User-ID` must equal the record's `inspector_id` or the call is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The screening payload carries the boater's declaration and the inspector's findings. `prior_water_body_id` names the water body the boater declares the vessel last launched in; when it is omitted the service falls back to the vessel's recorded `last_water_body_id`, and when the vessel has none recorded and none is supplied the call is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. When it *is* supplied and the vessel already has a `last_water_body_id` on file, the two must agree, or the screening is rejected with `ERR_PRIOR_WATER_BODY_MISMATCH`. `residual_water_present` is the inspector's core observation — true when any residual water is found in the hull, bilge, ballast or livewell — and `standing_water_ml` is the measured volume in millilitres, defaulting to 0 and never negative. The three seal fields `presented_seal_number`, `presented_seal_state_code` and `presented_seal_issued_at` carry whatever out-of-state seal the boater is showing, and feed the decision described in §3.3. Screening also requires `expected_watercraft_version` per §2.7.

Screening advances `inspection_status` from `INITIATED` to `SCREENED`, and it may legitimately be repeated: a record already in `SCREENED` may be screened again, holding the `SCREENED → SCREENED` self-transition, because an inspector who mis-measured a bilge should be able to correct the observation on the spot and have the later screening supersede the earlier one. A re-screen overwrites `residual_water_present` and `standing_water_ml` with whatever the new payload supplies, re-runs the seal decision and both scoring variants, and overwrites `risk_score`, `advisory_risk_score`, `risk_band`, `decon_mandatory` and `quarantine_warranted`. Screening a record whose `inspection_status` has already left `SCREENED` is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_DISPOSED`. Every screening, first or repeat, sets `screening_completed_at` to the current logical time, writes the screened prior water body back onto the vessel's `last_water_body_id`, and advances both the vessel's `version` and the record's `version` by 1.

The **risk score** is a weighted point count over exactly two other rows: the water body named by `prior_water_body_id` and the vessel named by `watercraft_id`. No sibling inspection record contributes anything. Five point terms are formed. The water body's published `risk_tier` contributes 40 points when it is `HIGH`, 20 points when `MODERATE`, and 5 points when `LOW`. Its `infestation_status` contributes 30 points when `CONFIRMED_INFESTED`, 15 points when `SUSPECT`, and nothing when `CLEAN`. Residual water contributes nothing at all when `residual_water_present` is false; when it is true, it contributes a base of 20 points plus one further point for each whole 100 millilitres of `standing_water_ml` — that per-volume component being the smaller of 15 points and the measured millilitres divided by 100 and floored to a whole number, so the volume component saturates at 15 points once 1,500 millilitres is reached.

Two terms are *credits*, subtracted rather than added. The dry credit rewards a boat that has been out of the water long enough to die back: the standing time is the elapsed hours from the vessel's `last_removed_at` to the basis instant, floored to whole hours and treated as 0 if that would be negative, and taken as 0 outright when the vessel has no `last_removed_at` on file. The credit is then 5 points for each complete 24-hour day of standing time — the standing hours divided by 24 and floored, multiplied by 5 — capped at 25 points, which is to say the credit tops out after five full days out of the water. The reciprocity credit is 20 points when the record's `reciprocity_seal_honored` is true and 0 otherwise, reading the boolean the seal decision fixed earlier in the same request.

The last input is the hull. The vessel factor is 1.25 for a `PONTOON_MULTI_CHAMBER` or a `BALLASTED_WAKEBOAT` and 1.00 for every other hull class. **The order of operations is pinned.** The five point terms are combined first — the tier points plus the infestation points plus the water points, minus the dry credit, minus the reciprocity credit — that single sum is then multiplied by the vessel factor, the product is rounded half up to a whole number exactly once, and only then is the result clamped into the closed range from 0 to 100. Rounding any individual term before the multiplication yields a different integer whenever the vessel factor is not 1.00, and that alternative is not this program's rule.

The formula runs **twice** on every screening, differing only in the basis instant used for the standing time. The **arrival basis** uses the record's `arrived_at` — the moment the boater reached the station — and it is authoritative: its output is written to `risk_score`, its dry credit is written to `dry_credit_points`, and every gate downstream reads it, including the band, the decontamination requirement, the quarantine warrant, the base dry hours and the base hold hours. The **screening basis** uses the logical clock at the moment of the screening call instead, and its output is written to `advisory_risk_score` for audit only; it never gates anything. Because a boater waits in the queue between arriving and being screened, the standing time on the screening basis is longer or equal, the dry credit is larger or equal, and the two variants produce genuinely different integers whenever the extra wait crosses a 24-hour boundary. Both values are always present on a screened record, and a divergence between them is expected rather than a defect.

`risk_band` follows immediately from `risk_score` on the arrival basis, by three half-open thresholds: a score below 25 is `LOW`, a score from 25 up to but not including 50 is `MODERATE`, a score from 50 up to but not including 75 is `HIGH`, and a score of 75 or more is `EXTREME`. The boundaries are inclusive on the low side, so a score of exactly 50 is `HIGH` and not `MODERATE`, and a score of exactly 75 is `EXTREME` and not `HIGH`.

Two dispositional flags are then derived, and both are recorded on the record. `decon_mandatory` is true when the band is `HIGH` or `EXTREME`, or when the vessel carries `mandatory_decon_flag` true, or when the band is `MODERATE` and `residual_water_present` is true — a moderately-scoring boat with water still in it does not get to leave untreated. `quarantine_warranted` is true when the band is `EXTREME`, or when the prior water body's `infestation_status` is `CONFIRMED_INFESTED`. That second clause is why a quarantine order can be issued against an otherwise low-scoring vessel: a boat that just came off a confirmed-infested lake warrants a seal even if it scores 12.

### 3.5. Disposition, the Station Fee Ledger and the Enforcement Track

Every screened inspection ends in one of three dispositions, chosen by the owning inspector — again `X-User-ID` must equal the record's `inspector_id` — by supplying a `disposition` of `PASS`, `DECON` or `REFUSE`, with optional `notes`. The record must already be in `SCREENED`; a disposition attempted on a record still in `INITIATED` is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_NOT_SCREENED`, and one attempted on a record that has already been disposed is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_DISPOSED`.

`SCREENED → PASSED` releases the boat, and it is available only when decontamination is not owed. A pass is refused when the record's `decon_mandatory` is true, and equally when the vessel carries `mandatory_decon_flag` true, unless some decontamination order belonging to this record has already reached `VERIFIED`; the rejection is `ERR_DECON_REQUIRED_BEFORE_PASS`. `SCREENED → DECON_REQUIRED` opens the decontamination pathway of §3.6 and leaves the record's fee open for the treatment charge still to come. `SCREENED → REFUSED` records a boater who declined to be inspected — the situation the program's mandatory-inspection posting exists to address — and it does more than close the inspection: it immediately drives the record's enforcement dimension from `NONE` to `REFERRED` with `referral_reason` set to `INSPECTION_REFUSAL`, acting as the system rather than as a named officer, and with it the full strike cascade onto the vessel described later in this subsection.

All three dispositions run the fee calculation, always with the decontamination component forced to zero, writing `inspection_fee_cents`, `surcharge_cents` and `total_fee_cents` onto the record, adding the resulting `total_fee_cents` to the station's `collected_fees_cents`, and advancing both the station's `version` and the record's `version` by 1. A refused inspection is still a billable inspection.

The **fee formula** looks circular in the statute — the program surcharge is keyed on "the total fee", yet the total is itself the subtotal plus the surcharge — so the evaluation order is pinned and must be followed exactly. First the waiver is resolved: the base fee is waived when the record's `reciprocity_seal_honored` is true *and* the agreement recorded in `honored_agreement_id` carries `fee_waiver` true, in which case `inspection_fee_cents` is 0; otherwise `inspection_fee_cents` is 2,500 cents. Second the decontamination component is resolved: `decon_fee_cents` is 0 on any disposition call, and on a treatment call it is 450 cents for each minute of nozzle dwell, where the dwell in minutes is the recorded `dwell_seconds` divided by 60 and rounded **up** to a whole minute — a 90-second pass is billed as two minutes. Third the subtotal is formed as `inspection_fee_cents` plus `decon_fee_cents`. Fourth the surcharge rate is selected **from that pre-surcharge subtotal**: no surcharge at all when the subtotal is below 10,000 cents, 5 percent when the subtotal is from 10,000 cents up to but not including 25,000 cents, and 10 percent from 25,000 cents upward. A subtotal of exactly 25,000 cents therefore lands in the 10-percent tier, not the 5-percent one. Fifth `surcharge_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. Sixth `total_fee_cents` is the subtotal plus `surcharge_cents`. And seventh, the station's `collected_fees_cents` is adjusted by the *delta* — the new `total_fee_cents` minus whatever `total_fee_cents` the record previously carried — so that a re-run never double-books. The tier is never re-selected from `total_fee_cents`, and only the single decontamination order a treatment call targets contributes its dwell; sibling orders of the same record never contribute, because each treatment overwrites the decontamination component outright.

The **enforcement dimension** is the second, fully independent state dimension on an inspection record, and understanding its independence matters. `enforcement_status` advances on its own transition set with no reference whatsoever to `inspection_status`, and neither dimension gates the other. A record may sit in terminal `PASSED` while its enforcement dimension travels `NONE → REFERRED → CITED`, and a record may be `CITED` while its inspection dimension is still `DECON_REQUIRED`. An `EnforcementOfficer` drives it by supplying an `action` of `REFER`, `CITE` or `DISMISS`, optionally with a `referral_reason`, an `agency_code` naming the agency that accepted the referral, and free-text `notes`. An action not declared from the record's current enforcement standing — citing a record still at `NONE`, dismissing one already `CITED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENFORCEMENT_STATE`.

`NONE → REFERRED` is reachable three ways: directly by an officer, by the system cascade from a refused disposition, and by the system cascade from a repeatedly rejected quarantine release described in §3.7. It sets `enforcement_status` to `REFERRED`, sets `referral_reason` — the payload's value on an officer-driven call, `INSPECTION_REFUSAL` on the refusal cascade, `QUARANTINE_RELEASE_FAILURE` on the release-failure cascade, and `SEAL_TAMPER` on the breach cascade — and sets `agency_code` from the payload when one is supplied. `referral_reason` is one of `INSPECTION_REFUSAL`, `QUARANTINE_RELEASE_FAILURE`, `SEAL_TAMPER` or `DECON_EVASION`, and it is null for as long as `enforcement_status` remains `NONE`. The referral then cascades onto the vessel in three linked hops: the vessel's `compliance_strikes` is incremented by 1; because of that increment, `watchlist_status` flips from `CLEAR` to `WATCHLISTED` once `compliance_strikes` has reached 2; and because of that flip, `mandatory_decon_flag` is set true. Both the vessel's `version` and the record's `version` advance by 1. The practical consequence is severe and intended: a twice-referred boat carries a standing decontamination obligation that forces `decon_mandatory` at its next screening regardless of how cleanly it scores, and it will stay that way until a decontamination order reaches `VERIFIED`.

`REFERRED → CITED` books the civil penalty. `penalty_cents` is 15,000 cents plus 7,500 cents for each *prior* strike on the vessel, where the prior strike count is the vessel's `compliance_strikes` as it stands at the citation call minus one — the minus one excluding the strike this record's own referral booked — and never less than zero. A first-offence vessel is therefore cited at 15,000 cents flat, a vessel with one earlier strike at 22,500 cents. Every term is an integer, so no rounding arises. The record's `version` advances by 1.

`REFERRED → DISMISSED` is the compensating action. It sets `enforcement_status` to `DISMISSED`, resets `penalty_cents` to 0, decrements the vessel's `compliance_strikes` by 1, and re-derives both `watchlist_status` and `mandatory_decon_flag` from the reduced count, so a vessel that dropped below two strikes returns to `CLEAR` with its mandatory-decontamination obligation lifted. Both the vessel's `version` and the record's `version` advance by 1. A referral that is later dismissed leaves the vessel exactly as it was: same strike count, same watchlist status, same flag.

### 3.6. Decontamination: Treatment Cycles, Bay Capacity and Documented Dry Time

A record that has been dispositioned into `DECON_REQUIRED` is eligible for a decontamination order, written by an `Inspector` who names the record in `inspection_record_id` and echoes its version per §2.7. An order created against a record whose `inspection_status` is anything other than `DECON_REQUIRED` is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_NOT_AWAITING_DECON`. Creation copies `station_id` from the parent record, so the treatment is always billed and bay-checked at the station where the inspection happened, and it advances the record's `version` by 1. `treated_by_inspector_id` and `verified_by_inspector_id` both start null, `attempt_count` starts at 0, and the order's `status` — one of `ORDERED`, `TREATED`, `RETREAT_REQUIRED`, `VERIFIED` or `ABANDONED` — starts `ORDERED`. Every measurement field starts null: `water_temp_f`, `pressure_psi`, `dwell_seconds`, `treatment_started_at`, `treated_at`, `bay_released_at`, `treatment_effectiveness_pct`, `base_dry_hours`, `required_dry_hours` and `dry_until` are all written for the first time by a treatment.

The single most important thing creation does is **freeze the band**. `band_at_order` captures the parent record's `risk_band` at the moment the order is written — one of `LOW`, `MODERATE`, `HIGH` or `EXTREME` — and `base_dry_hours` is fixed from it at the same moment: 12 hours for `LOW`, 36 hours for `MODERATE`, 72 hours for `HIGH`, and 120 hours — five full days — for `EXTREME`. Both the dwell requirement and the dry-time obligation are keyed on this frozen value and on nothing else. If the parent record is later re-screened and its `risk_band` moves, the existing order's obligations do not move with it. Order listings may be narrowed by `inspection_record_id`, `station_id`, `treated_by_inspector_id`, `verified_by_inspector_id` and `status`.

Performing the treatment is the `DeconTechnician`'s exclusive act, and the payload is three instrument readings: `water_temp_f`, the hot-water delivery temperature in degrees Fahrenheit; `pressure_psi`, the delivery pressure; and `dwell_seconds`, how long the nozzle was held on the target. Four site and parameter gates apply before anything is written. The station must be equipped — `hot_water_capable` true and `decon_bay_count` of at least 1 — or the call fails with `ERR_STATION_NOT_HOT_WATER_CAPABLE`. The readings must meet the program minimums: `water_temp_f` of at least 120 degrees, `pressure_psi` of at least 1,500, and `dwell_seconds` of at least the band's required dwell, which is 30 seconds for `LOW`, 60 seconds for `MODERATE`, 120 seconds for `HIGH` and 180 seconds for `EXTREME` as keyed on `band_at_order`; a reading exactly equal to a minimum is accepted, and anything below is `ERR_TREATMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The order must not have exhausted its attempts, per the cap discussed below. And a bay must be free.

**Bay capacity** is evaluated as an interval-overlap count against the station's physical bays. The occupation this treatment would take is the closed interval beginning at the current logical time and ending at the current logical time plus `dwell_seconds` plus a 30-minute turnaround buffer. Against it, the service counts the *other* decontamination orders at the same station — the order being treated is always excluded from its own collision count — that have a non-null `treatment_started_at` and a `status` of either `TREATED` or `RETREAT_REQUIRED`, and whose own closed occupation interval from `treatment_started_at` to `bay_released_at` intersects the proposed one. Intervals that touch at a single instant do intersect. That count must be strictly less than the station's `decon_bay_count`; when it has reached the bay count, the treatment is rejected with `ERR_DECON_BAY_UNAVAILABLE`.

A treatment that clears every gate writes the readings onto the order and stamps the clock. `treatment_started_at` is the current logical time — the inclusive start of the order's bay occupation — `treated_at` is `treatment_started_at` advanced by `dwell_seconds` seconds, and `bay_released_at` is `treated_at` advanced by 30 minutes, forming the inclusive end of the occupation. `attempt_count` is incremented by 1, `treated_by_inspector_id` is set from `X-User-ID`, and `status` moves to `TREATED`.

**Effectiveness** is then scored from the three readings and the frozen band alone; no sibling order and no re-read of the parent record enters into it. Because the sub-minimum check has already passed, there is no zero branch. A temperature factor of 0.5 applies when `water_temp_f` is below 140 degrees and 1.0 from 140 degrees upward. A pressure factor of 0.6 applies when `pressure_psi` is below 3,000 and 1.0 from 3,000 upward. A dwell factor is the recorded `dwell_seconds` divided by the band's required dwell seconds, capped at 1.0 so that over-dwelling earns no bonus. `treatment_effectiveness_pct` is the three factors multiplied together, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places. Both factor thresholds are inclusive at the top: exactly 140 degrees gives a temperature factor of 1.0, and exactly 3,000 PSI gives a pressure factor of 1.0.

The **documented dry time** follows immediately, in a five-step order that is pinned and must not be rearranged. Step one takes `base_dry_hours` from `band_at_order`, using the same band-to-hours mapping given above. Step two reads `treatment_effectiveness_pct` from the treatment just scored. Step three computes the credit: the base dry hours multiplied by the effectiveness expressed as a fraction of 100, multiplied by 0.75, then floored to a whole number of hours — so even a perfect treatment can never earn back more than three quarters of the base obligation. Step four sets `required_dry_hours` to the base dry hours minus that credit, but never below 6 hours, which is the absolute floor no treatment quality can undercut. Step five sets `dry_until` to `treated_at` advanced by `required_dry_hours` hours. `required_dry_hours` is never back-derived from `dry_until`, and `base_dry_hours` is never re-keyed on a later re-screen of the parent record.

Recording a treatment is also a billing event. The fee calculation of §3.5 is re-run on the parent record with the `dwell_seconds` just recorded, overwriting `decon_fee_cents`, `surcharge_cents` and `total_fee_cents`, and the station's `collected_fees_cents` is adjusted by the resulting delta. The parent record's `version`, the station's `version` and the order's own `version` each advance by 1.

**Dry verification** belongs to the `ReleaseVerifier`, who supplies an `outcome` of `VERIFIED`, `RETREAT` or `ABANDON` with optional `notes`. The order must be in `TREATED`: verification aimed at an order still in `ORDERED` or already back in `RETREAT_REQUIRED` is `ERR_DECON_NOT_TREATED`, while verification aimed at an order already in `VERIFIED` or `ABANDONED` is `ERR_DECON_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, so the two 409s never both apply to the same call. The verifier's `X-User-ID` may not equal the order's `treated_by_inspector_id` — a technician may not sign off their own work — and a collision is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. For the two outcomes that are *judgements about the dry period*, `VERIFIED` and `RETREAT`, the current logical time must have reached or passed `dry_until`, and a time exactly equal to `dry_until` satisfies the requirement; earlier is `ERR_DRY_TIME_NOT_ELAPSED`. `ABANDON` is deliberately exempt from that gate: a vessel may be abandoned at any point after treatment.

`TREATED → VERIFIED` sets the order's `status` to `VERIFIED` and `verified_by_inspector_id` from `X-User-ID`, and then cascades twice. The parent record moves `DECON_REQUIRED → DECON_COMPLETED`, and that record transition in turn stamps the vessel's `last_decon_verified_at` at the current logical time and clears the vessel's `mandatory_decon_flag`. The vessel's `version`, the record's `version` and the order's `version` each advance by 1. `TREATED → RETREAT_REQUIRED` sets `status` to `RETREAT_REQUIRED` and `verified_by_inspector_id` from `X-User-ID`, vacates the bay, and deliberately leaves the parent record sitting in `DECON_REQUIRED`; only the order's `version` advances. `TREATED → ABANDONED` sets `status` to `ABANDONED` and `verified_by_inspector_id` from `X-User-ID`, then cascades: the parent record moves `DECON_REQUIRED → DECON_FAILED_ABANDONED`, `fee_refunded_cents` on that record is set to `decon_fee_cents` plus `surcharge_cents` — the treatment-attributable portion of the ledger — and that same amount is subtracted from the station's `collected_fees_cents` and added to its `refunded_fees_cents`. The station's `version`, the record's `version` and the order's `version` each advance by 1.

One subtlety governs all three outcomes. Because a record awaiting decontamination may carry more than one order, the parent-record cascade fires from whichever order the call targets, and it is **skipped outright** when the parent record has already left `DECON_REQUIRED` — the decontamination order's own transition still completes normally in that case. A second, superseded order can therefore be verified or abandoned after the record has already been completed by its sibling, without disturbing the record.

The retreat path closes a cycle: `RETREAT_REQUIRED → TREATED` repeats the treatment with the same gates and the same side effects, overwriting the previous treatment's readings, timestamps, effectiveness and dry-time obligation. `attempt_count` is monotonic across the whole cycle, and the cap is what terminates it: an order may be treated at most 3 times, so a fourth treatment on the same order is rejected with `ERR_DECON_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` while the order remains in `RETREAT_REQUIRED`. Both `VERIFIED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Quarantine: Seals, Hold Periods and Release Verification

Some vessels cannot safely be released even after treatment, and for those the program uses a physical numbered seal and a mandatory standing period. An `Inspector` issues a quarantine order against a record by naming it in `inspection_record_id` and supplying the `seal_number` physically affixed to the vessel, optionally with a `seal_state_code`. Five gates apply. The record's `quarantine_warranted` must be true, or the issuance is rejected with `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_WARRANTED`. The record must be awaiting decontamination — a quarantine order created against a record whose `inspection_status` is not `DECON_REQUIRED` is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_NOT_AWAITING_DECON`. The `seal_number` must be unused across every quarantine order ever written, terminal ones included, or the issuance fails with `ERR_SEAL_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`. The issuing station must have at least one seal on the shelf — `seal_stock_count` of 1 or more — or the issuance fails with `ERR_STATION_SEAL_STOCK_EXHAUSTED`. And the vessel must not already be sitting under an unexpired hold: an existing quarantine order for the same vessel whose `status` is `SEALED` or `RELEASE_REQUESTED` and whose `hold_until` the current logical time has not yet passed blocks a new one with `ERR_WATERCRAFT_ALREADY_QUARANTINED`. That upper endpoint is closed, so an order whose `hold_until` equals the current logical time exactly still blocks a new order.

Issuance copies rather than asks. `watercraft_id` and `station_id` are both copied from the parent record — the station whose seal inventory is debited is the station where the inspection took place — and `issued_by_inspector_id` is taken from `X-User-ID`. `seal_state_code` records the two-letter code of the issuing program: for a `WATERBODY_ACCESS` station it is copied from the `state_code` of the water body that station guards, and for a `HIGHWAY_CORRIDOR` station, which guards no particular water body, it is the program's own state code, `MT`. As with decontamination, the band is frozen: `band_at_issue` captures the record's `risk_band` at issuance, and `issued_at` is stamped at the current logical time as the inclusive start of the vessel's hold interval. The order's `status` is one of `SEALED`, `RELEASE_REQUESTED`, `RELEASED` or `VOIDED_BREACH`; it starts `SEALED`, `release_verified_by_inspector_id` starts null, and `rejected_release_count` starts at 0.

The **hold length** is keyed on the frozen band plus an escalation for every rejected release. `hold_hours_required` is the band's base hold — 24 hours for `LOW`, 48 hours for `MODERATE`, 72 hours for `HIGH`, 120 hours for `EXTREME` — plus 24 hours for each entry in `rejected_release_count`. `hold_until` is then `issued_at` advanced by `hold_hours_required` hours, forming the inclusive end of the hold, and the same instant is written onto the vessel as `quarantine_hold_until`, which is null whenever the vessel is under no hold. A `band_at_issue` of `LOW` is perfectly possible and is not a defect: it arises when `quarantine_warranted` was raised by a `CONFIRMED_INFESTED` prior water body on an otherwise low-scoring vessel. Note that `hold_until` is always recomputed from `issued_at` and never from the previous `hold_until`, so a vessel that has had two releases rejected sits under exactly its base hold plus 48 hours measured from issuance. No sibling quarantine order is ever consulted. Quarantine listings may be narrowed by `inspection_record_id`, `watercraft_id`, `station_id`, `issued_by_inspector_id`, `release_verified_by_inspector_id`, `status` and `seal_number`.

Release is a two-step, two-person process. First the **issuing** inspector raises the request, optionally with `notes`, moving the order `SEALED → RELEASE_REQUESTED` and advancing its `version` by 1. Only that inspector may raise it — `X-User-ID` must equal `issued_by_inspector_id` or the call is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — and the current logical time must have reached or passed `hold_until`, a time exactly equal to `hold_until` being sufficient; earlier is `ERR_HOLD_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. A request aimed at an order in a state that does not permit it is rejected with `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_SEALED`.

Then a **different** inspector, acting as `ReleaseVerifier`, decides it by supplying an `outcome` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` with optional `notes`. The order must be in `RELEASE_REQUESTED`, or the call is `ERR_QUARANTINE_RELEASE_NOT_REQUESTED`. The verifier must be in `ACTIVE` standing and hold `LEVEL_2` or `DECON_CERTIFIED`, and the verifier's `X-User-ID` may not equal `issued_by_inspector_id` — the inspector who sealed the boat may not be the one who unseals it, and a collision is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

`RELEASE_REQUESTED → RELEASED` carries two further conditions. The current logical time must again be at or after `hold_until`, and when the parent record's `decon_mandatory` is true, some decontamination order belonging to that record must have reached `VERIFIED`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_DECON_REQUIRED_BEFORE_RELEASE`. When `decon_mandatory` is false that condition is vacuous and no decontamination order need exist at all. An approved release sets `status` to `RELEASED` and `release_verified_by_inspector_id` from `X-User-ID`, then retires the seal: the station's `seals_in_circulation_count` is decremented by 1 and its `seals_retired_count` incremented by 1 — the seal is physically cut, so it never returns to stock — and the vessel's `quarantine_hold_until` is cleared. The station's `version`, the vessel's `version` and the order's `version` each advance by 1.

`RELEASE_REQUESTED → SEALED` is the rejection path, and it closes a cycle that can repeat: each rejection puts the vessel back under seal with a longer hold. It sets `status` back to `SEALED` and `release_verified_by_inspector_id` from `X-User-ID`, increments `rejected_release_count` by 1, and — caused by that increment — re-runs the hold computation, overwriting `hold_hours_required` and recomputing `hold_until` from `issued_at`, then writing the new `hold_until` onto the vessel's `quarantine_hold_until`. When `rejected_release_count` reaches 2, the same increment drives a further cascade: the parent record's enforcement dimension moves `NONE → REFERRED` with `referral_reason` set to `QUARANTINE_RELEASE_FAILURE`, that referral in turn increments the vessel's `compliance_strikes` and flips `watchlist_status` to `WATCHLISTED` once the strike count reaches 2, and that watchlist flip in turn sets the vessel's `mandatory_decon_flag` true. The vessel's `version` and the order's `version` each advance by 1.

A tampered or broken seal is reported separately, and either a `ReleaseVerifier` or an `EnforcementOfficer` may report it, optionally with `notes`. A breach report voids the order from either `SEALED` or `RELEASE_REQUESTED`, setting `status` to `VOIDED_BREACH`. The side effects are identical from both origins: the station's `seals_in_circulation_count` is decremented by 1 and `seals_retired_count` incremented by 1, because a breached seal is forfeited rather than returned; the vessel's `quarantine_hold_until` is cleared, since a voided order is no longer in `SEALED` or `RELEASE_REQUESTED` and therefore holds nothing; and the parent record's enforcement dimension is driven `NONE → REFERRED` with `referral_reason` set to `SEAL_TAMPER`, but only when that record's `enforcement_status` is still `NONE`. The station's `version`, the vessel's `version` and the order's `version` each advance by 1. Both `RELEASED` and `VOIDED_BREACH` are terminal.

### 3.8. Detection Reporting and Infestation Escalation

When an inspector actually finds something — a mussel on a trim tab, a fragment of milfoil in a trailer bunk — the finding has to reach the state database, and the path there is deliberately slow and reviewable. An `Inspector` drafts a report against the inspection during which the find occurred, naming it in `inspection_record_id` and supplying the state `species_code` for the organism (non-empty, at most 32 characters), the `detection_class` describing what was found, and optionally the `observed_at` instant of the observation. `detection_class` is one of `LIVE_ORGANISM`, `DEAD_ORGANISM` or `PLANT_FRAGMENT`, and only `LIVE_ORGANISM` carries the weight that confirms an infestation. Drafting copies `water_body_id` from the parent record's `prior_water_body_id` — the water the boat came from, which is the water at risk — and takes `reported_by_inspector_id` from `X-User-ID`. The report's `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`; it starts `DRAFT` with `submission_count` at 0, `state_db_ack_id` null and `rejection_reason` null; nothing has been reported to the state yet. Report listings may be narrowed by `inspection_record_id`, `water_body_id`, `reported_by_inspector_id`, `status` and `detection_class`.

Only the reporting inspector may push their own report to the state queue — `X-User-ID` must equal `reported_by_inspector_id`, or the call is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — and the inspector must be in `ACTIVE` standing. Submission moves the report `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, or `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` on a resubmission, incrementing `submission_count` by 1 and clearing any `rejection_reason` left over from a previous verdict, then advancing the report's `version` by 1. A submission aimed at a report that is neither `DRAFT` nor `REJECTED` is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`. Because the resubmission edge exists, a report the state bounces can be corrected and pushed again any number of times, and `submission_count` records how many attempts it took.

The `StateDataSteward` renders the verdict, supplying a `verdict` of `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, plus `state_db_ack_id` when the state database returns an acknowledgement identifier and `rejection_reason` when it does not. The report must be in `SUBMITTED`, or the call is `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`, and the acknowledging steward may not be the inspector who filed the report — a collision is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `REJECTED` verdict is the cheap case: it sets `status` to `REJECTED`, stores the supplied `rejection_reason`, touches no water body counter whatsoever, and advances the report's `version` by 1. `SUBMITTED → REJECTED → SUBMITTED` is a full cycle, and it repeats until the state accepts.

An `ACCEPTED` verdict is where the program's biggest cascade lives, and it runs four hops deep. The report's `status` becomes `ACCEPTED` — a terminal state — and `state_db_ack_id` is stored from the payload. In the second hop, the water body's `detection_count` is incremented by 1, and its `live_detection_count` is incremented by 1 as well when the report's `detection_class` is `LIVE_ORGANISM`; the escalation rule then runs **on the counters as they stand after those increments**. When `live_detection_count` has reached 2, the water body's `infestation_status` is set to `CONFIRMED_INFESTED` and its `risk_tier` is set to `HIGH`. Otherwise, when `detection_count` has reached 1 and the water body's `infestation_status` is still `CLEAN`, `infestation_status` is set to `SUSPECT` and `risk_tier` is left as it was. In every other case both fields are left as they were. The live check is evaluated first, which is why the second accepted `LIVE_ORGANISM` report jumps a `CLEAN` water body straight to `CONFIRMED_INFESTED` on that one call without ever passing through `SUSPECT`. Escalation is strictly one-way: no endpoint anywhere in this API walks `CONFIRMED_INFESTED` back to `SUSPECT` or `CLEAN`, or `SUSPECT` back to `CLEAN`, and reports still in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing to either counter.

The third hop is a policy change at the stations. *Because* the water body became `CONFIRMED_INFESTED`, every station whose `water_body_id` is that water body and whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `mandatory_inspection` is currently false has `mandatory_inspection` set to true — inspection at those sites stops being voluntary the moment the infestation is confirmed. The fourth hop follows from the third: *because* those stations flipped from voluntary to mandatory, every operating-hours window of each such station whose `staffing_level` is `SINGLE` is upgraded to `DOUBLE`, since a mandatory checkpoint needs two crew. The water body's `version` advances by 1, and so does the `version` of every station and every hours window touched by the third and fourth hops. The report's own `version` advances by 1 as well. A steward accepting a single report can therefore, in one request, change the risk tier of a lake, make inspection compulsory at four stations, and double the posted staffing on a dozen shift windows.

### 3.9. Public Education Campaigns: Allocation and Effectiveness Evaluation

Enforcement is only half the program; the other half is persuading boaters to clean, drain and dry voluntarily. A `ProgramAdmin` plans a campaign with a `name`, the `target_water_body_id` whose boater compliance it aims to improve, an inclusive `start_at` and an exclusive `end_at` that must be strictly later, and a `budget_cents` in whole cents. The budget must be greater than zero and must not exceed the program cap of 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars per campaign — and a budget over the cap is rejected with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Two further values may be declared up front: `impressions`, the reported public impressions, defaulting to 0 and never negative; and `evaluation_method`, one of `PRE_POST` or `CONTROL_CORRIDOR`, naming the comparison the office intends to use later. A campaign's `status` is one of `PLANNED`, `RUNNING`, `EVALUATED` or `CANCELLED`. A planned campaign starts in `PLANNED` status with an empty `station_allocations` array and no money allocated anywhere; `window_compliance_rate`, `baseline_compliance_rate`, `effectiveness_score` and `alternate_effectiveness_score` are all null until evaluation. Campaign listings may be narrowed by `target_water_body_id`, `status` and `evaluation_method`.

Launching a campaign moves it `PLANNED → RUNNING` and is the moment the money is committed. The candidate stations are exactly those whose `water_body_id` equals the campaign's `target_water_body_id` and whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED`; closed stations never qualify. If no station qualifies, the launch is rejected with `ERR_NO_STATIONS_FOR_CAMPAIGN`, and if the budget is too small to give every qualifying station at least one cent, it is rejected with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

The candidates are put into a **pinned order**: ascending `created_at` first, with ascending `name` compared byte for byte as the tie-break. A station's `id` is never consulted for ordering. Each candidate then gets a traffic weight of 1 plus the number of inspection records at that station whose `arrived_at` falls inside the half-open campaign window from `start_at` up to but not including `end_at`, counting records of any `inspection_status` at all — a station that saw thirty boats during the window weighs 31, a station that saw none weighs 1. The total weight is the sum of the candidates' weights.

**The rounding order is pinned.** Each candidate's allocation is first computed as the campaign's `budget_cents` multiplied by that candidate's weight, divided by the total weight, and floored to whole cents — floored per station, individually. The remainder is then the `budget_cents` minus the sum of those floored allocations, and it is handed out one cent at a time to the candidates in the pinned order: the first candidate in the order gets an extra cent, then the second, and so on until the remainder is exhausted. Summing exact fractional shares and rounding once at the end would distribute different cents to different stations, and that alternative is explicitly not this program's rule. Because the remainder is always at least zero and always smaller than the number of candidates, the resulting allocations sum to exactly `budget_cents`. The result is written to `station_allocations` as an array of objects, each carrying the candidate's `station_id` and its `allocated_cents`, emitted in the pinned order, and each amount is added to that station's `campaign_allocated_cents`. Every allocated station's `version` advances by 1, as does the campaign's.

Cancellation exists at both stages and behaves differently at each. `PLANNED → CANCELLED` touches no station at all, because nothing was ever allocated; it merely sets `status` to `CANCELLED` and advances the campaign's `version` by 1. `RUNNING → CANCELLED` is the compensating action for the launch: it subtracts from each station's `campaign_allocated_cents` exactly the `allocated_cents` recorded for that station in `station_allocations`, sets `station_allocations` back to the empty array, advances the `version` of every de-allocated station by 1, and advances the campaign's `version` by 1. Both `EVALUATED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

**Evaluation** closes the loop, and it is available only once the window has closed: the current logical time must have reached or passed `end_at`, or the call is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_WINDOW_NOT_ENDED`. The evaluation call may supply `evaluation_method` to choose the comparison, and when it is omitted the value declared at campaign creation is used; it may also supply a revised `impressions` count.

Everything rests on one measurement, the **compliance rate** over a scope and a half-open window on `arrived_at`. Its numerator is the number of inspection records in scope whose `inspection_status` is `PASSED` or `DECON_COMPLETED` and whose `arrived_at` falls at or after the window start and strictly before the window end. Its denominator is the number of records in the same scope and window whose `inspection_status` is `PASSED`, `DECON_COMPLETED`, `REFUSED` or `DECON_FAILED_ABANDONED`. Records still sitting in `INITIATED` or `SCREENED` are excluded from both counts, because a boater who has not yet been dispositioned has neither complied nor failed to. The rate is the numerator divided by the denominator, and it is undefined when the denominator is zero. Two scopes are used: the **target** scope covers records whose `prior_water_body_id` is the campaign's `target_water_body_id`, and the **statewide** scope covers every inspection record in the program regardless of prior water body.

The measured rate is the target scope over the campaign's own window, from `start_at` to `end_at`. An empty denominator here is a hard rejection rather than a score of zero: `ERR_NO_INSPECTIONS_IN_WINDOW`. Otherwise that rate, rounded half up to four decimal places, is stored as `window_compliance_rate`. Two baselines are then computed against it. The **pre/post** baseline is the target scope over the equal-length window immediately preceding `start_at` — the window length being `end_at` minus `start_at`, so the baseline window runs from `start_at` minus that length up to `start_at` — and if that baseline is undefined for want of any dispositioned record, it is taken as 0.0000 rather than rejected. The **control-corridor** baseline is the statewide scope over exactly the same window as the campaign; it can never be undefined here, because the target denominator is a subset of the statewide denominator and is already known to be non-empty.

The score is built the same way from whichever baseline it is given. An impressions bonus is computed first as the smaller of 10 points and the campaign's `impressions` divided by 5,000 and floored to a whole number — one bonus point per five thousand impressions, saturating at ten. Then the score is 50, plus the difference between `window_compliance_rate` and the baseline rounded half up to four decimal places, that difference multiplied by 100, plus the impressions bonus; the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places and clamped into the closed range from 0.00 to 100.00. A campaign that moved compliance not at all with no impressions scores exactly 50.00.

Both baselines are always scored, so the divergence between the two methods is observable rather than hidden. The baseline the selected `evaluation_method` names is stored as `baseline_compliance_rate`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and its score is stored as `effectiveness_score`; the other method's score is stored as `alternate_effectiveness_score`. Under `PRE_POST` the effectiveness score is the before/after comparison on the same water body and the alternate is the synthetic-control comparison; under `CONTROL_CORRIDOR` the two swap. On identical data the two differ whenever the target water body's own compliance history differs from the contemporaneous statewide rate, which is precisely the disagreement the program office wants to see. Evaluation sets `status` to `EVALUATED`, advances the campaign's `version` by 1, and deliberately leaves `station_allocations` and every station's `campaign_allocated_cents` exactly as they were — evaluating a campaign can never drift the money ledger.
