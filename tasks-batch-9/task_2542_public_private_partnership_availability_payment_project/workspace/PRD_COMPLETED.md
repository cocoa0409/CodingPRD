# PPP Availability Payment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A public authority that procures a road, a hospital or a water treatment plant under an availability-payment concession does not buy an asset; it buys a service, month after month, for twenty-five or thirty years. The money only flows if the asset is available and performing, and the contract that governs that flow is unforgiving in its detail: a Maximum Annual Service Payment fixed at signature, an indexation formula, a deduction regime that converts unavailability minutes and performance-standard failures into points and points into cents, a per-period deduction cap, an annual deduction cap, and a lifecycle reserve that must be funded throughout operations so the asset can be handed back in contract condition at expiry.

This API is the system of record for that contract. It carries a concession from drafting through financial close, tracks debt and equity drawdown against certified construction milestones, accrues liquidated damages when service commencement is late, and then — for every monthly service period — records the concessionaire's own performance return, the authority's verification of it, the deductions that follow, the disputes the concessionaire raises against those deductions, and the invoice and payment that close the period out. Around that spine sit the contract's escape valves and remedies: relief events, compensation events and change-in-law claims; refinancing gain sharing with the authority; the lifecycle reserve ledger and the handback condition survey; and, at the far end, the lenders' step-in rights and the authority's termination compensation regimes.

Three properties drive the design. First, **every figure is auditable**: money is held in integer cents, each formula rounds exactly once at a stated position, and each ledger and event set replays in an explicit recorded order rather than in whatever order the database happens to return. Second, **no party may certify its own position**: five roles hold disjoint write surfaces, an independent certifier holds the three certification monopolies, and four operations on the authority's own side are separated from each other by acting user identity. Third, **every consequence lands inside the request that caused it**: when a dispute is upheld, the deduction is zeroed, the period's allocation and net payment are recomputed, the concession's year-to-date totals move, the persistent-breach flag is re-evaluated and an open step-in may cure — all before the response is written.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole surface, which is mounted under the `/ppp` prefix (the single exception being the clock-driving endpoint described in §2.6).

### 2.1. Parties, Roles and Access Control

Every request to the `/ppp` surface must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts (the clock utility of §2.6 sits outside that surface and outside this requirement). If either header is absent, or if `X-User-ID` is present but empty or whitespace-only, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognised roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. The five roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `AuthorityOfficer`, `Concessionaire`, `LenderAgent` and `IndependentCertifier`, spelled exactly so and compared case-sensitively with no trimming or normalisation. When the role is recognised but is not among the roles permitted for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The roles divide the contract along its commercial fault lines. `PlatformAdmin` is a registry and ledger-integrity operator: it onboards sponsor vehicles, may draft a concession, appends compensating reversals to the lifecycle-reserve ledger and drives the server's logical clock. It holds no commercial authority whatsoever — it can neither verify a service period nor consent to a refinancing. `AuthorityOfficer` is the public authority's case officer: it closes the deal, opens service periods, verifies self-reported performance, issues and withdraws deductions, adjudicates disputes, determines relief and compensation claims, consents to refinancings, and both calculates and settles termination compensation. `Concessionaire` is the project company: it requests drawdowns, self-reports performance, invoices the net availability payment, disputes deductions, notifies claims, funds the lifecycle reserve and may propose a refinancing. `LenderAgent` acts for the senior lenders: it disburses certified milestones and holds the three step-in levers — notify, cure and escalate. `IndependentCertifier` is appointed jointly by the parties and holds the sole power to certify milestone cost, to certify service commencement and to survey handback condition, precisely because neither commercial party may make those three determinations about itself.

Identity semantics differ between the project side and everyone else. When `X-User-Role` is `Concessionaire`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Concessionaire` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Concessionaire` compare the owning concession's `concessionaire_id` field to `X-User-ID`. Because the surface is flat, that ownership check resolves transitively: a service period resolves to its concession, a deduction event through its service period to its concession, a deduction dispute through its deduction event and service period to its concession, and a drawdown milestone, relief claim, refinancing event, enforcement action or reserve transaction directly to its concession. A `Concessionaire` acting on a resource whose owning concession names a different `concessionaire_id` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_CONCESSION_PARTY` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `AuthorityOfficer`, `LenderAgent` or `IndependentCertifier`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server never compares such an identifier to an entity's `id`. It does, however, record it where the contract requires an audit trail or a separation of duties: the issuing officer of a deduction event is recorded in `issued_by_user_id` (or the literal string `SYSTEM` when the system itself raised the event), the adjudicating officer of a dispute in `adjudicated_by_user_id`, the proposing party of a refinancing in `proposed_by_user_id`, the consenting officer in `consented_by_user_id`, and the officer who computed termination compensation in `calculated_by_user_id`. Two further fields record only the role rather than the user: `created_by_role` on a reserve-ledger entry and `initiated_by_role` on an enforcement action, each of which carries the literal string `SYSTEM` when the row was appended by a cascade rather than by a caller. The concessionaire that raises a dispute is recorded in `raised_by_user_id`, which by construction equals the owning concession's `concessionaire_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.6 rather than from wall-clock time, so every response served at the same clock value carries the same timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 and is clamped into the range 1 to 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and is clamped at 0 from below. Neither parameter is ever a ground for rejection: a value outside those bounds is silently clamped into range rather than refused, an absent, empty or non-integer value falls back to the default, and the `meta` block echoes the effective `limit` and `offset` actually applied. List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a narrower rule is stated for the collection in question; because the logical clock only moves when a `PlatformAdmin` moves it, rows of the same collection routinely share a `created_at`, and such ties are broken by insertion order, tracked by an internal monotonic counter that appears in no response field; for the collections that carry an explicit `sequence_no` — drawdown milestones, deduction events and lifecycle-reserve entries — ascending `created_at` and ascending `sequence_no` describe the same order, because `sequence_no` is assigned in insertion order and never reused. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Each list endpoint also accepts the foreign-key and enumeration filters relevant to its collection, and those filters are the only way to discover rows the system creates by cascade rather than in response to a caller's create request. Concessionaires may be narrowed by `status`. Concessions may be narrowed by `concessionaire_id`, `status` and `handback_status`. Drawdown milestones accept `concession_id` and `status`. Service periods accept `concession_id`, `reporting_status` and `payment_status`. Performance reports accept `service_period_id` and `status`, and that list is the only way to see a period's superseded reporting history. Deduction events accept `service_period_id`, `performance_report_id`, `deduction_type` and `status`, and that list is the only way to reach the self-report-understatement and handback-shortfall events the cascades raise. Deduction disputes accept `deduction_event_id` and `status`. Relief claims accept `concession_id`, `claim_type` and `status`. Reserve transactions accept `concession_id`, `reverses_txn_id` and `txn_type`, and that list is the only way to reach the construction-seed and refinancing-sweep entries. Refinancing events accept `concession_id` and `status`. Enforcement actions accept `concession_id`, `escalated_to_action_id`, `action_type` and `status`, and that list is the only way to reach the termination row an escalation appends. No filter is ever a ground for rejection either. A filter is compared as an exact, case-sensitive string against the named field; a parameter that is absent, or present with an empty value, is simply not applied; a row whose filter field is null matches no supplied value, so there is no way to select for absence; several filters supplied together narrow conjunctively; and a value that matches nothing — a well-formed id naming no row included — yields an empty `data` array with `total_count` 0 rather than a 404 or a 400. Query parameters other than these filters and the two pagination parameters are ignored rather than refused.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Append-Only Exception

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: an opaque UUID with no type prefix, never accepted as an input and never used in any business decision, ordering or tie-break. Alongside it sits `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits stamped from the logical clock at insert, which serves as the documented fallback ordering key wherever no explicit `sequence_no` exists.

Ten of the eleven resource types additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increases by exactly 1 on every successful mutation of the row. The bump is per row and per request: however many of a row's fields move inside one request, its `updated_at` is restamped and its `version` raised exactly once, and an assignment that leaves a field at the value it already held is not a mutation and moves neither stamp on its own. This holds for mutations a cascade performs as much as for mutations a caller requests: when the settlement of a termination action moves a concession to `TERMINATED`, adjusts its invoiced periods and winds up its sponsor vehicle, each of those rows has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one. That property is what makes the concurrency rules of §2.7 observable from two strictly sequential requests.

The lifecycle-reserve ledger is the single exception. A reserve transaction carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is append-only. There is no update and no delete for a ledger entry. A mistaken entry is corrected by appending a reversal that points at it, never by rewriting it.

### 2.5. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer cents, held in fields whose names end in `_cents`. Percentages and ratios are decimal fractions between 0 and 1 inclusive, held in fields whose names end in `_pct`; a return on equity is expressed in basis points in a field ending in `_bps`, which is converted to a fraction by dividing by 10,000. Every division and every percentage multiplication is performed in decimal arithmetic and rounded **half up** — a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero, never to the nearest even.

The discipline that matters more than the rounding mode is *when* rounding happens. Each formula in this document prescribes exactly one rounding step, applied at a stated position to the fully composed expression, and prescribes the order in which its operands are combined. Re-associating the arithmetic — rounding an intermediate quotient before applying an uplift to it, or applying a credit before a cap rather than after — produces a different and wrong answer, and the divergence propagates into every figure derived downstream. Money results round to integer cents. The two published ratios round to four decimal places: a service period's `availability_ratio` and a concession's `cumulative_drawn_pct`. A concession's `indexation_pct` is accepted with at most six decimal places and its `deduction_cap_pct` with at most four; a performance report's `variance_pct` is computed to four decimal places.

Point counts, day counts, month counts and the ledger are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding step at all, so no drift is possible in them.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock, Calendar Days and Interval Conventions

The server keeps a logical clock, and every timestamp it stamps and every temporal comparison it makes reads that clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time, so every notification window, cure deadline and accrual span is deterministic before any caller touches it. The clock-driving endpoint is an operator utility sitting outside the authenticated `/ppp` surface: it is the one endpoint that performs neither the header authentication nor the role check of §2.1, so the `PlatformAdmin` named for it in §4 records the operator expected to use it rather than a restriction the server enforces. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant; every subsequent read returns that instant until the clock is set again, and the clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards. Setting the clock creates and mutates no business row. Instants the server stamps are always rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a `Z` suffix, while instants a caller supplies are accepted in a wider grammar: a bare calendar date `YYYY-MM-DD`, or a date and a time separated by `T` or a single space, with the seconds and up to nine fractional digits optional, and with either a `Z` suffix or a `±HH:MM` offset — the colon in the offset optional — that is normalised to UTC — a value with no zone at all is read as UTC. Anything outside that grammar, or a date that does not exist in the calendar, is a schema failure. Where such a caller-supplied instant is persisted on a row it is stored and echoed back verbatim as the caller spelled it, never reformatted into the server's own three-digit form; only the comparisons the server makes use the normalised UTC value.

All temporal spans in the contract are measured in whole calendar days; there are no partial days anywhere. Two interval conventions coexist and must not be confused. A service period's interval is **half-open**: `period_start` is inclusive, `period_end` is exclusive, and `days_in_period` is the whole-day span of that half-open interval. A relief claim's window is **closed**: both `event_start` and `event_end` are inclusive days of the claimed event. The interaction of the two conventions is what determines how many days of a granted compensation event fall inside a payment period, and it is spelled out where that credit is computed.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Parent Version

Three write operations are guarded against acting on a stale view of their parent resource. Each requires a non-optional version field in its request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the parent; if that value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is refused and nothing is created or changed.

Creating a drawdown milestone under a concession requires `expected_concession_version` to equal that concession's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONCESSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Milestone creation is additive and always legitimate on its own terms, but the equity/debt split the requester is relying on is a function of the concession's cumulative drawn state, so a view that predates another milestone's disbursement — or the construction auto-transition that disbursement triggered — would produce a split the requester did not intend.

Consenting to or refusing a proposed refinancing likewise requires `expected_concession_version` to equal the concession's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONCESSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Consent appends a reserve sweep sized from a gain computed at proposal time; if the concession has moved since the officer read it — a further drawdown, a period recompute, an enforcement cascade — the officer would be consenting on a view that no longer describes the project.

Creating a performance report against a service period requires `expected_service_period_version` to equal that period's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SERVICE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This one is load-bearing rather than defensive: because a second report on an already-self-reported period is a legitimate supersede-in-place resubmission, it would otherwise succeed silently against a stale view, and the writer would be superseding a report it never read.

No other operation takes a version field. Where a request body has no version field, no concurrency check is performed.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Within the authority's own write surface, four pairs of operations may never be performed by the same `X-User-ID`, and a second attempt by the same identity is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). An officer who issued any deduction event already attached to a service period may not verify that period. An officer who issued a deduction event may not adjudicate the dispute raised against it. An officer who proposed a refinancing may not consent to it — reachable because an `AuthorityOfficer` is one of the two roles permitted to propose. An officer who calculated a termination compensation may not settle it. In each pair the comparison is between the acting `X-User-ID` and the identifier the system recorded when the first half of the pair was performed; events raised by the system carry the literal `SYSTEM` in `issued_by_user_id`, which no caller's identifier ever equals. Where no identifier was recorded at all — the field is still null because the first half of the pair was never performed — there is nothing to compare and no violation arises.

### 2.9. Rejection Precedence

Where several rules could refuse the same request, they are evaluated in a fixed order and the first violation found is the one reported. The ladder runs: authentication; role authorization; existence of every id named in the path; concessionaire ownership of the resolved path resource; existence of every id supplied in the request body; request-body schema conformance; optimistic-concurrency version match; the target row's own state; the admissibility of an enforcement ground against the arm it is paired with; the owning concession's state; segregation of duties; the enforcement freeze; the reserve-withdrawal guard; cross-entity referential coherence; the relief notification window; the cure window; interval validity; service-period overlap; relief-window conflict; uniqueness and dispute-round limits; funding commitment ceilings; the annual deduction cap; the relief-day annual cap; the grant-versus-claim ceiling; and finally the refinancing-gain test.

The ordering is deliberate, and fiscal caps sit last on purpose. The authority would rather tell a caller "you are not the party to this concession" or "this project is under enforcement" than "your deduction exceeds the annual cap", because the latter leaks the project's financial position to a party that had no standing to ask. Three consequences follow that are easy to get wrong. A request that both names a non-existent path resource and carries a malformed body is a 404, not a 400. A `Concessionaire` naming a path id that resolves to no row at all receives that same 404 rather than the 403 of an ownership failure, because ownership is a property of the resolved row and cannot be tested before the row is in hand. And a request that is both stale on its parent version and invalid on its target state is a 412, not a 409.

Two rungs of the ladder are worth restating in full because they fan out across many resources. Path existence is validated left to right, and each resource type has its own error: a missing concessionaire yields `ERR_CONCESSIONAIRE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing concession `ERR_CONCESSION_NOT_FOUND`, a missing service period `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, a missing drawdown milestone `ERR_DRAWDOWN_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing performance report `ERR_PERFORMANCE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing deduction event `ERR_DEDUCTION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing dispute `ERR_DEDUCTION_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing relief claim `ERR_RELIEF_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, a missing reserve transaction `ERR_RESERVE_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, a missing refinancing event `ERR_REFINANCING_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` and a missing enforcement action `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. Identifiers supplied in a request *body* rather than in the path are a separate rung: the `concessionaire_id` named when drafting a concession, the optional `performance_report_id` named when issuing a deduction event, and the reversal target named when reversing a ledger entry all yield `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when they resolve to no row. Only a well-formed identifier reaches this rung: a body id that is absent, null, empty or not a string is never looked up at all and falls through to the schema rung below as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, where emptiness means the exact empty string — a whitespace-only id is well formed enough to be looked up, and so comes back as `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` rather than as a schema failure.

Schema conformance covers every create and amend operation and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400): a required field absent, an enumeration value outside its declared set, an amount field that is not a non-negative integer, a percentage field outside the range 0 to 1 inclusive, a basis-point field that is not a non-negative integer, a malformed ISO 8601 date, a reversal attempted through the plain ledger-append operation, or a compensation calculation on concessionaire default that omits any of its three market-value inputs. The same rung governs the shape of the request itself. The body must be a JSON object; an absent or empty body is read as an empty object, so its required fields simply come up missing, a body that is valid JSON but not an object is refused, and the request's content type is never examined. A field present with a `null` value counts as absent — required, it is a missing field; optional, it takes its documented default. A boolean is never accepted where an integer or a decimal fraction is required. A string field that is empty or whitespace-only, or that exceeds the maximum length stated for it, is refused here; and where §3 states no length for a string field the contract's standard maxima apply — 2,000 characters for a free-text note, reason or grounds field, 200 for a name or an external reference, and 500 for a reserve-ledger `reason` — so that `withdrawal_reason`, `referred_back_reason`, `outcome_note`, `determination_note` and `cure_note` each admit 2,000 characters, a `settlement_reference` 200, and a reversal's `reason` 500. Only an identifier supplied in the body is unbounded in length. And a decimal carrying more places than its field admits — `indexation_pct` beyond six, `deduction_cap_pct` beyond four — is refused here rather than silently rounded.

### 2.10. Resource Addressing and Reverse Reachability

The surface is flat. Every resource is readable at exactly one level under the prefix, and no resource's id is ever nested inside another resource's id in a read path. Creation, by contrast, is addressed under the owning parent so that the parent context is unambiguous — a service period is created under its concession, a performance report under its service period, a dispute under its deduction event — after which the child is a first-class top-level resource addressed by its own id alone. Where a path names more than one id, the ids are resolved left to right and the leftmost unresolvable id determines the error. A trailing slash on any path is not significant. A request the routing layer itself refuses never reaches any of these rules and is reported through the standard error envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carrying the routing status rather than 400 — HTTP 404 for a path outside this surface, HTTP 405 for a method the path does not admit — and the same envelope with the same `error_id` and HTTP 500 is the shape of any unexpected internal failure.

Because several rows are created by cascade rather than by a caller, and no create response ever returns them, the filtered lists of §2.3 are the contractual discovery channel for them: the self-report-understatement deduction event raised at verification, the handback-shortfall deduction event raised at a failed handback survey, the construction-seed and refinancing-sweep reserve entries, and the termination enforcement action appended when a step-in lapses.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Immediate Consistency

Consistency is immediate. Every cascade described in this document completes inside the request that triggered it, and every derived field is readable at its final value in the same response or in an immediately following read. There is no deferred settlement, no queue and no eventual convergence: a concession's `availability_deduction_ytd_cents`, `deduction_points_ytd`, `persistent_breach_flag`, `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents`, `senior_debt_drawn_cents`, `equity_drawn_cents` and `cumulative_drawn_pct`, and a service period's `total_deduction_cents`, `relief_credit_cents` and `net_payment_cents`, are always the exact functions of their inputs that this document defines. Immediacy is paired with all-or-nothing. Every mutating request runs against a snapshot of the entire store taken before its handler begins, and any rejection whatever — at any rung of §2.9, from a state guard or fiscal ceiling reached late in the handler, or from deep inside a cascade the request triggered — restores that snapshot in full before the error envelope is written. A refused request therefore leaves behind no partial row, no half-applied cascade, no `version` bump, no consumed `sequence_no` and no moved clock: the store is byte-for-byte what it was before the request arrived, and the only trace of the attempt is the error response itself.

Four cascades carry consequences across resource boundaries, and each is described where its trigger lives: upholding a dispute or withdrawing a deduction re-prices a period and can clear a persistent breach and cure an active step-in; disbursing a milestone can start construction and seed the lifecycle reserve; settling a termination compensation terminates the concession, adjusts its invoiced periods and winds up its sponsor; and consenting to a refinancing sweeps part of the concessionaire's gain into the lifecycle reserve. Within any single request, where more than one derived figure must be recomputed, the order in which the recomputations run is contractual and is stated at the point of use.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. From Sponsor Registration to Financial Close

A project begins with the sponsor vehicle. A `PlatformAdmin` registers the special-purpose vehicle that will hold the concession by supplying its `legal_name` — a non-empty string of at most 200 characters — and the `sponsor_equity_commitment_cents` the sponsors have committed across all of that vehicle's concessions, which may be zero but never negative. The vehicle is created in `ACTIVE` with a `concessions_count` of 0. That counter is not a free-standing number: it always equals the number of concession rows naming this vehicle, with no filtering by concession state, so a terminated project still counts. A sponsor vehicle travels a short lifecycle of `ACTIVE`, `STEPPED_IN` and `WOUND_UP`, and it never moves under its own power — every transition it makes is propagated to it from an enforcement action against one of its concessions.

Drafting the concession agreement is where the commercial deal is written down, and it may be performed by a `PlatformAdmin` or an `AuthorityOfficer`. The payload names the sponsor vehicle through `concessionaire_id` and carries the `project_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), the Maximum Annual Service Payment as `masp_cents` (strictly positive), the contractual `indexation_pct` uplift as a decimal fraction defaulting to 0 and expressed to at most six decimal places, the per-period `deduction_cap_pct` as a strictly positive fraction of at most four decimal places, the certified `total_project_cost_cents` (strictly positive), the `senior_debt_committed_cents` and `equity_committed_cents` available at financial close, the sponsors' `base_case_equity_irr_bps` return expectation in basis points (at least 0 and at most 5000), the contractual `scheduled_service_commencement` date that will later be the baseline for late delivery, the `ld_daily_rate_cents` at which liquidated damages accrue and the `ld_cap_cents` ceiling on them, and the `lifecycle_reserve_target_cents` balance the reserve is expected to hold during operations. The funding must add up: if `total_project_cost_cents` is greater than `senior_debt_committed_cents` plus `equity_committed_cents`, the draft is refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Otherwise the concession is created in `DRAFTED`, its `handback_status` is `NOT_SURVEYED`, every drawn, deduction and reserve running total is 0, `persistent_breach_flag` is false, `monthly_unitary_charge_cents`, `financial_close_date`, `actual_service_commencement`, `handback_survey_date`, `handback_rectification_cost_cents`, `handback_required_reserve_cents` and `handback_shortfall_cents` are all null, `ld_accrued_cents` is 0, and the sponsor vehicle's `concessions_count` rises by one. A concession's state travels `DRAFTED`, `FINANCIAL_CLOSE`, `CONSTRUCTION`, `OPERATIONS`, possibly `STEP_IN` and back to `OPERATIONS`, and terminally `TERMINATED`.

While — and only while — the concession is still `DRAFTED`, an `AuthorityOfficer` may amend the commercial parameters. The amendment accepts any subset of `masp_cents`, `indexation_pct`, `deduction_cap_pct`, `ld_daily_rate_cents`, `ld_cap_cents` and `lifecycle_reserve_target_cents`. Attempting it on a concession that has left `DRAFTED` is refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409); the restriction exists precisely so that an amendment can never rewrite the charge already frozen onto a live payment period. Amending does not derive a monthly charge — `monthly_unitary_charge_cents` stays null and is derived at financial close from whatever the amended values then are.

Financial close is an `AuthorityOfficer` operation and advances the concession `DRAFTED → FINANCIAL_CLOSE`. It requires the concession to be in `DRAFTED` — any other state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — and re-affirms that committed senior debt plus committed equity is at least the certified total project cost, refusing with `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) if that is not so. The supplied `close_date` is recorded as `financial_close_date`, and closing is the sole producer of `monthly_unitary_charge_cents`.

That charge is derived in a fixed order, and the order is contractual. Take the Maximum Annual Service Payment in cents, divide it by the twelve months of the contract year, multiply the resulting quotient by one plus the indexation fraction, and round that single composed expression half up to a whole cent. There is exactly one rounding step and it comes last. Dividing and rounding to cents *before* applying indexation is the canonical error: it can be a cent lower than the correct figure, and because every service period freezes a copy of this charge and derives its deduction cap from it, that one cent then contaminates the cap, the relief credit and the net payment of every period of the concession for the whole concession term.

### 3.2. Funding the Build: Milestone Drawdown and the Construction Trigger

Once the deal is closed, the project company draws the funding down against construction progress. A `Concessionaire` requests a drawdown by supplying a `milestone_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), the `requested_amount_cents` of cash it wants (strictly positive) and `expected_concession_version`, which must match the concession's current `version` as described in §2.7. The request is only entertained while the concession is in `FINANCIAL_CLOSE` or `CONSTRUCTION`; in any other state it is refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409). It is also frozen while enforcement is live: if any enforcement action of the concession sits in `NOTIFIED`, `STEP_IN_ACTIVE` or `COMPENSATION_CALCULATED`, the request is refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` (HTTP 409). A successful request creates the milestone in `REQUESTED` with its `certified_cost_cents` initialised to 0 rather than to null — no cost has been certified yet, and the figure only becomes meaningful when a certifier writes it — and assigns it a `sequence_no` equal to one more than the highest `sequence_no` any milestone of this concession already holds; numbers are never reused, and this is the only ordering key for replaying the drawdown waterfall. Creating the milestone also advances the concession's own `version` by one, which is what makes a second create against a stale view detectable.

Certification is the `IndependentCertifier`'s monopoly, and it is a fork with two branches whose effects are entirely disjoint. On the approving branch, the certifier supplies the `certified_cost_cents` actually incurred to this milestone — the figure the split is computed from, as distinct from the `requested_amount_cents` the concessionaire asked for — and the milestone advances `REQUESTED → APPROVED` with `approved_at` stamped from the logical clock, `equity_portion_cents` and `debt_portion_cents` written, and `rejection_reason` left null. On the refusing branch the certifier supplies a `rejection_reason` — non-empty and at most 2,000 characters — the milestone advances `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, `approved_at`, `disbursed_at`, `equity_portion_cents` and `debt_portion_cents` are all left null, `certified_cost_cents` is not written at all and so keeps the 0 it was created with, and not one concession figure moves: `senior_debt_drawn_cents`, `equity_drawn_cents` and `cumulative_drawn_pct` are untouched. A rejected milestone is terminal and never contributes to any drawn total. Both branches require the milestone to be in `REQUESTED`, and approval additionally requires the concession to be in `FINANCIAL_CLOSE` or `CONSTRUCTION`.

The split itself is an equity-first waterfall, and the sponsors' money goes in first. The size of the equity bucket is the lower of two independently sourced ceilings: thirty per cent of the certified total project cost, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the equity actually committed at financial close. Whichever of those two is smaller is the equity-first ceiling. The room still left in that bucket is the ceiling less the equity already drawn, floored at zero. This milestone's `equity_portion_cents` is the smaller of its certified cost and that remaining room, and its `debt_portion_cents` is the certified cost less the equity portion — exact integer arithmetic, with the only rounding step being the one on the percentage ceiling. Taking either ceiling alone rather than the lower of the two gives a different split whenever thirty per cent of total project cost and committed equity differ. Approval also tests the debt side against what is left of the senior facility: if the computed debt leg exceeds committed senior debt less senior debt already drawn — or if the split would push either drawn total past its commitment — the approval is refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Disbursement belongs to the `LenderAgent`, and it is the head of the funding cascade. It requires the milestone to be in `APPROVED` and the concession to be in `FINANCIAL_CLOSE` or `CONSTRUCTION`, and it is frozen by a live enforcement action exactly as milestone creation is, with the same `ERR_CONCESSION_UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` (HTTP 409). The milestone advances `APPROVED → DISBURSED` with `disbursed_at` stamped from the logical clock. The concession's `equity_drawn_cents` then rises by this milestone's equity portion and its `senior_debt_drawn_cents` by its debt portion. From those two running totals the concession's `cumulative_drawn_pct` is recomputed as their sum divided by `total_project_cost_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

That recomputed ratio is a threshold trigger. If the concession is still in `FINANCIAL_CLOSE` and the ratio is at least thirty hundredths — the boundary is inclusive, so a ratio of exactly 0.3000 fires — the concession advances `FINANCIAL_CLOSE → CONSTRUCTION` in the same request. The comparison is made against the four-decimal-place rounded ratio, not the raw quotient, so a drawn total whose raw ratio is 0.29995 rounds to 0.3000 and does fire, while one whose raw ratio is 0.29975 rounds to 0.2998 and does not. Because the transition consumes its from-state, it can fire at most once in the life of a concession. Firing it seeds the lifecycle reserve: one ledger entry is appended with `txn_type` of `CONTRIBUTION`, `created_by_role` of `SYSTEM`, a `reason` of `CONSTRUCTION_SEED` and an `amount_cents` equal to two per cent of the certified total project cost rounded half up to a whole cent; the concession's `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents` is then set to that new entry's `resulting_balance_cents`.

Three funding identities hold after every disbursement and are worth stating as the acceptance criteria for the whole drawdown chain. The concession's `equity_drawn_cents` equals the sum of `equity_portion_cents` over its disbursed milestones, and its `senior_debt_drawn_cents` equals the sum of `debt_portion_cents` over the same set; the two together equal the sum of `certified_cost_cents` over those milestones. Drawn equity never exceeds committed equity and drawn senior debt never exceeds committed senior debt. And `cumulative_drawn_pct` always equals the sum of the two drawn totals divided by total project cost, rounded half up to four decimal places. Milestones in `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing to any of these figures; only `DISBURSED` does.

### 3.3. Service Commencement, Late Delivery and the Opening of Payment Periods

Certifying that the service has actually commenced is the second of the `IndependentCertifier`'s three monopolies, and it moves the project out of the build and into the payment stream: the concession advances `CONSTRUCTION → OPERATIONS`. The certifier supplies an `actual_commencement_date`, which is recorded on the concession as `actual_service_commencement`. Three conditions guard the certification. The concession must be in `CONSTRUCTION`, and any other state yields `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409). The concession's `actual_service_commencement` must still be null, so commencement can be certified exactly once. And the supplied date must fall on or after the concession's `financial_close_date`; an earlier date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422).

Certification is also the moment liquidated damages for late delivery crystallise into `ld_accrued_cents`. Count the whole calendar days from the contractual `scheduled_service_commencement` to the certified actual commencement; if the project delivered early, that count is treated as zero rather than as a negative number. From that gross figure subtract the excused days, which are the sum of `granted_relief_days` over this concession's relief claims of `claim_type` `RELIEF_EVENT` that have reached `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED` — compensation-event and change-in-law claims are not excuses for late delivery, and claims still in `NOTIFIED` or already `REJECTED` excuse nothing. The remainder, floored at zero, is the net late-day count. Multiply the net late days by the contractual `ld_daily_rate_cents` and then take the lower of that product and `ld_cap_cents`. The ceiling applies to the money, never to the day count: twenty-one net late days at the contract rate may well exceed the cap, in which case exactly the cap accrues, but the day count itself is not capped first.

With the concession in `OPERATIONS`, an `AuthorityOfficer` opens the monthly payment periods. Each period is created with a `period_index` (at least 1, the contractual period number), a `period_start` and a `period_end`, and it is opened only while the concession is in `OPERATIONS` or `STEP_IN` — otherwise `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409). The interval is half-open, as §2.6 sets out, and three checks apply in the order given in §2.9. The interval must be valid: `period_start` strictly before `period_end`, with `days_in_period` equal to the whole-day span of the half-open interval and at least 1, failing which the response is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422); because that span is counted in whole calendar days rather than in elapsed hours, a `period_start` and `period_end` that fall on the same calendar day span zero days and are refused with that same error even though the two instants are strictly ordered. The interval must not intersect any existing period of the same concession, failing which it is `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409); adjacency is explicitly permitted, so a period beginning exactly at the previous period's `period_end` does not intersect it and is accepted. And the `period_index` must be unique within the concession, failing which it is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD_INDEX` (HTTP 409).

Opening the period freezes the commercial terms onto it, which is why amendment is confined to drafting. The period's `gross_unitary_charge_cents` is a frozen copy of the concession's `monthly_unitary_charge_cents` as it stands at that moment, and its `deduction_cap_cents` is that frozen gross charge multiplied by the concession's `deduction_cap_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, also frozen. Neither figure is ever restated: a later indexation change does not rewrite a period already opened. The period starts life with `reporting_status` `PENDING` and `payment_status` `UNBILLED`, with `unavailable_minutes_verified` and `availability_ratio` null, with `deduction_points_total`, `total_deduction_cents` and `relief_credit_cents` at 0, and with `verified_at`, `invoiced_at`, `paid_at` and `payment_reference` null. Because a fresh period has neither deductions nor relief credit, its `net_payment_cents` starts equal to its frozen gross charge; the figure is first properly settled at verification.

Those two status fields are two independent axes on the same row. The performance-monitoring axis runs `PENDING → SELF_REPORTED → VERIFIED`, with a revert edge back to `PENDING`, and it moves only under the reporting and verification operations. The payment axis runs `UNBILLED → INVOICED → PAID → ADJUSTED` and moves only under the invoicing and payment operations and the adjustment cascades. Neither axis gates the other, with exactly one exception, stated in §3.8: invoicing requires the reporting axis to have reached `VERIFIED`. Otherwise the two are independent — a period may sit at `VERIFIED` while still `UNBILLED`, and a transition on one axis never reads or writes the other axis's field.

### 3.4. Self-Reported Performance and the Authority's Verification

Performance monitoring in an availability-payment contract is self-reporting with verification, and the API models both halves. The `Concessionaire` reports a period's performance by supplying the `unavailable_minutes` during which the asset failed the availability definition, the `category_a_failures` count and the `category_b_failures` count — each at least zero — together with `expected_service_period_version` as described in §2.7. Reporting requires the period's reporting axis to be in `PENDING` or `SELF_REPORTED` — any other value, `VERIFIED` included, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — and the concession to be in `OPERATIONS`, failing which it is `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409).

A report is a durable row of its own, linked to the period through its `service_period_id`, stamped with `submitted_at` from the logical clock and created in status `SUBMITTED`. Its `reported_points` are computed from the three self-reported counts by a weighted score: two points for every unavailable minute, forty points for every Category A performance-standard failure and ten points for every Category B failure, added together. The arithmetic is exact integers, with no rounding step anywhere, so no drift is possible.

Reporting a second time is not an error — it is a supersede-in-place resubmission. Whichever report of the period was still `SUBMITTED` transitions `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED`, changing nothing but its status, `updated_at` and `version`; its `reported_points` are deliberately preserved for audit. The new report becomes the incumbent, so at most one report per period ever holds `SUBMITTED`. On the reporting axis, the first report moves the period `PENDING → SELF_REPORTED`; a resubmission is the self-loop `SELF_REPORTED → SELF_REPORTED`. Either way, the period's own `version` advances by one, which is exactly what makes the stale-version check of §2.7 bite on a client that resubmits against a view it has not refreshed.

An `AuthorityOfficer` who finds a return unacceptable may reject it rather than verify it, supplying a `rejection_reason` — non-empty and at most 2,000 characters — that is recorded on the report as it transitions `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. Rejection requires the target report to be in `SUBMITTED` and the period's reporting axis to be in `SELF_REPORTED`. What happens to the period afterwards depends on what is left: the axis reverts `SELF_REPORTED → PENDING` only when no other report of that period is still `SUBMITTED`, and otherwise the axis stays `SELF_REPORTED`. The period's `version` advances by one either way. This revert edge is what closes the reporting cycle, and a rejected report never returns to `SUBMITTED` — the concessionaire must file a new one.

Verification is the authority's own count set against the concessionaire's. An `AuthorityOfficer` supplies `verified_unavailable_minutes`, `verified_category_a_failures` and `verified_category_b_failures`. The period's reporting axis must be in `SELF_REPORTED`, the concession must be in `OPERATIONS`, and exactly one report of the period must hold `SUBMITTED` — zero such reports, or more than one, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Verification is also the first half of a separated pair: an officer whose `X-User-ID` matches the `issued_by_user_id` of any deduction event already attached to the period may not verify it, and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The verified minutes are recorded as `unavailable_minutes_verified`, and from them the period's `availability_ratio` is derived: the total minutes in the period are its `days_in_period` multiplied by the 1,440 minutes in a day, and the ratio is one minus the share of those minutes that were unavailable, composed in full and then rounded half up to four decimal places in a single step at the end. The verified minute count is not capped against the minutes available in the period, so a verified count larger than that total yields a negative ratio, which is published exactly as computed rather than clamped at zero. The ratio is a performance statistic reported alongside the money and is entirely independent of it.

The verifier's three counts are then scored with the same weighting used for the self-report — two points a minute, forty a Category A failure, ten a Category B failure — to give a verified point total, and that total is compared with the incumbent report's `reported_points` to produce the report's `variance_pct`. When the reported figure is greater than zero, the variance is the verified total minus the reported total, divided by the reported total, rounded half up to four decimal places. When the reported figure is zero, the variance is exactly 0 if the verified total is also zero, and exactly 1 otherwise. That variance is written only onto the report that was `SUBMITTED`, as it transitions `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`; reports in `SUPERSEDED` or `REJECTED` keep a null `variance_pct` forever.

Understatement is penalised, and the tolerance boundary is strict. If `variance_pct` is greater than five hundredths — a variance of exactly 0.0500 is within tolerance and triggers nothing — the system raises a deduction event of its own against the period. Its `deduction_type` is `SELF_REPORT_UNDERSTATEMENT`, its `issued_by_user_id` is the literal string `SYSTEM`, its `performance_report_id` points at the report that understated, and its `points` are the point *delta* — verified total less reported total — multiplied by one plus a twenty-five per cent uplift, rounded half up to a whole point. The uplift is applied to the delta, never to the verified total: a report that under-declared twenty points yields twenty-five penalty points, not a fraction of the whole return. This event is only reachable afterwards through the filtered deduction-event list, since the verification response returns the period rather than the event.

Verification then settles the period's money, and the order of the three steps is contractual: the period's deduction allocation is recomputed first — both variants of it — then the relief credit, and only then the net payment. Immediately afterwards the annual deduction cap is tested and the concession's persistent-breach position is re-evaluated, as §3.5 describes, and the concession's `availability_deduction_ytd_cents`, `deduction_points_ytd` and `persistent_breach_flag` are refreshed because this period has now entered the verified input set. The sequence inside that is pinned as well: once the cap gate has passed, the reporting axis advances `SELF_REPORTED → VERIFIED` and `verified_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and only after that advance are the three concession totals recomputed. That order is load-bearing rather than cosmetic, because the year-to-date sum reads only those periods whose reporting axis has already reached `VERIFIED`: refreshing the totals before the advance would leave this very period's deduction out of them.

### 3.5. Deduction Events, the Dual Cap Allocation and the Points Regime

Deductions are the mechanism by which poor availability and performance failures reduce the monthly payment. An `AuthorityOfficer` issues one against a period by supplying its `deduction_type` and its `points`, which must be strictly positive, plus optionally a `performance_report_id` when the deduction is evidenced by a particular return. Four regimes exist: `UNAVAILABILITY` and `PERFORMANCE_FAILURE`, which an officer issues with a point count of their own choosing, and `SELF_REPORT_UNDERSTATEMENT` and `HANDBACK_SHORTFALL`, which are system-only — the system alone raises them, at verification and at a failed handback survey respectively, and it computes their point counts itself. The enumeration an officer's request body is validated against nevertheless admits all four values, so naming either system-only regime passes the enumeration check and is then refused at that same schema rung with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); because the refusal is taken after `points` has been validated, a request that names a system-only regime *and* a non-positive `points` is reported against `points`. Issuing requires the concession to be in `OPERATIONS` or `STEP_IN`, and any other state is `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409). Nothing at all is required of the period's own two status axes: a deduction may be issued against a period still `PENDING` or `SELF_REPORTED` exactly as against one already `VERIFIED`, `INVOICED` or `PAID`. If a `performance_report_id` is supplied it must resolve to a real report — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — and that report must belong to the very period the deduction is being attached to, failing which the response is `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

A new event is created in status `ISSUED`, stamped with `issued_at` from the logical clock and with `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, its `withdrawal_reason` null, and its `sequence_no` set to one more than the highest `sequence_no` any event of that period already holds. Numbers are never reused, and this is the only ordering key both for the sequential waterfall and for the pro-rata remainder tie-break. Its `raw_amount_cents` is its point count multiplied by the contractual point value of 1,250 cents — twelve dollars fifty a point — as exact integer arithmetic with no rounding. A deduction event's own lifecycle is `ISSUED`, from which it may become `DISPUTED`, and terminally either `OVERTURNED` or `WITHDRAWN`.

The period's deduction position is then recomputed from scratch, and the input set for that recomputation is precise: the period's events whose status is `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED`, taken in ascending `sequence_no`. Events in `OVERTURNED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded, which is exactly why the whole allocation re-runs when a dispute is upheld or an event withdrawn. If that set is empty, the raw total is zero, every allocation figure is zero and the period's `total_deduction_cents` is zero. The period's `deduction_points_total` is the sum of `points` over that same set.

Two allocations are computed on every run, and both are persisted, because the authority publishes an audit shadow alongside the contractual figure. Both start from the same two inputs: the period's frozen `deduction_cap_cents` and the raw total, being the sum of `raw_amount_cents` over the input set.

The contractual allocation is a chronological waterfall and lands in each event's `allowed_amount_cents`. Begin with headroom equal to the cap. Walk the events in ascending `sequence_no`; each event is allowed the lesser of its own raw amount and the headroom that remains, and the headroom is then reduced by what it was allowed. Earlier events are therefore paid in full, the single event that straddles the cap absorbs the truncation, and every event after that one is allowed zero. The period's `total_deduction_cents` is the sum of `allowed_amount_cents` over the input set, and it is this figure — never the shadow — that feeds the payment. No rounding occurs anywhere in this variant. The result is entirely order-sensitive: feeding the same three raw amounts in the reverse sequence produces a different per-event allocation, which is why `sequence_no` and not `id` or arrival order governs the walk.

The audit shadow is a pro-rata allocation and lands in each event's `pro_rata_amount_cents`. If the raw total is at or below the cap, no scaling is needed and each event's pro-rata figure equals its own raw amount. If the raw total exceeds the cap, each event's share is its raw amount multiplied by the cap and divided by the raw total, **floored** to a whole cent. Flooring loses a residue: the cap less the sum of the floored shares. That residue is distributed one cent at a time — a single cent to each event in turn, wrapping around and starting again if the residue exceeds the number of events — over the events ordered by `raw_amount_cents` descending and, where two events share a raw amount, by `sequence_no` ascending. Unlike the waterfall, this variant truncates no event to zero and leaves no event at its full raw amount; on identical inputs it deliberately gives a different per-event answer while summing to the same total. It is surfaced by the concession's payment summary and never feeds `net_payment_cents`.

With both allocations sealed, the period's relief credit is computed as §3.7 describes and the net payment follows. The four steps are pinned and must run in this order: cap the event allocation; total the allowed amounts into `total_deduction_cents`; compute `relief_credit_cents`; and only then set `net_payment_cents` to the frozen gross charge less the excess of the total deduction over the relief credit, where that excess is floored at zero. The relief credit offsets the **capped** deduction, never the raw deduction. Applying the credit before the cap is the canonical error, because it can pull a raw total that exceeded the cap down under it, so the cap never bites and the concessionaire is paid more than the contract allows. Two invariants close the arithmetic: the net payment is never negative and never exceeds the frozen gross charge, and the period's total deduction never exceeds the period's frozen cap. The pro-rata shadow satisfies its own identity — the sum of `pro_rata_amount_cents` over the input set equals the lower of the raw total and the cap — and the whole chain is reversible: issuing an event and then withdrawing it returns both variant totals and the net payment to their exact pre-issue values.

Two concession-level gates then fire. The **annual deduction cap** is derived as the concession's `masp_cents` multiplied by ten per cent, rounded half up to a whole cent. The projected year-to-date figure is the sum of `total_deduction_cents` over the concession's periods whose reporting axis has reached `VERIFIED`, taken once the period in hand has been re-priced. At verification that period is still `SELF_REPORTED` and so sits outside that sum, which makes the projection the concession's current `availability_deduction_ytd_cents` plus the period's freshly recomputed `total_deduction_cents`. On an officer's issue the sum is instead recomputed in place over the periods that are already `VERIFIED`, so it reaches the newly issued deduction only where the period charged has itself already reached `VERIFIED`: an issue against a period still `PENDING` or `SELF_REPORTED` projects a figure that does not include the new charge at all, and the cap therefore cannot bite on that charge until the period is verified and the gate sees it. The comparison is strict: if the projection is greater than the annual cap the write is refused with `ERR_ANNUAL_DEDUCTION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), and landing exactly on the cap is permitted. Otherwise `availability_deduction_ytd_cents` takes the projected value; equivalently, it is the sum of `total_deduction_cents` over the concession's periods whose reporting axis has reached `VERIFIED`. This gate is evaluated at verification — after the net payment has been computed and before the period is allowed to leave `SELF_REPORTED` — and again on every officer-issued deduction whatever the charged period's reporting status, so an officer's deduction that arrives after verification re-enters the same gate rather than slipping past it; the system-raised handback-shortfall charge of §3.9 is the one deduction never tested against this cap.

The **persistent-breach** evaluation follows. The concession's `deduction_points_ytd` is the sum of `points` over every deduction event of every one of its periods whose status is `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED`, and `persistent_breach_flag` is true exactly when that total is at least 500 points — the boundary is inclusive, so exactly 500 points does set the flag while 499 does not. The flag is recomputed, never latched. Withdrawing an event or having one overturned can drop the total back below the threshold and flip the flag from true to false, and that flip is itself a trigger: it cures an open persistent-breach step-in, as §3.11 sets out.

An officer who accepts that a deduction was wrongly issued withdraws it, supplying a `withdrawal_reason`. Withdrawal is the compensating action for issue and is admitted from either `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED` — any other status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — and, unlike issue, it is gated on no concession state at all, so a wrongly issued deduction may still be withdrawn once the concession has reached `TERMINATED`; the event lands in `WITHDRAWN` with both `allowed_amount_cents` and `pro_rata_amount_cents` set to zero, and it thereby leaves the allocation input set. If the event was `DISPUTED`, the open dispute against it — whichever of `RAISED`, `REFERRED_BACK` or `RESUBMITTED` it was sitting in — is closed to `UPHELD` with an `outcome_note` of `WITHDRAWN_BY_AUTHORITY` and an `adjudicated_by_user_id` of `SYSTEM`. The owning period then re-runs the allocation, the relief credit and the net payment in that pinned order, and the concession's `availability_deduction_ytd_cents`, `deduction_points_ytd` and `persistent_breach_flag` are all recomputed. If the period had already been paid, the money it was paid is now wrong, so the payment axis moves `PAID → ADJUSTED`; that adjustment is taken only when the recomputed `total_deduction_cents` actually differs from the figure in force when `paid_at` was stamped.

### 3.6. Disputing a Deduction: the Bounded Referral Cycle

The concessionaire's remedy against a deduction is a dispute, and the dispute machinery is a bounded cycle rather than a single exchange. A `Concessionaire` raises one against a deduction event by supplying its `grounds` — non-empty and at most 2,000 characters. The event must be in `ISSUED`: an event already `WITHDRAWN` or `OVERTURNED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), while an event sitting in `DISPUTED` with a dispute still open against it is refused at that same state rung with the narrower `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409) — reported there, ahead of the period check below, rather than waiting for the uniqueness rung of §2.9. The period that owns the event must also have reached `VERIFIED` on its reporting axis, since there is nothing to argue about before the authority has verified the period; a dispute raised earlier is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). At most one dispute per event may be open — that is, sitting in `RAISED`, `REFERRED_BACK` or `RESUBMITTED` — and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409). A successful raise creates the dispute in `RAISED` with `round_no` 1, `raised_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `referred_back_reason`, `adjudicated_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_at` and `outcome_note` all null, and moves the event `ISSUED → DISPUTED`. Crucially, no money moves: a disputed event remains inside the allocation input set, so `total_deduction_cents` and `net_payment_cents` are untouched by the act of disputing. Neither raising a dispute nor any of the three operations that follow it is gated on the concession's `status` or frozen by a live enforcement action: the whole dispute machinery runs against a concession in any state, a `TERMINATED` one included.

An `AuthorityOfficer` who wants more evidence refers the dispute back, supplying a `referred_back_reason` that is recorded — and overwritten on each subsequent referral. A referral is admitted from `RAISED` or from `RESUBMITTED`, both landing in `REFERRED_BACK`, and it does **not** consume a round: `round_no` is unchanged. The disputed event stays `DISPUTED` throughout. The concessionaire answers a referral by resubmitting with fresh `grounds`, which replace the previous grounds on the row; that moves the dispute `REFERRED_BACK → RESUBMITTED` and increments `round_no` by exactly one. The cycle `REFERRED_BACK → RESUBMITTED → REFERRED_BACK` may therefore repeat, but it is budgeted: neither a referral nor a resubmission may act on a dispute whose `round_no` has already reached three, so the referral loop admits exactly three submissions in total and must then be closed by adjudication. An attempt beyond that budget is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409).

Adjudication is the terminal act, and it is a fork. An `AuthorityOfficer` supplies a `decision` of either `UPHOLD` or `REJECT` together with an `outcome_note`, and may adjudicate a dispute sitting in `RAISED`, `REFERRED_BACK` or `RESUBMITTED`. The officer must not be the one who issued the deduction under dispute: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the event's `issued_by_user_id`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Either decision records `adjudicated_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_at` and `outcome_note`.

Upholding the dispute means the concessionaire has won, and it is the head of the longest cascade in the system. The dispute lands in `UPHELD` and the deduction event lands in `OVERTURNED` — note that the two rows use different words for the same outcome — with `allowed_amount_cents` and `pro_rata_amount_cents` both set to zero, so the event leaves the allocation input set. The owning period then re-runs its allocation, its relief credit and its net payment in that pinned order, and a period that had already been paid moves `PAID → ADJUSTED` because the sum it was paid is no longer the sum the contract prescribes. The concession's `availability_deduction_ytd_cents`, `deduction_points_ytd` and `persistent_breach_flag` are recomputed next. And if that recomputation flips `persistent_breach_flag` from true to false, an enforcement action of `trigger_reason` `PERSISTENT_BREACH` that is sitting in `STEP_IN_ACTIVE` is cured in the same request: it moves `STEP_IN_ACTIVE → CURED` with `cure_note` set to `PERSISTENT_BREACH_CLEARED` and `cured_at` stamped, the concession returns `STEP_IN → OPERATIONS`, and the sponsor vehicle returns `STEPPED_IN → ACTIVE`. A single adjudication can therefore reach five rows.

Rejecting the dispute is deliberately inert by comparison. The dispute lands in `REJECTED` and the deduction event returns `DISPUTED → ISSUED`. Because the event never left the allocation input set, `allowed_amount_cents` and `pro_rata_amount_cents` are unchanged, nothing about the period is recomputed, and no concession figure moves — no `total_deduction_cents`, no `net_payment_cents`, no year-to-date total, no breach flag, and no payment-axis adjustment.

### 3.7. Relief Events, Compensation Events and Change in Law

Long concessions need escape valves, and this contract has three, distinguished by `claim_type`. A `RELIEF_EVENT` excuses late delivery. A `COMPENSATION_EVENT` compensates the concessionaire for days on which the authority or a third party prevented performance, and it does so by crediting the monthly payment. A `CHANGE_IN_LAW` claim compensates in money alone. A `Concessionaire` notifies a claim by supplying the `claim_type`, the `event_start` and `event_end` of the claimed window, the `claimed_relief_days` it seeks and the `claimed_cost_cents` it seeks; both claimed figures may be zero but never negative. Notification is gated on no concession state and is not frozen by a live enforcement action, so a claim may be notified from drafting onwards and after termination alike. The claim window is a **closed** interval, so both endpoint days are part of the event. A window whose `event_start` falls after its `event_end` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422).

Notification is time-limited. The claim's `notified_at` is stamped from the logical clock at creation, and the whole calendar days between the claimed `event_start` and that instant must be at most twenty. Exactly twenty days after the event began is still in time; twenty-one days is `ERR_RELIEF_NOTIFICATION_LATE` (HTTP 422). Notifying before the event has begun yields a negative span, which is treated as zero and accepted.

Claims of the same type may not crowd each other. Each claim's closed window is expanded by three calendar days on each side, and a new claim is refused with `ERR_RELIEF_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) if its expanded window intersects the expanded window of any existing claim of the **same** `claim_type` that is still live — that is, sitting in `NOTIFIED`, `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`. Touching at a single day counts as intersecting, because the intervals are closed. In practice this means two claims of the same type need at least six clear calendar days between the earlier window's end and the later window's start: five clear days conflicts, six does not. Claims of different types never conflict with each other, and a claim that has been `REJECTED` releases its window entirely.

A notified claim starts in `NOTIFIED` with `granted_relief_days` and `granted_cost_cents` at 0 and with `determined_at` and `determination_note` null. Determination is the `AuthorityOfficer`'s, requires the claim to be in `NOTIFIED`, and takes a `decision` of `GRANT` or `REJECT` together with the optional `granted_relief_days`, `granted_cost_cents` and `determination_note`, each of which carries a documented default for §2.9's null-counts-as-absent rule to fall back on: the two granted figures default to 0 and the note to null. Two fiscal gates guard a grant, and their order matters: the relief-day annual budget is tested first, and only then the grant-versus-claim ceiling. The budget is the sum of `granted_relief_days` over this concession's claims already in `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED` — every one of them, whatever its `claim_type`, since a compensation event and a change in law consume the same annual day budget as a relief event — plus the days being granted now, and it may not exceed sixty days in the year; exceeding it is `ERR_RELIEF_DAYS_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The ceiling is that a determination may never grant more than was claimed: `granted_relief_days` may not exceed `claimed_relief_days` and `granted_cost_cents` may not exceed `claimed_cost_cents`, and a breach of either is `ERR_GRANT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM` (HTTP 422).

The outcome state is read off the granted figures rather than declared. A `GRANT` in which both granted figures equal their claimed counterparts lands the claim in `GRANTED`; a `GRANT` in which at least one granted figure is strictly lower than what was claimed lands it in `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`. The two outcomes are otherwise identical in every effect — only the label differs. A `REJECT` lands the claim in `REJECTED` and forces `granted_relief_days` and `granted_cost_cents` to 0 whatever the request body said; a rejected claim never enters the relief-credit input set and never excuses a late-delivery day, so no period is recomputed and the concession's `ld_accrued_cents` is left exactly as it stands. Every determination stamps `determined_at` from the logical clock and records the `determination_note`.

What a grant then does depends on the head of claim. A granted `COMPENSATION_EVENT` immediately re-prices every service period of the concession whose half-open interval intersects the claim's closed window: each such period re-runs its relief credit and then its net payment, in that order, and any of them that had already been paid moves `PAID → ADJUSTED`. A granted `RELIEF_EVENT` recomputes nothing at the time — while the concession is still in `CONSTRUCTION` there is no payment period to credit — and its granted days instead sit waiting to be netted off the late-delivery day count when service commencement is certified, as §3.3 describes. A granted `CHANGE_IN_LAW` claim contributes its `granted_cost_cents` and nothing else; it neither credits a period nor excuses a late day.

The relief credit itself is a day-count proportion of the period's frozen charge, and the day counting is where the two interval conventions meet. For each granted compensation-event claim of the concession, take the distinct calendar days that lie both inside the claim's closed window and inside the period's half-open interval — days on or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. Then **merge across claims before counting**: a calendar day covered by two granted claims is credited once, not twice, so the credited day count is the size of the union of those clipped day sets and never the sum of their sizes. The credited count is then capped at the period's own `days_in_period`. Finally, and with exactly one rounding step, the period's `relief_credit_cents` is its frozen `gross_unitary_charge_cents` multiplied by the credited day count and divided by `days_in_period`, rounded half up to a whole cent. A period with no credited days has a relief credit of exactly zero, with no rounding performed at all. Claims of type `RELIEF_EVENT` or `CHANGE_IN_LAW` are excluded from this input set, as are claims still in `NOTIFIED` or already `REJECTED`.

### 3.8. Invoicing, Payment and Post-Payment Adjustment

The payment axis is the concessionaire's route to being paid, and it is deliberately short. The `Concessionaire` invoices a period — no payload is required — and the period moves `UNBILLED → INVOICED` with `invoiced_at` stamped from the logical clock. This is the one place where the two axes touch: invoicing requires the period's reporting axis to have reached `VERIFIED`, and attempting it on a period still in `PENDING` or `SELF_REPORTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Invoicing also requires the concession to be in `OPERATIONS`.

An `AuthorityOfficer` then records payment against the invoice, supplying the authority's `payment_reference` — non-empty and at most 200 characters — which is echoed onto the period as the period moves `INVOICED → PAID` with `paid_at` stamped from the logical clock. Payment likewise requires the concession to be in `OPERATIONS`, and the period must be in `INVOICED`. The `payment_reference` is null on any period whose payment axis has not reached `PAID` or `ADJUSTED`.

`ADJUSTED` is the terminal state of the payment axis and is reached in two quite different ways, neither of which a caller requests directly. The first is a re-pricing after the fact: when a dispute is upheld, when a deduction is withdrawn, or when a compensation event is granted over a window touching the period, the period's money is recomputed, and a period already in `PAID` moves `PAID → ADJUSTED`. Those three are the only re-pricing routes into `ADJUSTED`: issuing a *fresh* deduction event against a period already sitting at `PAID` re-prices that period just as fully and yet leaves its payment axis at `PAID`, as does the handback-shortfall charge of §3.9 — the move belongs to the compensating actions and to the compensation-event credit, never to a new charge. For the two deduction routes — an upheld dispute and a withdrawal — that move is taken only where the recomputed `total_deduction_cents` genuinely differs from the figure in force when `paid_at` was stamped; a granted compensation event, by contrast, moves the period unconditionally, because what its re-pricing moves is the relief credit rather than the deduction total and that comparison would therefore never fire. The second is termination: when a concession is terminated on settlement of a compensation, every period of it still sitting in `INVOICED` moves `INVOICED → ADJUSTED` and has its `net_payment_cents` frozen at whatever value it then holds, with no further recomputation ever performed on that period.

Alongside the per-period figures, the concession exposes a derived payment summary that writes no row at all. It reports the concession's own id as `concession_id`, its `monthly_unitary_charge_cents`, its `availability_deduction_ytd_cents`, the derived `annual_cap_cents` (the Maximum Annual Service Payment at ten per cent, rounded half up to a whole cent), its `deduction_points_ytd` and `persistent_breach_flag`, its `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents`, a `sequential_total_cents` figure being the sum of `total_deduction_cents` over every period whose reporting axis has reached `VERIFIED`, the matching `pro_rata_total_cents` audit shadow being the sum of `pro_rata_amount_cents` over those same periods' deduction events, and `unpaid_invoiced_cents`, the sum of `net_payment_cents` over the concession's periods still sitting in `INVOICED`. Publishing the two totals side by side is the whole point of computing both allocation variants: they sum to the same capped figure while attributing it differently across events.

### 3.9. The Lifecycle Reserve Ledger and the Handback Condition

The lifecycle reserve is the fund from which major maintenance and end-of-term renewal are paid, and the contract obliges the concessionaire to maintain it against the `lifecycle_reserve_target_cents` recorded on the concession at drafting. The API models it as a strict append-only ledger, for the reason set out in §2.4: an auditor must be able to replay it, so nothing in it may ever be rewritten.

A `Concessionaire` appends an entry by supplying its `txn_type`, a strictly positive `amount_cents` and a `reason` — non-empty, at most 500 characters — recorded for audit. Three kinds may be appended this way: a `CONTRIBUTION` paying money in, a `WITHDRAWAL` taking money out, and a `HANDBACK_TOPUP` paying money in specifically to close a handback shortfall. The fourth kind, `REVERSAL`, may not be appended this way at all; an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), because reversals go through their own dedicated operation. Each entry receives a `sequence_no` equal to one more than the highest `sequence_no` any entry of that concession already holds, and a `created_by_role` recording the `X-User-Role` that appended it — or the literal string `SYSTEM` where a cascade appended it. Its `reverses_txn_id` must be null unless it is a reversal. Appending is gated on no concession state and is not frozen by a live enforcement action: the ledger may be funded from drafting onwards and stays appendable after the concession has reached `TERMINATED`.

From the type and the magnitude comes the entry's `signed_amount_cents`: positive for a `CONTRIBUTION` and for a `HANDBACK_TOPUP`, negative for a `WITHDRAWAL`, and for a `REVERSAL` the exact arithmetic negation of the signed amount of the entry it reverses. The entry's `resulting_balance_cents` is the sum of `signed_amount_cents` over the entries of the same concession whose `sequence_no` is less than or equal to its own, and the concession's `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents` is set to the `resulting_balance_cents` of the entry with the highest `sequence_no`. Replay order is `sequence_no` ascending — with `created_at` ascending as the documented fallback — and never `id`. There is no rounding anywhere in the ledger.

Withdrawals are guarded twice. A `WITHDRAWAL` may not drive `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents` below zero, and no `WITHDRAWAL` at all is permitted once the concession's `handback_status` has left `NOT_SURVEYED` — that is, once the handback survey has been recorded, whether it certified or failed. Either breach is `ERR_RESERVE_WITHDRAWAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409).

Correcting a mistaken entry is a `PlatformAdmin` privilege and is done by appending a compensating reversal with a `reason` of its own. The new entry carries `txn_type` `REVERSAL`, its `reverses_txn_id` pointing at the target entry, an `amount_cents` copied from the target rather than taken from the request, and a `signed_amount_cents` that is the target's signed amount negated. Reversing a `WITHDRAWAL` therefore adds money back, and reversing a `CONTRIBUTION` removes it. The concession's `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents` is then set to the new entry's `resulting_balance_cents`, which — where the reversed entry was the latest — is the balance that stood before it. Two refusals apply, tested in this order: a target that already has a reversal pointing at it gives `ERR_TRANSACTION_ALREADY_REVERSED` (HTTP 409), and a target that is itself a `REVERSAL` gives `ERR_CANNOT_REVERSE_REVERSAL` (HTTP 409). No cross-entity refusal arises on this operation, because the flat path of §2.10 names the target entry alone and the concession whose ledger is being corrected is derived from that entry: there is no second identifier that could disagree with it. Each row may be reversed at most once. Reversal, like the plain append, is gated on no concession state.

Two identities hold over the ledger after every append and every reversal. The concession's `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents` equals the sum of `signed_amount_cents` over all of its entries, and equals the `resulting_balance_cents` of its highest-`sequence_no` entry. And for every entry individually, its `resulting_balance_cents` equals the sum of `signed_amount_cents` over the entries with `sequence_no` at or below its own. The compensating pair — a withdrawal followed by its reversal — demonstrates both: the ledger grows by two rows, the balance returns exactly to its pre-withdrawal value, and not one existing row is mutated. Any drift here is a defect, not a rounding artefact, because no rounding exists in this arithmetic.

At the end of the concession term the asset must be handed back in contract condition, and the third of the `IndependentCertifier`'s monopolies is the survey that determines whether it is. The certifier supplies the `handback_survey_date` and the assessed `handback_rectification_cost_cents`, both of which are recorded on the concession. The survey requires the concession to be in `OPERATIONS`, otherwise `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409), and it is frozen by a live enforcement action with `ERR_CONCESSION_UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` (HTTP 409). The concession's own `handback_status` is not itself a guard: a concession already `CERTIFIED` or `FAILED` may be surveyed again, and the later survey simply overwrites all four handback fields from its own inputs and, where it fails again, raises a further shortfall event of its own.

The requirement is then sized in a **single pass**, and this is the most easily mis-implemented calculation in the contract. The base requirement is the assessed rectification cost. The pre-penalty shortfall is that base requirement less the current `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents`, floored at zero. The penalty is fifteen per cent of that pre-penalty shortfall, rounded half up to a whole cent. The concession's `handback_required_reserve_cents` is the base requirement plus that penalty, and its `handback_shortfall_cents` is the required reserve less the current balance, floored at zero. The penalty is sized from the **pre-penalty** shortfall and is never re-based on the larger shortfall it creates: iterating this calculation to a fixed point, so that each new shortfall feeds a new penalty, is the canonical wrong answer and converges on a materially larger figure.

The concession's `handback_status` is then `CERTIFIED` when `handback_shortfall_cents` is exactly zero and `FAILED` otherwise. A failure is not merely recorded — it is charged. The system appends one deduction event of `deduction_type` `HANDBACK_SHORTFALL` against the concession's most recent verified service period, meaning the one with the highest `period_index` among those whose reporting axis has reached `VERIFIED`, sized so that its raw amount reproduces the shortfall exactly: its `points` are the shortfall divided by the 1,250-cent point value, rounded half up to a whole point, its `issued_by_user_id` is the literal `SYSTEM` and its `performance_report_id` is null, since no self-reported return evidences it. That period then re-runs its allocation, relief credit and net payment in the pinned order of §3.5, where its own frozen `deduction_cap_cents` may well truncate the charge, and the concession's `availability_deduction_ytd_cents`, `deduction_points_ytd` and `persistent_breach_flag` are recomputed straight afterwards. This cascade is deliberately narrower than the one at verification in two respects: the charge is **not** tested against the annual deduction cap of §3.5, so a handback shortfall may carry the year-to-date total past that cap, and a period already sitting at `PAID` is **not** moved to `ADJUSTED` by it. And where the concession has no verified service period at all, there is nothing to charge against: the survey still records `FAILED` with its required reserve and its shortfall, and no deduction event is raised.

### 3.10. Refinancing and Sharing the Gain with the Authority

When market conditions improve, the project company can refinance its senior debt and release cash. The contract entitles the public authority to a share of that gain, and the share rises in bands. Either commercial party — a `Concessionaire` or an `AuthorityOfficer` — may propose a refinancing by supplying its proposed `refinancing_date`, the `new_senior_debt_cents` face amount of the replacement facility, the `outstanding_senior_debt_cents` immediately before the refinancing, and the `refinancing_cost_cents` of transaction costs. The proposal requires the concession to be in `OPERATIONS`, otherwise `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409), and is frozen by a live enforcement action with `ERR_CONCESSION_UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` (HTTP 409). The row is created in `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, with `consented_by_user_id` and `consented_at` null and with `reserve_sweep_cents` at 0.

The whole share block is computed at proposal time and frozen thereafter. The `gross_gain_cents` is the new senior debt less the outstanding senior debt less the transaction costs. There must be a real gain: the new facility must be strictly greater than the outstanding debt, and the resulting gross gain must be strictly positive. A proposal that fails either test is refused with `ERR_NO_REFINANCING_GAIN` (HTTP 422).

The gain is then banded against two ceilings derived from the concession's Maximum Annual Service Payment: a first ceiling at one half of `masp_cents` and a second at one and a half times `masp_cents`, each rounded half up to a whole cent. The tiering is **average-rate, not marginal**: exactly one rate is selected by the band the whole gain falls into, and that single rate is then applied to the *entire* gain rather than slicing the gain across bands. A gain at or below the first ceiling attracts an authority share of thirty per cent; a gain above the first ceiling but at or below the second attracts fifty per cent; a gain above the second attracts sixty per cent. Both boundaries are inclusive downward, so a gain sitting exactly on a ceiling stays in the lower band. The selected rate is recorded as `tier_pct_applied`, the `authority_share_cents` is the gross gain multiplied by that rate and rounded half up to a whole cent, and the `concessionaire_share_cents` is the exact residual — the gross gain less the authority's share — so that the two shares always add back to the gross gain with no drift. Because the rate applies to the whole gain, one extra cent of gain that crosses a ceiling can move the authority's share by tens of millions of cents; and because rounding is half up, a share landing exactly on a half cent rounds up, never down. Tiers never aggregate across refinancing events: each proposal is banded on its own gain alone.

Consent is the `AuthorityOfficer`'s, and it is separated from proposal: an officer whose `X-User-ID` equals the recorded `proposed_by_user_id` may not decide the proposal and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The decision takes a `decision` of `CONSENT` or `REJECT` plus the `expected_concession_version` required by §2.7. It requires the refinancing event to be in `PROPOSED`, the concession to be in `OPERATIONS`, and no enforcement action of the concession to be sitting in `NOTIFIED`, `STEP_IN_ACTIVE` or `COMPENSATION_CALCULATED`. Both decisions record `consented_by_user_id` and `consented_at`, and both advance the concession's own `version` by one.

The two branches then diverge completely. On consent, the event lands in `CONSENTED` and its `reserve_sweep_cents` is set to twenty-five per cent of the concessionaire's share, rounded half up to a whole cent; one ledger entry is appended with `txn_type` `CONTRIBUTION`, `created_by_role` `SYSTEM` and a `reason` of `REFINANCING_GAIN_SWEEP` for exactly that amount, and the concession's `lifecycle_reserve_balance_cents` is set to the new entry's `resulting_balance_cents`. On refusal, the event lands in `REJECTED`, `reserve_sweep_cents` stays 0, no ledger entry is appended at all and the reserve balance does not move by a cent.

### 3.11. Enforcement: Step-In, Cure, Escalation and Termination

Enforcement is where the contract's remedies live, and its lifecycle is a **fork rather than a line**. An enforcement action opens in `NOTIFIED` and then travels one of two arms depending on its `action_type`. Its full set of states is `NOTIFIED`, `STEP_IN_ACTIVE`, `CURED`, `LAPSED`, `COMPENSATION_CALCULATED` and `SETTLED`, of which `CURED`, `LAPSED` and `SETTLED` are terminal; but no single action visits all of them. A step-in row leaves `NOTIFIED` immediately and can only ever reach `CURED` or `LAPSED`, and can never be compensated or settled. A termination row stays in `NOTIFIED` until it is compensated and can never be cured or lapsed. Implementing this as one linear sequence, or permitting a step-in row to be compensated, is a mis-reading of the contract.

Either the public side or the lenders may pull the trigger: an `AuthorityOfficer` or a `LenderAgent` notifies an action against a concession by supplying the `action_type` — `STEP_IN` or `TERMINATION` — the contractual `trigger_reason` and the `effective_date` from which the action bites, which is recorded verbatim in the shape the caller supplied it (a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date is stored and returned as a bare calendar date, and is never widened into an instant). The row records `initiated_by_role` as the acting `X-User-Role`. Grounds are not interchangeable between the arms: `PERSISTENT_BREACH` and `LENDER_DEFAULT_CURE` are the only grounds admitted for a step-in, and `AUTHORITY_VOLUNTARY`, `CONCESSIONAIRE_DEFAULT`, `AUTHORITY_DEFAULT` and `FORCE_MAJEURE` are the only grounds admitted for a termination. Pairing a ground with the wrong arm is refused with `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_GROUND_NOT_ADMITTED` (HTTP 422), and so is a step-in on the ground of `PERSISTENT_BREACH` where the concession's `persistent_breach_flag` is not currently true — the authority cannot assert a breach the points ledger does not support.

The two arms then behave quite differently in the same request. A step-in requires the concession to be in `OPERATIONS`, and it does not pause in `NOTIFIED`: the system auto-advances it to `STEP_IN_ACTIVE` before responding, sets its `cure_deadline` to the `effective_date` plus 90 calendar days — rendered in the same shape as that `effective_date`, so an `effective_date` given as a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date produces a `cure_deadline` that is likewise a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, while an `effective_date` carrying a time component produces the three-fractional-digit instant form of §2.4 — moves the concession `OPERATIONS → STEP_IN`, and moves the sponsor vehicle `ACTIVE → STEPPED_IN`. A single notification therefore writes three rows. A termination, by contrast, is admitted against a concession in any status but `TERMINATED` — `DRAFTED`, `FINANCIAL_CLOSE`, `CONSTRUCTION`, `OPERATIONS` and `STEP_IN` all qualify, and only a concession already `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409) — and it stays exactly where it was created: it sits in `NOTIFIED`, its `cure_deadline` stays null, and not one field of the concession or the sponsor vehicle moves.

While an action is live — that is, while it sits in `NOTIFIED`, `STEP_IN_ACTIVE` or `COMPENSATION_CALCULATED` — commercial writing on the project is frozen. New drawdown requests, milestone disbursement, refinancing proposals, refinancing decisions and the handback survey are all refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` (HTTP 409). That list is exhaustive, and notifying a further enforcement action is deliberately not on it: a second action may be notified against a concession that already carries a live one. The freeze lifts the moment the action reaches one of its terminal states, so a cured step-in restores normal commercial operation completely.

The step-in arm is resolved by the lenders, and the two ways of resolving it are separated by a **single instant**. A `LenderAgent` cures an active step-in by supplying a `cure_note`; the cure is admitted only while the logical clock is at or before the `cure_deadline`, inclusively. The same agent escalates a step-in that has run out of time by calling for escalation with no request body at all; escalation is admitted only once the logical clock is strictly after the `cure_deadline`. Both predicates read the stored `cure_deadline` as an instant — where it is a bare calendar date, that instant is midnight UTC at the start of that day — and so they partition the timeline with neither gap nor overlap: at the exact deadline instant the cure is permitted and the escalation is not, and one second later the reverse holds. Whichever of the two is attempted outside its window is refused with `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409).

A cure records the `cure_note` and stamps `cured_at` from the logical clock, moves the action `STEP_IN_ACTIVE → CURED`, returns the concession `STEP_IN → OPERATIONS` and returns the sponsor vehicle `STEPPED_IN → ACTIVE`. This is the same cure the persistent-breach clearance cascade of §3.6 performs automatically, and the two paths converge on identical row states; the only distinguishing mark is that the cascade writes a `cure_note` of `PERSISTENT_BREACH_CLEARED`.

An escalation is the join between the two arms of the fork. The lapsed action moves `STEP_IN_ACTIVE → LAPSED`, and the system appends a **second, brand-new enforcement action** against the same concession: `action_type` `TERMINATION`, `trigger_reason` `CONCESSIONAIRE_DEFAULT`, `effective_date` equal to the current logical clock's calendar day, written as a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` date rather than as a full instant, `status` `NOTIFIED` and `initiated_by_role` the literal `SYSTEM`. The lapsed row's `escalated_to_action_id` is then set to the appended row's identifier. The concession stays in `STEP_IN`, and so does the sponsor vehicle at `STEPPED_IN` — escalation terminates nothing by itself. The appended row is not returned in the escalation response, so the only way to reach it is by listing the concession's enforcement actions or by following the `escalated_to_action_id` link, which is precisely why both of those are filterable, as §2.10 explains.

Compensating a termination is the `AuthorityOfficer`'s, and it is the most branch-heavy calculation in the contract. The officer supplies the `termination_date`, the `outstanding_senior_debt_cents` standing at that date, and optionally a `market_value_estimate_cents`, a `rectification_cost_cents` and an `authority_reprocurement_cost_cents`. The action must be in `NOTIFIED` with an `action_type` of `TERMINATION`, and the concession must be in `OPERATIONS` or `STEP_IN`. The `termination_date` must not precede the concession's `actual_service_commencement`, failing which the response is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422). And where the `trigger_reason` is `CONCESSIONAIRE_DEFAULT`, all three of the optional cost inputs are in fact mandatory, because the variant that applies reads every one of them; an absent input there is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — taken at the schema rung, and so ahead of the status and `action_type` checks above. Under any other ground an omitted optional input is simply left null on the row, since the formula that applies never reads it. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `calculated_by_user_id`.

Three shared terms are computed first, whichever variant applies. The `elapsed_operational_months` is the whole-day span from the concession's `actual_service_commencement` to the `termination_date`, divided by a 30-day month basis and truncated toward zero — so 365 days is 12 months, not 12.17 and not 13 — and it is 0 where `actual_service_commencement` is null. The `accrued_equity_return_cents` is the concession's `equity_drawn_cents` multiplied by its `base_case_equity_irr_bps` divided by 10,000, multiplied by `elapsed_operational_months` divided by 12, rounded half up to a whole cent **exactly once at the end**: the basis-point conversion and the month proration are carried at full precision and only the final figure is rounded, so rounding after the basis-point step gives a different answer. The `unpaid_invoiced_cents` is the sum of `net_payment_cents` over the concession's periods whose payment axis sits at `INVOICED`, and the `deduction_offset_cents` is the sum of `allowed_amount_cents` over the concession's deduction events whose status is `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED` — the capped allowed amounts, not the raw amounts, and not the pro-rata shadow.

The `compensation_cents` is then one of four formulae, selected by the `trigger_reason`, and the four read overlapping but deliberately non-identical inputs. On `AUTHORITY_VOLUNTARY` the sponsor is made whole: the outstanding senior debt plus `equity_drawn_cents` plus the accrued equity return plus the unpaid invoices, less the deduction offset. On `CONCESSIONAIRE_DEFAULT` the basis changes completely — the debt, equity and accrued-return terms drop out entirely and three cost terms no other variant reads come in: the `market_value_estimate_cents` less the `rectification_cost_cents` less the `authority_reprocurement_cost_cents` less the deduction offset. On `AUTHORITY_DEFAULT` the make-whole is enlarged and its offset removed: the outstanding senior debt plus `equity_drawn_cents` plus the accrued equity return plus a further ten per cent of `equity_drawn_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, plus the unpaid invoices — with **no** deduction offset subtracted, because the authority may not net its own deductions off its own default. On `FORCE_MAJEURE` capital is returned but no profit is: the outstanding senior debt plus `equity_drawn_cents` plus the unpaid invoices less the deduction offset, with the accrued equity return omitted since nobody is at fault. Whichever formula applies, the result is clamped at zero, so `compensation_cents` is never negative. The action then moves `NOTIFIED → COMPENSATION_CALCULATED`, and — importantly — no concession field moves yet: calculating compensation is a valuation, not a termination.

Settlement is what terminates the project, and it is separated from the valuation: an `AuthorityOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the recorded `calculated_by_user_id` may not settle, and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The officer supplies a `settlement_reference`, which is recorded together with a `settled_at` stamp from the logical clock. The action requires status `COMPENSATION_CALCULATED` and a non-null `compensation_cents`. It then moves `COMPENSATION_CALCULATED → SETTLED` and fires the widest cascade in the system, in three links: the concession moves from `OPERATIONS` or `STEP_IN` to `TERMINATED`; **every** service period of that concession whose payment axis sits at `INVOICED` moves `INVOICED → ADJUSTED`, with its `net_payment_cents` frozen at whatever it then held, because those invoices will now be dealt with inside the compensation sum rather than paid in the ordinary course; and the sponsor vehicle moves to `WOUND_UP`. Periods sitting at `UNBILLED`, `PAID` or already `ADJUSTED` are untouched — the cascade sweeps one payment state only. Once the concession is `TERMINATED` nothing further can be written against it: every operation gated on concession state refuses with `ERR_CONCESSION_STATE_FORBIDS_OPERATION` (HTTP 409).
