# Pavement Management & Resurfacing Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

City streets fail slowly and then all at once. A public works department that treats the right block-face in the right year with the right treatment can hold a network's average condition steady for a decade on a flat budget; a department that resurfaces whatever generates the loudest complaints spends the same money and watches the network slide. The Pavement Management & Resurfacing Program API exists to make that difference operational. It carries a city's pavement inventory, the annual condition survey that measures it, the deterioration model that projects it forward, the treatment selection and pricing rules that turn condition into candidate work, the funding-level scenario analysis that decides which candidates get built, and the construction delivery record — utility clearance, resident notification, quality acceptance, warranty moratorium and contractor performance — that closes the loop.

Two pressures shape every rule in this document. The first is fiscal: an annual resurfacing appropriation is finite, candidates compete for it, and every dollar committed to an awarded contract must be traceable back to the scenario that funded it and released cleanly if the award is unwound. The second is protective: freshly paved asphalt is a capital asset that utilities want to trench through, so the system stamps a moratorium on new pavement, charges a tiered degradation fee for cutting it, and refuses to let a paving crew work over an open trench. Between those two pressures sit the people — residents who need 72 hours of warning before their driveway is blocked, inspectors who may not sign off on work they themselves witnessed, budget officers who may not award from a program they personally adopted.

This document is the complete functional specification. All resources are mounted under the `/pavement` prefix. Every collection is addressed flat — resources reference their parents by identifier rather than by nested URL — and every temporal rule in the system reads a single server-side logical clock rather than wall time, so that condition ages, service-level clocks, notice lead times and moratorium expiries are all reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across every endpoint. Where §3 describes a workflow, this section describes the frame that workflow operates inside.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty — and a header holding nothing but whitespace counts as empty — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; surrounding whitespace is otherwise trimmed from both header values before anything is compared or recorded. If the declared role is not one of the six recognised roles, or is recognised but not permitted for the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The six roles are `PavementEngineer`, `BudgetOfficer`, `UtilityCoordinator`, `Inspector`, `Contractor` and `Resident`.

A `PavementEngineer` owns the technical program: pavement inventory loading, survey campaigns and quality control of distress data, survey sealing and candidate generation, treatment overrides, contractor registration, project scoping and bundling, resident notices, cut-permit decisions, and complaint triage and closure. A `BudgetOfficer` owns the money: scenario adoption, manual allocations, award and award cancellation. A `UtilityCoordinator` acts on behalf of the water, sewer and franchise utilities: answering clearance requests, scheduling and reporting pre-paving work, applying for pavement cuts and reporting a trench restored. An `Inspector` owns pavement quality acceptance: submitting test measurements, deciding tests and signing final project acceptance. A `Contractor` is the awarded paving firm and may only declare substantial completion on its own project. A `Resident` is a member of the public who may report a pavement complaint and read the public-facing network, project-line and complaint views.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because several rules compare `X-User-ID` against stored values. When `X-User-Role` is `Contractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Contractor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. If a caller declares the `Contractor` role but `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing `Contractor` row, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_CONTRACTOR_IDENTITY`. When `X-User-Role` is any of `PavementEngineer`, `BudgetOfficer`, `UtilityCoordinator`, `Inspector` or `Resident`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `adopted_by_user_id` on a scenario, as `accepting_inspector_user_id` on a project, as `decided_by_user_id` on an acceptance test, or as the `witness_inspector_user_id` supplied when a test is submitted — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two segregation-of-duties rules follow from those recorded identifiers, and both reject with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The inspector who witnessed the coring of an acceptance test may not be the inspector who decides that test, and may not be the inspector who signs the enclosing project's final acceptance. The budget officer whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as the funding scenario's `adopted_by_user_id` may not award a project from that scenario. Separately, a `Contractor` caller acting on a project whose `contractor_id` is not the caller's `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_CONTRACTOR`; this guards both the substantial-completion action and the single-project read.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty upper-case alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read — like every other instant this API publishes — from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- The catalogue of §5 covers the errors the business rules raise. A request that never reaches a rule still answers in this envelope: a path this API does not define yields `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, a defined path addressed with the wrong method yields `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405, and a body that is not parseable JSON, or is JSON but not an object, yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. A request body may be omitted entirely wherever every field an operation reads is optional, and is then treated as the empty object.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the ordered result and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a list endpoint says otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. One collection departs from that default: scenario allocations are returned ordered by `rank` ascending, which is the order the budget waterfall assigned them. Utility clearance rows use the general `created_at` ascending rule, which for a freshly opened roster reproduces the creation order `WATER`, `SEWER`, `FRANCHISE_GAS`, `FRANCHISE_TELECOM`. Because the logical clock stands still between requests, rows routinely share a `created_at` — and two allocations can share a `rank` once a manual add keeps its rank across a re-run — so every ordering in this system, including the internal orderings the cascades and the candidate generator walk, breaks ties by insertion order, making each list stable and reproducible. List filters are exact string matches taken from the query string, and any query parameter a list endpoint does not recognise is ignored rather than rejected; beyond the filter sets named in §3, condition surveys filter on `survey_year`, `survey_status` and `collection_method`, distress observations on `survey_id`, `segment_id` and `qc_status`, and budget scenarios on `survey_id`, `scenario_status` and `strategy`.

Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the envelope's `meta` object.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. A single server-side logical clock supplies "now" to every timestamp stamp, every age computation, every service-level deadline, every notice lead-time gate and every moratorium comparison. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this default is mandatory, and an implementation that starts from real time will disagree with every worked example in this specification.

An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and is open to all six roles; it answers `200 OK` with a `data` object carrying the single field `current_utc`, the newly set instant rendered in this API's usual three-fractional-digit form, rather than any stored resource. Setting the clock mutates no entity and recomputes no derived field on its own — a segment's stored `condition_index` does not move because the clock moved. Fields that are evaluated against the clock at read time (a complaint's breach flag, a permit's remaining moratorium at the moment of computation) will of course reflect the new instant the next time they are evaluated.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Path Resolution

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, plus a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. That rendering is the one this API uses for every instant it publishes, not just for the audit fields: whether the server stamped the instant from the logical clock (`sealed_at`, `analysis_date`, `notice_delivered_at`, `accepted_at`, `closed_at` and the like), derived it (`sla_due_at`, `moratorium_until`), or took it from a request body (`last_treatment_date`, `planned_start_date`, `planned_end_date`, `work_window_start`, `work_window_end`, `parking_restriction_start`, `parking_restriction_end`, `planned_work_start`, `planned_work_end`, `requested_start`, `requested_end`), the value published back is that same instant normalised to UTC and written with exactly three fractional digits — a client-supplied timestamp is re-rendered in that form rather than echoed back character-for-character as the client spelled it, and an instant that is not set is published as null. Every resource in this system also carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`; a freshly created resource is published with `version` 1 and with `updated_at` equal to its `created_at`, and thereafter `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` is incremented on every mutation of that resource, including mutations that only change derived fields. The increment is once per request per resource, not once per field written: however much a single request rewrites on an already-stored resource — one field, several fields, a client-supplied field alongside a re-derived counter, or one level of a cascade — that resource's `version` advances by exactly one. Conversely a write that lands on no changed value is not a mutation: when a recompute reproduces exactly the values already stored, that resource's `updated_at` and `version` stand still, the one deliberate exception being the scenario run of §2.9, which advances the scenario's `version` unconditionally. When a survey seal republishes a segment's condition, or an award moves a scenario's committed total, the affected parent resource is mutated in exactly that sense: its `updated_at` advances and its `version` increments.

Path identifiers are resolved before any body content is inspected. A path `id` that does not resolve to a row of the addressed collection yields the not-found code belonging to that collection — `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUT_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_TEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved next, in the order the body declares them, which for the whole API is: `survey_id`, then `segment_id`, then `candidate_id`, then `project_id`, then `contractor_id`, then `capital_project_id`. The first unresolvable reference wins and reports the not-found code of its own collection, so a request naming both a bad survey and a bad segment reports `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_FOUND`. A `project_segment_id` that does not resolve reports `ERR_PROJECT_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a `target_project_id` that does not resolve reports `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Every rounding operation in this specification is round-half-up. Where a formula pins an intermediate rounding step, that step is binding: rounding earlier or later than specified produces a different published number and is wrong even when the difference is a single cent or a single tenth of a condition point.

The precision conventions are as follows. Pavement areas — a segment's `area_sy`, a project line's `line_area_sy`, a project's `planned_area_sy` — carry two decimal places. Condition indices — `baseline_condition_index`, `condition_index`, `measured_condition_index`, `network_condition_index`, `selection_condition_index`, `treatment_pci_gain`, `projected_network_pci`, `do_nothing_network_pci` — carry one decimal place. Distress percentages and the `deduct_total` carry two decimal places. Money is held in integer cents throughout, with no fractional cents anywhere; every monetary field in this system ends in `_cents` and every rounding to money rounds to a whole cent. Benefit-cost ratios and pay factors carry four decimal places: `benefit_cost_ratio`, `computed_pay_factor`, the line's stored `pay_factor`, and the intermediate coverage factor of the distress math. Contractor score components carry two decimal places and the composite scores `interim_performance_score` and `final_performance_score` carry one. A permit's `fee_multiplier` carries one decimal place. Segment geometry inputs `length_ft` and `width_ft` carry two decimal places, as does an acceptance test's `thickness_in`. These conventions describe the precision the server publishes and the precision a well-formed client is expected to supply; they are not an extra admissibility test on the request. A client-supplied number carrying more decimal places than its convention allows is accepted rather than rejected. Where the value is itself re-published at a fixed precision it is stored round-half-up at that precision — `baseline_condition_index` at one place, `design_thickness_in` and `thickness_in` at two, `core_density_pct` at one. Where the value is only ever an input to a formula and is never re-published — `length_ft`, `width_ft`, `cut_area_sqft`, the six distress measurements and an acceptance test's `iri_in_per_mi` — it is stored exactly as the client supplied it and feeds the formulas at that full precision, so that only the formula's own published result is rounded.

### 2.7. Pavement Condition: Indices, Bands and the Deterioration Model

The pavement condition index — PCI — is the currency of this system, and a segment's `condition_index` is a **stored derived field**, never recomputed on read. Exactly three events write it: the segment's own creation, the sealing of a condition survey that carries an accepted observation on that segment, and the final acceptance of a project line that resurfaced it. The companion field `condition_index_source` records which of two computation variants produced the current value, and takes the value `SURVEY_MEASURED` when the last writer was a survey seal, or `MODEL_PROJECTED` when the last writer was segment creation or project acceptance.

The two variants disagree on purpose. The `SURVEY_MEASURED` variant sets `condition_index` to the accepted observation's `measured_condition_index`, which is pure distress-deduct arithmetic: it ignores `baseline_condition_index` and pavement age entirely. The `MODEL_PROJECTED` variant ignores every distress measurement and derives the index from the deterioration model at creation, or from the treatment's restorative gain at acceptance. For identical field data the two variants publish different PCI values, and only the `SURVEY_MEASURED` variant can push a segment into a worse condition band than its age alone implies.

Whatever wrote it, `condition_index` is immediately banded into `condition_band`: `GOOD` when the index is at least 70.0, `FAIR` when it is at least 50.0 but below 70.0, `POOR` when it is at least 25.0 but below 50.0, and `FAILED` when it is below 25.0.

The deterioration model itself is a two-slope decay anchored on the last treatment. The anchor date is the segment's `last_treatment_date` when that field is populated, and the segment's `created_at` when it is null — a segment that has never been treated ages from the moment it entered the inventory. Age in whole years is the number of whole days from the anchor date to the evaluation instant, divided by 365, with any fraction discarded. Each segment's decay rates are looked up from its `functional_class` and stored on the segment: `decay_coefficient` is the PCI points lost per year before the knee and `decay_knee_coefficient` is the *additional* PCI points lost per year after it. An `ARTERIAL` segment loses 2.2 points per year with a further 1.6 after the knee; a `COLLECTOR` loses 1.8 with a further 1.3; a `LOCAL` loses 1.4 with a further 1.0. The knee falls at 8 years of age. The projected index is therefore the `baseline_condition_index`, reduced by the pre-knee rate multiplied by the age in years, and reduced again by the knee rate multiplied by the number of years of age beyond 8 (which is zero for any segment younger than the knee), then clamped into the range 0.0 through 100.0 and rounded to one decimal place. No survey data participates in the model projection.

### 2.8. Parallel State Dimensions, Derived Counters and Cascade Propagation

Two entities in this system carry two independent state dimensions at once, and neither dimension gates the other except where this document says so explicitly. A `ResurfacingProject` carries `delivery_status` (the contracting and construction track) alongside `quality_status` (the pavement-acceptance track); a test submitted on the first line moves `quality_status` without touching `delivery_status`. A `ProjectSegment` carries `work_state` (the construction track) alongside `notice_state` (the resident-notification track); a notice can be scheduled, delivered and rescheduled while the line's construction state stands still.

Counters on parents are derived, not client-supplied, and are recomputed by the server whenever the rows they count change. A survey's `observation_count` counts every distress observation on it in any quality-control state, while `accepted_observation_count` counts only those accepted. A scenario's `allocation_count` counts its allocations that are funded or deferred, `funded_cost_cents` sums the allocated amounts of the funded ones, `backlog_cents` sums the estimated costs of the deferred ones, `committed_cents` sums the award amounts of its awarded, in-construction, substantially-complete and accepted projects, and `uncommitted_cents` is the funding total less the committed total. A project's `segment_count` and `planned_area_sy` count and sum its lines that have not been removed, `clearance_outstanding_count` counts its utility rows not yet cleared, and `open_remediation_count` counts its lines awaiting remediation. A line's `test_count` counts every acceptance test on it in any state. A segment's `active_project_count` counts the project lines referencing it whose `work_state` is `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `WORK_COMPLETE` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, and its `open_service_request_count` counts the complaints on it whose `request_status` is `NEW`, `TRIAGED_PATCH` or `DEFERRED_TO_CAPITAL`. A contractor's `passed_test_count`, `failed_test_count`, `remediation_event_count`, `days_late_total` and `accepted_project_count` are likewise derived from its projects and their tests.

Several operations in this system are **cascades**: one client request mutates a chain of entities, each level triggered by the level above it. Sealing a survey republishes segment condition, which supersedes stale candidates, which withdraws their allocations, which re-totals the affected scenarios. Failing an acceptance test drives a line into remediation, which drives the project's quality dimension into remediation, which increments a contractor counter, which re-derives that contractor's award standing. Accepting a project accepts its lines, which republishes pavement condition and opens a moratorium, which re-prices pending cut permits and closes complaints deferred to that project. Each level is described in the workflow that triggers it, and every level is mandatory: an implementation that performs the first level and skips the third has not performed the operation.

### 2.9. Stale-Read Protection on Parent Aggregates

Two write operations add a child row to a parent aggregate whose totals the client is reasoning about, and both are protected against acting on a superseded read of that parent. Each requires a version field in its request body; the field is not optional, and endpoints other than these two accept no version field at all — supplied to any other endpoint it is simply ignored rather than rejected, as is every other body key an endpoint does not read.

A `POST /pavement/scenario-allocations` request must carry `expected_scenario_version` matching the target `BudgetScenario`'s current `version`. Adding a manual allocation is legitimately repeatable — a second, strictly later request carrying a fresh version would itself succeed — so the stale-version check is the only deterministic guard available. Every scenario run, adoption, award, award cancellation and prior manual add advances that `version`, and a run advances it whether or not the recomputation it performs changes any stored value — an idempotent re-run that reproduces the identical allocation table, the identical totals and the identical projection arms still advances the scenario's `version` by one; if the value supplied no longer equals the scenario's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCENARIO_VERSION` and HTTP 412, preventing a client from adding to a funding picture it has not observed.

A `POST /pavement/project-segments` request must carry `expected_project_version` matching the target `ResurfacingProject`'s current `version`. Adding lines is also legitimately repeatable, since each add names a different segment, so no state precondition can reject the later writer; the stale-version check is what protects a bundle whose cost, area and segment roster the client is reasoning about from a superseded read. A mismatch with the project's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence: the Order in Which Requests Fail

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fully determined. Checks run in the following order, and the first violation short-circuits every later check:

1. **Authentication.** Both identity headers present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation.** The declared role recognised and permitted for the operation, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Caller identity resolution.** A `Contractor` caller's `X-User-ID` resolves to a contractor row, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_CONTRACTOR_IDENTITY`.
4. **Caller ownership.** A `Contractor` caller acts only on its own project, else `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_CONTRACTOR`.
5. **Segregation of duties**, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. **Request shape.** Required body fields present, JSON types correct, enum values in range, numeric fields within their declared bounds, pagination parameters legal, timestamps ISO 8601 UTC, and every window's end strictly after its start — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. **Path existence**, then **body foreign-key existence** in declared body order, else the matching not-found code.
8. **Stale-read protection**, else `ERR_STALE_SCENARIO_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`.
9. **The acting entity's own state** — the state-machine preconditions of the resource being driven.
10. **Worker-safety rules** — the two rules that protect people from equipment.
11. **Temporal rules** — lead times, window ordering against reference dates.
12. **Referential and roster rules** — cross-entity membership and matching.
13. **Uniqueness and cross-entity-state gates** — duplicate prevention and readiness checks over child collections.
14. **Capacity and overlap rules** — crew capacity and interval conflicts.
15. **Fiscal rules** — funding and tolerance checks, last of all.

Three consequences of that ordering are worth stating explicitly because they are easy to get backwards. First, on an award attempt, the project's own delivery state outranks the fiscal check: an award aimed at a project still coordinating, for an amount far beyond what the scenario has left uncommitted, reports `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_BID_READY` and never `ERR_SCENARIO_FUNDS_EXCEEDED`. Second, on the transition that puts crews on the street, the open-trench safety rule outranks the notice-completeness rule: a project holding both an open approved trench and an undelivered resident notice reports `ERR_OPEN_CUT_ON_SEGMENT` and never `ERR_NOTICES_NOT_DELIVERED`. Third, on a notice scheduling or rescheduling action, the lead-time rule outranks the window-overlap rule: a window that is both inside the lead time and buffered-overlapping a sibling line's window reports `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT` and never `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities of the Program

Three quantities in this system are conserved, meaning that a compensating action returns them to their exact pre-action values with no drift. They are stated here as invariants because they are what the compensating actions in §3 exist to preserve.

**Appropriated funding.** For any scenario, the committed total is the sum of `award_amount_cents` over its projects whose `delivery_status` is `AWARDED`, `IN_CONSTRUCTION`, `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` or `ACCEPTED`, and the uncommitted total is `funding_cents` less that committed total. Both are stored fields, seeded at zero when the scenario is created and rewritten together — `committed_cents` to that sum and `uncommitted_cents` to `funding_cents` less it — by every operation that re-totals the scenario; no read recomputes them. From a scenario's first re-total onward the funding total therefore always equals committed plus uncommitted, exactly. The one exception is a scenario still carrying its creation-time zeroes of §3.4, where `uncommitted_cents` reads zero while `funding_cents` may be any amount. Awarding an amount and then cancelling that award on the same project restores both totals to their exact pre-award values for any amount, because cancellation subtracts the stored `award_amount_cents` rather than any recomputed estimate.

**Pavement area under plan.** For any project, `planned_area_sy` is the sum of `line_area_sy` over its lines whose `work_state` is not `REMOVED`, rounded to two decimal places, and `segment_count` is the count of those same lines. For any segment, `active_project_count` is the count of project lines referencing it whose `work_state` is `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `WORK_COMPLETE` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`. Adding a line and then removing it leaves all three at their exact pre-add values and returns the consumed candidate to the pool.

**Open complaint volume.** For any segment, `open_service_request_count` is the count of complaints on it whose `request_status` is `NEW`, `TRIAGED_PATCH` or `DEFERRED_TO_CAPITAL`. The counter decrements exactly once per complaint that leaves that open set, whether it leaves by a patch closure, by a duplicate closure, or by the automatic closure that rides along with capital resurfacing. Triage never changes it, and it never goes negative.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building and Maintaining the Pavement Inventory

The managed asset is the **network segment**: one block-face of city pavement. A `PavementEngineer` loads a segment by supplying its `street_name`, the `from_node` and `to_node` cross-street labels that bound it, the `geo_zone` label used later to bundle segments into geographic projects, its `functional_class`, its `surface_type`, its centreline `length_ft`, its curb-to-curb `width_ft`, and its `baseline_condition_index`. Optionally the engineer supplies `last_treatment_date`, the instant of the last treatment placed on the street; when it is omitted the field is null and the deterioration model ages the segment from `created_at` instead. Street names run 1 to 120 characters, node labels 1 to 80 characters, and the zone label 1 to 40 characters. `functional_class` is one of `ARTERIAL`, `COLLECTOR` or `LOCAL`, and `surface_type` is one of `ASPHALT` or `COMPOSITE`. Length must be strictly greater than zero; width must be at least 8 feet; the baseline index must lie between 0 and 100 inclusive at one decimal place.

Creation computes the segment's `area_sy`: the length in feet multiplied by the width in feet, divided by the nine square feet that make up one square yard, rounded to two decimal places. The two decay rates for the segment's class are stamped onto `decay_coefficient` and `decay_knee_coefficient`, and the deterioration model of §2.7 is evaluated at the current logical clock to seed `condition_index`, with `condition_index_source` set to `MODEL_PROJECTED` and `condition_band` derived from the result. A newly loaded segment records `last_treatment_type` as `NONE` — the field otherwise takes `CRACK_SEAL`, `MILL_AND_OVERLAY` or `RECONSTRUCTION` once a treatment has been accepted on it — and starts with `moratorium_until` null, `active_project_count` zero and `open_service_request_count` zero. Geometry is immutable after creation: `length_ft` and `width_ft` are never revised, so `area_sy` is computed once and never again.

The network can be listed with filters on `geo_zone`, `functional_class` and `condition_band`, which is how an engineer assembles the candidate list for a geographic bundle, and every role in the system may read the inventory.

### 3.2. The Condition Survey Campaign: Collection, Quality Control and Sealing

A survey campaign is opened by a `PavementEngineer` with a `survey_year` — a whole-number program year between 2020 and 2040 inclusive — and a `collection_method` of either `AUTOMATED_LCMS` for van-based imaging or `MANUAL_WALK` for a walking inspection. A new campaign has `survey_status` `OPEN`, both observation counters at zero, `network_condition_index` null and `sealed_at` null. `OPEN` and `SEALED` are the only two states a survey ever holds; sealing is the sole exit and there is no un-seal.

While the campaign is `OPEN`, the engineer files one **distress observation** per surveyed segment, naming the `survey_id` it belongs to and the `segment_id` it measured, along with the collected distress: `alligator_cracking_sqft` for fatigue cracking area, `longitudinal_cracking_lf` for combined longitudinal and transverse cracking in linear feet, `rutting_depth_in` for the mean wheelpath rut depth, `patching_sqft` for existing patch area, `iri_in_per_mi` for the International Roughness Index, and `sample_coverage_pct` for the percentage of the segment the collection van actually imaged. The three area and length quantities must be at least zero; rut depth must be at least zero and at most 4 inches; roughness must be at least zero and at most 600 inches per mile; coverage must be at least 25 and at most 100 percent. Filing an observation against a survey that is not `OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_OPEN`, and a second observation for the same survey-and-segment pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBSERVATION`. Each accepted filing increments the survey's `observation_count`.

Filing computes the observation's measured condition index, and the rounding order here is pinned. The segment's plan area in square feet is its length multiplied by its width. The alligator-cracking percentage is the alligator area divided by that plan area, multiplied by 100, rounded to two decimal places; the patching percentage is the patch area over the same plan area, multiplied by 100 and rounded to two decimal places; the crack density is the longitudinal-and-transverse linear footage divided by the segment length in feet, multiplied by 100 and rounded to two decimal places. Rut excess is the rut depth less the 0.25 inches the model treats as free, floored at zero. Roughness excess is the roughness less the 95 inches per mile the model treats as free, also floored at zero. The raw deduct is then the sum of five weighted terms — 0.45 times the alligator percentage, 0.18 times the crack density, 40.0 times the rut excess in inches, 0.12 times the patching percentage, and 0.08 times the roughness excess — and that raw sum is *not* rounded. A coverage factor scales the raw deduct up for partial imaging: it is 100 divided by `sample_coverage_pct`, rounded to four decimal places. The stored `deduct_total` is the raw deduct multiplied by that coverage factor, rounded to two decimal places. Finally `measured_condition_index` is 100 less the `deduct_total`, clamped into the range 0.0 through 100.0 and rounded to one decimal place. Rounding the weighted sum before the coverage scale-up produces a different number and is wrong.

Quality control decides whether an observation feeds the network's condition picture. Every observation starts with `qc_status` `PENDING` and a null `qc_note`. A `PavementEngineer` submits a `qc_decision` of `ACCEPT`, `FLAG` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `qc_note` of 1 to 500 characters that is recorded on the row; when the note is omitted the row keeps whatever note an earlier decision left there rather than having it cleared. Accepting moves the observation to `ACCEPTED` and increments the survey's `accepted_observation_count`. Flagging moves it to `FLAGGED`, records the note, and leaves it out of the accepted count and out of the seal cascade until it is decided again. Rejecting moves it to `REJECTED`, records the note, and guarantees it never contributes to any condition index. `FLAGGED` is revisitable in both directions — a flagged observation may later be accepted, which records the new note and increments the accepted count, or rejected, which records the note — while `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` are terminal. Every quality-control decision requires the parent survey to still be `OPEN`, failing with `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_OPEN` otherwise, and requires the observation's `qc_status` to be `PENDING` or `FLAGGED`, failing with `ERR_OBSERVATION_ALREADY_DECIDED` when the observation has already reached a terminal decision.

Sealing is the campaign's terminal act and the system's most consequential cascade. A `PavementEngineer` seals a survey, moving `survey_status` from `OPEN` to `SEALED`; a second attempt on an already-sealed survey reports `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_OPEN`. Two readiness conditions guard it: no observation on the survey may still be `PENDING`, else `ERR_OBSERVATIONS_PENDING_QC`, and at least one observation must be `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_SURVEY_HAS_NO_ACCEPTED_OBSERVATIONS`. Sealing stamps `sealed_at` with the logical clock and computes `network_condition_index` as the area-weighted mean measured index over the accepted observations: for each accepted observation, multiply its segment's `area_sy` by the observation's `measured_condition_index`; sum those products; divide by the sum of the same segments' `area_sy`; round to one decimal place. Because at most one observation exists per survey-and-segment pair, no segment is double-weighted, and the result is insensitive to evaluation order.

The cascade then runs in four levels. **First**, for every accepted observation on the survey, the referenced segment's `condition_index` is overwritten with that observation's `measured_condition_index`, its `condition_index_source` is set to `SURVEY_MEASURED`, and its `condition_band` is recomputed from the new value. **Second**, because those segments' condition changed, every treatment candidate whose `candidate_status` is `RECOMMENDED` or `OVERRIDDEN`, whose segment is one of those segments, and whose own `survey_id` is a *different* survey, moves to `SUPERSEDED`. **Third**, because those candidates were superseded, every scenario allocation referencing them whose `allocation_status` is `FUNDED` or `DEFERRED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and its `allocated_cents` is set to zero. **Fourth**, because those allocations were withdrawn, every affected scenario recomputes `allocation_count`, `funded_cost_cents` and `backlog_cents`.

### 3.3. From Sealed Survey to Priced Treatment Candidates

Once a survey is `SEALED`, a `PavementEngineer` runs treatment selection over it, which emits one **treatment candidate** per eligible segment. Selection may only run on a sealed survey — attempting it earlier reports `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_SEALED` — and may only run once per survey; a second run reports `ERR_CANDIDATES_ALREADY_GENERATED`. That guard tests for candidates already standing on the survey, so a survey whose eligible pool was empty created nothing, is not considered generated, and may be run again. The run answers `201 Created` with `response_type` `array` and a `data` array holding the candidates it just created, in the generation order described below, and no pagination fields in `meta`; an empty pool is not an error and yields an empty array.

The eligible pool is the set of segments that have an `ACCEPTED` observation on this survey and whose `condition_index` is below the no-treatment ceiling of 90.0. Segments whose only observation was flagged or rejected are not in the pool, and a segment at exactly 90.0 yields no candidate at all. For each pooled segment the system freezes `selection_condition_index` as a snapshot of the segment's `condition_index` at generation time; every subsequent selection and benefit computation for that candidate reads the snapshot and never the live field. Treatment follows from the snapshot: an index of at least 70.0 selects `CRACK_SEAL`, an index below 70.0 but at least 40.0 selects `MILL_AND_OVERLAY`, and an index below 40.0 selects `RECONSTRUCTION`. The floors are inclusive, so exactly 70.0 selects crack seal while 69.9 selects mill-and-overlay, and exactly 40.0 selects mill-and-overlay while 39.9 selects reconstruction. Candidates are generated worst-first: ordered by the segment's `condition_index` ascending, with ties broken by the segment's `created_at` ascending. Each new candidate records the generating `survey_id` and the target `segment_id`, and starts with `candidate_status` `RECOMMENDED` and a null `override_reason`.

Every candidate is priced as it is generated. The construction rate is a cents-per-square-yard figure looked up on the pair of treatment and roadway class. Crack seal runs 350 cents per square yard on a `LOCAL` street, 420 on a `COLLECTOR` and 500 on an `ARTERIAL`. Mill-and-overlay runs 2,800, 3,300 and 4,100 cents per square yard on the same three classes. Reconstruction runs 9,500, 11,000 and 13,500. Two multipliers adjust that rate. The width factor recognises that wide streets cost more per yard to stage: it is one plus one hundredth of the amount by which `width_ft` exceeds the 24 feet treated as free, clamped to no less than 1.00 and no more than 1.25, and rounded to four decimal places — so a 24-foot street and any narrower street both take 1.0000, a 40-foot street takes 1.1600, and anything at or beyond 49 feet is capped at 1.2500. The condition penalty recognises that badly broken pavement needs more repair before the new surface goes down: it is 1.12 when the snapshot index bands as `FAILED`, 1.05 when it bands as `POOR`, and 1.00 otherwise, using the same band thresholds as §2.7 applied to `selection_condition_index`. The candidate's `estimated_cost_cents` is then the segment's `area_sy` multiplied by the base rate, then by the width factor, then by the condition penalty, all at full precision, with that single product rounded once to whole cents — and only *after* that rounding is a mobilisation charge added: 250,000 cents ($2,500) for crack seal, and 850,000 cents ($8,500) for either mill-and-overlay or reconstruction.

The benefit side of the ledger is expressed in PCI-years-square-yards per thousand dollars. The candidate's `expected_service_life_years` is fixed by treatment: 5 years for `CRACK_SEAL`, 15 for `MILL_AND_OVERLAY`, 30 for `RECONSTRUCTION`. Its `treatment_pci_gain` is the smaller of the treatment's gain cap — 8.0 points for crack seal, 45.0 for mill-and-overlay, 100.0 for reconstruction — and the headroom actually available, which is 100 less the `selection_condition_index`, rounded to one decimal place. The benefit quantity is that gain multiplied by the expected service life in years and again by the segment's `area_sy`, rounded to two decimal places. The cost is restated in thousands of dollars by dividing `estimated_cost_cents` by 100,000, with mobilisation included. The stored `benefit_cost_ratio` is the benefit quantity divided by that cost in thousands, rounded to four decimal places.

Model selection is advisory, not final. A `PavementEngineer` may override a candidate's `treatment_type`, supplying the new type and a mandatory `override_reason` of 1 to 500 characters, which moves `candidate_status` from `RECOMMENDED` to `OVERRIDDEN`. Three conditions guard the override, and they are tested in the order written here rather than in the general order of §2.10. The candidate must be in a state that permits it — a `CONSUMED` or `SUPERSEDED` candidate reports `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_OVERRIDABLE`, and it does so even when the requested type is also the type already selected. The requested `treatment_type` must actually differ from the current one; an override that changes nothing reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. And no scenario on the candidate's survey may have reached `ADOPTED`, because the program of record is frozen once adopted; an attempt after adoption reports `ERR_SURVEY_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. The override records the reason and re-derives `expected_service_life_years`, `treatment_pci_gain`, `estimated_cost_cents` and `benefit_cost_ratio` from the new treatment type against the unchanged `selection_condition_index` snapshot — the snapshot never moves, so re-pricing an overridden candidate is fully reproducible. Candidates may be listed and filtered by `survey_id`, `segment_id`, `candidate_status` and `treatment_type`.

### 3.4. Funding-Level Analysis: the Waterfall, Manual Adds and Adoption

A **budget scenario** is a what-if at one funding level over one sealed survey's candidate pool. Either a `BudgetOfficer` or a `PavementEngineer` creates one, supplying the `survey_id` whose pool it draws on, a `scenario_name` label of 1 to 80 characters such as "FY27 at $4.0M", the `funding_cents` available to allocate (which must not be negative), the ranking `strategy`, and the `horizon_years` over which network condition should be projected (between 1 and 20 inclusive). The named survey need only exist — creation does not re-test that it is sealed, and there is no error code for an unsealed one; a scenario opened over an `OPEN` survey simply finds an empty candidate pool when it runs. Creation stamps `analysis_date` with the logical clock — every age computation inside this scenario is measured from that instant, not from the clock as it stands later — and sets `scenario_status` to `DRAFT`, with `adopted_by_user_id` null, `allocation_count`, `funded_cost_cents`, `backlog_cents`, `committed_cents` and `uncommitted_cents` all seeded at zero — `uncommitted_cents` included, so a scenario that has not yet been re-totalled publishes zero there rather than mirroring `funding_cents` — and both projection outputs, `projected_network_pci` and `do_nothing_network_pci`, null until the first run.

Running the scenario performs the waterfall and moves `scenario_status` from `DRAFT` to `COMPUTED`. The pool is every candidate whose `survey_id` matches the scenario's, with `candidate_status` `RECOMMENDED`, `OVERRIDDEN` or `CONSUMED`; superseded candidates are excluded. The pool is then ranked according to `strategy`. Under `WORST_FIRST`, candidates sort by `selection_condition_index` ascending, ties broken by segment `area_sy` descending, then by candidate `created_at` ascending. Under `BENEFIT_COST`, candidates sort by `benefit_cost_ratio` descending, ties broken by `estimated_cost_cents` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Under `HYBRID`, the pool splits into two partitions: candidates whose `selection_condition_index` is below the triage threshold of 40.0 form the first partition, ordered by `selection_condition_index` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and every remaining candidate forms the second, ordered by `benefit_cost_ratio` descending then `created_at` ascending — the whole first partition precedes the whole second.

The waterfall then walks the ranked pool with a running remaining balance that starts at `funding_cents`. A `rank` of 1 goes to the first pool member and increments for *every* pool member, funded or not. For each candidate, if its `estimated_cost_cents` is less than or equal to the remaining balance, an allocation is created with `allocation_status` `FUNDED`, `allocated_cents` equal to the candidate's estimated cost, and `source` `AUTO_RUN`, and the remaining balance is reduced by that cost. Otherwise an allocation is created with `allocation_status` `DEFERRED`, `allocated_cents` zero and `source` `AUTO_RUN`. Critically, **the scan does not stop at the first candidate that does not fit**: a later, cheaper candidate that still fits within the remaining balance is funded. A reading that halts at the first unaffordable candidate and defers everything after it is explicitly wrong. Because the affordability test is "cost no greater than remaining", a candidate costing exactly the remaining balance is funded. After the walk, `funded_cost_cents` is the sum of `allocated_cents` over funded rows, `backlog_cents` is the sum of the candidates' `estimated_cost_cents` over deferred rows, `allocation_count` is the number of funded plus deferred rows, and `uncommitted_cents` is refreshed.

The same run computes both arms of the network-condition projection over the scenario's horizon, using the decay rates and the 8-year knee of §2.7. The projection pool is the segments carrying an `ACCEPTED` observation on the scenario's survey. Each segment's current age in whole years is the whole days from its anchor date to the scenario's `analysis_date`, divided by 365 with the fraction discarded. For a segment left unfunded, the incremental loss over the horizon is the pre-knee rate multiplied by the horizon in years, plus the knee rate multiplied by the number of years by which the sum of current age and horizon exceeds 8, minus the knee rate multiplied by the number of years by which the current age alone exceeds 8 — each of those two excess terms floored at zero. The segment's unfunded projected index is its `condition_index` less that incremental loss, clamped into 0.0 through 100.0 and rounded to one decimal place. For a segment that *is* funded, the treatment resets the clock, so the projection starts from the post-treatment level — the smaller of 100.0 and the sum of the candidate's `selection_condition_index` and its `treatment_pci_gain` — and subtracts only the pre-knee rate multiplied by the horizon, plus the knee rate multiplied by the amount by which the horizon alone exceeds 8 years, then clamps into 0.0 through 100.0 and rounds to one decimal place. A segment counts as funded when an allocation of *this* scenario with `allocation_status` `FUNDED` references a candidate on it. `projected_network_pci` is then the area-weighted mean of each segment's per-segment projected index — funded segments on the reset curve, unfunded segments on the incremental-decay curve — where each per-segment index is rounded to one decimal place *before* the weighting, and the weighted mean itself is rounded to one decimal place. `do_nothing_network_pci` is the same weighted mean computed with every segment on the unfunded curve regardless of allocation; it is the comparison arm that shows what the network does at zero funding. When the projection pool is empty — no accepted observation on the survey, so no area to weight by — both arms are written as null rather than zero, exactly as they read before the first run.

A scenario may be re-run any number of times while nothing is committed, staying in `COMPUTED`. A re-run first deletes every allocation on the scenario whose `source` is `AUTO_RUN` — including rows a prior seal cascade had already withdrawn — then re-allocates from the current pool; allocations whose `source` is `MANUAL_ADD` survive the re-run and keep their `rank`. All the totals and both projection arms are then recomputed. A run or re-run is only permitted while `scenario_status` is `DRAFT` or `COMPUTED`; on an `ADOPTED` or `SUPERSEDED` scenario it reports `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_RUNNABLE`, and that state test runs first. Any run is additionally blocked once any project on the scenario has moved past `DRAFT`, reporting `ERR_SCENARIO_HAS_PROGRAMMED_PROJECTS`, because the delivery program would no longer match the allocation table; the gate is written the same way for a first run as for a re-run.

A `BudgetOfficer` may also add a candidate to a scenario by hand, outside the waterfall, naming the `scenario_id`, the `candidate_id` and the stale-read guard `expected_scenario_version` described in §2.9. The manual row is created with `source` `MANUAL_ADD`, `allocation_status` `FUNDED`, `allocated_cents` equal to the candidate's `estimated_cost_cents`, and a `rank` one greater than the highest existing rank on the scenario; the parent then recomputes `allocation_count`, `funded_cost_cents` and `uncommitted_cents`, and its `version` increments. Manual adds require the scenario to be `DRAFT` or `COMPUTED`, since an `ADOPTED` or `SUPERSEDED` scenario is frozen and reports `ERR_SCENARIO_FROZEN`. The named candidate must belong to the scenario's survey, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_SURVEY_MISMATCH`. And the addition must not push `funded_cost_cents` above `funding_cents`, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_FUNDING`. Allocations can be listed and filtered by `scenario_id`, `candidate_id`, `allocation_status` and `source`. `WITHDRAWN` is the only terminal allocation state, reached solely through the seal cascade of §3.2; `FUNDED` and `DEFERRED` are the two values the waterfall assigns at creation and they never convert into one another without a re-run, which deletes the automatic rows outright.

Adoption makes a scenario the capital program of record and belongs exclusively to a `BudgetOfficer`. It moves `scenario_status` from `COMPUTED` to `ADOPTED`; on any other starting state it reports `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_ADOPTABLE`, and on a computed scenario with no allocations at all — an empty candidate pool — it reports `ERR_SCENARIO_HAS_NO_ALLOCATIONS`. Adoption records the acting officer's `X-User-ID` as `adopted_by_user_id`, which is the anchor for the award segregation rule of §2.1, and refreshes `uncommitted_cents` as `funding_cents` less `committed_cents`. Adopting a scenario also moves every *other* `ADOPTED` scenario on the same survey to `SUPERSEDED`; projects already created under a superseded scenario keep their `scenario_id` but can no longer receive new awards, which surface as `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_ADOPTED`.

### 3.5. Assembling a Geographic Bundle and Clearing the Utilities

A **resurfacing project** is a geographic bundle of funded segments delivered as one construction contract. A `PavementEngineer` creates one under an adopted scenario, supplying the `scenario_id`, a `project_name` of 1 to 80 characters, the `geo_zone` the bundle covers, and the planned construction window as `planned_start_date` and `planned_end_date`. The planned end must be strictly after the planned start, and the planned start must be on or after the scenario's `analysis_date`; a start earlier than the analysis date reports `ERR_PROJECT_WINDOW_BEFORE_ANALYSIS_DATE`. The referenced scenario must be `ADOPTED`, else `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_ADOPTED` — and per §2.10 the temporal window test outranks that state test, so a start before the analysis date of a scenario that is still `COMPUTED` reports `ERR_PROJECT_WINDOW_BEFORE_ANALYSIS_DATE` and never `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_ADOPTED`. A new project has `delivery_status` `DRAFT` and `quality_status` `NOT_STARTED`, with `contractor_id` null, `award_amount_cents` null, `accepted_at`, `substantial_completion_at` and `accepting_inspector_user_id` all null, `days_late` zero, and every money field and counter at zero. Projects can be listed and filtered by `scenario_id`, `contractor_id`, `delivery_status`, `quality_status` and `geo_zone`.

Lines are added to the bundle one funded candidate at a time. Each **project line** names its `project_id`, the `segment_id` being treated, the `candidate_id` it executes, the half-open construction occupancy window `work_window_start` and `work_window_end`, and the `expected_project_version` guard of §2.9; optionally it supplies `design_thickness_in`, the design lift thickness that the thickness pay factor is measured against, which defaults to 2.0 inches and must lie between 0.5 and 12.0 inclusive. Creation copies `treatment_type` from the candidate and `line_area_sy` from the segment's `area_sy`, computes the line's `line_cost_cents`, and starts the line at `work_state` `PLANNED`, `notice_state` `PENDING` and `notice_round` 1, with `parking_restriction_start`, `parking_restriction_end`, `driveway_access_plan`, `notice_delivered_at` and `pay_factor` all null and `test_count` zero. The candidate moves from `RECOMMENDED` or `OVERRIDDEN` to `CONSUMED` and is locked against override while consumed. The parent project increments `segment_count`, adds the line's `line_area_sy` to `planned_area_sy`, recomputes its estimate and increments its `version`; the referenced segment increments `active_project_count`.

Four referential rules police what may enter a bundle, and all four are business-level rejections rather than shape errors. The candidate must reference the same segment as the line, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_SEGMENT_MISMATCH`. The candidate must belong to the survey behind the project's scenario and must hold a `FUNDED` allocation in that scenario, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FUNDED`. The segment's `geo_zone` must equal the project's `geo_zone`, because a bundle is a geographic package and may not reach outside its zone, else `ERR_SEGMENT_ZONE_MISMATCH`. Two uniqueness rules follow: at most one non-removed line per project-and-segment pair, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROJECT_SEGMENT`, and at most one non-removed line per candidate anywhere in the system, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_ALREADY_PROGRAMMED`. Finally the line's occupancy window is checked against every other non-removed line on the same segment as a **half-open interval** running from `work_window_start` up to but not including `work_window_end`: overlapping windows are rejected with `ERR_SEGMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, while windows that merely touch — one line ending at exactly the instant the next begins — are allowed. Lines may be listed and filtered by `project_id`, `segment_id`, `candidate_id`, `work_state` and `notice_state`, and are readable by every role including `Resident`.

Pulling a planned line back out of the bundle is the compensating counterpart. It moves the line's `work_state` from `PLANNED` to `REMOVED` and is permitted only from `PLANNED`; on any other work state it reports `ERR_PROJECT_SEGMENT_NOT_REMOVABLE`. Both adding and removing lines require the project's `delivery_status` to be `DRAFT` or `COORDINATING`; once the bundle has moved beyond coordination its scope is fixed and both operations report `ERR_PROJECT_SCOPE_LOCKED`. On a removal the line's own work state is tested first, so a line that is no longer `PLANNED` under a project that is no longer scoped reports `ERR_PROJECT_SEGMENT_NOT_REMOVABLE` and never `ERR_PROJECT_SCOPE_LOCKED`. Removal decrements the project's `segment_count`, subtracts the line's `line_area_sy` from `planned_area_sy`, decrements the segment's `active_project_count`, recomputes `bundle_base_cents`, `contingency_cents`, `utility_share_cents` and `project_total_estimate_cents`, and increments the project's `version` — restoring the conserved quantities of §2.11 exactly. The freed candidate returns to the pool, and which state it returns to depends on its history: a candidate with a null `override_reason` returns to `RECOMMENDED`, while a candidate carrying an `override_reason` returns to `OVERRIDDEN` with its override intact.

Bundle pricing is a two-step aggregation whose rounding order is pinned. First, each non-removed line's `line_cost_cents` is computed individually as the line's `line_area_sy` multiplied by the cents-per-square-yard base rate for its `treatment_type` and its segment's `functional_class`, then by the same width factor and the same condition penalty defined in §3.3, and that product is rounded to whole cents — with no mobilisation at the line level. Second, the already-rounded line costs are summed. Rounding per line and then summing is the specification; summing the raw products and rounding once at the end differs by cents and is wrong. Removed lines contribute nothing. One mobilisation charge is then added for the whole bundle: 250,000 cents when *every* non-removed line is a `CRACK_SEAL` line, and 850,000 cents otherwise. The sum of construction and mobilisation is the project's `bundle_base_cents`.

Two further amounts ride on that base, and both are keyed on the base rather than on the total — keying them on the total would be circular, since the total contains them. The contingency percentage is 15 percent when `bundle_base_cents` is below 5,000,000 cents ($50,000), 10 percent when it is below 50,000,000 cents ($500,000), and 7 percent at or above that; the comparisons are strict, so a base of exactly 5,000,000 cents takes 10 percent. `contingency_cents` is the base multiplied by that percentage, rounded to whole cents. `utility_share_cents` covers the city's share of trench restoration and is the base multiplied by 3 percent, rounded to whole cents, but only when the project has at least one utility clearance row in `CLEARED_AFTER_WORK`; otherwise it is zero. `project_total_estimate_cents` is the sum of `bundle_base_cents`, `contingency_cents` and `utility_share_cents`. A project holding no non-removed line at all is priced at zero rather than at bare mobilisation: all four of those money fields read zero, which is also how they read on a freshly created project.

Utility coordination opens when a `PavementEngineer` advances the project with an `action` of `START_COORDINATION`, moving `delivery_status` from `DRAFT` to `COORDINATING`. The project must be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_DRAFT`, and must hold at least one non-removed line, else `ERR_PROJECT_HAS_NO_SEGMENTS`. The transition creates four **utility coordination** rows on the project — one each for `WATER`, `SEWER`, `FRANCHISE_GAS` and `FRANCHISE_TELECOM` — each with `coordination_status` `CLEARANCE_REQUESTED`, `round_count` 1, and `planned_work_start`, `planned_work_end`, `work_completed_at` and `response_note` null, and sets `clearance_outstanding_count` to 4.

A `UtilityCoordinator` answers each clearance request with a `response`, optionally accompanied by a `response_note` of 1 to 500 characters that is recorded as the latest note on the row; when the note is omitted the row keeps the note an earlier round left there. A `response` of `CLEAR` from a first-round row moves it to `CLEARED`; a `CLEAR` from a row whose `round_count` is 2 or more — meaning the utility already performed work in an earlier round — moves it to `CLEARED_AFTER_WORK` instead, which is what switches on the project's utility restoration share at the next estimate recompute. Either clearing decrements the project's `clearance_outstanding_count`. A `response` of `CONFLICT` moves the row to `CONFLICT_DECLARED` and records the note, leaving `clearance_outstanding_count` unchanged because the clearance is still outstanding. Both `CLEAR` and `CONFLICT` require the row to be in `CLEARANCE_REQUESTED`, else `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_PENDING`. From `CONFLICT_DECLARED`, a `response` of `SCHEDULE_WORK` moves the row to `WORK_SCHEDULED` and records the utility's `planned_work_start` and `planned_work_end`; it requires the row to be in `CONFLICT_DECLARED`, else `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_IN_CONFLICT`, requires both instants to be supplied with the end strictly after the start, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and requires the planned work to finish on or before the project's `planned_start_date`, because utilities dig before the paver and never after — a window ending later reports `ERR_UTILITY_WORK_AFTER_PAVING`. The coordinator then reports the work finished, moving the row from `WORK_SCHEDULED` to `WORK_COMPLETE` and stamping `work_completed_at` with the logical clock; from any other state that report is rejected with `ERR_COORDINATION_WORK_NOT_SCHEDULED`. Finally a `response` of `RE_REQUEST` returns a `WORK_COMPLETE` row to `CLEARANCE_REQUESTED` and increments `round_count` by 1, closing the cycle; from any other state it reports `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_WORK_COMPLETE`. That cycle — clearance requested, conflict declared, work scheduled, work complete, clearance requested again — may run any number of rounds, and `round_count` is what decides whether the row's terminal state is `CLEARED` or `CLEARED_AFTER_WORK`. Clearance rows can be listed and filtered by `project_id`, `coordination_status` and `utility`.

Advancing with an `action` of `BID_READY` moves the project from `COORDINATING` to `BID_READY` and prices the bundle, recomputing `bundle_base_cents`, `contingency_cents`, `utility_share_cents` and `project_total_estimate_cents`. It requires the project to be in `COORDINATING`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_COORDINATING`; it requires at least one non-removed line, else `ERR_PROJECT_HAS_NO_SEGMENTS`; and it requires `clearance_outstanding_count` to have reached zero, meaning every utility row on the project is either `CLEARED` or `CLEARED_AFTER_WORK`, else `ERR_UTILITY_CLEARANCE_PENDING`.

### 3.6. Resident Notification, Parking and Driveway Access

Residents get notice before their block is closed, and the notification track runs independently of construction on the same line. A `PavementEngineer` drives it with a notice `action` of `SCHEDULE`, `DELIVER` or `RESCHEDULE`.

`SCHEDULE` moves `notice_state` from `PENDING` to `SCHEDULED` and publishes the restriction: the engineer supplies `parking_restriction_start` and `parking_restriction_end` for the no-parking window, and a `driveway_access_plan` of `MAINTAINED` when driveways stay usable throughout, `LIMITED_NIGHTLY` when access is restored each night, or `BLOCKED_WITH_ALT` when access is blocked and an alternative is arranged. All three must be supplied, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The line must be in `PENDING`, else `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_SCHEDULED`. The line's `work_state` must be `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` — a line already at `WORK_COMPLETE`, `ACCEPTED` or `REMOVED` has nothing left to notify and reports `ERR_LINE_NOT_NOTIFIABLE`. The parking window must begin at least 72 hours after the logical clock stands at scheduling time; a shorter runway reports `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT`, and per §2.10 that lead-time test runs *before* the overlap test. The window, expanded by a 24-hour buffer on each side and compared as a **closed** interval, must not overlap the notice window of any other non-removed line on the same segment whose `notice_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED`; an overlap reports `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Only the window being scheduled is expanded — the sibling's window is compared exactly as it stands, so the buffer the rule enforces between two windows is 24 hours and not 48 — and a sibling whose window is not populated cannot conflict. A first schedule leaves `notice_round` at 1.

`DELIVER` moves `notice_state` from `SCHEDULED` to `DELIVERED` and stamps `notice_delivered_at` with the logical clock; from any other notice state it reports `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_SCHEDULED`. There is deliberately no lead-time re-check at delivery: the 72-hour gate was enforced when the window was scheduled. Delivery is what unblocks this line for the project's construction start.

`RESCHEDULE` moves `notice_state` from `DELIVERED` back to `SCHEDULED` when a window has to move, and requires the line to be in `DELIVERED`, else `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_DELIVERED`; it also requires that the line's `work_state` is not yet `ACCEPTED`, and a reschedule aimed at an accepted line reports `ERR_LINE_NOT_NOTIFIABLE`. A reschedule supplies a fresh window that must clear both gates again — at least 72 hours of lead time from the current logical clock, checked first, and no buffered closed-interval overlap with any sibling line's scheduled or delivered window on the same segment. It overwrites `parking_restriction_start`, `parking_restriction_end` and `driveway_access_plan`, clears `notice_delivered_at` back to null, and increments `notice_round` by 1. The delivered-to-scheduled-to-delivered cycle may repeat, and each round re-arms the 72-hour gate from scratch.

### 3.7. Award, Construction and Contractor Performance

Contractors are registered by a `PavementEngineer` with a `legal_name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `max_concurrent_projects` crew capacity between 1 and 10, defaulting to 2. A new firm starts at `standing` `GOOD_STANDING` with `interim_performance_score` 100.0, `final_performance_score` null, and `passed_test_count`, `failed_test_count`, `remediation_event_count`, `days_late_total` and `accepted_project_count` all zero. Contractors may be listed and filtered by `standing`.

Awarding is a `BudgetOfficer` action that moves `delivery_status` from `BID_READY` to `AWARDED`, naming the `contractor_id` receiving the work and the `award_amount_cents` of the contract, which must be a non-negative integer number of cents. Five conditions gate it, and §2.10 fixes their order. The project must be in `BID_READY`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_BID_READY`. The funding scenario must still be `ADOPTED`, else `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_ADOPTED`. The chosen contractor's `standing` must be `GOOD_STANDING`; a firm on `WATCH` or `SUSPENDED` cannot take new work and the award reports `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. Crew capacity must hold: count the contractor's projects whose `delivery_status` is `AWARDED` or `IN_CONSTRUCTION` and whose **closed** interval from `planned_start_date` through `planned_end_date` intersects the target project's closed interval — touching endpoints *do* count as an intersection — and if awarding this project would leave the firm holding more overlapping projects than its `max_concurrent_projects`, report `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Then the two fiscal tests: `award_amount_cents` must not exceed the scenario's `uncommitted_cents`, else `ERR_SCENARIO_FUNDS_EXCEEDED`, and it must lie within 10 percent of the project's `project_total_estimate_cents` in either direction, else `ERR_AWARD_OUTSIDE_ESTIMATE_TOLERANCE` — the tolerance band is inclusive at both ends, so an amount exactly ten percent above or below the estimate is accepted and only a strictly larger deviation is refused. A successful award sets `contractor_id` and `award_amount_cents` on the project, adds the awarded amount to the scenario's `committed_cents`, recomputes the scenario's `uncommitted_cents` as `funding_cents` less `committed_cents`, and increments the scenario's `version` by 1. Recall from §2.1 that the officer who adopted the scenario may not award from it.

Cancelling an award is the compensating action, also a `BudgetOfficer` capability, and returns the project from `AWARDED` to `BID_READY`; from any other delivery state it reports `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_AWARDED`. A `cancellation_reason` of 1 to 500 characters may optionally accompany the request; it is accepted for the caller's convenience and is not persisted on any resource. Cancellation subtracts the project's **stored** `award_amount_cents` from the scenario's `committed_cents` — never a recomputed estimate — recomputes `uncommitted_cents`, clears both `contractor_id` and `award_amount_cents` back to null, and increments the scenario's `version` by 1, restoring the funding conservation invariant of §2.11 exactly.

Advancing with an `action` of `START_CONSTRUCTION` puts crews on the street, moving `delivery_status` from `AWARDED` to `IN_CONSTRUCTION`; from any other delivery state it reports `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_AWARDED`. Two gates protect that moment. Every non-removed line must have `notice_state` `DELIVERED`, else `ERR_NOTICES_NOT_DELIVERED`. And no segment beneath a non-removed line may carry an `APPROVED` cut permit whose `cut_completed_at` is still null — an open trench under the paving crew — else `ERR_OPEN_CUT_ON_SEGMENT`, which per §2.10 is reported in preference to the notice failure when both apply. The transition moves every line whose `work_state` is `PLANNED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, which makes each line eligible for acceptance testing; because both `PLANNED` and `IN_PROGRESS` count as active occupancy, the affected segments' `active_project_count` values do not change.

Substantial completion is declared by the awarded `Contractor` itself and moves `delivery_status` from `IN_CONSTRUCTION` to `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE`. The project must be in construction, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_CONSTRUCTION`; the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the project's `contractor_id`, else `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_CONTRACTOR`; and no line on the project may still be `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_LINES_WORK_INCOMPLETE`. The declared completion instant is not a body field — the request carries no payload and the declaration is simply the logical clock as it stands — and it must be on or after the project's `planned_start_date`; a clock standing earlier reports `ERR_COMPLETION_BEFORE_START`, and that test runs after the line-completeness test rather than before it. The transition stamps `substantial_completion_at` from the logical clock and computes `days_late` as the number of whole days from `planned_end_date` to `substantial_completion_at`, floored at zero — an early or on-time finish records zero. The contractor's `interim_performance_score` is then recomputed, now that schedule lateness on this project is realised, and its `standing` re-derived.

Contractor performance is measured by a three-component weighted score. The quality component is 100.00 when the contractor has no decided tests at all; otherwise it is 100 multiplied by the passed-test count, divided by the sum of the passed and failed counts, rounded to two decimal places. The schedule component is 100 less 2.5 points per late day, clamped into 0.00 through 100.00 and rounded to two decimal places. The remediation component is 100 less 8 points per remediation event, clamped into 0.00 through 100.00 and rounded to two decimal places. The composite score is 0.45 times the quality component plus 0.30 times the schedule component plus 0.25 times the remediation component, rounded to one decimal place. The rounding order is pinned: each component rounds to two decimal places first, and only then does the weighted sum round to one. Rounding only at the end is wrong.

Two variants of that score are maintained side by side, and they are allowed to disagree. The **interim** variant, stored as `interim_performance_score`, is recomputed on every acceptance-test decision, on substantial completion and on final acceptance, and it is the variant that derives `standing`. It counts test decisions and remediation events across *all* of the contractor's projects regardless of `delivery_status`, but counts schedule lateness only for projects that have reached `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` or `ACCEPTED` — an in-flight project contributes zero late days even when the logical clock has already passed its `planned_end_date`. It is therefore optimistic on schedule and pessimistic on quality. The **final** variant, stored as `final_performance_score`, is written on project acceptance and is restricted to projects whose `delivery_status` is `ACCEPTED`: only tests on lines of accepted projects feed its quality component, only remediation events on accepted projects feed its remediation component, and `days_late_total` — which sums `days_late` over accepted projects only — feeds its schedule component. It remains null while `accepted_project_count` is zero. For a contractor with one clean accepted project and one in-flight failing project, the final score stays at 100.0 while the interim score drops.

`standing` is derived from `interim_performance_score` alone: `GOOD_STANDING` when the interim score is at least 80.0, `WATCH` when it is at least 60.0 but below 80.0, and `SUSPENDED` when it is below 60.0. Every recompute re-derives the band, so a firm can move `GOOD_STANDING → WATCH`, `WATCH → SUSPENDED`, or `GOOD_STANDING → SUSPENDED` directly when a single decision drops the score past both floors; and it can recover as passing tests accumulate, moving `SUSPENDED → WATCH` and `WATCH → GOOD_STANDING`. Only `GOOD_STANDING` permits new awards; `WATCH` and `SUSPENDED` both refuse them with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`, while awards already in hand continue undisturbed.

A dedicated computed read publishes the whole scorecard: the `contractor_id`, the current `standing`, both composite scores, and each score's component breakdown as `interim_quality_component`, `interim_schedule_component`, `interim_remediation_component`, `final_quality_component`, `final_schedule_component` and `final_remediation_component`, together with the underlying `passed_test_count`, `failed_test_count`, `remediation_event_count`, `days_late_total` and `accepted_project_count`. It is a computed read and carries none of the audit fields of §2.5 — no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` — and the three `final_*` components are always published as computed numbers even while `final_performance_score` itself is null, which for a firm with no accepted project means three components at 100.00 beside a null composite. The scorecard is readable by the firm itself as well as by city staff, and neither it nor the single-contractor read is scoped to the caller: a `Contractor` whose `X-User-ID` resolves to a registered firm may read any firm's row and any firm's scorecard, since the ownership rule of §2.1 guards only the project actions and the single-project read.

### 3.8. Quality Acceptance, Remediation and Final Acceptance of the Pavement

Pavement quality acceptance runs on the project's second, independent state dimension. An `Inspector` submits an **acceptance test** for one line, naming the `project_segment_id` under test and reporting three measurements — `core_density_pct`, the in-place density as a percentage of the reference maximum, between 70 and 105 inclusive at one decimal place; `iri_in_per_mi`, the as-built smoothness in inches per mile, at least zero and at most 400; and `thickness_in`, the measured core thickness in inches, strictly greater than zero at two decimal places — plus the `witness_inspector_user_id` of the inspector who witnessed the coring. That witness may not later decide this test, and may not sign the enclosing project's final acceptance. A test may only be submitted for a line that is actually on the street: `work_state` must be `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, since a `PLANNED` line has no pavement to core and a `WORK_COMPLETE`, `ACCEPTED` or `REMOVED` line has already been signed off — anything else reports `ERR_LINE_NOT_TESTABLE`. A line may not carry two undecided tests at once; if any test on the line is still `SUBMITTED`, the new submission reports `ERR_TEST_ALREADY_PENDING`.

Submission assigns `test_round`: 1 for the first test on the line, and one greater than the prior maximum for each retest. Submitting a higher round supersedes the prior decided round — any test on the line whose `test_status` is `FAILED` or `RETEST_ORDERED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. When the superseded test was `FAILED`, the line returns from `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` to `IN_PROGRESS` and the project's `open_remediation_count` decreases by 1; when it was `RETEST_ORDERED` there is no work-state change, because the line was already `IN_PROGRESS`. The first test submitted anywhere on a project moves the project's `quality_status` from `NOT_STARTED` to `TESTING`, leaving `delivery_status` untouched — the two dimensions advance independently — and if the project's quality had bounced to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, it returns to `TESTING` once `open_remediation_count` reaches zero again. The contractor's `remediation_event_count` is *not* decremented when a line recovers: the event is historical.

The composite pay factor is computed at submission and frozen. The density factor is 1.00 when `core_density_pct` is at least 96.0, 0.98 when it is at least 94.0, 0.95 when it is at least 92.0, and 0.00 below that. The smoothness factor is 1.02 when `iri_in_per_mi` is at most 60.0, 1.00 when it is at most 95.0, 0.97 when it is at most 120.0, and 0.00 above that. The thickness factor is 1.00 when `thickness_in` is at least the line's `design_thickness_in`, 0.96 when it is at least the design thickness less the 0.25-inch tolerance, and 0.00 below that. A factor of 0.00 marks a failing component: if any of the three is 0.00, then `computed_pay_factor` is 0.0000 and `computed_outcome` is `FAIL`. Otherwise `computed_pay_factor` is the product of the three factors, capped at 1.02, rounded to four decimal places — the cap applies after multiplication, so a test with bonus smoothness, full density and full thickness yields exactly 1.0200 — and `computed_outcome` is `PASS` when the pay factor is at least 0.95 and `FAIL` otherwise. Every test starts with `test_status` `SUBMITTED`, `decided_by_user_id` null and `decision_note` null, and each submission increments the line's `test_count`. Tests may be listed and filtered by `project_segment_id` and `test_status`.

A second `Inspector` then decides the test with a `decision` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `RETEST`, optionally with a `decision_note` of 1 to 500 characters, which is recorded on all three paths — the no-fault `RETEST` path included — and left untouched when the note is omitted. Every decision requires `test_status` to be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_TEST_ALREADY_DECIDED`, and requires the deciding `X-User-ID` to differ from the test's `witness_inspector_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The pay-factor arithmetic is binding on the pass path only: deciding `PASS` on a test whose `computed_outcome` is `FAIL` reports `ERR_TEST_DECISION_CONFLICTS_COMPUTED`, while `FAIL` and `RETEST` remain available regardless of the computed outcome.

A `PASS` decision moves the test to `PASSED`, records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note`, moves the line's `work_state` from `IN_PROGRESS` to `WORK_COMPLETE` provided the decided test carries the highest `test_round` on that line, and copies the test's `computed_pay_factor` onto the line's `pay_factor`. The contractor's `passed_test_count` increments, its `interim_performance_score` is recomputed and its `standing` re-derived. The project's quality dimension may then bubble from `TESTING` to `QUALITY_ACCEPTED`, which happens exactly when `open_remediation_count` is zero and every non-removed line on the project has reached `WORK_COMPLETE`; reaching `QUALITY_ACCEPTED` is what unlocks final acceptance.

A `FAIL` decision moves the test to `FAILED`, records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note`, and drives a four-level cascade. **First**, the line — again, provided the decided test holds the highest `test_round` on it — moves from `IN_PROGRESS` to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` with its `pay_factor` set to the failed test's `computed_pay_factor`. **Second**, the parent project's `open_remediation_count` increases by 1 and its `quality_status` moves from `TESTING` to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, recounted as the number of its lines sitting in `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`. **Third**, the contractor's `remediation_event_count` and `failed_test_count` each increase by 1 and its `interim_performance_score` is recomputed. **Fourth**, because the interim score dropped, the contractor's `standing` is re-derived into whichever band the new score implies — `GOOD_STANDING → WATCH` when the score lands at or above 60.0 but below 80.0, `GOOD_STANDING → SUSPENDED` when a contractor's only decided test fails and the score lands below 60.0, or `WATCH → SUSPENDED`. A line's `pay_factor` always reflects the decided test with the highest `test_round` on that line, whether that test passed or failed, and is null until some test on the line has been decided.

A `RETEST` decision is the no-fault path — the inspector is rejecting the sample, not the work. It moves the test to `RETEST_ORDERED`, records `decided_by_user_id`, leaves the line at `IN_PROGRESS`, and moves no contractor counter at all. `PASSED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the terminal test states; a `FAILED` or `RETEST_ORDERED` test is superseded when a higher round is submitted on the same line, and the quality dimension can bounce from `TESTING` to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` and back once per failed line.

Final acceptance belongs to an `Inspector` and moves `delivery_status` from `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` to `ACCEPTED`, optionally accompanied by an `acceptance_note` of 1 to 500 characters, which — like the award's `cancellation_reason` — is accepted but is not persisted on any resource. The project must be `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE`. Its `quality_status` must be `QUALITY_ACCEPTED` — an outstanding remediation blocks acceptance even though the delivery dimension is ready, and this is the one place the two dimensions gate each other — else `ERR_REMEDIATION_OUTSTANDING`. Every non-removed line must satisfy acceptance on its own terms: a `CRACK_SEAL` line needs no acceptance test at all, while every `MILL_AND_OVERLAY` or `RECONSTRUCTION` line must hold a `PASSED` test at its highest `test_round`, else `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_TESTS_INCOMPLETE`. And the signing inspector's `X-User-ID` must not appear as the `witness_inspector_user_id` of any acceptance test on the project, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock and records the signer as `accepting_inspector_user_id`, then runs the system's widest cascade. **First**, every line on the project whose `work_state` is `WORK_COMPLETE` moves to `ACCEPTED`. **Second**, because each of those lines was accepted, its segment absorbs the new pavement: `last_treatment_type` records the line's `treatment_type`, `last_treatment_date` is set to the project's `accepted_at`, `baseline_condition_index` is reset to the smaller of 100.0 and the sum of the segment's current `condition_index` and the executing candidate's `treatment_pci_gain`, `condition_index` is set to that same value, `condition_index_source` becomes `MODEL_PROJECTED`, `condition_band` is recomputed, `active_project_count` is decremented, and `moratorium_until` is set to `accepted_at` plus the moratorium length for the treatment placed — one calendar year after a `CRACK_SEAL`, three calendar years after a `MILL_AND_OVERLAY`, and five calendar years after a `RECONSTRUCTION`. A calendar year here advances the year component and holds the month, day and time of day fixed, so the expiry inherits the acceptance instant's clock time exactly; the single date that has no counterpart, 29 February, falls back to 28 February of the target year. **Third**, because `moratorium_until` advanced on those segments, every cut permit on them whose `permit_status` is `PENDING` moves to `MORATORIUM_REVIEW`, and its `moratorium_remaining_days`, `fee_multiplier` and `degradation_fee_cents` are recomputed against the new moratorium. **Fourth**, because those segments were resurfaced under this project, every complaint on them whose `request_status` is `DEFERRED_TO_CAPITAL` and whose `capital_project_id` is this project moves to `CLOSED_BY_CAPITAL` with `closure_code` `CAPITAL_RESURFACED` and `closed_at` set to `accepted_at`, its `sla_breached` flag recomputed, and the segment's `open_service_request_count` decremented. Alongside the cascade, the contractor's `accepted_project_count` increases by 1, `days_late_total` increases by this project's `days_late`, both `final_performance_score` and `interim_performance_score` are recomputed, and `standing` is re-derived.

### 3.9. Life on Finished Pavement: Cut Permits, Moratorium and Complaint Response

New pavement is a capital asset, and the moratorium is how the city protects it. A `UtilityCoordinator` applies for a **cut permit** naming the `segment_id` to be cut, the `applicant_utility` making the request, the requested cut window as `requested_start` and `requested_end` (the end strictly after the start), the trench `cut_area_sqft` (strictly greater than zero), and an `emergency` flag that defaults to false and is set true for leaks, gas odours and similar urgent repairs.

The permit's initial state branches on the segment's moratorium. When the segment's `moratorium_until` is null, or has already passed the logical clock, or when the application is flagged as an emergency, the permit opens at `permit_status` `PENDING`. When the segment's `moratorium_until` is still in the future and the application is not an emergency, the permit opens at `MORATORIUM_REVIEW` instead, requiring a written waiver before it can be approved.

Two rules gate the application itself. Worker safety comes first: a permit may not be created for a segment carrying an active construction line — any line on that segment whose `work_state` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` — and that rejection, `ERR_CUT_BLOCKED_ACTIVE_CONSTRUCTION`, outranks every roster, moratorium and fee consideration. Then the roster check: the `applicant_utility` must appear on the city's coordination roster, meaning some utility coordination row somewhere in the system carries the same utility value, else `ERR_UTILITY_NOT_ON_COORDINATION_ROSTER`. `applicant_utility` is itself an enum over the four utilities `WATER`, `SEWER`, `FRANCHISE_GAS` and `FRANCHISE_TELECOM`, so a value outside that set is a shape error reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at step 6 and never reaches the roster check; the roster rule bites only on a recognised utility for which no coordination row has ever been opened.

The degradation fee is computed at application and recomputed by the acceptance cascade of §3.8 while the permit is still `PENDING`. First, `moratorium_remaining_days` is zero when the segment's `moratorium_until` is null; otherwise it is the whole seconds from the logical clock to `moratorium_until` divided by the 86,400 seconds in a day with the fraction discarded, floored at zero. Second, a tier multiplier follows from that remaining count: 3.0 when at least 1,096 days remain, 2.0 when at least 366 remain, 1.5 when at least 1 remains, and 1.0 when none remains. The comparisons are inclusive, so exactly 366 remaining days takes 2.0 while 365 takes 1.5. Third, an emergency application gets relief of half a point off the tier multiplier, floored at 1.0, and the result is stored as `fee_multiplier`; a non-emergency application stores the tier multiplier unchanged. Fourth, the base fee is the trench area multiplied by 850 cents per square foot, rounded to whole cents, but never less than the 45,000-cent ($450) minimum. `degradation_fee_cents` is that base fee multiplied by `fee_multiplier`, rounded to whole cents.

A `PavementEngineer` — never the applicant utility, since the city decides its own cuts — decides the permit with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`. A permit may only be decided while `permit_status` is `PENDING` or `MORATORIUM_REVIEW`; an already-approved or already-denied permit reports `ERR_CUT_PERMIT_ALREADY_DECIDED`. Approving a `MORATORIUM_REVIEW` permit requires a non-empty `waiver_reason` of up to 500 characters, which is recorded on the permit; without it the approval reports `ERR_MORATORIUM_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. A `PENDING` permit needs no waiver, and its `waiver_reason` stays null even when one is supplied; a denial likewise never records one. Approval from either state is refused while the segment carries a line in active construction, again with `ERR_CUT_BLOCKED_ACTIVE_CONSTRUCTION` — and on this decision the waiver requirement is tested first, so approving a `MORATORIUM_REVIEW` permit with no waiver over a segment under active construction reports `ERR_MORATORIUM_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. Approval moves the permit to `APPROVED`, stamps `decided_at` and freezes `degradation_fee_cents` at its current value. Denial from either state moves the permit to `DENIED` and stamps `decided_at`; the computed fee stays on the record for audit but is not charged.

An approved cut leaves an open trench, and an open trench blocks the paver. The `UtilityCoordinator` reports the trench backfilled and restored, which stamps `cut_completed_at` from the logical clock and releases the construction-start block of §3.7. That report is permitted only once and only on an approved permit; a permit that is not `APPROVED`, or one whose `cut_completed_at` is already set, reports `ERR_CUT_NOT_APPROVED_OR_ALREADY_COMPLETE`. Permits may be listed and filtered by `segment_id`, `permit_status` and `applicant_utility`.

The other way the public experiences finished pavement is by complaining about it, and settlement over an old trench is one of the things they complain about. A `Resident` — or a `PavementEngineer` taking a report on their behalf — files a **service request** against a segment, naming the `segment_id`, a `complaint_type` of `POTHOLE`, `CRACKING`, `PONDING` or `UTILITY_CUT_SETTLEMENT`, a `reported_severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, and a `location_note` of 1 to 200 characters describing where on the block the problem sits. The request opens at `request_status` `NEW` with `capital_project_id`, `triaged_at`, `closed_at` and `closure_code` all null, and the segment's `open_service_request_count` increments.

The service-level clock starts immediately. `sla_due_at` is the request's `created_at` plus the response allowance for its severity: 72 hours for `HIGH`, 168 hours for `MEDIUM`, and 720 hours for `LOW`. The `sla_breached` flag compares a reference instant against that deadline — the reference is `closed_at` once the request has reached a closed state, and the logical clock while it is still open — and is true only when the reference is *strictly* later than `sla_due_at`; landing exactly on the deadline is not a breach. The flag is re-evaluated on every read and again at closure.

Triage is a `PavementEngineer` action and branches. A `disposition` of `PATCH` moves `request_status` from `NEW` to `TRIAGED_PATCH`, stamps `triaged_at`, and leaves `capital_project_id` null — the complaint stays with the maintenance crew. A `disposition` of `DEFER_TO_CAPITAL` moves `request_status` from `NEW` to `DEFERRED_TO_CAPITAL`, stamps `triaged_at`, and sets `capital_project_id` to the supplied `target_project_id` — mandatory on this branch and ignored entirely on the `PATCH` branch, which neither requires it nor records it — attaching the complaint to a resurfacing project so that it closes automatically when the street is rebuilt rather than being closed by a patch crew. The deferral target must be eligible: a project whose `delivery_status` is anything other than `ACCEPTED` and which contains a non-removed line on this request's `segment_id`; anything else reports `ERR_TARGET_PROJECT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Triage requires the request to still be `NEW`, else `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_NOT_NEW`, and — per §2.11 — never changes the segment's `open_service_request_count`.

Closure takes a `closure_code`. `PATCHED` closes a `TRIAGED_PATCH` request to `CLOSED_PATCHED`; attempted from any other open state it reports `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_NOT_TRIAGED`, so a `NEW` or `DEFERRED_TO_CAPITAL` request can only be closed as a duplicate — but the already-closed test runs first, so `PATCHED` aimed at a request that has already reached any closed state reports `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_ALREADY_CLOSED` and never `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_NOT_TRIAGED`. `DUPLICATE` closes a request in `NEW`, `TRIAGED_PATCH` or `DEFERRED_TO_CAPITAL` to `CLOSED_DUPLICATE`, leaving any `capital_project_id` as it stands for audit. Either closure stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `sla_breached` against `closed_at`, and decrements the segment's `open_service_request_count`. Closing an already-closed request reports `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The third closed state, `CLOSED_BY_CAPITAL` with `closure_code` `CAPITAL_RESURFACED`, is never reached by a client request — it arrives only through the acceptance cascade of §3.8. Complaints may be listed and filtered by `segment_id`, `capital_project_id`, `request_status` and `reported_severity`, and residents may read their own complaint views — a request records no reporter identity, so a `Resident` caller reads and lists the whole complaint collection rather than a subset scoped to them.
