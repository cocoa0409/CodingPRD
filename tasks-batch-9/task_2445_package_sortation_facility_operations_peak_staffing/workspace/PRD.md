# Package Sortation Peak Staffing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A parcel sort center is a building that turns a stream of inbound trailers into a stream of outbound trailers, and it does so under two pressures that pull against each other. The physical pressure is throughput: trailers arrive on a schedule, dock doors and sortation chutes are finite, jams stop lines mid-shift, and every outbound trailer has a cut time after which its departure is worthless. The human pressure is labor: peak season doubles volume, headcount has to be sized from a volume forecast before the volume actually shows up, seasonal hires churn, and the flex staffing pool that absorbs the gap is a shared, exhaustible resource that several shift plans compete for.

This service is the operational system of record for both pressures at once. It models the facility surface (dock doors, sortation chutes, destination lanes), the inbound pipeline (trailer appointments, unload sequencing, jam recovery), the labor pipeline (shift plans, hiring, onboarding and training throughput, attrition scoring, line staffing, attendance), the outbound pipeline (trailer loading by size class, cube utilization, sealing, dispatch), and the exception and safety pipelines that cut across all of them (damaged, unlabeled, oversized and mis-sorted packages; injury reports). Every one of these pipelines writes into a small set of derived aggregates on the sort center itself — an operational posture that throttles the building, a flex pool ledger, and a package conservation ledger — so that a jam on one line, a serious injury on another, and a no-show on a third all converge on the same governing state.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read before anything else. First, the service is **deterministic and clock-driven**: all time comparisons read a server-side logical clock that tests drive explicitly, never wall-clock time. Second, the service is **conservative with shared resources**: headcount drawn from the flex pool and packages moved between counters are both tracked as ledgers that must balance after every request, and writes against a shared parent (a chute, a center) must pin the parent's version so a caller cannot spend a resource it has not observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `FacilityAdmin`, `InboundCoordinator`, `SortSupervisor`, `LaborPlanner`, `OpsManager`, and `Worker`. They own disjoint families of resources:

- A `FacilityAdmin` owns the physical surface of the building — sort centers, dock doors, sortation chutes, destination lanes, and the center's automation utilization.
- An `InboundCoordinator` runs the inbound yard and the unload sequence: trailer arrivals, unload starts, jam holds, and induction of manifests into work-in-process.
- A `SortSupervisor` runs the sort floor and the outbound dock: mapping destinations onto chutes, scanning packages into trailers, sealing trailers, and resolving package exceptions.
- A `LaborPlanner` builds shift plans, hires and onboards workers, and staffs the lines. A planner never approves a plan.
- An `OpsManager` holds the money and the safety authority: plan approval, rejection and closure, dispatch release, and injury-report closure.
- A `Worker` is an hourly associate acting as itself, able only to clock in and out of its own assignments.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the associate role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityAdmin`, `InboundCoordinator`, `SortSupervisor`, `LaborPlanner`, or `OpsManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `submitted_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a shift plan, where the corresponding transition writes it) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Worker`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Worker` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Worker` compare the resource's worker foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a `Worker` clocks in or out of a staff assignment, the assignment's `worker_id` must equal `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Segregation of duties is enforced on the shift-plan approval fork. The opaque operator identifier that submitted a plan is retained on the plan, and an `OpsManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals that stored submitter may neither approve nor reject the plan; both attempts are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Because operator identifiers are opaque, this check is a pure string comparison against the stored submitter, not a lookup.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two pagination query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1, and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest row appears first and the ordering is stable across pages. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside `meta`: `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Endpoint-specific filters (for example filtering trailer appointments by `status` or workers by `hire_class`) narrow the matched set before `total_count` is computed.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this service carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits; `created_at` is stamped once at creation and `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the row suffers as a downstream consequence of somebody else's request. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Every entity type in this system — sort centers, dock doors, sortation chutes, destination lanes, chute assignments, trailer appointments, shift plans, workers, staff assignments, outbound loads, package exceptions and injury reports — carries all four.

All temporal reasoning in the service reads a single server-side **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until a caller moves it, every temporal comparison in the system — trailer appointment windows, chute assignment windows, shift windows, dispatch cut times, worker tenure — evaluates against that default. Any of the five operator roles may move the clock by supplying the target instant as `current_utc`; moving the clock mutates no entity and recomputes no posture, it merely repositions "now", and the response simply echoes the `current_utc` now in effect. Consequently, an operation that is legal at one clock position may become illegal after the clock advances (a dispatch is the clearest case), and worker tenure grows only when the clock is moved forward.

Timestamps written by transitions — `arrived_at`, `unloaded_at`, `checked_in_at`, `checked_out_at`, `dispatched_at`, `resolved_at`, `last_posture_change_at` — are always the logical clock's value at the instant the transition succeeds, never a client-supplied value. Two timestamps are exceptions because they describe facts outside the system's control and are therefore supplied by the client: `occurred_at` on an injury report and `dispatch_cut_time` on an outbound load. Both must be parseable RFC 3339 UTC instants; if they are not, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).

### 2.5. The Operational Posture Gate

Each sort center carries a derived aggregate field, `ops_posture`, whose value is always one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, or `FROZEN`. It is never written directly by a client. Instead it is recomputed by the server at the end of every request that changes one of its inputs: the number of jams currently held, the number of open serious injuries, any active lane's pressure ratio, or the center's automation utilization. The inputs are themselves derived counters on the center: `open_jam_count` is the number of trailer appointments of that center currently in `JAM_HELD`; `open_serious_injury_count` is the number of injury reports of that center whose `severity` is `SERIOUS` and whose `status` is `OPEN`; and the center's peak lane pressure is the largest `lane_pressure_ratio` among destination lanes of that center whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, taken as zero when the center has no active lane.

The derivation is evaluated top-down, and the first matching clause wins:

1. The posture is `FROZEN` when at least one serious injury is open, or when the number of held jams has reached three.
2. Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` when at least one jam is held, or when the peak lane pressure among active lanes is greater than or equal to 1.50, or when the center's `automation_utilization_pct` is below 45.
3. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

Because the `FROZEN` test is applied first, a center with one open serious injury is frozen even when no jam is held and every lane is quiet; and a center with three held jams is frozen rather than merely restricted. The pressure comparison uses the stored `lane_pressure_ratio` value as already rounded to four decimal places, not an unrounded intermediate.

Whenever a recomputation changes the stored value, the center records the transition: `ops_posture` takes the new value, `last_posture_change_at` is set to the logical clock, and the center's `version` increases by 1. `last_posture_change_at` is null until the first posture change ever occurs on that center. All six directed changes are reachable and none is terminal — `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` (typically the first jam hold, a lane crossing the pressure ratio, or an automation downgrade), `RESTRICTED` back to `OPEN` (the jam cleared, pressure back under the ratio, automation restored to at least 45, and no serious injury open), `OPEN` to `FROZEN` and `RESTRICTED` to `FROZEN` (a serious injury filed, or the third concurrent jam hold), and `FROZEN` back to `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` back to `OPEN` (typically the closure of the serious injury report, landing on `RESTRICTED` if a jam is still held or pressure is still high and on `OPEN` if nothing else is outstanding).

The posture is a gate on child operations, not a label. While a center is `FROZEN`, the following operations are refused with `ERR_CENTER_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): recording a trailer arrival, starting an unload, completing an unload, scanning packages into an outbound load, dispatching an outbound load, approving a shift plan, creating a staff assignment, checking a worker in, and resolving a package exception. Everything that helps the building recover is deliberately still permitted while frozen: recording a jam hold, resuming an unload after a jam, filing and closing injury reports, creating a package exception, sealing or closing an outbound load, closing a shift plan, and all facility administration and labor-pipeline paperwork. A `RESTRICTED` posture blocks nothing; its effect is fiscal and it is felt at the next headcount sizing, where the requirement is inflated by a posture multiplier of 1.00 for `OPEN`, 1.10 for `RESTRICTED`, and 1.25 for `FROZEN`.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Shared Parents

Three write operations spend a resource that lives on a shared parent row, and all three require the client to pin the parent's `version` as a non-optional input field. No other operation in this API accepts or requires a version field.

Creating a chute assignment must carry `expected_chute_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target sortation chute. If that value does not equal the chute's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CHUTE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The chute's version advances whenever another writer re-chutes a destination with the supersede flag (which legitimately retires the prior assignment at the new window's start) or whenever a jam-hold cascade has flipped the chute to `CONGESTED`; in both cases the requesting client's picture of the chute's occupancy is stale, and the reject prevents it from layering a new destination on top of a chute state it never saw.

Approving a shift plan must carry `expected_center_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the owning sort center, and closing a shift plan must do the same. A mismatch against the center's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CENTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Both operations move headcount in or out of the center's shared flex pool: an approve sizes its draw against the pool it read, and a close reconciles its release against the pool it read. Another plan's approval, a no-show flex top-up, or a posture change will all have advanced the center's version in the meantime, so the pinned value is the only evidence that the caller is spending a pool it has actually observed. Note that approving a *different* plan is a perfectly legitimate concurrent action — that is precisely why the stale view is caught by version pinning rather than by a state precondition.

### 2.7. Nested Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

Several resources are addressed as children of a sort center, and their path segments are resolved strictly left to right: the center named by the leading segment must exist before the child identifier is even considered. A center that does not exist yields `ERR_SORT_CENTER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and the same error is produced when a request body names a `sort_center_id` that does not resolve. Beyond the center, existence failures are grouped by resource family so that the error identity itself tells the caller which family failed: a missing dock door, sortation chute, destination lane or chute assignment yields `ERR_FACILITY_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; a missing trailer appointment yields `ERR_TRAILER_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a missing shift plan yields `ERR_SHIFT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; a missing worker or staff assignment yields `ERR_LABOR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; a missing outbound load or package exception yields `ERR_OUTBOUND_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All five are HTTP 404. Within one request, referenced identifiers are validated left to right through the path first and then in the declaration order of the body fields.

Because many operations carry several checks that could each fail, the order of evaluation is fixed. Implementations must evaluate in exactly this precedence, and the first failing check is the one reported:

1. Header authentication — both headers present and the role recognized, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization for the called operation, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Request-body shape on every creating or modifying operation — required fields present, enum values inside their declared sets, integer counts non-negative, percentages within 0 through 100 inclusive, cube and count fields strictly positive where the field requires it, and any pinned version field an integer — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. Existence of the sort center named by path or body, else `ERR_SORT_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Existence of referenced dock doors, sortation chutes, destination lanes and chute assignments, else `ERR_FACILITY_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. This applies to updating a dock door, reading one chute, reading one destination lane, listing and creating chute assignments, creating a trailer appointment, creating a staff assignment, creating an outbound load, filing an injury report, and creating a package exception.
6. Existence of the referenced trailer appointment, on every trailer-appointment sub-operation and on package-exception creation, else `ERR_TRAILER_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
7. Existence of the referenced shift plan, on every shift-plan sub-operation and on staff-assignment creation, else `ERR_SHIFT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.
8. Existence of the referenced worker or staff assignment — reading one worker, recording an onboarding event, every staff-assignment sub-operation, and filing an injury report — else `ERR_LABOR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
9. Existence of the referenced outbound load or package exception, on every sub-operation of either, else `ERR_OUTBOUND_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
10. Assignment ownership for the `Worker` role on check-in and check-out, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER`.
11. Time-window well-formedness on trailer appointment creation, chute assignment creation, shift plan creation, outbound load creation and injury report filing, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.
12. Uniqueness within the owning scope on facility and plan creation, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACILITY_RESOURCE`.
13. Trailer code uniqueness on trailer appointment and outbound load creation, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAILER_CODE`.
14. Trailer appointment state, else `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`.
15. Shift plan state — on submit, approve, reject, revise and close, and on staff-assignment creation — else `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_PLAN_STATE`.
16. Outbound load state across both of its state dimensions — on scanning, closing the load, sealing and dispatching — else `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`.
17. Staff assignment state on check-in, check-out and no-show, else `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.
18. Package exception state on resolve, and injury report state on close, else `ERR_INVALID_EXCEPTION_STATE`.
19. Chute version pinning on chute assignment creation, else `ERR_STALE_CHUTE_VERSION`.
20. Center version pinning on shift plan approve and close, else `ERR_STALE_CENTER_VERSION`.
21. Segregation of duties on shift plan approve and reject, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
22. The frozen-posture gate on the nine gated operations listed in §2.5, else `ERR_CENTER_FROZEN`.
23. The line headcount ceiling on shift plan approve and on staff assignment creation, else `ERR_LINE_HEADCOUNT_EXCEEDED`.
24. Ergonomic rotation and injury clearance on staff assignment creation, else `ERR_ERGONOMIC_ROTATION_REQUIRED`.
25. Worker readiness on staff assignment creation, on check-in, and on the certifying onboarding event, else `ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED`.
26. Dock door serviceability and kind matching on trailer appointment and outbound load creation, else `ERR_DOOR_OUT_OF_SERVICE`.
27. Door window conflict on trailer appointment creation, else `ERR_DOOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
28. Chute concurrency capacity on chute assignment creation, else `ERR_CHUTE_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`.
29. Jam retry limit on recording a jam hold, else `ERR_JAM_RETRY_LIMIT_REACHED`.
30. Trailer cube capacity on scanning packages, else `ERR_TRAILER_CUBE_EXCEEDED`.
31. Exception source sufficiency on package exception creation, else `ERR_EXCEPTION_COUNT_EXCEEDS_SOURCE`.
32. Seal state at dispatch, else `ERR_LOAD_NOT_SEALED`.
33. Dispatch cut time at dispatch, else `ERR_DISPATCH_CUT_PASSED`.
34. Lane-to-chute referential coverage on outbound load creation and on package exception resolve, else `ERR_LANE_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_CHUTE`.
35. Cross-center integrity on staff assignment creation, chute assignment creation, outbound load creation and injury report filing, else `ERR_WORKER_CENTER_MISMATCH`.
36. Non-empty reroute chute pool on package exception resolve, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CHUTE`.
37. Flex pool sufficiency, else `ERR_FLEX_POOL_EXHAUSTED`.
38. Labor budget sufficiency on shift plan approve, else `ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

The ordering has visible consequences that the narrative sections revisit. A frozen center surfaces as `ERR_CENTER_FROZEN` rather than being masked by a downstream fiscal check; a stale pinned version surfaces before segregation of duties and before any fiscal check, so a stale approve never reports a budget problem; and both are evaluated only after the plan's own state has been confirmed, so approving an already-approved plan reports `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_PLAN_STATE` and never a version mismatch.

### 2.8. The Two Conservation Ledgers

Two invariants must hold after every request, and they are the fastest way to check an implementation against this specification.

The **package conservation identity** governs the center's five package counters and its lanes. After every request that moves packages, the center's `packages_inducted_count` must equal the sum of its `wip_package_count`, its `exception_hold_count`, its `disposed_package_count`, and the total `sorted_package_count` across *all* destination lanes of that center regardless of lane status (a closed lane still holds the packages already sorted into it). Each package movement preserves the identity by construction: completing an unload adds the manifest quantity to both `packages_inducted_count` and `wip_package_count`; scanning packages onto an outbound load moves that quantity out of `wip_package_count` and into the destination lane's `sorted_package_count`; creating a package exception moves its quantity into `exception_hold_count`, debiting the lane's `sorted_package_count` when the exception names a source lane and the center's `wip_package_count` when it does not; resolving with a reroute moves the quantity out of `exception_hold_count` into the target lane's `sorted_package_count` while also adding it to `missort_corrected_count`; resolving with a disposal moves it out of `exception_hold_count` into `disposed_package_count`; and resolving with a relabel moves it out of `exception_hold_count` back into `wip_package_count`. Note that `missort_corrected_count` is an audit tally of re-laned packages and does not participate in the identity, and that a mis-sort exception followed by a reroute resolution is a forward action and its compensating action: together they leave the identity exactly as it was.

The **flex pool conservation identity** governs headcount. A center's `flex_pool_total` is supplied once at creation and never changes afterwards; at all times it must equal the sum of `flex_pool_available` and `flex_pool_reserved`. Furthermore, `flex_pool_reserved` must equal the sum of `flex_draw_count` across the center's shift plans whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `AT_RISK`; plans in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `CLOSED` hold no reservation at all. Approval reserves the plan's draw, a no-show top-up reserves an additional amount, and closure releases exactly the plan's accumulated `flex_draw_count` back to `flex_pool_available`. These are asserted after every request that reserves or releases flex headcount: plan approval, a staff-assignment no-show, and plan closure.

### 2.9. Precision, Rounding and Interval Semantics

All rounding in this service is **round half up**, and the number of decimal places is fixed per field rather than per caller.

- Monetary amounts are whole integer cents: `labor_budget_cents`, `planned_labor_cents`, and `actual_labor_cents`. Cost-per-package figures — `provisional_cost_per_package_cents` and `final_cost_per_package_cents` — are cents carried to two decimal places, because a fraction of a cent per package is meaningful at parcel volumes.
- Durations are hours to two decimal places: `shift_hours` and `worked_hours`.
- Cube figures are cubic feet to two decimal places: `trailer_cube_cuft` and `cube_used_cuft`; `cube_utilization_pct` is a percentage to two decimal places. `automation_utilization_pct` is a percentage to two decimal places as well.
- `lane_pressure_ratio` and `attrition_risk_score` carry four decimal places.
- Headcount figures — `required_headcount`, `committed_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, `unfilled_gap`, `flex_draw_count` — and all package counters are exact integers that never go negative.

Where a calculation sums several rounded lines, the specification pins whether rounding happens per line or on the total, because the two orders produce different values at parcel scale; the workflow sections state the pinned order for each affected calculation and it must be followed literally.

Interval semantics are equally pinned. Every window in this system — a trailer appointment's `window_start` to `window_end`, a chute assignment's window, a shift plan's `shift_window_start` to `shift_window_end` — is half-open: the start instant is inside the window and the end instant is outside it. In every case the end must be strictly after the start, and a request violating that is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. Two rules build on the half-open convention with deliberately different treatments of adjacency: dock door scheduling inflates each interval by the door's turnaround buffer on both sides, so two appointments that merely touch at an instant *do* conflict, while chute concurrency applies no buffer at all, so two assignments that touch at an instant do *not* overlap. The dispatch cut time comparison is strict: a logical clock exactly equal to `dispatch_cut_time` is already too late. The trailer cube comparison is not strict: filling a trailer exactly to its `trailer_cube_cuft` is accepted and only a strictly greater result is refused.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline Across Aggregates

Many operations in this service mutate more than the resource named in the request. A jam hold reaches four levels deep — appointment, chute, chute assignments, destination lanes, and finally the center's posture — and a no-show reaches from an assignment through its plan's headcount into the center's flex pool and back into the plan's status. Three rules apply to every such cascade.

First, cascades are **immediate and in-request**: there is no deferred queue, no eventual consistency, and no background sweep. By the time the response is written, every downstream row has already been updated and every derived aggregate has already been recomputed, so a read immediately after the request observes the finished picture.

Second, cascades **stamp what they touch**: any row a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if it had been written directly. A center whose posture is recomputed to the same value it already held is not a mutation and its `version` does not move; a center whose posture actually changes is a mutation and its version does.

Third, cascades are **ordered and idempotent in their own terms**: the derived counters (`open_jam_count`, `open_serious_injury_count`, `assigned_capacity_pph`, `lane_pressure_ratio`, `committed_headcount`, `staffing_gap`) are recomputed from their defining populations rather than incremented blindly, so replaying a recomputation produces the same value. Counters that are explicitly cumulative — `jam_count` on an appointment, `jam_hold_count` on a chute, `no_show_count` and `training_sessions_completed` on a worker, `packages_inducted_count`, `disposed_package_count` and `missort_corrected_count` on a center — are the exception: they only ever increase, and a compensating action never rolls them back.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Commissioning the Facility Surface

Everything in this system hangs off a sort center, and a `FacilityAdmin` is the only role that may create one. Registration supplies a `code` (the facility's short identifier, between 3 and 10 characters and unique across all sort centers), a human-readable `name`, the planned `automation_utilization_pct` of the sorter, and the size of the building's flex staffing bench as `flex_pool_total`. Automation utilization is a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive carried to two decimal places and defaults to 0 when omitted; `flex_pool_total` must be at least 0, defaults to 0, and is immutable once the center exists — no later operation may change it. At creation the center's bench is entirely unspent, so `flex_pool_available` is initialized to `flex_pool_total` and `flex_pool_reserved` to 0, every package and jam counter (`open_jam_count`, `open_serious_injury_count`, `packages_inducted_count`, `wip_package_count`, `exception_hold_count`, `disposed_package_count`, `missort_corrected_count`) starts at 0, `last_posture_change_at` is null, and `version` is 1. One subtlety catches implementers: the posture is derived immediately at creation rather than defaulted, so a center registered with automation utilization below 45 is born `RESTRICTED` rather than `OPEN`.

Automation is the one center attribute an admin may restate later — an automation outage drops it, commissioning a new sorter raises it — and restating it recomputes `ops_posture`, increments the center's `version`, and changes the throughput tier that the next headcount sizing will use. Sort centers can be listed with an optional `code` filter and an optional `ops_posture` filter, and read individually to inspect the posture, the flex pool triple and the conservation counters.

Dock doors are the building's physical interface to trailers. Each door is created under a center with a `door_number` (at least 1, unique among the doors of that center) and a `kind` that is either `INBOUND` for unload doors or `OUTBOUND` for load doors. Every door also carries `turnaround_buffer_min`, the mandatory clear time in minutes that must separate two appointments on that door; it defaults to 15 minutes when omitted and must be at least 0. A door's `status` starts `ACTIVE` and can be moved to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and back, and its buffer can be retuned, by an admin; either change bumps the door's `version`. Taking a door out of service does not disturb the windows of appointments already booked on it, but it does block work: while the door is `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, starting an unload on an appointment booked to it is refused with `ERR_DOOR_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Doors of a center may be listed with optional `kind` and `status` filters, or read one at a time.

Sortation chutes are the building's lines. Commissioning one supplies a `chute_number` (at least 1, unique within the center), a rated `throughput_pph` of at least 1 package per hour, and optionally `max_concurrent_destinations` — the aggregate interval capacity governing how many active destination mappings may overlap on that chute at any instant — which defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. A new chute is born with `status` `ACTIVE`, `jam_hold_count` at 0, and `version` 1. Chute `status` is never set by a client: it is flipped to `CONGESTED` by a jam-hold cascade and back to `ACTIVE` by the corresponding resume cascade. Commissioning a chute also matters to labor: the count of chutes with `status` either `ACTIVE` or `CONGESTED` is the center's line count, and each additional line raises the per-line minimum of 4 associates that any shift plan's requirement must clear. Chutes may be listed with an optional `status` filter, and a single-chute read exposes both the `version` that a chute-assignment create must pin and the cumulative `jam_hold_count`.

Destination lanes are the outbound side of the sort: each represents a downstream destination that packages are sorted toward. Opening a lane supplies a `lane_code` (unique within the center), a `region` label, a `service_class` of `GROUND`, `EXPRESS` or `FREIGHT`, and a `forecast_hourly_volume` of at least 0 packages per hour. The forecast is not decoration: it is the exact backlog increment that one hour of suspended chute capacity adds to that lane. A new lane starts with `status` `ACTIVE` and with `sorted_package_count`, `backlog_package_count` and `assigned_capacity_pph` all 0 and `lane_pressure_ratio` at 0.0000. An admin may later close or reopen a lane (moving `status` between `ACTIVE` and `CLOSED`) or restate `forecast_hourly_volume`; either change recomputes the lane's pressure ratio, bumps its `version`, and recomputes the center's posture, because a lane leaving `ACTIVE` also leaves the population from which the center's peak lane pressure is taken. Lanes may be listed with optional `status` and `service_class` filters, and a single-lane read exposes `backlog_package_count`, `assigned_capacity_pph` and `lane_pressure_ratio`.

Uniqueness violations anywhere on this surface — a duplicate center `code`, a duplicate `door_number`, `chute_number` or `lane_code` within one center — are all reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACILITY_RESOURCE`. Every child resource records the owning facility as `sort_center_id`, taken from the path segment it was created under rather than from the body.

### 3.2. Mapping Destinations onto Chutes, and Lane Pressure

A chute on its own sorts nothing; it has to be told which destination it is currently serving. That mapping is a chute assignment, created by a `SortSupervisor` or a `FacilityAdmin`, and it names the occupied chute as `chute_id`, the destination as `destination_lane_id`, and the occupation interval as `window_start` (inclusive) and `window_end` (exclusive, and required to be strictly after the start). The create also carries the mandatory `expected_chute_version` described in §2.6, and optionally `supersede`, a flag whose default is false. A fresh assignment is born with `status` `ACTIVE`, `suspended_reason` null and `effective_window_end` null. The chute and the lane must belong to the same sort center; a request that crosses centers is refused with `ERR_WORKER_CENTER_MISMATCH`.

Chute capacity is an interval-aggregate constraint rather than a simple row count. The population under test is the assignments already on that chute whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; rows already `SUPERSEDED` or `ENDED` are excluded, as is the row that this request's supersede clause is about to retire. Two intervals overlap when the new window's start is before the existing window's end and the existing window's start is before the new window's end — half-open comparison, no buffer, so two assignments that merely touch at a shared endpoint do not overlap. The requested occupancy is one plus the largest number of existing assignments that overlap at any single instant inside the new window, and when that number exceeds the chute's `max_concurrent_destinations` the create is refused with `ERR_CHUTE_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`. On a chute permitting two concurrent destinations, therefore, two overlapping assignments are legal and only the third simultaneous destination is turned away.

Re-chuting the same destination is expressed by the supersede flag rather than by deleting a row. When `supersede` is true and an `ACTIVE` assignment already exists for exactly the same `chute_id` and `destination_lane_id` pair, that prior row transitions `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `effective_window_end` is set to the incoming assignment's `window_start` — the prior mapping is treated as having ended precisely when the new one begins. A superseded row stops counting toward both chute concurrency and lane capacity from that moment on.

Every chute-assignment create increments the target chute's `version` by 1, recomputes the assigned capacity and pressure of every destination lane whose assignment set changed, recomputes the owning center's `ops_posture`, and increments the center's `version` by 1. Lane capacity and pressure are defined as follows. A lane's `assigned_capacity_pph` is the sum of `throughput_pph` over the distinct chutes that currently hold an `ACTIVE` assignment to that lane, and each qualifying chute contributes its rated throughput exactly once even if it holds two active assignments to the same lane. Assignments in `SUSPENDED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `ENDED` contribute no capacity at all. The lane's `lane_pressure_ratio` is then its `backlog_package_count` divided by its assigned capacity, with the divisor floored at 1 so that a lane with no assigned capacity does not divide by zero, and the quotient rounded half up to four decimal places. Because the center's posture reads the largest pressure ratio among its active lanes, suspending capacity on a busy lane can restrict the whole building.

Assignments may be listed with optional `chute_id`, `destination_lane_id` and `status` filters, and read individually to inspect `status`, `suspended_reason` and `effective_window_end`. A referenced chute, lane or assignment that does not exist yields `ERR_FACILITY_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.3. Inbound Trailer Scheduling, Unload Sequencing, and Jam Recovery

Inbound work begins with a trailer appointment: an `InboundCoordinator` (or a `FacilityAdmin`) books a carrier trailer onto an inbound dock door for a window. The booking names the receiving facility as `sort_center_id`, the reserved door as `door_id`, the carrier's `trailer_code`, the half-open occupation window as `window_start` and `window_end`, an unload `priority_class` of `CRITICAL`, `EXPRESS`, `STANDARD` or `DEFERRED`, and the `manifest_package_count` declared by the carrier, which must be at least 0. A new appointment is born `SCHEDULED` with `jam_count` at 0, `unload_sequence_rank` null, and `arrived_at` and `unloaded_at` both null.

Two gates guard the booking. The named door must have `status` `ACTIVE` and its `kind` must be `INBOUND`; a door that is out of service or an outbound door offered for an inbound trailer is refused with `ERR_DOOR_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. And the `trailer_code` must be free at that center: a code already held by a trailer appointment that has not reached `UNLOADED`, or by an outbound load that has not reached `DISPATCHED`, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAILER_CODE`.

Door scheduling itself is a buffered interval check, and the buffer is what makes it interesting. The population under test is the appointments already on that same door whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED`, `UNLOADING` or `JAM_HELD`; an appointment that has reached `UNLOADED` has released the door and is ignored. The door's own `turnaround_buffer_min` is inflated onto **both** sides of the comparison: the booking conflicts with an existing appointment when the new window's start is before the existing window's end advanced by the buffer, and simultaneously the existing window's start is before the new window's end advanced by the buffer. Both comparisons are strict. The practical consequences are worth stating plainly: two appointments that touch exactly (the new window starting at the instant the existing one ends) *do* conflict, because zero clearance is less than any positive buffer; and a gap of exactly the buffer's length is *accepted*, because the strict comparison just fails to trigger. A conflict is reported as `ERR_DOOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

When the trailer physically shows up in the yard, the coordinator records arrival, moving the appointment `SCHEDULED → ARRIVED`, stamping `arrived_at` with the logical clock, and re-ranking the center's arrival queue. The queue is the point of the exercise: the yard has more arrived trailers than open doors, and `unload_sequence_rank` tells the floor which one to pull next. Ranking considers only the appointments of that same center whose `status` is currently `ARRIVED` — scheduled, unloading, jam-held and unloaded appointments are excluded and hold a null rank — and orders them by four keys in order: first by priority band, where `CRITICAL` sorts ahead of `EXPRESS`, `EXPRESS` ahead of `STANDARD`, and `STANDARD` ahead of `DEFERRED`; then by `window_start` earliest first; then by `manifest_package_count` largest first, so the heavier trailer wins a tie between two equally urgent trailers booked for the same window; and finally by `created_at` earliest first as the last tie-break. The resulting `unload_sequence_rank` is the 1-based position in that ordering, and the row's `id` is never used as a tie-break.

Pulling a trailer to the line advances it `ARRIVED → UNLOADING`. That requires the appointment to still be `ARRIVED`, the door named by `door_id` to still be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_DOOR_OUT_OF_SERVICE`), and the center not to be frozen. Because the appointment leaves the ranked population when it starts unloading, the remaining `ARRIVED` appointments of that center are re-ranked within the same request — a rank is only ever a snapshot of the current queue.

Jams are the normal state of a high-volume sort line, so the appointment lifecycle is cyclic rather than linear. An `InboundCoordinator` or a `SortSupervisor` records a jam against an unloading appointment, supplying the jammed line as `chute_id` and a free-text `jam_reason`. Two preconditions apply beyond the appointment being `UNLOADING`: the named chute must belong to the same sort center as the appointment, and the appointment's `jam_count` must still be below the retry limit of three. A fourth jam on the same appointment is refused with `ERR_JAM_RETRY_LIMIT_REACHED` — the line has proven it cannot run and needs engineering, not another retry.

A successful jam hold runs a five-link cascade that reaches four levels below the appointment, and every link must land:

1. The appointment transitions `UNLOADING → JAM_HELD` and its `jam_count` increases by 1.
2. Caused by the first link, the named chute flips `ACTIVE → CONGESTED`, its `jam_hold_count` increases by 1, and its `version` increments — which is exactly why a chute-assignment create holding a pre-jam `expected_chute_version` will now be rejected.
3. Caused by the second link, every chute assignment on that chute whose `status` is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, and each one records the request's `jam_reason` as its `suspended_reason`.
4. Caused by the third link, each destination lane that lost an assignment absorbs one hour of unsorted volume: the lane's `backlog_package_count` increases by that lane's own `forecast_hourly_volume`, its `assigned_capacity_pph` is recomputed over the assignments that remain `ACTIVE`, and its `lane_pressure_ratio` is recomputed from the new backlog and capacity.
5. Caused by the fourth link, the center's `open_jam_count` is recomputed, its `ops_posture` is re-derived from the new jam count and the new peak lane pressure, and its `version` increments by 1.

Clearing the jam is the compensating action and restores the picture exactly. An `InboundCoordinator` or `SortSupervisor` resumes the unload, again naming the `chute_id`; the appointment must be `JAM_HELD` and the named chute must currently be `CONGESTED`. The appointment returns `JAM_HELD → UNLOADING`, but `jam_count` is deliberately **not** decremented — it is a retry counter, not a gauge, so three holds and three resumes still exhaust the retry limit. The named chute flips `CONGESTED → ACTIVE` and its `version` increments; every assignment on that chute whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` transitions `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and its `suspended_reason` returns to null; each affected lane's `backlog_package_count` is reduced by that lane's `forecast_hourly_volume` with a floor of 0 so the backlog can never go negative, and its capacity and pressure are recomputed; and finally the center's `open_jam_count` and `ops_posture` are recomputed and its `version` increments. Note that a frozen center still accepts jam holds and resumes — freezing must never trap the building in a jam it cannot clear.

Finishing the unload advances the appointment `UNLOADING → UNLOADED`, which is terminal. This requires the appointment to be `UNLOADING` and the center not to be frozen. The transition stamps `unloaded_at` with the logical clock and inducts the manifest: the center's `packages_inducted_count` and `wip_package_count` both increase by the appointment's `manifest_package_count`, the center's `version` increments, and its posture is recomputed. Induction is the left side of the package conservation identity, and from this moment those packages are the building's responsibility until they leave on a trailer, are disposed of, or sit in exception hold. The door is also freed for later appointments, since an `UNLOADED` appointment no longer occupies it.

Appointments may be listed with optional `sort_center_id`, `door_id`, `status` and `priority_class` filters, and read individually to inspect `unload_sequence_rank` and `jam_count`. Requesting any transition from the wrong state — arriving twice, unloading a scheduled trailer, resuming an appointment that is not held — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`, and a target appointment that does not exist yields `ERR_TRAILER_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.4. Drafting a Shift Plan and Sizing the Headcount Requirement

Labor for a sort center is planned one shift at a time. A `LaborPlanner` drafts a plan under a center, supplying the calendar date of the shift as `shift_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the half-open shift interval as `shift_window_start` and `shift_window_end` (the end strictly after the start), the `forecast_volume` of packages expected across the shift (at least 1), the headcount the planner is prepared to commit from each permanent bench as `planned_core_headcount` and `planned_seasonal_headcount` (each at least 0), and the fiscal ceiling for the shift as `labor_budget_cents` (at least 0). A drafted plan starts in `DRAFT` with `shift_hours` computed straight from the window, `required_headcount` at 0 until submission, and every committed, actual and cost field at zero or null: `flex_draw_count`, `planned_labor_cents`, `committed_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, `unfilled_gap`, `sorted_package_count`, `missort_package_count` and `actual_labor_cents` are 0, while `provisional_cost_per_package_cents`, `final_cost_per_package_cents`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` are null. One plan per shift date per center is the rule, with a deliberate escape hatch: a second plan for the same center and the same `shift_date` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACILITY_RESOURCE` unless every existing plan for that date has been rejected, since a rejected plan holds no claim on the date. Plans of a center may be listed with optional `status` and `shift_date` filters.

Submitting the plan advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, records the submitting operator's `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`, and freezes the headcount requirement by recomputing `shift_hours` and then `required_headcount`. The requirement is the heart of the labor model, and it is computed in a pinned order:

1. `shift_hours` is the length of the shift window expressed in hours, rounded half up to two decimal places.
2. The throughput of one associate is selected from the **center's** current `automation_utilization_pct`, evaluated top-down: an automation utilization of at least 85 gives 1,200 packages per associate per hour; otherwise an automation utilization of at least 60 gives 950 packages per associate per hour; otherwise 700 packages per associate per hour. The tier is read from the center, not from anything on the plan.
3. The center's line count is the number of its sortation chutes whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `CONGESTED`. A congested line still counts — it is jammed, not decommissioned.
4. The raw requirement is the plan's `forecast_volume` divided by the product of that per-associate throughput and `shift_hours`. This quotient is kept exact; no rounding happens at this step.
5. The raw requirement is then multiplied by the posture multiplier of the center's `ops_posture` at submit time — 1.00 when `OPEN`, 1.10 when `RESTRICTED`, 1.25 when `FROZEN`.
6. Only now is the result rounded **up** to the next whole associate, and `required_headcount` is the larger of that ceiling and the per-line floor of 4 associates per line (4 multiplied by the line count).

The order in step 4 through step 6 is contractual and not merely suggestive: the posture multiplier is applied to the exact quotient and the ceiling is taken exactly once, afterwards. Rounding up first and multiplying second is explicitly *not* the contract and will produce a requirement one associate too high in ordinary cases. Submitting a plan that is not in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_PLAN_STATE`, and a plan identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_SHIFT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.5. Approval, Pricing, Rejection and Revision

Approval is where the plan becomes a financial and staffing commitment, so it carries the densest set of gates in the API. Only an `OpsManager` may approve, and the request body carries `expected_center_version` and nothing else. The checks run in the fixed order of §2.7, which for this operation means: the plan must be `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_PLAN_STATE`); the pinned `expected_center_version` must match the center's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_CENTER_VERSION`); the approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the plan's stored `submitted_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); the center must not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_CENTER_FROZEN`); the bodies the plan wants to put on the floor must fit the lines (`ERR_LINE_HEADCOUNT_EXCEEDED`); the flex bench must cover the draw (`ERR_FLEX_POOL_EXHAUSTED`); and the priced shift must fit the budget (`ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`). A stale approve therefore never reports a budget problem, and a frozen center is surfaced as such rather than being masked by a fiscal check.

The flex draw is derived, never supplied. The plan needs from the bench whatever its requirement exceeds its own two committed benches: the needed flex is the plan's `required_headcount` minus `planned_core_headcount` minus `planned_seasonal_headcount`, floored at 0 so an over-staffed plan draws nothing. The line-capacity gate then tests `planned_core_headcount` plus `planned_seasonal_headcount` plus that needed flex against the physical ceiling of 22 associates per line multiplied by the center's line count (chutes whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `CONGESTED`), refusing the approve with `ERR_LINE_HEADCOUNT_EXCEEDED` when the committed bodies would not fit. The bench gate then requires the center's `flex_pool_available` to be greater than or equal to the needed flex, refusing with `ERR_FLEX_POOL_EXHAUSTED` when the bench is short — this is the point at which two centers' worth of ambition meets one bench, and it is why approvals of different plans race through version pinning.

Pricing the shift is a small constraint-propagation problem, because the flex premium depends on the flex ratio, the flex ratio depends on the draw, and the draw depends on the requirement. The evaluation order is pinned:

1. The needed flex is computed as described above (requirement minus core minus seasonal, floored at 0).
2. The draw equals the needed flex. If the bench could not cover it, the request has already been refused.
3. The total headcount for the shift is `planned_core_headcount` plus `planned_seasonal_headcount` plus the draw.
4. The base cost is assembled per hire class and **rounded per class line before summing**: the core line is `planned_core_headcount` multiplied by `shift_hours` multiplied by 2,150 cents per hour, rounded half up to whole cents; the seasonal line is `planned_seasonal_headcount` multiplied by `shift_hours` multiplied by 1,875 cents per hour, rounded half up to whole cents; the flex line is the draw multiplied by `shift_hours` multiplied by 2,480 cents per hour, rounded half up to whole cents. The three rounded lines are then added.
5. The flex ratio is the draw divided by the total headcount, kept exact, and taken as 0 when the total headcount is 0.
6. The premium tier follows from that ratio: no premium at all when the ratio is below 0.20; a premium of 5 percent when the ratio is at least 0.20 but below 0.35; a premium of 12 percent when the ratio is at least 0.35.
7. `planned_labor_cents` is the base cost multiplied by one plus the premium, rounded half up to whole cents.
8. The budget gate compares that post-premium `planned_labor_cents` against the plan's `labor_budget_cents` and refuses with `ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) when the priced shift is the larger. Comparing the pre-premium base against the budget is not the contract.

Alongside the labor price, approval writes the forward-looking unit cost. `provisional_cost_per_package_cents` is the sum of `planned_labor_cents` and the fixed overhead of 12,000 cents per shift, divided by the plan's full `forecast_volume`, rounded half up to two decimal places. This provisional figure knowingly flatters the shift: it charges no rework and it divides by every forecast package, including the ones that will later be mis-sorted.

The approval's own side effects then land together: `status` becomes `APPROVED`, `approved_by_user_id` records the approver's `X-User-ID`, `flex_draw_count` is set to the draw, the center's `flex_pool_available` decreases by that draw while `flex_pool_reserved` increases by the same amount, the center's `version` increments by 1, and `committed_headcount` and `staffing_gap` are recomputed. Committed headcount is `planned_core_headcount` plus `planned_seasonal_headcount` plus `flex_draw_count`, minus the number of this plan's staff assignments whose `status` is `NO_SHOW`; assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `CHECKED_IN` and `COMPLETED` all count as committed and only a no-show subtracts. The staffing gap is `required_headcount` minus `committed_headcount`, floored at 0 so it never goes negative.

Rejection is the sibling branch out of `SUBMITTED`, and its side-effect set diverges from approval's deliberately. An `OpsManager` rejects with a `rejection_reason`, the plan transitions `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, and the reason is recorded — and that is all that happens. No flex is reserved, `flex_pool_available` and `flex_pool_reserved` are untouched, no cost field is written, and `approved_by_user_id` stays null. Rejection is nonetheless subject to the same segregation-of-duties rule as approval: the operator who submitted the plan may not reject it either, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Revision closes the loop. A `LaborPlanner` returns a rejected plan to `REJECTED → DRAFT`, optionally restating any of `forecast_volume`, `planned_core_headcount`, `planned_seasonal_headcount` and `labor_budget_cents`; supplied values overwrite the stored ones and omitted values keep whatever they had. Revision also clears the traces of the failed round: `rejection_reason` returns to null, `submitted_by_user_id` returns to null (so the next submitter is the one the segregation check will use), and `required_headcount` resets to 0 until the next submission recomputes it. The full revision cycle is therefore `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REJECTED → DRAFT`, and a plan may go around it as many times as the planner needs. A single-plan read exposes `required_headcount`, `flex_draw_count`, `staffing_gap`, `unfilled_gap` and both cost-per-package variants.

### 3.6. The Peak Hiring Pipeline: Onboarding, Training Throughput and Attrition

Peak season is staffed by hiring ahead of the volume, and the pipeline from applicant to certified associate is modelled explicitly because its throughput and its leakage are what determine whether a shift plan can actually be filled. A `LaborPlanner` hires an applicant into a center with a `full_name`, a `hire_class` of `CORE`, `SEASONAL` or `FLEX`, and a `hire_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; the worker's home facility is the center in whose path the hire was recorded, retained as `sort_center_id`. A new worker starts in `APPLIED` with every counter at zero — `training_sessions_completed`, `training_sessions_missed`, `no_show_count`, `completed_assignment_count` and `consecutive_completed_shifts` — and with an attrition score already computed on the cohort curve, so `attrition_risk_basis` reads `COHORT_BASELINE` from the moment of hire.

Everything that subsequently happens to a worker is recorded as an onboarding event carrying a single `event_type`, and the event both advances the worker's `status` and re-scores attrition. Seven event types exist and each has a defined effect:

- `ONBOARDING_STARTED` moves a worker `APPLIED → ONBOARDING`.
- `BACKGROUND_CLEARED` moves a worker `ONBOARDING → CLEARED`.
- `TRAINING_SESSION_COMPLETED` increases `training_sessions_completed` by 1 without changing `status`.
- `TRAINING_SESSION_MISSED` increases `training_sessions_missed` by 1 without changing `status`.
- `REST_DAY_RECORDED` resets `consecutive_completed_shifts` to 0 without changing `status`.
- `CERTIFIED` moves a worker `CLEARED → CERTIFIED`, but only once training has actually been delivered: `training_sessions_completed` must be at least 2, and a premature certification is refused with `ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED`.
- `SEPARATED` moves a worker to the terminal `SEPARATED` state, either as `CLEARED → SEPARATED` or as `CERTIFIED → SEPARATED`.

The three counter events are self-loops, and they are available symmetrically on both sides of certification. A `CLEARED` worker recording any of `TRAINING_SESSION_COMPLETED`, `TRAINING_SESSION_MISSED` or `REST_DAY_RECORDED` stays `CLEARED` (`CLEARED → CLEARED`), updates the counter that event names, and has `attrition_risk_score`, `attrition_risk_basis` and `attrition_flagged` recomputed. A `CERTIFIED` worker recording any of the same three events stays `CERTIFIED` (`CERTIFIED → CERTIFIED`), updates the same counter, and has the same three attrition fields recomputed. The pre-certification self-loop is the *only* mechanism that raises `training_sessions_completed` toward the two sessions that certification demands, and the post-certification self-loop is what keeps training and rest counters mutable for an associate already on the floor. Any event requested from a state that does not permit it — certifying an `APPLIED` worker, clearing a background twice — is refused with `ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED` where the readiness rule governs and otherwise leaves the worker untouched; a worker identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_LABOR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

Separation is not a quiet event. Whether the worker separates out of `CLEARED` or out of `CERTIFIED`, the consequence is identical: every staff assignment of that worker whose `status` is still `ASSIGNED` immediately flips to `NO_SHOW`, and the complete no-show cascade described in §3.7 below runs for each affected shift plan — headcount recomputation, possible flip to `AT_RISK`, flex top-up, possible flip back to `APPROVED`. A single separation during peak can therefore ripple through several plans and consume bench headcount at more than one of them.

Attrition scoring is recomputed on every mutation of a worker — every onboarding event, every check-out, every no-show — and it is a two-variant weighted score. The common part is built as follows. `tenure_days` is the number of whole days from the worker's `hire_date` to the logical clock's date, taking the floor, and it is refreshed whenever the worker is touched. The tenure bucket contributes a baseline risk: 0.42 for a tenure of 14 days or fewer, 0.31 for 15 through 45 days, 0.18 for 46 through 120 days, and 0.09 from 121 days onward. The hire class contributes an adjustment: nothing at all for `CORE`, 0.12 for `SEASONAL`, and 0.07 for `FLEX`. The observed adjustment, used by only one of the two variants, adds 0.06 for each recorded no-show and 0.05 for each missed training session, then subtracts a credit of 0.04 when the worker's `status` is `CERTIFIED`.

Which variant applies is decided by `completed_assignment_count`, and the switch is exactly three completions. While a worker has completed fewer than 3 assignments there is not enough individual evidence to score them personally: `attrition_risk_basis` is `COHORT_BASELINE` and `attrition_risk_score` is the tenure baseline plus the hire-class adjustment, capped at a ceiling of 0.95 and rounded half up to four decimal places, with the observed adjustment ignored entirely — a brand-new seasonal hire with two missed sessions still scores exactly as its cohort does. Once the worker has completed at least 3 assignments, `attrition_risk_basis` becomes `INDIVIDUAL_OBSERVED` and `attrition_risk_score` is the tenure baseline plus the hire-class adjustment plus the observed adjustment, clamped to no less than 0.02 and no more than 0.95 and rounded half up to four decimal places. The same worker record therefore scores differently the moment its third assignment completes — upward when missed training and no-shows dominate, downward when the certification credit dominates. Finally, `attrition_flagged` is true exactly when `attrition_risk_score` is greater than or equal to 0.55, and it is what a planner filters on when deciding whom to re-recruit. Workers of a center may be listed with optional `status`, `hire_class` and `attrition_flagged` filters, and a single-worker read exposes `tenure_days`, `attrition_risk_score`, `attrition_risk_basis` and `attrition_flagged`.

### 3.7. Staffing the Lines, Attendance, and the No-Show Cascade

With an approved plan and a trained bench, a `LaborPlanner` or a `SortSupervisor` places workers on lines. An assignment names the staffed shift as `shift_plan_id`, the worker as `worker_id`, the sort line as `line_chute_id`, and the position on that line as `role_on_line`, which is one of `UNLOADER`, `PRIMARY_SORTER`, `SECONDARY_SORTER`, `LOADER` or `JAM_TECH`. A new assignment is born `ASSIGNED` with `worked_hours` at 0 and `checked_in_at` and `checked_out_at` both null.

Five gates guard placement, and they are evaluated in the precedence of §2.7. The plan must be in `APPROVED` or `AT_RISK` — staffing a draft, submitted, rejected or closed plan is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_PLAN_STATE`. The center must not be `FROZEN`. The line must have room: counting the assignments already on that same `line_chute_id` whose `status` is anything other than `NO_SHOW`, adding this one must not exceed 22 bodies on the line, and a violation is refused with `ERR_LINE_HEADCOUNT_EXCEEDED`. Ergonomics and safety must clear: the worker's `consecutive_completed_shifts` must be strictly below 5, and the worker must have no injury report in `OPEN` status whose `severity` is `RECORDABLE` or `SERIOUS`; either failure is refused with `ERR_ERGONOMIC_ROTATION_REQUIRED`. Readiness must clear: the worker's `status` must be `CLEARED` or `CERTIFIED`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED`. Finally, cross-center integrity holds throughout: the worker's `sort_center_id` must equal the plan's `sort_center_id`, and the named line must belong to the same center, with a mismatch refused as `ERR_WORKER_CENTER_MISMATCH`. Creating an assignment recomputes the plan's `committed_headcount` and `staffing_gap`. Assignments may be listed with optional `shift_plan_id`, `worker_id`, `line_chute_id`, `status` and `role_on_line` filters, and read individually to inspect `checked_in_at`, `checked_out_at` and `worked_hours`.

Attendance is the one place where the `Worker` role acts for itself. Clocking in advances the assignment `ASSIGNED → CHECKED_IN` and stamps `checked_in_at` with the logical clock. It requires that the caller's `X-User-ID` equal the assignment's `worker_id` (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER`), that the worker's own `status` be `CERTIFIED` — merely `CLEARED` is enough to be scheduled but not enough to work a line, and the difference is refused with `ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED` — that the owning plan still be `APPROVED` or `AT_RISK` (`ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_PLAN_STATE`), and that the center not be `FROZEN`. Clocking out advances the assignment `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED`, again only for its own worker, and settles the shift: `checked_out_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `worked_hours` becomes the elapsed time between check-in and check-out expressed in hours and rounded half up to two decimal places. Check-out also advances the worker: `completed_assignment_count` and `consecutive_completed_shifts` each increase by 1, and the attrition score is recomputed — which is exactly where the basis flips to `INDIVIDUAL_OBSERVED` as the third completion lands. Checking in twice, or checking out an assignment that was never checked in, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.

A no-show is recorded by a `LaborPlanner` or a `SortSupervisor` against an assignment that is still `ASSIGNED`, and it runs the system's second deep cascade — six links, ending back where it started, on the plan's own status:

1. The assignment transitions `ASSIGNED → NO_SHOW` and the worker's `no_show_count` increases by 1.
2. Caused by the first link on the worker, `attrition_risk_score`, `attrition_risk_basis` and `attrition_flagged` are recomputed — each no-show adds 0.06 to the observed adjustment for a worker already scored individually.
3. Caused by the first link on the plan, `committed_headcount` is recomputed (the new `NO_SHOW` row now subtracts one body) and `staffing_gap` is recomputed as the requirement minus the committed count, floored at 0.
4. Caused by the third link, a plan that is currently `APPROVED` and now shows a staffing gap greater than 0 transitions `APPROVED → AT_RISK`. The at-risk threshold is 0, so any unfilled body at all puts the plan at risk.
5. Caused by the fourth link, the plan automatically draws a top-up from the bench: the draw is the smaller of the current `staffing_gap` and the center's `flex_pool_available`, that amount moves from `flex_pool_available` into `flex_pool_reserved`, `flex_draw_count` increases by the draw, `committed_headcount` and `staffing_gap` are recomputed, `unfilled_gap` is set to whatever gap remains after the draw, and the center's `version` increments by 1. Because the draw takes only what the bench actually has, a depleted bench does not fail the no-show — it leaves a positive `unfilled_gap` for the planner to see. `ERR_FLEX_POOL_EXHAUSTED` is the guard on a plan approval sizing a draw the bench cannot cover, not on this opportunistic top-up.
6. Caused by the fifth link, a plan whose `staffing_gap` has returned to 0 after the top-up transitions `AT_RISK → APPROVED`, and `unfilled_gap` is set to 0. When the bench has capacity, therefore, the plan can leave and re-enter `APPROVED` inside a single request; `unfilled_gap` is 0 for any plan sitting in `APPROVED` and positive only while a plan sits in `AT_RISK`.

Marking an assignment that has already completed, or one that is already a no-show, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, and an assignment identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_LABOR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.8. Outbound Loading, Cube Utilization, Dispatch and Package Exceptions

An outbound load is one trailer being filled for one destination. A `SortSupervisor` stages it at an outbound door, naming the loading facility as `sort_center_id`, the door as `door_id`, the destination it will carry as `destination_lane_id`, the trailer's identifier as `trailer_code`, the usable `trailer_cube_cuft` in cubic feet (strictly greater than 0, carried to two decimal places), and the `dispatch_cut_time` — the latest logical-clock instant at which this trailer may still leave. The door must be `ACTIVE` and its `kind` must be `OUTBOUND`, otherwise `ERR_DOOR_OUT_OF_SERVICE`; the `trailer_code` must not already be in use at that center by a live appointment or an undispatched load, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAILER_CODE`; the door, lane and center must be consistent, otherwise `ERR_WORKER_CENTER_MISMATCH`; and the destination lane must have at least one chute assignment on record in *any* status, otherwise `ERR_LANE_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_CHUTE` — a destination that has never been mapped to a chute cannot receive packages, even historically. A staged load begins with `load_status` `STAGING`, `seal_status` `UNSEALED`, `cube_used_cuft` 0, `cube_utilization_pct` 0.00, `package_count` 0, `seal_code` null and `dispatched_at` null.

An outbound load carries **two independent state dimensions**, and this is the single most commonly mis-implemented part of the specification. `load_status` tracks the loading progression `STAGING`, `LOADING`, `LOADED`, `DISPATCHED`, while `seal_status` tracks the physical seal through `UNSEALED`, `SEALED`, `BROKEN`. Neither dimension constrains the other's transitions except where explicitly stated, and dispatch is the single join point where both must have reached their target values.

Packages enter the trailer by scanning batches by size class. The request supplies up to four optional counts — `small_count`, `medium_count`, `large_count` and `oversized_count` — and any omitted count is treated as 0. Cube consumption is then computed in a pinned rounding order that must be followed literally:

1. Each class line is computed on its own and rounded half up to two decimal places: the small line is its count multiplied by 0.335 cubic feet per package, the medium line its count multiplied by 1.205, the large line its count multiplied by 3.755, and the oversized line its count multiplied by 11.505.
2. The four rounded lines are added together to give the batch's cube.
3. The batch's cube is added to the load's stored `cube_used_cuft` and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places again.

Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is explicitly *not* the contract and diverges from it on realistic batch mixes. Once the new `cube_used_cuft` is known, `cube_utilization_pct` becomes that value divided by `trailer_cube_cuft`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places, and `package_count` grows by the sum of the four class counts. The capacity gate is checked against the post-scan figure and is deliberately non-strict: a batch that fills the trailer to *exactly* its `trailer_cube_cuft` is accepted, and only a result strictly greater than the trailer's cube is refused, with `ERR_TRAILER_CUBE_EXCEEDED`.

Scanning also moves packages through the conservation ledger and touches the labor ledger. The first successful scan advances the load `STAGING → LOADING`; every subsequent scan is a self-loop `LOADING → LOADING` that accumulates on top of the stored totals. Either way the center's `wip_package_count` decreases by the scanned quantity, the destination lane's `sorted_package_count` increases by the same quantity, the center's `version` increments by 1 and its `ops_posture` is recomputed, and the scanned quantity is also credited to a shift plan's `sorted_package_count` so that the shift can be costed later. Scanning requires the center not to be `FROZEN`, and scanning a load that is already `LOADED` or `DISPATCHED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`.

Which plan absorbs the credit is decided by attribution rather than supplied. The eligible plans are those of the same sort center whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `AT_RISK` and whose shift window contains the logical clock, where containment is half-open — `shift_window_start` at or before the clock and `shift_window_end` strictly after it. Among those, the plan with the earliest `shift_window_start` wins, with the earliest `created_at` breaking a tie and the row's `id` never used. When no plan qualifies, nothing is attributed: no plan counter moves, the request still succeeds, and the lane and center counters are updated exactly as they would have been. The same attribution rule is used when a package exception is created, which is how a mis-sort finds the plan that will carry its rework cost.

Closing the load advances it `LOADING → LOADED` and refreshes `cube_utilization_pct`; it changes `seal_status` not at all. Closing a load that is still `STAGING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`. Sealing is the other dimension and moves independently: supplying a `seal_code` takes `seal_status` from `UNSEALED → SEALED`, and the same operation takes it from `BROKEN → SEALED` with the new code replacing the old. In both cases the load's `load_status` must be `LOADING` or `LOADED` — a `STAGING` trailer has nothing to seal — and an attempt outside those states is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`. Sealing never changes `load_status`. The seal is broken by the system, not by a request of its own: when a package exception is created naming this load and the load is currently `SEALED`, the seal transitions `SEALED → BROKEN` and `seal_code` returns to null, while `load_status` is untouched — a `LOADED` trailer stays `LOADED` but can no longer leave until it has been re-sealed. That closes the seal cycle `UNSEALED → SEALED → BROKEN → SEALED`.

Dispatch is the join of the two dimensions and the only operation an `OpsManager` performs on the outbound dock. The load must be `LOADED`, so a trailer still accepting scans cannot leave; `seal_status` must be `SEALED`, and a trailer that is `UNSEALED` or `BROKEN` is refused with `ERR_LOAD_NOT_SEALED`; the logical clock must be strictly before `dispatch_cut_time`, so a clock exactly equal to the cut time is already too late and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_CUT_PASSED`; and the center must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_CENTER_FROZEN`. On success the load transitions `LOADED → DISPATCHED`, which is terminal, and `dispatched_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The packages on a dispatched trailer remain counted in the destination lane's `sorted_package_count` — leaving the building does not remove them from the conservation identity, because the lane total is the identity's sorted term. Loads may be listed with optional `sort_center_id`, `door_id`, `destination_lane_id`, `load_status` and `seal_status` filters, and read individually to inspect `cube_used_cuft`, `cube_utilization_pct`, both status dimensions and `seal_code`. A load or exception identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_OUTBOUND_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

Not every package sorts cleanly, and the exception queue is where the conservation ledger earns its keep. A `SortSupervisor` or an `InboundCoordinator` records a batch of problem packages as a package exception, naming the holding facility as `sort_center_id`, the family of problem as `exception_type` (`DAMAGED`, `UNLABELED`, `OVERSIZED` or `MISSORT`), and the size of the batch as `package_count`, which must be at least 1. Three optional references locate the batch: `from_destination_lane_id` is the lane the packages were pulled back out of and is required for a `MISSORT` and null otherwise; `outbound_load_id` names the load the packages were found on and is null when the batch was found on the inbound side; and `trailer_appointment_id` names the inbound trailer the packages arrived on and is null when the batch was found on the outbound side. The exception is created in `OPEN` with `resolution`, `to_destination_lane_id`, `resolved_via_chute_id` and `resolved_at` all null.

Creating the exception debits the pool the packages came from and credits exception hold. When `from_destination_lane_id` is supplied, that lane's `sorted_package_count` decreases by `package_count`; when it is not, the center's `wip_package_count` decreases by the same amount. Either way the center's `exception_hold_count` increases by `package_count`, its `version` increments by 1, and its `ops_posture` is recomputed. The source pool must be able to fund the batch: a `package_count` exceeding the named lane's `sorted_package_count`, or exceeding the center's `wip_package_count` when no source lane is named, is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_COUNT_EXCEEDS_SOURCE`. The exception also picks up the attributed plan described above as its `shift_plan_id` — which is how a `MISSORT` batch lands on that plan's `missort_package_count` and eventually on its final unit cost — and it is null when no plan covers the clock. Finally, an exception naming a `SEALED` load breaks that load's seal as described above.

Resolution closes the batch out of the queue. A `SortSupervisor` resolves an `OPEN` exception with a `resolution` of `REROUTE`, `DISPOSE` or `RELABEL`, optionally supplying `to_destination_lane_id`. An exception that is already `RESOLVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EXCEPTION_STATE`, and a frozen center refuses resolution with `ERR_CENTER_FROZEN`. A reroute carries three additional requirements: `to_destination_lane_id` must be present and name a lane whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; that lane must have at least one chute assignment on record in any status, otherwise `ERR_LANE_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_CHUTE`; and the pool of chutes actually able to take the packages right now must be non-empty, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CHUTE`. The order matters: the referential check on the lane's assignment history is evaluated before the live-eligibility check, so a lane that was never mapped reports the referential error and never the empty-pool error.

The eligible pool for a reroute is narrow by design. A chute assignment qualifies when its `destination_lane_id` is the target lane, its `status` is `ACTIVE`, its window covers the logical clock with the usual half-open convention (`window_start` at or before the clock and `window_end` strictly after it), and the chute it occupies has `status` `ACTIVE` — a `CONGESTED` chute is not eligible, because a jammed line cannot absorb re-laned packages. Among the qualifying assignments the winner is the one on the chute with the highest `throughput_pph`, with the earliest `created_at` of the assignment breaking a tie and the row's `id` never used, and `resolved_via_chute_id` records the winning chute.

All three resolutions stamp `resolved_at` from the logical clock, record the chosen `resolution`, decrease the center's `exception_hold_count` by `package_count`, increment the center's `version` by 1 and recompute `ops_posture`; they then differ in where the packages go. A `REROUTE` credits the target lane's `sorted_package_count` with `package_count`, adds the same amount to the center's `missort_corrected_count`, and records `resolved_via_chute_id`. A `DISPOSE` adds `package_count` to the center's `disposed_package_count`, removing those packages from the network permanently. A `RELABEL` returns `package_count` to the center's `wip_package_count`, putting the packages back in front of the sorter to be scanned again. Exceptions may be listed filtered by any of their references — `sort_center_id`, `from_destination_lane_id`, `to_destination_lane_id`, `outbound_load_id`, `trailer_appointment_id`, `shift_plan_id`, `resolved_via_chute_id` — and by `exception_type` and `status`, and read individually to inspect `resolution` and `resolved_via_chute_id`.

### 3.9. Safety Reporting and Shift Settlement

Safety on a high-volume line is enforced through the injury record, and it has the sharpest teeth in the system. A `SortSupervisor` or an `OpsManager` files a report naming the facility as `sort_center_id`, the injured worker as `worker_id`, the `severity` as `FIRST_AID`, `RECORDABLE` or `SERIOUS`, the injured `body_part`, and the instant the injury occurred as `occurred_at`; the line involved may be named as `chute_id` and is null when the injury was not line-specific. The worker and the named chute must belong to the filing center, otherwise `ERR_WORKER_CENTER_MISMATCH`, and a worker that does not resolve yields `ERR_LABOR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. A new report is `OPEN` with `lost_time_days` at 0. Filing recomputes the center's `open_serious_injury_count` and re-derives `ops_posture`, and increments the center's `version` by 1 — which is how a single `SERIOUS` injury freezes an entire building in one request, immediately blocking arrivals, unloads, scanning, dispatch, plan approvals, staffing and exception resolution. An `OPEN` report whose severity is `RECORDABLE` or `SERIOUS` also blocks its worker from being assigned to any line at all, through `ERR_ERGONOMIC_ROTATION_REQUIRED`.

Only an `OpsManager` closes a report, supplying the `lost_time_days` finally recorded. The report must be `OPEN`; closing an already-closed report is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EXCEPTION_STATE`. Closure transitions the report `OPEN → CLOSED`, records the lost time, recomputes the center's `open_serious_injury_count`, re-derives `ops_posture` and increments the center's `version` by 1. Because closure is one of the operations a frozen center still accepts, it is the ordinary way a building thaws: the posture drops from `FROZEN` to `RESTRICTED` when a jam is still held or a lane is still above the pressure ratio or automation is still below 45, and all the way to `OPEN` when nothing else is outstanding. Reports may be listed with optional `sort_center_id`, `worker_id`, `chute_id`, `severity` and `status` filters, and read individually.

Settlement of the shift is the final act, performed by an `OpsManager` on a plan in `APPROVED` or `AT_RISK` and carrying `expected_center_version`. Both source states settle identically — the plan transitions to the terminal `CLOSED` and the same side effects run in each case — and a plan in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_PLAN_STATE`. Closure is permitted even while the center is `FROZEN`, because a shift that has already happened must be costed regardless of the building's posture.

The actual labor cost is aggregated from attendance rather than from plan, and only completed work counts. The population is this plan's staff assignments whose `status` is `COMPLETED`; rows still `ASSIGNED`, still `CHECKED_IN`, or marked `NO_SHOW` contribute nothing at all, so a worker who clocked in and never clocked out costs the shift nothing. Each completed row contributes its `worked_hours` multiplied by the hourly rate of that worker's `hire_class` — 2,150 cents per hour for `CORE`, 1,875 cents for `SEASONAL`, 2,480 cents for `FLEX` — rounded half up to whole cents **per assignment line**, and the rounded lines are then summed into the base cost. The flex premium is applied on top using the same tier table as planned pricing, but with an actual ratio: the flex numerator is the number of completed rows whose worker `hire_class` is `FLEX` plus the plan's `flex_draw_count`, and the denominator is the total number of completed rows plus the same `flex_draw_count`, with the ratio taken as 0 when that denominator is 0. A ratio below 0.20 attracts no premium, a ratio of at least 0.20 but below 0.35 attracts 5 percent, and a ratio of at least 0.35 attracts 12 percent. `actual_labor_cents` is the base cost multiplied by one plus that premium, rounded half up to whole cents.

The final unit cost is then the mirror image of the provisional one and will ordinarily differ from it for the very same shift. Rework is charged at 35 cents for every mis-sorted package, where the quantity is the plan's `missort_package_count` — the sum of `package_count` across package exceptions whose `exception_type` is `MISSORT` and whose attributed plan is this one, counted whether those exceptions are still `OPEN` or already `RESOLVED`. The numerator is `actual_labor_cents` plus the fixed overhead of 12,000 cents per shift plus that rework charge. The denominator is the plan's `sorted_package_count` minus its `missort_package_count`, floored at 1 so a shift that sorted nothing does not divide by zero. `final_cost_per_package_cents` is that quotient rounded half up to two decimal places. Where the provisional figure divided planned money by the full forecast, the final figure divides actually-worked money plus rework by actually-sorted-and-correct packages.

Closure also unwinds the shift's claims on the building. The bench is released exactly: the center's `flex_pool_available` increases by the plan's `flex_draw_count` and `flex_pool_reserved` decreases by the same amount, which is the precise compensating action of the reservation taken at approval and of any top-up taken by a no-show, so `flex_pool_total` is conserved across the whole lifecycle; the center's `version` increments by 1. And the sort floor is tidied: every chute assignment of that center whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `window_end` falls at or before the plan's `shift_window_end` transitions to the terminal `ENDED` — `ACTIVE → ENDED` and `SUSPENDED → ENDED` alike — with `effective_window_end` set to that assignment's own `window_end`, and every destination lane whose assignment set changed has its `assigned_capacity_pph` and `lane_pressure_ratio` recomputed. Assignments whose windows extend past the shift are left alone to serve the next shift.
