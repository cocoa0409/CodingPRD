# Commuter Benefit Program Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employers who sponsor a commuter benefit program sit between two unforgiving forces. On one side sits the tax code, which permits a fixed monthly amount of transit and parking spending to be excluded from taxable wages and not one cent more; on the other sits a growing body of local commuter benefit ordinances that compel employers above a headcount threshold to offer a qualifying transit benefit at or above a minimum monthly limit. Between them sit real commuters who elect an amount, change their minds mid-month, lose a paper pass in the rain, swipe a benefit card at a merchant that turns out not to be a transit merchant, and occasionally leave the company three days before a benefit month closes. A commuter benefit administrator is the party that has to make all of this reconcile to the cent, every month, for every employee, and then hand payroll a deduction file it can actually process.

This document specifies the service that administrators, procurement desks, compliance reviewers and payroll integrators use to run that program. The service owns program setup (eligible benefit categories and the monthly exclusion limits that govern them), the election calendar (when employees may elect, when the order cutoff falls, and whether mid-month changes are permitted), the money (a strictly conserved per-employee benefit ledger with contributions, spend, reversals, forfeitures and refunds), the physical and virtual media supply chain (paper passes, smart card loads and regional debit card products, procured in batches against a vendor cutoff), the substantiation of card spending against merchant category codes and submitted documentation, ordinance compliance assessment, and the payroll deduction file itself.

Three design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **money is conserved**: every movement of benefit dollars is an append-only ledger entry, and a benefit account's spendable balance is at all times exactly the signed sum of its ledger. Second, **compensation is exact**: every forward action that moves money — funding an election, posting a card spend, charging a replacement fee — has a compensating action that returns the affected balances to the value they held before, and a fair implementation must be able to run the forward and compensating pair and land back on the original number. Third, **duties are segregated**: the operator who seals a procurement batch may not be the operator who releases it to the vendor, and the reviewer who approved a substantiation may not be the reviewer who later reopens it for audit. All resources in this service are mounted under the `/benefits` prefix, with the single exception of the test-driving clock endpoint.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capability set the caller is acting under. If either header is absent or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the five roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before every other check in the system. Once the caller is authenticated, the service confirms that the declared role appears in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called; a role acting outside its remit is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `Employee`, `OrderDesk`, `ComplianceReviewer` and `PayrollProcessor`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the commuter benefit administrator itself. It registers and configures employer programs, maintains the benefit category catalogue and the employee roster, publishes and locks election windows, opens benefit accounts, ingests settled card transactions, reverses election funding, runs the monthly close, and drives the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

An `Employee` is an enrolled commuter acting on their own records: filing and amending elections, cancelling them, confirming or disputing media delivery, and submitting expense substantiation. When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee` row, and ownership checks compare the target resource's owning employee to that value. Concretely: for an election the compared field is the election's `employee_id`; for a media order it is the `employee_id` of the election named by the order's `election_id`; for a card transaction it is the `employee_id` of the election named by the transaction's `election_id`; for a substantiation claim it is the `employee_id` of the election behind the claim's `card_transaction_id`; and for a benefit account it is the account's own `employee_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This ownership gate guards exactly eight operations: `POST /benefits/elections`, `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`, `POST /benefits/elections/{election_id}/cancel`, `POST /benefits/media-orders/{order_id}/report-undelivered`, `POST /benefits/media-orders/{order_id}/confirm-delivery`, `POST /benefits/substantiation-claims`, `GET /benefits/benefit-accounts/{account_id}` and `GET /benefits/benefit-accounts/{account_id}/ledger-entries`.

An `OrderDesk` is the transit media procurement operator: it curates the regional media catalogue, assembles procurement batches against the order cutoff calendar, seals and releases them, records shipments, and reissues lost or undelivered media. When `X-User-Role` is `OrderDesk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does record it — as `sealed_by_user_id` when a batch is sealed and as `released_by_user_id` when a batch is released — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

A `ComplianceReviewer` reviews substantiation evidence and ordinance posture: it approves and rejects substantiation claims, denies unsupported swipes, reopens settled substantiation postures for audit, and runs ordinance compliance assessments. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `reviewed_by_user_id` on the reviewed claim, and as `approved_by_user_id` or `denied_by_user_id` on the underlying card transaction, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

A `PayrollProcessor` is the payroll integration operator. It creates and generates the employer deduction file, transmits it to the payroll vendor, and records the vendor's acknowledgement. When `X-User-Role` is `PayrollProcessor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server neither persists it on any row nor compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two operations additionally compare the acting `X-User-ID` against an identifier the server recorded during an earlier step of the same workflow. Releasing a sealed procurement batch requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the batch's `sealed_by_user_id`, and reopening a card transaction that currently sits in a substantiated posture requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from that transaction's `approved_by_user_id`. Either collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403); the two guarded operations are `POST /benefits/order-batches/{batch_id}/release` and `POST /benefits/card-transactions/{transaction_id}/reopen`. A recorded identifier that is still null cannot collide with anything, so the comparison passes trivially — which is why reopening a swipe that reached its settled posture automatically, and therefore carries no `approved_by_user_id`, is never refused on segregation grounds. Note that this is a comparison between two opaque operator identifiers and is not an ownership check — it does not resolve either identifier to a persisted entity.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters each drawn at random from the uppercase Latin letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, so the whole value is always twenty-four characters long.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from real server time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so moving the logical clock never changes it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. A serialized resource always carries its entity's complete declared field set, identically on reads, creates and transitions: a field that currently holds no value is emitted as JSON `null` and is never omitted from the object, and no field is ever added or dropped based on the resource's state.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and are absent altogether from the `meta` of any response whose `response_type` is not `array`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. This same envelope also carries the failures that fall outside the catalogue of §5: a request to a path this service does not route answers HTTP 404 with `error_id` `ERR_NOT_FOUND`, a request using an HTTP method the routed path does not accept answers HTTP 405 with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED`, and an unexpected server-side failure answers HTTP 500 with `ERR_INTERNAL`. The first two are decided by routing alone and never consult the headers of §2.1, so an unrouted path answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` even when both headers are missing.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer no smaller than 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and must be an integer no smaller than 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422). A `limit` or `offset` whose text is not a run of base-ten digits, optionally preceded by a single `-`, is rejected with that same `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and never with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an empty value, a decimal, a leading `+` and any other non-numeric text all fail this way. Surrounding whitespace is trimmed before the value is read, so ` 5 ` is a valid limit; only a parameter omitted from the query string entirely falls back to its default, while one that is present but empty or all-whitespace fails.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with the row's insertion order as the residual tie-break; because `created_at` is minted strictly increasing (§2.4) that tie-break can never actually be reached, so the ordering is a total order. The four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` appear in the `meta` object of every list response: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource in this service is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID string carrying no type prefix and no embedded meaning — clients must never construct one. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits recorded at insertion and never modified afterwards. Wherever this document calls for an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `api_request_timestamp`, and the clock-stamped `locked_at`, `sealed_at` and `transmitted_at` — the rendering is literally `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`: a `T` separator, exactly three digits of milliseconds, and a trailing `Z` rather than a numeric offset. The `created_at` stamp is minted from real server time and not from the logical clock of §2.5, and the mint is strictly increasing: each stamp the process issues is at least one whole millisecond later than the one before it, so no two rows — of the same entity or of different entities — ever carry the same `created_at`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits drawn from that same strictly increasing real-time mint: it is set byte-for-byte equal to `created_at` when the row is inserted and refreshed to a strictly later instant on every subsequent mutation. Alongside it sits `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. The `version` counter is the substrate of the stale-version rejection described in §2.11, and it is also the auditor's evidence that a cascade actually touched a row: when a workflow says a row was mutated, that row's `version` advanced and its `updated_at` moved.

Two entities are deliberate exceptions because they are append-only audit records rather than mutable resources. A `LedgerEntry` carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and there is no endpoint that updates or deletes one. A `ComplianceAssessment` likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`, and is immutable from the moment it is appended.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, cutoff, grace period and coverage-interval comparison in this specification reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. At process startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until a caller moves it, every temporal comparison in the system must use that value; falling back to real wall-clock time is incorrect. A `ProgramAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock endpoint; the field is required, and a missing or unparseable value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An instant written without a timezone offset is read as UTC, and one written with an offset is converted to UTC — this holds for `to_iso8601` and for every other ISO 8601 datetime body field the service accepts. That conversion governs every comparison the service makes, but it does **not** rewrite what the client wrote: a client-supplied datetime that is persisted on a row — an employee's `hire_date` and `termination_date`, an election window's `opens_at` and `cutoff_at`, a media line's `coverage_start_at` and `coverage_end_at`, and a card transaction's `transaction_at` — is stored and echoed back byte-for-byte as the request spelled it, offset, missing offset, absent milliseconds and all, while the normalized UTC instant derived from it is kept only for internal comparison and is never surfaced. Only the three fields the server stamps from the logical clock itself are rendered in the canonical form. Setting the clock mutates no entity, fires no cascade, and answers HTTP 200 with an empty data object. Wherever the service stamps a field from that clock — an election window's `locked_at`, a procurement batch's `sealed_at` and a payroll file's `transmitted_at` — the field carries that instant rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits, irrespective of how the instant was spelled when the clock was set.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error returned. The mapping from path variable to entity is fixed: `employer_id` resolves an Employer, `employee_id` an Employee, `window_id` an ElectionWindow, `category_id` a BenefitCategory, `account_id` a BenefitAccount, `election_id` an Election, `media_id` a TransitMedia, `batch_id` an OrderBatch, `order_id` a MediaOrder, `transaction_id` a CardTransaction, `claim_id` a SubstantiationClaim, `file_id` a PayrollFile, `entry_id` a LedgerEntry and `assessment_id` a ComplianceAssessment. A failure yields HTTP 404 carrying the code for the entity that could not be found: `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BENEFIT_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BENEFIT_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSIT_MEDIA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEDIA_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARD_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYROLL_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` — the last two being the codes returned when an `entry_id` or an `assessment_id` fails to resolve.

The same 404 family applies to identifiers supplied in a request **body** rather than a path. Every creation payload that names another record — `employer_id`, `employee_id`, `election_window_id`, `benefit_category_id`, `transit_media_id`, `election_id`, `order_batch_id`, `card_transaction_id` — must resolve to an existing row, and the failure carries the same per-entity code (with a body `order_batch_id` reported as `ERR_ORDER_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, a body `card_transaction_id` as `ERR_CARD_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, and a non-resolving benefit account as `ERR_BENEFIT_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`). When one payload names several such records they are resolved in the order that payload's own description gives them, and — exactly as for path parameters — the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error: an election create resolves `employee_id`, then `election_window_id`, then `benefit_category_id`, then `transit_media_id`; a media line create resolves `election_id`, then `transit_media_id`, then `order_batch_id`; and a benefit account create resolves `employee_id` before `benefit_category_id`. Because the entity topology of this service is flat — every record lives in its own top-level collection and is linked by explicit identifier fields in the body — nested read paths exist only for the two append-only logs. Each of those is also exposed as a filterable top-level collection so that every reference is reverse-reachable: an account's ledger can be read either beneath the account or from the global ledger collection filtered by `benefit_account_id`, and an employer's assessments either beneath the employer or from the global assessment collection filtered by `employer_id`.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence

Several checks can be simultaneously true for one request, and callers depend on which code comes back. The service therefore evaluates checks in a single fixed ladder, returning the first failure it encounters and never masking an earlier check with a later one. In ascending order of evaluation:

1. Header presence and role validity, as described in §2.1 — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. Endpoint role authorization — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Left-to-right path identifier resolution — the per-entity 404 codes of §2.6. Applies to every endpoint that has path parameters.
4. Request shape: every required body field present, JSON types matching the declared field types, integer fields at or above their declared minimum and — where the field's description states an upper bound — at or below that maximum, string fields within their stated character-length bounds and — where a minimum length is stated — not blank, so an all-whitespace value fails even when it is long enough, `benefit_month`, `period_month` and `assessment_month` matching exactly the four-digit year, a hyphen, and the two-digit month, ISO 8601 datetimes parsing, and `coverage_end_at` strictly after `coverage_start_at` — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Three further shape rules are settled here. A declared field supplied explicitly as JSON `null` fails this check unless that field is one of the two the service admits `null` for — an election's `transit_media_id` and a payroll acknowledgement's `acknowledgement_code` — and a `null` for a field carrying a default does *not* fall back to the default. Body keys the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. And an entirely absent or empty request body is treated as an empty JSON object, so it fails only through the required-field rule, whereas a body that parses to JSON that is not an object fails this check outright. Applies to every POST, PUT and PATCH endpoint. Separately, a boolean-typed list query filter whose value reads case-insensitively as neither `true`/`1` nor `false`/`0` is reported with this same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than as an enum error, but it is evaluated among the query filters at step 5's position rather than ahead of them, so a simultaneously invalid enum filter is what the caller sees.
5. Enumerated values: every enum-typed body or query field carrying a value matched exactly and case-sensitively against that field's declared set, covering `region_code`, `work_region_code`, `category_type`, `eligible_category_type`, `media_type`, `payroll_cycle`, `spill_policy`, `rollover_policy`, `doc_type`, `rejection_reason`, the `reason` supplied when reporting media undelivered, the acknowledgement `outcome`, and the `entry_type`, `source_entity`, `status`, `program_status`, `ordinance_mandate_status`, `mandate_status`, `finding`, `funding_status`, `fulfillment_status` and `substantiation_status` values used as list filters — `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`. Applies to every endpoint accepting an enum body or query field.
6. Pagination bounds — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Applies to every list endpoint.
7. Body identifier resolution, as described in §2.6 — the per-entity 404 codes. Applies to every POST endpoint carrying reference fields in its body.
8. Employee ownership scope — `ERR_EMPLOYEE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, on the eight operations enumerated in §2.1.
9. Owning employer program active: the Employer that owns the target resource must have `program_status` of `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYER_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). This gate guards six operations: `POST /benefits/election-windows/{window_id}/open`, `POST /benefits/elections`, `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`, `POST /benefits/election-windows/{window_id}/lock`, `POST /benefits/order-batches` and `POST /benefits/media-orders`.
10. Election window posture: creating an election requires the window's `status` to be exactly `OPEN`; amending one requires the window's `status` to be either `OPEN` or `LOCKED`. A `SCHEDULED` or `CLOSED` window rejects both with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The gate guards `POST /benefits/elections` and `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`.
11. Batch openness: a media line may only be added to a batch whose `status` is `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_BATCH_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Guards `POST /benefits/media-orders`.
12. Transition existence: the target row's current state must have a declared outgoing transition for the requested action, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Guards every action endpoint — activate, retire, terminate, discontinue, open, lock, cancel, reverse-funding, seal, release, ship, confirm-delivery, report-undelivered, reissue, deny-substantiation, reopen, approve, reject, generate, transmit, acknowledge and month-close. A precondition an action places on a row *other* than its own target belongs to this same check and is reported with this same code: the governing election window's `status` on an election cancel, on a funding reversal and on a batch release, the owning batch's `status` and the catalogue product's `requires_physical_fulfillment` on a shipment, the underlying card transaction's `substantiation_status` on a claim approval or rejection, the existence of a `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` window for the file's month on a payroll generate, and a `participant_count` of at least 1 on a payroll transmit are all transition-existence conditions, so none of them ever surfaces as `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_ORDER_BATCH_NOT_OPEN` or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
13. Amendment eligibility: the amended election's `funding_status` must be `PENDING`, `HELD` or `FUNDED`. A `REVERSED`, `SETTLED` or `CANCELLED` election is closed to amendment and the employee must file a fresh election instead — `ERR_ELECTION_ALREADY_FUNDED` (HTTP 409). Guards `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`.
14. Terminality: the target row must not already sit in one of its entity's terminal states, otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). Applies to every action endpoint on Employer, BenefitCategory, Employee, TransitMedia, ElectionWindow, Election, OrderBatch, MediaOrder, SubstantiationClaim and PayrollFile, and additionally to the employer adjustment `PATCH /benefits/employers/{employer_id}`; the election amendment `PATCH` is *not* guarded by it, because check 13 already covers exactly the funding postures that are terminal there. The terminal set of each entity is exactly `SUSPENDED` for an Employer, `RETIRED` for a BenefitCategory, `TERMINATED` for an Employee, `DISCONTINUED` for a TransitMedia, `CLOSED` for an ElectionWindow, `REVERSED`, `CANCELLED` and `SETTLED` for an Election's `funding_status`, `RECONCILED` for an OrderBatch, `CANCELLED` for a MediaOrder, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` for a SubstantiationClaim, and `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `REJECTED` for a PayrollFile; a CardTransaction has no terminal state and is therefore never refused this way. Despite its position in this list, this check is evaluated **ahead of** check 12 wherever both could fire on the same row, because a row parked in a terminal state has no declared outgoing transition for any trigger at all: an action attempted against a terminal row therefore always answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` and never `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
15. Ordinance posture — `ERR_ORDINANCE_MANDATE_UNMET` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}` (see §3.9).
16. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on `POST /benefits/order-batches/{batch_id}/release` and `POST /benefits/card-transactions/{transaction_id}/reopen`.
17. Substantiation hold — `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_HOLD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`.
18. Category retirement — `ERR_BENEFIT_CATEGORY_RETIRED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `POST /benefits/benefit-accounts`.
19. Media eligibility and region — `ERR_MEDIA_REGION_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `POST /benefits/media-orders`.
20. Media catalogue availability — `ERR_TRANSIT_MEDIA_DISCONTINUED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `POST /benefits/media-orders`.
21. Account and batch coherence — `ERR_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `POST /benefits/media-orders`.
22. Allocation type permission — `ERR_ALLOCATION_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`.
23. Order cutoff — `ERR_ORDER_CUTOFF_PASSED` (HTTP 422), on four operations: `POST /benefits/elections`, `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`, `POST /benefits/media-orders` and `POST /benefits/order-batches/{batch_id}/seal`.
24. Election window calendar overlap — `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), on `POST /benefits/election-windows`.
25. Media coverage overlap — `ERR_MEDIA_COVERAGE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), on `POST /benefits/media-orders`.
26. Mid-month change permission — `ERR_MID_MONTH_CHANGE_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422), on `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`.
27. Mid-month change count, evaluated only after check 26 has passed — `ERR_CHANGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`.
28. Non-empty batch — `ERR_EMPTY_ORDER_BATCH` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/order-batches/{batch_id}/seal`.
29. Reissue limit and fee-waiver eligibility — `ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_REPLACEMENT_FEE_NOT_WAIVABLE` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/media-orders/{order_id}/reissue`. Within this rung the limit is tested first, so a line already at the reissue allowance that simultaneously asks to waive the fee on an unwaivable reason surfaces as `ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
30. Reopen limit — `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/card-transactions/{transaction_id}/reopen`.
31. Stale parent version — `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ELECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `POST /benefits/media-orders` respectively (see §2.11).
32. Pre-tax overage under a rejecting spill policy — `ERR_EXCLUSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/elections` and `PATCH /benefits/elections/{election_id}`.
33. Sufficient benefit balance — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BENEFIT_BALANCE` (HTTP 402), on `POST /benefits/card-transactions`.
34. Claim amount agreement — `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), on `POST /benefits/substantiation-claims/{claim_id}/approve`.

The ordering matters in specific, testable ways. Because checks 15 through 17 precede checks 18 through 23, an employer found deficient under its local ordinance surfaces as `ERR_ORDINANCE_MANDATE_UNMET` even when the election also names a retired category or a discontinued media product; because check 26 precedes check 27, an amendment against a locked window that forbids mid-month changes surfaces as `ERR_MID_MONTH_CHANGE_NOT_ALLOWED` and never as `ERR_CHANGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; and because check 31 precedes check 32, a stale parent version is reported before any allocation arithmetic is judged.

### 2.8. Whole-Cent Arithmetic and Rounding Discipline

All monetary values in this service are integers denominated in whole cents. No monetary field ever holds a fractional value, and no monetary field is ever expressed in dollars. Wherever a percentage is applied to a cent amount, the result is converted back to whole cents at a pinned rounding point using **round half up** — a result exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

Three rounding decisions are pinned because a different order produces a different number of cents. First, the pre-tax ratio cap on an election truncates toward zero rather than rounding: the capped amount is the largest whole number of cents not exceeding the requested total multiplied by the employer's permitted pre-tax percentage divided by one hundred. Second, the regional handling surcharge on a procurement batch is rounded to whole cents **per line** and only then summed; summing the gross amounts first and rounding a single aggregate surcharge is forbidden. Third, the bulk discount on a batch is rounded exactly once, applied to the already-summed subtotal. Every other computation in this document is exact integer arithmetic with no rounding step at all.

### 2.9. The Benefit Dollar Ledger and Its Conservation Invariant

Benefit dollars live in a `BenefitAccount`, one per employee per benefit category, and every movement of those dollars is recorded as an immutable `LedgerEntry`. A ledger entry names the account it applies to through `benefit_account_id`, classifies the movement through `entry_type`, carries a signed `amount_cents` where a positive value credits the account's spendable balance and a negative value debits it, records the account's spendable balance immediately after the movement in `balance_after_cents`, explains itself in a short human-readable `reason` string, and points back at whatever caused it through the pair `source_entity` and `source_id`. That pair is polymorphic and must be resolved together: `source_entity` names the kind of record — one of `BenefitAccount`, `Election`, `CardTransaction` or `MediaOrder` — and `source_id` holds that record's identifier. Neither half is meaningful on its own. The permitted `entry_type` values are `ROLLOVER_IN`, `CONTRIBUTION`, `CONTRIBUTION_ADJUSTMENT`, `SPEND`, `SPEND_REVERSAL`, `FORFEITURE`, `REFUND`, `FUNDING_REVERSAL` and `REPLACEMENT_FEE`. The global ledger collection is listable with filters on `benefit_account_id`, `entry_type`, `source_entity` and `source_id`; the nested per-account ledger accepts the `entry_type` filter alone, since the account is already fixed by the path.

Each account maintains seven cumulative buckets alongside its spendable balance. `rollover_in_cents` holds the opening carried-in balance credited when the account was created. `contributed_cents` accumulates every benefit dollar credited by election funding. `spent_cents` accumulates every dollar posted against a card transaction. `forfeited_cents` accumulates dollars forfeited at a month close, `refunded_cents` dollars returned to the employer under the termination grace rule, `reversed_cents` dollars removed by a funding reversal, and `fee_charged_cents` media replacement fees debited from the account. All seven are lifetime-cumulative: a month close never resets any of them. The account also tracks `ledger_entry_count`, the number of ledger rows written against it.

Two identities must hold after **every single mutation** that touches an account, without exception. First, `available_balance_cents` equals `rollover_in_cents` plus `contributed_cents`, minus `spent_cents`, minus `forfeited_cents`, minus `refunded_cents`, minus `reversed_cents`, minus `fee_charged_cents`. Second, `available_balance_cents` equals the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every ledger entry whose `benefit_account_id` is this account — a sum taken with no status filter, because the log is append-only and there is nothing to exclude. A third, order-sensitive identity governs the log itself: each entry's `balance_after_cents` equals the running signed total of all entries for that account up to and including itself, taken in ascending `created_at` order. These identities are re-evaluated after account creation, after an election funding credit, after the contribution adjustment of a mid-month amendment to a funded election, after a funding reversal, after a card-transaction spend posting, after a substantiation denial reverses a spend, after a reopen re-posts one, after a media reissue fee debit, and after a month close.

### 2.10. Media Unit Conservation

A second quantity is conserved alongside money, and it is a count rather than a currency. Each election records the number of physical media units the employee is entitled to for the benefit month in `entitled_media_units`. The net media units actually on order for that election is the sum of `quantity` over the election's media order rows whose `status` is `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED` or `UNDELIVERED`; rows whose `status` is `CANCELLED` are the only exclusion, and that includes every row cancelled because a later create superseded it. After any completed forward-plus-compensating media cycle — reporting a line undelivered and then reissuing it, or creating a line and then superseding it with a newer one — that net must equal `entitled_media_units` exactly. No cycle may mint extra media units, which is why a reissue reuses the same row with the same `quantity` rather than opening a second one. This quantity is re-evaluated after every media order creation, supersede-cancellation, shipment, delivery confirmation, undelivered report and reissue.

### 2.11. Stale-Version Rejection on Dependent Writes

Two creation operations write into an aggregate they do not own, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against that aggregate's `version`.

Creating an election requires a non-optional `expected_account_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the `BenefitAccount` resolved from the election's `employee_id` and `benefit_category_id`. If that value does not equal the account's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because an election create legitimately supersedes any prior live election for the same employee, window and category triple: a writer holding an out-of-date view of the account cannot know what balance and what prior election it is about to displace.

Creating a media order requires a non-optional `expected_election_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the `Election` named by `election_id`. If that value does not equal the election's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ELECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a media order create legitimately supersedes the prior pending line for the same election and media product, so a writer holding a stale election version cannot know which line it is about to cancel.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above must not require one and must not reject a request on version grounds.

### 2.12. Cascade Propagation and Version Semantics

A single request in this system frequently mutates several rows, and it does so transitively: each hop is caused by the preceding hop, not by the original request. Two chains run four levels deep. Locking an election window changes the window, the window change re-funds that window's pending elections, each funding credits that election's benefit account, and it is the account credit that moves the employer's committed pre-tax total — which may in turn push the total past the employer's program cap and auto-suspend the program. Denying substantiation on a card swipe changes the transaction, the transaction change credits a spend reversal into the benefit account, the ledger credit creates a receivable against the employee, and it is the receivable that raises the employee's hold flag — which is what freezes that employee's pending elections. Neither final hop is triggered directly by the request; each is a consequence of the hop above it.

Every row a cascade actually mutates has its `version` incremented by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed. Rows a cascade evaluates but does not change are left alone, and their `version` does not move. Computed rollup counters are recomputed as part of the same request rather than lazily: `enrolled_employee_count` on an employer, `active_election_count` on a benefit category, `election_count` on an election window, `ledger_entry_count` on a benefit account and `line_count` on a procurement batch are all correct at the moment the response is emitted. `line_count` in particular is at all times the number of that batch's media order lines whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, so it is refreshed both when a line is added to the batch and when a line of the batch is cancelled; refreshing that count alone, with nothing else about the batch row changing, does not advance `OrderBatch.version` or `updated_at`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Employer Program

A `ProgramAdmin` begins by registering the sponsoring employer. The registration payload carries the employer's `legal_name` — between 1 and 200 characters and never blank — together with the `region_code` of the primary work location, which decides which local commuter benefit ordinance will be applied later and must be one of `US-CA-SFO`, `US-NY-NYC`, `US-IL-CHI`, `US-WA-SEA` or `US-TX-AUS`. Three money ceilings are required at registration: `transit_group_limit_cents`, the monthly pre-tax exclusion limit shared by the whole transit side of the program; `parking_group_limit_cents`, the equivalent monthly limit for parking; and `program_cap_cents`, an aggregate ceiling on funded pre-tax exclusion across the entire employer. All three must be zero or greater. A newly registered employer lands in `program_status` `DRAFT` with `ordinance_mandate_status` `UNASSESSED`, `total_pretax_committed_cents` and `enrolled_employee_count` both at zero, and `version` 1.

Four policy dials are optional at registration and fall back to defaults that suit the common case. `pretax_ratio_cap_pct` bounds the share of any election's requested total that may be taken pre-tax; it is an integer from 0 through 100 inclusive and defaults to 100, meaning no ratio restriction. `spill_policy` decides what happens when a pre-tax request exceeds the available exclusion headroom and defaults to `SPILL_TO_POSTTAX`, the alternative being `REJECT_OVERAGE`. `rollover_policy` governs unused dollars at month close and defaults to `CAP_ROLLOVER`, the alternatives being `NO_ROLLOVER` and `FULL_ROLLOVER`. `rollover_cap_cents` is the ceiling applied to carried-over dollars when the policy is `CAP_ROLLOVER` and defaults to 5000 cents — fifty dollars a month. Alongside them, `forfeiture_grace_days` sets how many whole days after a departing employee's `termination_date` unused dollars are refunded to the employer rather than forfeited, and defaults to 30. All eight of these setup values — the two group limits, the program cap, the ratio cap, the two policy flags, the rollover cap and the grace period — remain editable while the program is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` through the employer adjustment operation, which overwrites only the fields supplied and advances `Employer.version` by 1 — unconditionally, so even an adjustment that supplies no fields at all still advances the counter. A `SUSPENDED` employer is closed to adjustment and is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, evaluated after the request-shape check so a malformed adjustment against a suspended program still surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Employer programs are listable with filters on `region_code`, `program_status` and `ordinance_mandate_status`, each matched against its declared set.

Before the program can accept anyone, at least one benefit category must exist. A category declares its owning `employer_id`, its `category_type` — one of `TRANSIT_PASS`, `VANPOOL` or `PARKING` — and its own `monthly_exclusion_limit_cents`, a per-category monthly pre-tax sub-limit that applies **on top of** the employer's group limit. Two booleans control which halves of an allocation the category will accept: `pretax_allowed` and `posttax_allowed`, both defaulting to true. The service derives `limit_group` from the category type rather than accepting it: a category whose `category_type` is `TRANSIT_PASS` or `VANPOOL` belongs to the `TRANSIT` limit group, and a `PARKING` category belongs to the `PARKING` limit group. That derived group is what selects between `transit_group_limit_cents` and `parking_group_limit_cents` in every later exclusion computation. A new category opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `active_election_count` at zero; that counter tracks the number of elections referencing the category whose `funding_status` is `PENDING`, `HELD` or `FUNDED`. Declaring a category is not gated on the employer's `program_status`, which is why the go-live check below can be satisfied from `DRAFT`. Retiring a category moves it `ACTIVE → RETIRED`, which is terminal, and deliberately touches nothing else — existing elections continue exactly as they were, and only *new* elections and *new* benefit accounts are refused, with `ERR_BENEFIT_CATEGORY_RETIRED`. Categories are listable with filters on `employer_id`, `category_type` and `status`.

Activating the program is the `ProgramAdmin`'s go-live step, moving the employer `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. It requires that `program_status` be exactly `DRAFT` and that at least one benefit category exist for this employer with `status` `ACTIVE`. Both of those requirements are transition-existence conditions, so an already-`ACTIVE` program and a `DRAFT` program that owns no `ACTIVE` category are alike refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, the posture test being evaluated first; a `SUSPENDED` program is refused earlier still, with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. On success the employer's `program_status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `enrolled_employee_count` is recomputed as the number of that employer's employees whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and `Employer.version` advances by 1. The only other posture an employer can reach is `SUSPENDED`, which is terminal and is never requested directly — it arrives as the last hop of the funding cascade described in §3.4.

Employees are enrolled one at a time. An enrolment payload names the `employer_id`, the employer-side payroll identifier `external_ref` (between 1 and 64 characters, and unique within an employer), the `work_region_code` where the employee reports — drawn from the same five-region set — and the deduction cadence `payroll_cycle`, either `SEMI_MONTHLY` or `MONTHLY`, which the payroll integration honours. The payload also carries `hire_date`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant that later decides whether the employee counts toward an ordinance headcount. An `external_ref` that collides with an existing employee of the same employer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that uniqueness test runs only after the body's `employer_id` has resolved, so an unresolvable employer beats a duplicate reference. A new employee opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `termination_date` null, `substantiation_hold` false and `repayment_due_cents` at zero; enrolment also recomputes the employer's `enrolled_employee_count` and advances `Employer.version` by 1 — unconditionally, even when the recomputed count is unchanged. Enrolment is not gated on the employer's `program_status`, so employees may be loaded into a `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` program alike. Employees are listable with filters on `employer_id`, `status`, `work_region_code` and the boolean `substantiation_hold`.

Termination is the mirror image and is deliberately destructive of anything still in flight. It carries the `termination_date` instant, requires that the employee currently be `ACTIVE`, and requires that the supplied `termination_date` be no earlier than the employee's `hire_date` — a `termination_date` exactly equal to the `hire_date` is accepted, and an earlier one is a transition-existence failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than as a request-shape error, evaluated after the posture test. On success the employee moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with `termination_date` recorded. Then the cascade runs: every election of that employee whose `funding_status` is `PENDING` or `HELD` is moved to `CANCELLED` and has `excluded_amount_cents`, `posttax_spill_cents`, `posttax_effective_cents` and `total_amount_cents` all zeroed; for every election so cancelled, every media order of that election whose `status` is `PENDING` or `SUBMITTED` is moved to `CANCELLED`; the `fulfillment_status` of every touched election is re-derived; the employer's `enrolled_employee_count` is recomputed; and `Employee.version` advances by 1, as does the `version` of every election, media order and employer row the cascade actually changed. Note what termination does *not* do: it does not touch elections already `FUNDED`, `REVERSED` or `SETTLED`, and it does not immediately move money. What it does is switch the employee's benefit accounts onto a different month-close branch, described in §3.8.

The last piece of setup is the money container itself. A benefit account is opened for one `employee_id` in one `benefit_category_id`, and the pair must be unique — that pair is exactly how an election later resolves which account it belongs to. The account may be seeded with a carried-in balance through the optional `opening_rollover_cents` field, which lands on the account as `rollover_in_cents` and defaults to zero. When a non-zero opening balance is supplied, the account's very first ledger entry is written at creation with `entry_type` `ROLLOVER_IN` and a positive `amount_cents` equal to that opening balance, with `source_entity` `BenefitAccount` and `source_id` the account's own identifier, and the account's `available_balance_cents` is set accordingly. The referenced category must be `ACTIVE`; a retired category refuses account creation with `ERR_BENEFIT_CATEGORY_RETIRED`, and that refusal is evaluated before the uniqueness test, so a second account for a retired category reports the retirement rather than the collision. A second account for an employee and category pair that already has one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A fresh account carries `version` 1 and zero in `contributed_cents`, `spent_cents`, `forfeited_cents`, `refunded_cents`, `reversed_cents` and `fee_charged_cents`; the opening `ROLLOVER_IN` entry is written without advancing that `version`, so an account seeded with a carried-in balance still reads `version` 1 with `ledger_entry_count` 1. Benefit accounts are listable with filters on `employee_id` and `benefit_category_id`, and that list is not narrowed by the employee ownership gate of §2.1.

### 3.2. The Order Cutoff Calendar

Elections are collected inside windows, and windows occupy slots on the employer's order cutoff calendar. Creating one names the `employer_id`, the `benefit_month` it collects elections for — exactly four digits of year, a hyphen, and two digits of month — and the pair of instants that bound its calendar occupation: `opens_at`, the inclusive start, and `cutoff_at`, the exclusive end and simultaneously the hard order cutoff. Two optional settings ride along: `fulfillment_lead_days`, the whole days of vendor lead time expected between batch release and media delivery, defaulting to 5; and `mid_month_change_allowed`, a flag deciding whether elections may still be amended once the window has locked, defaulting to false. The two instants are taken as supplied and are not required to be ordered: only `coverage_end_at` against `coverage_start_at` on a media line is shape-checked for ordering, so a window whose `cutoff_at` falls at or before its `opens_at` is accepted, and the overlap test below simply applies its two comparisons literally to whatever pair of instants was supplied. Window creation is not gated on the employer's `program_status` either. A new window is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `locked_at` null and `election_count` at zero; that counter tracks elections in the window whose `funding_status` is `PENDING`, `HELD`, `FUNDED` or `SETTLED`. Windows are listable with filters on `employer_id`, `status` and `benefit_month`.

The calendar admits no double-booking. A window's occupation interval runs from `opens_at` inclusive up to `cutoff_at` exclusive, and a new window is refused if that half-open interval intersects the half-open interval of any existing window of the same employer whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN` or `LOCKED`. Concretely, the two intervals conflict when the new window's `opens_at` falls strictly before the existing window's `cutoff_at` **and** the new window's `cutoff_at` falls strictly after the existing window's `opens_at`. Because both endpoints are treated half-open, adjacency is entirely legal: a window whose `opens_at` is exactly equal to another window's `cutoff_at` does not conflict, and back-to-back monthly windows are the normal case. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Windows already `CLOSED` are ignored by the check, so last month's calendar slot never blocks this month's.

Publishing a window advances it `SCHEDULED → OPEN`, which is what makes it visible to electing employees. It requires the window to be `SCHEDULED` and the owning employer's `program_status` to be `ACTIVE`; a `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED` program is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. Once `OPEN`, the window accepts new elections until the cutoff.

Locking the window is the single most consequential operation in the system, and §3.4 walks its arithmetic. Structurally, it advances the window `OPEN → LOCKED`, stamps `locked_at` with the current logical clock instant, recomputes `election_count`, and then sets off a chain that re-prices and funds every pending election in the window, credits each election's benefit account, and finally re-tests the employer's aggregate commitment against its program cap. Locking requires only that the window currently be `OPEN` and that the owning employer's `program_status` be `ACTIVE`. The window's final resting state, `CLOSED`, is reached only through the monthly close described in §3.8; an already-`CLOSED` window rejects further transitions with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and any action with no declared outgoing transition from the current state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.3. Filing, Superseding, Amending and Cancelling an Election

An election is the central allocation record of the whole system, and it is unusual in carrying **two entirely independent state dimensions on the same row**. `funding_status` tracks the money side and moves through `PENDING`, `HELD`, `FUNDED`, `REVERSED`, `CANCELLED` and `SETTLED`. `fulfillment_status` tracks the media side and moves through `NOT_REQUIRED`, `AWAITING_ORDER`, `ORDERED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`, `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` and `CANCELLED`. The two axes never read or write each other: an election sitting in `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` because a pass was stolen is still perfectly fundable, and an election sitting in `FUNDED` does not advance one step of fulfillment on its own. The only bridge between them is cancellation, which forces both axes down at once.

Either the electing `Employee` or a `ProgramAdmin` files an election. The payload names the `employee_id`, the `election_window_id` it is being collected in, and the `benefit_category_id` being elected, and it supplies the requested split: `pretax_amount_cents`, which must be zero or greater, and the optional `posttax_amount_cents`, which defaults to zero and must likewise be zero or greater. Two optional media fields ride along: `transit_media_id`, naming the catalogue product the employee wants fulfilled, which is null for a cash-reimbursement-only election; and `media_quantity`, the number of physical media units the employee is entitled to for the month, which lands on the row as `entitled_media_units` and defaults to zero. Finally, the payload must carry `expected_account_version`, the optimistic-concurrency token described in §2.11. The service resolves `benefit_account_id` itself, as the unique benefit account whose `employee_id` and `benefit_category_id` both match the election; if no such account exists, or if the resolved account's `employee_id` or `benefit_category_id` fails to match the election's, the create is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. Every employer-level posture and limit the create consults — the `program_status` gate of §2.7 check 9, the ordinance gate of check 15 and the group limit of the exclusion waterfall of §3.4 — is read from the employer that owns the named `election_window_id` and never from the employer that owns the elected category; the amendment path below and the media line create of §3.5 resolve their governing employer the same way.

Several gates protect a create beyond those already listed in §2.7. The window must be `OPEN` — a `SCHEDULED` or `CLOSED` window yields `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` — and the current logical clock instant must fall strictly before the window's `cutoff_at`, since the occupation interval is half-open and the cutoff instant itself is already past; at or after the cutoff the create is refused with `ERR_ORDER_CUTOFF_PASSED`. The elected category must be `ACTIVE`. An employee whose `substantiation_hold` is true — meaning an unresolved repayment receivable is outstanding — may neither file nor amend an election, and is refused with `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_HOLD_ACTIVE`. The allocation halves must be permitted by the category: a positive `pretax_amount_cents` requires the category's `pretax_allowed` to be true, and a positive `posttax_amount_cents` requires its `posttax_allowed` to be true, with `ERR_ALLOCATION_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED` otherwise. And when a `transit_media_id` is named, that product must still be `ACTIVE` in the catalogue (otherwise `ERR_TRANSIT_MEDIA_DISCONTINUED`) and must be regionally and categorically eligible: the product's `eligible_category_type` must equal the elected category's `category_type` and the product's `region_code` must equal the electing employee's `work_region_code`, with `ERR_MEDIA_REGION_INELIGIBLE` otherwise.

A successful create runs the pre-tax exclusion split of §3.4 under its elected basis, writing `total_amount_cents`, `excluded_amount_cents`, `posttax_spill_cents` and `posttax_effective_cents` and setting `exclusion_basis` to `ELECTED_BASIS`. The new row opens with `funding_status` `PENDING` and `change_count` at zero. Its `fulfillment_status` is derived immediately: an election created with `media_quantity` of zero, or with the field omitted altogether, opens in `NOT_REQUIRED`, because no media will ever be needed for it; an election with a positive `media_quantity` opens in `AWAITING_ORDER`. The create also advances the resolved benefit account's `version` by 1 and recomputes both the window's `election_count` and the category's `active_election_count`.

Filing a second election for the same employee, window and category triple does not stack — it **supersedes**. When a prior election exists for that exact triple whose `funding_status` is `PENDING` or `HELD`, the create sets the new row's `superseded_election_id` to the prior election's identifier and drives the prior election down: the prior `funding_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, its `excluded_amount_cents`, `posttax_spill_cents`, `posttax_effective_cents` and `total_amount_cents` are all zeroed, its `fulfillment_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, every media order of the prior election whose `status` is `PENDING` or `SUBMITTED` is moved to `CANCELLED`, and the prior `Election.version` advances by 1. This is precisely the write the stale-account-version guard protects: a writer holding an out-of-date account `version` has, by definition, not seen the election it is about to displace.

Amending an election is the mid-month change path, available to the electing `Employee` and to a `ProgramAdmin`. The amendment payload accepts any subset of `pretax_amount_cents`, `posttax_amount_cents` and `media_quantity`. Amendment is permitted while the window's `status` is `OPEN` or `LOCKED`. Against an `OPEN` window it is unrestricted. Against a `LOCKED` window it is gated twice, and in this order: first the window's `mid_month_change_allowed` flag must be true, otherwise `ERR_MID_MONTH_CHANGE_NOT_ALLOWED`; and only if that flag gate passes is the count checked — the election's `change_count` must be strictly below the single-change allowance of one, so an election that has already absorbed one mid-month change is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Amendments against an `OPEN` window are exempt from both gates. The election's `funding_status` must be `PENDING`, `HELD` or `FUNDED`; a `REVERSED`, `SETTLED` or `CANCELLED` election is closed to amendment and returns `ERR_ELECTION_ALREADY_FUNDED`.

Every successful amendment re-runs the exclusion split under the elected basis, increments `change_count` by 1, re-derives `fulfillment_status` from the election's media orders, and advances `Election.version` by 1. When the amended election is already `FUNDED`, the money has to move too, and it moves as a delta rather than a restatement: the service computes the new `total_amount_cents` minus the old `total_amount_cents` and, unless that delta is exactly zero, appends one ledger entry with `entry_type` `CONTRIBUTION_ADJUSTMENT` and `amount_cents` equal to that signed delta, with `source_entity` `Election` and `source_id` the election's identifier. The same signed delta is added to the account's `contributed_cents`, `available_balance_cents` is recomputed against the conservation identities of §2.9, `ledger_entry_count` is incremented and `BenefitAccount.version` advances by 1. A funded amendment also recomputes the employer's `total_pretax_committed_cents` and advances `Employer.version` by 1 — unconditionally, so an amendment that moves only the post-tax half, and therefore recomputes the very same committed total, still advances that counter. When the delta is zero no ledger entry is written and the account is left untouched, while the employer recomputation and its version bump run regardless.

Cancellation is available to the electing `Employee` and to a `ProgramAdmin` while the election's `funding_status` is `PENDING` or `HELD` and the window's `status` is `OPEN` or `LOCKED`. Both source postures produce the identical effect set: `funding_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, `excluded_amount_cents`, `posttax_spill_cents`, `posttax_effective_cents` and `total_amount_cents` are zeroed, every media order of the election whose `status` is `PENDING` or `SUBMITTED` is moved to `CANCELLED`, `fulfillment_status` is set to `CANCELLED`, the window's `election_count` and the category's `active_election_count` are recomputed, and `Election.version` advances by 1 alongside the `version` of every media order the cascade cancelled. `PENDING` and `HELD` are symmetric here — neither posture gets a shortcut and neither gets extra work.

Reversing funding is the exact compensating action for having funded an election, and only a `ProgramAdmin` may perform it. It requires the election's `funding_status` to be `FUNDED` and the owning window's `status` to be `LOCKED`, meaning the month has not yet settled the funding; it carries a free-text `reason`, which is required but carries no length bound of its own, so an empty string is accepted here. The election moves `FUNDED → REVERSED`, and one ledger entry with `entry_type` `FUNDING_REVERSAL` is appended to the election's benefit account carrying a negative `amount_cents` equal to the election's `total_amount_cents`, with `source_entity` `Election` and `source_id` the election's identifier. The account's `reversed_cents` increases by that same `total_amount_cents`, `available_balance_cents` is recomputed, `ledger_entry_count` is incremented, and `BenefitAccount.version` advances by 1. The employer's `total_pretax_committed_cents` is then recomputed and `Employer.version` advances by 1 — unconditionally, even when the recomputed total comes back unchanged — and `Election.version` advances by 1. The reversed election keeps its computed allocation figures exactly as they stand: `total_amount_cents`, `excluded_amount_cents`, `posttax_spill_cents` and `posttax_effective_cents` are *not* zeroed the way a cancellation zeroes them, and neither the election's media orders nor its `fulfillment_status` are touched. Because reversal is an exact compensation, funding an election and then reversing it must leave the account's `available_balance_cents` back at precisely the value it held before the funding, with the conservation identities of §2.9 holding at every step.

Reading an election returns both axes together, and the election collection can be filtered on `employee_id`, `election_window_id`, `benefit_category_id`, `benefit_account_id`, `transit_media_id`, `superseded_election_id`, `funding_status` and `fulfillment_status` — the last of which makes every reference into an election reverse-reachable, including the self-referential supersede chain.

### 3.4. Pre-Tax Exclusion Headroom, Spill, and the Funding Cascade

The arithmetic that decides how much of an employee's requested pre-tax deduction actually qualifies for exclusion is a waterfall of four independent ceilings, and its evaluation order is fixed.

The first ceiling is the **group headroom**. The elected category's `limit_group` — `TRANSIT` for a `TRANSIT_PASS` or `VANPOOL` category, `PARKING` for a `PARKING` category — selects the employer's group limit: `transit_group_limit_cents` for the transit group, `parking_group_limit_cents` for the parking group. Against that limit the service charges what the same employee has already consumed of the same group *in the same window through other categories*. Precisely, it sums `excluded_amount_cents` over every other election belonging to the same `employee_id` and the same `election_window_id` whose `benefit_category_id` differs from this election's and whose category sits in the same `limit_group`, restricted to the funding postures listed in the variant rule below. Sibling elections in the *same* category are deliberately excluded from that sum, because a second election for the same employee, window and category supersedes the first rather than stacking on it. The group headroom is then the group limit minus that prior consumption, floored at zero — it never goes negative even if a limit was lowered after elections were already funded. Elections whose `funding_status` is `CANCELLED` or `REVERSED` are never counted in the prior-consumption sum under either variant. The aggregation is a plain sum of signed integers and is therefore order-independent; no tie-break is needed.

The second ceiling is the **category cap**: the elected category's own `monthly_exclusion_limit_cents`. The third is the **ratio cap**, which is derived from the requested figures and only from the requested figures. The election's `total_amount_cents` is the requested `pretax_amount_cents` plus the requested `posttax_amount_cents`, computed before any spill has been decided; the ratio cap is that requested total multiplied by the employer's `pretax_ratio_cap_pct` and divided by one hundred, truncated toward zero to a whole number of cents. It is computed once, from the requested total, and is never re-derived from the post-spill split. The fourth ceiling is simply the requested `pretax_amount_cents` itself — the employee never gets more exclusion than they asked for.

`excluded_amount_cents` is then the smallest of those four values: the requested `pretax_amount_cents`, the group headroom, the category cap, and the ratio cap. Whatever pre-tax the employee asked for beyond that becomes the overage: `posttax_spill_cents` is the requested `pretax_amount_cents` minus `excluded_amount_cents`. Here the employer's conditional `spill_policy` flag decides the outcome. Under `SPILL_TO_POSTTAX` the overage is simply reclassified, and `posttax_effective_cents` becomes the requested `posttax_amount_cents` plus `posttax_spill_cents`, so the employee's total deduction is unchanged and only its tax treatment shifts. Under `REJECT_OVERAGE`, an overage is not tolerated at all: whenever the spill would be greater than zero, the election create or amendment is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), and the employee must lower their pre-tax request until the spill would be exactly zero. All of this arithmetic is exact whole-cent integer arithmetic; the ratio cap's truncation is the only place a fraction is discarded, and no other rounding occurs anywhere in the split.

The same waterfall runs in two **variants**, differing only in which sibling elections consume group headroom, and the difference is observable. Under the **elected basis** — which runs when an election is created and again on every amendment, and which sets `exclusion_basis` to `ELECTED_BASIS` — the prior-consumption sum includes sibling elections whose `funding_status` is `PENDING`, `HELD` or `FUNDED`. Every live sibling consumes headroom the moment it is elected, whether or not any money has moved, so a commuter who elects transit and vanpool in the same month sees the second election immediately constrained by the first. Under the **funded basis** — which runs at lock time and sets `exclusion_basis` to `FUNDED_BASIS` — the prior-consumption sum includes only siblings whose `funding_status` is exactly `FUNDED`. Headroom is measured against money that has actually been committed. The consequence is deliberate and worth stating plainly: a sibling election that was `PENDING` when this election was created but was cancelled or superseded before the lock releases its headroom, and this election's `excluded_amount_cents` therefore *increases* at lock time from exactly the same stored inputs, with no client action at all.

Locking a window is where the funded basis fires, and it fires as a four-level transitive chain in which each level is caused by the level above it rather than by the lock request. **The first level** changes the window itself: `status` becomes `LOCKED`, `locked_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, and `ElectionWindow.version` advances by 1. **The second level is caused by that window change**: every election in the window whose `funding_status` is `PENDING` is re-priced under the funded-basis variant, rewriting `excluded_amount_cents`, `posttax_spill_cents` and `posttax_effective_cents` and setting `exclusion_basis` to `FUNDED_BASIS`. Because a re-priced election consumes headroom that the next one then sees, the processing order is pinned: elections are processed in ascending `created_at`, then — for ties — in ascending `category_type` taken alphabetically, then in descending `pretax_amount_cents`. The re-pricing at this level never rejects: the employer's `spill_policy` is not consulted here, so even under `REJECT_OVERAGE` a spill that appears at lock time is simply reclassified into `posttax_effective_cents` and `ERR_EXCLUSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` is raised only by an election create or amendment. Each re-priced election then lands in one of two postures: `PENDING → HELD` when the electing employee's `substantiation_hold` is true, and `PENDING → FUNDED` otherwise. A move to `FUNDED` additionally requires the owning employer's `program_status` to be `ACTIVE`; an election that clears the hold test but finds the program no longer `ACTIVE` — which can only happen when an earlier election in this same lock already drove the fourth level's auto-suspension — keeps `funding_status` `PENDING` and gets no account credit, while still carrying its re-priced amounts and its `FUNDED_BASIS`. Either way `Election.version` advances by 1, including for an election that stayed `PENDING`.

**The third level is caused by each second-level move into `FUNDED`**, and it is the only level that touches money. One ledger entry is appended to that election's benefit account with `entry_type` `CONTRIBUTION`, a positive `amount_cents` equal to the election's `total_amount_cents`, `source_entity` `Election` and `source_id` the election's identifier; the account's `contributed_cents` increases by that same `total_amount_cents`, `available_balance_cents` is recomputed against the conservation identities, `ledger_entry_count` is incremented, and `BenefitAccount.version` advances by 1. An election that landed in `HELD` instead gets none of this: no ledger entry is appended and its benefit account is not touched at all.

**The fourth level is caused by each third-level account credit.** The employer's `total_pretax_committed_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `excluded_amount_cents` over every election whose window belongs to this employer and whose `funding_status` is exactly `FUNDED`. Elections that are `PENDING`, `HELD`, `SETTLED`, `CANCELLED` or `REVERSED` are all excluded from that sum, which is why a month close — moving funded elections to `SETTLED` — releases the commitment. If the recomputed total is **strictly greater** than the employer's `program_cap_cents`, the employer is driven `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and `Employer.version` advances by 1. A committed total exactly equal to `program_cap_cents` does **not** suspend the program; only a breach does. The recomputation runs once per third-level credit and its effect on the employer row is visible to the elections processed after it, but the employer row's `version` advances by at most 1 for the whole lock request no matter how many elections were funded: a lock that funds five elections and moves the committed total leaves the employer at exactly one version higher, and a lock whose recomputed total is unchanged and whose program is not suspended leaves the employer untouched. Auto-suspension is never requested directly and is never triggered by the lock request itself — it happens only because an account credit pushed the committed total past the cap, and `SUSPENDED` is terminal. The same commitment recomputation runs after an amendment to an already-`FUNDED` election and after a funding reversal.

### 3.5. Transit Media Catalogue and Procurement Batches

The `OrderDesk` maintains a catalogue of media products shared across employers. A catalogue entry carries a vendor `sku` of 1 to 40 characters, unique across the catalogue — a collision with any existing product, of any employer and any region, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a `media_type` of `PAPER_PASS`, `SMART_CARD_LOAD` or `DEBIT_CARD`; the `region_code` the product is valid in, drawn from the same five-region set as everything else; the single `eligible_category_type` the product may be ordered against, one of `TRANSIT_PASS`, `VANPOOL` or `PARKING`; and a `unit_price_cents` catalogue price of zero or greater. Three operational settings complete it: `lead_days`, the vendor's production lead time in whole days, defaulting to 3; `replacement_fee_cents`, the base fee charged to an employee's benefit account when lost or undelivered media is reissued, defaulting to 500 cents — five dollars; and `requires_physical_fulfillment`, a boolean defaulting to true that marks products which must actually be shipped, as paper passes and smart card loads are, rather than provisioned digitally. A new product opens in `status` `ACTIVE`. Discontinuing one moves it `ACTIVE → DISCONTINUED`, which is terminal and blocks new elections and new media lines against it, but leaves every existing media order alone — those rows keep the price they snapshotted and a sealed invoice never moves because of a later catalogue edit. The catalogue can be listed by `region_code`, `media_type`, `eligible_category_type` and `status`.

Procurement happens in batches, one per region per window. Opening a batch names the `election_window_id` whose cutoff calendar governs it and the `region_code` of fulfillment, which is what selects the regional handling surcharge at pricing time; the owning employer's `program_status` must be `ACTIVE`. A new batch opens in `status` `OPEN` with `line_count`, `subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents` and `total_cents` all zero and both `sealed_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` null, as well as `sealed_at`. Procurement batches are listable with filters on `election_window_id`, `status` and `region_code`.

A media line is added to an open batch by the `OrderDesk`. The payload names the `election_id` being fulfilled, the `transit_media_id` being ordered, the `order_batch_id` receiving the line, a `quantity` of at least 1, the inclusive `coverage_start_at` and `coverage_end_at` bounds of the interval the media covers on the employee's card or pass — where the end must be strictly after the start — and `expected_election_version`, the concurrency token of §2.11. The batch must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_BATCH_NOT_OPEN`; the current logical clock instant must fall strictly before the governing window's `cutoff_at`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_CUTOFF_PASSED`; the owning employer's `program_status` must be `ACTIVE`; the named product must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSIT_MEDIA_DISCONTINUED`; and the product must be eligible, with its `eligible_category_type` matching the election's category `category_type` and its `region_code` matching the electing employee's `work_region_code`, otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_REGION_INELIGIBLE`. Three further coherence checks apply, all reported as `ERR_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` and evaluated in this order: a benefit account must still resolve for the election's `employee_id` and `benefit_category_id` pair, the supplied `transit_media_id` must equal the election's own `transit_media_id`, and the batch's `election_window_id` must equal the election's `election_window_id` — a line cannot be smuggled into a batch built for a different month.

Coverage intervals are treated as **closed** on both ends, and unlike the election calendar, adjacency is a conflict here rather than a courtesy. A new media line is refused if its interval from `coverage_start_at` through `coverage_end_at` inclusive intersects the closed coverage interval of any existing media order for the same combination of electing employee and `transit_media_id` whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`. The one row excluded from the comparison is the pending order for the same election and product pair that this create is about to supersede. Concretely, the two intervals conflict when the new line's `coverage_start_at` falls at or before the existing line's `coverage_end_at` **and** the new line's `coverage_end_at` falls at or after the existing line's `coverage_start_at`, so two intervals whose endpoints merely touch do collide. The rejection is `ERR_MEDIA_COVERAGE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

A successful line create opens the row in `status` `PENDING` with `unit_price_cents_snapshot` copied from the catalogue product's current `unit_price_cents`, `tracking_ref` and `undelivered_reason` null, `replacement_count` and `replacement_fee_charged_cents` at zero, and `line_gross_cents`, `line_surcharge_cents` and `line_total_cents` all zero until the batch is priced. Like elections, media lines supersede rather than stack: when a `PENDING` line already exists for the same `election_id` and `transit_media_id` pair, that older line is moved to `CANCELLED`, the new row's `superseded_media_order_id` is set to the older line's identifier, and `MediaOrder.version` advances by 1 on the cancelled row. The create then re-derives the parent election's `fulfillment_status` and advances `Election.version` by 1 — which is exactly why the create carries `expected_election_version`. Media lines are listable by `election_id`, `transit_media_id`, `order_batch_id`, `superseded_media_order_id` and `status`.

Sealing the batch is where the invoice is priced, and the rounding order is pinned because a different order produces a different number of cents. Sealing requires the batch to be `OPEN`, the current logical clock instant to fall strictly before the governing window's `cutoff_at`, and at least one media line in the batch to be `PENDING` — an empty batch cannot be sealed and is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_ORDER_BATCH`. Exactly those `PENDING` lines are priced; lines already cancelled by a supersede create are excluded, and no line can be added after the seal because the batch has left `OPEN`. For each priced line, `line_gross_cents` is `unit_price_cents_snapshot` multiplied by `quantity`; `line_surcharge_cents` is that gross amount multiplied by the batch region's handling surcharge percentage and divided by one hundred, **rounded half up to whole cents for that line alone**; and `line_total_cents` is the gross plus the rounded surcharge. The regional surcharge percentages are 7.5 percent for `US-CA-SFO`, 6.25 percent for `US-NY-NYC`, 5.0 percent for `US-IL-CHI`, 4.0 percent for `US-WA-SEA` and 2.5 percent for `US-TX-AUS`. Summing the gross amounts first and rounding one aggregate surcharge is forbidden and will produce the wrong invoice.

The batch's `line_count` is then the number of lines priced, and `subtotal_cents` is the plain integer sum of their `line_total_cents` values — an order-free sum of amounts that have already been rounded. A bulk discount follows, keyed on the line count: batches of 4 lines or fewer earn no discount at all, batches of 5 through 9 lines earn 1.5 percent, and batches of 10 or more lines earn 3.0 percent. `discount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by that percentage and divided by one hundred, rounded half up exactly once against the already-summed subtotal, and `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents`. The batch then moves `OPEN → SEALED`, `sealed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, `sealed_at` records the current logical clock instant, and `OrderBatch.version` advances by 1 alongside the `version` of every priced media line.

Release hands the sealed batch to the vendor, and it is the segregation-of-duties boundary of the procurement side. It requires the batch to be `SEALED`, the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the recorded `sealed_by_user_id`, and the governing window's `status` to be `OPEN` or `LOCKED`. On success the batch moves `SEALED → RELEASED` with `released_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`, every media line in the batch whose `status` is `PENDING` moves `PENDING → SUBMITTED`, the `fulfillment_status` of every election referenced by those lines is re-derived, and `OrderBatch.version` advances by 1 alongside the `version` of every released line and every affected election. The batch's own final posture, `RECONCILED`, is a bubble-up rather than a request: whenever a media line in a `RELEASED` batch changes status, the batch is re-evaluated, and once every media order in the batch sits in `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` — with `line_count` at least 1 — the batch moves `RELEASED → RECONCILED` and its `version` advances by 1. `RECONCILED` is terminal.

### 3.6. Fulfillment, Undelivered Media and Reissue

The election's `fulfillment_status` is never set directly by any caller. It is re-derived from the election's media orders every time one of them changes status, every time the election is created, and every time it is cancelled, and the derivation walks a strict top-down precedence in which the first matching clause wins. The candidate set is the election's media orders excluding those whose `status` is `CANCELLED`. If the election's `funding_status` is `CANCELLED`, the result is `CANCELLED`. Otherwise, if the election's `entitled_media_units` is zero, the result is `NOT_REQUIRED`. Otherwise, if the candidate set is empty — which happens exactly when `entitled_media_units` is greater than zero and either no order has been created yet or every order created has been cancelled — the result is `AWAITING_ORDER`. Otherwise, if any candidate order has `status` `UNDELIVERED`, the result is `REPLACEMENT_PENDING`. Otherwise, if every candidate order has `status` `DELIVERED` **and** the sum of their `quantity` values is at least the election's `entitled_media_units`, the result is `DELIVERED`. Otherwise, if any candidate order has `status` `SHIPPED`, the result is `IN_TRANSIT`. In every remaining case the result is `ORDERED`. This precedence is what makes the outcome deterministic; a partially-delivered election whose remaining lines are still `SUBMITTED` sits in `ORDERED`, not in `DELIVERED`.

Creating a media line for an election that was waiting therefore advances the election `AWAITING_ORDER → ORDERED`, provided `entitled_media_units` is greater than zero and at least one of the election's media orders is not `CANCELLED`. Each such re-derivation advances `Election.version` by 1, and it does so unconditionally: the re-derivation is a write rather than a comparison, so every operation that performs it — a media line create, a batch release, a shipment, a delivery confirmation, an undelivered report and a reissue — advances the parent election's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at` even when the value re-derived is identical to the one already stored. This is the one deliberate exception to §2.12's rule that a row a cascade evaluates but does not change is left alone: reissuing one of two `UNDELIVERED` lines, shipping a second line for an election already `IN_TRANSIT`, or adding a line to an election whose `entitled_media_units` is zero and which therefore stays `NOT_REQUIRED`, all still bump the election.

Shipment is recorded by the `OrderDesk`. It requires the media line's `status` to be `SUBMITTED`, the catalogue product's `requires_physical_fulfillment` to be true — a digitally provisioned product is never shipped — and the owning batch's `status` to be `RELEASED` or `RECONCILED`. The payload carries `tracking_ref`, the carrier tracking reference, which is required, must be at least one character and — by the blank rule of §2.7 check 4 — must not be all whitespace, and which is stored on the row exactly as supplied. The line moves `SUBMITTED → SHIPPED`, the parent election's `fulfillment_status` is re-derived, and the `version` of both the line and its election advances by 1. At the election level this is the `ORDERED → IN_TRANSIT` step, which holds when no media order of the election is `UNDELIVERED` and at least one is `SHIPPED`.

Delivery confirmation may be recorded either by the `OrderDesk` or by the receiving `Employee`, and requires the line's `status` to be `SHIPPED`. The line moves `SHIPPED → DELIVERED`, the parent election's `fulfillment_status` is re-derived, the owning batch's `RELEASED → RECONCILED` bubble-up is re-evaluated, and the `version` of the line, its election and — where the bubble-up fires — its batch advances by 1. At the election level this is the `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED` step, which holds only when every non-cancelled media order of the election is `DELIVERED` and the sum of their `quantity` values covers `entitled_media_units`.

Reporting media undelivered is available to both the `Employee` and the `OrderDesk`, and it is deliberately reachable from two source postures, because commuter media gets lost after it arrives as often as it fails to arrive at all. A line whose `status` is `SHIPPED` moves `SHIPPED → UNDELIVERED`, and a line whose `status` is `DELIVERED` moves `DELIVERED → UNDELIVERED`; both paths carry the identical payload and produce the identical effects. The payload supplies `reason`, one of `NEVER_ARRIVED`, `LOST`, `STOLEN` or `DAMAGED`, which is stored on the row as `undelivered_reason`. In both cases the parent election's `fulfillment_status` is re-derived to `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` — reached as `IN_TRANSIT → REPLACEMENT_PENDING` from the shipped path and as `DELIVERED → REPLACEMENT_PENDING` from the delivered path — and the `version` of both the line and its election advances by 1. `DELIVERED` is intentionally not a terminal state for a media order.

Reissue closes the loop, and only the `OrderDesk` may perform it. It requires the line's `status` to be `UNDELIVERED` and its `replacement_count` to be strictly below the reissue allowance of 2, so a line that has already been reissued twice is refused with `ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The payload accepts an optional `waive_replacement_fee` flag; when it is true, the line's `undelivered_reason` must be `NEVER_ARRIVED`, the only waivable reason, and any other reason with the flag set is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_FEE_NOT_WAIVABLE`. Media that was lost, stolen or damaged in the employee's own custody is always chargeable.

The fee itself follows a three-branch rule. When `waive_replacement_fee` is true — which, by the gate above, means the reason was `NEVER_ARRIVED` — the fee is zero. Otherwise, when the line's `replacement_count` is currently zero, the fee is the catalogue product's `replacement_fee_cents` exactly. Otherwise — meaning this is the second reissue of the same row — the fee is that `replacement_fee_cents` multiplied by two, an escalation intended to make repeated loss visible. The arithmetic is whole-cent integer multiplication with no rounding. When the resulting fee is non-zero, one ledger entry is appended to the electing employee's benefit account with `entry_type` `REPLACEMENT_FEE` and a negative `amount_cents` equal to the fee, with `source_entity` `MediaOrder` and `source_id` the line's identifier; the account's `fee_charged_cents` increases by the fee, `available_balance_cents` is recomputed, `ledger_entry_count` is incremented and `BenefitAccount.version` advances by 1. When the fee is zero, no ledger entry is written and the account is untouched.

Alongside the fee, the reissue increments the line's `replacement_count` by 1 and adds the fee to its `replacement_fee_charged_cents`, moves the line `UNDELIVERED → SUBMITTED` on the **same row with the same `quantity`**, and clears `tracking_ref` back to null so the next shipment can record a fresh one. The parent election's `fulfillment_status` is re-derived back to `ORDERED` — the `REPLACEMENT_PENDING → ORDERED` step, which holds when no media order of the election is `UNDELIVERED` after the reissue and none is yet `SHIPPED` — and the `version` of the line, its election and, where a fee was charged, its benefit account advances by 1. Reusing the same row with the same `quantity` is what preserves the media unit conservation of §2.10: a full cycle of report-undelivered followed by reissue mints no additional media units.

### 3.7. Card Spending, Substantiation and the Receivable Cascade

A `ProgramAdmin` ingests settled commuter debit card activity. The payload names the `election_id` whose benefit dollars funded the swipe, the settled `amount_cents` of at least 1, the four-digit `merchant_category_code` reported by the card network as an integer from 1000 through 9999, the network's `merchant_name` descriptor of 1 to 120 characters, and the ISO 8601 UTC settlement instant `transaction_at`. The service copies `benefit_account_id` onto the transaction from the named election's own `benefit_account_id` at ingestion, so the two can never disagree and no `ERR_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` is raised anywhere on this path; if that copied identifier does not resolve to a benefit account at all, the ingestion is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) under the body-reference rule of §2.6.

Substantiation posture is decided at ingestion by a two-stage test. First the merchant category is checked against the set permitted for the election's category `limit_group`. A `TRANSIT` limit group permits merchant category codes 4111, 4112, 4131 and 4789 — local and commuter passenger transport, and bridge and toll charges. A `PARKING` limit group permits merchant category code 7523 and nothing else. A swipe whose `merchant_category_code` falls outside the permitted set for its group is created directly in `substantiation_status` `INELIGIBLE` with `posted_spend_cents` at zero; no ledger entry is written, no spend is posted, and the benefit account is not touched at all. Second, for a swipe whose merchant category *is* permitted, the amount decides: a swipe of 2500 cents or less — twenty-five dollars, with the boundary inclusive, so exactly 2500 clears and 2501 does not — is created in `AUTO_SUBSTANTIATED`, and a larger swipe is created in `PENDING_DOCS` awaiting documentation. In both of those cases `posted_spend_cents` equals the full `amount_cents`.

Whenever `posted_spend_cents` is greater than zero the money moves at ingestion: one ledger entry is appended with `entry_type` `SPEND` and a negative `amount_cents` equal to the posted spend, with `source_entity` `CardTransaction` and `source_id` the transaction's identifier; the account's `spent_cents` increases by the posted amount and `available_balance_cents` is recomputed. Because benefit dollars cannot be overdrawn by a card swipe, a swipe whose merchant category is inside the permitted set is admitted only when its `amount_cents` is no greater than the benefit account's current `available_balance_cents`; a larger swipe is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BENEFIT_BALANCE` (HTTP 402). That balance gate is confined to this one ingestion path and no other movement in the service consults it: the media replacement fee of §3.6 and the spend that a reopen from `INELIGIBLE` re-posts are both debited with no balance test whatsoever, so either of them may drive an account's `available_balance_cents` below zero — the conservation identities of §2.9 simply continue to hold at that negative figure. A swipe that was rejected as ineligible on merchant grounds never reaches the balance test at all, because it posts nothing.

Documentation is submitted as a substantiation claim, by the `Employee` who owns the underlying swipe or by a `ProgramAdmin`. A claim names the `card_transaction_id` it supports, the `claimed_amount_cents` the document evidences (at least 1), and the `doc_type`, one of `RECEIPT`, `FARE_STATEMENT`, `PARKING_INVOICE` or `EMPLOYER_ATTESTATION`. It opens in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `reviewed_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` both null. Nothing about the underlying swipe gates the submission: a claim may be filed against a transaction in any `substantiation_status` — including one already `AUTO_SUBSTANTIATED`, `SUBSTANTIATED` or `INELIGIBLE` — a single transaction may carry any number of claims, and the submission neither touches the transaction row nor advances its `version`; the swipe's posture is tested only when a reviewer acts on the claim. Claims are listable by `card_transaction_id` and `status`.

A `ComplianceReviewer` approves a claim only when three things hold: the claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, the underlying transaction's `substantiation_status` is `PENDING_DOCS`, and the claim's `claimed_amount_cents` equals the transaction's `amount_cents` exactly. An amount disagreement must be rejected rather than approved, and an approval attempt against a mismatched amount returns `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. On approval the claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` with `reviewed_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`, and the underlying swipe moves `PENDING_DOCS → SUBSTANTIATED` with `approved_by_user_id` recording that same acting `X-User-ID`. Approval deliberately moves no money: `posted_spend_cents` and the benefit account are left exactly as they were, because the spend was already posted at ingestion. The `version` of both the claim and the transaction advances by 1. An approval and a rejection alike answer with the **claim** row in `data`, whereas `deny-substantiation` and `reopen` answer with the **card transaction** row.

Rejecting a claim is the mirror action and requires the claim to be `SUBMITTED` and the transaction to be `PENDING_DOCS`. The claim moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with the supplied `rejection_reason` — one of `AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `INELIGIBLE_EXPENSE`, `ILLEGIBLE_DOCUMENT` or `LATE_SUBMISSION` — and `reviewed_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`. Rejection then drives the underlying swipe `PENDING_DOCS → INELIGIBLE`, which is to say it fires the whole receivable cascade below. Both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal for a claim. A reviewer may also drive a swipe to `PENDING_DOCS → INELIGIBLE` directly, without a claim, by denying substantiation with a free-text `reason`; the effects are identical. That `reason` is required, must be at least one character and must not be blank, and it is not persisted anywhere — the only trace the denial leaves of its author is `denied_by_user_id`.

The receivable cascade runs four levels deep, and again each level is caused by the level above it. **The first level** sets the transaction's `substantiation_status` to `INELIGIBLE`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `denied_by_user_id`, zeroes `posted_spend_cents`, and advances `CardTransaction.version` by 1. **The second level is caused by that transaction change**: one ledger entry is appended to the transaction's benefit account with `entry_type` `SPEND_REVERSAL` and a **positive** `amount_cents` equal to the transaction's `amount_cents`, with `source_entity` `CardTransaction` and `source_id` the transaction's identifier; the account's `spent_cents` decreases by that amount, `available_balance_cents` is recomputed, `ledger_entry_count` is incremented and `BenefitAccount.version` advances by 1. The benefit dollars have been given back. **The third level is caused by that ledger credit**: because the employee spent money the program has now decided was not a commuter expense, the employee owes it back, so `repayment_due_cents` on the employee increases by the credited amount, `substantiation_hold` is set to true exactly when `repayment_due_cents` is greater than zero, and `Employee.version` advances by 1. **The fourth level is caused by the hold flag**: every election of that employee whose `funding_status` is `PENDING` is driven `PENDING → HELD`. A held election keeps its `excluded_amount_cents` and `posttax_effective_cents` exactly as they stand and its benefit account is not touched; only `funding_status` changes, and `Election.version` advances by 1. This is the serialization point between compliance review and funding: an employee with an open receivable is frozen out of new funding, and — by the gate in §2.7 — out of filing or amending elections at all.

Reopening a transaction for audit is the compensating direction and is available only to a `ComplianceReviewer`. It carries a free-text `reason` — required, at least one character, not blank, and likewise never persisted — and can be entered from any of the three settled postures — `AUTO_SUBSTANTIATED → PENDING_DOCS`, `SUBSTANTIATED → PENDING_DOCS` or `INELIGIBLE → PENDING_DOCS` — because a card transaction has no terminal state at all; audit can always come back. Every reopen requires `reopen_count` to be strictly below the reopen allowance of 2, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Reopening from `AUTO_SUBSTANTIATED` or from `SUBSTANTIATED` additionally requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the transaction's recorded `approved_by_user_id`: the reviewer who blessed a swipe may not be the reviewer who reopens it. Reopening from either of those two postures does nothing but set `substantiation_status` to `PENDING_DOCS`, increment `reopen_count` by 1 and advance `CardTransaction.version` by 1 — no money moves, because none had moved on approval.

Reopening from `INELIGIBLE` is different, and it is the exact compensation of the receivable cascade, hop for hop. Its first level sets `substantiation_status` to `PENDING_DOCS`, restores `posted_spend_cents` to the transaction's full `amount_cents`, increments `reopen_count` by 1, and advances `CardTransaction.version` by 1. Its second level, caused by that change, appends one ledger entry with `entry_type` `SPEND` and a negative `amount_cents` equal to the transaction's `amount_cents`, with `source_entity` `CardTransaction` and `source_id` the transaction's identifier; the account's `spent_cents` increases by that amount, `available_balance_cents` is recomputed, `ledger_entry_count` is incremented and `BenefitAccount.version` advances by 1. Its third level, caused by that ledger debit, decreases the employee's `repayment_due_cents` by the same amount and sets `substantiation_hold` to true exactly when `repayment_due_cents` remains greater than zero — so a single outstanding receivable cleared by a single reopen drops the flag to false — and advances `Employee.version` by 1. Its fourth level, caused by the flag clearing, drives every election of that employee whose `funding_status` is `HELD` back through `HELD → PENDING`, but only where the election's window `status` is `OPEN` or `LOCKED`; each such election advances its `version` by 1. That closes the funding cycle: a held election becomes fundable again without the employee having to re-elect anything. Taken together, a denial followed by a reopen must leave the benefit account's conservation identities holding and the employee's `repayment_due_cents` back at zero. Card transactions are listable by `election_id`, `benefit_account_id` and `substantiation_status`.

### 3.8. Closing a Benefit Month: Rollover, Forfeiture and Termination Refunds

Closing a benefit month is a `ProgramAdmin` operation that carries a single `benefit_month` value in the shape of a four-digit year, a hyphen and a two-digit month. The only posture it demands of the employer is that the program not be `SUSPENDED` — a `SUSPENDED` employer is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, while a `DRAFT` program is closed exactly as an `ACTIVE` one is, and a month with no matching `LOCKED` window is not an error but simply a close that settles nothing before it sweeps. The operation answers with the employer row. It does three things in sequence. Every election window of that employer whose `status` is `LOCKED` and whose `benefit_month` equals the supplied month moves `LOCKED → CLOSED`. Every election in those windows whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` moves `FUNDED → SETTLED`, which is terminal, and — because the employer's commitment total counts only elections that are exactly `FUNDED` — settlement is what releases that commitment. Then the close sweeps benefit accounts.

The sweep covers every benefit account whose `benefit_category_id` references a category belonging to the closed employer, regardless of whether that category is still `ACTIVE` or has been `RETIRED`. Accounts are processed in ascending `created_at`; no tie-break is needed, because each account's computation reads only its own fields and never another account's balances. For each account, the unused amount is exactly its `available_balance_cents` at the close instant — the value the ledger conservation identities pin it to. It is non-negative in the ordinary case, because a card swipe is balance-gated before it posts; the one way it can be negative is the unguarded replacement-fee debit or reopen re-post of §3.7, and an account sitting below zero at the close instant yields neither a forfeiture nor a refund delta, so the sweep appends no ledger entry to it and leaves that row — and its `version` — untouched.

Which branch runs is decided by the account holder's employment `status`, and the two branches send identical balances to completely different destinations. Under the **standard close**, which runs when the employee's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the carried-forward amount depends on the employer's `rollover_policy`: under `NO_ROLLOVER` nothing carries forward; under `FULL_ROLLOVER` the entire unused balance carries forward; and under `CAP_ROLLOVER` the carried-forward amount is the smaller of the unused balance and the employer's `rollover_cap_cents`. Whatever does not carry forward is forfeited — the forfeiture amount is the unused balance minus the carried-forward amount — and nothing at all is refunded to the employer.

Under the **termination close**, which runs when the employee's `status` is `TERMINATED`, the carried-forward amount is unconditionally zero no matter what `rollover_policy` says, because a departed employee has no next benefit month. The whole unused balance goes to exactly one of two destinations, decided by the grace period. The service computes the whole days elapsed since the employee's `termination_date` by taking the difference between the current logical clock instant and `termination_date`, dividing by the 86400 seconds in a day, and discarding any fraction. If that whole-day count is less than or equal to the employer's `forfeiture_grace_days`, the entire unused balance is refunded to the employer and nothing is forfeited. If it exceeds the grace period, the entire unused balance is forfeited and nothing is refunded.

The two resulting deltas are then applied in a fixed order, with one ledger entry appended per non-zero delta. If the forfeiture amount is greater than zero, one entry is appended first with `entry_type` `FORFEITURE` and a negative `amount_cents` equal to that amount. If the refund amount is greater than zero, one entry is appended next with `entry_type` `REFUND` and a negative `amount_cents` equal to that amount. Both entries carry `source_entity` `BenefitAccount` and `source_id` the account's own identifier. The account's `forfeited_cents` increases by the forfeiture amount and its `refunded_cents` by the refund amount, and `available_balance_cents` is recomputed against the conservation identities. Nothing else is reset: `rollover_in_cents`, `contributed_cents`, `spent_cents` and `fee_charged_cents` are lifetime-cumulative buckets and a close never zeroes them. What carries forward into the next benefit month is simply whatever survives the forfeiture or refund debit. Two invariants make this checkable, both stated for the ordinary non-negative balance: after a standard close the account's `available_balance_cents` equals exactly the carried-forward amount, and after a termination close it equals exactly zero — while an account already below zero is swept without any entry at all and keeps the negative figure it came in with. All of this is exact whole-cent integer arithmetic with no rounding step. The close advances the `version` of every employer, election window, election and benefit account row it actually mutated by 1, and `ledger_entry_count` on each account rises by the number of entries appended to it.

### 3.9. Ordinance Compliance and the Payroll Deduction File

Local commuter benefit ordinances are assessed one month at a time, and an assessment is an immutable audit record rather than a mutable resource. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `ComplianceReviewer` runs one by supplying an `assessment_month` in the four-digit-year, hyphen, two-digit-month shape, and an assessment may be run against an employer in any `program_status` — `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` alike, since the operation appends an audit record rather than transitioning the employer's own posture. The service first resolves the last instant of that month — for an `assessment_month` of `2026-09` the boundary is `2026-09-30T23:59:59.999Z` — and then counts the covered headcount: the employees of this employer whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `work_region_code` equals the employer's own `region_code`, and whose `hire_date` falls at or before that month-end boundary. Terminated employees never count, employees who report for work in a different region never count, and an employee hired after the assessed month ends never counts. The result is recorded on the assessment as `covered_employee_count`, and the employer's `transit_group_limit_cents` at assessment time is snapshotted alongside it as `assessed_transit_group_limit_cents`.

The verdict then follows a strict ladder. If the employer's `region_code` is not one of the three covered jurisdictions — `US-CA-SFO`, `US-NY-NYC` and `US-WA-SEA` — the assessment records `mandate_status` `EXEMPT` with `finding` `EXEMPT_REGION_NOT_COVERED`; employers whose primary region is `US-IL-CHI` or `US-TX-AUS` fall here. Otherwise, if the covered headcount is **strictly fewer than 20** employees, the assessment records `EXEMPT` with `finding` `EXEMPT_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; a headcount of exactly 20 is mandated, not exempt. Otherwise the employer is mandated and the quality of its offering is examined. The service counts the qualifying transit offerings — the employer's benefit categories whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `pretax_allowed` is true, and whose `category_type` is `TRANSIT_PASS` or `VANPOOL` — and records that count as `qualifying_transit_offering_count`. Retired categories and post-tax-only offerings do not satisfy the mandate. Both counts are in fact computed and recorded on every assessment regardless of which rung of the ladder settles the verdict, so an `EXEMPT` assessment still reports the employer's true `qualifying_transit_offering_count` rather than zero, exactly as it still snapshots `assessed_transit_group_limit_cents`. If the qualifying count is zero, the assessment records `mandate_status` `MANDATED_NONCOMPLIANT` with `finding` `DEFICIENT_NO_QUALIFYING_TRANSIT_OFFERING`. Otherwise, if the employer's `transit_group_limit_cents` is strictly below the ordinance minimum of 30000 cents — three hundred dollars a month — the assessment records `MANDATED_NONCOMPLIANT` with `finding` `DEFICIENT_LIMIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Otherwise it records `MANDATED_COMPLIANT` with `finding` `COMPLIANT`. Both count aggregations are plain counts and therefore order-independent.

Whatever verdict is reached is written back onto the employer as `ordinance_mandate_status`, advancing `Employer.version` by 1, and that write-back has teeth: an employer whose `ordinance_mandate_status` is `MANDATED_NONCOMPLIANT` may not take on new elections or amend existing ones until the deficiency is cured — by adding a qualifying pre-tax transit offering, or by raising `transit_group_limit_cents` to the ordinance minimum — and re-assessed. Attempts in the meantime are refused with `ERR_ORDINANCE_MANDATE_UNMET` (HTTP 422). An employer that has never been assessed sits in `UNASSESSED` and is not blocked. Assessments are readable individually, listed beneath their employer newest-first with an `assessment_month` filter, and listed across employers oldest-first with filters on `employer_id`, `assessment_month`, `mandate_status` and `finding`.

The payroll deduction file is the program's hand-off to payroll, and it is created empty. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `PayrollProcessor` opens one for an `employer_id` and a `period_month`, and it lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `participant_count`, `total_pretax_cents`, `total_posttax_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `pre_waiver_charge_cents`, `fee_waiver_cents` and `employer_charge_cents` all at zero and both `transmitted_at` and `acknowledgement_code` null. Creation is gated on nothing beyond the resolution of `employer_id` and the `YYYY-MM` shape of `period_month`: the employer and month pair need not be unique, so several files may coexist for the same employer and month and each is generated, transmitted and acknowledged independently, and the employer's `program_status` is consulted by no payroll-file operation at all — a `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED` employer's file is created and generated exactly as an `ACTIVE` one's is.

Generating the file moves it `DRAFT → GENERATED` and requires that at least one election window of the employer whose `benefit_month` equals the file's `period_month` be in `status` `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` — a month whose elections have not yet locked has nothing to deduct. Generation then runs a five-step rollup whose evaluation order is pinned, because one step looks circular and is not. **Step one** establishes scope and the raw sums. In scope are the elections whose window belongs to the file's employer, whose window `benefit_month` equals the file's `period_month`, and whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SETTLED`; elections that are `PENDING`, `HELD`, `CANCELLED` or `REVERSED` are excluded. `participant_count` is the number of distinct `employee_id` values across that scope, and each participating employee's `external_ref` is what the file echoes to payroll under that employee's `payroll_cycle` cadence. `total_pretax_cents` is the sum of `excluded_amount_cents` across the scope, and `total_posttax_cents` the sum of `posttax_effective_cents`. All three aggregates are order-free.

**Step two** derives the administration fee from headcount alone: `admin_fee_cents` is the larger of a 5000-cent monthly minimum — fifty dollars — and the participant count multiplied by 425 cents per participant. **Step three** computes `pre_waiver_charge_cents` as `total_pretax_cents` plus `total_posttax_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`. **Step four** selects the fee waiver tier, and it selects it *from `pre_waiver_charge_cents` and from nothing else*: a pre-waiver charge below 50000 cents earns no waiver, a pre-waiver charge from 50000 cents up to but not including 200000 cents earns a 25 percent waiver, and a pre-waiver charge of 200000 cents or more earns a 50 percent waiver. `fee_waiver_cents` is the `admin_fee_cents` multiplied by that waiver percentage and divided by one hundred, rounded half up exactly once. **Step five** sets `employer_charge_cents` to `pre_waiver_charge_cents` minus `fee_waiver_cents`, and the tier is **never** re-derived from that final figure — a charge that drops below a tier boundary because of its own waiver keeps the tier it was assigned. Generation advances `PayrollFile.version` by 1.

Only a `PayrollProcessor` transmits. Transmission requires the file's `status` to be `GENERATED` and its `participant_count` to be at least 1 — an empty file is never sent — and moves it `GENERATED → TRANSMITTED`, stamping `transmitted_at` with the current logical clock instant. The vendor's response is recorded through the acknowledgement operation, which carries a mandatory `outcome` of either `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` plus an optional `acknowledgement_code`. An `outcome` of `ACCEPTED` moves the file `TRANSMITTED → ACKNOWLEDGED`; an `outcome` of `REJECTED` moves it `TRANSMITTED → REJECTED`. Both are terminal, both store the supplied `acknowledgement_code` (leaving it null when the field is omitted), and both advance `PayrollFile.version` by 1. Payroll files are listable by `employer_id`, `status` and `period_month`.
