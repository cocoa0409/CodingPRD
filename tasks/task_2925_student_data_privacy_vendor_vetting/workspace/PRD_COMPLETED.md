# Student Data Privacy Vendor Vetting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every school district that buys educational technology inherits a legal duty it cannot delegate: the district, not the vendor, answers to families and to the state when student records leak. The district's educational-technology privacy office currently runs that duty on spreadsheets. A teacher emails a request for a new reading-practice tool, an analyst chases the vendor for a subprocessor list, someone remembers that the same vendor had a breach eighteen months ago, and three weeks later an approval is granted whose data-minimization reasoning exists only in an inbox. Meanwhile the district's network collectors keep reporting traffic to product domains nobody in the privacy office has ever heard of.

This document specifies the service that replaces those spreadsheets. It is a single system of record for vendor vetting: teachers file application requests with written instructional justification; analysts review the vendor's data practices against the federal and state student-privacy frameworks; the vendor's liaison commits, element by element, to a retention period and a disposal method; a lead analyst who did not author the review records the terminal decision; and only then may an approved product be published to the district catalog for a bounded authorization window that must be renewed. Around that spine the service runs three continuous obligations — unauthorized-application detection from network, browser and device-management telemetry; statutory breach-notification deadlines on vendor incidents; and an annual reauthorization sweep that retires stale authorizations before they silently expire.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the operator who owns the registry cannot author findings, the analyst who authored a review cannot decide it, and the vendor answers its own findings without the privacy office answering on its behalf. Second, **every derived compliance number is recomputed from stored rows rather than accumulated incrementally**, so that a rejected commitment restores exactly the exposure total that existed before it was accepted, and a reassigned review moves its workload without changing the district-wide total. Implementers should treat every score in this document as a pure function of the rows named in its input set and of the server's logical clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, empty, or contains only whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. If `X-User-Role` is present but is not one of the five role names declared below, or is a valid role that the target endpoint does not list among its authorized roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `Teacher`, `PrivacyAnalyst`, `VendorLiaison`, `PrivacyOfficeAdmin`, and `TelemetryAgent`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `Teacher` is a classroom teacher who requests instructional applications and reads the published district catalog.
- A `PrivacyAnalyst` is a district privacy office analyst who reviews vendor data practices, dispositions disclosed subprocessors and data-element commitments, and — at the `LEAD` credential only — records the final review decision.
- A `VendorLiaison` is the vendor's own representative, who discloses subprocessors, registers products, answers findings, proposes data-element commitments, and self-reports incidents.
- A `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` is the privacy office operator who owns the registry, the catalog, the nightly sweep, incident notification, and the logical clock. This role deliberately cannot author findings, decide reviews, disposition commitments, or remediate on a vendor's behalf.
- A `TelemetryAgent` is the district's network, browser-extension and device-management collector fleet: a machine identity whose only privilege is minting observations.

Three of the roles are entity-backed, which is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Teacher`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Teacher` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Teacher` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PrivacyAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PrivacyAnalyst` row; every credential and segregation-of-duties check reads that analyst's stored `credential_level` and compares `X-User-ID` to the analyst foreign keys recorded on the target row. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor` row — the liaison is not a separate person record, it *is* the vendor — so ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the vendor reached from the target resource.

The remaining two roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PrivacyOfficeAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as `triaged_by_user_id` on a `TelemetryDetection` the operator triages) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `TelemetryAgent`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque collector identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded where a transition names it and never compared to a stored `id`.

Two further rejections belong to the access-control family and are enforced after the role check. `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) is raised when an entity-backed caller reaches for a row that is not theirs — an acting `Teacher` whose `X-User-ID` is not the request's `requester_teacher_id`, or an acting `VendorLiaison` whose `X-User-ID` is not the vendor reached from the target row. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) is raised when an analyst attempts a terminal disposition they are not permitted to record, either because their `credential_level` is not `LEAD` where `LEAD` is required, or because they are the same analyst who authored the work being dispositioned. The precise scope of each of these two rules is given in §2.6 and again in the workflow narratives of §3.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the server's real wall-clock time and never from the logical clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. It is advisory only and no client behaviour depends on its exact wording: a rejection that carries no bespoke sentence simply repeats its own `error_id` in this field.

### 2.3. Pagination, Sorting and Collection Filters

Collection endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` accepts an integer from 1 through 100 and defaults to 10 when omitted; `offset` accepts any integer of at least 0 and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside that inclusive range, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something other than a base-ten integer, is a malformed request rather than an empty page; either parameter supplied as an empty string is read as though it had been omitted and takes its default. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and that ordering is stable across pages so that a client walking a collection by increasing `offset` sees every row exactly once. Because `created_at` is stamped from wall-clock time truncated to milliseconds, two rows can legitimately share one value; such ties are broken by the order in which the server created the rows, which is what makes the ordering total and therefore stable. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for the query, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as true whenever rows remain beyond the returned page.

Filters narrow a collection before pagination is applied, and combining several filters intersects them; a filter is matched by exact string equality against the stored value. A query parameter that is neither `limit`, `offset`, nor one of the filters the endpoint declares below is an unknown field and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a value that falls outside the declared enumeration of a filter whose underlying field is enumerated. The vendor registry may be filtered by registry `status` and by computed `privacy_posture`. Disclosed subprocessors may be filtered by `vendor_id` for one vendor's disclosure chain, by `processor_vendor_id` to walk the reverse edge into vendors that appear as somebody else's subprocessor, and by `status` or `country_code`. Applications may be filtered by `vendor_id`, `status`, `category` and `catalog_visibility` — the published district catalog is simply the application collection filtered to a `catalog_visibility` of `VISIBLE`. Teacher requests may be filtered by `application_id`, `requester_teacher_id`, `status` and `priority_band`. Reviews may be filtered by `source_request_id`, `application_id`, `vendor_id`, `assigned_analyst_id`, `decided_by_analyst_id` and `status`. Commitments may be filtered by `review_id`, by `superseded_by_commitment_id` to follow a supersede chain, by `decided_by_analyst_id`, by `status` and by `element_code`. Catalog entries may be filtered by `application_id`, `review_id`, `predecessor_entry_id` and `status`. Detections may be filtered by `application_id`, `matched_catalog_entry_id`, `linked_incident_id`, `classification`, `status` and `school_code`. Incidents may be filtered by `vendor_id`, by either status dimension — `response_status` or `notification_status` — and by computed `severity`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource in this service carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits, and they are the one pair of stored instants stamped from the server's real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock introduced below — driving the logical clock never changes what a row's `created_at` reads, which is what keeps the `created_at` ordering of §2.3 stable however the clock is moved. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Any operation that changes a row refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version`, and this applies to rows touched indirectly by a cascade just as much as to the row the caller named — a catalog entry placed on hold by a vendor suspension, or an application whose visibility flipped as a consequence, has its own `version` advanced.

Two `version` counters are load-bearing for concurrency and deserve to be called out. A review's `version` advances when a commitment is attached to it, when findings are issued, when a remediation is submitted, when the review is reassigned, and when it is decided. An application's `version` advances when the district records its integration attestation and when one of its catalog entries is reauthorized. §2.7 explains how clients are required to echo these two counters back.

All temporal behaviour in this service reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Deadlines, expiries, incident decay, authorization windows and sweep eligibility are all evaluated against that clock. Before it has ever been set, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes it back as the single member `current_utc` of the response's `data` object, rendered in the canonical `Z` form with three fractional digits whatever admissible form the caller wrote it in; setting the clock modifies no stored row and implies no sweep — the nightly compliance sweep must always be invoked explicitly.

### 2.5. Request Admission: Shape, Ranges and Reference Resolution

A request that survives authentication and role authorization is next checked for shape, and any shape failure is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Shape validation covers: every required input field present; no unknown fields; each field of its declared JSON type; every enumerated value drawn from its declared set; every timestamp a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant; and every integer inside its declared range. An explicitly supplied JSON `null` is never read as an omission: `null` in any declared field, optional ones included, is a shape failure. A boolean is not an acceptable integer. A request whose body is absent, empty or whitespace-only is read as the empty JSON object, so an endpoint with no required field may be called with no body at all, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is a shape failure. A timestamp is admitted with `T`, `t` or a single space between the date and the time, with anything from one to nine fractional digits or none at all, and with either a `Z`/`z` suffix or an explicit numeric `±HH:MM` offset — an offset form is accepted and normalised to UTC before any comparison, and every instant is stored and echoed back in the `Z` form with exactly three fractional digits. The declared ranges are: `breach_notification_hours` and `sub_breach_notification_hours` from 1 through 720; `data_deletion_days` from 1 through 3650; `retention_days` from 1 through 1825; `estimated_minutes` from 15 through 1200; `review_capacity_minutes` from 15 through 12000; `student_count_estimate` from 1 through 50000; `max_data_tier` from 1 through 4; `affected_student_count` from 0 through 500000; `distinct_user_count` from 0 through 100000; `session_count` from 0 through 1000000; and every grade-band field — `grade_band_low`, `grade_band_high`, `authorized_grade_band_low` and `authorized_grade_band_high` — from 0 through 12, where 0 denotes kindergarten, with the low bound never greater than the high bound of the same pair. Free-text lengths are bounded too: `legal_name` and `name` from 1 through 200 characters, `full_name` from 1 through 120, `employee_number` from 1 through 32, `school_code` from 1 through 16, `observed_domain` from 1 through 253, `purpose` and `justified_purpose` from 1 through 500, and `instructional_justification` from 1 through 2000. Of those, `legal_name`, `name`, `full_name`, `employee_number`, `school_code` and `observed_domain` must additionally be non-blank, so a whitespace-only value that sits inside the length bound is still a shape failure, while `purpose`, `justified_purpose` and `instructional_justification` are bounded in length only and admit a whitespace-only value. The free-text note and reason payload members this document introduces — a suspension `reason`, a `revoked_reason`, a `reviewed_note`, a `triage_note`, a `decision_note`, a `remediation_note` and a triage `note` — are plain strings with no declared length bound and no non-blank requirement at all. Both `hq_country_code` and `country_code` must be exactly two upper-case letters, following ISO 3166-1 alpha-2. The array fields must be non-empty where the domain requires it: `grade_bands_taught` is a non-empty array of integers from 0 through 12, `data_categories` is a non-empty array of student data-element codes, `requested_data_elements` is an array of the same codes, and `framework_set` is a non-empty array drawn from `FERPA`, `COPPA`, `PPRA` and `STATE_ADDENDUM` with no duplicates. Every array field is stored and echoed back in exactly the order the client supplied, and duplicate entries are preserved in each of them except `framework_set`, the only array field that forbids duplicates. On any catalog write, `expires_at` must be strictly after `effective_from`; equal or inverted instants are a shape failure. The clock-relative instant bounds that §3 states belong to this same family: an `observed_at`, `detected_at` or `contained_at` later than the current logical clock, and a `contained_at` earlier than its own incident's `detected_at`, are all reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than as a state or business failure.

Once the shape is sound, identifiers are resolved. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable segment determines the error even when a later segment is also unknown. The path variables in play are `vendorId`, `subprocessorId`, `applicationId`, `teacherId`, `analystId`, `requestId`, `reviewId`, `commitmentId`, `entryId`, `detectionId` and `incidentId`, and each resolves to its own 404: `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEACHER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ANALYST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATALOG_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETECTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after the path, in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields, and they draw from the same catalogue of 404 codes selected by the referenced entity. Drafting a teacher request therefore resolves `application_id` before `requester_teacher_id`, and opening a review resolves `source_request_id` before `assigned_analyst_id`. A body `vendor_id` or `processor_vendor_id` that names no stored vendor yields `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`; a body `review_id` that names no stored review yields `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`; a body `application_id` yields `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`; a body `requester_teacher_id` yields `ERR_TEACHER_NOT_FOUND`; a body `assigned_analyst_id` yields `ERR_ANALYST_NOT_FOUND`; and a body `source_request_id` yields `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. Order of Evaluation: the Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is never ambiguous: rules are evaluated as a strictly ordered ladder, and the **first** rule whose condition is violated determines the response. No later rung is evaluated once a rung has failed. The ladder is the same for every endpoint; rungs that do not apply to the endpoint in question are simply skipped. In ascending order of evaluation:

1. Both authentication headers present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The role is a declared role and is authorized for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Request shape, types, enumerations, ranges and timestamp formats as specified in §2.5 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. Every path identifier resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code.
5. Every body foreign key resolves, in declared field order — otherwise the matching not-found code.
6. Ownership for entity-backed callers — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This rung guards eight operations: drafting a teacher request, submitting or resubmitting a teacher request, reading a single teacher request, disclosing a subprocessor, issuing a round of findings, submitting a remediation, reporting a vendor incident, and containing a vendor incident.
7. Segregation of duties on terminal dispositions — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This rung guards exactly three operations: deciding a review, dispositioning a commitment, and publishing a catalog entry.
8. The teacher request is in a status that permits the attempted operation — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.
9. The vendor is in a registry status that permits suspension or reinstatement — otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_STATE_INVALID`.
10. The request named as a review's source has been triaged for review — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_TRIAGED`.
11. The review is in a status that permits the attempted operation — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`.
12. The review has not already reached a terminal decision — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED`.
13. The commitment is still dispositionable — otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED`.
14. The catalog entry is in a revocable status — otherwise `ERR_CATALOG_ENTRY_NOT_ACTIVE`.
15. The catalog entry is in a renewable status — otherwise `ERR_CATALOG_ENTRY_NOT_RENEWABLE`.
16. The detection has not been triaged yet — otherwise `ERR_DETECTION_ALREADY_TRIAGED`.
17. The incident's response dimension permits containment or resolution — otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`.
18. The incident's notification dimension is still open — otherwise `ERR_NOTIFICATION_ALREADY_SENT`.
19. The subprocessor has not been removed — otherwise `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_STATE_INVALID`.
20. The application is in a status that permits attestation — otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`.
21. The echoed review version matches the stored one — otherwise `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION`.
22. The echoed application version matches the stored one — otherwise `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION`.
23. The vendor reached from the target row is not frozen — otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_FROZEN`.
24. The vendor's posture permits publication — otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_PUBLICATION`.
25. The committed retention period respects the tier-conditional statutory ceiling — otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_EXCEEDS_STATUTORY_CAP`.
26. The committed element's sensitivity tier is permitted for the application — otherwise `ERR_ELEMENT_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED`.
27. The subprocessor's notification terms are at least as strict as the vendor's — otherwise `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_NOTIFICATION_TERMS_WEAKER`.
28. The vendor asserts a complete subprocessor disclosure — otherwise `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`. This rung guards publication and the fully-cleared review decision.
29. The review cited by a publication is decided favourably and belongs to the same application — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLEARED`.
30. A whitelist disposition has an active catalog entry behind it — otherwise `ERR_WHITELIST_REQUIRES_ACTIVE_ENTRY`.
31. The proposed authorization window does not overlap a sibling window — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
32. The proposed incident window does not collide with a buffered sibling window — otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_WINDOW_BUFFER_CONFLICT`.
33. The remediation window is still open — otherwise `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
34. The instructional justification is long enough to submit — otherwise `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_TOO_SHORT`.
35. At least one data element is requested — otherwise `ERR_NO_DATA_ELEMENTS`.
36. The revision allowance has not been exhausted — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
37. The findings-round allowance has not been exhausted — otherwise `ERR_REMEDIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`.
38. The application's accessibility conformance suffices for publication — otherwise `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_CONFORMANCE_INSUFFICIENT`.
39. The application's single sign-on integration is verified — otherwise `ERR_SSO_NOT_VERIFIED`.
40. The published grade band is contained in the application's own band — otherwise `ERR_GRADE_BAND_OUT_OF_SCOPE`.
41. The submitted disposition is applicable to the target row's current data — otherwise `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_APPLICABLE`.
42. Analyst capacity, checked last of all — otherwise `ERR_ANALYST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth internalising because they are easy to get backwards. First, the two optimistic-concurrency rungs sit *above* every business-safety rule, so a client holding a stale token is told its token is stale even when the operation would also have failed a posture or window check. Second, capacity is the very bottom rung by design: every state, ownership, safety, referential, temporal and constraint failure on the same request wins over a capacity failure, so `ERR_ANALYST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` is only ever seen by a request that is otherwise entirely valid.

One uniqueness rule sits outside the numbered ladder because it guards a single write: enrolling a teacher whose `employee_number` already exists on another teacher is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_NUMBER` (HTTP 409), checked after shape validation.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Reviews and Applications

Exactly two write operations require the client to prove that it is acting on a view of a parent row it has actually observed. Both use the parent's `version` counter as the token, and both reject a mismatch before any posture, window, tier or other constraint rule is consulted — but not before the target row's own state gate, because rungs 11 and 15 of §2.6 sit above the two concurrency rungs: a stale token presented against an already-decided review reports `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`, and one presented against a still-fresh `PUBLISHED` entry reports `ERR_CATALOG_ENTRY_NOT_RENEWABLE`, rather than the staleness.

A `POST /data-element-commitments` request must carry `expected_review_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `DataPracticeReview` named by `review_id`. If that value does not equal the review's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a commitment for the same `element_code` may already have been re-authored by another writer — which legitimately supersedes the earlier proposal — so a writer working from a stale view of the review, of its `round_number`, and of its commitment set could silently supersede a row it never saw.

A `POST /catalog-entries/{entryId}/reauthorize` request must carry `expected_application_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Application`. If that value does not equal the application's current `version`, the reauthorization is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Publication eligibility is read off the application row itself — `sso_verified`, `accessibility_conformance_level`, `vpat_on_file`, `status` and `exposed_sensitivity_points` — so a successor entry minted from a stale view of the application would carry an authorization the district never re-confirmed. Recording an integration attestation is the write most likely to have advanced that counter out from under a client.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. In particular, first publication, revocation, findings, remediation, dispositions, triage, containment and the sweep all take no concurrency token, and supplying one is an unknown field.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Score Ceilings

Every monetary-style and score-style number in this service is a decimal carried to **2 decimal places**, and every rounding step uses **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. This applies to `risk_score`, `reauthorization_risk_score`, `exposed_sensitivity_points`, `element_points`, `priority_score`, `provisional_compliance_score`, `final_compliance_score` and `exposure_score`. Counters and minute totals — `allocated_review_minutes`, `open_incident_count`, `flagged_subprocessor_count`, `open_request_count`, `revision_round`, `round_number`, `open_findings_count`, `unresolved_findings_count`, `notified_party_count` — are integers and are never rounded. Tier fields (`sensitivity_tier`, `max_permitted_tier`, `max_data_tier`) are integers from 1 through 4. Every decimal leaves the service as a JSON number rather than a string, serialised in its shortest round-tripping form, so a value stored as 63.80 appears in the payload as `63.8` and one stored as 0.00 appears as `0.0`.

Three rounding conventions are pinned and must not be varied. **Clamping happens before rounding**, never after: a compliance score is clamped into the closed interval from 0 through 100, and a risk score and an exposure score are clamped to a ceiling of 100.00, and only the clamped value is then rounded. No other decimal in the service is clamped at all: `priority_score`, `element_points` and `exposed_sensitivity_points` carry no ceiling and legitimately exceed 100.00. **Per-element rounding happens before summation** in the sensitivity-exposure calculation: each element's contribution is rounded to 2 decimals, the rounded contributions are added, and the total is rounded again. Summing raw products and rounding once at the end is the wrong order and produces a different total. Finally, **derived timestamps** — `notification_due_at`, `remediation_due_at`, `reauthorization_due_at` — are rendered as ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, computed by adding or subtracting an exact number of hours or days to a stored instant with no calendar-boundary rounding.

### 2.9. Privacy Posture: the District-Wide Gate

Each vendor carries a computed `privacy_posture`, one of `CLEAR`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is the district's single summary of whether that vendor may currently receive new work, and almost every gate in §3 consults it. Posture is derived, never written by a client, and it is evaluated as a **first-match-wins** ladder read strictly from top to bottom:

1. The posture is `FROZEN` if the vendor's registry `status` is `SUSPENDED`, or if any incident of that vendor has a `notification_status` of `OVERDUE`, or if any incident of that vendor has a `severity` of `CRITICAL` while its `response_status` is still `REPORTED`.
2. Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` if the vendor's `flagged_subprocessor_count` is greater than zero, or if any incident of that vendor has a `response_status` of `REPORTED`, `CONTAINED` or `ESCALATED`.
3. Otherwise the posture is `WATCH` if the vendor's `risk_score` is at least 45. The comparison is inclusive, so a `risk_score` of exactly 45.00 is `WATCH` while 44.99 falls through.
4. Otherwise the posture is `CLEAR`.

A frozen vendor is cut off from four operations, each rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_FROZEN`: submitting or resubmitting a teacher request for one of its applications, dispositioning one of its data-element commitments, publishing one of its applications to the catalog, and reauthorizing one of its catalog entries. Publication is gated more tightly still: it additionally requires the posture to be `CLEAR` or `WATCH`, so a `RESTRICTED` vendor is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_PUBLICATION` even though it is not frozen. Issuing a round of findings is likewise refused while the vendor is frozen.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Recomputation Order

Many operations in this service change more than one row. When they do, three rules hold without exception. First, **derived values are recomputed in dependency order**: a vendor's `risk_score` is always recomputed before its `privacy_posture` in any operation whose effects name both, so posture never reads a stale score. The full dependency chain runs from a commitment decision to the owning application's `exposed_sensitivity_points`, from there to the vendor's `risk_score`, and from there to the vendor's `privacy_posture` — one analyst decision therefore walks four rows.

Second, **aggregates are recomputed by full re-evaluation over their input set, never by incremental delta**. A teacher's `open_request_count` is recounted over that teacher's requests in `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `TRIAGED_FOR_REVIEW`; an analyst's `allocated_review_minutes` is re-summed over that analyst's reviews in `SCREENING`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`; a vendor's `open_incident_count` is recounted over that vendor's incidents in `REPORTED`, `CONTAINED` or `ESCALATED`; a vendor's `flagged_subprocessor_count` is recounted over that vendor's subprocessors in `FLAGGED`; and an application's `exposed_sensitivity_points` is re-summed from scratch. Full recomputation is what makes the compensating actions of §3 exact: rejecting a previously accepted commitment returns the application's exposure total to precisely the value it held before that commitment was accepted, with no drift.

Third, **cascades are transactional and visible**. Either every hop of a cascade is applied or none is; a request that fails a validation leaves no partial cascade behind. Every row a cascade actually changes has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, including rows the caller never named — but the stamp follows the data, so a cascade hop that re-evaluates an aggregate and arrives at the value already stored leaves that row completely alone, `updated_at` and `version` included. A teacher whose `open_request_count` is recounted to the same number by a `RETURN` triage or by a resubmission out of `RETURNED`, and an application whose `catalog_visibility` is recomputed to the same value by a revocation that leaves another visible entry standing, are therefore not re-stamped. Two hops stamp unconditionally and are the deliberate exceptions: a vendor whose `risk_score` and `privacy_posture` an operation recomputes always advances its own `version` even when both values come out identical, and the owning application always advances its `version` on a commitment disposition (§3.4) and on a reauthorization (§3.7) even when its recomputed `exposed_sensitivity_points` or `catalog_visibility` did not move.

### 2.11. Two Deliberately Different Interval Conventions

This service compares time intervals in two places, and the two conventions are **not** the same. Implementers who unify them will fail one of the two.

Catalog authorization windows are **half-open**: a window runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `expires_at` exclusive, and no buffer is applied to either end. Two windows of the same application conflict only if they genuinely overlap, so exact adjacency is legal — a new window whose `effective_from` equals an existing window's `expires_at` is accepted. Overlap is tested only against sibling entries of the same application in status `PUBLISHED`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `SUSPENDED_HOLD`, and a conflict is reported as `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The same half-open reading governs whether an observation falls inside an authorization window: an observation exactly at `effective_from` is inside, and one exactly at `expires_at` is outside.

Incident containment windows use the opposite convention: they are **closed** at both ends, and the existing row is expanded by a cooling buffer before the comparison. An incident's bare window starts at its `detected_at` and ends at its `contained_at`, or, when containment has not been recorded, 72 hours after `detected_at`. When a new incident is proposed for a vendor, each existing incident of that vendor in `REPORTED`, `CONTAINED`, `ESCALATED` or `REMEDIATED` has its bare window widened by 72 hours at the start and 72 hours at the end; the incoming window is **not** widened. The incoming window collides if it intersects any widened window as closed intervals — that is, if the incoming window's start is at or before the widened window's end **and** the incoming window's end is at or after the widened window's start. Because both intervals are closed, **touching endpoints collide**, and the collision is reported as `ERR_INCIDENT_WINDOW_BUFFER_CONFLICT`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The nine subsections below follow the district's own working order: first the parties and products are put on the register, then a teacher asks for something, then the privacy office reviews it, negotiates commitments, decides, publishes, watches the network, and finally responds when something goes wrong. Rules are stated where the business does them, which means a single endpoint's inputs are frequently introduced in more than one place — the core payload where the operation lives, the safety threshold where the compliance narrative explains it.

### 3.1. The Registry: Vendors, Their Products and Their Subprocessor Chain

Everything starts with a vendor on the register. A `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` registers an education-technology company by supplying its registered `legal_name`, its headquarters country as `hq_country_code`, and the two contractual terms the district negotiates hardest: `breach_notification_hours`, the maximum hours the vendor accepts between detecting a breach and notifying the district, and `data_deletion_days`, the maximum days it accepts for completing deletion after the contract ends. Two boolean assertions may accompany the registration and both default to false when omitted: `signed_state_addendum`, meaning the vendor has countersigned the state student-privacy addendum, and `subprocessor_disclosure_complete`, meaning the vendor asserts its disclosed subprocessor list is complete. A freshly registered vendor lands in registry status `REGISTERED` with `open_incident_count` and `flagged_subprocessor_count` both zero, `reauthorization_risk_score` at 0.00, `suspended_at` null, and its `risk_score` and `privacy_posture` computed immediately as part of creating the row, so that a freshly registered vendor still reads `version` 1 — which for a vendor with no children means the contractual-term penalty is the only non-zero contribution to its score.

That score deserves a careful reading, because four different narratives feed it. A vendor's **live risk score** is the sum of four terms, clamped to a ceiling of 100.00 and then rounded half up to two decimals.

- The **sensitivity load** takes every application of this vendor whose `status` is `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, adds up their `exposed_sensitivity_points`, multiplies that sum by one half, and rounds the result half up to two decimals before it enters the total. Applications still in `CANDIDATE`, and applications that ended in `BLOCKED`, contribute nothing.
- The **foreign-subprocessor penalty** adds 10 points for each subprocessor of this vendor whose `country_code` is anything other than `US`, counting only rows in `DISCLOSED`, `VERIFIED` or `FLAGGED`. Removed disclosures stop counting.
- The **contractual-term penalty** reads `breach_notification_hours` alone: it is 0 when the vendor has accepted three days or less (72 hours or fewer), 10 when it has accepted more than that but no more than one week (168 hours), and 25 for anything longer.
- The **incident load** weights each incident of this vendor by its `severity` — 3 points for `LOW`, 8 for `MODERATE`, 18 for `HIGH`, 30 for `CRITICAL` — and multiplies that weight by an age decay factor. The incident's age in whole days is the number of complete 24-hour periods between its `detected_at` and the current logical clock, discarding any partial day. An incident aged 90 days or fewer carries its full weight; one aged more than 90 but no more than 365 days carries half its weight; one older than 365 days contributes nothing at all. Because the age is the floor of the elapsed complete 24-hour periods, an incident whose `detected_at` has been left ahead of the clock — reachable only by driving the clock backwards after the incident was filed — yields a non-positive age and therefore carries its full weight. Which incidents participate depends on the variant, and that is the subject of §3.9.

The live variant of the score is written to `risk_score`, and it is recomputed whenever a subprocessor is disclosed or dispositioned, a commitment is dispositioned, an incident is reported, contained, notified or resolved, a review is decided, the nightly sweep runs — for the vendors that run touches, in the scope §3.7 gives that word — or a catalog entry is reauthorized. Whenever an operation recomputes `risk_score`, it recomputes `privacy_posture` immediately afterwards using the ladder in §2.9 — never the other way around.

A vendor's products are registered as applications, either by the `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` or by a `VendorLiaison`; unlike subprocessor disclosure this write carries no ownership rung at all, so an acting liaison whose `X-User-ID` differs from the submitted `vendor_id` is not refused. The payload names the owning vendor through `vendor_id`, the product `name` as it will appear in the district catalog, an instructional `category` drawn from `INSTRUCTIONAL_CONTENT`, `ASSESSMENT`, `COMMUNICATION`, `ANALYTICS` and `ADMINISTRATIVE`, the offered grade range as `grade_band_low` and `grade_band_high`, the asserted single sign-on protocol `sso_protocol` from `NONE`, `SAML2`, `OIDC` and `LTI13`, and the asserted `accessibility_conformance_level` from `NONE`, `WCAG21_A`, `WCAG21_AA` and `WCAG21_AAA`. An optional `vpat_on_file` boolean states whether an accessibility conformance report has been filed with the district; it defaults to false. The new application lands in `CANDIDATE` status with `sso_verified` false, `catalog_visibility` `HIDDEN` and `exposed_sensitivity_points` at 0.00.

The `category` also fixes, once and for all, how sensitive a data element the district will ever accept for that product. The application's `max_permitted_tier` is looked up from its category at creation and never recomputed, because category is immutable: `INSTRUCTIONAL_CONTENT` permits tiers up to 2, `COMMUNICATION` and `ANALYTICS` up to 3, and `ASSESSMENT` and `ADMINISTRATIVE` up to 4.

Vendor assertions about integration are exactly that — assertions — until the district checks them. A `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` records the outcome of the district's single sign-on and accessibility review by supplying `sso_verified` as the required outcome of the end-to-end integration test, optionally correcting `accessibility_conformance_level` and `vpat_on_file` at the same time; optional fields that are omitted leave the stored values unchanged. Attestation is permitted from `CANDIDATE`, `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` and `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, because both facts are gated at publication time — that is, after the review has already been decided — and an approved application must still be able to finish its attestation. Only a `BLOCKED` application refuses to re-attest, with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`. Each attestation increments the application's `version`, which is the write a concurrent reauthorization will observe as a stale token.

Subprocessor disclosure is the vendor's own obligation, though the privacy office may record it on the vendor's behalf. A disclosure names the disclosing vendor through `vendor_id`, the subprocessor's `name` and processing `country_code`, the `data_categories` of student data it touches (drawn from the same element codes the commitments use), a written `purpose` for the processing, and `sub_breach_notification_hours` — the hours within which the subprocessor must notify the primary vendor of a breach. When the subprocessor is itself a vendor already on the district register, the optional `processor_vendor_id` links to that vendor row, which is how the district walks a chain of processors that are also suppliers; it is null otherwise, and nothing forbids it from naming the disclosing vendor itself. An acting `VendorLiaison` may only disclose against its own vendor, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Every new disclosure lands in `DISCLOSED` with `reviewed_note` null, and immediately recomputes the vendor's `risk_score` and then `privacy_posture` — a non-`US` `country_code` therefore raises the vendor's risk the moment it is disclosed, before any analyst has looked at it.

A `PrivacyAnalyst` then dispositions each disclosure, optionally recording a `reviewed_note` that is stored on the row as the note from the most recent review that supplied one; a disposition that omits the field leaves the stored note exactly as it stood rather than clearing it. Three dispositions exist. `VERIFY` moves `DISCLOSED` to `VERIFIED`, but only when the subprocessor's notification terms are at least as strict as the vendor's own: `sub_breach_notification_hours` must be less than or equal to the vendor's `breach_notification_hours`, and a weaker downstream term is refused with `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_NOTIFICATION_TERMS_WEAKER`. `FLAG` moves `DISCLOSED` to `FLAGGED` with no term condition; because the vendor's `flagged_subprocessor_count` then rises above zero, the vendor's posture re-evaluates to `RESTRICTED` unless the frozen branch already claims it. `REMOVE` retires a disclosure to the terminal `REMOVED` state, and is legal from both `VERIFIED` and `FLAGGED`; removing a flagged row lowers `flagged_subprocessor_count` again, which can release the vendor from `RESTRICTED`. Every disposition recounts `flagged_subprocessor_count` over the vendor's subprocessors currently in `FLAGGED`, then recomputes `risk_score`, then `privacy_posture`.

Two refusals guard those dispositions and their relative order matters. A subprocessor already in `REMOVED` refuses any further review with `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_STATE_INVALID`. Among the remaining rows, the applicability of the submitted disposition is checked *after* the notification-terms rule: `VERIFY` and `FLAG` each require the row to be in `DISCLOSED`, and `REMOVE` requires it to be in `VERIFIED` or `FLAGGED` — a `DISCLOSED` row must be verified or flagged before it can be removed, and a row that has already been verified or flagged can no longer be flagged again — and a violation of any of the three yields `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_APPLICABLE`. Consequently, a `VERIFY` aimed at an already-`FLAGGED` row whose terms are also too weak reports the weaker-terms failure, not the inapplicable-disposition failure.

### 3.2. Teacher Intake: Drafting, Submitting, Revising and Triage

Teachers are enrolled by the privacy office with a `full_name`, a district `employee_number`, the site code `school_code` where they are assigned, and `grade_bands_taught`, the grade levels they currently teach. The employee number is the district's own key and must be unique across every teacher row; a second enrolment reusing one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_NUMBER`. A new teacher is `ACTIVE` — the only other employment state is `INACTIVE` — and starts with `open_request_count` at zero.

A `Teacher` drafts a request to use an application. The draft names the product through `application_id` and the filing teacher through `requester_teacher_id`, which must be the acting teacher's own identifier; a teacher filing on somebody else's behalf is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and the same ownership rule governs reading a single request back. The draft carries the written `instructional_justification`, the `requested_data_elements` the teacher believes the product needs, the `student_count_estimate` of pupils expected to use it, and the intended rollout range as `grade_band_low` and `grade_band_high`. A drafted request sits in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at zero, `submitted_at` null, `triage_note` null, `priority_score` at 0.00 and `priority_band` at `LOW`.

Submission is a separate act, and it is where the request is scored. A teacher may supply `instructional_justification`, `requested_data_elements` or `student_count_estimate` again on submission; any supplied value overwrites the stored draft value **before** any check or calculation runs, so a teacher can fix a too-short justification in the same call that submits it. Those effective values are what every check and every calculation below reads, but they reach the stored row only when the whole submission succeeds: a submission that is refused leaves the stored draft exactly as it was, supplied fields included. Submission is legal from `DRAFT` and from `RETURNED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. Four further conditions then apply in this order: the vendor behind the requested application must not be frozen, or the submission is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_FROZEN`; the effective `instructional_justification` must be at least 120 characters long, or `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_TOO_SHORT`; the effective `requested_data_elements` must contain at least one element code, or `ERR_NO_DATA_ELEMENTS`; and a resubmission is permitted only while `revision_round` is still below 2, so once the counter has reached 2 any further submission is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Note the consequence of that ordering: a request whose justification is far too short but whose vendor has meanwhile been frozen reports the frozen vendor, not the short justification.

A successful submission moves the request to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock, and — when the prior status was `RETURNED` — increases `revision_round` by 1; the first submission out of `DRAFT` leaves the counter alone. It then computes the request's priority and recounts the filing teacher's `open_request_count` over that teacher's requests in `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `TRIAGED_FOR_REVIEW`.

**Priority scoring** runs on every submission, initial or repeat, and its evaluation order is pinned in four steps. First, a raw score is assembled: the grade-band span is the high grade minus the low grade plus one, so a request covering only grade 5 has a span of 1 and a kindergarten-through-twelfth request has a span of 13; the span is multiplied by 20; one hundredth of the `student_count_estimate` is added; and a justification bonus is added, worth 15 points when the effective `instructional_justification` is at least 400 characters, 8 points when it is at least 240 but under 400, and nothing below that. Second, the band is read off that raw score and stored in `priority_band`: `HIGH` when the raw score is at least 60, `STANDARD` when it is at least 30 but below 60, and `LOW` below 30. Third, the band selects a multiplier — 1.20 for `HIGH`, 1.10 for `STANDARD`, 0.85 for `LOW`. Fourth, `priority_score` is the raw score multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half up to two decimals.

The dependency in that sequence looks circular but is not, and getting it wrong is the classic error here. The band is a function of the **raw** score only. It is pinned at step two and never re-derived from the final `priority_score`. A request whose raw score is 58 is `STANDARD` and stores 63.80 — re-deriving the band from 63.80 would wrongly promote it to `HIGH` and would also change the stored score to 69.60.

Triage is the privacy office's decision on a submitted request, taken by a `PrivacyAnalyst` or a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` with a `disposition` of `ACCEPT_FOR_REVIEW`, `RETURN` or `DENY` and an optional `triage_note` recorded as the note from the most recent triage that supplied one; a triage that omits the field leaves the stored note unchanged. Only a `SUBMITTED` request may be triaged; anything else is `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. `ACCEPT_FOR_REVIEW` advances `SUBMITTED → TRIAGED_FOR_REVIEW` and, as a secondary effect, moves the named application from `CANDIDATE` to `IN_REVIEW` — an application already further along is left as it is. `RETURN` sends the request back to the teacher as `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, which is the entry point of the revision loop described above. `DENY` ends the request at the terminal `DENIED`. All three dispositions recount the filing teacher's `open_request_count` over the same three open statuses, so a denial immediately frees that teacher's count while a return does not.

### 3.3. Opening a Data Practice Review and Managing Analyst Workload

Analysts are enrolled by the privacy office with a `full_name`, a `credential_level` of `JUNIOR`, `SENIOR` or `LEAD`, and `review_capacity_minutes`, the total review minutes that analyst may hold across non-terminal reviews at any one time. A new analyst is `ACTIVE` — the alternative staffing state is `INACTIVE` — with `allocated_review_minutes` at zero. Only a `LEAD` may later record a final review decision.

A review is opened against a triaged request by a `PrivacyAnalyst` or a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin`. The payload names the authorizing request through `source_request_id`, the analyst who will author findings through `assigned_analyst_id`, the `framework_set` of privacy frameworks the review covers — a non-empty, duplicate-free selection from `FERPA`, `COPPA`, `PPRA` and `STATE_ADDENDUM` — and `estimated_minutes`, the effort the review will hold against the assignee's capacity. The source request must be in `TRIAGED_FOR_REVIEW`; a review opened against a request in any other status is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_TRIAGED`. The assignee's own staffing `status` is not consulted when a review is opened — an `INACTIVE` analyst may be named at that point, and only reassignment insists on an `ACTIVE` incoming analyst.

Two foreign keys on the new review are copied rather than supplied, and neither is ever rewritten afterwards: `application_id` is copied from the source request, and `vendor_id` is copied from that application's owning vendor. This is why a review can be filtered by vendor even though the client never named one. The review opens in `SCREENING` with `round_number` at 1, `open_findings_count` and `unresolved_findings_count` at zero, `provisional_compliance_score` and `final_compliance_score` null, `remediation_due_at` null, `decided_by_analyst_id` null and `decision_note` null. Opening a review also moves the vendor from `REGISTERED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` when it was still `REGISTERED`, recomputing that vendor's `privacy_posture` and advancing its `version`.

Workload accounting is a full recomputation, never a running total. An analyst's `allocated_review_minutes` is the sum of `estimated_minutes` over every review assigned to that analyst whose status is `SCREENING`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`; reviews that reached `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCKED` are excluded. It is recomputed when a review is opened, when a review is reassigned, and when a review is decided. Capacity is then enforced as the very last rung of the ladder: the assignee's freshly recomputed `allocated_review_minutes` plus the new review's `estimated_minutes` must not exceed that analyst's `review_capacity_minutes`, and an overload is refused with `ERR_ANALYST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because this check sits at the bottom of the ladder, an overloaded assignee named on a request that also cites an untriaged source request will see the untriaged failure first.

Reassignment moves a non-terminal review to a different analyst, and only a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` may do it. The payload carries the incoming `assigned_analyst_id`. Reassignment is confined to reviews still in `SCREENING`: once findings are on the record the review stays with its analyst, so an attempt from `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. Beyond that state gate, the incoming analyst must be different from the current assignee, their `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and they must have room — their recomputed `allocated_review_minutes` plus this review's `estimated_minutes` must not exceed their `review_capacity_minutes`. All three of those conditions are enforced together at that same bottom rung and every one of them is refused with `ERR_ANALYST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, so naming the current assignee again and naming an `INACTIVE` analyst both report a capacity failure rather than a code of their own. A successful move recomputes `allocated_review_minutes` on **both** the outgoing and the incoming analyst, which leaves the district-wide sum of allocated minutes unchanged, and it advances the review's `version`.

### 3.4. Data-Element Commitments: Minimization, Retention and the Exposure Chain

The heart of the review is a row-by-row negotiation over student data. For each student data element the product touches, the vendor's liaison — or an analyst working alongside them — authors a commitment against the open review. The payload names the review through `review_id`, the element through `element_code`, and carries the minimization argument `justified_purpose`, the committed `retention_days` the vendor will keep that element after its last instructional use, and the committed `deletion_method` at the end of retention, one of `HARD_DELETE`, `ANONYMIZE` or `AGGREGATE_ONLY`. Every create must also carry `expected_review_version` as described in §2.7. Commitments may be attached while the review is in `SCREENING`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`; once the review is decided, attachment is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`.

The district, not the vendor, decides how sensitive each element is. The commitment's `sensitivity_tier` is looked up from `element_code` at creation and never supplied by the client. Tier 1 covers `STUDENT_NAME`, `GRADE_LEVEL` and `PARENT_CONTACT`. Tier 2 covers `STUDENT_ID`, `EMAIL` and `DOB`. Tier 3 covers `GEOLOCATION`, `BEHAVIORAL_TELEMETRY` and `PHOTO_VIDEO`. Tier 4 — the special-category tier — covers `IEP_STATUS`, `HEALTH_RECORD`, `BIOMETRIC` and `DISCIPLINE_RECORD`.

The tier then imposes a statutory retention ceiling that is stricter than the unconditional range check of §2.5. Tiers 1 and 2 may be retained for up to 1825 days, tier 3 for up to 730 days, and tier 4 for no more than 365 days. A commitment breaching its tier's ceiling is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_EXCEEDS_STATUTORY_CAP`, so a `HEALTH_RECORD` commitment at 400 days never reaches the register even though 400 is inside the general 1 to 1825 range.

Whether a commitment counts as **minimized** is a computed flag, `is_minimized`, and all four of its conditions must hold: the element's `sensitivity_tier` is no greater than the owning application's `max_permitted_tier`; `retention_days` is no more than 365, so exactly 365 days is minimized and 366 is not; `deletion_method` is anything other than `AGGREGATE_ONLY`; and `justified_purpose` runs to at least 40 characters. The flag is evaluated when the commitment is created and again when it is dispositioned.

Each commitment also carries `element_points`, its own contribution to the owning application's exposure. That contribution is the product of three factors. The tier weight is 1.00 for tier 1, 2.50 for tier 2, 6.00 for tier 3 and 13.00 for tier 4. The retention multiplier is 1.00 when `retention_days` is at most 90, 1.25 when it is above 90 but at most 365, 1.60 when it is above 365 but at most 730, and 2.10 above 730. The deletion multiplier is 1.00 for `HARD_DELETE`, 1.15 for `ANONYMIZE` and 1.45 for `AGGREGATE_ONLY`. The three factors are multiplied together and the product is rounded half up to two decimals — and that per-element rounding happens **before** anything is added up.

An application's `exposed_sensitivity_points` is then the sum of the already-rounded `element_points` of every commitment that is currently `ACCEPTED` and belongs to any review of that application, with the sum itself rounded half up to two decimals. Commitments in `PROPOSED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` contribute nothing. Addends are taken in ascending `created_at` order; because each addend is already rounded the order cannot change the total, but it is pinned so that `element_points` assignment is reproducible.

Re-authoring an element is allowed and is how a vendor improves an offer. When a new commitment is created for a review and element code that already has a `PROPOSED` commitment, the system moves that earlier row to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state, records the new row's identifier in the earlier row's `superseded_by_commitment_id`, and advances the earlier row's `version`. Only earlier rows still in `PROPOSED` are superseded this way: an earlier commitment for the same element that has already reached `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is left exactly as it stands, so re-authoring an element whose predecessor was already accepted leaves both rows live, and both then contribute to the exposure total if the new one is accepted too. The new row itself lands in `PROPOSED` with `decided_by_analyst_id` null. Creating a commitment advances the parent review's `version` by 1, which is precisely why the next writer must re-read the review before it can attach its own row. Nothing else moves on a create: the owning application's `exposed_sensitivity_points` and the vendor's `risk_score` and `privacy_posture` are not recomputed at all — a `PROPOSED` row contributes to none of them — so neither the application nor the vendor is re-stamped, and an `expected_application_version` a client is holding survives a commitment create untouched.

Dispositioning a commitment is an analyst's act and is the segregation-of-duties boundary of this workflow: the analyst assigned to the review proposes and organizes the commitments, and a **different** analyst accepts or rejects them. An acting analyst whose identifier equals the review's `assigned_analyst_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The only payload field is `disposition`, either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, and the acting analyst's identifier is recorded on the row as `decided_by_analyst_id`.

Four more conditions guard a disposition, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.6. The parent review must not be `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCKED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. The commitment itself must still be dispositionable — `PROPOSED` or `ACCEPTED` — because `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal and yield `ERR_COMMITMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The vendor behind the review must not be frozen, or `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_FROZEN`. An `ACCEPT` additionally requires the element's `sensitivity_tier` to be no greater than the application's `max_permitted_tier`, and an over-tier element is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED` — such an element may be proposed and recorded, but it may never be accepted. Finally, applicability: `ACCEPT` requires the row to be in `PROPOSED`, since an already-`ACCEPTED` row can only be rejected and never re-accepted, and an attempt to re-accept is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

A successful disposition walks four rows in a fixed order. The commitment's `status` becomes `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` and `decided_by_analyst_id` is stamped; the owning application's `exposed_sensitivity_points` is recomputed from scratch over its accepted commitments; the vendor's `risk_score` is recomputed with the live variant; and the vendor's `privacy_posture` is re-evaluated. The commitment, the application and the vendor each have their `version` advanced by 1.

Rejection from `ACCEPTED` is the deliberate compensating move of this workflow — an analyst who accepted an element and then learns the retention argument was false rejects it. Because the exposure total is re-summed from scratch rather than adjusted by a delta, the application's `exposed_sensitivity_points` returns to exactly the value it held before that commitment was accepted, with zero drift, and the vendor's `risk_score` and `privacy_posture` follow it back down. Rejection from `PROPOSED` runs the identical three-hop recomputation chain even though the exposure total does not change, because a proposed row never contributed to it in the first place.

### 3.5. Findings, Remediation and the Negotiation Cycle

Once commitments are on the table, the assigned analyst issues a round of findings. Findings are authored **only** by the analyst named in the review's `assigned_analyst_id`; any other analyst is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The payload carries `findings_count`, the number of deficiencies this round records — an integer of at least zero carrying no upper bound, so a round of zero findings is admissible and leaves the review immediately clearable — and an optional `decision_note`, which is written to the review's own `decision_note` field and which the terminal decision overwrites only if it supplies a note of its own; an issuance that omits the field leaves the stored note unchanged. Issuing findings is legal from `SCREENING` and from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, and from any other status it is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. Two further conditions hold: at least one commitment of this review must exist in `PROPOSED` or `ACCEPTED`, since there is nothing to make findings about otherwise — a review with no such commitment is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID` as well, on the same rung as the status gate and therefore ahead of the posture check — and the vendor must not be frozen, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_FROZEN`.

A first round issued out of `SCREENING` leaves `round_number` at 1. A round issued out of `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` increases `round_number` by 1, and that increase may never carry the counter past 3: a fourth round is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. Either way the review moves to `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, `open_findings_count` and `unresolved_findings_count` are both set to the supplied `findings_count`, the review's `version` advances, and the remediation clock is (re)started: `remediation_due_at` is set to the current logical clock plus fourteen days — 336 hours — measured exactly.

The vendor answers through its own liaison, and only its own liaison: the privacy office cannot remediate on the vendor's behalf, and an acting liaison whose identifier is not the review's `vendor_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The payload carries `resolved_findings_count`, the number of findings the vendor claims to have closed — again an integer of at least zero with no upper bound — and an optional `remediation_note`, which the service retains internally and does not surface as a field of the review payload. The review must be in `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. The window is inclusive: a remediation arriving when the logical clock is exactly at `remediation_due_at` succeeds, and one arriving after it is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

A successful remediation moves the review `FINDINGS_ISSUED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and rewrites both finding counters: `unresolved_findings_count` becomes `open_findings_count` minus `resolved_findings_count`, floored at zero so that a vendor claiming more resolutions than there were outstanding simply closes all of them, and `open_findings_count` is then set to that same number. The review's `version` advances. From here the assigned analyst may issue another round — the `FINDINGS_ISSUED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → FINDINGS_ISSUED` loop is the negotiation cycle, capped at three rounds — or a lead analyst may take the review to a terminal decision.

**Provisional scoring.** Every issuance of findings recomputes the review's `provisional_compliance_score`. The score starts from a 100-point base assembled from six weighted factors and then has a penalty subtracted.

- **Framework coverage, worth 30 points.** The count of the four required frameworks — `FERPA`, `COPPA`, `PPRA`, `STATE_ADDENDUM` — that appear in the review's `framework_set`, divided by four, multiplied by 30. A review covering three of the four earns 22.5 points.
- **Minimization, worth 25 points.** The number of commitments in the variant's input set whose `is_minimized` is true, divided by the number of commitments in that set, multiplied by 25. When the input set is empty the ratio is treated as zero.
- **Retention discipline, worth 15 points.** The factor is 1.0 when every commitment in the input set has `retention_days` of at most 365 **and** a `deletion_method` other than `AGGREGATE_ONLY`; otherwise 0.5 when every commitment in the set has `retention_days` of at most 730; otherwise 0.0. An empty input set scores 0.0.
- **Subprocessor discipline, worth 15 points.** The factor is 0.0 when the vendor's `subprocessor_disclosure_complete` is false; 0.0 when the vendor's `flagged_subprocessor_count` is above zero; 0.5 when any subprocessor of the vendor in `DISCLOSED` or `VERIFIED` has `sub_breach_notification_hours` greater than the vendor's own `breach_notification_hours`; and 1.0 otherwise. The conditions are read in that order, first match winning.
- **Accessibility, worth 10 points.** The factor is 1.0 when the application's `accessibility_conformance_level` is `WCAG21_AA` or `WCAG21_AAA` and `vpat_on_file` is true; 0.5 when the level is exactly `WCAG21_A` and `vpat_on_file` is true; and 0.0 otherwise, including whenever no conformance report is on file.
- **Single sign-on, worth 5 points.** The factor is 1.0 when the application's `sso_protocol` is `SAML2`, `OIDC` or `LTI13` **and** `sso_verified` is true, and 0.0 otherwise.

For the provisional variant the input set is optimistic: it is every commitment of this review whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `ACCEPTED`, crediting rows the vendor has proposed as though they will be accepted. The provisional penalty is 8 points for each finding counted in `open_findings_count` — read after this round has already rewritten that counter to the supplied `findings_count`, never from the value it held before — plus 12 points for each of the vendor's flagged subprocessors, and the missing-addendum penalty described in §3.6 is deliberately **not** applied in this variant. The base minus the penalty is clamped into the range from 0 through 100 and then rounded half up to two decimals, in that order.

### 3.6. The Terminal Decision and Its Four-Hop Cascade

A review is closed by a `PrivacyAnalyst` whose stored `credential_level` is `LEAD` and who is **not** the review's `assigned_analyst_id`; a non-lead, or the assignee themself, is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — and so is an `X-User-ID` that matches no stored analyst row at all, since it carries no credential the server could read as `LEAD`. The payload carries a `disposition` of `CLEAR`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCK` and an optional `decision_note`. A decision is only reachable from `FINDINGS_ISSUED`: a review still in `SCREENING` or sitting in `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`, which makes at least one findings round mandatory, and a review already in `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCKED` is refused one rung later with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED`.

Each disposition has its own applicability condition, checked at the bottom of the ladder and reported as `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_APPLICABLE`. `CLEAR` requires `unresolved_findings_count` to be exactly zero — a fully clean record. `CONDITIONAL` requires `unresolved_findings_count` to be greater than zero, since a conditional approval is what the district records when something is still outstanding. `BLOCK` carries no such condition and may be recorded whatever the counters say. `CLEAR` alone additionally requires the vendor's `subprocessor_disclosure_complete` to be true; a vendor that has not asserted a complete chain cannot be fully cleared and is refused with `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`, whereas the `CONDITIONAL` branch deliberately imposes no disclosure-completeness requirement.

**Final scoring.** The decision computes `final_compliance_score` exactly once, using the same six weighted factors described in §3.5 but with two deliberate differences. The input set is pessimistic: only commitments of this review whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` participate, so proposed-but-undecided rows are dropped from both the minimization ratio and the retention factor. The penalty is 8 points for each finding counted in `unresolved_findings_count`, plus 12 points for each of the vendor's flagged subprocessors, plus a flat 20 points whenever the vendor's `signed_state_addendum` is false. As in the provisional variant, the base minus the penalty is clamped into the range from 0 through 100 and then rounded half up to two decimals. Because both the input-set filter and the penalty terms differ, identical stored rows produce a different number under the two variants — `provisional_compliance_score` and `final_compliance_score` are not expected to agree, and neither is recomputed after the fact.

Whichever disposition is recorded, the same cascade runs, and each hop is a consequence of the one before it. The review's own row is written first: `status` becomes `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCKED`, `decided_by_analyst_id` is stamped with the acting analyst, `decision_note` is recorded when the payload supplies one and otherwise left as whatever the last findings round wrote, `final_compliance_score` is computed, and the review's `version` advances. Every commitment of this review still sitting in `PROPOSED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, after which the owning application's `exposed_sensitivity_points` is recomputed over the accepted set. Because the review has left the non-terminal set, the assigned analyst's `allocated_review_minutes` is re-summed and their held workload drops.

The second hop moves the application: from `IN_REVIEW` it becomes `APPROVED` on `CLEAR`, `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` on `CONDITIONAL`, and `BLOCKED` on `BLOCK`. The third hop follows the application: every request of that application still sitting in `TRIAGED_FOR_REVIEW` moves to `CLOSED_APPROVED` when the application was approved either way, and to `CLOSED_BLOCKED` when it was blocked. The fourth hop follows those requests: because they have left the open set, each filing teacher's `open_request_count` is recounted.

The decision also settles the vendor's registry standing. A vendor that was still `REGISTERED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` moves to `APPROVED` on `CLEAR`, `CONDITIONAL` on `CONDITIONAL`, and the terminal `REJECTED` on `BLOCK`; a rejected vendor can never be reinstated and can only re-enter the district as a newly registered row. The vendor's `risk_score` is then recomputed with the live variant and its `privacy_posture` re-evaluated, in that order.

One side effect belongs to the `BLOCK` branch alone and to no other: because the application became `BLOCKED`, every catalog entry of that application in `PUBLISHED`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `SUSPENDED_HOLD` is moved to the terminal `REVOKED` state with `revoked_reason` recorded as `REVIEW_BLOCKED` — an entry taken from `SUSPENDED_HOLD` keeps the `hold_restored_from` it was carrying, which an explicit revocation would instead have cleared — after which the application's `catalog_visibility` recomputes to `HIDDEN`. A `CLEAR` or `CONDITIONAL` decision never touches existing catalog entries.

### 3.7. The District Catalog: Publication, Expiry Sweep, Reauthorization and Revocation

Publication is the moment an approved product becomes visible to teachers. A `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` or a `PrivacyAnalyst` publishes by naming the product through `application_id`, the decided review that authorizes it through `review_id`, and the bounded window through `effective_from` and `expires_at`, together with the grade range the district authorizes as `authorized_grade_band_low` and `authorized_grade_band_high`. When a `PrivacyAnalyst` publishes, they must not be the analyst recorded as that review's `decided_by_analyst_id` — the analyst who decided a review does not also publish it — and a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` publishing is under no such restriction.

Publication is the most heavily gated write in the service, and the gates are evaluated in ladder order. The vendor must not be frozen (`ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_FROZEN`), and its posture must be `CLEAR` or `WATCH`, so a `RESTRICTED` vendor is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_PUBLICATION`. The vendor must assert a complete subprocessor chain, or `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`. The cited review must be in `CLEARED` or `CONDITIONAL` **and** its own `application_id` must equal the submitted `application_id`, or `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLEARED` — a decided review from a different product cannot be borrowed. The proposed window must not overlap a sibling window under the half-open, zero-buffer rule of §2.11, or `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The application's `accessibility_conformance_level` must be `WCAG21_AA` or `WCAG21_AAA` and its `vpat_on_file` must be true, or `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_CONFORMANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. Its `sso_protocol` must be something other than `NONE` and `sso_verified` must be true, or `ERR_SSO_NOT_VERIFIED`. Finally the published band must sit inside the product's own band: `authorized_grade_band_low` must be at least the application's `grade_band_low` and `authorized_grade_band_high` must be no more than the application's `grade_band_high`, or `ERR_GRADE_BAND_OUT_OF_SCOPE`.

A successful publication lands in `PUBLISHED` with `predecessor_entry_id` null, `revoked_reason` null and `hold_restored_from` null. Two computed values are written at that moment. `reauthorization_due_at` is `expires_at` minus 30 days — the district's reauthorization lead time — and it is the instant at or after which the sweep will start warning. `published_review_score` is a snapshot of the cited review's `final_compliance_score` taken at publication and **never** refreshed afterwards, so a catalog entry keeps reporting the score the district saw when it authorized the product even if the review row were later re-read. The owning application's `catalog_visibility` is then recomputed: it is `VISIBLE` when at least one catalog entry of that application is in `PUBLISHED` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, and `HIDDEN` otherwise.

The **nightly compliance sweep** is how the catalog ages, and only a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` may run it. It takes no payload, reads the logical clock, and is idempotent for a fixed clock — running it twice without moving the clock changes nothing the second time. It performs three passes. Every `PUBLISHED` entry whose `reauthorization_due_at` is at or before the clock while its `expires_at` is still ahead of the clock moves to `EXPIRING_SOON`. Every `PUBLISHED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` entry whose `expires_at` is at or before the clock moves to `EXPIRED` — which means a `PUBLISHED` entry whose expiry is already past skips `EXPIRING_SOON` entirely and lands on `EXPIRED` in one sweep. Every incident whose `notification_status` is `PENDING` and whose `notification_due_at` is strictly before the clock moves to `OVERDUE`. Afterwards the sweep recomputes `catalog_visibility` on every touched application, and `risk_score` followed by `privacy_posture` on every touched vendor — which is how an overdue notification silently freezes a vendor overnight. *Touched* is scoped to the rows this particular run actually moved: an application is touched when one of its own entries changed status in this sweep, and a vendor is touched when it owns a touched application or owns an incident this sweep moved to `OVERDUE`. A vendor behind nothing that moved is left exactly as it stood, score, posture and `version` alike, which is also what makes a second sweep at an unchanged clock a no-op. The sweep answers with five counters: `evaluated_entry_count`, `expiring_soon_count`, `expired_count`, `overdue_notification_count` and `recomputed_vendor_count`. `evaluated_entry_count` is the number of catalog entries the run actually considered — those sitting in `PUBLISHED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` when the run began, taken from that opening snapshot and therefore unaffected by the transitions the run itself then makes; an entry already in `EXPIRED`, `SUSPENDED_HOLD`, `REVOKED` or `SUPERSEDED` is neither evaluated nor moved by a sweep, and incidents never contribute to this counter. `expiring_soon_count` and `expired_count` are the numbers of entries this run moved into those two states, and `overdue_notification_count` the number of incidents it moved to `OVERDUE`, so all three count transitions rather than resting populations and all three read zero on a repeat run at an unchanged clock. `recomputed_vendor_count` is the number of distinct vendors touched in the sense just given — zero when the sweep moved no row at all.

**Annual reauthorization** is a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` operation and it is deliberately dependent on the sweep. Only an entry in `EXPIRING_SOON` or `EXPIRED` may be reauthorized; a still-fresh `PUBLISHED` entry is not renewable and is refused with `ERR_CATALOG_ENTRY_NOT_RENEWABLE`, so the sweep must first walk the entry into its reauthorization window. The payload carries the new `expires_at`, the required `expected_application_version` token of §2.7, and optionally `authorized_grade_band_low` and `authorized_grade_band_high`; when the band fields are omitted the successor inherits the predecessor's band, and either bound may be supplied on its own. Because a missing bound is inherited, the low-not-above-high check of §2.5 is made on this one endpoint against the *effective* pair — each supplied bound against the other supplied or inherited one — and therefore only once `entryId` has been resolved, so an unresolvable `entryId` reports `ERR_CATALOG_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` even when the bounds submitted alongside it are inverted. The strictly-after check on the new `expires_at` behaves the same way, being measured against the predecessor's own `expires_at`. Beyond the state gate and the concurrency token, reauthorization requires the vendor not to be frozen (`ERR_VENDOR_POSTURE_FROZEN`) and its posture to be `CLEAR` or `WATCH`; the review named for the renewal must be `CLEARED` or `CONDITIONAL` and must belong to the same application; the new window must not overlap any other sibling entry in `PUBLISHED`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `SUSPENDED_HOLD` (`ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`); and the authorized band must remain inside the application's own band (`ERR_GRADE_BAND_OUT_OF_SCOPE`). That enumeration is exhaustive: reauthorization deliberately does not re-run first publication's subprocessor-disclosure, accessibility-conformance or single-sign-on gates, so an entry whose application has since had its VPAT withdrawn or its single sign-on unverified is still renewable, and no `ERR_SUBPROCESSOR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_CONFORMANCE_INSUFFICIENT` or `ERR_SSO_NOT_VERIFIED` can arise from this endpoint.

A successful reauthorization does five things. The predecessor entry moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state. A successor entry is created in `PUBLISHED`, with `predecessor_entry_id` pointing at the predecessor, `effective_from` set to the predecessor's `expires_at` — exact adjacency, which is legal precisely because windows are half-open — `expires_at` set to the supplied instant, `reauthorization_due_at` recomputed as the new expiry minus 30 days, and `published_review_score` carried over from the predecessor. The owning application's `catalog_visibility` is recomputed and its `version` advances by 1. And the vendor is rescored **twice**: `reauthorization_risk_score` is written with the reauthorization variant, and `risk_score` with the live variant, after which `privacy_posture` is re-evaluated. The two variants differ in exactly one respect — the live variant counts incidents whose `response_status` is `REPORTED`, `CONTAINED`, `ESCALATED` or `REMEDIATED`, while the reauthorization variant forgives remediated incidents and counts only `REPORTED`, `CONTAINED` and `ESCALATED`. Identical stored rows therefore yield a strictly lower reauthorization number than live number whenever the vendor has any remediated incident that decay has not yet retired. Before the first reauthorization ever runs for a vendor, `reauthorization_risk_score` holds the 0.00 written at registration.

Revocation is the district's early exit, available to a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` on an entry in `PUBLISHED`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `SUSPENDED_HOLD`; any other status is refused with `ERR_CATALOG_ENTRY_NOT_ACTIVE`. The payload carries the mandatory `revoked_reason`, which is recorded on the row. Revocation moves the entry to the terminal `REVOKED` state, clears `hold_restored_from`, and recomputes the application's `catalog_visibility`, which drops to `HIDDEN` once no `PUBLISHED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` entry of that application remains. A revoked authorization is never the same thing as an expired one, a distinction that matters in §3.8.

### 3.8. Telemetry: Detecting Applications Nobody Approved

The district's collectors report what is actually in use, which is rarely the same as what was approved. Minting an observation is exclusive to the `TelemetryAgent` role: analysts and privacy-office operators may read and triage detections but may never create one. The collector posts the fully qualified `observed_domain` it saw, the `school_code` of the site where it saw it, the `source` that produced the observation — `NETWORK_DNS`, `BROWSER_EXTENSION` or `MDM_INVENTORY` — the `observed_at` instant, which must be at or before the current logical clock, and the two volume figures `distinct_user_count` and `session_count`. It supplies `application_id` when its own domain table matched the traffic to a district-registered product, and omits it when nothing matched. The row is created in `NEW` with `triaged_by_user_id` and `linked_incident_id` both null.

Two values are computed at creation, in order. First the **classification selector** runs, and it is a strict first-match ladder that also fixes `matched_catalog_entry_id`.

1. If `application_id` is null, the `classification` is `UNKNOWN_DOMAIN` and `matched_catalog_entry_id` is null. Nothing else is examined: an unmatched domain is never compared to any window.
2. Otherwise, consider the catalog entries of that application whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` and whose window contains `observed_at` under the half-open reading of §2.11 — `effective_from` at or before `observed_at`, `expires_at` strictly after it. If that set is non-empty, the `classification` is `AUTHORIZED` and `matched_catalog_entry_id` is the entry of that set with the greatest `expires_at`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`.
3. Otherwise, consider the entries of that application whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, `EXPIRING_SOON`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` and whose `expires_at` is at or before `observed_at`. If that set is non-empty, the `classification` is `EXPIRED_AUTHORIZATION` and `matched_catalog_entry_id` is again the entry with the greatest `expires_at`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. Entries in `REVOKED` are deliberately excluded from this set, because a revoked authorization is not an expired one — traffic against a revoked product is not "late", it is unauthorized.
4. Otherwise the `classification` is `UNAUTHORIZED` and `matched_catalog_entry_id` is null.

Only the named application's own entries ever participate; no other application's catalog rows are consulted. Because branch 2 uses the half-open convention, an observation landing exactly on an entry's `expires_at` is outside that window and falls through to branch 3, where that very same entry becomes the match — the boundary instant flips a detection from `AUTHORIZED` to `EXPIRED_AUTHORIZATION` while keeping the same `matched_catalog_entry_id`.

Second, `exposure_score` is computed from this detection row alone: 0.4 points per `distinct_user_count`, plus 0.05 points per `session_count`, plus a base that depends entirely on the classification just assigned — 0 for `AUTHORIZED`, 25 for `EXPIRED_AUTHORIZATION`, 45 for `UNAUTHORIZED` and 30 for `UNKNOWN_DOMAIN`. Note the ordering of those bases: a completely unrecognised domain scores *lower* than a recognised product being used without authorization, because the district treats a known vendor operating outside its window as the more serious governance failure. The sum is clamped to a ceiling of 100.00 and only then rounded half up to 2 decimals.

**Triage** is performed by a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` or a `PrivacyAnalyst`, who supply a `disposition` and an optional `note` — retained internally for the district's own audit trail and never surfaced as a field of the detection payload — and it may happen only once: a detection whose `status` is no longer `NEW` is refused with `ERR_DETECTION_ALREADY_TRIAGED`. The optional `affected_student_count` and `max_data_tier` members that the `ESCALATE` branch below consumes are declared by this endpoint for every disposition, so supplying either one alongside a `CONFIRM_AUTHORIZED`, `BLOCK` or `WHITELIST` is admitted rather than refused as an unknown field, and is then simply ignored. Whatever the disposition, `triaged_by_user_id` is stamped with the caller's `X-User-ID` — recorded verbatim, never compared to a stored `id`, since both triaging roles may be opaque operators.

Each of the four dispositions has its own applicability condition, and a violation is `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_APPLICABLE`. `CONFIRM_AUTHORIZED` moves the detection to `CONFIRMED_AUTHORIZED` and requires the `classification` to be exactly `AUTHORIZED` — the operator is confirming the machine's finding, not overriding it. `BLOCK` moves it to `BLOCKED` and applies to the three adverse classifications `UNAUTHORIZED`, `EXPIRED_AUTHORIZATION` and `UNKNOWN_DOMAIN`; it is the only disposition available for an unmatched domain, because a domain with no `application_id` has no vendor to escalate to and no entry to whitelist against. `WHITELIST` and `ESCALATE` both require the `classification` to be `UNAUTHORIZED` or `EXPIRED_AUTHORIZATION`.

`WHITELIST` moves the detection to `WHITELISTED` and carries one extra, deliberately time-sensitive gate: the detection's `matched_catalog_entry_id` must be non-null **and** that catalog entry must currently be in `PUBLISHED` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, or the call is refused with `ERR_WHITELIST_REQUIRES_ACTIVE_ENTRY`. The critical subtlety is that this gate reads the entry's state at the **current logical clock**, not at `observed_at`: a detection classified `EXPIRED_AUTHORIZATION` last month becomes whitelistable once the product is reauthorized and the matched entry is live again, while a detection classified `UNAUTHORIZED` can never be whitelisted at all because it has no matched entry to point at.

`ESCALATE` moves the detection to `ESCALATED` and additionally requires `application_id` to be non-null, since the escalation must land on a vendor. It runs a four-hop cascade. The detection's own `status` and `triaged_by_user_id` are written. Because the detection became `ESCALATED`, a `VendorIncident` is minted for the vendor of the matched application, with `incident_type` fixed at `UNAUTHORIZED_DISCLOSURE`, `detected_at` copied from the detection's `observed_at`, `affected_student_count` taken from the triage payload when supplied and otherwise defaulted to the detection's `distinct_user_count`, `max_data_tier` taken from the payload when supplied and otherwise defaulted to 3, and `response_status` `REPORTED`; `reported_at` is stamped with the current logical clock and the notification axis opens exactly as a self-report's does, at `PENDING` with `notified_party_count` zero and `contained_at`, `notification_sent_at` and `remediation_note` all null; the new incident's `severity` and `notification_due_at` are computed exactly as in §3.9, and the detection's `linked_incident_id` is set to the new row. Because a `REPORTED` incident now exists, the vendor's `open_incident_count` and `risk_score` are recomputed. Because the score and the open-incident set changed, the vendor's `privacy_posture` is re-evaluated — which, for a `CRITICAL` escalation, freezes the vendor immediately. One exemption is deliberate: the incident minted by an escalation **bypasses the incident-window buffer check of §2.11 entirely**, because that rung guards only incidents filed through `POST /vendor-incidents`, whichever of the two roles files them. A telemetry escalation therefore always succeeds in creating its incident, even when a colliding incident window already exists for that vendor.

### 3.9. Incident Response: Two Independent Dimensions, and District-Wide Suspension

A vendor incident is the one entity in this service that advances along **two independent state axes**. Its `response_status` runs `REPORTED` to `CONTAINED` and then to either `REMEDIATED` or `ESCALATED`. Its `notification_status` runs `PENDING` to either `SENT` or `OVERDUE`, and from `OVERDUE` to `SENT_LATE`. Neither axis constrains the other: an incident may be contained and remediated while its notification is still `PENDING`, and it may be notified while its response is still `REPORTED`. No transition on one axis ever writes the other, and the workflow narrative below should be read as two parallel tracks.

An incident is opened by the vendor's own `VendorLiaison` — whose `X-User-ID` must equal the `vendor_id` on the payload, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — or by a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` on the vendor's behalf. The payload names the `vendor_id`, an `incident_type` of `BREACH`, `SUBPROCESSOR_CHANGE`, `RETENTION_VIOLATION` or `UNAUTHORIZED_DISCLOSURE`, the `detected_at` instant, which must be at or before the current logical clock, the `affected_student_count`, and the `max_data_tier` involved. The row is created with `reported_at` set to the logical clock, `response_status` `REPORTED`, `notification_status` `PENDING`, `notified_party_count` 0, and `contained_at`, `notification_sent_at` and `remediation_note` all null. Every incident filed through this endpoint — by the vendor's own liaison and equally by a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` acting on its behalf — is subject to the closed, doubly-buffered window collision of §2.11, refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_WINDOW_BUFFER_CONFLICT`; the district's intent is that a vendor cannot file a stream of adjacent incidents to dilute a single event, and the one incident that escapes the check is the one §3.8's telemetry escalation mints. A successful report then walks the vendor: because a new `REPORTED` incident has joined the open set, `open_incident_count` is recounted, `risk_score` is recomputed with the live variant and `privacy_posture` is re-evaluated, in that order, so a `CRITICAL` incident freezes its vendor the moment it is filed.

`severity` is derived from two of the payload fields as a first-match threshold ladder: `CRITICAL` when `affected_student_count` is at least 1000 **or** `max_data_tier` is exactly 4, otherwise `HIGH` when `affected_student_count` is at least 100, otherwise `MODERATE` when it is at least 10, otherwise `LOW`. Every comparison is inclusive, so exactly 1000 affected students is `CRITICAL` and 999 with a `max_data_tier` of 3 or less is `HIGH`. The tier disjunct means a single student's biometric or health record — tier 4 — is `CRITICAL` regardless of volume.

`notification_due_at` then combines the contract with the statute. The statutory ceiling is 24 hours for `CRITICAL`, 48 for `HIGH`, 72 for `MODERATE` and 168 for `LOW`. The effective allowance is the **minimum** of the vendor's contractual `breach_notification_hours` and that statutory ceiling — never the maximum — and `notification_due_at` is `detected_at` plus the effective allowance. A vendor that negotiated a generous 96-hour contractual term therefore still owes a `CRITICAL` notification within 24 hours, while a vendor that promised 12 hours is held to its own tighter promise on a `LOW` incident. The deadline is measured from `detected_at`, not from `reported_at`, so a vendor that sits on a discovery for two days has already consumed its allowance before the row exists.

On the response axis, **containment** is recorded by the vendor's own `VendorLiaison` — again `X-User-ID` must equal the incident's `vendor_id` — or by a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin`. It requires `response_status` to be exactly `REPORTED`, and otherwise fails with `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`. The payload carries `contained_at`, which must be at or after the incident's `detected_at` and at or before the current logical clock; when it is omitted the current logical clock is used. An optional `remediation_note` may accompany it, and when supplied it is written to the incident's own surfaced `remediation_note` field; when omitted, whatever that field already held is left unchanged. The transition writes `response_status` `CONTAINED` and `contained_at`, deliberately leaves `notification_status` untouched, and then recomputes the vendor's `risk_score` followed by its `privacy_posture` — which is how containing a `CRITICAL` incident lifts the freeze that the `CRITICAL`-and-`REPORTED` branch of §2.9 had imposed, provided no other freeze condition still holds.

**Resolution** is a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` operation, requires `response_status` to be exactly `CONTAINED` — otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID` — and takes a `disposition` of `REMEDIATE` or `ESCALATE` plus an optional `remediation_note` that is recorded on the row. `REMEDIATE` writes `response_status` `REMEDIATED` and then recomputes the vendor's `open_incident_count`, `risk_score` and `privacy_posture` in that order; because `REMEDIATED` leaves the open-incident set, this is the transition that can finally return a vendor's posture to `WATCH` or `CLEAR`. `ESCALATE` writes `response_status` `ESCALATED` and the same note, but `ESCALATED` remains inside the open-incident set, so `open_incident_count` keeps counting the row and the posture stays `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — escalation is explicitly not a way out. Note also the asymmetry with §3.7's scoring: a `REMEDIATED` incident still counts in the live `risk_score` scope, and is forgiven only by the reauthorization variant.

On the notification axis, **sending the notification** is a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` operation — the privacy office, not the vendor, records that affected parties were told — and it takes `notified_party_count`, an integer of at least zero carrying no upper bound. It requires `notification_status` to be `PENDING` or `OVERDUE`; `SENT` and `SENT_LATE` are terminal for this dimension and a repeat attempt is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_ALREADY_SENT`. The transition writes `notification_sent_at` to the current logical clock and records the supplied `notified_party_count`, then chooses the landing state purely from the clock: `SENT` when the clock is at or before `notification_due_at`, and `SENT_LATE` otherwise. The consequence is that all three of `PENDING` to `SENT`, `PENDING` to `SENT_LATE` and `OVERDUE` to `SENT_LATE` are reachable from this one call — a vendor that notifies past the deadline without waiting for a sweep lands directly on `SENT_LATE` from `PENDING`, and never passes through `OVERDUE`. The vendor's `privacy_posture` is then re-evaluated, because clearing the last `OVERDUE` notification can lift a freeze. The fourth transition on this axis, `PENDING` to `OVERDUE`, belongs to the sweep alone and fires when `notification_due_at` is **strictly before** the clock; a clock sitting exactly on the deadline leaves the notification `PENDING`, and the same instant would still permit a `SENT` notification.

**Suspension** is the district's blunt instrument, available to a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` on a vendor whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`; any other status is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_STATE_INVALID`. The payload carries a mandatory `reason`, which the service retains internally for its own audit trail but does not surface as a field of the vendor payload. The cascade has four hops. The vendor's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and its `suspended_at` is set to the current logical clock. Because the status became `SUSPENDED`, the posture ladder re-evaluates and `privacy_posture` becomes `FROZEN` — unconditionally, since that is the ladder's top rung. Because the posture became `FROZEN`, every catalog entry belonging to any application of that vendor whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` moves to `SUSPENDED_HOLD`, recording in `hold_restored_from` the status it just left. Because those entries left the visible set, the `catalog_visibility` of each affected application is recomputed and drops to `HIDDEN`. The vendor's `risk_score` is recomputed with the live variant, and `version` is incremented by 1 on the vendor, on every entry moved to `SUSPENDED_HOLD`, and on every application whose visibility changed.

**Reinstatement** is the exact compensating action, available to a `PrivacyOfficeAdmin` on a vendor whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` — any other status is again `ERR_VENDOR_STATE_INVALID` — with an optional `note`, likewise retained internally and not surfaced on the vendor payload. Its four hops undo the suspension's four. The vendor's `status` returns to `APPROVED`, or to `CONDITIONAL` if that is where it stood before the suspension, and `suspended_at` is cleared to null. The `privacy_posture` is re-evaluated, and it leaves `FROZEN` **only** if no other freeze condition still holds — an `OVERDUE` notification or a `CRITICAL` incident still in `REPORTED` keeps the vendor frozen even after the suspension is formally lifted. Because the posture left `FROZEN`, every catalog entry of that vendor's applications still in `SUSPENDED_HOLD` is restored, and the landing state is chosen **purely from the clock position, never from `hold_restored_from`**: the entry returns to `PUBLISHED` when the clock is before its `reauthorization_due_at`, to `EXPIRING_SOON` when the clock is at or after `reauthorization_due_at` but still before `expires_at`, and to `EXPIRED` when the clock is at or after `expires_at`. An entry held through a long suspension therefore comes back aged, exactly as if the sweep had run while it was on hold, and `hold_restored_from` is cleared to null afterwards. Finally each affected application's `catalog_visibility` is recomputed, the vendor's `risk_score` is recomputed with the live variant, and the vendor's `version` is incremented by 1. A vendor in the terminal `REJECTED` state is outside this cycle altogether: it can never be suspended and never reinstated, only re-registered as a new row.

## 4. API Endpoint Specifications

The service exposes 50 endpoints, all unprefixed and all consuming and producing JSON. Path variables are named exactly as shown, and a trailing slash on any path resolves to the same endpoint as the path without it. All list endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3 in addition to the filters named in their descriptions, and all responses use the envelope of §2.2. ## 5. Error Code Catalogue
