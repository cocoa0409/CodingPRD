# Wholesaling Assignment Desk API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Real-estate wholesaling sits in an uncomfortable regulatory gap. A wholesaler finds a distressed homeowner, signs a purchase contract at a discount, and then sells its position in that contract to a cash buyer for a fee — often without ever holding title and, in most states, without holding a real-estate licence. Everything that makes the trade profitable also makes it dangerous: the seller may not realise the buyer never intends to close, the assignment fee may quietly consume the seller's remaining equity, the earnest money may be forfeited on a technicality, and a wholesaler who negotiates the resale on the seller's behalf without a licence may be committing unlicensed brokerage. The Wholesaling Assignment Desk exists to make each of those risks a checkable, recorded, auditable condition rather than a matter of good intentions.

This document specifies the API behind that desk. It manages the full arc of a wholesale deal: outbound marketing campaigns whose creative must disclose both the intent to assign and the advertiser's licensure posture; distressed-seller leads that are screened onto a protection tier before anyone may contract with them; deal analyses that derive an after-repair value and a binding maximum allowable offer from comparable sales and a repair scope; purchase contracts carrying three independent lifecycle dimensions; a curated buyer list gated on verified proof of funds; a bounded counter-and-revise negotiation over the assignment fee; a choice between assigning the contract and double-closing it through a transactional lender; title coordination under dual control; and the unwinding paths — cancellation, earnest-money forfeiture, seller disputes and compliance adjudication — that a real desk needs far more often than it would like.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **seller protection is structural, not advisory**: the protection tier a lead lands on tightens the assignment-fee cap, imposes a cooling-off window, and raises an equity floor beneath the purchase price, and those rules deliberately outrank the wholesaler's own fiscal ceilings so that a request failing both reports the seller-protection failure. Second, **money is conserved, never minted**: earnest money always re-sums to the amount deposited across four mutually exclusive buckets, and a buyer's committed deposits are always reconcilable against their verified liquidity. Implementers should treat both as invariants that hold after every single request, not as end-of-day reconciliation jobs.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this API is mounted at the server root — there is no versioned or domain prefix, so the wholesaler roster lives at `/wholesalers`, contracts at `/purchase-contracts`, and so on. All request and response bodies are JSON.

### 2.1. Principals, Headers, and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-Role` names the role the caller is acting as, and `X-User-ID` names the individual principal. There are exactly six roles, spelled precisely as follows: `Wholesaler`, `Seller`, `CashBuyer`, `TitleAgent`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `PlatformAdmin`. If `X-User-Role` is absent, empty, or is any string outside that set of six, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); the same code is returned when `X-User-ID` is missing on any endpoint whose actor identity is recorded or compared. Authentication resolves before anything else — before body validation, before pagination bounds, and before any path identifier is looked up — so an unauthenticated call against a nonexistent resource reports 401 and not 404. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not permitted to invoke the endpoint, the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Three of the six roles are backed by persisted entities, and for those roles `X-User-ID` is a real foreign key rather than an audit string. When `X-User-Role` is `Wholesaler`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Wholesaler`; ownership checks on any campaign, lead, analysis, contract, buyer, proof-of-funds record, offer, agreement or title order compare the wholesaler reachable from that resource to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Seller`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SellerLead`; a seller may act only on the contract whose `seller_lead_id` equals `X-User-ID`, together with that contract's disclosure acknowledgements and disputes. When `X-User-Role` is `CashBuyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CashBuyer`; a buyer may act only on proof-of-funds records and offers whose `cash_buyer_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Any mismatch between the acting principal and the target aggregate is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403), and because ownership is an authorization concern it outranks every state precondition — a wholesaler poking at a rival's cancelled contract gets 403, never 409.

The remaining three roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `TitleAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it — as `cleared_by_user_id` and `funded_by_user_id` on a `TitleOrder` — and compares those two recorded values against one another for dual control, but never compares either to an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise opaque; the server records it as `opened_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `ComplianceReview` and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `DisputeCase`, and as `verified_by_user_id` nowhere — that field belongs to the wholesaler who verified a proof of funds. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that the server may record but never compares to any entity's `id`; the platform administrator registers wholesalers, reads the wholesaler roster, and drives the logical clock, and holds no deal-level authority whatsoever.

A note on cross-aggregate ownership that catches implementers out: an `AssignmentOffer` binds a `CashBuyer` to a `PurchaseContract`, and both must sit under the same `Wholesaler`. A buyer on wholesaler A's list bidding on a contract reachable to wholesaler B is an ownership failure, reported as `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Deterministic List Ordering

Every collection-listing endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 400). Pagination bounds are part of schema validation and are therefore checked before any path identifier is resolved, so a bad `limit` against a nonexistent parent reports 400 rather than 404.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where two records share a `created_at` value the server must still produce a stable order, but ordering never falls back to `id`. The `meta` block of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the count of records matching the filters before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current window).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Timestamps, and the Version Counter

Every record's `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every record carries a `created_at` timestamp — an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, read from the logical clock described in §2.5. Almost every record additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp with the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of that record, whether the mutation was requested directly by a client or applied as a cascade from another aggregate's write.

There is exactly one exception. `DisclosureAcknowledgement` is an immutable legal artefact: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely. There is no endpoint that updates or deletes an acknowledgement — a seller who wishes to acknowledge a revised disclosure packet records a second acknowledgement.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. A request naming both a parent and a child identifier reports the parent's absence first, so a bad parent identifier always surfaces before a bad child identifier. Each aggregate has its own not-found code — `ERR_WHOLESALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKETING_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SELLER_LEAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEAL_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EARNEST_MONEY_DEPOSIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASH_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROOF_OF_FUNDS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DISPUTE_CASE_NOT_FOUND` — each carrying HTTP 404. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after all path identifiers, and among themselves in the order the endpoint declares its input fields.

Because the topology is flat, child records are discovered through their parent's identifier on a filtered list rather than through nested URLs. The earnest money deposit created by the signing cascade, for instance, is never returned by the signing call itself; it is found by listing deposits filtered on `purchase_contract_id`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Deadline Arithmetic

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every deadline, expiry, cooling-off window and interval-overlap check reads a single server-held logical instant, `current_utc`. On a fresh server that instant is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it moves only when a platform administrator sets it explicitly, supplying the new instant as `to_iso8601`; the response body of that operation is an object carrying `current_utc`. All temporal arithmetic is performed in whole seconds, UTC.

Advancing the clock has a real consequence rather than being a bookkeeping change: a proof-of-funds record whose expiry has been crossed stops counting toward its buyer's verified liquidity, so every `CashBuyer`'s `verified_funds_cents`, `available_funds_cents` and `buyer_tier` are recomputed when the clock moves.

Four derived instants govern the deal. The inspection deadline, `inspection_deadline_at`, is the moment the seller signed plus the contract's inspection period expressed in whole days, each day being twenty-four hours. The assignment cutoff, `assignment_deadline_at`, is the earliest permitted close minus forty-eight hours. A protected seller's cooling-off window ends seventy-two hours after the instant the lead was last screened. And a double-close title order's buffered window end, `buffered_window_end_at`, is its funding window's end plus forty-eight hours. Each of these boundaries has a defined inclusivity, spelled out where the workflow that depends on it is described; none of them is approximate.

### 2.6. Cents, Rates, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in cents. There are no floating-point money fields, no currency codes, and no minor-unit ambiguity: a field named with a `_cents` suffix is a whole number of cents and nothing else.

Two rounding operations appear throughout, and they are not interchangeable. **Rounding half up** means quantizing to a whole cent such that a value exactly halfway between two cents goes to the larger magnitude away from zero. **Flooring** means taking the mathematical floor toward negative infinity, which matters because several computed amounts are legitimately negative. Where a rule specifies one, the other is wrong and will produce off-by-one-cent results that the acceptance tests detect.

Three disciplines are non-negotiable. First, where a rule sums a list and then applies a rate, the sum is accumulated in full precision, the rate is applied once, and the rounding happens once at the end — never per element. Second, where a rule decomposes an amount into several rate-driven components, each component is rounded to a whole cent on its own **before** any of them is added or subtracted; collapsing the rates into a single combined rate and rounding once produces a different answer on amounts whose fractional cents straddle the half, and is incorrect. Third, one percentage in this system is not a money quantity at all: a lead's `equity_pct` is a number carried to exactly four decimal places, rounded half up, from a division performed once in full precision.

The only percentage-like value quantized to four decimal places is `equity_pct`; every other rate in the system is a constant applied to an integer amount and immediately quantized to cents.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Rejection on Parent Aggregates

Two create operations in this system depend on the client having an up-to-date picture of a parent aggregate, and both use optimistic concurrency control against that parent's `version`.

Creating an `AssignmentOffer` requires the non-optional field `expected_purchase_contract_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PurchaseContract`. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no side effects whatsoever are applied — no offer row, no deposit encumbrance, no assignment-status change. A contract's `version` advances on every write that alters the contract or its offer set: a signature, a structure election, a counter, an acceptance, a superseding offer, or a cascade. A buyer who read the contract, went away to think, and bid after any of those has by definition submitted a stale version.

Creating a `DealAnalysis` requires the non-optional field `expected_seller_lead_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `SellerLead`. If that value does not equal the lead's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEAD_VERSION` (HTTP 412) with no side effects. A lead's `version` advances on every protection screen and on every change to its contract-driven status, so an analyst who underwrote a lead before it was re-screened onto a different protection tier cannot build an analysis on a stale equity picture.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Where the scaffold of this API does not name an expected-version input, none is accepted and none is required.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

A single request can violate many rules at once, and which error the client sees is part of the contract. Rules are evaluated in strictly ascending rank; the first violation found is the one reported, and evaluation stops there. The ladder is as follows.

1. **`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`** (401) — `X-User-Role` absent, empty, or outside the six declared roles; or `X-User-ID` absent on an endpoint whose actor identity is recorded or compared. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`** (403) — the caller's role is not among those the endpoint permits. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`** (400) — malformed body: a value of the wrong type, a missing required field, an enum value outside its declared set, an integer outside its declared range, a datetime that is not ISO 8601 UTC, `closing_window_start_at` not strictly after `option_start_at`, `closing_window_end_at` not strictly after `closing_window_start_at`, `funding_window_end_at` not strictly after `funding_window_start_at`, `inspection_period_days` outside 1 through 30 inclusive, or a `repair_scope_items` element whose `high_cents` is below its `low_cents`. Applies to every endpoint, and runs before any database lookup — so a malformed body aimed at a nonexistent identifier still reports 400.
4. **`ERR_PAGINATION_OUT_OF_RANGE`** (400) — `limit` below 1, `limit` above 100, or `offset` below 0, on any of the fourteen list endpoints (the wholesaler roster and the lists of seller leads, purchase contracts, assignment offers, compliance reviews, marketing campaigns, cash buyers, deal analyses, earnest money deposits, title orders, dispute cases, disclosure acknowledgements, proof-of-funds records and assignment agreements).
5. **The aggregate-specific not-found codes** (404) — any templated path segment resolving to no row, resolved left to right.
6. **The aggregate-specific not-found codes** (404) — any foreign key supplied in a request body resolving to no row, resolved after path identifiers and in the order the endpoint declares its inputs. This applies to the ten body-carrying creates: campaign, lead, analysis, contract, buyer, offer, agreement, title order, compliance review and dispute case.
7. **`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`** (403) — the acting principal does not own the target aggregate: a wholesaler acting on another wholesaler's lead, campaign, contract, buyer, offer, agreement or analysis; a seller whose `X-User-ID` is not the target contract's `seller_lead_id`; a buyer whose `X-User-ID` is not the target offer's `cash_buyer_id`; or an offer whose buyer belongs to a different wholesaler than the contract. This guards nineteen operations: campaign publication and archival, the protection screen, analysis creation and finalization, contract creation, signing, structure election and cancellation, disclosure acknowledgement, proof-of-funds submission and verification, offer creation, counter, revise and accept, agreement execution, dispute opening, and dispute evidence submission.
8. **`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`** (403) — the adjudicating `X-User-ID` equals the review's `opened_by_user_id`. Guards compliance-review adjudication only, and applies even while the review is still `OPENED`.
9. **`ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_REQUIRED`** (403) — the funding `X-User-ID` equals the title order's `cleared_by_user_id`. Guards fund-and-close only.
10. **`ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`** (412) — `expected_purchase_contract_version` does not match the contract's `version`. Guards offer creation only.
11. **`ERR_STALE_LEAD_VERSION`** (412) — `expected_seller_lead_version` does not match the lead's `version`. Guards analysis creation only.
12. **`ERR_INVALID_STATE`** (409) — the residual state-machine guard: the target entity is in no state from which the requested trigger has an outgoing transition, and no more specific rule at ranks 13 through 22 covers the case. Where one of those more specific rules does cover the case, that rule is reported instead. The residual guard is what rejects re-publishing an already `PUBLISHED` campaign, archiving an `ARCHIVED` campaign, signing an already `SIGNED` contract, finalizing a `FINALIZED` or `SUPERSEDED` analysis, verifying an already-decided proof of funds, countering an `ACCEPTED` or terminal offer, revising an offer that is not `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER`, settling an already `SETTLED` or `TERMINATED_UPSTREAM` agreement, clearing a title order that is not `OPENED`, funding a title order that is not `CLEARED_TO_FUND`, adjudicating an already-adjudicated review or dispute, submitting evidence twice for the same party, and forfeiting a deposit that has already left `HELD`.
13. **`ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`** (409) — the governing contract's `contract_status` is `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`. This guards twelve operations: signing, structure election, disclosure acknowledgement, offer creation, counter, revise, accept, agreement execution, agreement settlement, title-order creation, clear-to-fund and fund-and-close. It is deliberately **not** applied to dispute opening, dispute evidence submission, dispute adjudication or earnest-money forfeiture — a seller must still be able to open and prosecute a dispute over a cancelled deal, and escrow must remain dispositive after cancellation.
14. **`ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_PUBLISHED`** (409) — the campaign named on a new lead is not in status `PUBLISHED`. Guards lead creation; omitting `marketing_campaign_id` skips the check entirely.
15. **`ERR_LEAD_NOT_SCREENED`** (409) — the referenced lead's status is `PROSPECT` or `CLOSED`. Guards contract creation. A lead already `UNDER_CONTRACT` passes this rule, which is why a non-overlapping backup contract is reachable and the occupancy conflict at rank 34 is the rule that actually rejects double encumbrance.
16. **`ERR_DEAL_ANALYSIS_NOT_FINALIZED`** (409) — the referenced analysis is not `FINALIZED`, or it references a different `seller_lead_id` than the contract does. Guards contract creation.
17. **`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED`** (409) — the governing contract is still in `DRAFT` and was never signed. Guards offer creation, title-order creation and earnest-money forfeiture.
18. **`ERR_POF_NOT_VERIFIED`** (409) — the bidding buyer has no proof-of-funds record in `VERIFIED` status at all. Guards offer acceptance, and is checked before the expiry rule at rank 33 so that an unverified proof reports as unverified rather than as expired.
19. **`ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACCEPTED`** (409) — the referenced offer's status is not `ACCEPTED`. Guards agreement execution, and outranks every fiscal check on the same request.
20. **`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ASSIGNED`** (409) — an `ASSIGNMENT`-structured title order is being cleared while the contract's `assignment_status` is not yet `ASSIGNED`. Guards clear-to-fund.
21. **`ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_HELD`** (409) — the earnest money deposit is not in the status the requested disposition needs. Forfeiture requires `HELD`; dispute adjudication requires `FORFEITED_TO_SELLER`. Guards forfeiture and dispute adjudication.
22. **`ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`** (409) — the dispute's `seller_evidence_ref` or `wholesaler_evidence_ref` is still null. Guards dispute adjudication; the fork-join is enforced, so both parties must have filed before an officer may rule.
23. **`ERR_WHOLESALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`** (403) — the governing wholesaler's `compliance_hold` is true. This guards seven operations: campaign creation, campaign publication, lead creation, the protection screen, contract creation, offer acceptance and agreement execution. A wholesaler on hold may still cancel contracts, cooperate with disputes and let existing closings settle.
24. **`ERR_INTENT_TO_ASSIGN_NOT_DISCLOSED`** (422) — publishing a campaign whose `discloses_intent_to_assign` is false.
25. **`ERR_LICENSURE_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`** (422) — publishing a campaign for a wholesaler whose `licensure_status` is `UNLICENSED` while the creative's `discloses_unlicensed_status` is false. A `LICENSED_AGENT` or `LICENSED_BROKER` need not carry that statement.
26. **`ERR_DNC_SCRUB_REQUIRED`** (422) — publishing a campaign whose `channel` is `SMS` or `COLD_CALL` while `dnc_scrubbed` is false. Conditional: the scrub is required for those two telephonic channels only.
27. **`ERR_DISCLOSURE_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_MISSING`** (422) — signing when no acknowledgement on the contract has `acknowledged_intent_to_assign` true, or the wholesaler is `UNLICENSED` and no acknowledgement has `acknowledged_unlicensed_status` true, or the acknowledgement's `disclosure_template_version` differs from the wholesaler's current `disclosure_template_version`.
28. **`ERR_EQUITY_SKIMMING_PROHIBITED`** (422) — contracting an `ELEVATED` or `PROTECTED` lead below the statutory equity floor. Conditional: `STANDARD`-tier leads are exempt. This safety rule deliberately outranks the fiscal offer ceiling at rank 40.
29. **`ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED`** (422) — monetising a `PROTECTED` lead's contract inside its cooling-off window. Guards offer acceptance and agreement execution, and is deliberately ranked above the assignment-deadline rule at rank 32, so a request that is both inside the cooling-off window and past the assignment cutoff reports the seller-protection failure.
30. **`ERR_UNLICENSED_BROKERAGE_ACTIVITY`** (422) — an `UNLICENSED` wholesaler countering an offer on a contract whose `assignment_rights_granted` is false, or executing an `ASSIGNMENT`-structured agreement on such a contract.
31. **`ERR_INSPECTION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`** (409) — a wholesaler-initiated cancellation at or after the inspection deadline, or a forfeiture strictly before it. Guards cancellation and forfeiture.
32. **`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_DEADLINE_PASSED`** (409) — the logical clock has reached or passed `assignment_deadline_at`. Guards offer acceptance and agreement execution.
33. **`ERR_PROOF_OF_FUNDS_EXPIRED`** (422) — every one of the buyer's `VERIFIED` proofs has an `expires_at` at or before the current instant, or a verification request targets a proof whose `expires_at` is at or before the current instant. Guards offer acceptance and proof-of-funds verification.
34. **`ERR_PROPERTY_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`** (409) — the candidate option-to-closing interval collides with a live contract on the same lead. Guards contract creation.
35. **`ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`** (409) — the candidate double-close funding window collides with at least as many live buffered windows as the wholesaler's capacity allows. Guards title-order creation.
36. **`ERR_STRUCTURE_MISMATCH`** (409) — the agreement's `structure` does not match the contract's `structure_election`, or that election is still `UNDECIDED`. Guards agreement execution.
37. **`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`** (409) — the offer's `negotiation_round` has reached the four-round cap. Guards counter and revise.
38. **`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_QUALIFYING_COMPS`** (422) — the analysis has fewer than three comparables passing the distance, recency and size filters. Guards analysis finalization.
39. **`ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_OFFER`** (409) — the same buyer already has a live offer on the same contract and the new request does not supersede it via `supersedes_offer_id`. Guards offer creation.
40. **`ERR_MAO_EXCEEDED`** (422) — `purchase_price_cents` exceeds the referenced analysis's `max_allowable_offer_cents`. Guards contract creation.
41. **`ERR_OFFER_BELOW_CONTRACT_PRICE`** (422) — `buyer_price_cents` is below the contract's `purchase_price_cents`. Guards offer creation and revise.
42. **`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VERIFIED_FUNDS`** (422) — `buyer_deposit_cents` exceeds the buyer's `available_funds_cents`, or `buyer_price_cents` exceeds the buyer's `verified_funds_cents`. Guards offer creation and revise.
43. **`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED`** (422) — the implied assignment fee exceeds the seller's protection-tier cap. Guards offer acceptance and agreement execution, and sits at the very bottom of the ladder, so any state or safety failure on the same request masks it.

### 2.9. Compliance Posture and the Hold Cascade

A wholesaler's regulatory standing is not a flag an operator sets; it is derived. `adverse_finding_count` is the number of that wholesaler's compliance reviews whose status is `ADJUDICATED_ADVERSE`. `compliance_hold` becomes true once `adverse_finding_count` reaches two, and while it is true the wholesaler may not originate new business or execute assignments. `executed_assignment_count` is the number of assignment agreements reachable through that wholesaler's contracts whose `settlement_status` is either `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` or `SETTLED` — an agreement terminated upstream by a cancellation stops counting.

The hold does not stop at the wholesaler record. When an adverse adjudication drives `compliance_hold` from false to true within a single request, four levels of effect are applied in that same request: the review itself is finalised; the wholesaler's adverse count is incremented and the hold flag set; every one of that wholesaler's campaigns then in `PUBLISHED` status is driven to `SUSPENDED` with its `suspension_count` incremented by one; and `lead_gen_blocked` is set true on every seller lead of that wholesaler, including every lead attached to a campaign that was just suspended. Each of those touched records has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as a direct write would.

A lead's `lead_gen_blocked` is true whenever the owning wholesaler's `compliance_hold` is true, or the lead names a `marketing_campaign_id` whose campaign status is `SUSPENDED`. Lifting the hold is a matter of the derived count falling below two; re-publishing a suspended campaign is possible only once the hold is gone, and re-publication recomputes `lead_gen_blocked` on every lead attributed to that campaign.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Dual Control

Two operations in this system deliberately require two distinct human principals, and both compare recorded `X-User-ID` values rather than roles.

A compliance review records the officer who opened it in `opened_by_user_id`, captured from the `X-User-ID` header and never accepted from the request body. The officer who adjudicates it is recorded in `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and must be a different principal; an attempt by the opening officer to rule on their own review fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A title order records the agent who cleared the file to fund in `cleared_by_user_id` and the agent who wired the funds in `funded_by_user_id`, both from the `X-User-ID` header. These must differ; the clearing agent attempting to also fund fails with `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_REQUIRED`. Both roles involved are `TitleAgent` — the constraint is on the principal, not on the role.

### 2.11. Escrow Conservation and Buyer Capital Reconciliation

Two arithmetic invariants must hold after every request that touches the relevant aggregate, without exception and without a reconciliation pass.

**Escrow conservation.** An earnest money deposit's `amount_cents` is fixed at signing and never mutates. Its four buckets — `held_cents`, `refunded_to_wholesaler_cents`, `forfeited_to_seller_cents` and `applied_at_closing_cents` — always sum to exactly `amount_cents`, and no bucket is ever negative. Every disposition is a transfer between buckets, never a mint and never a burn, and every applied disposition increments `disposition_event_count` by exactly one.

**Buyer capital reconciliation.** A cash buyer's `committed_deposit_cents` always equals the sum of `buyer_deposit_cents` across that buyer's offers in a live negotiation status, and `active_offer_count` always equals the count of those same offers. The live statuses are `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER`, `REVISED_BY_BUYER` and `ACCEPTED`; an offer that reaches `CONSUMED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `LAPSED` releases its deposit. `available_funds_cents` is `verified_funds_cents` less `committed_deposit_cents`, and while it is normally non-negative it may legitimately go negative if a verification is later downgraded.

### 2.12. Occupancy and Funding-Window Interval Semantics

Two interval-overlap checks exist in this system, and they use deliberately different semantics. Implementers who share one comparison routine between them will fail one of the two.

**Property occupancy** treats the interval from a contract's `option_start_at` to its `closing_window_end_at` as **fully closed**, with no buffer and no capacity allowance — a property may be under exactly one live contract at a time. Only contracts whose `contract_status` is `DRAFT`, `SIGNED` or `PENDING_CLOSE` occupy the property; a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` contract releases it immediately. A candidate conflicts with an existing occupant when the candidate's `option_start_at` is at or before the existing contract's `closing_window_end_at` **and** the candidate's `closing_window_end_at` is at or after the existing contract's `option_start_at`. Adjacency therefore collides: a candidate whose `option_start_at` equals an existing contract's `closing_window_end_at` to the second is a conflict, and one second later is accepted. Any conflict at all is rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`.

**Double-close funding windows** treat the interval from a title order's `funding_window_start_at` to its `funding_window_end_at` as **half-open on the right**, and additionally extend every interval by a forty-eight-hour trailing buffer to its `buffered_window_end_at`. Only title orders whose `structure` is `DOUBLE_CLOSE` and whose status is `OPENED` or `CLEARED_TO_FUND` occupy a window; `ASSIGNMENT`-structured orders consume no funding capacity at all. A candidate conflicts with an existing occupant when the candidate's `funding_window_start_at` is strictly before the existing order's `buffered_window_end_at` **and** the existing order's `funding_window_start_at` is strictly before the candidate's `buffered_window_end_at`. Touching edges do not conflict: a candidate starting exactly at an existing buffered end is accepted. Unlike property occupancy, a single conflict is tolerated — the request is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_CONFLICT` only once the number of conflicting occupants reaches the wholesaler's `funding_window_capacity`, which defaults to 1 and may be raised at registration to reflect a larger transactional-lender line.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Wholesaling Desk

A platform administrator brings a wholesaling entity onto the desk by registering it with its `legal_name`, the two-uppercase-letter `state_code` of its home state, and the `disclosure_template_version` tag identifying the intent-to-assign disclosure packet it serves to sellers. Two further inputs are optional at registration. `licensure_status` declares the entity's real-estate licensure posture and must be one of `UNLICENSED`, `LICENSED_AGENT` or `LICENSED_BROKER`, defaulting to `UNLICENSED` — the posture that triggers the strictest disclosure and brokerage-boundary rules. `funding_window_capacity` declares how many buffered double-close funding windows the entity's transactional-lender line can support at once; it must be at least 1 and defaults to 1. The `legal_name` is capped at 200 characters and must be non-empty; `disclosure_template_version` is capped at 32 characters and must be non-empty.

A freshly registered wholesaler starts clean: `adverse_finding_count` at 0, `compliance_hold` false, and `executed_assignment_count` at 0. All three are derived values that the desk maintains, never client inputs. Compliance staff and platform staff can page through the roster, narrowing it by `state_code` or by `licensure_status`; deal-level participants cannot read the roster at all, though any authenticated role may read a single wholesaler identified by its `wholesaler_id` in order to see the compliance counters that gate the deals they are party to.

### 3.2. Marketing Campaigns and the Disclosure Gates

Seller acquisition begins with a campaign. A wholesaler drafts one by naming its own `wholesaler_id`, the outbound `channel` — one of `DIRECT_MAIL`, `SMS`, `COLD_CALL`, `BANDIT_SIGN` or `ONLINE_AD` — the creative `headline` shown to prospective sellers (non-empty, at most 200 characters), and the `target_state_code` the campaign advertises into, again exactly two uppercase letters. Three boolean flags describe the creative's compliance content and all three default to false: `discloses_intent_to_assign` states that the advertiser intends to assign the resulting purchase contract rather than close on it, `discloses_unlicensed_status` states that the advertiser is not a licensed real-estate broker or agent, and `dnc_scrubbed` records that the target list was scrubbed against the do-not-call registry. A newly drafted campaign enters status `DRAFT` with `suspension_count` at 0 and a null `published_at`. A wholesaler already carrying a compliance hold cannot even draft a campaign — the attempt is rejected with `ERR_WHOLESALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`.

Publication is where the disclosure gates bite. Driving a campaign from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` requires four conditions beyond ownership: the owning wholesaler's `compliance_hold` must be false, `discloses_intent_to_assign` must be true, an `UNLICENSED` wholesaler must additionally have `discloses_unlicensed_status` true, and a campaign on the `SMS` or `COLD_CALL` channel must additionally have `dnc_scrubbed` true. Those four are reported, in that order, as `ERR_WHOLESALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`, `ERR_INTENT_TO_ASSIGN_NOT_DISCLOSED`, `ERR_LICENSURE_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` and `ERR_DNC_SCRUB_REQUIRED`. On success the campaign's `published_at` is stamped with the current logical instant. A campaign already in `PUBLISHED` cannot be published again; that attempt is the residual state guard and returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The `SUSPENDED → PUBLISHED` edge exists because suspension is not a death sentence. A campaign the hold cascade drove to `SUSPENDED` can be brought back to `PUBLISHED` — but only once the hold has been lifted, so the `compliance_hold` precondition is checked exactly as it is on the `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` edge, and so are the intent-to-assign, unlicensed-status and do-not-call preconditions with the same three error codes. Re-publication stamps `published_at` afresh, and it carries one extra effect the draft edge does not have: `lead_gen_blocked` is recomputed on every seller lead whose `marketing_campaign_id` points at this campaign, which unblocks leads that were blocked solely because their campaign was suspended.

Suspension itself is never client-driven. The `PUBLISHED → SUSPENDED` edge fires only as part of the compliance-hold cascade, when an adverse adjudication moves the owning wholesaler's `compliance_hold` from false to true inside the same request. That edge increments `suspension_count` by one and sets `lead_gen_blocked` true on every seller lead attributed to the campaign.

Archival is the terminal exit and is reachable from all three live statuses — `DRAFT → ARCHIVED`, `PUBLISHED → ARCHIVED` and `SUSPENDED → ARCHIVED` — with the single precondition that the campaign is not already `ARCHIVED`, and with no side effects at all. Campaigns can be listed and filtered by `wholesaler_id`, by `status` and by `channel`, or read one at a time by `marketing_campaign_id`.

### 3.3. Seller Leads and the Distressed-Seller Protection Screen

A lead records a distressed homeowner and their property. Its required inputs are the owning `wholesaler_id`, the `owner_name` of the record owner (non-empty, at most 200 characters), the `property_address` of the subject property (non-empty, at most 300 characters), the two-letter `state_code` in which the property sits, the as-is `estimated_market_value_cents` (strictly positive), and `recorded_senior_lien_cents`, the balance of recorded first-position liens, which may be zero but never negative. Four inputs are optional. `marketing_campaign_id` attributes the lead to a campaign and, when supplied, that campaign must be in status `PUBLISHED` at the moment the lead is created — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_PUBLISHED`; omitting the attribution skips that check entirely. `junior_lien_cents` and `delinquent_property_tax_cents` each default to 0 and each must be at least 0, capturing unrecorded junior debt and delinquent property tax respectively. `distress_flags` defaults to an empty array and holds distinct values drawn from `FORECLOSURE_NOTICE`, `PROBATE`, `TAX_DELINQUENT`, `CODE_VIOLATION`, `DIVORCE` and `RELOCATION`.

A new lead enters status `PROSPECT` with every equity field null — `screening_variant`, `net_equity_cents`, `equity_pct`, `protection_tier` and `screened_at` all begin unset. Its `lead_gen_blocked` is derived immediately from the owning wholesaler's hold and from the attributed campaign's status. Origination is itself gated: a wholesaler on a compliance hold cannot record a lead, and the attempt reports `ERR_WHOLESALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`.

**The protection screen** is the pivotal operation on a lead, and it may be run by the owning wholesaler or by a compliance officer. Its only input is `screening_variant`, which must be either `STATUTORY` or `EFFECTIVE`, and it is permitted only while `lead_gen_blocked` is false. The screen drives `PROSPECT → SCREENED` on first use, and is fully re-runnable thereafter as a `SCREENED → SCREENED` self-transition that overwrites the previously stored tier under the newly requested variant. Either way it writes `screening_variant`, `net_equity_cents`, `equity_pct`, `protection_tier` and `screened_at`, stamps `screened_at` with the current logical instant, and increments the lead's `version` — which is precisely why an analyst holding a pre-screen read of the lead will be rejected when they try to underwrite it.

The two variants disagree on what a distressed seller actually stands to lose. Under `STATUTORY`, net equity is the estimated market value less the recorded senior lien and nothing else, so equity — and therefore the protection tier — reads high. Under `EFFECTIVE`, net equity is the estimated market value less the recorded senior lien, less the junior lien balance, less the delinquent property tax, and less a seller closing-cost allowance of eight percent of the estimated market value, that allowance being rounded half up to a whole cent before it is subtracted. The `EFFECTIVE` variant can therefore drop the same lead a whole tier. `net_equity_cents` is an integer number of cents and may be negative.

From net equity the desk derives `equity_pct` as net equity divided by the estimated market value, the division performed once in full precision and the result quantized to exactly four decimal places, rounded half up; it too may be negative. The protection tier then follows from that percentage together with the number of distinct entries in `distress_flags`. A lead is `PROTECTED` when its equity percentage is at least 0.35 **and** it carries at least two distress flags. It is `ELEVATED` in either of two situations: the equity percentage is at least 0.35 with exactly one distress flag, or the equity percentage is below 0.35 with at least two distress flags. In every other case it is `STANDARD`. The equity threshold is inclusive: a lead landing exactly on 0.3500 counts as high equity.

Leads advance through the rest of their lifecycle under the control of contracts rather than of any lead-specific operation. `SCREENED → UNDER_CONTRACT` fires when a purchase contract is created against the lead, provided no other contract on the lead occupies an overlapping option-to-closing interval. `UNDER_CONTRACT → UNDER_CONTRACT` is the legitimate backup-contract case: a second, non-overlapping contract on the same lead leaves the status untouched and merely grows the set of intervals the property is occupied for. `UNDER_CONTRACT → SCREENED` fires when a contract on the lead is cancelled and that contract was the lead's only non-terminal contract, returning the lead to eligibility for a fresh contract — the deliberate cyclic re-entry in this lifecycle. `UNDER_CONTRACT → CLOSED` fires when the lead's contract reaches `CLOSED` within a funding request, and `CLOSED` is terminal: a closed lead can never be re-contracted.

Leads may be read individually by `seller_lead_id` and listed with filters on `wholesaler_id`, `marketing_campaign_id`, `status` and `protection_tier`.

### 3.4. Underwriting: Comparables, Repairs, and the Offer Ceiling

Before a wholesaler may contract with a screened seller, the deal must be underwritten. A deal analysis names the `seller_lead_id` of the subject property, the `subject_gla_sqft` — the gross living area of the subject house in square feet, strictly positive, which becomes the centre of the comparable size band — and two arrays. It also requires `expected_seller_lead_version`, the analyst's last observed `version` of the lead; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEAD_VERSION` and nothing is written.

The `comparables` array holds between zero and twenty after-repair-value comparable sales. Each element is an object with five members: `sale_price_cents` (strictly positive), `distance_miles` (a number, at least 0), `closed_days_ago` (an integer, at least 0), `gla_sqft` (a strictly positive integer) and `adjustment_cents` (an integer that may be negative, capturing condition and feature adjustments). The `repair_scope_items` array holds between zero and forty repair line items; each element carries a `code` string naming the trade, a `low_cents` figure of at least 0 and a `high_cents` figure that must be at least `low_cents` — an element inverting that relationship is a malformed body and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A new analysis enters status `DRAFT` with `valuation_variant`, `arv_cents`, `repair_estimate_cents`, `max_allowable_offer_cents` and `finalized_at` all null. One thing is computed at creation time: `qualifying_comp_count`. Creating a new analysis for a lead that already has one drives the prior analysis to `SUPERSEDED` — from `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` unconditionally, and from `FINALIZED → SUPERSEDED` only when no non-terminal purchase contract already cites the analysis being superseded; when a live contract does cite it, the create is rejected by the residual state guard as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because the price justification under a live contract may not be pulled out from underneath it.

**Which comparables qualify.** A comparable qualifies only when all three filters pass, and the filters are identical under both valuation variants. Its `distance_miles` must be at most 0.50 miles from the subject, inclusive. Its `closed_days_ago` must be at most 180 days, inclusive. And its size must sit within twenty percent of the subject's gross living area, inclusive — evaluated in exact integer arithmetic so the band edge is unambiguous: the absolute difference between the comparable's `gla_sqft` and `subject_gla_sqft`, multiplied by one hundred, must be at most `subject_gla_sqft` multiplied by twenty. `qualifying_comp_count` is the number of comparables passing all three, and it is recomputed whenever the analysis is created. Throughout the valuation, each qualifying comparable's **adjusted price** is its `sale_price_cents` plus its `adjustment_cents`, a figure that may itself be negative.

**Finalization** locks the price ceiling. It is available only from status `DRAFT`, requires at least three qualifying comparables — fewer reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_QUALIFYING_COMPS` — and takes a single input, `valuation_variant`, which must be `CONSERVATIVE` or `AGGRESSIVE`. On success the analysis moves `DRAFT → FINALIZED`, and `valuation_variant`, `arv_cents`, `repair_estimate_cents`, `max_allowable_offer_cents` and `finalized_at` are all written. The two variants disagree on every one of the three numeric outputs, so the same comparable set yields two different price ceilings depending on which variant the wholesaler finalizes under.

Under `CONSERVATIVE`, the after-repair value is the **median** of the qualifying adjusted prices. The qualifying comparables are ordered by adjusted price ascending; where two share an adjusted price the one with the smaller `closed_days_ago` comes first, and where they also share that, the one appearing earlier in the submitted array comes first. When the count of qualifying comparables is odd, `arv_cents` is the adjusted price of the middle element of that ordering. When the count is even, `arv_cents` is the sum of the two middle adjusted prices divided by two, taken to the floor.

Under `AGGRESSIVE`, the after-repair value is a **recency-weighted mean** of the qualifying adjusted prices. Each qualifying comparable's weight is one hundred and eighty-one minus its `closed_days_ago`, a quantity that is always at least 1 because a comparable older than 180 days cannot qualify. The numerator is the sum over qualifying comparables of adjusted price multiplied by weight, the denominator is the sum of the weights, both accumulated in full precision; the single division is performed once and the quotient rounded half up to a whole cent. There is no per-comparable rounding at any point.

Repairs diverge just as sharply. Under `CONSERVATIVE`, `repair_estimate_cents` is the sum of every line item's `high_cents`, multiplied by a contingency factor of 1.10, with the sum accumulated first, the multiplication applied once to that sum, and the result rounded half up to a whole cent exactly once — the contingency is emphatically **not** applied per line item. Under `AGGRESSIVE`, `repair_estimate_cents` is simply the sum of every line item's `low_cents`, with no contingency at all.

The ceiling itself is then assembled the same way under both variants, but from a different multiplier: seventy percent of the after-repair value under `CONSERVATIVE`, seventy-five percent under `AGGRESSIVE`. `max_allowable_offer_cents` is the after-repair value multiplied by that variant's multiplier and taken to the floor, then reduced by the repair estimate, then reduced by a minimum assignment margin of one million cents — ten thousand dollars the wholesaler must be left with for the deal to be worth doing. The result is stored exactly as computed and is **not** clamped at zero: a thin deal legitimately yields a negative ceiling, which then rejects every positive purchase price. Taken together, `CONSERVATIVE` produces the lower of the two ceilings — median value, high-side repairs inflated by contingency, the smaller multiplier — and `AGGRESSIVE` the higher.

Analyses are read individually by `deal_analysis_id` and listed with filters on `seller_lead_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Putting a Property Under Contract

A wholesaler puts a screened lead under contract by naming the `seller_lead_id` and the `deal_analysis_id` that justifies the price, then the commercial terms: `purchase_price_cents` payable to the seller (strictly positive), `earnest_money_cents` the wholesaler will place into escrow (strictly positive), `inspection_period_days` as a whole number of days between 1 and 30 inclusive, and the three instants that bound the deal. `option_start_at` is the moment the contract begins encumbering the property; `closing_window_start_at` is the earliest permitted close and must be strictly after `option_start_at`; `closing_window_end_at` is the latest permitted close and must be strictly after `closing_window_start_at`. One input is optional: `assignment_rights_granted` defaults to true and, when false, records a contract the seller refused to make assignable — a contract that can only ever be exited by double close.

Contract creation is the most heavily guarded operation on the desk, and the ordering of its guards is deliberate. A wholesaler on a compliance hold is stopped first, with `ERR_WHOLESALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`. The lead must have been screened: a lead still in `PROSPECT` or already `CLOSED` reports `ERR_LEAD_NOT_SCREENED`, while a lead already `UNDER_CONTRACT` passes this particular check because a non-overlapping backup contract is a legitimate thing to write. The analysis must be `FINALIZED` and must reference the same `seller_lead_id` as the contract; otherwise `ERR_DEAL_ANALYSIS_NOT_FINALIZED`. Then comes the equity-skimming protection, then the property-occupancy interval check, and only then the fiscal ceiling.

**The equity floor.** Where the lead's `protection_tier` is `ELEVATED` or `PROTECTED`, the seller must be left with at least sixty percent of their statutory equity above the recorded senior lien. Concretely: subtract `recorded_senior_lien_cents` from `estimated_market_value_cents`, multiply that statutory equity by 0.60, take the floor, and add `recorded_senior_lien_cents` back; a `purchase_price_cents` strictly below that figure is rejected with `ERR_EQUITY_SKIMMING_PROHIBITED`. `STANDARD`-tier leads are exempt from the rule entirely. This is a seller-protection rule and it deliberately outranks the underwriting ceiling, so a contract that both skims equity and busts the analysis ceiling reports the skimming failure.

**The fiscal ceiling.** Last of all, `purchase_price_cents` must not exceed the referenced analysis's `max_allowable_offer_cents`, or the create is rejected with `ERR_MAO_EXCEEDED`. Which ceiling binds depends entirely on the variant the analysis was finalized under — the same comparables finalized `AGGRESSIVE` may permit a price that the `CONSERVATIVE` finalization forbids.

A successful create initialises all three of the contract's independent status dimensions: `contract_status` to `DRAFT`, `assignment_status` to `NOT_MARKETED` and `structure_election` to `UNDECIDED`. These three dimensions advance independently of one another and none of them constrains another's transitions except where a precondition explicitly says so. The create also derives `assignment_deadline_at` as `closing_window_start_at` less forty-eight hours, and moves the lead to `UNDER_CONTRACT`. `signed_at`, `inspection_deadline_at`, the four double-close funding figures, `cancellation_reason` and `cancelled_at` all begin null.

**The disclosure acknowledgement.** Before the seller can sign, the seller must acknowledge the disclosures, and this is one of the few operations the `Seller` role performs exclusively. The acknowledgement names the `purchase_contract_id` it attaches to, the `disclosure_template_version` the seller is acknowledging, the two booleans `acknowledged_intent_to_assign` and `acknowledged_unlicensed_status`, and a `signature_ref` — an opaque reference to the stored signature artefact, non-empty and at most 120 characters. The acknowledged template version must equal the owning wholesaler's current `disclosure_template_version`, and the record's `acknowledged_at` is stamped from the logical clock. An acknowledgement is immutable: it carries `id` and `created_at` and nothing else in the way of common properties, there is no update or delete for it, and a seller correcting a mistake files a second one. Acknowledgements are read individually by `disclosure_acknowledgement_id` and listed with a filter on `purchase_contract_id`.

**Signing.** The seller drives `contract_status` from `DRAFT` to `SIGNED`, and it is the seller alone who may do so. The signature is refused unless some acknowledgement on this contract has `acknowledged_intent_to_assign` true, and — where the owning wholesaler's `licensure_status` is `UNLICENSED` — unless some acknowledgement also has `acknowledged_unlicensed_status` true, and unless the acknowledgement's `disclosure_template_version` matches the wholesaler's current one. Any of those three failures reports `ERR_DISCLOSURE_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_MISSING`. Signing a contract that is not in `DRAFT` is the residual state guard, `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and signing a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` contract reports `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL` instead.

Signing does three things beyond the status change. It stamps `signed_at` with the current logical instant. It derives `inspection_deadline_at` as `signed_at` plus `inspection_period_days` twenty-four-hour days — a half-open window, so cancellation by the wholesaler remains available at every instant strictly before that deadline and forfeiture becomes available at exactly that deadline. And it creates the contract's earnest money deposit, whose `purchase_contract_id` points back at the contract, whose `amount_cents` is copied from the contract's `earnest_money_cents` and never afterwards mutated, whose `status` is `HELD`, whose `held_cents` equals `amount_cents` with the other three buckets at zero, and whose `disposition_event_count` is 0. The deposit's `amount_cents` is never client-supplied. The contract's `version` increments. Because the signing response returns the contract and not the deposit, the deposit is discovered by listing deposits filtered on `purchase_contract_id` — that list, optionally narrowed by `status`, is the only way to find it — or read directly once its `earnest_money_deposit_id` is known.

Contracts are read individually by `purchase_contract_id`, which returns all three status dimensions and the double-close schedule, and listed with filters on `seller_lead_id`, `deal_analysis_id`, `contract_status`, `assignment_status` and `structure_election`.

### 3.6. The Buyer List: Proof of Funds and Committed Capital

A wholesaler curates its own buyer list. Adding a cash buyer takes the owning `wholesaler_id`, the buyer's `legal_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters) and a `contact_email` for offer correspondence, which must be non-empty, must contain an at-sign, and must be at most 200 characters. A new buyer starts with `verified_funds_cents` at 0, `committed_deposit_cents` at 0, `available_funds_cents` at 0, `active_offer_count` at 0 and `buyer_tier` at `SUSPENDED` — no verified liquidity means no bidding capacity. Buyers are read individually by `cash_buyer_id` and listed with filters on `wholesaler_id` and `buyer_tier`.

Liquidity is evidenced by proof-of-funds records, which the buyer uploads themselves. Each names the `cash_buyer_id` it belongs to, a `stated_funds_cents` figure the buyer claims is available (strictly positive), a `document_ref` pointing at the uploaded bank letter or statement (non-empty, at most 120 characters), and an `expires_at` instant after which the document is stale. A new record enters `verification_status` `SUBMITTED` and contributes nothing whatsoever to the buyer's verified funds; `verified_funds_cents`, `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id` on the record are all null until a decision is recorded. Records are read individually by `proof_of_funds_id` and listed with filters on `cash_buyer_id` and `verification_status`.

Verification is the wholesaler's job, not the buyer's. The decision takes `verification_decision` and two optional inputs: `verified_funds_cents`, which lets the verifier record a figure lower than the buyer claimed, and `verification_note` for the verifier's remarks. Approving a record drives `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`, and requires both that the record is still `SUBMITTED` and that its `expires_at` is strictly after the current logical instant — a proof whose expiry equals the current instant is already stale, and the attempt reports `ERR_PROOF_OF_FUNDS_EXPIRED`. On approval the record's `verified_funds_cents` is set to the supplied override, or, when no override is supplied, defaults to `stated_funds_cents`; `verified_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `verified_by_user_id` records the verifying wholesaler user's `X-User-ID`. Rejecting a record drives `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, requires only that the record is still `SUBMITTED`, and leaves `verified_funds_cents` on the record null. Verifying a record that has already been decided one way or the other is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both outcomes recompute the owning buyer's `verified_funds_cents`, `available_funds_cents` and `buyer_tier`.

**How buyer capital is reconciled.** A buyer's `verified_funds_cents` is the **largest** `verified_funds_cents` among that buyer's proof-of-funds records that are in `VERIFIED` status and whose `expires_at` is strictly after the current logical instant; where no such record exists it is 0. Proofs do not stack — two verified letters for two hundred thousand dollars each give the buyer two hundred thousand dollars of capacity, not four hundred. The buyer's `committed_deposit_cents` is the sum of `buyer_deposit_cents` across their offers in a live negotiation status, `active_offer_count` is the count of those same offers, and `available_funds_cents` is verified funds less committed deposits. `buyer_tier` is then `SUSPENDED` whenever `verified_funds_cents` is 0; otherwise it is `PRIORITY` when `available_funds_cents` is at least twenty-five million cents — two hundred and fifty thousand dollars, the threshold being inclusive — and `STANDARD` in every other case. The tier drives no pricing; it exists so that the desk can assert, after any cascade, that the buyer's capital picture is still coherent.

All four of these figures are recomputed together on every event that could change them: a proof-of-funds verification or rejection, every offer creation, revision, acceptance, withdrawal and lapse, the contract-cancellation cascade, and the logical clock advancing past a proof's expiry.

### 3.7. Assignment Offers: Bidding, Negotiation, and Acceptance

A cash buyer bids to take over a signed contract by submitting an offer naming the `purchase_contract_id` being bid on, their own `cash_buyer_id`, the `buyer_price_cents` they will pay in total (strictly positive), the `buyer_deposit_cents` they commit non-refundably alongside the bid (strictly positive), and `expected_purchase_contract_version`. Optionally the offer names `supersedes_offer_id`, the identifier of one of the buyer's own earlier offers on the same contract that this bid replaces; when supplied, the referenced offer must belong to the same contract and the same buyer.

Ordering of the guards on offer creation is worth stating plainly, because a realistic bid can trip several. Ownership is checked first: the bidding buyer must sit on the same wholesaler's list as the contract's lead, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Then the version check — a mismatch on `expected_purchase_contract_version` reports `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` and writes nothing. Then contract state: a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` contract reports `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`, and a contract still in `DRAFT` reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED` — nothing may be marketed before the seller has signed. Then the duplicate check: a buyer who already has a live offer on this contract and does not name it in `supersedes_offer_id` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_OFFER`, because a buyer improves a bid by superseding, not by stacking. Then the price floor: `buyer_price_cents` below the contract's `purchase_price_cents` reports `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_CONTRACT_PRICE`, since an assignment can never be sold for less than the wholesaler owes the seller. Last, the funding check: `buyer_deposit_cents` exceeding the buyer's `available_funds_cents`, or `buyer_price_cents` exceeding the buyer's `verified_funds_cents`, reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VERIFIED_FUNDS`. That last check is deliberately aggregate — each individual offer may look affordable while the buyer's live deposits collectively exceed their verified liquidity.

A superseding bid changes the arithmetic of that funding check in a specific way. The superseded offer is driven to `WITHDRAWN` **first**, releasing its `buyer_deposit_cents` from the buyer's `committed_deposit_cents`, and the replacement bid is then measured against the freed capacity. The `WITHDRAWN` transition is reachable from `SUBMITTED`, from `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER` and from `REVISED_BY_BUYER`, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

A successful offer enters status `SUBMITTED` with `negotiation_round` at 1, `countered_price_cents` null, `accepted_at` null and `assignment_fee_cents` null. It recomputes the buyer's `committed_deposit_cents`, `available_funds_cents`, `buyer_tier` and `active_offer_count`. It also touches the contract: the first offer drives `assignment_status` from `NOT_MARKETED` to `MARKETED`, which requires the contract's `contract_status` to be `SIGNED` or `PENDING_CLOSE` and requires either `assignment_rights_granted` to be true or `structure_election` to be `DOUBLE_CLOSE_ELECTED` — a contract with no assignment rights and no double-close election has no marketing path, and the attempt is refused by the residual state guard as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every offer creation increments the contract's `version`, which is the parent write that makes any other client's cached read stale.

**The negotiation cycle.** Price is settled by a bounded counter-and-revise loop capped at four rounds. The wholesaler counters by supplying `countered_price_cents`, which drives the offer to `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER` from either `SUBMITTED` or `REVISED_BY_BUYER`, records the countered price on the offer, and increments `negotiation_round` by one. Countering requires `negotiation_round` to be strictly below four — at four it reports `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` — and requires the owning contract not to be in a terminal `contract_status`. Countering an `ACCEPTED` offer, or one already `CONSUMED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `LAPSED`, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

One licensure boundary lives on the counter. Where the owning wholesaler's `licensure_status` is `UNLICENSED` and the contract's `assignment_rights_granted` is false, the wholesaler countering a buyer's price would be negotiating the resale on the seller's behalf without a licence, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_UNLICENSED_BROKERAGE_ACTIVITY`. The compliant path for such a deal is a double close, where the wholesaler buys and resells in its own name.

The buyer answers a counter by revising, which drives `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER → REVISED_BY_BUYER`. Revision supplies fresh `buyer_price_cents` and `buyer_deposit_cents`, which overwrite the values on the offer, and it increments `negotiation_round` by one. Revision is available only from `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER` — revising an offer in any other status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and only while `negotiation_round` is strictly below four, the fourth round reporting `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. The revised figures are re-checked against exactly the same two fiscal rules the original bid faced: the new `buyer_price_cents` must not fall below the contract's `purchase_price_cents`, on pain of `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_CONTRACT_PRICE`, and the new `buyer_deposit_cents` must fit within the buyer's `available_funds_cents` while the new `buyer_price_cents` must fit within their `verified_funds_cents`, on pain of `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VERIFIED_FUNDS`. Revision then recomputes the buyer's `committed_deposit_cents`, `available_funds_cents` and `buyer_tier`. Because the cycle can run `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER → REVISED_BY_BUYER → COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER` and round again, the round counter — not the status — is what terminates it; acceptance remains available on a fourth-round offer even though no further counter or revision is.

**Acceptance.** The wholesaler accepts, driving the offer to `ACCEPTED` from `SUBMITTED`, from `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER` or from `REVISED_BY_BUYER`. All three edges carry identical preconditions and identical effects. The acceptance takes no request body. Its guards, in the order they are reported: a wholesaler on a compliance hold is stopped with `ERR_WHOLESALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`; a bidding buyer with no `VERIFIED` proof-of-funds record at all reports `ERR_POF_NOT_VERIFIED`; a `PROTECTED` lead still inside its cooling-off window reports `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED`; the assignment cutoff having been reached or passed reports `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_DEADLINE_PASSED`; a buyer whose only `VERIFIED` proofs have all expired reports `ERR_PROOF_OF_FUNDS_EXPIRED`; and an implied fee above the seller's protection-tier cap reports `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The unverified-proof check sitting above the expiry check is intentional, so that a buyer who never got verified hears that rather than being told their document is stale. The cooling-off check sitting above the deadline check is likewise intentional: a request that is both inside a protected seller's cooling-off window and past the assignment cutoff reports the seller-protection failure.

The temporal boundaries here are exact. Acceptance is permitted at every instant strictly before `assignment_deadline_at`, which is forty-eight hours before the earliest permitted close, and refused from that instant onward. Where the lead's `protection_tier` is `PROTECTED`, acceptance is refused at every instant strictly before seventy-two hours after the lead's `screened_at`, and permitted from that instant onward. And a proof of funds counts only while its `expires_at` is strictly after the current instant.

**The protection-tier fee cap.** The implied assignment fee is the offer's `buyer_price_cents` less the contract's `purchase_price_cents`. The cap is a share of the contract price that tightens as the seller's protection tier rises: twelve percent for a `STANDARD` lead, eight percent for `ELEVATED`, five percent for `PROTECTED`. The cap in cents is the contract's `purchase_price_cents` multiplied by that share and taken to the floor, and the operation is rejected only when the implied fee is **strictly greater** than the cap — a fee landing exactly on the cap is permitted. The same cap is re-checked when the agreement is executed.

Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock and writes `assignment_fee_cents` on the offer as the implied fee fixed at that moment. It then lapses the competition: every sibling offer on the same contract still in a live status is driven to `LAPSED`, releasing each of their deposits and recomputing each affected buyer's committed capital and tier. Where the contract has elected a double close, acceptance also re-evaluates the contract's `double_close_spread_cents`.

Offers are read individually by `assignment_offer_id` and listed with filters on `purchase_contract_id`, on `cash_buyer_id`, on the self-referential `supersedes_offer_id`, and on `status`.

### 3.8. Structuring the Exit: Assignment, Double Close, and Title

`structure_election` is the third and most freely mutable of the contract's status dimensions, and the wholesaler drives it by supplying `structure_election` as the request input. Electing `ASSIGNMENT_ELECTED` from `UNDECIDED` requires the contract not to be `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` and requires `assignment_rights_granted` to be true — the seller must actually have granted the right being exercised. Electing `DOUBLE_CLOSE_ELECTED` from `UNDECIDED` requires only that the contract is not `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, which is why the double close is the compliant exit for a contract with no assignment rights. Every election increments the contract's `version`.

The election is re-elective in both directions while nothing has been papered. Moving `ASSIGNMENT_ELECTED → DOUBLE_CLOSE_ELECTED` requires only that `assignment_status` is not yet `ASSIGNED`. Moving `DOUBLE_CLOSE_ELECTED → ASSIGNMENT_ELECTED` requires three things: `assignment_status` is not yet `ASSIGNED`, `assignment_rights_granted` is true, and no title order on this contract is in `OPENED` or `CLEARED_TO_FUND` status with `structure` `DOUBLE_CLOSE` — a funding window already reserved with a lender may not be silently abandoned. Reverting to `ASSIGNMENT_ELECTED` clears `funding_base_cents`, `transactional_lender_fee_cents`, `total_funding_required_cents` and `double_close_spread_cents` all back to null.

**Sizing the double close.** Electing `DOUBLE_CLOSE_ELECTED` — from `UNDECIDED` or from `ASSIGNMENT_ELECTED` — runs the funding schedule. Step one grosses up the purchase price with closing and recording costs: `funding_base_cents` is `purchase_price_cents` plus two point one percent of `purchase_price_cents`, that percentage product rounded half up to a whole cent before it is added. Step two selects the transactional lender's fee tier **from the grossed-up base**, never from the raw purchase price and never from the final total — this is the single most commonly mis-implemented part of the schedule, and it means a contract whose purchase price sits just under a tier edge is nevertheless priced in the next tier up. A base below fifteen million cents (one hundred and fifty thousand dollars) sits in the first tier, at a rate of three percent with a minimum fee of twenty-five thousand cents. A base from fifteen million cents up to but not including thirty million cents (three hundred thousand dollars) sits in the second tier, at two and a quarter percent with a minimum fee of forty-five thousand cents. A base at or above thirty million cents sits in the third tier, at one and three-quarter percent with a minimum fee of seventy thousand cents. The tier edges are half-open: a base landing exactly on fifteen million cents is in the second tier, and one landing exactly on thirty million cents is in the third.

Step three applies the tier: `transactional_lender_fee_cents` is the greater of that tier's minimum fee and the base multiplied by the tier rate, the product rounded half up to a whole cent **before** the minimum-fee floor is applied. Step four totals: `total_funding_required_cents` is `funding_base_cents` plus `transactional_lender_fee_cents`. Step five is the spread, and it stays null until an offer on this contract reaches `ACCEPTED`: `double_close_spread_cents` is the accepted offer's `buyer_price_cents` less `total_funding_required_cents`. It may be negative, in which case the double close is underwater — the desk records that fact and does not reject it.

**Executing the paperwork.** The wholesaler papers the exit by executing an agreement naming the `assignment_offer_id` it papers and the `structure` being papered, which must be `ASSIGNMENT` or `DOUBLE_CLOSE`. Exactly one agreement exists per offer. The guards, in order: a wholesaler on a compliance hold reports `ERR_WHOLESALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`; a referenced offer whose status is not `ACCEPTED` reports `ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACCEPTED`, and this outranks every fiscal check on the same request; a `PROTECTED` lead inside its cooling-off window reports `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED`; an `UNLICENSED` wholesaler papering an `ASSIGNMENT`-structured agreement on a contract whose `assignment_rights_granted` is false reports `ERR_UNLICENSED_BROKERAGE_ACTIVITY`; the assignment cutoff having been reached reports `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_DEADLINE_PASSED`; a `structure` that does not match the contract's `structure_election` exactly — `ASSIGNMENT` requiring `ASSIGNMENT_ELECTED`, `DOUBLE_CLOSE` requiring `DOUBLE_CLOSE_ELECTED`, and a still-`UNDECIDED` election matching neither — reports `ERR_STRUCTURE_MISMATCH`; and an implied fee above the protection-tier cap reports `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Executing against an offer that already has an agreement is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A successful execution copies `assignment_fee_cents` from the accepted offer onto the agreement — this copied figure is the base of every settlement component — stamps `executed_at` from the logical clock, and enters `settlement_status` `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` with `title_holdback_cents`, `state_transfer_surcharge_cents`, `marketing_cost_recovery_cents` and `wholesaler_net_cents` all at 0 and `settlement_reference` and `settled_at` null. It drives the offer from `ACCEPTED` to `CONSUMED`, drives the contract's `assignment_status` from `MARKETED` to `ASSIGNED`, recomputes `double_close_spread_cents` where the contract elected a double close, and increments the wholesaler's `executed_assignment_count`.

**Settlement.** A title agent settles the agreement by supplying a `settlement_reference`, which drives `PENDING_SETTLEMENT → SETTLED` and stamps `settled_at`. Settlement requires the agreement to still be `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` — an already `SETTLED` or `TERMINATED_UPSTREAM` agreement reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and requires the owning contract not to be `CANCELLED`, which surfaces as `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`. Settlement splits the fee into three deduction components and a residual net. `title_holdback_cents` is three and a quarter percent of `assignment_fee_cents`; `state_transfer_surcharge_cents` is three-quarters of one percent of it; `marketing_cost_recovery_cents` is one and fifty-five hundredths percent of it. **Each of those three products is rounded half up to a whole cent on its own, before any of them is subtracted.** `wholesaler_net_cents` is then `assignment_fee_cents` less the three rounded components. Collapsing the three rates into a single five and fifty-five hundredths percent rate and rounding once produces a different, wrong answer on fee amounts whose fractional cents straddle the half. Agreements are read individually by `assignment_agreement_id` and listed with filters on `assignment_offer_id` and `settlement_status`.

**Title coordination.** The wholesaler opens escrow by creating a title order naming the `purchase_contract_id` it coordinates, the `title_company_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), an `escrow_officer_ref` for the officer handling the file (non-empty, at most 120 characters), and the wired-funds window the transactional lender must reserve: `funding_window_start_at` and `funding_window_end_at`, the latter strictly after the former. The order's `structure` is copied from the contract's `structure_election` at creation — it is never client-supplied — and for a `DOUBLE_CLOSE` order the desk computes `buffered_window_end_at` as `funding_window_end_at` plus forty-eight hours. Creation requires the contract to have been signed, a `DRAFT` contract reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED`, and a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` one reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`; then the buffered funding window is checked against the wholesaler's `funding_window_capacity`, with a violation reported as `ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A new order enters status `OPENED` with `cleared_by_user_id` and `funded_by_user_id` null. Orders are read individually by `title_order_id` and listed with filters on `purchase_contract_id`, `status` and `structure`.

Closing runs under dual control across two distinct title-agent principals. The first agent clears the file, driving `OPENED → CLEARED_TO_FUND`; this requires the order to still be `OPENED` and requires the structure-appropriate readiness — for an `ASSIGNMENT`-structured order the contract's `assignment_status` must already be `ASSIGNED`, and clearing one that is not reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ASSIGNED`, while for a `DOUBLE_CLOSE`-structured order the contract's `structure_election` must be `DOUBLE_CLOSE_ELECTED`. Clearing records the acting `X-User-ID` in `cleared_by_user_id` and drives the contract's `contract_status` from `SIGNED` to `PENDING_CLOSE`.

The second agent funds and closes, driving `CLEARED_TO_FUND → FUNDED_AND_CLOSED`. This requires the order to be in `CLEARED_TO_FUND` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the recorded `cleared_by_user_id`, the same principal attempting both halves being rejected with `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_REQUIRED`. Funding records `funded_by_user_id`, drives the contract's `contract_status` from `PENDING_CLOSE` to `CLOSED`, drives the owning lead's status to `CLOSED`, and moves the earnest money deposit from `HELD` to `APPLIED_AT_CLOSING` by transferring the whole of `held_cents` into `applied_at_closing_cents` and incrementing `disposition_event_count` by one. Both `FUNDED_AND_CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a title order.

### 3.9. Unwinding a Deal: Cancellation, Forfeiture, Disputes, and Compliance

Not every deal closes, and the unwinding paths carry more cascading consequence than any other part of the desk. Three roles may cancel a contract — the `Wholesaler`, the `Seller`, and a `ComplianceOfficer` — and all three supply a single input, `reason`, which is recorded on the contract as `cancellation_reason`, alongside a `cancelled_at` stamped from the logical clock. Cancellation is reachable from `DRAFT`, from `SIGNED` and from `PENDING_CLOSE`; a contract already `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` cannot be cancelled again.

The one time-box on cancellation applies to the wholesaler only. A wholesaler may cancel at any instant **strictly before** `inspection_deadline_at` and is refused from that instant onward with `ERR_INSPECTION_PERIOD_EXPIRED` — the inspection window is half-open on the right, so the last instant inside it still permits cancellation. Seller-initiated and compliance-officer-initiated cancellation is not time-boxed at all; a seller may walk away, and a regulator may unwind a deal, at any point before it closes.

Cancelling a contract that was never signed is simple: `contract_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, the reason and instant are recorded, and the owning lead returns to `SCREENED`. Cancelling a `SIGNED` or `PENDING_CLOSE` contract runs a four-level cascade, and both edges run exactly the same one. Level one is the contract itself. Level two lapses the market: every offer on this contract in `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED_BY_WHOLESALER`, `REVISED_BY_BUYER` or `ACCEPTED` status is driven to `LAPSED`. Level three terminates the paperwork: every agreement whose offer just lapsed and whose `settlement_status` is `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` is driven to `TERMINATED_UPSTREAM`, with `title_holdback_cents`, `state_transfer_surcharge_cents`, `marketing_cost_recovery_cents` and `wholesaler_net_cents` all zeroed. Level four restores buyer capital: `committed_deposit_cents`, `available_funds_cents`, `buyer_tier` and `active_offer_count` are recomputed on every affected cash buyer, since the lapsed offers no longer encumber anyone's funds.

Two further effects ride alongside the cascade. The earnest money deposit moves from `HELD` to `REFUNDED_TO_WHOLESALER`, transferring the whole of `held_cents` into `refunded_to_wholesaler_cents` and incrementing `disposition_event_count` by one — a wholesaler who cancels inside its inspection period gets its money back. And every title order on the contract in `OPENED` or `CLEARED_TO_FUND` status is driven to `CANCELLED`, releasing its buffered funding window so that a later double-close order may reuse the capacity. Finally, the owning lead returns from `UNDER_CONTRACT` to `SCREENED` when the cancelled contract was the lead's only non-terminal contract; where a non-overlapping backup contract remains live, the lead stays `UNDER_CONTRACT`.

**Forfeiture** is the mirror image of cancellation, and it belongs to the title agent. Supplying a `forfeiture_reason`, the agent drives the deposit from `HELD` to `FORFEITED_TO_SELLER`, transferring the whole of `held_cents` into `forfeited_to_seller_cents` and incrementing `disposition_event_count`. Forfeiture requires a deposit still in `HELD` — anything else reports `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_HELD` — requires the governing contract to have been signed, reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED` otherwise, and becomes available only once the logical clock has reached or passed `inspection_deadline_at`; a forfeiture attempted strictly before that instant reports `ERR_INSPECTION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`. Note the deliberate asymmetry with cancellation: at the exact instant of the deadline, wholesaler cancellation has just closed and forfeiture has just opened. Forfeiture is one of the four operations that remain available on a cancelled contract, because escrow must stay dispositive after the deal dies.

**Seller disputes.** A seller opens a dispute over the deal by naming the `purchase_contract_id`, the `dispute_basis` — one of `EARNEST_MONEY_FORFEITURE`, `UNDISCLOSED_ASSIGNMENT`, `EQUITY_SKIMMING` or `FAILURE_TO_CLOSE` — and the `claimed_amount_cents` they assert (strictly positive). The case enters status `OPENED` with `seller_evidence_ref` and `wholesaler_evidence_ref` both null and `award`, `award_seller_cents`, `award_wholesaler_cents` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` all null. Opening, prosecuting and ruling on a dispute all remain available after the contract has been cancelled or closed — the terminal-contract guard is deliberately not applied to any of them.

The dispute lifecycle is a two-branch fork-join over party evidence. Either party — the `Seller` or the `Wholesaler` — files its bundle by supplying `evidence_ref`, and the desk records that reference into `seller_evidence_ref` or into `wholesaler_evidence_ref` according to which role is acting; that is the fork. The first filing drives `OPENED → EVIDENCE_PARTIAL`, and requires that the submitting party has not already filed — a second filing by the same party is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The second filing, made while the other party's reference is already present, drives `EVIDENCE_PARTIAL → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`; that is the join, and it is defined by both references being non-null.

A compliance officer then rules, supplying `award` and a `rationale`, driving `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → ADJUDICATED`. Two preconditions guard the ruling. Both evidence references must be present — a null on either side reports `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` — and the contract's earnest money deposit must not be in `HELD` or `APPLIED_AT_CLOSING` status, reporting `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_HELD` when it is, because there is nothing to redistribute until the money has actually been forfeited. In practice adjudication operates on a deposit in `FORFEITED_TO_SELLER`. The officer's `X-User-ID` is recorded as the dispute's `adjudicated_by_user_id`.

The `award` decides how the escrow is re-bucketed, and all three outcomes preserve the conservation invariant exactly. Under `SELLER_KEEPS_EARNEST_MONEY`, the buckets are left untouched, `award_seller_cents` is set to the current `forfeited_to_seller_cents` and `award_wholesaler_cents` to 0. Under `REFUND_TO_WHOLESALER`, the whole of `forfeited_to_seller_cents` is transferred into `refunded_to_wholesaler_cents` and `forfeited_to_seller_cents` is set to 0, `award_seller_cents` is 0 and `award_wholesaler_cents` is the deposit's full `amount_cents`; the deposit's own status moves `FORFEITED_TO_SELLER → REFUNDED_TO_WHOLESALER`. Under `SPLIT_50_50`, the seller's share is the deposit's `amount_cents` divided by two and taken to the floor; `forfeited_to_seller_cents` is set to that share, `refunded_to_wholesaler_cents` is set to `amount_cents` less that share, `award_seller_cents` equals the share and `award_wholesaler_cents` equals `amount_cents` less the share — so an odd deposit amount leaves the extra cent with the wholesaler. The deposit's status moves `FORFEITED_TO_SELLER → SPLIT_SETTLED`, which is terminal. Every one of the three outcomes increments `disposition_event_count` by exactly one. Disputes are read individually by `dispute_case_id` and listed with filters on `purchase_contract_id`, `status` and `dispute_basis`.

**Compliance reviews.** A compliance officer opens a review of a wholesaler by naming the `wholesaler_id` under review, the `review_type` — one of `LICENSURE_BOUNDARY`, `MARKETING_COMPLIANCE` or `EQUITY_SKIMMING` — and an `allegation_note` describing the allegation (non-empty, at most 1000 characters). One input is optional: `subject_seller_lead_id` scopes the review to a single lead and is populated for lead-specific allegations such as equity skimming, while review types concerning the wholesaler as a whole leave it null. The opening officer's `X-User-ID` is captured into `opened_by_user_id` and is never accepted from the request body. The review enters status `OPENED` with `finding`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_at` all null. Reviews are read individually by `compliance_review_id` and listed with filters on `wholesaler_id`, `subject_seller_lead_id`, `status` and `review_type`.

A **second** compliance officer adjudicates, supplying `decision`, `finding` and a `rationale`. The review must still be `OPENED` and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `opened_by_user_id`, the opening officer's own attempt being rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even while the review is still open. A `decision` of `CLEAR` paired with a `finding` of `NO_VIOLATION` drives `OPENED → ADJUDICATED_CLEARED`, setting `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_at` and nothing more. A `decision` of `ADVERSE` paired with a `finding` other than `NO_VIOLATION` — one of `UNLICENSED_BROKERAGE_ACTIVITY`, `DISCLOSURE_FAILURE` or `EQUITY_SKIMMING` — drives `OPENED → ADJUDICATED_ADVERSE` and sets off the four-level hold cascade described in §2.9: the finding, adjudicating officer and instant are recorded; the wholesaler's `adverse_finding_count` is incremented; when that count reaches two, `compliance_hold` becomes true; while the hold is true, every `PUBLISHED` campaign of that wholesaler is driven to `SUSPENDED` with its `suspension_count` incremented; and `lead_gen_blocked` is set true on every seller lead attached to a suspended campaign and on every seller lead of that wholesaler. Both adjudicated statuses are terminal, and re-adjudicating is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Driving the clock.** Because every deadline, expiry, cooling-off window and interval check in this system reads a logical instant rather than the wall clock, a platform administrator can drive time forward for testing and for operational replay by supplying the new instant as `to_iso8601`; the response carries the resulting `current_utc`. The move is not inert: crossing a proof-of-funds expiry recomputes `verified_funds_cents`, `available_funds_cents` and `buyer_tier` on every cash buyer whose liquidity picture changed, which can drop a buyer to `SUSPENDED` and make a subsequent acceptance fail on `ERR_PROOF_OF_FUNDS_EXPIRED`.
