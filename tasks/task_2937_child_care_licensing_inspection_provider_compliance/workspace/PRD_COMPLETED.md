# Child Care Licensing Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state child care licensing agency carries a statutory duty that is unusual in its breadth: it must decide who may care for children, verify continuously that those providers still deserve that permission, act quickly when an allegation arrives, and publish what it finds so that families can choose with open eyes. Today that work is spread across disconnected registers — a clearance spreadsheet held by the central background-check unit, inspection paperwork carried in field folders, a complaint intake log, and a separate enforcement docket maintained by regional supervisors. Because no single record links a disqualified household member to the classroom she was leading, or a substantiated abuse allegation to the point balance that should have triggered probation, the agency routinely discovers its own escalation triggers weeks late.

This document specifies the licensing and compliance service that replaces those registers. The service is mounted under `/licensing` and models the whole regulatory arc in one consistent data set: the provider application and its rooms, the people attached to the site and their registry clearances, the scheduled visits and the citations they produce, the point balance those citations accumulate, the corrective action plans that retire them, the complaint casework that runs on a statutory clock, the mandatory reporting of serious injuries and fatalities, the progressive enforcement ladder from warning to revocation with its appeal path, changes to the licence record itself, and the consumer-facing publication of every finalized report.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every derived number is a consequence, never an input** — a provider's point balance, its risk tier, a room's ratio compliance, an inspection's compliance score, a fine amount: all of these are recomputed by the service from primary records, and no client may set them. Second, **the agency must never be able to hide from its own arithmetic**: when a citation is vacated, the counters return to exactly the values they held before the citation existed; when a suspension is overturned on appeal, the capacity slots it froze return in full. The engineering consequence of both commitments is that cascades must be complete and conservation invariants must hold after every single mutation.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this service carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that operator acts. There is exactly one exception to the first header's necessity — the endpoint that creates a provider application, because at that moment the applicant's identity is the very record being created. Anywhere else, an absent `X-User-ID`, or one sent as an empty string or as nothing but whitespace, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same error and status answer a request whose `X-User-Role` is missing or names something outside the eight roles the agency recognises. When the role is recognised but is not among the roles the target endpoint admits, the answer is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `AgencyAdmin`, `Applicant`, `LicensingInspector`, `ComplaintIntakeSpecialist`, `ComplaintInvestigator`, `LicensingSupervisor`, `BackgroundCheckUnit`, and `PublicPortalReader`. An `AgencyAdmin` is the state agency administrator: it owns configuration (the checklist catalog and the inspector register), enforcement effectuation and appeal adjudication, licence amendment decisions, the public register, and the logical clock. An `Applicant` is the provider applicant or licensee acting on its own record. A `LicensingInspector` conducts visits, verifies rooms, cites violations and verifies corrections. A `ComplaintIntakeSpecialist` receives allegations and makes the screen-in or screen-out decision, and never investigates or determines. A `ComplaintInvestigator` is the investigator assigned to a screened-in complaint who records the substantiation determination. A `LicensingSupervisor` is the regional supervisor who clears checks, decides licences, schedules and finalizes inspections, reviews corrective action plans, assigns investigators, closes complaints, dispositions incident reports, and proposes and issues enforcement. The `BackgroundCheckUnit` is the central clearance unit that opens checks and records registry results, and it may never grant a waiver — that is the supervisor's call. A `PublicPortalReader` is an unprivileged consumer-education reader with read-only access and no mutating capability whatsoever.

Three of these roles are **entity-backed**, which is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's provider reference to `X-User-ID`, directly for provider-scoped operations and transitively through the owning provider for staff members, background checks, age-group rosters, corrective action plans, enforcement actions and licence amendments. When `X-User-Role` is `LicensingInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector`, and it must equal the target inspection's assigned inspector. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplaintInvestigator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Inspector`, and it must equal the target complaint's assigned investigator. A mismatch in any of these three comparisons is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Separately, an entity-backed role whose `X-User-ID` does not resolve to a row of its backing entity at all — no such `Provider`, no such `Inspector` — is treated as an authentication failure and answered with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; the sole endpoint exempted from that resolution requirement is provider creation, where the `Applicant` identity does not yet exist.

The remaining five roles carry **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyAdmin`, `LicensingSupervisor`, `ComplaintIntakeSpecialist`, `BackgroundCheckUnit`, or `PublicPortalReader`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a supervisor's identifier is stored as `reviewed_by_user_id` when that supervisor approves a corrective action plan, and as `issued_by_user_id` when an enforcement action is issued — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. Those two recorded identifiers exist for one purpose only: the segregation-of-duties rules of §2.8.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest is 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0. Both parameters must be sent as bare integer literals: anything else — a decimal, a word, an empty value — as well as a `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range or a negative `offset`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and because the logical clock of §2.4 can leave many rows sharing one `created_at`, ties are broken by the order in which the rows were created, so the ordering is total and stable; a stable page boundary therefore does not shift as new rows arrive at the end of the collection. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the request's filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` states whether any row remains beyond the current window.

Filter parameters on list endpoints are likewise optional and default to unset, meaning no filtering on that attribute. A request that omits every optional parameter is a well-formed request. Two refinements apply to every query parameter in this service, on list endpoints and on the two computed reads alike. A parameter whose value set is an enum — any status, type, tier, age-band or `variant` selector — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (HTTP 422) when the supplied value is outside that set, rather than being ignored or treated as unset. A free-text identifier filter is matched literally, so sending it as an empty string asks for rows whose attribute is the empty string and therefore matches nothing rather than disabling the filter. And a query parameter the endpoint does not declare at all is ignored rather than refused.

### 2.4. The Logical Licensing Clock

Nothing in this domain reads the host machine's wall clock. Every deadline, expiry, timeliness judgement, window comparison and lookback in this specification reads a single server-held **logical clock**, and an administrator-only test-driving capability sets that clock to an explicit UTC instant supplied as `to_iso8601`, answering with a computed object whose single field is the resulting `current_utc`, rendered in the same ISO 8601 three-fractional-digit UTC form as every other instant this service emits. That form is also how every instant-typed *input* in this specification is read: the value is parsed as an ISO 8601 instant and normalised to UTC, so a value carrying a numeric zone offset is accepted and converted rather than refused, a value with no zone marker is read as UTC, a value with more than three fractional digits keeps its full precision internally while being rendered back to milliseconds, and a value that cannot be parsed at all is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Wherever this specification adds or subtracts a whole number of months — a clearance expiry, the provisional period, the 12-month segregation lookback, the 24-month risk and repeat lookbacks — the arithmetic keeps the day of the month and the time of day, clamping the day back to the last day of the target month when that day does not exist there. The clock starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any call to that capability the clock reads that instant and must not fall back to real time. It may be moved forward or backward freely, and moving it mutates no stored entity and recomputes no derived field by itself — but because clearance expiry and every deadline comparison read the clock at the moment they are evaluated, a provider can lose its clearance standing purely by the clock advancing past a check's expiry.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Revision Counters and Optimistic Concurrency

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix — and a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at creation. Every other instant-valued field the service stamps for itself — `updated_at`, the several `*_at` transition stamps, the deadlines and the expiries — is rendered in that identical form, so all of them are directly comparable as strings. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of the row. The one exception is the health-and-safety checklist catalog: a `ChecklistItem` is immutable configuration, has no update or delete capability, and carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and callers must not expect either field on it.

Two write operations are guarded by **optimistic concurrency control**, because in both cases a second sequential write would itself legitimately succeed by superseding the first, so the only way to distinguish an informed writer from an uninformed one is the revision the request carries.

The first is the creation of a licence amendment. That request requires a non-optional `expected_provider_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `Provider` that the client last observed. If it does not equal the provider's current `version` — because some other writer advanced the provider row after the client read it — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and it is rejected *before* any supersede of an earlier open amendment takes place.

The second is the submission of a corrective action plan. That request requires a non-optional `expected_inspection_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `Inspection` that the client last observed. A value that no longer equals the inspection's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412); the carried revision is what distinguishes a provider who has seen the inspection's current violation set and score from one who has not.

No other endpoint in this service takes a version field. In particular, resubmitting a rejected plan, reviewing a plan, deciding an amendment, dispositioning a violation and every enforcement step carry no expected-version input, and supplying one is an unknown body key.

### 2.6. Scoring Precision and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this domain are integer cent counts; there is no fractional currency. Point assessments are whole numbers. Compliance scores, display scores and risk scores are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places; that scale is a property of the arithmetic, not of the wire format — these values travel as JSON numbers rather than as strings, so a value whose hundredths happen to be zero may well serialise as `0.0` or `37.0`, and a reader must compare them numerically. Wherever a value must be shortened, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the specification is explicit about *how many times* and *at what stage* rounding is applied, because a rounding applied one step earlier or later produces a different answer. Three places in this document therefore state the rounding stages normatively: the point assessment of a single citation rounds exactly once, to a whole point, after severity aggravation and the repeat multiplier and before the per-citation cap; the compliance score rounds each violation's deduction line to 2 decimal places, then rounds the sum of those already-rounded lines to 2 decimal places, then rounds the final score to 2 decimal places; and the fine calculation rounds its surcharge and its credit independently, each to whole cents.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

A single request can violate several rules at once, and which error the caller sees is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Checks are evaluated in a fixed order, and the **first** rule violated determines the response — no later rule is evaluated, and no later rule's error can mask an earlier one.

The order is as follows. Authentication comes first: the presence and non-blankness of `X-User-ID`, then the presence and validity of `X-User-Role`, then whether that role is admitted by the target endpoint, then whether an entity-backed role's identifier resolves to a row of its backing entity. Next comes request shape, itself in three stages. First the body must be a JSON object and must contain no unknown keys — that scan runs before any judgement about the declared fields, so a request that both names a field the endpoint does not declare and omits one it requires reports the unknown key — and then every non-optional input field must be present and of its declared scalar type (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400). Second, every enum-typed input value must be a member of its declared value set (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`, HTTP 422). Third and last, every remaining field constraint — length bounds, not-blank, patterns, numeric bounds, non-emptiness, duplicate-freedom, and the parseability of a date or an instant — is applied and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; because the enum stage completes across the whole body before this stage begins, a body carrying both an out-of-set enum and an over-long string reports the enum error. Throughout all three stages an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as an omission: it fails the presence test when the field is non-optional, and otherwise leaves the field unset rather than clearing a stored value. An endpoint that declares no input fields at all accepts an absent body, an empty body and `{}` interchangeably, and treats any key whatsoever as unknown. Existence follows: templated path segments are resolved left to right, then foreign keys supplied in the request body are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. Ownership is checked after existence, since a resource must exist before it can be owned; segregation of duties is checked immediately after ownership. Then come the two optimistic-concurrency comparisons — the provider revision on amendment creation, and the inspection revision on plan submission — which precede every state check. State preconditions follow, in this internal order: the provider's application dimension, then the provider's licence dimension, then the inspection, the violation, the complaint, the corrective action plan, the enforcement action, the amendment, the incident report, the posting, the background check, and finally whether a referenced inspection is of the required type. Business rules are evaluated after all state checks, in three groups: safety and regulatory rules first, then temporal rules, then non-temporal constraint rules and referential-agreement rules, and last of all the capacity and fiscal rules. Two consequences of that ordering are worth stating explicitly because they are easy to get wrong: on an amendment decision the unresolved-critical-violation safety check is evaluated *before* the capacity-ceiling fiscal check, and when scheduling a visit the two window-conflict checks are evaluated *before* the inspector-certification check.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties Across the Casework Chain

Three separations of duty are enforced against `X-User-ID`, and all three answer with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

An inspector who has finalized any inspection of a provider within the last 12 months may not be assigned as the investigator of a complaint against that same provider; the lookback compares each candidate inspection's finalization instant against the instant 12 months before the logical clock, and the lower edge of the window is exclusive, so an inspection finalized exactly 12 months ago does not bar the assignment.

The supervisor whose identifier is recorded as the reviewer of a corrective action plan may not later issue an enforcement action whose evidentiary inspection is that plan's inspection. Concretely, when an enforcement action is issued, no corrective action plan may exist whose inspection is the action's source inspection, whose recorded reviewer identifier equals the issuing `X-User-ID`, and whose status is `APPROVED`, `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`.

The administrator who issued an enforcement action may not decide the appeal against it: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the identifier recorded when the action was issued.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities: Points and Capacity Slots

Two quantities in this domain are conserved, and every operation that touches them must leave the books balanced.

The first is **violation points**. A provider carries three point counters. The open balance is the sum of the assessed points of the provider's violations whose status is `CITED`, `CORRECTION_PENDING` or `UNCORRECTED`. The resolved balance is the sum over violations whose status is `VERIFIED_CORRECTED`. The lifetime balance must always equal the open balance plus the resolved balance, and violations in status `VACATED` contribute to none of the three. All three balances are defined over one source set only — the violations of that provider's `FINALIZED` inspections, as §3.5 states normatively — so the per-operation bookkeeping below describes what each operation does *once its inspection is finalized*, and a citation recorded on a visit still `IN_PROGRESS` or `PENDING_REVIEW` moves none of the three until finalization lands it. Per operation, the bookkeeping is: citing a violation adds its assessed points to both the open and the lifetime balance; verifying a correction subtracts those points from the open balance and adds them to the resolved balance, leaving the lifetime balance untouched; recording a violation as uncorrected changes no counter, because an uncorrected violation is still open; and vacating a violation — from `CITED`, from `CORRECTION_PENDING` or from `UNCORRECTED` — subtracts its points from both the open and the lifetime balance while leaving the resolved balance alone. A citation followed by a vacation therefore restores all three counters to exactly their pre-citation values, leaving no residue. Violations belonging to an inspection in status `VOIDED` are discarded and contribute to nothing. Although the counters are maintained incrementally, they must always agree with a full recomputation from the underlying violations.

The second is **capacity slots**. A provider's total authorised slots is its licensed capacity plus its suspended capacity. That total changes for exactly two reasons: an approved capacity amendment changes it by exactly the amendment's capacity delta, and effectuating a revocation zeroes it deliberately and permanently. Every suspension effectuation and every appeal that vacates a suspension merely *moves* slots between the two fields — the suspension moves the whole licensed capacity into suspended capacity and records the moved amount on the action, and the vacating appeal moves exactly that recorded amount back — so the total is identical before and after. Any operation that would leave suspended capacity negative, or that would change the total on a suspension or appeal path, is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_SLOT_IMBALANCE` (HTTP 409). The ceiling test that applies to amendments is that the licensed capacity plus the suspended capacity plus the amendment's capacity delta must be at most the statutory ceiling for the provider's programme class, landing exactly on the ceiling being permitted; exceeding it is `ERR_CAPACITY_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Cascades and Cross-Entity Consistency

This service has no deferred jobs and no eventual consistency. Whenever one operation changes a second entity, that change is visible immediately in the same request, and where the second change causes a third, the third happens too. Two chains in particular run four links deep and are described in full in §3: a disqualifying registry result reaches from the check to the person, from the person to every room that person led, and from there to the provider's clearance standing and its scheduled initial visits; and a substantiated complaint determination reaches from the complaint to an auto-cited violation, from that violation to the provider's counters and the inspection's score, and from those counters to an automatically proposed enforcement action.

Two mechanical consequences apply to every cascade. A row whose fields are changed by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, exactly as if it had been the direct target of the request. And a recomputed derived field is recomputed from current primary data rather than adjusted by a guessed delta, so that the conservation invariants of §2.9 survive arbitrary sequences of operations.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Not-Found Semantics

Path parameters are resolved left to right, and the first segment that does not resolve produces that entity's own not-found error with HTTP 404. The identifiers appearing in paths are `provider_id`, `inspector_id`, `staff_member_id`, `background_check_id`, `roster_id`, `inspection_id`, `violation_id`, `complaint_id`, `cap_id`, `action_id`, `amendment_id`, `report_id`, `posting_id` and `checklist_item_id`, and their respective failures are `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BACKGROUND_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROSTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CHECKLIST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`.

The same catalogue answers unresolvable foreign keys supplied in a request body, resolved in the order the endpoint declares its inputs. A `provider_id` that matches no provider is `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`; an `inspector_id` or an `investigator_id` that matches no inspector is `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`; a `staff_member_id`, a `lead_staff_member_id` or a `new_director_staff_member_id` that matches no person is `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`; an `inspection_id`, a `source_inspection_id` or a `pre_move_inspection_id` that matches no visit is `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`; a `violation_id`, any member of `violation_ids`, or a `repeat_of_violation_id` that matches no citation is `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `complaint_id` that matches no allegation is `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`; and a `checklist_item_id` that matches no catalog row — or a checklist code that a cascade needs and that has never been registered — is `ERR_CHECKLIST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`.

Two refusals sit above that catalogue because they are decided before any identifier is looked at. A request whose path matches no route of this service at all is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carrying HTTP 404, and a request that addresses a real path with a method that path does not support is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carrying HTTP 405; neither case produces an entity-specific not-found identifier, because no entity was ever addressed. A trailing slash is not significant: `/licensing/providers/` and `/licensing/providers` are the same route.

Because every collection in this service sits at depth one under the `/licensing` prefix, children are discovered through filtered list endpoints rather than nested paths: the rosters of a provider, the checks of a person, the violations of an inspection, the plans of an inspection, the complaints assigned to an investigator and the postings of a provider are all reached by filtering the corresponding collection.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Regulatory Catalog and the Field Workforce

Before any provider can be inspected, two configuration registers must exist, and both are owned by the agency administrator.

The first is the **health-and-safety checklist catalog**. Each entry registers one regulation as a row carrying a `code` that must match the pattern of two uppercase letters, a hyphen and three digits (for example `SU-101`) and that must be unique across the whole catalog — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Alongside the code sit a short human label in `title`, from 1 to 160 characters and not blank; a `category` naming the checklist section, one of `SUPERVISION`, `SANITATION`, `NUTRITION`, `FIRE_SAFETY`, `MEDICATION`, `BACKGROUND_CHECK`, `RATIO`, `FACILITY` or `RECORDS`; a `default_severity` of `CRITICAL`, `SERIOUS`, `MODERATE` or `MINOR`, which is the severity a citation of this requirement inherits when the inspector supplies no override; a non-empty `age_groups_applicable` list, without duplicates, drawn from `INFANT`, `TODDLER`, `PRESCHOOL` and `SCHOOL_AGE`; and an optional `infant_sensitive` flag that defaults to false when omitted and marks requirements whose breach is aggravated when the citation concerns infants. Catalog rows are immutable: there is no way to edit or remove one, which is why they carry no revision counter. The catalog can be listed with optional `code`, `category` and `default_severity` filters, and read individually; inspectors, supervisors, investigators and public readers may all read it, but only an administrator may add to it.

The second register is the **inspector roll**. Registering a field inspector records `full_name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank), a `county_code` matching the pattern of the literal `CTY-` followed by exactly two digits, a non-empty duplicate-free `certified_age_groups` list drawn from the same four age bands, and an optional `max_daily_visits` ceiling between 1 and 8 that defaults to 3 when omitted. A new inspector starts with `status` equal to `ACTIVE` and `scheduled_visit_count` at 0; that count is always the number of the inspector's inspections whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `PENDING_REVIEW`. An administrator moves an inspector between `ACTIVE` and `ON_LEAVE` by supplying the desired `status`, and that capability is the only producer of the `ON_LEAVE` state. Going on leave does **not** void visits already scheduled; it blocks only *new* scheduling and *new* investigator assignment, each of which is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_ON_LEAVE` while the inspector is on leave. Every leave-status call increments the inspector's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`, including one that restates the status the inspector already holds — the capability is a write, not a conditional write. Inspectors are listed with optional `county_code` and `status` filters.

### 3.2. Assembling a Provider Application: The Dossier, Its Rooms and Its People

An operator opens its own file. Creating a provider application records the `legal_name` (1 to 200 characters, not blank), the `county_code` of jurisdiction in the same `CTY-` plus two digits form, the `site_address` of the care site (1 to 300 characters, not blank), the `program_type` — `CENTER`, `GROUP_HOME` or `FAMILY_HOME` — and the `requested_capacity`, the total number of children the applicant asks to be licensed for. From the programme class the service derives `capacity_ceiling`, the statutory maximum: 120 children for a `CENTER`, 40 for a `GROUP_HOME`, 12 for a `FAMILY_HOME`. The requested capacity must be at least 1 and at most that ceiling; a value below 1 fails as an ordinary field-constraint breach with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a value above the ceiling is refused with `ERR_CAPACITY_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. The new record starts with `application_status` at `DRAFT` and `license_status` at `NONE`; with `licensed_capacity`, `suspended_capacity`, `open_violation_points`, `resolved_violation_points`, `lifetime_violation_points`, `critical_open_count`, `critical_violation_count_24mo`, `substantiated_complaint_count_24mo`, `inconclusive_complaint_count_24mo`, `risk_score`, `current_ladder_step` and `probation_cycle_count` all at 0; with `risk_tier` at `LOW`; with `clearance_complete` false; and with `license_number`, `licensed_at`, `provisional_started_at` and `decision_reason` all null. This is the one operation in the service that sends no `X-User-ID`, because the identifier the applicant will use from then on is the `id` this call returns. Providers can be listed — this is also the consumer-education search surface — with optional `county_code`, `program_type`, `license_status`, `application_status` and `risk_tier` filters.

**People are attached next.** Every person who works at, volunteers at, or lives in the care site is recorded as a staff member against a `provider_id`, which is immutable once set. The record carries the person's `full_name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank), a `member_type` of `DIRECTOR`, `LEAD_TEACHER`, `ASSISTANT`, `VOLUNTEER` or `HOUSEHOLD_MEMBER`, a `date_of_birth` as an ISO calendar date in year-month-day form used only for record completeness — being decorative, it carries no range test at all, so any well-formed calendar date is accepted, including one in the future — a duplicate-free `qualified_age_groups` list naming the age bands the person may supervise as a room lead, which is a required input but may legitimately be sent as the empty list for a household member, and an optional `role_title`. Household members of family-home and group-home providers are cleared on exactly the same rules as paid staff. A newly attached person starts with `clearance_status` at `PENDING`, `supervision_eligible` false and `barred_offense_tier` at `NONE`, and attaching them immediately recomputes the provider's `clearance_complete` — adding an uncleared person to a fully cleared provider flips that flag to false — and increments the provider's `version`. A person is `supervision_eligible` exactly when their `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` and their `member_type` is `DIRECTOR`, `LEAD_TEACHER` or `ASSISTANT`; volunteers and household members are never supervision-eligible, however cleared they are. Staff members are listed with optional `provider_id`, `member_type` and `clearance_status` filters.

**Rooms are declared as age-group rosters.** Each roster names its owning `provider_id` — immutable after creation — and its `age_group`, and at most one roster may exist per provider and age band; a second roster for the same pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The applicant states a `requested_group_size` of at least 1, optionally an `enrolled_count` and a `staff_assigned_count` (each defaulting to 0 and never negative), and optionally a `lead_staff_member_id` designating the room lead. `approved_group_size` is not an input: it stays 0 until the licence is issued, at which point it is set to the requested group size, and afterwards it changes only through an approved capacity or age-group amendment.

Four statutory numbers govern rooms. The staff-to-child ratio is one adult for every 4 infants, every 6 toddlers, every 10 preschoolers or every 15 school-age children. The maximum group size is 8 infants, 12 toddlers, 20 preschoolers or 30 school-age children. From the first of those, `required_staff_count` is the enrolled count divided by the ratio for the room's age band and rounded **up** to a whole number, so a single extra child adds a whole adult; when no children are enrolled the required count is exactly 0. The room is ratio-compliant when its assigned staff count is greater than or equal to the required staff count — equality is compliant. It is group-size-compliant when the enrolled count is at most the smaller of the approved group size and the statutory maximum for the age band, which means that while the approved group size is still 0 the check degenerates into requiring zero enrolled children. And it is supervision-compliant when no children are enrolled, or else when a lead is named, that lead is `CLEARED`, is supervision-eligible, is a `DIRECTOR` or a `LEAD_TEACHER`, and has the room's age band among their qualified age groups. `requested_group_size` may never exceed the statutory maximum for the age band at creation, and `enrolled_count` may never exceed the effective cap: either failure is `ERR_GROUP_SIZE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A lead who fails any of the four lead tests is `ERR_SUPERVISION_INELIGIBLE_LEAD` (HTTP 409) on room creation, on room update, and on explicit verification alike. A room whose assigned staff falls below the required staff count is `ERR_RATIO_NONCOMPLIANT` (HTTP 409) on room update and on explicit verification. Across a provider, the sum of `approved_group_size` over all its rooms may never exceed the provider's `licensed_capacity`, or — before the licence is issued, when licensed capacity is still 0 — its `requested_capacity`; a breach is `ERR_ROSTER_CAPACITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409), enforced on room creation, on room update, and at application submission. When more than one of these tests could fail on the same request they are evaluated in one fixed order, the same order on every room operation that applies them: the named-lead eligibility test first, then the ratio test, then the group-size tests, then — on creation only — the one-roster-per-provider-and-age-band uniqueness test, and the provider-wide capacity agreement last of all.

Updating a room accepts any of `enrolled_count`, `staff_assigned_count` and `lead_staff_member_id`, and re-derives the required staff count and all three compliance flags. Each of the three is optional and an omitted one keeps its stored value; since an explicit `null` counts as an omission (§2.7), an update cannot detach a room's lead — only the clearance cascade of §3.3 ever clears `lead_staff_member_id`. The lead-eligibility test on an update is applied only to a lead the request actually names: a room that is already carrying a lead who has since become ineligible can still be updated in its other two fields. That propagation is deliberately shallow: changing any one of the enrolled count, the assigned staff count, the approved group size or the lead's clearance re-derives the three flags on that room, then re-checks the provider's roster-to-capacity agreement, and then stops. It reaches a fixed point in exactly two hops and cannot loop. Rooms are listed with optional `provider_id`, `lead_staff_member_id` and `age_group` filters, and a single room can be read back with its three flags.

An inspector or a supervisor may also demand an **explicit room verification**, which is a conditional action rather than a plain read: it recomputes the required staff count and the three flags and then *requires all three to be true*, refusing with the corresponding ratio, group-size or supervision error when any of them is false, and testing them in that order — ratio, then group size, then supervision — when more than one is false. Ahead of all three, and unlike the update path, the lead-eligibility test is applied whenever the room carries a lead at all: a room whose named lead has lost their clearance is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISION_INELIGIBLE_LEAD` even when the room has no children enrolled and its `supervision_compliant` flag is therefore true. On success it stamps `last_verified_at` with the logical clock and increments the room's `version`. An infant room with 8 approved places and no children enrolled verifies successfully with zero staff assigned, because zero children require zero adults; the same room with one child enrolled and no staff assigned requires one adult and is refused as ratio-noncompliant; with four children and one adult it is compliant, and with five children and one adult it requires two adults and is refused.

**Submitting the application** moves `application_status` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`. Only the owning applicant may submit, and the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` (HTTP 409) unless the application is still in `DRAFT`. Four substantive preconditions guard it: the provider must have at least one staff member whose member type is `DIRECTOR`, and the absence of a director is reported as `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`; the provider must have at least one age-group roster, and it must have none is reported as `ERR_CAPACITY_SLOT_IMBALANCE`; the sum of the `requested_group_size` values across the provider's rooms must equal the provider's `requested_capacity` exactly — neither more nor less — and a mismatch is likewise `ERR_CAPACITY_SLOT_IMBALANCE`; and the requested capacity must not exceed the programme ceiling, reported as `ERR_CAPACITY_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. They are evaluated in exactly that order, and the roster-to-capacity agreement described above is checked last of all, after the ceiling test, as `ERR_ROSTER_CAPACITY_MISMATCH`. A successful submission increments the provider's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`.

### 3.3. Clearance: Registry Checks, Disqualification Cascades and Waivers

No adult may be around children until the registries have spoken. A check is opened against a `staff_member_id` for a single `check_type`, one of `FBI_FINGERPRINT`, `CHILD_ABUSE_REGISTRY`, `STATE_CRIMINAL` or `SEX_OFFENDER_REGISTRY`, and is created in status `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_at` set to the logical clock, `decided_at`, `expires_at`, `disqualifying_offense_tier`, `waiver_requested_at`, `waiver_rationale` and `waiver_decided_at` all null. Only one *live* check per person per type may exist: if any other check of the same person and the same type is already in `SUBMITTED`, `CLEARED`, `WAIVER_PENDING` or `WAIVED`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHECK_TYPE` (HTTP 409). A `DISQUALIFIED` check is not live, so a person whose check came back disqualifying may have a fresh one of the same type opened. Checks are listed with optional `staff_member_id`, `check_type` and `status` filters. Only the background-check unit may record a result; the provider itself or an administrator may open checks.

Recording a result supplies `result`, either `CLEARED` or `DISQUALIFIED`, and optionally a `disqualifying_offense_tier`. The `result` is an input only: it is never kept as a field of the check and never appears in the check's representation, because the outcome it names is already carried by the check's own `status`. The check must still be `SUBMITTED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CHECK_STATE` (HTTP 409). A `CLEARED` result requires the tier to be omitted or exactly `NONE` — any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it sets `status` to `CLEARED`, stamps `decided_at` with the clock and sets `expires_at` to sixty months after that instant. A `DISQUALIFIED` result *requires* a tier of `TIER_1_PERMANENT`, `TIER_2_FIVE_YEAR` or `TIER_3_WAIVABLE` — omitting it or sending `NONE` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it sets `status` to `DISQUALIFIED` and stamps `decided_at`, and `expires_at` stays null. Whichever of the two results is recorded, and whether or not any further link of the cascade below actually fires, the call refreshes the owning provider's `updated_at` and increments its `version`: the agency's clearance picture has been touched, so the provider row moves.

Three of a person's own fields are always derived, never supplied. A person's `clearance_status` is `BARRED` if any of their checks is `DISQUALIFIED`; otherwise `WAIVER_PENDING` if any of their checks is `WAIVER_PENDING`; otherwise `CLEARED` if the set of check types whose status is `CLEARED` or `WAIVED` and whose `expires_at` is either null or strictly later than the clock covers both `FBI_FINGERPRINT` and `CHILD_ABUSE_REGISTRY`, and no check of theirs is disqualified or awaiting a waiver; and `PENDING` in every other case. A disqualification therefore outranks everything, and one bad result bars a person who has ten good ones. Their `barred_offense_tier` is the highest tier across their disqualified checks, ranked permanent above five-year above waivable above none, and it returns to `NONE` once no disqualified check remains. And `supervision_eligible` follows the rule already stated: cleared, and of a supervising member type.

A provider's `clearance_complete` is an aggregate over *every* attached person — there is no separation or inactive state in this domain, so nobody drops out of the denominator. It is true when the provider has at least one `DIRECTOR` and, for every attached person, that person's own `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` **and** their unexpired cleared-or-waived check types cover both `FBI_FINGERPRINT` and `CHILD_ABUSE_REGISTRY`. The expiry comparison is strict: a check whose `expires_at` equals the current instant has already stopped counting. A consequence worth stating plainly is that a provider can lose `clearance_complete` purely by the clock advancing, with no request made at all, and that attaching one uncleared assistant to a provider whose director is perfectly cleared flips the flag to false.

The flag is read as a precondition in three places: the supervisor's clearance advance, which moves `application_status` from `SUBMITTED` to `CHECKS_CLEARED` and recomputes the flag before requiring it to be true; the finalization of an `INITIAL` inspection, which is the cross-entity dependency between an inspection and the registry results behind it; and the issuing licence decision. In all three, a false flag is `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409).

**A disqualification is not a single write — it is a four-link chain, each link caused by the link before it, not by the request.** Link one records the result: the check becomes `DISQUALIFIED`. Link two, *because* that check is now disqualified, recomputes the person: `clearance_status` becomes `BARRED`, `supervision_eligible` becomes false, and `barred_offense_tier` becomes the highest tier across their disqualified checks. Link three, *because* that person is no longer supervision-eligible, sweeps every room that person leads: `lead_staff_member_id` is set to null, `staff_assigned_count` is decremented by one but never below zero, and `required_staff_count`, `ratio_compliant` and `supervision_compliant` are recomputed on each such room. Link four, *because* the provider's `clearance_complete` has flipped from true to false, reverts `application_status` from `CHECKS_CLEARED` to `SUBMITTED` — the cyclic edge of that dimension — and voids every `INITIAL` inspection of that provider whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Because voiding a visit vacates the open citations it carried, the same link then recomputes the provider's point counters, its two 24-month complaint counters and its `risk_score` and `risk_tier` under enforcement-review weighting, so the books of §2.9 balance before the response is written. The provider's `version` is incremented. That last link is deliberately narrow: follow-up, annual, complaint and amendment inspections are **not** voided, and an `INITIAL` inspection already in `PENDING_REVIEW` or `FINALIZED` is **not** voided. Link four also fires only on a true-to-false flip of `clearance_complete` and only from `CHECKS_CLEARED`; a provider still in `DRAFT` or already `LICENSED` keeps its application status.

**The waiver path is the loop back.** The provider — and only the provider that owns the barred person, checked transitively through that person's `provider_id` — may request a waiver on a `DISQUALIFIED` check, supplying a `waiver_rationale` of 10 to 1000 characters. A check that is not `DISQUALIFIED` is `ERR_INVALID_CHECK_STATE`, and a check whose `disqualifying_offense_tier` is `TIER_1_PERMANENT` can never enter this path at all: it is refused with `ERR_PERMANENT_BAR_NOT_WAIVABLE` (HTTP 409). Note the order — the state test precedes the tier test, so a waiver request against an already-`WAIVER_PENDING` permanent bar reports the state error. A successful request sets `status` to `WAIVER_PENDING`, stamps `waiver_requested_at`, stores the rationale, moves the person's `clearance_status` to `WAIVER_PENDING`, leaves `supervision_eligible` false, and recomputes the provider's `clearance_complete`, which stays false. Unlike the recording of a registry result, neither the waiver request nor the waiver decision below touches the provider row for its own sake: on those two paths the provider's `updated_at` and `version` move only when the recomputation actually changes something on that row, which on the request path it does not.

A supervisor then decides, supplying `decision` as either `GRANT` or `REFUSE`; a check not in `WAIVER_PENDING` is `ERR_INVALID_CHECK_STATE`. A grant sets `status` to `WAIVED`, stamps `waiver_decided_at`, sets `expires_at` to sixty months after that instant, leaves the check's own `disqualifying_offense_tier` exactly as the registry result recorded it — the tier is the historical fact the waiver forgives, not one it erases — and recomputes the person's clearance fields — their `barred_offense_tier` returns to `NONE` once no disqualified check of theirs remains — and then the provider's `clearance_complete`. A refusal sets `status` back to `DISQUALIFIED`, closing the disqualified-to-pending-to-disqualified loop, stamps `waiver_decided_at`, and returns the person to `BARRED` with `supervision_eligible` false. A refused waiver may be requested again, since the check is disqualified once more; a granted one is terminal.

### 3.4. The Inspection Visit — Scheduling Through Finalization

Scheduling a visit names the `provider_id`, the `inspector_id`, the `inspection_type` — `INITIAL`, `ANNUAL`, `COMPLAINT`, `FOLLOW_UP` or `AMENDMENT_PRE_MOVE` — the `scheduled_for` start instant, the non-empty `target_age_groups` the visit will cover, an optional `duration_minutes` between 30 and 480 that defaults to 120, and a `complaint_id` that is **required exactly when** the type is `COMPLAINT` and **forbidden otherwise**; a complaint reference supplied on any other type, or omitted on a complaint visit, or naming a complaint whose `provider_id` differs from the inspection's, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The service derives `visit_window_end` as the start instant plus the duration in minutes, creates the record in `SCHEDULED` with `started_at`, `submitted_at`, `finalized_at`, `age_groups_observed`, `outcome`, `preliminary_compliance_score`, `final_compliance_score` and `raw_deduction_points` all null, `violation_count` and `reopen_count` at 0 and `cap_required` false, and increments the inspector's `scheduled_visit_count` by one — a change to the inspector row, so that inspector's `version` advances with it. Supervisors and administrators schedule; the visit itself is worked by the named inspector.

Three occupancy tests guard the calendar, and their interval arithmetic differs on purpose. The **active set** for all three is every inspection whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`: a visit that has moved on to `PENDING_REVIEW`, `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED` releases its windows and never conflicts again.

- The **inspector's** window is half-open — it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant — so two visits for the same inspector conflict only when their half-open windows genuinely overlap. Back-to-back visits, where one ends at the exact instant the next begins, do **not** conflict. An overlap is `ERR_INSPECTOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (HTTP 409).
- The **provider's** window is closed — it includes both endpoints — and is additionally padded by 60 minutes on each side before comparison. Two visits at the same site therefore conflict when their padded closed windows touch at even a single instant: a visit ending at ten o'clock and another beginning at noon are 120 minutes apart, their padded windows meet exactly at eleven o'clock, and the second is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_VISIT_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Widening the gap to 121 minutes clears it.
- The inspector's **daily load** is the count of their active-set inspections falling on the same UTC calendar date as the requested start, and that count must be strictly below the inspector's `max_daily_visits`. At the limit the request is `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAILY_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). The date is taken from the UTC calendar day of `scheduled_for`, so a visit at 23:30 UTC and one at 00:30 UTC the next morning fall on different days however close they are in time.

Two further scheduling rules complete the picture: `scheduled_for` must be strictly after the logical clock, and a start exactly at the clock is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422); and `target_age_groups` must be a non-empty subset of the assigned inspector's `certified_age_groups`, or the request is `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED_FOR_AGE_GROUP` (HTTP 409). The inspector must also be `ACTIVE`, or the request is `ERR_INSPECTOR_ON_LEAVE` (HTTP 409). The relative order of these refusals is fixed and observable: the two window conflicts are evaluated **before** the certification check, so a request that is both double-booked and outside the inspector's certification reports the double-booking. In full, once the request's `provider_id`, `inspector_id` and any `complaint_id` have all resolved, the schedule call is refused in this order: the inspector's overlap, then the provider's buffered overlap, then the past-start test, then the certification subset, then the inspector's `ACTIVE` standing, then the daily ceiling, and last of all the three complaint-reference coherence rules — so a start instant in the past that also falls inside an occupied window reports the occupancy rather than `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST`, and a `COMPLAINT` visit that omits its `complaint_id` while the named inspector is on leave reports `ERR_INSPECTOR_ON_LEAVE`.

The visit then walks a five-state machine. The **assigned inspector**, and nobody else, starts it: the visit must be `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`, HTTP 409), the caller's identity must equal the inspection's `inspector_id` (otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`), and the inspector must still be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_ON_LEAVE`). Starting sets `status` to `IN_PROGRESS`, stamps `started_at` and increments the inspection's `version`.

The same inspector submits findings, supplying the `outcome` — `PASS`, `CONDITIONAL_PASS` or `FAIL` — and the `age_groups_observed`. The visit must be `IN_PROGRESS`, the caller must own it, `age_groups_observed` must be a non-empty subset of that inspector's `certified_age_groups` — it is tested against the inspector's certification alone and need not be a subset of, or even overlap, the visit's own `target_age_groups` — and the outcome must agree with what was actually cited: the outcome **must** be `FAIL` when at least one violation of this inspection has effective severity `CRITICAL` and a status other than `VACATED`, and **must** be `PASS` when the inspection has no violation at all in a status other than `VACATED`. Any other pairing is `ERR_OUTCOME_SEVERITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). Between those two poles the choice is free: with non-critical violations cited and none vacated away, `PASS`, `CONDITIONAL_PASS` and `FAIL` are all acceptable. Success sets `status` to `PENDING_REVIEW`, stamps `submitted_at`, stores the outcome and the observed age groups, and increments the `version`.

A **supervisor** may send the report back. Reopening requires `PENDING_REVIEW` and requires `reopen_count` to be strictly below 2; a third reopen is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`, the same error as a wrong-state reopen. Reopening returns `status` to `IN_PROGRESS` — the revert edge — increments `reopen_count`, and clears `outcome`, `age_groups_observed` and `submitted_at` back to null, which means the inspector must re-declare all of them on the next submission.

**Finalization is the heavy write of this service.** A supervisor finalizes a `PENDING_REVIEW` inspection; when the type is `INITIAL` the provider's `clearance_complete` must be true, or the call is `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`. On success, in this order: `status` becomes `FINALIZED` and `finalized_at` is stamped; every violation of the inspection still in `CITED` moves to `CORRECTION_PENDING` and receives its `correction_due_date`; `violation_count` is recomputed and `cap_required` is set to true exactly when that count is greater than zero; `preliminary_compliance_score` is computed and **frozen forever**; `final_compliance_score` and `raw_deduction_points` are computed; the provider's `open_violation_points`, `resolved_violation_points`, `lifetime_violation_points`, `critical_open_count` and `critical_violation_count_24mo` are recomputed; the provider's two 24-month complaint counters, `substantiated_complaint_count_24mo` and `inconclusive_complaint_count_24mo`, are refreshed from current data in the same sweep; the provider's `risk_score` and `risk_tier` are recomputed under the enforcement-review weighting; and when the type is `INITIAL` and the outcome is `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS`, the provider's `application_status` moves from `CHECKS_CLEARED` to `INITIAL_INSPECTION_PASSED`. The inspection's, the provider's and the assigned inspector's `version` counters all advance: the provider's row is touched unconditionally, even when not one of its counters actually moved, and the inspector's because the visit leaving `PENDING_REVIEW` drops that inspector's `scheduled_visit_count` by one. When an `INITIAL` inspection finalizes with outcome `FAIL` the application status stays at `CHECKS_CLEARED` and the inspection still finalizes normally. Finalization **never** creates an enforcement action: enforcement is always either proposed explicitly or produced by the substantiated-complaint cascade.

**Voiding** cancels a visit that has not been finalized. A supervisor may void from `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `PENDING_REVIEW`; a finalized inspection can never be voided, because the report is a public record from that moment, and the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`. Voiding sets `status` to `VOIDED`, vacates every violation of the inspection whose status is `CITED` or `CORRECTION_PENDING` — recording `vacated_reason` as an inspection-voided note — recomputes the inspector's `scheduled_visit_count`, releasing both the inspector's and the provider's occupied windows, recomputes the provider's violation-point counters — which, because every one of them reads only `FINALIZED` inspections, cannot actually move on this path, so the provider's row is left entirely untouched and its `version` does not advance — and increments the inspection's `version`. Voiding does **not** recompute the inspection's own `violation_count`, its `cap_required` flag or either of its stored scores: those keep whatever values they held at the instant of the void, so a voided visit can legitimately be read back reporting a positive `violation_count` and a non-null score while every citation it carried now sits in `VACATED`. The system voids inspections on its own in three places: the clearance-revert cascade, a licence denial, and an effective revocation.

Inspections are listed with optional `provider_id`, `inspector_id`, `complaint_id`, `status` and `inspection_type` filters — the reverse-reachability surface for all three of the inspection's foreign keys — and a single inspection can be read back carrying both of its compliance scores. Those two reads are open to administrators, applicants, supervisors, inspectors, investigators and the public portal reader — the intake specialist and the background-check unit are the two roles not admitted — while the live scoring read described in §3.5 is narrower still, admitting only administrators, supervisors and inspectors. None of the three applies an ownership filter of any kind: an applicant may list and read the visits of providers other than its own, and an inspector may read a visit assigned to somebody else.

### 3.5. Citations, Point Assessment and the Two Compliance Scores

While a visit is `IN_PROGRESS` the assigned inspector cites findings against the catalog. A citation names the `inspection_id`, the `checklist_item_id` of the requirement breached, the `primary_age_group` the finding concerns, a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters that is not blank, an optional `severity_override` when the inspector judges the breach worse or milder than the catalog default, and an optional `repeat_of_violation_id` pointing at the earlier citation this one repeats. Citing against an inspection that is not `IN_PROGRESS` is `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`. The catalog row's `age_groups_applicable` list is **not** enforced against the citation: a requirement declared applicable to preschoolers only may be cited with `primary_age_group` `INFANT`, and the finding stands with full effect.

The `severity` actually recorded on the citation is the `severity_override` when one is supplied and the checklist item's `default_severity` otherwise; this is the *effective severity*, and every downstream rule — points, correction deadline, critical counting, outcome agreement — reads it rather than the catalog default. The override itself is retained verbatim in `severity_override`, which stays null whenever none was supplied, while `severity` always carries the effective value.

A repeat reference is validated hard. The row named by `repeat_of_violation_id` must belong to the **same provider**, must name the **same `checklist_item_id`**, must have been created within 24 months of the new citation instant with the lower edge exclusive, and must not itself be `VACATED`. Any failure is `ERR_REPEAT_REFERENCE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Same-provider is judged through the parent citation's own inspection, so the parent may be a citation of the very visit now in progress or of any earlier visit of that provider, whatever status that earlier visit is in. From a valid parent, `repeat_chain_depth` is the smaller of 2 and one more than the parent's own depth; with no parent it is 0. The chain therefore saturates: a third-generation repeat has depth 2, and so does a fourth.

`infant_exposure` is true when either the citation's own `primary_age_group` is `INFANT`, **or** the cited checklist item is `infant_sensitive` and `INFANT` appears in the inspection's `age_groups_observed` as that field stands at the instant the citation is written. Because `age_groups_observed` is null for the whole of `IN_PROGRESS` — it is stamped only at submission and is cleared again by a reopen — the second disjunct cannot fire for a citation an inspector records during the visit: such a citation is infant-exposed exactly when its own `primary_age_group` is `INFANT`. The disjunct bites only on the system-generated citations that the substantiated-complaint and late-incident cascades write against an inspection that has already been submitted and therefore already carries observed age groups; that is why an auto-cited `infant_sensitive` requirement recorded against preschoolers, at a visit where infants were also observed, still counts as infant-exposed.

`assessed_points` is then computed once and **frozen at creation** — no later event ever recomputes it. Start from the base points for the effective severity: 15 for `CRITICAL`, 8 for `SERIOUS`, 4 for `MODERATE`, 1 for `MINOR`. If the citation is infant-exposed, add 50 percent of that base. Multiply the result by the repeat multiplier for the chain depth: 1.0 at depth 0, 2.0 at depth 1, 3.0 at depth 2. Round that product to a whole number, rounding a value ending in exactly one half upward. Finally cap the whole number at 30 points. **The step order is normative.** Aggravate first, then multiply for repetition, then round, then cap; rounding before the repeat multiplier, or capping before it, gives different answers. Worked through: a critical infant-exposed first citation goes 15, then 22.5, then 22.5, then rounds to 23; a critical citation with no infant exposure stays 15; a serious infant-exposed citation goes 8, then 12, and stays 12; a moderate non-infant repeat at depth 1 goes 4, then 4, then 8; a minor infant-exposed citation goes 1, then 1.5, and rounds **up** to 2, not down to 1; and a critical infant-exposed repeat at depth 1 goes 15, then 22.5, then 45, and is capped to 30.

A newly cited violation starts in `CITED` with `correction_due_date` and `corrected_at` null, `vacated_reason` null and `auto_generated` false. Citing recomputes the inspection's `violation_count` and both of its scores and increments the inspection's `version`, and recomputes the provider's counters, risk score and tier and increments the provider's `version` — the counters themselves do not move, since they read only finalized inspections, but the row is touched all the same. Citations are listed with optional `inspection_id`, `checklist_item_id`, `repeat_of_violation_id`, `status` and `severity` filters and read individually; both of those reads are open to administrators, applicants, supervisors, inspectors, investigators and the public portal reader, with no ownership filter applied.

**The provider's point counters are definitions, not running totals**, and every one of them is restricted to violations whose owning inspection is `FINALIZED`. Over that source set — all inspections of the provider, all time, no window — `open_violation_points` is the sum of `assessed_points` across violations in `CITED`, `CORRECTION_PENDING` or `UNCORRECTED`; `resolved_violation_points` is the same sum restricted to `VERIFIED_CORRECTED`; and `lifetime_violation_points` is the same sum restricted to any status other than `VACATED`. It follows, and is required to hold after every operation, that lifetime points always equal open points plus resolved points. `critical_open_count` counts the members of the open set whose effective `severity` is `CRITICAL`. `critical_violation_count_24mo` counts violations with severity `CRITICAL` and status other than `VACATED` whose inspection is finalized and whose inspection's `finalized_at` is strictly later than 24 months before the current instant. Because all four counters read only finalized inspections, a citation recorded during a visit that has not yet been finalized contributes nothing to the provider's totals; finalization is the moment the points land.

**Every inspection carries two scores computed by the same arithmetic over different input sets.** For each violation in the input set, the line deduction is the violation's `assessed_points` multiplied by the exposure weight of its `primary_age_group` — 1.335 for `INFANT`, 1.150 for `TODDLER`, 1.000 for `PRESCHOOL`, 0.875 for `SCHOOL_AGE` — multiplied by that violation's status factor, and the product is rounded to two decimal places with halves going up. `raw_deduction_points` is the sum of those already-rounded line deductions, itself rounded to two decimals. The score is 100 minus the raw deduction, floored at 0, rounded to two decimals. **The rounding mode is normative: round each line, then sum.** Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end produces a different answer and is wrong.

The status factors are 1.000 for `CITED`, 1.000 for `CORRECTION_PENDING`, 0.400 for `VERIFIED_CORRECTED`, 1.250 for `UNCORRECTED` and 0.000 for `VACATED`.

- The **preliminary** variant is the as-cited view: how bad the site was on the day of the visit. Its input set **excludes** violations whose status is `VACATED`, and every included violation's status factor is forced to 1.000 regardless of its real status. `preliminary_compliance_score` is rewritten by every citation while the visit is still open and then written one last time at finalization, where it freezes for ever; no disposition, plan completion or cascade ever changes the stored value afterwards.
- The **final** variant is the post-correction view. Its input set **includes every** violation of the inspection, vacated rows included — they contribute exactly zero through their 0.000 factor — and each violation's real status factor applies. `final_compliance_score` and `raw_deduction_points` are stored at finalization and recomputed on every disposition, on plan completion, and by the substantiated-complaint cascade. Because a correction shrinks a line to 0.400 of its weight while a non-correction grows it to 1.250, the final score can end up either above or below the preliminary one.

Both variants are also computable live for any inspection — in any status, a visit that has never been finalized included — through a scoring read that takes an optional `variant` selector naming either the preliminary or the final view. That read answers with a computed object rather than with the inspection row: it carries the echoed `variant`, the `compliance_score` and the `raw_deduction` it has just computed, and the `violation_count` of the rows that fed them — a count that under the final view includes the vacated citations and can therefore exceed the inspection's own stored `violation_count`, while under the preliminary view it is exactly the number of the inspection's non-vacated citations. That read recomputes from current data, so for a frozen preliminary score it can legitimately disagree with the stored field: take an inspection finalized with five citations totalling 82.62 raw points and a stored preliminary score of 17.38, then verify one citation as corrected, mark another uncorrected and vacate a third. The live final view now sums 4.00, 12.28, 38.38, 0.00 and 8.00 to 62.66 raw for a score of 37.34, while the live preliminary view drops the vacated row and forces every factor to 1.000, summing 4.00, 30.71, 30.71 and 8.00 to 73.42 raw for a score of 26.58 — and the stored `preliminary_compliance_score` remains 17.38. Same stored data, three different legitimate numbers, each answering a different question.

**The provider's risk score** is a weighted sum of that provider's own counters and nothing else. Take the smaller of `open_violation_points` and 60, weighted at 1.0. Add 8 points for each complaint counted in `substantiated_complaint_count_24mo`. Add the variant's inconclusive weight for each complaint counted in `inconclusive_complaint_count_24mo`. Add a flat 10 when `critical_violation_count_24mo` is at least 1. Add a flat 15 when `license_status` is `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`. Cap the total at 100 and round to two decimals with halves going up. `risk_tier` then bands the score with inclusive lower edges and exclusive upper edges: `LOW` below 20, `MODERATE` from 20 up to but not including 45, `HIGH` from 45 up to but not including 70, and `SEVERE` at 70 or above. A score of exactly 20.00 is `MODERATE`, exactly 45.00 is `HIGH`, exactly 70.00 is `SEVERE`.

The two counters over complaints, and the 24-month critical counter, all use a lower edge that is strictly exclusive — an event exactly 24 months old has just fallen out of the window — and screened-out complaints and vacated violations never count anywhere.

The two risk variants differ in exactly one weight. Under **intake screening**, inconclusive complaints are weighted **0**: unproven allegations are given no weight at all, so that unsubstantiated reports cannot inflate a provider's public risk profile. Under **enforcement review**, each inconclusive complaint is weighted **4**. The enforcement-review variant is the one used whenever the ladder is evaluated and is the **only** variant ever persisted into `risk_score` and `risk_tier`; the intake-screening variant is used when a complaint is screened and when a public-facing tier is displayed, and is never written to the provider row. A provider read of the risk assessment takes an optional `variant` selector naming either weighting and recomputes on demand, so requesting the intake-screening view returns a number that may be lower than the provider's stored `risk_score` — for a provider with two inconclusive complaints the gap is exactly 8 points — without changing the stored value. That read answers with a computed object rather than with the provider row: it carries the echoed `variant`, the `risk_score` and `risk_tier` it just computed, and the four counters that fed them — `open_violation_points`, `substantiated_complaint_count_24mo`, `inconclusive_complaint_count_24mo` and `critical_violation_count_24mo` — each of those counters itself re-derived from current primary data for the purposes of the response only, so a stale stored counter shows up as a discrepancy between this read and the provider row while nothing is written to either.

### 3.6. Remediation: Corrective Action Plans and Violation Disposition

Once an inspection is finalized, each of its citations sits in `CORRECTION_PENDING` with a `correction_due_date`. That date is the citation instant plus a per-severity allowance: 3 days for `CRITICAL`, 10 days for `SERIOUS`, 30 days for `MODERATE`, 45 days for `MINOR`, read from the citation's effective severity. Despite the field's name the value is a full ISO-8601 UTC instant in the same three-fractional-digit form as every other stamp this service emits, not a bare calendar date, and the instant it counts from is the citation's own `created_at` rather than the finalization instant — so two citations of the same visit created a minute apart carry deadlines a minute apart.

**Dispositioning** a violation supplies a `disposition` of `VERIFIED_CORRECTED`, `UNCORRECTED` or `VACATED`, plus a `vacated_reason` of 1 to 500 characters that is required and must be non-blank when vacating; that conditional requirement is a body-shape rule and so fires before the citation itself is even looked up, and a reason supplied alongside a correction or a non-correction is accepted and then silently discarded rather than stored. The permitted origin states differ per branch: a correction may be recorded from `CORRECTION_PENDING` **or** from `UNCORRECTED` — late correction is explicitly accepted and reverses the earlier finding — a non-correction may be recorded only from `CORRECTION_PENDING`, and a vacation may be recorded from `CITED`, `CORRECTION_PENDING` or `UNCORRECTED`. Anything else is `ERR_INVALID_VIOLATION_STATE` (HTTP 409). A `VERIFIED_CORRECTED` violation is terminal and can never afterwards be vacated.

Authority also differs per branch. An inspector may verify a correction or record a non-correction, but **vacating is supervisor-only**: an inspector who sends a vacating disposition is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), even though the same inspector may use the same capability for the other two dispositions. Neither of those two is tied to the citing inspector or to the visit's assigned inspector: any `LicensingInspector` may record a correction or a non-correction against any citation, and this capability performs no ownership comparison at all. The authority test also precedes the origin-state test, so an inspector who tries to vacate an already `VERIFIED_CORRECTED` citation is answered `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than `ERR_INVALID_VIOLATION_STATE`.

Each branch moves different numbers. A verified correction stamps `corrected_at`, subtracts the violation's `assessed_points` from `open_violation_points` and adds them to `resolved_violation_points`, leaving `lifetime_violation_points` untouched. A non-correction moves no points at all — `UNCORRECTED` is still inside the open set — but the inspection's final score **drops**, because the status factor rises from 1.000 to 1.250. A vacation stores the reason, subtracts the points from **both** `open_violation_points` and `lifetime_violation_points`, leaves `resolved_violation_points` alone, decrements the inspection's `violation_count` by one, and recomputes the final score with a 0.000 factor for that line — restoring exactly the totals that stood before the citation existed. This is the compensating action of a citation, and the conservation identity between lifetime, open and resolved points must hold again once it completes. Every disposition also recomputes `critical_open_count`, the 24-month critical count where applicable, and the provider's `risk_score` and `risk_tier` under enforcement-review weighting, and it recomputes the inspection's `final_compliance_score` and `raw_deduction_points` while never touching `preliminary_compliance_score`. Every disposition advances both the inspection's `version` and the provider's `version` on every branch, including a non-correction that moves no counter at all and a vacation of a citation whose inspection was never finalized and so never contributed to the provider's books.

**A corrective action plan** is the provider's written promise to fix what was cited. Submitting one names the `inspection_id` it answers, the `violation_ids` it covers, a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters that is not blank, a `proposed_completion_date` as an ISO calendar date in year-month-day form, and the `expected_inspection_version` that carries the optimistic-concurrency check; the plan's `provider_id` is not supplied but copied from the inspection. The revision the plan was submitted with is retained on the plan and echoed in its representation as `expected_inspection_version`, and no later resubmission refreshes it. The `proposed_completion_date` is recorded exactly as supplied and compared with nothing at all — not with `due_at`, not with the inspection's `finalized_at` and not with the clock — so a date already in the past is accepted. The inspection must be `FINALIZED`. `violation_ids` must be a non-empty list without duplicates, every element must be a violation whose `inspection_id` equals the plan's inspection and whose status is not `VACATED`, and that inspection must belong to the same provider as the plan; a mismatch is `ERR_VIOLATION_INSPECTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

The submission window is bounded: the current instant must be at or before the inspection's `finalized_at` plus 10 days, with the upper edge **inclusive**. Submitting exactly at the ten-day instant succeeds; one second later is `ERR_CAP_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The same window is re-checked on every resubmission, measured from the same `finalized_at`, so a plan rejected near the end of the window can run out of time to be resubmitted.

The plan's own deadline, `due_at`, is the current instant plus the **minimum** correction allowance across the violations the plan covers — that is, the tightest of the 3, 10, 30 and 45 day allowances implied by those violations' effective severities. A plan covering one critical and one minor violation is therefore due in 3 days, not 45. The plan is created in `SUBMITTED` with `review_cycle_count` at 1, `submitted_at` stamped, and `reviewed_at`, `approved_at`, `completed_at`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` null. Creating a plan for an inspection that already has an open plan **supersedes** the older one: any plan of the same inspection in `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` and has its `version` incremented — but only after the new plan's concurrency check has passed, so a stale submission supersedes nothing. A successful creation also increments the inspection's `version`, which means a client holding the version it just used cannot immediately reuse it.

A supervisor reviews a `SUBMITTED` plan with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `review_note`; the note is **required and must be non-blank when rejecting**, or the call is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Review also re-confirms that the referenced inspection is `FINALIZED`. An approval sets `status` to `APPROVED`, stamps both `reviewed_at` and `approved_at`, records the reviewing identity in `reviewed_by_user_id`, stores the note when one was supplied, and moves every violation named in `violation_ids` that is still `CITED` on to `CORRECTION_PENDING`, stamping each such citation's `correction_due_date` from its own creation instant and effective severity exactly as finalization would. Only when at least one citation actually moves does the approval recompute the inspection's `final_compliance_score` and `raw_deduction_points` and advance the inspection's `version`; because finalization has already emptied `CITED` and both cascades write their auto-citations straight into `CORRECTION_PENDING`, the ordinary approval finds nothing to move and leaves the inspection row untouched. Neither branch recomputes the provider's counters, since `CITED` and `CORRECTION_PENDING` sit inside the same open set. A rejection sets `status` to `REJECTED`, stamps `reviewed_at`, records `reviewed_by_user_id` and stores the mandatory note. Both increment the plan's `version`. The identity captured in `reviewed_by_user_id` is what later disqualifies that same supervisor from issuing enforcement on the same inspection.

The provider may **resubmit** a `REJECTED` plan, and only the owning provider may do so. `review_cycle_count` must be strictly below 3, or the call is `ERR_CAP_REVIEW_CYCLE_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409): with the count starting at 1, this permits two resubmissions, taking the count to 2 and then 3, and refuses the third. Resubmission returns `status` to `SUBMITTED` — closing the submitted-rejected-submitted cycle — increments `review_cycle_count`, re-stamps `submitted_at`, replaces `narrative`, `proposed_completion_date` and `violation_ids` with the new values, recomputes `due_at` from the new violation set, and re-checks both the ten-day submission window and the violation-to-inspection agreement. The window is tested **before** the cycle allowance, so a plan that is both out of time and out of cycles reports `ERR_CAP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Resubmission touches neither the inspection nor the provider, and it leaves the previous review's traces exactly as the rejection left them: `reviewed_at`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` all keep their old values on a plan that is back in `SUBMITTED`.

A supervisor finally records the outcome with a `result` of either `COMPLETED` or `FAILED` against an `APPROVED` plan. A completion **requires** every violation named in `violation_ids` to be already `VERIFIED_CORRECTED` or `VACATED`, or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_VIOLATION_STATE`; it sets `status` to `COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at`, and recomputes the inspection's `final_compliance_score` and `raw_deduction_points` and the provider's `risk_score` and `risk_tier`. A failure **requires** the current instant to be strictly after the plan's `due_at`, so a plan cannot be failed before its own deadline and the attempt is `ERR_CAP_WINDOW_CLOSED`; it sets `status` to `FAILED`, stamps `completed_at`, moves every covered violation still in `CORRECTION_PENDING` to `UNCORRECTED` — which lowers the inspection's final score through the 1.250 factor — and recomputes the provider's risk score and tier. Either result recomputes the inspection's `violation_count`, its `final_compliance_score` and its `raw_deduction_points` together with the provider's whole counter set, and advances both the inspection's and the provider's `version`, even on a completion where not one number changes. Plans are listed with optional `provider_id`, `inspection_id` and `status` filters, and both plan reads admit administrators, supervisors and applicants alike with no ownership filter applied, so an applicant may read a plan belonging to another provider.

### 3.7. Complaint Casework and Serious-Incident Reporting

An allegation reaches the agency through an intake specialist or an administrator. Intake records the `provider_id` complained about, the `intake_channel` — `PHONE`, `WEB`, `MAIL`, `WALK_IN`, `MANDATED_REPORTER` or `AGENCY_INCIDENT` — the `allegation_category` of `ABUSE`, `SUPERVISION`, `RATIO`, `BACKGROUND`, `FACILITY`, `NUTRITION` or `RECORDS`, the `affected_age_group`, a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters that is not blank, and optionally `alleged_serious_harm`, `complainant_name` (1 to 200 characters, not blank) and `anonymous`; `alleged_serious_harm` and `anonymous` each default to false when omitted, and `complainant_name` to null. Two input rules bind at intake: `AGENCY_INCIDENT` is reserved for the system's own use, so a client that names that channel is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (HTTP 422) even though it is a member of the declared set; and an `anonymous` complaint requires `complainant_name` to be omitted or null, and supplying both is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both of those tests sit *after* the whole three-stage field-shape scan of §2.7 and *before* the `provider_id` is resolved, and the reserved-channel test answers before the anonymity test — so a reserved-channel intake naming an unknown provider reports the channel, while a reserved-channel intake that also carries an over-long narrative reports the narrative. The record is created in `RECEIVED` with `received_at` at the logical clock and `screened_at`, `assigned_investigator_id`, `determination`, `determined_at`, `timeliness_flag` and `closure_note` null. Intake writes nothing at all to the provider: no counter moves and the provider's `version` does not change.

**Priority is computed once at intake and frozen there — never recomputed, however the provider's standing later changes.** First reduce the affected age group to a band: infants and toddlers form the young band, preschool and school-age children the older one. Then read the base priority from the category-by-band matrix. Abuse is `P1` in both bands. Supervision is `P1` for the young band and `P2` for the older. Ratio, facility and nutrition are each `P2` for the young band and `P3` for the older. Background is `P2` in both bands. Records is `P3` in both. Then, **if and only if** the provider's `license_status` at intake is `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, escalate one level: `P3` becomes `P2`, `P2` becomes `P1`, and `P1` stays `P1` — the escalation saturates rather than overflowing. Finally, `investigation_due_at` is `received_at` plus the determination allowance for the resulting priority: 30 days for `P1`, 45 for `P2`, 60 for `P3`. Adding whole days preserves the time-of-day component exactly. With the clock at 2026-08-10T00:00:00Z, a supervision allegation about preschoolers at a provider in good standing is `P2` and due 2026-09-24T00:00:00Z; the same allegation about an infant is `P1` and due 2026-09-09T00:00:00Z; the same preschool allegation against a provider on `PROBATION` is escalated to `P1` and due 2026-09-09T00:00:00Z; a records allegation about school-age children is `P3` and due 2026-10-09T00:00:00Z, or `P2` and due 2026-09-24T00:00:00Z if that provider is on probation; and an abuse allegation against a probationary provider is `P1` with the escalation acting as a no-op.

**Screening** is the intake specialist's decision, supplied as `decision` of either `SCREEN_IN` or `SCREEN_OUT`, and requires the complaint to be `RECEIVED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` (HTTP 409), which is the error every complaint-state precondition in this section reports. Screening in moves the complaint to `SCREENED_IN` and stamps `screened_at`. Screening out moves it to the terminal `SCREENED_OUT` and also stamps `screened_at`, leaving `timeliness_flag` null for ever. But **a complaint whose computed priority is `P1` may never be screened out**, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_P1_CANNOT_BE_SCREENED_OUT` (HTTP 409) — including the case where the `P1` arose only from the probation escalation, since the frozen priority is what is tested. That test runs *after* the origin-state test, so a second attempt to screen out an already-`SCREENED_OUT` `P1` reports the state rather than the `P1` rule.

**Assignment** is the supervisor's, supplied as an `investigator_id`. The named inspector must exist, and the segregation rule is then applied — **before either state test**, exactly as §2.7 orders segregation ahead of every state precondition: the proposed investigator must not have finalized **any** inspection of that complaint's provider whose `finalized_at` is strictly later than 12 months before the current instant. A violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The lower edge is exclusive, so an inspection finalized exactly 12 months ago no longer disqualifies its inspector, while one finalized a second later still does. Only then are the two remaining tests taken, in this order: the complaint must be `SCREENED_IN` or the call is `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`, and the named inspector must be `ACTIVE` or the call is `ERR_INSPECTOR_ON_LEAVE`. A conflicted inspector proposed against a complaint that is not `SCREENED_IN` therefore reports the segregation failure, and an on-leave inspector proposed against such a complaint reports the complaint's state. Success moves the complaint to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and records `assigned_investigator_id`.

**The determination is the deepest cascade in the service.** Only the assigned investigator may record it, supplying `determination` as `SUBSTANTIATED`, `UNSUBSTANTIATED` or `INCONCLUSIVE` against an `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` complaint. Two extra preconditions bind only the substantiated branch: a `FINALIZED` inspection whose `complaint_id` is this complaint must exist to carry the automatic citation, or the call is `ERR_COMPLAINT_INSPECTION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409); and the checklist catalog must contain the item whose `code` is the one mapped from the allegation category, or the call is `ERR_CHECKLIST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). That mapping is fixed: abuse and supervision allegations both map to `SU-101`, ratio to `RA-201`, background to `BG-301`, facility to `FA-401`, nutrition to `NU-501`, and records to `RE-601`.

Link one always runs: `status` becomes `DETERMINED`, the `determination` is stored, `determined_at` is stamped, and `timeliness_flag` is set to `ON_TIME` when `determined_at` is at or before `investigation_due_at` — the edge is inclusive — and to `LATE` otherwise. Link one also always closes by recomputing the provider's point counters, its two 24-month complaint counters and its `risk_score` and `risk_tier`, and by incrementing the provider's `version`: an `UNSUBSTANTIATED` or an `INCONCLUSIVE` determination therefore still bumps the provider row, an inconclusive one visibly raising `inconclusive_complaint_count_24mo` and with it the enforcement-review risk score, while touching no inspection, citing nothing and proposing nothing.

Link two runs **only** for a substantiated determination and only *because* the determination is substantiated. One violation is auto-cited with `auto_generated` true. Its target inspection is selected from the candidate set of inspections whose `complaint_id` is this complaint and whose status is `FINALIZED`, ranked by `finalized_at` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; the top-ranked row wins, and the candidate set cannot be empty because that was a precondition. The citation's `checklist_item_id` is the item whose code the allegation category maps to, its `primary_age_group` is the complaint's `affected_age_group`, its `severity` is that item's `default_severity`, its `repeat_of_violation_id` is null, its `assessed_points` follow the ordinary point assessment, and its `correction_due_date` follows the ordinary correction allowance. It is created directly in `CORRECTION_PENDING` rather than `CITED`, because the inspection carrying it is already finalized.

Link three runs *because* that violation now exists: the provider's `open_violation_points`, `lifetime_violation_points`, `critical_open_count`, `critical_violation_count_24mo` and `substantiated_complaint_count_24mo` are recomputed, the provider's `risk_score` and `risk_tier` are recomputed under enforcement-review weighting, and the target inspection's `final_compliance_score`, `raw_deduction_points` and `violation_count` are recomputed. Note that the frozen `preliminary_compliance_score` of that inspection is not touched, so a cascade citation makes the two scores diverge.

Link four runs *because* the recomputed points crossed a threshold, and it is conditional: when the provider's new `open_violation_points` is at least 30 **and** the provider's `current_ladder_step` is strictly below 2, one enforcement action is auto-created in `PROPOSED`, with `source_inspection_id` set to the target inspection, `action_type` and `ladder_step` taken from the ladder rule, `basis_points` set to the recomputed `open_violation_points`, `fine_amount_cents` computed from the fine rule, `requested_action_type` equal to that derived `action_type`, and `rationale` null — an auto-proposed action never carries a rationale. Note that this auto-proposal answers to the 30-point floor alone: it does not re-apply the manual proposal's 15-point basis test or its no-over-enforcement test, both of which it satisfies by construction. If the points stay below 30, or the provider is already at ladder step 2 or higher, the chain stops after link three and no action is created. The determination increments the provider's `version` on every branch, and the target inspection's `version` on the substantiated branch only.

A supervisor **closes** a `DETERMINED` complaint with a `closure_note` — required, 1 to 2000 characters and not blank — which sets `status` to `CLOSED` and moves no provider counter, a closed complaint counting in the 24-month complaint tallies exactly as it did while `DETERMINED`. Closing matters beyond bookkeeping: an open investigation blocks public postings for that provider, and closing releases the block. Complaints are listed with optional `provider_id`, `assigned_investigator_id`, `status`, `priority` and `determination` filters, and a single complaint reads back with its deadline and timeliness flag.

**Serious incidents run on a shorter clock.** A provider, or the agency on its behalf, files a report naming the `provider_id`, the `incident_type` — `FATALITY`, `MISSING_CHILD`, `SERIOUS_INJURY`, `MEDICATION_ERROR` or `COMMUNICABLE_DISEASE` — the `occurred_at` instant, the `affected_child_age_group`, and a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters that is not blank. An `occurred_at` strictly after the logical clock is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_OCCURRED_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422); an incident occurring exactly at the current instant is accepted. `reported_at` is the logical clock at submission. `reporting_deadline_at` is `occurred_at` plus the type's reporting window: 2 hours for a fatality, 2 hours for a missing child, 24 hours for a serious injury, 24 hours for a medication error, 72 hours for a communicable disease. `reporting_timeliness` is `TIMELY` when `reported_at` is at or before that deadline — the upper edge is **inclusive** — and `LATE_REPORT` otherwise, compared on the exact instant to second resolution. A serious injury occurring at 2026-08-05T00:00:00Z is due by 2026-08-06T00:00:00Z: filing exactly then is timely, filing one second later is late. A fatality occurring at 2026-08-05T10:00:00Z is due by 2026-08-05T12:00:00Z, and one second past noon is late. The record is created in `REPORTED` with `late_report_violation_created` false, `generated_complaint_id`, `reviewed_at`, `closed_at` and `agency_note` null.

A late filing triggers a **conditional cascade** at creation. The candidate set is the provider's inspections whose status is `FINALIZED`, ranked by `finalized_at` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending. If that set is non-empty, one violation is auto-cited on the top-ranked inspection with `auto_generated` true, status `CORRECTION_PENDING`, the checklist item whose `code` is `RE-601`, a `severity_override` of `CRITICAL` — its effective `severity` is `CRITICAL` too, so its `assessed_points` are assessed from the critical base of 15 and its `correction_due_date` is the critical three-day allowance — `primary_age_group` set to the report's `affected_child_age_group`, and `repeat_of_violation_id` null, an auto-cited violation never joining a repeat chain; `late_report_violation_created` becomes true, and the target inspection's `violation_count`, `final_compliance_score` and `raw_deduction_points` together with the provider's point counters and risk figures are recomputed, incrementing both of those rows' `version`. If the catalog holds no item whose code is `RE-601`, the whole filing is refused with `ERR_CHECKLIST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) and no report is stored at all. If the set is **empty** — a brand-new provider that has never been inspected — nothing is cited, `late_report_violation_created` stays false, and the report itself is still created successfully. The late filing is recorded either way; only the citation is conditional.

The two operations on a report demand specific origin states, and a wrong one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INCIDENT_STATE` (HTTP 409): dispositioning requires `REPORTED`, and closing accepts `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INVESTIGATION_OPENED`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

A supervisor **dispositions** a `REPORTED` report with a `disposition` of `MONITOR` or `OPEN_INVESTIGATION` and an optional `agency_note`. Monitoring moves the report to `UNDER_REVIEW`, stamps `reviewed_at`, stores the note and leaves `generated_complaint_id` null — but a `FATALITY` or `MISSING_CHILD` report may **never** be merely monitored, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_REQUIRES_INVESTIGATION` (HTTP 409). Opening an investigation moves the report to `INVESTIGATION_OPENED`, stamps `reviewed_at`, stores the `agency_note` when one was supplied, and auto-creates one complaint for the report's provider directly in `SCREENED_IN` — intake screening is implicit for agency-initiated investigations — with `intake_channel` `AGENCY_INCIDENT`, `allegation_category` `ABUSE` when the incident type is a fatality, a serious injury or a missing child and `RECORDS` otherwise, `affected_age_group` copied from the report's `affected_child_age_group`, `narrative` copied from the report's narrative, `alleged_serious_harm` true, `anonymous` false, `complainant_name` null, `priority` `P1` written outright rather than derived from the category-by-band matrix, `received_at` at the current instant, and `investigation_due_at` 30 days later. Its `screened_at` nevertheless stays **null**, because no screening act ever took place — the state was written directly. The new complaint's identifier is stored in the report's `generated_complaint_id`, and the report's own `version` is incremented while the provider's is not. Because that complaint is `P1`, it can never afterwards be screened out, and because it is already `SCREENED_IN` it goes straight to assignment.

A supervisor **closes** a report from `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INVESTIGATION_OPENED`, optionally supplying a final `agency_note`, which sets `status` to `CLOSED` and stamps `closed_at`. One cross-entity precondition applies: when `generated_complaint_id` is not null, that complaint's status must be `CLOSED` or `SCREENED_OUT`, or the close is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` (HTTP 409). A report that opened an investigation therefore cannot be closed while the investigation it started is still running. Reports are listed with optional `provider_id`, `generated_complaint_id`, `status`, `incident_type` and `reporting_timeliness` filters.

### 3.8. Licensure Decisions, the Enforcement Ladder and Appeals

**The licence decision** is the supervisor's, taken with a `decision` of `ISSUE` or `DENY` and an optional `decision_reason`. The two branches accept different application states, and the reason is meaningful on only one of them: `decision_reason` is stored on a denial and is accepted but discarded on an issuance, whose `decision_reason` therefore stays null.

An `ISSUE` requires `application_status` to be `INITIAL_INSPECTION_PASSED`, or the call is `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`; it further requires `clearance_complete` to be true (`ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`) and `critical_open_count` to be exactly 0 (`ERR_UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL_VIOLATION`). On success it moves `application_status` to `LICENSED` and `license_status` from `NONE` to `PROVISIONAL`; stamps both `licensed_at` and `provisional_started_at` with the current instant, so the two are equal at issuance; sets `approved_group_size` equal to `requested_group_size` on every one of the provider's rooms and re-derives all three of that room's compliance flags together with its `required_staff_count`, incrementing each such room's own `version`; sets `licensed_capacity` to the sum of those approved group sizes and `suspended_capacity` to 0; and mints the `license_number`. The number is the literal `LIC-`, then the two digits of the provider's `county_code` after its `CTY-` prefix, then a hyphen, then a sequence number padded with leading zeros to four digits. The sequence is one more than the count of providers in the same county whose `licensed_at` is not null, counted **before** this provider is stamped: the first licence issued in county `CTY-07` is `LIC-07-0001`, the second `LIC-07-0002`, while the first in `CTY-12` is `LIC-12-0001`.

A `DENY` requires `application_status` to be `SUBMITTED` or `CHECKS_CLEARED`. It moves the status to the terminal `DENIED`, stores `decision_reason`, and voids every inspection of that provider whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `PENDING_REVIEW` — note that this denial sweep is wider than the clearance-revert sweep, since it takes pending-review inspections and inspections of every type. Both branches increment the provider's `version`.

**Promotion to full licensure** carries four preconditions and is refused with a different error for each, and they are evaluated in exactly this order, so a request that violates several of them reports the first one listed here: `license_status` must be `PROVISIONAL` (`ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`); there must exist a `FINALIZED` `ANNUAL` inspection of that provider whose outcome is `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS` and whose `finalized_at` is at or after `provisional_started_at`, and its absence is reported as `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`; `critical_open_count` must be 0 (`ERR_UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL_VIOLATION`); the current instant must be at or after `provisional_started_at` plus 6 months, with the lower edge inclusive, so promoting exactly at the six-month instant succeeds and a moment earlier is `ERR_PROVISIONAL_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`; and `open_violation_points` must be exactly 0 (`ERR_OUTSTANDING_VIOLATION_POINTS`). Success sets `license_status` to `FULL` and increments the provider's `version`.

**Restoration from probation** is the other supervisor-driven licence move and its point test is deliberately looser: `license_status` must be `PROBATION`; every corrective action plan of that provider must be in `COMPLETED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and any plan still open, rejected, approved or failed is reported as `ERR_INVALID_CAP_STATE`; `critical_open_count` must be 0 (`ERR_UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL_VIOLATION`); and `open_violation_points` must be **strictly below** 15 rather than zero (`ERR_OUTSTANDING_VIOLATION_POINTS`). Those four are evaluated in exactly the order just listed, so the safety test on open critical violations answers before the point-balance test. Success sets `license_status` to `FULL`, closing the full-to-probation-to-full cycle, resets `current_ladder_step` to 0, and increments the provider's `version`. Restoration is **rejected from `SUSPENDED`**: a suspended licence returns to probation only through a vacated appeal, never through this capability.

**The enforcement ladder has four rungs** — warning, probation, suspension, revocation — numbered 1 through 4 in that order, and a provider's `current_ladder_step` is the highest rung it has reached, 0 meaning it has never been sanctioned.

A supervisor proposes an action naming the `provider_id`, the `requested_action_type`, and optionally a `source_inspection_id` and a `rationale`. Two tests run against the provider's `open_violation_points` read at that instant. First, the points must be at least 15; below that there is no statutory basis at all and the proposal is refused with `ERR_NO_ENFORCEMENT_BASIS` (HTTP 409). Second, the recommended rung is derived from the points by inclusive thresholds — revocation at 60 or more, else suspension at 45 or more, else probation at 30 or more, else warning at 15 or more — and the requested rung's number may not exceed the recommended rung's number. **The agency may under-enforce but never over-enforce**: requesting a lower rung than recommended is always allowed, and requesting a higher one is `ERR_LADDER_STEP_SKIP` (HTTP 409). At 30 points, probation is exactly recommended and a probation proposal is accepted; at 29 points the recommendation is only a warning, so a probation proposal is refused while a warning proposal succeeds; at 14 points every proposal fails on the basis test. Nothing else guards a proposal: the provider's `license_status` is not consulted at all, so a proposal may be raised against a licence in any state, `REVOKED` included, the licence-state compatibility test binding only effectuation; and there is no uniqueness rule, so several proposals may sit in `PROPOSED` for one provider at once until a revocation's sweep withdraws them.

On acceptance, `action_type` is set to the requested type, `ladder_step` to that type's rung number, and `basis_points` is **frozen** at the provider's `open_violation_points` as read at proposal time — later corrections never move it. `status` starts `PROPOSED`, `proposed_at` is stamped, and `issued_at`, `effective_at`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `appealed_at`, `appeal_note`, `resolved_at`, `appeal_decision` and `issued_by_user_id` are null, `rationale` holds the supplied text or null when it was omitted, and `capacity_held_slots` starts at 0. `requested_action_type` records the client's request verbatim, and neither it nor `action_type` is ever rewritten afterwards, so an upheld or a vacated action still reads back at the rung originally requested.

**The fine is computed once, at proposal, and frozen**; neither issuance nor effectuation recomputes it. Begin with the gross amount: the base for the action type — 0 cents for a warning, 50000 for probation, 150000 for suspension, 500000 for revocation — plus 2500 cents for each of the frozen `basis_points`. Derive the pricing tier from that **gross** amount: the first tier below 100000 cents, the second from 100000 up to but not including 300000, the third at 300000 or above. The surcharge percentage is 0, 10 and 20 percent for the three tiers respectively; the credit percentage is 0, 5 and 15 percent. Multiply the gross by the surcharge percentage and round to whole cents with halves going up; add that to the gross to get the net before credit. Multiply that net by the credit percentage, round to whole cents the same way, and subtract it. The result is `fine_amount_cents`. **The tier is derived exactly once, from the gross, and the same tier supplies both percentages** — re-deriving the tier from the net after the surcharge has pushed it across a floor is wrong. A suspension on 50 basis points is worth 150000 plus 125000, so 275000 gross, which is the second tier; the surcharge is 27500, the net 302500, the credit 15125, and the fine 287375. An implementation that re-tiered on the 302500 net into the third tier would take a 15 percent credit of 45375 and return 257125, which is incorrect. A warning on 15 basis points grosses 37500, sits in the first tier, takes no surcharge and no credit, and its fine is 37500 cents.

Each of the four operations that advance an enforcement action demands a specific origin state, and a wrong one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENFORCEMENT_STATE` (HTTP 409): issuance requires `PROPOSED`, effectuation requires `ISSUED`, filing an appeal requires `EFFECTIVE`, and deciding an appeal requires `APPEALED`. `UPHELD`, `VACATED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

**Issuance** is a supervisor's act on a `PROPOSED` action. The segregation rule applies here in its strongest form: the issuing operator must not be the `reviewed_by_user_id` of any corrective action plan whose `inspection_id` equals the action's `source_inspection_id` and whose status is `APPROVED`, `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`. The supervisor who accepted a remediation plan for an inspection may never issue enforcement arising from that same inspection, and the attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that a plan still in `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` does not disqualify anybody — only a plan the operator actually carried past review does. Issuance sets `status` to `ISSUED`, stamps `issued_at`, records `issued_by_user_id`, and sets `appeal_deadline_at` to 15 days after `issued_at`.

**Effectuation** is the administrator's act on an `ISSUED` action, and it is where the licence actually moves. Each action type demands a compatible current `license_status`, and an incompatible one is `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`: a warning requires `PROVISIONAL`, `FULL` or `PROBATION`; probation requires `PROVISIONAL` or `FULL`; suspension requires `FULL` or `PROBATION`; revocation requires `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`. The action becomes `EFFECTIVE` with `effective_at` stamped, and then:

- A **warning** raises `current_ladder_step` to the greater of its current value and 1, and changes `license_status` not at all.
- **Probation** sets `license_status` to `PROBATION`, raises `current_ladder_step` to the greater of its current value and 2, and increments `probation_cycle_count`.
- **Suspension** sets `license_status` to `SUSPENDED`, raises `current_ladder_step` to the greater of its current value and 3, records `capacity_held_slots` as the provider's `licensed_capacity`, adds those slots to `suspended_capacity`, and sets `licensed_capacity` to 0. The sum of licensed and suspended capacity is unchanged by the move — the slots are frozen, not destroyed.
- **Revocation** sets `license_status` to `REVOKED`, sets `current_ladder_step` to 4 outright, records `capacity_held_slots` as `licensed_capacity` plus `suspended_capacity`, and zeroes both. It then sweeps the provider: every licence amendment in `SUBMITTED` or `PRE_INSPECTION_REQUIRED` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, every **other** enforcement action of that provider still in `PROPOSED` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with `resolved_at` stamped, and every inspection in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` becomes `VOIDED`, vacating that visit's open citations as any void does.

Because raising the ladder step uses a maximum rather than an assignment, effectuating a warning on a provider already at step 3 leaves it at 3. **Every** effectuation — a warning's included, not only a revocation's — then refreshes the provider's live public register: every posting of that provider whose `posting_status` is `POSTED` or `CORRECTED` has its `enforcement_banner` and `display_score` recomputed from the provider's new `current_ladder_step` and the posted inspection's current `final_compliance_score`, and its own `version` incremented, while a posting still `PENDING_REDACTION` or already `WITHDRAWN` is left untouched. A revocation therefore stamps every live posting with the revocation banner and a score floored at 0.00, and a warning stamps them with the warning banner and a 2.00 penalty. Every effectuation recomputes the provider's `risk_score` and `risk_tier` under enforcement-review weighting — which, for probation and suspension, gains the flat 15-point standing penalty — and increments the provider's `version`. No operation may leave `suspended_capacity` negative or change the sum of licensed and suspended capacity on a suspension or appeal path; a would-be breach is `ERR_CAPACITY_SLOT_IMBALANCE` (HTTP 409).

**Appeal.** The provider — and only the owning provider — appeals an `EFFECTIVE` action with an `appeal_note`, and the current instant must be at or before `appeal_deadline_at`. **The upper edge is inclusive**: filing at exactly the deadline instant is accepted, and one second later is `ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 409). The action becomes `APPEALED` with `appealed_at` stamped and the note stored. **Filing an appeal does not stay the action**: the provider's `license_status`, `licensed_capacity` and `suspended_capacity` are all left exactly as the effectuation left them.

**The appeal decision** is the administrator's, supplied as `appeal_decision` of `UPHELD` or `VACATED` against an `APPEALED` action, and the deciding operator must not be the action's `issued_by_user_id` — nobody adjudicates their own issuance, and the attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Upholding sets `status` to `UPHELD` and `resolved_at`, and changes no provider field: the action's effects stand. Vacating sets `status` to `VACATED` and `resolved_at`, and then runs the compensation appropriate to the action type:

- Vacating a **suspension** adds `capacity_held_slots` back to `licensed_capacity`, subtracts the same number from `suspended_capacity`, resets `capacity_held_slots` to 0, sets `license_status` to `PROBATION` — never directly back to `FULL` — and sets `current_ladder_step` to 2. The sum of licensed and suspended capacity is bit-for-bit what it was before the effectuation.
- Vacating a **revocation** adds `capacity_held_slots` back to `licensed_capacity`, resets `capacity_held_slots` to 0, sets `license_status` to `PROBATION`, and sets `current_ladder_step` to 2. The amendments withdrawn and the inspections voided by the revocation are **not** resurrected — that part of the sweep is permanent.
- Vacating a **probation** sets `license_status` to `FULL` and `current_ladder_step` to 1. `probation_cycle_count` is **not** decremented: it is a lifetime counter of how many times the provider has ever been placed on probation.
- Vacating a **warning** sets `current_ladder_step` to 0 and changes no licence field.

Every vacating decision recomputes the provider's `risk_score` and `risk_tier` and increments the provider's `version`. Unlike effectuation, **no** appeal decision refreshes the public register: a posting keeps the banner and the `display_score` the effectuation stamped on it until it is itself corrected, so a live posting can go on advertising a banner for an action that has since been vacated. Enforcement actions are listed with optional `provider_id`, `source_inspection_id`, `status` and `action_type` filters and are readable by the public portal reader alongside the agency roles, with their frozen fine and appeal deadline visible. No read anywhere in this arc is owner-scoped: a list or a single read of an enforcement action, an incident report, a licence amendment or a posting returns any provider's rows to any admitted role, and any `ComplaintInvestigator` may likewise read any complaint — in this arc ownership binds the mutating operations only.

### 3.9. Amendments to the Licence Record and the Public Inspection Register

A licensee changes its own licence through an amendment. Requesting one names the `provider_id`, the `amendment_type` — `CAPACITY_INCREASE`, `CAPACITY_DECREASE`, `SITE_MOVE`, `AGE_GROUP_ADD` or `DIRECTOR_CHANGE` — and the `expected_provider_version` that carries the concurrency guard, plus whichever of `requested_capacity`, `new_site_address`, `new_age_group`, `new_group_size` and `new_director_staff_member_id` the type needs: a capacity increase or decrease needs `requested_capacity` (an integer of at least 1); a site move needs `new_site_address` (1 to 300 characters, not blank); an age-group addition needs **both** `new_age_group` and `new_group_size` (at least 1); and a director change needs `new_director_staff_member_id`. A missing one of those is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, tested before the `provider_id` is resolved, so an incomplete request against an unknown provider reports the missing field. A field the type does not need is accepted and stored verbatim when supplied and plays no part in the type's effect. The person named by `new_director_staff_member_id` must exist (`ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`) but need **not** belong to the amending provider — no cross-provider agreement is checked on that field.

`requires_inspection` is derived from the type, never supplied: a site move, an age-group addition and a capacity amendment whose `effective_capacity_delta` is greater than zero all require a pre-move visit; a capacity amendment whose delta is zero or negative and a director change do not. When it is true the record is created **directly** in `PRE_INSPECTION_REQUIRED`; when false, directly in `SUBMITTED`. Both are therefore legitimate creation states.

`effective_capacity_delta` is computed at creation and stored: for a capacity amendment it is the `requested_capacity` minus the provider's current `licensed_capacity`, so a decrease yields a negative number; for an age-group addition it is the `new_group_size`; and for a site move or a director change it is 0. The type name never overrides the arithmetic: a `CAPACITY_DECREASE` whose `requested_capacity` is above the provider's current `licensed_capacity` yields a *positive* delta and is thereafter treated in every respect as an increase — it requires a pre-move visit, it is guarded by the unresolved-critical and ceiling tests, and on approval it raises `licensed_capacity`. Nor is the provider's standing consulted: an amendment may be requested against a licence in any `license_status`, `NONE` included. Creating an amendment **supersedes** the provider's earlier open amendment *of the same type*: any amendment of that provider and type in `SUBMITTED` or `PRE_INSPECTION_REQUIRED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `decided_at` stamped and its `version` incremented. Amendments of *different* types coexist untouched. As with plans, superseding happens only after the concurrency guard passes. Creation stamps `submitted_at`, leaves `decided_at`, `decision_rationale` and `pre_move_inspection_id` null, and increments the provider's `version` — which means the very version the client just supplied is stale for its next request.

Each amendment operation demands a specific origin state, and a wrong one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE` (HTTP 409): attaching the pre-move inspection requires `PRE_INSPECTION_REQUIRED`, deciding requires `SUBMITTED`, and withdrawing accepts either `SUBMITTED` or `PRE_INSPECTION_REQUIRED`. `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

A supervisor **attaches the pre-move inspection** to an amendment in `PRE_INSPECTION_REQUIRED`, supplying a `pre_move_inspection_id`. Five conditions are checked, each with its own error, and they are evaluated in exactly the order §2.7 prescribes: the inspection must exist (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`); it must be `FINALIZED` (`ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`); the amendment must be awaiting its pre-move visit (`ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE`); the inspection's `inspection_type` must equal `AMENDMENT_PRE_MOVE` (`ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_TYPE`, HTTP 409), the required-type test being the last of the state checks; and only then must the inspection belong to the same provider as the amendment (`ERR_VIOLATION_INSPECTION_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422), that referential-agreement test answering last of all. A finalized inspection of the right provider but the wrong type is therefore refused on the type, not the state, and a finalized `AMENDMENT_PRE_MOVE` visit of the *wrong* provider is refused on the mismatch. Success records the identifier, moves the amendment to `SUBMITTED`, and increments its `version`.

An administrator then **decides** a `SUBMITTED` amendment with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `decision_rationale`, required and non-blank for a denial — a requirement tested before the amendment identifier is resolved, so a rationale-less denial aimed at an unknown amendment reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Approval is guarded three ways, and **all three are evaluated in exactly this order**. First, for a capacity amendment whose delta is positive, `critical_open_count` must be 0, or the approval is `ERR_UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL_VIOLATION`. Second, no enforcement action of that provider may be `EFFECTIVE` with `action_type` `SUSPENSION` or `REVOCATION`, or the approval is `ERR_ACTIVE_ENFORCEMENT_BLOCKS_AMENDMENT` (HTTP 409) — a suspended provider cannot enlarge or move its programme. Third, for any capacity amendment, the provider's `licensed_capacity` plus `suspended_capacity` plus the `effective_capacity_delta` must be at or below the programme ceiling for its `program_type`, inclusive, or the approval is `ERR_CAPACITY_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. **That whole order is observable**: the unresolved-critical safety check is evaluated **before** the capacity-ceiling fiscal check, exactly as §2.7 states, and before the active-enforcement block as well, so a capacity increase that trips all three reports the critical violation, while one blocked only by an effective suspension and by the ceiling reports the suspension.

An approval sets `status` to `APPROVED`, stamps `decided_at`, and applies the amendment's effect to the provider: a capacity amendment adds `effective_capacity_delta` to `licensed_capacity` while leaving `capacity_ceiling` untouched; a site move replaces `site_address` with `new_site_address`; a director change writes **nothing** to the provider, because the `Provider` carries no director field of its own — the designated director is simply what the approved amendment's `new_director_staff_member_id` records, and the approval's only visible effects are the amendment's status, `decided_at` and `version` together with the provider's `version` bump; and an age-group addition creates a new room for `new_age_group` with both `requested_group_size` and `approved_group_size` set to `new_group_size`, `enrolled_count` and `staff_assigned_count` at 0, `lead_staff_member_id` and `last_verified_at` null, `required_staff_count` at 0 and all three compliance flags true, an empty room being trivially compliant. An age-group addition does **not** move `licensed_capacity`, even though its `effective_capacity_delta` is the positive `new_group_size`: only a capacity amendment moves that field, and the ceiling test binds only capacity amendments too, so this route can leave the sum of the provider's approved group sizes above its `licensed_capacity`. A denial sets `status` to `DENIED`, stamps `decided_at`, stores the rationale, and changes no provider field. Both increment the amendment's `version`, and an approval also increments the provider's. The owning provider may instead **withdraw** an amendment from `SUBMITTED` or `PRE_INSPECTION_REQUIRED`, which sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, stamps `decided_at` and increments the `version`; the system performs the same withdrawal on every open amendment when a revocation becomes effective. Amendments are listed with optional `provider_id`, `pre_move_inspection_id`, `new_director_staff_member_id`, `status` and `amendment_type` filters.

**The public register is the last stage of the arc.** An administrator prepares a posting for a `FINALIZED` inspection, supplying the `inspection_id`, a `consumer_summary` and optionally `redaction_complete`, which defaults to false. The record is created in `PENDING_REDACTION` with `provider_id` copied from the inspection rather than supplied, `display_score`, `posted_at`, `expires_at`, `corrected_at` and `withdrawn_at` null, `enforcement_banner` at `NONE` and `correction_count` at 0. An inspection that is not `FINALIZED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE` — a test taken before the uniqueness test — and **at most one posting may ever exist per inspection**: a second attempt for the same `inspection_id` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that slot is never released, so even a `WITHDRAWN` posting permanently prevents its inspection from being posted again.

The two operations on a posting demand specific origin states, and a wrong one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POSTING_STATE` (HTTP 409): publishing requires `PENDING_REDACTION`, and revising accepts `POSTED` or `CORRECTED`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

Making a posting public runs four tests **in exactly this order**: the referenced inspection must still be `FINALIZED`, or the call is `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`; the posting must be `PENDING_REDACTION`; **no complaint of that posting's provider may be in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` at that moment**, or the call is `ERR_POSTING_BLOCKED_OPEN_INVESTIGATION` (HTTP 409); and `redaction_complete` must be true, or the call is `ERR_REDACTION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). An unredacted posting held by an open investigation therefore reports the investigation, not the redaction. That investigation condition is evaluated live against every complaint of the provider, not merely those connected to the inspection being posted, which is why closing an unrelated investigation can unblock a posting. On success `posting_status` becomes `POSTED`, `posted_at` is stamped, and `expires_at` is set to 36 months after that instant.

The consumer-facing number is not the inspection's score verbatim. The posting's `enforcement_banner` is read from the provider's `current_ladder_step`: `NONE` at step 0, `WARNING` at 1, `PROBATION` at 2, `SUSPENSION` at 3, `REVOCATION` at 4. `display_score` is then the inspection's `final_compliance_score` minus that banner's penalty — 0.00 for no banner, 2.00 for a warning, 5.00 for probation, 10.00 for a suspension, 20.00 for a revocation — floored at 0 and rounded to two decimals with halves going up. A provider on probation with a final score of 37.34 is published as 32.34; a revoked provider with a final score of 15.00 is published as 0.00 rather than a negative number.

An administrator **revises** a posting that is `POSTED` or `CORRECTED`, supplying an `action` of `CORRECT` or `WITHDRAW` and a `revision_note` that is required and non-blank for both branches and is stored on the posting on both. A correction requires `correction_count` to be strictly below 2, or it is `ERR_POSTING_CORRECTION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409); it sets `posting_status` to `CORRECTED`, increments `correction_count`, stamps `corrected_at`, and recomputes `display_score` and `enforcement_banner` from the inspection's and provider's current values — so a posting corrected after the provider slid down the ladder shows the harsher banner and the lower score. A posting already `CORRECTED` may be corrected again: that self-loop is the cyclic element of this lifecycle, and it is bounded, so the second correction succeeds and takes the count from 1 to 2 while the third is refused. A withdrawal sets `posting_status` to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, stamps `withdrawn_at`, sets `display_score` to null while leaving `enforcement_banner` at whatever it last carried and `correction_count` where it stood, and excludes the posting from the public register — and because a later enforcement effectuation refreshes only live postings, a withdrawn one is never restamped again. Either branch increments the posting's `version`. Postings are listed with optional `provider_id`, `inspection_id` and `posting_status` filters, and both the list and the single read are open to the public portal reader.

## 4. API Endpoints

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/licensing` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described in §2.4. Successful responses carry HTTP 201 when the call creates a new resource and HTTP 200 for every other success, including state-transition actions, updates and reads; there are no 204 responses, because every operation returns either the affected resource or a computed object in the `data` field of the standard envelope. List endpoints accept the `limit` and `offset` pagination parameters of §2.3 in addition to the filters named in their descriptions, and all filter parameters are optional and default to unset. The `Authorized Role(s)` column is exhaustive: a request whose `X-User-Role` is not listed for the endpoint it addresses is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
