# Ski Area Mountain Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A ski area lives or dies by a single daily question: *what can we open, and can we keep it open?* Answering it means reconciling facts that arrive from a dozen different places and change hour by hour — how deep the settled base measured at the snow stakes actually is, whether the cat crew finished its route overnight, whether the serving lift passed its mechanical inspection inside the last twenty-four hours, whether the wind is above the hold threshold on the top-terminal anemometer, whether patrol has enough bodies on the hill, and whether the snowmaking plant has water left on the season's withdrawal right. Today that reconciliation happens on whiteboards, radios and spreadsheets, and it fails in the expensive direction: terrain opens without a current avalanche clearance, guns run in marginal wet-bulb conditions and produce slush, or an operator is double-booked across two lifts an hour apart.

The Ski Area Mountain Operations API is the system of record for that reconciliation. It is mounted under `/mountain-ops` and it models the mountain as a set of interlocking operational objects — a season configuration that carries the water right and the energy price windows, lifts with independent service and mechanical state, terrain areas with independent opening and terrain-park state, snowmaking sessions metered against a water ledger, overnight grooming tasks sequenced onto cat routes, patrollers with certifications and duty status, avalanche mitigation missions, patrol incidents, and a per-date operating day plan that carries weather, comfortable carrying capacity and guest admission.

Two properties drive the design. First, **every readiness fact is derived, not asserted**: base depth is recomputed from stake readings, groom currency is recomputed from the last completed grooming task, carrying capacity is recomputed from open lift capacity and open acreage, and the season water ledger is recomputed from session state. An operator never types in a readiness flag. Second, **deterioration cascades and improvement does not**: a wind reading above a lift's threshold pushes that lift onto a hold, which pushes every terrain area it serves onto a patrol hold, which defers that area's grooming and aborts its snowmaking, which releases the aborted sessions' water reservations back to the season ledger — all in one request. Recovery, by contrast, is always an explicit, individually gated decision. The specification below defines that behaviour precisely enough that two independent implementations produce byte-identical results.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold everywhere in the API: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and versioning, rounding discipline, the ordering of validation, optimistic concurrency, and the cascade discipline that ties the mountain's objects together.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six recognised roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not permitted to invoke the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `MountainOpsDirector`, `SnowmakingSupervisor`, `GroomingLead`, `LiftOpsManager`, `Patroller`, and `GuestServicesAgent`. The `MountainOpsDirector` owns season configuration, the terrain inventory, patrol rostering, the operating day and the daily open/close decision. The `SnowmakingSupervisor` owns the snowmaking plant — pump houses, session scheduling against the water right, and the run cycle. The `GroomingLead` plans and dispatches cat routes and closes out coverage. The `LiftOpsManager` owns lift mechanical inspections, lift service posture including wind-hold release, and the operator roster. A `Patroller` executes and signs off avalanche mitigation, inspects terrain parks, and owns the medical side of incident response. A `GuestServicesAgent` sells and manages day access.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Patroller`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patroller` row; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references a patroller compare that resource's patroller reference — `executed_by_patroller_id`, `cleared_by_patroller_id` or `responder_patroller_id` — to `X-User-ID`. A `Patroller`-owned action whose `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing patroller row is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `MountainOpsDirector`, `SnowmakingSupervisor`, `GroomingLead`, `LiftOpsManager` or `GuestServicesAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server may record it — for example as `park_built_by_user_id` on a terrain area when a `GroomingLead` records a park build — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. Because a terrain park may be built by either a `GroomingLead` or a `Patroller`, `park_built_by_user_id` and `park_inspected_by_user_id` are stored as raw header values and compared to one another as strings, never resolved as foreign keys.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A value outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered set before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation the server applies to that row — including mutations the row suffers as the downstream victim of a cascade, not only mutations the caller aimed at it directly. The one exception is the snow stake reading: a `SnowDepthReading` is an immutable observation and carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in its representation.

All time in this system is read from a **logical clock** rather than from wall-clock time. Before any call is made the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; the clock may be advanced or rewound, and setting it mutates no entity row and fires no cascade. Every expiry, window, freshness and elapsed-time rule in this document evaluates against the logical clock at the moment the request is handled.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Templated path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve decides the error. Each resource family has its own not-found code: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERRAIN_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUMP_HOUSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GROOMING_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATROLLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DAY_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. A templated segment that resolves to no row and has no family-specific code falls back to `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

References carried in a request body are resolved the same way, in the order the fields are declared on the endpoint: `season_id`, then `serving_lift_id`, then `terrain_area_id`, then `pump_house_id`, then `lift_id`, then `day_plan_id`, then `assigned_terrain_area_id`, then `patroller_id`. Each unresolvable reference raises its own family's not-found code. A reference that *does* resolve but belongs to a different season than a sibling reference in the same request — a terrain area whose serving lift lives in another season, for instance — is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_SEASON_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). Cross-season checking applies to season creation of dependent inventory and workflow rows: terrain area creation, lift registration, pump house registration, snowmaking session creation, patroller rostering, day plan creation, and incident dispatch.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Rounding in this system is always **half-up**, and it is always applied at the step the rule names — never deferred and applied once at the end. The stored precision of each derived quantity is fixed:

- Snow depths and grooming percentages are 1 decimal place: `base_depth_in`, `groom_coverage_pct` and `coverage_pct`. Terrain acreage totals (`terrain_open_acres`, and the reported open and openable acreages) are also 1 decimal place, as is `park_risk_score`, `terrain_open_pct`, `wind_speed_mph`, `wet_bulb_f` and `new_snow_in`.
- Water volumes in acre-feet are 3 decimal places: `water_right_seasonal_af`, `water_reserved_af`, `water_used_af`, `water_af_reserved`, `water_af_actual`, `snow_swe_af`, `snow_produced_swe_af` and `machine_swe_af`.
- Energy is 3 decimal places for kilowatt-hours (`energy_kwh`) and 2 decimal places for currency (`energy_cost_usd`).
- Guest counts, carrying capacities, parking stalls, gallons per minute, charges, passes and minutes are integers. Where an integer is derived from a product, the reduction is a **floor** (truncation toward zero) unless the rule explicitly says half-up; `raw_ccc`, `guest_capacity` and `non_season_pool` all floor, while `response_minutes` rounds half-up.
- Where a percentage or score is capped, the cap is applied **after** rounding, not before.

Two derived scores are exact rather than rounded: `snow_yield_factor` takes one of a small set of exact two-decimal values, and no rounding is applied when selecting which one.

### 2.7. The Validation Ladder

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract: the **first** failing check decides the response, and later checks are never reported. Validation runs in the following strict priority order, lowest number first.

1. Header presence and role recognition — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. Role permission for the endpoint, and, for `Patroller`-owned actions, resolution of `X-User-ID` to an existing patroller — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. Left-to-right resolution of every templated path segment — the family-specific 404 code, or `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint that has a templated segment.
4. Resolution of every body reference in declaration order, and the cross-season consistency check — `ERR_CROSS_SEASON_REFERENCE` (422) for the mismatch case, on terrain area creation, lift registration, pump house registration, snowmaking session creation, patroller rostering, day plan creation and incident dispatch.
5. Request-body schema conformance — required fields present, types correct, enum values inside their declared domains, numbers inside their declared ranges, arrays inside their declared cardinality — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint.
6. Interval sanity: every declared window has its start strictly before its end — `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (422), on snowmaking session creation, grooming task planning and lift staff assignment creation.
7. Energy price windows tile the whole day exactly — `ERR_ENERGY_WINDOW_COVERAGE_GAP` (422), on season creation.
8. A winch grooming task names its anchor — `ERR_WINCH_ANCHOR_REQUIRED` (422), on grooming task planning.
9. A stake reading is not timestamped in the future — `ERR_READING_TIMESTAMP_FUTURE` (422), on stake reading recording.
10. Scoped uniqueness of codes — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409), on season creation, lift registration, terrain area creation, pump house registration, patroller rostering, grooming task planning, day plan creation and reservation creation.
11. The governing season lifecycle state permits the write — `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPERATING` (409), on every write endpoint under the mountain-operations mount except season creation itself.
12. The target terrain area's opening state permits the operation — `ERR_TERRAIN_STATE_INVALID` (409), on snowmaking session creation, grooming task dispatch, the opening decision, park build and mitigation execution.
13. The target snowmaking session's state permits the requested target state — `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID` (409), on the session status change.
14. The target lift's service status permits the requested target status — `ERR_LIFT_STATE_INVALID` (409), on the lift service-status change.
15. The target workflow row is in a state that accepts the action — `ERR_WORKFLOW_STATE_INVALID` (409), on grooming task dispatch and completion, mitigation execution and clearance, incident dispatch, transport and close, lift staff assignment release, and reservation check-in and release.
16. The season has an active day plan, and where the operation requires it the plan's posture is a published one — `ERR_DAY_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409), on the opening decision, the lift service-status change, the session status change, reservation creation and reservation check-in.
17. The declared terrain area version matches the stored one — `ERR_STALE_TERRAIN_AREA_VERSION` (412), on snowmaking session creation.
18. The declared day plan version matches the stored one — `ERR_STALE_DAY_PLAN_VERSION` (412), on reservation creation.
19. Segregation of duties on `X-User-ID` — `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` (409), on mitigation clearance and park inspection.
20. The acting patroller is on duty — `ERR_PATROLLER_NOT_ON_DUTY` (422), on mitigation execution, mitigation clearance, park inspection and incident dispatch.
21. Blaster certification for the method — `ERR_BLASTER_CERT_REQUIRED` (422), on mitigation execution.
22. A ski-cut partner is available — `ERR_SKI_CUT_PARTNER_REQUIRED` (422), on mitigation execution.
23. Medical certification floor — `ERR_PATROLLER_CERT_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on mitigation clearance and incident dispatch.
24. The nominated responder is not already committed — `ERR_PATROLLER_BUSY` (409), on incident dispatch.
25. Base depth meets the area's minimum — `ERR_BASE_DEPTH_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on the opening decision.
26. Groom currency — `ERR_GROOM_NOT_CURRENT` (422), on the opening decision.
27. Avalanche clearance currency — `ERR_MITIGATION_NOT_CURRENT` (422), on the opening decision.
28. Patrol staffing floor — `ERR_PATROL_STAFFING_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on the opening decision.
29. Terrain park inspection — `ERR_PARK_NOT_INSPECTED` (422), on the opening decision.
30. Terrain park risk ceiling — `ERR_PARK_RISK_TOO_HIGH` (422), on the opening decision.
31. The serving lift is open — `ERR_SERVING_LIFT_NOT_OPEN` (422), on the opening decision.
32. The lift's mechanical inspection is current — `ERR_LIFT_INSPECTION_EXPIRED` (422), on the lift service-status change and on the opening decision.
33. Wind is inside the hold-release band — `ERR_WIND_ABOVE_RELEASE_THRESHOLD` (422), on the lift service-status change.
34. Wet bulb is cold enough to make snow — `ERR_WET_BULB_TOO_WARM` (422), on the session status change.
35. The pump house is online — `ERR_PUMP_HOUSE_OFFLINE` (422), on snowmaking session creation and the session status change.
36. The transport mode is allowed at the incident's severity — `ERR_TRANSPORT_MODE_NOT_ALLOWED` (422), on ordering transport.
37. The lift has enough covering operators — `ERR_LIFT_OPERATORS_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on the lift service-status change.
38. Lift role slots are not double-filled — `ERR_LIFT_ROLE_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409), on lift staff assignment creation.
39. Operator turnaround between shifts — `ERR_OPERATOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409), on lift staff assignment creation.
40. Cat-route sequencing — `ERR_GROOM_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` (409), on grooming task dispatch.
41. Snowcat exclusivity — `ERR_SNOWCAT_CONFLICT` (409), on grooming task dispatch.
42. Pump-house concurrent flow ceiling — `ERR_PUMP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), on snowmaking session creation.
43. Plant-wide concurrent flow ceiling — `ERR_PLANT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), on snowmaking session creation.
44. The seasonal water right — `ERR_WATER_RIGHT_EXCEEDED` (422), on snowmaking session creation.

Three consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. The eight readiness gates of the opening decision run in exactly the order 25 through 32, so an area that fails base depth *and* has an unopened lift reports `ERR_BASE_DEPTH_INSUFFICIENT`, never `ERR_SERVING_LIFT_NOT_OPEN`. On grooming dispatch, cat-route sequencing is checked before snowcat exclusivity, so a task that violates both reports `ERR_GROOM_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`. And on snowmaking session creation the three quantitative ceilings run last and in the order pump house, then plant, then water right — a request that busts the pump house and the plant simultaneously reports `ERR_PUMP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, and a request that clears both flow ceilings but exceeds the season's remaining withdrawal allowance reports `ERR_WATER_RIGHT_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.8. Stale-Read Protection on Plan-Bearing Writes

Two write operations commit scarce, shared resources on the strength of a parent record the caller read moments earlier. Both require the caller to declare the parent's `version` as a non-optional field, and both reject a declaration that no longer matches.

Snowmaking session creation must carry `expected_terrain_area_version`, holding the `version` of the target `TerrainArea` as the client last observed it. Scheduling a session reserves water against the season ledger, commits pump flow across the requested window, and may supersede an overlapping session on the same area — all decided from the area's current opening state, base depth and groom readiness. If `expected_terrain_area_version` does not equal the terrain area's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TERRAIN_AREA_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and the caller must re-read the area and re-derive the plan from fresh gate inputs. This check sits at priority 17 — after existence, schema, interval, uniqueness and state checks, and before the safety, staffing and capacity checks.

Access reservation creation must carry `expected_day_plan_version`, holding the `version` of the target `DayPlan` as the client last observed it. Admission is allocated against `guest_capacity`, `non_season_pool` and `parking_stalls_total`, every one of which moves whenever terrain opens, a lift goes on hold, or another party is admitted or released. If `expected_day_plan_version` does not equal the day plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DAY_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so an admission decision can never be made against a capacity figure the server has already superseded. This check sits at priority 18 — after existence, schema, uniqueness and posture checks, and before the waterfall allocation and its parking check.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, session status changes, opening decisions, lift service-status changes, grooming dispatch and reservation check-in and release carry no expected-version input and are never rejected on version grounds.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Deterministic Ordering

A single request routinely mutates rows the caller never named. The rule is that **deterioration propagates automatically and recovery does not**. The full deterioration chain, from its deepest head, runs: a weather observation puts lifts on a wind hold → each held lift pushes every terrain area it serves that was open onto a patrol hold → each held area defers its planned and dispatched grooming tasks and aborts its scheduled, running and paused snowmaking sessions → each aborted session releases its water reservation back to the season ledger → the active day plan's counters, carrying capacity and posture are recomputed. Every row touched anywhere along that chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1.

Because these cascades touch collections, iteration order is normative and is never the row `id`:

- Lifts are processed in ascending `lift_code` order.
- Terrain areas are processed in ascending `trail_code` order.
- Grooming tasks within an area are processed in ascending `sequence_index` order.
- Snowmaking sessions within an area are processed in ascending `created_at` order.
- Waitlisted reservations are processed in ascending `created_at` order.

Recovery is always an explicit, individually gated act. Releasing a wind hold, passing a mechanical inspection, or putting a patroller back on duty may make an area eligible to reopen, but an area sitting on a patrol hold is only returned to service when every one of the opening gates is re-satisfied at the current logical clock — and a duty change or an incident closure alone never auto-reopens a held area.

### 2.10. The Active Day Plan

Almost every operational rule needs "today's" weather, posture and capacity, and the system resolves that to exactly one record called the **active day plan**. The candidates are the day plans of the season whose `posture` is `PUBLISHED_PARTIAL`, `PUBLISHED_FULL` or `WEATHER_HOLD`. Those candidates are ranked by `operating_date` descending, and where two share a date, by `created_at` descending; the first candidate is the active day plan. The row `id` is never a ranking input. Plans in `DRAFT` and `DAY_CLOSED` are excluded; a plan on `WEATHER_HOLD` deliberately remains active, so that wind readings and hold releases still resolve against it. If no candidate exists — a season whose only plan is still a draft, for example — every operation that needs an active plan is rejected with `ERR_DAY_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). Because two plans of one season can never share an `operating_date`, the `created_at` tiebreak is a formality rather than a routine path.

Operations that require an active day plan are the terrain opening decision, the lift service-status change when the target is the open posture, snowmaking session status changes, reservation creation and reservation check-in. For reservation creation and check-in the posture must additionally be `PUBLISHED_PARTIAL` or `PUBLISHED_FULL`: a `WEATHER_HOLD` posture blocks admission and gate check-in, while still permitting a wind-hold release.

### 2.11. The Season Water and Snow Ledger

The season configuration carries a running ledger of its snowmaking withdrawal, and that ledger obeys a strict identity at all times: **water drawn plus water reserved plus water remaining equals the seasonal water right**. Concretely, `water_used_af` accumulates the metered draw of completed sessions, `water_reserved_af` holds the planned reservations of sessions that are still scheduled, running or paused, and the remaining allowance is the seasonal right less both figures. Creating a session adds its planned reservation to `water_reserved_af`; completing a session removes that reservation and adds the metered actual to `water_used_af`; aborting a session removes the reservation and adds nothing at all, which returns the ledger exactly to its pre-create value. `snow_produced_swe_af` accumulates the snow-water-equivalent yield of completed sessions only. Aborted sessions contribute to none of the three ledgers.

The season configuration also carries `open_area_count`, the number of its terrain areas currently in the open state, and it is recomputed on every opening decision and on every cascade that moves an area in or out of the open state.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Season and the Mountain Inventory

Everything begins with a season configuration, created by the `MountainOpsDirector`. The payload declares a `season_label` — a human label such as "2026-27", between 1 and 32 characters and unique across every season ever created — together with the four numbers that bound the winter: `water_right_seasonal_af`, the seasonal withdrawal allowance in acre-feet, which must be greater than zero and is held to three decimal places; `plant_max_gpm`, the plant-wide pumping ceiling in gallons per minute across every pump house of the season, which must be at least 1; `snowmaking_kwh_per_af`, the energy intensity of pumping expressed as kilowatt-hours consumed per acre-foot delivered, which must be greater than zero; and `energy_windows`, the daily-recurring price schedule described below. Three readiness policies are optional and carry defaults: `base_depth_policy` selects which stake statistic defines an area's base depth and is either `MEAN_OF_STAKES` (the default) or `MIN_OF_STAKES`; `min_patrollers_per_open_area` is the number of available patrollers an area must carry to stay open and defaults to 2 with a minimum of 1; and `groom_freshness_hours` is how long a completed groom keeps an area current and defaults to 18 hours with a minimum of 1.

The `energy_windows` array is the fussiest part of the payload. It holds between 1 and 6 entries, each an object with a `code` label, an integer `start_hour_utc`, an integer `end_hour_utc`, a `price_usd_per_kwh` rate, and a `tier` drawn from `OFF_PEAK`, `MID_PEAK`, `ON_PEAK` and `CRITICAL_PEAK`. Each entry covers the half-open span of UTC hours from its start hour up to but not including its end hour, so a window's end hour is the next window's start hour. Every entry must satisfy a start hour of at least 0, strictly below its end hour, and an end hour of at most 24. Taken together and sorted by start hour, the entries must tile the whole day exactly: the first must start at hour 0, the last must end at hour 24, consecutive entries must touch with no gap, and no two may overlap. A submitted set with a hole, an overlap, or a missing day-end is rejected with `ERR_ENERGY_WINDOW_COVERAGE_GAP`.

A newly created season starts in `season_state` `PRESEASON`, with `water_reserved_af`, `water_used_af` and `snow_produced_swe_af` all at 0.0 and `open_area_count` at 0. The season stays in `PRESEASON` — during which grooming, snowmaking, stake reading, mitigation and inventory work all proceed normally — until the first day plan of that season is published, at which point the season advances `PRESEASON → OPERATING` and its `version` increments by 1. It reaches the terminal `CLOSED` state only through a final day close, described in §3.9. While `season_state` is `CLOSED`, every write against that season's objects is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPERATING`; the terrain opening decision, snowmaking session status changes and guest admission additionally require `OPERATING` rather than merely not-closed, whereas publishing a day plan is permitted from either `PRESEASON` or `OPERATING`, precisely because the first publish is what performs the promotion.

Lifts are registered next, by either a `LiftOpsManager` or the `MountainOpsDirector`. A registration names its `season_id`, a `lift_code` unique within that season (for example "L1"), a display `name`, a `lift_type` of `SURFACE`, `FIXED_GRIP`, `DETACHABLE` or `GONDOLA`, a `wind_hold_threshold_mph` greater than zero and carried to one decimal place, and an `hourly_capacity_pph` of at least 1 describing uphill capacity in people per hour while the lift is running. `required_operator_count`, the number of covering operator assignments needed to run the lift, is optional and defaults to 2 with a minimum of 1. A fresh lift lands with `service_status` `CLOSED`, `mechanical_status` `INSPECTION_DUE`, `staffed_operator_count` 0, and `last_inspection_at`, `last_inspection_result`, `inspection_valid_until` and `wind_hold_since` all null.

Terrain areas — trails, glades and terrain parks — are created by the `MountainOpsDirector` alone. Each area names its `season_id` and the `serving_lift_id` of the single lift that must be running for the area to be skiable, along with a `trail_code` unique per season, a display `name`, a `terrain_class` of `GREEN`, `BLUE`, `BLACK` or `PARK`, an `acres` figure greater than zero at one decimal place, and an integer `min_open_base_depth_in` of at least 0 giving the settled base in inches required to open the area. The optional `avalanche_terrain` flag defaults to false and, when true, makes a current avalanche clearance a precondition of opening. A serving lift that belongs to a different season than the declared one is refused with `ERR_CROSS_SEASON_REFERENCE`. New areas begin with `opening_state` `CLOSED` and `park_state` `NO_PARK`, `base_depth_in` 0.0 and `base_depth_stake_count` 0, `groom_coverage_pct` 0.0 and `groom_ready` false, `last_groomed_at` and `mitigation_clearance_expires_at` null, `assigned_patroller_count` 0, `machine_swe_af` 0.0, `park_feature_count` 0, `park_risk_score` 0.0, and both `park_built_by_user_id` and `park_inspected_by_user_id` null.

Pump houses complete the inventory and may be registered by the `SnowmakingSupervisor` or the `MountainOpsDirector`. A pump house declares its `season_id`, a `pump_house_code` unique per season, a display `name`, a `max_gpm` of at least 1 giving the greatest aggregate flow it can deliver at any instant, and a `gun_count` of at least 1 for the guns plumbed to it. Its `status` is either `ONLINE` (the default) or `MAINTENANCE`, and the `SnowmakingSupervisor` may toggle it later: moving a pump house to `MAINTENANCE` blocks new sessions on it and blocks any attempt to start or resume an existing one, both rejected with `ERR_PUMP_HOUSE_OFFLINE`, while sessions already running are left untouched and may still be paused or completed. Each pump house tracks `peak_committed_gpm`, the greatest instantaneous committed flow across its scheduled, running and paused sessions, and `active_session_count`, the number of its sessions currently running or paused; both start at 0.

Reading back the inventory is available to every role. Lifts may be listed filtered by `season_id`, by `service_status` and by `mechanical_status`; terrain areas by `season_id`, `serving_lift_id`, `opening_state` and `terrain_class`; pump houses by `season_id` and `status`. All of these accept `limit` and `offset` and default to ascending `created_at` order.

### 3.2. Lift Mechanical Integrity, Operator Staffing and Service Posture

A lift carries two state dimensions that evolve independently on the same row. `mechanical_status` records what the mechanics know: `INSPECTION_DUE`, `INSPECTION_PASSED` or `INSPECTION_FAILED`. `service_status` records what the guests see: `CLOSED`, `STANDBY`, `OPEN`, `WIND_HOLD` or `MECH_HOLD`. A lift may perfectly well sit `CLOSED` with `INSPECTION_PASSED`, or `WIND_HOLD` with `INSPECTION_PASSED`; the dimensions only couple where this section says they do.

Recording an inspection is the `LiftOpsManager`'s privilege and takes a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL` plus optional free-text `notes`. Either result stamps `last_inspection_at` with the logical clock and copies the outcome into `last_inspection_result`. A passing inspection sets `mechanical_status` to `INSPECTION_PASSED` and sets `inspection_valid_until` to 24 hours after the inspection instant, which is the entire basis of the currency test used elsewhere: an inspection is current while the logical clock is strictly before `inspection_valid_until`. A passing inspection is accepted from any of the three mechanical states, so `INSPECTION_DUE → INSPECTION_PASSED` and `INSPECTION_FAILED → INSPECTION_PASSED` are both ordinary paths, as is re-passing an already-passed lift to extend its validity window. A passing inspection additionally releases a mechanical hold: if `service_status` was `MECH_HOLD` it moves `MECH_HOLD → STANDBY`, and every terrain area served by the lift that is sitting on `PATROL_HOLD` is re-gated and returned to `OPEN` only if every opening gate holds at the current clock.

A failing inspection is the opposite and it is loud. `mechanical_status` moves to `INSPECTION_FAILED` — again accepted from any of the three mechanical states, so `INSPECTION_PASSED → INSPECTION_FAILED` and a re-failure both apply — and `inspection_valid_until` is set to null. If `service_status` was `OPEN`, `STANDBY` or `WIND_HOLD`, it is forced to `MECH_HOLD`, and that drives the full deterioration chain: every terrain area served by this lift whose `opening_state` was `OPEN` moves `OPEN → PATROL_HOLD`; each such area moves its `PLANNED` and `DISPATCHED` grooming tasks to `DEFERRED`; each such area moves its `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING` and `PAUSED` snowmaking sessions to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `TERRAIN_HOLD`; and each aborted session's `water_af_reserved` is subtracted from the season's `water_reserved_af`. The active day plan's `open_lift_capacity_pph`, `open_terrain_count`, `terrain_open_acres`, `raw_ccc`, `guest_capacity`, `non_season_pool` and `posture` are then recomputed. The lift's own `version` increments by 1, as does the `version` of every row the chain touched.

Moving a lift's service posture by hand is also the `LiftOpsManager`'s privilege and takes a single `target_status`. The permitted moves are narrow. `CLOSED → STANDBY` requires a current inspection: `mechanical_status` must be `INSPECTION_PASSED` and the logical clock must be strictly before `inspection_valid_until`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_LIFT_INSPECTION_EXPIRED`. `STANDBY → OPEN` requires the same current inspection, requires `staffed_operator_count` to be at least `required_operator_count` — otherwise `ERR_LIFT_OPERATORS_INSUFFICIENT` — and requires that, where the season has an active day plan at all, that plan's `posture` is not `WEATHER_HOLD`. `WIND_HOLD → OPEN` is the hold release and carries one extra hurdle described in the next paragraph. Requesting a `target_status` that the current `service_status` does not admit — asking for `OPEN` straight from `CLOSED`, for instance — is refused with `ERR_LIFT_STATE_INVALID`, and the same code guards the one absolute rule of the service dimension: `MECH_HOLD` can only be left by a passing inspection, never by a service-status request. Setting `CLOSED` is accepted from `OPEN`, `STANDBY` or `WIND_HOLD`; it clears `wind_hold_since` and cascades every open terrain area the lift serves onto `PATROL_HOLD` with the same grooming, snowmaking and water-ledger chain as a mechanical hold.

Releasing a wind hold deliberately demands more than the wind falling back below the threshold that caused it, because a lift that flaps between running and holding on every gust is worse than a lift that stays down. There is a five-mile-per-hour hysteresis band: the season's active day plan must exist and its `wind_speed_mph` must be no greater than the lift's `wind_hold_threshold_mph` less 5.0. A release attempted while the wind sits inside that band — above the release figure but at or below the hold threshold — is refused with `ERR_WIND_ABOVE_RELEASE_THRESHOLD`. A successful release sets `service_status` to `OPEN`, sets `wind_hold_since` back to null, and conditionally recovers terrain: every area served by this lift sitting on `PATROL_HOLD` is re-gated against the current clock, those satisfying every opening gate return to `OPEN`, and those that do not simply stay on `PATROL_HOLD`. The active day plan's counters and `posture` are recomputed and its `version` increments by 1.

Staffing is tracked as a roster of assignments, each created by the `LiftOpsManager` with a `lift_id`, an `operator_code` of 1 to 32 characters (an employee code, not an entity reference), a `role_on_lift` of `TOP_OP`, `BOTTOM_OP`, `MID_LOAD` or `LIFTIE_FLOAT`, and a shift bounded by `shift_start` inclusive and `shift_end` exclusive, with the start strictly before the end. New assignments begin in `state` `ASSIGNED` with `released_at` null. One lift may hold at most one `ASSIGNED` assignment per `role_on_lift`, with the deliberate exception of `LIFTIE_FLOAT`, which may repeat freely; a second assignment for an already-filled non-float slot is refused with `ERR_LIFT_ROLE_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Separately, the same `operator_code` may not hold two `ASSIGNED` assignments whose shifts come within a half-hour turnaround of one another: two shifts conflict when the new shift starts before 30 minutes after the peer's end **and** 30 minutes after the new shift's end falls after the peer's start, and such a request is refused with `ERR_OPERATOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. The turnaround applies across lifts, not merely within one.

`staffed_operator_count` on a lift is never typed in — it is the number of that lift's assignments in `state` `ASSIGNED` whose shift covers the current logical clock, meaning `shift_start` is at or before the clock and `shift_end` is strictly after it. Releasing an assignment sets its `state` to `RELEASED` and stamps `released_at` with the logical clock, then recomputes the lift's coverage. If the recomputed count drops below `required_operator_count` while the lift is `OPEN`, the lift falls `OPEN → STANDBY`, and every terrain area it serves whose `opening_state` is `OPEN` cascades onto `PATROL_HOLD` with the usual downstream grooming and snowmaking consequences. Both the assignment's `version` and the lift's `version` increment by 1. A lift's roster can be listed filtered by assignment `state` and by `role_on_lift`.

### 3.3. Snow Stakes, Overnight Grooming and the Readiness Ladder

Base depth is measured, never declared. A `GroomingLead`, `SnowmakingSupervisor`, `Patroller` or the `MountainOpsDirector` may record a stake reading against a terrain area, supplying a `stake_code` of 1 to 16 characters identifying the stake within the area, a `depth_in` of at least 0 at one decimal place giving the settled snow depth in inches, and a `measured_at` instant. A reading whose `measured_at` falls after the logical clock is refused with `ERR_READING_TIMESTAMP_FUTURE`. Two optional fields round out the observation: `density_pct`, the water content of the snowpack as a percentage, which defaults to 10.0 and must be greater than 0 and at most 60, and `source`, which is `MANUAL_STAKE` by default or `AUTOMATED_SENSOR`. Readings are immutable observations and may be listed for an area filtered by `stake_code` and by `source`.

Every reading re-derives the area's `base_depth_in` and `base_depth_stake_count`, and the derivation is deliberately forgetful. Only readings whose `measured_at` falls inside a two-day lookback — that is, at or after the instant 48 hours before the current logical clock — are considered at all; anything older is discarded even when it is the only reading a stake has ever produced, which means `base_depth_stake_count` shrinks over time if the stakes go unread. Within that window the readings are collapsed to one per stake: for each distinct `stake_code`, the reading with the greatest `measured_at` wins, and where two readings share that instant the one with the later `created_at` wins. The row `id` is never a tiebreak. `base_depth_stake_count` is then the number of distinct stakes surviving that collapse. If no stake survives, `base_depth_in` is 0.0, which fails every non-zero `min_open_base_depth_in`. Otherwise the season's `base_depth_policy` decides: under `MEAN_OF_STAKES` the depth is the sum of the surviving depths divided by the surviving stake count, rounded half up to one decimal place; under `MIN_OF_STAKES` it is the smallest surviving depth, rounded half up to one decimal place. The minimum policy is never more generous than the mean policy on the same reading set, and it is the policy that keeps a run closed because of one thin spot.

Grooming is planned as a task on a cat route by the `GroomingLead`. A task names the `terrain_area_id` it will groom, a `cat_route_code` of 1 to 24 characters identifying the route it belongs to, a `sequence_index` of at least 1 giving its position inside that route for the night, and a `snowcat_unit_code` of 1 to 24 characters naming the machine that will run it — an opaque resource code, not an entity reference. The task's `window_start` (inclusive) and `window_end` (exclusive) bound the planned pass, and the UTC date of `window_start` is what identifies the groom night the task belongs to. Winch grooming is requested with `winch_required`, which defaults to false; when it is true a non-empty `winch_anchor_code` naming the anchor point must accompany it, and its absence is refused with `ERR_WINCH_ANCHOR_REQUIRED`. Two tasks on the same cat route may not claim the same `sequence_index`, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A new task begins in `state` `PLANNED` with `passes_completed` 0, `coverage_pct` 0.0, and `dispatched_at` and `completed_at` null. Tasks may be listed filtered by `terrain_area_id`, `cat_route_code`, `snowcat_unit_code` and `state`.

Dispatching sends the machine out and is where overnight sequencing bites. A task may be dispatched from `PLANNED` or from `DEFERRED`, and both paths enforce the same conditions: the season must not be `CLOSED` — grooming is expressly permitted in `PRESEASON`, since it is how an area first becomes ready — the target area's `opening_state` must be neither `PATROL_HOLD` nor `SEASON_CLOSED`, every task on the same `cat_route_code` with a lower `sequence_index` must already be `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, no other `DISPATCHED` task using the same `snowcat_unit_code` may have a window overlapping this one, and a winch task must carry its anchor. Sequencing is checked before machine exclusivity, so a task that is both out of order and double-books its cat reports `ERR_GROOM_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` rather than `ERR_SNOWCAT_CONFLICT`. Overlap is judged on half-open windows, so two windows that merely touch — one ending at the instant the other begins — never conflict. A successful dispatch sets `state` to `DISPATCHED`, stamps `dispatched_at` with the logical clock, and, if the area was sitting in `CLOSED`, moves it `CLOSED → PREP` and increments the area's `version` by 1 as well as the task's.

Completion is reported by the `GroomingLead` with a `passes_completed` count of at least 1, and it is what converts machine work into a readiness fact. The coverage achieved is the acreage the cat actually groomed expressed as a percentage of the area's acreage. Groomed acreage is the number of passes multiplied by the swath a single cat pass covers — 0.75 acres per pass — then multiplied by two efficiency factors: a winch factor of 0.8 when `winch_required` is true and 1.0 otherwise, and a class factor of 0.9 when the area's `terrain_class` is `BLACK` and 1.0 otherwise. That groomed acreage is divided by the area's `acres`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to one decimal place, and finally capped at 100.0 — the cap applied after the rounding. The result is stored as the task's `coverage_pct` and copied onto the area as `groom_coverage_pct`. Coverage is never accumulated across tasks: only the completing task's own passes count, so the most recently completed task on an area defines that area's coverage outright. The two factors multiply, so a winched black run earns 0.72 of the groomed acreage that the same number of passes earns on an unwinched blue run of the same size, and extra passes past full coverage change nothing observable because of the cap.

Completion also stamps the area's `last_groomed_at` with the logical clock and sets `groom_ready` to true when the coverage figure is at least 70.0 percent and the clock is still strictly before `last_groomed_at` plus the season's `groom_freshness_hours` — which, at the default policy, means an area counts as groomed for the 18 hours following the completing pass and then quietly stops counting. The task moves `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped, and both the task's and the area's `version` increment by 1. Falling short of the coverage floor is not an error: the task completes normally, `groom_ready` simply stays false, and any later opening decision on that area is refused with `ERR_GROOM_NOT_CURRENT`. Attempting to dispatch a task that is not `PLANNED` or `DEFERRED`, or to complete a task that is not `DISPATCHED`, is refused with `ERR_WORKFLOW_STATE_INVALID`.

Grooming work is also the first casualty of deterioration. When an area moves onto `PATROL_HOLD`, or its serving lift goes onto a wind or mechanical hold, that area's tasks in `PLANNED` and `DISPATCHED` are moved to `DEFERRED`, processed in ascending `sequence_index` order; a task deferred out of `DISPATCHED` keeps its `dispatched_at` stamp. A `DEFERRED` task is not dead — it may be dispatched again once the area is workable — but a final season close moves every task still in `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED` or `DEFERRED` to the terminal `CANCELLED`, with each task's `version` incrementing by 1. Closing an area by decision likewise defers its `PLANNED` and `DISPATCHED` tasks.

All of this feeds a five-gate readiness ladder on the area's `opening_state`. An area sits in `CLOSED` until work starts on it: scheduling a snowmaking session on it, or dispatching a grooming task against it, moves `CLOSED → PREP` provided the season is not `CLOSED`, and increments the area's `version` by 1. From `PREP` the system re-evaluates readiness after every mutation that could change a gate input — a new stake reading, a grooming completion, an avalanche clearance, a park inspection, or a patroller being rostered onto the area — and moves `PREP → READY` when all five gates hold at once: `base_depth_in` is at least `min_open_base_depth_in`; `groom_ready` is true; either `avalanche_terrain` is false or `mitigation_clearance_expires_at` is set and the logical clock is strictly before it; `assigned_patroller_count` is at least the season's `min_patrollers_per_open_area`; and either the `terrain_class` is not `PARK` or the area's `park_state` is `PARK_INSPECTED` with a `park_risk_score` no greater than 60.0. The ladder is symmetric: any mutation that makes at least one of those five conditions false while the area sits in `READY` moves it `READY → PREP`. A new stake reading that lowers `base_depth_in` under the minimum-of-stakes policy is the canonical example. Both directions increment the area's `version` by 1.

### 3.4. Scheduling Snowmaking: Water Rights and Plant Capacity

Scheduling a snowmaking session is the `SnowmakingSupervisor`'s exclusive act and the single most heavily gated create in the system, because it simultaneously commits a scarce water right, a scarce pumping capacity, and a terrain area whose readiness the supervisor read a moment ago. The payload names the `terrain_area_id` being covered, the `pump_house_id` supplying the water, the window bounded by `window_start` (inclusive) and `window_end` (exclusive), a `gun_count` of at least 1 for the guns committed, a `gpm` of at least 1 for the flow the session will occupy on its pump house across the whole window, and the `expected_terrain_area_version` described in §2.8. A window whose start is not strictly before its end is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. The area must not be on `PATROL_HOLD` or `SEASON_CLOSED`, else `ERR_TERRAIN_STATE_INVALID`; the pump house must be `ONLINE`, else `ERR_PUMP_HOUSE_OFFLINE`.

The session's `planned_minutes` is the number of whole minutes between `window_start` and `window_end`, and the water it reserves follows directly: the committed flow in gallons per minute is multiplied by `planned_minutes` to give gallons, that total is divided by the 325,851 gallons that make up one acre-foot, and the quotient is rounded half up to three decimal places to give `water_af_reserved`. A seven-hour window at 1,200 gallons per minute, for instance, is 420 minutes of pumping, 504,000 gallons, and a reservation of 1.547 acre-feet. That figure is a deliberate upper bound: it bills the entire declared window whether or not the guns run for all of it. On success it is added to the season's `water_reserved_af` and the season's `version` increments by 1.

The water right is checked before the reservation is booked, and it is checked against the whole ledger rather than against the remaining balance alone: the season's `water_used_af` plus its current `water_reserved_af` plus this session's new `water_af_reserved` must not exceed `water_right_seasonal_af`. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_WATER_RIGHT_EXCEEDED`. Because this is the last check in the ladder, a request that is malformed, mistimed or over-capacity in any other way never reports a water-right problem.

Pump capacity is verified by a boundary sweep rather than by pairwise overlap tests, because three sessions can each be individually admissible while their union busts the pump house. The peers considered are that pump house's sessions in `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING` or `PAUSED`; sessions that are `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` occupy nothing, so an abort immediately frees its flow for a later create. Collect every `window_start` and every `window_end` among those peers and the proposed session into one sorted set of instants, and read the timeline as the consecutive half-open spans between adjacent instants. For each such span that intersects the proposed session's own window, add the proposed session's `gpm` to the `gpm` of every peer whose window starts before the span's end and ends after the span's start; if that sum exceeds the pump house's `max_gpm`, the request is refused with `ERR_PUMP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because overlap is half-open, a peer that ends exactly when a span begins is not an occupant of that span, and a boundary-aligned back-to-back schedule is therefore always admissible.

Once the request is accepted, `peak_committed_gpm` on that pump house is recomputed as the greatest summed flow of its occupants over **every** adjacent span of that timeline — not merely the spans the new session touches — where the occupant set is the peers plus the newly accepted session, filtered to rows whose window starts before the span's end and ends after the span's start. Sweeping every span is what keeps a pre-existing peer-only peak from being silently forgotten. The same construction is then repeated plant-wide, summing flow across every pump house of the season over every boundary span; if that plant-wide peak exceeds the season's `plant_max_gpm` the request is refused with `ERR_PLANT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The pump-house ceiling is always tested before the plant ceiling, so a request that busts both reports the pump-house code. Flow figures are whole gallons per minute and are never rounded.

Two side effects complete a successful create. If the terrain area is sitting in `CLOSED`, it moves `CLOSED → PREP`. And any session on that same terrain area that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose window overlaps the new one is superseded: it moves `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `SUPERSEDED`, its `water_af_reserved` is subtracted back out of the season's `water_reserved_af`, and its own `version` increments by 1. This is precisely why the create demands the area version the caller observed — superseding a colleague's plan on the strength of a stale read is the failure mode the check exists to prevent.

A new session lands in `state` `SCHEDULED` with `accumulated_run_minutes` 0, `water_af_actual` 0.0, `snow_swe_af` 0.0, `energy_kwh` 0.0, `energy_cost_usd` 0.00 and `demand_surcharge_applied` false, and with `wet_bulb_f`, `snow_yield_factor`, `started_at`, `last_running_since`, `ended_at` and `abort_reason` all null. The pump house's `version` increments by 1 as its committed peak moves. Sessions may be listed filtered by `terrain_area_id`, `pump_house_id`, `state` and `abort_reason`.

### 3.5. Running the Guns: Wet-Bulb Bands, Metering and Tiered Energy Cost

A session is driven through its life by the `SnowmakingSupervisor` naming a `target_state` of `RUNNING`, `PAUSED` or `COMPLETED`. The reachable moves are exactly these: from `SCHEDULED` only `RUNNING`; from `RUNNING` either `PAUSED` or `COMPLETED`; from `PAUSED` either `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`; and from `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED`, nothing at all. Any other request — asking for `PAUSED` straight from `SCHEDULED`, or touching a finished session — is refused with `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`. The `RUNNING` and `PAUSED` states may alternate as many times as the night demands, and that is the whole point: the metered figures depend on how many times the cycle was traversed, not merely on where the session finished.

Starting a session — `SCHEDULED → RUNNING` — requires the season to be `OPERATING`, the pump house to be `ONLINE`, the terrain area to be neither `PATROL_HOLD` nor `SEASON_CLOSED`, and, above all, the weather to cooperate. The gate is the `wet_bulb_f` of the season's active day plan, and it selects a yield factor by band. A wet bulb above 27.0 degrees Fahrenheit is refused outright with `ERR_WET_BULB_TOO_WARM` and the session stays `SCHEDULED`. Otherwise, a wet bulb above 24.0 sets `snow_yield_factor` to 0.60; a wet bulb at or below 24.0 but above 20.0 sets it to 0.85; and a wet bulb at or below 20.0 sets it to 1.00. Every band test is strictly greater-than, which means each boundary value — 27.0, 24.0 and 20.0 — falls into the colder, more productive side: a reading of exactly 27.0 starts successfully at a factor of 0.60, and 27.1 does not start at all. A successful start copies the active day plan's `wet_bulb_f` onto the session, stamps both `started_at` and `last_running_since` with the logical clock, increments the pump house's `active_session_count` by 1, and increments both the session's and the pump house's `version` by 1.

Pausing — `RUNNING → PAUSED` — closes the open run segment. The whole minutes elapsed since `last_running_since` are added to `accumulated_run_minutes`, with any partial minute discarded, so a ninety-second segment contributes one minute. `last_running_since` is then cleared to null and the session's `version` increments by 1. Resuming — `PAUSED → RUNNING` — re-checks that the pump house is `ONLINE`, that the active day plan's `wet_bulb_f` is still at or below 27.0, and that the terrain area is still neither held nor season-closed, then stamps `last_running_since` afresh. Crucially, a resume **re-reads** the current wet bulb and recomputes `snow_yield_factor` from it, so cycling across a warming trend lowers the productivity of the session as a whole; a session that started at 1.00 and resumed at 25.0 degrees finishes at 0.60. A later weather update on its own never retroactively changes the factor of a session already running.

Completion is reached from either `RUNNING` or `PAUSED`. When the previous state was `RUNNING`, the open segment is closed first — whole minutes since `last_running_since` added to `accumulated_run_minutes` — before any metering happens; when the previous state was `PAUSED`, `accumulated_run_minutes` is already final. The session then moves to `COMPLETED`, `ended_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `last_running_since` is cleared. Metered water follows the same arithmetic as the reservation but on real minutes: the committed `gpm` multiplied by `accumulated_run_minutes`, divided by the 325,851 gallons in an acre-foot, rounded half up to three decimal places, stored as `water_af_actual`. Because it bills only the minutes actually spent running, it is strictly below the planned reservation for any session that did not run its whole window — the 1.547 acre-foot reservation of the earlier example, run from 05:00 to 08:00, paused for an hour, resumed to 12:00 and completed with 360 accumulated minutes, meters at 1.326 acre-feet. `snow_swe_af` is then the metered water multiplied by `snow_yield_factor`, rounded half up to three decimal places.

Completion moves four ledgers at once. The season's `water_reserved_af` is decreased by this session's `water_af_reserved`, its `water_used_af` is increased by `water_af_actual`, and its `snow_produced_swe_af` is increased by `snow_swe_af`; the terrain area's `machine_swe_af` is increased by `snow_swe_af`. The pump house's `active_session_count` is decremented by 1 and its `peak_committed_gpm` is recomputed over the remaining occupants. The session's, the terrain area's, the pump house's and the season's `version` all increment by 1.

Energy is priced last, and the order of rounding is part of the contract. Total consumption `energy_kwh` is the metered water multiplied by the season's `snowmaking_kwh_per_af`, rounded half up to three decimal places. That total is then allocated across the season's daily-recurring price windows according to when the guns actually ran: the priced interval begins at the session's `window_start` and extends for `accumulated_run_minutes`. Taking the energy windows in ascending `start_hour_utc` order, count the whole minutes of that priced interval falling inside the window's span on the calendar date of `window_start` — from that date at the window's `start_hour_utc` up to but not including that date at its `end_hour_utc`. A window contributing zero minutes is skipped entirely and contributes no rounding remainder. For each contributing window, its share of consumption is the total kilowatt-hours multiplied by that window's minutes and divided by `accumulated_run_minutes`, rounded half up to three decimal places; its cost is that share multiplied by the window's `price_usd_per_kwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The base cost is the sum of those already-rounded per-window costs, taken in ascending start-hour order. Rounding per window and then summing is normative — summing unrounded costs and rounding once at the end produces a figure that can differ by a cent, and that reading is wrong. Because the windows recur daily, a priced interval that runs past the end of the day wraps into the first window of the following day.

One surcharge sits on top. The plant demand surcharge applies when the pump house's `peak_committed_gpm` is at least eighty percent of the season's `plant_max_gpm`; `demand_surcharge_applied` records whether it fired. When it fires, `energy_cost_usd` is the base cost multiplied by 1.12, rounded half up to two decimal places — the multiplication and its rounding applied to the summed base cost, never to the individual window costs. When it does not fire, `energy_cost_usd` is the base cost unchanged.

Aborting is never a caller's request; it is always something the mountain does to a session. An area moving onto `PATROL_HOLD` or `SEASON_CLOSED`, and a create that supersedes an overlapping plan, both push their victims from `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING` or `PAUSED` to `ABORTED`. The session's `ended_at` is stamped, `abort_reason` records `TERRAIN_HOLD`, `SUPERSEDED` or `SEASON_CLOSE`, and every metered figure is forced flat: `water_af_actual` 0.000, `snow_swe_af` 0.000, `energy_kwh` 0.000 and `energy_cost_usd` 0.00 — an aborted session is never priced, however many minutes it ran. Its `water_af_reserved` is subtracted from the season's `water_reserved_af` as a compensating release while `water_used_af` is left untouched, which returns the season ledger to exactly the value it held before the session was created. `accumulated_run_minutes` is frozen at whatever it already held and is deliberately **not** advanced for a session aborted out of `RUNNING`. The pump house's `active_session_count` is decremented by 1 only when the session was `RUNNING`, and its `peak_committed_gpm` is recomputed. The session's, the pump house's and the season's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.6. Avalanche Mitigation and Terrain Park Risk

Avalanche terrain cannot open on a hunch, and the mitigation record is what makes the clearance auditable. A mission is planned by a `Patroller` or the `MountainOpsDirector` against a `terrain_area_id`, naming a `route_code` of 1 to 24 characters identifying the mitigation route within the area, a `method` of `EXPLOSIVE`, `SKI_CUT` or `GAS_EXPLODER`, and a `hazard_rating_pre` drawn from `LOW`, `MODERATE`, `CONSIDERABLE`, `HIGH` and `EXTREME` recording the forecast hazard before work begins. `planned_charges` is optional, defaults to 0, must lie between 0 and 24 inclusive, and must be 0 for the two non-explosive methods. A new mission sits in `state` `PLANNED` with `charges_used` 0, `road_closure_minutes` 0, and `hazard_rating_post`, `executed_by_patroller_id`, `cleared_by_patroller_id`, `executed_at` and `cleared_at` all null. Its `clearance_valid_hours` is fixed at creation by the method it will use — 24 hours for `EXPLOSIVE` and for `GAS_EXPLODER`, and only 8 hours for `SKI_CUT`, which reflects how much less of the slope a ski cut actually tests — and it is that figure the eventual clearance applies. Missions may be listed filtered by `terrain_area_id`, `executed_by_patroller_id`, `cleared_by_patroller_id`, `method` and `state`.

Execution is a `Patroller`-only act, and the acting patroller is taken from `X-User-ID` rather than from the body. It requires the mission to be `PLANNED`, the season not to be `CLOSED` — mitigation, like grooming, runs in `PRESEASON` so that avalanche terrain can reach readiness before the mountain opens — and the acting patroller to be on duty, otherwise `ERR_PATROLLER_NOT_ON_DUTY`. The method dictates the qualification: an `EXPLOSIVE` or `GAS_EXPLODER` mission demands that the acting patroller carry `blaster_certified` true, refused otherwise with `ERR_BLASTER_CERT_REQUIRED`; a `SKI_CUT` mission demands a partner, meaning at least one *other* patroller of the same season who is on duty and whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` at the moment of execution, refused otherwise with `ERR_SKI_CUT_PARTNER_REQUIRED`. And mitigation may never be thrown over open terrain: the area's `opening_state` must not be `OPEN`, else `ERR_TERRAIN_STATE_INVALID`. A successful execution moves `PLANNED → EXECUTED`, stamps `executed_at` with the logical clock, records the acting patroller as `executed_by_patroller_id`, takes `charges_used` from the request (which is 0 for a ski cut), and computes `road_closure_minutes` as twenty minutes of road closure for each charge actually used — four charges close the access road for eighty minutes. The mission's `version` increments by 1.

Signing off an executed mission is where segregation of duties is enforced. The clearing `Patroller` must not be the patroller who executed it — a matching `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` — must be on duty, and must carry a `cert_level` of `EMT` or `PARAMEDIC`, with `BASIC` and `OEC` refused as `ERR_PATROLLER_CERT_INSUFFICIENT`. The clearer submits a `hazard_rating_post` recording the hazard actually observed, and that single value forks the outcome. A `hazard_rating_post` of `LOW` or `MODERATE` moves the mission `EXECUTED → CLEARED`, stamps `cleared_at`, records `cleared_by_patroller_id`, and sets the terrain area's `mitigation_clearance_expires_at` to the logical clock plus the mission's `clearance_valid_hours`; readiness is then re-evaluated for the area, which may fire `PREP → READY`. A `hazard_rating_post` of `CONSIDERABLE`, `HIGH` or `EXTREME` moves the mission `EXECUTED → HOLD_EXTENDED` instead, still stamping `cleared_at` and recording the rating, but setting the area's `mitigation_clearance_expires_at` to null and pushing the area `OPEN → PATROL_HOLD` if it happened to be open. Either fork increments both the mission's and the area's `version` by 1. A `HOLD_EXTENDED` mission is final in itself: the only route back to open avalanche terrain is a fresh mission, planned, executed and cleared again. A final season close moves every mission still `PLANNED` or `EXECUTED` to `ABORTED`, incrementing its `version` by 1. Attempting to execute a mission that is not `PLANNED`, or clear one that is not `EXECUTED`, is refused with `ERR_WORKFLOW_STATE_INVALID`.

Terrain parks live on a second, wholly independent state dimension of the terrain area called `park_state`, cycling among `NO_PARK`, `PARK_BUILT`, `PARK_INSPECTED` and `PARK_CLOSED`. It evolves separately from `opening_state`: a park area may be `OPEN` with `park_state` `PARK_INSPECTED`, or `CLOSED` with `park_state` `PARK_BUILT`, and no `park_state` is terminal — a torn-down park may be rebuilt inside the same season.

A build is recorded by a `GroomingLead` or a `Patroller` and submits the park's `features` as an array, each entry declaring a `feature_type` of `JUMP`, `RAIL`, `JIB` or `HALFPIPE` and a `size_class` of `S`, `M` or `L`. The area's `terrain_class` must be `PARK` and its `opening_state` must not be `SEASON_CLOSED`; a build against a non-park area is refused with `ERR_TERRAIN_STATE_INVALID`. A park may be rebuilt while the area is `CLOSED`, `PREP`, `READY`, `OPEN` or `PATROL_HOLD`, and the build is accepted from `park_state` `NO_PARK`, `PARK_BUILT` or `PARK_CLOSED`. Each feature carries a base weight by type — 6.0 for a jump, 4.0 for a rail, 3.0 for a jib, and 12.0 for a halfpipe — multiplied by a size multiplier of 1.0 for small, 1.5 for medium and 2.0 for large. Those per-feature weights are summed into a raw score. `park_feature_count` is the number of submitted features, and the count then drives an exposure factor: the first five features are free, and each feature beyond the fifth adds one tenth to a factor that starts at 1.0. Six features therefore carry a factor of 1.1 and seven carry 1.2, while five features or fewer carry exactly 1.0 — the allowance is a floor at zero extra features, never a discount below 1.0. `park_risk_score` is the raw score multiplied by the exposure factor, rounded half up to one decimal place, then capped at 100.0 with the cap applied after the rounding. A six-feature park of a large jump, a medium jump, a medium rail, a small rail, a small jib and a small halfpipe raws at 46.0, takes a factor of 1.1, and scores 50.6.

Every build records `park_built_by_user_id` as the caller's `X-User-ID` and resets `park_inspected_by_user_id` to null, moves `park_state` to `PARK_BUILT`, and increments the area's `version` by 1. Submitting an **empty** `features` array from `PARK_INSPECTED` is how a park is torn down: `park_state` moves `PARK_INSPECTED → PARK_CLOSED`, `park_feature_count` returns to 0, `park_risk_score` returns to 0.0, `park_inspected_by_user_id` is nulled, and if the area was `OPEN` with `terrain_class` `PARK` it is pushed onto `PATROL_HOLD`. Because a rebuild always nulls the inspector, re-inspection by a different user is mandatory before the area can open again — the rebuild re-arms both the inspection gate and the risk gate of the opening ladder.

Inspection is a `Patroller`-only sign-off, optionally carrying an `inspection_note`. It requires `park_state` to be `PARK_BUILT`, requires the acting patroller to be on duty, and requires `X-User-ID` to differ from `park_built_by_user_id` — the same user may not both build and sign off a park, and doing so is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION`. A successful inspection records `park_inspected_by_user_id`, moves `park_state` `PARK_BUILT → PARK_INSPECTED`, triggers a readiness re-evaluation that may fire `PREP → READY`, and increments the area's `version` by 1. Only an inspected park whose `park_risk_score` is at or below 60.0 may be opened; a park-class area opened before inspection is refused with `ERR_PARK_NOT_INSPECTED`, and one whose score exceeds the ceiling with `ERR_PARK_RISK_TOO_HIGH`. Adding a large halfpipe as a seventh feature to the park above raises the raw score to 70.0 and the factor to 1.2, scoring 84.0 — inspected or not, that park cannot open.

### 3.7. Ski Patrol: Duty, Incident Response and Medical Transport

Patrol staffing is what makes terrain legally skiable, and it is the gate most likely to close a run mid-afternoon. Rostering a patroller is the `MountainOpsDirector`'s exclusive act and names the `season_id`, a `patroller_code` unique within that season, and a `cert_level` of `BASIC`, `OEC`, `EMT` or `PARAMEDIC`. A display `full_name` is optional; so is `assigned_terrain_area_id`, which names the area the patroller covers and may be left unset for an unassigned patroller. Two flags default to false: `blaster_certified`, which authorises explosive mitigation, and `on_duty`, which declares the patroller rostered for the current operating period. A patroller's `status` is derived, not supplied: it is `AVAILABLE` when on duty and uncommitted, `ON_INCIDENT` while committed to an incident, and `OFF_DUTY` whenever `on_duty` is false. A patroller created with `on_duty` true therefore lands directly in `AVAILABLE`, and one created off duty lands in `OFF_DUTY`. Rostering recomputes the assigned area's `assigned_patroller_count` and re-evaluates that area's readiness, which may fire `PREP → READY`, incrementing the area's `version` by 1. Patrollers may be listed filtered by `season_id`, `assigned_terrain_area_id`, `status`, `on_duty` and `cert_level`.

`assigned_patroller_count` is the number of patrollers whose `assigned_terrain_area_id` points at the area **and** whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`. Patrollers who are `OFF_DUTY` and patrollers who are `ON_INCIDENT` both drop out of the count, and that is the mechanism by which an ordinary ambulance run can close a trail. Toggling duty is again the director's act, supplying `on_duty` directly. Setting it false moves the patroller `AVAILABLE → OFF_DUTY`; setting it true moves an off-duty patroller `OFF_DUTY → AVAILABLE`. Either way the assigned area's count is recomputed, and when the recomputed count falls below the season's `min_patrollers_per_open_area` while that area is `OPEN`, the area is pushed `OPEN → PATROL_HOLD` with the full downstream chain — grooming tasks deferred, snowmaking sessions aborted, water reservations released, the active day plan's counters and posture recomputed. Coming back on duty may re-satisfy the readiness gates and fire `PREP → READY`, but an area already sitting on `PATROL_HOLD` is never auto-reopened by a duty change. The patroller's `version` increments by 1.

Incidents may be reported by a `Patroller`, a `GuestServicesAgent`, the `MountainOpsDirector` or a `LiftOpsManager` — the guest services desk and the lift crew see as much as patrol does. A report names the `terrain_area_id` where it happened, a `reporter_code` of 1 to 32 characters identifying the staff member or desk that called it in, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL` that triages the response, and an optional free-text `location_note`. The incident lands in `state` `REPORTED` with `reported_at` stamped from the logical clock and `response_minutes` initialised to 0, and with `responder_patroller_id`, `transport_mode`, `disposition`, `dispatched_at`, `transport_ordered_at` and `closed_at` all null. Incidents may be listed filtered by `terrain_area_id`, `responder_patroller_id`, `severity` and `state`.

Dispatching a responder is the `MountainOpsDirector`'s exclusive act and nominates a `patroller_id` in the body. Three conditions guard it, and their relative order matters when a nomination fails more than one: the nominated patroller must be on duty (`ERR_PATROLLER_NOT_ON_DUTY`), then a `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL` incident additionally requires that patroller's `cert_level` to be `EMT` or `PARAMEDIC` (`ERR_PATROLLER_CERT_INSUFFICIENT`), and only then is the patroller's availability tested — a patroller already `ON_INCIDENT` cannot be dispatched again and the request is refused with `ERR_PATROLLER_BUSY`. On success the incident moves `REPORTED → DISPATCHED`, `dispatched_at` is stamped, `responder_patroller_id` is recorded, and `response_minutes` is computed as the whole seconds elapsed from `reported_at` to `dispatched_at` divided by 60 and rounded half up to a whole number of minutes: seven minutes and thirty seconds becomes 8, while seven minutes and twenty-nine seconds becomes 7, and a dispatch in the same logical instant as the report yields 0. The responder then moves `AVAILABLE → ON_INCIDENT`, which recomputes their assigned area's `assigned_patroller_count`, which may push that area `OPEN → PATROL_HOLD`, which defers that area's `PLANNED` and `DISPATCHED` grooming tasks and aborts its `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING` and `PAUSED` sessions with their water released, which recomputes the active day plan's `open_terrain_count`, `terrain_open_acres`, `guest_capacity` and `posture`. The incident's, the patroller's and the day plan's `version` each increment by 1.

Medical transport is ordered by the responding `Patroller` and only by them: `X-User-ID` must match the incident's `responder_patroller_id`. The order names a `transport_mode` of `TOBOGGAN`, `SNOWMOBILE`, `AMBULANCE` or `HELICOPTER`, and the mode is gated by triage severity — `HELICOPTER` is permitted only when `severity` is `CRITICAL`, and `AMBULANCE` only when `severity` is `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL`, with any other combination refused as `ERR_TRANSPORT_MODE_NOT_ALLOWED`. A toboggan or a snowmobile is available at any severity. A successful order moves the incident `DISPATCHED → TRANSPORT_ORDERED`, stamps `transport_ordered_at` and records the mode, incrementing the incident's `version` by 1.

Closing an incident is likewise the responding `Patroller`'s act and carries a `disposition`, and transport is optional in the sense that the incident forks at close. From `TRANSPORT_ORDERED` the disposition must be `TRANSPORTED_TO_CLINIC` or `TRANSFERRED_TO_EMS`. From `DISPATCHED` — a patient treated and released on the hill, with no transport ever ordered — the disposition must be `RELEASED_ON_HILL` and `transport_mode` remains null. Either path moves the incident to the terminal `CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at`, records the disposition, and returns the responder `ON_INCIDENT → AVAILABLE`, which recomputes the assigned area's `assigned_patroller_count`. Recovery is again explicit: an area already on `PATROL_HOLD` is not reopened merely because its patroller came free. Both the incident's and the patroller's `version` increment by 1, and a disposition that does not match the state the incident is closed from is refused with `ERR_WORKFLOW_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.8. The Operating Day: Publication, the Weather Cascade and Opening Decisions

An operating day is created by the `MountainOpsDirector` for a single date, naming its `season_id`, an `operating_date` written as a four-digit year, a two-digit month and a two-digit day separated by hyphens and unique within the season, and a `parking_stalls_total` of at least 0 giving the guest stalls available that day. A duplicate date within one season is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The plan begins in `posture` `DRAFT` with `wind_speed_mph` 0.0, `wet_bulb_f` 32.0 and `new_snow_in` 0.0 — a freezing-point default that makes snowmaking impossible until real weather is posted — and with `open_terrain_count`, `terrain_open_acres`, `open_lift_capacity_pph`, `raw_ccc`, `guest_capacity`, `non_season_pool`, `admitted_guest_count`, `non_season_admitted_count`, `waitlisted_guest_count` and `parking_stalls_used` all at 0, and `weather_updated_at`, `published_at` and `closed_at` null. Day plans may be listed filtered by `season_id`, `operating_date` and `posture`.

Publishing takes no payload and is the director's act. It requires the plan to be in `DRAFT`, requires the season to be `PRESEASON` or `OPERATING`, requires at least one terrain area of the season to be in `READY`, `OPEN` or `PATROL_HOLD`, and requires at least one lift of the season to be in `OPEN` or `STANDBY` — a mountain with nothing ready and nothing turning has no day to publish. Publication moves the plan `DRAFT → PUBLISHED_PARTIAL`, stamps `published_at`, computes the capacity fields described next, and — on the season's very first publish — moves the season `PRESEASON → OPERATING`, incrementing both the day plan's and the season's `version` by 1.

Comfortable carrying capacity is computed in a fixed, single-pass order, and the order is normative because a naïve implementation will iterate to a different fixed point. First, `open_lift_capacity_pph` is the sum of `hourly_capacity_pph` across the season's lifts whose `service_status` is `OPEN`; a lift on a wind or mechanical hold contributes nothing at all. Second, `open_terrain_count` is the number of the season's areas whose `opening_state` is `OPEN`, and `terrain_open_acres` is the sum of those areas' `acres`, rounded half up to one decimal place. Third, `raw_ccc` — the uncrowded comfortable carrying capacity — is the open uphill capacity multiplied by 0.60, floored to a whole number of guests. Fourth, a crowding density is derived as `raw_ccc` divided by `terrain_open_acres`, or 0.0 when no acreage is open; this density is computed from `raw_ccc` and is **never** recomputed from the reduced figure. Fifth, a density factor of 0.85 applies when that density is strictly greater than 25.0 guests per acre, and 1.00 otherwise — a density of exactly 25.0 does not trigger the reduction. Sixth, `guest_capacity` is `raw_ccc` multiplied by the density factor, floored. Finally, `non_season_pool` is `guest_capacity` multiplied by 0.70, floored. Two open lifts of 1,100 and 700 people per hour serving three open areas of 18.0, 14.0 and 8.0 acres give 1,800 uphill, a `raw_ccc` of 1,080, a density of 27.0 which trips the reduction, a `guest_capacity` of 918, and a `non_season_pool` of 642. Re-testing the density on 918 — which would fall back under the threshold and restore capacity to 1,080 — is the forbidden reading; the threshold is evaluated exactly once, on `raw_ccc`. Note also that a `WEATHER_HOLD` posture does not zero these stored figures; it blocks admission through a separate rule.

Posting the mountain weather observation is shared between the `MountainOpsDirector` and the `LiftOpsManager`, and it is the head of the deepest cascade in the system. The payload carries `wind_speed_mph`, `wet_bulb_f` and an optional `new_snow_in` recording new snow since the previous update; all three are stored on the plan and `weather_updated_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Then three independent consequences are evaluated. First, every lift of the season whose `service_status` is `OPEN` and whose `wind_hold_threshold_mph` is **strictly below** the reported wind moves `OPEN → WIND_HOLD` with `wind_hold_since` stamped, processed in ascending `lift_code` order; a lift whose threshold exactly equals the reported wind stays open. Each lift entering the hold cascades its `OPEN` terrain areas onto `PATROL_HOLD` in ascending `trail_code` order; each such area defers its `PLANNED` and `DISPATCHED` grooming tasks in ascending `sequence_index` order and aborts its `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING` and `PAUSED` sessions in ascending `created_at` order with `abort_reason` `TERRAIN_HOLD`; and each aborted session releases its `water_af_reserved` back out of the season's `water_reserved_af`. Second, a `new_snow_in` of 12.0 inches or more — a foot of new snow — invalidates avalanche work outright: `mitigation_clearance_expires_at` is set to null on every `avalanche_terrain` area of the season, and any such area that was `OPEN` is dropped to `PATROL_HOLD`. Third, a reported `wind_speed_mph` of 60.0 or more moves the plan's `posture` to `WEATHER_HOLD` and puts every lift of the season in `OPEN` or `STANDBY` onto `WIND_HOLD`, again in ascending `lift_code` order; a later reading strictly below 60.0 returns the posture `WEATHER_HOLD → PUBLISHED_PARTIAL` but deliberately releases no lift — every held lift must be released individually under the hysteresis band of §3.2. Finally, the capacity fields are recomputed, the plan's `version` increments by 1, and so does the `version` of every lift, terrain area, grooming task, session and season row the cascade touched.

The daily open/close decision is the `MountainOpsDirector`'s exclusive act on one terrain area, carrying a `decision` of `OPEN` or `CLOSE` and an optional `note`. A `decision` of `OPEN` is admissible only from `opening_state` `READY` or `PATROL_HOLD`, and `CLOSE` only from `OPEN` or `PATROL_HOLD`; anything else is refused with `ERR_TERRAIN_STATE_INVALID`. Opening requires the season to be `OPERATING` and the season's active day plan to exist with a `posture` of `PUBLISHED_PARTIAL` or `PUBLISHED_FULL`, else `ERR_DAY_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED`. It then re-checks the entire readiness ladder against the current logical clock, in the order given in §2.7 — base depth at or above `min_open_base_depth_in`, groom currency, avalanche clearance currency for `avalanche_terrain` areas, patrol staffing at or above `min_patrollers_per_open_area`, terrain park inspection, terrain park risk at or below 60.0, the serving lift's `service_status` being `OPEN`, and the serving lift's `mechanical_status` being `INSPECTION_PASSED` with the logical clock strictly before its `inspection_valid_until`. Re-checking rather than trusting the stored `READY` marker is deliberate: the marker may have been set an hour and a lift failure ago.

A successful opening moves the area `READY → OPEN`, or `PATROL_HOLD → OPEN` when the director explicitly reopens a held area. A `CLOSE` decision moves `OPEN → CLOSED` or `PATROL_HOLD → CLOSED` and, in the former case, defers the area's `PLANNED` and `DISPATCHED` grooming tasks. Either decision recomputes the season's `open_area_count` and increments the season's `version` by 1; recomputes the active day plan's `open_terrain_count`, `terrain_open_acres`, `open_lift_capacity_pph`, `raw_ccc`, `guest_capacity` and `non_season_pool` and increments the day plan's `version` by 1; and increments the area's own `version` by 1.

The plan's published posture then tracks how much of the mountain is actually spinning. After any recompute, `PUBLISHED_PARTIAL → PUBLISHED_FULL` fires when `open_terrain_count` equals the number of the season's terrain areas whose `opening_state` is anything other than `SEASON_CLOSED` — that is, when everything that could be open is open. The reverse, `PUBLISHED_FULL → PUBLISHED_PARTIAL`, fires as soon as `open_terrain_count` drops below that number, whether through an explicit close or through a patrol-hold cascade. Each posture change increments the day plan's `version` by 1, and the pair may oscillate any number of times within one operating day.

### 3.9. Guest Admission, Waitlist Promotion, Closing the Day and Season Reporting

Admission is the `GuestServicesAgent`'s work. A party is admitted for a day by naming the `day_plan_id`, a `party_code` unique within that day plan, a `pass_type` of `SEASON`, `EMPLOYEE`, `RECIPROCAL` or `DAY_TICKET`, a `party_size` of at least 1, an optional `vehicle_count` of at least 0 defaulting to 0, and the `expected_day_plan_version` the agent observed. The version guard matters here more than anywhere else in the system, because guest capacity and parking move on every terrain decision, every lift status change and every other admission; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DAY_PLAN_VERSION` and no reservation is created. Admission also requires the season to be `OPERATING` and the active day plan to be in `PUBLISHED_PARTIAL` or `PUBLISHED_FULL`.

The outcome is decided by a waterfall of four buckets consumed in a fixed order, and the first bucket that fails decides the outcome — no later bucket is consulted and no earlier one is revisited. Bucket zero is whole-mountain sanity: a party whose `party_size` alone exceeds the day's `guest_capacity` is set to `DENIED` with `waitlist_reason` `NONE` and `allocated_stalls` 0, and `DENIED` is terminal — such a party is never waitlisted, because no release could ever make it fit. Bucket one is parking: the free stalls are `parking_stalls_total` less `parking_stalls_used`, and a `vehicle_count` exceeding that figure sets `WAITLISTED` with `waitlist_reason` `PARKING`. Bucket two is the pass-class pool, which `SEASON` and `EMPLOYEE` parties bypass entirely: for a `RECIPROCAL` or `DAY_TICKET` party, `non_season_admitted_count` plus `party_size` exceeding `non_season_pool` sets `WAITLISTED` with `waitlist_reason` `CAPACITY`. Bucket three is overall capacity: `admitted_guest_count` plus `party_size` exceeding `guest_capacity` also sets `WAITLISTED` with reason `CAPACITY`. A party that clears all four becomes `CONFIRMED` with `waitlist_reason` `NONE`, `allocated_stalls` equal to its `vehicle_count`, and `confirmed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Exact fits are admissions, not rejections: a party that consumes the last remaining seat of the pool is confirmed. The bucket order is observable — with 20 free stalls, 630 of a 642-seat pool taken and a `DAY_TICKET` party of 10 guests arriving in 25 vehicles, the reported reason is `PARKING` even though the party would also have busted the pool, because parking is tested first.

The day plan's four admission counters are recomputed from the reservation set after every admission decision, never adjusted incrementally, and only `CONFIRMED` and `CHECKED_IN` rows consume anything: `admitted_guest_count` is the sum of `party_size` over reservations in those two states, `non_season_admitted_count` the same sum restricted to `DAY_TICKET` and `RECIPROCAL` parties, `waitlisted_guest_count` the sum of `party_size` over `WAITLISTED` rows, and `parking_stalls_used` the sum of `allocated_stalls` over `CONFIRMED` and `CHECKED_IN` rows. `WAITLISTED`, `RELEASED` and `DENIED` rows consume no seats and no stalls. A successful admission increments the day plan's `version` by 1. Reservations may be read individually, and listed for one day plan filtered by `state`, `pass_type` and `waitlist_reason` — always in ascending `created_at` order, the same order the promotion sweep uses.

Checking a party in at the gate moves it `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN` and stamps `checked_in_at`; it is admissible only from `CONFIRMED`, requires the active day plan to be `PUBLISHED_PARTIAL` or `PUBLISHED_FULL`, and increments the reservation's `version` by 1. Check-in changes no counter, because a checked-in party consumed its seat and its stalls at confirmation.

Releasing a reservation is the compensating operation and the trigger for waitlist promotion. The row moves to `RELEASED` — admissible from `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `WAITLISTED` — with `released_at` stamped and `allocated_stalls` zeroed, after which the day's counters are recomputed, freeing whatever seats and stalls the party held. The promotion sweep then walks **the whole** `WAITLISTED` set of that day plan in ascending `created_at` order, re-running the four-bucket waterfall for each candidate against the counters as they stand at that moment. A candidate that now fits is promoted to `CONFIRMED` with `confirmed_at` stamped, `allocated_stalls` set to its `vehicle_count` and `waitlist_reason` reset to `NONE`, and the counters are recomputed before the next candidate is evaluated. A candidate that still does not fit is skipped and left `WAITLISTED`, and — critically — the sweep continues past it rather than stopping. Order is chronological only: parties are never reordered by `party_size` to pack the freed capacity more tightly, and `DENIED` rows are never candidates. With capacity 918 fully taken and waitlisted parties of 40, 10 and 25 guests in that chronological order, releasing a confirmed party of 30 promotes exactly one party — the 10-guest party, because 40 does not fit and 25 no longer fits once the 10 are seated — and the 40-guest party keeps its place for a later release. The same sweep runs after a weather update whose recompute raises `guest_capacity`. A release increments the reservation's `version` and the day plan's `version` by 1 each.

Closing the operating day is the `MountainOpsDirector`'s act and carries an optional `season_final` flag. The plan's `posture` moves to `DAY_CLOSED` from `PUBLISHED_PARTIAL`, `PUBLISHED_FULL` or `WEATHER_HOLD`, `closed_at` is stamped, and every `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` and `WAITLISTED` reservation of the day moves to `RELEASED` with `allocated_stalls` zeroed and all admission counters recomputed. The mountain is then put to bed: every `OPEN` or `PATROL_HOLD` terrain area of the season moves to `CLOSED` in ascending `trail_code` order, deferring each area's `PLANNED` and `DISPATCHED` grooming tasks as it goes, and every lift in `OPEN`, `STANDBY` or `WIND_HOLD` moves to `CLOSED`.

A `season_final` of true additionally tears the season down for good: the `SeasonConfig` moves to `CLOSED`, every terrain area to `SEASON_CLOSED`, every grooming task not already in a terminal state to `CANCELLED`, every snowmaking session not already terminal to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `SEASON_CLOSE` and its reserved water released back to the season, and every mission still `PLANNED` or `EXECUTED` to `ABORTED`. Closing the day increments the day plan's `version` by 1 and the season's `version` by 1, alongside the increments on every row the cascade touched. Once the season is `CLOSED`, every write under the mountain operations service is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPERATING`; only reads remain.

Two read-only rollups close the loop, both available to all six roles. The season snow and water accounting rollup reports, for one season, its `season_id`, `water_right_seasonal_af`, `water_used_af` as the sum of `water_af_actual` over `COMPLETED` sessions, `water_reserved_af` as the sum of `water_af_reserved` over `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING` and `PAUSED` sessions, `water_right_remaining_af` as the seasonal right less those two figures, `snow_produced_swe_af` as the sum of `snow_swe_af` over `COMPLETED` sessions — each of these five rounded half up to three decimal places — `energy_cost_total_usd` as the sum of `energy_cost_usd` over `COMPLETED` sessions rounded half up to two decimals, `completed_session_count` and `aborted_session_count`, `open_area_count`, `open_acres` as the summed `acres` of `OPEN` areas, `openable_acres` as the summed `acres` of areas in `READY`, `OPEN` or `PATROL_HOLD`, both rounded half up to one decimal, and `terrain_open_pct` as `open_acres` divided by the summed `acres` of all areas not in `SEASON_CLOSED`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to one decimal, reported as 0.0 when the season has no acreage at all. The state filtering is the whole of the difficulty: an `ABORTED` session contributes to none of the three ledgers, because its water was released when it aborted. A season with one completed session of 1.326 acre-feet actual water and 1.127 acre-feet of snow water equivalent, one aborted session whose 0.900 acre-feet were released, and a seasonal right of 250.000 acre-feet reports 1.326 used, 0.000 reserved, 1.127 produced and 248.674 remaining. Per-area machine snow water equivalent is reported in ascending `trail_code` order.

The terrain opening report answers, for one operating day, what is open, what is openable and why the rest is not. It reports the `day_plan_id`, `operating_date`, `posture`, `wind_speed_mph`, `wet_bulb_f`, `open_terrain_count`, `terrain_open_acres`, `openable_acres`, `terrain_open_pct`, `open_lift_capacity_pph`, `raw_ccc`, `guest_capacity`, `non_season_pool`, `admitted_guest_count`, `non_season_admitted_count`, `waitlisted_guest_count`, `parking_stalls_total`, `parking_stalls_used`, a `held_area_count` giving the number of the season's areas in `PATROL_HOLD`, a `wind_held_lift_count` giving the number of lifts in `WIND_HOLD`, and a `mech_held_lift_count` giving the number in `MECH_HOLD`. The acreage and percentage figures follow the same three definitions and one-decimal rounding as the season rollup.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/mountain-ops` prefix and obeys the envelope, pagination, authentication and validation rules of §2. Endpoints that create a resource return HTTP 201 with the created object; endpoints that modify or read a resource return HTTP 200 with the current object, and list endpoints return HTTP 200 with an array plus the pagination `meta` block.
