# School Board Policy Governance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A local school board is, at bottom, two machines bolted together. One is a compliance machine: an open-meeting statute that dictates how far in advance a meeting must be noticed, when an agenda closes, and the fact that minutes of one meeting can only be approved at a *later* meeting; alongside it runs a subscription legal update service that files dated statutory mandates against individual board policies and starts a clock the board must beat. The other is a negotiation machine: a policy revision has to survive two separate readings at two separate meetings, and a second reading that fails does not kill the revision — it throws it back to the drafting table so the whole cycle runs again. Boards today manage this with spreadsheets, shared drives, and the institutional memory of one very tired clerk. When the memory fails, a policy gets adopted at the same meeting it was first read, a trustee with a vendor relationship votes on the contract policy that pays them, or the published manual quietly claims compliance with a statute that lapsed eleven months ago.

This document specifies the Governance Records API, the system of record for a district board's policy and meeting lifecycle. It carries fourteen resource families — trustees, committees and committee seats, conflict disclosures, policies and their versions, legal mandates, meetings, agenda items, speaker cards, waiver requests, superintendent evaluation cycles and their rubric ratings, and manual editions — and it treats every one of them as a public record. The API is mounted under `/governance`, except for a single administrative clock endpoint used to drive deterministic testing.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **time is explicit**: every deadline, notice lead, waiver window, term expiry and review cycle is evaluated against a server-held logical clock, never against wall-clock time, so that a governance year can be replayed in a test in seconds. Second, **nothing is computed lazily**: when a policy is adopted, the cascade that supersedes the prior version, satisfies the outstanding mandates, recomputes the policy's compliance posture and re-certifies every published manual edition that pins that policy all runs inside the same request. A reader of any resource sees a posture that is already correct.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Board Officers, Staff Roles, and Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's governance role. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` names a role that is not one of the eight declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `BoardClerk`, `BoardPresident`, `BoardMember`, `PolicyAnalyst`, `Superintendent`, `SchoolPrincipal`, `LegalUpdateService`, and `Public`. Their responsibilities are partitioned by segregation of duties rather than by seniority.

A **`BoardClerk`** is secretary to the board and builds the record: seats trustees and records their resignations, charters committees and manages committee seats, schedules meetings, posts the statutory notice, builds agendas, adjourns, drafts and approves minutes, opens superintendent evaluation cycles, files speaker cards on behalf of walk-in members of the public, runs the compliance sweep, and drives the logical clock. A clerk may never record a vote and may never draft policy text.

The **`BoardPresident`** presides. The president calls meetings to order, records roll-call votes, clears conflict disclosures, advances the superintendent evaluation cycle, and may revoke a granted waiver. Segregation of duties bars the president from building the agenda they will preside over and from drafting the policy text they will call to a vote.

A **`BoardMember`** is a sitting trustee. Trustees file their own conflict disclosures, submit their own rubric ratings, and — when they hold an `ACTIVE` `CHAIR` seat on a reviewing committee — record that committee's waiver recommendation. A trustee whose disclosure on a policy classifies as `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` may not be counted in any vote on that policy.

A **`PolicyAnalyst`** is the staff drafter and manual editor: registering policy shells, drafting versions, withdrawing versions, and building and publishing manual editions. The analyst never votes, never presides, and never touches the meeting record. The **`Superintendent`** is the chief executive the board evaluates; the superintendent may propose policy text and revoke granted waivers, but may never submit a rubric rating and may never advance their own evaluation cycle. A **`SchoolPrincipal`** is a site administrator who asks the board for policy exceptions on behalf of one school. The **`LegalUpdateService`** is the subscription feed that files dated statutory mandates against tracked policies and may also trigger the compliance sweep; it is an operational identity, not a person. Finally, **`Public`** is any member of the community exercising open-meeting rights: reading the public record and filing speaker cards.

**How `X-User-ID` binds to the record.** Two of the eight roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardPresident`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BoardMember`; ownership checks compare a resource's trustee foreign key to `X-User-ID`, and the acting trustee must additionally be a `BoardMember` whose `status` is `SEATED` and whose `office` is `PRESIDENT`. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardMember`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `BoardMember`, and ownership checks on any resource that references a trustee compare that resource's trustee foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a disclosure filed by a trustee must carry a `board_member_id` equal to `X-User-ID`; a rubric rating must carry a `board_member_id` equal to `X-User-ID`; and a committee recommendation is only accepted from the trustee whose `X-User-ID` holds a committee seat with `status` `ACTIVE` and `role_on_committee` `CHAIR` on the waiver's reviewing committee. Any of these mismatches — including a `BoardPresident` caller who is not the seated president — is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `BoardClerk`, `PolicyAnalyst`, `Superintendent`, `SchoolPrincipal`, `LegalUpdateService`, or `Public`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check is performed for those six roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, a negative `offset`, a `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer at all, and a filter query parameter whose value is not a member of that field's enumeration are all malformed requests and are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, ties broken by order of creation on the server, so that records minted at the same logical instant list in the order they were written. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current page. The `response_type` for a list is `"array"`.

### 2.4. The Logical Governance Clock

The server holds a **logical clock**, and every temporal rule in this specification — notice lead times, meeting separation, waiver windows, mandate deadlines, term expiry, the policy review cycle, and every stamped timestamp on a governance record — is evaluated against that clock alone. The clock starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` when the server starts and never falls back to real wall-clock time.

An administrative capability lets a caller move the clock: it accepts a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the ISO 8601 UTC instant the clock should read, and answers with the new position in a `current_utc` field. Moving the clock mutates no entity. A mandate whose deadline the clock has just passed is still `OPEN` until the compliance sweep is invoked explicitly; the clock move is an observation, and the sweep is the action.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps, and Revision Counters

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, assigned at creation and never reused. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

All fourteen resource families in this system additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that record — and a `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. There are no exceptions: trustees, committees, committee seats, disclosures, policies, policy versions, legal mandates, meetings, agenda items, speaker cards, waiver requests, evaluation cycles, rubric ratings and manual editions all expose `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. The increment is counted per request, not per write: a record that a single request writes to more than once — directly and then again through a cascade — still ends that request exactly one higher than it began, a write that leaves every field at the value it already held refreshes nothing and increments nothing, and a record the request itself created stays at `version` 1 however many cascades in that same request touch it.

The `version` field is not decorative. Two counters echo a parent's post-write `version` back to the client so that a chain of writes needs only one read: when a new policy version is drafted, the parent policy's `version` after the write is echoed on the created record as `policy_version_after`; when an agenda item is added, the parent meeting's `version` after the write is echoed on the created item as `meeting_version_after`. Both exist so a client can immediately issue the next concurrency-guarded write without re-reading the parent.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Policy Record and the Agenda

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency, because both are operations a client may legitimately repeat and neither can be protected by a state precondition.

Drafting a new version of a policy requires a non-optional `expected_policy_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target policy. If that value does not equal the policy's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The product deliberately allows more than one `DRAFT` version to coexist on a policy, so a second drafting request is legitimate on its own terms; a stale `expected_policy_version` is what tells the server that the author was working from a policy record that has since moved — a newer draft, an adoption, a mandate satisfaction, or a compliance recompute.

Adding an item to an agenda requires a non-optional `expected_meeting_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target meeting. If that value does not equal the meeting's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Adding a second item to the same agenda is a legitimate operation in its own right, so this is not a state precondition; the stale `expected_meeting_version` records that the clerk was composing against an agenda snapshot that no longer reflects the meeting, because another item was inserted or the notice was posted and the agenda locked.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for concurrency reasons.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and the Order of Refusal

Resource identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved **left to right**, and the first segment that fails to resolve decides the error. Each family has its own not-found code: `ERR_BOARD_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MANDATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEETING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGENDA_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, all returned with HTTP 404. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved the same way after the body has been shape-checked, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and they use the same not-found codes. A rubric rating has no not-found code of its own: a rating id in a URL path that fails to resolve answers `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`.

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, refusals are evaluated in a fixed order and **the first failure wins**. The ladder, from highest precedence downward, is:

1. Authentication — headers present and naming a declared role (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorization — the caller's role is listed for this endpoint (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`).
3. Path existence — every id in the URL resolves, left to right (the twelve not-found codes).
4. Request-body shape — required fields present, types correct, enum members valid, numbers in range, datetimes well formed, conditionally-required combinations satisfied (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).
5. Rubric weight normalization on evaluation creation (`ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED`).
6. Body foreign-key existence, in the endpoint's declared input order (the not-found codes).
7. Identity binding for entity-backed roles (`ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`).
8. Optimistic concurrency (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`).
9. Scoped uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NOTICE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PUBLICATION_LABEL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVALUATION_CYCLE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEAT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSIGNMENT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATING`).
10. State preconditions on the resource named in the path (`ERR_INVALID_MEETING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MINUTES_STATE`, `ERR_AGENDA_ITEM_NOT_POSTED`, `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_EVALUATION_STAGE_INVALID`, `ERR_PUBLICATION_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_SEATED`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_ALREADY_CLEARED`).
11. The parent meeting of a voted item is open (`ERR_MEETING_NOT_OPEN`).
12. Speaker sign-up is still open on the meeting (`ERR_PUBLIC_COMMENT_CLOSED`).
13. Cross-entity state preconditions (`ERR_COMMITTEE_INQUORATE`, `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_SEATED`).
14. Open-meeting safety, in this internal order: notice lead time (`ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`), recused voter (`ERR_RECUSED_MEMBER_VOTED`), quorum (`ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`), same-meeting second reading (`ERR_SECOND_READING_SAME_MEETING`), unapproved first-reading minutes (`ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`), voter not in attendance (`ERR_VOTER_NOT_IN_ATTENDANCE`).
15. Temporal rules, in this internal order: agenda lock (`ERR_AGENDA_LOCKED`), waiver window overlap (`ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), meeting separation (`ERR_MEETING_SEPARATION_VIOLATION`), meeting not yet due (`ERR_MEETING_NOT_YET_DUE`), invalid approving meeting (`ERR_INVALID_APPROVING_MEETING`).
16. Capacity and tally constraints, in this internal order: waiver window too short (`ERR_INVALID_WAIVER_WINDOW`), waiver window over cap (`ERR_WAIVER_DURATION_EXCEEDS_CAP`), duplicate voter (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTER`), committee seats full (`ERR_COMMITTEE_SEATS_FULL`).
17. Referential eligibility, in this internal order: linked-object eligibility at agenda-build time (`ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_IN_REVIEW`), version on a live agenda (`ERR_VERSION_ON_LIVE_AGENDA`), no adopted policies to publish (`ERR_NO_ADOPTED_POLICIES`), no ratings submitted (`ERR_NO_RATINGS_SUBMITTED`).
18. Public-comment capacity: no comment period on this meeting (`ERR_PUBLIC_COMMENT_NOT_PERMITTED`), then speaker limit reached (`ERR_SPEAKER_LIMIT_REACHED`).

One consequence deserves emphasis because it is easy to get backwards: a second reading held at the same meeting as the first reading is refused with `ERR_SECOND_READING_SAME_MEETING` **before** the minutes-approval rule is consulted, because a same-meeting second reading is void no matter what the minutes say.

### 2.8. Arithmetic, Rounding, and Day Counting

Two families of numbers exist in this system, and they round differently.

Public-comment minutes are **whole minutes only**. Every intermediate share in the allocation is floored — rounded down to the next whole minute — and never rounded to nearest. Superintendent evaluation scores are **decimal, carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up**, and the rounding is applied precisely where the formula below says and nowhere else.

Every duration in days is computed as an integer by taking the difference between two instants in seconds and dividing by 86400 seconds, discarding any remainder. There are no partial days anywhere in this system. Notice lead times are expressed in hours and compared directly against the difference between two instants. Vote thresholds are integer arithmetic: a supermajority threshold rounds **up** to the next whole trustee, and a simple-majority threshold is computed by halving, discarding the remainder, and adding one.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and the Three-Phase Compliance Recompute

Several operations in this system mutate more than one record. All such cascades run inside the originating request, synchronously, before the response is returned; a client never has to poll for a settled posture. Each record touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 exactly as if it had been mutated directly.

At the centre of the compliance machinery is a recompute that runs on every event capable of moving a mandate, an adoption, or a waiver — that is, on both branches of the policy adoption cascade, on a waiver being granted, revoked or expired, on a mandate being filed, on a policy being registered, and on the compliance sweep. **The evaluation order is pinned as three one-way phases, and no phase may be re-entered inside the same event.** This is what makes the apparent circularity well defined: a manual edition's certification depends on a policy whose adopted version that same edition pinned.

*Phase 1 — mandate refresh.* For each legal mandate on the policy: if the mandate is `OPEN` or `IN_PROGRESS` and the logical clock has reached or passed its `compliance_deadline`, it becomes `OVERDUE`. Then `days_past_deadline` is set — to 0 if the mandate is `SATISFIED`; otherwise, if the clock is at or past the deadline, to the whole number of days between the deadline and the clock; otherwise to 0.

*Phase 2 — policy aggregate, reading only phase-1 output.* `open_mandate_count` becomes the number of that policy's mandates in `OPEN` or `IN_PROGRESS`; `overdue_mandate_count` becomes the number in `OVERDUE`; `active_waiver_count` becomes the number of that policy's waiver requests in `GRANTED` whose `granted_end_date` is strictly after the logical clock. Then `compliance_state` is decided by the first matching rule: if `overdue_mandate_count` is greater than zero, `NON_COMPLIANT`; otherwise if `open_mandate_count` is greater than zero, `ACTION_REQUIRED`; otherwise if the policy's `status` is `UNADOPTED` and its `legal_requirement_level` is `REQUIRED_BY_LAW`, `ACTION_REQUIRED`; otherwise if `last_review_date` is set and the whole number of days from `last_review_date` to the logical clock is greater than the three-year review cycle of 1095 days, `ACTION_REQUIRED`; otherwise `COMPLIANT`.

*Phase 3 — publication reconciliation, reading only phase-2 output.* For every manual edition in `PUBLISHED` whose pinned entries include this policy, `compliance_gap_count` becomes the number of its pinned entries whose policy's `compliance_state` is anything other than `COMPLIANT`, and `certification_state` becomes `CERTIFIED` when that count is zero and `LAPSED` otherwise.

The recompute has two scopes, and the difference between them is load-bearing. When it is invoked from the compliance sweep, all three phases run for **every** policy in the system, in ascending `code` order. When it is invoked from an adoption, grant, revoke or expiry cascade, all three phases run for **the single affected policy only**, and in that scope phase 1 does *not* move any mandate to `OVERDUE` — only the sweep may do that. A mandate whose deadline has quietly passed therefore stays `OPEN` until the next sweep even though the clock is already past it.

### 2.10. Conservation Ledgers of the Governance Record

Three identities must hold at all times — before a forward action, after it, and after any compensating action — with zero drift.

**Public-comment minutes.** A meeting's `public_comment_minutes_budget` always equals its `public_comment_minutes_remaining` plus the sum of `allotted_minutes` over every speaker card in `GRANTED` status attached to any agenda item on that meeting. Opening a meeting is the forward action that debits the budget by every granted minute; releasing cards — when an item is tabled at adjournment, or withdrawn because its waiver died — is the compensating action that credits every released minute back.

**Committee seats.** A committee's `seat_capacity` always equals `filled_seat_count` plus `vacant_seat_count`, and `filled_seat_count` always equals the number of that committee's seats whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED`. Creating an assignment is the forward action; removing one is the compensating action. Recusing and reinstating a seat holder move `voting_seat_count` only and must leave both sides of this identity untouched.

**The vote ledger.** On any agenda item that has been acted on, `vote_yes` plus `vote_no` plus `vote_abstain` plus `recused_member_count` plus `absent_member_count` always equals the meeting's `seated_member_count_snapshot`. This ledger is written once and frozen; there is no compensating action, and the identity is the audit check performed on the write itself.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Seating the Board and Chartering Its Committees

Everything in this system ultimately hangs off the roster of elected trustees. Seating a trustee is a clerk operation and records the trustee's `full_name`, the numbered `seat_number` they occupy — an integer from 1 through 15 — the `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` that bound the elected term as ISO 8601 UTC instants, and the `employer_name` retained for later conflict-of-interest review. The term must be a real interval: `term_end_date` has to be strictly after `term_start_date`, and a request where it is not is malformed. An optional `office` may be supplied from `PRESIDENT`, `VICE_PRESIDENT` and `MEMBER`; when it is omitted the trustee is seated as `MEMBER`. A newly seated trustee's `status` is `SEATED`, and both derived counters — `open_recusal_count`, the number of that trustee's disclosures still requiring recusal, and `active_committee_count`, the number of committee seats the trustee holds in `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED` status — start at 0.

Two uniqueness rules guard the roster, and both surface as `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEAT_NUMBER`: no two trustees whose `status` is `SEATED` may share a `seat_number`, and at most one `SEATED` trustee may hold the `PRESIDENT` office. A seat number belonging to a trustee who has since resigned or whose term has expired is free to reuse.

Resignation is the trustee's exit door, and it is a four-hop cascade. The trustee's `status` moves `SEATED → RESIGNED`. Because the trustee has left the board, every committee seat they hold in `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED` status moves to `REMOVED` with `removed_on` stamped from the logical clock. Because those seats emptied, each affected committee recomputes `filled_seat_count`, `voting_seat_count` and `vacant_seat_count`, and flips from `ACTIVE` to `INQUORATE` when `voting_seat_count` has fallen below its `quorum_size`. And because the trustee can no longer vote, every one of their disclosures in `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` moves to `CLEARED` with `cleared_on` stamped, dropping `open_recusal_count` to 0. Once the trustee's `status` is `RESIGNED` the seat is vacant for good; resigning a trustee whose `status` is not `SEATED` is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_SEATED`.

Chartering a committee records its `name`, its subject-matter `charter_type` — one of `POLICY`, `FINANCE`, `PERSONNEL` or `AUDIT` — a `seat_capacity` between 3 and 9 inclusive, and a `quorum_size` of at least 2 that may not exceed `seat_capacity`. A new committee opens `INQUORATE` with zero seats filled, so `filled_seat_count` and `voting_seat_count` are 0 and `vacant_seat_count` equals `seat_capacity`. A committee's `status` is derived, not set: it flips to `ACTIVE` the moment `voting_seat_count` reaches `quorum_size` and back to `INQUORATE` when it falls below.

Seating a trustee on a committee is likewise a clerk operation and carries a `committee_id`, a `board_member_id`, and an optional `role_on_committee` of `CHAIR` or `MEMBER` defaulting to `MEMBER`. The seat is created `ACTIVE` with `assigned_on` stamped from the logical clock and `removed_on` left null; the committee's `filled_seat_count` and `voting_seat_count` each rise by one and `vacant_seat_count` falls by one, the committee may flip `INQUORATE → ACTIVE`, and the trustee's `active_committee_count` is recomputed. A committee with `vacant_seat_count` at zero cannot take another seat holder and refuses with `ERR_COMMITTEE_SEATS_FULL`. Two duplicate conditions both answer `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSIGNMENT`: the same trustee already holding a seat on that committee in `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED` status, and the committee already having an `ACTIVE` `CHAIR` when a second chair is proposed.

A seated trustee's committee seat moves through three clerk-driven actions, supplied as an `action` field. `RECUSE` takes an `ACTIVE` seat to `RECUSED`: `voting_seat_count` falls by one while `filled_seat_count` is untouched, and the committee may flip `ACTIVE → INQUORATE`. `REINSTATE` takes a `RECUSED` seat back to `ACTIVE` — permitted only while the underlying trustee is still `SEATED`, and otherwise refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_SEATED` — raising `voting_seat_count` by one and possibly flipping the committee `INQUORATE → ACTIVE`. `REMOVE` takes a seat in either `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED` to `REMOVED`, stamping `removed_on`, lowering `filled_seat_count` and `voting_seat_count` and raising `vacant_seat_count` so that capacity still equals filled plus vacant. Recusing a seat that is not `ACTIVE`, reinstating one that is not `RECUSED`, or removing one that is already `REMOVED` are all refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Committee seats can be listed and filtered by `committee_id`, by `board_member_id`, or by `status`, with the usual `limit` and `offset` paging.

### 3.2. Conflict Disclosure, Classification, and Recusal

A trustee files a disclosure against a specific policy in their own name: the payload carries the `board_member_id` of the disclosing trustee — which must equal the caller's `X-User-ID` — the `policy_id` the disclosed interest touches, the `nature` of the interest, and a `narrative` describing it for the public record. The permitted values of `nature` are `FINANCIAL`, `VENDOR`, `EMPLOYMENT` and `FAMILIAL`. The instant of filing is stamped as `disclosed_on` from the logical clock, and `cleared_on` stays null.

The disclosure's `status` is not supplied by the filer; it is classified at creation from exactly two inputs, the submitted `nature` and the linked policy's `category`, and no other record participates. A `FINANCIAL` or `VENDOR` interest is categorically disqualifying and lands as `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` regardless of which policy it touches. An `EMPLOYMENT` interest lands as `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` only when the linked policy's `category` is `PERSONNEL`. A `FAMILIAL` interest lands as `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` only when the linked policy's `category` is `STUDENTS`. In every other combination the disclosure lands as `ADVISORY_ONLY` and the trustee may still vote on that policy. After classification, the trustee's `open_recusal_count` is recomputed as the number of their disclosures whose `status` is `RECUSAL_REQUIRED`.

Clearing a disclosure is a presidential act, permitted while the disclosure is `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` or `ADVISORY_ONLY`, and it stamps `cleared_on` from the logical clock and recomputes the trustee's `open_recusal_count`. Clearing is blocked, however, while any agenda item whose policy — the policy of the linked version for a reading item, the policy of the linked waiver for a `WAIVER_DECISION` item — is that same policy is currently `POSTED` on a meeting whose `status` is `OPEN`; that block is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MEETING_STATE`, because the board may not dissolve a conflict in the middle of the debate it would affect. Clearing a disclosure that is already `CLEARED` is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_ALREADY_CLEARED`, and that refusal is taken first, so an already-cleared disclosure answers `ERR_DISCLOSURE_ALREADY_CLEARED` even while such an item sits posted on an open meeting. Disclosures are part of the public record and can be listed by anyone, filtered by `board_member_id`, by `policy_id`, or by `status`.

Note the deliberate asymmetry between the two ways a trustee can leave the board. A resignation clears that trustee's `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` disclosures; a term expiry does not, because an expired term does not retroactively dissolve a disclosed interest.

### 3.3. The Policy Register, Version Drafting, and the Legal Update Service

A policy is registered as a shell — the text itself lives on version rows. Registration is an analyst operation carrying the policy `code` (globally unique across all policies, between 3 and 20 characters, for example `BP-4119`), the `title` as it appears in the manual, the manual chapter `category` from `GOVERNANCE`, `INSTRUCTION`, `PERSONNEL`, `STUDENTS` and `BUSINESS`, the `legal_requirement_level` from `REQUIRED_BY_LAW`, `RECOMMENDED` and `LOCAL_DISCRETION`, and the `manual_section` ordering key such as `4000.19`. A code already in use is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE`. A new policy opens with `status` `UNADOPTED`, `adopted_version_no` null, `last_review_date` null, `version_count` 0, `open_mandate_count`, `overdue_mandate_count` and `active_waiver_count` all 0, and its `compliance_state` computed immediately by the policy-scoped three-phase recompute — which, for a `REQUIRED_BY_LAW` policy with no adopted version, lands on `ACTION_REQUIRED`. A policy's `status` is one-way: it stays `UNADOPTED` until one of its versions reaches `ADOPTED`, and then it is `ADOPTED` forever.

The `legal_requirement_level` is the most consequential field on a policy, because it reaches into two unrelated corners of the system: it raises the vote threshold at a second reading, and it caps how long a waiver against that policy may run.

Drafting a version is the one write shared by the analyst and the superintendent. The payload carries the `body_text` of the version, a one-line `summary_of_change`, and the concurrency guard `expected_policy_version` described in section 2.6. On creation the server assigns `version_no` sequentially per policy starting at 1, in creation order, never reusing a number even when a version is later withdrawn. The new row opens with `status` `DRAFT`, `reading_count` 0, `first_reading_meeting_id`, `adopting_meeting_id`, `adopted_at`, `effective_date`, `superseded_at` and `withdrawn_reason` all null. Creating the version increments the parent policy's `version` by 1, echoes that new value back as `policy_version_after`, and recomputes the policy's `version_count` as the number of version rows on that policy in any status. Drafting also moves the legal record: every legal mandate on that policy whose `status` is `OPEN` transitions to `IN_PROGRESS`, signalling that the board has started work. That transition changes nothing in `open_mandate_count`, because `OPEN` and `IN_PROGRESS` both count as open.

An analyst may withdraw a version whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `FIRST_READING_PASSED`, supplying a `withdrawn_reason` that is recorded on the row. Withdrawal from `FIRST_READING_PASSED` retains `first_reading_meeting_id` for the historical record; withdrawal from `DRAFT` has no such field to retain. Either way the policy's `version_count` is recomputed and the policy's `version` rises by 1. Withdrawal is refused with `ERR_VERSION_ON_LIVE_AGENDA` while any agenda item in `PROPOSED` or `POSTED` status still references the version, and with `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` when the version is already `ADOPTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Versions can be listed and filtered by `policy_id`, by `status`, by `first_reading_meeting_id`, or by `adopting_meeting_id`; policies themselves can be listed and filtered by `status`, by `category`, or by `compliance_state`.

The legal update service files dated statutory notices against tracked policies. Each notice carries a globally unique `notice_code` such as `LUS-2026-014` — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NOTICE_CODE` — the `policy_id` it targets, the statutory `citation` it rests on, the `issued_on` instant the service published it, the `compliance_deadline` by which the board must act, and the `required_action` it compels, which is one of `ADOPT_NEW`, `REVISE_EXISTING` and `RESCIND`. The deadline must be strictly after `issued_on`. A filed mandate opens `OPEN` with `satisfied_by_version_no` null and `days_past_deadline` 0, and its arrival immediately drives the policy-scoped recompute so that the target policy's `open_mandate_count` and `compliance_state` are correct the moment the notice lands. Mandates can be listed and filtered by `policy_id` and by `status`.

A mandate is satisfied by adoption, not by drafting. When a version of the mandate's policy reaches `ADOPTED` with its `adopted_at` at or after the mandate's `issued_on`, every mandate on that policy whose `status` is `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `OVERDUE` and whose `required_action` is `ADOPT_NEW` or `REVISE_EXISTING` transitions to `SATISFIED`, recording the adopting version's `version_no` in `satisfied_by_version_no` and resetting `days_past_deadline` to 0. Late compliance is explicitly supported: an `OVERDUE` mandate satisfied this way lowers the policy's `overdue_mandate_count`, which can carry the policy back to `COMPLIANT` and flip a published manual edition from `LAPSED` back to `CERTIFIED`. Mandates whose `required_action` is `RESCIND` are never satisfied by an adoption.

### 3.4. Scheduling a Meeting, Posting Notice, and Building the Agenda

A meeting is scheduled by the clerk with a `title` as it will appear on the public notice, a `scope` of either `FULL_BOARD` or `COMMITTEE`, a `meeting_type` of `REGULAR`, `SPECIAL` or `EMERGENCY`, and the `scheduled_start_utc` instant at which it will be called to order. A `committee_id` must be supplied exactly when `scope` is `COMMITTEE` and must be omitted or null when `scope` is `FULL_BOARD`; getting that combination wrong is a malformed request. The presiding officer may also select the `public_comment_rule` that will govern speaker time, choosing `FIRST_COME_CAPPED` or `EQUAL_SPLIT`; when omitted it defaults to `FIRST_COME_CAPPED`.

Two calendars must not collide. Before a meeting is created, the system looks for another meeting with the same `scope` and the same `committee_id` — where a null committee matches a null committee — whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `NOTICED` or `OPEN`, and whose `scheduled_start_utc` sits strictly less than four hours away from the proposed start in either direction. Because the buffer is symmetric around the instant and measured as 240 minutes, two meetings exactly four hours apart are permitted, while anything closer is refused with `ERR_MEETING_SEPARATION_VIOLATION`.

A newly scheduled meeting opens with `status` `DRAFT` and `minutes_status` `NOT_DRAFTED` — two independent axes on the same record — and with `notice_posted_at`, `agenda_locked_at`, `opened_at`, `adjourned_at`, `minutes_approved_at_meeting_id` and `public_comment_allocation_variant` all null, `seated_member_count_snapshot` 0, and `attending_member_ids` an empty array. Its `public_comment_minutes_budget` is fixed at creation from the `meeting_type`: a regular meeting carries half an hour of public comment, thirty minutes; a special meeting carries five minutes; an emergency meeting carries none at all. `public_comment_minutes_remaining` starts equal to the budget and stays there until the meeting is called to order. The derived `quorum_required` is a bare majority of the board: before the meeting opens it is half the current count of `SEATED` trustees with the remainder discarded, plus one; once opened it is computed the same way from the frozen `seated_member_count_snapshot`.

The agenda is built while the meeting is still `DRAFT`. An item carries the `meeting_id` it sits on, an `item_type`, a `title` for the agenda line, the concurrency guard `expected_meeting_version` described in section 2.6, and — depending on the type — a linked object. The five item types are `POLICY_FIRST_READING`, `POLICY_SECOND_READING`, `WAIVER_DECISION`, `SUPERINTENDENT_EVALUATION` and `CONSENT`. A `policy_version_id` must be present exactly when `item_type` is `POLICY_FIRST_READING` or `POLICY_SECOND_READING` and must be null otherwise; a `waiver_request_id` must be present exactly when `item_type` is `WAIVER_DECISION` and must be null otherwise. On creation the item receives a `sequence_no` assigned sequentially per meeting starting at 1 in ascending `created_at` order and never reused when an item is later withdrawn, a `status` of `PROPOSED`, and a `requires_public_comment` flag that is true exactly when `item_type` is one of `POLICY_FIRST_READING`, `POLICY_SECOND_READING` or `WAIVER_DECISION` and false for the other two. Its whole vote ledger starts empty: `vote_yes`, `vote_no`, `vote_abstain`, `recused_member_count` and `absent_member_count` at 0, `required_yes_votes`, `vote_outcome` and `acted_at` null. Adding the item raises the parent meeting's `version` by 1 and echoes the new value back as `meeting_version_after`.

Agenda-build time applies its own eligibility check on the linked object, and it is deliberately looser than the check applied at the vote. For a `POLICY_FIRST_READING` item the linked version's `status` must be `DRAFT`. For a `POLICY_SECOND_READING` item the linked version's `status` may be either `DRAFT` or `FIRST_READING_PASSED` — a clerk is allowed to pre-list the second reading on a later agenda before the first reading has actually been held. In both cases a version that is `WITHDRAWN`, `ADOPTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. For a `WAIVER_DECISION` item the linked waiver's `status` must be `SUBMITTED` or `BOARD_PENDING`, so an item may be listed while the committee is still deliberating — which is exactly what lets a committee denial withdraw it later — and any other waiver status is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_IN_REVIEW`. The vote itself, as section 3.6 describes, still demands exactly `FIRST_READING_PASSED` for a second reading and exactly `BOARD_PENDING` for a waiver decision.

Posting the statutory public notice is the clerk's act that closes the agenda, and it moves the conduct axis `DRAFT → NOTICED`. It is permitted only when the difference between `scheduled_start_utc` and the logical clock is at least the notice lead time for the meeting's `meeting_type`: 72 hours — three full days — for a `REGULAR` meeting, 48 hours for a `SPECIAL` meeting, and 24 hours for an `EMERGENCY` meeting. The boundary is inclusive: a notice posted at exactly the lead time is good, and anything shorter is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`. Posting notice stamps `notice_posted_at` from the logical clock and stamps `agenda_locked_at` with that same instant, and it moves every agenda item on the meeting from `PROPOSED` to `POSTED` without renumbering anything, because `sequence_no` was fixed at creation. Once `agenda_locked_at` is set, no further item may be added and the attempt is refused with `ERR_AGENDA_LOCKED`. Attempting to post notice on a meeting whose `status` is not `DRAFT` — like attempting to open one that is not `NOTICED`, or adjourn one that is not `OPEN` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MEETING_STATE`.

Meetings can be listed and filtered by `committee_id`, by `status`, by `minutes_status`, or by `minutes_approved_at_meeting_id`; agenda items can be listed and filtered by `meeting_id`, by `policy_version_id`, by `waiver_request_id`, or by `status`.

### 3.5. Public Comment: Speaker Cards and the Minute Allocation

Members of the community sign up to speak by filing a speaker card against a specific agenda item, and the clerk may file one on their behalf for a walk-in. The card carries the `speaker_name` signed on it, the `speaker_affiliation` the speaker claims — `PARENT`, `STAFF`, `STUDENT`, `COMMUNITY` or `VENDOR` — and the `requested_minutes` they are asking for, an integer from 1 through 5. The card records the `agenda_item_id` of the item the speaker signed up to address, and is created `REQUESTED` with `allotted_minutes` 0 and `denial_reason` null, and `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. That stamp is the primary ordering key for everything that follows; cards are listed in ascending `submitted_at` order.

Sign-up closes the moment the meeting is called to order. A card may only be filed while the parent meeting's `status` is `DRAFT` or `NOTICED`, and a card filed against an `OPEN` or `ADJOURNED` meeting is refused with `ERR_PUBLIC_COMMENT_CLOSED`. Two capacity rules follow. A meeting whose `public_comment_minutes_budget` is zero has no public-comment period at all — this is the emergency meeting case — and so does an agenda item whose `requires_public_comment` is false; both are refused with `ERR_PUBLIC_COMMENT_NOT_PERMITTED`. And no agenda item may hold more than five speaker cards in `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED` status at once; the sixth is refused with `ERR_SPEAKER_LIMIT_REACHED`.

**The allocation.** When the meeting is called to order, the system distributes the meeting's minute budget across the speaker cards in one pass, and the result is written into every card and back onto the meeting. The input set is every card whose `status` is `REQUESTED` and whose parent agenda item belongs to this meeting, is in `POSTED` status, and has `requires_public_comment` true; cards on `PROPOSED`, `TABLED`, `ACTED` or `WITHDRAWN` items are excluded entirely. That set is ordered by ascending `submitted_at`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and any remaining tie broken by ascending `speaker_name` compared lexicographically with case sensitivity. A card's `id` is never consulted for ordering.

Before either allocation variant runs, duplicates are suppressed. Walking the ordered set once, the first card bearing a given `speaker_name` survives; every later card with an identical `speaker_name` is set to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `DUPLICATE_SPEAKER` and `allotted_minutes` 0, and is dropped from the set. No minutes are charged for a suppressed duplicate.

The variant is then chosen from the surviving card count. The meeting runs the **equal-split** variant when the meeting's `public_comment_rule` is `EQUAL_SPLIT`, or when the number of surviving cards has reached four or more — four is the crowd threshold at which honouring individual requests stops being fair. Otherwise it runs the **first-come-capped** variant. Whichever ran is recorded on the meeting as `public_comment_allocation_variant`.

Under **first-come-capped**, the surviving cards are walked in strict ascending `submitted_at` order against a running remainder that starts at the full budget. Each card's grant is the smallest of three quantities: the `requested_minutes` the speaker asked for, the per-speaker ceiling of three minutes, and whatever is left in the remainder. If that grant is at least one minute — the per-speaker floor — the card becomes `GRANTED`, its `allotted_minutes` is set to the grant, and the remainder falls by the same amount. If the grant is below one minute the card becomes `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` and `allotted_minutes` 0. This variant honours each speaker's request as an upper bound, so a speaker who asked for one minute receives exactly one minute and the rest of the budget stays unspent, and later cards can be starved entirely once the budget runs out.

Under **equal-split**, `requested_minutes` is ignored completely. The base share is the budget divided by the number of surviving cards with the remainder discarded — the flooring happens first, before any cap is applied — and the capped base is the smaller of that base and the three-minute per-speaker ceiling. If the capped base comes out at zero, meaning there is less than one whole minute per speaker, then the cards are walked in order and the first however-many cards the budget can cover each receive exactly the one-minute floor as `GRANTED` while the rest are `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`; the number of cards that can be covered is the budget itself, since each costs one minute. Otherwise every surviving card is `GRANTED` the capped base and the remainder is reduced accordingly, after which the integer remainder is walked out in a **single** sweep in the same ascending `submitted_at` order: each card that is still below the three-minute ceiling gains one more minute and the remainder falls by one, and the sweep stops the instant the remainder reaches zero. Minutes still unassigned after that single sweep stay in the remainder and are **not** redistributed in a second sweep. On identical card data, equal-split produces strictly larger per-speaker allotments and a strictly smaller `public_comment_minutes_remaining` than first-come-capped whenever the speakers asked for less than their equal share.

Whatever is left after allocation is written to the meeting as `public_comment_minutes_remaining`. A card's `denial_reason` is drawn from `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`, `DUPLICATE_SPEAKER` and `ITEM_WITHDRAWN`, and is null while the card is `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED`.

Granted minutes are given back whenever the item they were granted for dies. A `GRANTED` card whose parent item leaves `POSTED` without being acted on — tabled at adjournment, or withdrawn because its waiver died — moves to `RELEASED` with `denial_reason` `ITEM_WITHDRAWN`, its `allotted_minutes` reset to 0, and exactly that many minutes credited back to the meeting's `public_comment_minutes_remaining`. A card still in `REQUESTED` when its item is withdrawn before the meeting ever opened also moves to `RELEASED` with `denial_reason` `ITEM_WITHDRAWN`, but its `allotted_minutes` stays 0 and the meeting's budget is untouched, because nothing was ever debited for it. Speaker cards can be listed per item and filtered by `status`.

### 3.6. Conducting the Meeting: Quorum, the Roll-Call Vote, and Adjournment

Calling a meeting to order is the president's act and moves the conduct axis `NOTICED → OPEN`. The payload carries `attending_member_ids`, the array of trustee identifiers recorded present. The meeting may not be opened before its own start time — the logical clock must have reached or passed `scheduled_start_utc`, and an early attempt is refused with `ERR_MEETING_NOT_YET_DUE`. Every identifier in `attending_member_ids` must resolve to a trustee — one that matches no trustee at all is refused with `ERR_BOARD_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, resolved as an ordinary body foreign key at rung 3 of the ladder, before the presidential identity check — and every trustee so resolved must be `SEATED`, one that is not being refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_SEATED`. The number of attendees must be at least `quorum_required`, and a thinner room is refused with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`.

Opening stamps `opened_at` from the logical clock, freezes `seated_member_count_snapshot` at the count of trustees whose `status` is `SEATED` at that instant, and freezes `attending_member_ids` as supplied. Both are frozen for good: every vote tally on this meeting's items is measured against that snapshot even if a trustee resigns mid-meeting. Opening also runs the public-comment allocation described in section 3.5 and writes `public_comment_allocation_variant` and `public_comment_minutes_remaining`.

**Recording a roll-call vote** is likewise a presidential act, and it is where most of this system's business logic converges. The payload carries three arrays of trustee identifiers: `yes_member_ids`, `no_member_ids` and `abstain_member_ids`. The item must be `POSTED`, and a vote on an item in any other status is refused with `ERR_AGENDA_ITEM_NOT_POSTED`; the item's parent meeting must be `OPEN`, and otherwise the vote is refused with `ERR_MEETING_NOT_OPEN`.

Before any counting happens, four guards run in the order given in section 2.7. No trustee named in any of the three arrays may hold a disclosure in `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` status on the policy behind this item — the policy behind a reading item is the policy of its linked version, the policy behind a `WAIVER_DECISION` item is the policy of its linked waiver, and evaluation and consent items have no policy behind them at all — and a recused trustee appearing in any array is refused with `ERR_RECUSED_MEMBER_VOTED`. The number of votes cast plus the recused headcount must be at least the meeting's `quorum_required`, and otherwise the vote is refused with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. Every identifier in the three arrays must appear in the meeting's frozen `attending_member_ids`, and a stranger is refused with `ERR_VOTER_NOT_IN_ATTENDANCE`. And no trustee may appear more than once across the three arrays, nor twice within one array; a double vote is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTER`.

**The tally.** `vote_yes`, `vote_no` and `vote_abstain` are the lengths of the three supplied arrays, and votes cast is their sum. `recused_member_count` is the number of trustees in the meeting's frozen `attending_member_ids` who hold a disclosure in `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` status on the policy behind this item, measured at the instant the vote is recorded; for an item with no linked policy it is zero. `absent_member_count` is the frozen `seated_member_count_snapshot` less the sum of `vote_yes`, `vote_no`, `vote_abstain` and `recused_member_count`, which means attendees who cast no vote and are not recused are counted as absent for ledger purposes.

The threshold `required_yes_votes` depends on the item type and, for one branch, on the policy's legal weight. For a `POLICY_SECOND_READING` item on a policy whose `legal_requirement_level` is `REQUIRED_BY_LAW`, the threshold is a two-thirds supermajority of the whole seated body: two times the frozen `seated_member_count_snapshot`, divided by three, rounded **up** to the next whole trustee. For a `POLICY_SECOND_READING` item on any other policy, and for a `WAIVER_DECISION` item, the threshold is a simple majority of the whole seated body: the frozen snapshot halved with the remainder discarded, plus one. For `POLICY_FIRST_READING`, `SUPERINTENDENT_EVALUATION` and `CONSENT` items, the threshold is a simple majority of the votes actually cast: votes cast halved with the remainder discarded, plus one. The distinction matters: when the denominator is votes cast, abstentions raise the bar while absences do not; when the denominator is the frozen seated body, absences and abstentions both count against the motion.

`vote_outcome` is `PASSED` when `vote_yes` has reached `required_yes_votes` and `FAILED` otherwise. Recording the vote moves the item `POSTED → ACTED`, stamps `acted_at` from the logical clock, and freezes the whole ledger. The downstream transition on the linked policy version or waiver request then fires, as sections 3.7 and 3.8 describe.

**Adjournment** is the clerk's act and moves the conduct axis `OPEN → ADJOURNED`, stamping `adjourned_at`. Every agenda item still in `POSTED` status when the gavel falls moves to `TABLED`, and every `GRANTED` speaker card on a newly tabled item moves to `RELEASED` with `denial_reason` `ITEM_WITHDRAWN`, `allotted_minutes` reset to 0, and its minutes credited back to `public_comment_minutes_remaining`. `ADJOURNED` is terminal on the conduct axis: a meeting cannot be re-noticed or reopened, and consequently a `TABLED` item has no path back onto that meeting's agenda.

### 3.7. The Two-Reading Adoption Cycle, the Minutes Axis, and the Adoption Cascade

A policy version must survive two readings at two different meetings before it becomes board policy, and the minutes of the first meeting must have been approved before the second reading can be held. Those three facts make the adoption cycle the most tightly coupled workflow in this system.

**The regular path.** A `POLICY_FIRST_READING` item that passes carries its linked version `DRAFT → FIRST_READING_PASSED`, sets `reading_count` to 1, records the hosting meeting in `first_reading_meeting_id`, raises the version row's own `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`. Nothing on the policy itself moves and no mandate is satisfied. The version must be in `DRAFT` at that moment whatever the vote will decide; anything else is refused with `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A `POLICY_FIRST_READING` item that fails leaves the version exactly where it stood — still `DRAFT`, `reading_count` still 0, `first_reading_meeting_id` still null and its own `version` untouched — because only a passing first reading moves the version at all.

A `POLICY_SECOND_READING` item may only be heard on a version whose `status` is `FIRST_READING_PASSED`, and again a version in any other status is refused with `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Two further guards apply and they are ordered. First, the second reading may not be held at the meeting that held the first reading: if the item's meeting is the same meeting recorded in the version's `first_reading_meeting_id`, the vote is refused with `ERR_SECOND_READING_SAME_MEETING`. Only then is the minutes rule consulted: the meeting named by `first_reading_meeting_id` must have `minutes_status` `APPROVED`, and otherwise the vote is refused with `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`.

When the second reading passes, the full adoption cascade fires, and every hop of it must land inside the same request. The version's `reading_count` becomes 2, `adopting_meeting_id` records the hosting meeting, `adopted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `effective_date` is set to 30 days after `adopted_at` — the standard delay before a regularly adopted policy takes force. Because a newer version is now board policy, the policy's prior `ADOPTED` version, if there is one, moves `ADOPTED → SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped from the logical clock; the policy's `status` becomes `ADOPTED`, its `adopted_version_no` becomes this version's `version_no`, its `last_review_date` is set to `adopted_at`, and its `version` rises by 1. Because `adopted_version_no` changed, every legal mandate on that policy in `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `OVERDUE` status whose `required_action` is `ADOPT_NEW` or `REVISE_EXISTING` and whose `issued_on` is at or before `adopted_at` becomes `SATISFIED` with `satisfied_by_version_no` recorded. Because mandate statuses changed, the policy's `open_mandate_count` and `overdue_mandate_count` are recomputed and phase 2 of the compliance recompute rewrites `compliance_state`. And because `compliance_state` changed, every manual edition in `PUBLISHED` status whose pinned entries include this policy recomputes its `compliance_gap_count` and flips `certification_state` — `CERTIFIED` when the gap count has reached zero, `LAPSED` otherwise.

**The revision loop.** A `POLICY_SECOND_READING` item that fails does not kill the version. The item is marked `ACTED` with `vote_outcome` `FAILED` and `acted_at` stamped, and the version goes `FIRST_READING_PASSED → DRAFT` with `reading_count` reset to 0 and `first_reading_meeting_id` cleared back to null. No policy field changes and no mandate is satisfied. The version must climb the entire two-reading ladder again, from a first reading at some future meeting. This genuine return to a prior state is what makes the adoption machine non-linear. Note that the minutes guard still applies to a failing second reading: a second reading cannot even be held, let alone lost, while the first-reading meeting's minutes are unapproved.

**The emergency branch.** At a meeting whose `meeting_type` is `EMERGENCY`, a `POLICY_FIRST_READING` item that passes with `vote_yes` reaching at least one fewer than the frozen `seated_member_count_snapshot` — that is, near unanimity of the seated board — waives the second reading entirely. Such an item takes its version straight from `DRAFT` to `ADOPTED`, skipping `FIRST_READING_PASSED` altogether: `reading_count` becomes 2, `adopting_meeting_id` and `adopted_at` are written, and `effective_date` equals `adopted_at` exactly, with no delay, because an emergency adoption takes force immediately. The same four downstream hops then run — prior version superseded, policy adopted and its counters refreshed, matching mandates satisfied, and published manual editions re-certified — identically to the regular path. At an emergency meeting where the passing margin falls short of that near-unanimous bar, and at every `REGULAR` or `SPECIAL` meeting regardless of margin, a passing first reading takes the ordinary `DRAFT → FIRST_READING_PASSED` route.

**The minutes axis.** A meeting's `minutes_status` is a second, independent state axis that advances on its own trigger. It only starts moving after the conduct axis has reached its terminal state. The clerk supplies an `action`: `DRAFT` moves `NOT_DRAFTED → DRAFTED` and is permitted only once the meeting's `status` is `ADJOURNED`; `APPROVE` moves `DRAFTED → APPROVED` and additionally requires an `approving_meeting_id` in the payload, which must be supplied exactly when the action is `APPROVE`. Either advance raises the meeting's `version` by 1. A `DRAFT` action on minutes that are not `NOT_DRAFTED`, an `APPROVE` action on minutes that are not `DRAFTED`, and a `DRAFT` action on a meeting that has not adjourned are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_MINUTES_STATE`.

Minutes may only be approved at a genuinely later meeting. The meeting named by `approving_meeting_id` must currently be `OPEN`, must not be the meeting whose minutes are being approved, and must have a `scheduled_start_utc` strictly after that of the meeting whose minutes are on the table. Naming the same meeting, or one that is not scheduled strictly later, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPROVING_MEETING`. On approval the meeting records `minutes_approved_at_meeting_id` — a self-reference from one meeting to another — and, in doing so, unblocks any second reading whose version records that meeting in `first_reading_meeting_id`. This is the cross-meeting dependency at the heart of the open-meeting rules: the vote at meeting N+1 depends on the minutes state of meeting N.

### 3.8. Policy Waivers: Site Request, Committee Review, Board Decision, and Revocation

A school site that cannot live under a board policy asks for a time-boxed exception. The request is filed by the site's principal and carries the `policy_id` the school wants relief from, the `reviewing_committee_id` of the standing committee that must recommend before the board votes, the `requesting_school_code` identifying the site (for example `HS-01`), a `justification` narrative recorded in the public file, and the half-open window the relief would cover: `requested_start_date` inclusive and `requested_end_date` exclusive, where the end must be strictly after the start.

Three checks run on the window, and they run in the order given. First, no live waiver for the same policy and the same school may already cover part of it. A waiver is live for this purpose when its `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `BOARD_PENDING` or `GRANTED`, and the windows overlap when the existing request's `requested_start_date` falls before the new window's end **and** the new window's start falls before the existing request's `requested_end_date` — it is always the requested window of the existing waiver that is compared, never a granted or clipped one. Because the intervals are half-open, exact adjacency — where one window's end is the same instant as the next window's start — is permitted; a genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Second, the window must be at least one whole day long, measured as the difference between the two instants in seconds divided by 86400 with the remainder discarded; anything shorter is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WAIVER_WINDOW`. Third, that same whole-day count may not exceed the cap the linked policy's `legal_requirement_level` imposes, which is recorded on the request as `max_allowed_days` at creation: 90 days for a `REQUIRED_BY_LAW` policy, 180 days for a `RECOMMENDED` one, and a full 365 days for one at `LOCAL_DISCRETION`. Exceeding the cap is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_DURATION_EXCEEDS_CAP`.

A filed request opens `SUBMITTED` with `committee_recommendation`, `denial_stage`, `granted_start_date`, `granted_end_date` and `revoked_on` all null and `granted_days` 0.

**Committee review** is recorded by the chair of the reviewing committee acting as a trustee, and the payload carries a single `recommendation`. The waiver must be `SUBMITTED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_IN_REVIEW` — and the reviewing committee's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, with an inquorate committee refused by `ERR_COMMITTEE_INQUORATE`. The two recommendation values produce strictly different outcomes. `RECOMMEND_GRANT` records `committee_recommendation` and advances the waiver `SUBMITTED → BOARD_PENDING`; nothing else moves. `RECOMMEND_DENY` records `committee_recommendation`, sets `denial_stage` to `COMMITTEE`, and takes the waiver straight to the terminal `DENIED` state — and because the waiver is now terminal, every agenda item referencing it in `PROPOSED` or `POSTED` status moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and every speaker card on those items in `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED` status moves to `RELEASED` with `denial_reason` `ITEM_WITHDRAWN`, `allotted_minutes` reset to 0, and any granted minutes credited back to the meeting's `public_comment_minutes_remaining`.

**The board decision** rides on a `WAIVER_DECISION` agenda item, and the waiver must be `BOARD_PENDING` when the vote is recorded; any other status is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_IN_REVIEW`. A passing vote takes the waiver `BOARD_PENDING → GRANTED` and writes the effective window. `granted_start_date` is the later of the requested start and the logical clock at the moment of the grant; `granted_end_date` is the requested end unchanged; and `granted_days` is the whole number of days between them, never below zero. Two shapes follow from that rule: when the board grants before the requested start has arrived, the grant is forward-dated, `granted_start_date` equals `requested_start_date` and `granted_days` covers the full requested span; when the board grants on or after the requested start, the window is clipped forward to the clock and `granted_days` is strictly less than the requested span. Granting then recomputes the policy's `active_waiver_count` and rewrites its `compliance_state` through phase 2 of the compliance recompute. A failing vote takes the waiver `BOARD_PENDING → DENIED` with `denial_stage` set to `BOARD`, leaving `granted_start_date` and `granted_end_date` null; no other agenda item is withdrawn in that case, because the deciding item itself has already reached `ACTED`.

**Revocation** is available to the superintendent and to the president, and only on a waiver whose `status` is `GRANTED` — otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_IN_REVIEW`. It is a four-hop cascade that leaves the waiver in the terminal `REVOKED` state. `revoked_on` is stamped from the logical clock, `granted_end_date` is rewritten to that same instant, and `granted_days` is recomputed as the whole number of days from `granted_start_date` to the new end, never below zero. Because the waiver is no longer live, the policy's `active_waiver_count` is recomputed and phase 2 rewrites `compliance_state`. Because the waiver is now terminal, every agenda item referencing it in `PROPOSED` or `POSTED` status moves to `WITHDRAWN`. And because those items were withdrawn, every speaker card on them in `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED` status moves to `RELEASED` with `denial_reason` `ITEM_WITHDRAWN`, `allotted_minutes` reset to 0, and exactly the released total credited back to the meeting's `public_comment_minutes_remaining`.

Waiver requests can be listed and filtered by `policy_id`, by `reviewing_committee_id`, by `status`, or by `requesting_school_code`. The `EXPIRED` terminal state is reached only through the compliance sweep, as section 3.9 describes.

### 3.9. Annual Cycles: Superintendent Evaluation, Manual Publication, and the Compliance Sweep

**The superintendent evaluation.** A cycle is opened by the clerk and carries a `cycle_year` between 2020 and 2100 that must be unique across all cycles — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVALUATION_CYCLE` — the `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` bounding the evaluation window, a `midyear_checkpoint_date` that must fall strictly between them, a `goal_count` between 1 and 10 giving the number of annual goals adopted, and the four rubric weights: `weight_instructional`, `weight_fiscal`, `weight_community` and `weight_governance`. Each weight is a number between 0.00 and 1.00 carried to two decimals, and the four must sum to exactly 1.00; a rubric that does not normalize is refused with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED`. The cycle opens in `PLANNING` with `reopen_count` 0, `rating_count` and `eligible_rater_count` 0, and `midyear_formative_score`, `summative_composite_score` and `adopted_at` all null.

Trustees submit rubric scores one domain at a time, each in their own name. A rating carries the `evaluation_id`, the `board_member_id` of the submitting trustee — which must equal the caller's `X-User-ID` — the `domain` scored, an integer `score` from 1 through 5, and an optional `comments` narrative. The four domains are `INSTRUCTIONAL_LEADERSHIP`, `FISCAL_STEWARDSHIP`, `COMMUNITY_RELATIONS` and `GOVERNANCE_SUPPORT`. The trustee must be `SEATED`, and otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_SEATED`. On creation `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `phase` is stamped from the cycle's current stage: `SUMMATIVE` when the cycle is in `SUMMATIVE_DRAFT` or `ADOPTED`, and `FORMATIVE` in every other stage — `PLANNING`, `GOALS_SET` and `MIDYEAR_REVIEW` alike. The derived `counted_in_summative` flag is true exactly when the submitting trustee's `status` is `SEATED` and their `term_end_date` is at or after the cycle's `window_end_date`. Submitting the rating recomputes the cycle's `rating_count`, the number of ratings on that cycle, and its `eligible_rater_count`, the number of distinct trustees among those ratings whose `status` is `SEATED` and whose `term_end_date` is at or after `window_end_date`. A trustee may submit only one rating per combination of cycle, `domain` and `phase`; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATING`. Ratings can be listed and filtered by `evaluation_id`, by `board_member_id`, or by `domain`.

The president drives the cycle with an `action` on the ladder `SET_GOALS`, `MIDYEAR`, `DRAFT_SUMMATIVE`, `REOPEN` and `ADOPT`; an action that is not declared from the cycle's current stage is refused with `ERR_EVALUATION_STAGE_INVALID`. `SET_GOALS` moves `PLANNING → GOALS_SET`, permitted while `goal_count` is at least 1, after which ratings are stamped `FORMATIVE`. `MIDYEAR` moves `GOALS_SET → MIDYEAR_REVIEW` and requires at least one rating on the cycle, refusing with `ERR_NO_RATINGS_SUBMITTED` otherwise; it writes `midyear_formative_score`. `DRAFT_SUMMATIVE` moves `MIDYEAR_REVIEW → SUMMATIVE_DRAFT`, after which ratings are stamped `SUMMATIVE`. `REOPEN` moves `SUMMATIVE_DRAFT → GOALS_SET`, raising `reopen_count` by 1 and resetting `summative_composite_score` to null while retaining `midyear_formative_score`; a reopened cycle must climb the ladder again, so more ratings can be added and the composite recomputed. `ADOPT` moves `SUMMATIVE_DRAFT → ADOPTED`, requires both at least one rating and an `eligible_rater_count` of at least 1 — failing either is `ERR_NO_RATINGS_SUBMITTED` — writes `summative_composite_score`, and stamps `adopted_at` from the logical clock.

The two composite scores are computed by strictly different rules, and both are clamped to lie between 1.00 and 5.00 after rounding. The **mid-year formative** score pools every rating on the cycle whose `phase` is `FORMATIVE` and whose submitting trustee's `status` is `SEATED`; no term-expiry filter applies. It is the plain arithmetic mean of those `score` values — their sum divided by their count — rounded half up to two decimal places, then clamped. Every domain is pooled together and the four rubric weights are not used at all. If no rating survives the filter, the advance is refused with `ERR_NO_RATINGS_SUBMITTED`.

The **summative** score takes every rating on the cycle, of **either** phase — a `FORMATIVE` rating still counts toward the summative composite — whose submitting trustee's `status` is `SEATED` and whose `term_end_date` is at or after the cycle's `window_end_date`. If no rating survives that filter, the adoption is refused with `ERR_NO_RATINGS_SUBMITTED`. The rounding order is pinned and must not be collapsed into a single rounding at the end. For each domain that received at least one surviving rating, the mean of that domain's `score` values is computed and rounded half up to two decimal places. Each rounded domain mean is then multiplied by that domain's weight — `weight_instructional` for `INSTRUCTIONAL_LEADERSHIP`, `weight_fiscal` for `FISCAL_STEWARDSHIP`, `weight_community` for `COMMUNITY_RELATIONS`, `weight_governance` for `GOVERNANCE_SUPPORT` — and those products are summed. That sum is then divided by the sum of the weights of only the domains that were actually rated, which renormalizes the rubric over the domains present. The quotient is rounded half up to two decimal places and clamped. On identical rating rows this yields a strictly different number from the mid-year formative score whenever the ratings are not evenly distributed across domains.

**Publishing the manual.** An analyst starts a new edition with a `publication_label` such as `2026-FALL`, globally unique across all editions and otherwise refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PUBLICATION_LABEL`, and an `as_of_date` stating the instant the edition claims to describe. The edition opens `BUILDING` with `certification_state` `PENDING`, `pinned_entries` an empty array, `entry_count` and `compliance_gap_count` 0, and `published_at` and `superseded_by_label` null.

Publishing freezes a snapshot. It is permitted only on an edition whose `status` is `BUILDING` — otherwise `ERR_PUBLICATION_ALREADY_PUBLISHED` — and only when at least one policy has `status` `ADOPTED`, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_POLICIES`. Every policy whose `status` is `ADOPTED` and whose `adopted_version_no` is not null at that instant contributes one entry; `UNADOPTED` policies are excluded entirely. The entries are ordered by ascending `manual_section` and, within an equal section, by ascending `code`, both compared lexicographically with case sensitivity, and a policy's `id` never participates in the ordering. Each entry is an object recording the policy's identifier under `policy_id`, its `code` under `policy_code`, the `adopted_version_no` current at that instant under `version_no`, and its `manual_section`. Those entries become `pinned_entries`, `entry_count` becomes their number, `compliance_gap_count` becomes the number of entries whose policy's `compliance_state` is anything other than `COMPLIANT`, `certification_state` becomes `CERTIFIED` when that gap count is zero and `LAPSED` otherwise, `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the edition moves `BUILDING → PUBLISHED`. Every other edition already in `PUBLISHED` status moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_by_label` set to this edition's `publication_label`, keeping its own `pinned_entries` verbatim as the historical record of what the manual said at that time. When no other edition was published, no supersede cascade fires and `superseded_by_label` stays null everywhere.

The version numbers inside `pinned_entries` are frozen forever: a later adoption on the same policy changes that policy's `adopted_version_no` but never rewrites an already-published edition's entry. `compliance_gap_count` and `certification_state`, by contrast, are live — phase 3 of the compliance recompute rewrites them whenever a pinned policy's posture moves, in either direction.

**The compliance sweep.** Clock movement alone changes nothing; the sweep is what turns a new clock position into consequences, and it may be triggered by the clerk or by the legal update service. In one pass it marks every mandate whose `compliance_deadline` the clock has reached or passed and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `IN_PROGRESS` as `OVERDUE`, refreshing `days_past_deadline`; expires every waiver whose `status` is `GRANTED` and whose `granted_end_date` the clock has reached or passed, taking it `GRANTED → EXPIRED`, recomputing the policy's `active_waiver_count` and `compliance_state`, and withdrawing every agenda item referencing it in `PROPOSED` or `POSTED` status while releasing those items' speaker cards and crediting their minutes back; and expires every trustee whose `status` is `SEATED` and whose `term_end_date` the clock has reached or passed, taking them `SEATED → TERM_EXPIRED`, moving every committee seat they hold in `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED` status to `REMOVED`, and letting each affected committee recompute its seat counters and posture — while pointedly leaving their `RECUSAL_REQUIRED` disclosures alone. It then runs all three phases of the compliance recompute over every policy in the system in ascending `code` order, and every `PUBLISHED` edition recomputes its `compliance_gap_count` and `certification_state`.

A mandate that becomes `OVERDUE` drives a three-hop cascade of its own: `days_past_deadline` is recomputed, the policy's `overdue_mandate_count` is recomputed and its `compliance_state` becomes `NON_COMPLIANT`, and every `PUBLISHED` edition pinning that policy recomputes `compliance_gap_count` and flips `certification_state` from `CERTIFIED` to `LAPSED`.

The sweep answers with five counters, and their semantics differ in a way worth stating precisely. `mandates_marked_overdue` counts the mandates **this** sweep moved into `OVERDUE`. `waivers_expired` counts the waivers this sweep moved into `EXPIRED`. `members_term_expired` counts the trustees this sweep moved into `TERM_EXPIRED`. `policies_recomputed` counts every policy whose phase-2 block ran, which is all of them. `publications_lapsed`, uniquely, is not a delta: it counts every `PUBLISHED` edition whose `certification_state` is `LAPSED` **after** the sweep finishes, whether or not this sweep is what flipped it.
