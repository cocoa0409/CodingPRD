# Audit Independence Monitoring API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Audit firms operate under an independence regime that regulators, client audit committees and network member firms all police at the same time. A single engagement can be compromised in several independent ways: a signing partner who has served one client for too long, a covered person holding a material financial interest in the audited entity, a non-audit service that was performed without audit-committee pre-approval, or a member firm in another jurisdiction that has quietly stopped honoring the network's shared independence obligations. The platform specified here is the system of record for those obligations. It evaluates client acceptance and continuance, monitors independence across the firm and its global affiliates, enforces partner rotation and cooling-off periods, administers audit-committee pre-approval of non-audit services, and tracks every independence finding from discovery through remediation to closure.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. The first is that the platform is temporal. All deadlines, decay windows, cooling-off periods, rotation clocks and inspection response windows are evaluated against a single server-side logical clock that starts at a fixed instant and advances only when an administrator moves it. This makes every time-dependent verdict reproducible: a finding assessed twenty-five months before "now" decays differently from one assessed five months before "now", and the same request replayed against the same clock always yields the same answer. The second commitment is that independence is contagious across the object graph. An engagement's posture is derived from the findings, threats, prohibited services and affiliate attestations attached to it and to its client; a client's posture is the worst posture among its engagements; a material personal interest declared against a client raises a critical finding on every engagement that client owns. Independent reviewers, committees and engagement partners therefore observe a single, consistently propagated view rather than a set of locally cached flags.

The API is organized around a flat resource topology mounted under the `/audit` prefix. Reference data (clients, affiliates, professionals) is registered first; engagements are opened against a client; acceptance evaluations and their integrity evidence govern whether an engagement may be activated; role assignments staff it; findings, threats, attestations and non-audit services continuously move its independence posture; quality control reviews and regulatory inspections sit alongside it; and a final closure action retires both the engagement and, when it was the last one standing, the client itself. The remainder of this document defines the global contracts that apply to every resource, the business workflows that drive each lifecycle, the endpoint catalogue, and the error catalogue.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. A request missing either header is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` and HTTP status 401 before any other processing occurs. When both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not among the roles authorized for the targeted operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` and HTTP status 403. Role authorization is evaluated per endpoint; the authorized set for each operation appears in the Authorized Role(s) column of §4.

Six roles exist in the system, and their names are spelled exactly as follows.

- `PlatformAdmin` — the firm operations administrator, who owns reference data (clients, affiliates, professionals), affiliate attestation intake and the logical clock.
- `EngagementPartner` — the signing partner accountable for an engagement, who opens engagements, staffs them, ends assignments and closes them.
- `ClientAcceptanceCommittee` — the firm committee that evaluates client acceptance and continuance, deliberately segregated from engagement staffing.
- `IndependenceOfficer` — the independence office, which raises and remediates findings and threats, advances affiliate attestations, and may revoke an approved non-audit service.
- `AuditCommitteeLiaison` — the liaison to the client audit committee, who requests non-audit services and records their pre-approval.
- `QualityReviewer` — the engagement quality control reviewer, who owns quality reviews and regulatory inspection responses.

The binding between `X-User-ID` and persisted data depends on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `EngagementPartner` or `QualityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Professional`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Professional` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ClientAcceptanceCommittee`, `IndependenceOfficer` or `AuditCommitteeLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The role model enforces segregation of duties rather than data ownership: the committee that approves an acceptance never staffs the engagement, the independence office that raises a finding never signs the engagement, and the liaison that requests a non-audit service does not control the cap that constrains it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list operation accepts two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of records returned in one page; it defaults to 10, must be at least 1 and must not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip from the beginning of the result set and defaults to 0. Both parameters are optional; when omitted, the defaults apply. List responses include four pagination fields in the `meta` object: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports the total number of records matching the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is a boolean that is true when further records remain beyond the returned page. Unless a filter narrows the result, list endpoints return records ordered by `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

All twelve top-level resources in this API — clients, engagements, client acceptances, integrity assessments, professionals, engagement role assignments, independence findings, independence threats, non-audit services, affiliates, affiliate attestations and quality reviews — are created with the same four system-managed fields.

- `id` (string) — a unique, server-minted identifier for the resource, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix and no embedded resource name.
- `created_at` (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits (for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`) recording when the resource was first created.
- `updated_at` (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the resource was last modified. Any change to any field of the resource, including changes made by a server-driven cascade rather than by a direct client request, refreshes this value.
- `version` (integer) — a system-managed counter that begins at its initial value and increments by 1 on every mutation of the resource. It is the value that optimistic concurrency control compares against, as described in §2.8.

Every entity in this API carries the complete set of four fields; no resource omits `updated_at` or `version`. Derived and computed fields are ordinary fields of the resource for this purpose: recomputing `Client.aggregate_independence_status`, `Engagement.independence_status`, `Engagement.assigned_workload_points`, `Professional.active_workload_points`, `EngagementRoleAssignment.rotation_status` or `Client.naf_approved_fee_cents` is a mutation, and a mutation refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` on the resource that owns the field.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Temporal Windows

The server maintains a single logical clock. It starts at the fixed default instant `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to wall-clock time; every temporal verdict the system renders is computed against this value. A `PlatformAdmin` may advance or set it through the clock capability described in §4, which accepts a `current_utc` value and returns the clock's new reading. Because the clock is the only source of "now", the same request evaluated against the same clock reading always produces the same answer.

Four families of rules are evaluated against the logical clock. First, the integrity decay tiers of §3.2 count whole months from each assessment's `assessed_on` to the clock's current date. Second, cooling-off windows for employment relationships are anchored on `effective_on` and compared against the clock whenever a threat is read or a professional is considered for staffing. Third, rotation tenure measures an assignment's effective end as its `ended_on` when that is set and otherwise as the earlier of its `scheduled_end_on` and the clock; an ended assignment becomes `ELIGIBLE` only once the clock reaches `eligible_return_on`. Fourth, inspection response deadlines are computed forward from `received_on`. Two validation rules also depend on the clock directly: an integrity assessment whose `assessed_on` falls later than the clock is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE`, and a role assignment whose `started_on` falls earlier than the clock is rejected with `ERR_BACKDATED_ASSIGNMENT`.

### 2.6. The Independence Posture Ladder and Its Propagation

Independence posture is expressed on two levels that share one vocabulary: `CLEAR`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN`. An engagement carries its own posture in `Engagement.independence_status`; a client carries the roll-up in `Client.aggregate_independence_status`. Both are recomputed — never set directly by a client request — whenever an independence finding, an independence threat, a non-audit service or an affiliate attestation is created or changes status, and on acceptance approval. The recompute reads the engagement's attached evidence and selects the worst posture that any rule justifies.

For a given engagement, five counts drive the verdict. The first is the number of its independence findings that are still live — that is, in status `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION`, `RE_TEST` or `REOPENED` — and carry severity `CRITICAL`. The second is the number of its live findings that carry severity `HIGH`. The third is the number of non-audit services that belong to the engagement or to its client, have `is_prohibited_for_pie` true, and are in status `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED`. The fourth is the number of the engagement's affiliate attestations whose `attestation_status` is `BREACHED`. The fifth is the network risk points accumulated under §2.7 for the engagement.

The posture is `FROZEN` when any one of these holds: at least one live `CRITICAL` finding, at least two live `HIGH` findings, at least one prohibited non-audit service in the statuses named above, at least one material financial-interest threat on the client whose status is `DECLARED` or `IN_COOLING_OFF`, or network risk points of 12 or more. If the engagement is not `FROZEN`, its posture is `RESTRICTED` when exactly one live `HIGH` finding exists, or at least one affiliate attestation is `BREACHED`, or network risk points reach 6 or more. Otherwise the posture is `CLEAR`. The client roll-up applies the same vocabulary one level up: `Client.aggregate_independence_status` is `FROZEN` when any engagement of the client is `FROZEN`, `RESTRICTED` when none is `FROZEN` but at least one is `RESTRICTED`, and `CLEAR` otherwise.

Posture propagates into lifecycle status automatically, without a client request on the affected resource. When an engagement that is `ACTIVE` recomputes to `FROZEN`, its status is set to `SUSPENDED`; while it is suspended, its `ACTIVE` role assignments keep their status but are excluded from workload ceilings until the engagement resumes. When the recomputed posture leaves `FROZEN` for `RESTRICTED` or `CLEAR`, an engagement suspended by that rule returns to `ACTIVE` provided no live `CRITICAL` finding remains. The client follows the same pattern one level up: a client whose aggregate posture becomes `FROZEN` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, and it returns to `ACTIVE` once no engagement of the client is `FROZEN`. Every one of these automatic transitions increments the `version` of the resource it touches and refreshes its `updated_at`.

### 2.7. Global Network Risk Weighting

Multinational engagements inherit independence exposure from every network member firm that touches them, and that exposure is quantified as network risk points. An engagement that is not multinational accrues no network risk at all: its network risk points are zero regardless of what any affiliate reports. For a multinational engagement, the raw score is the sum, over every affiliate attestation attached to the engagement, of that attestation's `breach_count` multiplied by the weight of the attesting firm's `network_tier`. Member firms weigh 3, correspondents weigh 2, and alliance firms weigh 1. Cumulative breach counts are never reset by the weighting: `breach_count` accumulates over the life of the attestation and every unit of it is weighted on each recompute. The raw score is then clamped into the range 0 to 50 inclusive — never below zero, never above 50.

Network risk feeds the posture ladder of §2.6 at two thresholds: 12 points or more forces `FROZEN`, and 6 points or more forces `RESTRICTED` when the engagement is not already frozen. Because a member-firm breach weighs three points, four breaches reported by member firms are sufficient to freeze a multinational engagement on network grounds alone. The `Affiliate.open_breach_count` field is the companion counter at the firm level: it reports how many of that affiliate's attestations are currently in the `BREACHED` state.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Engagement-Scoped Writes

Two write operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, both of them because they mutate an engagement's staffing or independence evidence from a snapshot the client may no longer hold. The first is the creation of an engagement role assignment; the second is the raising of an independence finding. Both are engagement-scoped writes, and both require the request body to carry `expected_engagement_version` as a non-optional field holding the client's last observed value of the target engagement's `version`.

The server compares the supplied value against the engagement's current `version`. If the two differ — because a sibling assignment, an acceptance decision, an independence recompute or any other mutation advanced the engagement since the client last read it — the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP status 412, and nothing is created. If the values match, the write proceeds and, on success, the engagement's `version` advances by 1 as part of the same operation. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field in its request body; a request that supplies one to an endpoint not listed here has supplied an unknown field.

### 2.9. Scoring, Fee Pooling and Rounding Precision

Three numeric families exist in this system, and each has its own precision contract. Risk and integrity scores — `ClientAcceptance.risk_score`, `ClientAcceptance.combined_risk_score` and `ClientAcceptance.integrity_score` — are stored and returned with exactly 2 decimal places using the round-half-up method. The clamping bounds for the combined score are the exact integers 0 and 100: a computed risk score below 0 is reported as 0, and one above 100 is reported as 100.

Non-audit service fees are handled in whole cents, but the approved fee pool is a whole-dollar quantity. Each individual service fee is rounded to the nearest whole dollar using round-half-up before it is admitted to the pool, and the pool is the sum of those already-rounded amounts; rounding the aggregate afterwards is explicitly not this algorithm and yields different cent totals whenever two or more services carry sub-dollar remainders. The rounding unit is 100 cents, so a fee of 12,340 cents contributes 12,300 cents to the pool and a fee of 12,350 cents contributes 12,400 cents. Monetary thresholds and ceilings — `Client.naf_annual_cap_cents`, `Client.naf_approved_fee_cents`, `NonAuditService.fee_cents`, `NonAuditService.approved_fee_cents` and `IndependenceThreat.value_cents` — are exact integer cents and are never rounded for storage; the materiality comparison of §3.4 is likewise an exact integer comparison whose boundary is inclusive.

Workload points are integers. The only rounding in the workload calculation occurs at the per-assignment base step, where the product of the role's base points and the engagement risk tier's multiplier is rounded half-up to a whole number; every later step is exact integer addition. Rotation tenure is measured in whole months with the floor applied, and count-based quantities — the open-finding counts, deficiency counts and breach counts — are exact integers that are never rounded.

### 2.10. The Validation Cascade and Its Precedence

Every request passes through a fixed sequence of checks, and the first check that fails determines the error the caller sees. The order matters whenever two rules could both be violated: a caller must never receive a downstream error when an earlier, more fundamental check would already have rejected the request.

1. **Request shape.** The body must match the endpoint's schema: required fields present, every enum-valued field carrying a member of its declared set, and numeric ranges respected, including `score_impact` between 0 and 40 inclusive, `fee_cents` not negative, and `value_cents` not negative. Failures are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400.
2. **Authentication headers.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must both be present. A missing header is `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` with HTTP 401.
3. **Role authorization.** `X-User-Role` must appear in the endpoint's authorized roles. Otherwise the response is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` with HTTP 403.
4. **Path resource existence.** The resource named by the path identifier must exist in its collection, and the error carries the identifier's entity-specific code as enumerated in §2.11. The status is 404.
5. **Body foreign-key resolution.** Every foreign key supplied in the body — `client_id`, `engagement_id`, `professional_id`, `affiliate_id`, `acceptance_id` or `reviewer_id` — must resolve to an existing row. A dangling reference is `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404.
6. **State admissibility.** The target resource must be in a state from which the requested action has a declared transition, and a resource that is already terminal admits none. Two creation endpoints also gate on the engagement's status: creating a role assignment and raising an independence finding each require the target engagement's status to be one of `DRAFT`, `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_STATE` with HTTP 409.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** For the two guarded writes of §2.8, the supplied `expected_engagement_version` must equal the engagement's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412.
8. **Prohibited services.** A non-audit service whose `is_prohibited_for_pie` is true may not leave `DRAFT`. The rejection is `ERR_PROHIBITED_SERVICE` with HTTP 422.
9. **Material financial interests.** A professional who holds an independence threat of type `FINANCIAL_INTEREST` with `is_material` true and status `DECLARED` or `IN_COOLING_OFF` for the engagement's client may not be assigned to that engagement. The rejection is `ERR_MATERIAL_INTEREST_CONFLICT` with HTTP 422.
10. **Cooling-off windows.** A professional inside an active cooling-off window for the engagement's client may not be assigned to that engagement. The rejection is `ERR_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE` with HTTP 422.
11. **Single-holder roles.** At most one `ACTIVE` role assignment may exist per engagement and role for the roles `LEAD_PARTNER`, `CONCURRING_PARTNER` and `EQ_REVIEWER`. A second concurrent holder is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROLE_HOLDER` and HTTP 409.
12. **Assignment interval overlap.** Assignment intervals on the same engagement and role must not overlap; the interval is half-open, running from `started_on` inclusive to `scheduled_end_on` exclusive, so an assignment that begins exactly at another's `scheduled_end_on` is permitted. Overlaps are rejected with `ERR_ROLE_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409.
13. **Review buffering.** Quality reviews for the same `reviewer_id` and `review_type` must be separated by a mandatory buffer: the candidate's `received_on` must be at least 5 days after the latest `due_on` among that reviewer's overlapping or adjacent reviews of the same type. Violations are `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CONFLICT` with HTTP 409.
14. **Rotation limits.** The consecutive service run for the professional, client and role must remain below the applicable time-on limit for the client's variant. Otherwise the assignment is rejected with `ERR_ROTATION_LIMIT_REACHED` and HTTP 422.
15. **Workload ceilings.** After the candidate assignment is applied, the professional's active workload points must not exceed `max_workload_points`. Otherwise the assignment is rejected with `ERR_WORKLOAD_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422.
16. **Remediation attempts.** A finding must carry fewer than 3 failed re-tests before it may re-enter remediation. Otherwise the transition is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` and HTTP 422.
17. **Acceptance decision conditions.** The requested decision must be admissible for the acceptance's combined risk score band and must not leave an unresolved `CRITICAL` integrity assessment standing. Violations are `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_CONDITIONS_UNMET` with HTTP 422.
18. **Non-audit fee cap.** The whole-dollar-rounded candidate fee added to the client's current approved fee pool must not exceed the client's annual cap. This is evaluated as a pre-check when a service is submitted for approval and authoritatively when it is approved; both rejections are `ERR_NAF_CAP_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 422.
19. **Assessment dating.** `IntegrityAssessment.assessed_on` must not be later than the logical clock. Otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE` with HTTP 422.
20. **Assignment dating.** `EngagementRoleAssignment.started_on` must not be earlier than the logical clock. Otherwise `ERR_BACKDATED_ASSIGNMENT` with HTTP 422.
21. **Frozen engagements.** An engagement whose `independence_status` is `FROZEN` may not be activated, staffed or resumed. The rejection is `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN` with HTTP 409, and it guards engagement transitions, role assignment creation and acceptance decisions alike.
22. **Open findings at closure.** An engagement may not be closed while any of its independence findings remains in `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION`, `RE_TEST` or `REOPENED`. The rejection is `ERR_FINDINGS_OPEN` with HTTP 409.
23. **Incomplete attestations.** A multinational engagement may not be activated while any affiliate attestation for it is still `PENDING`. The rejection is `ERR_ATTESTATION_INCOMPLETE` with HTTP 409.

### 2.11. Nested Resource Retrieval

When a request path names more than one resource, the server resolves the identifiers from left to right and rejects the request at the first identifier that does not correspond to an existing row; later identifiers are not examined once an earlier one has failed. Each resource family has its own not-found code so the caller can tell which segment of the path failed: a client path segment yields `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, an engagement segment yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, an acceptance segment yields `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, an integrity assessment segment yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a professional segment yields `ERR_PROFESSIONAL_NOT_FOUND`, a role assignment segment yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a finding segment yields `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, a threat segment yields `ERR_THREAT_NOT_FOUND`, a non-audit service segment yields `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_FOUND`, an affiliate segment yields `ERR_AFFILIATE_NOT_FOUND`, an attestation segment yields `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_FOUND`, and a quality review segment yields `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve carry HTTP status 404.

A missing identifier in the request body is a different failure from a missing path segment: foreign keys supplied in a payload — `client_id`, `engagement_id`, `professional_id`, `affiliate_id`, `acceptance_id` or `reviewer_id` — are resolved during the cascade of §2.10 and, when dangling, produce `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties Across the Acceptance and Staffing Pipeline

The role matrix encodes a separation that the API enforces structurally rather than by convention. Acceptance decisions belong exclusively to the `ClientAcceptanceCommittee`, which also records the integrity evidence those decisions rest on; staffing belongs exclusively to the `EngagementPartner`, who opens engagements and creates and ends role assignments; findings and threats belong exclusively to the `IndependenceOfficer`, who also advances affiliate attestations and may revoke an approved non-audit service; non-audit service requests and their pre-approval lifecycle belong to the `AuditCommitteeLiaison`, except for the revocation action that the independence office owns; and quality reviews and inspection responses belong exclusively to the `QualityReviewer`. Reference data and the logical clock are the `PlatformAdmin`'s exclusive domain.

Read access is deliberately broader than write access. Every role may read the client, engagement, acceptance, professional, assignment, finding, threat, service, attestation and review collections; the exclusive-operation lists restrict only mutation. A request that attempts a mutation outside the caller's role is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` per §2.1, and the authorized set for every endpoint is enumerated in §4.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Client Onboarding and Engagement Opening

A client relationship begins when a `PlatformAdmin` registers the entity that is being audited or pursued. Registration captures `legal_name`, the `industry_code` that will drive the industry-risk index of the client risk score, the `jurisdiction_country` as an ISO-3166 alpha-2 code that feeds the jurisdiction-risk index, and the `is_public_interest_entity` flag, which defaults to false but changes the client's treatment in three separate places: it adds a surcharge to the risk score, it makes every direct financial interest in the client material regardless of value, and it accelerates the rotation regime for the engagement's signing and concurring partners. Two further fields are captured at registration. `parent_client_id` optionally names the ultimate parent client when the registrant belongs to a controlled group, and is left null for a standalone client. `naf_annual_cap_cents` records the audit committee's pre-approved annual ceiling for non-audit service fees and defaults to 0, meaning that a client registered without a cap may not have any non-audit service approved until the cap is raised.

The `industry_code` and `industry_expertise_required` vocabularies are shared: a value must be one of `TECH`, `FINANCIAL_SERVICES`, `HEALTHCARE`, `MANUFACTURING`, `RETAIL`, `ENERGY` or `PUBLIC_SECTOR`. Registration leaves the new record in `PROSPECT` status, and its `naf_approved_fee_cents` is computed through the fee pool rule of §3.6 — zero at this point, since no service has been approved. A client's `status` axis runs `PROSPECT → ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, with `TERMINATED` as the only terminal state reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; the transitions out of `PROSPECT` and into `TERMINATED` are both driven by the system, the first when an acceptance decision approves the engagement and the second when the client's last non-closed engagement is closed. The client also carries two computed roll-ups, `aggregate_independence_status` and `open_finding_count`, whose derivation is given in §2.6 and §3.5 respectively.

An engagement is opened by an `EngagementPartner` against an existing client. The payload names the `client_id`, the `engagement_type` — one of `STATUTORY_AUDIT`, `INTEGRATED_AUDIT` or `REVIEW_ENGAGEMENT` — the `fiscal_year_end` as an ISO-8601 UTC instant, and the `is_multinational` flag, which defaults to false. Opening an engagement places it on two independent axes at once. The lifecycle axis starts at `DRAFT`, and the independence axis starts at `CLEAR`; the risk axis starts at `LOW` with a `combined_risk_score` of 0 until an acceptance decision supplies a real value. Three further fields on the engagement are computed rather than supplied: `assigned_workload_points` sums the base workload points of the engagement's `ACTIVE` assignments, `lead_partner_id` and `concurring_partner_id` name the professionals currently holding the `ACTIVE` `LEAD_PARTNER` and `CONCURRING_PARTNER` assignments (or null when the seat is vacant), and `quality_review_required` is true when the engagement's `risk_tier` is `HIGH` or `SIGNIFICANT`, or when its client is a public interest entity.

A newly opened engagement is inert until its acceptance evaluation succeeds. In `DRAFT` it cannot be staffed into an active state, cannot be closed, and does not contribute to its client's active posture; the acceptance pipeline described in §3.2 is the only route out of `DRAFT`.

### 3.2. Acceptance and Continuance Decision-Making

Client acceptance and continuance is governed by an evaluation record opened by a `ClientAcceptanceCommittee` member against a client and the engagement it governs. The evaluation captures `client_id`, `engagement_id`, `proposed_by` — the name of the engagement partner proposing acceptance — and `industry_expertise_required`, the industry code of the expertise the engagement team must hold, drawn from the same seven-member industry vocabulary as the client's own `industry_code`. A boolean `industry_expertise_confirmed` records whether the committee has seen that expertise evidenced on the team; it defaults to false and must be set true before the evaluation can be submitted. A new evaluation begins in `DRAFT` with `decision_notes` and `decided_at` both null, and with its two derived scores — `integrity_score` and `risk_score` — computed immediately under the provisional variant described below.

Management integrity evidence is recorded as separate assessment rows attached to the evaluation. Each row carries a `source_type` describing where the adverse information came from — `REGULATORY_ACTION`, `LITIGATION`, `MEDIA_ADVERSE`, `PRIOR_AUDIT_FINDING` or `CREDIT_EVENT` — a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, a `score_impact` between 0 and 40 inclusive expressing how many integrity points the matter deducts before time decay, the `assessed_on` date on which the evidence was evaluated, and a `resolved` flag, defaulting to false, recording whether management has remediated the underlying matter. The `assessed_on` date is a temporal anchor in its own right: an assessment dated later than the logical clock is rejected outright with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. Recording an assessment recomputes both of the evaluation's derived scores using whichever variant is currently active.

The integrity score starts from a perfect 100 and subtracts decayed penalties. For each assessment row attached to the evaluation, the whole number of months from `assessed_on` to the logical clock's current date determines a decay factor: an age of 12 months or less decays not at all, an age above 12 months and up to 24 months halves the impact, and an age beyond 24 months quarters it. Each row contributes its `score_impact` multiplied by its decay factor, those contributions are summed across every assessment row regardless of whether it has been resolved, and the sum is subtracted from 100. The result is floored at 0 and reported to 2 decimal places with round-half-up.

The client risk score is built in two layers. The base layer adds the client's industry-risk index value — 20 for `TECH`, 35 for `FINANCIAL_SERVICES`, 25 for `HEALTHCARE`, 20 for `MANUFACTURING`, 15 for `RETAIL`, 30 for `ENERGY` and 10 for `PUBLIC_SECTOR` — to the jurisdiction-risk index value of the client's country: 0 for `US`, 5 for `GB`, `DE` and `CA`, 10 for `AU`, 15 for `IN`, 20 for `BR` and 25 for `CN`. A country outside that table is treated as a default jurisdiction risk of 10 rather than being rejected. When the client is a public interest entity, a further surcharge of 15 is added to the base. The penalty layer mirrors the integrity calculation's decay: each contributing assessment adds its `score_impact` multiplied by the same three-tier decay factor, and the unclamped `risk_score` is the base plus that penalty. The stored `combined_risk_score` is the clamped form — the raw score raised to a floor of 0 and lowered to a ceiling of 100.

The two variants differ only in which assessments contribute. While the evaluation is still in `DRAFT`, the provisional variant counts only rows whose `resolved` flag is true, so unresolved matters are excluded from the input set entirely and the provisional score is lower than the final score for the same evidence. Submitting the evaluation switches to the final variant, in which every assessment row contributes its full decayed impact whether or not it has been resolved. Submission by a committee member requires three things to hold at once: at least one integrity assessment must exist for the evaluation, `industry_expertise_confirmed` must already be true, and no assessment may carry an `assessed_on` later than the logical clock. A successful submission recomputes `integrity_score`, recomputes `risk_score` under the final variant, re-clamps `combined_risk_score`, and moves the evaluation from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` while simultaneously advancing the governing engagement to `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`.

The committee then records its decision, supplying a `decision` value and, optionally, `decision_notes` carrying the rationale. Five decisions exist and each has its own admissibility conditions. An `APPROVE` decision on a submitted evaluation requires the clamped combined risk score to be below 70 and requires that no integrity assessment of `CRITICAL` severity remain unresolved; on success `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the governing engagement becomes `ACTIVE` with the evaluation's `combined_risk_score` and `risk_tier` copied onto it, and the client becomes `ACTIVE`. A `CONDITIONAL` decision requires the combined score to be at least 50 and below 70 and requires at least one unresolved integrity assessment; it stamps `decided_at` and forces the governing engagement's `quality_review_required` flag to true, leaving the engagement in `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING` until a later approval. A `RETURN` decision sends the evaluation back to `DRAFT`, overwrites `decision_notes` with the return rationale, and returns the governing engagement to `DRAFT` with its `risk_tier` reset to `LOW` and its `combined_risk_score` reset to 0. A `REJECT` decision moves the evaluation to its terminal `REJECTED` state, stamps `decided_at`, returns the governing engagement to `DRAFT` with its `risk_tier` reset to `LOW` and its `combined_risk_score` reset to 0, and additionally returns the client to `PROSPECT` when that client has no other engagement outside `DRAFT`. A `WITHDRAW` decision is available only on an evaluation that is already `CONDITIONAL`; it moves the evaluation to its terminal `WITHDRAWN` state and returns the governing engagement to `DRAFT`. An evaluation that is `CONDITIONAL` may also be approved later, but that second approval drops the risk-band requirement and instead requires `industry_expertise_confirmed` to be true and no unresolved `CRITICAL` assessment to remain. Any decision whose conditions are not met is rejected with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_CONDITIONS_UNMET`.

The engagement's own activation carries three further gates, all evaluated as part of the same cascade. The governing evaluation must have reached `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` with a combined risk score below 70. Every `ACTIVE` assignment on the engagement must reference a professional who has no live independence finding — that is, none in `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION`, `RE_TEST` or `REOPENED` — of `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` severity. And when the engagement is multinational, no affiliate attestation for it may still be `PENDING`, a condition whose violation surfaces as `ERR_ATTESTATION_INCOMPLETE`. An engagement whose independence posture is `FROZEN` can never be activated or resumed, and an attempt to do so is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN`; the same guard applies to staffing and to acceptance decisions on a frozen engagement.

### 3.3. Staffing, Rotation Tenure and Workload Ceilings

Engagements are staffed from a roster of registered professionals. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each professional with a `full_name`, a `grade` of `PARTNER`, `DIRECTOR`, `SENIOR_MANAGER` or `MANAGER`, the `home_affiliate_id` of the network member firm that employs them, and a `rotation_role_class` of `LEAD_PARTNER`, `CONCURRING_PARTNER`, `EQ_REVIEWER` or `NONE` describing the rotation regime the professional is subject to. The optional `max_workload_points` field sets a personal ceiling on concurrent workload and defaults to 100. Registration computes the professional's `active_workload_points` and `availability` immediately; both are recomputed whenever the professional's assignments change or an engagement's `risk_tier` moves. A professional also carries `rotation_exposure_months`, the longest consecutive-service run in months across all of that professional's assignments, which gives the independence office a single-number view of who is closest to a rotation limit.

An `EngagementPartner` staffs a seat by creating a role assignment with the `engagement_id`, the `professional_id`, the `role` — one of `LEAD_PARTNER`, `CONCURRING_PARTNER`, `EQ_REVIEWER` or `ENGAGEMENT_MANAGER` — and the assignment interval, whose start is `started_on` (inclusive) and whose planned end is `scheduled_end_on` (exclusive). The body must also carry `expected_engagement_version`, the caller's last observed version of the engagement, because staffing is one of the two optimistic-concurrency-guarded writes described in §2.8; a value that no longer matches the engagement's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 before any other staffing rule is examined. The new assignment starts in `ACTIVE` status with `ended_on` null, and the engagement must be in `DRAFT`, `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — an engagement already `CLOSED`, or one whose posture is `FROZEN`, cannot be staffed, the latter surfacing as `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN`. A `started_on` earlier than the logical clock is rejected as a backdated assignment with `ERR_BACKDATED_ASSIGNMENT`.

Four staffing rules constrain which professional may take which seat. At most one `ACTIVE` assignment may exist for a given engagement and role when the role is `LEAD_PARTNER`, `CONCURRING_PARTNER` or `EQ_REVIEWER`; a second live holder of the same seat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROLE_HOLDER`. Intervals for the same engagement and role may not overlap — the interval is half-open, so an assignment starting exactly at the instant another's `scheduled_end_on` falls is permitted while any true overlap is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP`. A professional who holds a material financial interest in the engagement's client may not be assigned to it, and the rejection is `ERR_MATERIAL_INTEREST_CONFLICT`; a professional who is inside an active cooling-off window for that client is likewise barred, with the rejection `ERR_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE`. Both of these personal-independence bars are derived in §3.4. Finally, the rotation and workload limits described below are evaluated as part of the same staffing request, and either may reject it.

Rotation tenure measures how long a professional has continuously served one client in one role. The measurement operates over the ordered run of assignments sharing the same professional, the same client — reached through the engagement — and the same role, sorted by `started_on` ascending and, when two rows share a start instant, by `created_at` ascending. Each row's effective end is its `ended_on` when that has been stamped and otherwise the earlier of its `scheduled_end_on` and the logical clock. The run accumulates month counts: the first row contributes the whole months, floored, from its `started_on` to its effective end; each subsequent row contributes the same measure, but only after the gap test. The gap between two rows is the whole floored months from the previous row's effective end to the next row's `started_on`; when that gap is 12 months or less the run continues and the next row's months are added to the accumulated total, and when the gap exceeds 12 months the run restarts from the next row's own month count. The accumulation stops at the assignment being evaluated, so a row's `consecutive_service_months` reflects only the history up to and including itself. Both `ACTIVE` and `ENDED` rows participate in the run; an assignment that has ended still counts toward the continuity of the professional's service.

The applicable time-on limit and time-off requirement depend on the client's variant and the assignment's role. For a client that is not a public interest entity, the standard regime applies: lead and concurring partners each have a time-on limit of 84 months and owe 24 months of time off once the assignment ends; engagement quality reviewers have the same 84-month limit but owe 36 months; engagement managers have no time-on limit at all and owe 12 months. For a public interest entity, the accelerated regime applies to the two partner roles: both the lead partner and the concurring partner see their time-on limit cut to 60 months and their required time off extended to 60 months, while the reviewer and manager figures are unchanged at 84 months with 36 months off and no limit with 12 months off respectively. A time-on limit of zero means unlimited service, and an assignment in such a role can never be `ROTATION_DUE`.

The assignment's `rotation_status` follows from those numbers. While the assignment is `ACTIVE` and its role has no time-on limit, the status is `ON_SERVICE`. While it is `ACTIVE` and its `consecutive_service_months` has reached or exceeded the applicable limit, the status is `ROTATION_DUE` — the boundary is inclusive, so a partner at exactly 60 months under the accelerated regime is already due for rotation, and a staffing request that would leave the run at or beyond the limit is rejected with `ERR_ROTATION_LIMIT_REACHED`. An `ACTIVE` assignment below its limit is `ON_SERVICE`. Once the assignment has ended, its `time_off_months_required` is fixed from the same tables and its `eligible_return_on` is set to `ended_on` plus that many months; while the logical clock has not yet reached `eligible_return_on` the status is `IN_TIME_OFF`, and once the clock reaches or passes it the status becomes `ELIGIBLE`. An assignment that is still `ACTIVE` has a null `eligible_return_on`.

Workload points are computed in a fixed four-step order that resolves an apparent circularity between the assignment and the professional. In the first step, each `ACTIVE` assignment's base is the role's base points multiplied by the multiplier of the risk tier of the engagement the assignment sits on, rounded half-up to a whole number; the base points are 40 for a lead partner, 25 for a concurring partner, 20 for an engagement quality reviewer and 15 for an engagement manager, and the tier multipliers are 1.0 for `LOW`, 1.25 for `MODERATE`, 1.5 for `HIGH` and 2.0 for `SIGNIFICANT`. This step-1 value is what the assignment stores in its own `workload_points`, and it is the only place rounding occurs. In the second step, the professional's base sum is the total of those step-1 values across all of that professional's `ACTIVE` assignments; `ENDED` assignments contribute nothing. In the third step, an overload surcharge is selected from that base sum: the highest tier whose threshold the base sum strictly exceeds supplies its points, so a base sum above 60 adds 5 points and a base sum above 80 adds 10 points, and a base sum at or below 60 adds nothing. The surcharge is never written back onto any assignment, which is exactly why the order is pinned: step 1 cannot depend on step 3. In the fourth step, the professional's `active_workload_points` is the base sum plus the surcharge, and the engagement's `assigned_workload_points` is the sum of the step-1 bases of that engagement's own `ACTIVE` assignments.

The professional's availability posture is the last derived value in the chain. A professional whose `active_workload_points` exceeds their `max_workload_points` is `OVERLOADED`. A professional with no `ACTIVE` assignment at all, but with at least one `ENDED` assignment whose `eligible_return_on` is still later than the logical clock, is `IN_TIME_OFF`. Every other professional is `AVAILABLE`. A staffing request that would push the professional's active workload points above their personal ceiling is rejected with `ERR_WORKLOAD_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` before the assignment is created.

Ending an assignment is an `EngagementPartner` action that stamps `ended_on` from the logical clock, recomputes the assignment's rotation tenure so that `time_off_months_required` and `eligible_return_on` are populated, and refreshes the affected professional's `active_workload_points` and availability as well as the engagement's `assigned_workload_points`, `lead_partner_id` and `concurring_partner_id`. The engagement's `version` advances by 1, which is why a client holding an older engagement snapshot must refresh before staffing again. Because a `LEAD_PARTNER` or `CONCURRING_PARTNER` assignment ended by this action leaves `lead_partner_id` or `concurring_partner_id` null until a replacement is staffed, the seat can be refilled only by a professional who is not barred by the rotation clock.

### 3.4. Personal Independence: Financial Interests and Employment Relationships

Covered persons declare personal threats to independence through the `IndependenceOfficer`, who records a threat against a `professional_id` and a `client_id`. The declaration names the `threat_type`, which is either `FINANCIAL_INTEREST` or `EMPLOYMENT_RELATIONSHIP`, and then the type-specific detail. A financial interest carries an `instrument_type` of `EQUITY`, `DEBT`, `MUTUAL_FUND`, `DERIVATIVE` or `TRUST`, and a `value_cents` fair value that defaults to 0; its `relationship_type` is null. An employment relationship carries a `relationship_type` of `IMMEDIATE_FAMILY_EMPLOYED_BY_CLIENT`, `FORMER_FIRM_PROFESSIONAL_JOINED_CLIENT`, `PROFESSIONAL_PRIOR_CLIENT_EMPLOYEE` or `CLIENT_BOARD_MEMBERSHIP`, and its `instrument_type` is null and its `value_cents` stays 0. Both kinds carry `effective_on`, the date the interest was acquired or the relationship began, and both begin their remediation lifecycle in `DECLARED` status.

Materiality is decided by the financial interest rule and applies only to financial interests; an employment relationship is never material, and its `is_material` flag is always false. For a financial interest, the first question is whether the holding is direct or indirect. Equity, debt, derivatives and trust interests are direct; a mutual fund holding is indirect. When the holding is direct and the client is a public interest entity, the interest is material regardless of its size — the value comparison is skipped entirely. In every other case the comparison is exact and inclusive: the interest is material when `value_cents` is greater than or equal to the professional's grade threshold in cents, multiplied by two when the holding is indirect. The grade thresholds are 500,000 cents for a `PARTNER`, 100,000 cents for a `DIRECTOR`, 50,000 cents for a `SENIOR_MANAGER` and 25,000 cents for a `MANAGER`. The boundary is inclusive at the cent, so a manager's 25,000-cent direct holding in a non-public-interest client is material while a 24,999-cent holding is not.

Employment relationships are governed by a cooling-off window instead of a materiality test. The window ends at `effective_on` plus a fixed number of months for the relationship type, preserving the day of the month: a former firm professional who joined the client triggers 12 months, a professional who was previously employed by the client triggers 24 months, and an immediate family member employed by the client or a client board membership triggers no window at all. The threat's initial status follows directly from that number and from materiality. A relationship with a positive cooling-off window opens in `IN_COOLING_OFF`. A relationship with a zero-month window opens in `CLEARED` when it is immaterial and in `MITIGATED` when it is material. A financial interest, which never has a cooling-off window, always opens in `DECLARED`. `cooling_off_end_on` equals `effective_on` for the zero-month relationship types, and for a financial interest it likewise equals `effective_on`.

The cooling-off window is a staffing bar, not merely a label. While a threat for a given professional and client pair sits in `IN_COOLING_OFF`, that professional may not hold an `ACTIVE` assignment on any engagement of that client when the assignment's `started_on` falls inside the window, and the window is closed at both ends — an assignment starting exactly on `effective_on` or exactly on `cooling_off_end_on` is inside it. A staffing attempt that violates the bar is rejected with `ERR_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE`. The bar lifts on its own: once the logical clock reaches `cooling_off_end_on`, the threat recomputes from `IN_COOLING_OFF` to `CLEARED` on the next read or mutation, and `cleared_on` is stamped from the clock at that moment.

Material financial interests propagate outward the instant they are declared. When a newly created threat is material, the system raises one `CRITICAL` independence finding against every engagement of that client whose status is `DRAFT`, `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and recomputes the independence posture of each of those engagements. Because a single live `CRITICAL` finding is sufficient to freeze an engagement under the ladder of §2.6, a partner's material holding can suspend an entire client relationship in one step. The same guard bars the professional from being staffed: a professional holding a threat of type `FINANCIAL_INTEREST` that is material and still in `DECLARED` or `IN_COOLING_OFF` status for the engagement's client cannot be assigned to that engagement, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_MATERIAL_INTEREST_CONFLICT`.

Three remediation actions are available to the independence office. A material financial interest in `DECLARED` status may be divested through the `DIVEST` action; that action is available only when the threat is of type `FINANCIAL_INTEREST` and `is_material` is true, and it moves the threat to `DIVESTED` and recomputes the independence posture of every engagement of the client. An employment relationship in `DECLARED` status whose cooling-off window is empty — that is, whose `cooling_off_end_on` equals its `effective_on` — may be mitigated through the `MITIGATE` action, moving it to `MITIGATED`. And either a `DIVESTED` or a `MITIGATED` threat may be confirmed for clearance through the `CONFIRM_CLEARANCE` action, which stamps `cleared_on` from the logical clock and, in the divestment case, again recomputes the posture of every engagement of the client. `CLEARED` is the terminal state of the threat lifecycle; once a threat is cleared it no longer contributes to any posture computation, and the professional becomes eligible for that client's engagements again subject to the rotation clocks of §3.3.

### 3.5. Findings, Remediation and Regulatory Escalation

Every independence breach the monitoring channels detect is recorded as an independence finding raised by the `IndependenceOfficer` against a `professional_id` and an `engagement_id`. The raising payload names the `origin` channel that surfaced the matter — `DECLARATION`, `FINANCIAL_INTEREST`, `EMPLOYMENT_RELATIONSHIP`, `PROHIBITED_SERVICE`, `AFFILIATE_BREACH`, `WORKLOAD_LIMIT` or `INSPECTION` — and an `origin_ref_id` holding the identifier of the source record inside its own collection; that reference is informational and carries no foreign-key constraint, so a finding survives even when the record that triggered it is later removed from view. The payload also names the `finding_type`, one of `FINANCIAL_INTEREST`, `EMPLOYMENT_RELATIONSHIP`, `PROHIBITED_SERVICE`, `AFFILIATE_OBLIGATION` or `WORKLOAD_LIMIT`, the `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, and the `discovered_on` date on which the matter came to light. Raising a finding is the second of the two optimistic-concurrency-guarded writes: the body must carry `expected_engagement_version` matching the engagement's current `version`, and a stale snapshot is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 rather than silently attaching the finding to an engagement the caller has not observed. The engagement must additionally be in `DRAFT`, `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`.

A new finding opens in `OPEN` status with `closed_on` null and `remediation_attempts` at 0. Its `remediation_due_on` is computed as the `discovered_on` date plus the severity's configured remediation window expressed in days, so a more severe finding carries a shorter tolerance for remediation than a low-severity one. Raising a finding immediately recomputes the engagement's independence posture and the client's `open_finding_count`, the counter of that client's findings that are not yet `CLOSED`, and advances the engagement's `version`. A `CRITICAL` finding therefore freezes the engagement the moment it is created, and a second `HIGH` finding on an engagement that already carries one live `HIGH` finding does the same.

The remediation lifecycle is a loop with a terminal exit. An `OPEN` finding may enter remediation through the `START_REMEDIATION` action, which sets it to `REMEDIATION` and (re)computes `remediation_due_on`; the transition is barred once the finding has already accumulated 3 failed re-tests, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. Alternatively, a finding whose `severity` is exactly `LOW` may be closed with the `CLOSE_NO_ACTION` action, which stamps `closed_on` from the logical clock and recomputes both the engagement's posture and the client's open-finding count. From `REMEDIATION`, the officer submits the finding for re-test with the `SUBMIT_FOR_RETEST` action, moving it to `RE_TEST` and recomputing the engagement's posture; from `RE_TEST`, a passing re-test (`PASS_RETEST`) closes the finding — stamping `closed_on`, recomputing the engagement's posture and the client's open-finding count — while a failing re-test (`FAIL_RETEST`) moves it to `REOPENED` and increments `remediation_attempts` by 1. A failure also recomputes `remediation_due_on` from the logical clock rather than from the original discovery date, giving the professional a fresh window from the moment of failure. A `REOPENED` finding may restart remediation through the `RESTART_REMEDIATION` action, returning it to `REMEDIATION` and again recomputing the due date from the clock, provided the attempt budget has not been exhausted.

The attempt budget is also an escalation trigger. When a failing re-test brings `remediation_attempts` to 3, the engagement's independence posture is forced to `FROZEN` and the engagement's status is set to `SUSPENDED` if it was `ACTIVE`, regardless of how the other posture inputs stand; a professional who has failed remediation three times on the same matter is treated as an unresolved critical exposure until the independence office clears it. Findings in `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION`, `RE_TEST` and `REOPENED` are the "live" statuses used throughout this document: they keep the engagement's posture elevated, they bar the engagement from being closed, and they bar any professional named on them from being staffed onto a new engagement of the same client. Attempting to close an engagement that still carries any live finding is rejected with `ERR_FINDINGS_OPEN`.

### 3.6. Non-Audit Services and Audit-Committee Pre-Approval

Non-audit work for an audit client is requested by the `AuditCommitteeLiaison`, who records a service against a `client_id` and, when the work relates to a specific engagement, an `engagement_id` that may be left null for client-level services. The request names the `service_category` — one of `BOOKKEEPING`, `INTERNAL_AUDIT_OUTSOURCING`, `VALUATION`, `TAX_PREPARATION`, `TAX_ADVISORY`, `IT_SYSTEMS_DESIGN`, `ACTUARIAL`, `MANAGEMENT_FUNCTION`, `LEGAL` or `OTHER` — the proposed `fee_cents` before rounding, an optional `committee_resolution_ref` recording the audit committee's resolution, and an optional `approval_expires_on` date after which the pre-approval lapses. A service is created in `DRAFT` status, and its `is_prohibited_for_pie` flag is computed at creation from the pairing of the service category with the client's `is_public_interest_entity` flag: the categories on the prohibited list are prohibited only when the client is a public interest entity, and a service that is not prohibited for a public interest entity is never flagged.

The pre-approval lifecycle runs from `DRAFT` through `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED`, with a rejection branch and a revocation exit. Submission for approval — the `SUBMIT_FOR_APPROVAL` action — requires two things: the service must not be flagged as prohibited for a public interest entity, and its `fee_cents` must be greater than zero. A prohibited service attempting to leave `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_PROHIBITED_SERVICE` and HTTP 422, and this is the rule that keeps a prohibited category from ever reaching the committee. Submission also runs the fee-cap test described below as a pre-check, so a service that plainly cannot be admitted under the client's annual ceiling is stopped before the committee spends time on it.

Approval is the authoritative admission decision. The `APPROVE` action requires `committee_resolution_ref` to be present, and it re-evaluates the cap: the candidate's whole-dollar-rounded fee added to the client's current `naf_approved_fee_cents` must not exceed the client's `naf_annual_cap_cents`. A breach of the ceiling at either the pre-check or the authoritative check is rejected with `ERR_NAF_CAP_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. On success the service's `approved_fee_cents` is set to its fee rounded to the nearest whole dollar, `approved_on` is stamped from the logical clock, the client's `naf_approved_fee_cents` is recomputed through the fee pool rule, the client's `aggregate_independence_status` is recomputed, every engagement belonging to that client has its independence posture recomputed, and the client's `version` advances by 1.

The fee pool is the sum of individually rounded amounts, not a rounded sum. Each service's rounded fee is its `fee_cents` divided by 100, rounded half-up to a whole number of dollars, then multiplied back by 100 to return to cents; the pool is the total of those already-rounded figures across all of the client's `APPROVED` services, and only `APPROVED` rows count — services in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` are excluded entirely. The rounding order is pinned deliberately: rounding each service first and then summing produces different cent totals than summing raw fees and rounding once, whenever two or more services carry sub-dollar remainders, and this specification requires the former.

Rejection is available from either `DRAFT` or `PENDING_APPROVAL` through the `REJECT` action and moves the service to `REJECTED`, which is not terminal — a rejected service may be returned to `DRAFT` for revision and resubmission through the `RETURN_TO_DRAFT` action. A rejection from `DRAFT` recomputes the client's aggregate posture and the posture of every engagement of that client; a rejection from `PENDING_APPROVAL` recomputes the posture of the linked engagement when the service names one. Revocation is the independence office's prerogative rather than the liaison's: an `APPROVED` service may be revoked through the `REVOKE` action, which resets `approved_fee_cents` and `approved_on` to null, recomputes the client's fee pool so the revoked fee leaves the ceiling calculation, recomputes the client's aggregate posture and every engagement's posture, and advances the client's `version`.

Non-audit services also participate directly in the independence posture ladder. A service that is flagged as prohibited for a public interest entity and sits in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` status counts against the posture of the engagement it names or, for a client-level service, against every engagement of the client, because the posture recompute of §2.6 reads prohibited services by engagement or by client. The practical consequence is that a prohibited service which somehow reaches `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` — for example because the client's public-interest flag was set after the service was created — freezes the affected engagements until the service is rejected or revoked.

### 3.7. Global Network Affiliates and Multinational Attestations

The firm's global network is registered as a collection of affiliates. A `PlatformAdmin` records each member firm with its `firm_name`, its `country_code` as an ISO-3166 alpha-2 value, its `network_tier` — `MEMBER_FIRM`, `CORRESPONDENT` or `ALLIANCE` — and the `independence_framework` it attests under, one of `SEC_RESTRICTED`, `EU_LOCAL` or `IFAC_BASELINE`. A new affiliate starts in `ACTIVE` status. Each affiliate carries `open_breach_count`, the number of its attestations currently in `BREACHED` state, which is recomputed whenever one of its attestations changes status.

A multinational engagement requires an attestation from every network firm in scope, and those attestations are opened by the `PlatformAdmin` against an `affiliate_id` and an `engagement_id`, with an optional `breach_count` that defaults to 0 and accumulates over the attestation's life. A new attestation begins in `PENDING` status with `attested_on` null. The attestation lifecycle has four moves. Attesting — the `ATTEST` action — moves a `PENDING` attestation to `ATTESTED`, stamps `attested_on` from the logical clock, and recomputes the engagement's independence posture. Waiving — the `WAIVE` action — moves a `PENDING` attestation to `WAIVED` and is available only in two situations: the attesting firm is an `ALLIANCE`-tier member, or the engagement is not multinational at all — an attestation is never waivable for a correspondent or member firm on a genuinely multinational engagement. Waiving also stamps `attested_on` and recomputes the engagement's posture.

Reporting a breach (`REPORT_BREACH`) is the event that makes the network mechanism consequential. It is available on an `ATTESTED` attestation, moves it to `BREACHED`, and increments that attestation's `breach_count` by 1. It then recomputes the affiliate's `open_breach_count` and suspends the affiliate — setting its status to `SUSPENDED` — once that count reaches 3 or more. It also cascade-creates an independence finding against the engagement, with `origin` `AFFILIATE_BREACH`, `finding_type` `AFFILIATE_OBLIGATION`, severity `HIGH`, and `origin_ref_id` equal to the attestation's identifier, and recomputes the engagement's independence posture. Because a single `HIGH` finding moves an engagement to `RESTRICTED` and a second moves it to `FROZEN`, the network channel feeds the same ladder as every other independence input. The network risk points described in §2.7 are computed from the same attestation rows, weighting each firm's cumulative `breach_count` by its tier, so a member firm's breach is worth three points, a correspondent's two and an alliance firm's one, up to a ceiling of 50.

A breached attestation can be reset through the `RESET_ATTESTATION` action. The reset returns it to `PENDING` and clears `attested_on`, but only when no finding raised from that attestation — identified by `origin_ref_id` — remains in `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION`, `RE_TEST` or `REOPENED` status; a breach whose remediation is still open cannot be quietly wiped from the record. The reset also recomputes the affiliate's `open_breach_count`, which may lift the affiliate out of `SUSPENDED`. Independent of the attestation mechanics, a multinational engagement cannot be activated while any attestation for it is still `PENDING`; that gate surfaces as `ERR_ATTESTATION_INCOMPLETE` during the acceptance cascade of §3.2.

### 3.8. Quality Control Reviews and Inspection Response

The `QualityReviewer` owns a collection of reviews that covers both the firm's own engagement quality control process and external regulatory inspections. A review is created against an `engagement_id` and a `reviewer_id` and names its `review_type` — `ENGAGEMENT_QUALITY_REVIEW`, `INSPECTION_READINESS` or `REGULATORY_INSPECTION`. For a regulatory inspection the payload also names the `inspection_body`, one of `PCAOB`, `SEC`, `LOCAL_REGULATOR` or `INTERNAL_QC`; for the other review types `inspection_body` is null. Every review carries the `received_on` date on which it was received and a planned completion date in `due_on`, an optional `deficiency_count` that defaults to 0, and an optional `remediation_plan_ref` naming the remediation plan filed with the regulator, which stays null until it is filed. A new review starts in `SCHEDULED` status.

Inspection reviews carry a computed response deadline. A review whose `review_type` is not `REGULATORY_INSPECTION` has a null `response_due_on`. A regulatory inspection takes its base window from the inspection body: 30 days for `PCAOB`, 21 days for `SEC`, 45 days for `LOCAL_REGULATOR` and 15 days for `INTERNAL_QC`. That window is then halved when the review carries five or more deficiencies — the boundary is inclusive, so exactly 5 deficiencies halves the window, and the halved figure is rounded half-up to a whole day — and the resulting effective day count is added to `received_on` to produce `response_due_on`. A PCAOB inspection with 5 deficiencies therefore yields a 15-day window rather than 30, while the same inspection with 4 deficiencies keeps the full 30 days.

Review scheduling is buffered per reviewer. Two reviews sharing the same `reviewer_id` and the same `review_type` must be separated by a mandatory 5-day buffer: the candidate's `received_on` must fall at least 5 days after the latest `due_on` among that reviewer's overlapping or adjacent reviews of that type. A request that lands inside the buffer is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The review lifecycle begins with the `START` action, which moves a `SCHEDULED` review to `IN_PROGRESS`. From `IN_PROGRESS`, the `PASS` action requires `deficiency_count` to be exactly 0 and moves the review to its terminal `PASSED` state, clearing the governing engagement's `quality_review_required` flag. The `FAIL` action requires `deficiency_count` to be greater than 0 and moves the review to `DEFICIENT`; when the deficiency count is 3 or more and the governing engagement is `ACTIVE`, the fail also suspends the engagement. From `DEFICIENT`, the `REMEDIATE` action requires `remediation_plan_ref` to be present and moves the review to `REMEDIATED`, restoring the engagement to `ACTIVE` if this review was what suspended it and its independence posture is not `FROZEN` — a frozen engagement is never revived by a quality review. `REMEDIATED` is a terminal state, but it is not a dead end: a remediated review may be reopened with the `REOPEN` action back to `IN_PROGRESS` when the reviewer determines the remediation did not hold.

### 3.9. Engagement Closure and Client Termination

An engagement's manual lifecycle is driven by an `EngagementPartner` through an `action` value, and three actions are available. The `SUSPEND` action moves an `ACTIVE` engagement to `SUSPENDED`. The `RESUME` action moves a `SUSPENDED` engagement back to `ACTIVE`, but only when its independence posture is not `FROZEN`; a frozen engagement is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN`. The `CLOSE` action retires the engagement permanently, and `CLOSED` is the only terminal state on the engagement's status axis.

Closure from `ACTIVE` carries two gates. No live independence finding — none in `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION`, `RE_TEST` or `REOPENED` — may remain against the engagement, and no quality review may remain in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `DEFICIENT` status. A closure attempt that still carries a live finding is rejected with `ERR_FINDINGS_OPEN`. Closure from `SUSPENDED` carries the finding gate but not the review gate, because an engagement that has already been suspended by the independence machinery is not held open by an outstanding quality review. In both cases, every `ACTIVE` assignment on the engagement is ended in the same operation, each with its `ended_on` stamped from the logical clock, which starts the rotation clock for every seated professional: each ended assignment's `eligible_return_on` is fixed from its `time_off_months_required`, and each affected professional's `active_workload_points` and availability are recomputed. Because closure ends the assignments rather than deleting them, the consecutive-service history that §3.3 measures survives the engagement itself.

Closing an engagement also drives the client. When the closed engagement was the client's last engagement that is not itself `CLOSED`, the client moves to its terminal `TERMINATED` state from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and the client's `aggregate_independence_status` is recomputed one final time over the engagements that remain — none of them active. In parallel, the client's status tracks its posture independently of closure: a client whose `aggregate_independence_status` becomes `FROZEN` is suspended automatically, and a suspended client is restored to `ACTIVE` automatically once no engagement of that client is `FROZEN`. Both of those automatic transitions advance the client's `version` by 1 and refresh its `updated_at`, just as the closure cascade does.

With the engagement closed, the audit relationship's independence obligations do not disappear. The findings, threats, attestations and non-audit services that were raised against it remain readable through their own collections, the affiliate's `open_breach_count` continues to reflect any attestation still in `BREACHED` state, and the professionals who served the engagement remain inside their rotation clocks until the logical clock carries them past their `eligible_return_on`. A client that was terminated and later re-engaged begins a fresh acceptance cycle under §3.2, with its earlier findings still on the record but its status axis starting again from `PROSPECT` only if the new engagement's evaluation is approved.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Two status-code rules govern the operations below. A request that creates a new, addressable resource returns `201 Created`; a request that reads or modifies an existing resource returns `200 OK`. Every create operation returns the created resource object in the response `data` field, and every modification returns the updated resource object, so a caller never needs a follow-up read to observe the effect of a write. The engagement independence summary operation is a read that returns a computed roll-up rather than an entity: its `data` object carries `engagement_id`, `independence_status`, `critical_open_count`, `high_open_count`, `prohibited_service_count`, `breached_attestation_count`, `material_interest_count` and `network_risk_points`, and it recomputes both the engagement's independence posture and its network risk points before answering.
