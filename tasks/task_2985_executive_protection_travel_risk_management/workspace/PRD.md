# Executive Protection & Travel Risk Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Corporate security departments that protect senior executives live with a structural problem: the intelligence picture, the travel plan, the staffing roster, the insurance position and the tax-disclosure position are all maintained by different people, on different cadences, and they all describe the same journey. An analyst revises a country advisory on Monday; by Tuesday a trip that was approved and briefed last week is protecting a principal against a threat band that no longer exists, staffed by a detail whose advance work is now insufficient, under an insurance policy whose coverage limit is now below the required minimum. Nobody is negligent — the department simply has no system in which one edit propagates to every artefact that depended on it.

This document specifies the Executive Protection & Travel Risk Management API, a single service that owns that propagation. The service maintains the protectee registry with its threat monitoring, public-exposure analysis and residential security surveys; the destination risk ratings that translate world conditions into a numeric band; the risk-assessment review cycle that turns analyst judgement into an approved threat level; protective-detail planning with advance work, route and hotel surveys and local liaison; traveller tracking across itinerary legs with explicit location-consent boundaries; the approval ladder and insurance-verification gates that must clear before anyone flies; incident response covering medical evacuation, political unrest and natural disaster with automatic vendor activation; communication protocols with check-in cadences and duress procedures; and finally the cost allocation that determines how much of a trip's protection cost is a business expense and how much is a disclosable executive perquisite.

Two design commitments run through every section that follows. First, **derived data is never entered by hand**: risk bands, advance-work requirement sets, approval levels, check-in intervals, costs and perquisite splits are all computed by the service from stated formulas, and every one of them is re-derived when an input beneath it changes. Second, **duty of care outranks paperwork and paperwork outranks money**: when several rules would reject the same request, the service reports the safety defect first, the structural defect second and the budget defect last, so that a spending ceiling can never mask an uncertified agent or a missing hospital survey. The whole business surface is mounted under `/exec-protection`, with a single test-driving clock endpoint deliberately placed outside that namespace.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — without exception, including the clock endpoint — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the role they are acting as. If either header is absent, present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Header validation happens before anything else: an unauthenticated caller never learns whether the resource they addressed exists. Once the headers are accepted, the named role is checked against the set of roles authorised for the target endpoint in §4; a role that is not on that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `SecurityAnalyst`, `ProtectionManager`, `SecurityDirector`, `Executive`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `SystemOperator`, spelled exactly as shown.

A `SecurityAnalyst` is the threat-monitoring and assessment analyst. The role owns the intelligence inputs — public-exposure signals, residential security surveys and destination ratings — and drafts risk assessments, but may never approve one. A `ProtectionManager` is the detail planner and trip owner: it builds trips, legs, protective-detail assignments and vendor engagements, submits trips for approval and settles them at close, but may never decide its own trip. A `SecurityDirector` is the approving authority for assessments and for trips at the first approval stage, and is the only role permitted to waive advance work, suspend a vendor or close an incident. An `Executive` is the protectee: it controls its own tracking-consent boundary, acknowledges pre-trip briefings and files check-ins including duress signals. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns insurance verification and the second approval hop for the highest approval level, and never plans or costs a detail. A `SystemOperator` performs operational bootstrap and holds the logical clock, so that the first staff rows can be registered before any staff-backed role exists.

Four of these roles are backed by persisted entities, and two are not:

- When `X-User-Role` is `SecurityAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SecurityStaff` row; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references a `SecurityStaff` compare the resource's staff foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProtectionManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SecurityStaff` row, compared the same way.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SecurityDirector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SecurityStaff` row, compared the same way.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Executive`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Executive` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Executive` compare the resource's executive foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and a compliance officer is therefore never narrowed to a subset of protectees.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, never compared to any entity's `id`.

A staff row declares which of the three staff-backed roles it may authenticate as through its `staff_role` field: `ANALYST` corresponds to `SecurityAnalyst`, `MANAGER` to `ProtectionManager`, and `DIRECTOR` to `SecurityDirector`.

Ownership narrowing applies specifically to `Executive` callers. Five operations are restricted to the protectee's own record: reading one protectee, changing the tracking-consent boundary, reading the perquisite-disclosure position, acknowledging a pre-trip briefing, and filing a check-in. For the first three the comparison is between `X-User-ID` and the `Executive` identifier in the path; for the last two the comparison is between `X-User-ID` and the `executive_id` recorded on the trip being addressed. An `Executive` caller who addresses a protectee or a trip that is not their own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The same restriction governs an `Executive` reading a trip, a trip's legs, a trip's check-ins, or a protectee's insurance policies. `ComplianceOfficer` and `SecurityDirector` callers may read any protectee's perquisite-disclosure position.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer, or an integer below 1 or above 100, and an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). This applies to every list endpoint in the service, including the flat collection lists of risk assessments, trips, protective-detail assignments and vendor engagements, and every sub-resource list nested under an executive or a trip.

Unless a list endpoint's description in §4 states a different ordering, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Where §4 states a domain ordering — exposure signals by newest `observed_at` first, legs in ascending `sequence_no` order, check-ins by newest `reported_at` first, incidents by newest `opened_at` first, and the risk-assessment, trip, assignment and engagement collections by newest `created_at` first — that stated ordering governs.

The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective skip count), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond this page). Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`. It is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind — the service never emits identifiers like `trip_…` or `exec_…`. Clients never supply an `id`; a value supplied in a creation payload is ignored.

Most resources also carry the full audit triple. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was first persisted, always rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. `updated_at` uses the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the caller did not directly request — a row touched by a cascade is a mutated row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, again including cascade-driven mutations. Whenever a rule in this document says that some entity's `version` is incremented, it means exactly one increment per mutated row per operation.

Two of the service's thirteen entity types are append-only event records rather than mutable resources, and they carry only `id` and `created_at`. A public-exposure signal — the `PublicExposureSignal` type — has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all; the field is absent from its representation, not present-and-null, because a signal is observed once and never revised. A check-in — the `CheckIn` type — is likewise identity-plus-`created_at` only: no `updated_at`, no `version`. The other eleven types all carry `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`: the protectee (`Executive`), the staff member (`SecurityStaff`), the risk assessment (`RiskAssessment`), the destination rating (`DestinationRating`), the insurance policy (`InsurancePolicy`), the trip (`Trip`), the itinerary leg (`TripLeg`), the protective-detail assignment (`ProtectiveDetailAssignment`), the vendor (`Vendor`), the vendor engagement (`VendorEngagement`) and the incident (`Incident`).

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a logical UTC clock, and every window, expiry, decay, due-date, lateness and settlement rule in this document is evaluated against that clock. Before the clock has ever been set the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that fixed startup default must never fall back to real system time. A `SystemOperator` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the response echoes back `current_utc`. Setting the clock modifies no business entity: no score is recomputed, no state advances, no `version` is incremented. Time only becomes visible when a subsequent business request evaluates a rule against it.

### 2.6. Nested Paths and Left-to-Right Resolution

Two URL families nest. Exposure signals, insurance-policy lists and the perquisite-disclosure read hang beneath a protectee; legs, check-ins and incidents hang beneath a trip. Every other relationship in the service is a body-level foreign key on a flat collection — a protective-detail assignment names its leg in its payload rather than living under it, an incident's response track names no parent in its path beyond the incident itself, and cross-aggregate references such as a trip pointing at a risk assessment or an engagement pointing at an incident are peer links, not ownership paths.

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A request against a protectee's sub-resources whose protectee does not exist is rejected with `ERR_EXECUTIVE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before the sub-resource payload is examined at all, and the same identifier is returned when an `executive_id` named in the body of an insurance-policy, risk-assessment or trip creation resolves to nothing. A request against any trip sub-resource whose trip does not exist yields `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), as does a body-level `trip_id` that resolves to nothing. A destination-rating revision whose `country_code` path segment names no published rating yields `ERR_DESTINATION_RATING_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and so does a trip creation whose `primary_destination_country_code` or a leg creation whose `destination_country_code` names a country for which no rating has been published. The remaining existence failures — `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIP_LEG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND` — all carry HTTP 404 and are raised whichever way the missing row was named, by path segment or by a foreign-key field in the body. Body-level foreign keys are checked in the same pass: a staff identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND` whether it arrived as `authored_by_staff_id`, `created_by_staff_id` or `staff_id`, and a risk-assessment identifier yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` whether it arrived in a path or as `risk_assessment_id`. An incident report whose `affected_leg_sequence_no` does not resolve to a leg of the addressed trip is also an `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND` condition, because the leg the incident claims to have happened on does not exist.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

All risk and index scores in the service are non-negative decimal numbers in the inclusive range from 0.00 to 100.00, stored and returned with exactly 2 decimal places, and every rounding operation on them uses round-half-up. Where a formula both scales and caps a value, the rounding happens exactly once — after all scaling and before the cap comparison — so a value is never rounded twice along one derivation.

All money in the service is an integer count of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere. Where a money figure is the product of an hour count, a rate and a multiplier, the rounding to a whole cent is applied at line-item granularity — once per protective-detail assignment, once per perquisite split — using round-half-up. Aggregations then sum those already-rounded integers and never re-round the total. Summing unrounded products and rounding the sum afterwards is explicitly wrong and yields a different figure; implementations must round per line item.

Hour counts are whole integers. Where a duration is converted from an instant pair into hours, the elapsed time is taken in whole minutes, divided by 60, and rounded **up** to the next whole hour unless it is already exact.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Planning Parents

Two write operations require the client to declare the version of the parent row it planned against. Both are creations, and both use a strict equality check against the parent's current `version`.

A `POST /exec-protection/detail-assignments` request must carry `expected_trip_leg_version` as a non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the target `TripLeg` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the leg's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRIP_LEG_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Two assignments for different agents over non-colliding windows on the same leg are both legitimate, so this is not a state precondition — the rejection exists because a leg's `version` advances whenever its risk band, its required advance-work set or its coverage flag is re-derived, and a writer holding a stale view may be planning coverage against a band that no longer applies.

A `POST /exec-protection/vendor-engagements` request must carry `expected_vendor_version` as a non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the target `Vendor` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the vendor's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Overlapping engagements against one vendor are legitimate up to that vendor's concurrency ceiling, so a second create is not blocked by any state field; the rejection exists because every engagement create, completion, cancellation or vendor suspension advances the vendor's `version`, and a stale writer's view of that vendor's capacity and rates is no longer the vendor's. Re-reading the vendor is the only way to obtain a fresh `version` between two engagement creations.

No other endpoint in the service takes a version field. Engagements created automatically by the incident-triage cascade are server-initiated and carry no expected-version input at all.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

The department's control model separates authorship from approval in two places, and both comparisons are made against `X-User-ID`. The staff member who authored a risk assessment may not review it: on the assessment review action, `X-User-ID` must differ from the assessment's `authored_by_staff_id`. The staff member who created a trip may not decide it: on the trip decision action, `X-User-ID` must differ from the trip's `created_by_staff_id`. A violation of either is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Because the check is by persisted identifier rather than by role, a director who happens to have drafted an assessment under an analyst hat is still barred from reviewing it.

A second, independent control governs which role may act at which approval stage. A `SecurityDirector` may take a trip decision only while the trip's `approval_state` is `PENDING_REVIEW`; a `ComplianceOfficer` may take one only while it is `PENDING_COMPLIANCE`. A director acting on a compliance-stage trip, or a compliance officer acting on a review-stage trip, is rejected with `ERR_APPROVAL_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). This is deliberately distinct from role authorisation: both roles are authorised for the decision endpoint, and it is the stage they act at that is wrong.

### 2.10. The Three Commitment Ledgers

Three quantities are maintained as running ledgers rather than recomputed on read, and each must be exactly restored by the compensating action of whatever incremented it.

**The money ledger.** For every protectee, `remaining_budget_cents` is `annual_security_budget_cents` less `committed_budget_cents` less `spent_budget_cents`. Equivalently, at every observable instant the sum of `committed_budget_cents`, `spent_budget_cents` and `remaining_budget_cents` equals `annual_security_budget_cents` exactly. `committed_budget_cents` accumulates the provisional cost of trips whose `approval_state` is `PENDING_REVIEW`, `PENDING_COMPLIANCE` or `APPROVED` and whose `readiness_state` is not `CLOSED`; `spent_budget_cents` accumulates the final cost of trips whose `readiness_state` is `CLOSED`. Submitting a trip for approval adds its provisional cost to the committed figure, and denying that trip subtracts exactly the same amount, restoring the ledger to its pre-submission value.

**The staff-hours ledger.** For every staff member, `committed_detail_hours` is the sum of `planned_billable_hours` over that person's protective-detail assignments whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, and `released_detail_hours` is the sum of `final_billable_hours` over those whose `status` is `RELEASED`. Creating an assignment adds its planned hours to the committed figure; cancelling it through the trip-denial cascade subtracts exactly those planned hours again.

**The vendor-hours ledger.** For every vendor, `committed_engagement_hours` is the sum of `engagement_hours` over that vendor's engagements whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Creating an engagement adds its hours; the vendor-suspension cascade that cancels it subtracts exactly those hours again.

All three ledgers are integers and involve no rounding, so a compensated pair must return the exact pre-action value. Two further protectee roll-ups follow the same discipline: `annual_disclosed_perquisite_cents` accumulates the protectee-borne share of every trip whose `readiness_state` is `CLOSED`, `business_borne_security_cents` accumulates the company-borne share of the same trips, and `open_degraded_trip_count` is the number of that protectee's trips whose `readiness_state` is currently `DEGRADED`.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule would reject a request, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the ordering below. The ordering is a property of the specification, not of the implementation's convenience.

1. **Authentication** — missing, empty or undeclared headers, giving `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation** — the acting role is not on the endpoint's list, giving `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`.
3. **Payload shape** — a missing required field, a wrong JSON type, an enum value that is not a declared member, a number outside its declared range, a country code that is not exactly two uppercase letters A–Z, or a datetime that is not a valid ISO-8601 UTC instant, all giving `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) on every creating and revising endpoint. Unknown extra fields are ignored rather than rejected.
4. **Pagination bounds** — giving `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
5. **Interval ordering** — every start/end pair must be strictly ordered, giving `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID` (HTTP 422). This governs an insurance policy's `effective_to` against its `effective_from`, a trip's `return_at` against its `depart_at`, a leg's `depart_at` against its `arrive_at`, an assignment's `ends_at` against its `starts_at`, an engagement's `ends_at` against its `starts_at`, and a release's `actual_ends_at` against the assignment's `starts_at`.
6. **Existence** — the 404 family described in §2.6, resolved left to right.
7. **Ownership** — an `Executive` caller outside their own records, giving `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
8. **Segregation of duties and approval stage** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` then `ERR_APPROVAL_LEVEL_MISMATCH`.
9. **State preconditions** — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TRIP_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INCIDENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_POLICY_INVALID_STATE` and `ERR_VENDOR_SUSPENDED`, in that relative order.
10. **Optimistic-concurrency parent version** — `ERR_STALE_TRIP_LEG_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_VERSION`, evaluated after every state precondition above and before any business-band rule below.
11. **Business band (a) — safety, duty of care and compliance.** In order: `ERR_RESIDENTIAL_SURVEY_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_STAFF_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_ADVANCE_WORK_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_BRIEFING_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ERR_STAFF_REST_BUFFER_CONFLICT`.
12. **Business band (b) — temporal and structural constraints.** In order: `ERR_LEG_OUTSIDE_TRIP_WINDOW`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_ENDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEG_SEQUENCE`, `ERR_DESTINATION_RATING_EXISTS`, `ERR_TRIP_HAS_NO_LEGS`, `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR`, `ERR_INCIDENT_TRACK_INCOMPATIBLE`.
13. **Business band (c) — referential and coverage prerequisites.** In order: `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`, `ERR_INSURANCE_COUNTRY_EXCLUDED`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENTS_BLOCK_CLOSE`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENTS_NOT_SETTLED`, `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`.
14. **Business band (d) — fiscal capacity, always last.** `ERR_SECURITY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

A protective-security domain must never let a budget ceiling mask an unqualified-agent or missing-advance-work defect, which is why every fiscal check sorts after every safety, structural and coverage check. The trip submission endpoint is where this matters most, and the concrete ladder there is: advance work, then the empty-itinerary check, then assessment approval, then insurance verification, then country exclusion and policy window, then coverage sufficiency, then budget.

### 2.12. Cascade Discipline

Five named cascades carry a change from where it was made to every artefact that depended on it. They are referenced throughout §3 and summarised here so their shape is visible in one place.

- **Cascade A** begins at vendor suspension and is the deepest chain in the service: suspension cancels engagements, cancellation can strip a completed local-liaison advance item from a leg, the stripped leg can drop a trip out of readiness, and the dropped trip tightens its check-in cadence and increments its protectee's degraded-trip counter.
- **Cascade B** begins at risk-assessment approval: the approved assessment resets the protectee's threat level, supersedes the previously approved assessment, re-points that assessment's non-closed trips at the new one, and re-derives every leg band, advance requirement, approval level and check-in cadence beneath them.
- **Cascade C** begins at a destination-rating revision: the rating recomputes, then every non-closed leg pointing at that country re-bands, then affected trips re-derive their maximum band and approval level and can lose readiness, and finally an approved trip whose approval level rose is pushed back into review with its briefing acknowledgement cleared.
- **Cascade D** begins at a trip denial: the trip is denied, every confirmed assignment on its legs is cancelled and voided, and both the staff-hours and money ledgers are restored.
- **Cascade E** begins at a duress check-in: the trip's duress flag is raised, its cadence is forced to the duress interval and its readiness is degraded, and an incident is opened automatically.

Two invariants govern all five. First, every row a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly one, exactly as if the caller had edited it directly. Second, a cascade that is rejected has no partial effect: when the vendor selector at incident triage finds no candidate, the incident stays in its pre-triage state, no engagement is created, and no `version` anywhere is incremented.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Department Roster and the Protectee Registry

Before any protective work can be planned, the department's own people must exist in the system. Registering a security staff member records their `full_name` (a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters), their `staff_role` — one of `ANALYST`, `MANAGER` or `DIRECTOR`, which decides which API role that person may later authenticate as — their `certification_expires_at`, an ISO-8601 UTC instant after which they may not be placed on a protective detail, and their `hourly_rate_cents`, a billable rate per hour in cents that must be strictly greater than zero. A newly registered staff member starts with both labour figures at zero: `committed_detail_hours` and `released_detail_hours` are each 0. Reading one staff member returns those two figures alongside the input fields, and it is the department's view of who is over-committed. Registration is available to a `SystemOperator` — which is how the very first roster row is created before any staff-backed role exists — and to a `SecurityDirector`.

Registering a protectee captures both the identity of the person and the money and consent boundaries that will govern every trip they take. The payload carries `full_name` (non-blank, 1 to 120 characters), `tier` — one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, the protection tier that later uplifts risk scores and approval levels — `role_title` (1 to 120 characters, the corporate title printed on briefings), `base_country_code` (exactly two uppercase letters A–Z, the ISO-3166 alpha-2 home country), and `annual_security_budget_cents`, the yearly protection ceiling in cents, which must be zero or greater. Two further fields are optional: `consent_scope`, which defaults to `NONE` when omitted and is otherwise `TRIP_ONLY` or `CONTINUOUS`, and `consent_expires_at`, which defaults to null. When `consent_scope` is anything other than `NONE` and `consent_expires_at` is supplied as a non-null value, that instant must be strictly later than the current logical clock reading; a consent grant that has already lapsed at the moment it is granted is a malformed payload.

A newly registered protectee is entirely un-assessed and entirely un-committed. Their `residential_survey_status` is `PENDING`, their `residential_component` is 0, their `public_exposure_score` is 0.00, and their `threat_level` is `LOW`. Their `committed_budget_cents` and `spent_budget_cents` are both 0, so their `remaining_budget_cents` equals the `annual_security_budget_cents` just supplied. Their `annual_disclosed_perquisite_cents`, `business_borne_security_cents` and `open_degraded_trip_count` are all 0. Reading one protectee returns the whole picture — tier, threat level, exposure score and the entire budget ledger — and is available to every staff-backed role, to a `ComplianceOfficer`, and to that protectee themselves.

The consent boundary is the protectee's own to move. Revising it replaces `consent_scope` and, when supplied, `consent_expires_at`; a protectee may do this for their own record and a `ProtectionManager` may do it on their behalf. Consent revision is deliberately **not** retroactive: check-in rows already recorded are never rewritten, so a check-in whose location was withheld under a `NONE` scope stays withheld forever even after consent is later granted, and a check-in whose location was retained under `CONTINUOUS` stays retained even after consent is later revoked. What consent governs is the treatment of each check-in at the instant it is filed, and nothing else.

### 3.2. Threat Monitoring and the Risk-Assessment Cycle

Two intelligence feeds sit beneath every assessment: the residential security survey and the public-exposure signal log. A `SecurityAnalyst` records a survey outcome by supplying `survey_outcome`, which is either `COMPLETE` or `DEFICIENT`, together with `residential_component`, a residential-hardening deficiency score between 0 and 100 carried to two decimal places. The survey write sets `residential_survey_status` to the supplied outcome and replaces `residential_component` outright. It does not retro-fit assessments that have already been submitted: those carry their own frozen copy of the figure.

Exposure signals form an append-only log — a signal is never updated and never deleted. Appending one records `signal_type`, which selects the exposure channel and is one of `MEDIA_MENTION`, `SOCIAL_GEOTAG`, `PUBLIC_FILING` or `PROTEST_MENTION`; `severity`, an analyst-assigned intensity that must be an integer from 1 to 5 inclusive; `observed_at`, the ISO-8601 UTC instant the signal was seen, which must be at or before the current logical clock reading; and an optional `source_note`, a nullable provenance string of up to 500 characters. Signals can be listed for one protectee, newest `observed_at` first, optionally narrowed by `signal_type`.

Every appended signal immediately re-derives that protectee's `public_exposure_score`, using the **decay-weighted** reading of the exposure formula. The formula works as follows. For each signal, its age in whole days is the number of seconds between its `observed_at` and the current clock reading, divided by 86,400 and rounded down to a whole number. A signal is inside the window when its age in days is at least 0 and strictly less than 90 days — the window is a rolling quarter, half-open at its far edge, and a signal that has aged out is excluded outright rather than down-weighted to a small contribution. Each in-window signal has a weight: under the decay-weighted reading the weight is one half raised to the power of the signal's age in days divided by a 30-day half-life, evaluated as a real number, so a signal observed 30 days ago contributes exactly half of its intensity, one observed 60 days ago exactly a quarter, and one observed today its full intensity. Each in-window signal contributes its `severity`, multiplied by its channel multiplier, multiplied by its weight; the channel multipliers are 1.0 for `MEDIA_MENTION`, 1.5 for `SOCIAL_GEOTAG`, 0.5 for `PUBLIC_FILING` and 2.0 for `PROTEST_MENTION`. Those contributions are accumulated in ascending `observed_at` order, and where two signals share an `observed_at` instant, in ascending `created_at` order. The accumulated total is multiplied by a scale factor of 5, that scaled value is rounded half-up to two decimal places exactly once, and the score is the smaller of the rounded value and the cap of 100.00. There is no lifecycle filter on the input set, because an exposure signal has no lifecycle.

An assessment begins as a draft. A `SecurityAnalyst` creates it by naming the protectee through `executive_id`, naming themselves through `authored_by_staff_id` — which must equal the creating request's `X-User-ID` — and supplying `threat_monitoring_score`, their own 0.00-to-100.00 reading of the threat-monitoring feed. The new row's `status` is `DRAFT` and its `revision_round` is 0; its `approved_by_staff_id`, `supersedes_assessment_id`, `review_notes`, `submitted_at` and `approved_at` are all null, and its three derived components are 0. Assessments can be listed newest `created_at` first, narrowed by any of `executive_id`, `authored_by_staff_id`, `approved_by_staff_id`, `supersedes_assessment_id` and `status`, and a single assessment can be read with all four of its score figures visible.

Submitting an assessment advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and the same action re-closes the revision cycle by advancing a returned assessment `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`. Both edges are available only to the authoring analyst — `X-User-ID` must equal `authored_by_staff_id` — and both require that the protectee's residential survey has actually been performed: `residential_survey_status` must be anything other than `PENDING`, so both `COMPLETE` and `DEFICIENT` pass and only `PENDING` blocks with `ERR_RESIDENTIAL_SURVEY_INCOMPLETE`. Submitting from any status other than `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

Each submit re-snapshots the assessment's inputs, and it does so identically on both edges. `public_exposure_component` is recomputed using the **rolling-window** reading of the exposure formula: the same 90-day half-open window, the same channel multipliers, the same accumulation order, the same scale factor of 5, the same single half-up rounding to two decimals and the same cap of 100.00, but with every in-window signal counted at full weight regardless of age. For any identical set of signals, the rolling-window value is greater than or equal to the decay-weighted value, and the two differ whenever any in-window signal is at least one whole day old. `residential_component_snapshot` is set to a copy of the protectee's current `residential_component`. `submitted_at` is set to the current clock reading. `revision_round` is explicitly **not** changed by a submit — only a return moves it.

The composite score is then recomputed, in a pinned order of operations. The weighted base is 0.45 times `threat_monitoring_score`, plus 0.35 times the `public_exposure_component` just written, plus 0.20 times the `residential_component_snapshot` just written. That base is then multiplied by the protectee's tier uplift: 1.20 for `TIER_1`, 1.10 for `TIER_2`, and 1.00 for `TIER_3`. Next comes the protest floor: if the same in-window signal set that produced `public_exposure_component` contains at least one signal whose `signal_type` is `PROTEST_MENTION` and whose `severity` is 4 or greater, the uplifted value is replaced by the larger of itself and 60.00; otherwise it passes through unchanged. Finally the value is capped at 100.00 and rounded half-up to two decimal places, and the result is stored as `composite_risk_score`. The order — weighting, then tier uplift, then protest floor, then cap, then round — is fixed and produces different answers if rearranged.

Director review is where the cycle branches. A `SecurityDirector` supplies `outcome`, which is either `APPROVE` or `RETURN`, and optionally `review_notes`. Review requires `status` to be `SUBMITTED`. A `RETURN` outcome requires `review_notes` to be present and 1 to 500 characters long, increments `revision_round` by 1 first, and then decides between two destinations: if the incremented `revision_round` is 3 or less the assessment goes `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` and the notes are stored, and if the incremented `revision_round` is greater than 3 the assessment goes `SUBMITTED → VOIDED` instead. The maximum revision allowance is three rounds, so the first, second and third `RETURN` leave `revision_round` at 1, 2 and 3 respectively with the assessment `RETURNED`, while the fourth leaves `revision_round` at 4 with the assessment `VOIDED`. The incremented value is stored in both cases. `VOIDED` is terminal: such an assessment can never be submitted again and never approved.

An `APPROVE` outcome requires that `X-User-ID` differs from `authored_by_staff_id`, and it opens the deepest recomputation in the assessment domain. Hop one advances the row `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, sets `approved_by_staff_id` to `X-User-ID`, sets `approved_at` to the current clock reading, and recomputes the protectee's `threat_level` from this assessment's `composite_risk_score` alone — no other assessment contributes. The banding is: `LOW` when the composite is below 30.00, `ELEVATED` when it is at least 30.00 and below 55.00, `HIGH` when it is at least 55.00 and below 80.00, and `SEVERE` when it is 80.00 or above; the comparisons run against the stored two-decimal value, with every lower bound inclusive and every upper bound exclusive. Hop two finds the protectee's previously approved assessment — the unique other row with the same `executive_id` and `status` of `APPROVED` — moves it `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, and records its identifier in the new row's `supersedes_assessment_id`; when no such prior row exists, `supersedes_assessment_id` stays null. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal; nothing further can happen to that row. Hop three re-points every trip that referenced the superseded assessment and whose `readiness_state` is not `CLOSED`, rewriting each one's `risk_assessment_id` to the newly approved assessment. Hop four then re-derives everything beneath those re-pointed trips, because both the assessment they point at and the protectee's `threat_level` have moved: each of their legs recomputes `leg_risk_band` and `required_advance_items`, and each trip recomputes `max_leg_risk_band`, `required_approval_level` and `check_in_interval_hours`. A re-pointed trip whose `approval_state` was `APPROVED` and whose `required_approval_level` has now risen is pushed back `APPROVED → PENDING_REVIEW` and has its `briefing_ack_at` cleared to null, because the briefing the protectee acknowledged no longer describes the destination posture. A re-pointed trip whose `readiness_state` was `READY` but which now has a leg whose `advance_complete` is false moves `READY → DEGRADED` and increments the protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count`. Every row this cascade touches — the approving assessment, the superseded assessment, each re-pointed trip and each recomputed leg — has its `version` incremented by one.

### 3.3. Destination Advisories, Coverage Floors and Insurance Verification

A destination rating is the department's published view of one country, and the country itself is the natural key: `country_code` must be exactly two uppercase letters A–Z and must be unique across all ratings. A `SecurityAnalyst` publishes one by supplying four indices, each a number from 0.00 to 100.00 — `crime_index` for violent and property crime, `political_instability_index` for political unrest, `medical_infrastructure_index` for medical capability, and `natural_hazard_index` for natural-disaster exposure — together with `advisory_effective_at`, the ISO-8601 UTC instant the advisory took effect, and an optional `advisory_note` of up to 500 characters. Attempting to publish a second rating for a country that already has one is rejected with `ERR_DESTINATION_RATING_EXISTS`.

Three fields are derived from those four indices and never supplied by the caller. `composite_destination_score` is 0.30 times `crime_index`, plus 0.30 times `political_instability_index`, plus 0.20 times the medical shortfall, plus 0.20 times `natural_hazard_index`, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The **medical shortfall** is what makes the medical index behave in the opposite direction to the other three: a higher `medical_infrastructure_index` means better care, so the index enters the composite inverted, as 100 minus its value. A country with perfect medical infrastructure therefore contributes nothing from that term, while a country with a medical index of 0 contributes the full 0.20 weight.

`rating_band` is the threshold band of that composite: `LOW` when the composite is below 25.00, `MODERATE` when it is at least 25.00 and below 50.00, `HIGH` when it is at least 50.00 and below 75.00, and `EXTREME` when it is 75.00 or above. `insurance_required_coverage_cents` is then read straight off the band, and these are the department's contractual coverage floors: 25,000,000 cents (a quarter of a million dollars) for `LOW`, 50,000,000 cents for `MODERATE`, 150,000,000 cents for `HIGH`, and 500,000,000 cents — five million dollars — for `EXTREME`.

Revising an advisory is where the destination cascade begins. An analyst may send any subset of `crime_index`, `political_instability_index`, `medical_infrastructure_index`, `natural_hazard_index`, `advisory_effective_at` and `advisory_note`; fields not sent are left alone. Hop one recomputes `composite_destination_score`, `rating_band` and `insurance_required_coverage_cents` from the revised indices. Hop two recomputes `leg_risk_band` and `required_advance_items` on every trip leg whose `destination_country_code` matches this country and whose owning trip's `readiness_state` is not `CLOSED`. Hop three recomputes each affected trip's `max_leg_risk_band` and `required_approval_level`, and moves any of those trips whose `readiness_state` was `READY` but which now has a leg that lost `advance_complete` to `READY → DEGRADED`, incrementing the protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count`; a trip whose only recorded advance item was removed and whose `readiness_state` was `ADVANCE_IN_PROGRESS` returns `ADVANCE_IN_PROGRESS → NOT_READY`. Hop four handles the approval consequence: any affected trip whose `approval_state` was `APPROVED` and whose `required_approval_level` has risen reverts `APPROVED → PENDING_REVIEW` and has `briefing_ack_at` cleared to null. Every rating, leg and trip the revision touches has its `version` incremented by one.

Insurance runs on a parallel track, and it exists because coverage floors are meaningless unless somebody has confirmed the policy is real. A policy is created for one protectee by naming `executive_id` and supplying `policy_type` — one of `MEDICAL_EVAC`, `KIDNAP_RANSOM` or `TRAVEL_ACCIDENT` — `coverage_cents`, the maximum indemnity in cents, which must be strictly greater than zero, `effective_from`, the inclusive UTC start of cover, `effective_to`, the exclusive UTC end of cover, which must be strictly later than `effective_from`, and optionally `excluded_country_codes`, an array of two-uppercase-letter codes the policy does not cover, defaulting to an empty array. Either a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `ProtectionManager` may create one. The new policy's `status` is `UNVERIFIED`, and its `verified_at` and `verification_note` are null.

Verification is exclusively a `ComplianceOfficer` act, and it is a one-shot branch. The officer supplies `outcome`, either `VERIFY` or `REJECT`, and optionally `verification_note` (up to 500 characters). Verification requires `status` to be `UNVERIFIED`; a second attempt on an already-decided policy yields `ERR_POLICY_INVALID_STATE`. A `VERIFY` outcome additionally requires that the policy has not already expired against the logical clock: `effective_to` must be strictly later than the current clock reading. It moves the policy `UNVERIFIED → VERIFIED`, sets `verified_at` to the current clock reading and records the note. A `REJECT` outcome requires `verification_note` to be present and 1 to 500 characters long, records it, and moves the policy `UNVERIFIED → REJECTED`; a rejected policy can never be carried on a trip submission. Both `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED` are terminal. Policies can be listed for one protectee, narrowed by `status` and `policy_type`, and that list is visible to a `ComplianceOfficer`, a `ProtectionManager`, a `SecurityDirector` and the protectee themselves.

### 3.4. Trip Construction: Legs, Risk Bands and Advance Work

A `ProtectionManager` opens a trip by naming its protectee through `executive_id`, naming themselves through `created_by_staff_id` — which must equal the creating request's `X-User-ID`, and which is remembered as the segregation-of-duties counterparty for the later decision — and naming the assessment the trip is planned against through `risk_assessment_id`. The payload also carries `trip_purpose`, one of `BUSINESS_TRAVEL`, `PUBLIC_APPEARANCE` or `BOARD_EVENT`; `primary_destination_country_code`, two uppercase letters that must resolve to a published destination rating; `depart_at`, the inclusive UTC start of the trip window; and `return_at`, its exclusive end, which must be strictly later than `depart_at`. Two fields are optional: `insurance_policy_id`, which may be attached at creation or left null and attached later, and `cost_allocation_method`, which defaults to `PRORATA` and is otherwise `BUSINESS_ONLY` or `EXEC_PERQUISITE`. Choosing `BUSINESS_ONLY` is permitted only when `trip_purpose` is `BUSINESS_TRAVEL`; requesting it for a public appearance or a board event is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`. Any `policy_type` may be attached to a trip — the type only matters at a medical-evacuation triage — but the policy must be verified by the time the trip is submitted.

A new trip carries both of its state dimensions at their initial values: `approval_state` is `DRAFT` and `readiness_state` is `NOT_READY`. Its `required_approval_level` is `MANAGER`, its `max_leg_risk_band` is null because it has no legs, and its `check_in_interval_hours` is seeded at 24 hours, the cadence for the lowest risk band. All of its cost figures — `provisional_security_cost_cents`, `total_security_cost_cents`, `perquisite_taxable_cents`, `business_borne_cents` — are 0, `perquisite_rate_applied` is 0, `missed_check_in_count` is 0, `duress_active` is false, and `next_check_in_due_at`, `briefing_ack_at`, `denied_reason` and `closed_at` are all null. The two state dimensions advance on entirely disjoint triggers and are gated against each other only at close. Trips can be listed newest `created_at` first, narrowed by `executive_id`, `created_by_staff_id`, `risk_assessment_id`, `insurance_policy_id`, `approval_state` and `readiness_state`; a single trip read shows both state dimensions, every cost field and the check-in cadence.

Legs may only be added while the trip's `approval_state` is `DRAFT`; adding one to a submitted, approved or denied trip yields `ERR_TRIP_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`. A leg carries `sequence_no`, its 1-based position within the trip, which must be at least 1 and must not already be used by another leg of the same trip — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEG_SEQUENCE`. It carries `destination_country_code`, two uppercase letters that must resolve to a published destination rating, `arrive_at`, the inclusive UTC start of on-ground presence, and `depart_at`, its exclusive end, which must be strictly later than `arrive_at`. The leg must also sit inside the trip's own window: its `arrive_at` must be at or after the trip's `depart_at` and its own `depart_at` must be at or before the trip's `return_at`, and a leg that pokes out of either end is rejected with `ERR_LEG_OUTSIDE_TRIP_WINDOW`. Legs are listed in ascending `sequence_no` order.

Each leg is risk-banded the moment it is created, and re-banded by every cascade that disturbs its inputs. The band derives from the destination's `composite_destination_score` plus an uplift keyed on the travelling protectee's current `threat_level`: 0 points for `LOW`, 5 points for `ELEVATED`, 10 points for `HIGH` and 20 points for `SEVERE`. That sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places and then capped at 100.00, and the resulting adjusted figure is banded on the same cut-points the destination composite uses: `leg_risk_band` is `LOW` below 25.00, `MODERATE` at 25.00 and above but below 50.00, `HIGH` at 50.00 and above but below 75.00, and `EXTREME` at 75.00 and above. The protectee whose threat level applies is the one named on the leg's owning trip.

The band in turn dictates the advance work. `required_advance_items` is derived, never supplied: a `LOW` leg requires only a route survey; a `MODERATE` leg requires a route survey and a hotel survey; a `HIGH` leg requires a route survey, a hotel survey, a hospital survey and a communications test; and an `EXTREME` leg requires all of those plus local liaison. On top of the band-derived set, any leg belonging to a trip whose `trip_purpose` is `PUBLIC_APPEARANCE` additionally requires a crowd plan. The array is always emitted in the canonical order `ROUTE_SURVEY`, `HOTEL_SURVEY`, `HOSPITAL_SURVEY`, `COMMS_TEST`, `LOCAL_LIAISON`, `CROWD_PLAN`, filtered to the members actually required — not in the order the items happened to be derived or recorded.

Recording advance work appends to one of two arrays on the leg. The payload carries `item_type` — one of the six item types above — `outcome`, which is either `COMPLETE` or `WAIVED`, and an optional `note`. A `COMPLETE` outcome appends `item_type` to `completed_advance_items`; a `WAIVED` outcome appends it to `waived_advance_items`. Recording the same item twice is idempotent in the array — the item is not duplicated — but the request still succeeds with 200 OK. Waiving is tightly held: only a `SecurityDirector` may record a `WAIVED` outcome at all, and even a director may never waive `ROUTE_SURVEY` or `CROWD_PLAN`, which are non-waivable by every role. A waiver attempt by any other role, or a waiver of either of those two items by anyone, is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Recording a `COMPLETE` outcome is open to a `SecurityAnalyst`, a `ProtectionManager` and a `SecurityDirector` alike. Advance work may be recorded whenever the owning trip's `approval_state` is not `DENIED` and its `readiness_state` is not `CLOSED`.

`advance_complete` on a leg is true exactly when every member of `required_advance_items` appears in either `completed_advance_items` or `waived_advance_items` — the union of the two arrays must cover the required set. Because the required set is derived, it can move underneath the recorded arrays: shrinking it, as when a band falls, can flip `advance_complete` to true with no new work recorded, and expanding it, as when a band rises, can flip it to false. Items already recorded are never discarded by a band change; the only thing that ever removes a recorded item is the vendor-suspension cascade described in §3.5.

Readiness tracks that progress. The first advance item recorded on any leg of a trip moves the trip `NOT_READY → ADVANCE_IN_PROGRESS`. The reverse edge exists and is genuinely reachable: when a cascade removes the trip's only recorded advance item, so that every leg of the trip has both an empty `completed_advance_items` and an empty `waived_advance_items`, the trip returns `ADVANCE_IN_PROGRESS → NOT_READY`. Readiness is re-derived from the world, not ratcheted forward. Every leg creation and every band change also re-derives the owning trip's `max_leg_risk_band` — the highest `leg_risk_band` across the trip's legs, ordered `LOW` below `MODERATE` below `HIGH` below `EXTREME`, and null when the trip has no legs at all — together with its `required_approval_level`, which §3.6 specifies.

### 3.5. Protective Details and Vendor Engagements

Staffing a leg means creating a protective-detail assignment. The payload names the leg through `trip_leg_id`, the agent through `staff_id`, and the position through `role_on_detail` — one of `LEAD_AGENT`, `AGENT`, `DRIVER` or `ADVANCE` — and bounds the agent's shift with `starts_at`, the inclusive UTC start, and `ends_at`, the exclusive end, which must be strictly later than `starts_at`. It also carries `expected_trip_leg_version`, the leg version the client last observed, checked as described in §2.8. Only a `ProtectionManager` may create one, and a new assignment's `status` is `CONFIRMED`.

Four conditions gate the creation beyond the payload itself. The agent's certification must cover the whole shift: `certification_expires_at` must be strictly later than the request's `ends_at`, and an instant exactly equal to `ends_at` **fails** — a certification that lapses at the very moment the detail ends is not good enough, and the rejection is `ERR_STAFF_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. The owning trip's risk assessment must be in `APPROVED` status, so a detail can never be planned against intelligence that has been superseded, voided or never reviewed; a trip whose assessment is in any other status is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED`. The owning trip must be live: its `approval_state` must not be `DENIED` and its `readiness_state` must not be `CLOSED`, or the create is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`.

The fourth condition is the **rest buffer**, a duty-of-care rule that protects agents rather than protectees. The department's rest buffer is 12 hours. A new assignment is rejected when there exists any other `CONFIRMED` assignment of the same staff member — call it the neighbour — such that the new assignment's `starts_at` falls strictly before the neighbour's `ends_at` plus 12 hours, and the neighbour's `starts_at` falls strictly before the new assignment's `ends_at` plus 12 hours. Both comparisons are strict, so a new assignment whose `starts_at` lands exactly on the neighbour's `ends_at` plus 12 hours passes. Assignments already `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` are ignored entirely — only live commitments constrain new ones. This is a pairwise exclusion, not an aggregate one: a single conflicting neighbour is enough to reject, and the error is `ERR_STAFF_REST_BUFFER_CONFLICT`.

At creation, `planned_billable_hours` is frozen as the elapsed time from `starts_at` to `ends_at` measured in whole minutes, divided by 60 and rounded up to the next whole hour. `cost_cents` is then computed on the **provisional** basis and `cost_basis` is set to `PROVISIONAL`: the hour count is multiplied by the staff member's `hourly_rate_cents` and then by the position multiplier — 1.25 for `LEAD_AGENT`, 1.00 for `AGENT`, 0.85 for `DRIVER` and 1.10 for `ADVANCE` — and the product is rounded half-up to a whole cent at this line item. The creation adds `planned_billable_hours` to the staff member's `committed_detail_hours`, re-derives the leg's `lead_agent_covered` flag and increments the leg's `version` by one. Assignments can be listed newest `created_at` first, narrowed by `trip_leg_id`, `staff_id` and `status`, and a single read shows the cost basis alongside both hour figures.

`lead_agent_covered` on a leg is true exactly when some assignment on that leg has `status` of `CONFIRMED`, has `role_on_detail` of `LEAD_AGENT`, has a `starts_at` at or before the leg's `arrive_at`, and has an `ends_at` at or after the leg's `depart_at`. A lead agent who arrives late or leaves early does not cover the leg. When an assignment restores full lead-agent coverage across a trip and every leg of that trip is also `advance_complete`, the trip returns `DEGRADED → READY`.

Releasing an assignment settles it. The manager supplies `actual_ends_at`, which must be strictly later than the assignment's `starts_at`, and the assignment moves `CONFIRMED → RELEASED`; a release attempted on an assignment that is not `CONFIRMED` is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. `actual_ends_at` is stored, and `final_billable_hours` is computed as the larger of the four-hour minimum and the elapsed whole minutes from `starts_at` to `actual_ends_at` divided by 60 and rounded up to the next whole hour. That minimum is what makes the two cost bases differ: an agent released after 1 hour 47 minutes still bills 4 hours on the final basis, where the same window planned in advance would have billed 2. `cost_cents` is recomputed on the **final** basis — the same rate-times-multiplier product, rounded half-up at the line item, but using `final_billable_hours` — and `cost_basis` becomes `FINAL`. The staff member's `committed_detail_hours` falls by `planned_billable_hours` and their `released_detail_hours` rises by `final_billable_hours`. The leg's `lead_agent_covered` is re-derived, and when releasing the last covering lead agent flips it to false the owning trip moves `READY → DEGRADED` and the protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count` increments.

An assignment can also be cancelled, and never by direct request — only by cascade. Two cascades do it. A trip denial cancels **every** `CONFIRMED` assignment on every leg of that trip, recording `cancelled_reason` as `TRIP_DENIED`. An incident triaged onto the extraction track cancels only those `CONFIRMED` assignments whose `starts_at` is strictly after the current clock reading, recording `cancelled_reason` as `EXTRACTION`; an assignment already under way is left `CONFIRMED`. On either path the assignment moves `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, its `cost_cents` is forced to 0, its `cost_basis` becomes `VOID`, and the staff member's `committed_detail_hours` falls by that assignment's `planned_billable_hours`, restoring the pre-creation figure exactly. Both `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Vendors supply the capabilities the department does not carry in-house. Registering one records `vendor_name` (non-blank, 1 to 120 characters), `vendor_type` — the single capability family this vendor may be engaged for, one of `MEDICAL_EVAC`, `GROUND_SECURITY`, `LOCAL_LIAISON` or `INTEL` — `country_code`, the two-letter country the vendor operates in, `max_concurrent_engagements`, the number of overlapping active engagements the vendor can carry at any instant, which must be at least 1, `sla_response_minutes`, the contracted response time in minutes, which must be strictly greater than zero, `hourly_rate_cents`, strictly greater than zero, and `retainer_cents`, a flat activation charge levied once per engagement, which must be zero or greater. A new vendor's `activation_status` is `AVAILABLE`, its `committed_engagement_hours` is 0 and its `suspension_reason` is null. Reading one vendor returns those figures and is also the only way to re-observe that vendor's `version` between two engagement creations, which the concurrency rule in §2.8 requires.

Engaging a vendor for a leg names `vendor_id` and `trip_leg_id`, states `engagement_purpose` — again one of `MEDICAL_EVAC`, `GROUND_SECURITY`, `LOCAL_LIAISON` or `INTEL` — bounds the work with `starts_at` and `ends_at` (the latter strictly later), and carries `expected_vendor_version`. Three eligibility conditions apply. The vendor must be `AVAILABLE`; engaging a suspended vendor is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_SUSPENDED`. The `engagement_purpose` must equal the vendor's own `vendor_type`, and the vendor's `country_code` must equal the leg's `destination_country_code`; a mismatch on either is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR`. And the vendor's aggregate capacity must not be exceeded: the count of that vendor's `ACTIVE` engagements whose half-open window from `starts_at` up to `ends_at` intersects the requested window must be strictly less than `max_concurrent_engagements`. That is an aggregate check — each existing engagement is individually legal and only their collective count fails — and the rejection is `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Engagements may only be created while the owning trip's `approval_state` is not `DENIED` and its `readiness_state` is not `CLOSED`.

A created engagement's `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `incident_id` is null because it is a planned engagement rather than an incident response, and its `cancelled_reason` is null. Its `engagement_hours` is frozen as the elapsed whole minutes from `starts_at` to `ends_at` divided by 60 and rounded up to the next whole hour, and its `engagement_cost_cents` is the vendor's `retainer_cents` plus `engagement_hours` multiplied by the vendor's `hourly_rate_cents`. The creation adds `engagement_hours` to the vendor's `committed_engagement_hours` and increments the vendor's `version` by one. One purpose has a side effect on the itinerary: when `engagement_purpose` is `LOCAL_LIAISON`, `LOCAL_LIAISON` is appended to the named leg's `completed_advance_items` and that leg's `advance_complete` is re-derived — engaging a local liaison vendor *is* how the local-liaison advance item gets done. Either a `ProtectionManager` or a `SecurityDirector` may engage a vendor, and engagements are listed newest `created_at` first, narrowed by `vendor_id`, `trip_leg_id`, `incident_id` and `status`; `incident_id` is the only discovery channel for engagements the incident-triage cascade created.

Completing an engagement moves it `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` and releases the vendor's capacity: `committed_engagement_hours` falls by that engagement's `engagement_hours` and the vendor's `version` increments. `engagement_cost_cents` is deliberately left unchanged, because a completed engagement's cost still has to be settled — it enters the owning trip's final cost when `incident_id` is null, and the incident's response cost when `incident_id` is set. Completing anything that is not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

Suspending a vendor is a `SecurityDirector` act and the deepest chain in the service. It requires a `suspension_reason` and requires that the vendor is currently `AVAILABLE`; suspending an already-suspended vendor is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_SUSPENDED`. Hop one moves `activation_status` from `AVAILABLE` to `SUSPENDED`, stores the `suspension_reason` and increments the vendor's `version`. Hop two moves every `ACTIVE` engagement of that vendor `ACTIVE → CANCELLED`, forcing each `engagement_cost_cents` to 0, recording `cancelled_reason` as `VENDOR_SUSPENDED`, and reducing `committed_engagement_hours` by each cancelled engagement's `engagement_hours` so the vendor-hours ledger returns to its pre-engagement value. Hop three reaches back into the itinerary: for each cancelled engagement whose `engagement_purpose` was `LOCAL_LIAISON`, `LOCAL_LIAISON` is **removed** from that leg's `completed_advance_items` and the leg's `advance_complete` is re-derived. This is the only mechanism in the service that ever un-records an advance item. Hop four propagates the loss upward: every affected trip that is not `CLOSED` and whose leg lost `advance_complete` moves `READY → DEGRADED`, or `ADVANCE_IN_PROGRESS → NOT_READY` when the removed item was that trip's only recorded item. Hop five tightens the consequences: the newly degraded trips have their `check_in_interval_hours` re-derived under the cadence rules of §3.7, and each protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count` increments once per newly degraded trip. Every vendor, engagement, leg and trip the chain touches has its `version` incremented by one.

### 3.6. The Approval Ladder, the Briefing and the Provisional Commitment

How far up the department a trip must climb is derived, not chosen. `required_approval_level` is computed in a pinned order of three steps. The **band base** comes from `max_leg_risk_band`: `MANAGER` when the maximum band is `LOW` or when the trip has no legs at all, `DIRECTOR` when it is `MODERATE` or `HIGH`, and `DIRECTOR_AND_COMPLIANCE` when it is `EXTREME`. The **purpose step-up** then applies: when `trip_purpose` is `PUBLIC_APPEARANCE` the base is raised one step along the ordering `MANAGER`, then `DIRECTOR`, then `DIRECTOR_AND_COMPLIANCE`, capped at `DIRECTOR_AND_COMPLIANCE`; for any other purpose the base passes through unchanged. The **tier override** then applies independently: when the protectee's `tier` is `TIER_1` and `max_leg_risk_band` is `HIGH` or `EXTREME`, a level of `DIRECTOR_AND_COMPLIANCE` is proposed. `required_approval_level` is the higher of the stepped-up value and the tier override under that same three-step ordering. Every leg of the trip contributes to `max_leg_risk_band` regardless of `sequence_no`, and the level is re-derived whenever a leg is created or any leg's band changes. A trip with zero legs derives a null `max_leg_risk_band` and a `MANAGER` level, but its submission is refused before that level is ever consulted.

Before any approval hop can complete, the protectee must have acknowledged their pre-trip briefing. Acknowledgement is exclusively the protectee's own act, it is permitted only while the trip's `approval_state` is `PENDING_REVIEW` or `PENDING_COMPLIANCE`, and it sets `briefing_ack_at` to the current clock reading. The acknowledgement therefore always sits between submission and decision. It is also perishable: both the assessment-approval cascade and the destination-revision cascade clear `briefing_ack_at` back to null whenever they push an approved trip back into review, so a protectee who was briefed against a `MODERATE` posture must be re-briefed once the posture becomes `EXTREME`.

Submitting a trip for approval is a `ProtectionManager` act, requires `approval_state` to be `DRAFT`, and moves the trip `DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW`. It is the single densest gate in the service, and the checks are evaluated in a fixed order so that the reported defect is always the most serious one present. First, safety: every leg of the trip must have `advance_complete` of true, and a trip with any leg still short of its required advance work is rejected with `ERR_ADVANCE_WORK_INCOMPLETE`. Second, structure: the trip must have at least one leg, since an empty itinerary cannot be risk-rated at all, and an empty one is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_HAS_NO_LEGS`. Third, intelligence: the assessment named by `risk_assessment_id` must have `status` of `APPROVED`, so a `SUPERSEDED`, `VOIDED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `DRAFT` assessment blocks the trip even though the reference itself resolves cleanly, and the rejection is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED`. Fourth, insurance existence: `insurance_policy_id` must be non-null and the named policy's `status` must be `VERIFIED`, or the submission is rejected with `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_VERIFIED` — this is the check that guarantees the two insurance rules below never have to reason about a null policy. Fifth, insurance scope: no leg's `destination_country_code` may appear in the policy's `excluded_country_codes`, and the policy window must span the trip window, meaning `effective_from` at or before the trip's `depart_at` and `effective_to` at or after the trip's `return_at`; a failure of either is `ERR_INSURANCE_COUNTRY_EXCLUDED`. Sixth, coverage adequacy: the policy's `coverage_cents` must be greater than or equal to the largest `insurance_required_coverage_cents` demanded by the destination ratings of any of the trip's legs, or the submission is rejected with `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. Only after all six does money enter the picture.

The submission then computes the trip's **provisional** cost. That figure is the sum of two components. The first is the total of `cost_cents` over every protective-detail assignment whose `trip_leg_id` names one of this trip's legs and whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`. The second is the total of `engagement_cost_cents` over every vendor engagement whose `trip_leg_id` names one of this trip's legs, whose `incident_id` is null, and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Within each of those two sums, addends are accumulated in ascending `created_at` order. Every addend is already a whole-cent integer, so the total is never re-rounded. Incidents contribute nothing at all to the provisional figure. Cancelled assignments and cancelled engagements contribute nothing because their cost was forced to zero.

That provisional figure is the last gate and the fiscal one: `provisional_security_cost_cents` must be less than or equal to the protectee's `remaining_budget_cents`. Exact equality passes; a one-cent excess is rejected with `ERR_SECURITY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). When it passes, the figure is stored in `provisional_security_cost_cents`, the protectee's `committed_budget_cents` rises by that amount, and `remaining_budget_cents` is re-derived, falling by the same amount. The submission also establishes the communication cadence: `check_in_interval_hours` is derived under the rules of §3.7, and `next_check_in_due_at` is set to the later of the trip's `depart_at` and the current clock reading, plus that many hours.

The decision is where the approval dimension forks. The decider supplies `decision`, either `APPROVE` or `DENY`, and optionally `denied_reason`. A decision is only permitted while `approval_state` is `PENDING_REVIEW` or `PENDING_COMPLIANCE`. The decider must not be the trip's creator, and the acting role must match the stage, both as specified in §2.9. An `APPROVE` decision additionally requires that `briefing_ack_at` is non-null, or it is rejected with `ERR_BRIEFING_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`; a `DENY` decision carries no such requirement, because denying an un-briefed trip is entirely sensible.

The same endpoint and the same payload land in two different places depending on the computed level. A `SecurityDirector` approving a trip in `PENDING_REVIEW` whose `required_approval_level` is `MANAGER` or `DIRECTOR` takes the trip `PENDING_REVIEW → APPROVED` in one hop, and that hop additionally requires that the trip's `readiness_state` is `READY` or `ADVANCE_IN_PROGRESS`. A `SecurityDirector` approving a trip in `PENDING_REVIEW` whose `required_approval_level` is `DIRECTOR_AND_COMPLIANCE` instead takes it `PENDING_REVIEW → PENDING_COMPLIANCE`, where it waits for a second approval. That second hop belongs to a `ComplianceOfficer`, takes the trip `PENDING_COMPLIANCE → APPROVED`, and carries one further precondition of its own: the trip's `cost_allocation_method` must be anything other than `BUSINESS_ONLY`, or if it is `BUSINESS_ONLY` then its `trip_purpose` must be `BUSINESS_TRAVEL` — compliance re-verifies at the last moment that the allocation the manager chose is still legitimate for the purpose recorded.

Whichever route reaches `APPROVED`, the readiness dimension is then reconciled. A trip that arrives at `APPROVED` while every one of its legs has both `advance_complete` of true and `lead_agent_covered` of true moves `ADVANCE_IN_PROGRESS → READY`, and `next_check_in_due_at` is set to the later of the trip's `depart_at` and the current clock reading, plus `check_in_interval_hours`. A trip that arrives at `APPROVED` without full coverage stays where its readiness dimension already was.

A `DENY` decision requires `denied_reason` to be present and 1 to 500 characters long, and it is identical in effect whether it arrives from `PENDING_REVIEW` or from `PENDING_COMPLIANCE`. Hop one moves the trip to `DENIED` — a terminal approval state — and stores the reason. Hop two cancels every `CONFIRMED` protective-detail assignment on every leg of the trip, forcing each `cost_cents` to 0, each `cost_basis` to `VOID`, and each `cancelled_reason` to `TRIP_DENIED`. Hop three restores both ledgers: every affected staff member's `committed_detail_hours` falls by that assignment's `planned_billable_hours`, and the protectee's `committed_budget_cents` falls by the trip's `provisional_security_cost_cents` with `remaining_budget_cents` re-derived, returning the money ledger to exactly the value it held before the submission. The trip's `version` and each cancelled assignment's `version` each increment by one.

One further edge into `PENDING_REVIEW` exists and is never triggered by a caller. When either the assessment-approval cascade or the destination-revision cascade recomputes an approved, non-closed trip's `required_approval_level` and finds it strictly higher than the level in force when the trip was approved — strictly higher under the ordering `MANAGER` below `DIRECTOR` below `DIRECTOR_AND_COMPLIANCE` — the trip is pushed `APPROVED → PENDING_REVIEW`, its `briefing_ack_at` is cleared to null, and its `version` increments. A level that stays the same or falls does not disturb an approved trip.

### 3.7. In-Flight Communications: Check-Ins, Consent Boundaries and Duress

Once a trip is approved and in flight, the protectee is expected to make contact on a cadence the department derives from the risk picture. `check_in_interval_hours` is selected under a pinned precedence: duress first, degradation second, band third. When the trip's `duress_active` is true the interval is 1 hour. Otherwise, when the trip's `readiness_state` is `DEGRADED` the interval is 2 hours. Otherwise the interval comes from `max_leg_risk_band`: 24 hours for `LOW`, 12 hours for `MODERATE`, 8 hours for `HIGH` and 4 hours for `EXTREME`, and a trip whose `max_leg_risk_band` is still null uses the 24-hour figure. In plain terms, a routine low-risk journey reports once a day, an extreme-risk journey six times a day, a degraded journey every two hours, and a journey under duress every hour.

Filing a check-in is exclusively the protectee's own act and is permitted only while the trip's `approval_state` is `APPROVED` and its `readiness_state` is `READY` or `DEGRADED`. The payload carries `kind` — one of `SCHEDULED`, `AD_HOC` or `DURESS` — an optional `location_country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, and an optional `note` of up to 500 characters. `reported_at` is set by the server to the current clock reading and is never supplied by the caller.

The consent boundary is evaluated per check-in, at the instant the check-in is recorded. `consent_scope_snapshot` captures the effective scope: it is `NONE` when the protectee's `consent_expires_at` is non-null and at or before this check-in's `reported_at`, and otherwise it is whatever the protectee's `consent_scope` currently says — `NONE`, `TRIP_ONLY` or `CONTINUOUS`. An expired consent grant therefore behaves exactly like no grant at all. Location retention follows from that snapshot: when `consent_scope_snapshot` is anything other than `NONE`, `recorded_location_country_code` is set to the reported `location_country_code` and `location_withheld` is false; when it is `NONE`, `recorded_location_country_code` is left null and `location_withheld` is set to true — but only if a `location_country_code` was actually supplied. A protectee who reports no location at all under no consent has nothing withheld, so `location_withheld` stays false and both location fields stay null. The reported value is never persisted outside the consent boundary.

Lateness is judged against the due instant that was in force **before** this row is applied. `late` is true exactly when `kind` is `SCHEDULED` and `reported_at` falls strictly after the trip's `next_check_in_due_at` as it stood a moment ago; the comparison is strict, so a scheduled check-in arriving exactly at the due instant is on time. Only scheduled contacts can ever be late — an ad-hoc or duress report is never late by construction. `missed_check_in_count` on the trip is then the number of that trip's check-in rows whose `late` is true.

The due instant is then re-anchored, and how depends on the kind. A `SCHEDULED` check-in sets `next_check_in_due_at` to its own `reported_at` plus the interval currently in force. An `AD_HOC` check-in leaves `next_check_in_due_at` exactly as it was — reporting in early buys no extension. A `DURESS` check-in sets `next_check_in_due_at` to its own `reported_at` plus 1 hour. Check-ins can be listed for a trip, newest `reported_at` first, optionally narrowed by `kind`, and that list is visible to a `ProtectionManager`, `SecurityDirector`, `ComplianceOfficer` and the protectee.

Two missed contacts are the escalation threshold. When `missed_check_in_count` reaches 2, a trip whose `readiness_state` is `READY` moves `READY → DEGRADED`. That degradation edge — wherever it comes from — carries a fixed side-effect set: `check_in_interval_hours` becomes 2 hours, or 1 hour when `duress_active` is true; `next_check_in_due_at` becomes the earlier of its existing value and the current clock reading plus the new interval, so degradation can only pull the next contact forward, never push it back; and the protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count` increments by one. The same edge is reachable from several directions: a leg losing `advance_complete`, a leg losing `lead_agent_covered`, a duress flag being raised, or the missed-contact count reaching 2 — at least one of those conditions must hold for a `READY` trip to degrade.

A duress signal is the gravest thing the protectee can send, and it opens its own chain. Hop one sets the trip's `duress_active` to true; that flag is **never** cleared by any operation in this service. Hop two forces `check_in_interval_hours` to 1 hour and moves the trip `READY → DEGRADED`. Hop three opens an incident automatically: its `incident_type` is `UNREST`, its `severity` is 5, its `duress_flag` is true, and its `affected_leg_sequence_no` is the `sequence_no` of the lowest-numbered leg whose on-ground window contains the current clock reading, or, when no leg's window contains it, simply the lowest `sequence_no` on the trip. The protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count` increments as part of the same chain.

Recovery from degradation is possible but conditional, and it is driven by the operations that fix the underlying defect — recording the outstanding advance work, or creating an assignment that restores lead-agent coverage. A trip moves `DEGRADED → READY` only when all five of these hold: its `readiness_state` is `DEGRADED`, its `approval_state` is `APPROVED`, its `duress_active` is false, its `missed_check_in_count` is strictly below 2, and every one of its legs has both `advance_complete` and `lead_agent_covered` of true. Recovery resets `check_in_interval_hours` to the plain band-derived value with no degradation floor applied, and decrements the protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count` by one. The consequence of the duress condition is deliberate and permanent: because `duress_active` is never cleared, a trip that has ever received a duress check-in can never return to `READY`, and will run on a one-hour cadence until it is closed.

### 3.8. Incident Response: The Triage Fork and its Join

An incident is reported against an approved, in-flight trip — `approval_state` of `APPROVED` and `readiness_state` of `READY` or `DEGRADED`. Either a `ProtectionManager` or a `SecurityDirector` may report one, supplying `affected_leg_sequence_no`, which must be at least 1 and must resolve to an existing leg of that trip; `incident_type`, one of `MEDICAL`, `UNREST` or `NATURAL_DISASTER`; `severity`, an integer from 1 to 5 inclusive; and `narrative`, a free-text description of 1 to 1000 characters. The new incident's `status` is `REPORTED`, its `opened_at` is the current clock reading, its `response_track` is null until triage, and its `duress_flag` is false — that flag is true only for incidents the duress chain opened. Its `triaged_at`, `resolved_at`, `closed_at` are null and its `response_cost_cents` is 0. Merely reporting an incident degrades the trip: the trip moves `READY → DEGRADED`, its `check_in_interval_hours` is re-derived under §3.7's precedence, and the protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count` increments — but only when the trip was not already `DEGRADED`, so the counter is never double-charged for one trip. Incidents can be listed for a trip, newest `opened_at` first, narrowed by `status` and `incident_type`.

Triage is the fork. It requires `status` to be `REPORTED` and takes a single field, `response_track`, which is `MEDEVAC`, `SHELTER_IN_PLACE` or `EXTRACTION`. Which tracks are permitted depends on the nature of the event and, for one of them, on how bad it is: a `MEDICAL` incident permits only `MEDEVAC`; an `UNREST` incident permits `SHELTER_IN_PLACE`, and permits `EXTRACTION` only when its `severity` is 4 or greater; a `NATURAL_DISASTER` incident permits either `MEDEVAC` or `SHELTER_IN_PLACE` but never `EXTRACTION`. An impermissible combination is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_TRACK_INCOMPATIBLE` (HTTP 422).

Every track activates a vendor, and the vendor is chosen by the service rather than named by the caller. The affected leg is the one whose `trip_id` matches the incident's trip and whose `sequence_no` equals the incident's `affected_leg_sequence_no`. The capability required is `MEDICAL_EVAC` on the medical-evacuation track and `GROUND_SECURITY` on both the shelter-in-place and extraction tracks. The activation window runs from the current clock reading for 24 hours, half-open at its far edge. A vendor is a candidate when all four of these hold: its `vendor_type` equals the required capability; its `activation_status` is `AVAILABLE`; its `country_code` equals the affected leg's `destination_country_code`; and the count of that vendor's engagements whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose window overlaps the activation window — meaning the engagement's `starts_at` falls before the window's end and its `ends_at` falls after the window's start — is strictly less than the vendor's `max_concurrent_engagements`. Candidates are then ranked by `sla_response_minutes` ascending, and where those tie by `hourly_rate_cents` ascending, and where those also tie by `retainer_cents` ascending. If the ranking keys tie completely, the earlier `created_at` wins, compared at millisecond precision; the vendor's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. The chosen vendor is the first element of that ordered list. If the candidate set is empty, the whole triage request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR` (HTTP 409): the incident stays `REPORTED`, no engagement is created, and no `version` field anywhere is incremented.

The medical-evacuation branch carries an insurance gate the other two do not. The trip must have a non-null `insurance_policy_id` whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `policy_type` is `MEDICAL_EVAC`, or the triage is rejected with `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`; and that policy's `coverage_cents` must be at least the `insurance_required_coverage_cents` of the affected leg's destination rating. On success the incident moves `REPORTED → MEDEVAC_ACTIVE`, `triaged_at` is set to the current clock reading and `response_track` is stored. A vendor engagement is auto-created against the chosen vendor with `status` of `ACTIVE`, `incident_id` set to this incident, `engagement_purpose` of `MEDICAL_EVAC`, `trip_leg_id` of the affected leg, `starts_at` at the current clock reading and `ends_at` 24 hours later; that vendor's `committed_engagement_hours` rises by the engagement's `engagement_hours`. The trip moves `READY → DEGRADED` and its `check_in_interval_hours` is forced to 2 hours. The incident's, the vendor's and the trip's `version` each increment by one.

The shelter-in-place branch has **no insurance precondition whatsoever** — that absence is the deliberate branch divergence, because sheltering costs the department nothing an insurer would underwrite. The incident moves `REPORTED → SHELTER_ACTIVE`, `triaged_at` and `response_track` are set, and a vendor engagement is auto-created identically except that its `engagement_purpose` is `GROUND_SECURITY`, again running from the current clock reading for 24 hours with `incident_id` set. The trip's `check_in_interval_hours` is forced to 2 hours, its `next_check_in_due_at` is set to the current clock reading plus those 2 hours, and it moves `READY → DEGRADED`. The incident's, the vendor's and the trip's `version` each increment by one.

The extraction branch is the most disruptive. It requires `incident_type` of `UNREST` and `severity` of 4 or greater. The incident moves `REPORTED → EXTRACTION_ACTIVE`, `triaged_at` and `response_track` are set, and a `GROUND_SECURITY` engagement is auto-created on the same 24-hour window with `incident_id` set. Uniquely to this branch, the standing detail is stood down: every protective-detail assignment on the trip's legs whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `starts_at` is strictly after the current clock reading moves `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` with `cost_cents` forced to 0, `cost_basis` set to `VOID` and `cancelled_reason` set to `EXTRACTION`. An assignment whose `starts_at` is at or before the current clock reading is already under way and is left `CONFIRMED` — you do not cancel the agents currently standing next to the principal. The other two tracks leave every assignment untouched. Each affected staff member's `committed_detail_hours` falls out of the labour ledger automatically, because that ledger sums only `CONFIRMED` rows. The protectee's `committed_budget_cents` is deliberately **not** touched here: a trip's provisional commitment is reversed only by a denial or by a close. The trip moves `READY → DEGRADED` and its `check_in_interval_hours` is forced to 2 hours. The incident's, the vendor's, the trip's and each cancelled assignment's `version` each increment by one.

All three branches rejoin at a single edge. Resolving an incident requires its `status` to be `MEDEVAC_ACTIVE`, `SHELTER_ACTIVE` or `EXTRACTION_ACTIVE`, and requires that every vendor engagement whose `incident_id` names this incident has a `status` of `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` — an engagement still `ACTIVE` blocks the join with `ERR_INCIDENT_INVALID_STATE`. The resolver may supply an optional `resolution_note`. The incident moves to `RESOLVED` and `resolved_at` is set to the current clock reading.

Closing an incident is a `SecurityDirector` act, requires `status` of `RESOLVED`, moves the incident `RESOLVED → CLOSED` — its terminal state — sets `closed_at` to the current clock reading, and freezes the response cost. `response_cost_cents` is the total of `engagement_cost_cents` over every vendor engagement whose `incident_id` names this incident and whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, accumulated in ascending `created_at` order, plus a flat evacuation surcharge of 2,500,000 cents — twenty-five thousand dollars — when and only when the incident's `response_track` is `MEDEVAC`. Engagements still `ACTIVE` cannot appear, because the join gate already required them to be settled; cancelled ones contribute nothing, their cost having been forced to zero. The sum is of whole-cent integers plus a whole-cent surcharge, so no rounding occurs.

### 3.9. Settlement: Close, Final Cost and Perquisite Disclosure

Closing a trip is the moment the two state dimensions finally meet, and it is the only place they are gated against each other. A `ProtectionManager` closes a trip whose `readiness_state` is `READY` or `DEGRADED` and whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED`; any other combination is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`. Three further conditions apply. The trip's window must have ended: the current clock reading must be at or after the trip's `return_at`, with an exactly equal instant passing, and a premature close is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_ENDED`. Every incident of the trip must have `status` of `CLOSED`, or the close is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENTS_BLOCK_CLOSE` — an open incident would otherwise be silently omitted from the final cost, which is precisely why the gate exists. And no protective-detail assignment on any of the trip's legs may still be `CONFIRMED`: every one must be `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` so that its final cost basis exists, or the close is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENTS_NOT_SETTLED`. The trip then moves `READY → CLOSED` or `DEGRADED → CLOSED`, and `CLOSED` is terminal.

The close first computes `total_security_cost_cents` on the **final** basis, which is the sum of three components rather than the provisional figure's two. The first is the total of `cost_cents` over every protective-detail assignment on this trip's legs whose `status` is `RELEASED` — the released rows carry their four-hour-floored actual hours, so this component differs from its provisional counterpart whenever any release changed the hours. The second is the total of `engagement_cost_cents` over every vendor engagement on this trip's legs whose `incident_id` is null and whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. Engagements whose `incident_id` is set are deliberately excluded from this component because their cost already sits inside an incident's `response_cost_cents`, and that exclusion is exactly what prevents double counting. The third is the total of `response_cost_cents` over every incident whose `trip_id` is this trip and whose `status` is `CLOSED`. Within each of the three sums, addends are accumulated in ascending `created_at` order; every addend is already a whole-cent integer and the total is never re-rounded. Cancelled assignments and cancelled engagements contribute nothing, and incidents that are not `CLOSED` contribute nothing — which is why the close is gated on all of them being closed.

The perquisite split then runs, in a strictly pinned five-step order, because the tier it uses is keyed on a figure this very trip is about to change. **Step one, pinned first:** read the protectee's `annual_disclosed_perquisite_cents` as it stands right now. This trip is not yet closed, so its own share is not yet inside that figure — call this the prior disclosure. **Step two:** select the tier rate from the prior disclosure alone, never from the post-trip total. A prior disclosure below 5,000,000 cents (fifty thousand dollars) selects a rate of 0.10; a prior disclosure of at least 5,000,000 cents but below 20,000,000 cents (two hundred thousand dollars) selects 0.25; a prior disclosure of 20,000,000 cents or more selects 0.40. The boundaries are inclusive at the lower edge, so a prior disclosure of exactly 5,000,000 cents selects 0.25 while 4,999,999 cents selects 0.10. **Step three:** the trip's `cost_allocation_method` overrides the tier — `BUSINESS_ONLY` yields a rate of 0.00 regardless of the tier, `EXEC_PERQUISITE` yields a rate of 1.00 regardless of the tier, and only `PRORATA` actually uses the tier rate selected in step two. **Step four:** `perquisite_taxable_cents` is `total_security_cost_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half-up to a whole cent; `business_borne_cents` is `total_security_cost_cents` less `perquisite_taxable_cents`; and `perquisite_rate_applied` records the rate actually used, pinned for audit, and is therefore always exactly one of 0.00, 0.10, 0.25, 0.40 or 1.00. **Step five, pinned last:** roll up — the protectee's `annual_disclosed_perquisite_cents` rises by `perquisite_taxable_cents` and their `business_borne_security_cents` rises by `business_borne_cents`.

The dependency here *looks* circular, since the rate is keyed on a cumulative total that this trip feeds. It is resolved by pinning the read in step one strictly before the write in step five, which makes the value well-defined for any single close. A visible consequence follows and is intended: two trips closed in different orders can receive different rates, and the close order is the observable that decides which.

The close also settles the ledgers in one pass. The protectee's `committed_budget_cents` falls by `provisional_security_cost_cents`, their `spent_budget_cents` rises by `total_security_cost_cents`, and `remaining_budget_cents` is re-derived from those two — so a trip that came in under its provisional estimate hands the difference back to the annual budget, and one that came in over consumes more of it. `closed_at` is set to the current clock reading and `next_check_in_due_at` is cleared to null, because a closed trip has no next contact. A trip closed out of `DEGRADED` additionally decrements the protectee's `open_degraded_trip_count` by one.

The disclosure position is available as a computed read, aggregated over that protectee's closed trips only. It returns `executive_id`, `closed_trip_count` — how many of that protectee's trips have reached `CLOSED` — `annual_disclosed_perquisite_cents`, `business_borne_security_cents`, `total_security_cost_cents` as the aggregate protection spend across those closed trips, and `latest_perquisite_rate_applied`, the rate pinned by the most recently closed of them. A protectee may read their own position; a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `SecurityDirector` may read anyone's. This read is the department's answer to the tax question — how much of the year's protection spend is a business expense borne by the company and how much is a disclosable perquisite attributed to the executive — and it is why the close order and the pinned rate matter enough to be specified this precisely.
