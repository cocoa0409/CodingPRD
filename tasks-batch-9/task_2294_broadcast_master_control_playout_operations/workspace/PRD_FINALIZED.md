# Broadcast Master Control API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A television station's master control room is the last place a scheduling mistake can be caught and the first place one becomes visible to the public. Every second of the broadcast day is sold, booked, cleared and logged, and the machinery that reconciles those four views of the same second is currently spread across a traffic system, an automation controller, a quality-control bench, a transmitter telemetry panel and a compliance filing cabinet. This document specifies the API that unifies them: a single service that ingests the traffic system's playlist for a broadcast day, verifies that the media behind every element has actually arrived and passed inspection, drives the automation through cue/start/end events, arbitrates network joins and breakaways against a feed clock the station does not control, carries emergency override authority for crawls and cut-ins, watches the transmission plant, and produces the as-run log against which advertiser orders are settled.

The system is deliberately unforgiving about two things. First, **airtime is a conserved quantity**: seconds of local programming, kilobits per second of transport-stream bandwidth, and units of sold commercial inventory are each tracked as ledgers that must balance before and after every operation, including the compensating operations (rebalances, makegoods, traffic revisions) that reverse an earlier action. Second, **authority is separated**: the quality-control technician who fails a program may not be the person who waives that failure, and the operator who requests a breaking-news cut-in may not be the person who puts it on air. Both properties are enforced at the API boundary rather than by convention, because the consequences of getting them wrong are a make-good invoice, a regulatory filing, or dead air.

Time in this system is explicit. There are no background jobs, no cron sweeps and no reliance on wall-clock time; a server-held **logical clock** is the sole source of "now" for every deadline, guard window, certification expiry and outage duration, and it advances only when a client asks it to. Nothing expires, escalates or auto-clears until the clock moves. Implementers should read every temporal rule in this document as a comparison against that logical clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API surface. Where §3 narrates a workflow, it assumes these rules already hold.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including read-only ones — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which the actor is operating. A request that omits either header, or supplies either as an empty string, is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` before the body is parsed at all, so a request that is both unauthenticated and malformed reports the missing headers rather than the malformed body. The value of `X-User-Role` is matched case-sensitively against exactly seven role names — `MasterControlOperator`, `QCTechnician`, `ProgramDirector`, `BroadcastEngineer`, `TrafficCoordinator`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `StationAdmin` — and anything outside that set is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`. Once the role is recognised, the endpoint's own permitted-role list governs: a recognised role attempting an operation it does not own is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The authorised roles for each endpoint are listed in §4.

Four of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the header identifies a persisted `Operator` record. When `X-User-Role` is `MasterControlOperator`, `QCTechnician`, `ProgramDirector` or `BroadcastEngineer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Operator`; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references an operator compare that resource's operator reference field to `X-User-ID`. If `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing `Operator` row while one of those four roles is claimed, the request fails with `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND`. If the row exists but its `role_class` does not correspond to the claimed role, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — the mapping is fixed: `MASTER_CONTROL` corresponds to `MasterControlOperator`, `QC` to `QCTechnician`, `PROGRAM_DIRECTOR` to `ProgramDirector`, and `ENGINEER` to `BroadcastEngineer`. A genuine operator presenting the wrong role class is stopped at identity verification, not at authorisation.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `TrafficCoordinator`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `StationAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on a `TechnicalFault` or as `recorded_by_user_id` on an as-run entry, where a transition writes it) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Opaque roles skip operator resolution and role-class matching entirely. This has a consequence worth stating plainly: `StationAdmin` may perform every mutating operation in the domain, but because it acts with an opaque identifier it can never satisfy one half of a two-person rule — segregation of duties binds by operator identity, not by role, and an override activation still needs two distinct `Operator` ids.

Two further gates apply to the acting operator once resolved. A suspended operator — one whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` rather than `ACTIVE` — is refused every mutating request with `ERR_OPERATOR_SUSPENDED`, while read endpoints remain available to them. And on the air-critical mutations the operator's certification must be live: `ERR_OPERATOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` is returned when the logical clock has passed `certification_expires_utc`. Certification is treated as valid at exactly `certification_expires_utc` and expired one second later. The certification gate applies to taking a channel to a new air state, driving playlist status, reporting playout events, substituting an element, joining or breaking away from a feed window, creating an emergency override, activating one, releasing one, submitting a QC report, signing a QC waiver, and changing transmitter power state. It is not applied to any read endpoint.

Two roles carry authority that the RBAC list alone does not express, and both are worth calling out here. `QCTechnician` may fail an asset but may never waive its own failure — waiving is a `ProgramDirector` duty. `ComplianceOfficer` is the only role (alongside `StationAdmin`) that files with the regulator, and it can change nothing about power, air state or content. `BroadcastEngineer` owns the transmission plant and the multiplex and holds no programming or trafficking authority; `TrafficCoordinator` owns the traffic-system side — orders, playlist ingest, revisions, expected assets and makegood placement — and never touches the air chain and never authorises anything. `MasterControlOperator` is the default operator on shift and is the only role (alongside `StationAdmin`) that writes the as-run log through playout events, executes feed joins and breakaways, and changes channel air state. `ProgramDirector` is the counter-signature half of every segregation-of-duties pair and additionally holds retraction and waiver authority.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1, and may not be higher than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. Both appear echoed back in the `meta` object of the response envelope alongside `total_count` (the number of records matching the query before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 states otherwise, list endpoints return records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Endpoints whose §4 description names a different ordering — the notification list ordered by newest `triggering_utc`, the playlist list ordered by newest `broadcast_day`, the feed-window list ascending by `join_utc`, the item list in `sequence_number` order, the as-run views in `event_utc` order — follow the ordering stated there.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

There is exactly one notion of "now" in this system and it is a server-held value that clients set explicitly. Before any client touches it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real wall-clock time. Every deadline comparison in this document — delivery deadlines, makegood deadlines, notification due instants, guard windows, fault dwell times, outage durations, certification expiry — is evaluated against this clock.

Advancing the clock is also the system's **ops-run checkpoint**, and it is the only mechanism by which anything expires. There are no background jobs. A clock advance must move strictly forward: a request whose `current_utc` equals or precedes the present clock value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`. When the advance is accepted, the checkpoint performs five sweeps in this pinned order, and the order matters because an earlier sweep can create rows that a later sweep then evaluates within the same call:

1. Every transmitter currently off air whose outage has reached the notification threshold receives an outage filing obligation, at most one per outage epoch.
2. Every notification still awaiting filing whose due instant now lies in the past moves to `OVERDUE`.
3. Every asset still merely expected whose delivery grace window has elapsed moves to `EXPIRED`, dragging its clearance cascade with it.
4. Every fault still `DETECTED`, never acknowledged, of severity `MINOR`, and older than the auto-clear dwell, moves to `AUTO_CLEARED`, with channel posture recomputed.
5. Every operator's `certification_state` is re-derived against the new clock value.

Because sweep 1 can create a filing obligation whose due instant is already in the past when the clock jump is large, sweep 2 must see the rows sweep 1 created. `certification_state` is a derived enum with exactly two values, `VALID` and `EXPIRED`: it reads `VALID` while the logical clock is at or before `certification_expires_utc`, and `EXPIRED` once the clock has moved past it.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Record Versioning

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, refreshed on every mutation and formatted identically, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that record — including mutations the client did not directly request, such as a recomputed derived counter or a state change driven by a cascade from another entity.

Two record types are append-only records of fact rather than mutable resources, and they carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`: an as-run entry and a QC report. Both have `id` and `created_at` only. A QC report is never edited after submission — a later inspection creates a new report and marks the earlier same-basis report as `superseded` — and an as-run entry is never edited or deleted at all, because it is the reconciliation record the station is audited against. Every other entity in the domain (transmitters, multiplexes, channels, operators, assets, playlists, playlist items, spot orders, network feed windows, emergency overrides, technical faults and regulatory notifications) carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Version increments are load-bearing, not cosmetic. When a transmitter power change starves a channel, both the transmitter's `version` and the affected channel's `version` advance; when an override cut-in truncates a playing element, the override, the channel and the item each advance. §3 states, for each transition, which records advance, and §2.6 explains why a client's view of a parent's `version` is sometimes required as request input.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Six write operations mutate a shared parent aggregate in a way that legitimately supersedes whatever the client last read, and each of those six requires the client to state the parent `version` it observed. The field is not optional: omitting it is a missing-required-field failure, and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is rejected before any state precondition is evaluated.

- Ingesting a traffic revision against a playlist requires `expected_playlist_version` to equal the target playlist's current `version`. A revision legitimately supersedes every still-scheduled item of the prior revision, so a writer holding a stale view would silently discard item edits, substitutions or makegood placements it never observed. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAYLIST_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- Placing a makegood spot requires `expected_playlist_version` to equal the target playlist's current `version`. Makegood placement is position-sensitive — it re-derives the hourly commercial inventory map and both timing variances — so a stale view would place the spot against a schedule that no longer exists. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAYLIST_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- Creating a channel requires `expected_multiplex_version` to equal the parent multiplex's current `version`. Every channel creation re-slices the whole bit pool and legitimately supersedes the prior allocation set, so a writer holding a stale view of the multiplex would compute its bitrate expectations against a pool that has already been re-divided. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MULTIPLEX_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- Changing a channel's bitrate demand, floor, priority rank or pinned allocation requires `expected_multiplex_version` to equal the parent multiplex's current `version`, because the new allocation set supersedes the one the client based its request on. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MULTIPLEX_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- Re-running the bitrate waterfall across a multiplex requires `expected_multiplex_version` to equal that multiplex's current `version`. A rebalance legitimately supersedes the standing allocation set, so a stale-view writer would confirm an allocation it never observed. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MULTIPLEX_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- Submitting a QC report requires `expected_asset_version` to equal the target asset's current `version`. A new report legitimately supersedes the prior same-basis report and rewrites the asset verdict, so a stale view would grade media that has since been redelivered, waived or retracted. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none should be invented for them. When a request carries a stale parent version *and* targets a resource whose own state would also reject the operation, the 412 stale-version error is what the client sees — the version check is strictly earlier in the ladder than any state precondition.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Paths in this API are shallow but they are still resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. Each URL segment maps to its own not-found code: a transmitter segment yields `ERR_TRANSMITTER_NOT_FOUND`, a multiplex segment `ERR_MULTIPLEX_NOT_FOUND`, a channel segment `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, an operator segment `ERR_OPERATOR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, an asset segment `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, a QC-report segment `ERR_QC_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, a playlist segment `ERR_PLAYLIST_NOT_FOUND`, a playlist-item segment `ERR_PLAYLIST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, a spot-order segment `ERR_SPOT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, a network-feed-window segment `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, an emergency-override segment `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_FOUND`, a technical-fault segment `ERR_FAULT_NOT_FOUND`, and a regulatory-notification segment `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`. All of these are HTTP 404. Every path parameter in this API is written `{id}` and identifies a row of the entity named by the segment preceding it.

References supplied inside a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved and before any concurrency check runs. They are checked in the declaration order of the endpoint's input list and they reuse exactly the same not-found codes: an unknown `asset_id` inside a submitted revision yields `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `channel_id` yields `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `inspected_by_operator_id`, `waived_by_operator_id`, `requested_by_operator_id` or `authorized_by_operator_id` yields `ERR_OPERATOR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `target_playlist_id` yields `ERR_PLAYLIST_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `spot_order_id` yields `ERR_SPOT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `playlist_item_id` or `replacement_asset_id` or `replaces_item_id` yields the corresponding item or asset code, and an unknown `transmitter_id` or `multiplex_id` yields its own. Body-reference resolution applies to channel creation, playlist ingest, traffic revision, element substitution, feed-window planning, override creation, override activation, fault reporting, spot-order creation, makegood placement, QC report submission and QC waiver signing.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

Every request walks a single ordered ladder of checks. The ladder is evaluated strictly in ascending order; **the first failing check is the whole response and no later check is evaluated**. This ordering is normative — it is not an implementation hint — because several checks can fail simultaneously on the same request and the client must be able to predict which code comes back. The bands, in order, are: authentication (1–4), authorisation (5), actor fitness (6–7), field shape (8–10), path existence (11), body-reference existence (12), optimistic concurrency (13), state preconditions (14–24), safety and regulatory rules (25–34), temporal rules (35–46), structural constraints (47–58), referential consistency (59–62), and finally fiscal and capacity rules (63–67).

Concretely, in priority order: header presence (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`), role recognition (`ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`), operator resolution for entity-backed roles (`ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND`), role-class agreement (`ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`), endpoint authorisation (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`), operator not suspended (`ERR_OPERATOR_SUSPENDED`), operator certification valid (`ERR_OPERATOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`), required fields present (`ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`), enum literals valid (`ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`), types and ranges valid (`ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`), path segments resolve (the per-segment 404 codes of §2.7), body references resolve (the same 404 codes), parent version matches (`ERR_STALE_PLAYLIST_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_MULTIPLEX_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION`), then the per-entity state-transition legality checks, then the safety band, the temporal band, the constraint band, the referential band, and the fiscal band. Within the fiscal band, `ERR_MULTIPLEX_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` is the very last check in the entire ladder.

Four consequences of the ordering are worth pinning down explicitly, because each is a pair of rules that can fail together:

- **Suspension outranks state.** A suspended operator calling a mutation on a resource that is also in the wrong state receives `ERR_OPERATOR_SUSPENDED`, never the state error.
- **Suspended posture outranks capacity.** A request that is both blocked because the target channel's `posture` is `SUSPENDED` and over the multiplex's capacity receives `ERR_CHANNEL_SUSPENDED_POSTURE`, never `ERR_MULTIPLEX_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
- **Feed-window overlap outranks anchor resolution.** A feed-window plan that both overlaps an existing window and names a sequence number matching no anchor element receives `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, never `ERR_ANCHOR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`.
- **Stale version outranks state.** A request carrying a stale parent version against a resource that is also in the wrong state receives the 412 stale-version error, never the 409 state error.

Two more orderings apply inside single features. On the playlist timeline walk, gaps and overlaps share one pass: whichever anomaly appears first in `sequence_number` order determines whether `ERR_SCHEDULE_GAP_DETECTED` or `ERR_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP_DETECTED` is returned. On playout events, an out-of-order timestamp is evaluated before a future timestamp, so an event that is both earlier than the previous as-run entry and later than the logical clock reports `ERR_PLAYOUT_EVENT_OUT_OF_ORDER` rather than `ERR_EVENT_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`. And within the QC waiver rules, the caption safety rule outranks the score threshold: an asset that requires captions, has none, and also scores below the waiver floor reports `ERR_CAPTION_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`, not `ERR_QC_WAIVER_SCORE_TOO_LOW`.

### 2.10. Precision, Rounding and Truncation

Different quantities in this domain round differently, and the differences are deliberate.

- **Bandwidth** is integer kilobits per second throughout. Every division performed while slicing a transport stream is truncated downward to the next whole kilobit; no fractional kilobit ever survives a step, and nothing is ever rounded up.
- **Time** is whole seconds. Every datetime comparison in this document is made at second granularity, and every duration, variance, guard band, dwell and tolerance is a whole number of seconds. Variance figures are signed integers.
- **Money** is whole cents. The per-spot rate is held as an exact rational value while it is being used, but the credit attributable to each individual spot is rounded to whole cents using round-half-up **before** those per-spot figures are added together. The order is pinned: round each item, then sum. Summing exact rationals and rounding the total once at the end is wrong and can differ from the specified result by as much as one cent less than the number of spots involved.
- **Technical scores** are integer points. The one ratio inside the scoring formula is rounded *upward* to the next whole number, and the resulting score is clamped so it is never below 0 and never above 100.
- **Hour indices** in the hourly commercial inventory map are computed by truncating downward and are exposed as decimal strings of a zero-based integer.
- **The backtiming tolerance ratio** is truncated downward to whole seconds. No other value in the backtiming plan is rounded.
- **Invariant assertions** are exact integer equalities with no tolerance whatsoever, in both kilobits and cents and counts.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

Three ledgers must balance at all times, and each has a compensating action that must return it to its exact pre-action value.

**Transport-stream bandwidth.** For every multiplex, the sum of `available_kbps` and `assigned_kbps` equals its `effective_capacity_kbps` — which is itself the nominal `total_capacity_kbps` scaled by the parent transmitter's `derate_factor`, truncated downward, less `pilot_reserved_kbps`, and floored at 0. Equivalently, `assigned_kbps` equals the sum of `allocated_bitrate_kbps` over every channel on that multiplex whose `allocation_state` is `ACTIVE` or `STANDBY`. `pilot_reserved_kbps` sits permanently outside the allocatable pool and never enters `assigned_kbps`. Bandwidth is never created or destroyed by a cascade: a channel driven to `STARVED` contributes exactly 0 kbps and the kilobits it forgoes reappear in `available_kbps`. Admitting a channel as `ACTIVE` and later moving it to `STANDBY` and back, or reversing a power-state change and rebalancing, leaves the identity holding with identical values before and after.

**Sold commercial inventory.** For every spot order, `ordered_spots` equals the sum of `scheduled_spots`, `aired_spots`, `short_aired_spots`, `credited_spots` and `unplaced_spots`. `unplaced_spots` absorbs the difference between `ordered_spots` and the number of playlist items linked to the order — the linked set of §3.9, from which an element a makegood has superseded has already been removed, so that placing a makegood neither grows nor shrinks the count — which means the identity holds even before any playlist has been ingested: at that point everything is unplaced and the other four counters are 0. When a cut-in or a capacity derate preempts one scheduled spot, `scheduled_spots` falls by one, `credited_spots` rises by one, and `credit_accrued_cents` rises by the rounded per-spot figure. When a makegood places that spot again, `credited_spots` falls by one, `scheduled_spots` rises by one, and `credit_accrued_cents` falls by exactly the same rounded per-spot figure — so no rounding drift accumulates across any number of preempt-and-makegood cycles, and the invariant holds before the preemption, between the two operations, and after the makegood.

**Local tail seconds.** For every executed backtiming plan, the pre-plan tail length — the sum of `scheduled_duration_seconds` over items at or after `anchor_sequence_number` whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED` at plan time — equals the tail seconds still scheduled after the plan, plus `backtime_trim_seconds`, plus the summed `scheduled_duration_seconds` of the items whose sequence numbers appear in `skipped_item_sequences`. In parallel, the interval from `plan_computed_utc` to `join_utc` equals the tail seconds still scheduled after the plan, plus `backtime_pad_seconds`, plus `residual_dead_air_seconds`, less `late_join_seconds`. Skipped items keep their `scheduled_duration_seconds` value intact — only their `playout_status` changes — which is what makes the accounting reversible: a trim followed by a traffic revision that reinstates the trimmed elements, restoring each `scheduled_duration_seconds` and resetting `trim_applied_seconds` to 0, returns the pre-plan tail length to its original value exactly. A plan never destroys and never invents scheduled seconds.

### 2.12. Cascade Discipline and Hierarchical Consistency

Four named cascades run through this domain, and their existence is the reason so many operations mutate records the caller never mentioned. **Cascade A** starts at a transmitter power change and runs down through the bit pool: derate, then multiplex capacity, then the channel allocation waterfall, then channel posture and air status, then playlist items, then spot-order credit. **Cascade B** starts at an asset retraction and runs through the elements referencing that asset, the playlists owning those elements, and the spot orders behind any commercial among them. **Cascade B′** is the same shape but starts at an asset expiring for non-delivery, and it stops one hop short: it blocks elements and reverts playlists but does not itself accrue spot credit. **Cascade C** starts at an emergency cut-in and runs through channel air status and posture, the currently playing and imminently scheduled elements, spot-order credit, and the playlist's as-run variance.

Cascades are immediate and synchronous. By the time a response is returned, every derived counter, every dependent state and every ledger figure the operation touched is already consistent — there is no eventual reconciliation and no deferred job. Each record a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, so a client that had read a child before an unrelated parent operation will find its cached version stale.

Cascades are also **not automatically reversible**. Restoring transmitter power re-runs the waterfall and can bring a starved channel back, but items already flipped to `PREEMPTED` are never resurrected. Releasing an override hands the channel back to its playlist, but truncated and preempted elements stay truncated and preempted, and the credits they accrued stay accrued — the makegood placement, not the release, is what compensates the advertiser. Redelivering corrected media does not optimistically unblock the elements that were blocked by the earlier failure; a fresh passing inspection or a waiver is required. These asymmetries are intentional: on-air time that was lost cannot be recovered, only compensated.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Transmission Plant: Power, Outages and Regulatory Filings

A `BroadcastEngineer` registers each radiating facility by supplying its licensed `facility_id` — unique across all transmitters and between 1 and 32 characters — the `call_sign` the facility carries, and its licensed `authorized_erp_kw`, the effective radiated power in kilowatts, which must be greater than zero. A newly registered transmitter starts radiating at full licence: `operational_state` is `FULL_POWER`, `current_power_pct` is 100, `derate_factor` is 1.00, `outage_started_utc` is null, and `last_outage_seconds`, `cumulative_outage_seconds` and `notification_pending_count` are all 0. `current_power_pct` expresses the current operating power as a percentage of `authorized_erp_kw` and is constrained to the range 0 through 100 inclusive.

Changing the plant's power is done by naming a `target_state` and, where the target is a reduced-power operation, a `power_pct`; an off-air transition additionally requires a `reason`, and omitting it is a missing-required-field failure. Only the declared moves are legal, and requesting anything else yields `ERR_TRANSMITTER_INVALID_STATE`: from `FULL_POWER` the plant may go to `REDUCED_POWER` or `OFF_AIR`; from `REDUCED_POWER` it may go to `FULL_POWER`, to `REDUCED_POWER` again (a change of percentage), or to `OFF_AIR`; from `OFF_AIR` it may go to `FULL_POWER`, `REDUCED_POWER`, or remain `OFF_AIR` under the clock checkpoint. Notably, `FULL_POWER` to `FULL_POWER` is not a declared transition and is refused. When the target is `REDUCED_POWER`, `power_pct` must be present and must be at least 1 and strictly below 100.

Reducing power writes `current_power_pct` from the supplied `power_pct` and publishes a new `derate_factor`, which is 0.75 when `power_pct` is at least 50 and 0.50 when it is below 50. It also creates a regulatory obligation when the reduction is deep enough: a reduction to below 80 percent of licensed power creates a `POWER_REDUCTION` notification in status `PENDING`, with `triggering_utc` set to the logical clock instant of the change, `due_utc` set to 24 hours after that instant, and `observed_power_pct` recording the percentage observed at the triggering event; the transmitter's `notification_pending_count` rises by one. The threshold is strict: a reduction to exactly 80 percent creates no filing obligation, while 79 percent does. `notification_pending_count` is by definition the number of notification rows on the transmitter whose `status` is `PENDING` or `OVERDUE`.

Going off air is the harshest operation in the system. It writes `current_power_pct` to 0, `derate_factor` to 0.00, and `outage_started_utc` to the current logical instant, opening an outage epoch. Cascade A then runs with a zero derate: every child multiplex recomputes its `effective_capacity_kbps`, which floors at 0; every channel on every one of those multiplexes is driven to `allocation_state` `STARVED`; each such channel's `posture` becomes `SUSPENDED` and its `air_status` becomes `DARK`; every element of those channels' on-air playlists that has not already reached a terminal playout state is driven to `PREEMPTED`, with the playing element, if any, truncated first; and spot credit is accrued for each affected commercial. The transmitter's `version` advances by one.

Restoring power is guarded. A restoration to `FULL_POWER`, from either `REDUCED_POWER` or `OFF_AIR`, is refused with `ERR_RF_FAULT_BLOCKS_RESTORE` while any technical fault on that transmitter has `fault_code` `RF_POWER_FAULT` and a `state` of `DETECTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `ESCALATED`. A clean restoration writes `current_power_pct` to 100 and `derate_factor` to 1.00 and re-runs the bitrate waterfall across every child multiplex: channels that had been `STARVED` and can now be granted their floor return to `allocation_state` `ACTIVE`, channels that had been held as `STANDBY` return to `STANDBY`, and every affected channel's `posture` is recomputed. Channels leaving `SUSPENDED` posture have `air_status` restored from `BARS_AND_TONE` to `ON_AIR`, but only when the channel's playlist is itself `ON_AIR`; the restoration is conditional and elements already `PREEMPTED` are not resurrected. The transmitter's `version` advances by one.

Leaving `OFF_AIR` for either `FULL_POWER` or `REDUCED_POWER` closes the outage epoch. `last_outage_seconds` is written as the whole number of seconds between `outage_started_utc` and the current logical instant, that figure is added to `cumulative_outage_seconds` — which is the running sum over every closed outage epoch on the transmitter — and `outage_started_utc` is cleared to null. If the epoch just closed lasted at least 30 minutes and no `OUTAGE` notification already exists for that epoch, one is created in status `PENDING` with `triggering_utc` set to the epoch's `outage_started_utc`, `due_utc` set to 24 hours after the restoration instant, and `outage_seconds` set to `last_outage_seconds`; an epoch of exactly 30 minutes does create the filing, an epoch of 29 minutes and 59 seconds does not. Independently, every restoration from `OFF_AIR` creates a `RESUMPTION` notification in status `PENDING` with `triggering_utc` at the restoration instant and `due_utc` 24 hours later. Restoring to `REDUCED_POWER` then sets `derate_factor` from the supplied `power_pct` under the same 50-percent rule and re-runs the waterfall on every child multiplex.

An outage that is still running can also cross the threshold without anyone restoring power, and this is what the clock checkpoint's first sweep is for. When the clock advances such that a transmitter still in `OFF_AIR` with a non-null `outage_started_utc` has been dark for at least 30 minutes, and no `OUTAGE` notification exists whose `triggering_utc` equals that `outage_started_utc`, the checkpoint creates one — status `PENDING`, `triggering_utc` equal to `outage_started_utc`, `due_utc` 24 hours after the new clock instant, and `outage_seconds` equal to the elapsed seconds observed at detection — raises `notification_pending_count`, and advances the transmitter's `version`. Exactly one `OUTAGE` row is ever created per outage epoch; the epoch is keyed on `outage_started_utc` and a second row for the same epoch is never created. This transmitter-to-itself move is the only `OFF_AIR` to `OFF_AIR` transition and it has no actor other than the clock.

Filing is a `ComplianceOfficer` duty. A filing supplies `filed_reference`, the regulator-assigned reference, and `narrative`, the filing text; either being absent yields `ERR_FILING_DETAILS_REQUIRED`. The request may optionally carry `filed_utc`, which like every supplied timestamp must not exceed the logical clock or the filing is refused with `ERR_EVENT_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`; the recorded `filed_utc` is the logical clock instant at which the filing was accepted. A notification whose `status` is `PENDING` becomes `FILED` when the logical clock is at or before `due_utc` — the boundary instant counts as on time — and becomes `LATE_FILED` when the clock has passed `due_utc`. A notification the checkpoint has already swept to `OVERDUE` becomes `LATE_FILED`. Either terminal outcome decrements the transmitter's `notification_pending_count` and advances the notification's `version` by one. The filing endpoint evaluates lateness for itself rather than depending on the checkpoint having run, so a `PENDING` notification filed after its `due_utc` lands directly in `LATE_FILED`. Filing against a notification already `FILED` or `LATE_FILED` is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_INVALID_STATE`.

The checkpoint's second sweep is the counterpart: every notification still `PENDING` whose `due_utc` now lies strictly before the new clock instant flips to `OVERDUE` and its `version` advances by one. The notification catalogue for a facility can be read newest `triggering_utc` first and narrowed by `status` or by `kind`. Two field-population rules distinguish the kinds: `observed_power_pct` is populated only for `POWER_REDUCTION` rows and is null for `OUTAGE` and `RESUMPTION` rows, while `outage_seconds` is populated for `OUTAGE` and `RESUMPTION` rows and is null for `POWER_REDUCTION` rows. `filed_reference`, `narrative` and `filed_utc` are all null until the filing is recorded.

### 3.2. The Bit Pool: Multiplex Capacity and the Channel Bitrate Waterfall

Each transport stream is modelled as a multiplex hanging off a transmitter. Creating one names the carrying `transmitter_id`, an operator-facing `name`, and the nominal payload capacity `total_capacity_kbps`, which must be at least 1000. Two further inputs are optional with defaults: `pilot_reserved_kbps`, the kilobits permanently set aside for PSIP tables and null padding, defaults to 500 and must be at least 0 and strictly less than `total_capacity_kbps`; and `live_encoder_units_total`, the number of live-encoder units the multiplex can lend to feed windows, defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. Reserved pilot bandwidth is never allocatable to a channel.

The pool a multiplex can actually offer is `effective_capacity_kbps`, and it is derived rather than supplied: take `total_capacity_kbps`, multiply by the parent transmitter's current `derate_factor`, truncate the product downward to a whole kilobit, subtract `pilot_reserved_kbps`, and floor the result at 0. `derate_factor` itself is one of exactly four values published by the transmitter's RF state — 1.00 while the transmitter is at `FULL_POWER`; 0.75 while it is at `REDUCED_POWER` with `current_power_pct` at least 50; 0.50 while it is at `REDUCED_POWER` with `current_power_pct` below 50; and 0.00 while it is `OFF_AIR`. The station therefore has two structurally different pools: a full pool, where the whole nominal capacity less pilot reservation is available, and a derated pool, where the shrunken capacity starves the tail of the priority order even though no channel changed its own request.

A `BroadcastEngineer` admits channels into that pool. Admission names the target `multiplex_id`, the `expected_multiplex_version` discussed in §2.6, the `call_sign` (for example WXYZ-DT2), the virtual `channel_number` written as major and minor parts separated by a dot — one to three digits, a dot, then one or two digits, for example 12.2 — the `service_class`, the `resolution_class`, the `requested_bitrate_kbps` the channel asks for, the `floor_bitrate_kbps` below which the channel cannot be carried at all, the `priority_rank` where 1 is the highest priority, and, for a subchannel, the `parent_channel_id` of the primary it hangs off. `channel_number` is unique across the entire station and across all multiplexes, so two subchannels can never both claim 5.2; a collision yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_NUMBER`. `floor_bitrate_kbps` must itself be at least 100 and `priority_rank` at least 1.

The self-referential shape of the channel graph is checked in four ways. A `service_class` of `SUBCHANNEL` submitted without a `parent_channel_id` yields `ERR_SUBCHANNEL_PARENT_REQUIRED`; a `service_class` of `PRIMARY` submitted *with* a `parent_channel_id` yields `ERR_PRIMARY_CHANNEL_PARENT_FORBIDDEN` — which of the two codes applies is selected by the submitted `service_class`. The parent chain is exactly one level deep: `parent_channel_id` must reference a channel whose own `service_class` is `PRIMARY`, so a subchannel may not parent another subchannel and no channel may reference itself; a violation yields `ERR_INVALID_CHANNEL_PARENT`. And the parent must sit on the same multiplex as the `multiplex_id` supplied in the body, because a subchannel cannot hang off a primary carried by another transmitter's transport stream; a cross-stream parent yields `ERR_CROSS_MULTIPLEX_PARENT`. A `PRIMARY` channel carries a null `parent_channel_id`.

Two bitrate shape rules apply both at admission and whenever the demand is later changed. `floor_bitrate_kbps` may not exceed `requested_bitrate_kbps`; equality is legal and makes the channel an all-or-nothing claimant in the waterfall, while a floor above the request yields `ERR_BITRATE_FLOOR_EXCEEDS_REQUEST`. And `floor_bitrate_kbps` must be at least the minimum for its `resolution_class` — 8000 for `HD_1080I`, 5000 for `HD_720P`, 2000 for `SD_480I` — where exactly the minimum is legal and one kilobit below it is not, yielding `ERR_BITRATE_FLOOR_BELOW_RESOLUTION_MINIMUM`.

Whenever a channel is admitted, whenever its demand is changed, whenever the multiplex is rebalanced, and whenever a transmitter power change republishes a `derate_factor`, the whole pool is re-sliced by a **five-step waterfall whose evaluation order is pinned**. The apparent circularity — allocation depends on the remainder, which depends on the granted floors, which depend on the capacity, which depends on the derate — is well defined precisely because the steps run in this order and each later step reads only completed results of earlier ones.

*Step one* establishes `effective_capacity_kbps` from the derate as described above. *Step two* grants floors in strict waterfall order. The candidate set is every channel on this multiplex whose `allocation_state` is `ACTIVE`, `STANDBY` or `STARVED` — starved channels deliberately re-enter the waterfall on every run so that recovery is possible — sorted by `priority_rank` ascending and, for channels sharing a rank, by `created_at` ascending. A channel's own `id` is never consulted as a tiebreaker. Channels on any other multiplex never participate. Walking that order with a running floor total that starts at zero: if the running total plus this channel's `floor_bitrate_kbps` is less than or equal to `effective_capacity_kbps`, the channel is granted, its floor is added to the running total, and its `allocation_state` is set to `STANDBY` if it was already `STANDBY` and to `ACTIVE` otherwise. An exact fit — running total plus floor equal to the capacity — is granted. If the floor does not fit, the channel's `allocation_state` becomes `STARVED`, its `allocated_bitrate_kbps` is set to 0, and it is excluded from steps three through five entirely.

*Step three* distributes the remainder by weight. The remainder is `effective_capacity_kbps` less the running floor total. Each granted channel's weight is its `requested_bitrate_kbps` less its `floor_bitrate_kbps`, a quantity that is never negative given the floor-versus-request rule. If the total weight across granted channels is zero, every channel's share is zero. Otherwise each granted channel's share is the smaller of two figures: the remainder multiplied by that channel's weight and divided by the total weight with the quotient truncated downward, and the channel's own weight. Each granted channel's `allocated_bitrate_kbps` is then its `floor_bitrate_kbps` plus its share.

*Step four* sweeps up what truncation left behind, one kilobit at a time. The leftover is the remainder less the sum of the shares just granted. While leftover kilobits remain, the granted channels whose `allocated_bitrate_kbps` is still strictly below their `requested_bitrate_kbps` are gathered in the same deterministic order — `priority_rank` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — and each in turn receives one additional kilobit until either the leftover reaches zero or no under-served channel remains. If no channel is still under its request, the sweep stops and the residual kilobits remain unassigned, surfacing in `available_kbps`.

*Step five* rolls the pool up. `effective_capacity_kbps` is written to the multiplex; `assigned_kbps` is the sum of `allocated_bitrate_kbps` over the candidate channels whose `allocation_state` ended as `ACTIVE` or `STANDBY`; `available_kbps` is `effective_capacity_kbps` less `assigned_kbps`; and `starved_channel_count` is the number of candidates that ended as `STARVED`. The bandwidth invariant of §2.11 is asserted after every single run of this waterfall.

Two fiscal-band gates guard the waterfall's feasibility. If the sum of `floor_bitrate_kbps` over the channels that would have to be `ACTIVE` exceeds `effective_capacity_kbps`, no feasible allocation exists and the request is refused with `ERR_BITRATE_FLOOR_UNSATISFIABLE`; a floor sum exactly equal to the effective capacity is feasible, since every channel then gets precisely its floor and the remainder pool is zero. Separately, an explicit pin — `pin_allocated_kbps` on a demand change — or an admission that must be accepted as `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_MULTIPLEX_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when the resulting pinned total would exceed the multiplex's `effective_capacity_kbps`; a total exactly equal to the effective capacity is accepted. This is the final check in the entire validation ladder.

Changing a channel's demand after admission is done by supplying `expected_multiplex_version` together with any of `requested_bitrate_kbps`, `floor_bitrate_kbps`, `priority_rank` or `pin_allocated_kbps`, and it re-runs the waterfall for the whole multiplex, not just for the channel named. Rebalancing is the blunter instrument: it supplies `expected_multiplex_version` and, optionally, `clear_pins` to discard any pinned allocations, and re-runs the waterfall across every channel on the multiplex. An accepted pin is remembered on the channel and re-applied on every later run of the waterfall until it is discarded, and its effect on the five steps is narrow: the pinned channel still competes for its floor in step two on its `floor_bitrate_kbps` like any other candidate and still takes a weighted share in step three, it is **excluded from the one-kilobit sweep of step four** so that leftover kilobits go only to unpinned under-served channels, and then, if step two granted it, its `allocated_bitrate_kbps` is overwritten with the pinned figure before the step-five rollup reads it. That overwrite is where an accepted pin becomes observable, because `pin_allocated_kbps` is an input on the demand change and is **not itself projected as an attribute of the channel**; a pin above the channel's own request or below its own floor is honoured exactly as given. A pinned channel that step two starves keeps its pin but is allocated 0 like any other starved channel. `clear_pins` discards the remembered pin outright, and the run of the waterfall it triggers allocates that channel from its floor and its request as though it had never been pinned. **Rebalancing the multiplex is the only way a channel leaves `STARVED`** — starvation is never cleared by the channel itself, and a channel driven `STARVED` by a derate stays starved until either the multiplex is rebalanced or the transmitter's power is restored and the resulting cascade re-runs the waterfall. A freshly admitted channel begins with `allocated_bitrate_kbps` at 0 before the waterfall runs, `air_status` `DARK`, and `posture` `NORMAL`. Channels can be listed narrowed by `multiplex_id`, by `parent_channel_id` to see the subchannels of a primary, by `posture` or by `air_status`; multiplexes can be listed narrowed by `transmitter_id`.

### 3.3. Channel Air State, Posture and Technical Fault Response

A channel carries two independent status dimensions that must not be confused. `air_status` is what the transmitter is radiating for that virtual channel and is changed deliberately by a `MasterControlOperator`; `posture` is a derived health summary that nobody sets directly and that gates downstream operations.

`air_status` begins at `DARK`. A `MasterControlOperator` moves it by supplying `target_air_status` and, optionally, a free-text `reason`. The reachable moves are exactly these: `DARK` to `ON_AIR`; `ON_AIR` to `BARS_AND_TONE`; `BARS_AND_TONE` back to `ON_AIR`; `ON_AIR` to `EAS_OVERRIDE` and `EAS_OVERRIDE` back to `ON_AIR`, both of which are reached only by the emergency-override cascade; `ON_AIR` to `DARK`; and `BARS_AND_TONE` to `DARK`. No state is terminal. A `target_air_status` that is not reachable from the channel's present `air_status` is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_INVALID_AIR_STATE`, and **`EAS_OVERRIDE` is never reachable through the air-state endpoint at all** — asking for it there is refused with the same code even when the channel is `ON_AIR`, because that state belongs exclusively to the override cascade of §3.8.

Going to air is conditional on four things being simultaneously true, and each has its own refusal code. The channel's `allocation_state` must be `ACTIVE`, and its `allocated_bitrate_kbps` must be at least its `floor_bitrate_kbps`; either failure is reported as `ERR_CHANNEL_ALLOCATION_STARVED`, so a `STARVED` or `STANDBY` channel cannot be lit and a channel whose allocation was pinned below its own floor cannot be lit either. The parent transmitter's `operational_state` must not be `OFF_AIR`, a cross-entity dependency that reads through the channel's multiplex to the transmitter, and a dark transmitter yields `ERR_TRANSMITTER_OFF_AIR`. And the channel's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, which yields `ERR_CHANNEL_SUSPENDED_POSTURE`. The same four preconditions apply when returning from `BARS_AND_TONE` to `ON_AIR`, and that return carries one extra condition the initial lighting does not: there must be no technical fault on the channel whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `state` is `DETECTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `ESCALATED`, otherwise the restoration is refused with `ERR_CRITICAL_FAULT_OPEN`. A critical fault therefore pins a channel to bars-and-tone until it is mitigated, while it does not by itself prevent a dark channel being brought up — the asymmetry between the two ON_AIR-bound transitions is deliberate.

Dropping to `BARS_AND_TONE` has no preconditions at all and can be commanded at any time, as it is the safe fallback. It interrupts whatever is playing: every item of the channel's on-air playlist whose `playout_status` is `PLAYING` flips to `TRUNCATED` with its `measured_duration_seconds` set to the whole seconds between its `actual_start_utc` and the current logical time, and a truncation row is appended to the as-run log for each. Going `DARK` from `ON_AIR` is the harsher form: any item whose `playout_status` is `PLAYING` is first truncated exactly as above, and then every item whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CUED` or `PLAYING` flips to `PREEMPTED`, with as-run rows appended for each. Going `DARK` from `BARS_AND_TONE` interrupts nothing, since nothing is playing. An operator may not take a channel `DARK` while an emergency override on that channel is in state `ACTIVE_CRAWL` or `ACTIVE_CUTIN`; the attempt is refused with `ERR_EAS_OVERRIDE_ACTIVE`. **The system cascade bypasses that check** — when a transmitter goes off air, its channels are driven `DARK` regardless of any active override, because there is nothing left to radiate the alert on. Every air-state change recomputes `posture` and increments the channel's `version` by one.

`posture` is recomputed, never supplied, and it is recomputed on a deliberately wide set of triggers: whenever a technical fault is reported, whenever a fault changes state, whenever an emergency override is activated or released, whenever a transmitter's power state changes, whenever a channel's air state changes, whenever a multiplex is rebalanced, whenever a channel's allocation is changed, and on every advance of the logical clock. The computation examines the channel's open faults — defined as exactly those technical faults on the channel whose `state` is `DETECTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `ESCALATED`; faults in `MITIGATED`, `RESOLVED` or `AUTO_CLEARED` never count — and its active overrides, defined as exactly those emergency overrides on the channel whose `state` is `ACTIVE_CRAWL` or `ACTIVE_CUTIN`; overrides in `PENDING`, `RELEASED` or `REJECTED` do not count.

The five clauses are evaluated in a fixed order and **the first one that matches wins**, so a channel that satisfies several of them takes the earliest. First, `posture` is `SUSPENDED` if the channel's `allocation_state` is `STARVED` or its transmitter's `operational_state` is `OFF_AIR`. Otherwise, second, it is `EMERGENCY` if the channel has an active override. Otherwise, third, it is `DEGRADED_CRITICAL` if any open fault has `severity` `CRITICAL`. Otherwise, fourth, it is `DEGRADED` if the number of open faults with `severity` `MAJOR` is at least two, or if the transmitter's `operational_state` is `REDUCED_POWER`; **exactly two major faults already degrade the channel**, one does not, and reduced transmitter power degrades every channel it carries on its own with no faults at all. Otherwise, fifth, it is `NORMAL`. In the same pass, `open_fault_count` is written as the total number of open faults on the channel regardless of severity. A starved channel whose transmitter is also off air is `SUSPENDED` once, not twice; a channel with an active override and an open critical fault reports `EMERGENCY`, and only once that override is released does the underlying `DEGRADED_CRITICAL` become visible.

Faults are reported by a `MasterControlOperator` or a `BroadcastEngineer` supplying a `fault_code`, a `severity`, the `detected_utc` at which the condition was observed, and at least one target: a `channel_id`, a `transmitter_id`, a `playlist_item_id`, plus optional `notes`. `detected_utc` may not lie in the future relative to the logical clock, which yields `ERR_EVENT_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`. Targeting is checked coherently: when both `channel_id` and `playlist_item_id` are supplied, the item must belong to a playlist of that channel; when `transmitter_id` is supplied, it must be the transmitter carrying that channel's multiplex; a fault coded `RF_POWER_FAULT` or `LOSS_OF_SIGNAL` requires a `transmitter_id` because those conditions are properties of the plant rather than of a service; and a fault coded `AUDIO_LOSS`, `BLACK_FRAME` or `CAPTION_LOSS` requires a `channel_id` because those are observed on a service. Any of those mismatches yields `ERR_FAULT_TARGET_MISMATCH`. A new fault is created in `state` `DETECTED`, immediately increments the channel's `open_fault_count`, and immediately recomputes `posture`, which may drive the channel to `DEGRADED`, `DEGRADED_CRITICAL` or, through no path of its own, leave `SUSPENDED` untouched. When the fault names a `playlist_item_id`, it also becomes an input to the air-check scoring of that item's asset described in §3.4.

Although `severity` may be supplied, the station also maintains a canonical severity per fault code, and the derived form maps `AUDIO_LOSS` and `BLACK_FRAME` to `MAJOR`, `CAPTION_LOSS` and `AUTOMATION_STALL` to `MINOR`, `LOSS_OF_SIGNAL` and `ENCODER_FAILURE` to `CRITICAL`, and `RF_POWER_FAULT` to `MAJOR`; that derived severity is then raised one step when the fault's `duration_seconds` has reached 120 seconds or when the fault has reached `ESCALATED`.

A fault advances by `target_state`, optionally carrying a `mitigation_code`, a `cleared_utc` and `notes`. The reachable moves are `DETECTED` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` or to `AUTO_CLEARED`; `ACKNOWLEDGED` to `ESCALATED` or to `MITIGATED`; `ESCALATED` to `MITIGATED`; and `MITIGATED` to `RESOLVED` or back to `DETECTED`. `RESOLVED` and `AUTO_CLEARED` are terminal. A recurrence — moving a `MITIGATED` fault back to `DETECTED` — is legal and puts the fault back among the open faults; reopening a `RESOLVED` fault is not legal. Any unreachable move is refused with `ERR_FAULT_INVALID_STATE`.

Acknowledging a fault writes the acknowledging user's identifier into `acknowledged_by_user_id`, recomputes `duration_seconds` and `severity`, and recomputes the channel's `posture` — the open-fault counts are unchanged by acknowledgement, but the severity may have escalated with elapsed duration, so the posture can worsen without any new fault. Escalating requires that `duration_seconds` has reached at least 120 seconds; **at exactly 120 seconds escalation is permitted**, and below that it is refused with `ERR_FAULT_ESCALATION_TOO_EARLY`. Escalation raises `severity` one step, from `MINOR` to `MAJOR` and from `MAJOR` to `CRITICAL`, with `CRITICAL` staying `CRITICAL`, and it recomputes `posture`, which may flip the channel to `DEGRADED_CRITICAL` and thereby block playlist arming as described in §3.5.

Mitigating a fault, whether from `ACKNOWLEDGED` or from `ESCALATED`, requires a `mitigation_code`; its absence yields `ERR_MITIGATION_CODE_REQUIRED`. The two source states behave identically. The code is written to the fault, and when that code is `BARS_AND_TONE` and the channel's `air_status` is currently `ON_AIR`, the mitigation itself drives the channel from `ON_AIR` to `BARS_AND_TONE`, carrying that transition's own consequence — the currently playing item is truncated and an as-run truncation row is appended. Resolving a mitigated fault writes `cleared_utc` at the current logical time and freezes `duration_seconds` at the elapsed interval, decrements the channel's `open_fault_count` and recomputes `posture`, which may return the channel from `DEGRADED_CRITICAL` or `DEGRADED` to `NORMAL`. When the resolved fault named a `playlist_item_id`, the air-check variant of the asset technical score becomes recomputable for that item's asset.

Faults also age out on their own, but only narrowly. When the logical clock advances past a fault's `detected_utc` plus 900 seconds, that fault moves from `DETECTED` to `AUTO_CLEARED` — but only if it is still in `DETECTED` and was never acknowledged, and only if its `severity` is `MINOR`. The auto-clear writes `cleared_utc` at the current logical time, decrements `open_fault_count` and recomputes `posture`. **Major and critical faults never auto-clear and must be worked by hand**, and a fault that an operator acknowledged is likewise excluded from ageing out no matter how long it sits. Every fault transition increments the fault's `version`, and those that change the channel increment the channel's `version` as well. Faults can be listed narrowed by `channel_id`, by `transmitter_id`, by `playlist_item_id`, by `state` or by `severity`.

### 3.4. Operators, Certification, and the Content Acquisition and Quality Control Chain

#### Operator records and certification

The station keeps an operator record for every person who can touch air, distinct from the request headers that identify them. A `StationAdmin` creates one with the person's `full_name`, the `role_class` that fixes which role header that person is entitled to present, the `override_authority` that fixes which emergency modes they may authorize, and the `certification_expires_utc` that is the last instant at which their master-control certification is valid. `certification_state` is never supplied: it reads `VALID` while the logical clock is at or before `certification_expires_utc` and `EXPIRED` once the clock has passed it, so **an expiry instant exactly equal to the current logical time is still valid and one second later is expired**. It is re-derived when the operator is created, whenever the operator record is amended, and at every advance of the logical clock. `status` starts `ACTIVE`. A `StationAdmin` may later suspend or reinstate an operator, or extend their certification, by supplying any of `status`, `certification_expires_utc` or `override_authority`; each such change re-derives `certification_state`.

Certification is enforced against the acting user, not against a body field, and it is enforced only on the air-critical mutations: changing a channel's air state, driving a playlist's state, recording a playout event, substituting an item, joining or breaking away from a network feed, creating, activating or releasing an emergency override, submitting a QC report, granting a QC waiver, and changing a transmitter's power state. An expired certification on any of those yields `ERR_OPERATOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. Read operations are never gated on certification. Any operator identifier supplied in a path or a body — inspector, requester, authorizer, waiver signer — that does not resolve to an existing operator yields `ERR_OPERATOR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`.

#### Registering and receiving assets

An asset is registered before it arrives. A `TrafficCoordinator` or `QCTechnician` supplies the `house_number` that traffic and master control both use to refer to the media, the `title`, the `source`, the `expected_duration_seconds` the schedule has been built around, the `delivery_deadline_utc` by which the file must land, the `required_resolution_class` the channel needs, and `captions_required`. `house_number` is unique across every asset ever registered **including retracted and expired ones — a house number is never recycled**, and a collision yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_HOUSE_NUMBER`. The asset is created in `status` `EXPECTED`.

Recording delivery supplies `received_utc`, the `measured_duration_seconds` of the file as it actually arrived, the `video_codec`, the `delivered_resolution_class`, the `frame_rate`, the `audio_loudness_lkfs`, the `audio_channel_config`, whether a `caption_present` track exists and, optionally, its `caption_language`. Delivery is legal from `EXPECTED` and from `QC_FAILED`, the latter being the redelivery of corrected media; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. `measured_duration_seconds` must be at least 1.

Lateness is recorded rather than punished, up to a point. The station allows a delivery grace of 3600 seconds — one hour — beyond the deadline. `late_delivery` is written as true when `received_utc` is strictly after `delivery_deadline_utc` and false otherwise, so **a `received_utc` exactly at the deadline is on time and carries `late_delivery` false, one second past the deadline is late but still accepted, and the deadline plus 3600 seconds is the last instant that is accepted at all**. A delivery attempted once the logical clock has moved beyond the deadline plus that one-hour grace is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_DEADLINE_PASSED`, because by then the clock checkpoint will have swept the asset to `EXPIRED`; and a delivery whose `received_utc` itself lies beyond that grace is refused with the same code even if the sweep has not yet run. `received_utc` may not lie in the future of the logical clock, which yields `ERR_EVENT_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`.

A successful delivery writes `received_utc` and all the delivered format fields, sets `late_delivery`, and **resets `technical_score_ingest`, `technical_score_aircheck` and `qc_verdict` to null** — a redelivery therefore discards the previous grading entirely. On redelivery from `QC_FAILED` the delivered format fields and `received_utc` are overwritten, and items that were blocked because of this asset **stay blocked** until a new passing report or a waiver is recorded; there is no optimistic unblocking on the strength of a fresh file alone. An asset may loop from `QC_FAILED` to `RECEIVED` and back any number of times while the grace window holds.

When the logical clock advances past `delivery_deadline_utc` plus 3600 seconds and the asset is still `EXPECTED`, it moves to `EXPIRED`, which is terminal, and its expiry propagates: every playlist item referencing that asset whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED` has its `clearance_status` flipped to `BLOCKED`, and, as a consequence of that flip, each owning playlist increments `blocked_item_count` and, when its `status` is `VALIDATED` or `ARMED`, reverts to `IMPORTED` with `armed_at_utc` cleared.

#### Quality control scoring

A QC report grades one asset. A `QCTechnician` supplies `expected_asset_version` for the concurrency check, the `inspected_by_operator_id` of the inspecting operator, the `basis` of the inspection, the list of `defect_codes` observed, the `measured_duration_seconds` this inspection measured, and optionally an `audio_loudness_lkfs` reading and free-text `notes`. `inspected_by_operator_id` must reference an operator whose `role_class` is `QC` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a wrong role class yields `ERR_OPERATOR_ROLE_CLASS_INVALID`. An ingest-basis report requires the asset to be in `RECEIVED`; an air-check-basis report requires the asset to be in `QC_PASSED` or `QC_WAIVED`; either mismatch yields `ERR_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. A report record carries only its own identifier and creation timestamp as housekeeping fields — it has no independent version or update stamp, because a report is never edited, only superseded.

The technical score starts from 100 and subtracts four penalties, each computed independently, with the result floored at 0 and never exceeding 100. All arithmetic is in whole points.

The *defect penalty* is the sum of the per-defect point values over the **distinct** defect codes in the report, so a code listed twice is counted once: `LOUDNESS_OUT_OF_SPEC` costs 12, `AUDIO_LOSS` costs 25, `BLACK_FRAMES` costs 18, `CAPTION_MISSING` costs 20, `CAPTION_MISTIMED` costs 8, `FORMAT_MISMATCH` costs 15, and `VIDEO_ARTIFACT` costs 10.

The *duration penalty* compares the report's `measured_duration_seconds` with the asset's `expected_duration_seconds`. Let the discrepancy be the absolute difference in seconds. If that discrepancy is 2 seconds or less the penalty is zero — **exactly 2 seconds is inside tolerance**. Otherwise the discrepancy is divided by two, that quotient is rounded *upward* to the next whole number, the result is multiplied by 3, and the penalty is the smaller of that product and a cap of 30 points. A 3-second discrepancy therefore costs 6 points, a 4-second discrepancy costs 6 points, a 5-second discrepancy costs 9, and any discrepancy of 20 seconds or more costs the capped 30.

The *loudness penalty* is a flat 12 points when the asset's `audio_loudness_lkfs` is present and its absolute distance from the station target of -24.0 LKFS exceeds 2.0 LKFS, and zero otherwise. A reading of -26.0 or -22.0 is exactly on tolerance and costs nothing; -26.1 or -21.9 costs the 12 points. A null loudness reading costs nothing.

The *fault penalty* exists only for air-check reports and is forced to zero for ingest reports. For an air-check report it is the sum of per-fault-code point values over the technical faults whose `state` is `RESOLVED` and whose `playlist_item_id` names an item that references this asset and whose `playout_status` is `PLAYED` or `TRUNCATED`; the sum is then capped at 40 points. The per-code values are 25 for `AUDIO_LOSS`, 18 for `BLACK_FRAME`, 20 for `CAPTION_LOSS`, 30 for `LOSS_OF_SIGNAL`, 30 for `ENCODER_FAILURE`, 10 for `RF_POWER_FAULT` and 8 for `AUTOMATION_STALL`. Faults in any other state, faults attached to items that never aired, and faults with no `playlist_item_id` at all contribute nothing.

The verdict follows from the score together with three absolute disqualifiers. The verdict is `FAIL` if the score is below 70, **or** if any of the report's distinct defect codes is `AUDIO_LOSS` or `FORMAT_MISMATCH`, which are the non-negotiable defects, **or** if the asset's `captions_required` is true while its `caption_present` is false, **or** if the asset's `delivered_resolution_class` differs from its `required_resolution_class`. Otherwise the verdict is `PASS_WITH_EXCEPTION` if the score is below 85, and `PASS` if it is 85 or above. **A score of exactly 70 is `PASS_WITH_EXCEPTION` and a score of exactly 85 is `PASS`**, but a perfect 100 with a single `AUDIO_LOSS` defect code is still a `FAIL`, and so is a flawless HD file delivered as `SD_480I` against an `HD_1080I` requirement.

The two bases differ in exactly two respects. An ingest report forces the fault penalty to zero, uses the ingest measurement, and its score — written to `technical_score_ingest` — is the one that decides whether the asset moves from `RECEIVED` to `QC_PASSED` or to `QC_FAILED`. An air-check report adds the capped fault penalty and uses the post-air measurement, so **the same asset with the same defect list scores strictly lower on air-check than on ingest whenever at least one resolved fault is linked to an aired element**; its score is written to `technical_score_aircheck` and it never changes the asset's `status`. A report also records its own component figures — `defect_penalty_points`, `duration_penalty_points`, `loudness_penalty_points`, `fault_penalty_points` — the resulting `technical_score`, the `verdict`, its `superseded` flag, which defaults to false, and its `notes`.

Submitting a report supersedes any earlier report of the same `basis` on that asset by setting the earlier report's `superseded` to true. **Only the newest non-superseded report of a given basis governs** the asset's `qc_verdict`; superseded reports keep their own stored score forever but never drive asset status again. Reports can be read individually or listed narrowed by `asset_id`, by `inspected_by_operator_id` or by `basis`; superseded reports are included in the list unless `include_superseded` is used to exclude them.

An ingest report whose computed verdict is `PASS` or `PASS_WITH_EXCEPTION` moves the asset from `RECEIVED` to `QC_PASSED` and clears its downstream: every playlist item referencing the asset whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED` has its `clearance_status` flipped to `CLEARED`, **unless the asset requires captions and has none**, in which case it is left as it stands. An ingest report whose verdict is `FAIL` moves the asset to `QC_FAILED` and blocks its downstream: those same items flip to `BLOCKED`, each owning playlist increments `blocked_item_count`, and any owning playlist in `VALIDATED` or `ARMED` reverts to `IMPORTED`. Either outcome increments the asset's `version` by one.

#### Waivers

A `ProgramDirector` may waive a QC failure so the media airs anyway. The waiver supplies `waived_by_operator_id` and a `waiver_justification`, and is legal only from `QC_FAILED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. Four further conditions apply. The `waiver_justification` must be at least 20 characters — **exactly 20 is accepted, 19 is not** — otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`. The acting user must not be the `inspected_by_operator_id` of the governing report, since **an operator never signs off on their own inspection**, otherwise `ERR_SELF_AUTHORIZATION_FORBIDDEN`. The governing non-superseded ingest score in `technical_score_ingest` must be at least 40 — **exactly 40 is waivable, 39 is not** — otherwise `ERR_QC_WAIVER_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. And an asset whose `captions_required` is true and whose `caption_present` is false **can never reach `QC_PASSED` or `QC_WAIVED` at all**: a missing caption track is a non-waivable defect and the waiver is refused with `ERR_CAPTION_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` regardless of how high the technical score is. That caption safety rule outranks the score threshold, so an uncaptioned asset scoring 90 that failed only on captions reports the caption code and not the score code.

A granted waiver writes `waived_by_operator_id` and `waiver_justification`, moves the asset to `QC_WAIVED`, flips every item referencing the asset whose `clearance_status` is `BLOCKED` and whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED` to `CLEARED`, and decrements each owning playlist's `blocked_item_count` accordingly.

#### Retraction

A `TrafficCoordinator` or `ProgramDirector` withdraws an asset from all use — a rights pull, a wrong version, a legal hold — by retracting it with a `reason`. Retraction is legal from any status except `EXPIRED` and `RETRACTED`, and attempting it from either of those yields `ERR_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`; `RETRACTED` is terminal. Retraction is the entry point of a three-hop cascade. First, every playlist item referencing the asset whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED` has its `clearance_status` flipped to `BLOCKED`. Second, caused by that flip, each owning playlist increments `blocked_item_count` and, when its `status` is `VALIDATED` or `ARMED`, reverts to `IMPORTED` with `armed_at_utc` cleared. Third, also caused by that flip, every newly blocked item that carries a `spot_order_id` moves one spot on that order from `scheduled_spots` to `credited_spots`, adds the full per-spot credit to `credit_accrued_cents`, and refreshes the order's `status` to `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` or `CLOSED_WITH_CREDIT` as described in §3.9. Items that had already reached `PLAYING`, `PLAYED`, `TRUNCATED` or `PREEMPTED` are untouched, because what has aired cannot be unaired. Assets can be listed narrowed by `status`, by `house_number` or by `waived_by_operator_id`.

### 3.5. Playlist Ingest, Timeline Integrity, Inventory and Arming

#### Ingest

Traffic hands master control a broadcast-day playlist for one channel. A `TrafficCoordinator` supplies the `channel_id`, the `broadcast_day` the list covers, the `schedule_start_utc` at which element one is booked to begin, an `items` array carrying one object per element, and optionally the `window_duration_seconds` the day is measured against, which defaults to 86400 — a full 24 hours expressed in seconds — and must be at least 60. Only one playlist may exist per channel per `broadcast_day`.

Each object in the `items` array carries a `sequence_number`, an `element_kind`, and, as applicable, a `house_number`, an `asset_id`, a `spot_order_id`, a `scheduled_start_utc`, a `scheduled_duration_seconds` and a `max_trim_seconds`. The timeline is expressed to the second. `sequence_number` is at least 1 and unique within the playlist; `scheduled_duration_seconds` is at least 1; `max_trim_seconds` defaults to 0 and must be between 0 and the item's own `scheduled_duration_seconds` inclusive. An `asset_id` or `spot_order_id` that does not resolve is refused with the corresponding not-found code, and an item's `spot_order_id` must reference a spot order whose own `channel_id` equals the playlist's `channel_id`, otherwise `ERR_SPOT_ORDER_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`.

The playlist is created in `status` `IMPORTING` and, at the end of a successful ingest transaction, moves automatically to `IMPORTED`. **At least one item must have been supplied**; an ingest with an empty `items` array is refused with `ERR_PLAYLIST_EMPTY_INGEST`. The ingest creates every item row with `playout_status` `SCHEDULED`, sets `traffic_revision` to 1, and derives each item's `clearance_status`: an item is created `CLEARED` when its `asset_id` points at an asset whose `status` is `QC_PASSED` or `QC_WAIVED`; it is created `BLOCKED` when its asset is `RETRACTED`, `EXPIRED` or `QC_FAILED`; and it is otherwise created `UNCLEARED`. Elements that need no asset at all — those whose `element_kind` is `NETWORK_JOIN`, `LIVE_EVENT` or `FILLER` — are treated as needing no clearance. The ingest then recomputes `item_count`, `blocked_item_count`, `scheduled_total_seconds`, `commercial_seconds_by_hour`, `projected_end_variance_seconds` and `asrun_end_variance_seconds`, and for every item carrying a `spot_order_id` it increments that order's `scheduled_spots` and recomputes its `unplaced_spots`.

#### Timeline integrity

The channel timeline uses **half-open intervals**: an element occupies the span from its `scheduled_start_utc` up to but not including that instant plus its `scheduled_duration_seconds`, and **adjacency is mandatory — neither gaps nor overlaps are tolerated**. The integrity pass is run on ingest, on every traffic revision, on every makegood placement, on every substitution, and again when the playlist is driven to `VALIDATED`.

The pass considers the playlist's items whose `playout_status` is *not* `SKIPPED` and not `PREEMPTED`, ordered by `sequence_number` ascending; **a skipped or preempted element is removed from the timeline before the walk, so it does not create a gap**, while an item whose `clearance_status` is `BLOCKED` remains on the timeline and must still be contiguous. If that set is empty, the playlist has no content to air and the pass fails with `ERR_PLAYLIST_NO_PROGRAM_CONTENT`. If the first item's `scheduled_start_utc` is not exactly the playlist's `schedule_start_utc`, the pass fails with `ERR_SCHEDULE_GAP_DETECTED`. Then, walking each consecutive pair in sequence order, the end of the earlier element is its `scheduled_start_utc` plus its `scheduled_duration_seconds`: if the next element's `scheduled_start_utc` is *after* that end there is a hole and the pass fails with `ERR_SCHEDULE_GAP_DETECTED` — **a one-second hole is a gap** — and if it is *before* that end the elements collide and the pass fails with `ERR_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP_DETECTED`. **A difference of exactly zero seconds is contiguous and legal**; touching elements are the normal case. Because the walk proceeds in sequence order and stops at the first anomaly, **the first anomaly in sequence order determines which of the two error identifiers is returned**: a playlist containing an early gap and a later overlap reports the gap, and one containing an early overlap and a later gap reports the overlap. A successful pass writes `scheduled_total_seconds` as the sum of `scheduled_duration_seconds` over the items on the timeline. All datetime comparisons here are at second granularity.

#### Commercial inventory by hour

Commercial load is aggregated into clock hours measured from the playlist's own `schedule_start_utc`: hour zero is the span from `schedule_start_utc` up to but not including one hour later, hour one the next such span, and so on, each hour being 3600 seconds long. Only items whose `element_kind` is `COMMERCIAL` count — **`PROMO` and `INTERSTITIAL` elements are not commercial inventory** — and items already `SKIPPED` or `PREEMPTED` are excluded, so a preemption frees inventory for a makegood in the same hour. Each qualifying element is attributed to **exactly one hour, chosen by its `scheduled_start_utc`, even when the element straddles the hour boundary**: subtract the playlist's `schedule_start_utc` from the element's `scheduled_start_utc`, divide the resulting seconds by 3600 and truncate downward to get the zero-based hour index, then add that element's `scheduled_duration_seconds` to that hour's total. `commercial_seconds_by_hour` is keyed by the decimal string of that index. No hour may carry **more than 960 seconds — sixteen minutes — of commercial elements; exactly 960 is accepted and 961 is refused** with `ERR_COMMERCIAL_INVENTORY_EXCEEDED`. The aggregate is recomputed on ingest, on every traffic revision, on every makegood, on every substitution, and on validation. Recomputing and enforcing are not the same thing, and the ceiling is enforced — the whole request refused — at four of those five points: on ingest, on every traffic revision, on every makegood placement and at validation. A substitution recomputes the map but never applies the ceiling to it, and it cannot in any case lengthen a commercial element beyond the span already booked for it. Because the ingest and the revision each refuse before their items are allowed to stand, an hour over the ceiling never survives into stored state by those routes, and validation's inventory gate re-checks a condition the earlier writes have already refused rather than being the first place a 961-second hour is caught.

#### Timing variance: two figures, two answers

The playlist carries two variance figures, both signed whole seconds against the same `window_duration_seconds`, and **they deliberately disagree for identical stored data whenever any item's `measured_duration_seconds` differs from its `scheduled_duration_seconds`**. A positive figure means overrun — the day runs long — and a negative figure means underrun, that is dead air to be filled.

`projected_end_variance_seconds` is the forward-looking figure: sum `scheduled_duration_seconds` over every item of the playlist whose `playout_status` is not `SKIPPED` and not `PREEMPTED`, then subtract `window_duration_seconds`. It trusts the booked schedule for everything that has not been dropped, **including items that already aired short**.

`asrun_end_variance_seconds` is the chronological figure: walk the items in `sequence_number` ascending order accumulating, for each, its `measured_duration_seconds` when its `playout_status` is `PLAYED` or `TRUNCATED`, zero when its `playout_status` is `SKIPPED` or `PREEMPTED`, and its `scheduled_duration_seconds` in every other case; then subtract `window_duration_seconds`. A truncated element therefore shortens the as-run day while the projected figure still counts its full booked length.

Both figures scan every item of the playlist including makegood items, filler items and items whose `clearance_status` is `BLOCKED`. **Neither figure consults the as-run log**: the item's own `measured_duration_seconds` is the single source of aired length. Both are recomputed on ingest, on traffic revision, on validation and on close, on every playout event, on every backtiming plan execution, on every makegood placement, and on every cascade that flips an item to `TRUNCATED`, `SKIPPED` or `PREEMPTED`.

#### Driving the lifecycle

A playlist's `status` is driven by supplying a `target_status` and an optional `reason`. The reachable moves are `IMPORTING` to `IMPORTED` or `ABANDONED`; `IMPORTED` to `VALIDATED`, back to `IMPORTING`, or `ABANDONED`; `VALIDATED` to `ARMED`, back to `IMPORTED`, or `ABANDONED`; `ARMED` to `ON_AIR`, back to `IMPORTED`, or `ABANDONED`; and `ON_AIR` to `CLOSED`. `CLOSED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal. Anything else is refused with `ERR_PLAYLIST_INVALID_STATE`. The cycle `IMPORTED` to `VALIDATED` to `ARMED` and back to `IMPORTED` is the ordinary revision loop and may be traversed any number of times.

**Validating** a playlist runs four gates. There must be at least one item and at least one item whose `element_kind` is `PROGRAM`, otherwise `ERR_PLAYLIST_NO_PROGRAM_CONTENT` — a list made entirely of commercials, promos and filler is not a broadcast day. The timeline integrity pass must succeed. No clock hour may exceed the 960-second commercial ceiling. And `blocked_item_count` must be exactly 0, otherwise `ERR_UNCLEARED_CONTENT_ON_AIR`. Validation then recomputes both variance figures and increments the playlist's `version`.

**Arming** a validated playlist is the last checkpoint before automation owns it, and it runs five gates. The channel's `posture` must be `NORMAL` or `DEGRADED`; `SUSPENDED`, `EMERGENCY` and `DEGRADED_CRITICAL` all refuse the arm with `ERR_CHANNEL_SUSPENDED_POSTURE`, a dependency that reads from the playlist through to its channel. The parent transmitter's `operational_state` must not be `OFF_AIR`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSMITTER_OFF_AIR`, a dependency that reads from the playlist through the channel and multiplex to the transmitter. The channel must have no open technical fault of `severity` `CRITICAL` — that is, none in `DETECTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `ESCALATED` — otherwise `ERR_CRITICAL_FAULT_OPEN`; **major and minor faults never block arming, they only change posture**. Every item whose `element_kind` is `PROGRAM`, `COMMERCIAL`, `INTERSTITIAL` or `PROMO` must have `clearance_status` `CLEARED`, otherwise `ERR_UNCLEARED_CONTENT_ON_AIR`. And the absolute value of `projected_end_variance_seconds` must not exceed 30 seconds — **exactly plus or minus 30 seconds arms successfully and 31 does not** — otherwise `ERR_ARM_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`. Note that arming is gated on the *projected* figure, never the as-run one. A successful arm writes `armed_at_utc` at the current logical time and increments `version`.

**Taking a playlist to air** requires three things: the channel's own `air_status` must already be `ON_AIR`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ON_AIR`; the logical clock must have reached at least the playlist's `schedule_start_utc` less a 300-second preroll tolerance, so **at exactly five minutes before the booked start the transition is permitted** and any earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_PLAYLIST_AIR_TOO_EARLY`; and no other playlist on the same channel may already be `ON_AIR`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_PLAYLIST_ALREADY_ON_AIR`. There is no upper bound on lateness.

**Closing** an on-air playlist requires that no item is still `PLAYING`, otherwise `ERR_PLAYLIST_ITEM_STILL_PLAYING`, and that no emergency override on the channel is in `ACTIVE_CRAWL` or `ACTIVE_CUTIN`, otherwise `ERR_EAS_OVERRIDE_ACTIVE`. The close writes `closed_at_utc` at the current logical time, flips every item still in `SCHEDULED` or `CUED` to `SKIPPED` with a system-sourced skip row appended to the as-run log for each, runs the final spot settlement of §3.9 over every spot order this playlist touched — writing each order's `aired_spots`, `short_aired_spots`, `credited_spots`, `unplaced_spots`, `credit_accrued_cents` and `status`, and the playlist's own `reconciliation_credit_cents` — and recomputes `asrun_end_variance_seconds`.

**Abandoning** a playlist is available from `IMPORTED` and from `VALIDATED`, and only while **no item has reached `CUED`, `PLAYING`, `PLAYED` or `TRUNCATED`**; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_PLAYLIST_INVALID_STATE`. Abandonment flips all items to `SKIPPED` and, for every referenced spot order, decrements `scheduled_spots` and recomputes `unplaced_spots`, keeping the spot-count invariant of §2.11 intact.

#### Traffic revisions

A revision is applied by supplying `expected_playlist_version`, an `items` array of the elements to add or replace, and optionally `removed_sequence_numbers` naming elements to drop. Revisions are accepted only while the playlist's `status` is `IMPORTING`, `IMPORTED`, `VALIDATED` or `ARMED`; **a revision against an `ON_AIR`, `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED` playlist is refused** with `ERR_PLAYLIST_INVALID_STATE`. A stale `expected_playlist_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_PLAYLIST_VERSION`. Within one playlist, `sequence_number` is unique and the submitted revision's items must form **a dense run from 1 to N with no duplicates and no holes**, otherwise `ERR_SEQUENCE_NUMBER_CONFLICT`.

Applying a revision increments `traffic_revision` by one and **pulls a `VALIDATED` or `ARMED` playlist back to `IMPORTED`**, clearing `armed_at_utc` to null. Every existing item whose `playout_status` is still `SCHEDULED` is deleted and replaced by the supplied revision items, while items whose `playout_status` is `CUED`, `PLAYING`, `PLAYED`, `TRUNCATED`, `SKIPPED` or `PREEMPTED` are **retained untouched** — a revision can never rewrite history or steal an element automation has already cued. Spot orders referenced by the removed items and by the added items have their `scheduled_spots` and `unplaced_spots` recomputed so the spot-count invariant holds, every computed field of the playlist is recomputed, seconds released by trims that the revision reinstates are returned to the airtime ledger, and the playlist's `version` is incremented.

Playlists can be listed for a channel narrowed by `broadcast_day` or by `status`, and their elements listed in sequence order narrowed by `playout_status`, by `clearance_status` or by `element_kind`. Elements can also be listed across playlists narrowed by `playlist_id`, by `asset_id`, by `spot_order_id`, by `feed_window_id`, by `replaces_item_id` or by `playout_status`.

### 3.6. Playout Execution, Substitution and the As-Run Log

#### The two independent axes of an element

Every playlist element carries two status dimensions that evolve independently. `playout_status` is the automation axis, driven only by playout events and cascades. `clearance_status` is the content axis, driven only by asset lifecycle events and waivers and **never by automation events**. The combinations are not all reachable in both directions: an element cannot be cued while `UNCLEARED`, but an element that is `PLAYED` and `BLOCKED` is perfectly reachable, because an asset may be retracted after the element has already aired.

On the clearance axis, an element starts `UNCLEARED`. It becomes `CLEARED` when its asset moves from `RECEIVED` to `QC_PASSED`, when its asset moves from `QC_FAILED` to `QC_WAIVED`, or at creation time when the referenced asset is already `QC_PASSED` or `QC_WAIVED`; the asset's `status` must be one of those two, and an asset that requires captions but has none cannot clear an element unless the asset's own `status` is `QC_WAIVED`, otherwise the clearance is refused on the caption requirement. Clearing an element that was previously `BLOCKED` decrements the owning playlist's `blocked_item_count`. It becomes `BLOCKED` when its asset fails QC, expires or is retracted, or at creation time against an already failed, expired or retracted asset; that increments `blocked_item_count` and reverts a `VALIDATED` or `ARMED` playlist to `IMPORTED`. An element already `CLEARED` reverts to `BLOCKED` on a retraction, or when a passing report is superseded by a failing one, **but only while its `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED`** — items already `PLAYING` or terminal keep the clearance they had. And a `BLOCKED` element returns to `CLEARED` on a waiver or on a later passing report for a redelivered asset, again only while its `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED`, decrementing `blocked_item_count`. The cycle from `CLEARED` to `BLOCKED` and back across a retraction, redelivery and waiver sequence is expected. No clearance state is terminal.

#### Playout events

Automation reports what it did by posting a playout event against an element, supplying the `event_kind`, the `event_utc` at which it happened, and, where applicable, a `measured_duration_seconds`, a `source` and a `force_preempt_guaranteed` flag. Every accepted event appends **exactly one** as-run entry; the as-run log is the only durable record of what aired.

Two timestamp rules bind every event, and their order matters. `event_utc` must be at or after the `event_utc` of the most recent as-run entry for the same playlist, because the log is append-only in time order; an out-of-order event is refused with `ERR_PLAYOUT_EVENT_OUT_OF_ORDER`. `event_utc` must also be at or before the logical clock; a future-dated event is refused with `ERR_EVENT_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`. **The ordering rule is evaluated first**, so an event that is both earlier than the previous entry and later than the clock reports the out-of-order code. An `event_utc` exactly equal to the logical clock is accepted, and an `event_utc` exactly equal to the previous entry's `event_utc` is accepted.

An event is legal only for certain current `playout_status` values: `CUE` requires `SCHEDULED`, `START` requires `CUED`, `END` requires `PLAYING`, `TRUNCATION` requires `PLAYING`, and `SKIP` requires `SCHEDULED` or `CUED`. An event against an element that is already `PLAYED`, `TRUNCATED`, `SKIPPED` or `PREEMPTED` is refused with `ERR_ITEM_INVALID_PLAYOUT_STATE`; those four are terminal on the automation axis. `SCHEDULED` is the initial state.

**Cueing** an element requires four things: the owning playlist's `status` must be `ON_AIR`, otherwise `ERR_PLAYLIST_NOT_ON_AIR`; for elements whose `element_kind` is `PROGRAM`, `COMMERCIAL`, `INTERSTITIAL` or `PROMO` the `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED`, otherwise `ERR_UNCLEARED_CONTENT_ON_AIR`, a dependency that reads from the element through to its asset; the channel's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_SUSPENDED_POSTURE`; and **no other element of the same playlist may already be `CUED`**, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_INVALID_PLAYOUT_STATE`. A cue appends a cue entry to the as-run log with `source` `AUTOMATION` and a `measured_duration_seconds` of 0.

**Starting** a cued element requires that no other element of the same playlist is already `PLAYING`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_INVALID_PLAYOUT_STATE` — one channel radiates one element at a time. The start writes the element's `actual_start_utc` from the supplied `event_utc` and appends a start entry sourced `AUTOMATION`. A channel whose allocation is `STARVED` cannot accept a start event at all, which is reported as `ERR_CHANNEL_ALLOCATION_STARVED`; such a channel may only be set to bars-and-tone or dark.

**Ending** a playing element requires a `measured_duration_seconds` that is present and at least 0. It writes that value onto the element, appends an end entry carrying the same figure, recomputes the playlist's `asrun_end_variance_seconds`, and, when the element carries a `spot_order_id`, moves that spot out of `scheduled_spots` into either `aired_spots` or `short_aired_spots` according to the full-air boundary of §3.9 and accrues any short-air credit.

**Truncating** a playing element requires that `actual_start_utc` is not null. Its `measured_duration_seconds` is written as the whole seconds between `actual_start_utc` and the event instant, or as the value explicitly supplied on a direct call. It appends a truncation entry, recomputes `asrun_end_variance_seconds`, and, when the element carries a `spot_order_id`, moves that spot into `short_aired_spots` and accrues the pro-rata credit of §3.9. Truncation also arrives without a direct call — as hop three of the override cut-in cascade, as hop five of the transmitter cascade, and as the consequence of a hard-cut backtiming plan.

**Skipping** an element is legal from `SCHEDULED` and from `CUED`. It appends a skip entry, and when the element carries a `spot_order_id` it moves that spot from `scheduled_spots` to `credited_spots` and accrues the **full** per-spot credit, then recomputes both of the playlist's variance figures. Skips also arrive from a skip-elements backtiming plan, from a substitution, and from the sweep performed when a playlist closes. When the trigger is a backtiming plan, an element whose `element_kind` is `COMMERCIAL` may not be skipped and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TRIM_EXCEEDS_MAX`, because commercial elements are never trimmable.

**Preemption** is never requested directly; it arrives from hop five of the transmitter cascade, from hop three of the override cut-in cascade, or from a channel being taken from `ON_AIR` to `DARK`, and it applies to elements in `SCHEDULED` or `CUED` while the owning playlist is `ON_AIR`. It appends a preemption entry with `source` `SYSTEM` and, when the element carries a `spot_order_id`, moves that spot from `scheduled_spots` to `credited_spots` with the full per-spot credit accrued. Preemption is fiscally gated: only spots whose order's `priority_class` is `PREEMPTIBLE` or `REMNANT` are eligible, and a `GUARANTEED` spot is preempted **only when the request carries `force_preempt_guaranteed` set to true**, in which case it accrues full credit. A direct attempt to preempt a guaranteed spot without that flag is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PREEMPTIBLE`.

While a channel's `air_status` is `EAS_OVERRIDE`, ordinary programming mutations on that channel are refused with `ERR_EAS_OVERRIDE_ACTIVE`: cue and start events, substitutions, feed joins and playlist state changes other than the override release path. Only releasing the override and reporting faults are accepted.

Every accepted event increments the element's `version` by one.

#### Substitution

A `MasterControlOperator` or `ProgramDirector` may replace a scheduled or cued element with a different asset — a late delivery, a QC failure, a rights pull — by naming the `replacement_asset_id` and optionally a new `scheduled_duration_seconds` and a `reason`. The target element's `playout_status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `CUED`; an element already `PLAYING` or terminal cannot be substituted and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ITEM_INVALID_PLAYOUT_STATE`. The replacement asset's `delivered_resolution_class` must equal the outgoing element's asset's `required_resolution_class`, and the replacement's `measured_duration_seconds` must lie within the outgoing element's `max_trim_seconds` of the outgoing `scheduled_duration_seconds`; either failure is refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_RESOLUTION_MISMATCH`. The replacement must not already be placed as an unaired element in the same playlist hour, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_PLAYLIST_MISMATCH`. Any trim the substitution applies must satisfy the element's own `trim_applied_seconds` not exceeding its `max_trim_seconds`, otherwise `ERR_TRIM_EXCEEDS_MAX`; **a trim exactly equal to `max_trim_seconds` is accepted**, and commercial elements always carry a `max_trim_seconds` of 0 so they are never trimmable.

A successful substitution moves the outgoing element to `SKIPPED` with an as-run skip entry, creates a new element that records the outgoing element's identifier in its `replaces_item_id`, derives the new element's `clearance_status` from the replacement asset's status, re-runs the timeline integrity pass, recomputes the projected variance, and asserts the local-tail seconds ledger of §2.11.

#### The as-run log

An as-run entry is immutable and append-only; it carries only its own identifier and creation timestamp as housekeeping fields, and it is never updated, versioned or deleted. Each entry records the `playlist_id` of the broadcast day it belongs to, the `playlist_item_id` of the element it describes, a **snapshot** of that element's `sequence_number`, `house_number` — null when the element had no asset — and `element_kind` taken at write time, the `event_kind`, the `event_utc` at which the event occurred, a `measured_duration_seconds` that carries the seconds aired for end and truncation events and **0 for every other event kind**, the `source`, and the `recorded_by_user_id` holding the opaque user identifier of the actor whose call produced the entry, **null for entries written by a cascade**. Because the identifying fields are snapshots, a later traffic revision that renumbers or removes elements never rewrites what the log says aired.

The event kinds that appear in the log are `CUE`, `START`, `END`, `TRUNCATION`, `SKIP`, `PREEMPTION`, `OVERRIDE_IN`, `OVERRIDE_OUT`, `FILLER_INSERT`, `JOIN` and `BREAKAWAY`; the sources are `AUTOMATION`, `OPERATOR` and `SYSTEM`, where `SYSTEM` marks entries written by a cascade rather than by a direct call. The log can be read for a whole playlist in `event_utc` order, narrowed by `event_kind` or bounded by `from_utc` and `to_utc`, and it can be read for a single element in `event_utc` order.

### 3.7. Network Feed Windows: Backtiming, Joining and Breaking Away

#### Planning a window

A network feed window is the station's contract with a clock it does not control. A `MasterControlOperator` or `ProgramDirector` plans one by naming the `channel_id`, the `feed_source`, the `anchor_sequence_number` of the element at which the local schedule hands over, the `join_utc` at which the feed begins, the `breakaway_utc` at which it ends, the `backtiming_mode` that governs how much slop is tolerated, and optionally the `guard_seconds` of tolerance around each boundary, which defaults to 2 and must be between 0 and 30 inclusive, and the `live_encoder_units` the window will consume, which defaults to 1 and must be at least 1. `breakaway_utc` must be strictly after `join_utc`. The window is created in `state` `PLANNED`, and the backtiming plan is computed immediately.

`anchor_sequence_number` must be at least 1 and must match an existing element's `sequence_number` in the channel's most recently armed or on-air playlist, and **that element's `element_kind` must be `NETWORK_JOIN`**; otherwise the plan is refused with `ERR_ANCHOR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`.

Two capacity checks guard window creation. First, **windows may not overlap on a channel, and the comparison is made on the guarded interval using closed semantics**: a new window's span from `join_utc` less `guard_seconds` through `breakaway_utc` plus `guard_seconds`, endpoints included, must not intersect the equally guarded span of any other window on the same channel whose `state` is `PLANNED` or `JOINED`. Because the endpoints are included, **a new join placed exactly at another window's guarded breakaway instant is rejected, and one second later is accepted**; the refusal is `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. When a request both overlaps an existing window and names a non-existent anchor element, **the overlap is reported** — the temporal rule is evaluated before the anchor rule.

Second, live encoder units are a **multiplex-level pool, not a channel-level one**, and overlap in that pool is legal — only the summed occupancy is bounded. Occupancy is measured on the **half-open** span from `join_utc` up to but not including `breakaway_utc`, so a window that ends at an instant frees its units for a window starting at that same instant. The peak is found by taking every window on any channel of the same multiplex whose `state` is `PLANNED` or `JOINED` — **windows in `BROKEN_AWAY` or `ABANDONED` are excluded, and windows on sibling channels of the same multiplex are included** — generating one claim of that window's `live_encoder_units` at its `join_utc` and one release of the same magnitude at its `breakaway_utc`, sorting those points by timestamp ascending and, at an identical timestamp, **applying releases before claims**, then walking them with a running occupancy and recording the highest value reached. That highest value is written to the multiplex's `live_encoder_units_peak`. The proposed window is refused with `ERR_ENCODER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when the resulting peak would **exceed** `live_encoder_units_total`; **a peak exactly equal to the total is accepted**, and a peak one unit above it is not. Because releases precede claims at equal instants, back-to-back windows are feasible even at full capacity. The peak is re-evaluated before every window insert, on every join, on every breakaway, and on every multiplex rebalance. Timestamps here are compared at second granularity.

#### The backtiming plan

The plan reconciles five interacting inputs — the time remaining to the feed, the length of the pending local tail, the guard band, the tolerance mode, and the trimmable and skippable makeup of the tail — and selects exactly one of five actions. It is computed when the window is planned, recomputed and then executed at the join, and recomputed again against `breakaway_utc` for the return to local programming.

The target instant is the window's `join_utc` for a join and its `breakaway_utc` for a breakaway. **Remaining** is the signed seconds from the logical clock to that target. The **tail** is the elements of the channel's on-air playlist whose `sequence_number` is at or after `anchor_sequence_number` and whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED`, taken in ascending `sequence_number` order; **a `PLAYING` element is excluded from the tail** because it is handled by truncation at the join instant, and `PLAYED`, `SKIPPED`, `PREEMPTED` and `TRUNCATED` elements are excluded entirely. The **pending tail** is the sum of `scheduled_duration_seconds` over the tail, and the **slack** is remaining less pending tail, a signed figure: positive slack means the local content ends before the feed starts, negative slack means the local content runs long.

**Tolerance** depends on `backtiming_mode`. In `STRICT` mode the tolerance is exactly `guard_seconds`. In `TOLERANT` mode it is the larger of `guard_seconds` and a proportional allowance, where that allowance is the smaller of two percent of the pending tail — the pending tail multiplied by 0.02 and truncated downward to whole seconds — and a hard cap of 15 seconds. A tolerant window over a short tail therefore behaves exactly like a strict one, while a tolerant window over a long tail gains up to fifteen seconds of latitude.

The five branches are evaluated in a fixed order and **the first matching clause wins**.

If **remaining is zero or negative**, the feed has already started and the action is `JOIN_IN_PROGRESS`: `late_join_seconds` is the magnitude of the overshoot, and the pad, the trim and the skip list are all empty. Note that this clause is tested before tolerance, so a join called exactly at `join_utc` is not late and falls through to the next clause, while one second later is a join in progress.

Otherwise, if the **absolute value of the slack is within tolerance**, the action is `HARD_CUT` with no pad, no trim, no skips and no late-join seconds. **A slack exactly equal to the tolerance is a hard cut**, not a pad and not a trim.

Otherwise, if the **slack exceeds tolerance** — the local tail is too short and there is dead time to fill — the action is `PAD_WITH_FILLER`. The pad is the smaller of the slack and a ceiling of 120 seconds, and any slack the pad cannot absorb is recorded as `residual_dead_air_seconds`. The station will never insert more than two minutes of filler; a five-minute hole yields two minutes of filler and three minutes of recorded dead air.

Otherwise the slack is more negative than the tolerance and the day is **running long**. The deficit is the magnitude of the slack. The trimmable pool is the sum, over tail elements whose `element_kind` is `PROMO`, `INTERSTITIAL` or `FILLER`, of the smaller of that element's `scheduled_duration_seconds` less one second and its `max_trim_seconds` — every element must retain at least one second, and **`COMMERCIAL` and `PROGRAM` elements are never trimmable or skippable**. If the trimmable pool covers the deficit, the action is `TRIM_TAIL` with the trim equal to the whole deficit and no skips; the trim is consumed in ascending `sequence_number` order, each element giving the smaller of the remaining need and its own trimmable allowance. If the trimmable pool does not cover the deficit, the trim takes the entire pool and the remaining need is met by dropping whole elements: walking the tail in ascending `sequence_number` order, each element whose `element_kind` is `PROMO`, `INTERSTITIAL` or `FILLER` is added to `skipped_item_sequences` and the need is reduced by that element's `scheduled_duration_seconds` less whatever trim was already taken from it, stopping as soon as the need is met. If the need is met, the action is `SKIP_ELEMENTS` with no late-join seconds. If the need survives even the skippable pool — because **commercials are protected and a plan may never drop a `COMMERCIAL` element** — the plan degrades to `HARD_CUT` and the surviving need is recorded as `late_join_seconds`, with the boundary element truncated at the join instant.

Ties are broken purely by ascending `sequence_number`, with `created_at` as the fallback that the uniqueness rule makes unreachable; an element's `id` is never consulted. Every figure is in whole seconds and the only rounding anywhere in the plan is the downward truncation of the tolerance ratio. Computing a plan writes `plan_computed_utc` at the current logical time.

#### Executing the join

The join is executed by a `MasterControlOperator`, optionally supplying the `actual_join_utc` at which it really happened. Four conditions apply: the channel's `air_status` must be `ON_AIR`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_ON_AIR`; the channel's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_SUSPENDED_POSTURE`; the channel must have a playlist in `status` `ON_AIR`, otherwise `ERR_PLAYLIST_NOT_ON_AIR`; and the computed action must not be a skip-elements plan that would drop a guaranteed commercial, otherwise `ERR_TRIM_EXCEEDS_MAX`. The join must also be issued inside the guard window: the logical clock must be at or after `join_utc` less `guard_seconds` and at or before `join_utc` plus a late-join limit of 30 seconds. Earlier is premature and later forfeits the window; either failure is `ERR_JOIN_OUTSIDE_GUARD_WINDOW`, and **exactly `join_utc` plus 30 seconds is accepted while plus 31 is refused**.

The join recomputes the plan against the current logical clock and writes `plan_action`, `backtime_pad_seconds`, `backtime_trim_seconds`, `residual_dead_air_seconds`, `late_join_seconds`, `skipped_item_sequences` and `plan_computed_utc`. **A late call therefore converts what was planned as a pad into a join in progress**, because remaining has gone negative between planning and execution.

Each branch then acts. A `PAD_WITH_FILLER` join creates one element whose `element_kind` is `FILLER`, appended with the next free `sequence_number`, whose `scheduled_duration_seconds` equals `backtime_pad_seconds` and whose `clearance_status` is `CLEARED`, and appends a filler-insert entry to the as-run log. A `TRIM_TAIL` join reduces `scheduled_duration_seconds` and sets `trim_applied_seconds` on the trimmable tail elements in ascending `sequence_number` order, **never taking an element below its `scheduled_duration_seconds` less its `max_trim_seconds`**. A `SKIP_ELEMENTS` join flips the elements named in `skipped_item_sequences` to `SKIPPED`, each with its own as-run skip entry and its own spot-credit accrual under the skip rules of §3.6. A `JOIN_IN_PROGRESS` join truncates the currently playing element, if any, and records `late_join_seconds`. A `HARD_CUT` join inserts no filler and applies no trim; the playing element is truncated at the join instant. In every branch a join entry sourced `OPERATOR` is appended, both playlist variance figures are recomputed, and the window's `version` is incremented. Joining moves the window from `PLANNED` to `JOINED`.

Every executed plan must leave the local-tail seconds ledger of §2.11 intact, in both of its forms. The pre-plan tail total must equal the tail seconds still scheduled after the plan, plus `backtime_trim_seconds`, plus the sum of `scheduled_duration_seconds` over the elements named in `skipped_item_sequences` — skipped elements **keep their `scheduled_duration_seconds` field intact**, which is exactly what makes the accounting reversible. And the interval from `plan_computed_utc` to `join_utc` must equal the tail seconds still scheduled after the plan, plus `backtime_pad_seconds`, plus `residual_dead_air_seconds`, less `late_join_seconds`. A trim-tail plan followed by a traffic revision that reinstates the trimmed elements — each element's `scheduled_duration_seconds` restored and its `trim_applied_seconds` reset to 0 — returns the tail total to exactly its original value: **the plan never destroys or invents scheduled seconds**.

#### Breaking away and abandonment

The breakaway is executed by a `MasterControlOperator`, optionally supplying the `actual_breakaway_utc`. It requires that the logical clock has reached at least `breakaway_utc` less `guard_seconds`; **at exactly that instant the breakaway is permitted** and any earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_BREAKAWAY_TOO_EARLY`. The breakaway computes `residual_dead_air_seconds` for the return to local, appends a breakaway entry sourced `OPERATOR`, re-cues the local tail, releases the window's `live_encoder_units` — which lowers the multiplex's `live_encoder_units_peak` at the next evaluation — recomputes the as-run variance, and moves the window from `JOINED` to `BROKEN_AWAY`, which is terminal. Any trim the breakaway plan applies is bound by the same `max_trim_seconds` rule and refused with `ERR_TRIM_EXCEEDS_MAX`.

A `PLANNED` window is abandoned by the system when its channel is taken from `ON_AIR` to `DARK` or when the transmitter cascade drives the channel to `SUSPENDED` posture; abandonment requires that the channel's `posture` is `SUSPENDED` or its `air_status` is `DARK`, releases the window's `live_encoder_units`, and increments the window's `version`. `ABANDONED` is terminal. A join or breakaway attempted from a state that does not allow it is refused with `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`. Windows can be read individually or listed for a channel narrowed by `state`, ascending by `join_utc`.

### 3.8. Emergency Override Authority

#### Requesting

An override is a two-person operation, and requesting one changes nothing on air. A `MasterControlOperator` or `ProgramDirector` requests one by naming the `channel_id` to be overridden, the `requested_by_operator_id` of the requesting operator, the `alert_class`, the `override_mode` wanted, the `requested_start_utc` at which the override is intended to begin, the `expected_duration_seconds` it is planned to run — at least 1 — and, when the mode is a crawl, the `crawl_text` itself. The request is created in `state` `PENDING` with `active_mode` and `authorized_by_operator_id` both null, `truncated_item_count` and `preempted_item_count` both 0, and `rejection_reason` null.

A channel accepts **at most one non-terminal override at a time**. Because an override's effective interval runs from activation until release with no predetermined end, any override on the channel whose `state` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE_CRAWL` or `ACTIVE_CUTIN` blocks a new request with `ERR_OVERRIDE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; only `RELEASED` and `REJECTED` overrides, which are terminal, leave the channel free.

#### Activating

Activation is performed by supplying the `authorized_by_operator_id`, the `override_mode` to put on air, and optionally an `activated_utc`, a `crawl_text` and a `force_preempt_guaranteed` flag. It is accepted from `PENDING`, from `ACTIVE_CRAWL` and from `ACTIVE_CUTIN` — the two active states permit the declared escalation and relaxation moves — and **when the override is already active the requested `override_mode` must differ from the current `active_mode`**; anything else is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_INVALID_STATE`. An `activated_utc` in the future of the logical clock is refused with `ERR_EVENT_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`.

Authority is checked against the authorizing operator's `override_authority` ladder. A crawl requires `CRAWL_ONLY`, `CRAWL_AND_CUTIN` or `FULL`. A cut-in requires `CRAWL_AND_CUTIN` or `FULL`. **An `override_authority` of `NONE` authorizes nothing at all**, and an `alert_class` of `EAS_MANDATORY` additionally requires `FULL` regardless of the mode requested. Insufficient authority is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_AUTHORITY_INSUFFICIENT`. The authorizing operator must also reference an operator whose `role_class` is `PROGRAM_DIRECTOR` or `MASTER_CONTROL`, otherwise `ERR_OPERATOR_ROLE_CLASS_INVALID`, and their certification must be live, otherwise `ERR_OPERATOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`.

Segregation of duties applies: the acting user must differ from the override's `requested_by_operator_id`, otherwise `ERR_SELF_AUTHORIZATION_FORBIDDEN`. Note the shape of this rule carefully — **the self-authorization bar is unconditional for a `BREAKING_NEWS` alert and is the one alert class where the station will not let a single operator both request and air the interruption**; the other classes are permitted only where the acting user is genuinely a different operator, so an operator authorizing their own request is refused. Because `StationAdmin` acts with an opaque identifier that never resolves to an operator record, it can never supply the second half of this pair.

The channel's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_SUSPENDED_POSTURE` — a channel whose transmitter is dark or whose allocation has starved cannot carry an alert. And a crawl activation, whether from `PENDING` or as a relaxation from a cut-in, requires a non-null `crawl_text`, otherwise `ERR_CRAWL_TEXT_REQUIRED`.

**Activating a crawl** writes `authorized_by_operator_id` from the acting user, `activated_utc` at the current logical time, and `active_mode` `CRAWL`. The channel's `posture` recomputes to `EMERGENCY`, but **`air_status` is unchanged because a crawl is an overlay rather than a substitution** — programming keeps running underneath it. An override-in entry sourced `OPERATOR` is appended against the currently playing element of the channel's on-air playlist when one exists. No element is truncated and no element is preempted.

**Activating a cut-in** is the entry point of a five-hop cascade. Hop one writes `authorized_by_operator_id`, `activated_utc` at the current logical time, and `active_mode` `CUT_IN`. Hop two, caused by hop one, drives the channel's `air_status` to `EAS_OVERRIDE` and its `posture` to `EMERGENCY`. Hop three, caused by hop two, rewrites the playlist: the element that was `PLAYING` on the channel's on-air playlist flips to `TRUNCATED` with its `measured_duration_seconds` set to the whole seconds from its `actual_start_utc` to the current logical time; and **every element whose own booked span, from its `scheduled_start_utc` up to but not including that instant plus its `scheduled_duration_seconds`, lies entirely inside the override's expected span, from the current logical time up to but not including that instant plus `expected_duration_seconds`, and whose `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CUED`, flips to `PREEMPTED`**. An element that merely straddles the end of the override's expected span is not preempted. `truncated_item_count` and `preempted_item_count` are written as the tally of **this pass alone** — a later activation pass overwrites whatever an earlier one left rather than adding to a running total, and a pass that finds no playlist on air on the channel writes both counters to 0 — one as-run entry is appended per affected element, and an override-in entry is appended as well. Hop four, caused by hop three, settles the money: every affected element carrying a `spot_order_id` moves that spot to `short_aired_spots` when it was truncated or to `credited_spots` when it was preempted, accrues the corresponding credit, and refreshes the order's `status`. Hop five, also caused by hop three, recomputes the playlist's `asrun_end_variance_seconds`. Preemption here obeys the same fiscal gate as elsewhere: only spots whose order's `priority_class` is `PREEMPTIBLE` or `REMNANT` are eligible, and a `GUARANTEED` spot is preempted only when the activation carries `force_preempt_guaranteed` set to true, otherwise the activation is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PREEMPTIBLE`.

**Escalating** an active crawl to a cut-in runs the whole of hops two through five and overwrites `authorized_by_operator_id` with the escalating operator, who must independently hold cut-in authority and must not be the requester. **De-escalating** a cut-in back to a crawl sets `active_mode` to `CRAWL` and returns the channel's `air_status` from `EAS_OVERRIDE` to `ON_AIR` while `posture` stays `EMERGENCY`; it requires a non-null `crawl_text`, and, when `alert_class` is `EAS_MANDATORY`, it requires that at least 120 seconds have elapsed since `activated_utc` — **at exactly 120 seconds the de-escalation is permitted** and any earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_EAS_MINIMUM_DURATION_NOT_MET`. **Elements already truncated or preempted are not restored**: there is no compensation on de-escalation. The cycle from crawl to cut-in and back may repeat any number of times, each pass truncating and preempting afresh.

#### Releasing and rejecting

Releasing takes the override off air and hands the channel back to the playlist. It is accepted from `ACTIVE_CRAWL` and from `ACTIVE_CUTIN`, optionally supplying a `released_utc`, and, when `alert_class` is `EAS_MANDATORY`, it requires the same minimum of 120 seconds on air since `activated_utc`, refused otherwise with `ERR_EAS_MINIMUM_DURATION_NOT_MET`. A release writes `released_utc` at the current logical time and sets `active_mode` to null. Releasing a crawl recomputes `posture`, which clears `EMERGENCY` and falls back to `DEGRADED_CRITICAL`, `DEGRADED` or `NORMAL` according to the channel's open faults and its transmitter's state. Releasing a cut-in additionally returns `air_status` from `EAS_OVERRIDE` to `ON_AIR`, which is permitted only when no override on the channel remains in `ACTIVE_CUTIN`. Both paths append an override-out entry sourced `OPERATOR` and move the override to `RELEASED`, which is terminal.

The release is deliberately **not** a compensating transaction. **Preempted commercial spots stay credited; the spot ledger is not reversed by the release itself.** Only a makegood placement moves a spot back from `credited_spots` to `scheduled_spots`, and that is the subject of §3.9.

A `PENDING` request may instead be rejected by a `ProgramDirector`, which requires a `rejection_reason`, otherwise `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED`. Rejection writes `rejection_reason`, has **no channel or element side effects whatsoever**, and moves the override to `REJECTED`, which is terminal. Overrides can be read individually or listed narrowed by `channel_id`, by `requested_by_operator_id`, by `authorized_by_operator_id` or by `state`.

### 3.9. Spot Inventory, Credit Settlement and Makegoods

#### Orders

A `TrafficCoordinator` sells inventory on a channel by creating a spot order naming the `channel_id`, the `advertiser_name` of record, the traffic system's `order_reference`, the number of `ordered_spots` sold — at least 1 — the contracted `unit_duration_seconds` of each spot — at least 5 — the gross `rate_cents_total` in cents for all ordered spots — at least 1 — the `daypart`, the `priority_class` and the `makegood_deadline_utc`, which is the last instant, inclusive, at which a makegood may be placed. `order_reference` is unique across all orders and a collision yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORDER_REFERENCE`.

An order is created in `status` `OPEN` with every counter at 0 except `unplaced_spots`, which starts equal to `ordered_spots`. That is not an accident: `unplaced_spots` is the slack variable that absorbs the difference between what was sold and what has actually been placed on a playlist, which is what allows the spot-count identity of §2.11 to hold even before any playlist has been ingested. Orders can be read individually or listed narrowed by `channel_id`, by `status` or by `priority_class`.

#### Classifying every spot

Settlement is computed over the elements linked to the order — every playlist element whose `spot_order_id` equals the order's identifier, across every playlist of its channel — taken in ascending `scheduled_start_utc` order and, for elements sharing an instant, in ascending `sequence_number` order. **That processing order is observable in the reconciliation read; an element's `id` is never consulted.** Makegood elements, those whose `is_makegood` is true, participate exactly like ordinary spots. **One class of element is excluded from the linked set: an element whose `id` is named as the `replaces_item_id` of some element whose `is_makegood` is true has been superseded, and it leaves the set entirely — it is not classified, it earns no credit, and it no longer counts towards the number of linked elements.** A makegood therefore substitutes for the element it replaces rather than joining it, the linked count is left unchanged by the placement, and the spot-count identity of §2.11 survives the round trip even when the order also has unplaced spots. The exclusion is keyed on `is_makegood` being true: an element replaced by a *substitution* under §3.6 is not superseded in this sense and stays in the linked set.

Each linked element is classified into exactly one of four buckets. It is **aired in full** when its `playout_status` is `PLAYED` and its `measured_duration_seconds` is at least the order's `unit_duration_seconds` less a one-second air tolerance — so **a thirty-second spot that ran twenty-nine seconds counts as aired in full**, while twenty-eight seconds does not. It is a **short air** when its `playout_status` is `TRUNCATED`, or when its `playout_status` is `PLAYED` but its `measured_duration_seconds` falls below that tolerance boundary. It is **not aired** when its `playout_status` is `SKIPPED` or `PREEMPTED`. And it is **pending** in every remaining case, that is while its `playout_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CUED` or `PLAYING`.

#### Computing the credit

The per-spot rate is the order's `rate_cents_total` divided by its `ordered_spots`, and **that quotient is kept as an exact value and is deliberately not rounded at this stage**. An element that aired in full earns no credit. An element classified as a short air earns the per-spot rate multiplied by the shortfall and divided by the contracted length, where the shortfall is the `unit_duration_seconds` less the element's `measured_duration_seconds` treated as zero if negative. An element that did not air earns the whole per-spot rate.

**The rounding order is pinned and it matters.** Each per-element credit is rounded to whole cents on its own, half-cent values rounding upward, and only then are the rounded figures summed. Summing the exact values first and rounding once at the end is **wrong** and will differ from the correct answer by as much as one cent for every element beyond the first. Elements classified as pending contribute nothing to the sum.

Two bases select what "everything" means. On the **preliminary** basis, which is the default while a playlist is still on air, elements still `SCHEDULED`, `CUED` or `PLAYING` are excluded from the credit and are counted only in `scheduled_spots`, and ordered-but-never-scheduled spots contribute nothing; no unplaced credit is added. On the **final** basis, every ordered spot must be accounted for: the unplaced count is the order's `ordered_spots` less the number of linked elements, floored at zero, and each unplaced spot is credited in full at the rounded per-spot rate, that total being added to the credit. **The preliminary total is therefore strictly lower than the final total whenever any spot is unplaced or any spot is still pending.** By the time the final basis runs as part of a playlist close, the close sweep has already turned every pending element into a skipped one, so no pending element survives to be excluded.

Settlement then writes the order's counters: `aired_spots` is the number of linked elements that aired in full; `short_aired_spots` the number classified as short airs; `credited_spots` the number of linked elements that did not air, on either basis and nothing more than that; `scheduled_spots` the number still pending; `unplaced_spots` the `ordered_spots` less the number of linked elements, floored at zero; and `credit_accrued_cents` the credit total. **The unplaced spots are credited in money only.** On the final basis their full per-spot credit enters `credit_accrued_cents` as described above, but the spots themselves stay counted in `unplaced_spots` and are never moved into `credited_spots` — which is what allows the five counters to keep summing to `ordered_spots` across a close, and what the status rule below means by "the four settled counters plus `unplaced_spots`". The playlist's own `reconciliation_credit_cents` is the sum of those credit totals over every order this playlist's elements reference. Where an order has elements on more than one playlist, all of them count towards the order's counters, but **only the closing playlist's share enters that playlist's `reconciliation_credit_cents`.**

A read-only reconciliation view is available for a playlist, taking an optional `basis` that defaults to the preliminary form. It computes and returns the settlement figures without persisting anything — **it never mutates the orders** — which makes it safe to consult mid-day. What it returns is a computed projection and not a stored entity, so the object in the response carries **no `id` of its own**: the identity fields of §2.5 belong to entities, and this view is not one.

#### Order status

An order's `status` is a function of its counters rather than an independently commanded value, and it can move backwards. It leaves `OPEN` for `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` on any credit accrual — an element preempted, skipped or truncated, the transmitter cascade's spot hop, the asset retraction cascade's spot hop, the override cut-in cascade's spot hop, or the close settlement — whenever the sum of `credited_spots` and `short_aired_spots` is strictly greater than zero and strictly less than `ordered_spots`. It moves from `OPEN` to `FULLY_AIRED` at close settlement when `aired_spots` equals `ordered_spots` with `credited_spots` and `short_aired_spots` both zero, and `credit_accrued_cents` stays at 0. It moves from `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` to `CLOSED_WITH_CREDIT` at close settlement when the four settled counters plus `unplaced_spots` account for the whole `ordered_spots`, `scheduled_spots` is zero, and the sum of `credited_spots` and `unplaced_spots` is greater than zero, writing the final-basis credit into `credit_accrued_cents` and into the playlist's `reconciliation_credit_cents`. No order status is terminal.

#### Makegoods: the compensating action

A makegood places a credited spot again, and it is the compensating action that keeps the spot ledger honest. A `TrafficCoordinator` or `ProgramDirector` places one by naming the `target_playlist_id` the replacement element will live in, the `expected_playlist_version` of that playlist, the `sequence_number` and `scheduled_start_utc` the replacement will occupy, and optionally a `scheduled_duration_seconds`.

Four conditions apply, and their order is fixed. The logical clock must be at or before the order's `makegood_deadline_utc`, and the substitute slot's `scheduled_start_utc` must also be at or before that deadline; **a makegood placed exactly at the deadline instant is accepted and one second later is refused** with `ERR_MAKEGOOD_DEADLINE_PASSED`, and **the deadline is checked before the inventory and credit rules**. The order must have `credited_spots` of at least 1, because there must be a credited spot to reverse; **a `credited_spots` of 0 is refused with `ERR_NO_CREDITED_SPOT_TO_MAKEGOOD` even when the order is `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` for other reasons**, such as short airs alone. The target playlist's `channel_id` must equal the order's `channel_id`, otherwise `ERR_SPOT_ORDER_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`, and the target playlist must belong to the order's channel, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_PLAYLIST_MISMATCH`. And `expected_playlist_version` must match the target playlist's `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_PLAYLIST_VERSION`. A makegood requested against an order whose `status` is `FULLY_AIRED` or `CLOSED_WITH_CREDIT` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_FULLY_SETTLED`. The submitted `sequence_number` must respect the playlist's dense-run rule, otherwise `ERR_SEQUENCE_NUMBER_CONFLICT`, and the placement must not push any clock hour past the 960-second commercial ceiling, otherwise `ERR_COMMERCIAL_INVENTORY_EXCEEDED`.

A successful makegood creates a playlist element with `is_makegood` set to true, `element_kind` `COMMERCIAL`, and `replaces_item_id` pointing at the credited element, in `playout_status` `SCHEDULED`. It moves one spot from `credited_spots` back to `scheduled_spots` and subtracts **exactly the credit that spot had accrued** from `credit_accrued_cents`. It recomputes the target playlist's `commercial_seconds_by_hour`, `scheduled_total_seconds` and both variance figures, re-runs the timeline integrity pass, and reverts a `VALIDATED` or `ARMED` target playlist to `IMPORTED`. It increments both the order's `version` and the playlist's `version`. An order that had been `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` returns to `OPEN` once every credited spot has a makegood placed — **a backward status move that is entirely expected**.

The round trip is exact. When a forward action such as an override cut-in or a transmitter derate preempts one scheduled spot, `scheduled_spots` decreases by one, `credited_spots` increases by one, and `credit_accrued_cents` increases by the rounded per-spot figure. When the makegood places that spot again, `credited_spots` decreases by one, `scheduled_spots` increases by one, and `credit_accrued_cents` decreases by **the same rounded per-spot figure**. The spot-count identity of §2.11 holds before the preemption, between the preemption and the makegood, and after the makegood; and `credit_accrued_cents` returns to exactly its pre-preemption value, so **no rounding drift accrues across any number of preempt-and-makegood cycles.**
