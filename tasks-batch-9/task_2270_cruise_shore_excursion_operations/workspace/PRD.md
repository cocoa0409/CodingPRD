# Cruise Shore Excursions API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A cruise line's shore excursion department sells a promise that is unusually hard to keep: that a guest who steps off the ship in a foreign port will see something worth seeing and be back on board before the gangway lifts. Between that promise and its delivery sit a vetted third-party operator, an insurance certificate that must not lapse, a tender boat with a fixed landing window, a pier meeting point that can only hold so many groups at once, an all-aboard deadline that never moves no matter how late the ship berths, and a guest whose age, mobility and declared medical conditions may quietly disqualify them from the tour they just paid for. Today those constraints live in spreadsheets, port-agent phone calls and the institutional memory of a handful of managers. This document specifies the API that replaces them.

The system this document describes owns the whole chain: catalog development by port with operator vetting and safety auditing, capacity allocation split between the pre-cruise and onboard sales channels, guest booking with eligibility screening and medical acknowledgement, ticketing and check-in, tender priority coordination, projected-return risk banding against the all-aboard deadline, weather and operational cancellation with refunds and alternative offers, guest incident reporting, operator liability claims, and finally commission settlement with each operator plus revenue reporting per port. Every one of those concerns touches money or safety, and several touch both — which is why the specification below pins evaluation order, rounding, and boundary inclusivity far more tightly than a typical CRUD service would.

Two design decisions colour everything that follows. First, every temporal rule reads a **logical clock** rather than the wall clock, so that sales cutoffs, refund tiers and insurance runways can be exercised deterministically. Second, the API is mounted under `/shore-excursions`, is flat rather than deeply nested — objects reference each other by identifier in request bodies rather than by URL ancestry — and it never silently repairs a conflicting request. When two rules would both reject a write, the specification says which one speaks.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. A request whose `X-User-ID` is absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) before anything else happens — before the route's own existence checks, before body validation, before everything. The single exception is the logical-clock capability described in §2.5, which is a test-driving affordance and requires no identity. Once identity is resolved, the declared role must appear in the operation's authorised role list; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), and that refusal precedes any existence check so that a wrong-role caller cannot use 404s to probe which identifiers exist.

Seven roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `Guest`, `ShoreExcursionDesk`, `ExcursionManager`, `OperatorVettingOfficer`, `PortAgent`, `RiskOfficer`, and `FinanceController`.

The `Guest` role is the only entity-backed identity. When `X-User-Role` is `Guest`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Guest` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Guest` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Guest` caller may only reach a `Booking` whose `guest_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, and only a refund-ledger entry belonging to such a booking. Three operations are guarded by this row-level rule — reading a single booking, reading a single refund-ledger entry, and cancelling a booking — and a violation is reported as `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_OWNED` (403). Desk, manager, risk and finance roles are exempt from the ownership rule entirely.

The remaining six roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `ShoreExcursionDesk`, `ExcursionManager`, `OperatorVettingOfficer`, `PortAgent`, `RiskOfficer` or `FinanceController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `auditor_user_id` on a `SafetyAudit` and mirrored onto the operator's `latest_audit_auditor_user_id`, as `filed_by_user_id` on a `GuestIncident`, and as `submitted_by_user_id` and later `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `LiabilityClaim` — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

Role responsibilities, in brief. A `Guest` browses the catalog, books their own seats and cancels them. The `ShoreExcursionDesk` registers guests, sells and tickets bookings, checks guests in at the meeting point, marks no-shows and files incident reports; it may never publish a catalog item, vet an operator, dispatch a departure, adjudicate a claim or settle a statement. The `ExcursionManager` owns ports, meeting points, the catalog, the departure schedule, the channel seat split and departure cancellation. The `OperatorVettingOfficer` registers operators, records safety audits and drives the vetting lifecycle. The `PortAgent` publishes tender waves, records arrival delay, drives the port-call lifecycle and dispatches and completes departures, but cannot price, sell, cancel or settle anything. The `RiskOfficer` closes incidents, opens liability claims and adjudicates them. The `FinanceController` owns the commission statement lifecycle end to end and reads per-port revenue.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a shape error and is reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). For each list response, `meta` carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filter before the window is applied — and `has_more`, true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a specific collection says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two collections deviate deliberately, and both deviations exist to make the collection reconcile with a calculation: the safety-audit collection is ordered by `conducted_on` descending and then `created_at` descending, matching the scoring window described in §3.2, while the refund-ledger collection is ordered by `created_at` ascending so a booking's money history reads in the order the money actually moved.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Mutation Counters and Append-Only Records

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID with no type prefix, never supplied by the client. Most resources also carry `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed on the same mutations that bump `version`. Both fields are read-only.

Two record types are deliberately append-only and therefore lack the mutation apparatus. A `SafetyAudit` carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no operation ever rewrites one; a correction is recorded as a new audit that supersedes the old one in the scoring window. A `RefundLedgerEntry` is likewise `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`; a reversal is a new entry, never an edit. For both, `created_at` is the logical-clock instant at which the row was written, and for audits it doubles as the tie-break key when two audits share the same `conducted_on`.

Because the topology is flat, a mutation frequently bumps more than one `version`. Selling a seat bumps both the `Booking` and its `ExcursionDeparture`. Suspending an operator bumps the `TourOperator`, every excursion it withdraws, every departure those excursions cancel, and every booking those departures terminate. Whenever the narrative in §3 says a cascade "runs", every row the cascade touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock: Every Deadline Is Read From It

The service keeps a logical clock and reads it for every temporal decision — the 60-minute sales cutoff, the check-in window, the four guest-refund tiers, the pre-cruise seat release, the 30-day insurance runway for publication, the all-aboard comparison on completion, and both timing-risk projections. The clock's value is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until it is set. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it; every subsequent request reads that instant as "now". The call itself mutates no stored entity, and unlike every other operation it does not require the `X-User-ID` header. Any role may invoke it.

Nothing in the system is scheduled to happen "later" by a background timer. Clock-derived state changes are evaluated on request, at the points named in §3 — notably the closure of a departure whose sales cutoff has passed, which lands on the stored row the next time that row or its port call is read or written.

### 2.6. Cents, Basis Points and the Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in the system is an integer number of cents, and every rate is an integer number of basis points, where ten thousand basis points is one hundred percent. Applying a rate means multiplying the amount by the basis points, dividing by ten thousand, and rounding the result half up to a whole number of cents — a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero, so a raw 427.5 becomes 428 and never 427. Percentages and ratios that the system reports back, such as the cancellation and no-show rates in the per-port revenue report, are likewise whole basis points produced by a single half-up rounding.

Where a total spans several rows, the rounding point is pinned and matters. Commission is rounded per booking line and the rounded lines are then added exactly; summing the raw bases and rounding once at the end is a different, wrong number. Ticket totals go the other way: the per-guest price is rounded once and the party total is an exact integer multiple of that rounded figure, never a re-derivation from the base price. The safety score for an operator is the opposite again: the weighted average and both penalty deductions are computed in exact arithmetic and rounded exactly once at the very end.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Departures and Operators

Three write operations are optimistically concurrent on a parent row's `version`, and each one demands a non-optional version field in its request body.

Creating a booking requires `expected_departure_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ExcursionDeparture`. If that value does not equal the departure's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEPARTURE_VERSION` (412). The rule exists because a client's view of the channel allocation buckets may predate another writer's booking, cancellation or allocation rebalance on the same departure, any of which legitimately changes the seats the new booking would consume.

Rebalancing a departure's channel split likewise requires `expected_departure_version` matching the departure's current `version`, and a mismatch yields the same `ERR_STALE_DEPARTURE_VERSION` (412). A rebalance supersedes the prior split outright, so the stale-version check is the only way to tell the caller that seats were sold out of the bucket they were about to shrink.

Creating a commission statement requires `expected_operator_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `TourOperator` being settled; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OPERATOR_VERSION` (412). Because a create voids any existing draft statement for the same operator and port call, a stale read means the commission rate, safety score or reliability strikes the statement would be built on are no longer the ones the client observed.

No other operation accepts a version field. In particular, catalog actions, vetting actions, port-call transitions, departure cancellation, fulfillment actions and statement transitions take no expected-version input at all.

### 2.8. The Order in Which Objections Are Raised

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the lowest-numbered rule in the ladder below. The ladder is global — it is not re-ordered per endpoint — and no later rule may pre-empt an earlier one. Priorities 1 through 47 are:

**Identity and authority (1–5).** 1: `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. 2: the caller's role is authorised for the operation, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. 3: a `Guest`-role caller targets only their own bookings and their own ledger entries, else `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_OWNED`. 4: on a claim adjudication, `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `submitted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. 5: on a vetting approval, `X-User-ID` differs from the operator's `latest_audit_auditor_user_id`, else `ERR_AUDITOR_CANNOT_APPROVE`.

**Request shape (6).** Every required body field present, every field of its declared type, every enum value inside its declared set, every number inside its declared range, every array a subset of its declared value set, and pagination bounds respected — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`. Shape validation deliberately runs before existence, because a missing or mistyped identifier cannot be looked up: a request that both omits a required field and names a nonexistent path identifier returns `ERR_VALIDATION`, not a 404.

**Existence (7–8).** 7: every identifier in the URL path resolves to a stored row, else that entity's own 404 code. 8: every foreign key in the request body resolves to a stored row, else the referenced entity's own 404 code. See §2.9 for the vocabulary and the resolution order.

**Cross-field coherence (9).** Instant and range orderings hold: a port call's `arrival_utc` is strictly before its `all_aboard_utc`, which is at or before its `departure_utc`; a tender wave's `window_start_utc` is strictly before its `window_end_utc`; a safety audit's `conducted_on` is at or before the logical clock; an incident's `occurred_utc` is at or before the logical clock; an excursion's `max_age` is either absent or at least its `min_age`; and a completion's `actual_return_utc` falls at or after the departure's `meeting_time_utc` and at or before the logical clock. A breach is `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. This rule guards seven operations: creating a port call, publishing a tender wave, recording a safety audit, creating an excursion, amending an excursion, filing an incident, and the dispatch-and-complete operation on a departure. Note that the `max_age` versus `min_age` ordering is checked here at priority 9 and never as part of the single-field range check at priority 6 — an excursion with `max_age` of 10 and `min_age` of 12 is an `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`, not an `ERR_VALIDATION`.

**Uniqueness (10–11).** 10: `port_code` is unique across all ports, case-sensitive on the normalised upper-case code, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_PORT_CODE`. 11: `wave_index` is unique within one port call — two calls may reuse the same ordinal, one call may not — else `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAVE_INDEX`.

**Stale reads (12–13).** 12: `expected_departure_version` equals the departure's current `version` on booking creation and on allocation rebalance, else `ERR_STALE_DEPARTURE_VERSION`. 13: `expected_operator_version` equals the operator's current `version` on statement creation, else `ERR_STALE_OPERATOR_VERSION`. Placing the stale-version check above every state and business rule means a caller holding a stale read is told to re-read, rather than being told why the write was otherwise unacceptable.

**Declared-transition checks (14–22).** 14: the requested port-call transition is declared for the call's current `status` and its clock precondition holds, else `ERR_INVALID_PORT_CALL_ACTION`; the same code refuses a delay recorded against a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` call. 15: the requested catalog action is declared for the excursion's `catalog_status`, and an amendment targets only an excursion in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_REVIEW`, else `ERR_INVALID_CATALOG_ACTION`. 16: the requested vetting action is declared for the operator's `vetting_status`, else `ERR_INVALID_VETTING_ACTION` — `TERMINATED` is terminal, so every action on a terminated operator fails here. 17: the requested departure operation is declared for the departure's `status` and the owning port call is in the state that operation needs, else `ERR_INVALID_DEPARTURE_STATE`; this covers dispatch without an `IN_PORT` call, completion from anything but `OPERATING`, rebalancing or rescheduling a terminal departure, guest cancellation against a departure that has moved on, and check-in against a departure that is no longer live. 18: a new booking names a departure whose `status` is `SELLING`, else `ERR_DEPARTURE_NOT_SELLING`; the ticketing action additionally accepts a `CLOSED` departure so guests booked before the cutoff can still be ticketed. 19: the requested fulfillment action is declared for the booking's current pair of `fulfillment_status` and `financial_status`, else `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_ACTION`; both dimensions are consulted, so ticketing requires `financial_status` of `AUTHORIZED`, a guest cancellation requires `fulfillment_status` of `BOOKED` or `TICKETED`, check-in requires the owning port call to be `IN_PORT`, and an incident may only be filed against a booking whose `fulfillment_status` is `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`. 20: the requested claim action is declared for the claim's `status`, a claim is opened only on an incident in `FILED` with no claim already attached, an incident that already carries a claim is not closed, and a claim is not sent to a `TERMINATED` operator — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_ACTION`. 21: the requested statement transition is declared for the statement's `status`, and settlement requires a `FINAL` `basis`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_ACTION`; a `PROVISIONAL` statement can be issued, disputed and adjusted but never settled. 22: issuing a `FINAL` statement requires the owning port call to have reached `CLOSED`, else `ERR_PORT_CALL_NOT_CLOSED`.

**Operator and guest safety (23–33).** 23: the operator's `insurance_expiry_date` is strictly after the logical clock on every vetting action, and at least 30 days after it on a catalog publication, else `ERR_INSURANCE_EXPIRED`. 24: `insurance_liability_limit_cents` is at least 100000000 before approval, else `ERR_INSURANCE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`. 25: at least one safety audit exists for the operator before submission, else `ERR_AUDIT_REQUIRED`. 26: `safety_score` is at least 60 before approval or reinstatement, else `ERR_SAFETY_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR` — exactly 60 satisfies the floor and 59 does not. 27: `vetting_cycle_count` is at most 3 before a resubmission, else `ERR_VETTING_CYCLE_EXHAUSTED`. 28: the operator's `vetting_status` is `APPROVED` both when publishing that operator's excursion and when ticketing a booking on it, else `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_APPROVED`. 29: `age_at_meeting` lies inside the excursion's inclusive age band, else `ERR_AGE_RESTRICTION`. 30: the guest's mobility rank meets the excursion's requirement and no excluded medical flag accompanies a `LEVEL_4` excursion, else `ERR_MOBILITY_RESTRICTION`. 31: `medical_warning_acknowledged` is true whenever the guest's `medical_flags` intersect the excursion's `medical_warning_codes`, else `ERR_MEDICAL_ACK_REQUIRED`. 32: `language_choice` is one of the excursion's `languages`, else `ERR_LANGUAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. 33: the guest's `voyage_code` equals the `voyage_code` of the port call the booked departure runs on, else `ERR_VOYAGE_MISMATCH`.

**Deadlines (34–37).** 34: a departure's `meeting_time_utc` lies inside the port call's inclusive published window from `arrival_utc` to `all_aboard_utc`, measured against the published schedule and not the delayed arrival, else `ERR_MEETING_TIME_OUTSIDE_CALL`; this guards both scheduling a departure and rescheduling its meeting. 35: the logical clock is strictly earlier than 60 minutes before `meeting_time_utc` when a new booking is taken, else `ERR_SALES_CUTOFF_PASSED` — at the cutoff instant itself the sale is already refused. 36: `timing_risk_band_actual` is not `RED` when a departure is dispatched, else `ERR_TIMING_RISK_RED`. 37: the logical clock lies inside the inclusive window from 45 minutes before `meeting_time_utc` to 10 minutes after it when a guest is checked in, else `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

**Capacity and pairing constraints (38–40).** 38: a new tender wave's closed window shares no instant with any other non-cancelled wave on the same call, else `ERR_TENDER_WAVE_OVERLAP`. 39: a departure's meeting-point window does not push the peak concurrent group count above the meeting point's limit, else `ERR_MEETING_POINT_CONGESTED`; this guards both scheduling a departure and rescheduling its meeting. 40: the tender-wave eligibility pool is non-empty on a tender assignment, a `TENDER`-mode call has at least one `EXCURSION_PRIORITY` wave before sales open, and a departure at a `TENDER` port has a wave assigned before dispatch — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TENDER_WAVE`, which therefore guards three operations: the tender assignment, the port-call transition, and the dispatch-and-complete operation.

**Referential and liability constraints (41–44).** 41: no claim for the same operator and port call is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW` or `DISPUTED` when a statement is issued, and no claim at all is open for the operator when it is reinstated or terminated, else `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_ISSUE`; the rule therefore guards both statement transitions and vetting actions. 42: the computed `operator_charge_cents` does not exceed the operator's `insurance_liability_limit_cents` — the limit itself is acceptable — else `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_INSURANCE_LIMIT`. 43: `dispute_round_count` is below 2 before a dispute is recorded, and at most 2 before a disputed claim is returned to the operator, else `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. 44: the alternative-departure pool is non-empty whenever a cancellation asks for an alternative, and a publication's nominated alternative is itself published at the same port, else `ERR_NO_ALTERNATIVE_AVAILABLE`; the rule guards both departure cancellation and catalog actions.

**Seat arithmetic (45–47).** 45: `precruise_allocation` plus `onboard_allocation` equals `total_capacity` on both departure creation and allocation rebalance, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH`. 46: on a rebalance, `precruise_allocation` is at least `precruise_sold_seats` and `onboard_allocation` is at least `onboard_sold_seats`, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_BELOW_SOLD`. 47: a booking's `party_size` fits inside the effective available seats of its own channel, else `ERR_CHANNEL_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`. Priority 47 is the floor of the ladder on purpose: every safety, temporal and constraint objection is reported before the seat count is even consulted.

### 2.9. Path Resolution, Body References and the 404 Vocabulary

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so a request naming two nonexistent identifiers reports the outer one. Body foreign keys are resolved after the whole path, in the order the fields are declared for that operation. Each entity answers with its own code, all of them 404: `ERR_PORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEETING_POINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORT_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TENDER_WAVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCURSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPARTURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. The same code answers for a path identifier and for a body reference to the same entity type — a nonexistent `alternative_excursion_id` in a body is an `ERR_EXCURSION_NOT_FOUND` just as a nonexistent excursion in a path is.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties Across Audit, Approval and Adjudication

Two gates in this system are keyed on `X-User-ID` rather than on role, because in both cases the role is identical on either side of the gate and only the human differs.

The officer who recorded an operator's newest safety audit may not approve that operator's vetting. The operator tracks this as `latest_audit_auditor_user_id`, which holds the `auditor_user_id` of the operator's most recent audit ordered by `conducted_on` descending and then `created_at` descending, and is null while no audit exists. An approval whose `X-User-ID` equals that value is refused with `ERR_AUDITOR_CANNOT_APPROVE` (403). The gate binds the approval action only — the same officer may still submit, request information, suspend, reinstate and terminate.

The officer who opened a liability claim may not adjudicate it. The claim records `submitted_by_user_id` at creation and `adjudicated_by_user_id` when it is decided, and the two must differ; an adjudication whose `X-User-ID` equals `submitted_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This gate binds the two adjudication actions only — the same officer may still send the claim to the operator and record the operator's dispute.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Ports, Meeting Points and the Anatomy of a Ship Call

Everything begins with a port. An `ExcursionManager` registers one with a `port_code` — an upper-case UN/LOCODE-style code of 3 to 5 characters drawn from A to Z, unique across every port on file, with a duplicate refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_PORT_CODE` — a display `name`, a two-letter upper-case `country_code`, and a `docking_mode` of either `ALONGSIDE`, where guests simply walk off, or `TENDER`, where they must be ferried ashore. Two tuning knobs come with defaults. `risk_buffer_minutes` defaults to 30 and must lie between 0 and 240 inclusive; it is the port-specific safety margin subtracted from every departure's slack before the timing risk is banded. `tender_egress_minutes` defaults to 25 and must lie between 0 and 180 inclusive; it is the ship-to-shore leg charged to a departure at a `TENDER` port and is ignored entirely at an `ALONGSIDE` one.

Within a port's pier area the manager creates meeting points, each carrying the `name` printed as signage on guest tickets, a `zone` of `PIER_APRON`, `TENDER_PONTOON` or `TERMINAL_HALL`, a `max_concurrent_groups` between 1 and 12 inclusive, and a `slot_buffer_minutes` clear-down period that defaults to 10 and must lie between 0 and 60 inclusive. The buffer is appended to the end of each departure's occupation window and to nothing else — see §3.4. Meeting points may be listed narrowed by `port_id` and `zone`; ports may be listed narrowed by `country_code` and `docking_mode`.

A port call is one ship's visit. Creating it requires the `port_id`, a `voyage_code` such as AURORA-2609, a `ship_code` such as MS-AURORA, and three instants: `arrival_utc`, the scheduled first-line-ashore moment; `all_aboard_utc`, the published deadline by which every guest must be back aboard; and `departure_utc`, the scheduled last-line-away moment. `arrival_utc` must be strictly earlier than `all_aboard_utc`, which must be at or before `departure_utc`; any other ordering is `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. The call is born in `PLANNED` with `delay_minutes` at 0 and `effective_arrival_utc` equal to `arrival_utc`. Calls may be listed narrowed by `port_id`, `status` and `voyage_code`.

A `PortAgent` records arrival delay as it accumulates, supplying `additional_delay_minutes` and optionally a `reason_code`. Each report adds to `delay_minutes`, which may never be negative, and `effective_arrival_utc` is recomputed as `arrival_utc` shifted later by `delay_minutes`. The published `all_aboard_utc` is **never** shifted — the ship sails on schedule regardless of when it berthed — so a delay burns slack rather than moving the deadline. Recording a delay against a call already in `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PORT_CALL_ACTION`. Every departure on the call has its actual-basis projection and `timing_risk_band_actual` recomputed on the spot, and the recomputation can cascade: each departure that is `SELLING` and whose `timing_risk_band_actual` has just become `RED` moves `SELLING → CLOSED`, and each of that departure's never-charged bookings — those still in `BOOKED` with `financial_status` `AUTHORIZED` — moves to `CANCELLED_BY_LINE` with `financial_status` `VOIDED`, an `AUTH_VOID` ledger entry of `amount_cents` 0 on the `LINE_CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL` `basis`, and its seats returned to its own channel bucket.

The port-call lifecycle runs `PLANNED → OPEN_FOR_SALE → IN_PORT → CLOSED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from either of the first two states. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. An `ExcursionManager` opens sales, which requires the three-instant ordering above to hold and, at a `TENDER` port, requires at least one `EXCURSION_PRIORITY` tender wave to already exist on the call — without one the transition is refused as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TENDER_WAVE`. Opening sales flips every `SCHEDULED` departure on the call whose excursion is `PUBLISHED` to `SELLING`, freezing each one's `return_guarantee_eligible` as it goes.

A `PortAgent` marks arrival, moving `OPEN_FOR_SALE → IN_PORT`, and may do so only once the logical clock has reached `effective_arrival_utc`; earlier, the request is `ERR_INVALID_PORT_CALL_ACTION`. The same agent closes the call at the all-aboard deadline, moving `IN_PORT → CLOSED`, permitted only once the logical clock has reached `all_aboard_utc`. Closure is the busiest cascade of the day: every departure still `SELLING` moves `SELLING → CLOSED`; every booking still in `TICKETED` moves `TICKETED → NO_SHOW` with `financial_status` moving `CHARGED → RETAINED` and a `RETENTION` ledger entry whose `amount_cents` equals the booking's `charge_total_cents` on the `NO_SHOW` `basis`; and every booking whose `alternative_offer_status` is `OFFERED` flips to `EXPIRED`, because an unaccepted rebooking offer has no meaning once the ship has sailed.

Cancelling a call — from `PLANNED` or from `OPEN_FOR_SALE` — is always treated as an act of weather, never of operator fault. Every departure on the call in `SCHEDULED`, `SELLING` or `CLOSED` is cancelled with `cancellation_cause` `WEATHER`, and each of those cancellations drives its own bookings through the weather path described in §3.7: the entire `charge_total_cents` refunded, no goodwill credit, and seats returned to each booking's own channel bucket.

Tender waves are published on a call by a `PortAgent`. A wave carries a `wave_index` between 1 and 40 inclusive that must be unique within the call — reuse across two different calls is fine, reuse within one is `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAVE_INDEX` — a `window_start_utc` and a `window_end_utc` that must be strictly later than the start, a `guest_capacity` of at least 1, and a `priority_class` of either `EXCURSION_PRIORITY`, meaning the wave may carry excursion groups, or `GENERAL`, meaning it serves independent guests only and is never eligible for an excursion. Wave windows are **closed** intervals with zero buffer and single occupancy: a candidate wave whose window shares even one instant with another non-cancelled wave on the same call is refused with `ERR_TENDER_WAVE_OVERLAP`, and because the interval is closed, a wave beginning at the exact instant another ends does collide — one pontoon cannot serve two waves at that instant. A new wave starts with `assigned_guest_count` and `assigned_departure_count` both 0; each is maintained as the running total over the departures assigned to that wave whose `status` is neither `CANCELLED_WEATHER` nor `CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL`, the former summing those departures' `total_capacity` and the latter counting them. Waves may be listed narrowed by `port_call_id` and `priority_class`.

### 3.2. Vetting Tour Operators and Scoring Their Safety

An `OperatorVettingOfficer` registers each tour operator against the single `port_id` at which it is vetted to trade, recording its `legal_name`, a `contact_email` for operations, the `insurance_expiry_date` at which its public liability cover lapses, the `insurance_liability_limit_cents` per-occurrence limit of that cover (which may not be negative), and the `commission_rate_bps` the line retains from its ticket value, which must lie between 0 and 5000 inclusive. The operator is born in `vetting_status` `DRAFT` with `vetting_cycle_count` 0, `safety_score` 0, `reliability_strikes` 0, `liability_reserve_cents` 0 and `latest_audit_auditor_user_id` null. Operators may be listed narrowed by `port_id` and `vetting_status`.

Audits are the raw material of standing. Recording one requires an `audit_type` of `INITIAL`, `ANNUAL` or `INCIDENT_TRIGGERED`, the `conducted_on` instant at which the inspection happened — which may not be later than the logical clock, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` — a `raw_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and a `critical_finding_count` between 0 and 20 inclusive counting life-safety findings. The recording officer's `X-User-ID` is stored on the audit as `auditor_user_id`, and when the new audit becomes the operator's newest by `conducted_on` descending then `created_at` descending, that same identifier is copied onto the operator's `latest_audit_auditor_user_id`. Audits may be listed narrowed by `operator_id` and `audit_type`. A severe guest incident (see §3.8) is the business trigger for an `INCIDENT_TRIGGERED` audit: an operator suspended in the wake of a `SEVERE` incident is expected to be re-audited under that type before anyone attempts to reinstate it, since reinstatement turns on a score that only a fresh audit can lift.

**Standing is a weighted score, computed the same way every time it is touched.** Order the operator's audits by `conducted_on` descending and then `created_at` descending, and take at most the first three; if there are none, `safety_score` is 0 and nothing further is computed. Assign base weights of 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2 to the audits in that order, truncated to however many audits are in the window, then divide each base weight by the sum of the base weights actually used, so that with three audits the weights stay 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2, with two they become 0.625 and 0.375, and with one it becomes 1.0. The weighted average is the sum of each audit's `raw_score` multiplied by its renormalised weight. From that average subtract 8 points for every critical finding, counting `critical_finding_count` summed across the audits in the window only, and subtract a further 5 points for every liability claim against this operator whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED_UPHELD` or `SETTLED`. All of that arithmetic is exact; the result is then rounded half up to a whole number exactly once and clamped into the range 0 to 100, and the clamped figure is `safety_score`. Recording an audit recomputes it immediately, as does every vetting action and every upheld claim adjudication.

The vetting lifecycle begins at `DRAFT` and ends at `TERMINATED`, the only terminal state. Submitting moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and demands two things: at least one safety audit already exists for this operator, else `ERR_AUDIT_REQUIRED`; and `insurance_expiry_date` is strictly later than the logical clock, else `ERR_INSURANCE_EXPIRED`. Submission recomputes `safety_score`.

Reviewers who need more paperwork move `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED`, which has no preconditions and increases `vetting_cycle_count` by 1. The operator answers and the officer moves `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, permitted only while `vetting_cycle_count` is at most 3 — a fourth round is refused with `ERR_VETTING_CYCLE_EXHAUSTED` — and only while `insurance_expiry_date` is strictly later than the logical clock, else `ERR_INSURANCE_EXPIRED`. This resubmission also recomputes `safety_score`. The pair of edges forms a genuine cycle: `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` may repeat until the cycle cap bites.

Approval moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and is the most heavily guarded edge in the system. Four conditions must hold: the approving `X-User-ID` differs from `latest_audit_auditor_user_id`, else `ERR_AUDITOR_CANNOT_APPROVE`; `insurance_expiry_date` is strictly later than the logical clock, else `ERR_INSURANCE_EXPIRED`; `insurance_liability_limit_cents` is at least 100000000 — one million currency units of per-occurrence cover — else `ERR_INSURANCE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`; and `safety_score` is at least 60, else `ERR_SAFETY_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`. A score of exactly 60 passes.

Suspension moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` and has no preconditions of its own, but it has three separate triggers. An officer may suspend deliberately. The system suspends automatically when a recomputed `safety_score` lands below 60 — whether the recomputation followed a new safety audit or an upheld liability claim. And the system suspends automatically when `reliability_strikes` reaches 3 after an operational departure cancellation.

**Suspension is the deepest cascade in the system, and it has four hops.** Hop one is the operator's own move to `SUSPENDED`. Hop two withdraws catalog: every excursion belonging to this operator whose `catalog_status` is `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`. Hop three cancels schedule: each of those withdrawn excursions cancels its own departures in `SCHEDULED`, `SELLING` or `CLOSED`, recording `cancellation_cause` `OPERATIONAL`. Hop four settles guests: each cancelled departure drives its bookings down the line-cancellation path — `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED_BY_LINE`, `financial_status` to `REFUNDED` for charged bookings or `VOIDED` for merely authorised ones, a ledger entry appended for each, seats returned to each booking's own channel bucket, and the assigned tender wave's counters released. Because hop three records an operational cause, every money consequence of the operational branch in §3.7 applies, including goodwill credits and the settlement penalty.

Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → APPROVED` and requires three things: `safety_score` at least 60, else `ERR_SAFETY_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`; `insurance_expiry_date` strictly later than the logical clock, else `ERR_INSURANCE_EXPIRED`; and no liability claim against this operator in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW` or `DISPUTED`, else `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_ISSUE`. Reinstatement resets `reliability_strikes` to 0 and does **not** republish anything: excursions withdrawn by the suspension stay `WITHDRAWN` until an `ExcursionManager` publishes them again, and departures cancelled during the suspension stay cancelled forever. `APPROVED` and `SUSPENDED` may therefore alternate any number of times.

Termination moves `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED` and requires that no claim against the operator is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW` or `DISPUTED`, else `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_ISSUE`. Termination archives the paperwork: every excursion of this operator whose `catalog_status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW` or `WITHDRAWN` moves to `ARCHIVED`. Every vetting action bumps the operator's `version`, and each action may carry an optional free-text `note` alongside its `action`.

### 3.3. Building and Publishing the Excursion Catalog

An `ExcursionManager` composes each catalog item against the `port_id` it is sold at and the `operator_id` that will deliver it. The guest-facing `title` sits alongside `duration_minutes`, the ashore tour duration excluding transfers, which must lie between 30 and 720 inclusive. Eligibility is declared up front: `min_age` between 0 and 21 inclusive, an optional `max_age` that is either absent (meaning no ceiling) or an integer between 0 and 120 inclusive, and a `mobility_level` of `LEVEL_1`, `LEVEL_2`, `LEVEL_3` or `LEVEL_4`. An optional `medical_warning_codes` array, empty by default, draws from `CARDIAC`, `RESPIRATORY`, `PREGNANCY`, `MOTION_SICKNESS` and `ALTITUDE`. A non-empty `languages` array declares the guide languages offered, drawn from `EN`, `ES`, `DE`, `FR`, `IT`, `PT` and `ZH`. Commercially, `base_price_cents` is the per-guest list price before any adjustment and must be at least 100, and `price_tier` is one of `VALUE`, `STANDARD`, `PREMIUM` or `SIGNATURE`. Finally, `is_line_operated` declares whether the cruise line itself runs the tour; the read-only `return_guarantee` mirrors it exactly, because the line guarantees a return before all-aboard only for its own excursions.

An excursion may nominate a fallback through `alternative_excursion_id`, a self-reference to another catalog item offered to guests whose departure of this excursion gets cancelled; it defaults to absent. The item is created in `catalog_status` `DRAFT` with `published_at` null. Catalog items may be listed narrowed by `port_id`, `operator_id`, `alternative_excursion_id`, `catalog_status` and `price_tier`.

While an item is still in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_REVIEW`, the manager may amend `title`, `duration_minutes`, `min_age`, `max_age`, `mobility_level`, `medical_warning_codes`, `languages`, `base_price_cents`, `price_tier` and `alternative_excursion_id`; supplied fields are replaced and omitted fields are left untouched. Once `catalog_status` has left those two states the amendment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CATALOG_ACTION`. Amending `duration_minutes` recomputes the timing projections of every departure of the item, since the ashore leg has changed length.

The catalog lifecycle is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `PUBLISHED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `ARCHIVED`, with `ARCHIVED` terminal. Submitting for review moves `DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW` and requires `languages` to be non-empty. A reviewer may bounce it back, moving `PENDING_REVIEW → DRAFT` with no preconditions at all.

Publication from review moves `PENDING_REVIEW → PUBLISHED` and demands three checks. The owning operator's `vetting_status` must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_APPROVED`. The operator's `insurance_expiry_date` must be at least 30 days after the logical clock — a stricter runway than vetting's mere requirement of live cover, so that a tour is never put on sale with cover about to lapse — else `ERR_INSURANCE_EXPIRED`. And if `alternative_excursion_id` is set, the excursion it names must itself be `PUBLISHED` and belong to the same `port_id`, else `ERR_NO_ALTERNATIVE_AVAILABLE`. Publication records `published_at` as the logical-clock instant, and it opens sales downstream: every departure of this excursion that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose port call is `OPEN_FOR_SALE` moves `SCHEDULED → SELLING`.

Republication moves `WITHDRAWN → PUBLISHED` and is deliberately less demanding: it requires the operator to be `APPROVED` and the same 30-day insurance runway, but it performs no nominated-alternative check, and it too stamps `published_at` with the current logical-clock instant. Departures cancelled during the withdrawal are not resurrected. `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN → PUBLISHED` may therefore repeat indefinitely.

Withdrawal moves `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN` with no preconditions, whether the manager asks for it directly or the operator's suspension forces it. Withdrawal always cancels schedule: every departure of this excursion in `SCHEDULED`, `SELLING` or `CLOSED` is cancelled with `cancellation_cause` `OPERATIONAL`, which in turn cascades into those departures' bookings exactly as an explicit operational cancellation would.

Archiving reaches `ARCHIVED` by three routes with materially different conditions. `WITHDRAWN → ARCHIVED` is permitted only while no departure of the excursion is in `SELLING`, `CLOSED` or `OPERATING`. `DRAFT → ARCHIVED` carries no precondition. `PENDING_REVIEW → ARCHIVED` exists only as a system consequence of the owning operator moving `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`, and it is never requested directly. All three bump the excursion's `version`, and every catalog action accepts an `action` plus an optional `note`.

### 3.4. Scheduling Departures: Meeting-Point Congestion, Tender Waves and Timing Risk

A departure is one operating instance of a catalog item on one call. The `ExcursionManager` supplies the `excursion_id`, the `port_call_id`, the `meeting_point_id` where the group assembles, the `meeting_time_utc` at which it assembles, a `dispatch_dwell_minutes` between 5 and 120 inclusive describing how long the group occupies the meeting point after the meeting time while it loads, a `total_capacity` between 1 and 500 inclusive, and the channel split of `precruise_allocation` and `onboard_allocation`, neither of which may be negative. The `meeting_time_utc` must fall inside the port call's inclusive published window from `arrival_utc` to `all_aboard_utc` — measured against the published schedule, not against a delayed `effective_arrival_utc` — else `ERR_MEETING_TIME_OUTSIDE_CALL`. Departures may be listed narrowed by `excursion_id`, `port_call_id`, `meeting_point_id`, `tender_wave_id` and `status`.

**Meeting-point occupancy is an aggregate test over half-open windows.** A departure occupies its meeting point from 15 minutes before its `meeting_time_utc` — the setup period — up to but not including the instant `dispatch_dwell_minutes` plus the meeting point's `slot_buffer_minutes` after its `meeting_time_utc`. The clear-down buffer is added at the end of the window only; the 15-minute setup period extends the start and is not a buffer. The peers considered are every other departure sharing the same `meeting_point_id` whose `status` is none of `CANCELLED_WEATHER`, `CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL` and `COMPLETED` — a cancelled or completed group frees its slot. Two windows collide when they share at least one instant, so a window that ends at exactly the instant another begins does not collide. The check is not pairwise: sweep the candidate's window together with the peers' windows and take the greatest number of windows covering any single instant, counting the candidate itself. If that peak exceeds the meeting point's `max_concurrent_groups`, the request is refused with `ERR_MEETING_POINT_CONGESTED` — the violation belongs to the group that pushes the peak over the limit, and no individual pair need be in breach.

A new departure lands in `status` `SCHEDULED`, or straight into `SELLING` when its excursion is already `PUBLISHED` and its port call is already `OPEN_FOR_SALE`. Its `tender_wave_id` starts absent, `precruise_sold_seats` and `onboard_sold_seats` start at 0, `seats_available_total` starts equal to `total_capacity`, both effective channel availability figures are computed, and both timing projections, both slack figures and both risk bands are computed. `cancellation_cause` and `actual_return_utc` are both absent until the relevant event occurs.

**Timing risk is projected twice, from two different clocks.** First compute the egress leg: at an `ALONGSIDE` port it is zero minutes; at a `TENDER` port it is the port's `tender_egress_minutes`, plus a further 20 minutes of penalty when the departure has no `tender_wave_id` assigned, because an unscheduled group waits for whatever boat it can get. The ashore leg is then the excursion's `duration_minutes` plus 30 minutes of transfer buffer — 15 minutes at each end — plus the egress leg.

On the **scheduled basis**, the group is assumed to start at its ticketed `meeting_time_utc`, ignoring both the call's accumulated `delay_minutes` and the assigned wave's landing window entirely. `projected_return_scheduled_utc` is that start advanced by the ashore leg. `slack_minutes_scheduled` is the number of minutes from `projected_return_scheduled_utc` to the call's `all_aboard_utc`, less the port's `risk_buffer_minutes`; it may be negative.

On the **actual basis**, the ticketed meeting time does not move when the ship berths late — tickets are not reissued — but the group cannot start before its tender wave has landed, and the tender schedule does slip with the ship. So when a wave is assigned, the start is the later of `meeting_time_utc` and the wave's `window_end_utc` advanced by the call's `delay_minutes`; when no wave is assigned, the start is `meeting_time_utc` advanced by `delay_minutes`. `projected_return_actual_utc` is that start advanced by the same ashore leg, and `slack_minutes_actual` is the minutes from it to `all_aboard_utc`, less `risk_buffer_minutes`. Because `all_aboard_utc` never moves, every minute of delay is a minute of slack lost.

Both slack figures are banded identically into `timing_risk_band_scheduled` and `timing_risk_band_actual`: a slack of 0 or fewer minutes is `RED`, a slack of 45 or fewer minutes but more than 0 is `AMBER`, and anything above 45 minutes is `GREEN`. The two bands do different jobs. `timing_risk_band_scheduled` gates the return guarantee and nothing else. `timing_risk_band_actual` gates sales and dispatch. Both are recomputed when the departure is created, when a tender wave is assigned, when a delay is recorded on the call, and on every read of the departure.

The scheduled band's one job happens at the `SCHEDULED → SELLING` transition, which freezes `return_guarantee_eligible` to true exactly when the excursion's `return_guarantee` is true and `timing_risk_band_scheduled` is not `RED` at that instant. It is frozen thereafter and is never re-frozen — not by a reschedule, not by a delay, not by a republication.

**Tender assignment picks a wave by policy, not by the caller's choice.** The eligible pool is every wave on the departure's own port call whose `priority_class` is `EXCURSION_PRIORITY`, whose `window_end_utc` is at or before the departure's `meeting_time_utc` (a wave ending exactly at the meeting time is eligible), and whose `assigned_guest_count` plus the departure's whole `total_capacity` is at most its `guest_capacity` (an exact fit is eligible). `GENERAL` waves are never eligible, and partial fits do not exist — the wave must take the whole departure. Among the eligible pool the wave with the latest `window_end_utc` wins, because it keeps the group waiting on the pier for the shortest time; ties are broken by the lower `wave_index`, and then by the earlier `created_at`. A wave's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. If the pool is empty, the assignment is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TENDER_WAVE` and the departure keeps no wave — and therefore keeps carrying the 20-minute unassigned penalty in both projections. On success the departure's `tender_wave_id` becomes the winner's identifier, the winner's `assigned_guest_count` grows by the departure's `total_capacity` and its `assigned_departure_count` by 1, and both projections are recomputed — assigning a wave removes the 20-minute penalty and may simultaneously floor the actual-basis start. Reassignment is permitted and is not additive: the previously assigned wave's `assigned_guest_count` and `assigned_departure_count` are decremented first.

Rescheduling a departure's meeting supplies a new `meeting_time_utc` and optionally a new `dispatch_dwell_minutes`. It is refused unless the departure's `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `SELLING`, it re-checks the port call's published window and the meeting-point congestion sweep, and it recomputes both projections and both bands. An assigned tender wave whose `window_end_utc` now falls after the new meeting time no longer satisfies the eligibility rule, so it is released — its counters decremented and the departure's `tender_wave_id` cleared.

**Sales close on a departure by three different routes, and one of them is a trap.** A departure moves `SELLING → CLOSED` when its port call moves `IN_PORT → CLOSED`; when a recorded delay pushes its `timing_risk_band_actual` to `RED`; or when the logical clock reaches 60 minutes before its `meeting_time_utc`. That last, clock-driven closure is evaluated on every read of the departure, on every write to the owning port call, and on every write to the departure itself — but explicitly **not** when a booking is attempted. A booking attempt inside the final 60 minutes therefore leaves the stored `status` untouched at `SELLING` and is refused with `ERR_SALES_CUTOFF_PASSED` rather than `ERR_DEPARTURE_NOT_SELLING`; the closure then materialises on the next read or port-call write. Whichever route triggers it, closure cancels only the never-charged bookings: every booking on the departure whose `fulfillment_status` is `BOOKED` moves `BOOKED → CANCELLED_BY_LINE` with `financial_status` moving `AUTHORIZED → VOIDED`, an `AUTH_VOID` ledger entry of `amount_cents` 0 on the `LINE_CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL` `basis`, and its seats returned to its own channel bucket. Bookings already in `TICKETED` survive closure and go on to check-in.

### 3.5. Selling Seats: Eligibility Screening, Pricing and the Two Channels

Before a guest can book, the `ShoreExcursionDesk` registers them with a `full_name` as manifested, a `birth_date` recorded at midnight UTC, the `voyage_code` they are sailing, a declared `mobility_level` of `WHEELCHAIR`, `ASSISTED` or `FULL`, a `language_pref` drawn from the same seven languages the catalog uses, an optional `medical_flags` array defaulting to empty and drawn from `CARDIAC`, `RESPIRATORY`, `PREGNANCY`, `MOTION_SICKNESS` and `ALTITUDE`, and an optional `loyalty_tier` of `NONE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `PLATINUM`, defaulting to `NONE`. Guests may be listed narrowed by `voyage_code` and `loyalty_tier`.

A booking names the `departure_id` and the `guest_id` of the lead guest whose age, mobility and medical flags gate the whole party, a `party_size` between 1 and 20 inclusive, a `channel` of `PRE_CRUISE` or `ONBOARD`, a `language_choice` from the same seven-language set, the `expected_departure_version` demanded by §2.7, and an optional `medical_warning_acknowledged` boolean that defaults to false. The guest's `voyage_code` must equal the `voyage_code` of the port call the departure runs on, else `ERR_VOYAGE_MISMATCH` — nobody books ashore time on a voyage they are not sailing. Bookings may be listed narrowed by `departure_id`, `guest_id`, `alternative_departure_id`, `fulfillment_status`, `financial_status` and `channel`.

**Eligibility screening has four gates.** Age first: `age_at_meeting` is the whole years attained by the calendar date of the departure's `meeting_time_utc`, computed in UTC with no time-of-day component. Take the meeting date's year minus the birth date's year, then subtract one if the meeting date's month-and-day pair falls before the birth date's month-and-day pair — so a guest whose birthday falls exactly on the meeting date has already attained the higher age. The computed `age_at_meeting` is stored on the booking. If it is below the excursion's `min_age`, or if the excursion declares a `max_age` and the age exceeds it, the booking is refused with `ERR_AGE_RESTRICTION`; both bounds are inclusive.

Mobility second, as a rank comparison. The guest's `mobility_level` ranks `WHEELCHAIR` at 1, `ASSISTED` at 2 and `FULL` at 3. The excursion's `mobility_level` requires rank 1 for `LEVEL_1`, rank 2 for `LEVEL_2`, and rank 3 for both `LEVEL_3` and `LEVEL_4`. A guest whose rank is below the required rank is refused with `ERR_MOBILITY_RESTRICTION`. On a `LEVEL_4` excursion a second, absolute exclusion applies: a guest whose `medical_flags` include any of `CARDIAC`, `RESPIRATORY` or `PREGNANCY` is refused with the same `ERR_MOBILITY_RESTRICTION` even at `FULL` mobility, and no acknowledgement can waive it.

Medical warnings third, and only conditionally. Intersect the guest's `medical_flags` with the excursion's `medical_warning_codes`; if that intersection is non-empty and `medical_warning_acknowledged` is not true, the booking is refused with `ERR_MEDICAL_ACK_REQUIRED`. When the intersection is empty the acknowledgement flag is simply ignored, whatever its value.

Language fourth: `language_choice` must be a member of the excursion's `languages`, else `ERR_LANGUAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. The guest's own `language_pref` is advisory and is never validated against anything.

**The two sales channels partition the seats, and one of them expires.** `precruise_allocation` and `onboard_allocation` must at all times sum exactly to `total_capacity`; there is no slack bucket, and a violation is `ERR_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH`. `precruise_sold_seats` counts the seats consumed by bookings on this departure whose `channel` is `PRE_CRUISE` and whose `fulfillment_status` is neither `CANCELLED_BY_GUEST` nor `CANCELLED_BY_LINE`; `onboard_sold_seats` counts the same for `ONBOARD`. `seats_available_total` is `total_capacity` less both sold counts.

Pre-cruise selling expires two days out. Whenever the number of minutes from the logical clock to `meeting_time_utc` has fallen to 2880 or below, the pre-cruise release has happened: the effective pre-cruise allocation collapses to `precruise_sold_seats` exactly, and the effective onboard allocation becomes `total_capacity` less `precruise_sold_seats`, so every unsold pre-cruise seat joins the onboard pool. Before the release, the effective allocations are simply `precruise_allocation` and `onboard_allocation` as stored. From those, `effective_precruise_available` is the effective pre-cruise allocation less `precruise_sold_seats`, floored at 0, and `effective_onboard_available` is the effective onboard allocation less `onboard_sold_seats`, floored at 0. A booking is admitted only when its `party_size` is at most the effective available seats of its own `channel`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`. Note carefully that `seats_available_total` may be positive while the requested channel is exhausted — an exhausted channel refuses the sale even with free seats on the departure — and that every pre-cruise request made at or after the release is refused here.

Seat returns follow the same logic in reverse. A cancellation always reduces the sold count of the booking's own `channel` by the booking's `party_size`. If the pre-cruise release has already happened and the cancelled booking was `PRE_CRUISE`, the freed seat also changes bucket: `precruise_allocation` falls by the party size and `onboard_allocation` rises by it, so `effective_precruise_available` stays 0 and the two buckets still sum to `total_capacity`. Before the release, the allocations are untouched and the seat simply returns to its own bucket. A no-show returns nothing at all: that seat was consumed.

An `ExcursionManager` may rebalance a live departure by supplying `expected_departure_version`, a new `precruise_allocation` and `onboard_allocation`, and optionally a new `total_capacity`. Beyond the stale-version check of §2.7, the two buckets must sum to the effective `total_capacity`, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH`, and neither bucket may fall below the seats already sold out of it — `precruise_allocation` at least `precruise_sold_seats` and `onboard_allocation` at least `onboard_sold_seats` — else `ERR_ALLOCATION_BELOW_SOLD`, since a rebalance may not strand sold seats. Both effective availability figures and `seats_available_total` are recomputed afterwards.

**Pricing runs in five pinned steps and rounds exactly four times.** Step one is the tier multiplier: `price_tier` selects 9000 basis points for `VALUE`, 10000 for `STANDARD`, 11500 for `PREMIUM` and 13000 for `SIGNATURE`, and the list price is `base_price_cents` multiplied by that figure, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents.

Step two is the channel adjustment, and it is asymmetric. An `ONBOARD` booking always carries an adjustment of plus 800 basis points. A `PRE_CRUISE` booking earns a reduction of 500 basis points only when the booking is made at least a full week ahead: take the number of minutes from the logical clock to the departure's `meeting_time_utc`, divide by 1440 and discard the remainder to get whole days to the meeting, and apply the reduction only when that whole-day figure is 7 or more; below 7 the pre-cruise adjustment is zero. The channel price is the list price multiplied by ten thousand plus the adjustment in basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up.

Step three blends two discounts without rounding anything. The loyalty component is 0 basis points for `NONE`, 250 for `SILVER`, 500 for `GOLD` and 800 for `PLATINUM`. The group component is 1000 basis points when `party_size` is 6 or more, 500 when it is 4 or 5, and 0 below 4. The blended discount is the larger of the two components plus half of the smaller with any fraction discarded, and the result is then capped at 1200 basis points — twelve percent is the most any booking may ever be discounted.

Step four rounds once and multiplies. `per_guest_price_cents` is the channel price multiplied by ten thousand minus the blended discount in basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. `quoted_total_cents` is then `per_guest_price_cents` multiplied by `party_size` exactly — an integer multiple of the rounded per-guest figure, never a re-derivation from `base_price_cents`.

Step five adds the fee. `service_fee_cents` is the smaller of 1500 cents and 300 basis points of `quoted_total_cents` rounded half up; it is retained by the line and is never commissionable. `charge_total_cents` is `quoted_total_cents` plus `service_fee_cents`, and it is the amount authorised at booking and captured at ticketing. All five steps run once, at creation, and the four money figures are frozen onto the booking; nothing ever re-quotes a booking.

A new booking is created with `fulfillment_status` `BOOKED`, `financial_status` `AUTHORIZED`, `alternative_offer_status` `NONE`, `alternative_departure_id` absent, `return_guarantee_honored` true, `commission_accrued_cents` 0, `refunded_cents` 0, `retained_cents` 0 and `goodwill_credit_cents` 0. Its `party_size` seats are consumed from its own channel bucket, the relevant sold counter and `seats_available_total` are updated, and the departure's `version` is bumped alongside the new booking's own.

### 3.6. Ticketing, Check-In, Dispatch and the Return Guarantee

A booking evolves along two independent dimensions. `fulfillment_status` runs `BOOKED`, `TICKETED`, `CHECKED_IN` and then one of the terminal states `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED_BY_GUEST` and `CANCELLED_BY_LINE`. `financial_status` runs from `AUTHORIZED` through `CHARGED` and `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` to the terminal states `REFUNDED`, `RETAINED` and `VOIDED`. The two advance on their own schedules — a booking can be `CANCELLED_BY_GUEST` and `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, and later become `REFUNDED` without its `fulfillment_status` changing at all.

Ticketing is the desk's charge event. It moves `fulfillment_status` `BOOKED → TICKETED` and requires three things: the booking's `financial_status` is `AUTHORIZED`, else `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_ACTION`; the departure's `status` is `SELLING` or `CLOSED`, else `ERR_DEPARTURE_NOT_SELLING`; and the operator behind the departure's excursion still has `vetting_status` `APPROVED`, else `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_APPROVED`. That last check is a genuine cross-entity dependency: an operator suspended between booking and ticketing blocks the charge even though the booking was legitimately taken. Ticketing simultaneously moves `financial_status` `AUTHORIZED → CHARGED`, capturing `charge_total_cents` — a transition that presupposes `charge_total_cents` is greater than zero — and sets `commission_accrued_cents` on the provisional basis described in §3.9.

Check-in moves `TICKETED → CHECKED_IN` and needs the owning port call to be `IN_PORT`, else `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_ACTION`, and the departure's `status` to still be `SELLING` or `CLOSED`, else `ERR_INVALID_DEPARTURE_STATE`. It is permitted only while the logical clock lies inside the inclusive window running from 45 minutes before `meeting_time_utc` to 10 minutes after it; outside that window it is refused with `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The grace period after the meeting time is deliberately much shorter than the setup period before it.

A guest who never appears is marked a no-show, moving `TICKETED → NO_SHOW`. That has no preconditions and three money consequences: `financial_status` moves `CHARGED → RETAINED` with `retained_cents` set to the full `charge_total_cents`; a `RETENTION` ledger entry is appended with `amount_cents` equal to `charge_total_cents`, `refund_pct_bps` 0 and `basis` `NO_SHOW`; and `commission_accrued_cents` is re-rated on the no-show floor, meaning the smaller of the operator's `commission_rate_bps` and 500 basis points, because the line performed no service. Seats are **not** returned — a no-show consumed its seat. Each fulfillment action carries an `action` plus an optional `note`.

Dispatch is the `PortAgent`'s decision to send the group ashore, moving the departure `CLOSED → OPERATING`. Four conditions must hold: the owning port call is `IN_PORT`, else `ERR_INVALID_DEPARTURE_STATE`; at least one booking on the departure has reached `CHECKED_IN`, else `ERR_INVALID_DEPARTURE_STATE`; at a `TENDER` port the departure has a `tender_wave_id` assigned, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TENDER_WAVE`; and `timing_risk_band_actual` is not `RED`, else `ERR_TIMING_RISK_RED`. A projected return that eats the port's whole `risk_buffer_minutes` forbids dispatch outright, and because the band is recomputed from the live `delay_minutes` on every request, a call that fell far enough behind can no longer send anyone anywhere. Dispatch also sweeps the manifest: every booking still in `TICKETED` moves `TICKETED → NO_SHOW` with the same retention treatment as an explicit no-show.

Completion moves `OPERATING → COMPLETED` and carries the `actual_return_utc` instant at which the group actually got back. That instant must be at or after the departure's `meeting_time_utc` and at or before the logical clock; otherwise the request is `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. It is recorded on the departure, and every booking in `CHECKED_IN` moves `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED`.

**The return guarantee is settled at completion.** If `actual_return_utc` is later than the port call's `all_aboard_utc`, every booking that just completed has `return_guarantee_honored` set to false. For those bookings, and only if the departure's frozen `return_guarantee_eligible` is true, the line pays: a `REFUND` ledger entry with `refund_pct_bps` 10000 and `amount_cents` equal to `quoted_total_cents` — the ticket value only, since the tour was in fact delivered and `service_fee_cents` is retained — moving `financial_status` `CHARGED → REFUNDED`, plus a `REPATRIATION_ALLOWANCE` ledger entry of a flat 25000 cents per booking regardless of `party_size`. A departure that returns on time leaves `return_guarantee_honored` true on every booking, and so does a late return on an excursion that never carried the guarantee — the flag reflects breach of promise, not lateness as such. Completion also re-accrues every line on the final commission basis of §3.9.

### 3.7. Cancellations, Refunds and Alternative Offers

Money leaving the system always leaves through the refund ledger, and every ledger entry is written by the system as a side effect of a cancellation, a no-show, a refund or a return-guarantee breach — no operation creates one directly. An entry records the `booking_id` it belongs to, an `entry_kind` of `REFUND`, `AUTH_VOID`, `GOODWILL_CREDIT`, `REPATRIATION_ALLOWANCE` or `RETENTION`, a non-negative `amount_cents` (an `AUTH_VOID` always carries 0), a `refund_pct_bps` giving the basis points of `quoted_total_cents` that the entry applied (0 for `AUTH_VOID` and `RETENTION`), a `basis` naming the policy branch that produced it — `GUEST_INITIATED`, `LINE_CANCELLED_WEATHER`, `LINE_CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL`, `NO_SHOW` or `RETURN_GUARANTEE_BREACH` — and a free-form `reason_code` kept for audit. On the booking side, `refunded_cents` is the running sum of `REFUND` entries, `goodwill_credit_cents` is the running sum of `GOODWILL_CREDIT` and `REPATRIATION_ALLOWANCE` entries together, and `retained_cents` is `charge_total_cents` less `refunded_cents` once `financial_status` is terminal and 0 while it is still `AUTHORIZED`. Ledger entries may be listed narrowed by `booking_id`, `entry_kind` and `basis`.

**A guest-initiated cancellation is the only time-tiered branch and the only one that can retain money.** Either the `Guest` themselves or the desk on their behalf may cancel, optionally supplying a `reason_code`, and only while the departure's `status` is none of `OPERATING`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED_WEATHER` and `CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEPARTURE_STATE`. A booking still in `BOOKED` moves `BOOKED → CANCELLED_BY_GUEST` with `financial_status` `AUTHORIZED → VOIDED`, an `AUTH_VOID` entry of `amount_cents` 0 and `refund_pct_bps` 0 on the `GUEST_INITIATED` `basis`, `retained_cents` left at 0, and its seats returned to its channel bucket.

A booking already in `TICKETED` moves `TICKETED → CANCELLED_BY_GUEST` and is settled by tier. Measure the minutes from the logical clock to the departure's `meeting_time_utc`. At 4320 minutes or more — three days out — the refund is 10000 basis points and, uniquely, the whole `charge_total_cents` comes back including `service_fee_cents`. Below that but at 1440 minutes or more, the refund is 7500 basis points of `quoted_total_cents`. Below that but at 180 minutes or more, it is 5000 basis points of `quoted_total_cents`. Below 180 minutes nothing is refunded at all. In every tier below the top one the service fee is retained. A refund of more than zero writes a `REFUND` entry for the computed amount; a refund of zero instead writes a `RETENTION` entry whose `amount_cents` is the whole `charge_total_cents`. `financial_status` then follows from the arithmetic: add the refund to the booking's existing `refunded_cents`, and if the new total is zero the status is `RETAINED`, if it is at least `charge_total_cents` the status is `REFUNDED`, and otherwise it is `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, with `retained_cents` set to `charge_total_cents` less the new refunded total. `commission_accrued_cents` is re-accrued on the surviving net basis and becomes 0 when the refund was total. Seats return to the booking's channel bucket per §3.5, and the departure's `version` is bumped along with the booking's.

**A line cancellation of a departure forks on cause, and the two branches differ in three specific ways.** The `ExcursionManager` supplies a `cause` of `WEATHER` or `OPERATIONAL`, an optional `offer_alternative` boolean and an optional `note`. Cancellation is permitted from `SCHEDULED`, `SELLING` or `CLOSED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_DEPARTURE_STATE`. The departure's `status` becomes `CANCELLED_WEATHER` or `CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL` accordingly and `cancellation_cause` records `WEATHER` or `OPERATIONAL`. Cancelling a departure that never sold anything simply releases the assigned tender wave's counters, since there are no bookings to cascade into.

Both branches converge on the same skeleton. Every booking whose `fulfillment_status` is `BOOKED`, `TICKETED` or `CHECKED_IN` moves to `CANCELLED_BY_LINE`. A booking whose `financial_status` was `CHARGED` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` moves to `REFUNDED` with a `REFUND` entry of `refund_pct_bps` 10000 and `amount_cents` equal to `charge_total_cents` less whatever `refunded_cents` the booking had already accumulated, so the guest ends up whole including the service fee. A booking whose `financial_status` was `AUTHORIZED` moves to `VOIDED` with an `AUTH_VOID` entry of `amount_cents` 0. A booking that had already been cancelled by the guest keeps its `fulfillment_status` of `CANCELLED_BY_GUEST` untouched, but if its `financial_status` was `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` the retained remainder is refunded too, moving `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED → REFUNDED` with a `REFUND` entry for `charge_total_cents` less `refunded_cents`; a booking already `RETAINED`, `REFUNDED` or `VOIDED` is left entirely alone. In both branches seats return to each booking's own channel bucket and `seats_available_total` is recomputed, the assigned tender wave's `assigned_guest_count` is reduced by the departure's `total_capacity` where a wave was assigned, every booking line's `commission_accrued_cents` is set to 0, and any draft commission statement in scope is recomputed.

The `WEATHER` branch stops there: no goodwill credit, no reliability strike for the operator, no settlement penalty. The `OPERATIONAL` branch adds all three, because the operator is at fault. Every booking that had reached `CHARGED` additionally receives a `GOODWILL_CREDIT` entry of 1000 basis points of `quoted_total_cents` rounded half up, on the `LINE_CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL` `basis` — but a booking the guest had already walked away from receives no goodwill, since the guest had already left before the operator failed. The operator's `reliability_strikes` increases by 1, and when it reaches 3 the operator moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED`, which itself starts the four-hop suspension cascade of §3.2. And an operational penalty of 2000 basis points of the gross ticket value forgone on this departure, rounded half up, is recorded for settlement, where it lands in `operational_penalty_cents`.

**An alternative offer is selected by policy when the cancellation asks for one.** When `offer_alternative` is true, each cancelled booking receives an `alternative_departure_id` and its `alternative_offer_status` becomes `OFFERED`. A candidate departure is sellable for a given booking when it is not the cancelled departure itself, sits on the same port call, has `status` `SELLING`, belongs to an excursion whose `catalog_status` is `PUBLISHED`, has `seats_available_total` at least the booking's `party_size`, has a `meeting_time_utc` at least 15 minutes ahead of the logical clock so the group can be re-briefed, and passes every hard gate of the eligibility screen in §3.5 for that booking's guest. Ranking then works in two stages. If the cancelled excursion nominated an `alternative_excursion_id` and any sellable candidate belongs to that excursion, the nominated candidates win outright and the price comparison never runs; among them the earliest `meeting_time_utc` wins, then the earliest `created_at`. Otherwise every sellable candidate competes on price distance: the winner is the one whose excursion's `base_price_cents` is closest in absolute terms to the cancelled excursion's `base_price_cents`, with ties broken by the earliest `meeting_time_utc` and then the earliest `created_at`. A departure's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. If the sellable pool is empty, the whole cancellation is refused with `ERR_NO_ALTERNATIVE_AVAILABLE` rather than half-applied, so the caller can retry without asking for an alternative. The offer reserves nothing; it merely flips to `EXPIRED` when the port call closes.

### 3.8. Guest Incidents and Operator Liability Claims

The desk files an incident against a booking that actually reached the shore — a booking whose `fulfillment_status` is `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`; against anything else the filing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_ACTION`. The report carries a `category` of `INJURY`, `MEDICAL`, `PROPERTY_LOSS`, `LEFT_BEHIND` or `SERVICE_FAILURE`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, the `occurred_utc` instant which may not be later than the logical clock (else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`), and a `narrative` giving the reporter's account. The filing desk agent's `X-User-ID` is stored as `filed_by_user_id`, and the incident is created in `status` `FILED`. Incidents may be listed narrowed by `booking_id`, `status` and `severity`.

An incident's lifecycle is short. From `FILED` the `RiskOfficer` either closes it without a claim, moving `FILED → CLOSED_NO_CLAIM` and optionally recording a `resolution_note`, or opens a claim, moving `FILED → CLAIM_OPENED`. A claim may only be opened when no claim already exists for the incident, and an incident that already carries a claim may not be closed; both breaches are `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_ACTION`. Thereafter the incident's `status` is driven entirely by its claim: it becomes `CLOSED_UPHELD` when the claim is adjudicated in the guest's favour and `CLOSED_DENIED` when it is not. `CLOSED_NO_CLAIM`, `CLOSED_UPHELD` and `CLOSED_DENIED` are all terminal.

Opening a claim requires only a `claimed_amount_cents` of at least 1 and a `basis_code` of `NEGLIGENCE`, `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`, `GUIDE_CONDUCT`, `ROUTE_DEVIATION` or `MEDICAL_RESPONSE`. The caller does not name the operator: `operator_id` and `port_call_id` are both resolved by the server, tracing the incident's `booking_id` to its booking, that booking's `departure_id` to its departure, and from there to the departure's `excursion_id` and its excursion's `operator_id` for the operator, and to the departure's `port_call_id` for the call. The claim is created in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` from the header, `dispute_round_count` 0, and `liability_share_bps`, `settlement_amount_cents`, `operator_charge_cents` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` all at their zero or absent defaults. Claims may be listed narrowed by `incident_id`, `operator_id`, `port_call_id` and `status`.

The claim lifecycle is cyclic in its middle. Sending the claim to the operator moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW`, permitted only while the operator's `vetting_status` is not `TERMINATED`, else `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_ACTION`. Recording the operator's rebuttal moves `UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW → DISPUTED` and increases `dispute_round_count` by 1, permitted only while `dispute_round_count` is below 2, else `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Sending a disputed claim back moves `DISPUTED → UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW`, permitted while `dispute_round_count` is at most 2, else the same `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The loop `UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW → DISPUTED → UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW` therefore runs at most twice.

Adjudication is reachable from both `UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW` and `DISPUTED`, and the two source states are treated identically in every respect. Upholding a claim requires the `liability_share_bps` share of the settlement, which must lie between 1 and 10000 inclusive (else `ERR_VALIDATION`), and the `settlement_amount_cents` gross settlement, which may not exceed the claim's `claimed_amount_cents` (else `ERR_VALIDATION`). The `operator_charge_cents` actually charged to the operator is `settlement_amount_cents` multiplied by `liability_share_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up, and it may not exceed the operator's `insurance_liability_limit_cents` — the limit itself is acceptable — else `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_INSURANCE_LIMIT`. And the adjudicating `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the adjudicator's identifier is stored as `adjudicated_by_user_id`.

Upholding then moves four things. The claim reaches `ADJUDICATED_UPHELD` and records `operator_charge_cents`. The operator's `liability_reserve_cents` grows by that amount. The operator's `safety_score` is recomputed, now carrying a 5-point penalty for this newly adverse claim, and if the recomputed score falls below 60 the operator moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED`, dragging the whole four-hop suspension cascade of §3.2 with it — an adjudication can therefore cancel live departures and refund guests, even though the `RiskOfficer` has no power to cancel a departure directly. And the underlying incident moves `CLAIM_OPENED → CLOSED_UPHELD`.

Denying a claim moves it to `ADJUDICATED_DENIED`, a terminal state, requires only the same segregation-of-duties check, moves no money, and closes the incident `CLAIM_OPENED → CLOSED_DENIED`. Each claim action carries an `action` and an optional `note`. The one remaining edge, `ADJUDICATED_UPHELD → SETTLED`, is never requested directly: it fires when a final commission statement for the same operator and port call settles, as described in §3.9. `SETTLED` is terminal, and reaching it reduces the operator's `liability_reserve_cents` by that claim's `operator_charge_cents`, releasing the reserve now that the money has actually been netted off.

### 3.9. Commission Settlement and Per-Port Revenue Reporting

Commission is the slice of ticket revenue the line keeps, and it accrues on two different bases. **On the provisional basis**, the lines in scope are the bookings whose `financial_status` is `CHARGED` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, and each line's commission is the booking's full `quoted_total_cents` multiplied by the operator's `commission_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up, with no deduction for refunds. **On the final basis**, the lines in scope are instead the bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`, and each line's basis is `quoted_total_cents` less `refunded_cents`, floored at zero; a `COMPLETED` line is rated at the operator's `commission_rate_bps` while a `NO_SHOW` line is rated at the smaller of `commission_rate_bps` and 500 basis points, since the line performed no service. Either way the rounding order is pinned: round each booking line to whole cents first, then add the rounded lines exactly. Summing the raw bases and rounding once at the end is a different number and is wrong.

The provisional figure is what lands in a booking's `commission_accrued_cents` at ticketing; the final figure replaces it when the departure completes or the booking becomes a no-show; a refund or retention event re-accrues it in between; and a line cancellation zeroes it.

A `FinanceController` drafts a statement for one operator on one port call, supplying the `operator_id`, the `port_call_id`, a `basis` of `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, and the `expected_operator_version` required by §2.7. Creating a statement supersedes rather than accumulates: any existing statement for the same operator and port call that is still in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → VOIDED`. The new statement is created in `DRAFT` with `issued_at` and `settled_at` both absent, and all nine waterfall steps computed onto it. Statements may be listed narrowed by `operator_id`, `port_call_id`, `status` and `basis`.

**The payable is a nine-step waterfall whose order is pinned.** Step one aggregates gross: with a `PROVISIONAL` `basis`, the lines in scope are the in-scope bookings whose `financial_status` is `CHARGED` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` and `gross_ticket_cents` is the exact sum of their `quoted_total_cents`; with a `FINAL` `basis`, the lines are the in-scope bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW` and `gross_ticket_cents` is the exact sum of each one's `quoted_total_cents` less its `refunded_cents`, each term floored at zero. The scope itself is every booking whose departure sits on the statement's `port_call_id` and whose excursion belongs to the statement's `operator_id`. `booking_line_count` is the number of lines that entered the gross, and `refunds_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over those lines' `REFUND` ledger entries.

Step two charges the port infrastructure fee on gross, before any commission is deducted: `port_fee_cents` is 250 basis points of `gross_ticket_cents` rounded half up. Charging it on a post-commission net instead would produce a smaller fee and a different payable, and is wrong. Step three sets `line_commission_cents` to the commission accrual for this statement's own `basis`, computed per booking line and summed as described above. Step four sets `claim_offset_cents` to the exact sum of `operator_charge_cents` over the liability claims for the same operator and port call whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED_UPHELD` or `SETTLED`. Step five sets `operational_penalty_cents` to the sum, over the in-scope departures whose `status` is `CANCELLED_OPERATIONAL`, of 2000 basis points of that departure's forgone gross ticket value rounded half up.

Step six computes `payable_pre_rebate_cents` as `gross_ticket_cents` less `port_fee_cents`, less `line_commission_cents`, less `claim_offset_cents`, less `operational_penalty_cents`, floored at zero — all exact integer arithmetic. Step seven selects the volume rebate rate from that figure, exactly once: `rebate_bps` is 150 when `payable_pre_rebate_cents` is 500000 or more, 75 when it is 200000 or more but below 500000, and 0 below 200000. Because the rate is chosen from the pre-rebate figure, the rebate never re-enters its own tier test. Step eight computes `rebate_cents` as the negation of the half-up rounding of `payable_pre_rebate_cents` multiplied by `rebate_bps` divided by ten thousand — it is stored as a negative amount, because the rebate is a deduction the operator grants the line. Step nine sets `operator_payable_cents` to `payable_pre_rebate_cents` plus `rebate_cents` plus `adjustment_cents`, exactly.

The statement lifecycle is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED`, `ADJUSTED`, and the terminal `SETTLED` and `VOIDED`. Issuing moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`, re-runs and freezes all nine steps onto the row, and stamps `issued_at` with the logical-clock instant. It requires that no liability claim for the same operator and port call is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_OPERATOR_REVIEW` or `DISPUTED`, else `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_ISSUE`, because money does not move while liability is unresolved; and on a `FINAL` `basis` it additionally requires the owning port call to have reached `CLOSED`, else `ERR_PORT_CALL_NOT_CLOSED`, since the final basis is not knowable until the all-aboard deadline has passed and every booking has reached a terminal fulfillment state.

An operator who disagrees drives `ISSUED → DISPUTED`, which has no preconditions. Adjusting a disputed statement moves `DISPUTED → ADJUSTED` and carries a signed `adjustment_cents`, permitted only while `payable_pre_rebate_cents` plus `rebate_cents` plus the proposed `adjustment_cents` is at least zero, else `ERR_VALIDATION`. An adjustment rewrites `adjustment_cents` and recomputes `operator_payable_cents` as step nine only: `payable_pre_rebate_cents`, `rebate_bps` and `rebate_cents` are left exactly as issued, and in particular the rebate tier is **not** re-selected from the new, smaller payable.

Settlement is reachable from both `ISSUED` and `ADJUSTED`, identically in both cases, and requires the statement's `basis` to be `FINAL`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_ACTION` — a provisional statement may be issued, disputed and adjusted but never settled. Settling stamps `settled_at` with the logical-clock instant, then closes out liability: every claim for the same operator and port call whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED_UPHELD` moves to `SETTLED`, and each such move debits the operator's `liability_reserve_cents` by that claim's `operator_charge_cents`. Each statement transition carries an `action` plus an optional `note` and bumps the statement's `version`.

Finally, the per-port revenue report gives finance and the catalog owners one read-only view of a port's trading, optionally narrowed to a single `voyage_code`. The scope is every port call at the named port, filtered by that voyage code when supplied, and every booking on those calls' departures. The report echoes `port_id` and the `voyage_code` filter, reports `port_call_count` as the number of calls in scope, and `booking_count` as the count of every booking ever taken in scope — nothing is excluded from that denominator. `gross_ticket_cents` is the sum of `quoted_total_cents` over the bookings whose `financial_status` is `CHARGED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, `REFUNDED` or `RETAINED`, and `service_fee_cents` is the sum of those same bookings' `service_fee_cents`. `refunds_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over those bookings' `REFUND` ledger entries, and `goodwill_cents` the sum over their `GOODWILL_CREDIT` and `REPATRIATION_ALLOWANCE` entries together. `line_commission_cents` and `operator_payable_cents` are the sums of the same-named fields over the commission statements in scope whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED`, `ADJUSTED` or `SETTLED`. `net_line_revenue_cents` is then `gross_ticket_cents` plus `service_fee_cents`, less `refunds_cents`, less `goodwill_cents`, less `operator_payable_cents`; it may legitimately be negative when the line refunded everything and paid goodwill on top. Three counts complete the picture: `cancelled_booking_count` over bookings whose `fulfillment_status` is `CANCELLED_BY_GUEST` or `CANCELLED_BY_LINE`, `completed_booking_count` over those in `COMPLETED`, and `no_show_booking_count` over those in `NO_SHOW`. From them come two ratios: `cancellation_rate_bps` is the cancelled count multiplied by ten thousand, divided by `booking_count`, rounded half up, and `no_show_rate_bps` is the same computation on the no-show count. Both are guarded: when `booking_count` is zero both ratios report 0, never an error and never a null, so a port that has never traded still reports cleanly with every money figure at 0.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — a port, a meeting point, a port call, a tender wave, an operator, a safety audit, an excursion, a departure, a guest, a booking, an incident, a claim or a commission statement — return `201 Created`.
