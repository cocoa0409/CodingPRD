# Host Travel Agency Advisor Commissions API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A host travel agency does not sell travel. It carries the accreditations, the errors-and-omissions tower, the seller-of-travel registrations and the merchant relationships that let several hundred independent advisors sell travel under its umbrella — and it stands between those advisors and the suppliers who owe them money. Commission does not arrive when a client books; it arrives weeks or months after the client has travelled, in amounts that frequently disagree with what the booking promised, and occasionally not at all. The host is the party that must reconcile the difference, decide when an unpaid receivable becomes a claim, and settle each advisor's share on a monthly cadence that must be defensible to an auditor.

Two failure modes dominate the operational reality this API is built to eliminate. The first is **premature recognition**: an advisor sees a confirmed booking, assumes the commission is earned, and is then surprised when a supplier short-pays or a client cancels after the money has already been paid out. The system therefore keeps two separate production figures for every advisor — one provisional, driven by expected commission on work in the pipeline, and one final, driven exclusively by cash the supplier actually remitted — and permits only the final figure to drive settlement. The second failure mode is **compliance drift**: a lapsed state registration, an expired E&O policy or a missing supplier accreditation quietly invalidates the advisor's authority to sell, and nobody notices until a regulator does. Compliance artifacts here are first-class records with in-force windows, a review decision trail and cross-entity consequences: losing the last valid seller-of-travel registration suspends the advisor automatically.

This document specifies the complete contract for the service, which is mounted under the `/host-agency` prefix. It covers advisor onboarding and agreement terms, compliance credentialing, client registration and client trust-account handling, booking submission across three distinct sales channels, supplier remittance tracking with receivable aging, the commission-claim negotiation cycle, monthly settlement with tiered splits, platform fees, consortium marketing-fund accrual and cancellation chargebacks, and the orderly departure of an advisor together with the transition of that advisor's book of business. Every monetary figure in the system is an integer count of cents; every temporal comparison reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, common properties, arithmetic precision, concurrency control, rejection ordering, and the cross-cutting financial and compliance principles that individual workflows in Section 3 assume without restating.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it is acting. If either header is absent, or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognized role names compared case-sensitively, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint in the Section 4 table, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `HostOperations`, `HostController`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Advisor` and `SupplierPortal`, spelled exactly as written.

`HostOperations` is the agency's operations desk. It owns reference data — split schedules, consortiums and suppliers — onboards advisors, signs their agreements, activates them, marks bookings as travelled, resolves commission claims and opens each monthly settlement run. It deliberately cannot calculate or post a run. `HostController` is the agency's controller: it calculates and posts settlement runs, pays posted statements, and is the only role permitted to move money out of a client trust account. `ComplianceOfficer` decides every credential, suspends and reinstates advisors, and is the only role that may finalize a departure and thereby re-home a book of business; it can neither onboard advisors nor sign agreements nor touch money. `Advisor` is an independent travel advisor acting for itself. `SupplierPortal` is a travel supplier acting through the agency's supplier portal: it confirms bookings, records what commission it actually remitted, and denies claims it disputes.

Identity resolution differs by role, and it determines what ownership means:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Advisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Advisor` row. Ownership checks compare the resource's advisor-bearing foreign key to `X-User-ID`: an `Advisor` row is owned when its `id` equals `X-User-ID`, a `Client` when its `owner_advisor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, a `Booking` when its `advisor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a `CommissionClaim` when its `advisor_id` equals `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierPortal`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier` row. A `Booking` is owned when its `supplier_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a `CommissionClaim` is owned when the booking it references has a `supplier_id` equal to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `HostOperations`, `HostController` or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server records it where an audit trail is required — as `activated_by_user_id` on an activated `Advisor`, as `verified_by_user_id` on a verified `ComplianceCredential`, as `calculated_by_user_id` and `posted_by_user_id` on a `SettlementRun`, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on an appended `TrustLedgerEntry` — but it carries no referential meaning beyond that record.

A violated ownership check yields `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This is distinct from `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`: the role was permitted to call the endpoint, but not against that particular row.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; it is the literal prefix `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the system read from the host machine's wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, because it describes the transport rather than a business fact.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position, defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside these bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), as is a value that is not the plain decimal spelling of an integer — a fraction, a word or an empty value all fail the same way.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending; because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock, every row minted without an intervening clock advance shares one timestamp, and such ties are broken by ascending server-side creation sequence, so the effective order is always insertion order. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as a boolean stating whether any further rows exist beyond the returned page — true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is less than `total_count`. Single-resource reads omit all four.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall time — as is every `updated_at`. Client-supplied values for either are ignored. Every client-supplied instant is likewise parsed as ISO 8601 and normalised to UTC before it is stored or compared: a trailing `Z` or an explicit UTC offset is honoured, a value carrying no offset at all is read as already being UTC, and a date-only value is read as that date's first instant; anything that does not parse is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation the server applies to that row. Where a workflow in Section 3 states that a cascade "touches" a row, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though the client never addressed the row directly. This applies to `Advisor`, `AdvisorAgreement`, `SplitSchedule`, `ComplianceCredential`, `Consortium`, `Supplier`, `Client`, `Booking`, `CommissionClaim`, `SettlementRun` and `AdvisorStatement`.

`TrustLedgerEntry` is the single exception. Trust ledger lines are immutable financial records: once appended they are never rewritten, there is no update or delete operation for them, and consequently the entity carries neither `updated_at` nor `version` — a trust ledger row exposes only `id` and `created_at` alongside its own fields.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock as the System's Only Time Source

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock for business purposes. Instead the server maintains a **logical clock**, which starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the process starts and no client has ever set it, and which is moved only by an explicit administrative capability that accepts a `to_iso8601` instant. Credential in-force windows, travel-completion eligibility, receivable aging, claim aging and every statement timestamp are all evaluated against this clock.

The clock is monotonic: a request naming a `to_iso8601` earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock to exactly its current value is permitted. Advancing the clock is also what materialises time-driven state, and the response reports how much moved: `current_utc` echoes the new clock, `expired_credential_count` reports how many verified credentials lapsed as a result, and `refreshed_claim_count` reports how many open commission claims — those in status `SUBMITTED` or `DENIED`, counted whether or not their figures actually changed — had their aging recomputed. Advancing the clock never recomputes advisor production figures, booking amounts or settlement-run totals — those follow their own triggers, described in Section 3. Supplier receivable aging is the one exception: every supplier's buckets are recomputed against the new clock date on each advance, as §3.7 restates.

### 2.6. Money Representation, Rounding and the Round-Then-Sum Rule

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; no monetary field is ever a decimal or a string. Percentages and commission rates are decimal fractions between 0 and 1 — a rate of `0.14` means fourteen percent — and rates are never rounded, only the cent amounts derived from them.

Wherever a cent amount is produced by multiplying a cent amount by a rate, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent: a computed value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. The product being rounded is formed exactly, from the decimal spelling of the rate as supplied rather than from a binary floating-point approximation of it, so a half-cent fractional part is recognised precisely where exact decimal arithmetic puts it and never lost to representation error. Every rounding step named in Section 3 is independent; two rounding steps applied in sequence each round their own intermediate result rather than deferring to a single rounding at the end.

One consequence deserves stating up front because it is the most common way an implementation diverges. When a set of bookings is rolled up into an advisor's settlement share, each booking's share is rounded to whole cents **first** and the rounded per-booking figures are then summed as integers. The sum is never rounded, and the alternative of multiplying the summed commission by the percentage and rounding once is incorrect. The two approaches disagree whenever two or more bookings each round upward from a half cent: two bookings of 100,015 cents each at a seventy-percent advisor share produce 70,011 plus 70,011, that is 140,022 cents, whereas rounding the combined 200,030 cents once would produce 140,021 cents. The former is the required behaviour.

### 2.7. Version Pinning and Optimistic Concurrency

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row, and both use the same discipline: the request body must carry a non-optional field naming the parent's `version` as the client last observed it, and the server compares that value to the parent's current `version` before doing anything else of substance.

Signing a new advisor agreement requires `expected_advisor_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Advisor`. If it does not equal the advisor's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_ADVISOR_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412) and no agreement row is written. This matters precisely because the operation is otherwise legitimate: a new agreement supersedes the advisor's existing `ACTIVE` one, so a second create against an unchanged advisor would succeed on its own terms. The version echo is what forces the caller to re-read the advisor before replacing terms it may no longer be looking at.

Appending a line to a client's trust ledger requires `expected_client_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Client`. A mismatch with the client's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412) and nothing is appended. Ledger lines are additive and a second append against an unchanged client would legitimately succeed — the balance rule only rejects a draw-down that would go negative — so this is not a state precondition in disguise. It exists to stop an operator from disbursing against a balance snapshot that another ledger line has already moved.

No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind. Where Section 3 describes an operation that is not one of these two, the request body carries no expected-version input and supplying one is an unknown field.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order in Which Requests Are Rejected

When several things are wrong with one request, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fully determined by the ordering below. Implementations must evaluate in this order rather than in whatever order is convenient, because the observable error identifier is part of the contract.

1. **Header validity** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization** — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), on every endpoint, against the authorized-role list in Section 4.
3. **Request shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint that has a request body. The body must be a JSON object, every required input field must be present, every field must parse to its declared type, every enum-valued field must carry a declared member, and no unknown field may be present. Unknown fields are rejected outright, never ignored. An endpoint that declares at least one input field requires a body: an absent or empty body is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An endpoint that declares no input field at all — activation, reinstatement, departure finalization, booking confirmation and travel completion — accepts an absent body or an empty JSON object interchangeably, but any field whatsoever inside that body is an unknown field. The same latitude extends to claim resubmission, whose one declared field `note` is itself optional: the body may be omitted outright. Settlement-run calculation and settlement-run posting declare no input field either and take that same latitude, accepting an absent body or an empty JSON object and rejecting any field inside one as unknown.
4. **Existence** — HTTP 404. Resources named in the URL path are resolved strictly left to right, so a nested address reports the outermost missing segment first; only afterwards are foreign keys supplied in the body resolved, in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint. The identifier reported names the missing kind: `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSORTIUM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPLIT_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_RUN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Ownership** — `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403), for the two entity-backed roles, on `POST /advisors/{id}/departure`, `POST /clients`, `POST /clients/{id}/trust-ledger-entries`, `POST /bookings`, `POST /bookings/{id}/confirm`, `POST /bookings/{id}/cancel`, `POST /bookings/{id}/commission-receipt`, `POST /bookings/{id}/commission-claims`, `POST /commission-claims/{id}/deny` and `POST /commission-claims/{id}/resubmit`. On `POST /advisors/{id}/departure` this band is the one place where ownership outranks a body foreign key: the path advisor's ownership is checked before `transition_advisor_id` is resolved, so an `Advisor` addressing another advisor's departure reports `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` even when the named successor resolves to nothing.
6. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_ADVISOR_VERSION_STALE` or `ERR_CLIENT_VERSION_STALE` (412), on `POST /advisors/{id}/agreements` and `POST /clients/{id}/trust-ledger-entries` respectively.
7. **Parent run posting status** — `ERR_RUN_NOT_POSTED` (409), on `POST /advisor-statements/{id}/pay` only. This deliberately outranks the generic state check below, so that a statement whose run has not been posted reports the run's problem rather than the statement's own `DRAFT` status.
8. **Own-state validity** — HTTP 409. The target row must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. This band covers `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_ACTIVE` and `ERR_INVALID_ADVISOR_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMMISSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE` and `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`, and it applies to every state-transition endpoint plus booking submission and client registration.
9. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409), on `POST /settlement-runs/{id}/post` only.
10. **Compliance gates**, in this internal order: seller-of-travel validity (`ERR_SELLER_OF_TRAVEL_INVALID`, 422) on `POST /bookings`, `POST /advisors/{id}/activate` and `POST /advisors/{id}/reinstate`; then E&O sufficiency (`ERR_EO_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, 422) on `POST /advisors/{id}/activate`, `POST /advisors/{id}/reinstate` and `POST /bookings`; then training certification (`ERR_TRAINING_CERT_MISSING`, 422) on `POST /advisors/{id}/activate`; then the payment-processing regime check (`ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_REQUIRED` or `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_NOT_PERMITTED`, 422) on `POST /bookings`.
11. **Referential coherence** — credential shape coherence (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_FIELDS_INCOHERENT`, 422) on `POST /advisors/{id}/credentials`, then supplier accreditation (`ERR_SUPPLIER_ACCREDITATION_MISSING`, 422) on `POST /bookings`.
12. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_ADVISOR_EMAIL_DUPLICATE` on `POST /advisors`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` on `POST /settlement-runs`, and `ERR_TRANSITION_ADVISOR_INVALID` on `POST /advisors/{id}/departure`, all HTTP 409.
13. **Temporal conflicts** — credential coverage overlap (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`, 409) on `POST /advisors/{id}/credentials`; then travel-window capacity (`ERR_TRAVEL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409) on `POST /bookings`; then simple range sanity (`ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, 422) on `POST /bookings`, `POST /advisors/{id}/credentials` and `POST /bookings/{id}/commission-receipt`.
14. **Amount and round-count constraints** — `ERR_CLAIM_ROUND_LIMIT` (409) on `POST /commission-claims/{id}/resubmit`; `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422) on `POST /bookings/{id}/commission-claims`; `ERR_CLAIM_RESOLUTION_INVALID` (422) on `POST /commission-claims/{id}/resolve`; `ERR_RECEIPT_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED` (422) on `POST /bookings/{id}/commission-receipt`.
15. **Trust-ledger authority and solvency** — `ERR_TRUST_DIRECTION_FORBIDDEN` (403) then `ERR_TRUST_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT` (402), both on `POST /clients/{id}/trust-ledger-entries`; and `ERR_TRUST_DEPOSIT_SHORTFALL` (402) on `POST /bookings/{id}/confirm`.
16. **Fiscal resolvability and closure rules** — `ERR_SPLIT_TIER_UNRESOLVED` (422) on `POST /settlement-runs/{id}/calculate` and `POST /bookings`, and equally on any other operation that seeds or re-derives a split tier as part of its own work or its cascade, `POST /advisors/{id}/activate`, `POST /advisors/{id}/reinstate`, `POST /advisors/{id}/departure/finalize`, `POST /bookings/{id}/confirm`, `POST /bookings/{id}/cancel`, `POST /bookings/{id}/commission-receipt` and `POST /commission-claims/{id}/resolve` among them; `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` (409) on `POST /advisor-statements/{id}/pay`; `ERR_DEPARTURE_BLOCKED` (409) on `POST /advisors/{id}/departure/finalize`; `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (422) on the clock capability.

A rejected request has no effect whatsoever: no row is created, no counter moves, no `version` increments. Requests that never reach a declared endpoint at all are answered in the same error envelope with an `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, but at the transport's own status rather than 400: an unrouted path yields HTTP 404 and a declared path addressed with an undeclared method yields HTTP 405. A trailing slash is not significant — every route matches with or without one.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Several operations in this system reach far beyond the row the caller addressed. Two chains are deep enough to be named explicitly and are referred to throughout Section 3 as the **chargeback cascade** and the **departure cascade**. Both are strictly ordered, and each link is caused by the preceding link rather than by the original request — which matters because a link fires only when its cause actually happened. A supplier that does not cross its probation threshold stops the chargeback cascade at its second link; a client whose owner did not actually change stops the departure cascade at its third.

Derived fields are never client-supplied. Any computed or system-sourced field named in Section 3 is written by the server from the stated inputs, and a value for it in a request body is an unknown field. Where a derived total exists on a parent row — a supplier's aging buckets, a consortium's marketing fund balance, a settlement run's monetary totals, an advisor's production and lifetime accumulators — it is recomputed from its children rather than incremented in place, except where this document explicitly says a field is *replaced* or *increased by* a specific amount. Recomputing a derived figure is not by itself a mutation of the row that carries it: where a booking, receipt or claim event refreshes the owning advisor's production figures and split tiers as a by-product, the advisor's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they were. An advisor's `version` moves only where this document says the advisor row is itself transitioned or is explicitly touched — as the cascade links of §3.6 and §3.9 and the settlement posting of §3.8 say in so many words.

Two conserved quantities must hold at every observable moment. First, a client's `trust_balance_cents` always equals the sum of its ledger lines' signed amounts and is never negative. Second, an advisor's recognized share — `lifetime_settled_share_cents` minus `lifetime_chargeback_cents` — always equals the sum, across that advisor's statements in status `POSTED` or `PAID`, of each statement's `advisor_share_cents` minus its `chargeback_cents`; statements still in `DRAFT` are excluded from both sides. Settlement posting is the forward action that credits share, and the chargeback path is the compensating action that debits it, reversing exactly the cents that were paid rather than recomputing from today's tier. When a debit exceeds a period's share the remainder is carried forward and recognized by the following run, so the identity defers but never drifts.

### 2.10. Dual-Basis Production Accounting

Advisor production is tracked on two bases simultaneously, and confusing them is the most consequential modelling error available in this domain. The **provisional** basis answers "what has this advisor sold this year?" and the **final** basis answers "what has this advisor actually been paid for this year?". Both are calendar-year-to-date figures, both are stored on the advisor row, and each drives its own split tier — `production_ytd_provisional_cents` drives `split_tier_provisional`, and `production_ytd_final_cents` drives `split_tier_final`.

Only the final basis may drive settlement. `split_tier_final` is the tier the settlement engine applies; `split_tier_provisional` exists to show advisors their pipeline standing and may legitimately sit one or more tiers above the settlement tier. For identical booking data the two figures return different integers whenever any booking is unpaid, short-paid, or has a travel start in a different calendar year from the month the commission was received. Both tier fields default to 1 on a newly onboarded advisor and both production figures default to 0.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties Across the Settlement Cycle

The monthly settlement cycle is split across two roles on purpose. `HostOperations` opens a run; `HostController` calculates it and posts it; `HostController` also pays the resulting statements. Within `HostController`, the control tightens further: the `X-User-ID` that calculated a run may not be the `X-User-ID` that posts it. The server records the calculating operator in `calculated_by_user_id` at calculate time and the posting operator in `posted_by_user_id` at post time, and a post attempt whose `X-User-ID` equals the recorded `calculated_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Compliance decisions are similarly walled off: only `ComplianceOfficer` may verify, reject or revoke a credential, suspend or reinstate an advisor, or finalize a departure.

### 2.12. Seller-of-Travel Regimes and Trust-Handling Obligations

Certain states require a travel seller to hold client funds in a dedicated trust account rather than sweeping them into operating cash. The system encodes this as a derived obligation rather than an operator choice: an advisor's `trust_account_required` is true when the advisor's `seller_of_travel_state` is one of California, Florida, Hawaii, Iowa or Washington — the two-letter codes `CA`, `FL`, `HI`, `IA` and `WA` — and false for every other state. It is recomputed whenever `seller_of_travel_state` is supplied, at advisor creation and again when a draft advisor is activated, and it depends on nothing but that single field. A value supplied by a client for `trust_account_required` is ignored.

The obligation is then enforced at the point of sale, as described with booking submission in Section 3. Every advisor's `seller_of_travel_state` must be exactly two uppercase letters, as must every client's `home_state_code` and every registration credential's `jurisdiction_state_code`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows the money: first the reference data that defines how commission is split, then the advisor and the compliance artifacts that authorize selling, then clients and their trust funds, then the booking itself through pricing, fulfilment and collection, then monthly settlement, and finally the departure of an advisor. Field names, thresholds and rejection conditions appear where the business event that uses them is described; a reader assembling one endpoint's full behaviour will generally need two or three of these subsections.

### 3.1. Reference Data: Split Schedules, Consortiums and Suppliers

Before any advisor can be onboarded, the operations desk must register at least one **commission split schedule**. A schedule carries a human label in `name` — non-empty and at most 200 characters, as is every other `name` input in this section — and two ladders. The first, `tiers`, is the production ladder: an array of objects, each carrying a `tier_level` integer, a `min_production_cents` floor and an `advisor_pct` fraction. At least one tier must be present; `tier_level` values run from 1 upward with no gaps; exactly one entry must sit at a `min_production_cents` of 0 so that a brand-new advisor always resolves somewhere; `min_production_cents` must increase strictly as `tier_level` increases; and each `advisor_pct` must be greater than 0 and at most 1. The second ladder, `platform_fee_tiers`, is the host's own fee schedule: an array of objects each carrying a `min_share_cents` floor and a `fee_pct` fraction, again with at least one entry, exactly one entry at a `min_share_cents` of 0, strictly increasing floors, and each `fee_pct` at least 0 and strictly below 1. A schedule also carries `chargeback_retention_pct`, the fraction of an already-settled advisor share that the advisor is allowed to keep when a supplier is the party that cancelled; it defaults to 0.10 when omitted and must be at least 0 and strictly below 1. On creation the production ladder is stored sorted by `min_production_cents` ascending and the fee ladder sorted by `min_share_cents` ascending, regardless of the order supplied.

A **consortium** is a buying group that member advisors join for better rates and marketing money. Registering one requires `name`, `membership_fee_cents` (the annual membership fee, which may be 0 but never negative), `marketing_fund_accrual_pct` (the fraction of a statement's post-fee share diverted into the group's marketing fund, at least 0 and strictly below 1) and `consortium_channel_bonus_pct` (an additive rate bonus earned on consortium-channel bookings, likewise at least 0 and strictly below 1). Its `marketing_fund_balance_cents` initialises to 0 and thereafter is a running total of `marketing_fund_cents` across every statement in status `POSTED` or `PAID` belonging to an advisor whose `consortium_id` names this consortium.

Suppliers are registered with their commercial terms in full: `name`, an `accreditation_scheme` of `IATA`, `CLIA`, `ARC` or `TIDS` naming which accreditation number an advisor must hold to sell them, a `base_commission_pct` and a `preferred_override_commission_pct` (both strictly between 0 and 1), a `rate_ceiling_pct` (greater than 0 and at most 1) that caps the effective rate after any channel bonus, a `max_commission_per_booking_cents` absolute cap (strictly positive), and `remit_terms_days`, the number of days after a booking's travel end at which the supplier's commission becomes due (0 or more). Three inputs are optional: `consortium_id`, which is null when the supplier belongs to no consortium's preferred programme; `program_status`, which defaults to `STANDARD` and may also be supplied as `PREFERRED`; and `probation_chargeback_threshold`, the chargeback count at or above which the supplier is demoted, which defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. The third member of the `program_status` enumeration, `PROBATION`, is reachable only through the chargeback cascade described in §3.6 and is not a value an operator sets directly: supplying it on registration is not a declared member of the input enumeration and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A freshly registered supplier has `chargeback_count`, all four aging buckets — `aging_0_30_cents`, `aging_31_60_cents`, `aging_61_90_cents` and `aging_over_90_cents` — and `total_outstanding_cents` all at 0.

### 3.2. Advisor Onboarding, Agreement Terms and Activation

Onboarding an advisor records `legal_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters, as it appears on the independent-contractor agreement), `email` (non-empty, at most 320 characters, stored exactly as supplied and compared for uniqueness by exact byte equality, so two addresses differing only in letter case are two distinct advisors), `seller_of_travel_state` and optionally `consortium_id`; the advisor lands in `DRAFT`. The `email` must be unique across every advisor row that has ever existed regardless of status — a departed advisor's address is not released — and a collision is reported as `ERR_ADVISOR_EMAIL_DUPLICATE`. A `consortium_id` that names no consortium yields `ERR_CONSORTIUM_NOT_FOUND`. At this point `trust_account_required` is derived from the home state as described in §2.12, `status` is `DRAFT`, and every accumulator on the row starts empty: `production_ytd_provisional_cents`, `production_ytd_final_cents`, `eo_coverage_verified_cents`, `lifetime_settled_share_cents`, `lifetime_chargeback_cents` and `carryforward_chargeback_cents` at 0, `split_tier_provisional` and `split_tier_final` at 1, and `activated_by_user_id`, `departure_effective_date` and `transition_advisor_id` all null. The advisor lifecycle spans `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `DEPARTING` and `DEPARTED`, of which `DEPARTED` is terminal.

Signing an agreement is the act that gives an advisor economic terms. The operations desk supplies `split_schedule_id` naming the schedule that will govern the advisor's splits — an unresolvable value yields `ERR_SPLIT_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND` — an `effective_date` instant from which the terms govern newly submitted bookings, a `post_departure_advisor_pct` between 0 and 1 inclusive, and the `expected_advisor_version` echo described in §2.7. The server stamps `signed_at` from the logical clock, sets the new row's `status` to `ACTIVE`, and moves the advisor's previously `ACTIVE` agreement, if one exists, from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`; the supersession transition is declared only where the new agreement's `effective_date` is at or after the prior agreement's `effective_date`. Where the new `effective_date` falls earlier, the prior row simply stays `ACTIVE` beside the new one, and every later reference to *the* advisor's `ACTIVE` agreement — activation's exactly-one test excepted, which then fails with `ERR_ACTIVE_AGREEMENT_MISSING` — resolves to the earliest-created row still in `ACTIVE`. The advisor's own `version` increments by exactly 1 as a result. Crucially, bookings that were already submitted keep the `agreement_id` they were pinned to and are never repriced by a new agreement: terms are version-pinned at submission time, which is why an advisor who renegotiates mid-year still settles last quarter's work on last quarter's percentages. The `post_departure_advisor_pct` on an agreement is dormant until the advisor departs, at which point it becomes the percentage applied to that agreement's bookings, as §3.9 describes. An agreement's lifecycle is `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED`, the latter two terminal.

Activation is the onboarding gate, and it is deliberately strict: everything must be in force at the logical clock simultaneously. A `DRAFT` advisor becomes `ACTIVE` only when the advisor has exactly one agreement in status `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_ACTIVE_AGREEMENT_MISSING` (HTTP 422) — and holds three separate verified credentials, each with an in-force window meaning that `effective_on` is at or before the logical clock and the clock is strictly before `expires_on`: a `SELLER_OF_TRAVEL` registration whose `jurisdiction_state_code` equals the advisor's `seller_of_travel_state`, failing which the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SELLER_OF_TRAVEL_INVALID`; an `EO_POLICY` whose `coverage_amount_cents` is at least one hundred million cents, that is one million dollars of per-occurrence errors-and-omissions cover, failing which `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`; and a `TRAINING_CERT`, failing which `ERR_TRAINING_CERT_MISSING`. Activating an advisor who is not in `DRAFT` is not a declared transition and yields `ERR_INVALID_ADVISOR_STATE`. On success `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `activated_by_user_id` records the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, `trust_account_required` is recomputed from `seller_of_travel_state`, `eo_coverage_verified_cents` is set to the greatest `coverage_amount_cents` across the advisor's verified E&O credentials — or 0 when that set is empty — both `split_tier_provisional` and `split_tier_final` are seeded from the active agreement's schedule at a production basis of 0, and the advisor's `version` increments by 1.

Suspension and reinstatement form the advisor's only cycle. A compliance officer suspends an `ACTIVE` advisor with a stated `reason` — a required non-empty string that is validated as an input but is not persisted on the advisor row or anywhere else — moving `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` and incrementing `version`; nothing else moves. No booking is touched, no `booking_status` or `commission_status` changes, and no statement is affected — suspension's entire effect is prospective, blocking new booking submissions and new client registrations. Reinstatement runs the other way, from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, and it re-verifies compliance against the *current* clock rather than trusting the state the advisor had when it was suspended: an in-force verified `SELLER_OF_TRAVEL` registration matching the home state and an in-force verified `EO_POLICY` at or above the one-million-dollar floor must both be present, reported as `ERR_SELLER_OF_TRAVEL_INVALID` and `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` respectively. Reinstatement does not re-check training certification. On success `eo_coverage_verified_cents` is recomputed, `production_ytd_provisional_cents` is itself recomputed from the advisor's qualifying bookings in the current logical clock's calendar year exactly as §3.5 describes and `split_tier_provisional` is then re-derived from that freshly computed figure, and `version` increments by 1. Suspending an advisor who is not `ACTIVE`, or reinstating one who is not `SUSPENDED`, is reported as `ERR_INVALID_ADVISOR_STATE`.

### 3.3. Compliance Credentials: Filing, Review and Lapse

Credentials are filed against a specific advisor, either by the advisor personally or by the operations desk. No ownership check applies to a filing — which is why `POST /advisors/{id}/credentials` is absent from the §2.8 band 5 list — so an `Advisor` may file a credential against any advisor id and is never refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` here. Each filing declares a `credential_type`, one of `SELLER_OF_TRAVEL`, `EO_POLICY`, `TRAINING_CERT` or `SUPPLIER_ACCREDITATION`; a non-empty `credential_number` exactly as issued, such as a California `ST-` registration number or an IATA/ARC number; an `effective_on` instant from which the credential is in force, inclusive; and an `expires_on` instant at which it lapses, treated as an exclusive upper bound. `expires_on` must be strictly after `effective_on`, and a filing that violates this is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.

Three further inputs are conditional, and this is where the credential record turns polymorphic: `credential_type` decides which of them is required and which must be absent. A `SELLER_OF_TRAVEL` filing must carry `jurisdiction_state_code`, the two-letter code of the state the registration covers, and must leave both `supplier_id` and `coverage_amount_cents` null. An `EO_POLICY` filing must carry `coverage_amount_cents`, the per-occurrence limit in cents as an integer of at least 0, and must leave `jurisdiction_state_code` and `supplier_id` null. A `SUPPLIER_ACCREDITATION` filing must carry `supplier_id`, naming the supplier the accreditation number is good for, and must leave `jurisdiction_state_code` and `coverage_amount_cents` null. A `TRAINING_CERT` filing must leave all three null. Any other combination — an E&O row carrying a jurisdiction, a registration row with no jurisdiction, an accreditation row with a coverage amount — is rejected with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_FIELDS_INCOHERENT`. A `supplier_id` that resolves to nothing is instead reported as `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, and an advisor id that resolves to nothing as `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_FOUND`.

Coverage periods for the same thing may not overlap, and here the interval semantics are **closed on both ends with adjacency treated as a collision**. Two credentials are compared when they belong to the same advisor, carry the same `credential_type`, and share the same coverage key — the coverage key being `jurisdiction_state_code` for a registration, `supplier_id` for an accreditation, and nothing at all (a single shared key) for E&O policies and training certificates. Only existing rows in status `PENDING_REVIEW` or `VERIFIED` participate; rejected, revoked and expired rows are ignored. The new filing collides with such a row whenever the new `effective_on` is at or before the existing `expires_on` **and** the existing `effective_on` is at or before the new `expires_on`, and the collision is reported as `ERR_CREDENTIAL_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`. Note the deliberate strictness at the touching boundary: a new period whose `effective_on` falls exactly on an existing period's `expires_on` *is* a conflict, because a registration period must be handed over cleanly with no shared instant. A filed credential enters `PENDING_REVIEW` with `verified_by_user_id` and `verified_at` both null, and it changes nothing about the advisor — a credential awaiting review never satisfies an activation precondition.

A compliance officer then records a `decision` of `VERIFY`, `REJECT` or `REVOKE`, optionally with a free-text `note` — which may be the empty string, may not exceed 2000 characters, and is validated but not persisted on the credential or anywhere else. Verifying moves `PENDING_REVIEW` to `VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_by_user_id` with the officer's `X-User-ID` and `verified_at` with the logical clock; verification additionally requires that the logical clock is still strictly before `expires_on`, since verifying an already-lapsed window is not a declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE`. Verifying an `EO_POLICY` row recomputes the owning advisor's `eo_coverage_verified_cents` as the greatest coverage amount across that advisor's verified E&O rows. Rejecting moves `PENDING_REVIEW` to `REJECTED` and does nothing else. Revoking moves `VERIFIED` to `REVOKED`; for an E&O row it likewise recomputes `eo_coverage_verified_cents`.

Revocation carries the system's sharpest cross-entity consequence. When the revoked row was a `SELLER_OF_TRAVEL` registration and, after the revocation, the owning advisor has no remaining credential that is a `SELLER_OF_TRAVEL`, is `VERIFIED`, matches the advisor's `seller_of_travel_state` and is in force at the logical clock, then that advisor — if currently `ACTIVE` — is moved from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` by the system, with the advisor's `version` incrementing by 1. Losing the last valid state registration therefore suspends the advisor without any operator action. What revocation does *not* do is retro-invalidate work: bookings already submitted stay exactly as they are, and only new submissions are blocked. Applying `VERIFY` or `REJECT` to a row that is not `PENDING_REVIEW`, or `REVOKE` to a row that is not `VERIFIED`, is reported as `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE`.

Lapse is the one credential transition nobody requests. Whenever the logical clock is advanced, every credential in status `VERIFIED` whose `expires_on` is at or before the new clock value moves to `EXPIRED`, its `version` increments, and — for E&O rows — the owning advisor's `eo_coverage_verified_cents` is recomputed. Because expiry is evaluated on every clock advance rather than lazily at read time, a system whose clock has never been set expires nothing spuriously. `REJECTED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

### 3.4. Clients, Client Ownership and Trust-Account Handling

Registering a travel client requires `owner_advisor_id` naming the owning advisor, `full_name` (non-empty and at most 200 characters), and `home_state_code` as two uppercase letters. The named advisor must be in status `ACTIVE`; a client registered against a `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED`, `DEPARTING` or `DEPARTED` advisor is refused with `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. Two inputs are optional and both matter later. `ownership_mode` decides who keeps the client relationship when the advisor leaves the host: it defaults to `HOST_OWNED` and may also be supplied as `ADVISOR_OWNED`. `max_concurrent_trips` is the client's aggregate travel capacity — how many live bookings may cover the same instant — and defaults to 1 with a minimum of 1. A new client's `portability_status` initialises to `RETAINED`, the only other possible value being `RELEASED`, and its `trust_balance_cents` initialises to 0.

Where the advisor's home state mandates trust handling, client money is tracked in an append-only ledger hanging off the client. Each line declares a `direction` of `DEPOSIT`, `DISBURSEMENT` or `REFUND`, a strictly positive `amount_cents` magnitude, a free-text `memo` for the audit trail — a required field which may nonetheless be the empty string and may not exceed 2000 characters, and which unlike the notes elsewhere in this document *is* persisted, on the ledger line itself — optionally a `booking_id` attributing the movement to a specific booking (null for an unallocated deposit, and reported as `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` if it names nothing), and the `expected_client_version` echo of §2.7. The server derives `signed_amount_cents` as a positive `amount_cents` for a `DEPOSIT` and a negative `amount_cents` for both a `DISBURSEMENT` and a `REFUND`, and records `recorded_by_user_id` as the caller's `X-User-ID`.

The client's balance is the running sum of those signed amounts. Reading the client's ledger lines in `created_at` ascending order and accumulating the signed amounts from zero, each line's `balance_after_cents` is the accumulated total immediately after that line, and the client's `trust_balance_cents` is the final accumulated total. Two rules protect the account. Authority first: role `Advisor` may append only `DEPOSIT` lines, since taking a client deposit is the advisor's job while moving money out of a trust account belongs exclusively to `HostController`; an advisor attempting a `DISBURSEMENT` or `REFUND` is refused with `ERR_TRUST_DIRECTION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). Solvency second: any `DISBURSEMENT` or `REFUND` that would drive the running balance below zero is refused with `ERR_TRUST_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402) and appends nothing at all. A successful append updates the client's `trust_balance_cents` to the new `balance_after_cents` and increments `Client.version` by exactly 1.

Ledger history is readable two ways — everything belonging to one client, and everything attributed to one booking — and both return lines in `created_at` ascending order. Neither view exposes an `updated_at` or a `version`, for the reason given in §2.4.

### 3.5. Booking Submission and Commission Pricing

Only an advisor submits a booking, and only for itself: the ownership check of §2.8 band 5 compares the submitted `advisor_id` to `X-User-ID` and consults no other reference, so naming a `client_id` whose `owner_advisor_id` is a different advisor is not by itself an ownership violation and the submission goes on to the gates below. A submission names `advisor_id`, `supplier_id` and `client_id`; declares the `channel` through which the reservation was placed, one of `SUPPLIER_DIRECT`, `CONSORTIUM` or `INTERNAL_TOOL`; declares the `payment_method`, one of `CLIENT_TRUST`, `MERCHANT_DIRECT` or `HOST_MERCHANT`; states a strictly positive `gross_sale_cents` total sale value; states a `deposit_amount_cents` of client money that must be on hand before the supplier may confirm, which must be at least 0 and no greater than `gross_sale_cents`; and states the travel window as `travel_start_date`, inclusive, and `travel_end_date`, exclusive. Optionally it states `non_commissionable_cents`, the taxes and port fees that earn no commission, which defaults to 0 and must be at least 0 and no greater than `gross_sale_cents`. A `travel_end_date` that is not strictly after `travel_start_date` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.

Five gates stand between a submission and a stored booking, and they are checked in the order §2.8 fixes. The submitting advisor must be `ACTIVE`, failing which `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_ACTIVE` — this is how suspension bites. The advisor must hold an in-force verified state registration matching its own `seller_of_travel_state`, failing which `ERR_SELLER_OF_TRAVEL_INVALID`, and an in-force verified E&O policy at or above one hundred million cents of cover, failing which `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. The payment-processing regime must match the advisor's obligation: when `trust_account_required` is true the `payment_method` must be `CLIENT_TRUST` and anything else is refused with `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_REQUIRED`, while when `trust_account_required` is false the `payment_method` must be either `MERCHANT_DIRECT` or `HOST_MERCHANT` and a `CLIENT_TRUST` booking is refused with `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_NOT_PERMITTED`. And the advisor must be accredited with this particular supplier: a credential of type `SUPPLIER_ACCREDITATION` whose `supplier_id` is the booking's supplier, in status `VERIFIED` and in force at the logical clock, failing which `ERR_SUPPLIER_ACCREDITATION_MISSING`. This last gate is what makes the advisor-to-supplier relationship a many-to-many one: an advisor may hold accreditations with many suppliers, and a supplier may be sellable by many advisors.

The client's calendar is then checked, and this rule uses **half-open interval semantics where adjacency is permitted**, together with an aggregate capacity. The occupants of the requested window are the bookings of the same client whose `booking_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELED` and whose window genuinely overlaps the new one — meaning the existing `travel_start_date` is strictly before the new `travel_end_date` and the new `travel_start_date` is strictly before the existing `travel_end_date`. Cancelled bookings never occupy the calendar. If the number of occupants plus one would exceed the client's `max_concurrent_trips`, the submission is refused with `ERR_TRAVEL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the comparison is strict at both ends, back-to-back trips do not collide: an existing booking whose `travel_end_date` falls exactly on the new booking's `travel_start_date` leaves the two windows disjoint, and a client with the default capacity of 1 can still hold an unbroken chain of consecutive trips.

Pricing then runs, in four ordered steps, over exactly one booking plus the supplier and advisor it references and — only when the advisor belongs to a consortium — that consortium row. No other booking participates.

**Step one** computes `commissionable_base_cents` as `gross_sale_cents` less `non_commissionable_cents`.

**Step two** selects the base rate by branching on the supplier's programme standing *and* consortium co-membership together. When the supplier's `program_status` is `PREFERRED`, the supplier's `consortium_id` is not null, and that `consortium_id` equals the advisor's `consortium_id`, the base rate is the supplier's `preferred_override_commission_pct`. In every other case — including a `PREFERRED` supplier whose consortium the advisor has not joined, and a supplier in `STANDARD` or `PROBATION` standing regardless of consortium — the base rate is the supplier's `base_commission_pct`.

**Step three** selects the channel bonus. When the booking's `channel` is `CONSORTIUM`, the advisor's `consortium_id` is not null and it equals the supplier's `consortium_id`, the bonus is that consortium's `consortium_channel_bonus_pct`. Otherwise, when the `channel` is `INTERNAL_TOOL`, the bonus is five thousandths — a flat half a percentage point granted for keeping the reservation on the host's own booking tool. In every remaining case, which is to say a `SUPPLIER_DIRECT` booking or a `CONSORTIUM` booking without genuine co-membership, the bonus is zero.

**Step four** applies the ceiling and then the cap, in that order and not the reverse. The effective rate is the lesser of the base rate plus the channel bonus, and the supplier's `rate_ceiling_pct`; this value is stored on the booking as `applied_commission_rate` and is never rounded. The raw amount is `commissionable_base_cents` multiplied by that effective rate; it is rounded half up to whole cents, and `expected_commission_cents` is then the lesser of that rounded amount and the supplier's `max_commission_per_booking_cents`. Because the two variants of step two operate on the identical booking body, the same `gross_sale_cents` yields a strictly larger `expected_commission_cents` for a co-member of a preferred supplier's consortium than for anyone else — and a supplier demoted to `PROBATION` silently reprices its whole pending book down to the base ladder, as §3.6 explains.

A stored booking pins the terms it will settle under. Its `agreement_id` is set to the advisor's currently `ACTIVE` agreement and is never re-pinned afterwards except by the departure cascade in §3.9; its `statement_id` is null until a settlement run claims it. The `booking_status` axis begins at `SUBMITTED` and the `commission_status` axis begins at `PENDING`. `received_commission_cents`, `shortfall_cents`, `settled_share_cents` and `chargeback_amount_cents` all start at 0; `commission_received_on`, `settled_tier_level`, `chargeback_recognized_at` and `cancellation_reason` all start null. Submission finally refreshes the advisor's provisional standing: `production_ytd_provisional_cents` and `split_tier_provisional` are recomputed as described below. If the governing schedule has no tier whose `min_production_cents` is at or below the resulting basis — a misconfigured ladder with no zero floor — the submission fails with `ERR_SPLIT_TIER_UNRESOLVED`.

The provisional production figure is a calendar-year-to-date sum. Taking the calendar year of the current logical clock, with the year's first instant inclusive and the next year's first instant exclusive, the provisional set comprises this advisor's bookings whose `booking_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELED`, whose `commission_status` is neither `CHARGED_BACK` nor `WRITTEN_OFF`, and whose `travel_start_date` falls inside that year window. Iterating those bookings by `created_at` ascending and adding each one's `expected_commission_cents` gives `production_ytd_provisional_cents`; because every term is an integer, no rounding occurs. Tier selection then takes the schedule referenced by the advisor's `ACTIVE` agreement — or, where the advisor holds no agreement in `ACTIVE` at all, as after a departure has moved its agreements to `TERMINATED`, the schedule of the advisor's `ACTIVE`-or-`TERMINATED` agreement with the greatest `effective_date`, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, which is the very agreement §3.8's platform-fee step reaches for; a terminated ladder therefore keeps governing the tier fields instead of freezing them — keeps every tier whose `min_production_cents` is at or below the basis — the boundary is inclusive, so a basis exactly equal to a tier's floor selects that tier — and picks the surviving tier with the greatest floor, which is unique because floors increase strictly. That tier's `tier_level` becomes `split_tier_provisional` and its `advisor_pct` is the percentage that tier implies. An empty candidate set is the `ERR_SPLIT_TIER_UNRESOLVED` configuration error.

### 3.6. Fulfilment: Confirmation, Travel Completion and Cancellation

A booking carries two independent state axes on one row, and they advance on separate triggers. The fulfilment axis, `booking_status`, runs `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED` and then either `TRAVELED` or `CANCELLED`, the last two terminal. The money axis, `commission_status`, runs `PENDING`, `EXPECTED`, `RECEIVED` and `CLAIMED`, terminating in either `WRITTEN_OFF` or `CHARGED_BACK`. Neither axis waits for the other: a supplier that remits on deposit moves the money axis while the trip is still months away, and a trip that has been travelled may still be awaiting payment.

Confirmation belongs to the supplier, acting through the supplier portal, and only for its own bookings. It advances `SUBMITTED` to `CONFIRMED`. Three preconditions apply beyond the state itself: the acting supplier's `X-User-ID` must equal the booking's `supplier_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`; the booking's advisor must be in `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `DEPARTING` status, so a booking whose advisor has reached `DEPARTED` or is somehow still `DRAFT` cannot be confirmed and reports `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_ACTIVE`; and where the booking's `payment_method` is `CLIENT_TRUST`, the client's `trust_balance_cents` must already be at least the booking's `deposit_amount_cents`, failing which the confirmation is refused with `ERR_TRUST_DEPOSIT_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 402). Confirming a booking that is not `SUBMITTED` yields `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`, and that own-status test runs ahead of both the advisor-status and the trust-balance preconditions, so `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE` wins over `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_ACTIVE` and over `ERR_TRUST_DEPOSIT_SHORTFALL` wherever more than one of the three holds at once.

Confirmation is also the moment the money axis first moves, and it forks. When `expected_commission_cents` is greater than zero, `commission_status` advances `PENDING` to `EXPECTED` and the advisor's `production_ytd_provisional_cents` and `split_tier_provisional` are refreshed. When `expected_commission_cents` is exactly zero — a booking whose `non_commissionable_cents` equals its `gross_sale_cents`, which earns nothing at all — `commission_status` advances `PENDING` straight to `WRITTEN_OFF`, skipping the receivable lifecycle entirely and never joining any supplier aging cohort, since its outstanding amount is zero. Where the `payment_method` is `CLIENT_TRUST`, confirmation additionally sweeps the deposit out of trust automatically: a ledger line is appended with `direction` `DISBURSEMENT`, `amount_cents` equal to the booking's `deposit_amount_cents`, `booking_id` set to this booking, `recorded_by_user_id` set to the acting supplier and a server-written `memo` reading exactly `Deposit swept to supplier on booking confirmation.`, after which the client's `trust_balance_cents` is recomputed and `Client.version` increments by 1. A `CLIENT_TRUST` booking whose `deposit_amount_cents` is 0 has nothing to sweep: no ledger line is appended and `Client.version` does not move. The supplier's aging buckets are recomputed, and `Booking.version` increments by 1. What confirmation does *not* do is move the booking between production bases: no final-basis figure changes here, because no money has been remitted.

Marking travel complete belongs to the operations desk and advances `CONFIRMED` to `TRAVELED`, which is terminal on the fulfilment axis. The logical clock must have reached or passed the booking's `travel_end_date`; an attempt made earlier is refused with `ERR_TRAVELED_TOO_EARLY` (HTTP 409), and an attempt against a booking that is not `CONFIRMED` with `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE` — and the booking's own status is the test that runs first, so a `SUBMITTED`, already-`TRAVELED` or `CANCELLED` booking reports `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE` even where the clock has also not reached the travel end. The money axis is untouched — whether the supplier has paid is a wholly separate question — but the supplier's aging buckets are recomputed, since a travelled booking becomes eligible for an aging cohort once its due date arrives.

Cancellation may be requested by the owning advisor or by the operations desk, from either `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED`, and it requires a `cancellation_reason` of `CLIENT_INITIATED`, `ADVISOR_ERROR` or `SUPPLIER_INITIATED`. When the caller acts as `Advisor`, its `X-User-ID` must equal the booking's `advisor_id`. Cancelling a booking already `TRAVELED` or `CANCELLED` yields `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The fulfilment axis moves to `CANCELLED`, the reason is recorded, and `Booking.version` increments by 1. What happens on the money axis depends entirely on where that axis had got to, and only `EXPECTED` and `RECEIVED` have any consequence there at all. A `PENDING` booking stays `PENDING` — nothing was ever expected — and so does a booking already sitting in `CLAIMED`, `WRITTEN_OFF` or `CHARGED_BACK`, each of which is left exactly as it stands. Every branch nevertheless recomputes the supplier's aging buckets and the owning advisor's `production_ytd_provisional_cents` and `split_tier_provisional`, because a `CANCELLED` booking leaves the provisional set whatever its money axis says; that matters most for the `PENDING` case, where the booking had been counting at its full `expected_commission_cents` right up to the cancellation. An `EXPECTED` booking moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`, its `expected_commission_cents` left in place as the historical estimate, the supplier's aging buckets and `total_outstanding_cents` recomputed as it leaves the cohort, and the advisor's `production_ytd_provisional_cents` and `split_tier_provisional` recomputed as it leaves the provisional set. A `RECEIVED` booking is the interesting case: the supplier has already paid, so the money must come back, and the booking moves to `CHARGED_BACK`. Where the `payment_method` was `CLIENT_TRUST` and confirmation had swept the deposit out, no compensating trust line is written automatically — a refund to the client is an explicit controller action, which is why an unrefunded balance can later block the advisor's departure.

The chargeback amount reverses what was actually paid, not what today's tier would produce, and it comes in two variants selected by the `cancellation_reason`. Where the reason is `CLIENT_INITIATED` or `ADVISOR_ERROR`, the full settled share is clawed back: `chargeback_amount_cents` is set to exactly the booking's `settled_share_cents`. Where the reason is `SUPPLIER_INITIATED`, the advisor keeps a retention: `chargeback_amount_cents` is the booking's `settled_share_cents` multiplied by one minus the `chargeback_retention_pct` of the schedule reached through the booking's pinned `agreement_id`, rounded half up to whole cents. The divergence is material on the same row — a settled share of 112,000 cents against a retention of one tenth yields 112,000 cents clawed back under the first variant and 100,800 cents under the second, an 11,200-cent difference decided purely by who cancelled. A booking that was never settled has a `settled_share_cents` of 0, so both variants return 0 and nothing is disturbed. The `chargeback_recognized_at` stamp is deliberately left null: the chargeback is unrecognized until a settlement run folds it into a statement, which is what guarantees it is counted exactly once.

Recognising a chargeback then sets off the **chargeback cascade**, four links deep, each caused by the one before it rather than by the cancellation request. *Link one* writes `commission_status` as `CHARGED_BACK` and `chargeback_amount_cents` as computed above. *Link two*, caused by link one, recomputes the supplier's `chargeback_count` as the number of its bookings now sitting in `CHARGED_BACK`; when that count reaches or exceeds the supplier's `probation_chargeback_threshold`, a supplier in `PREFERRED` standing is demoted to `PROBATION` and `Supplier.version` increments by 1. The recomputed count is written to the supplier row either way, but `Supplier.version` increments only where the demotion itself happens: a supplier still below its threshold, or one already in `STANDARD` or `PROBATION` standing, has its `chargeback_count` updated silently. *Link three*, caused specifically by link two's change of `program_status` and therefore not firing at all when the threshold was not crossed, reprices every booking of that supplier still sitting in `commission_status` `PENDING`: each such booking has `applied_commission_rate` and `expected_commission_cents` recomputed by the four-step pricing rule, and since a `PROBATION` supplier no longer grants `preferred_override_commission_pct` those bookings drop to `base_commission_pct`, with `Booking.version` incrementing on every row touched. *Link four*, caused by link three's rewrite of expected amounts, recomputes `production_ytd_provisional_cents` and re-derives `split_tier_provisional` for every advisor owning a repriced booking, incrementing `Advisor.version` on each row touched. Finally, if the charged-back booking had already been swept into a statement — its `statement_id` is not null — the chargeback is **not** retro-applied to that posted statement; it is accrued into the advisor's next statement, and the supplier's aging buckets and `total_outstanding_cents` are recomputed.

### 3.7. Supplier Remittance, Receivable Aging and Commission Claims

When a supplier pays, it says so through the portal, recording the `amount_cents` it actually remitted — an integer of at least 0, so a supplier may record a nil remittance and thereby fix the whole expectation as `shortfall_cents` — and the `received_on` instant of the remittance. The booking's `commission_status` must be `EXPECTED` and its `booking_status` must be `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELED`; anything else reports `ERR_INVALID_COMMISSION_STATE`. The acting supplier's `X-User-ID` must equal the booking's `supplier_id`. The remitted `amount_cents` may not exceed the booking's `expected_commission_cents` — a supplier cannot pay more than it owes — and an excess is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED`. A `received_on` earlier than the booking's own `created_at` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. On success `received_commission_cents` takes the remitted amount, `commission_received_on` takes the `received_on` instant, `shortfall_cents` becomes `expected_commission_cents` less `received_commission_cents` — a figure that is zero before any receipt is recorded and never negative, since the receipt can never exceed the expectation — and the money axis advances `EXPECTED` to `RECEIVED`. No advisor share is recognised at this point: recognition happens only when a settlement run posts.

A receipt is also the only event that moves the final production basis. That basis is a calendar-year sum built from a different set than the provisional one: this advisor's bookings whose `booking_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELED`, whose `commission_status` is exactly `RECEIVED`, and whose `commission_received_on` falls inside the calendar year of the current logical clock, with the year's first instant inclusive and the next year's first instant exclusive. Iterating those by `created_at` ascending and adding each `received_commission_cents` gives `production_ytd_final_cents`, and `split_tier_final` is then derived from it by the same inclusive-floor tier selection described in §3.5. The two bases therefore key on different dates and different amounts: a supplier that remits on deposit moves the final basis before travel while the provisional basis had already been counting the booking at its expected amount, and a supplier that short-pays contributes less to the final basis than to the provisional one.

Receivable aging is maintained per supplier and is a snapshot as of the logical clock's calendar date. A booking joins the supplier's aging cohort when its `booking_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELED`, its `commission_status` is `EXPECTED` or `CLAIMED`, and its due date has arrived — the due date being the UTC calendar date of its `travel_end_date` advanced by the supplier's `remit_terms_days`. Bookings still `PENDING` (never priced by a confirmation), fully collected in `RECEIVED`, or closed out in `WRITTEN_OFF` or `CHARGED_BACK` are all excluded, as are bookings whose due date has not yet arrived. For each cohort member, iterated by `created_at` ascending, the age is the whole count of UTC days from its due date to the clock's date — zero on the due date itself — and the amount contributed is its `expected_commission_cents` less its `received_commission_cents`. An age of 0 through 30 days inclusive accumulates into `aging_0_30_cents`; 31 through 60 inclusive into `aging_31_60_cents`; 61 through 90 inclusive into `aging_61_90_cents`; and 91 days or more into `aging_over_90_cents`. Both edges of every band are inclusive, so an age of exactly 30 lands in the first bucket and exactly 31 in the second. `total_outstanding_cents` is the sum of the four buckets, and an empty cohort leaves all five figures at 0 rather than null. The buckets are recomputed on every booking fulfilment and money-axis transition, on every recorded receipt, on every claim resolution, on every clock advance, and again whenever a supplier is read — on the single-supplier read and, for every row it returns, on the supplier list endpoint — so a supplier read always reflects the current clock.

An unpaid or short-paid receivable becomes a claim, filed by the owning advisor against the booking, declaring the `claimed_amount_cents` being sought from the supplier, a plain integer on which no bound of its own is imposed at the request-shape stage, so a zero or negative figure is caught by the equality tests below and reported as `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` rather than as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. There are exactly two entry paths and they are symmetric in their strictness. From `commission_status` `EXPECTED`, the booking must also be `TRAVELED` and the clock's date must have reached or passed the due date — that is, `travel_end_date` advanced by the supplier's `remit_terms_days` — and the `claimed_amount_cents` must equal `expected_commission_cents` less `received_commission_cents` exactly. On that path the two preconditions carry different identifiers, checked in the order given: a booking not yet `TRAVELED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`, whereas a booking whose remittance due date has not yet arrived is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMMISSION_STATE`. From `commission_status` `RECEIVED`, the booking's `shortfall_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, its `statement_id` must still be null, and the `claimed_amount_cents` must equal that `shortfall_cents` exactly. Any amount that does not match the outstanding figure, a booking with no shortfall to claim, or a booking already swept into a statement, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; filing from any other money-axis state — `PENDING`, `CLAIMED`, `WRITTEN_OFF` or `CHARGED_BACK` — reports `ERR_INVALID_COMMISSION_STATE`. A successful filing creates a claim in status `SUBMITTED` with `round_count` at 1, `advisor_id` copied from the booking rather than accepted from the caller, `denial_reason` null, `aging_days` at 0, `escalation_level` at `NORMAL` and `resolved_amount_cents` at 0, and advances the booking's `commission_status` to `CLAIMED`. Where the claim was filed from `RECEIVED`, the booking leaves the final production basis, so `production_ytd_final_cents` and `split_tier_final` are recomputed — a short-paid booking under claim is no longer settlement-eligible.

Claim negotiation is a genuine cycle. The supplier may deny a `SUBMITTED` claim with a `denial_reason` — a required non-empty string of at most 2000 characters, and one of the few free-text inputs that *is* persisted, on the claim's own `denial_reason` field — moving it to `DENIED` while the booking stays `CLAIMED`; the denying supplier's `X-User-ID` must equal the `supplier_id` of the booking the claim references. The advisor may then resubmit, optionally with a `note` — which may be the empty string, may not exceed 2000 characters, is validated but persisted nowhere, and may be left out along with the request body altogether — moving `DENIED` back to `SUBMITTED` and incrementing `round_count` by 1 while `denial_reason` is cleared back to null. The cycle is capped: resubmission is permitted only while `round_count` is still below three, and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ROUND_LIMIT`. Denying a claim that is not `SUBMITTED`, or resubmitting one that is not `DENIED`, or acting at all on an already-resolved claim, reports `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. Aging is not reset by a new round: `aging_days` is the floored whole count of UTC days from the claim's `created_at` to the logical clock and is unaffected by `round_count`, and `escalation_level` is `NORMAL` below sixty days, `ESCALATED` from sixty days up to but not including one hundred and twenty, and `CRITICAL` at one hundred and twenty days or more — so an age of 59 is still `NORMAL`, 60 is `ESCALATED`, 119 is `ESCALATED` and 120 is `CRITICAL`. Both figures are refreshed at claim creation, on every claim transition, on every advance of the logical clock, and again whenever an open claim is read — on the single-claim read and, for every `SUBMITTED` or `DENIED` row it returns, on the claim list endpoint — so an open claim always reports its aging as of the current clock. A claim that has reached `RESOLVED_PAID` or `RESOLVED_WRITTEN_OFF` is refreshed no further and keeps the `aging_days` and `escalation_level` its own resolution froze it at, however far the clock later moves.

Resolution belongs to the operations desk and closes the claim from either `SUBMITTED` or `DENIED`. The `resolution` input is either `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. A `PAID` resolution requires a `recovered_amount_cents` that is strictly greater than zero and no greater than the claim's `claimed_amount_cents`; a `WRITTEN_OFF` resolution requires `recovered_amount_cents` to be absent or zero. Violations of either shape — a paid resolution with a missing, zero or excessive amount, or a written-off resolution carrying money — are refused with `ERR_CLAIM_RESOLUTION_INVALID`. On a paid resolution the claim moves to `RESOLVED_PAID` with `resolved_amount_cents` set to the recovered amount, the booking's `received_commission_cents` increases by that amount, `shortfall_cents` is recomputed as `expected_commission_cents` less the new `received_commission_cents`, `commission_received_on` is stamped with the logical clock, and the booking's `commission_status` returns from `CLAIMED` to `RECEIVED`; the supplier's aging buckets and the advisor's `production_ytd_final_cents` and `split_tier_final` are then recomputed. On a written-off resolution the claim moves to `RESOLVED_WRITTEN_OFF` with `resolved_amount_cents` set to 0 and the booking's `commission_status` moves from `CLAIMED` to `WRITTEN_OFF`, a terminal state; `shortfall_cents` is deliberately left at its current value as the permanent audit record of the loss, the supplier's aging buckets and `total_outstanding_cents` are recomputed as the booking leaves the cohort, and the advisor's final production figure and `split_tier_final` are recomputed. `RESOLVED_PAID` and `RESOLVED_WRITTEN_OFF` are both terminal, and each resolution increments the claim's `version` by 1. Either resolution refreshes **both** production bases rather than the final one alone: `production_ytd_provisional_cents` and `split_tier_provisional` are recomputed alongside `production_ytd_final_cents` and `split_tier_final`, which is what lets a written-off booking drop out of the provisional set at the same instant it leaves the final one.

### 3.8. Monthly Settlement: Opening, Calculating, Posting and Paying

Settlement is monthly and period-scoped. The operations desk opens a run for a `period_month` written as a four-digit year, a hyphen and a two-digit month between `01` and `12`; the run covers the half-open window from the first instant of that month, inclusive, to the first instant of the following month, exclusive. Only one run may exist per period, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`. A new run begins in `OPEN` with `statement_count` and all six monetary totals at 0 and both `calculated_by_user_id` and `posted_by_user_id` null. The run lifecycle is `OPEN`, `CALCULATED`, `POSTED` and finally `CLOSED`, which is terminal and — as described below — is never requested directly.

Calculation is the controller's first act, moving `OPEN` to `CALCULATED`; a run in any other status reports `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, and calculation additionally requires that no other run for the same `period_month` has already reached `CALCULATED`, `POSTED` or `CLOSED`. Working advisor by advisor, the engine assembles two sets. The **inclusion set** for an advisor comprises that advisor's bookings whose `booking_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELED`, whose `commission_status` is exactly `RECEIVED`, whose `statement_id` is still null so a booking can never be settled twice, and whose `commission_received_on` falls inside the period window — at or after the period's first instant and strictly before the following month's first instant — iterated by `created_at` ascending. The **chargeback set** comprises that advisor's bookings whose `commission_status` is `CHARGED_BACK` and whose `chargeback_recognized_at` is still null, likewise iterated by `created_at` ascending.

Each booking in the inclusion set is then priced against the percentage that governs it, which is read from the agreement the booking was pinned to at submission rather than from whatever agreement is current. Where the owning advisor's `status` is `DEPARTED`, the percentage is that pinned agreement's `post_departure_advisor_pct` and the booking's `settled_tier_level` is left null, since no tier was consulted. Otherwise the percentage is the `advisor_pct` of the tier that the final production basis selects against the schedule named by the pinned agreement's `split_schedule_id`, and the booking's `settled_tier_level` records that tier's `tier_level`. That final production basis is not read from the stored `production_ytd_final_cents` and is not narrowed to the run's own period window: it is recomputed at calculate time by the §3.7 rule, over the advisor's `CONFIRMED` or `TRAVELED` bookings in `commission_status` `RECEIVED` whose `commission_received_on` falls in the calendar year of the **current logical clock**, so a run calculated for an earlier month still selects the tier the whole of the clock's current year has earned. Rounding order is pinned and mandatory: each booking's `settled_share_cents` is its `received_commission_cents` multiplied by its own percentage, **rounded half up to whole cents per booking**, and the statement's `advisor_share_cents` is the plain integer sum of those already-rounded figures. Multiplying the combined commission by a single percentage and rounding once is the forbidden alternative and produces a different integer, as §2.6 demonstrates. Alongside the share, the statement records `gross_commission_cents` as the sum of `received_commission_cents` across the inclusion set and `booking_count` as the number of bookings in it, and each contributing booking has its `statement_id` set to the new statement. Writing those three booking fields is a mutation the server applies to the booking row, so every contributing booking's `version` increments by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed from the logical clock.

Chargebacks are folded in at the same moment. The statement's `chargeback_cents` is the sum of `chargeback_amount_cents` across the chargeback set plus whatever the advisor was carrying in `carryforward_chargeback_cents` at calculate time, and every booking in the chargeback set has `chargeback_recognized_at` stamped with the logical clock so that no later run can count it a second time; stamping it is likewise a mutation, so each booking in the chargeback set has its `version` increment by exactly 1 too. The statement also records `tier_level_applied` as the advisor's `split_tier_final` as observed at calculate time and `advisor_pct_applied` as the `advisor_pct` of the tier that same final basis selects. Both are read after calculation has refreshed the advisor's figures, so `tier_level_applied` carries the freshly derived `split_tier_final` rather than the value the row held on entry; and the ladder consulted for this statement-level pair is the one governing the advisor's live tier standing under §3.5 — the `ACTIVE` agreement's schedule, or the settlement agreement's where none is `ACTIVE` — which need not be the schedule any individual booking of the inclusion set was priced against. The pair is recorded this way even for an advisor in `DEPARTED` status, whose bookings were each priced at their pinned agreement's `post_departure_advisor_pct` with `settled_tier_level` null: the statement still names the `tier_level` and `advisor_pct` the ladder resolves for that advisor's final basis, and never the post-departure percentage.

The host's own economics are then applied as a strict five-step waterfall, and the evaluation order is what makes the result well defined rather than circular. **Step one** takes the gross share to be the statement's `advisor_share_cents`. **Step two** selects the platform-fee tier, and it keys that selection on the **pre-fee** gross share, never on the eventual net payout; the ladder used is the `platform_fee_tiers` of the schedule reached through the advisor's agreement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `TERMINATED` and whose `effective_date` is the greatest, ties broken by `created_at` ascending. Every fee tier whose `min_share_cents` is at or below the gross share is a candidate — the boundary is inclusive — and the candidate with the greatest floor wins, which always exists because exactly one entry sits at a floor of 0. **Step three** sets `platform_fee_cents` to the gross share multiplied by that tier's `fee_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. **Step four** accrues the consortium marketing fund, and it keys on the **post-fee** base rather than the gross share: when the advisor has no `consortium_id`, `marketing_fund_cents` is 0; otherwise it is the gross share less `platform_fee_cents`, multiplied by the consortium's `marketing_fund_accrual_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents independently of step three. **Step five** is pure integer arithmetic: the raw net is the gross share less `platform_fee_cents`, less `marketing_fund_cents`, less the statement's `chargeback_cents`. When that raw net is zero or positive, `net_payout_cents` takes it and `carryforward_applied_cents` is 0. When it is negative, `net_payout_cents` is floored at 0 and `carryforward_applied_cents` takes the magnitude of the shortfall — the portion of the chargeback the period could not absorb.

The pinned order is worth demonstrating, because keying the fee on the payout is the intuitive and wrong reading. Take a gross share of 1,020,000 cents, a fee ladder of five percent from a floor of 0 and three percent from a floor of 1,000,000 cents, a marketing accrual of two percent and no chargebacks. Keying the tier on the gross share selects three percent, giving a fee of 30,600 cents; the marketing fund is two percent of the remaining 989,400 cents, that is 19,788 cents; and the net payout is 969,612 cents. Keying the tier on that net payout instead would see 969,612 fall below the one-million floor, select five percent, and produce a fee of 51,000 cents — a different and incorrect statement.

Not every advisor gets a statement. A statement is created only for an advisor whose inclusion set is non-empty, or whose chargeback set is non-empty, or whose `carryforward_chargeback_cents` is non-zero; an advisor with none of the three is skipped entirely, so a period in which nothing happened yields a `statement_count` of 0. Statements are created in a deterministic order — by advisor `created_at` ascending, then by advisor `email` ascending — and each is born in status `DRAFT` with `settlement_run_id` naming the run, `advisor_id` naming the advisor, and `paid_at` null. If the tier ladder cannot resolve a tier for a basis production amount, the whole calculation fails with `ERR_SPLIT_TIER_UNRESOLVED`. Calculation also refreshes each participating advisor's `production_ytd_final_cents` and `split_tier_final`, records `calculated_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and recomputes the run's aggregates as sums over its statements: `statement_count` as the number created, and `gross_commission_cents`, `advisor_share_total_cents`, `chargeback_total_cents`, `platform_fee_total_cents`, `marketing_fund_total_cents` and `net_payout_total_cents` as the sums of the corresponding statement fields.

Posting is the second controller act and the moment money is recognised. It moves `CALCULATED` to `POSTED`, and it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the run's recorded `calculated_by_user_id`; a run that is not `CALCULATED` reports `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. Every statement of the run in status `DRAFT` moves to `POSTED`, and each statement reaching `POSTED` causes three further effects on rows the caller never named: the owning advisor's `lifetime_settled_share_cents` and `lifetime_chargeback_cents` are recomputed as the sums of `advisor_share_cents` and `chargeback_cents` across that advisor's statements in status `POSTED` or `PAID`; the advisor's `carryforward_chargeback_cents` is **replaced** by that statement's `carryforward_applied_cents` rather than incremented, so an unabsorbed chargeback is carried exactly once; and where the advisor has a `consortium_id`, that consortium's `marketing_fund_balance_cents` is recomputed with `Consortium.version` incrementing by 1. `Advisor.version` increments by 1 for each advisor touched. `posted_by_user_id` records the poster. An empty run is not an error: a run whose `statement_count` is 0 may be posted, and it transitions straight through to `CLOSED` within the same request. The posting and that immediate closure are two separate mutations of the run row, so an empty run's `version` increments twice over the one request.

Payment closes the loop. The controller records payment of a `POSTED` statement with a `payment_reference` — a required non-empty string of at most 200 characters which, like the notes elsewhere in this document, is validated on the way in but persisted nowhere: the statement records only that it reached `PAID` and when — which moves it to `PAID` and stamps `paid_at` with the logical clock. Two state facts are checked, and the order between them is deliberate: the parent run must have reached `POSTED` or already moved on to `CLOSED`, failing which `ERR_RUN_NOT_POSTED` — this outranks the statement's own status so that an unposted run is reported as the run's problem, while a run that has already closed satisfies the gate rather than failing it, so a second payment attempt against the last statement of a now-`CLOSED` run reports that statement's own `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` and never `ERR_RUN_NOT_POSTED` — and only then must the statement itself be `POSTED`, so a statement already `PAID`, or one still `DRAFT` beneath a run that has somehow been posted, reports `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`. The owning advisor must also not still be in `DRAFT`, reported as `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. `DRAFT`, `POSTED` and `PAID` are the statement's three states, the last terminal. Finally the run's own terminal state bubbles up from its children rather than being requested: once every statement belonging to a `POSTED` run has reached `PAID`, the run itself transitions `POSTED` to `CLOSED` and its `version` increments.

### 3.9. Advisor Departure and the Transition of a Book of Business

Departure happens in two stages, deliberately, because re-homing a book of business is not something anyone should be able to trigger by accident. The first stage merely arms it, and may be requested either by the departing advisor personally or by the operations desk. It declares an `effective_date` and a `transition_advisor_id` naming the successor who will inherit the host-owned relationships. The departing advisor must be in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status — a `DRAFT` or already-`DEPARTED` advisor reports `ERR_INVALID_ADVISOR_STATE` — and the named successor must resolve to an advisor whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `id` differs from the departing advisor's own. A successor naming the departing advisor itself, or one who is not `ACTIVE`, is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ADVISOR_INVALID`; a successor id that resolves to nothing is `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_FOUND`. On success the advisor's `status` becomes `DEPARTING`, `departure_effective_date` records the supplied `effective_date`, `transition_advisor_id` records the successor, and the advisor's `version` increments by 1. Nothing else moves at all: no client, no booking and no agreement is touched at this stage.

The second stage is the compliance officer's, and it is gated on the departing advisor having genuinely wound down. Finalization requires the advisor to be `DEPARTING`; it requires that no commission claim of that advisor is still open in either `SUBMITTED` or `DENIED`; it requires every client owned by that advisor to have a `trust_balance_cents` of exactly 0, which is why an unrefunded trust deposit from a cancelled `CLIENT_TRUST` booking blocks a departure until the controller has recorded the refund; and it requires the recorded `transition_advisor_id` to still resolve to an advisor whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and who holds exactly one agreement in status `ACTIVE`. Any of these unmet is refused with `ERR_DEPARTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409), the no-longer-qualifying successor included: at this second stage that condition is reported as `ERR_DEPARTURE_BLOCKED` and never as `ERR_TRANSITION_ADVISOR_INVALID`, which belongs to the first stage alone; finalizing an advisor who is not `DEPARTING` reports `ERR_INVALID_ADVISOR_STATE`.

When it passes, the **departure cascade** runs four links deep, and — as with the chargeback cascade — each link is caused by the previous one rather than by the request. *Link one* moves the advisor's `status` from `DEPARTING` to `DEPARTED`, a terminal state, and moves every agreement of that advisor in status `ACTIVE` to `TERMINATED`. *Link two*, caused by the departing advisor now having no `ACTIVE` agreement, re-homes that advisor's clients: a client whose `ownership_mode` is `HOST_OWNED` has its `owner_advisor_id` reassigned to the successor while its `portability_status` stays `RETAINED`, whereas a client whose `ownership_mode` is `ADVISOR_OWNED` keeps its `owner_advisor_id` and instead has `portability_status` set to `RELEASED` — the advisor keeps the relationship they own, the host keeps the ones it owns. `Client.version` increments by 1 on every row touched, in both branches. *Link three*, caused by link two and firing only for those clients whose `owner_advisor_id` actually changed, moves the in-flight work: every booking of such a client whose `booking_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED` has its `advisor_id` set to the successor and its `agreement_id` re-pinned to the successor's `ACTIVE` agreement, and among those, any whose `commission_status` is still `PENDING` has `applied_commission_rate` and `expected_commission_cents` recomputed by the four-step pricing rule under the successor's consortium context — which can raise or lower the expected commission, since co-membership with the supplier's consortium may now hold where it did not before, or vice versa. `Booking.version` increments on every row touched. *Link four*, caused by link three having actually moved at least one booking and therefore silent when the re-homed clients held no booking in `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED`, recomputes `production_ytd_provisional_cents` and re-derives `split_tier_provisional` for **both** advisors, the departing one and the successor, with `Advisor.version` incrementing on the successor's row.

Two exclusions complete the picture. Bookings of the departing advisor whose `booking_status` is already `TRAVELED` or `CANCELLED` are not transitioned: they stay with the departed advisor, and when a later settlement run reaches them they settle at the now-`TERMINATED` agreement's `post_departure_advisor_pct` with no tier consulted, exactly as §3.8 describes for a `DEPARTED` owner. And the departing advisor's own `version` increments by 1 for the status change itself, independently of the increment link four applies to the successor.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`. Every other successful operation — state transitions, ledger reads, list reads, single-resource reads and the clock capability — returns HTTP 200 with the affected or requested resource in `data`. There are no 204 responses and no endpoints that update or delete an existing row: this system is append-and-transition only, so a correction is always expressed as a further transition or a compensating record rather than an edit.

List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3 plus the filters named in their descriptions below, which double as reverse-lookup facilities across the model: suppliers by `consortium_id`, `program_status` and `accreditation_scheme`; advisors by `consortium_id`, `transition_advisor_id`, `status` and `seller_of_travel_state`; agreements by `advisor_id`, `split_schedule_id` and `status`; credentials by `advisor_id`, `supplier_id`, `credential_type` and `status`; clients by `owner_advisor_id`, `ownership_mode` and `portability_status`; bookings by `advisor_id`, `supplier_id`, `client_id`, `agreement_id`, `statement_id`, `booking_status` and `commission_status`; claims by `booking_id`, `advisor_id`, `status` and `escalation_level`; and statements by `settlement_run_id`, `advisor_id` and `status`. A filter takes effect only when its query parameter is present and non-empty; an empty value, and any query parameter not named for that endpoint, is ignored rather than rejected. Matching is exact equality against the row's own stored value — integer-valued fields are compared numerically and boolean-valued fields against the spellings `true` and `false` — and a row whose field is null matches no supplied filter value at all, so a filter cannot be used to select the null case. The request-body fields each write endpoint expects are specified in the Section 3 narrative that describes the corresponding business event.
