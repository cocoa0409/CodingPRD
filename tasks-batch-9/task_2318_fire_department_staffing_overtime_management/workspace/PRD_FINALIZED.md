# Fire Staffing & Overtime API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A municipal fire department cannot decide tomorrow's staffing tomorrow. Every calendar date carries a fixed number of seats that must be occupied before the first alarm — an engine cannot roll with three of four seats filled, a medic unit cannot answer a cardiac call without a paramedic aboard — and the department has exactly one lever for filling a seat nobody is scheduled for: it buys the hours from an off-duty member at premium rates, out of a fiscal overtime budget that is fixed by the city council long before anyone knows how many members will be on injury leave in March. Today that arithmetic lives in a spreadsheet on a battalion chief's desk, is reconciled by hand against a labor agreement of several hundred pages, and is audited months later by a payroll analyst who has no way to reconstruct why a particular member was held over on a particular night.

This document specifies the Fire Staffing & Overtime API, the system of record for that arithmetic. It governs the full chain from master data (stations, apparatus, members, hiring rosters) through the daily staffing cycle (publish a shift, materialise its open seats, adjudicate the leave queue against the contractual floor, run the hiring order, respond to offers, close a company when nothing else works) to the payroll tail (mark the shift worked, convert encumbered overtime into actual spend, close the Fair Labor Standards Act work period on the hours actually worked). Alongside the transactional core it exposes two forward-looking analytics: an overtime forecast the budget office reads twice a month, and an academy hiring signal that converts chronic open seats into a recommended recruit-class size.

Two properties are non-negotiable in the design. First, **every selection is deterministic**: given the same members and the same shift, the hiring order returns the same member, the leave queue is adjudicated in the same sequence, and the closure order picks the same company — because a member who is passed over has a grievance right and the department must be able to explain the choice. Second, **money promised is money tracked**: a dispatched offer encumbers the station's budget from the instant it is dispatched, and every path that withdraws the promise credits back exactly the amount it debited, so the encumbrance ledger never drifts.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint. Where §3 describes what the department does, this section describes how the API behaves while it does it.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Four roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `StaffingOfficer`, `CompanyOfficer`, `Firefighter`, and `PayrollAnalyst`. A request that omits either header, or that presents an `X-User-Role` value outside those four, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). A request whose role is one of the four but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).

The four roles implement a strict segregation of duties. The `StaffingOfficer` is the battalion staffing office: it owns all master data, opens and publishes shifts, runs the ops-run checkpoint, dispatches the hiring order, and takes companies out of service — but it may not seat a member, may not adjudicate leave, may not decide a trade, and may not close out a shift for payroll. The `CompanyOfficer` is the officer on duty: it seats and un-seats members, adjudicates the leave queue against the staffing floor, and decides trades — but never a trade it is a party to. The `Firefighter` is a line member acting only on their own records: their own leave requests, their own trade proposals and countersignatures, their own overtime offers. The `PayrollAnalyst` closes out elapsed shifts, records hour adjustments, and reads the two station analytics; it has no staffing authority of any kind.

Identity resolution differs by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Firefighter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting member. Ownership checks on any resource that references a member compare the resource's member reference to `X-User-ID`: the leave request's `firefighter_id` on create and on revoke, the offer's `firefighter_id` on a response, the `requesting_firefighter_id` on a trade create, and the `counterparty_firefighter_id` on a countersignature. A mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `CompanyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting member, because a company officer is a firefighter with a rank. That identity is what the trade-decision segregation rule tests: an officer whose `X-User-ID` equals either the `requesting_firefighter_id` or the `counterparty_firefighter_id` of the trade they are deciding is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The deciding officer's `X-User-ID` is also persisted on the trade as `decided_by_user_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `StaffingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PayrollAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and is never compared to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection endpoints are paginated by two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records and defaults to 0. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

The general default ordering for list endpoints is `created_at` ascending. Because staffing work is read by position — "the third vacancy on the engine", "the next member in the hiring order" — several collections declare a stronger, fully deterministic ordering, and in every one of them `created_at` ascending is the final tie-break:

- Stations are ordered by `battalion_code` ascending, then `name` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Apparatus are ordered by `closure_priority` ascending, then `unit_designation` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Firefighters are ordered by `seniority_rank` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Hiring rosters are ordered by `roster_type` ascending, then `certification_scope` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Duty shifts are ordered by `shift_date` ascending, then `platoon` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Shift assignments are ordered by the parent shift's `shift_date` ascending, then the seat's company `closure_priority` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Leave requests are ordered by `decision_order_index` ascending with unadjudicated (null) requests placed last, then `created_at` ascending.
- Vacancies are ordered by the parent shift's `shift_date` ascending, then the company's `closure_priority` ascending, then `seat_index` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — so a list read of a freshly published shift reproduces the seat order exactly as generated.
- Work periods are ordered by `period_start_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Trade requests and overtime offers are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Every collection endpoint additionally accepts filters on its own foreign keys and enumerated fields. Stations may be filtered by `battalion_code` and `budget_posture`. Apparatus may be filtered by `station_id`, `apparatus_type` and `status`. Firefighters may be filtered by `station_id`, by the self-referential `acting_supervisor_id`, and by `platoon`, `rank`, `employment_status` and `roster_standing`. Hiring rosters accept `station_id`, `roster_type` and `certification_scope`. Duty shifts accept `station_id`, `platoon`, `status`, `shift_date` and `staffing_posture`. Shift assignments accept `duty_shift_id`, `firefighter_id`, `apparatus_id`, `source_offer_id`, `relieved_by_leave_request_id`, `assignment_type` and `status`. Leave requests accept `firefighter_id`, `duty_shift_id`, `status` and `leave_type`. Trade requests accept `requesting_firefighter_id`, `counterparty_firefighter_id`, `duty_shift_id`, `traded_assignment_id` and `status`. Vacancies accept `duty_shift_id`, `apparatus_id`, `filled_by_assignment_id`, `driver`, the polymorphic `driver_source_id`, `required_certification` and `status`. Overtime offers accept `vacancy_id`, `firefighter_id`, `roster_id`, `hiring_mode` and `status`. Work periods accept `firefighter_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in this system — stations, apparatus, firefighters, hiring rosters, duty shifts, shift assignments, leave requests, trade requests, vacancies, overtime offers and work periods alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the record is first persisted and never changed afterwards.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotone counter that increments by one on every mutation of the record.

Recomputation of a derived field is a mutation. When a leave approval reduces a shift's `assigned_headcount`, when an offer rescission credits a station's `ot_committed_cents`, or when a work period is re-priced, the affected record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented — even though no client sent a request naming that record. This matters directly for the concurrency rules in §2.7: a shift's `version` moves whenever anything about its staffing moves.

### 2.5. The Logical Duty Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — overtime offer expiry, the trade lead-time window, the check that a shift has elapsed before it may be closed out, the forecast horizon, and the calendar date a new work period starts on — is evaluated against a single logical UTC instant rather than wall-clock time. A dedicated administrative capability, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, sets that instant from a supplied `now` datetime and is available to all four roles; it returns 200 OK and mutates no entity, but every subsequent request evaluates its temporal rules against the new instant. **Until that capability is called for the first time, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.** That default is mandatory, not illustrative.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the highest-precedence violated rule. No side effect of any lower-precedence rule is applied, and a request that fails any check writes nothing at all. The precedence order is fixed and total:

1. **Header presence and role validity** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This runs before path resolution, so a request with a missing header aimed at a duty shift that does not exist returns 401, not 404.
2. **Role authorisation** — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). This also precedes existence, so a `Firefighter` attempting a company closure on a non-existent shift receives 403, not 404.
3. **Request shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), applied to every POST endpoint. The body must parse; every required field must be present and non-null; every field must carry its declared type; every enumerated field's value must be a member of its declared set; every integer must lie inside its declared bounds; dates must be ISO 8601 calendar dates and datetimes must be ISO 8601 UTC instants. Shape precedes existence: a body carrying both an unresolvable shift reference and an out-of-set enumeration value returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Reference existence** — the 404 family. Path parameters are resolved first, strictly left to right; then the body's foreign keys are resolved in the order the endpoint declares them. A request with two unresolvable references therefore always reports the earlier one. The specific code identifies the reference class: `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND` for a station reference, `ERR_FIREFIGHTER_NOT_FOUND` for `firefighter_id`, `acting_supervisor_id`, `requesting_firefighter_id` or `counterparty_firefighter_id`, `ERR_DUTY_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND` for `duty_shift_id`, `ERR_VACANCY_NOT_FOUND` for a vacancy reference, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` for an overtime offer reference, `ERR_LEAVE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for a leave request reference, `ERR_TRADE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for a trade request reference, and `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for every other reference class — an `apparatus_id`, a `hiring_roster_id`, a `shift_assignment_id` or a `work_period_id`. The path parameters covered by the station, member, shift, vacancy, offer, leave and trade codes are `station_id`, `firefighter_id`, `duty_shift_id`, `vacancy_id`, `overtime_offer_id`, `leave_request_id` and `trade_request_id` respectively.
5. **Member ownership** — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403), on the five member-scoped actions named in §2.1. It deliberately runs after existence so that an ownership check never leaks the existence of a record the caller may not see.
6. **Segregation of duties on a trade decision** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). An officer who is a party to the trade cannot decide it, even when the trade is properly countersigned and the role header is valid.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412), on the three write endpoints named in §2.7. This precedes every state and business check, so a stale version supplied against an already-completed shift returns 412, not 409.
8. **State admissibility** — the 409 family: `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_VACANCY_NOT_OFFERABLE`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_LEAVE_NOT_REVOCABLE`, `ERR_TRADE_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. There is one code per state machine so a client can tell which entity blocked the call. The elapsed-shift check for close-out, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_ELAPSED`, is also a 409.
9. **Cross-entity coherence** (422): the references resolved at step 4 exist but do not fit together — `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_HELD`, `ERR_LEAVE_ALREADY_BACKFILLED`, `ERR_ROSTER_NOT_CONFIGURED`. This band sits strictly above the temporal rules, so a member from another station seated on the wrong platoon returns `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` rather than a rotation error.
10. **Rotation compatibility** (422): `ERR_PLATOON_ROTATION_VIOLATION` is evaluated before `ERR_KELLY_DAY_CONFLICT`, so a member of the wrong platoon who also happens to be on a Kelly day for that date returns `ERR_PLATOON_ROTATION_VIOLATION`.
11. **Double booking** — `ERR_DOUBLE_BOOKED_SHIFT` (422).
12. **Rest-period ceiling** — `ERR_REST_PERIOD_VIOLATION` (422). It runs after the overlap check because merging intervals into a continuous tour is only meaningful once they are known not to overlap.
13. **Trade lead time** — `ERR_TRADE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).
14. **Staffing floor** — `ERR_STAFFING_FLOOR_BREACH` (422).
15. **Company seat capacity** — `ERR_APPARATUS_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
16. **Work-period hour cap** — `ERR_FLSA_WORK_PERIOD_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
17. **Empty selector pools and closure justification** — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CANDIDATE`, `ERR_NO_CLOSURE_ELIGIBLE_COMPANY`, `ERR_CLOSURE_NOT_JUSTIFIED` (422). Within this band, `ERR_CLOSURE_NOT_JUSTIFIED` is evaluated before the closure pool is built, so a shift that is merely short cannot close a company even when an eligible company exists.
18. **The fiscal gate** — `ERR_OT_BUDGET_WOULD_EXCEED` (422). Money is checked last of all, because the check needs a priced offer and a priced offer needs a selected candidate: every safety and eligibility rule must already have passed before the budget is consulted.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shifts and Members

Three write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a named parent record's `version`. On each of them the version field is a **required, non-optional** input; omitting it is a shape violation. No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none applies a version check.

- Creating a shift assignment (`POST /fire-ops/shift-assignments`) requires `expected_duty_shift_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `DutyShift`. If it does not equal the shift's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The seat arithmetic the client based its request on — `assigned_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, certification coverage, remaining seat capacity on the chosen company — is entirely derived state that any other writer's assignment, relief, vacancy or closure has already moved. Refusing the stale request forces the officer to re-read the shift before committing a body to a seat.
- Closing a company (`POST /fire-ops/duty-shifts/{duty_shift_id}/company-closure`) requires `expected_duty_shift_version` on the same terms, rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) on a mismatch. Closures legitimately supersede one another — a second closure on the same shift is a valid operation that selects the next company in closure order — so the operation cannot be guarded by a state precondition. The version check is the only mechanism that forces the officer to re-read `required_staffing` and `staffing_posture` before removing another company from service.
- Setting a member's availability (`POST /fire-ops/firefighters/{firefighter_id}/availability`) requires `expected_firefighter_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Firefighter`, rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) on a mismatch. This endpoint is idempotent by product design: a member may be moved from `ACTIVE` to `INJURY_LEAVE` and back any number of times, and `roster_standing` may be reset repeatedly, so no state precondition rejects a repeat call. The version check is the only guard that stops an operator from overwriting an availability change made after their last read — including a `PASSED` standing that an offer-expiry sweep has just written.

### 2.8. The Rotation Calendar: Platoons and Kelly Days

The department runs a four-platoon rotation with an eight-position Kelly-day cycle, and the whole calendar is a pure function of a calendar date and four constants — it reads no entity state. The rotation epoch is **1 January 2026** (`2026-01-01`); every duty date in scope falls on or after it, and `elapsed` denotes the number of whole calendar days from the epoch to the date in question (the epoch itself has an elapsed value of zero). All date arithmetic is integer day arithmetic and dates are treated as UTC calendar dates with no timezone conversion.

The platoon on duty for a date is selected from the fixed cycle `A`, `B`, `C`, `D` by the remainder of `elapsed` divided by four: remainder zero puts `A` on duty, remainder one puts `B`, remainder two puts `C`, and remainder three puts `D`. The Kelly slot in force for that date is derived from the same figure one level up: take `elapsed` divided by four discarding any fractional part, take the remainder of that quotient divided by the eight-position cycle length, and add one — yielding a slot number from 1 through 8 inclusive.

A member is **on a Kelly day** for a given date when both conditions hold: the platoon on duty for that date equals the member's own `platoon`, and the member's `kelly_day_cycle_position` equals the Kelly slot in force for that date. Either condition alone is not enough.

### 2.9. Hours, Cents and Rounding Discipline

Money is held in whole cents as integers throughout; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and the single rounding method is **round half up**. Hours are held as numbers but every hour figure the system computes or compares is a whole hour.

Three rounding rules are load-bearing and must be applied exactly where stated rather than deferred to the end of a computation:

- **Offer pricing rounds twice, in order.** The certification and fatigue uplifts are folded into the hourly rate and rounded to whole cents *first*; only the already-rounded effective rate is then multiplied by hours and the mode multiplier and rounded again. Rounding only once at the end yields a different integer and is wrong.
- **The vacancy-driven forecast rounds per vacancy, before summation.** Each open seat's uplifted cost is rounded to whole cents individually and the rounded amounts are then added. Summing first and uplifting once yields a different integer and is wrong.
- **Budget posture is computed in basis points.** The consumed fraction is expressed as ten-thousandths and rounded to a whole basis point before it is compared against the band boundaries, so the boundaries themselves are exact.

The overtime encumbrance ledger performs no rounding of its own: it is integer addition and subtraction of amounts that were already rounded when they were priced.

### 2.10. The Overtime Encumbrance Ledger and Budget Posture

Each station carries two money counters and a posture derived from them. `ot_committed_cents` is the **encumbrance ledger** — overtime promised but not yet paid — and starts at 0 when the station is registered. `ot_actual_cents` is overtime premium already worked and posted, and likewise starts at 0. `budget_posture` is a derived three-valued band over their sum against `fiscal_ot_budget_cents`, and reads `WITHIN` at zero consumption.

The encumbrance ledger is an invariant, not a running tally that happens to work out. At every observable point, `ot_committed_cents` equals the sum of `projected_cost_cents` over every overtime offer at that station still in `OFFERED` status, plus the sum of `projected_premium_cents` over every shift assignment at that station still in `SCHEDULED` status whose `assignment_type` is `OVERTIME`, `HOLDOVER` or `CALLBACK`. Every path that promises overtime debits the ledger, and every path that withdraws the promise credits back **exactly** the amount it debited: dispatching an offer, accepting one, declining one, letting one expire, rescinding one, mandating a holdover, cancelling a seat, approving a trade, closing a company and closing out a shift all move the ledger, and an accept-then-cancel or offer-then-expire round trip returns it to its starting value with no drift. Accepting an offer is the subtle case: the amount simply moves from the offer term of the sum to the assignment term, so the total does not change and nothing is re-added. Closing out a shift moves the amount out of the ledger and into `ot_actual_cents`.

`budget_posture` is recomputed from scratch after every mutation of either counter, and is also reflected on a station read. Add `ot_actual_cents` and `ot_committed_cents` to get consumption; express that consumption as a fraction of `fiscal_ot_budget_cents` in basis points — multiply by ten thousand, divide by the budget, round half up to a whole basis point. Consumption at or below 9,000 basis points is `WITHIN`; above that but at or below 10,000 basis points is `WATCH`; anything above 10,000 basis points is `OVER`. In plain terms: exactly 90.00 percent consumption is still `WITHIN`, and exactly 100.00 percent consumption is `WATCH`, not `OVER`.

### 2.11. Derived Staffing State and Cascade Propagation

A duty shift's staffing picture is entirely derived. `required_staffing`, `assigned_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, `certification_gap_count`, `leave_floor_headcount`, `staffing_posture` and `open_vacancy_count` are never supplied by a client; they are recomputed from the underlying apparatus, assignments and vacancies whenever any of those change, and every such recomputation increments the shift's `version`.

`required_staffing` is the sum of `minimum_crew_size` over the station's **open companies** for that shift — the apparatus whose `station_id` is the shift's station, whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE`, and whose `id` does not appear in the shift's `closed_apparatus_ids`. Units that are `OUT_OF_SERVICE` contribute nothing, and company closure is the only lever that lowers the figure.

`assigned_headcount` is the count of shift assignments on that shift whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`. `staffing_gap` is `required_staffing` minus `assigned_headcount`, floored at zero so it is never negative.

`certification_gap_count` measures specialty coverage per company as a multiset difference. For each open company, take the multiset of its `required_certifications` and subtract the multiset of `seat_certification` values carried by that company's `SCHEDULED` assignments on this shift, ignoring seats whose `seat_certification` is `NONE`; the size of the remaining multiset is that company's gap. Because the difference is taken per value, a company requiring two hazmat technicians and carrying one is still short one. `certification_gap_count` is the sum of those per-company gaps over the open companies. Assignments belonging to a company that has been closed for this shift contribute to no company's held multiset — their unit is out of service — although they continue to count in `assigned_headcount`.

`staffing_posture` is a three-valued band, and the order of its four branches is normative because certification coverage outranks headcount: a fully-crewed engine with no paramedic aboard cannot answer a medical call. Evaluate in this order — if `certification_gap_count` is greater than zero the posture is `CRITICAL`; otherwise if `staffing_gap` is zero the posture is `FULLY_STAFFED`; otherwise if `staffing_gap` is at most two the posture is `SHORT`; otherwise the posture is `CRITICAL`.

`leave_floor_headcount` is the headcount below which discretionary leave may not push a shift. It is the larger of the station's contractual `minimum_staffing_floor` and `required_staffing` reduced by a negotiated slack of two bodies. Taking the larger of the two is what stops a station with a high contractual floor from using company closures to manufacture extra leave: once closures have pulled `required_staffing` down to the contractual floor, the floor stops falling.

Cascades propagate immediately and completely, in the hop order stated wherever a workflow in §3 describes one, and a shift oscillates freely between `HIRING` and `STAFFED` as a consequence. Whenever any mutation leaves a shift in `HIRING` with `open_vacancy_count` at zero, the shift moves to `STAFFED` and its `version` increments. Whenever any mutation creates a vacancy on a `STAFFED` shift so that `open_vacancy_count` becomes one or more, the shift falls back to `HIRING`, recomputes `assigned_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, `certification_gap_count` and `staffing_posture`, and increments its `version`. There is no limit on how many times a shift may oscillate before it is closed out. Neither transition requires a client request naming the shift.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Station: Companies, Members and Rosters

A `StaffingOfficer` begins by registering a **station**, supplying its `name` (for example "Station 12"), the `battalion_code` it reports to, the contractual `minimum_staffing_floor` it may never crew below (an absolute headcount, independent of apparatus arithmetic, which must be zero or greater), the `fiscal_ot_budget_cents` allotted for overtime in the current fiscal period (also zero or greater), and the period's boundaries as `fiscal_period_start_date` and `fiscal_period_end_date`, both ISO calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, with the end date on or after the start date. The station comes into existence with both money counters at zero and a `budget_posture` of `WITHIN`.

Onto a station the officer hangs its **apparatus** — the companies that actually roll. Each unit declares the `station_id` that houses it, a radio `unit_designation` such as "ENGINE-12" that must be unique among that station's units, and an `apparatus_type` drawn from `ENGINE`, `LADDER`, `RESCUE`, `MEDIC` and `HAZMAT`. Operationally the two decisive numbers are `minimum_crew_size`, the seats that must be filled for the company to run, which must be at least 1 and at most 8; and `closure_priority`, a non-negative ranking key used when a company has to be taken out of service. Specialty staffing is declared as `required_certifications`, a multiset whose every element is one of `PARAMEDIC`, `HAZMAT_TECH` or `TECHNICAL_RESCUE`; it defaults to empty and may not be longer than `minimum_crew_size`. Repetition in that list is meaningful — a hazmat unit listing `HAZMAT_TECH` twice needs two certified riders, not one. A unit may be marked `closure_exempt`, defaulting to false; a true value marks a first-due company that may never be taken out of service by a closure action. A unit's `status` is `IN_SERVICE` on registration and may otherwise read `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, in which case the unit stops contributing to the staffing requirement altogether.

**Members** are enrolled next. Each carries a `station_id` naming the home station used for roster and scope checks, a `full_name`, a `rank` from `FIREFIGHTER`, `ENGINEER`, `LIEUTENANT` and `CAPTAIN`, and a `platoon` from `A`, `B`, `C` and `D` identifying the rotation they belong to. Two ordering keys drive nearly every selection in the system: `seniority_rank`, an integer of at least 1 where 1 denotes the most senior member in the department and which is unique department-wide — an enrolment naming a `seniority_rank` already held by any existing member, whatever that member's `employment_status`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and that uniqueness test is applied after the `station_id` and `acting_supervisor_id` references have been resolved rather than alongside the shape checks of §2.6; and `kelly_day_cycle_position`, an integer from 1 through 8 naming which slot of the eight-position Kelly cycle the member takes off. `certifications` lists the specialties the member holds, each element one of `PARAMEDIC`, `HAZMAT_TECH` or `TECHNICAL_RESCUE`, defaulting to empty. `hourly_rate_cents` is the straight-time rate in cents and must be at least 1. An optional `acting_supervisor_id` points at another member and is the self-referential supervisor link used for audit trails; it defaults to null and is null for the station commander. Two lifecycle fields may be supplied but otherwise default: `employment_status` starts `ACTIVE` and may otherwise read `INJURY_LEAVE` or `SEPARATED`, and `roster_standing` starts `AVAILABLE` and may otherwise read `PASSED` or `UNAVAILABLE`.

Five counters exist on every member purely so that the hiring order can rank on them, and all five start at zero: `voluntary_ot_hours_charged` (overtime hours charged against the voluntary hiring order), `mandatory_holdover_count` (times held over under the mandatory-holdover article), `callback_count` (times called back from off duty), `ot_pass_count` (consecutive declines or expiries on voluntary offers) and `pending_offer_count` (how many of the member's offers currently sit in `OFFERED`). Enrolment also opens the member's first Fair Labor Standards Act work period, described in §3.9.

Finally the officer configures **hiring rosters**, one per hiring mode per station. A roster declares its `station_id`, a `roster_type` from `VOLUNTARY_OT`, `MANDATORY_HOLDOVER` and `CALLBACK`, and a `certification_scope` from `NONE`, `PARAMEDIC`, `HAZMAT_TECH` and `TECHNICAL_RESCUE`; the scope defaults to `NONE`, which denotes the general roster for that mode. The combination of station, type and scope must be unique. Each roster keeps a `dispatch_count` of the offers sent from it, starting at zero.

Availability changes are an ongoing, deliberately idempotent operation reserved to the `StaffingOfficer`, guarded only by the concurrency check of §2.7. Alongside `expected_firefighter_version` the request carries an `effective_date` and optionally a new `employment_status`, a new `roster_standing`, or both. Moving a member **to** `INJURY_LEAVE` cascades: every `SCHEDULED` assignment the member holds on a shift dated on or after `effective_date` moves to `CANCELLED`; any vacancy whose `filled_by_assignment_id` is one of those cancelled seats moves to `OPEN` with `filled_by_assignment_id` cleared to null, exactly as a seat cancellation releases the hole it was hired into; for each seat so vacated a vacancy is created in `OPEN` status with `driver` set to `INJURY` and `driver_source_id` set to the member's own id, with `apparatus_id` and `required_certification` copied from the vacated seat's `apparatus_id` and `seat_certification` and a `seat_index` continuing that company's seat count for the shift — so a cancelled seat that had itself filled a vacancy leaves two open vacancies behind, the reopened original and the new `INJURY` one; and every affected shift recomputes its staffing fields, so a `STAFFED` shift falls back to `HIRING`. Moving a member back **to** `ACTIVE` reverses the hole rather than the seat: every `OPEN` vacancy whose `driver` is `INJURY` and whose `driver_source_id` is this member, on a shift dated on or after `effective_date`, moves to `CANCELLED`. Independently, setting `roster_standing` to `AVAILABLE` resets the member's `ot_pass_count` to zero — this is the department's mechanism for putting a passed-over member back in the voluntary rotation. Each availability change increments the member's `version` by one.

### 3.2. Opening and Publishing a Duty Shift

A **duty shift** is one station, one calendar date, one platoon. The `StaffingOfficer` opens it by supplying `station_id`, `shift_date` as an ISO calendar date, and `platoon`. The duty period runs from 08:00:00 UTC on `shift_date` for twenty-four hours, treated as a half-open interval so the period that ends at 08:00 and the period that begins at 08:00 do not overlap. Only one shift may exist per station per date. The declared `platoon` must be the platoon the rotation calendar of §2.8 puts on duty for `shift_date`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PLATOON_ROTATION_VIOLATION`. The shift is created in `DRAFT` with `ops_run_index` at zero, `closed_apparatus_ids` empty and `assigned_headcount` at zero, and with `required_staffing`, `leave_floor_headcount` and `staffing_posture` already computed from the station's open companies.

Publishing the shift is the moment the department commits to a seat plan, and it is admissible only from `DRAFT` — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_DRAFT`. Publication recomputes `required_staffing` and then **materialises every unfilled seat as a vacancy row**, in a deterministic order that a list read can reproduce. Walk the open companies sorted by `closure_priority` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. For each company in turn, count the `SCHEDULED` assignments already sitting on it for this shift; the number of seats to materialise is `minimum_crew_size` less that count, floored at zero. Build the company's unmet specialty list by subtracting the multiset of `seat_certification` values its `SCHEDULED` seats already carry from the multiset of its `required_certifications`, and sort what remains into ascending lexicographic order so the emitted sequence is reproducible. Then create one vacancy per open seat, numbering them with a 1-based `seat_index` within the company: the first seat takes the first entry of the unmet list as its `required_certification`, the second seat the second entry, and so on; once the unmet list is exhausted, every remaining seat takes `NONE`. A hazmat unit with a three-body crew requiring one hazmat technician and holding no assignments therefore yields three vacancies — seat 1 requiring `HAZMAT_TECH`, seats 2 and 3 requiring `NONE`. Each vacancy is created in `OPEN` status with `driver` set to `VACANT_POSITION` and `driver_source_id` null, and with `filled_by_assignment_id` null.

Publication then recomputes `open_vacancy_count` — the number of vacancies on this shift in `OPEN` or `OFFERING` status — along with `assigned_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, `certification_gap_count`, `leave_floor_headcount` and `staffing_posture`, and increments the shift's `version`. Where the shift lands depends entirely on whether any seat was left open: if `open_vacancy_count` came out greater than zero the shift moves `DRAFT → HIRING`, and if it came out at zero — every seat already carrying a `SCHEDULED` assignment — the shift moves `DRAFT → STAFFED` directly. Both branches perform the same recomputation and the same version increment; only the destination differs.

### 3.3. Seating a Crew, and the Rules That Guard a Seat

Only a `CompanyOfficer` seats a member. The request names the `duty_shift_id`, the `firefighter_id` taking the seat, the `apparatus_id` whose company the seat belongs to, an `assignment_type` from `REGULAR`, `KELLY_RELIEF`, `OVERTIME`, `HOLDOVER`, `CALLBACK` and `TRADE`, a `seat_certification` from `NONE`, `PARAMEDIC`, `HAZMAT_TECH` and `TECHNICAL_RESCUE` (the specialty seat the member fills on that company, defaulting to `NONE`), `scheduled_hours` greater than zero and at most 24, and the `expected_duty_shift_version` required by §2.7. The seat's occupied interval runs from 08:00:00 UTC on the shift date for `scheduled_hours` hours, half-open at the end.

A seat request runs a gauntlet, and the precedence of §2.6 decides which failure a client sees. **Station scope** comes first among the business rules: the member, the company and the shift must all belong to the same station, else `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Then **certification**: if the seat declares a specialty, the member's `certifications` must contain it, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_HELD`. Then the **rotation rules**, both drawn from §2.8. For `REGULAR` and `KELLY_RELIEF` seats only, the member's `platoon` must be the platoon on duty for the shift date, else `ERR_PLATOON_ROTATION_VIOLATION`. And for every assignment type **other than** `KELLY_RELIEF` and `CALLBACK`, the member must not be on a Kelly day for that date, else `ERR_KELLY_DAY_CONFLICT` — the two excepted types exist precisely to seat somebody on their day off. Then **double booking**: the member must hold no other `SCHEDULED` seat whose half-open interval overlaps this one, else `ERR_DOUBLE_BOOKED_SHIFT`; because the intervals are half-open, a seat ending at 08:00 and a seat starting at 08:00 do not overlap and are both permitted.

Then the **rest-period ceiling**, which is where the half-open convention earns its keep. Take every `SCHEDULED` seat the member holds, each as the half-open interval beginning at 08:00:00 UTC on its shift date and running for its `scheduled_hours`, add the prospective interval, and sort them all by start instant ascending. Merge them left to right: an interval whose start is strictly later than the end of the block accumulated so far begins a new block, and any other interval extends the current block to the later of the two end instants. A gap of exactly zero therefore merges. The continuous duty figure is the length in hours of the merged block containing the prospective interval. A **fatigue premium applies** when that figure is strictly greater than 24 hours, and the request is refused with `ERR_REST_PERIOD_VIOLATION` when it is strictly greater than 48 hours. Two consecutive twenty-four-hour tours therefore merge into a single forty-eight-hour block: the fatigue premium applies because 48 exceeds 24, and the seat is allowed because 48 does not exceed 48. Adding a third consecutive tour reaches seventy-two hours and is refused.

Then **seat capacity**, an aggregate constraint in which each individual seat request is perfectly valid and only the collective breaches the ceiling. A company may carry one rider above its minimum crew — for training or transition — and no more. Count the `SCHEDULED` assignments already on that company for that shift; if adding one more would put the total above `minimum_crew_size` plus one, the request is refused with `ERR_APPARATUS_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A company with a four-body minimum crew therefore accepts a fifth rider and rejects the sixth. Finally the **work-period hour cap** of §3.9: if the seat's hours would push the covering work period's scheduled hours above 288, the request is refused with `ERR_FLSA_WORK_PERIOD_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

A seat that survives all of that is created in `SCHEDULED` status with `actual_hours` null, `source_offer_id` null and `relieved_by_leave_request_id` null. Its `projected_premium_cents` is zero for `REGULAR`, `KELLY_RELIEF` and `TRADE` seats — those carry no overtime premium — and for overtime-derived seats is set as described in §3.7. If the new seat matches an `OPEN` vacancy on the same company with the same required certification, that vacancy moves to `FILLED` and its `filled_by_assignment_id` is set to the new seat. The covering work period's scheduled hours increase by the seat's `scheduled_hours` and the projection is re-priced on the scheduled basis. The shift then recomputes `assigned_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, `certification_gap_count`, `open_vacancy_count` and `staffing_posture`, moves `HIRING → STAFFED` if that closed the last vacancy, and increments its `version`.

Un-seating is the compensating operation, also reserved to the `CompanyOfficer`, and it takes a free-text `reason`. It is admissible only on a seat still in `SCHEDULED` (else `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`) belonging to a shift in `HIRING` or `STAFFED` (else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`), and it is refused with `ERR_STAFFING_FLOOR_BREACH` when removing the body would drop `assigned_headcount` below the shift's `leave_floor_headcount`. When it proceeds, the seat moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`; if its `projected_premium_cents` was non-zero, that amount is credited back to the station's `ot_committed_cents` and `budget_posture` is recomputed, restoring the encumbrance ledger to its pre-hire value; the covering work period's scheduled hours are reduced by the seat's `scheduled_hours` and both the projected overtime hours and the premium are recomputed; any vacancy whose `filled_by_assignment_id` is this seat moves `FILLED → OPEN` with `filled_by_assignment_id` cleared to null; and the shift recomputes its derived staffing fields, falling back `STAFFED → HIRING` if a vacancy re-opened, with its `version` incremented.

### 3.4. Leave Adjudication Against the Staffing Floor

A member files their own leave request, naming their `firefighter_id`, the `duty_shift_id` the leave is taken from, a `leave_type` from `INJURY`, `SICK`, `KELLY_DAY`, `BEREAVEMENT` and `VACATION`, and `requested_hours` greater than zero and at most 24. Filing decides nothing: the request lands in `QUEUED` with `decision_order_index` and `denial_reason` both null, `backfill_vacancy_id` null, and no staffing field anywhere touched. A member filing for another member is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and a member filing against a shift at another station is rejected with `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

Adjudication is a **batch** operation performed by a `CompanyOfficer` on a whole shift at once, and its ordering is contractual. Collect every `QUEUED` request on the shift and sort them by leave-type priority ascending, where `INJURY` ranks 1, `SICK` ranks 2, `KELLY_DAY` ranks 3, `BEREAVEMENT` ranks 4 and `VACATION` ranks 5; break ties by `seniority_rank` ascending, so the most senior member is considered first; break remaining ties by `created_at` ascending. Then walk the queue with a running headcount initialised to the shift's current `assigned_headcount`. For each request in turn, stamp its `decision_order_index` with its 1-based position in this ordering — the first request considered gets 1 — and note whether the member actually holds a `SCHEDULED` seat on the shift, since only those requests release a body.

`INJURY` and `SICK` are **non-discretionary**: they are approved regardless of the floor, and the running headcount drops by one if the member held a seat. Every other type is discretionary and is approved only if the running headcount, reduced by the body this request would release, would still be at or above the shift's `leave_floor_headcount`; if it would not, the request is denied. The running counter is normative — each grant lowers the headcount the next request sees, so the batch is genuinely sequential and is not evaluated against the pre-batch headcount. Denials consume no headcount, which is why a junior member's discretionary leave is not rescued by a senior member's denial: the two face the same floor, and only a non-discretionary type slips past it.

An approval moves the request `QUEUED → APPROVED` with `denial_reason` set to null, and cascades in three hops. **Hop one:** the member's `SCHEDULED` seat on this shift, if they hold one, moves `SCHEDULED → RELIEVED` with `relieved_by_leave_request_id` set to the leave request's id. **Hop two**, caused by hop one: the covering work period's scheduled hours are reduced by the seat's `scheduled_hours` and re-priced, and a vacancy is created in `OPEN` status with `driver` set to `LEAVE`, `driver_source_id` set to the leave request's id, `apparatus_id` copied from the relieved seat, `required_certification` copied from the relieved seat's `seat_certification`, and a `seat_index` continuing that company's seat count for the shift; the leave request's `backfill_vacancy_id` is set to that vacancy. **Hop three**, caused by hop two: the shift recomputes `assigned_headcount`, `open_vacancy_count`, `staffing_gap`, `certification_gap_count` and `staffing_posture`, and a `STAFFED` shift moves to `HIRING`. Independently of the seat cascade, the covering work period's leave hours increase by the request's `requested_hours` — leave hours are tracked but are excluded from hours worked for overtime purposes.

A denial moves the request `QUEUED → DENIED`, stamps its `decision_order_index` the same way, and sets `denial_reason` to `STAFFING_FLOOR`. Nothing else happens at all: no seat is relieved, no vacancy is created, no work period is touched and no money moves. Note that a floor breach inside a batch is *recorded*, not raised — the adjudication call itself succeeds and reports its outcome. `ERR_STAFFING_FLOOR_BREACH` is raised only by the single-seat path, seat cancellation, as described in §3.3.

The adjudication response summarises the batch rather than returning a shift: it reports the `duty_shift_id` adjudicated, the `leave_floor_headcount` the batch was measured against, a `considered_count` of requests walked, `approved_count` and `denied_count`, the `approved_leave_request_ids` and `denied_leave_request_ids` in decision order, the `resulting_assigned_headcount` and `resulting_staffing_posture`, and the `created_vacancy_count`. The shift's `version` is incremented.

A denial is not final. As described in §3.7, the ops-run checkpoint returns floor-denied requests `DENIED → QUEUED` once closures or hires have restored the shift to `FULLY_STAFFED`, clearing `denial_reason` back to null while retaining `decision_order_index` until a later adjudication overwrites it. A requeued request can perfectly well be denied again by that later batch; the loop has no bound.

Revocation runs the approval cascade backwards. Only the owning member may revoke, and only a request in `APPROVED` status — anything else is `ERR_LEAVE_NOT_REVOCABLE` — on a shift still in `HIRING` or `STAFFED`, else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`. The decisive guard is the backfill: if the vacancy the approval created has already reached `FILLED`, somebody has already been hired into the hole and the revocation is refused with `ERR_LEAVE_ALREADY_BACKFILLED`. Otherwise the request moves `APPROVED → REVOKED` and four hops fire. **Hop one:** the `RELIEVED` seat carrying this request's id in `relieved_by_leave_request_id` moves `RELIEVED → SCHEDULED` and that field is cleared to null. **Hop two**, caused by hop one: the covering work period's scheduled hours are increased by the seat's `scheduled_hours` and both the projected overtime hours and the premium are recomputed, restoring the hour ledger of §3.9 exactly; the work period's leave hours are reduced by the request's `requested_hours`. **Hop three**, also caused by hop one: the backfill vacancy moves to `CANCELLED`. **Hop four**, caused by hop three: any offer still in `OFFERED` on that vacancy moves to `RESCINDED`, its `projected_cost_cents` is credited back to the station's `ot_committed_cents`, the offered member's `pending_offer_count` decreases by one, and `budget_posture` is recomputed. The shift then recomputes its derived staffing fields, returning `HIRING → STAFFED` when `open_vacancy_count` reaches zero, and increments its `version`.

### 3.5. Shift Trades Between Members

A trade lets a member hand a shift to a named colleague, and it is a three-party act: the giver proposes, the taker countersigns, an uninvolved officer decides. The proposal names the `requesting_firefighter_id` giving the shift away, the `counterparty_firefighter_id` taking it, and the `duty_shift_id` being traded; only the requesting member may create it, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. It is created in `PROPOSED` with `revision_count` at zero, `traded_assignment_id` null, `decided_by_user_id` null, and no staffing consequence whatsoever. Both the proposal and the countersignature are gated by lead time: the trade must be transacted at least 48 hours — two full days — before the duty period begins at 08:00:00 UTC on the shift date, measured against the logical clock of §2.5. Exactly forty-eight hours out is still inside the window; anything later is refused with `ERR_TRADE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A proposal against a shift at another station, or naming a counterparty who is not at that station, is refused with `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

**Countersigning is where the incoming member is vetted**, and it is admissible only from `PROPOSED` — anything else is `ERR_TRADE_STATE_INVALID` — and only by the counterparty themselves, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The counterparty's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The lead-time window must still be open, else `ERR_TRADE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The counterparty must hold no `SCHEDULED` assignment whose half-open interval overlaps this shift's duty period, else `ERR_DOUBLE_BOOKED_SHIFT`. They must hold the specialty the traded seat declares as its `seat_certification`, unless that is `NONE`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_HELD`. The rotation rules of §2.8 apply, so a platoon mismatch yields `ERR_PLATOON_ROTATION_VIOLATION` and a Kelly-day collision yields `ERR_KELLY_DAY_CONFLICT`, in that order. And their covering work period's scheduled hours plus the traded seat's hours must not exceed 288, else `ERR_FLSA_WORK_PERIOD_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A successful countersignature moves the trade `PROPOSED → COUNTERSIGNED` and changes nothing else: no staffing field, no ledger entry, no work-period hour moves, because a trade is still only a proposal until an officer decides it.

The decision is taken by a `CompanyOfficer` who is neither party, and carries a `decision` plus an optional `note`. It is admissible only from `COUNTERSIGNED`, else `ERR_TRADE_STATE_INVALID`, and an officer who is the requester or the counterparty is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. All three decisions record the officer's `X-User-ID` in `decided_by_user_id`.

- A decision of `APPROVE` moves the trade `COUNTERSIGNED → APPROVED`, but only if the requester still holds a `SCHEDULED` assignment on the shift; if their seat has left `SCHEDULED` in the meantime the approval is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. On approval the trade's `traded_assignment_id` is set to that seat, the seat's `firefighter_id` becomes the counterparty and its `assignment_type` becomes `TRADE`. The requester's covering work period loses the seat's `scheduled_hours` and is re-priced; the counterparty's covering work period gains the same hours and is re-priced. Trades are cost-neutral, so the seat's `projected_premium_cents` is set to zero, and if the seat previously carried a premium that old amount is credited back to the station's `ot_committed_cents` with `budget_posture` recomputed. The shift then recomputes its derived staffing fields: `assigned_headcount` is unchanged because one body simply replaced another, and certification coverage is unchanged because `seat_certification` is preserved through the re-badging.
- A decision of `REJECT` moves the trade `COUNTERSIGNED → REJECTED` and records `decided_by_user_id`. Nothing else changes — no seat, no ledger, no work period.
- A decision of `RETURN_FOR_REVISION` moves the trade `COUNTERSIGNED → PROPOSED`, increments its `revision_count` by one, and records `decided_by_user_id`. The counterparty must countersign again before another decision is possible. This loop may repeat without bound, incrementing `revision_count` each time round.

### 3.6. The Overtime Hiring Order

When a vacancy exists, the `StaffingOfficer` runs the hiring order against it, supplying a `hiring_mode` from `VOLUNTARY`, `MANDATORY_HOLDOVER` and `CALLBACK`, plus the `offer_hours` being offered. The operation selects exactly one member, prices the hours, checks the money, and dispatches. It is admissible only against a vacancy in `OPEN` status, and only when the vacancy's company is not among the shift's `closed_apparatus_ids` — either failure is `ERR_VACANCY_NOT_OFFERABLE` — on a shift in `HIRING` or `STAFFED`, else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`.

**Roster resolution comes first.** Each mode has its own roster type: `VOLUNTARY` draws from a `VOLUNTARY_OT` roster, `MANDATORY_HOLDOVER` from a `MANDATORY_HOLDOVER` roster, and `CALLBACK` from a `CALLBACK` roster. Within that type the certification-scoped roster wins over the general one: look for the station's roster of the wanted type whose `certification_scope` equals the vacancy's `required_certification`, and only if none exists fall back to the station's roster of that type whose `certification_scope` is `NONE`. If neither exists the request is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_NOT_CONFIGURED`. The roster that was used is recorded on the resulting offer as `roster_id`, and its `dispatch_count` increases by one.

**Then the candidate pool.** Every mode starts from the same base filter. A candidate must be a member of the shift's station; their `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE`; if the vacancy's `required_certification` is anything other than `NONE`, their `certifications` must contain it; they must hold no `SCHEDULED` assignment on this shift; they must hold no `APPROVED` leave request on this shift; their `pending_offer_count` must be exactly zero, so nobody carries two live offers at once; and their covering work period's scheduled hours plus the payable hours of this offer must not exceed 288.

Each mode then narrows that base differently and ranks it differently, and the labor agreement deliberately **inverts the seniority tie-break for mandatory holdover** relative to the two consensual modes.

- **`VOLUNTARY`.** Eligible members are those in the base pool whose `roster_standing` is `AVAILABLE` and who are not on a Kelly day for the shift date. They are ranked by `voluntary_ot_hours_charged` ascending — the member who has been charged the fewest overtime hours is owed the next opportunity — then by `seniority_rank` ascending so the most senior of equals goes first, then by `created_at` ascending.
- **`CALLBACK`.** Eligible members are those in the base pool whose `roster_standing` is `AVAILABLE` and whose `platoon` is **not** the platoon on duty for the shift date, because a callback by definition reaches somebody who is off duty. They are ranked by `callback_count` ascending, then by `seniority_rank` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending.
- **`MANDATORY_HOLDOVER`.** Eligible members are those in the base pool whose `roster_standing` is anything other than `UNAVAILABLE` — a `PASSED` standing does **not** exempt a member from being held over, only from being asked voluntarily — and whose `platoon` is the platoon the rotation calendar puts on duty for **the day before** the shift date, since a holdover keeps the outgoing crew on past the change of shift. They are ranked by `mandatory_holdover_count` ascending, then by `seniority_rank` **descending** so the least senior of equals is the one held over, then by `created_at` ascending.

In every mode `created_at` ascending is the final tie-break and the record `id` is never consulted, so the ranking is a total order. The selected member is the first element of the ranked pool, and their id is recorded on the offer as `firefighter_id`. If the pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CANDIDATE`.

**Then pricing.** The offer's payable hours are the greater of the requested `offer_hours` and the mode's guaranteed minimum: `VOLUNTARY` guarantees nothing, `MANDATORY_HOLDOVER` guarantees two hours, and `CALLBACK` guarantees three hours. The offer's stored `offer_hours` is that payable figure — so a three-hour callback minimum turns a two-hour request into three payable hours. Three multiplicative factors then compose in a fixed order. The certification premium is a percentage uplift keyed to the vacancy's `required_certification`: nothing for `NONE`, twelve percent for `PARAMEDIC`, eight percent for `HAZMAT_TECH` and eight percent for `TECHNICAL_RESCUE`. The fatigue premium is a further ten percent, applied when and only when the continuous-duty merge of §3.3 shows the new seat landing in a merged block longer than twenty-four hours. **Sum those two percentages into a single uplift, apply it to the member's `hourly_rate_cents`, and round the result half up to whole cents first** — that rounded figure is the effective rate. Only then multiply the effective rate by the payable hours and by the mode's overtime multiplier — one and a half times for `VOLUNTARY`, one and a half times for `MANDATORY_HOLDOVER`, and double time for `CALLBACK` — and round half up again to whole cents. That final integer is the offer's `projected_cost_cents`. The two roundings are ordered and are not interchangeable; collapsing them into a single rounding at the end produces a different integer and is incorrect. The rest-period ceiling and the seat-capacity ceiling of §3.3 both apply here too, refusing the dispatch with `ERR_REST_PERIOD_VIOLATION` or `ERR_APPARATUS_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` respectively.

**Then the money gate**, whose three steps run strictly in this order because the gate compares a quantity only the first step can produce and the posture recomputation in the third must see what the second wrote. Step one prices the offer, which requires the selected candidate and therefore requires the selector to have already run. Step two compares the prospective total — the station's `ot_actual_cents` plus its `ot_committed_cents` plus the priced cost — against `fiscal_ot_budget_cents`, and refuses the dispatch with `ERR_OT_BUDGET_WOULD_EXCEED` if that total is **strictly greater** than the budget; a hire landing exactly on the budget is allowed, and a refusal writes nothing at all. Step three adds the priced cost to `ot_committed_cents` and only then re-derives `budget_posture` from the new committed total. **Mandatory holdover skips step two entirely**: the labor agreement compels the seat to be filled, so a holdover is dispatched even when the encumbrance pushes the station's `budget_posture` to `OVER`.

What the dispatch produces depends on the mode. For `VOLUNTARY` and `CALLBACK`, an offer is created in `OFFERED` status with `expires_at` set to its `created_at` plus a two-hour response window, the selected member's `pending_offer_count` increases by one, and the vacancy moves `OPEN → OFFERING`. For `MANDATORY_HOLDOVER` there is no asking: the offer is created directly in `MANDATED` status with `expires_at` null, and the seat is created in the same request. A shift assignment is created in `SCHEDULED` status with `assignment_type` `HOLDOVER`, `seat_certification` copied from the vacancy's `required_certification`, `scheduled_hours` equal to the offer's payable `offer_hours`, and `source_offer_id` pointing at the offer; the vacancy moves `OPEN → FILLED` with `filled_by_assignment_id` set; the priced cost is added to `ot_committed_cents` and `budget_posture` recomputed; the member's `mandatory_holdover_count` increases by one; the covering work period's scheduled hours increase by the payable hours and are re-priced; and the shift recomputes its derived staffing fields, returning to `STAFFED` when `open_vacancy_count` reaches zero. Because a mandated offer never passes through a client-visible `OFFERED` state, a response call aimed at one is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_PENDING` — the member may not decline it.

### 3.7. Answering an Offer, and the Ops-Run Checkpoint

An offered member answers their own offer with a `response`, and only their own: a caller whose `X-User-ID` is not the offer's `firefighter_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The offer must be in `OFFERED` status, else `ERR_OFFER_NOT_PENDING`, on a shift in `HIRING` or `STAFFED`, else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`.

**Accepting** moves the offer `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and the vacancy `OFFERING → FILLED`, but first re-verifies the member: they must still hold the vacancy's `required_certification`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_HELD`; the seat must not double-book them, else `ERR_DOUBLE_BOOKED_SHIFT`; the merged continuous-duty block must stay within the forty-eight-hour ceiling, else `ERR_REST_PERIOD_VIOLATION`; the company must have a free seat, else `ERR_APPARATUS_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; and their covering work period's scheduled hours plus the offer's payable hours must not exceed 288, else `ERR_FLSA_WORK_PERIOD_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Acceptance then cascades in three hops. **Hop one:** a shift assignment is created in `SCHEDULED` status with `assignment_type` `OVERTIME` for a voluntary offer or `CALLBACK` for a callback offer, `source_offer_id` pointing at the offer, `scheduled_hours` equal to the offer's payable `offer_hours`, and `projected_premium_cents` set equal to the offer's `projected_cost_cents`; the vacancy's `filled_by_assignment_id` is set to that seat. **Hop two**, caused by hop one: the covering work period's scheduled hours increase by the payable hours and its projected overtime hours are recomputed. **Hop three**, caused by hop two: the work period's premium is re-priced on the scheduled basis. Alongside the cascade, the member's counters move — `voluntary_ot_hours_charged` increases by the payable hours for a voluntary offer, or `callback_count` increases by one for a callback offer; `ot_pass_count` resets to zero; and `pending_offer_count` decreases by one. The station's `ot_committed_cents` is **not** re-added, because the encumbrance has merely moved from the offer to the assignment (see §2.10), but `budget_posture` is recomputed all the same. The shift recomputes its derived staffing fields, returns to `STAFFED` if that closed the last vacancy, and increments its `version`.

**Declining** moves the offer `OFFERED → DECLINED` and returns the vacancy `OFFERING → OPEN`, from where it can be offered again to the next member in hiring order — an unbounded loop that walks down the roster until somebody takes the hours. The labor agreement, however, charges the pass: the member's `voluntary_ot_hours_charged` increases by the offer's payable `offer_hours` **exactly as if the hours had been worked**, their `ot_pass_count` increases by one, and their `pending_offer_count` decreases by one. When `ot_pass_count` reaches three or more, the member's `roster_standing` flips to `PASSED`, which excludes them from the voluntary and callback candidate pools while leaving them fully eligible for mandatory holdover. The station's `ot_committed_cents` is credited back the offer's `projected_cost_cents` and `budget_posture` is recomputed. The shift recomputes its derived staffing fields and increments its `version`.

**Non-response is treated as a decline.** The `StaffingOfficer` drives the clock forward with an ops-run checkpoint against a shift — a housekeeping operation admissible on any shift in `HIRING` or `STAFFED`, else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`, which leaves a `HIRING` shift in `HIRING` and a `STAFFED` shift in `STAFFED` unless the work it does moves the shift itself. It increments the shift's `ops_run_index` — a monotone counter of how many checkpoints the shift has seen — and then performs **four steps whose order is normative**, because step one changes what step two sees and step two decides whether step three fires:

1. **Expire due offers.** Every offer on this shift still in `OFFERED` whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock — the comparison is inclusive, so an offer expiring exactly now expires — moves to `EXPIRED`, processed in order of `expires_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending. Each expiry has precisely the same consequences as a decline: the vacancy returns to `OPEN`, the member's `voluntary_ot_hours_charged` increases by the payable `offer_hours`, their `ot_pass_count` increases by one and their `pending_offer_count` decreases by one, `roster_standing` flips to `PASSED` on reaching three passes, and the station's `ot_committed_cents` is credited back the `projected_cost_cents`.
2. **Recompute the staffing picture.** `required_staffing`, `assigned_headcount`, `staffing_gap`, `certification_gap_count`, `leave_floor_headcount`, `staffing_posture` and `open_vacancy_count` are all re-derived. This is where a shift that entered the checkpoint `STAFFED` can fall back to `HIRING`, because step one may have re-opened a vacancy.
3. **Requeue floor-denied leave.** If and only if the posture that step two produced is `FULLY_STAFFED`, every leave request on this shift in `DENIED` status returns to `QUEUED` with `denial_reason` cleared to null, retaining its `decision_order_index` until the next adjudication overwrites it.
4. **Recompute the station's budget posture** from the committed total that step one left behind.

The checkpoint increments the shift's `version`.

Offers may also be withdrawn by the department rather than by the member. Whenever a company closure, a leave revocation or a shift close-out cancels the underlying vacancy, any offer on it still in `OFFERED` moves to `RESCINDED`: the station's `ot_committed_cents` is credited back its `projected_cost_cents`, `budget_posture` is recomputed, and the member's `pending_offer_count` decreases by one. Crucially, a rescission charges the member **nothing** — neither `voluntary_ot_hours_charged` nor `ot_pass_count` moves — because the department, not the member, withdrew.

### 3.8. Company Closure of Last Resort

When a shift simply cannot be crewed, the last contractual lever is to take a company out of service for that shift, and it is reserved to the `StaffingOfficer`. The request carries the `expected_duty_shift_version` of §2.7 and a `justification`. Closure is admissible on a shift in `HIRING` or `STAFFED`, else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`, and it is permitted **only** when the shift's `staffing_posture` is `CRITICAL`; a merely `SHORT` shift is refused with `ERR_CLOSURE_NOT_JUSTIFIED`, and that justification test is evaluated before any candidate company is looked for.

Which company closes is contractual, not discretionary. The candidate pool is the station's apparatus that are `IN_SERVICE`, are not `closure_exempt`, and do not already appear in the shift's `closed_apparatus_ids`. If that pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_CLOSURE_ELIGIBLE_COMPANY`. Otherwise the pool is sorted by `closure_priority` **descending** — the highest priority number closes first — then by `minimum_crew_size` descending, then by `created_at` ascending, and the first company in that order is the one closed. The record `id` is never consulted. A station whose exempt first-due engine ranks priority 10, whose truck ranks 30 and whose hazmat unit ranks 20 therefore closes the truck first, the hazmat unit on a second closure, and refuses a third with `ERR_NO_CLOSURE_ELIGIBLE_COMPANY` because the engine is exempt.

Closure cascades in four hops, and the later hops are the ones implementers most often miss. **Hop one:** the selected company's id is appended to the shift's `closed_apparatus_ids`, in closure order, and `required_staffing` is recomputed without that company's `minimum_crew_size`. **Hop two**, caused by hop one: every vacancy on this shift belonging to the closed company and sitting in `OPEN` or `OFFERING` moves to `CLOSED_BY_COMPANY_CLOSURE`. **Hop three**, caused by hop two: every offer in `OFFERED` on those vacancies moves to `RESCINDED`. **Hop four**, caused by hop three: for each rescinded offer the station's `ot_committed_cents` is reduced by that offer's `projected_cost_cents` and the offered member's `pending_offer_count` decreases by one, and only after all of them have been credited back is the station's `budget_posture` recomputed from the new committed total.

Two consequences of a closure are easy to get wrong. `SCHEDULED` assignments sitting on the closed company **keep** their `SCHEDULED` status and **continue** to count in `assigned_headcount`, because the crew is administratively redistributed rather than sent home — but their `seat_certification` no longer counts toward any company's certification coverage, since their unit is out of service. And a closure can finish the job it was called for: after `staffing_gap`, `certification_gap_count`, `leave_floor_headcount`, `staffing_posture` and `open_vacancy_count` are recomputed, a shift whose `open_vacancy_count` has reached zero falls through to `STAFFED`. The shift's `version` is incremented.

### 3.9. Work Periods, Shift Completion and Staffing Analytics

Fire-protection employees are paid under the Fair Labor Standards Act section 7(k) exemption, which measures overtime over a **27-day work period** rather than a week. Enrolling a member opens their first period immediately: `period_start_date` is the logical clock's calendar date, `period_end_date` is twenty-six days later so the window spans twenty-seven days inclusive, `flsa_threshold_hours` is 204, `status` is `OPEN`, and every hour and money field starts at zero. A member's **covering** period for any given date is the period whose window contains that date.

`scheduled_hours` on a work period is a conserved quantity, and this is what makes the compensating operations elsewhere in this document exact. It is the sum of `scheduled_hours` over the member's assignments in `SCHEDULED` or `WORKED` status whose shift date falls inside the window, plus the period's `adjusted_scheduled_hours`. `worked_hours` is the same sum restricted to assignments in `WORKED` status and measured by `actual_hours`, plus `adjusted_worked_hours`. `leave_hours` is the sum of `requested_hours` over the member's `APPROVED` leave requests in the window, and is tracked separately precisely because leave is excluded from hours worked for overtime purposes. Every operation that puts a member in a seat adds the seat's hours and every operation that takes them out subtracts exactly the same number, so an approve-then-revoke or a hire-then-cancel round trip returns the field to its starting value with no drift.

Overtime is then a threshold. `projected_flsa_overtime_hours` is `scheduled_hours` less `flsa_threshold_hours`, floored at zero. `final_flsa_overtime_hours` is `worked_hours` less `flsa_threshold_hours`, floored at zero. `flsa_premium_cents` prices whichever of the two the period's `status` selects, at the **half-time** premium: multiply the selected hour figure by the member's `hourly_rate_cents`, halve it, and round half up to whole cents. An `OPEN` period prices on the **scheduled basis** — an upper-bound, forward-looking projection that includes seats which are only scheduled — and a `CLOSED` period prices on the **worked basis**, the payable figure, from which relieved, cancelled and leave-covered seats have dropped out, so the payable figure is at or below the projection and the delta is written to `flsa_premium_cents` at close. A period whose scheduled hours reach exactly 204 has zero projected overtime hours and a zero premium, because the threshold must be exceeded, not merely met. Independently of the threshold, no operation may push a period's scheduled hours above **288**; any that would is refused with `ERR_FLSA_WORK_PERIOD_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and 288 exactly is allowed while 289 is not.

The `PayrollAnalyst` may post an hour adjustment against a period — imported hours from another period, corrections, drill and training time — supplying `adjustment_hours`, an `adjustment_basis` of `SCHEDULED` or `WORKED`, and a `reason`. A `SCHEDULED` basis adds the hours to `adjusted_scheduled_hours` and recomputes `projected_flsa_overtime_hours`, together with `flsa_premium_cents` while the period is `OPEN`. A `WORKED` basis adds them to `adjusted_worked_hours` and recomputes `final_flsa_overtime_hours`, together with `flsa_premium_cents` while the period is `CLOSED`. An adjustment that would push the period's scheduled hours above 288 is refused with `ERR_FLSA_WORK_PERIOD_CAP_EXCEEDED`. This is how a period is brought to the threshold for testing and correction without materialising eight and a half shifts of seats.

**Closing out a shift** is the `PayrollAnalyst`'s act and the only path to a terminal shift. It is admissible from `HIRING` or `STAFFED`, else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_HIRING`, and only once the shift has elapsed: its `shift_date` must be on or before the logical clock's calendar date, else `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_ELAPSED`. Both source states run the identical side-effect list and both land in `COMPLETED`. Every assignment on the shift still in `SCHEDULED` moves to `WORKED` with `actual_hours` stamped equal to its `scheduled_hours`, and for each one the covering work period's `worked_hours` and `final_flsa_overtime_hours` are recomputed. The premium total of the completed `OVERTIME`, `HOLDOVER` and `CALLBACK` seats — the sum of their `projected_premium_cents` — is added to the station's `ot_actual_cents` and subtracted from its `ot_committed_cents`, moving the money from encumbered to spent without changing the pair's meaning. Every vacancy on the shift still in `OPEN` or `OFFERING` moves to `CANCELLED`, and each `OFFERED` offer on those vacancies moves to `RESCINDED` with its own rescission consequences from §3.7 — the credit back to `ot_committed_cents`, the decrement of `pending_offer_count`, and no charge to the member. Then every work period of the involved members whose `period_end_date` falls on or before the shift date moves `OPEN → CLOSED`, has its `flsa_premium_cents` re-priced on the worked basis instead of the scheduled basis, and spawns a successor period in `OPEN` status beginning the day after the closed period ended and running its own twenty-seven days. Finally the station's `budget_posture` is recomputed and the shift's `version` is incremented.

Two computed reads turn all of this into planning material, and both are pure: they persist nothing.

The **overtime forecast** projects a station's remaining overtime spend for the fiscal period, and offers two deliberately disagreeing methods selected by a `method` parameter that defaults to `VACANCY_DRIVEN`. Both begin from the same base. The pool is the station's members whose `employment_status` is anything other than `SEPARATED`; `avg_rate_cents` is the sum of their `hourly_rate_cents` divided by their number and rounded half up to whole cents, or zero when the pool is empty. `shift_base_cents` is `avg_rate_cents` multiplied by a twenty-four-hour tour and by the voluntary time-and-a-half multiplier, rounded half up.

- The **`VACANCY_DRIVEN`** method builds the number bottom-up from open seats and injury leave, ignoring realised spend and the calendar entirely. Its horizon is every vacancy at the station in `OPEN` or `OFFERING` status whose shift date is on or after the current date and on or before the station's `fiscal_period_end_date`; the count of those is reported as `horizon_vacancy_count`. For each one, uplift `shift_base_cents` by the certification premium keyed to that vacancy's `required_certification` — nothing for `NONE`, twelve percent for `PARAMEDIC`, eight percent for `HAZMAT_TECH`, eight percent for `TECHNICAL_RESCUE` — and round half up **per vacancy**; the sum of those rounded amounts is `vacancy_driver_cents`. Separately, `injury_headcount` counts the pool members whose `employment_status` is `INJURY_LEAVE`, and `injury_driver_cents` is that headcount multiplied by nine shifts per period and by `shift_base_cents`. `forecast_cents` is `vacancy_driver_cents` plus `injury_driver_cents`.
- The **`RUN_RATE`** method extrapolates realised spend forward, ignoring vacancies, certifications and injury leave entirely. `elapsed_days` is the number of whole days from the station's `fiscal_period_start_date` to the current date, plus one, so the first day of the period counts as one elapsed day. `remaining_days` is the number of whole days from the current date to `fiscal_period_end_date`, floored at zero. `daily_run_rate_cents` is `ot_actual_cents` divided by `elapsed_days`, rounded half up. `forecast_cents` is `daily_run_rate_cents` multiplied by `remaining_days`.

Both methods share a tail. `projected_period_total_cents` is `ot_actual_cents` plus `ot_committed_cents` plus `forecast_cents`; `variance_cents` is `fiscal_ot_budget_cents` less that total; and `forecast_posture` reads `WITHIN` when `variance_cents` is zero or positive and `OVER` when it is negative. The response echoes the `station_id` and the `method` in force alongside `avg_rate_cents`, `shift_base_cents`, `horizon_vacancy_count`, `vacancy_driver_cents`, `injury_headcount`, `injury_driver_cents`, `daily_run_rate_cents`, `remaining_days`, `forecast_cents`, `projected_period_total_cents`, `variance_cents` and `forecast_posture`. **Fields belonging to the method that was not selected are returned as zero.**

The **academy hiring signal** converts staffing pressure into a recruit-class recommendation, on the reasoning that chronic open seats and members out on injury leave both signal a shortage of bodies rather than a shortage of overtime. `open_vacancy_seats` counts every vacancy at the station in `OPEN` or `OFFERING` status, with no date restriction at all. `injury_headcount` counts the station's members whose `employment_status` is `INJURY_LEAVE`. `raw_need` is `open_vacancy_seats` plus twice `injury_headcount`, because a member on injury leave leaves a hole on every rotation rather than on one shift. `recommended_recruit_count` converts that need into bodies by dividing by the four-platoon coverage factor and **rounding up** to a whole recruit — one position must be covered on every platoon, and a raw need of one already recommends one recruit. The `signal_tier` then bands the recommendation: a recommendation of zero recruits is `NONE`; anything from one through eight inclusive is `MONITOR`; anything from nine through twenty inclusive is `RECRUIT`; anything above twenty is `SURGE`. The response reports the `station_id` alongside `open_vacancy_seats`, `injury_headcount`, `raw_need`, `recommended_recruit_count` and `signal_tier`.
