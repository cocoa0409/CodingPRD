# Family Child Care Network API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Family child care homes are small businesses — often a single licensed caregiver operating out of a residence — and the administrative load they carry is wildly out of proportion to their size. A provider caring for eight children is nonetheless expected to hold a current state license, negotiate a network membership agreement, track enrollment and tuition, file a monthly subsidy attendance claim with a public agency, file a separate monthly food program claim under a different reimbursement schedule, find a cleared substitute when they fall ill, buy supplies and insurance at a price they can afford, host coaching visits, answer compliance findings with corrective action plans, and eventually wind the business down without stranding the families who depend on it. A family child care network exists to absorb that load: it recruits and licenses providers, signs them to a shared-services agreement, and then runs the back office on their behalf.

This document specifies the API for that shared-services platform. The system is deliberately built around one uncomfortable fiscal reality: the network fronts money to providers before the subsidy agency has verified anything. A monthly attendance claim is priced optimistically at submission, eighty percent of that optimistic figure is advanced to the provider, and only later — after the agency verifies attendance, after children who left the home are excluded, after absence days are capped and discounted — is the claim priced again and the difference settled. Two different arithmetics therefore run over exactly the same attendance data, and the platform must keep both, keep them distinguishable, and keep the contract's authorization ledger honest through every submission, rejection, hold and reinstatement. Agency payments arrive in lumps that rarely match any single claim, so remittances are allocated to reconciled claims by a waterfall, and every cent that is not allocated is held as unallocated credit rather than being lost.

Quality and money are coupled by design. A coaching visit that produces a critical finding opens a compliance review; a corrective action plan that is rejected twice can be escalated to the licensing authority; escalation puts the provider on probation, which suspends their subsidy contracts, which holds their in-flight claims, which changes the revenue projection the network shows them. Reinstatement must unwind all four hops with no residue in the authorization ledger. Throughout, the platform is mounted under `/fccn`, all monetary amounts are integer cents, and every deadline, expiry and window is measured against a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time so that a full fiscal year of network operations can be exercised deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the public network application carries two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Anonymous`, `NetworkAdmin`, `FiscalOfficer`, `QualityCoach`, `Provider`, and `Substitute`. If `X-User-Role` is absent or holds a value outside that set of six, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` is returned. When the acting role is authenticated but does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called, the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Only `POST /fccn/providers/apply` is exempt from both header checks — a prospective provider has no identity in the system yet.

The three back-office roles carry **opaque identity**. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `opened_by_user_id` on a compliance review the operator opens — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `FiscalOfficer`: the identifier is opaque, is not resolved against any stored row, and no ownership check consults it. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityCoach`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `coach_user_id` and `conducted_by_user_id` on coaching visits and as `opened_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on compliance reviews, and compares those recorded values **to each other** for segregation-of-duties purposes, but never resolves them against an entity `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque value that the server neither stores nor resolves, and the only operation available is filing a network application.

The two field-facing roles carry **entity-backed identity**. When `X-User-Role` is `Provider`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider` row and must resolve to an existing provider, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` when it does not; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's `provider_id` to `X-User-ID`, and where the resource has no direct `provider_id` the comparison follows the parent chain — a subsidy claim or a remittance is reached through its `contract_id` to the contract's `provider_id`. A provider addressing anything outside that scope is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Substitute`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Substitute` row and must resolve to an existing pool member, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` otherwise; a substitute may only address `SubstituteAssignment` rows whose `substitute_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a violation returns `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

Segregation of duties is enforced between two `QualityCoach` operators rather than between roles. A visit may not be conducted by the same operator who scheduled it, and a compliance review may not be closed by the same operator who opened it (or, for a review opened automatically by a critical finding, by the operator who conducted the triggering visit). Either violation returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Escalation to the licensing authority is deliberately withheld from `QualityCoach` and reserved for `NetworkAdmin`; conversely, `FiscalOfficer` never touches quality or membership decisions.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a request-shape failure and returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a calculation explicitly pins a different ordering for its own internal purposes. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). Endpoint-specific filters — such as `county_tier`, `membership_status`, `quality_tier`, `status`, `period_month`, `category`, `staffing_status`, `billing_status`, `provider_id` and `predecessor_enrollment_id` — are all optional, combine conjunctively, and are documented with the workflows they belong to.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

All twelve resource types in this system — providers, membership agreements, enrollments, subsidy contracts, subsidy claims, remittances, food program claims, substitutes, substitute assignments, coaching visits, compliance reviews and group purchase orders — carry the same four housekeeping properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, with `updated_at` refreshed to the same moment. A mutation that a cascade performs on a row other than the request's primary target still bumps that row's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`; nothing in this system mutates a row silently.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this system — license expiry, background clearance expiry, corrective-action-plan deadlines, coaching-visit windows, claim service months, and month-bucketed aggregation — reads a server-controlled logical clock and never the host's wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `NetworkAdmin` may move it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock endpoint, and every subsequent comparison uses the new value. Dates stored as "UTC midnight" (`effective_on`, `start_on`, `exit_on`, `period_start_on`, `received_on`, `scheduled_on`, `visit_conducted_on`, `cap_due_on`, `license_expires_on`) are compared at day granularity against the date part of the logical clock; instants (`start_at`, `end_at`, `clearance_expires_on`, `executed_at`, `cap_submitted_at`) are compared at full precision.

### 2.6. Network Posture: the Gate on Provider-Initiated Writes

Each provider carries a derived gate called `network_posture`, recomputed from `membership_status` on every membership-status change and never set directly. The mapping is total: `APPLICANT`, `PENDING_AGREEMENT`, `EXITING` and `EXITED` all map to `FROZEN`; `ACTIVE` maps to `OPEN`; `PROBATION` maps to `RESTRICTED`. A `FROZEN` posture blocks every provider-initiated write. A `RESTRICTED` posture blocks new enrollments, new subsidy claims and new group purchase orders while continuing to allow compliance traffic and substitute-pool traffic — a provider on probation must still be able to answer findings and still be able to obtain coverage when they are ill. Either block returns `ERR_PROVIDER_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409). The gate is checked on enrollment creation, subsidy claim creation, group purchase order creation, food program claim creation and substitute coverage requests; for the latter two only the `FROZEN` case blocks, since `RESTRICTED` does not name them.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Agreement and Claim Creation

Two creation endpoints write into a parent aggregate whose state the client must have observed first, and both therefore require the parent's version as a mandatory input field.

A `POST /fccn/membership-agreements` request must carry `expected_provider_version` matching the target provider's current `version`. Issuing a new agreement legitimately supersedes any agreement of that provider still under negotiation, so a client holding a stale view of the provider would silently withdraw an agreement it never saw. If the provider's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is superseded.

A `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims` request must carry `expected_contract_version` matching the target contract's current `version`. Claim creation consumes the contract's service-month uniqueness and shifts the authorization headroom the client believed it had, both of which a stale reader may already have mis-measured. If the contract's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. `expected_provider_version` and `expected_contract_version` are non-optional on the two endpoints named above and are not accepted anywhere else.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned: the checks below run in strict ascending priority order and the first failing check short-circuits, so a lower-numbered failure always masks a higher-numbered one. This ordering is normative — it is the difference between a caller learning that they lack permission and a caller learning that a slot ledger is exhausted.

1. **Role authentication.** `X-User-Role` present and equal to one of the six declared roles. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint except `POST /fccn/providers/apply`.
2. **Identity authentication.** `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, and for the two entity-backed roles resolvable to an existing row of that entity. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint except `POST /fccn/providers/apply`.
3. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list. `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Ownership scope.** A `Provider` actor may only address resources whose `provider_id` equals `X-User-ID`, whether directly or through the `contract_id` chain to `SubsidyContract.provider_id`; a `Substitute` actor may only address `SubstituteAssignment` rows whose `substitute_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Applies to every `Provider`-role and `Substitute`-role endpoint. Provider violations return `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and substitute violations return `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, both HTTP 403.
5. **Path existence.** Every id embedded in the URL exists, validated left to right. HTTP 404, with the code naming the missing resource type: `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FOOD_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
6. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body exists — `provider_id`, `contract_id`, `substitute_id` or `requested_substitute_id`, each `enrollment_id` named inside claim lines, and `receiving_provider_id`. HTTP 404 with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Applies to every endpoint carrying foreign keys in its input.
7. **Request shape.** All required fields present; enum values inside their declared sets; integer amounts non-negative; `capacity_slots` between 1 and 12 inclusive; `days_per_week` between 1 and 5 inclusive; `observation_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive; `end_at` strictly later than `start_at`; `period_month` matching a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month between 01 and 12; and every array that is required to be non-empty actually non-empty. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400. Applies to every endpoint.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_provider_version` equals the provider's current `version` on `POST /fccn/membership-agreements`; `expected_contract_version` equals the contract's current `version` on `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims`. `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`, HTTP 412. Applies to exactly those two endpoints.
9. **Own-entity state.** The target resource's own status is a declared source state of the requested transition. HTTP 409, with the code naming the target type: `ERR_INVALID_PROVIDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ENROLLMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_VISIT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_REMITTANCE_STATE`. Applies to every transition endpoint.
10. **Cross-entity state.** A precondition on an entity other than the request's target: the referenced `SubsidyContract` must be `ACTIVE` both when a claim is submitted and when a remittance is posted. `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims/{id}/submit` and `POST /fccn/remittances`.
11. **Posture gate.** The `network_posture` rules of §2.6. `ERR_PROVIDER_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /fccn/enrollments`, `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims`, `POST /fccn/group-purchase-orders`, `POST /fccn/food-program-claims` and `POST /fccn/substitute-assignments`.
12. **Child safety and licensure.** Evaluated before any scheduling or fiscal arithmetic: the selected or requested substitute's `clearance_expires_on` must fall on or after the assignment's `end_at`, returning `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422); the provider's `license_expires_on` must fall on or after the logical clock date, returning `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422); and the operator closing a compliance review or conducting a coaching visit must not be the operator who opened or scheduled it, returning `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Applies to `POST /fccn/substitute-assignments`, `POST /fccn/enrollments`, `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims`, `POST /fccn/coaching-visits/{id}/conduct` and `POST /fccn/compliance-reviews/{id}/close`.
13. **Interval and calendar conflicts.** The requested substitute must have no thirty-minute-buffered overlap with an assignment of theirs in `OFFERED`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` staffing, returning `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409); the provider must have fewer than two coaching visits in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` status inside the inclusive twenty-nine-day-either-side window around the requested `scheduled_on`, returning `ERR_COACHING_WINDOW_SATURATED` (HTTP 409); no other claim outside `REJECTED` status may exist for the same contract and `period_month`, returning `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409); a corrective action plan must arrive on or before the review's `cap_due_on`, returning `ERR_CAP_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422); and a coaching visit may not be scheduled for a date earlier than the logical clock date, returning `ERR_VISIT_DATE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /fccn/substitute-assignments`, `POST /fccn/coaching-visits`, `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims` and `POST /fccn/compliance-reviews/{id}/submit-cap`.
14. **Referential coherence of body collections.** Every claim line's `enrollment_id` must name an enrollment of the contract's provider that is `subsidy_certified`, returning `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_ENROLLMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); every meal line's `child_ref` must match the `child_ref` of an enrollment of the claiming provider, returning `ERR_MEAL_LINE_CHILD_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 422); and a transfer's `receiving_provider_id` must name a different provider that is `ACTIVE` with `network_posture` `OPEN`, returning `ERR_RECEIVING_PROVIDER_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims`, `POST /fccn/food-program-claims` and `POST /fccn/enrollments/{id}/transfer`.
15. **Aggregate and uniqueness constraints.** An agreement's `round_index` may not exceed three negotiation rounds, returning `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409); for one `child_ref` on one `service_date` the sum of `breakfasts` and `lunches` may not exceed 2 and `snacks` may not exceed 1, returning `ERR_MEAL_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); the count of the provider's enrollments in `ENROLLED` or `TRANSITION_PENDING` status must stay below `capacity_slots`, returning `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409); a `child_ref` must be unique among that provider's non-terminal enrollments, returning `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHILD_REF` (HTTP 409); an exit may not be completed while any enrollment of the provider is still `TRANSITION_PENDING`, returning `ERR_EXIT_TRANSITIONS_PENDING` (HTTP 409); and a review may only be escalated once its `cap_rejection_count` has reached two, returning `ERR_CAP_REJECTION_LIMIT_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). Applies to `POST /fccn/membership-agreements/{id}/counter`, `POST /fccn/food-program-claims`, `POST /fccn/enrollments`, `POST /fccn/providers/{id}/complete-exit` and `POST /fccn/compliance-reviews/{id}/escalate`.
16. **Fiscal capacity, checked last.** A contract must have at least one available slot before a subsidy slot is committed, returning `ERR_SUBSIDY_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409); and at submission the contract's cumulative claimed amount plus this claim's provisional amount must not exceed the contract's authorized amount, returning `ERR_CONTRACT_AUTHORIZATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Applies to `POST /fccn/enrollments` and `POST /fccn/subsidy-claims/{id}/submit`.

### 2.9. Nested Reads and Path Resolution

Writes in this system are flat: a provider's enrollment is created by posting to the enrollments collection with a `provider_id` in the body, and a monthly claim is created by posting to the claims collection with a `contract_id` in the body. Nested paths exist only for reading, so that every child collection is reachable from its parent — a provider's agreements, subsidy contracts, food program claims, coaching visits, compliance reviews, group purchase orders and coverage requests all hang off the provider, a contract's claims and remittances hang off the contract, and a substitute's assignments hang off the substitute. Path parameters are resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. Asking for the claims of a contract id that does not exist yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty list; asking for the coaching visits of a nonexistent provider yields `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`. Existence of the parent is established before any filter or pagination parameter is considered.

### 2.10. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer count of cents; there are no fractional-cent amounts anywhere in stored state or in responses. Where a computation produces a fractional cent, it is rounded half up — a value exactly midway between two cents rounds away from zero to the larger cent. **The order in which rounding is applied is part of the specification, not an implementation detail.** Where a rule says a rounding happens per line before summing, summing first and rounding once is wrong and will produce a different total whenever several lines each carry a fractional part that individually rounds down but collectively rounds up. Where a rule says a downstream percentage tier is keyed on an intermediate subtotal, keying it on the final total is wrong even though the final total looks like the more natural input.

Percentages appear in three shapes and must not be confused. `network_fee_pct` and `absence_rate_pct` are expressed as percent numbers between 0 and 100 — a `network_fee_pct` of 7.5 means seven and a half percent — and `network_fee_pct` carries one decimal place with a permitted range of 0 through 15. `discount_pct` and the internal premium and administration-fee rates are expressed as fractions of one. Two derived quantities carry more than two decimals by design: `subsidy_enrollment_share` and `margin_pct` are each rounded half up to four decimal places. `quality_score` and `observation_score` are scores rather than money: `quality_score` is rounded half up to two decimal places within an inclusive range of 0 through 100, and `observation_score` is supplied with one decimal place in the same range.

### 2.11. Cascades, Compensation and Conserved Quantities

Two multi-hop cascades dominate this system, and each has an exact inverse. The quality escalation cascade runs four hops outward from a single escalation, and provider reinstatement runs the same four hops backwards; the network exit cascade runs from the provider through its enrollments to its slot ledgers, and is completed rather than reversed. Cascades in this system are **immediate and synchronous**: by the time a response is returned, every hop has been applied, every touched row's `version` has advanced, and every derived counter is consistent with the rows it summarises. There is no eventual consistency, no background sweep, and no deferred recomputation.

Three quantities are conserved and must reconcile after every operation. First, subsidy slots: a contract's `contracted_slots` always equals its `committed_slots` plus its `available_slots`, and `committed_slots` always equals the number of that provider's enrollments whose `slot_committed` flag is true. Second, remitted cents: a contract's `remitted_cents` always equals the sum of `paid_cents` across that contract's claims plus the contract's `unallocated_credit_cents`, both after a payment is posted and after one is reversed. Third, contract authorization: a contract's `claimed_amount_cents` always equals the sum of the amounts currently counted against authorization by its non-rejected, non-held claims, which means a hold that removes a provisional amount and a reinstatement that restores it must arrive back at exactly the same number with no drift.

Derived fields are recomputed, never patched. `network_posture`, `network_fee_pct`, `quality_score`, `quality_tier`, `quality_variant_applied`, `open_finding_count`, `available_slots`, `committed_slots` and `projected_monthly_revenue_cents` on a provider or contract are all outputs of the rules described in §3 and are never accepted as request input.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Recruitment, Licensing Support and Membership Agreements

Recruitment begins with an unauthenticated application. A prospective home supplies its `legal_name` — between 1 and 120 characters — together with the `county_tier` designation of its service area, which is either `HIGH_NEED` or `STANDARD` and defaults to `STANDARD` when omitted. Applicants may also volunteer their licensing details up front: a `license_number`, a `license_expires_on` date, and a `capacity_slots` figure that must fall between 1 and 12 whenever a value is present. All three of those licensing fields are null until the network verifies the license. A prospective home may also declare `fixed_monthly_cost_cents`, its reported fixed monthly operating cost, which must not be negative and defaults to 1,800 dollars — that is, 180000 cents — when the field is omitted; revenue modeling later leans on this figure heavily. The new provider lands in `APPLICANT` membership status with a `FROZEN` posture, a `quality_score` of 0, a `quality_tier` of `UNRATED`, a `quality_variant_applied` of `NONE`, a `network_fee_pct` of 0, an `open_finding_count` of 0, a `projected_monthly_revenue_cents` of 0, and a null `exit_reason`.

Licensing support is the network's first real service. Network staff record the state's verification result, supplying the authoritative `license_number` and `license_expires_on` and, when the state adjusted it, a revised `capacity_slots`; all supplied values overwrite whatever the applicant had claimed. Verification is only accepted while the provider is `APPLICANT`, only when the license expiry falls strictly later than the current logical clock instant, and only when licensed capacity sits between 1 and 12 children. A successful verification advances the provider `APPLICANT → PENDING_AGREEMENT` and increments the provider's `version` by one; the posture stays `FROZEN`, because a licensed provider with no signed agreement is still not a network member and still may not create any records.

Negotiating the membership agreement is a bounded back-and-forth. A `NetworkAdmin` issues an agreement naming the `provider_id`, the offered `network_fee_pct` (a percentage between 0 and 15 carried to one decimal place), the `shared_services` modules included — a non-empty selection drawn from `ENROLLMENT`, `BILLING`, `FOOD_PROGRAM`, `SUBSTITUTE_POOL` and `GROUP_PURCHASING` — the `effective_on` date on which the membership term starts, and the `expected_provider_version` described in §2.7. The new agreement is born in `SENT` status with a `round_index` of 1, a null `proposed_network_fee_pct`, a `superseded` flag of false, a null `withdrawal_reason` and a null `executed_at`. Issuing it is also a supersede: any other agreement of the same provider still sitting in `SENT` or `COUNTERED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN` with `superseded` set to true and `withdrawal_reason` set to the literal string `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_AGREEMENT`, and the provider's own `version` is incremented by one. That supersede is precisely why the stale-version check exists: a caller working from an old read would silently retire an agreement it had never seen.

The provider may push back rather than sign. Countering carries a `proposed_network_fee_pct` — the fee the provider is willing to accept — and is only available while the agreement is `SENT`, only to the provider named on the agreement, and only while `round_index` is still below three; a fourth round is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. A counter moves the agreement `SENT → COUNTERED`, stores the proposed figure and increments `round_index` by one. Network staff answer a counter by revising: a fresh `network_fee_pct` replaces the offered fee, `proposed_network_fee_pct` is cleared back to null, and the agreement returns `COUNTERED → SENT`, which is what makes the negotiation a genuine cycle bounded at three rounds. Staff may also abandon the negotiation entirely from either side of that cycle, moving the agreement `SENT → WITHDRAWN` or `COUNTERED → WITHDRAWN` and recording the supplied `reason` into `withdrawal_reason`; `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

Execution is the moment the provider becomes a member, and it is the provider — not staff — who performs it. The agreement must be in `SENT` status, must belong to the acting provider, the provider must be sitting in `PENDING_AGREEMENT`, and the provider's `license_expires_on` must fall strictly later than the current logical clock instant; an expired license blocks execution with `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`. On success the agreement moves `SENT → EXECUTED` with `executed_at` stamped to the current logical clock instant, and the same operation moves the provider `PENDING_AGREEMENT → ACTIVE`, recomputes `network_posture` to `OPEN`, copies the agreement's `network_fee_pct` onto the provider, and increments the provider's `version` by one. `EXECUTED` is terminal for the agreement; the fee percentage carried onto the provider is what the revenue model will later charge against gross monthly revenue.

### 3.2. Enrollment, Capacity and the Conservation of Subsidy Slots

Providers enroll children themselves. Each enrollment names the `provider_id` — which must be the acting provider — an opaque `child_ref` code that will later appear on food program meal lines, the household's `family_name` for transition correspondence, a `days_per_week` figure between 1 and 5, a `private_monthly_tuition_cents` amount that must not be negative and defaults to 0 for subsidy-only families, a `subsidy_certified` flag that defaults to false, and the `start_on` date of first care. The record begins in `ENROLLED` status with a null `predecessor_enrollment_id`, a null `exit_on`, and `slot_committed` false.

Four gates guard enrollment creation, and because they sit at different priorities they mask each other in a fixed order. The posture gate of §2.6 fires first, so a provider who is `FROZEN` or on probation learns that before anything else. Next comes licensure: a provider whose `license_expires_on` falls earlier than the logical clock date cannot enroll anyone, and the attempt returns `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`. Then the aggregate constraints: the count of the provider's enrollments in `ENROLLED` or `TRANSITION_PENDING` status must be strictly less than the provider's licensed `capacity_slots`, otherwise `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` is returned, and the supplied `child_ref` must not already be in use by any non-terminal enrollment of the same provider, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHILD_REF` is returned. Only after all of those does the fiscal check run.

Subsidy slots are a conserved quantity, and a subsidy-certified child consumes one. When `subsidy_certified` is true, the enrollment commits a slot on the provider's `ACTIVE` subsidy contract — taking the earliest-created `ACTIVE` contract when more than one exists — by increasing the contract's `committed_slots` by one, decreasing its `available_slots` by one, and setting the enrollment's `slot_committed` to true. If that contract has no available slot left, the enrollment is refused with `ERR_SUBSIDY_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`, and because this is the last check in the ladder, a request that is simultaneously over capacity and out of slots reports the capacity failure. Every successful enrollment also recomputes the provider's `projected_monthly_revenue_cents`.

Withdrawal is the ordinary way a family leaves. The provider supplies the `exit_on` date, and the enrollment moves to `WITHDRAWN` from either `ENROLLED` or `TRANSITION_PENDING`. `exit_on` is recorded, the provider's `projected_monthly_revenue_cents` is recomputed, and any committed subsidy slot is released — the contract's `committed_slots` falls by one and its `available_slots` rises by one — **but only when `slot_committed` was actually true**. This condition matters most on the `TRANSITION_PENDING → WITHDRAWN` path: a child whose enrollment was already marked for transition had its slot released by the exit cascade, `slot_committed` is already false, and no second decrement may occur. `WITHDRAWN` and `TRANSFERRED` are both terminal for an enrollment.

Transfers are the network's family transition support, and only network staff may place a child. The source enrollment must be sitting in `TRANSITION_PENDING`; a `receiving_provider_id` and a `transfer_on` date are supplied. The receiving home must be a *different* provider that is `ACTIVE` with `network_posture` `OPEN`, must have fewer enrollments in `ENROLLED` status than its own `capacity_slots`, and — when the transferring child is `subsidy_certified` — must hold an `ACTIVE` subsidy contract with at least one available slot; any of those failures returns `ERR_RECEIVING_PROVIDER_INELIGIBLE`. A successful placement moves the source enrollment `TRANSITION_PENDING → TRANSFERRED` with `exit_on` set to the transfer date, and creates a brand-new enrollment at the receiving home whose `predecessor_enrollment_id` points back at the source enrollment, whose status is `ENROLLED`, whose `start_on` is the transfer date, and whose `child_ref`, `family_name`, `days_per_week`, `private_monthly_tuition_cents` and `subsidy_certified` are copied unchanged from the source. When the child is subsidy-certified, a slot is committed on the receiving provider's `ACTIVE` contract exactly as it would be on a fresh enrollment, and the new row's `slot_committed` becomes true. Both providers' `projected_monthly_revenue_cents` are recomputed, and the response body is the newly created enrollment rather than the source. The `predecessor_enrollment_id` chain is what lets the network answer "where did this family go" months later, and enrollments can be listed by that predecessor link directly.

### 3.3. Subsidy Contract Administration and the Monthly Claim Ledger

The network administers subsidy contracts on providers' behalf, so a `NetworkAdmin` registers the contract rather than the provider. Registration names the `provider_id`, the `agency_code` identifying the funding agency, the `daily_rate_cents` full-day reimbursement rate — which must be greater than zero — the `contracted_slots` count of authorized subsidy slots, which must be at least 1, the `authorized_amount_cents` ceiling on cumulative claiming, which must be greater than zero, and the `period_start_on` first day of the authorization period. An `absence_rate_pct` may also be supplied; it is the percentage of the daily rate that an allowed absence day earns during final reconciliation, must lie between 0 and 100, and defaults to 50 when omitted. A new contract is `ACTIVE` with `committed_slots` at 0, `available_slots` equal to `contracted_slots`, and `claimed_amount_cents`, `remitted_cents` and `unallocated_credit_cents` all at 0.

Claims are monthly, and the provider drafts them. A draft names the `contract_id`, the `period_month` service month in four-digit-year-hyphen-two-digit-month form, the attendance `lines`, and the `expected_contract_version` of §2.7. Each entry of `lines` is an object carrying an `enrollment_id`, an `attended_days` count between 0 and 22, and an `absent_days` count between 0 and 22; the array may not be empty. Every named enrollment must belong to the contract's provider and must be `subsidy_certified`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_ENROLLMENT_MISMATCH`. No other claim outside `REJECTED` status may already exist for the same contract and the same `period_month`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`. The provider's license must still be current, or `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` is returned. Drafting is permitted while the contract is `SUSPENDED_FOR_QUALITY` — the point of a draft is that it survives a quality hold — but it is still blocked by a `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` posture. A created claim sits in `DRAFT` with `provisional_amount_cents`, `final_amount_cents`, `advance_paid_cents`, `paid_cents` and `settlement_delta_cents` all at 0, with `held_from_status`, `hold_reason` and `rejection_reason` all null, and the act of creating it increments the contract's `version` by one.

Submission is where the network's money starts moving, and it computes the **provisional advance** valuation. The claim must be in `DRAFT`, the contract must be `ACTIVE` — a suspended or terminated contract yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` — the provider's posture must be either `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, and every line's enrollment must still belong to the contract's provider. The provisional amount is built by walking the claim's lines **in order of the referenced enrollment's `created_at` ascending, never by identifier**, and for each line taking the payable day count as the lesser of two quantities: the sum of that line's `attended_days` and `absent_days`, or the monthly authorized ceiling of 22 days. Each line's payable days are multiplied by the contract's `daily_rate_cents` and accumulated into `provisional_amount_cents`. The provisional valuation is deliberately generous: absence days earn the **full** daily rate, there is no per-line absence allowance, and the enrollment's own status is not consulted — only the combined 22-day monthly cap applies. Before the transition is allowed, the contract's `claimed_amount_cents` plus this new provisional amount must not exceed the contract's `authorized_amount_cents`; exceeding the ceiling returns `ERR_CONTRACT_AUTHORIZATION_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 402. On success the claim moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and the contract's `claimed_amount_cents` grows by the provisional amount.

A `FiscalOfficer` then fronts cash against that provisional figure. The advance requires the claim to be `SUBMITTED` and the contract to be `ACTIVE`, moves the claim `SUBMITTED → ADVANCE_PAID`, and sets `advance_paid_cents` to eighty percent of `provisional_amount_cents` with any fraction of a cent discarded — the product of the provisional amount and 80, divided by 100, truncated downward. This is the only place in the system that truncates rather than rounding half up, and it is intentional: the network never advances a cent more than four-fifths of the optimistic valuation.

Reconciliation replaces optimism with the agency's verified attendance, and it computes the **final reconciled** valuation over the very same lines. The claim must be in `ADVANCE_PAID`. Lines are again walked in order of the referenced enrollment's `created_at` ascending. For each line, the attended component is the lesser of that line's `attended_days` and 22, multiplied by the contract's `daily_rate_cents` — note that attended days are now capped independently of absence days rather than jointly. The absence component depends on where the child now is: if the referenced enrollment's status is `WITHDRAWN` or `TRANSFERRED`, the absence component for that line is zero, because absence days are not payable for a child who has left the home. Otherwise the paid absent days are the lesser of that line's `absent_days` and the per-line absence allowance of 3 days, and the absence component is the product of those paid absent days, the contract's `daily_rate_cents` and the contract's `absence_rate_pct` divided by 100, **rounded half up for that line before it is added to anything else**. The attended and absence components of each line are summed into `final_amount_cents`. Because the enrollment's status is read at the instant reconciliation runs, a child who withdrew between submission and reconciliation changes the final valuation while leaving the provisional valuation untouched — which is the whole reason both figures are stored. Reconciliation also writes `settlement_delta_cents` as the final amount less the already-disbursed `advance_paid_cents`, adjusts the contract's `claimed_amount_cents` by the difference between the final and provisional amounts (so an over-optimistic provisional gives authorization headroom back and an under-estimate consumes more), and moves the claim `ADVANCE_PAID → RECONCILED`.

Rejection is the agency's veto and it unwinds authorization. A `FiscalOfficer` supplies a `rejection_reason`; the claim must be `SUBMITTED`, moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, records the reason, and the contract's `claimed_amount_cents` is reduced by that claim's `provisional_amount_cents`. Because the service-month uniqueness rule ignores rejected claims, the provider may immediately draft a replacement claim for the same `period_month`. `REJECTED` and `SETTLED` are the claim's two terminal states.

### 3.4. Agency Remittances and the Allocation Waterfall

Agency payments do not arrive claim by claim. A `FiscalOfficer` posts a remittance naming the `contract_id`, the agency's own `agency_reference` — which must be unique among that contract's remittances still in `POSTED` status — the `amount_cents` received, which must be greater than zero, and the `received_on` date. Posting requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`; a suspended or terminated contract returns `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A posted remittance starts in `POSTED` status and immediately runs a waterfall over the contract's outstanding claims.

The waterfall's candidate set is narrow: only claims of the same contract that are in `RECONCILED` status **and** whose `paid_cents` is still below their `final_amount_cents` participate. Claims in `SETTLED`, `HELD`, `SUBMITTED`, `ADVANCE_PAID`, `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` status are skipped entirely — a claim that has not been reconciled has no verified amount to pay against. Candidates are ordered by `period_month` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, never by identifier; ties cannot occur, because service months are unique per contract among non-rejected claims. Beginning with the remittance's full `amount_cents` as the remaining balance, each candidate in turn takes the lesser of the remaining balance and its own outstanding shortfall (its `final_amount_cents` less its `paid_cents`). That amount is added to the claim's `paid_cents` and appended to the remittance's `allocations` array as an object pairing a `claim_id` with the `allocated_cents` granted, in allocation order, and the remaining balance falls by the same amount. Any claim whose `paid_cents` reaches its `final_amount_cents` moves `RECONCILED → SETTLED`. The walk stops as soon as the remaining balance reaches zero. Whatever balance survives the walk becomes the remittance's `unallocated_cents`. No rounding occurs anywhere in the waterfall: every cent is either allocated to a claim or credited as unallocated. Finally, the contract's `remitted_cents` grows by the remittance's `amount_cents` and its `unallocated_credit_cents` grows by the `unallocated_cents`, which is what keeps the remitted-cents conservation rule of §2.11 true.

Reversal is the compensating action for a mis-posted payment, and it must leave zero drift. A `FiscalOfficer` supplies a `reversal_reason`; the remittance must be `POSTED`, and it moves `POSTED → REVERSED` with the reason recorded in `reversal_reason` — `REVERSED` is terminal. Every entry of the stored `allocations` is undone: each named claim's `paid_cents` is reduced by that entry's `allocated_cents`, and any claim in `SETTLED` status whose `paid_cents` consequently falls below its `final_amount_cents` returns `SETTLED → RECONCILED`, making it eligible for the next waterfall again. The contract's `remitted_cents` is reduced by the remittance's `amount_cents` and its `unallocated_credit_cents` is reduced by the remittance's `unallocated_cents`. After the reversal, the contract's `remitted_cents` must once more equal the sum of `paid_cents` across its claims plus its `unallocated_credit_cents`.

### 3.5. Food Program Claiming and Meal Reimbursement

The child nutrition program pays on meals served, not on attendance, and it pays at two very different rates depending on how needy the provider's service area and caseload are. A provider files one claim per month, supplying the `provider_id`, the `period_month`, and the `meal_lines` array. Each entry of `meal_lines` is an object carrying a `child_ref`, a `service_date`, and counts of `breakfasts`, `lunches` and `snacks`, each count between 0 and 2; the array may not be empty. A month already claimed by that provider may not be claimed again, and a second attempt is refused as an invalid request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every `child_ref` on a meal line must match the `child_ref` of one of the claiming provider's enrollments, or the filing is refused with `ERR_MEAL_LINE_CHILD_UNKNOWN`. Per-child daily meal caps are absolute: for a single `child_ref` on a single `service_date`, the sum of `breakfasts` and `lunches` may not exceed two meals and `snacks` may not exceed one, and a violation returns `ERR_MEAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Filing is also subject to the posture gate, so a frozen provider may not claim at all.

Tiering is decided once, at filing time, and then frozen onto the claim so that later enrollment churn cannot retroactively re-price a filed month. The system first computes `subsidy_enrollment_share`: when the provider has no enrollments in `ENROLLED` status the share is 0.0000, and otherwise it is the count of that provider's `ENROLLED` enrollments whose `subsidy_certified` flag is true divided by the total count of that provider's `ENROLLED` enrollments, rounded half up to four decimal places. `tier_applied` is then `TIER_I` when either the provider's `county_tier` is `HIGH_NEED` or the computed share is at least 0.5000, and `TIER_II` in every other case. The two schedules are: under `TIER_I`, 187 cents per breakfast, 348 cents per lunch and 100 cents per snack; under `TIER_II`, 67 cents per breakfast, 210 cents per lunch and 27 cents per snack. `claimed_amount_cents` is the sum, across every meal line, of that line's `breakfasts` multiplied by the applied breakfast rate plus its `lunches` multiplied by the applied lunch rate plus its `snacks` multiplied by the applied snack rate. The claim is created in `SUBMITTED` status with `reimbursed_amount_cents` at 0 and an empty `disallowed_child_refs` array.

Approval belongs to a `FiscalOfficer` and has two symmetric outcomes. When `disallowed_child_refs` is absent from the request or is supplied as an empty array, the claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and `reimbursed_amount_cents` is set equal to `claimed_amount_cents` in full. When `disallowed_child_refs` is supplied and non-empty, every `child_ref` it names must appear on at least one meal line of this claim — otherwise the request is rejected as invalid with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED_WITH_DISALLOWANCE`. In that branch the supplied array is stored on the claim, and `reimbursed_amount_cents` becomes `claimed_amount_cents` less the sum of the per-line amounts of every meal line whose `child_ref` appears in the disallowance list, where each such line's amount is computed at the tier already frozen on the claim. Disallowance removes lines; **it never re-tiers the claim**, even when removing those children would have flipped the subsidy share across the tier threshold. Both approval branches are terminal for the claim, and both recompute the provider's `projected_monthly_revenue_cents`, since the food program is one of the three revenue components.

### 3.6. Substitute Pool Coordination, Selection and Coverage Billing

The pool is a network asset. A `NetworkAdmin` adds a member by supplying their `full_name`, their `credential_level` — one of `NONE`, `CDA` or `CHILD_DEV_TEACHER` — the `clearance_expires_on` instant at which their background clearance lapses, their base `hourly_rate_cents`, which must be greater than zero, and the `service_county_tiers` they are willing to travel to, a non-empty selection from `HIGH_NEED` and `STANDARD`. New members are `AVAILABLE` with an `assignment_count_completed` of 0. Clearance determinations are recorded later: staff may supply a fresh `clearance_expires_on`, a new `pool_status` of either `AVAILABLE` or `SUSPENDED`, or both, and whichever is supplied overwrites the stored value. Suspending a member or shortening their clearance does **not** disturb assignments they have already confirmed; it only removes them from future selection.

Coverage requests come from providers. A request names the `provider_id`, the coverage window's `start_at` and `end_at` instants — the window is half-open, with `end_at` strictly later than `start_at` and treated as exclusive — the `reason` for coverage, which is `ILLNESS`, `VACATION` or `TRAINING`, and the `min_credential_level` the provider insists on, which defaults to `NONE`. A provider who has a particular pool member in mind may name a `requested_substitute_id`, in which case that member is honoured instead of running the selector. A new assignment is born with `staffing_status` `REQUESTED`, `billing_status` `UNBILLED`, a null `substitute_id`, an empty `declined_substitute_ids` array, and `billable_hours`, `pre_fee_subtotal_cents`, `network_admin_fee_cents` and `total_cost_cents` all at 0. Requests are subject to the posture gate, so a frozen provider cannot obtain coverage, while a provider on probation still can.

Selection is a deterministic single-row pick, and every eligibility clause must hold for a pool member to be a candidate. The member's `pool_status` must be `AVAILABLE`. Their `clearance_expires_on` must fall on or after the assignment's `end_at` — clearance must outlast the coverage, and a requested member who fails this returns `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`. Their credential must rank at or above the requested minimum, where `NONE` ranks 0, `CDA` ranks 1 and `CHILD_DEV_TEACHER` ranks 2. The requesting provider's `county_tier` must appear in the member's `service_county_tiers`. The member's id must not already appear in the assignment's `declined_substitute_ids`. Finally, the member must have no scheduling conflict: no other assignment of theirs whose `staffing_status` is `OFFERED`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` may overlap the new window once the new window is widened by a travel buffer of half an hour on each side. Concretely, a conflict exists when the new window's start, moved thirty minutes earlier, falls strictly before that other assignment's `end_at` **and** the new window's end, moved thirty minutes later, falls strictly after that other assignment's `start_at`. Assignments in `REQUESTED` or `UNFILLED` staffing hold no member and are therefore irrelevant to the conflict test. A requested member who conflicts returns `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`.

Among the surviving candidates, the winner is chosen by cheapest first — `hourly_rate_cents` ascending — then by fewest completed jobs, `assignment_count_completed` ascending, then by longest tenure in the pool, `created_at` ascending. Identifiers are never a ranking key. The selected member's id is written to `substitute_id` and the assignment advances `REQUESTED → OFFERED` while `billing_status` stays `UNBILLED`. When the candidate set is empty, no member is written, `substitute_id` stays null, and the assignment moves `REQUESTED → UNFILLED`, which is terminal.

The offered member answers for themselves. Accepting requires the assignment's `staffing_status` to be `OFFERED`, requires the acting substitute to be the one named on `substitute_id`, and re-checks that their `pool_status` is `AVAILABLE` and their `clearance_expires_on` still falls on or after the assignment's `end_at`; it moves the assignment `OFFERED → CONFIRMED` and leaves `billing_status` untouched. Declining also requires `OFFERED` status and the acting substitute's ownership; the declining member's id is appended to `declined_substitute_ids`, `substitute_id` is cleared to null, and the selector re-runs immediately with the grown exclusion set. On a hit, the assignment returns to `OFFERED` with the next-ranked member — passing through `REQUESTED` on the way, so the pair reads `OFFERED → REQUESTED → OFFERED` — and on an empty candidate set it lands in `UNFILLED`. The exclusion set only ever grows, so the cycle terminates.

Completion is claimed by the provider and is the only place coverage cost is computed. The assignment must be `CONFIRMED`, must belong to the acting provider, and its `end_at` must fall at or before the current logical clock instant — coverage cannot be billed before it has finished. The costing runs in a pinned order. First, the coverage duration is measured in whole minutes from `start_at` to `end_at`; `billable_hours` is the greater of the four-hour minimum call-out and that duration in minutes divided by 60 and rounded **up** to a whole number. Second, the base amount is `billable_hours` multiplied by the member's `hourly_rate_cents` as read at completion time. Third, a credential premium is applied: no premium for `NONE`, ten percent of the base for `CDA`, and eighteen percent of the base for `CHILD_DEV_TEACHER`, with the premium amount rounded half up to whole cents; `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` is the base plus that premium. Fourth — and this is the step implementations get wrong — the network administration fee percentage is selected from a ladder keyed on `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` and **not** on the final total: six percent when the pre-fee subtotal is at least 30000 cents, eight percent when it is at least 15000 cents, and ten percent otherwise. `network_admin_fee_cents` is that percentage of `pre_fee_subtotal_cents`, rounded half up. `total_cost_cents` is `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` plus `network_admin_fee_cents`. Completion moves `staffing_status` `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`, advances `billing_status` `UNBILLED → ACCRUED`, increases the member's `assignment_count_completed` by one, and recomputes the provider's `projected_monthly_revenue_cents`, since accrued coverage is a cost in the revenue model.

Billing runs on its own axis. An assignment carries two independent status dimensions, and invoicing moves only the second one: a `FiscalOfficer` bills accrued coverage to the provider's shared-services account, which requires `billing_status` to be `ACCRUED` and advances it `ACCRUED → INVOICED` while `staffing_status` remains `COMPLETED` throughout. `COMPLETED` and `UNFILLED` are the staffing dimension's terminal states; the billing dimension's own progression from `UNBILLED` through `ACCRUED` to `INVOICED` is what the revenue model reads.

### 3.7. Coaching Visits, Quality Scoring and Compliance Escalation

Coaching is scheduled by a `QualityCoach`, who names the `provider_id`, the `scheduled_on` visit date and the `focus_area`, which is one of `ENVIRONMENT`, `CURRICULUM`, `BUSINESS_PRACTICE` or `HEALTH_SAFETY`. The scheduling operator's identifier is recorded as `coach_user_id`, and the visit starts `SCHEDULED` with a null `conducted_by_user_id`, a null `visit_conducted_on`, a null `observation_score`, an empty `findings` array and a `critical_finding` flag of false. Two calendar rules apply. A visit may not be scheduled for a date earlier than the logical clock date, which returns `ERR_VISIT_DATE_IN_PAST`. And coaching is rationed: the provider must have fewer than two visits in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` status whose `scheduled_on` falls inside the inclusive window running twenty-nine days before to twenty-nine days after the requested date — a fifty-nine-day span in total, counting the requested date itself, so a home is not visited more than twice in any such stretch. A third visit inside that window returns `ERR_COACHING_WINDOW_SATURATED`.

Conducting the visit is a different operator's job. The request supplies the `visit_conducted_on` date the visit actually happened, the `observation_score` between 0 and 100 carried to one decimal place, the `findings` array of finding codes, and the `critical_finding` flag; it may also supply `critical_standard_codes` and a `cap_due_on` date, which are used only when the finding is critical. The visit must be `SCHEDULED`; the conducted date must fall on or after the visit's `scheduled_on` and on or before the logical clock date; and the acting operator must not be the operator recorded in `coach_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the observation is stored, `conducted_by_user_id` is set to the acting operator, and the visit moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, which is terminal.

Conducting a visit re-scores the provider using the **coaching-weighted** variant. The provider's coaching visits in `COMPLETED` status — visits still `SCHEDULED` never count towards either variant — are ordered by `visit_conducted_on` descending and then by `created_at` descending, and at most the first three are taken. If no completed visit exists at all, `quality_score` is 0.00 and `quality_tier` is `UNRATED`, and nothing further is computed. Otherwise the recency weights one-half, three-tenths and one-fifth are matched to the picked visits in that order, the sum of each visit's `observation_score` multiplied by its weight is divided by the sum of only the weights actually used — so a provider with a single completed visit scores exactly that visit's observation, and a provider with two visits has their scores blended five-eighths to three-eighths — and the quotient is rounded half up to two decimal places. There is no date window in this variant.

Closing a compliance review re-scores the provider using the **compliance-anchored** variant instead. Here every completed visit whose `visit_conducted_on` falls within the inclusive window that begins 180 days before the logical clock date and ends at the logical clock date is counted, no matter how many that is, and the score is the plain arithmetic mean of their `observation_score` values — their sum divided by their count — rounded half up to two decimal places. If that window contains no completed visit, `quality_score` is 0.00 and `quality_tier` is `UNRATED`.

Both variants then share a tail. The provider's open-finding penalty is four points per finding: the score is reduced by 4.0 multiplied by the provider's `open_finding_count`, the result is rounded half up to two decimal places, and then clamped into the inclusive range from 0.00 through 100.00. The penalty reads `open_finding_count` **after** the triggering transition has already applied its own increment or decrement, so a review that closes has already stopped counting against the provider by the time the score is computed. `quality_tier` is finally banded from the resulting score: `TIER_1` at 85 or above, `TIER_2` at 70 or above but below 85, and `TIER_3` below 70. Whichever variant ran writes its own name into `quality_variant_applied`, either `COACHING_WEIGHTED` or `COMPLIANCE_ANCHORED`, so a reader can always tell which arithmetic produced the current score. A provider's `open_finding_count` is by definition the number of that provider's compliance reviews sitting in `OPEN`, `CAP_SUBMITTED` or `CAP_REJECTED` status.

Compliance reviews arrive by two routes, and the `origin` field records which. A `QualityCoach` may open a scheduled audit directly, naming the `provider_id`, a non-empty `standard_codes` array of cited network quality standards, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, and the `cap_due_on` deadline for the corrective action plan; such a review carries `origin` `SCHEDULED_AUDIT`. Alternatively, a conducted visit whose `critical_finding` flag is true automatically opens a review with `origin` `COACHING_CRITICAL_FINDING`, a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `standard_codes` taken from the request's `critical_standard_codes` or defaulting to the single code `QS-CRITICAL` when that field is omitted, and a `cap_due_on` taken from the request or defaulting to thirty days after the logical clock date. Either way the review starts in `OPEN` status with `cap_rejection_count` at 0, `cap_narrative` and `cap_submitted_at` null, `closed_by_user_id` null, `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting operator — for an automatic review, the operator who conducted the triggering visit — and the provider's `open_finding_count` increased by one.

The corrective-action cycle is where providers and coaches iterate. The provider submits a plan by supplying a `cap_narrative`; the review must be in `OPEN` or `CAP_REJECTED` status, the review must belong to the acting provider, and the logical clock date must fall on or before `cap_due_on`, with a late submission refused as `ERR_CAP_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A submission stores the narrative — replacing any previous one — stamps `cap_submitted_at` with the current logical clock instant, and moves the review to `CAP_SUBMITTED` from whichever of the two source states it occupied. A coach who is unsatisfied rejects the plan: the review must be `CAP_SUBMITTED`, it moves `CAP_SUBMITTED → CAP_REJECTED`, and `cap_rejection_count` increases by one, which is the counter that eventually authorizes escalation. The cycle `CAP_SUBMITTED → CAP_REJECTED → CAP_SUBMITTED` may repeat. A review leaves that cycle in exactly one of two terminal states: `CLOSED_COMPLIANT` when a coach accepts the plan, or `ESCALATED_TO_LICENSING` when the network gives up on it.

A satisfied coach closes the review, and the closer may not be the opener. Closing requires the review to be `CAP_SUBMITTED` and requires the acting operator's identifier to differ from `opened_by_user_id`, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` otherwise. On success `closed_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting operator, the review moves `CAP_SUBMITTED → CLOSED_COMPLIANT` — a terminal state — the provider's `open_finding_count` decreases by one, and the provider's `quality_score` and `quality_tier` are recomputed with the compliance-anchored variant while `quality_variant_applied` becomes `COMPLIANCE_ANCHORED`.

Escalation is the network's last resort, is reserved to a `NetworkAdmin`, and sets off a four-hop cascade. The review must be sitting in `CAP_REJECTED`, its `cap_rejection_count` must have reached at least two — an earlier attempt returns `ERR_CAP_REJECTION_LIMIT_NOT_REACHED` — and the provider must currently be `ACTIVE`. The review moves `CAP_REJECTED → ESCALATED_TO_LICENSING`, which is terminal, and the provider's `open_finding_count` decreases by one. Hop one moves the provider `ACTIVE → PROBATION` and recomputes `network_posture` to `RESTRICTED`, incrementing the provider's `version` by one. Hop two moves every subsidy contract of that provider that is in `ACTIVE` status to `SUSPENDED_FOR_QUALITY`. Hop three moves every subsidy claim of those contracts that is in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` status to `HELD`, recording the status it came from in `held_from_status` and setting `hold_reason` to the literal string `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED_FOR_QUALITY`; a claim held out of `SUBMITTED` additionally gives its `provisional_amount_cents` back to its contract's `claimed_amount_cents`, while a claim held out of `DRAFT` had consumed no authorization and therefore returns none. Hop four recomputes the provider's `projected_monthly_revenue_cents`, and because the enrollments named on the lines of a newly held claim for the current clock month drop out of the subsidy revenue component, the escalation is visible in the provider's projected revenue as well as in their status.

Reinstatement is the exact inverse and must leave no residue. A `NetworkAdmin` lifts probation only when the provider is in `PROBATION` and their `open_finding_count` has fallen to zero; the provider moves `PROBATION → ACTIVE`, `network_posture` is recomputed to `OPEN`, and the provider's `version` increments by one, making `ACTIVE → PROBATION → ACTIVE` a genuine cycle. The compensating second hop returns every subsidy contract of that provider in `SUSPENDED_FOR_QUALITY` status to `ACTIVE`. The compensating third hop returns every `HELD` claim of those contracts to the status recorded in its `held_from_status` — `HELD → DRAFT` or `HELD → SUBMITTED` — clears both `held_from_status` and `hold_reason` back to null, and for a claim returning to `SUBMITTED` restores that claim's `provisional_amount_cents` to its contract's `claimed_amount_cents`, arriving back at exactly the figure the contract carried before the hold with zero drift. The compensating fourth hop recomputes `projected_monthly_revenue_cents`, which brings the previously held enrollments back into the subsidy component.

### 3.8. Group Purchasing and Provider Revenue Modeling

Group purchasing lets a small home buy at network prices. A provider opens an order naming the `provider_id`, the `category` — `SUPPLIES`, `CURRICULUM` or `INSURANCE` — and a non-empty `lines` array whose entries each carry a `sku`, a `unit_price_cents` greater than zero and a `quantity` of at least 1. A new order sits in `OPEN` status with `total_quantity`, `discount_pct`, `subtotal_cents`, `insurance_admin_fee_cents` and `total_cents` all at 0; nothing is priced until the provider commits.

Committing prices the order and is only available to the owning provider while the order is `OPEN` and while the provider's `network_posture` is `OPEN` — unlike most provider traffic, group purchasing is barred outright on probation, not merely restricted. Pricing begins with `total_quantity`, the sum of every line's `quantity`, which selects one rung of the volume ladder: a twelve percent discount at 100 units or more, an eight percent discount at 50 units or more, a five percent discount at 15 units or more, and no discount below 15 units. `discount_pct` records the selected fraction. **The rounding order is pinned:** walking the lines in the order they were supplied in the request body, which is also the order they are persisted in, each line's gross amount is its `unit_price_cents` multiplied by its `quantity`, the discounted amount is that gross multiplied by one minus the discount fraction and **then rounded half up to whole cents for that line alone**, and only those per-line rounded amounts are accumulated into `subtotal_cents`. Summing the gross amounts first and rounding once at the end is wrong and will disagree whenever several lines' fractional cents each round down individually but would round up in aggregate. `insurance_admin_fee_cents` is three percent of `subtotal_cents`, rounded half up, but only when the order's `category` is `INSURANCE`; for `SUPPLIES` and `CURRICULUM` it is 0. `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `insurance_admin_fee_cents`, and the order moves `OPEN → COMMITTED`. `COMMITTED` is the order's only terminal state, and a committed order cannot be committed again.

Revenue modeling is the network's tuition-setting guidance, and it exists in two forms: a stored denormalized figure and a full computed read. The stored figure is the provider's `projected_monthly_revenue_cents`, which holds nothing but the monthly revenue total and is refreshed whenever an enrollment's status changes, a subsidy slot commitment changes, a food program claim is approved, or a subsidy claim is held or released. The computed read returns the whole model — `monthly_revenue_cents`, `subsidy_component_cents`, `private_component_cents`, `food_component_cents`, `monthly_cost_cents`, `network_fee_cents`, `substitute_cost_cents`, `margin_pct`, `sustainability` and `recommended_private_tuition_cents` — recalculated at read time from current state, and its `monthly_revenue_cents` is by construction the same number the stored field carries.

The model is built as follows, with the **clock month** meaning the year-and-month of the logical clock. The relevant contract is the provider's subsidy contract whose status is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_FOR_QUALITY`, taking the earliest-created such contract, and there may be none. From that contract, the set of held enrollment references is collected: every `enrollment_id` named on the lines of that contract's claims that are in `HELD` status with a `period_month` equal to the clock month. The subsidy-bearing enrollments are the provider's enrollments in `ENROLLED` status whose `subsidy_certified` is true and whose `slot_committed` is true, excluding any whose identifier appears in that held set — this exclusion is exactly what makes the fourth hop of the escalation cascade observable. The private-pay enrollments are the provider's enrollments in `ENROLLED` status whose `subsidy_certified` is false. Enrollments in `TRANSITION_PENDING`, `TRANSFERRED` or `WITHDRAWN` status are excluded from both sets.

`subsidy_component_cents` is the count of subsidy-bearing enrollments multiplied by the contract's `daily_rate_cents` — treated as zero when the provider has no such contract — multiplied by the 22 authorized care days in a month. `private_component_cents` is the sum of `private_monthly_tuition_cents` across the private-pay enrollments. `food_component_cents` is the `reimbursed_amount_cents` of the provider's single most recent approved food program claim: among the provider's claims in `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_DISALLOWANCE` status, ordered by `period_month` descending and then by `created_at` descending, the first row's reimbursed amount is taken, and the component is 0 when no such claim exists. `monthly_revenue_cents` is the sum of those three components.

The cost stack has three parts. `network_fee_cents` is `monthly_revenue_cents` multiplied by the provider's `network_fee_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. `substitute_cost_cents` is the sum of `total_cost_cents` across the provider's substitute assignments whose `billing_status` is `ACCRUED` or `INVOICED` and whose `start_at` falls in the clock month. `monthly_cost_cents` is the provider's `fixed_monthly_cost_cents` plus `network_fee_cents` plus `substitute_cost_cents`.

Sustainability banding and tuition guidance close the model. When the provider has no enrollments at all in either set, `margin_pct` is 0.0000, `sustainability` is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, and `recommended_private_tuition_cents` is null. Otherwise `margin_pct` is `monthly_revenue_cents` less `monthly_cost_cents`, divided by `monthly_revenue_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and `sustainability` is `SUSTAINABLE` when that margin is at least 0.1500, `THIN` when it is at least 0.0500 but below 0.1500, and `AT_RISK` below 0.0500. Tuition guidance only exists when there is at least one private-pay family to charge: with no private-pay enrollment, `recommended_private_tuition_cents` is null. Otherwise the network targets a fifteen percent margin, so a denominator is formed as one, less the target margin of 0.15, less the provider's `network_fee_pct` divided by 100. The private revenue needed is the provider's `fixed_monthly_cost_cents` plus `substitute_cost_cents`, divided by that denominator and rounded **up** to a whole cent, then reduced by `subsidy_component_cents` and by `food_component_cents`, with a floor of zero so the figure is never negative. That needed amount is divided evenly by the number of private-pay enrollments, and `recommended_private_tuition_cents` is that per-child figure rounded **up** to the next whole multiple of 500 cents — providers set tuition in five-dollar steps, and the guidance never rounds down below the number that hits the target.

### 3.9. Network Exit and Family Transition Support

Exits are the hardest thing a network does, because a provider closing their doors strands families who have nowhere else to go. Initiating an exit is a `NetworkAdmin` action that requires the provider to be in `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` status and requires an `exit_reason` to be recorded on the provider. The provider moves to `EXITING` from whichever of those two states it occupied, `network_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN` so no further provider-initiated write succeeds, and the provider's `version` increments by one. The exit's first cascade hop moves every enrollment of that provider in `ENROLLED` status to `TRANSITION_PENDING`. The second hop follows from the first: because those enrollments have left `ENROLLED`, each one that was holding a contracted subsidy slot releases it — the contract's `committed_slots` falls by one, its `available_slots` rises by one, and the enrollment's `slot_committed` becomes false — and this happens only for enrollments whose `slot_committed` was true. The provider's `projected_monthly_revenue_cents` is then recomputed, which will normally collapse it, since transition-pending enrollments contribute to no revenue component.

Every transition-pending family must then be resolved, either by transferring to another network home or by withdrawing, as described in §3.2. Only once none of the provider's enrollments remains in `ENROLLED` or `TRANSITION_PENDING` status may the exit be finalized; attempting to finalize earlier returns `ERR_EXIT_TRANSITIONS_PENDING`. Finalizing requires the provider to be in `EXITING` status, moves the provider `EXITING → EXITED` — the only terminal membership state — leaves `network_posture` at `FROZEN`, and increments the provider's `version` by one. Finalizing also terminates the money: every subsidy contract of that provider moves to `TERMINATED`, whether it was `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_FOR_QUALITY` beforehand, and no further claim may be created or submitted against a terminated contract. `TERMINATED` is the contract's terminal state, and because claim submission and remittance posting both demand an `ACTIVE` contract, both fail afterwards with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Already-reconciled claims retain their computed amounts and their allocation history; the exit closes the door on new fiscal activity without rewriting what already happened.
