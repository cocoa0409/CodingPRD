# Water Utility High-Bill Investigation & Leak Adjustment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Municipal water utilities receive their heaviest customer-service load from a single recurring event: a bill that arrives several times larger than the customer expected. Behind that one complaint sit half a dozen very different root causes — a burst service line under the front lawn, a running toilet flapper, a summer irrigation habit the household forgot about, a meter that has started over-registering, or a field crew that estimated a read instead of walking the route. Each cause carries a different fiscal consequence, a different evidentiary burden, and a different set of people authorised to sign off on the money involved. Today those decisions are made in spreadsheets and email threads, which means two customers with identical consumption can receive materially different credits, and no one can reconstruct afterwards who approved what.

This service replaces that improvisation with an auditable workflow. It screens every issued bill against a seasonal consumption baseline drawn from the same meter's own history, opens a formal investigation when the customer disputes the charge, collects evidence in bounded rounds (plumber repair proof, meter accuracy testing on the bench or in the field), records a single leak determination, and then computes the resulting credit from a published formula rather than from a representative's judgement. Around that spine sit the protections customers actually care about: service cannot be shut off while an investigation or a rebill is outstanding, an instalment plan can carry an unaffordable balance, and a customer who disagrees with the utility's determination can appeal to the utility board and have a clerk docket a hearing.

Two design commitments run through everything below. First, **every cent is a replay of an append-only ledger** — no balance is ever edited in place, and a reversal is expressed as a compensating entry rather than as a deletion, so the account balance after reversing a credit is bit-for-bit the balance before it was granted. Second, **the person who decides is never the person who pays out** — the representative who records a leak determination may not approve the credit that determination unlocks, and neither of them may sit as the board's hearing officer. The API is a flat collection of top-level resources with no URL prefix; relationships between them are carried by explicit identifier fields in request payloads.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Utility Staff, Customers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request except the logical-clock endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal is exercising. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, or is a declared role that the target operation does not list, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

The six roles are `Customer`, `CustomerServiceRep`, `MeterTechnician`, `BillingSupervisor`, `UtilityBoardClerk` and `SystemAdmin`. Their identity semantics differ in one important way. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ServiceAccount` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ServiceAccount` compare the resource's account identifier to `X-User-ID`, and a customer reaching a bill, meter, reading, investigation, repair proof, leak adjustment, arrangement, appeal or ledger entry that resolves to a different `ServiceAccount` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerServiceRep`, `MeterTechnician`, `BillingSupervisor`, `UtilityBoardClerk` or `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow step demands attribution — as `opened_by_user_id` and `determination_by_user_id` on an investigation, `verified_by_user_id` on a repair proof, `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a leak adjustment, `filed_by_user_id` and `hearing_officer_user_id` on a board appeal — but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and these five roles are unscoped across accounts.

Capability boundaries are deliberate rather than incidental. A `SystemAdmin` owns the reference data — rate schedules and billing periods, including closing and locking a period — and may register accounts, meters, readings and bills, but may not issue or rebill a bill, may not touch any leak adjustment or investigation action, may not write repair proofs, arrangements or appeals, may not schedule, execute or record payments on an account, and may not read the adjustment preview. A `BillingSupervisor` issues and rebills bills, returns, approves, denies and reverses leak adjustments, schedules and executes shutoffs, and reinstates defaulted arrangements — but may never draft or submit a leak adjustment and may never record a determination, because the drafter and the approver are separated at the role level as well as at the user level. A `CustomerServiceRep` runs the investigation itself: requesting evidence, determining the cause, closing without adjustment, verifying and rejecting repair proofs, and drafting and submitting leak adjustments; the rep may not approve, return, deny or reverse a credit, may not issue or rebill, may not post readings or drive meter tests, may not schedule or execute shutoffs, may not reinstate arrangements, and may not docket or decide appeals. A `MeterTechnician` owns the physical estate: posting meter readings, scheduling field tests, recording field results and recording bench results, plus registering meters; the technician touches no bill, investigation, proof, adjustment, arrangement or appeal. A `UtilityBoardClerk` dockets hearings and records board decisions but may not file an appeal — the clerk decides appeals, it does not bring them — and holds read access to investigations, repair proofs, leak adjustments and appeals only. A `Customer` opens investigations, submits repair proofs, requests meter tests, files appeals, proposes, activates and pays down arrangements, records account payments, and reads their own records; every reference-data write, every bill action, every investigation action, every proof review, every leak-adjustment operation, every meter or reading write, every meter-test action, hearing docketing and decision recording, shutoff scheduling and execution, arrangement reinstatement, and the adjustment preview are all closed to them.

Beyond role separation, two operations compare user identifiers directly. Approving, denying or reversing a leak adjustment fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the `determination_by_user_id` recorded on the parent investigation, so a single human holding both the rep and supervisor roles is still blocked. Recording a board decision fails with `ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_CONFLICT` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals either that same `determination_by_user_id` or the appeal's `filed_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection listing accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, list endpoints sort by `created_at` ascending. Two collections carry a domain-specific ordering instead: an account's ledger entries are returned oldest-first by `effective_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and meter readings are returned by `read_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. A bill's ledger entries are returned by `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix, never supplied by the caller. Most resources also carry three audit properties: `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by one on every mutation of that row. Any operation that changes a row refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`, including changes made as a downstream side effect of an operation aimed at a different resource.

`AccountLedgerEntry` is the one exception. Ledger entries are append-only: once written they are never updated and never deleted, so they carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all.

All date and time comparisons in this service read a **logical clock**, never the host wall clock. The clock starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the process starts, and an operator may move it by supplying `current_utc` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant to the clock-setting capability, which echoes back the accepted `current_utc`, mutates no entity and recomputes no derived field. That capability is the sole endpoint that does not require `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`; every declared role may call it. Wherever this document says "the current instant" or "today", it means the logical clock's value at the moment the request is processed.

### 2.5. Cents, Gallons and the Rounding Discipline

All money is expressed in integer cents and all volume in integer gallons; there are no fractional currency or volume fields. Wherever a division or a rate multiplication produces a fraction, the result is rounded **half up** — a value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero — and the rounding happens at the step in which the division appears, not at the end of the calculation. Volumetric water and sewer rates are quoted in cents per one thousand gallons, so converting a gallon volume into cents always divides by 1000 after multiplying by the rate. Percentage-style parameters are quoted in basis points, so converting a basis-point figure into a multiplier always divides by 10000.

Two rounding decisions are load-bearing and must not be "simplified". In the tiered water charge, each tier line is rounded to whole cents independently and the rounded lines are then summed; rounding a single grand total instead produces a different answer. In the instalment schedule, the per-instalment amount is produced by a floor division and the entire remainder is pushed onto the final instalment; rounding each instalment independently would not reconcile to the financed amount.

Three fields round to more than zero decimal places. A meter's `accuracy_pct` and a test request's `accuracy_pct` are percentages carried to 2 decimal places, rounded half up. A meter's `correction_factor` is carried to 6 decimal places, rounded half up. A bill's `anomaly_ratio` is carried to 3 decimal places, rounded half up, and every comparison against it uses the already-rounded value.

### 2.6. Guarding Parent Aggregates: Optimistic Concurrency

Two create operations mutate a parent aggregate as a side effect and therefore require the client to prove it was looking at the current state of that parent.

Drafting a leak adjustment requires `expected_investigation_version` as a non-optional field of the request payload, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `HighBillInvestigation`. If that value does not equal the investigation's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INVESTIGATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard is not cosmetic: a new draft legitimately supersedes a prior one whenever the earlier adjustment was denied and the investigation fell back to `DETERMINED`, so a create issued from a stale view of the investigation would silently overwrite work the client never saw. A successful create increments the investigation's `version` by one.

Proposing a payment arrangement requires `expected_account_version` as a non-optional field of the request payload, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `ServiceAccount`. If that value does not equal the account's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Creating an arrangement cancels any `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` arrangement already on the account, so a create issued from a stale view would cancel a plan the client never knew about and finance an already-superseded balance. A successful create increments the account's `version` by one.

No other operation in this service takes a version field, and no other operation rejects on version mismatch.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder: Order of Validation

Checks are evaluated in a strict ascending order and the **first** violated check short-circuits the request; nothing after it runs. A request that breaks several rules always reports the earliest one in this ladder, which is why the ladder itself is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail.

1. **Authentication.** Both user headers present and non-empty on every endpoint except the clock setter → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorisation.** `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and appears on the target operation's role list → otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). This is decided on the role string alone, before any body parsing, so a forbidden role sending a malformed body still receives 403 rather than 400.
3. **Request schema.** Body and query parse; every required field present; every field of its declared type; every enum value drawn from its declared set; every integer inside its declared range; `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Path resource existence**, resolved left to right across the URL so the leftmost unresolvable identifier is the one reported → `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_METER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_METER_READING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_METER_TEST_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPAIR_PROOF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_ARRANGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_BOARD_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, all 404.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every identifier supplied in a payload must resolve, checked in the order the fields appear in that operation's input list, reporting the matching not-found code above (404). This tier governs account creation, meter registration, reading posting, bill generation, investigation opening, meter-test requesting, repair-proof submission, leak-adjustment drafting, arrangement proposal and appeal filing.
6. **Ownership scoping.** A `Customer` caller may only read or act on rows reachable from the `ServiceAccount` whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`; any other account reached through the path, the body or a foreign key is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (403). This tier applies to every operation whose role list includes `Customer`. It sits after existence deliberately: a customer probing an unknown identifier gets a 404, not a 403, because the identifier space is not a secret even though row contents are.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** The expected parent version must match, on leak-adjustment drafting and arrangement proposal only → `ERR_STALE_INVESTIGATION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (412).
8. **State preconditions.** The subject entity must sit in a state from which the requested transition exists, and every cross-entity state precondition of that transition must hold → `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409) on every action operation. Two state checks carry their own codes: opening an investigation requires the target bill to be `ISSUED` and requires that no non-terminal investigation already exists for it, else `ERR_INVESTIGATION_ALREADY_OPEN` (409); generating a bill requires its period to be `OPEN`, else `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409).
9. **Business safety.** Segregation of duties on leak-adjustment approve, deny and reverse (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403); hearing-officer conflict on board decision recording (`ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_CONFLICT`, 403); shutoff execution refused while any investigation hold or rebill hold is outstanding (`ERR_SHUTOFF_HOLD_ACTIVE`, 409); shutoff execution refused before the notice instant (`ERR_SHUTOFF_NOTICE_NOT_ELAPSED`, 409).
10. **Business temporal.** A `LOCKED` period refuses every fiscal write touching one of its bills — leak-adjustment approve, leak-adjustment deny, leak-adjustment reverse, bill rebilling and `CORRECTED` reading creation — with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_LOCKED` (409). Rebilling past the period's `rebilling_deadline_date` returns `ERR_REBILL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). Filing an appeal too late returns `ERR_APPEAL_UNTIMELY` (422). Docketing a hearing with too little notice returns `ERR_HEARING_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT` (422). Recording a decision before the hearing date returns `ERR_HEARING_NOT_YET_HELD` (422). Scheduling a field test inside the post-test quarantine returns `ERR_METER_TEST_QUARANTINE` (409). Recording a field result outside the reserved window returns `ERR_TEST_RESULT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (422). Generating a bill before its period has ended returns `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (422). Posting a reading dated after today returns `ERR_READ_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422).
11. **Business constraints** — counts, capacities, overlaps and limits: `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409) on period creation, `ERR_METER_TEST_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409) on field-test scheduling, `ERR_DUPLICATE_BILL_FOR_PERIOD` (409) on bill generation, `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUND_LIMIT` (422) on evidence requests, `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422) on remand decisions, `ERR_HEARING_DOCKET_FULL` (409) on hearing docketing, `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT` (422) on arrangement reinstatement, `ERR_NEGATIVE_CONSUMPTION` (422) on reading posting, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BASELINE_HISTORY` (422) on investigation opening, `ERR_REPAIR_PROOF_REQUIRED` (422) on leak-adjustment drafting, `ERR_METER_NOT_BILLABLE` (422) on bill generation, `ERR_SUBMETER_PARENT_INVALID` (422) on meter registration, and `ERR_INSTALLMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422) on arrangement proposal.
12. **Cross-row coherence**, all 422: `ERR_LEAK_TYPE_MISMATCH` and `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_BILL_MISMATCH` on leak-adjustment drafting, `ERR_TEST_METER_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` on meter-test requesting, `ERR_ARRANGEMENT_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` on arrangement proposal, `ERR_BILL_METER_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` on bill generation.
13. **Fiscal rules**, all 422: `ERR_LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED` on leak-adjustment approval, `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_TOO_LOW` and `ERR_ARRANGEMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` on arrangement proposal, `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID` on account payments and on arrangement instalment recording, and `ERR_TEST_DEPOSIT_INVALID` on meter-test requesting.

Within tier 12, note that leak-type coherence is checked before bill coherence, matching the field order of the drafting payload. Within tier 13, arrangement proposal evaluates the down-payment floor before the financed-amount match.

### 2.8. Path Resolution, Foreign-Key Resolution and Customer Scoping

Nested reads exist in exactly two places — an account's ledger entries and a bill's ledger entries — and both are read-only projections of the append-only log. Everything else is a flat top-level collection whose relationships are carried in payloads, so the left-to-right path rule usually resolves a single identifier. Where a payload carries several references, the order of the operation's declared input list decides which not-found code surfaces first: bill generation resolves the account, then the period, then the meter; meter-test requesting resolves the investigation, then the meter; leak-adjustment drafting resolves the investigation, then the bill; arrangement proposal resolves the account, then the bill, then the optional investigation.

For a `Customer` caller, scoping applies transitively. A bill belongs to the customer when its account identifier equals `X-User-ID`; an investigation, meter test request, repair proof, leak adjustment, arrangement or appeal belongs to the customer when the account reached through its chain of references does. Because scoping is evaluated after existence but before state, a customer aiming a legitimate-looking action at someone else's row receives `ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` rather than a state error.

### 2.9. The Append-Only Account Ledger and Derived Collections State

Every movement of money on an account is a new `AccountLedgerEntry`. An entry names its `account_id`, optionally the `bill_id` it relates to (null for account-level payments), an `entry_type` from `CHARGE`, `CREDIT`, `PAYMENT` and `REVERSAL`, a signed `amount_cents`, a `source_kind` naming the rule that produced it, a `source_id` holding the identifier of the originating row, an optional free-text `memo`, and an `effective_at` instant taken from the logical clock. Because the row that produced an entry varies with `source_kind` — a bill, a leak adjustment, a meter test request or a payment arrangement — `source_id` is deliberately not a declared foreign key to any single collection. The eleven recognised values of `source_kind` are `BILL_CHARGE`, `REBILL_REVERSAL`, `REBILL_CHARGE`, `LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_CREDIT`, `LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_ADMIN_FEE`, `LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_REVERSAL`, `METER_TEST_DEPOSIT`, `METER_TEST_DEPOSIT_REFUND`, `CUSTOMER_PAYMENT`, `ARRANGEMENT_DOWN_PAYMENT` and `ARRANGEMENT_INSTALLMENT`.

Signs are fixed at insert time and never revisited: a `CHARGE` is positive, a `CREDIT` and a `PAYMENT` (including a deposit refund) are negative, and a `REVERSAL` carries whatever sign undoes the entry it compensates. An account's `balance_cents` is therefore not stored independently of the log — it is the sum of `amount_cents` over every ledger entry on that account, recomputed as a full replay after each insert.

`credit_status` is a pure function of the account's balance and its arrangements, recomputed after every ledger insert and after every change to a payment arrangement's status. It is evaluated as a strict precedence chain in which the first matching clause wins: if any arrangement on the account is `DEFAULTED`, the status is `ARRANGEMENT_DEFAULTED`; otherwise if any arrangement on the account is `ACTIVE`, the status is `IN_ARRANGEMENT`; otherwise if `balance_cents` is strictly greater than 5,000 cents (fifty dollars), the status is `PAST_DUE`; otherwise it is `CURRENT`. Because clause two outranks clause three, an account with an `ACTIVE` arrangement is never `PAST_DUE` no matter how large its balance grows, and a balance of exactly 5,000 cents with no arrangement is `CURRENT` because the comparison excludes the boundary.

Two more derived counters live on the account and act as safety interlocks. `open_investigation_hold_count` is the number of `HighBillInvestigation` rows on the account whose `shutoff_hold_active` is true, and `open_rebill_count` is the number of the account's bills sitting in `REBILL_PENDING`. Whenever either changes, the account's `service_status` is re-evaluated in the order described in Section 3.8: balance first, then `credit_status`, then `service_status`.

### 2.10. Period Gates: OPEN, CLOSED and LOCKED

A `BillingPeriod` advances through exactly two transitions, both driven by a `SystemAdmin`, and its `status` starts at `OPEN`. Closing an `OPEN` period moves it to `CLOSED`; from that point no further bill may be created for the period and an attempt returns `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_CLOSED`, but leak-adjustment posting and rebilling remain permitted so that in-flight disputes can still settle. Locking a `CLOSED` period moves it to `LOCKED`, the terminal state, after which every fiscal write touching a bill of that period is refused with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_LOCKED`: leak-adjustment approval, leak-adjustment denial, leak-adjustment reversal, rebilling, and the posting of a `CORRECTED` meter reading. Attempting to close a period that is not `OPEN`, or to lock a period that is not `CLOSED`, is an invalid transition.

Periods must not overlap. A new period is refused with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when the closed-closed interval from its `start_date` through its `end_date` intersects the closed-closed interval of any existing period — sharing a single endpoint date already counts as an intersection. Each period also publishes a `rebilling_deadline_date`, the last calendar day, inclusive, on which one of its bills may be rebilled or one of its reads corrected.

### 2.11. Filtering the Collections

Collections accept narrow, exact-match filters in addition to `limit` and `offset`. Rate schedules filter on `code`. Billing periods filter on `season`, `status` and `period_code`. Service accounts filter on `rate_schedule_id`, `account_number` and on either state dimension independently via `service_status` and `credit_status`. Meters filter on `account_id`, `parent_meter_id`, `meter_type` and `accuracy_status`. Meter readings filter on `meter_id`, `corrects_reading_id`, `superseded_by_reading_id` and `read_type`, and additionally accept `live_only`, which when true restricts the result to rows whose `superseded_by_reading_id` is null. Bills filter on `account_id`, `period_id`, `meter_id`, `billing_status`, `anomaly_flag` and `rebill_of_bill_id`, the last of which surfaces the replacement generated from a superseded bill. Investigations filter on `bill_id`, `account_id`, `status` and `leak_determination`. Meter test requests filter on `investigation_id`, `meter_id`, `status` and `requested_test_type`. Repair proofs filter on `investigation_id`, `status` and `leak_type`. Leak adjustments filter on `investigation_id`, `bill_id`, `supersedes_adjustment_id`, `status` and `variant_applied`. Payment arrangements filter on `account_id`, `bill_id`, `investigation_id` and `status`. Board appeals filter on `investigation_id`, `status` and `hearing_date`, and filtering by `hearing_date` is how a clerk inspects the docket load for a day. An account's ledger listing filters on `entry_type` and `source_kind`; a bill's ledger listing filters on `source_kind`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Rate Schedules, the Metering Estate and the Register

Nothing can be billed until an administrator publishes a rate schedule. A schedule carries a human-readable `code` that must be unique across all schedules, a fixed `base_charge_cents` of at least zero that is added to every bill regardless of consumption, and a three-tier volumetric ladder: `tier1_limit_gallons` (greater than zero) is the inclusive upper bound of the first tier and `tier1_rate_cents_per_kgal` (greater than zero) prices it, `tier2_limit_gallons` (which must be greater than `tier1_limit_gallons`) is the inclusive upper bound of the second tier and `tier2_rate_cents_per_kgal` (greater than zero) prices it, and `tier3_rate_cents_per_kgal` (greater than zero) prices every gallon above the second-tier bound. A `summer_surcharge_bp` of at least zero basis points loads the third tier only, and only in summer. The sewer side of the schedule holds `sewer_rate_cents_per_kgal` (greater than zero), a `sewer_cap_gallons` (greater than zero) beyond which no sewer volume is billed in a single period, and a `submeter_sewer_credit_cap_gallons` of at least zero limiting how much irrigation volume may be excluded from sewer billing in one period. Finally, `admin_fee_cents` (at least zero) is the processing fee deducted from a posted leak credit, and `admin_fee_waiver_threshold_cents` (at least zero) is the credit amount at or above which that fee is waived.

A service account is registered with an `account_number` unique across all accounts, a `customer_name` naming the account holder of record, a `service_address` describing the premise served, and a `rate_schedule_id` naming the schedule that will price every one of its bills. A fresh account starts fully clean: `service_status` is `ACTIVE`, `credit_status` is `CURRENT`, `balance_cents` is zero, both `open_investigation_hold_count` and `open_rebill_count` are zero, and `shutoff_scheduled_for` is null.

Meters are registered against an account with a `meter_serial` unique across the estate, a `meter_type`, a `size_inches` greater than zero, an `initial_register_value` of at least zero recording the cumulative register at installation, and — for devices that sit behind another meter — a `parent_meter_id`. Only a `POTABLE_MASTER` meter may ever carry a bill; an `IRRIGATION_SUBMETER` measures outdoor water that never reaches the sewer, and a `BACKFLOW_FIRE_LINE` measures fire-protection volume that is neither sewer-discharged nor added to the master's consumption. Parentage is strict: a `POTABLE_MASTER` must be registered with no `parent_meter_id` at all, while an `IRRIGATION_SUBMETER` or a `BACKFLOW_FIRE_LINE` must supply one that points at a `POTABLE_MASTER` meter on the same account. A payload that supplies a parent for a master, omits one for a submeter, or points at a meter that is not a master on the same account is refused with `ERR_SUBMETER_PARENT_INVALID`. Every newly registered meter starts with `accuracy_status` set to `UNTESTED`, `accuracy_pct` null, `correction_factor` null and `last_test_completed_at` null.

A technician posts reads against a meter by supplying `meter_id`, a `read_date`, a `read_type` of `ACTUAL`, `ESTIMATED` or `CORRECTED`, and a cumulative `reading_value` of at least zero. A read may not be dated in the future: if `read_date` falls after today's date on the logical clock the post is refused with `ERR_READ_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. Registers only advance, so an `ACTUAL` or `ESTIMATED` read whose `reading_value` is below the `reading_value` of the latest live read on that meter — or below the meter's `initial_register_value` when no read exists yet — is refused with `ERR_NEGATIVE_CONSUMPTION`. A `CORRECTED` read is exempt from that check because its whole purpose is to restate a figure downward or upward; in exchange it must name `corrects_reading_id`, the read it supersedes, and `corrects_reading_id` is forbidden on `ACTUAL` and `ESTIMATED` reads.

Each stored read carries `consumption_since_prior_gallons`, computed as its own `reading_value` minus the `reading_value` of the nearest live read strictly earlier than its `read_date` on the same meter, falling back to the meter's `initial_register_value` when no earlier live read exists. Liveness is tracked by `superseded_by_reading_id`: a row whose `superseded_by_reading_id` is null is live, and posting a `CORRECTED` read sets that field on the row named by `corrects_reading_id` to the new row's identifier. Because a correction restates history, it is refused with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_LOCKED` when the period containing the affected bill is `LOCKED`, and refused with `ERR_REBILL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` when that period's `rebilling_deadline_date` has already passed. When a `CORRECTED` read lands in a period whose bill is `ISSUED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `ADJUSTED`, that bill immediately recomputes every charge field and moves to `REBILL_PENDING`, which increments the account's `open_rebill_count` and may in turn move `service_status` from `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` to `SHUTOFF_HELD`.

### 3.2. Generating and Issuing a Bill

Bill generation names an `account_id`, a `period_id`, a `meter_id` and the `read_type` — `ACTUAL` or `ESTIMATED` — of the period-end read created alongside the bill. The caller never supplies consumption or money: `consumption_gallons` is derived from the live `ACTUAL` or `CORRECTED` reads bounding the period and can never be negative. Only a live `ACTUAL` or `CORRECTED` read may serve as either bound — an `ESTIMATED` read is never eligible as a bound, even though it is otherwise an ordinary live row. The opening register is the `reading_value` of the eligible read with the greatest `read_date` at or before the period's `start_date`, falling back to the meter's `initial_register_value` when no such read exists; the closing register is the `reading_value` of the eligible read with the greatest `read_date` inside the period's closed-closed `start_date`-through-`end_date` window, the most recently created row winning a tie on `read_date`; and `consumption_gallons` is the closing register minus the opening register with any negative result treated as zero. When no eligible closing read exists inside the window — the ordinary situation when the period-end read was `ESTIMATED` — `consumption_gallons` is zero, and every volume and charge field derived from it follows from that zero volume. Four business gates guard the operation. The period must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_CLOSED`. The period must already be over — today's date on the logical clock must be strictly later than the period's `end_date` — else `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. The meter's `meter_type` must be `POTABLE_MASTER`, else `ERR_METER_NOT_BILLABLE`. The meter must belong to the named account, else `ERR_BILL_METER_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. And no bill for the same meter and period may already exist in any state other than `REBILLED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_BILL_FOR_PERIOD`.

Generation then computes the bill's entire fiscal picture in one pass. `estimated_flag` is set true exactly when the live period-end read for this bill has `read_type` of `ESTIMATED`; that single boolean later suppresses irrigation-submeter sewer relief, because an estimated master read cannot be reconciled against a submeter read.

**The seasonal baseline.** A bill's `seasonal_baseline_gallons` is the mean consumption of the three most recent same-season historical bills for the same meter. The candidate pool is every other bill on the same `account_id` and the same `meter_id` whose period has the same `season` as the target's period, whose period's `end_date` is earlier than the target period's `start_date`, whose `billing_status` is `ISSUED`, `ADJUSTED` or `UPHELD_AS_BILLED`, whose `anomaly_flag` is false, and whose `estimated_flag` is false. Bills in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `REBILL_PENDING` or `REBILLED` never qualify, and neither do anomaly-flagged or estimated bills — the baseline must be built from settled, actually-read, ordinary periods. Candidates are ordered by their period's `end_date` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, and the first three form the sample. If fewer than three candidates exist, `seasonal_baseline_gallons` is null and `baseline_sample_count` records how many were found; otherwise `baseline_sample_count` is 3 and `seasonal_baseline_gallons` is the sum of the sample's `consumption_gallons` divided by three, rounded half up to a whole gallon. Because a rebilled bill drops out of the pool and its replacement takes its place, a rebill in one period can legitimately change a later period's baseline.

**The anomaly screen.** When `seasonal_baseline_gallons` is null or zero, `anomaly_ratio` is null and `anomaly_flag` is false. Otherwise `anomaly_ratio` is `consumption_gallons` divided by `seasonal_baseline_gallons`, rounded half up to three decimal places, and `anomaly_flag` is true when that rounded ratio is greater than or equal to 2.000. The boundary is included: a bill running at exactly twice its baseline is flagged, while a ratio that rounds to 1.999 is not.

**The tiered water charge.** Volume is split into three lines with nested clamping. The first-tier volume is the lesser of `consumption_gallons` and `tier1_limit_gallons`. The second-tier volume is the lesser of `consumption_gallons` and `tier2_limit_gallons`, minus `tier1_limit_gallons`, with any negative result treated as zero. The third-tier volume is `consumption_gallons` minus `tier2_limit_gallons`, again with any negative result treated as zero. Line one is the first-tier volume multiplied by `tier1_rate_cents_per_kgal`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up to whole cents. Line two is the second-tier volume multiplied by `tier2_rate_cents_per_kgal`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up. Line three depends on the period's `season`: in `WINTER` it is the third-tier volume multiplied by `tier3_rate_cents_per_kgal`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up; in `SUMMER` the surcharge is folded in *before* the rounding, so line three is the third-tier volume multiplied by `tier3_rate_cents_per_kgal`, multiplied by ten thousand plus `summer_surcharge_bp`, divided by one thousand and then by ten thousand, and only then rounded half up. `water_charge_cents` is `base_charge_cents` plus the three rounded lines. Rounding each line independently before the sum is mandatory; producing one unrounded grand total and rounding that instead yields a different answer, and the surcharge never touches lines one or two.

**The sewer charge and the irrigation-submeter credit.** Two buckets fill in a pinned order. First the raw irrigation volume is measured: for every meter whose `parent_meter_id` is this bill's meter and whose `meter_type` is `IRRIGATION_SUBMETER`, the opening read is the `ACTUAL` or `CORRECTED` read on that submeter with the greatest `read_date` at or before the period's `start_date`, falling back to the submeter's `initial_register_value` only when the submeter's own `created_at` precedes the period's `start_date` — otherwise that submeter is ineligible. The closing read is the `ACTUAL` or `CORRECTED` read on that submeter with the greatest `read_date` that is at or before the period's `end_date` and at or after the period's `start_date`; when no such read exists the submeter is ineligible. Each eligible submeter contributes its closing `reading_value` minus its opening `reading_value`, with any negative difference treated as zero, and the contributions are summed into the raw irrigation volume. `BACKFLOW_FIRE_LINE` devices are never credited and their volume is never added to the master's consumption, because fire-line water is not sewer-discharged. If the bill's `estimated_flag` is true, or if every candidate submeter was ineligible, `submeter_credit_gallons` is zero; otherwise it is the lesser of the raw irrigation volume and `submeter_sewer_credit_cap_gallons`.

Only then is the master volume processed, and the order here is load-bearing. Step one caps: the capped volume is the lesser of `consumption_gallons` and `sewer_cap_gallons`. Step two subtracts: `sewer_billable_gallons` is the capped volume minus `submeter_credit_gallons`, with any negative result treated as zero. `sewer_charge_cents` is `sewer_billable_gallons` multiplied by `sewer_rate_cents_per_kgal`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up once on that final product. Subtracting the irrigation credit before applying the statutory cap would produce a larger sewer charge whenever consumption exceeds the cap, so cap-then-credit is the only correct sequence.

`total_charge_cents` is `water_charge_cents` plus `sewer_charge_cents`, and at generation time `adjusted_total_cents` equals `total_charge_cents` because no credit has been posted. A newly generated bill sits in `DRAFT` with `rebill_of_bill_id` null and writes nothing to the ledger.

Issuing moves the bill from `DRAFT` to `ISSUED` and is permitted while the period is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`. Issuing stamps `due_date` as the period's `end_date` plus twenty-one calendar days, inserts a `CHARGE` ledger entry with `source_kind` `BILL_CHARGE` for the positive `total_charge_cents`, naming this bill in both `bill_id` and `source_id`, and then recomputes the account's `balance_cents`, then its `credit_status`, then its `service_status`, in that order. From that moment the bill is also eligible to serve as a seasonal-baseline sample for future periods.

### 3.3. Opening an Investigation and the Bounded Evidence Loop

A customer who disputes a charge, or a representative acting for them, opens an investigation by naming the `bill_id` and a free-text `reported_cause`. Three gates apply. The bill must be `ISSUED`. No non-terminal investigation may already exist for that bill, else `ERR_INVESTIGATION_ALREADY_OPEN`. And the bill must have a computed baseline — an investigation cannot proceed against a bill whose `seasonal_baseline_gallons` is null, because there would be nothing to compare consumption against and nothing to reprice a credit from, so the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BASELINE_HISTORY`.

A new investigation opens in `OPEN` with `leak_determination` at `UNDETERMINED`, `evidence_round_count` at 0 and `remand_count` at 0. Its `account_id` is copied from the bill rather than supplied, and `opened_by_role` is stamped as `CUSTOMER` or `CUSTOMER_SERVICE_REP` according to the acting role while `opened_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. Opening also arms the protection customers care about most: `shutoff_hold_active` becomes true, the account's `open_investigation_hold_count` increases by one — which moves `service_status` from `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` to `SHUTOFF_HELD` when a shutoff was already pending — and the bill moves from `ISSUED` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. The bill transition itself writes no field; while it sits in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` the bill is outside the baseline sample pool, and if its `anomaly_flag` is true it remains outside that pool even after it settles.

`shutoff_hold_active` is not a stored decision but a function of the investigation's own `status`: it is true while the status is `OPEN`, `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`, `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW`, `EVIDENCE_EXHAUSTED`, `DETERMINED`, `ADJUSTMENT_PENDING` or `APPEALED`, and false in `RESOLVED` and `CLOSED_NO_ADJUSTMENT`. Every time it flips, the account's `open_investigation_hold_count` moves with it.

A representative asks the customer for evidence, optionally attaching a `note`. That request is available from `OPEN` and again from `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`, it always lands the investigation in `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`, and it increments `evidence_round_count` by one. At most three evidence rounds may ever be opened on one investigation, counting the initial request, every rejection-driven re-request and the round a board remand grants; an attempt made when `evidence_round_count` has already reached three is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUND_LIMIT`.

Evidence arrives in one of two forms, and either form advances the investigation from `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` to `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW`: a repair proof is submitted, or a meter test request for this investigation reaches one of its closed states — `CLOSED_PASS`, `CLOSED_FAST` or `CLOSED_SLOW`. A repair proof carries the `investigation_id` it answers, the `plumber_license_no` of the licensed plumber who did the work, the `repair_date` on which the repair was completed, a `leak_type` of `SERVICE_LINE`, `UNDERGROUND`, `TOILET`, `IRRIGATION` or `POOL`, an `invoice_amount_cents` of at least zero that is recorded purely for audit and never enters any credit formula, and a free-text `description`. It opens in `SUBMITTED`.

A representative reviews each proof while the investigation is in `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW`. Verification moves the proof from `SUBMITTED` to `VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_by_user_id` with the reviewing `X-User-ID`, and additionally requires that the `repair_date` is not earlier than the `start_date` of the period of the investigation's bill — a repair completed before the disputed period began cannot explain that period's consumption. A verified proof is what unlocks a determination of `CUSTOMER_SIDE_LEAK` or `UTILITY_SIDE_LEAK` and a credit of the same `leak_type`. Rejection moves the proof from `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED` with a mandatory `rejection_reason`, and it is the rejection that makes this workflow cyclic: if `evidence_round_count` is still below three the investigation loops back from `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW` to `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` and `evidence_round_count` increases by one; if `evidence_round_count` has already reached three the investigation instead moves to `EVIDENCE_EXHAUSTED` and the counter is deliberately left unchanged, because the loop is being closed rather than re-opened.

Closing without a credit is available from `OPEN`, `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`, `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW` and `EVIDENCE_EXHAUSTED`, takes an optional `note`, and lands the investigation in `CLOSED_NO_ADJUSTMENT`. That drops `shutoff_hold_active` to false, decrements the account's `open_investigation_hold_count` — which may return `service_status` from `SHUTOFF_HELD` to `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED`, or all the way to `ACTIVE` — and settles the bill from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` to the terminal `UPHELD_AS_BILLED`, where `adjusted_total_cents` stays equal to `total_charge_cents` and no ledger entry whatsoever is written.

### 3.4. Meter Accuracy Testing and the Correction Cascade

When the customer suspects the meter rather than their own plumbing, an accuracy test is requested by naming the `investigation_id`, the `meter_id` to be tested, a `requested_test_type` of `FIELD` or `BENCH`, a `requested_by_kind` of `CUSTOMER` or `UTILITY`, and a `deposit_cents`. The tested meter must belong to the same account as the investigation, else `ERR_TEST_METER_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. Who pays is supplied in the payload rather than inferred from the caller's role, so a representative or a technician may file either kind on the customer's behalf — but the money must match the declaration exactly: a `CUSTOMER`-funded test requires `deposit_cents` of exactly 3,000 cents (thirty dollars) and a `UTILITY`-funded test requires exactly 0, and any other combination is refused with `ERR_TEST_DEPOSIT_INVALID`. A request opens in `REQUESTED` with `deposit_refunded` false; when `deposit_cents` is greater than zero a `CHARGE` entry with `source_kind` `METER_TEST_DEPOSIT` for the positive deposit is written to the ledger and the account's `balance_cents`, `credit_status` and `service_status` are recomputed in that order. A request whose `requested_test_type` is `BENCH` then advances itself immediately to `BENCH_TEST_PENDING`, after the deposit entry has been written.

A `FIELD` request must first be docketed onto the meter. Scheduling reserves the half-open interval from `window_start` up to but excluding `window_end`, and is available only from `REQUESTED` and only when `requested_test_type` is `FIELD`. The transition requires `window_end` to be strictly later than `window_start`; a request that fails any precondition of the scheduling transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Two live reservations on the same meter may not overlap: the requested interval must not intersect the half-open interval of any other test request on that same meter sitting in `FIELD_TEST_SCHEDULED` or `BENCH_TEST_PENDING`, else `ERR_METER_TEST_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because both intervals are half-open, two windows that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending exactly where the next begins — do not conflict. A meter also needs to settle after being handled: whenever `last_test_completed_at` is not null, `window_start` must be at or after that instant plus a forty-eight-hour (two full day) quarantine, else `ERR_METER_TEST_QUARANTINE`. Successful scheduling records `window_start` and `window_end` and moves the request to `FIELD_TEST_SCHEDULED`.

Recording a field outcome supplies `tested_at` and a `field_result` of `PASS` or `SUSPECT`, and is available only from `FIELD_TEST_SCHEDULED`. The `tested_at` instant must fall inside the reserved half-open window — at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end` — else `ERR_TEST_RESULT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. The two outcomes diverge sharply. `PASS` closes the request at `CLOSED_PASS`, sets the meter's `accuracy_status` to `VERIFIED_ACCURATE` and its `last_test_completed_at` to `tested_at`, forfeits the deposit — no refund entry is written and `deposit_refunded` stays false, because the customer's suspicion was unfounded — and advances the investigation from `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` to `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW`. `SUSPECT` records the `field_result` and escalates the request to `BENCH_TEST_PENDING`, releasing the meter reservation so that a later request may claim an adjacent window; no meter field is written and no deposit decision is taken yet.

The bench test supplies `tested_at` together with three registration percentages, `low_flow_pct`, `mid_flow_pct` and `high_flow_pct`, and is available only from `BENCH_TEST_PENDING`. The composite is computed by the server, never supplied: `accuracy_method` is selected from the meter's `size_inches`. A meter of 2.0 inches or smaller is graded `AWWA_WEIGHTED`, where `accuracy_pct` is `low_flow_pct` multiplied by fifteen hundredths, plus `mid_flow_pct` multiplied by seventy hundredths, plus `high_flow_pct` multiplied by fifteen hundredths, rounded half up to two decimal places — a weighting that deliberately dominates the composite with mid-flow behaviour. A meter larger than 2.0 inches is graded `SIMPLE_MEAN`, where `accuracy_pct` is the sum of the three percentages divided by three, rounded half up to two decimal places. The same three flow points on differently sized meters can therefore yield different verdicts.

`bench_result` classifies the already-rounded `accuracy_pct` with two strict comparisons: it is `FAST` when `accuracy_pct` is strictly greater than 101.50, `SLOW` when `accuracy_pct` is strictly less than 98.50, and `PASS` when it lies at or between those two bounds. A composite landing on exactly 101.50 is therefore a pass, not a fast meter. In all cases the meter's `correction_factor` is one hundred divided by `accuracy_pct`, rounded half up to six decimal places.

The three bench branches share two effects and diverge on everything else. All three set the meter's `accuracy_pct` and `last_test_completed_at`, and all three advance the investigation from `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` to `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW`. Beyond that:

- **`PASS`** closes the request at `CLOSED_PASS`, sets the meter's `accuracy_status` to `VERIFIED_ACCURATE`, stores `accuracy_pct` and `correction_factor` on the meter, and forfeits the deposit — no refund entry, `deposit_refunded` stays false.
- **`SLOW`** closes the request at `CLOSED_SLOW` and sets the meter's `accuracy_status` to `FAILED_SLOW`, storing `accuracy_pct` and `correction_factor`. When `deposit_cents` is greater than zero, a `CREDIT` entry with `source_kind` `METER_TEST_DEPOSIT_REFUND` for the negative deposit amount is written and `deposit_refunded` becomes true. Critically, **no** `CORRECTED` read is created and **no** bill enters `REBILL_PENDING`: a slow meter under-billed the customer, and the utility does not back-bill for water it failed to measure.
- **`FAST`** closes the request at `CLOSED_FAST` and refunds the deposit on the same terms as `SLOW`, but it also drives a four-link cascade, because a fast meter over-billed the customer and the record must be restated.

The cascade runs as follows. **Link one:** the meter's `accuracy_status` becomes `FAILED_FAST`, `accuracy_pct` is stored, `correction_factor` is stored as one hundred divided by `accuracy_pct` rounded half up to six decimal places, and `last_test_completed_at` becomes `tested_at`. **Link two**, caused by link one: exactly one `CORRECTED` read is created, aimed at the live read with the greatest `read_date` on that meter with `created_at` descending as the tie-break — the scope of a meter-error correction is the latest live read only, never a sweep across history. Because registers are cumulative, the correction is applied to the segment rather than to the register total: the live read immediately before the target (or a synthetic read carrying the meter's `initial_register_value` when the target is the first read) supplies the prior register value, the segment is the target's `reading_value` minus that prior value, and the corrected segment is that segment multiplied by the meter's `correction_factor`, rounded half up to a whole gallon. The new row carries the target's `meter_id` and `read_date`, a `read_type` of `CORRECTED`, a `reading_value` equal to the prior register value plus the corrected segment, `corrects_reading_id` pointing at the target, and `consumption_since_prior_gallons` equal to the corrected segment; the target's `superseded_by_reading_id` is set to the new row. This keeps the register monotonic and conserves every gallon already billed in earlier periods: on a meter whose reads are all `ACTUAL` or `CORRECTED`, the sum of `consumption_gallons` over every bill on the meter that is neither `DRAFT` nor `REBILLED` equals the greatest live `reading_value` on the meter minus the meter's `initial_register_value`. The identity is a consequence of the derivation in Section 3.2 rather than an independent rule, so it is not restored by hand where that derivation leaves a gap — a period whose only period-end read is `ESTIMATED` bills zero gallons even though the register has travelled. **Link three**, caused by link two: the bill whose period contains the corrected `read_date` recomputes `consumption_gallons` and every charge field and moves to `REBILL_PENDING`. **Link four**, caused by link three: the account's `open_rebill_count` increases by one, which moves `service_status` from `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` to `SHUTOFF_HELD`.

### 3.5. Restating a Charged Bill: The Rebill Window

A bill reaches `REBILL_PENDING` from `ISSUED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `ADJUSTED` in exactly two situations: a `CORRECTED` read changes its `consumption_gallons`, or a meter test on its meter closes `CLOSED_FAST`. Neither path is available once the period is `LOCKED`. Entering `REBILL_PENDING` recomputes `consumption_gallons`, `water_charge_cents`, `submeter_credit_gallons`, `sewer_billable_gallons`, `sewer_charge_cents` and `total_charge_cents` from the live reads, and increments the account's `open_rebill_count`, which is what drives the `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` to `SHUTOFF_HELD` protection. This third branch out of the investigation-era bill states is deliberately different from the other two: unlike a posted credit it changes the underlying charges rather than offsetting them, and unlike an upheld bill it is not terminal.

A supervisor then rebills. The operation is available only from `REBILL_PENDING`, is refused with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_LOCKED` when the period is `LOCKED`, and is refused with `ERR_REBILL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` when today's date on the logical clock is later than the period's `rebilling_deadline_date` — the deadline day itself is still inside the window. The superseded bill moves from `REBILL_PENDING` to the terminal `REBILLED`. A replacement bill is created carrying the superseded bill's `account_id`, `period_id`, `meter_id`, recomputed `consumption_gallons` and copied `read_type`, with `rebill_of_bill_id` naming the superseded bill; it is created in `DRAFT` and then immediately auto-advanced to `ISSUED` inside the same request. Two ledger entries land: a `REVERSAL` with `source_kind` `REBILL_REVERSAL` whose `amount_cents` is the negative of the sum of the superseded bill's `BILL_CHARGE` and `REBILL_CHARGE` entries, and then — from the replacement's auto-issue — a `CHARGE` with `source_kind` `REBILL_CHARGE` for the positive `total_charge_cents` of the replacement. The account's `open_rebill_count` decreases by one, which may return `service_status` from `SHUTOFF_HELD` to `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` or, if collections have caught up, to `ACTIVE`; `balance_cents`, `credit_status` and `service_status` are recomputed in that order. The superseded bill leaves the seasonal-baseline sample pool because `REBILLED` bills are excluded, and the replacement joins it because it is `ISSUED`. The operation returns the new replacement bill, not the superseded one.

### 3.6. Recording the Determination and Computing the Leak Credit

Once evidence is in hand, a representative records the cause. Determination is available only from `EVIDENCE_UNDER_REVIEW`, requires the bill's `seasonal_baseline_gallons` to be non-null, and accepts a `leak_determination` of `UNDETERMINED`, `CUSTOMER_SIDE_LEAK`, `UTILITY_SIDE_LEAK`, `METER_ERROR`, `NO_LEAK` or `IRRIGATION_USE`. Two of those values carry an evidentiary precondition: `CUSTOMER_SIDE_LEAK` and `UTILITY_SIDE_LEAK` each require at least one repair proof on the investigation to be `VERIFIED`, and `METER_ERROR` requires at least one meter test request on the investigation to sit in `CLOSED_FAST` or `CLOSED_SLOW`. Determination moves the investigation to `DETERMINED`, writes the supplied `leak_determination`, stamps `determination_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `determined_at` with the current logical-clock instant. Those two stamps are consequential far beyond this step: `determined_at` starts the appeal clock, and `determination_by_user_id` becomes the segregation-of-duties bar that every later approver, denier, reverser and hearing officer is measured against.

Before committing to a variant a representative or supervisor may pull a read-only dual-variant preview against a bill, supplying an `assumed_leak_type`. The preview runs the credit calculation twice on the same bill — once forced to `EXCESS_REPRICE` and once forced to `BASELINE_REBILL` — and returns `bill_id`, the echoed `assumed_leak_type`, a `recommended_variant` naming the variant the credit rule would actually select for that leak type, the `excess_gallons`, an `excess_reprice_net_cents`, a `baseline_rebill_net_cents`, and a `variant_delta_cents` equal to the baseline-rebill net minus the excess-reprice net. The preview creates no `LeakAdjustment` row and writes no ledger entry, and it recomputes from scratch on every call, so a rebill landing between two previews changes the answer.

Drafting the real credit names the `investigation_id`, the `bill_id`, the `leak_type` and the `expected_investigation_version` described in Section 2.6. The investigation must be `DETERMINED`. Unless the determination is `METER_ERROR`, at least one repair proof on the investigation must be `VERIFIED`, else `ERR_REPAIR_PROOF_REQUIRED`; and unless the determination is `METER_ERROR`, the supplied `leak_type` must equal the `leak_type` of at least one such `VERIFIED` proof, else `ERR_LEAK_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The supplied `bill_id` must equal the investigation's own `bill_id`, else `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_BILL_MISMATCH`. A successful draft opens in `DRAFT` with `created_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `board_ordered` false, `supersedes_adjustment_id` null, and every credit field computed; it moves the investigation from `DETERMINED` to `ADJUSTMENT_PENDING` and increments the investigation's `version` by one.

The credit itself is computed in six pinned steps.

**Step zero — the excess.** `excess_gallons` is `consumption_gallons` minus `seasonal_baseline_gallons`, with any negative result treated as zero.

**Step one — the water credit, in one of two variants.** `variant_applied` is selected from `leak_type`. Fixture leaks — `TOILET`, `IRRIGATION` and `POOL` — take `EXCESS_REPRICE`, on the principle that the customer still owes something for water that ran through their own fixture: the repriced amount is `excess_gallons` multiplied by `tier1_rate_cents_per_kgal`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up; the amount actually billed for the excess is `water_charge_cents` minus the water charge the same rate schedule and the same `season` would have produced had consumption been exactly `seasonal_baseline_gallons` (the fixed `base_charge_cents` appears in both figures and therefore cancels); and `water_credit_cents` is the billed-for-excess amount minus the repriced amount, with any negative result treated as zero. Buried leaks — `SERVICE_LINE` and `UNDERGROUND` — take `BASELINE_REBILL`, where the whole excess is forgiven and the bill is simply rewritten to the baseline: `water_credit_cents` is `water_charge_cents` minus the water charge that consumption of exactly `seasonal_baseline_gallons` in that same `season` would have produced, again with any negative result treated as zero. A `board_ordered` adjustment forces `variant_applied` to `BASELINE_REBILL` for every `leak_type` without exception.

**Step two — sewer relief.** Relief is granted only for leak types whose water did not reach the sewer, namely `SERVICE_LINE`, `UNDERGROUND`, `IRRIGATION` and `POOL`; `TOILET` is deliberately excluded because toilet water does discharge to the sewer. When the `leak_type` is one of the four eligible classes and `excess_gallons` is greater than zero, `sewer_relief_gallons` is the smallest of three quantities: `excess_gallons`, the statutory relief basis of 8,000 gallons, and the remaining headroom, where headroom is `sewer_billable_gallons` minus `submeter_credit_gallons` with any negative result treated as zero. Relief may never exceed what the customer was actually billed on the sewer side, never exceed the statutory basis, and never double-count gallons that an irrigation-submeter credit already removed from the sewer basis — which is why a bill that received a large submeter credit can end up with zero sewer relief. `sewer_credit_cents` is `sewer_relief_gallons` multiplied by `sewer_rate_cents_per_kgal`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up. For an ineligible `leak_type`, or when `excess_gallons` is zero, both `sewer_relief_gallons` and `sewer_credit_cents` are zero.

**Step three — the gross credit.** `gross_credit_cents` is `water_credit_cents` plus `sewer_credit_cents`.

**Step four — the cap.** `cap_cents` is the tighter of a proportional cap and an absolute cap: the proportional cap is `total_charge_cents` multiplied by six thousand basis points and divided by ten thousand — that is, sixty percent of the bill — rounded half up to whole cents, and the absolute cap is 50,000 cents (five hundred dollars); `cap_cents` is the lesser of the two. `capped_credit_cents` is the lesser of `gross_credit_cents` and `cap_cents`, and `cap_applied` is true exactly when `capped_credit_cents` is less than `gross_credit_cents`.

**Step five — the administrative fee, decided on the capped credit.** When `capped_credit_cents` is greater than or equal to the rate schedule's `admin_fee_waiver_threshold_cents`, `admin_fee_charged_cents` is zero. Otherwise `admin_fee_charged_cents` is the lesser of `admin_fee_cents` and `capped_credit_cents`, so the fee can never exceed the credit it is deducted from. Steps four and five are order-sensitive: because the waiver is decided on the capped figure rather than the gross figure, a gross credit that sat above the waiver threshold but is pushed below it by the cap still attracts the fee.

**Step six — the net.** `net_credit_cents` is `capped_credit_cents` minus `admin_fee_charged_cents`. Every division in these six steps is rounded half up at the step in which it appears, and every quantity is a whole number of cents or gallons.

### 3.7. Supervisory Approval, Posting and Compensating Reversal

The drafting representative submits the adjustment for approval. Submission is available only from `DRAFT`, requires the parent investigation to be in `ADJUSTMENT_PENDING`, and requires — unless the investigation's `leak_determination` is `METER_ERROR` — at least one `VERIFIED` repair proof on the investigation whose `leak_type` equals the adjustment's `leak_type`. Submission recomputes every credit field from the bill's current charge figures, so a rebill landing between drafting and submission can never leave a stale credit behind, and moves the adjustment to `PENDING_APPROVAL`.

A supervisor has three ways out of `PENDING_APPROVAL`, plus one way back in. Returning the adjustment records a `return_reason`, moves it back to `DRAFT`, and writes nothing to the ledger; the revision cycle from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `DRAFT` and back is permitted without limit. Denial records a `denial_reason`, moves the adjustment to the terminal `DENIED`, writes no ledger entry, and returns the investigation from `ADJUSTMENT_PENDING` to `DETERMINED` so that a fresh draft may be raised — which is precisely the situation the optimistic-concurrency guard on drafting exists to protect.

Approval is the fiscal moment. It is available only from `PENDING_APPROVAL`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the investigation's `determination_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the bill's period must not be `LOCKED`, else `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_LOCKED`; and a once-per-period ceiling applies — no other leak adjustment on the same account may already hold `APPROVED` or `POSTED` status with its bill's period `end_date` falling inside the rolling twelve-month window ending at this adjustment's bill's period `end_date`, excluding whichever row this adjustment names in `supersedes_adjustment_id`, else `ERR_LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The exclusion matters: a board-ordered replacement for a denied or reversed credit must not be blocked by the very row it replaces.

A successful approval carries the adjustment through `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED` and then automatically on to `POSTED` inside the same request, stamping `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `posted_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and firing a four-link cascade whose order is pinned. **Link one:** a `CREDIT` entry with `source_kind` `LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_CREDIT` for the negative `capped_credit_cents` is inserted against the bill, and then — only when `admin_fee_charged_cents` is greater than zero — a `CHARGE` entry with `source_kind` `LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_ADMIN_FEE` for the positive `admin_fee_charged_cents`. The bill then moves from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` to `ADJUSTED` and its `adjusted_total_cents` is recomputed as `total_charge_cents` minus the sum of `net_credit_cents` over the `POSTED` adjustments on that bill; the investigation moves from `ADJUSTMENT_PENDING` to `RESOLVED`, `shutoff_hold_active` becomes false and the account's `open_investigation_hold_count` decreases by one. **Link two**, caused by link one: the account's `balance_cents` is recomputed as the sum of every ledger entry's `amount_cents`. **Link three**, caused by link two: `credit_status` is recomputed from the new balance. **Link four**, caused by link three: when `credit_status` has become `CURRENT` or `IN_ARRANGEMENT`, `service_status` moves from `SHUTOFF_HELD`, `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` or `SHUTOFF_EXECUTED` to `ACTIVE` and `shutoff_scheduled_for` is set to null. The pinned sequence is: ledger inserts, then adjustment status, then bill, then investigation, then balance, then credit status, then service status.

Reversal exists because credits are sometimes granted in error. It is available only from `POSTED`, requires a `reversal_reason`, requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the investigation's `determination_by_user_id`, and requires that the bill's period not be `LOCKED`. Exactly one compensating entry is written: a `REVERSAL` with `source_kind` `LEAK_ADJUSTMENT_REVERSAL` whose `amount_cents` is the positive `capped_credit_cents` minus `admin_fee_charged_cents` — a single movement that undoes both the credit and the fee. The bill returns from `ADJUSTED` to `ISSUED` with `adjusted_total_cents` recomputed back to `total_charge_cents`; the investigation returns from `RESOLVED` to `DETERMINED`, `shutoff_hold_active` becomes true again and the account's `open_investigation_hold_count` increases by one — a reversal genuinely re-opens the dispute and re-arms the shutoff protection. `balance_cents`, `credit_status` and `service_status` are then recomputed in that order, and the invariant is exact: after a reversal the account's `balance_cents` equals its value immediately before the approval that it undoes.

### 3.8. Collections: Shutoff Notices, Holds and Instalment Plans

An account carries two independent state dimensions on one row, and they advance without blocking one another: a change in `credit_status` never by itself forbids a `service_status` trigger, and vice versa, although `service_status` triggers do consult `credit_status` in their preconditions. `credit_status` is entirely derived, as described in Section 2.9, and is recomputed after every ledger insert and after every change to an arrangement's status. Its own transitions follow that recomputation directly: `CURRENT` becomes `PAST_DUE` when a ledger insert leaves the balance above the threshold and no arrangement on the account is `ACTIVE` or `DEFAULTED`, and returns to `CURRENT` when a ledger insert brings the balance to or below the threshold under the same condition; either `CURRENT` or `PAST_DUE` becomes `IN_ARRANGEMENT` the moment an arrangement on the account reaches `ACTIVE`, and that pin holds regardless of how large `balance_cents` is; `IN_ARRANGEMENT` becomes `ARRANGEMENT_DEFAULTED` when that active arrangement defaults, and returns to `IN_ARRANGEMENT` when a supervisor reinstates it; and when the arrangement finally reaches `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, `credit_status` falls back to the plain balance comparison — `PAST_DUE` when `balance_cents` exceeds the threshold, otherwise `CURRENT`.

A supervisor schedules a shutoff on a delinquent account. The account must be in `ACTIVE` service and its `credit_status` must be `PAST_DUE` or `ARRANGEMENT_DEFAULTED`. Scheduling sets `shutoff_scheduled_for` to the current logical-clock instant plus a ten-calendar-day statutory notice and moves `service_status` to `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED`. Note carefully that the account lands in `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` even when `open_investigation_hold_count` plus `open_rebill_count` is already positive: `SHUTOFF_HELD` is reached only by the system transition that fires when that sum moves from zero to a positive number, so a shutoff scheduled while a hold is already outstanding simply sits in `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` and is stopped later, at execution time.

Those system transitions are the customer's real protection. When the sum of `open_investigation_hold_count` and `open_rebill_count` moves from zero to positive, an account in `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` moves to `SHUTOFF_HELD` with `shutoff_scheduled_for` preserved unchanged, so the notice period resumes rather than restarts when the hold lifts. When that sum returns to zero while `credit_status` is still `PAST_DUE` or `ARRANGEMENT_DEFAULTED`, the account moves back from `SHUTOFF_HELD` to `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` with `shutoff_scheduled_for` again unchanged. And whenever `credit_status` becomes `CURRENT` or `IN_ARRANGEMENT`, the account returns to `ACTIVE` from `SHUTOFF_HELD`, from `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED`, or from `SHUTOFF_EXECUTED` — in the last case service is restored — and `shutoff_scheduled_for` is set to null.

Executing a shutoff is available only from `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED` and carries four conditions. The current logical-clock instant must be at or after `shutoff_scheduled_for`, else `ERR_SHUTOFF_NOTICE_NOT_ELAPSED`. Both `open_investigation_hold_count` and `open_rebill_count` must be zero, else `ERR_SHUTOFF_HOLD_ACTIVE` — no household loses water while the utility is still deciding whether the bill was right. And `credit_status` must still be `PAST_DUE` or `ARRANGEMENT_DEFAULTED`. Execution moves `service_status` to `SHUTOFF_EXECUTED` and clears `shutoff_scheduled_for` to null.

Customers pay in two ways. An ad-hoc account payment supplies an `amount_cents` that must be strictly positive, else `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`, plus an optional `memo`; it writes a `PAYMENT` entry with `source_kind` `CUSTOMER_PAYMENT` for the negative amount, with `bill_id` and `source_id` both null and `effective_at` taken from the logical clock, and then recomputes `balance_cents`, `credit_status` and `service_status` in that order.

An instalment plan is proposed by naming the `account_id`, the `bill_id` it finances, a `total_amount_cents` greater than zero, a `down_payment_cents` of at least zero, an `installment_count` of at least one, the `expected_account_version` described in Section 2.6, and an optional `investigation_id` naming the dispute it was negotiated under (null when the plan is unrelated to any investigation). Four business rules guard it. `installment_count` may not exceed twelve, else `ERR_INSTALLMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The financed bill must belong to the named account, and when an `investigation_id` is supplied that investigation must belong to it too, else `ERR_ARRANGEMENT_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. `down_payment_cents` must be at least twenty percent of `total_amount_cents` — computed as `total_amount_cents` multiplied by two thousand basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up — and must not exceed `total_amount_cents`; either violation returns `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_TOO_LOW`. And `total_amount_cents` must equal the financed bill's `adjusted_total_cents` at the time of proposal, else `ERR_ARRANGEMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, so a plan can never finance a figure the credit workflow has already superseded.

The schedule is then computed in a pinned order. The financed amount is `total_amount_cents` minus `down_payment_cents`. `installment_cents` is the financed amount divided by `installment_count`, taking the floor — the largest whole number of cents not exceeding the quotient — and it applies to every instalment from the first through the second-to-last. The remainder is the financed amount minus `installment_cents` multiplied by `installment_count`, and `final_installment_cents` is `installment_cents` plus that entire remainder. `installment_cents` multiplied by one less than `installment_count`, plus `final_installment_cents`, therefore equals the financed amount exactly; rounding each instalment independently and summing would not reconcile. A new plan opens in `PROPOSED` with `paid_installment_count`, `missed_payment_count` and `reinstatement_count` all zero, and any other arrangement on the same account sitting in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` is moved to the terminal `CANCELLED`, which stops it counting toward the account's `credit_status` and therefore triggers a recompute.

Activation takes the down payment. It is available only from `PROPOSED` and requires either that the account's `service_status` is something other than `SHUTOFF_EXECUTED`, or that `down_payment_cents` is greater than zero — a household whose water is already off must put money down. Activation inserts a `PAYMENT` entry with `source_kind` `ARRANGEMENT_DOWN_PAYMENT` for the negative `down_payment_cents` against the plan's `bill_id`, recomputes `balance_cents`, pins `credit_status` to `IN_ARRANGEMENT`, and moves `service_status` to `ACTIVE` from `SHUTOFF_SCHEDULED`, `SHUTOFF_HELD` or `SHUTOFF_EXECUTED` precisely because `credit_status` became `IN_ARRANGEMENT`.

Each instalment is recorded with an `amount_cents` that must be strictly positive and an `on_time` boolean. Recording inserts a `PAYMENT` entry with `source_kind` `ARRANGEMENT_INSTALLMENT` for the negative amount against the plan's `bill_id`, increases `paid_installment_count` by one, and — when `on_time` is false — increases `missed_payment_count` by one as well. When `paid_installment_count` reaches `installment_count` the plan moves from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `COMPLETED` and `credit_status` falls back to the balance comparison. When `missed_payment_count` reaches two the plan moves from `ACTIVE` to `DEFAULTED` and `credit_status` becomes `ARRANGEMENT_DEFAULTED`, which re-enables shutoff scheduling. Either way `balance_cents`, `credit_status` and `service_status` are recomputed in that order.

A defaulted plan may be reinstated once and only once by a supervisor. Reinstatement is available only from `DEFAULTED` and is refused with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT` when `reinstatement_count` has already reached one. It returns the plan to `ACTIVE`, increases `reinstatement_count` by one, resets `missed_payment_count` to zero, restores `credit_status` to `IN_ARRANGEMENT`, and then recomputes `service_status`.

### 3.9. Appeal to the Utility Board

A customer who disagrees with the utility's conclusion may appeal to the board by naming the `investigation_id` and written `grounds`. The investigation must be in `DETERMINED`, `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED_NO_ADJUSTMENT`, and the filing must be timely: today's date on the logical clock must be no later than thirty calendar days after the reference instant, which is `determined_at` for a `DETERMINED` or `RESOLVED` investigation and the closure instant for one that is `CLOSED_NO_ADJUSTMENT`. A late filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_UNTIMELY`. A filed appeal opens in `FILED` with `filing_date` set from the logical clock and `filed_by_user_id` stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, and `hearing_date`, `hearing_officer_user_id`, `decision` and `decision_note` all still null. Filing moves the investigation to `APPEALED`, which sets `shutoff_hold_active` to true (or leaves it true) and increases the account's `open_investigation_hold_count` when the hold was not already outstanding — a pending appeal is itself a shutoff hold.

A clerk dockets the hearing by supplying a `hearing_date`. Docketing is available only from `FILED`. The customer is entitled to notice, so `hearing_date` must be at or after the appeal's `filing_date` plus ten calendar days, else `ERR_HEARING_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`. The board also sits only so many matters in a day: a date already holding three appeals in `SCHEDULED` status is full, and a further attempt is refused with `ERR_HEARING_DOCKET_FULL`. Successful docketing records `hearing_date`, moves the appeal to `SCHEDULED`, and adds one to that date's docket count.

Recording the outcome supplies a `decision` of `UPHOLD`, `REVERSE` or `REMAND` together with a `decision_note`, and is available only from `SCHEDULED`. Today's date on the logical clock must be at or after `hearing_date`, else `ERR_HEARING_NOT_YET_HELD`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ both from the investigation's `determination_by_user_id` and from the appeal's own `filed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_CONFLICT`. Every branch stamps `hearing_officer_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and records the `decision` and `decision_note`; beyond that the three branches diverge completely, and each carries its own full set of effects.

`UPHOLD` moves the appeal to the terminal `UPHELD`, moves the investigation from `APPEALED` to `RESOLVED` with `shutoff_hold_active` becoming false, settles the bill from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` to `UPHELD_AS_BILLED`, and writes no ledger entry at all.

`REVERSE` moves the appeal to the terminal `REVERSED` and additionally requires that at least one repair proof on the investigation be `VERIFIED` — the board cannot order a credit with no evidence on file. It creates a leak adjustment directly in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, bypassing `DRAFT`, carrying the `investigation_id`, a `bill_id` equal to the investigation's `bill_id`, a `leak_type` taken from the most recent `VERIFIED` repair proof on the investigation ordered by `created_at` descending with `version` descending as the tie-break, `board_ordered` set true, and `supersedes_adjustment_id` set to the most recent leak adjustment on the investigation whose status is `DENIED` or `REVERSED`, ordered by `created_at` descending with `version` descending as the tie-break, or null when no such row exists. That board-ordered adjustment is computed with `variant_applied` forced to `BASELINE_REBILL` regardless of its `leak_type`, and its `leak_type` is exempt from the verified-proof match precisely because the board, not a representative, chose it. The investigation moves from `APPEALED` to `ADJUSTMENT_PENDING`, where a supervisor's approval — subject to every rule in Section 3.7, including the rolling-window ceiling that excludes the superseded row — is what actually pays it out.

`REMAND` moves the appeal to the terminal `REMANDED` and sends the matter back for more work. Only one remand is ever absorbed per investigation: an attempt made when `remand_count` has already reached one is refused with `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A permitted remand moves the investigation from `APPEALED` back to `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`, increases `remand_count` by one and `evidence_round_count` by one, and returns the bill from `UPHELD_AS_BILLED` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` when it had previously been upheld; a bill still sitting in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` simply stays there.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are rooted at the service's top level with no shared prefix. Successful resource creation returns HTTP 201; every other successful operation — retrievals, list operations, and the action endpoints that modify an existing resource — returns HTTP 200. Recording a customer payment on an account counts as a creation rather than as a modification of the account, because its whole effect is a new append-only `AccountLedgerEntry`: it returns HTTP 201 with that newly written entry as its `data`. Action endpoints return the resource they acted upon, with two exceptions — the rebill action, which returns the newly created replacement bill rather than the superseded one, and the account-payment endpoint just described. Failures return the status code listed for their error identifier in Section 5.
