# Employment Practices Liability Underwriting & Claims API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employment practices liability is the line of business where an insurer's discipline is tested twice: once at the quote, where a mispriced exposure follows the carrier for a full policy term, and again at the claim, where defense counsel bills by the hour against a limit that erodes with every dollar paid. This document specifies the backend service that carries an employment practices liability (EPLI) programme end to end — from an applicant's headcount and turnover disclosures, through jurisdictional hazard scoring, premium indication and manager release, into bound coverage with wage-and-hour and third-party sublimits, and finally through claim intake, retention collection, panel-counsel engagement, litigation-guideline invoice audits and indemnity settlement.

Two operational pain points shaped the design. The first is **basis drift**: underwriters quote against a snapshot of the applicant's headcount, separations and purchased risk-management credits, and any of those can move between the moment a price is struck and the moment a manager releases it. The service therefore treats the credit basis as a first-class, invalidatable artefact rather than as a convenience — a stale basis blocks release outright and forces a re-price. The second is **limit conservation**: indemnity payments and audited defense invoices both draw on the same aggregate limit, and the insured's retention must be fully collected before insurer money leaves. Every debit is pre-checked, every void restores exactly what it consumed, and the policy carries an erosion posture that is tracked independently of whether coverage is currently available.

The service is mounted under `/epli`. It is deliberately deterministic and test-driven: time is supplied by a server-side logical clock rather than read from the host, every derived amount is whole cents with a pinned rounding order, every list is deterministically sorted, and every write that could be attempted against data the client has not re-read is guarded by an explicit revision check. Engineers implementing this specification should assume that any place where two readings of a rule are possible is a place where the wrong reading will be observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole surface. Where a rule here and a rule in §3 both bear on an operation, §2 describes the mechanism and §3 describes the business circumstances in which it fires; the two are complementary, never contradictory.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint under `/epli` must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the calling principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined — this is the very first check performed on any request. If the headers are present but the named role is not one of the roles the target endpoint admits, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Seven roles exist, and the exact spellings are `PlatformAdmin`, `Underwriter`, `UnderwritingManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `ClaimsManager`, `PanelCounsel` and `PolicyholderContact`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` is carrier operations: it owns reference data — jurisdictions, rate plans and the defense panel roster — and it owns the logical clock. It never participates in an individual risk or claim decision.
- An `Underwriter` prices risk: it registers applicants, purchases and manages risk-management services, opens and prices exposure assessments, and binds approved assessments into policies. It cannot release its own work.
- An `UnderwritingManager` is the second pair of eyes on pricing — it approves or rejects assessments — and it is the only role that may suspend or reinstate coverage.
- A `ClaimsAdjuster` runs claims day to day: intake, coverage decisions, pre-suit resolution offers and acceptances, defense engagement and closure, invoice audits and approvals, and the creation of indemnity payments.
- A `ClaimsManager` holds claims authority: it approves and voids money, reopens closed claims, voids audited invoices, and owns related-claim aggregation.
- A `PanelCounsel` principal is outside defense counsel: it submits invoices on its own matters and disputes audit reductions.
- A `PolicyholderContact` is an insured-side read-only contact with no write capability at all.

Identity semantics differ by role and matter for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `Underwriter`, `UnderwritingManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster` or `ClaimsManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition demands it — as `priced_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on an exposure assessment, as `audited_by_user_id` on a defense invoice, and as `created_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on an indemnity payment — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PanelCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DefensePanelFirm`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DefensePanelFirm` compare the resource's firm reference to `X-User-ID`, so a counsel principal may only submit or dispute invoices on defense assignments whose `panel_firm_id` equals its own identifier, and any other attempt is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_COUNSEL` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PolicyholderContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant`; every read that principal performs is scoped to rows belonging to that applicant, and a read of another applicant's applicant record, risk-management service, exposure assessment or policy is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Because the four-eyes gates described in §2.10 compare `X-User-ID` values rather than roles, two different principals in the same role satisfy a gate, while one principal that performed both halves of a two-person control does not — even if that principal legitimately holds both roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100, and a value outside that inclusive range is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0; a negative value is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `meta` object of a list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the total number of rows matching the filters, not the number returned on this page — and `has_more`, which is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Sorting is fixed per endpoint so that paging is stable. The default ordering for a list is `created_at` ascending, and that default governs rate plans, risk-management services, exposure assessments, related-claim groups, pre-suit resolutions and indemnity payments. Six lists override it: jurisdictions are ordered by `code` ascending; defense panel firms by `blended_rate_cents` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; policies by `effective_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; claims by `first_notice_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; defense assignments by `engagement_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; defense invoices by `period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted resource in this system — jurisdictions, rate plans, applicants, risk-management services, exposure assessments, policies, claims, related-claim groups, defense panel firms, defense assignments, defense invoices, indemnity payments and pre-suit resolutions alike — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by the client. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `updated_at` follows the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation, including mutations a row receives as a downstream effect of an operation performed on some other row. No entity in this system is exempt from `updated_at` or `version`.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, a cascade that touches four rows bumps four `version` counters, not one; the counters are the audit trail of the cascade. Second, because the optimistic-concurrency fields of §2.7 read a parent's `version`, a client that mutates a parent for any reason — including indirectly — must re-read it before its next guarded write.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a logical clock and never consults host wall-clock time for any business decision. At startup the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it moves only when a `PlatformAdmin` sets it. The setter takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, which must be at or after the current logical clock reading; an earlier instant is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock to the value it already holds is permitted.

Advancing the clock is not a passive assignment: it is the trigger for every time-gated system transition in the domain, all of which are evaluated in the same request. Policies that have reached their inception move from `BOUND` to `ACTIVE`; policies that have reached their expiry move to `EXPIRED` from `BOUND`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` alike; risk-management services whose window has closed move from `ACTIVE` to `LAPSED`, dragging the applicant's credit recomputation and its downstream assessment invalidation with them; and pre-suit resolution offers whose expiry has passed move from `OFFERED` to `LAPSED`. The response body reports the resulting state as `current_utc`, together with the four lists `activated_policy_ids`, `expired_policy_ids`, `lapsed_service_ids` and `lapsed_resolution_ids`.

### 2.6. Money, Hours and Rounding Discipline

All monetary values in this system are integers denominated in whole cents; no monetary field is ever a decimal or a string. Wherever a monetary result is produced by multiplying or dividing, the result is rounded to a whole number of cents using round-half-up — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, upward. Rounding is applied at the step that produces each component, never deferred and never accumulated: where a calculation in §3 names several intermediate components, each of those components is independently rounded to whole cents as it is computed, and the components are then combined by integer addition and subtraction.

Percentage-shaped and score-shaped fields carry their own precision. An applicant's `turnover_pct` and `risk_credit_pct` are carried to two decimal places with round-half-up, and its `exposure_score` likewise to two decimal places. An applicant's `hazard_load_pct` is carried to three decimal places and is exact rather than rounded, because every input to it is itself a three-decimal constant. A rate plan's `wage_hour_sublimit_pct` and `third_party_sublimit_pct` and a service's `credit_pct` are two-decimal fractions supplied by the client; supplying more than two decimal places is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Billed hours on a defense invoice — `partner_hours`, `associate_hours`, `paralegal_hours` and `block_billed_hours` — accept at most one decimal place, and a finer value is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Exactly two quantities in the whole system are deliberately carried unrounded through an intermediate step: the per-employee rate after the headcount volume factor is applied, and the aggregate-limit load factor. Both are described where they are used, in §3.3.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Revisions

Three write operations are guarded against writing on top of data the client has not observed. Each guard names a parent entity and a non-optional request field that must carry the client's last observed `version` of that parent; if the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected and nothing is persisted. No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and supplying one where it is not defined is an unknown body field and therefore `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

- Opening an exposure assessment requires `expected_applicant_version` to equal the current `version` of the `Applicant` named by `applicant_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists precisely because a second assessment on the same applicant is a legitimate product operation — it supersedes the earlier ones — so nothing else would tell the writer that a risk-management service was completed or revoked, or that headcount was revised, since it read the applicant and computed its intended terms.
- Recording an indemnity payment on a claim requires `expected_policy_version` to equal the current `version` of the owning `Policy`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Multiple payments on one claim are legitimate — a claim may settle in tranches — so without this guard a writer would happily create a second payment while still believing the sublimit and aggregate remainders it read before the first one landed.
- Attaching a claim to a related-claim group requires `expected_related_claim_group_version` to equal the current `version` of the `RelatedClaimGroup` named in the path. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GROUP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every attach re-derives the group's shared retention and its retention-basis member, so a second attach is a valid operation rather than an error; the guard is what tells a writer that computed its aggregation against an older membership to re-read the group first.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

Checks are evaluated in one strict order, and the **first** violated check produces the response — no later check is evaluated, and no partial write is performed. The order below is the whole of it, from highest precedence to lowest.

1. `X-User-ID` present and non-empty → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. The caller's role admitted by the endpoint → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. Request shape: required body fields present, no unknown body fields, correct types, valid enum members, numeric values inside their permitted ranges, decimal-place limits respected, ISO 8601 instants well formed, interval fields correctly ordered (a service or policy `effective_to` strictly after its `effective_from`, an `engagement_to` strictly after its `engagement_from`, an invoice `period_end` at or after its `period_start`), and pagination `limit` within 1 through 100 with `offset` at least 0 → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint.
4. Every identifier in the URL path resolves to an existing row of the expected type → otherwise the 404 matching that path segment: `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANEL_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RESOLUTION_NOT_FOUND`. This applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
5. Every foreign key and natural key supplied in the request **body** resolves — `applicant_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `exposure_assessment_id`, `policy_id`, `lead_claim_id`, `claim_id`, `related_to_claim_id`, `panel_firm_id` and every entry of `excluded_panel_firm_ids`, plus the jurisdiction natural keys `primary_jurisdiction_code`, each `code` inside `jurisdiction_headcounts` and each entry of `jurisdiction_codes` → otherwise the corresponding 404 from the same family (`ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANEL_FIRM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`). This applies to every write endpoint that accepts a foreign key or a jurisdiction code in its body.
6. Cross-aggregate ownership consistency → otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` or `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_COUNSEL` (both 403). Four situations are policed here: a claim's `related_to_claim_id` must point at a claim under the same `policy_id`; a related-claim group's lead claim and every subsequently attached claim must sit under the group's own `policy_id`; a defense invoice may be submitted or disputed only by the `PanelCounsel` principal whose identifier equals the assignment's `panel_firm_id`, which is the `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_COUNSEL` case; and a `PolicyholderContact` may read only rows belonging to its own applicant. Concretely this check guards claim intake, group creation, group attachment, invoice submission, invoice dispute, and every read performed by an entity-backed principal.
7. The optimistic-concurrency field of §2.7 matches its parent's current `version` → otherwise `ERR_STALE_APPLICANT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_GROUP_VERSION` (all 412). This applies to assessment creation, indemnity-payment creation and group attachment.
8. The target entity's own current status permits the requested transition → otherwise the state error naming that operation: `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVATABLE`, `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_COMPLETABLE`, `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_REVOCABLE`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_PRICEABLE`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_SUSPENDABLE`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_REINSTATABLE`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, `ERR_COVERAGE_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_CLOSABLE`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_REOPENABLE`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_CLOSABLE`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_AUDITABLE`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTABLE`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_APPROVABLE`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_VOIDABLE`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_APPROVABLE`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_VOIDABLE`, `ERR_RESOLUTION_NOT_ACCEPTABLE` or `ERR_PANEL_FIRM_NOT_SUSPENDABLE` (all 409). This applies to every action endpoint that drives a state transition.
9. Cross-entity state preconditions — the owning or referenced entity must itself be admissible even though the target's own state is fine → otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PANEL_FIRM_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_GROUP_LOCKED` or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CLOSED` (all 409). The specific requirements are: the owning policy must be `BOUND` or `ACTIVE` and the claim must not be coverage-gated for claim work; the referenced rate plan must be `ACTIVE` for a new assessment; a client-directed panel firm must be `PANEL_ACTIVE`; a member claim's related-claim group must be `OPEN` for a payment approval; and a defense assignment must not be `CLOSED` for a new invoice. The operations guarded here are assessment creation, claim intake, claim start-review, the coverage decision, claim reopen, defense-assignment creation, indemnity-payment creation, indemnity-payment approval and invoice submission.
10. Segregation of duties on all three four-eyes gates → otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on assessment approval, invoice approval and payment approval.
11. An exposure assessment whose credit basis has gone stale may not be approved → otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_BASIS_STALE` (409), on assessment approval only.
12. A claim that buckets to `THIRD_PARTY` may not be reported on a policy that did not elect third-party coverage → otherwise `ERR_THIRD_PARTY_COVERAGE_NOT_ELECTED` (422), on claim intake.
13. A claim's `first_notice_at` must fall inside the policy period → otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_OUTSIDE_POLICY_PERIOD` (422), on claim intake.
14. A pre-suit resolution may only be offered on, or accepted for, an administrative-charge claim → otherwise `ERR_PRE_SUIT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422), on both pre-suit offer creation and pre-suit acceptance.
15. On acceptance, the elapsed-day count must be within the jurisdiction's pre-suit window and the logical clock must not be past the offer's expiry → otherwise `ERR_PRE_SUIT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422), on pre-suit acceptance.
16. An invoice's billing period must lie inside its assignment's engagement window → otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT` (422), on invoice submission.
17. An invoice's billing period must not collide with a live invoice period on the same assignment → otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on invoice submission.
18. A new risk-management service must not conflict with an existing service window of the same type on the same applicant → otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on service purchase.
19. Policy periods for one applicant must not overlap → otherwise `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on policy binding.
20. A client-directed panel firm must have window capacity → otherwise `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on defense-assignment creation in its client-directed form; the automatic selector never raises this code because it filters over-capacity firms out of its candidate pool instead.
21. The candidate pool must be non-empty, and a client-directed firm must cover the applicant's primary jurisdiction and must not be excluded → otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PANEL_FIRM` (422), on defense-assignment creation.
22. A claim being nominated as a group lead or attached to a group must not already belong to a group → otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_GROUPED` (409), on group creation and group attachment.
23. A claim attached to a class-action or mass-claim-wave group must match the lead claim's coverage bucket → otherwise `ERR_GROUP_BASIS_MISMATCH` (422), on group attachment.
24. A claim may be reopened at most once → otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_REOPEN_LIMIT` (409), on claim reopen.
25. Insurer money may not leave before the retention attaches, so an invoice may only be approved once the claim's retention is satisfied or its group is locked → otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_SATISFIED` (409), on invoice approval.
26. The matter's defense budget must accommodate the invoice → otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409), on invoice approval.
27. A wage-and-hour-bucketed insurer portion must fit the wage-and-hour sublimit remainder → otherwise `ERR_WAGE_HOUR_SUBLIMIT_EXHAUSTED` (409), on payment approval.
28. A third-party-bucketed insurer portion must fit the third-party sublimit remainder → otherwise `ERR_THIRD_PARTY_SUBLIMIT_EXHAUSTED` (409), on payment approval.
29. The debiting amount must fit the policy's aggregate remainder → otherwise `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED` (409), on payment approval and on invoice approval.
30. An applicant's jurisdiction allocation must be internally consistent → otherwise `ERR_HEADCOUNT_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH` (422), on applicant registration and on applicant revision.
31. The logical clock may only move forward or stay equal → otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422), on the clock setter.
32. Reference-data names and codes are globally unique → otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATE_PLAN_NAME` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_FIRM_NAME` (all 409), on jurisdiction, rate-plan and panel-firm registration respectively.

Six situations show the ordering doing real work, and implementations that evaluate checks in a different sequence will report the wrong code for each of them. Approving a defense invoice that a dispute returned to `ADJUSTED` while its recomputed allowed amount would also blow the matter budget reports `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_APPROVABLE`, because the own-state check outranks the fiscal check. Auditing an invoice already in `APPROVED` on a matter whose budget is exhausted reports `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_AUDITABLE`, for the same reason. When the very user who audited an invoice tries to approve it on a claim whose retention is not yet satisfied, the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, because the four-eyes gate outranks the retention gate. Reporting a customer-claimant claim on a policy that never elected third-party coverage, with a `first_notice_at` also outside the policy period, reports `ERR_THIRD_PARTY_COVERAGE_NOT_ELECTED`, because the coverage-election check outranks the temporal check. Opening an exposure assessment with a stale `expected_applicant_version` against a rate plan still in `DRAFT` reports `ERR_STALE_APPLICANT_VERSION`, because concurrency outranks cross-entity state. And an invoice period that both falls outside the engagement window and collides with an existing invoice reports `ERR_INVOICE_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT`, because the containment check outranks the collision check.

### 2.9. Nested Paths and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Several resources are addressed through a parent path segment — a risk-management service is created under its applicant, a claim under its policy, a defense assignment, an indemnity payment and a pre-suit resolution each under their claim, an invoice under its defense assignment, and a group attachment under its group. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve produces its own 404; a request naming a nonexistent policy and a nonexistent claim reports the policy's code, never the claim's. The parent identifier is taken from the path and written onto the child — `applicant_id` on a risk-management service, `policy_id` on a claim, `claim_id` on a defense assignment, an indemnity payment and a pre-suit resolution, and `defense_assignment_id` on a defense invoice — and it is never accepted in the body of those creations.

Because children are addressed by their own identifier once created, every collection list carries filter parameters that make the reverse direction reachable. Risk-management services filter by `applicant_id`, `service_type` and `status`. Exposure assessments filter by `applicant_id`, `rate_plan_id` and `status`. Policies filter by `applicant_id`, `exposure_assessment_id`, `status` and `limit_posture`. Claims filter by `policy_id`, `related_to_claim_id`, `related_claim_group_id`, `status`, `claim_type` and `coverage_bucket`. Related-claim groups filter by `policy_id`, `lead_claim_id`, `retention_basis_claim_id`, `status` and `aggregation_basis`. Pre-suit resolutions filter by `claim_id` and `status`. Indemnity payments filter by `claim_id`, `status` and `sublimit_bucket`. Defense assignments filter by `claim_id`, `panel_firm_id` and `status`. Defense invoices filter by `defense_assignment_id` and `status`. Jurisdictions filter by `hazard_tier`; rate plans by `status`; and defense panel firms by `jurisdiction_code` — which matches firms whose `jurisdiction_codes` array contains the supplied value — as well as by `tier` and `status`. Filters combine conjunctively, and an unrecognised filter value that is not a member of the underlying enumeration is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

One read is purely computed rather than a stored row: the policy limit ledger reconciles a policy's aggregate limit against its indemnity and defense spend in a single object reporting `policy_id`, `aggregate_limit_cents`, `indemnity_paid_cents`, `defense_spend_cents`, `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents`, `wage_hour_sublimit_cents`, `wage_hour_sublimit_remaining_cents`, `third_party_sublimit_cents`, `third_party_sublimit_remaining_cents` and `limit_posture`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three money-or-authority gates in this system are two-person controls, and all three compare `X-User-ID` values rather than roles. Assessment approval requires that the approving `X-User-ID` differ from the `priced_by_user_id` recorded by the last price run. Invoice approval requires that the approving `X-User-ID` differ from the `audited_by_user_id` recorded by the last audit run. Payment approval requires that the approving `X-User-ID` differ from the `created_by_user_id` recorded when the payment was drafted. Any of these three violations is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Because the comparison is on the principal identifier and not the role, a manager who happens to have priced an assessment is blocked from approving it, while any second principal — in the role the endpoint admits — is free to approve. Note also that a dispute clears `audited_by_user_id` back to null, so the re-audit after a dispute re-establishes the gate against whoever performed that re-audit.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and the Two Policy Axes

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row, and they do so transitively: each hop is caused by the mutation the previous hop produced, not by the original request. Four such chains exist and each is described in full where it belongs in §3 — a service change propagating to the applicant and from there to that applicant's priced assessments; a policy suspension propagating to claims, from claims to defense assignments and from assignments to their open invoices; a claim closure propagating to its open assignments and from there to panel-firm capacity; and a payment approval propagating to the claim, the claim's group, the policy ledger and, when the aggregate limit reaches zero, onward to defense assignments and their invoices. In every hop, the mutated row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1.

A policy carries **two independent state axes** and they never read each other. The `status` axis — `BOUND`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `EXPIRED` — describes whether coverage is currently available. The `limit_posture` axis — `OPEN`, `ERODED`, `EXHAUSTED` — describes how much of the aggregate limit has been consumed. A policy may legitimately be `ACTIVE` and `EXHAUSTED`, or `SUSPENDED` and `OPEN`; a `status` transition never inspects `limit_posture`, and a `limit_posture` transition never inspects `status`.

### 2.12. Interval Conventions and Reference-Data Uniqueness

Time windows in this domain are not all the same shape, and the differences are deliberate. A risk-management service window, a policy period and a defense engagement window are all **half-open**: the `effective_from` / `engagement_from` instant is inside the window and the `effective_to` / `engagement_to` instant is outside it. Two half-open windows collide when the first begins strictly before the second ends and the second begins strictly before the first ends, which means windows that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant the next begins — do **not** collide. A defense invoice billing period is the exception: it is a **closed** interval in which both `period_start` and `period_end` are inside the period, so two billing periods collide when the first starts at or before the second ends and the second starts at or before the first ends, and touching endpoints therefore **do** collide.

Three reference-data identifiers are globally unique and are checked as such at registration: a jurisdiction's `code`, a rate plan's `name` and a defense panel firm's `name`. A duplicate jurisdiction code is `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`, a duplicate plan name is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATE_PLAN_NAME` and a duplicate firm name is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_FIRM_NAME`, all HTTP 409. Jurisdiction codes are additionally used as natural keys: applicants and panel firms reference jurisdictions by code rather than by identifier, and every such reference must resolve to a registered jurisdiction.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Carrier Reference Data and the Defense Panel Roster

Nothing can be underwritten until carrier operations has populated three reference tables, and all three are `PlatformAdmin` territory.

A **jurisdiction** is the geographic unit of employment-litigation exposure. Registering one supplies a two-letter `code` matching exactly two uppercase letters, a display `name`, a `hazard_tier` drawn from `T1`, `T2`, `T3` and `T4`, the `admin_agency_name` of the body that issues charges of discrimination there, a `pre_suit_window_days` count of at least 0 whole days, a `wage_hour_multiplier` of at least 1.00 carried to two decimal places, and a `class_action_propensity_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive. An optional `pre_suit_mediation_required` boolean records whether the agency mandates mediation before a civil action may be filed and defaults to false when omitted. Two of these fields are underwriting disclosure only and are deliberately never consumed by any calculation in this specification: `wage_hour_multiplier` and `class_action_propensity_pct` are published references that appear on reads and nowhere else. The `hazard_tier`, by contrast, drives both the premium hazard load and the exposure score, and `pre_suit_window_days` gates the pre-suit incentive credit.

A **rate plan** carries the pricing constants for a book of business: a globally unique `name`, a `base_rate_per_employee_cents` of at least 1 whole cent charged per covered employee before the headcount volume factor, a `min_premium_cents` floor of at least 1 whole cent, and the two sublimit fractions `wage_hour_sublimit_pct` and `third_party_sublimit_pct`, each greater than 0 and at most 1, carried to two decimal places. A plan is created in `DRAFT` and is inert there. Activating it moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, which is the only transition on the plan's lifecycle and is refused with `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVATABLE` on a plan that is not currently `DRAFT`; `ACTIVE` is terminal. Only an `ACTIVE` plan may be referenced by a new exposure assessment, and referencing a `DRAFT` plan is `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A **defense panel firm** joins the roster with a globally unique `name`, a `tier` of `NATIONAL`, `REGIONAL` or `LOCAL`, a `jurisdiction_codes` array of at least one unique jurisdiction code naming the jurisdictions the firm covers, three whole-cent hourly rates — `partner_rate_cents_per_hour`, `associate_rate_cents_per_hour` and `paralegal_rate_cents_per_hour`, each at least 1 — and a `capacity_open_matters` ceiling of at least 1. On registration the service computes the firm's **blended rate**, which is the ranking key the automatic panel selector uses: the partner rate is weighted at 0.35, the associate rate at 0.50 and the paralegal rate at 0.15 — weights that sum to exactly 1.00 — and the weighted sum is rounded half up to whole cents and stored as `blended_rate_cents`. The firm's `open_matter_count` starts at 0 and thereafter always equals the number of defense assignments naming that firm whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_FUNDING`. A firm joins in `PANEL_ACTIVE`. Suspending it moves it `PANEL_ACTIVE → PANEL_SUSPENDED` — refused with `ERR_PANEL_FIRM_NOT_SUSPENDABLE` if it is already suspended — and the effect is strictly forward-looking: the firm drops out of the candidate pool for every future engagement, but its existing defense assignments are left exactly as they are.

### 3.2. Applicant Intake, Exposure Data and the Risk-Management Credit Basis

An `Underwriter` registers a prospective insured employer with its `legal_name`, a `total_headcount` of at least 1 covered employee, a `prior_year_separations` count of at least 0 covering involuntary and voluntary separations in the twelve months preceding the application, a `jurisdiction_headcounts` array of at least one entry, and a `primary_jurisdiction_code`. An optional `separations_since_assessment` count defaults to 0. Each entry of `jurisdiction_headcounts` is an object pairing a `code` — a jurisdiction natural key — with a `headcount` of at least 1; codes must be unique within the array. Two allocation invariants are enforced together, both on registration and on any revision that supplies either field: the `headcount` values must sum to exactly `total_headcount`, and `primary_jurisdiction_code` must itself appear among the array's codes. A violation of either is `ERR_HEADCOUNT_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The primary jurisdiction carries no extra weight in any hazard computation; its single job is to be the jurisdiction the defense-panel eligibility filter and the pre-suit window read.

Four derived values are maintained on the applicant. **Turnover** is a straightforward ratio: a separations count multiplied by 100, divided by `total_headcount`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The applicant's own `turnover_pct` is always evaluated with `prior_year_separations` as that separations count, and it is recomputed on registration and on every revision that supplies `prior_year_separations` or `total_headcount`.

The **hazard load** aggregates jurisdictional severity. Let the jurisdiction count be the number of entries in `jurisdiction_headcounts`, and let the governing tier be the worst `hazard_tier` among the jurisdictions those entries resolve to, under the total order in which `T1` is lowest, then `T2`, then `T3`, and `T4` is highest. The base contribution is 0.025 when the governing tier is `T1`, 0.070 for `T2`, 0.135 for `T3` and 0.205 for `T4`. A multi-jurisdiction spread of 0.010 is then added for each jurisdiction beyond the first — that is, 0.010 multiplied by one less than the jurisdiction count. `hazard_load_pct` is the smaller of 0.30 and the sum of the base contribution and the spread, carried to three decimal places. Note carefully what this is not: the worst tier governs, not the tier of the primary jurisdiction, and not a headcount-weighted average of tiers.

The **risk-management credit** is what an applicant earns for investing in prevention. An `Underwriter` purchases a service on an applicant by supplying a `service_type` of `HOTLINE_SUBSCRIPTION`, `HANDBOOK_REVIEW` or `MANAGER_TRAINING`, a `credit_pct` between 0.00 and 0.15 inclusive at two decimal places, and the half-open window `effective_from` / `effective_to` with the end strictly after the start. Services are created in `PENDING` and contribute nothing there. A new service may not conflict with an existing non-`REVOKED` service of the **same** `service_type` on the same applicant, and the conflict test deliberately pads the existing window: a thirty-day gap buffer is added to the high side of the existing window, so the new service collides when its start falls strictly before thirty days past the existing end and the existing start falls strictly before the new end. A collision is `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Services of different types may overlap freely.

Completing a service moves it `PENDING → ACTIVE` and requires the logical clock to be inside the service's half-open window — at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`; a service that is not `PENDING`, or a clock outside that window, is `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_COMPLETABLE` (HTTP 409). Completion stamps `completed_at` with the logical-clock instant. Revoking a service, from `PENDING` or from `ACTIVE`, records a mandatory `revocation_reason` and moves it to `REVOKED`; a service already `LAPSED` or `REVOKED` is `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_REVOCABLE`. A service whose window closes while it is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → LAPSED` when the logical clock is advanced to or past its `effective_to`. `LAPSED` and `REVOKED` are both terminal, and `completed_at` remains null for any service that never reached `ACTIVE`.

Only `ACTIVE` services count toward the credit. `risk_credit_pct` is the smaller of 0.15 and the sum of `credit_pct` across the applicant's services whose status is `ACTIVE`, carried to two decimal places; `PENDING`, `LAPSED` and `REVOKED` rows contribute exactly nothing. Every one of the four service transitions that changes the `ACTIVE` set — complete, revoke from `PENDING`, revoke from `ACTIVE`, and lapse — recomputes both `risk_credit_pct` and the applicant's `exposure_score` and increments the applicant's `version` by 1.

That applicant mutation is the first hop of a two-hop chain, and the second hop is the point of the design. Because the applicant's credit basis has changed, every exposure assessment belonging to that applicant whose status is `PRICED` transitions `PRICED → REFERRED` and has `credit_basis_stale` set to true, with its own `version` incremented by 1; an assessment already in `REFERRED` is left in `REFERRED` but also has `credit_basis_stale` set to true. Neither hop is caused by the revoke or complete request directly — the second hop is caused by the applicant mutation the first hop produced.

Revising an applicant is a partial update accepting any of `total_headcount`, `prior_year_separations`, `separations_since_assessment`, `jurisdiction_headcounts` and `primary_jurisdiction_code`. It recomputes `turnover_pct`, `hazard_load_pct` and `exposure_score` and increments the applicant's `version` by 1. Whether it stales assessments depends on precisely which fields moved: a revision that supplies `total_headcount`, `prior_year_separations`, `jurisdiction_headcounts` or `primary_jurisdiction_code` fires the same `PRICED → REFERRED` staling described above, whereas a revision that supplies **only** `separations_since_assessment` does not — that field is read exclusively by the bound-audit premium variant and never by the quoting variant, so a change to it cannot invalidate a quote.

### 3.3. Exposure Scoring, Referral and the Quoted Premium

Before a price can be struck, the applicant is scored. The **exposure score** is a points model with four terms, and it is a cross-entity derived value: it moves when a jurisdiction is added to or removed from the applicant's allocation, when headcount or separations change, and when a risk-management service becomes `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED`.

The first term is headcount band points, read from `total_headcount`: 8 points for 1 through 50 employees, 16 points for 51 through 250, 26 points for 251 through 1000, and 34 points for 1001 or more. The second term is hazard points, built from the same governing tier the hazard load uses: 5 points for `T1`, 12 for `T2`, 20 for `T3` and 28 for `T4`, plus a multi-jurisdiction component equal to the smaller of 8.00 and 2.00 points for each jurisdiction beyond the first — that is, 2.00 multiplied by one less than the jurisdiction count, capped at 8.00. The third term is turnover points: the applicant's `turnover_pct` multiplied by 0.80, rounded half up to two decimal places, and then capped at 30.00. The rounding happens **before** the cap and before the sum, so rounding never accumulates across terms. The fourth term is a negative credit: each `ACTIVE` risk-management service subtracts points by type — 6.00 points for a `HOTLINE_SUBSCRIPTION`, 4.00 for a `HANDBOOK_REVIEW` and 3.00 for a `MANAGER_TRAINING` — and the subtractions of multiple active services accumulate. The four terms are added, and `exposure_score` is that sum clamped into the range 0.00 through 100.00 inclusive: never below 0.00, never above 100.00, carried to two decimal places.

An `Underwriter` opens an **exposure assessment** naming the `applicant_id` and the `rate_plan_id`, the `requested_aggregate_limit_cents` the applicant is asking for — at least 1,000,000 whole cents — the `requested_retention_cents` it is willing to bear per claim, at least 0, and the `expected_applicant_version` guard of §2.7. An optional `third_party_coverage_requested` boolean records whether coverage for customer and vendor claimants is wanted and defaults to false. The referenced plan must be `ACTIVE`. Creation has two side effects beyond the new `DRAFT` row: the applicant's `version` is incremented by 1, and every earlier assessment of the same applicant that sits in `PRICED`, `REFERRED` or `APPROVED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`. Superseding an already-`APPROVED` but unbound assessment is intentional — a re-quote legitimately displaces it — and it is exactly this legitimacy that makes the applicant-version guard necessary rather than decorative.

Pricing the assessment runs the **quoting variant** of the premium calculation and decides, from the resulting score, whether the risk can be quoted at the underwriter's own authority. The price action is callable from `DRAFT` and from `REFERRED`; on any other status it is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_PRICEABLE`. It requires the assessment's rate plan to still be `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. Whichever leg is taken, the same side effects occur: `exposure_score_snapshot` is set from the applicant's freshly recomputed `exposure_score` and `turnover_pct_snapshot` from its `turnover_pct`, all six premium components and the indicated premium are computed, `priced_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `credit_basis_stale` is set back to false. The referral decision is a closed threshold on the high side: when the snapshot score is at or above 72.00 the assessment lands in `REFERRED`, and when it is strictly below 72.00 it lands in `PRICED`. That gives four legs — `DRAFT → PRICED`, `DRAFT → REFERRED`, `REFERRED → PRICED` and the self-loop `REFERRED → REFERRED` — and the four differ only in the resulting status. The `REFERRED → PRICED` leg is the revision loop that makes risk management worth buying: a risk referred for a high score returns to quotable status once the applicant's credits bring the score under the threshold, while a re-price that still scores at or above 72.00 simply stays in `REFERRED`, with `credit_basis_stale` cleared either way.

The **premium calculation** proceeds in eight pinned steps and produces six components plus a result. Every component is rounded half up to whole cents at the step that produces it.

1. Component A, the base premium, begins with a headcount volume factor read from `total_headcount`: 1.000 for 1 through 50 employees, 0.925 for 51 through 250, 0.855 for 251 through 1000, and 0.785 for 1001 or more. The plan's `base_rate_per_employee_cents` is multiplied by that factor to give a per-employee amount which is **carried unrounded**. The entries of `jurisdiction_headcounts` are then iterated in ascending order of their `code`, and for each entry the entry's `headcount` is multiplied by the unrounded per-employee amount and that product is rounded half up to whole cents. Component A is the sum of those per-jurisdiction rounded shares. The order is pinned: each jurisdiction's share is rounded first and the rounded shares are then added. Rounding the single product of `total_headcount` and the per-employee amount instead can differ from the correct answer by as much as one cent for every jurisdiction beyond the first.
2. Component B, the turnover load, is component A multiplied by a load fraction read from the applicable turnover percentage: 0.00 when turnover is from 0.00 through 9.99, 0.06 from 10.00 through 19.99, 0.14 from 20.00 through 34.99, and 0.25 at 35.00 or above. Band boundaries are inclusive on the low side, so a turnover of exactly 35.00 lands in the 0.25 band.
3. Component C, the hazard load, is component A multiplied by the applicant's `hazard_load_pct`.
4. Component D, the limit load, is component A multiplied by a limit factor. The limit factor is the `requested_aggregate_limit_cents` divided by a reference limit of 100,000,000 cents, less 1, multiplied by 0.10, with the whole expression floored at 0 so that a request at or below the reference limit attracts no load at all. Like the per-employee amount, this factor is carried unrounded.
5. Credit E, the retention credit, is component A multiplied by a credit fraction read from `requested_retention_cents`: 0.00 for a retention from 0 through 2,499,999 cents, 0.05 from 2,500,000 through 9,999,999 cents, and 0.11 at 10,000,000 cents or above. Again the low side is inclusive, so a retention of exactly 2,500,000 cents earns the 0.05 credit.
6. The gross premium is the sum of components A, B, C and D. Loads only — neither credit is part of gross.
7. Credit F, the risk-management credit, is the gross premium multiplied by the applicable risk-management credit fraction. This is the hotline and policy-review credit the applicant bought in §3.2.
8. The net premium is the gross premium less credit E and less credit F, and the result is the larger of that net premium and the plan's `min_premium_cents`. The minimum-premium floor is applied **after** both credits, never before them.

The calculation has two variants that differ in three respects and in nothing else. The **quoting** variant runs at the price transition; its separations basis is `prior_year_separations` alone, its risk-management credit is summed over services whose status is `ACTIVE` at that moment, and it writes `base_premium_cents`, `turnover_load_cents`, `hazard_load_cents`, `limit_load_cents`, `retention_credit_cents`, `risk_mgmt_credit_cents` and `indicated_premium_cents` onto the assessment. The **bound-audit** variant runs once, at bind, and is described in §3.4; its separations basis is `prior_year_separations` plus `separations_since_assessment`, its risk-management credit is summed only over services that are both `ACTIVE` and whose `completed_at` is at or before the policy's `effective_from` — a service that only became active after inception earns no credit on the bound audit — and it writes `bound_premium_cents` and `premium_variance_cents` onto the policy. Note that because the separations basis differs, the turnover load band in step 2 can differ between the two variants for the same applicant.

Release is a manager decision. Approving an assessment is permitted from `PRICED` and from `REFERRED` — referred risks are bindable, but only a manager may release them — and on any other status the answer is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_DECIDABLE`. Two further gates apply, in this order: the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `priced_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and `credit_basis_stale` must be false, else `ERR_CREDIT_BASIS_STALE`, which forces a re-price against the current basis before release can be attempted again. A successful approval records `approved_by_user_id` and moves the assessment to `APPROVED`. Rejecting an assessment, likewise from `PRICED` or `REFERRED`, records a mandatory `rejection_reason` and moves it to `REJECTED`, which is terminal — as are `SUPERSEDED` and `BOUND`.

### 3.4. Binding Coverage and the Bound-Premium Audit

Binding turns an approved assessment into a policy. An `Underwriter` supplies the `exposure_assessment_id` of an assessment that is in `APPROVED` status, together with the policy period `effective_from` and `effective_to`, the latter strictly after the former. Policy periods for a single applicant are single-occupancy half-open intervals across all of that applicant's non-`EXPIRED` policies: the new period collides when it begins strictly before an existing period ends and that existing period begins strictly before the new one ends, so back-to-back terms that merely touch are fine while any genuine overlap is `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

Binding copies rather than references, so the policy is immune to later assessment edits. The `applicant_id` is copied from the assessment; `aggregate_limit_cents` is copied from `requested_aggregate_limit_cents`; `per_claim_retention_cents` is copied from `requested_retention_cents` and is the base retention before any of the multipliers of §3.7; and `third_party_coverage_elected` is copied from `third_party_coverage_requested`. The two sublimits are then carved out of the aggregate limit using the percentages of the rate plan the bound assessment referenced: `wage_hour_sublimit_cents` is the aggregate limit multiplied by that plan's `wage_hour_sublimit_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, and `third_party_sublimit_cents` is the aggregate limit multiplied by its `third_party_sublimit_pct`, rounded the same way. Note that the two sublimits are independent carve-outs and are not required to sum to anything in particular.

Bind is also the moment of the premium audit. The bound-audit variant of the premium calculation described in §3.3 is evaluated exactly once here and stored as `bound_premium_cents`, and the difference between it and the assessment's `indicated_premium_cents` is stored as `premium_variance_cents` — computed as the bound premium less the indicated premium, so it is negative whenever the audit produces a cheaper answer than the quote. Two side effects complete the bind: the source assessment transitions `APPROVED → BOUND`, and the policy's own `BOUND → ACTIVE` transition is immediately evaluated against the logical clock, so a policy bound with an inception already reached comes back `ACTIVE` rather than `BOUND`.

A fresh policy's ledger fields all begin at their empty values. `indemnity_paid_cents` is the running sum of the insurer portions of `APPROVED` indemnity payments across the policy's claims; `defense_spend_cents` is the running sum of the allowed amounts of `APPROVED` defense invoices across the policy's matters; `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents` is the aggregate limit less both of those sums and is never negative, because every debiting operation pre-checks it. `wage_hour_sublimit_remaining_cents` is the wage-and-hour sublimit less the insurer portions of `APPROVED` payments whose bucket is `WAGE_HOUR` on this policy's claims, and `third_party_sublimit_remaining_cents` is the third-party sublimit less the insurer portions of `APPROVED` payments whose bucket is `THIRD_PARTY`. Defense spend erodes the aggregate limit but never a sublimit.

### 3.5. Coverage Availability: Suspension, Reinstatement and Expiry

The coverage-availability axis of a policy has four states. A policy is `BOUND` from the moment of binding until its inception is reached; it becomes `ACTIVE` whenever the logical clock stands at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`, a condition evaluated both at the end of binding and again on every clock advance. Any non-expired policy — `BOUND`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` alike — moves to `EXPIRED` as soon as the logical clock reaches or passes its `effective_to`. `EXPIRED` is terminal.

Suspension is the carrier's emergency brake and belongs to the `UnderwritingManager` alone. It applies only to a policy currently in `ACTIVE`; on any other status the answer is `ERR_POLICY_NOT_SUSPENDABLE`. A mandatory `suspension_reason` is recorded and `suspended_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant. What makes suspension expensive is the three further hops it sets off, each one caused by the mutation the hop before it produced. In the first hop, every claim under this policy whose status is `UNDER_REVIEW`, `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_OPEN` has `coverage_gated` set to true, with its `version` incremented by 1. In the second hop, those claim mutations propagate into defense: every defense assignment on one of the claims gated in the first hop whose status is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_FUNDING` with `hold_reason` set to `POLICY_SUSPENDED`. In the third hop, those assignment mutations propagate into billing: every defense invoice on one of those assignments whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT` or `ADJUSTED` has `hold_reason` set to `POLICY_SUSPENDED`, again with its `version` incremented by 1. Claims outside those three statuses, assignments already `SUSPENDED_FUNDING` or `CLOSED`, and invoices already `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` are untouched.

Reinstatement is the exact compensating transition and closes the cycle `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. It applies only to a `SUSPENDED` policy and only while the logical clock is still strictly before `effective_to`; a policy in any other status, or one whose term has already run out, is `ERR_POLICY_NOT_REINSTATABLE`. On success `suspension_reason` and `suspended_at` are both cleared to null, and each of the three hops is reversed in turn: every claim of this policy carrying `coverage_gated` true has it cleared to false; every defense assignment on those claims that is in `SUSPENDED_FUNDING` with `hold_reason` equal to `POLICY_SUSPENDED` transitions `SUSPENDED_FUNDING → ACTIVE` with `hold_reason` cleared to null; and every defense invoice on those assignments whose `hold_reason` is `POLICY_SUSPENDED` has it cleared to null. Only holds attributable to the suspension are lifted — an invoice held for limit exhaustion, described in §3.9, is not touched by a reinstatement.

While a policy is suspended, claim work stops. Starting a review, deciding coverage, reopening a claim, engaging defense counsel and recording an indemnity payment all require the owning policy to be `BOUND` or `ACTIVE` and the claim not to be coverage-gated; when either condition fails the answer is `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE` (HTTP 409), even though the claim's own status would otherwise permit the action.

### 3.6. Claim Intake, Coverage Bucketing and the Coverage Decision

A `ClaimsAdjuster` reports a claim against a policy. The intake payload carries the `claim_type` — one of `WRONGFUL_TERMINATION`, `DISCRIMINATION`, `HARASSMENT`, `RETALIATION` or `WAGE_AND_HOUR` — the `claimant_class` of `EMPLOYEE`, `CUSTOMER` or `VENDOR`, the `notice_type` of `ADMIN_CHARGE`, `LAWSUIT` or `DEMAND_LETTER` describing the form in which the matter first surfaced, the `first_notice_at` instant at which the insured first received notice, and the `alleged_damages_cents` figure the claimant is asserting, which is at least 0 whole cents and is recorded for reporting only — no calculation in this specification reads it. Two optional fields complete the payload: `claimant_count` defaults to 1 and must be at least 1, with any value above 1 marking the matter as a class or mass claim, and `related_to_claim_id` optionally names an earlier claim this one is alleged to arise from. That earlier claim must sit under the same `policy_id`, and a cross-policy reference is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`.

Notice must fall inside the term. `first_notice_at` must be at or after the policy's `effective_from` and strictly before its `effective_to`; notice before inception or at or after expiry is `ERR_CLAIM_OUTSIDE_POLICY_PERIOD` (HTTP 422).

Intake computes the claim's **coverage bucket**, which decides which pot of limit the matter will draw on. The order of the test is pinned and matters: if `claim_type` is `WAGE_AND_HOUR` the bucket is `WAGE_HOUR`; otherwise, if `claimant_class` is `CUSTOMER` or `VENDOR` the bucket is `THIRD_PARTY`; otherwise the bucket is `FIRST_PARTY`. A wage-and-hour claim brought by a customer is therefore bucketed `WAGE_HOUR`, not `THIRD_PARTY`, and its insurer portion debits the wage-and-hour sublimit. `FIRST_PARTY` has no sublimit at all; a first-party insurer portion is checked only against the policy's aggregate remainder. Be careful not to carry this precedence over into the retention multipliers of §3.7, where the two attributes behave quite differently.

Third-party coverage is optional and gated at intake: a claim that buckets to `THIRD_PARTY` may not be reported on a policy whose `third_party_coverage_elected` is false, and the attempt is `ERR_THIRD_PARTY_COVERAGE_NOT_ELECTED` (HTTP 422). A claim is created in `REPORTED`, with `retention_basis` starting at `PER_CLAIM`, `related_claim_group_id` null, `coverage_gated` false, `reopen_count` at 0, and its three ledger figures — `retention_satisfied_cents`, `indemnity_paid_cents` and `defense_paid_cents` — all at 0.

Starting a review moves the claim `REPORTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. On any other status the answer is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_REVIEWABLE`; and even from `REPORTED` the transition is refused when the owning policy is not `BOUND` or `ACTIVE`, or when the claim is coverage-gated, which is the cross-entity guard that yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`.

The coverage decision is the branch point of the claim lifecycle. It applies only to a claim in `UNDER_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_ALREADY_DECIDED` — and requires the owning policy to be `BOUND` or `ACTIVE`. The payload carries a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `DENY`, plus an optional `denial_reason`. The two branches are asymmetric in both their payload rules and their side effects, and the asymmetry is deliberate. On `ACCEPT`, `denial_reason` must be absent — supplying it alongside an acceptance is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the claim moves `UNDER_REVIEW → COVERAGE_ACCEPTED`, and the retention calculation of §3.7 runs, populating `per_claim_retention_cents` and `applied_retention_cents`. On `DENY`, `denial_reason` is mandatory — its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the claim moves `UNDER_REVIEW → COVERAGE_DENIED`, the reason is recorded, and **no** retention is computed at all, because a denied claim will never collect one.

### 3.7. Retention Mechanics: Multipliers, Pre-Suit Incentives and Related-Claim Aggregation

The retention is the layer the insured bears before insurer money attaches, and it is where three separate mechanisms meet. The starting point is the policy's `per_claim_retention_cents`, copied at bind. From there a multiplier is assembled beginning at 1.00: a `claim_type` of `WAGE_AND_HOUR` multiplies it by 1.50, and a `claimant_class` of `CUSTOMER` or `VENDOR` multiplies it by 1.25. The two multipliers **stack multiplicatively** — they are not mutually exclusive, and the larger does not simply win — so a wage-and-hour claim brought by a vendor carries a combined multiplier of 1.875 while an employee harassment claim carries 1.00. The claim's own `per_claim_retention_cents` is the policy base retention multiplied by the assembled multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents, and it is always populated on an accepted claim regardless of which retention basis the claim ends up on.

A **pre-suit resolution** is the carrier's incentive to settle an administrative charge before it becomes a lawsuit. A `ClaimsAdjuster` extends an offer on a claim by supplying an `offer_expires_at` instant strictly later than the current logical clock and a `settlement_offer_cents` amount of at least 1 whole cent; the server stamps `mediation_offered_at` with the logical-clock instant and creates the offer in `OFFERED`. Only an administrative-charge matter is eligible: a claim whose `notice_type` is not `ADMIN_CHARGE` cannot have an offer extended on it and cannot have one accepted, and either attempt is `ERR_PRE_SUIT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

Accepting an offer is where the credit is earned. Acceptance requires the offer to be in `OFFERED` — otherwise `ERR_RESOLUTION_NOT_ACCEPTABLE` — and requires the claim to be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_OPEN`. Two temporal conditions then apply, and both failures report `ERR_PRE_SUIT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422): the logical clock must be at or before `offer_expires_at`, and the elapsed-day count must be inside the jurisdiction's window. That count, stored as `elapsed_days_at_accept`, is the number of whole days between the claim's `first_notice_at` and the acceptance instant — the difference in seconds divided by 86,400 and truncated downward to a whole number. It is compared against the `pre_suit_window_days` of the jurisdiction whose `code` equals the applicant's `primary_jurisdiction_code`, resolved through the claim's policy to its applicant, and the comparison is inclusive: an elapsed count exactly equal to the window still earns the credit, while a count even one day beyond it does not. The credit itself, `incentive_credit_cents`, is the `settlement_offer_cents` multiplied by 0.30 and rounded half up to whole cents, then capped at 750,000 cents — the cap is applied **after** the rounding. Acceptance posts that credit onto the claim's `pre_suit_credit_cents`, recomputes the claim's `applied_retention_cents`, and moves the claim to `RESOLVED` if it was in `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_OPEN`. An offer whose expiry passes before anyone accepts it moves `OFFERED → LAPSED` on the clock advance and posts no credit whatsoever; both `ACCEPTED` and `LAPSED` are terminal.

**Related-claim aggregation** is the third mechanism, and it changes which retention applies. A `ClaimsManager` opens a group by supplying the `policy_id` it covers, the `lead_claim_id` of its first member, and an `aggregation_basis` of `COMMON_DECISION_MAKER`, `COMMON_POLICY_PRACTICE`, `CLASS_ACTION_MEMBER` or `MASS_CLAIM_WAVE`. The lead claim must belong to that same policy, must not already be grouped — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_GROUPED` (HTTP 409) — and is attached automatically at creation, which sets its `related_claim_group_id`, flips its `retention_basis` to `GROUP_AGGREGATED`, sets its `applied_retention_cents` to 0 and increments its `version` by 1. Further members are attached one at a time, each attach carrying the `claim_id` to add and the `expected_related_claim_group_version` guard of §2.7. An attach is refused when the claim already belongs to a group (`ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_GROUPED`), when the claim's `policy_id` differs from the group's (`ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`), and when the group is already `LOCKED` (`ERR_GROUP_LOCKED`). Aggregation basis also constrains membership: a group whose basis is `CLASS_ACTION_MEMBER` or `MASS_CLAIM_WAVE` accepts only claims whose `claimant_count` is at least 1 and whose `coverage_bucket` is the same as the lead claim's, and a mismatch is `ERR_GROUP_BASIS_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); a group whose basis is `COMMON_DECISION_MAKER` or `COMMON_POLICY_PRACTICE` accepts mixed buckets freely.

Every attach re-derives the group's aggregation. `member_count` is the number of claims whose `related_claim_group_id` names this group. `shared_retention_cents` is the **largest** `per_claim_retention_cents` among all member claims, scanned without any status filter so that denied and closed members still count; `retention_basis_claim_id` names the member achieving that maximum, with ties broken by the earliest member `created_at`. The group is created `OPEN`, and `retention_paid_cents` is the sum of the retention portions of `APPROVED` indemnity payments across every member claim.

The two retention bases therefore differ in exactly which bucket the settlement waterfall draws from. Under `PER_CLAIM` basis — the basis of any claim whose `related_claim_group_id` is null — the claim's `applied_retention_cents` is its own `per_claim_retention_cents` less its `pre_suit_credit_cents`, floored at 0 so a credit larger than the retention simply zeroes it rather than going negative, and the waterfall draws the outstanding retention from that applied figure less the claim's `retention_satisfied_cents`. Under `GROUP_AGGREGATED` basis the claim's `applied_retention_cents` is 0 — the group bears the retention instead of the claim — and the waterfall draws the outstanding retention from the group's `shared_retention_cents` less its `retention_paid_cents`, a single pot shared across every member. Because retention already satisfied on a claim flows into the group's `retention_paid_cents` through that aggregation, switching a claim onto the group basis never double-collects retention.

A group is `OPEN` while its `retention_paid_cents` remains strictly below its `shared_retention_cents`, and transitions `OPEN → LOCKED` the moment an approved payment on any member brings the paid figure to or above the shared retention. Once `LOCKED`, the group's shared retention is spent and every further insurer portion on any member is fully insurer-funded. Voiding a payment on a member that pulls `retention_paid_cents` back below the shared retention transitions the group `LOCKED → OPEN` again.

### 3.8. Defense Counsel: Panel Selection, Engagement, Billing and Guideline Audits

Once coverage is accepted, a `ClaimsAdjuster` engages defense counsel. Engagement is only available on a claim in `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_OPEN`, requires the owning policy to be `BOUND` or `ACTIVE` and the claim not to be coverage-gated (else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`), and carries the half-open engagement window `engagement_from` and `engagement_to` — the end strictly after the start — together with a `budget_cap_cents` defense budget of at least 1 whole cent. Two optional inputs shape which firm is chosen: `panel_firm_id` names a firm directly, and `excluded_panel_firm_ids` is an array of firm identifiers to remove from consideration. A third optional input, `litigation_guideline_version`, records which edition of the litigation guidelines governs invoice audits on this matter; it is an integer of at least 1 and defaults to 1.

When `panel_firm_id` is omitted, the **automatic panel selector** runs. Its candidate pool is every panel firm satisfying all four eligibility conditions at once: the firm's status is `PANEL_ACTIVE`; the applicant's `primary_jurisdiction_code` — resolved from the claim through its policy to the applicant — appears among the firm's `jurisdiction_codes`; the firm's identifier is not among `excluded_panel_firm_ids`; and the firm's window-overlapping matter count is strictly less than its `capacity_open_matters`. That matter count is window-scoped rather than global: it counts the firm's defense assignments whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_FUNDING` and whose engagement window overlaps the requested one under the half-open rule, meaning the existing window begins strictly before the requested window ends and the requested window begins strictly before the existing one ends. `CLOSED` assignments consume no capacity, and a firm at capacity for one window can still take a disjoint window. Candidates are then ranked by `blended_rate_cents` ascending — cheapest blended rate first. Ties are broken first by panel tier ascending, where `LOCAL` ranks ahead of `REGIONAL` and `REGIONAL` ahead of `NATIONAL`, and then by `created_at` ascending. The firm's identifier is never itself a tie-break; the earliest creation timestamp is the final arbiter. The winner is recorded with `selection_reason` set to `AUTO_SELECTED`. If the candidate pool is empty, the engagement is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PANEL_FIRM` (HTTP 422).

When `panel_firm_id` **is** supplied the selector does not run and `selection_reason` becomes `CLIENT_DIRECTED`, but the same eligibility conditions are still enforced and each failure gets its own code: a named firm in `PANEL_SUSPENDED` is `ERR_PANEL_FIRM_NOT_ACTIVE`; a named firm that does not cover the applicant's primary jurisdiction, or that the caller has also listed in `excluded_panel_firm_ids`, is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PANEL_FIRM`; and a named firm already holding `capacity_open_matters` window-overlapping assignments in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_FUNDING` is `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The capacity code exists only for the client-directed form; the automatic selector never raises it, because it filters over-capacity firms out of the pool instead.

Engagement pins the firm's rate schedule onto the matter. The assignment's own `partner_rate_cents_per_hour`, `associate_rate_cents_per_hour` and `paralegal_rate_cents_per_hour` are snapshots of the firm's rates at the moment of engagement, and a later change to the firm's published schedule cannot retroactively move an audit already run — or a future audit on this matter. The assignment starts in `ACTIVE` with `hold_reason` and `funding_ceased_at` both null, `budget_spent_cents` at 0 and `budget_remaining_cents` equal to `budget_cap_cents`; thereafter `budget_spent_cents` is the sum of the allowed amounts of `APPROVED` invoices on the matter and `budget_remaining_cents` is the cap less that sum. The assigned firm's `open_matter_count` increases by 1. And on the **first** assignment for a claim, the claim itself transitions `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED → DEFENSE_OPEN`; a second concurrent assignment on a claim already in `DEFENSE_OPEN` leaves the claim's status alone.

Closing an assignment is an adjuster action available from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED_FUNDING`; an assignment already `CLOSED` is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_CLOSABLE`. Closure recomputes the assigned firm's `open_matter_count`, which drops by one, and increments that firm's `version` by 1, freeing the capacity slot for the next engagement. An adjuster-initiated closure leaves `funding_ceased_at` null; only the limit-exhaustion cascade of §3.9 stamps it.

**Billing** is counsel's side of the relationship. A `PanelCounsel` principal submits an invoice on one of its own assignments — an invoice on an assignment whose `panel_firm_id` is not the caller's own identifier is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_COUNSEL` — supplying the closed billing period `period_start` and `period_end`, with the end at or after the start. Four optional hour fields, `partner_hours`, `associate_hours`, `paralegal_hours` and `block_billed_hours`, each default to 0, must be at least 0 and carry at most one decimal place; `block_billed_hours` records the subset of the billed time recorded as block entries and may not exceed the sum of the three timekeeper hour figures, a violation being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An optional `expenses_cents` disbursement figure defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. An invoice may not be submitted on an assignment whose status is `CLOSED`, which is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CLOSED`. The billing period must lie inside the engagement window — `period_start` at or after `engagement_from` and `period_end` strictly before `engagement_to` — and a period escaping either edge is `ERR_INVOICE_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT` (HTTP 422). Because billing periods are closed intervals, a new period collides with an existing invoice on the same assignment when the new start is at or before the existing end and the existing start is at or before the new end; touching endpoints therefore do collide, and a collision with any invoice that is not `VOIDED` and not `REJECTED` is `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Invoices are created in `SUBMITTED` with every audit output field unpopulated.

The **guideline audit** is the carrier's cost control, run by a `ClaimsAdjuster` and re-run in full every time it is invoked. It proceeds in five steps, each rounding independently to whole cents so that the four reductions are never combined into one rounding.

1. `billed_fees_cents` is the partner hours multiplied by the assignment's snapshot partner rate, plus the associate hours multiplied by the snapshot associate rate, plus the paralegal hours multiplied by the snapshot paralegal rate, with that total rounded half up to whole cents.
2. `rate_cap_excess_cents` charges back rates above the litigation guidelines. The guidelines cap partner time at 75,000 cents per hour and associate time at 45,000 cents per hour; paralegal time has no rate cap at all. The excess is the partner hours multiplied by the amount by which the snapshot partner rate exceeds its cap — floored at zero when the rate is at or under the cap — rounded half up to whole cents, plus the associate hours multiplied by the amount by which the snapshot associate rate exceeds its cap, likewise floored at zero and rounded half up separately. The fees remaining after this step are `billed_fees_cents` less `rate_cap_excess_cents`.
3. `block_reduction_cents` penalises block billing, and it is applied to the **post-rate-cap** fees. The total billed hours are the sum of partner, associate and paralegal hours; the block share is `block_billed_hours` divided by that total, or exactly 0 when the total is 0. The reduction is the post-rate-cap fees multiplied by the block share and then by 0.20, rounded half up to whole cents. The allowed fees are the post-rate-cap fees less that reduction.
4. `expense_disallowance_cents` caps disbursements at 0.08 of the allowed fees. The expense allowance is the allowed fees from step 3 multiplied by 0.08 and rounded half up to whole cents, and the disallowance is the amount by which `expenses_cents` exceeds that allowance, floored at zero. The base of this cap is pinned: it is the allowed fees **after both** the rate-cap and block-billing reductions, not the originally billed fees and not the post-rate-cap fees.
5. `allowed_amount_cents` is the allowed fees from step 3 plus the surviving expenses, which are `expenses_cents` less `expense_disallowance_cents`. `guideline_reduction_cents` is the sum of `rate_cap_excess_cents`, `block_reduction_cents` and `expense_disallowance_cents`. `disallowed_amount_cents` is the sum of `billed_fees_cents` and `expenses_cents`, less `allowed_amount_cents`.

Auditing is callable from `SUBMITTED` and from `ADJUSTED`; from any other status it is `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_AUDITABLE`. Either leg lands the invoice in `UNDER_AUDIT`, writes all seven audit output fields, and sets `audited_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Counsel may push back. A `PanelCounsel` principal — again only the assignment's own firm, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_COUNSEL` — disputes an invoice in `UNDER_AUDIT`, moving it `UNDER_AUDIT → ADJUSTED`; an invoice in any other status is `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTABLE`. The dispute carries a mandatory `dispute_note` and may supply revised `partner_hours`, `associate_hours`, `paralegal_hours`, `block_billed_hours` and `expenses_cents`, which **overwrite** the stored values rather than adding to them. The audit is then re-run against the revised inputs, and `audited_by_user_id` is cleared to null — so the re-audit that follows re-establishes the four-eyes gate against whoever performs it. The pair of transitions `UNDER_AUDIT → ADJUSTED → UNDER_AUDIT` is the revision cycle, and it may repeat.

Approval releases the money and is an adjuster action on an invoice in `UNDER_AUDIT`; any other status is `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_APPROVABLE`. Four gates then apply in this order: the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `audited_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); the claim's retention must have attached, meaning its `retention_satisfied_cents` is at or above its `applied_retention_cents`, or else the claim belongs to a related-claim group whose status is `LOCKED` (`ERR_RETENTION_NOT_SATISFIED`); the matter budget must accommodate the invoice, meaning the assignment's `budget_spent_cents` plus this invoice's `allowed_amount_cents` is at most its `budget_cap_cents`, with equality allowed (`ERR_BUDGET_CAP_EXCEEDED`); and the allowed amount must be at most the policy's `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents`, again with equality allowed (`ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`). On success the invoice becomes `APPROVED`, the assignment's `budget_spent_cents` and `budget_remaining_cents` are recomputed, and the claim's `defense_paid_cents` together with the policy's `defense_spend_cents` and `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents` are recomputed — which may advance the policy's erosion posture as described in §3.9.

Voiding an approved invoice belongs to the `ClaimsManager` and is the exact compensating action: only an `APPROVED` invoice may be voided (otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_VOIDABLE`), a mandatory `void_reason` is recorded, the status becomes `VOIDED`, and the assignment's `budget_spent_cents` and `budget_remaining_cents` along with the claim's `defense_paid_cents` and the policy's `defense_spend_cents` and `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents` are all restored by exactly `allowed_amount_cents` — possibly regressing the policy's erosion posture. The conserved quantity is the matter's defense budget.

### 3.9. Indemnity Settlement, Limit Erosion, and Closing the File

Settling a claim is a two-person operation: a `ClaimsAdjuster` drafts the payment and a `ClaimsManager` releases it. Drafting requires the owning policy to be `BOUND` or `ACTIVE` and the claim not to be coverage-gated, and carries three fields: the gross `amount_cents` of at least 1 whole cent, before any retention split; a `payment_basis` of `SETTLEMENT`, `JUDGMENT` or `PRE_SUIT_SETTLEMENT`; and the `expected_policy_version` guard of §2.7. Drafting sets `created_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, copies the claim's `coverage_bucket` into the payment's `sublimit_bucket`, creates the payment in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, and increments the owning policy's `version` by 1 — which is exactly why a client drafting a second tranche must re-read the policy first.

The **settlement waterfall** is computed and frozen at drafting, not at approval. The outstanding retention depends on the claim's basis: under `PER_CLAIM` basis it is the claim's `applied_retention_cents` less its `retention_satisfied_cents`, floored at 0; under `GROUP_AGGREGATED` basis it is the group's `shared_retention_cents` less its `retention_paid_cents`, again floored at 0. The `retention_portion_cents` — the part the insured bears — is the smaller of `amount_cents` and that outstanding retention, so the retention bucket must fill completely before the insurer attaches. The `insurer_portion_cents` is `amount_cents` less `retention_portion_cents`. Both figures are whole cents throughout with no rounding, because every term entering them is already integral. Note that a payment sitting in `PENDING_APPROVAL` reserves nothing: `retention_satisfied_cents` and `retention_paid_cents` count only payments in `APPROVED`.

Approval is where the limits are tested. The payment must be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, else `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_APPROVABLE`. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. If the claim belongs to a related-claim group, that group must be `OPEN`; a payment on a member of a `LOCKED` group is `ERR_GROUP_LOCKED`. The limit checks then run in this pinned order, and each comparison is strictly greater-than so that a portion exactly equal to the remaining capacity is **allowed**: when `sublimit_bucket` is `WAGE_HOUR`, an insurer portion exceeding the policy's `wage_hour_sublimit_remaining_cents` is `ERR_WAGE_HOUR_SUBLIMIT_EXHAUSTED`; when it is `THIRD_PARTY`, an insurer portion exceeding `third_party_sublimit_remaining_cents` is `ERR_THIRD_PARTY_SUBLIMIT_EXHAUSTED`; and in every bucket, an insurer portion exceeding `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents` is `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`. A `FIRST_PARTY` payment skips the sublimit stage entirely and is bounded only by the aggregate.

A successful approval records `approved_by_user_id`, moves the payment to `APPROVED`, and sets off the deepest cascade in the system — four hops, each caused by the mutation the previous hop produced. In the first hop the claim's `retention_satisfied_cents` and `indemnity_paid_cents` are recomputed, and a claim sitting in `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_OPEN` transitions to `RESOLVED`. Alongside it, if the claim is a group member, that same claim mutation recomputes the group's `retention_paid_cents` and locks the group `OPEN → LOCKED` once the shared retention is satisfied. In the second hop the claim's ledger mutation recomputes the policy's `indemnity_paid_cents`, the applicable sublimit remainder and `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents`, and advances the policy's erosion posture. In the third and fourth hops — which fire only when the second hop lands the policy on `EXHAUSTED` — defense funding is cut off across the whole policy: every defense assignment on any claim of this policy whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_FUNDING` transitions to `CLOSED` with `funding_ceased_at` stamped at the logical-clock instant, and then each of those assignment mutations propagates to its own billing, so every invoice on a closed assignment whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT` or `ADJUSTED` transitions to `REJECTED` with `hold_reason` set to `LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`, with `open_matter_count` recomputed for every affected firm.

The **erosion posture** moves on its own axis, driven by any approval that debits the aggregate — an indemnity payment approval with a positive insurer portion or a defense invoice approval with a positive allowed amount — and by any void that restores it. From `OPEN` the posture advances to `ERODED` when the recomputed `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents` is greater than 0 but less than `aggregate_limit_cents`, and straight to `EXHAUSTED` when a single approval drives the remainder to exactly 0 — in which case the third and fourth cascade hops fire from that transition just as they do from `ERODED → EXHAUSTED`. From `ERODED` the posture advances to `EXHAUSTED` when the recomputed remainder reaches exactly 0. Voids run the axis backwards: `EXHAUSTED → ERODED` when a void restores the remainder to something greater than 0 but still less than the full aggregate limit, `ERODED → OPEN` when the last remaining debit is voided and the remainder returns to the full aggregate limit, and `EXHAUSTED → OPEN` when voiding the single debit that exhausted the policy restores the whole limit at once. Restoring the posture does **not** resurrect what the exhaustion cascade destroyed: assignments it closed stay `CLOSED` and invoices it rejected stay `REJECTED`. Only the posture and the ledger come back.

Voiding a payment is a `ClaimsManager` action from either `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED`, and it records a mandatory `void_reason`; a payment already `VOIDED` is `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_VOIDABLE`. The two source states differ sharply in consequence, and the difference is exactly the ledger. Voiding a `PENDING_APPROVAL` payment makes no ledger changes at all, because an unapproved payment never debited anything. Voiding an `APPROVED` payment restores precisely what it took: the claim's `retention_satisfied_cents` by exactly `retention_portion_cents` and its `indemnity_paid_cents` by exactly `insurer_portion_cents`; the group's `retention_paid_cents` by exactly `retention_portion_cents` when the claim is a group member, possibly unlocking the group `LOCKED → OPEN`; and the policy's `indemnity_paid_cents`, the applicable sublimit remainder and `aggregate_limit_remaining_cents` by exactly `insurer_portion_cents`, possibly regressing the posture. One thing is deliberately not undone: a claim that reached `RESOLVED` on the strength of that payment is **not** rolled back out of `RESOLVED`. The conserved quantity here is the policy aggregate limit, not the claim's lifecycle.

A claim reaches `RESOLVED` from either `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_OPEN`, and two events can take it there: the first indemnity payment on the claim reaching `APPROVED`, or a pre-suit resolution on the claim reaching `ACCEPTED`. Closing the file is an adjuster action available from `COVERAGE_DENIED` and from `RESOLVED`; from any other status it is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_CLOSABLE`. Closing transitions every defense assignment on the claim whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_FUNDING` to `CLOSED` and recomputes `open_matter_count` for each affected firm — a denied claim will normally have none, since counsel can only be engaged after acceptance.

Reopening is the bounded revision loop and belongs to the `ClaimsManager`. It takes a claim from `CLOSED` back to `UNDER_REVIEW`; a claim in any other status is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_REOPENABLE`. It requires the owning policy to be `BOUND` or `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`. And it is permitted at most once per claim: `reopen_count` starts at 0 and increases by 1 on each successful reopen, so a claim whose `reopen_count` has already reached 1 is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_REOPEN_LIMIT` (HTTP 409) and `CLOSED` becomes genuinely terminal for it.
