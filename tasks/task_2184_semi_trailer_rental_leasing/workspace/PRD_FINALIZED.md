# Semi-Trailer Rental & Leasing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A national semi-trailer lessor lives or dies by two numbers: how many roadworthy units are standing in the right yard on the right day, and how much of the money it invoiced it actually collects. The business runs a mixed book — short-term rentals that turn over in days, and full-service leases that run for years and end with the lessee holding a purchase option — against one shared physical fleet of dry vans, reefers, flatbeds and chassis. Every unit in that fleet carries a federal periodic inspection clock, a preventive-maintenance mileage clock, a depreciation clock, and an accumulating record of damage found at the gate. Any one of those clocks expiring can pull a trailer out from underneath a contract that was already reserved.

This document specifies the API that runs that book of business end to end. It governs the reference layer (yards, equipment classes, units, lessees, certificates of insurance and published rate cards), the contract lifecycle (draft, underwriting, insurance verification, equipment allocation, checkout, return, settlement and lease-end purchase), the shop lifecycle (preventive maintenance, DOT periodic inspection, damage repair), the damage-recovery lifecycle (assessment, chargeback, dispute, adjudication and cash application), and the repositioning lifecycle (relocation orders that move idle units between yards under yard-capacity constraints). The API is mounted under `/fleet`.

Two design pressures shape almost every rule below. The first is that fleet position must be conserved: a trailer is in exactly one yard, on the road, or out with a customer, and no operation may leak or duplicate a unit across those buckets. The second is that money must be reproducible: the quoted price of an agreement, the final settled price, the split of damage liability between carrier and lessee, and the cost of a repositioning move are all computed by pinned algorithms with pinned evaluation orders, so that two readings of the same record on two different days yield the same number.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines system-wide rules, contracts and conventions that apply across every endpoint of the API.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. Five roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `FleetAdmin`, `BranchManager`, `Inspector`, `ChargebackApprover`, and `Customer`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint (see the endpoint table in section 4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

`FleetAdmin` is corporate fleet operations: it owns the reference data, pricing publication, settlement and cash application. When `X-User-Role` is `FleetAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as `inspector_user_id` on a shop event the admin files) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. `Inspector` is the shop and gate inspector who records maintenance and federal inspections and authors damage assessments, and is structurally barred from adjudicating the resulting money. When `X-User-Role` is `Inspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `assessor_user_id` on any damage assessment the inspector opens and as `inspector_user_id` on any shop event the inspector files, but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. `ChargebackApprover` is the damage-recovery desk that adjudicates chargebacks. When `X-User-Role` is `ChargebackApprover`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `approver_user_id` on any chargeback it approves or waives, and compares it only against the `assessor_user_id` recorded on the assessment behind that chargeback — never against any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `BranchManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Branch`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Branch` compare that resource's branch-bearing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `BranchManager` may act on an agreement only when `X-User-ID` equals that agreement's `branch_id`; may accept a unit back only when `X-User-ID` equals the `return_branch_id` supplied in the return request; and may accept an inbound repositioning move only when `X-User-ID` equals that order's `destination_branch_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's customer-bearing field to `X-User-ID`, so a `Customer` may act only on agreements whose `customer_id` equals `X-User-ID` and on chargebacks whose `customer_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A mutation by an entity-backed caller against a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

One further access rule cuts across the damage-recovery desk. Because the same human being may hold both an inspector badge and an approver badge, the API enforces segregation of duties on identifier equality: an approver whose `X-User-ID` equals the `assessor_user_id` of the assessment behind a chargeback may neither approve nor waive that chargeback, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0, with a minimum accepted value of 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` reflects the number of records matching the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records matching those filters remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every date comparison this API performs — federal inspection expiry, certificate of insurance expiry, months of depreciation elapsed, and the age decay applied to chargebacks during a renewal risk review — reads a server-held logical clock rather than the host's wall clock. The server starts with that logical clock fixed at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to real time. A `FleetAdmin`-only test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant — normalised to UTC, with a naive instant read as already being UTC — and answers with the resulting `current_utc`, rendered in the same ISO 8601 UTC timestamp format with three fractional digits that section 2.5 prescribes, so a supplied `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` is answered as `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z` rather than echoed verbatim. Moving the clock does not eagerly rewrite stored rows: trailer service status and net book value are recomputed on the next read of any trailer, and a certificate's status is re-evaluated lazily on the next read of that certificate. Wherever this document says "the clock date", it means the calendar date of that logical clock in UTC.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every primary resource in this API — yards, equipment classes, trailers, lessees, certificates of insurance, rate cards, agreements, contract unit lines, damage assessments, chargebacks and relocation orders — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) recording when the record was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record.

Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation for these purposes: when a return stamps new mileage onto a trailer, when an allocation flips a trailer's availability, or when a settlement writes a final total onto an agreement, the affected record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances.

Shop events are the one exception. A `MaintenanceEvent` is an immutable ledger row: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely.

### 2.6. Money, Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this API are integer counts of cents, and every field carrying money is named with a `_cents` suffix. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value or an API response. Where a calculation multiplies an integer amount by a fractional factor — a severity multiplier, a peak uplift, a fuel surcharge percentage, an administrative fee percentage, a depreciation rate, or a lease credit rate — the intermediate result is rounded to a whole number of cents using the round-half-up convention, meaning a value exactly halfway between two whole cents rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Rounding happens only at the steps this document names explicitly; no other rounding step is introduced. Where a step is described as taking the floor of a quotient, the result is truncated downward to the next whole number, and where a step is described as taking the ceiling, it is raised upward to the next whole number.

Risk scores and damage points are integer-valued and are never rounded — they are built from integer additions, from floored quotients, and from integer multipliers.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Four write operations in this API modify a child record in a way that depends on the parent's current shape, and in each case the parent's `version` is the client's guard against acting on a view it has not observed. Each of these four requests carries a non-optional version field naming the parent it targets; if the supplied value does not equal that parent's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. Endpoints not named in this subsection accept no version field at all.

- Manually attaching a trailer to an agreement requires `expected_agreement_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Agreement`. Attaching a second trailer to the same agreement while its allocated count is still below its demanded count is entirely legitimate product behaviour, so no state check can catch a caller whose picture of the agreement is out of date; only the version comparison can, and a mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412.
- Running the server-side allocation selector against an agreement requires `expected_agreement_version` in the request body, likewise carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Agreement`. The selector's outcome depends on how many units are already attached and on which trailers are still idle, so a caller holding a stale agreement view would silently request a different fill quantity than intended; a mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412.
- Publishing a rate card requires `expected_branch_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Branch`. Publishing a card legitimately supersedes the prior active card for the same yard, equipment class and product line, so without this guard a later publish would silently overwrite pricing the caller never saw; a mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412.
- Opening a damage assessment against a returned contract unit line requires `expected_agreement_unit_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AgreementUnit`. A re-inspection that supersedes an earlier assessment on the same unit is legitimate product behaviour, so no state check rejects the second author; the version comparison is what tells the second inspector that their view is stale, and a mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412.

### 2.8. Nested Path Resolution

Several endpoints address a resource through a path that names more than one identifier — a yard and its availability projection, an agreement and its unit lines, a contract unit line and its damage assessments. For every such request the server resolves the path segments strictly left to right and rejects on the first identifier that does not resolve, with the 404 code belonging to that segment's resource type: `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAILER_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DAMAGE_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RELOCATION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_MAINTENANCE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`. A request naming a damage assessment under a non-existent contract unit line therefore reports the missing unit line, not the missing assessment.

Path variables are named after the resource they address: `branch_id`, `trailer_type_id`, `trailer_id`, `customer_id`, `certificate_id`, `rate_card_id`, `agreement_id`, `agreement_unit_id`, `maintenance_event_id`, `damage_assessment_id`, `chargeback_id` and `relocation_order_id`.

The same discipline applies to foreign keys supplied in a request body rather than in a path. Every identifier in a payload — the `trailer_type_id`, `home_branch_id`, `branch_id`, `customer_id`, `trailer_id`, `destination_branch_id` or `supersedes_assessment_id` a caller supplies — must resolve to an existing record, and an unresolvable one is rejected with that resource type's 404 code before any business rule runs. An unresolvable `supersedes_assessment_id` reports `ERR_DAMAGE_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. Order of Evaluation for Rejections

When a single request would fail more than one rule, the reported error is deterministic: checks run in the fixed precedence below, and the first check that fails is the one reported. Later checks are not evaluated.

1. Header authentication. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, and the role one of the five declared names, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation. The caller's role listed for the endpoint, or `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Path existence, resolved left to right, or the corresponding 404 code from section 2.8. This applies to every endpoint that takes a path parameter.
4. Foreign-key existence for identifiers supplied in the request body, or the corresponding 404 code. This applies to every endpoint whose payload carries a foreign key.
5. Payload shape. Required fields present, enum values members of their declared sets, numeric fields inside their declared bounds, `line_items` non-empty, and pagination within a `limit` of 1 through 100 and an `offset` of at least 0, or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This applies to every endpoint.
6. Date ordering, or `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. This guards seven operations: opening an agreement, registering a yard, filing a certificate of insurance, requesting a relocation, activating an agreement, accepting a unit return, and completing a relocation.
7. Natural-key uniqueness, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_BRANCH_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAILER_TYPE_CODE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER`. This guards yard registration, equipment-class registration and trailer registration respectively.
8. Ownership for entity-backed callers, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This guards every `Customer`-role and every `BranchManager`-role mutation.
9. Segregation of duties, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This guards exactly two operations: chargeback approval and chargeback waiver.
10. Optimistic concurrency on the parent's `version`, or `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. This guards exactly the four write operations named in section 2.7: manual unit attach, allocation, rate-card publication and damage-assessment creation.
11. The target record's from-state for the transition being requested, or the state code belonging to that transition (the full list appears in section 5, and each is named in section 3 beside the workflow it guards).
12. Domain rules, in this relative order: the named trailer being in service (`ERR_TRAILER_OUT_OF_SERVICE`); the named trailer's federal inspection outlasting the contract (`ERR_DOT_INSPECTION_EXPIRED`); a qualifying certificate of insurance existing (`ERR_INSURANCE_INVALID`); that certificate carrying a sufficient limit (`ERR_INSURANCE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`); the trailer's occupancy window being free (`ERR_TRAILER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`); the destination yard having inbound room (`ERR_BRANCH_YARD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); the trailer standing at the expected yard (`ERR_TRAILER_BRANCH_MISMATCH`); the trailer's availability being acceptable for the operation (`ERR_TRAILER_NOT_AVAILABLE`); the candidate pool being adequate (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TRAILER` or `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABILITY`); the lessee's outstanding balance being within its limit (`ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`); and the lessee's underwriting score being within tolerance (`ERR_RISK_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`).

Two orderings inside step 12 deserve emphasis because they are observable. The occupancy-window check runs strictly before the availability check, so a manual attach that names a trailer already booked over an overlapping window reports `ERR_TRAILER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` rather than `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_AVAILABLE`. And the in-service check runs before the federal-inspection check, so a trailer that is simultaneously out of service and short on inspection coverage reports `ERR_TRAILER_OUT_OF_SERVICE`.

The in-service check itself guards three operations: manual unit attach, relocation request, and relocation dispatch. The availability check guards five: manual unit attach, agreement activation, relocation dispatch, trailer retirement, and exercise of a lease-end purchase option — with the nuance, spelled out in section 3, that attach accepts two availability values while the other four each demand exactly one.

### 2.10. Fleet Position, Yard Counters and Cascades

Two live counters hang off every yard and are never supplied by a caller. `on_hand_count` is the number of trailers whose current yard is this yard and whose availability is either `AVAILABLE` or `ALLOCATED`. `serviceable_available_count` is the number of trailers whose current yard is this yard, whose availability is `AVAILABLE`, and whose service status is `SERVICEABLE`. Both are recomputed whenever any trailer's yard, availability or service status changes, and both are recomputed at *both* affected yards when a movement changes a trailer's yard.

The system maintains a fleet-position identity that must hold before a dispatch, while a unit is on the road, and after an abort: the sum of `on_hand_count` across all yards, plus the number of trailers whose availability is `IN_TRANSIT`, plus the number of trailers whose availability is `ON_RENT` or `ON_LEASE`, equals the number of trailers whose availability is anything other than `RETIRED` or `SOLD`. A dispatch debits exactly one unit from the origin yard's on-hand count and credits exactly one to the in-transit bucket; an abort reverses both movements exactly, restoring the identity with no drift.

A parallel identity governs the money owed by a lessee. `outstanding_chargeback_cents` on a lessee always equals the sum of `amount_cents` over that lessee's chargebacks whose status is `PENDING`, `DISPUTED` or `APPROVED`. Submitting a damage assessment adds exactly the created chargeback's `amount_cents` to that balance; waiving that chargeback subtracts exactly the same amount and additionally rolls the trailer's damage points back by the assessment's own point total. After a forward action and its compensating action the balance returns to its pre-submission value with zero drift.

Because a trailer sits underneath contracts, moves and shop events all at once, several operations propagate several hops deep. The server performs those propagations itself, without requiring an explicit client request on each affected record, and each affected record independently refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`. Section 3 states each cascade beside the operation that triggers it; the two deepest are worth naming here in outline. A failed federal inspection filed against a trailer forces its service status out of `SERVICEABLE`, which swaps out every reserved contract unit line standing on that trailer, which in turn reverts each affected agreement from `RESERVED` back to `PENDING_ALLOCATION`. A submitted damage assessment that pushes a trailer past its damage-point ceiling forces its service status to `OOS_DAMAGE`, which cancels every open relocation order on that trailer *and* swaps out every reserved contract unit line on it, which again reverts affected agreements to `PENDING_ALLOCATION`.

### 2.11. Interval Conventions

Two different interval conventions coexist in this API, deliberately, and confusing them produces wrong answers at the boundary.

Trailer occupancy windows are **half-open**. A contract unit line occupies its trailer over the range beginning at `planned_start_date` inclusive and ending at `planned_end_date` exclusive, and an agreement's own window likewise runs from `start_date` inclusive to `end_date` exclusive. Two such windows collide only when they genuinely overlap; a window whose right edge equals the next window's left edge does not collide, so a trailer coming off one contract on a given date may be booked onto another contract starting that same date. Billed windows follow the same convention: a unit is billed from `checkout_date` inclusive to `return_date` exclusive.

Inbound yard-capacity windows are **closed on both ends**. A relocation order occupies its destination yard over the range beginning one calendar day before its `planned_arrive_date` and ending three calendar days after its `planned_arrive_date`, with both endpoints included. Two such windows that merely touch at a single shared endpoint *do* collide. This is the opposite convention from trailer occupancy, and it is intentional: a yard needs buffer days on either side of an arrival, whereas a trailer can be handed straight from one lessee to the next.

Peak-season windows are stated as one explicit closed date range per yard, from `peak_season_start_date` through `peak_season_end_date` inclusive. A yard's peak window is a single concrete range of calendar dates, not a recurring annual pattern, so a date is inside the peak window exactly when it falls on or after `peak_season_start_date` and on or before `peak_season_end_date`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Fleet: Yards, Equipment Classes and Units

A `FleetAdmin` registers each yard before anything can stand in it. Registration supplies a short yard `code` of between 1 and 16 characters that must be unique across all yards — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BRANCH_CODE` — together with a human-readable `name` and a `region`, which must be one of `WEST`, `CENTRAL` or `EAST`. Capacity planning needs `yard_capacity`, the maximum number of trailers the yard may hold, which must be at least 1. Seasonal pricing and seasonal rationing both need the yard's single peak window, given as `peak_season_start_date` and `peak_season_end_date`, both ISO calendar dates of the form YYYY-MM-DD; the end date must be on or after the start date, and a reversed pair is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. Finally, an optional `lease_reserve_pct` records the fraction of the serviceable idle pool the yard holds back for full-service lease demand during that peak window; it defaults to 0.00 and must lie between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive. A newly registered yard begins with both of its live counters, `on_hand_count` and `serviceable_available_count`, at zero. Yards may be listed with an optional `region` filter alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

Equipment classes are registered next, and they carry the engineering and compliance parameters that every unit of that class inherits. The class `code` must be one of `DRY_VAN_53`, `REEFER_53`, `FLATBED_48` or `CHASSIS_40` and must be unique across all classes; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAILER_TYPE_CODE`. A free-text `description` summarises the specification. Two list rates, `base_daily_rate_cents` and `base_monthly_rate_cents`, are recorded as informational reference figures only — actual pricing always comes from a published rate card, and these list figures are never used in any charge computation. The `residual_floor_cents`, which must be at least 0, is a hard floor applied both to a unit's book value and to any lease-end purchase price. Compliance cadence comes from `dot_inspection_interval_days`, the number of days between required federal periodic inspections, which defaults to 365 and must be at least 1, and from `pm_interval_miles`, the hubodometer distance between preventive-maintenance services, which defaults to 30000 — that is, thirty thousand hub miles — and must be at least 1. Lastly, `required_physical_damage_limit_cents`, at least 0, is the minimum physical-damage limit an insurance certificate must carry before it can be accepted as covering this class of equipment. Equipment classes may be listed with an optional `code` filter.

Individual units are then added to the fleet. A unit is created with a fleet `unit_number` that must be unique across all trailers — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER` — a `trailer_type_id` naming its equipment class, a `home_branch_id` naming its accounting home yard, an `acquisition_cost_cents` of at least 0 that forms the basis for depreciation, an `in_service_date` that starts the depreciation clock, a `last_dot_inspection_date` recording its most recent passed federal periodic inspection, and an optional `hub_miles` opening hubodometer reading that defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. On creation the server sets `current_branch_id` to the supplied `home_branch_id`, sets `availability_status` to `AVAILABLE`, sets `last_pm_hub_miles` equal to the opening `hub_miles`, and sets `last_dot_passed` to true. It also computes `dot_expires_on`, evaluates the unit's `service_status`, and computes `net_book_value_cents`. Both counters at the home yard rise by one. The `home_branch_id` is an accounting attribute and is never changed by any repositioning move; only `current_branch_id` tracks where the unit physically stands.

Commercial availability and mechanical condition are tracked as two independent dimensions on the same unit. The `availability_status` dimension takes exactly one of `AVAILABLE`, `ALLOCATED`, `ON_RENT`, `ON_LEASE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `RETIRED` or `SOLD`, begins at `AVAILABLE`, and treats `RETIRED` and `SOLD` as terminal — a unit in either of those two states never re-enters any availability pool and never appears in a yard's `on_hand_count` again. The `service_status` dimension takes exactly one of `SERVICEABLE`, `PM_DUE`, `OOS_DAMAGE` or `OOS_DOT_EXPIRED`, begins at `SERVICEABLE`, and is never written directly by a caller; it is derived, and the derivation is spelled out in section 3.6.

Expiry of the federal periodic inspection is pure date arithmetic: `dot_expires_on` is the unit's `last_dot_inspection_date` advanced by exactly `dot_inspection_interval_days` calendar days, taken from the unit's equipment class. Book value is likewise recomputed from scratch on every read of the unit rather than stored as a running total. The server first counts whole thirty-day months elapsed since the unit entered service: it takes the number of days from `in_service_date` to the clock date and divides by 30, discarding any remainder. It then charges depreciation at ten percent of `acquisition_cost_cents` per year, prorated by those elapsed months over twelve, rounding the resulting depreciation charge half-up to whole cents. `net_book_value_cents` is then whichever is greater — the equipment class's `residual_floor_cents`, or the acquisition cost less that depreciation charge. Reading one unit therefore always returns a freshly evaluated `service_status`, a freshly summed `damage_points`, and a freshly computed `net_book_value_cents`. Units may be listed and narrowed by `trailer_type_id`, by `home_branch_id`, by `current_branch_id`, and by either status dimension using `availability_status` or `service_status`.

Retiring a unit is a `FleetAdmin` privilege and applies only to a unit standing idle: the unit's `availability_status` must be exactly `AVAILABLE`, and any other value is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Retirement moves `AVAILABLE → RETIRED`, records the supplied `reason` into `retired_reason`, and decrements both `on_hand_count` and `serviceable_available_count` at the unit's `current_branch_id`.

### 3.2. Lessees, Certificates of Insurance and Underwriting

A lessee record captures who is renting and how much exposure the lessor will tolerate. Registration supplies the lessee's `legal_name`, its `dot_number` operating authority, a `credit_tier` of `A`, `B` or `C`, and a `credit_limit_cents` of at least 0 that caps the outstanding damage-recovery balance the lessor will carry into a checkout. The lessee's `outstanding_chargeback_cents` starts at zero and is thereafter maintained by the system as described in section 2.10; it is never supplied by a caller.

Certificates of insurance are filed by either a `FleetAdmin` or the lessee itself, and a certificate names its `customer_id`, its `carrier_name` and `policy_number`, a `physical_damage_limit_cents` of at least 0 that will cap the insured share of any damage assessment, a `liability_limit_cents` of at least 0 that is recorded for contract terms only and enters no calculation, and the coverage span given as `effective_date` and `expiry_date`. Coverage is inclusive at both ends, and the expiry date must be on or after the effective date; a reversed pair is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. A filed certificate begins in status `ACTIVE`.

A certificate's status takes one of `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`. The move from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` is not driven by any request: it is evaluated lazily, on every read of the certificate and on every reference to it during insurance verification, and it fires as soon as the clock date has passed the certificate's `expiry_date`. Reading a certificate list evaluates that flip row by row. An expired certificate no longer qualifies any agreement to move forward out of insurance verification. Revocation, by contrast, is an explicit `FleetAdmin` action taken when the carrier cancels the policy: it moves `ACTIVE → REVOKED` and records the supplied `reason` into `revoked_reason`. Revocation demands that the certificate be in status `ACTIVE`; a certificate in any other status — including one that has already lapsed to `EXPIRED` — is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Revoking a certificate does not disturb agreements that have already pinned it; those keep their pinned `insurance_certificate_id`, and only future verifications see the change. Certificates may be listed and narrowed by `customer_id` and by `status`.

Underwriting turns a lessee's damage-recovery history into a single integer risk score, and the same scoring model is read under two different variants that differ only in which records enter the sums and whether an age discount applies. Both variants start from a base determined by the lessee's `credit_tier`: 10 points for tier `A`, 25 points for tier `B`, and 45 points for tier `C`. Both then add two points for every full one hundred dollars — that is, every full 10000 cents — of chargeback exposure, computed by dividing the qualifying chargeback total in cents by 10000, discarding the remainder, and multiplying the whole-number result by 2. Both then add three points for each qualifying damage assessment. Both finally clamp the result: the reported score is never below 0 and never above 100.

The **underwriting snapshot** variant is the one that gates a contract. It is deliberately blind to unadjudicated exposure: its chargeback total sums `amount_cents` over only those of the lessee's chargebacks whose status is `APPROVED` or `SETTLED`, and its assessment count counts only damage assessments in status `APPROVED` that are reachable from that lessee's contract unit lines. No age discount is applied. The **renewal review** variant is an advisory number for the renewal desk and is never persisted anywhere. Its chargeback total is drawn from a wider status set — `PENDING`, `DISPUTED`, `APPROVED` and `SETTLED` — and each such chargeback is weighted before summing: a chargeback whose age in days, measured from the calendar date of its `created_at` to the clock date, is strictly greater than 180 days contributes half its `amount_cents` with the remainder discarded, while a chargeback 180 days old or newer contributes its full `amount_cents`. Its assessment count counts damage assessments in status `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`. Because it sees unadjudicated exposure that the snapshot variant ignores, for identical data with an outstanding unadjudicated chargeback the renewal review returns a strictly higher number than the underwriting snapshot. The renewal review is exposed as a computed read available to `FleetAdmin` and `ChargebackApprover`, returning the subject's `customer_id`, the `variant` it evaluated, the resulting `risk_score`, and the working figures behind it: `base_points` from the tier, `chargeback_weight_points` contributed by the exposure, `assessment_points` contributed by the assessment count, the `weighted_chargeback_cents` total after the age discount, and the lessee's live `outstanding_chargeback_cents`.

### 3.3. Published Pricing and the Peak Uplift

Pricing is published, not negotiated per contract. A `FleetAdmin` publishes a rate card that governs exactly one combination of yard, equipment class and product line: the card names its `branch_id`, its `trailer_type_id`, and a `product` that is either `SHORT_TERM_RENTAL` or `FULL_SERVICE_LEASE`. The card carries a `daily_rate_cents` of at least 0, which is the off-peak per-unit daily price; a `monthly_rate_cents` of at least 0, which is the published monthly equivalent recorded for contract terms only and which enters no charge computation; a `peak_uplift_pct` between 0.00 and 2.00 inclusive, which is the multiplicative uplift applied to the daily rate on calendar days that fall inside the yard's peak window; a `mileage_included_per_month` of at least 0, which is the hub-mile allowance granted per thirty-day block and prorated by days out; an `excess_mileage_rate_cents_per_mile` of at least 0, charged for each hub mile beyond that allowance; and an `effective_date` recording when the card was published. The `effective_date` is informational: card selection is driven purely by status, never by date comparison.

A card's `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`, and a newly published card begins `ACTIVE`. Publishing a card for a yard, equipment class and product line that already has an `ACTIVE` card moves that prior card from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, so at most one card is ever `ACTIVE` for a given combination. Because publication overwrites what the lessor is currently charging, publication is one of the four version-guarded writes: the request must carry `expected_branch_version` matching the target yard's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful publication increments the yard's `version` by exactly 1. Cards may be read individually or listed with optional `branch_id`, `trailer_type_id`, `product` and `status` filters.

Wherever this document refers to a **peak daily rate**, it means the card's `daily_rate_cents` multiplied by the quantity one plus its `peak_uplift_pct`. A card with a `peak_uplift_pct` of 0.25 therefore prices a peak calendar day at one hundred twenty-five percent of its off-peak daily rate.

### 3.4. Opening a Contract: Draft, Underwriting and Insurance

A contract begins as a draft opened by a `FleetAdmin` or by the lessee itself. Opening supplies the lessee's `customer_id`, the originating `branch_id` — which is also the yard whose peak window and whose rate card will price the whole contract — a `product` of either `SHORT_TERM_RENTAL` or `FULL_SERVICE_LEASE`, the `trailer_type_id` of the equipment class demanded, a `unit_count` of at least 1, and the contract window as `start_date` and `end_date`. The window is half-open: `start_date` is inclusive, `end_date` is exclusive, and the end date must be strictly after the start date — an end date equal to or before the start date is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. A `term_months` value states the contract term in months; it is required when the product is `FULL_SERVICE_LEASE` and must be absent or null when the product is `SHORT_TERM_RENTAL`. A newly opened contract begins in status `DRAFT`, with `purchase_option_status` at `NOT_OFFERED` and `allocated_unit_count` at zero.

A contract's `status` traverses `DRAFT`, `PENDING_INSURANCE`, `PENDING_ALLOCATION`, `RESERVED`, `ACTIVE`, `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` and then either `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, the last two being terminal. Contracts may be listed and narrowed by `customer_id`, `branch_id`, `trailer_type_id`, by the pinned `rate_card_id`, by the pinned `insurance_certificate_id`, and by `status`.

Submitting a draft for underwriting is available to a `FleetAdmin` or to the owning lessee and advances the contract `DRAFT → PENDING_INSURANCE`. Three conditions gate it, and a contract in any status other than `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. A full-service lease must carry a `term_months` that is present and at least 1, and a lease submitted without one is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Finally, the lessee's risk score under the underwriting snapshot variant must be no greater than 85; a score of 86 or above is rejected with `ERR_RISK_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`, while a score of exactly 85 passes. A successful submission freezes that score into `underwriting_risk_score` on the contract — the number is a snapshot and is never re-evaluated afterwards — and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

Insurance verification is performed by a `FleetAdmin` or by the originating yard's `BranchManager` and advances the contract `PENDING_INSURANCE → PENDING_ALLOCATION`. A contract in any other status is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING_INSURANCE`. The server looks for a certificate belonging to the contract's lessee whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `effective_date` is on or before the contract's `start_date`, and whose `expiry_date` is on or after the contract's `end_date` — coverage must span the entire contract window. If no such certificate exists, verification is rejected with `ERR_INSURANCE_INVALID`. If one does exist but its `physical_damage_limit_cents` is below the `required_physical_damage_limit_cents` declared on the contract's equipment class, verification is rejected with `ERR_INSURANCE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`; a limit exactly equal to the requirement is sufficient. When more than one certificate qualifies, the server pins the one with the greatest `expiry_date`, breaking a tie by taking the earlier `created_at`. That certificate's identifier is written to the contract's `insurance_certificate_id` and stays pinned for the life of the contract, and the contract's `version` increments by 1.

Cancellation is open to a `FleetAdmin`, to the originating yard's `BranchManager`, or to the owning lessee, and it accepts a `reason` that is recorded into `cancel_reason`. It is permitted only while the contract is in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_INSURANCE`, `PENDING_ALLOCATION` or `RESERVED`; a contract already in `ACTIVE`, `PENDING_SETTLEMENT`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_TERMINAL`. Cancelling from `DRAFT` or from `PENDING_INSURANCE` touches no equipment, because none is committed yet. Cancelling from `PENDING_ALLOCATION` or from `RESERVED` releases the equipment: every contract unit line still in status `ALLOCATED` moves `ALLOCATED → RELEASED`, and each released line's trailer returns `ALLOCATED → AVAILABLE` — but only when no other contract unit line on that same trailer remains in status `ALLOCATED` or `OUT`; if another live booking still stands on the unit, the availability dimension is left where it is. The affected yards' counters are recomputed either way. In all four cases the contract lands in `CANCELLED` and its `version` increments by 1.

### 3.5. Committing Equipment: Allocation, Manual Attach and the Peak Lease Reserve

Equipment reaches a contract as **contract unit lines**, one line per physical trailer. A line records the `agreement_id` of the owning contract and the `trailer_id` of the unit committed to it, and it copies `planned_start_date` and `planned_end_date` from the contract's own `start_date` and `end_date` at the moment it is attached — these two dates form the half-open occupancy interval the trailer is booked over. Each line carries a `status` of `ALLOCATED`, `OUT`, `RETURNED`, `SETTLED`, `SWAPPED_OUT` or `RELEASED`, begins at `ALLOCATED`, and treats `SETTLED`, `SWAPPED_OUT` and `RELEASED` as terminal. Throughout, a contract's `allocated_unit_count` is the number of its lines whose status is `ALLOCATED`, `OUT`, `RETURNED` or `SETTLED`; lines that were swapped out or released do not count toward the demand and never did.

There are two ways to commit equipment, and both are available to a `FleetAdmin` or to the originating yard's `BranchManager`, and both demand that the contract be in `PENDING_ALLOCATION` — any other status is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING_ALLOCATION`.

The first way is a manual attach of one named trailer, supplying `trailer_id` alongside the `expected_agreement_version` guard described in section 2.7. Six domain conditions gate the attach, and the trailer must satisfy all of them. Its `availability_status` must be `AVAILABLE` or `ALLOCATED`, and any other value — `ON_RENT`, `ON_LEASE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `RETIRED` or `SOLD` — is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_AVAILABLE`; the acceptance of an already-`ALLOCATED` unit is deliberate, because forward-booking a unit for a later, non-overlapping window is normal commercial practice. Its `service_status` must be `SERVICEABLE`, and anything else is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Its `dot_expires_on` must fall strictly after the contract's `end_date`, so that the federal inspection outlasts the contract; an expiry on or before that date is rejected with `ERR_DOT_INSPECTION_EXPIRED`. Its `current_branch_id` must equal the contract's `branch_id`, because a yard cannot hire out a unit standing somewhere else, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_BRANCH_MISMATCH`. No existing contract unit line on that same trailer whose status is `ALLOCATED` or `OUT` may have an occupancy interval that overlaps the contract's own window; an overlap is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and per section 2.11 two windows that merely touch at a shared date do not overlap. And the contract must still have room: if `allocated_unit_count` has already reached `unit_count`, the attach is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABILITY`.

A successful attach creates one contract unit line in status `ALLOCATED`. If the trailer was `AVAILABLE` its availability moves `AVAILABLE → ALLOCATED`; if it was already `ALLOCATED` behind an earlier non-overlapping booking, the availability dimension is left exactly as it is. The yard's counters are recomputed in either case, and the contract's `version` increments by 1. The server then evaluates a bubble-up at the end of the attach: when `allocated_unit_count` has reached `unit_count`, the contract advances `PENDING_ALLOCATION → RESERVED`, which requires that an `ACTIVE` rate card exist for the contract's yard, equipment class and product line — if none exists the reservation is rejected with `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND` — and which pins that card's identifier into `rate_card_id` and writes the forward-looking quote into `quoted_total_cents`.

The second way is to let the server's selector fill every remaining unit in a single call, supplying only the `expected_agreement_version` guard. The selector first computes the remaining demand as `unit_count` less `allocated_unit_count`. It then builds an eligibility set of trailers, admitting a unit only when its `current_branch_id` equals the contract's `branch_id`, its `trailer_type_id` equals the contract's `trailer_type_id`, its `availability_status` is exactly `AVAILABLE` — the automatic selector takes only idle units, and forward-booking an already-`ALLOCATED` unit is reachable solely through the manual attach path — its `service_status` is `SERVICEABLE`, its `dot_expires_on` falls strictly after the contract's `end_date`, and no contract unit line on it in status `ALLOCATED` or `OUT` has an occupancy interval overlapping the contract's half-open window.

The eligible units are then ranked deterministically, with no random input whatsoever: ascending `hub_miles` first, so the least-utilised unit goes out first; then descending `dot_expires_on`, so the unit with the most inspection runway ahead of it is preferred; and finally ascending `created_at` as the tie-break. The record `id` is never used as a tie-break.

A peak-season lease reserve is then applied to that ranked list, but only when the contract's `product` is `SHORT_TERM_RENTAL` and only when the contract's half-open window from `start_date` to `end_date` intersects the yard's closed peak window from `peak_season_start_date` through `peak_season_end_date`. In that case the server multiplies the length of the ranked list by the yard's `lease_reserve_pct` and rounds the product upward to a whole number of units — so any non-zero reserve percentage holds back at least one unit — and then removes that many units *from the top of the ranking*, leaving the remainder as the candidate pool. Holding back the top-ranked units, not the bottom-ranked ones, is deliberate: the best equipment is saved for full-service lease demand during the season. When the product is `FULL_SERVICE_LEASE`, or when the contract window does not intersect the yard's peak window, the candidate pool is the whole ranked list.

The selector then either fills or refuses. An empty pool is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TRAILER`. A pool that is non-empty but smaller than the remaining demand is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABILITY`. Otherwise the server takes the first units of the pool in rank order, exactly as many as the remaining demand, and creates one contract unit line in status `ALLOCATED` for each, copying the contract's window into each line's `planned_start_date` and `planned_end_date`. Every selected trailer moves `AVAILABLE → ALLOCATED`, which leaves each yard's `on_hand_count` unchanged and lowers its `serviceable_available_count` by one per unit taken. Allocation also requires an `ACTIVE` rate card for the contract's yard, equipment class and product line, failing with `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND` when there is none. The contract advances `PENDING_ALLOCATION → RESERVED`, pins `rate_card_id`, writes `quoted_total_cents`, and increments its `version` by 1.

The forward-looking quote is computed once, at the moment the contract first reaches `RESERVED`, and it reads nothing but the contract itself and its pinned card — it never looks at contract unit lines. The server counts `term_days` as the number of calendar days from `start_date` inclusive to `end_date` exclusive. It counts the peak days in that same half-open span as the number of those calendar days that fall on or after the originating yard's `peak_season_start_date` and on or before its `peak_season_end_date`; peak days are always measured against the contract's own yard, never against any other. The off-peak day count is `term_days` less that peak count. A per-unit charge is then formed by multiplying the pinned card's `daily_rate_cents` by the off-peak day count, multiplying the peak daily rate by the peak day count, adding the two, and rounding that sum half-up to whole cents. `quoted_total_cents` is that per-unit charge multiplied by the contract's `unit_count`. The quote is deliberately optimistic in three fixed ways: it assumes zero excess mileage, zero drop charge, and that every one of the `unit_count` units runs the full planned window.

Once pinned, the card and the quote are permanent. If a reserved contract loses a unit — because that unit's trailer left service, as described in section 3.7 — the contract falls back `RESERVED → PENDING_ALLOCATION`, and when it is refilled and returns to `RESERVED` a second time, `rate_card_id` and `quoted_total_cents` keep the values they already hold and are not re-resolved or recomputed.

A yard's forward availability picture is exposed as a computed read for `FleetAdmin` and `BranchManager` callers, which reports how the idle pool at that yard would split for a given equipment class and product line over a given window. The request optionally narrows by `trailer_type_id` and by `product`, and optionally supplies the window as `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`; when those two window dates are omitted the yard's own peak window is used, which makes the window intersect the peak season by construction and causes the reserve to be reported. The response echoes `branch_id` and `trailer_type_id` and reports five counts. `on_hand_count` is every trailer standing at the yard whose availability is `AVAILABLE` or `ALLOCATED`. `serviceable_available_count` is every trailer standing at the yard of the requested equipment class whose availability is `AVAILABLE` and whose service status is `SERVICEABLE`. `lease_reserved_count` is that serviceable idle count multiplied by the yard's `lease_reserve_pct` and rounded upward to a whole unit when the requested product is `SHORT_TERM_RENTAL` and the requested window intersects the yard's peak window, and is zero in every other case. `rental_available_count` is `serviceable_available_count` less `lease_reserved_count`. And `inbound_committed_count` is the number of relocation orders bound for this yard whose status is `APPROVED` or `IN_TRANSIT`.

### 3.6. Checkout, Return, Settlement and the Lease-End Purchase Option

Checking a reserved contract out of the yard is done by a `FleetAdmin` or by the originating yard's `BranchManager` and advances the contract `RESERVED → ACTIVE`. The request supplies a single `checkout_date`. A contract in any status other than `RESERVED` is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_RESERVED`. Every one of the contract's `ALLOCATED` lines must sit on a trailer whose `availability_status` is exactly `ALLOCATED`; if any of them sits on a unit that is already out behind another contract's forward booking, checkout is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The lessee's `outstanding_chargeback_cents` must be no greater than its `credit_limit_cents`, and a balance strictly above the limit is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) — a balance exactly equal to the limit passes. And the supplied `checkout_date` must fall on or after the contract's `start_date` and strictly before its `end_date`, with anything outside that half-open span rejected as `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.

On a successful checkout, every contract unit line moves `ALLOCATED → OUT`, stamping the supplied `checkout_date` onto the line and capturing `checkout_hub_miles` as a snapshot of that trailer's `hub_miles` at that instant. Both fields are null on a line that has not yet been checked out, just as `return_branch_id`, `return_date` and `return_hub_miles` are null until the line is accepted back. Each trailer's availability then moves `ALLOCATED → ON_RENT` when the contract's product is `SHORT_TERM_RENTAL`, or `ALLOCATED → ON_LEASE` when it is `FULL_SERVICE_LEASE`, and each such move lowers `on_hand_count` by one at that trailer's `current_branch_id`. The contract's `version` increments by 1.

Accepting a unit back is done line by line, by a `FleetAdmin` or by the `BranchManager` of the yard receiving it, and moves the line `OUT → RETURNED`. The request supplies `return_branch_id` — which may perfectly well be a different yard from the one the unit left — together with `return_hub_miles` and `return_date`. A line whose status is not `OUT` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_OUT`. The `return_date` must be strictly after the line's `checkout_date`, so that at least one day is billed, and a same-day or earlier return date is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The `return_hub_miles` must be at least the line's `checkout_hub_miles`, since a hubodometer never runs backwards, and a lower reading is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A successful return stamps `return_branch_id`, `return_date` and `return_hub_miles` onto the line, then pushes those facts down onto the physical unit: the trailer's `hub_miles` is set to the reported `return_hub_miles`, its `current_branch_id` is set to the reported `return_branch_id`, and its availability moves `ON_RENT → AVAILABLE` or `ON_LEASE → AVAILABLE`. Because the unit may have changed yards, `on_hand_count` is recomputed at both the yard it left and the yard that received it. The trailer's `service_status` is then re-derived, and this is the ordinary way a unit becomes due for service: when the new `hub_miles` exceeds `last_pm_hub_miles` by at least the equipment class's `pm_interval_miles`, the unit lands in `PM_DUE`. The line's `version` increments by 1. Finally the server evaluates a bubble-up: when the contract has no line left in status `OUT` and has at least one line in status `RETURNED`, the contract advances `ACTIVE → PENDING_SETTLEMENT` and its `version` increments by 1. Contract unit lines may be read individually or listed with optional `agreement_id`, `trailer_id`, `return_branch_id` and `status` filters.

Settlement is a `FleetAdmin` privilege and closes the contract, advancing it `PENDING_SETTLEMENT → CLOSED`; a contract in any other status is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SETTLEABLE`. Settlement is where the actual, chronological price is computed, and it differs from the forward-looking quote in what it reads and in the order it rounds. It reads only lines whose status is `RETURNED` at the moment of settlement; lines that were swapped out or released are excluded and contribute nothing at all. It reads the same pinned rate card the contract has carried since it first reserved, and it measures peak days against the contract's own originating yard, never against the yard a unit happened to be dropped at.

The server walks the returned lines in ascending `created_at` order and prices each one. Days out is the number of calendar days from the line's `checkout_date` inclusive to its `return_date` exclusive, which is always at least 1. The line's `peak_days` is the number of those calendar days falling on or after the originating yard's `peak_season_start_date` and on or before its `peak_season_end_date`; off-peak days are the remainder. The base charge for the line is the pinned card's `daily_rate_cents` multiplied by the off-peak day count, plus the peak daily rate multiplied by `peak_days`. The mileage allowance for the line is the card's `mileage_included_per_month` multiplied by the days out and divided by 30, with any fractional part discarded — a thirty-day proration block. The line's `excess_miles` is the distance actually run, namely `return_hub_miles` less `checkout_hub_miles`, less that allowance, floored at zero so a unit that stays inside its allowance contributes no excess. The mileage charge is `excess_miles` multiplied by the card's `excess_mileage_rate_cents_per_mile`. The line's `drop_charge_cents` is 35000 cents — a flat three-hundred-fifty-dollar one-way drop fee — whenever the line's `return_branch_id` differs from the contract's own `branch_id`, and 0 when the unit came home to the yard that hired it out. The line's `line_charge_cents` is the base charge plus the mileage charge plus the drop charge, rounded half-up to whole cents.

The order of rounding here is pinned and is not interchangeable: each line is rounded to whole cents first, and `final_total_cents` is then the plain sum of those already-rounded line charges. Summing the unrounded lines and rounding once at the end produces a different and incorrect number. Settlement writes `peak_days`, `excess_miles`, `drop_charge_cents` and `line_charge_cents` onto every returned line, writes `final_total_cents` onto the contract, moves every one of those lines `RETURNED → SETTLED`, and increments the contract's `version` by 1. The final total diverges from the quote whenever a unit came back early or late, crossed a different number of peak days than planned, ran beyond its mileage inclusion, or was dropped at a foreign yard.

Settlement is also the only moment a lease-end purchase option can be offered. The `purchase_option_status` dimension on a contract takes one of `NOT_OFFERED`, `OFFERED`, `EXERCISED` or `DECLINED`, starts at `NOT_OFFERED`, and treats `EXERCISED` and `DECLINED` as terminal. During settlement the server tests three conditions together: the contract's `product` must be `FULL_SERVICE_LEASE`, its `unit_count` must be exactly 1, and the number of calendar days from its `start_date` to its `end_date` must be at least 720. A span of exactly 720 days qualifies; 719 does not. When all three hold, `purchase_option_status` moves `NOT_OFFERED → OFFERED` and `purchase_option_price_cents` is computed and written. When any of the three fails, `purchase_option_status` remains `NOT_OFFERED` and `purchase_option_price_cents` remains null.

The option price looks circular — the lease credit is capped as a share of the price, and the price is the book value less the credit — so its evaluation order is pinned, and the cap is keyed on the pre-credit base price. Step one takes the cumulative amount paid as the `final_total_cents` just written by this same settlement. Step two computes the uncapped credit as twelve percent of that cumulative amount, rounded half-up to whole cents. Step three computes the base price as whichever is greater, the unit's current `net_book_value_cents` or its equipment class's `residual_floor_cents`. Step four computes the credit cap as thirty percent of that base price with any fractional cent discarded. Step five takes the applied credit as the smaller of the uncapped credit and the cap. Step six sets `purchase_option_price_cents` to whichever is greater, the base price less the applied credit, or the equipment class's `residual_floor_cents`. Computing the cap from the post-credit price, or applying the credit before the residual floor, yields a different and incorrect number.

The lessee, and only the lessee, acts on the offer. Exercising it moves `purchase_option_status` `OFFERED → EXERCISED`; a contract whose option status is anything other than `OFFERED` is rejected with `ERR_PURCHASE_OPTION_NOT_OFFERED`, and the single contract unit line's trailer must have `availability_status` exactly `AVAILABLE`, with anything else rejected as `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_AVAILABLE`. On exercise the trailer moves `AVAILABLE → SOLD`, which removes it from `on_hand_count` and `serviceable_available_count` at its `current_branch_id` and from every availability pool permanently, and the contract's `version` increments by 1. Declining the offer moves `purchase_option_status` `OFFERED → DECLINED` under the same `ERR_PURCHASE_OPTION_NOT_OFFERED` guard; the trailer is untouched by a decline and simply stays `AVAILABLE` in the rental fleet, and the contract's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.7. Roadworthiness: Shop Events and the Service-Status Cascade

Shop work is recorded as an immutable event by a `FleetAdmin` or an `Inspector`. Each event names the `trailer_id` it was performed on, an `event_type` of `PM_SERVICE`, `DOT_INSPECTION` or `DAMAGE_REPAIR`, the `performed_on` date the work was done, a `hub_miles_at_event` reading of at least 0 taken by the shop, and a lessor-borne `cost_cents` of at least 0. A `passed` boolean records the outcome and is required when the `event_type` is `DOT_INSPECTION`; it is ignored for the other two event types. The server stamps the caller's `X-User-ID` onto the event as `inspector_user_id`. As noted in section 2.5, a shop event is a ledger row: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and has no `updated_at` and no `version`. Events may be read individually or listed with optional `trailer_id` and `event_type` filters.

A unit's `service_status` is never written by a caller. It is derived from the unit's own record, its equipment class and the clock date, and it is re-derived at every point where one of those inputs could have moved: at trailer creation, whenever a shop event is filed, whenever a contract unit line comes back from the road, whenever a damage assessment is submitted, whenever a chargeback is waived, and on every read of the trailer.

The derivation first sums the unit's live damage points. A damage assessment contributes its `damage_points` to that sum when it stands on this trailer, when its `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, and when either the unit has never had a damage repair recorded — its `last_damage_repair_at` is null — or the assessment's `created_at` is strictly later than that `last_damage_repair_at`. The `last_damage_repair_at` timestamp therefore acts as a watermark: repairing a unit does not delete its assessments, it simply drops every assessment created at or before the repair out of the live sum.

The four service states are then evaluated first-match-wins, in a pinned precedence, and once a clause matches the later clauses are not evaluated at all:

1. `OOS_DOT_EXPIRED`, when the unit's `last_dot_passed` is false, or when the clock date has reached or passed the unit's `dot_expires_on`. The federal-inspection boundary is inclusive: on the exact `dot_expires_on` date the unit is already expired.
2. `OOS_DAMAGE`, when the live damage-point sum is at least 25. The damage boundary is inclusive as well: exactly 25 points puts the unit out of service, and 24 points does not.
3. `PM_DUE`, when the unit's `hub_miles` exceeds its `last_pm_hub_miles` by at least the equipment class's `pm_interval_miles`.
4. `SERVICEABLE`, when none of the above matched.

The precedence matters in both directions. A unit that is both damaged past the ceiling and past its inspection date reports `OOS_DOT_EXPIRED`, not `OOS_DAMAGE`. A unit that is both damaged past the ceiling and over its maintenance mileage reports `OOS_DAMAGE`, not `PM_DUE`. Only `SERVICEABLE` clears a unit for a manual attach, for the automatic selector, for a relocation request, and for a relocation dispatch; `PM_DUE` is a soft condition in the sense that it does not disturb existing commitments, but it is still not `SERVICEABLE`, and a unit sitting in it is rejected from those operations with `ERR_TRAILER_OUT_OF_SERVICE`.

Each of the three shop event types touches the derivation inputs differently. A `PM_SERVICE` event sets the unit's `last_pm_hub_miles` to the event's `hub_miles_at_event`, which is what clears a `PM_DUE` unit back to `SERVICEABLE` — provided the unit is not simultaneously blocked by an earlier clause, meaning its `last_dot_passed` is true, the clock date is still before its `dot_expires_on`, and its live damage points are below 25. A `DOT_INSPECTION` event with `passed` true sets `last_dot_inspection_date` to the event's `performed_on` date, recomputes `dot_expires_on` from that new date, and sets `last_dot_passed` to true; that is what restores an `OOS_DOT_EXPIRED` unit to `SERVICEABLE`, again provided its live damage points are below 25 and its `hub_miles` has not run at least `pm_interval_miles` past its `last_pm_hub_miles`. A `DAMAGE_REPAIR` event sets the unit's `last_damage_repair_at` to that event's own `created_at`, sweeping every earlier assessment out of the live damage-point sum; when the resulting sum falls below 25 and neither an inspection problem nor a maintenance-mileage problem stands in the way, the unit moves `OOS_DAMAGE → SERVICEABLE`. Every shop event ends by re-deriving the unit's `service_status` and recomputing the counters at the unit's yard.

A `DOT_INSPECTION` event with `passed` false is the head of the deepest cascade in the fleet. It sets `last_dot_passed` to false, which forces the unit `SERVICEABLE → OOS_DOT_EXPIRED`. Every contract unit line standing on that unit in status `ALLOCATED` is then swapped out, moving `ALLOCATED → SWAPPED_OUT`. For each such swap the trailer's availability returns `ALLOCATED → AVAILABLE`, but only when no other contract unit line on that same trailer remains in status `ALLOCATED` or `OUT`; when another live booking still stands on the unit, the availability dimension is left unchanged. The yard's counters are recomputed either way. And for each swap, when the owning contract is in `RESERVED` and its `allocated_unit_count` has now fallen below its `unit_count`, that contract falls back `RESERVED → PENDING_ALLOCATION` — with, as section 3.5 states, its `rate_card_id` and `quoted_total_cents` left pinned at the values they already hold. Contract unit lines that are already `OUT` are not swapped out; a unit already with a customer stays on its contract until it is returned.

The same swap-out machinery fires when a unit is driven out of service by damage rather than by inspection, and that path is described in section 3.8 because it starts at the gate.

### 3.8. Damage at the Gate: Assessment, Liability Split and Chargeback Adjudication

When a unit comes back marked up, an `Inspector` — and only an `Inspector` — opens a damage assessment against the contract unit line that brought it home. The assessment is created with a `line_items` array carrying at least one element; an empty array is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each element describes one item of damage with a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `MAJOR`, a `quantity` of at least 1, a `unit_cost_cents` of at least 0, and a free-text `description`. A re-inspection additionally supplies `supersedes_assessment_id`, naming the earlier assessment on the same unit that this one replaces; a first assessment leaves it null. Creation also carries the `expected_agreement_unit_version` guard from section 2.7. The new assessment is written in status `DRAFT`, with `trailer_id` copied from the assessed line, with `agreement_unit_id` recording the line it stands on, and with `assessor_user_id` stamped from the authoring inspector's `X-User-ID`; the assessed line's own `version` increments by 1. Assessments may be read individually or listed with optional `agreement_unit_id`, `trailer_id`, `supersedes_assessment_id` and `status` filters.

An assessment's `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED`, with the last three terminal. Submitting one — again an `Inspector` action — moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is where all the money is decided. Three conditions gate submission. The assessment must be in `DRAFT`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. The contract unit line being assessed must have a `status` of `RETURNED` or `SETTLED`, since a unit still on the road cannot be inspected at the gate, and any other line status is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_RETURNED`. And when `supersedes_assessment_id` is set, the assessment it names must itself be in status `SUBMITTED`; naming an assessment in any other status is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_SUPERSEDED`.

Pricing the damage is a waterfall. First the gross value: for each line item the server multiplies the item's `quantity` by its `unit_cost_cents` by the severity multiplier — 1.00 for `MINOR`, 1.25 for `MODERATE`, 1.60 for `MAJOR` — and rounds that per-item product half-up to whole cents; `assessed_amount_cents` is the sum of those rounded per-item figures. In parallel the server accumulates `damage_points` as the sum, over the same line items, of the severity's point weight multiplied by the item's `quantity`, where a `MINOR` item is worth 3 points each, a `MODERATE` item 8 points each, and a `MAJOR` item 15 points each.

Next the split between carrier and lessee. The deductible is fixed by the lessee's `credit_tier`: 100000 cents for tier `A`, 150000 cents for tier `B`, and 250000 cents for tier `C` — that is, one thousand, fifteen hundred and twenty-five hundred dollars respectively. The applicable limit is the `physical_damage_limit_cents` of the certificate pinned in the owning contract's `insurance_certificate_id`, but only when such a certificate exists and its `status` is `ACTIVE` at the moment of submission; when the contract pinned no certificate, or when the pinned certificate is no longer `ACTIVE`, the applicable limit is 0 and the lessee carries the entire assessed amount. The `insured_portion_cents` is then the assessed amount less the deductible, floored at zero so a small claim inside the deductible yields nothing from the carrier, and then capped at the applicable limit. In other words: the lessee absorbs the deductible first, the carrier pays the next slice up to its limit, and anything above the limit falls back on the lessee. The `customer_liable_cents` is the assessed amount less the insured portion, and `admin_fee_cents` is eight percent of that customer-liable figure, rounded half-up to whole cents.

Submission then raises a **chargeback** in status `PENDING` whose `amount_cents` is the `customer_liable_cents` plus the `admin_fee_cents`, frozen at creation and never recomputed thereafter. The chargeback records the `damage_assessment_id` that produced it and the `customer_id` copied from the assessed line's contract. Its creation raises the lessee's `outstanding_chargeback_cents` by exactly that amount.

Submission also propagates into the fleet. The trailer's live damage-point sum is re-derived and rises by this assessment's `damage_points`, and its `service_status` is re-derived from that new sum. If the sum has reached 25 points the unit moves `SERVICEABLE → OOS_DAMAGE`, and two cascades fire from that single move. Every relocation order standing on that trailer whose status is `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` is cancelled, with `cancel_reason` recorded as `TRAILER_OUT_OF_SERVICE`; an order cancelled out of `APPROVED` additionally releases the inbound capacity window it was holding at its destination yard, while an order cancelled out of `REQUESTED` was never holding one. And every contract unit line on that trailer in status `ALLOCATED` is swapped out exactly as described in section 3.7 — moving `ALLOCATED → SWAPPED_OUT`, returning the trailer's availability `ALLOCATED → AVAILABLE` only when no other line on it remains `ALLOCATED` or `OUT`, recomputing yard counters either way, and reverting any owning contract in `RESERVED` back to `PENDING_ALLOCATION` when its allocated count falls below its demanded count.

Finally, when the submission carried a `supersedes_assessment_id`, the superseded predecessor moves `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED`, its own chargeback moves `PENDING → VOIDED` or `DISPUTED → VOIDED` depending on where it stood, the lessee's `outstanding_chargeback_cents` drops by that voided chargeback's `amount_cents`, and the trailer's live damage-point sum is re-derived so that the predecessor's points drop out of it. The assessment's `version` increments by 1.

Adjudication of the resulting chargeback belongs to the damage-recovery desk. A chargeback's `status` is one of `PENDING`, `DISPUTED`, `APPROVED`, `WAIVED`, `SETTLED` or `VOIDED`, beginning at `PENDING`, with `WAIVED`, `SETTLED` and `VOIDED` terminal. Chargebacks may be read individually or listed with optional `damage_assessment_id`, `customer_id` and `status` filters.

The lessee may dispute a chargeback, moving it `PENDING → DISPUTED` and recording the supplied `dispute_reason`. A chargeback in any status other than `PENDING` is rejected with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_PENDING`, and a lessee whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the chargeback's `customer_id` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Disputing changes no money: the amount stays counted in `outstanding_chargeback_cents`. The chargeback's `version` increments by 1. A `ChargebackApprover` may push a disputed item back into the queue, moving it `DISPUTED → PENDING` and clearing `dispute_reason`, after which it is approvable or waivable again; a chargeback whose status is not `DISPUTED` is rejected with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_DISPUTED`, and the `version` increments by 1. This `PENDING → DISPUTED → PENDING` cycle may repeat.

Approving a chargeback is a `ChargebackApprover` action that moves it `PENDING → APPROVED`; a chargeback in any other status is rejected with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_PENDING`, and an approver whose `X-User-ID` equals the `assessor_user_id` of the assessment behind it is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval records `approver_user_id` — which is null for as long as a chargeback sits in `PENDING` or `DISPUTED`, and is written only by an approval or a waiver — moves the linked assessment `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, leaves the amount counted in `outstanding_chargeback_cents`, leaves the assessment's damage points counted against the trailer, and increments the chargeback's `version` by 1.

Waiving is the compensating action and reverses everything the submission did to the money and to the fleet. A `ChargebackApprover` may waive from either `PENDING` or `DISPUTED`; a chargeback already in `APPROVED`, `WAIVED`, `SETTLED` or `VOIDED` is rejected with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_TERMINAL`, and the same segregation-of-duties comparison applies, rejecting with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the approver's `X-User-ID` equals the assessment's `assessor_user_id`. A waiver records `approver_user_id` and the supplied `waiver_reason`, moves the linked assessment `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, drops the lessee's `outstanding_chargeback_cents` by exactly the chargeback's `amount_cents`, and re-derives the trailer's live damage points and `service_status` — so a unit taken out of service by that assessment moves `OOS_DAMAGE → SERVICEABLE` once the remaining points fall below 25 and neither an inspection problem nor a maintenance-mileage problem stands in the way. The chargeback's `version` increments by 1.

Cash application closes the loop. A `FleetAdmin` records payment against an approved chargeback, moving it `APPROVED → SETTLED`; a chargeback in any other status is rejected with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_APPROVED`. Payment must be in full: the supplied `paid_amount_cents` must equal the chargeback's `amount_cents` exactly, and any other figure — short or over — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Settlement records `paid_amount_cents` — null on any chargeback that has not been paid — drops the lessee's `outstanding_chargeback_cents` by the chargeback's `amount_cents`, and increments its `version` by 1.

### 3.9. Repositioning the Fleet: Moves, Yard Capacity and Move Cost

Demand never sits where the equipment does, so idle units are repositioned between yards under a relocation order. A `FleetAdmin` or a `BranchManager` requests one by naming the `trailer_id` to move, the `destination_branch_id` receiving it, the `planned_depart_date` the move should leave, the `planned_arrive_date` it should land, and the `planned_miles` of loaded distance, which must be at least 0. The planned arrival must be on or after the planned departure, and a reversed pair is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The named unit must be in service — a unit whose `service_status` is anything other than `SERVICEABLE` is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_OUT_OF_SERVICE` — and the destination must actually be somewhere else: naming the yard the unit already stands in as the destination is rejected with `ERR_TRAILER_BRANCH_MISMATCH`. A new order is written in status `REQUESTED`, with `origin_branch_id` snapshotted from the trailer's `current_branch_id` at request time, and with `estimated_cost_cents` priced from the plan. Its `actual_miles`, `deadhead_miles`, `actual_arrive_date` and `actual_cost_cents` are all null until the move completes, and its `reject_reason` and `cancel_reason` are null unless the corresponding outcome occurs. Orders may be read individually or listed with optional `trailer_id`, `origin_branch_id`, `destination_branch_id` and `status` filters.

An order's `status` runs `REQUESTED`, then either `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`; an approved order may go `IN_TRANSIT`, and an in-transit order ends either `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. `REJECTED`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

The receiving yard decides whether to take the unit. Approval moves the order `REQUESTED → APPROVED`; an order in any other status is rejected with `ERR_RELOCATION_NOT_REQUESTED`, and a `BranchManager` caller whose `X-User-ID` is not the order's `destination_branch_id` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — only the yard being asked to absorb the unit may accept it. Approval is subject to a yard-capacity test. Each order occupies an inbound window at its destination running from one calendar day before its `planned_arrive_date` through three calendar days after it, with both endpoints included; as section 2.11 states, two such windows that merely touch at a single shared date do collide. The server counts the other orders bound for the same destination whose status is `APPROVED` or `IN_TRANSIT` and whose inbound window intersects the window of the order being approved. If the destination yard's `on_hand_count`, plus that count of colliding committed orders, plus the one unit now being accepted, would exceed the destination's `yard_capacity`, approval is rejected with `ERR_BRANCH_YARD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful approval leaves the order holding its inbound window and increments the order's `version` by 1. Refusing the move instead records the supplied `reason` into `reject_reason` and moves the order `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, under the same `ERR_RELOCATION_NOT_REQUESTED` guard; a rejection touches no trailer and no yard counter, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

Putting the unit on the road moves the order `APPROVED → IN_TRANSIT`; an order in any other status is rejected with `ERR_RELOCATION_NOT_APPROVED`. The unit's `availability_status` must be exactly `AVAILABLE`, with anything else rejected as `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and its `service_status` must be `SERVICEABLE`, with anything else rejected as `ERR_TRAILER_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Dispatch moves the trailer `AVAILABLE → IN_TRANSIT` while deliberately leaving `current_branch_id` at the origin — the unit is still booked to the yard that owns it until it lands — and lowers `on_hand_count` at `origin_branch_id` by one. The order's `version` increments by 1.

Landing the unit moves the order `IN_TRANSIT → COMPLETED`; an order in any other status is rejected with `ERR_RELOCATION_NOT_IN_TRANSIT`. Completion supplies the `actual_miles` actually run loaded, the `deadhead_miles` run empty to reposition, and the `actual_arrive_date`, which must be on or after the order's `planned_depart_date`, with an earlier date rejected as `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. Completion writes those three values, prices the actual move into `actual_cost_cents`, sets the trailer's `current_branch_id` to the `destination_branch_id` and its availability `IN_TRANSIT → AVAILABLE`, and raises `on_hand_count` at the destination yard by one. The order's `version` increments by 1.

Aborting a move already on the road is the compensating action for a dispatch. It moves the order `IN_TRANSIT → CANCELLED` under the same `ERR_RELOCATION_NOT_IN_TRANSIT` guard, records the supplied `reason` into `cancel_reason`, and leaves `actual_cost_cents` null — an aborted move is never priced. The trailer returns `IN_TRANSIT → AVAILABLE` with its `current_branch_id` still at `origin_branch_id`, and `on_hand_count` at the origin yard rises by one, exactly reversing the debit the dispatch took. The order's `version` increments by 1. The fleet-position identity of section 2.10 therefore holds before the dispatch, while the unit is in transit, and after the abort.

Move cost is priced by the same tiered model under two variants, differing only in which mileage figures feed it. The mileage charge is a marginal waterfall, not an average rate: the first 250 miles are charged at 260 cents per mile; miles beyond 250 and up to 600 are charged at 215 cents per mile; and miles beyond 600 are charged at 185 cents per mile. A subtotal is formed by adding a flat base dispatch fee of 15000 cents to that mileage charge and then adding the deadhead distance multiplied by 130 cents per mile. A fuel surcharge of seven percent of that subtotal, rounded half-up to whole cents, is added when — and only when — the loaded miles plus the deadhead miles is strictly greater than 500; the threshold is strict, so exactly 500 combined miles attracts no surcharge and 501 does. The cost is finally floored: it is whichever is greater, 40000 cents or the subtotal plus the surcharge.

The **estimated planned** variant runs at order creation and writes `estimated_cost_cents`. It prices `planned_miles` as the loaded distance and treats the deadhead distance as 0, and the number it writes is never revised afterwards. The **actual incurred** variant runs when the order completes and writes `actual_cost_cents`. It prices `actual_miles` as the loaded distance and `deadhead_miles` as the deadhead distance. Because deadhead counts toward the surcharge threshold as well as toward the subtotal, an actual move can cross the 500-mile surcharge threshold even when its loaded miles alone would not, so the actual cost can exceed the estimate by more than the loaded-mileage difference alone would suggest.
