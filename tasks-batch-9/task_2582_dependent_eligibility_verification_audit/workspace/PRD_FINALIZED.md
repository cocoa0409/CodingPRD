# Dependent Eligibility Verification Audit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Self-insured employers routinely discover that between four and eight percent of the dependents enrolled on their health plan are no longer eligible for it: an ex-spouse whose divorce was never reported, an adult child who aged out two plan years ago, a domestic partner whose relationship ended. Each ineligible dependent quietly consumes plan dollars every month, and by the time the discrepancy surfaces the plan has already paid claims that it had no obligation to pay. A **Dependent Eligibility Verification Audit** is the structured, defensible program that closes that gap — it selects a population, tells employees exactly what documentation proves each relationship, offers a penalty-free amnesty window for voluntary removals, tracks responses through a disciplined reminder cadence, escalates silence to a termination of coverage with proper notice and continuation rights, hears appeals for the genuinely hard cases such as domestic partnerships and contested custody, and finally recovers claims paid on dependents who should never have been covered.

This document specifies the API that runs such an audit end to end. The system is deliberately built as an auditable compliance record rather than a convenience tool: every state change is attributable to a role, every deadline is derived from a recorded announcement instant rather than from wall-clock guesswork, every monetary figure is reproducible from stored inputs, and every reversal — a granted appeal that reinstates coverage — compensates each downstream consequence it created instead of leaving orphaned notices and demands behind. Because an audit wave spans months of calendar time but must be testable in seconds, the service does not read the host machine's clock. It reads a **logical clock** that callers advance explicitly, and all deferred behaviour (amnesty expiry, response deadlines, notice finalization, point-of-enrollment grace lapses, continuation-election expiry) is realised by an idempotent sweep checkpoint rather than by background timers.

Four operational personas share the system, and their separation is a design requirement rather than a convenience: the benefits administrator who builds and governs the program, the analyst who reads documents, the independent reviewer who hears appeals, and the recovery agent who pursues paid claims. Alongside them sits the employee under audit, who sees only their own household. The rules below are written for the engineer who must implement all of it, and they favour explicitness over brevity wherever a number, an order of evaluation, or a boundary condition could otherwise be guessed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere in the API: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, resource identity, concurrency, arithmetic precision, and the order in which the service decides to reject a request. Domain workflows are specified in §3.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `VerificationAnalyst`, `AppealReviewer`, `RecoveryAgent`, and `Employee`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the benefits program administrator. They build the program, decide its scope, publish the communication plan, run the deferred-action sweep, measure savings, and close the program out. A `VerificationAnalyst` reads submitted proof documents, decides whether each one is acceptable, requests redaction of documents carrying sensitive identifiers, and drives the reminder cadence on unresponsive cases. An `AppealReviewer` is the independent adjudicator of documentation exceptions; they take an appeal only when segregation of duties permits it and may decide only the appeals assigned to them personally. A `RecoveryAgent` pursues claims already paid on behalf of ineligible dependents: they open recoveries, issue priced demands, and record payments. An `Employee` is the enrolled subscriber under audit.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example, `reviewed_by_user_id` on a proof submission and `reviewer_user_id` on an appeal are populated from the acting `X-User-ID` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `VerificationAnalyst`, `AppealReviewer`, and `RecoveryAgent`: each of these three roles supplies an opaque operator identifier, persisted where a workflow records who acted (a `VerificationAnalyst` identifier lands in `reviewed_by_user_id`, an `AppealReviewer` identifier lands in `reviewer_user_id`), and never resolved against an entity primary key.

When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee` record. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employee` compare that resource's employee reference to `X-User-ID`. Ownership resolves transitively: a dependent belongs to the employee named by its `employee_id`, an audit case belongs to the employee named by its `employee_id`, and a proof submission, an amnesty removal, an appeal, or a termination notice belongs to whichever employee owns the dependent or case it points at. An `Employee` caller who addresses a resource that does not resolve to their own employee record is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards a specific set of operations: reading one employee, listing an employee's dependents, reading one dependent, listing a dependent's amnesty removals, reading one termination notice, enrolling a dependent under an employee, submitting a proof document, uploading a redacted replacement document, recording an amnesty removal, filing an appeal, resubmitting a remanded appeal, and recording a continuation-coverage election. Operators in the four non-employee roles are not subject to it.

Two further identity rules are enforced deep inside the appeal workflow and are stated here because they are access-control rules rather than business rules. First, **segregation of duties**: the `X-User-ID` that appears as `reviewed_by_user_id` on any proof submission belonging to a dependent may never take that dependent's appeal; an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Second, **reviewer exclusivity**: once an appeal has been taken, only the `AppealReviewer` whose identifier is stored in `reviewer_user_id` may decide it, and any other user attempting a decision is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_REVIEWER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a workflow in §3 explicitly pins a different processing order for an internal computation. The four pagination fields reported in `meta` are `limit` (the effective page size applied), `offset` (the effective skip applied), `total_count` (the number of records matching the request's filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses omit all four.

Where a collection endpoint also accepts domain filters — for example a `status` filter on programs, cases, appeals, notices, or recoveries; a `coverage_status` filter on dependent listings; a `plan_year` filter on programs; `phase` and `relationship_type` filters on documentation rules; `program_id` and `employee_id` filters on cases; `kind` on communications; `case_id`, `dependent_id`, `review_status`, and `privacy_status` on proof submissions; `origin` on termination notices; and `termination_notice_id` and `recovery_variant` on claim recoveries — those filters are applied first and `total_count` reflects the filtered population.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`. It is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and it is never accepted from a request body. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — the same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation that changes the resource — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. `AuditProgram`, `Employee`, `Dependent`, `DocumentationRule`, `AuditCase`, `ProofSubmission`, `Appeal`, `TerminationNotice`, and `ClaimRecovery` all carry the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

Three record types are append-only historical evidence and therefore deliberately carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — for `AmnestyRemoval`, `PaidClaimLine`, and `CommunicationEvent`, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the resource entirely. These three are never mutated after creation; a correction is made by recording a new fact, not by editing an old one.

Time itself is explicit. The service maintains a single deterministic **logical clock** that begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` on a freshly started process, and every timestamp field, every window boundary comparison, and every effective-date computation in this specification reads that clock rather than the host machine's time of day. An operator in any of the four non-employee roles may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc` and no entity is mutated by the call. Because deadlines and grace periods are all derived from stored instants, moving the clock forward and then running the sweep is the only way scheduled consequences ever materialise.

### 2.5. Left-to-Right Path Resolution and Reverse-Lookup Reads

Entities in this system are all top-level collections joined by explicit reference fields; no business identity is carried in URL nesting. Sub-resource read paths exist purely as reverse-reachability conveniences — listing an employee's dependents walks `Dependent.employee_id`, listing a dependent's paid claims walks `PaidClaimLine.dependent_id`, listing a program's documentation rules walks `DocumentationRule.program_id`, listing a program's point-of-enrollment dependents walks `Dependent.poe_program_id`, listing a case's communications walks `CommunicationEvent.case_id`, and listing amnesty removals walks either `AmnestyRemoval.dependent_id` or `AmnestyRemoval.case_id` depending on the path used.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A missing program yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, a missing employee `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing dependent `ERR_DEPENDENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing case `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing proof submission `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, a missing appeal `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, a missing termination notice `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing claim recovery `ERR_RECOVERY_NOT_FOUND`. All eight carry HTTP 404. The identical family of errors is raised when a reference supplied inside a request body — rather than in the path — cannot be resolved: submitting proof against a nonexistent case, filing an amnesty removal or appeal against a nonexistent dependent, opening a recovery against a nonexistent termination notice, or enrolling a dependent under a nonexistent employee all produce the corresponding 404.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Program and Dependent Writes

Two write paths in this API can legitimately destroy work that another writer created, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent entity's `version`.

The first is the **scope run**. A request to decide or re-decide a program's audit scope must carry `expected_program_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the `AuditProgram` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters because a re-scope legitimately supersedes the previous case snapshot: it voids the prior `AuditCase` rows and returns their dependents to `ENROLLED`. A client whose view of the program predates an earlier scope run — or any other program mutation — would therefore discard case state it never read. `POST /audit-programs/{id}/scope` is the only endpoint that takes `expected_program_version`.

The second is **claim recovery creation**. A request to open a `ClaimRecovery` must carry `expected_dependent_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `Dependent` as the client last observed it. If it does not equal the dependent's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEPENDENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the destructive act is supersession: a new recovery row supersedes that dependent's prior `OPEN` row, or its prior `DEMAND_ISSUED` row that has collected nothing yet. A client operating on a stale view of the dependent would therefore replace a recalculated demand it never saw. `POST /claim-recoveries` is the only endpoint that takes `expected_dependent_version`.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind. In particular, proof decisions, appeal decisions, reminders, continuation elections, demand issuance, and payments carry no expected-version input, and a version field supplied to them is not part of their contract.

### 2.7. Monetary Precision and the Standard Cost Schedule

All money in this API is expressed as whole integer cents. Fields ending in `_cents` never hold a fractional value, and no field anywhere in the system stores a currency amount as a decimal.

Wherever a percentage or a load factor must be applied to an integer cent amount, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a computed value of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero, upward. The number of rounding steps and the point in the computation at which each one occurs are pinned individually by each calculation in §3; implementations must not consolidate or reorder them, because the pinned order and the arithmetically tempting alternative differ by a cent on real inputs. `admin_fee_pct` is the one field that stores a rate rather than an amount: it holds a two-decimal proportion (for example, five percent is stored as 0.05), and it is a computed output, never a request input.

Savings arithmetic is a different regime: it is a sum of exact integer products of a monthly cost and a whole number of months, and it involves **no rounding at all**. Counter fields — every field on `AuditProgram` and `AuditCase` whose name ends in `_count`, plus `sweep_epoch`, `batch_sequence_no`, `sequence_no`, `round_number`, `recovery_revision`, and `communication_sequence_no` — are plain integers.

The plan's per-dependent employer cost is not negotiated per person; it is a published schedule keyed by relationship class, and each dependent's `monthly_cost_cents` is taken from it at creation. A `SPOUSE` and a `DOMESTIC_PARTNER` each cost 49,713 cents per month. A `CHILD_BIOLOGICAL`, a `STEPCHILD`, an `ADOPTED_CHILD`, and a `LEGAL_GUARDIANSHIP` dependent each cost 30,500 cents per month. A `DISABLED_ADULT_CHILD` costs 36,238 cents per month, reflecting the higher medical component that class carries. Every savings figure and every continuation calculation in this system is driven from that schedule rather than from anything the employer reports about its own premium. The employer's own reported figure, `employer_monthly_premium_cents`, is captured on the `Employee` record for reporting completeness and is deliberately **not** an input to any savings computation.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must report the violation that comes first in the following precedence order. This ordering is a contract, not an implementation hint: a caller diagnosing a rejection relies on the earliest failure surfacing rather than being masked by a later one.

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Role authorisation against the endpoint's permitted roles — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. Existence of every path-segment resource, resolved left to right — the matching 404 from the eight-member family in §2.5.
4. Employee ownership of the addressed resource — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403), over the twelve operations enumerated in §2.1.
5. Request body shape: required fields present, enum values drawn from the declared member sets, integers inside their declared ranges, `attestation_signed` equal to true where required, `limit` inside 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0, and `work_email` unique across all employees — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. Existence of every reference supplied in the request body, on proof submission, amnesty removal, appeal filing, claim recovery creation, and dependent enrollment — the matching 404.
7. Segregation of duties on taking an appeal — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
8. Reviewer identity on deciding an appeal — `ERR_APPEAL_REVIEWER_MISMATCH` (403).
9. Program phase admits the operation — `ERR_PROGRAM_PHASE_INVALID` (409), across all program, case, submission, amnesty, and appeal write endpoints.
10. Case status admits the operation — `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID` (409), on proof submission, amnesty removal, appeal filing, and reminders.
11. Dependent coverage status and scope membership admit the operation — `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` (409), on proof submission, amnesty removal, appeal filing, and claim recovery creation.
12. Proof submission review and privacy status admit the operation — `ERR_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID` (409), on a proof decision and on a redaction upload.
13. Appeal status admits the operation, and no second open appeal exists for the dependent — `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (409), on appeal filing, assignment, decision, and resubmission.
14. Termination notice status and continuation-election status admit the operation — `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID` (409), on claim recovery creation and on a continuation election.
15. Claim recovery status admits the operation — `ERR_RECOVERY_STATE_INVALID` (409), on demand issuance and on a payment.
16. `expected_program_version` matches the program's `version` — `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (412), on the scope run.
17. `expected_dependent_version` matches the dependent's `version` — `ERR_STALE_DEPENDENT_VERSION` (412), on claim recovery creation.
18. A sensitive proof type carries `redaction_applied` equal to true — `ERR_PRIVACY_REDACTION_REQUIRED` (422), on proof submission.
19. An accept decision is not blocked by an outstanding privacy obligation — `ERR_PRIVACY_HOLD` (422), on a proof decision.
20. The amnesty window is still open — `ERR_AMNESTY_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), on amnesty removal.
21. The response window is still open — `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), on proof submission.
22. The appeal window is still open — `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), on appeal filing.
23. The continuation election window is still open — `ERR_CONTINUATION_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), on a continuation election.
24. The reminder cadence gap has elapsed — `ERR_REMINDER_CADENCE_VIOLATION` (409), on a reminder.
25. The reminder cap has not been reached — `ERR_REMINDER_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), on a reminder.
26. No employee would be placed under two intersecting audit response windows — `ERR_CASE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), on the announcement.
27. The offered proof type is acceptable for the dependent's relationship class — `ERR_PROOF_TYPE_NOT_ACCEPTED` (422), on proof submission.
28. A recency-scoped proof document is recent enough — `ERR_PROOF_STALE_DOCUMENT` (422), on proof submission.
29. The asserted appeal grounds are supported and their required reference is present — `ERR_APPEAL_GROUNDS_UNSUPPORTED` (422), on appeal filing.
30. The appeal round cap has not been reached — `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409), on appeal resubmission and on a remand decision.
31. No amnesty removal already exists for the dependent — `ERR_AMNESTY_ALREADY_RECORDED` (409), on amnesty removal.
32. An audit-phase documentation rule exists for every relationship class the operation needs — `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_RULE_MISSING` (422), on the scope run and on proof submission.
33. The documentation rule triple is unique and its grace field is phase-appropriate — `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_RULE_CONFLICT` (409), on documentation rule creation.
34. The scope parameters match the requested scope class — `ERR_SCOPE_PARAMETERS_INVALID` (422), on the scope run.
35. Scope candidate selection produced at least one employee — `ERR_SCOPE_EMPTY` (422), on the scope run.
36. Point-of-enrollment program selection reduced to a single program — `ERR_POE_PROGRAM_AMBIGUOUS` (409), on dependent enrollment.
37. The program name is unique within its plan year — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM` (409), on program creation.
38. The waiver credit does not exceed the recoverable subtotal — `ERR_WAIVER_EXCEEDS_SUBTOTAL` (422), on demand issuance.
39. The recoverable subtotal clears the de minimis floor — `ERR_RECOVERY_BELOW_DE_MINIMIS` (422), on demand issuance.
40. The payment does not exceed the outstanding demand — `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_DEMAND` (422), on a payment.
41. No appeal remains open at program close — `ERR_OPEN_APPEALS_REMAIN` (409).
42. No claim recovery remains outstanding at program close — `ERR_OPEN_RECOVERIES_REMAIN` (409).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because dependent-state screening at rank 11 admits a dependent already in `AMNESTY_REMOVED` for an amnesty removal attempt, a second removal attempt against the same dependent falls through to rank 31 and reports `ERR_AMNESTY_ALREADY_RECORDED` rather than a generic state error. And because both optimistic-concurrency checks sit at ranks 16 and 17 — after every state screen but before every business, privacy, temporal, and fiscal rule — a request that is simultaneously stale and, say, outside its window reports the staleness.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity and Counter Recomputation

This system's hardest correctness requirement is that a single request may legitimately mutate four or five entities, and every one of those mutations must land. Whenever a workflow in §3 states that an operation propagates to a parent, a sibling, or a downstream record, that propagation is part of the operation's contract: the whole set commits or the request is rejected and nothing changes. There is no partially-applied cascade in this API.

Each entity mutated inside a cascade has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1 — the parent program bumping its `version` does not exempt a case, a dependent, a notice, or a recovery that changed in the same request from bumping theirs. Conversely, an entity that a request merely reads is left untouched.

Counters are **derived**, never incrementally patched by hand from a request's local knowledge. Every `_count` field on `AuditProgram` and `AuditCase`, along with `provisional_annual_savings_cents`, `audited_annual_savings_cents`, `recovery_ledger_cents`, `recovered_total_cents`, `refund_due_total_cents`, `accepted_proof_type_count`, `paid_claims_total_cents`, `enrolled_dependent_count`, and `priority_score`, is recomputed from the entities it summarises at the end of any request that could have changed its inputs. A read of a program must therefore return counters that agree exactly with a full recount over its cases and dependents.

Each program counter has one and only one definition, and every recomputation must reproduce it exactly. `in_scope_case_count` is the number of that program's cases whose `status` is anything other than `VOIDED`. `in_scope_dependent_count` is the total length of the `in_scope_dependent_ids` arrays of those same non-voided cases. Five counters partition the in-scope dependent population by `coverage_status`: `pending_dependent_count` counts those in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `amnesty_removed_count` those in `AMNESTY_REMOVED`, `termination_pending_count` those in `TERMINATION_PENDING`, `terminated_dependent_count` those in `TERMINATED`, and `verified_dependent_count` those in **either** `VERIFIED` **or** `REINSTATED` — a dependent restored by a granted appeal is counted as verified, not as a separate category. Two counters describe cases rather than dependents: `responded_case_count` is the number of cases whose `status` is `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETE`, or `CLOSED` **and** which hold at least one proof submission or at least one amnesty removal, while `non_responsive_case_count` is the number of cases whose `status` is `NON_RESPONSIVE` or `ESCALATED`. On the case itself, `in_scope_dependent_count` is simply the length of its own `in_scope_dependent_ids`, `proof_submission_count` is the number of proof submissions naming that case, and `reminders_sent_count` is the number of that case's communication events whose `kind` is `REMINDER`.

The program-level in-scope population that all program counters and both savings figures are computed over is defined once and used everywhere: it is the set of dependents whose `id` appears in the `in_scope_dependent_ids` of an `AuditCase` of that program whose `status` is anything other than `VOIDED`. A voided case contributes nothing to any program counter, and a dependent returned to `ENROLLED` by a re-scope contributes nothing until a later scope run pulls it back in.

### 2.10. Privacy Handling of Proof Documentation

Eligibility documentation is intrusive by nature — tax returns and bank statements expose income and account numbers that an eligibility audit has no business retaining. Four document classes are therefore designated **sensitive**: `JOINT_TAX_RETURN`, `JOINT_ACCOUNT_STATEMENT`, `DISABILITY_CERTIFICATION`, and `TAX_DEPENDENCY_PROOF`. Every other document class is standard. A submission's `privacy_class` is computed from this designation alone and is never supplied by the caller: it is `SENSITIVE` when the offered `proof_type` is one of those four, and `STANDARD` for every other proof type.

Privacy is tracked on a dimension entirely separate from review. A submission's `review_status` moves through `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACCEPTED`, and `REJECTED`; its `privacy_status` moves independently through `NOT_REQUIRED`, `REDACTED`, `REDACTION_REQUIRED`, and `PURGED`. Neither dimension's transitions imply anything about the other's, and the two must be evolved independently by the implementation.

At program close, the retention obligation is discharged: every proof submission belonging to that program's cases whose `privacy_class` is `SENSITIVE` and whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED` has its `privacy_status` set to `PURGED`. The submission row itself survives, and its `document_reference` is retained as the audit trail of what was examined — what the purge records is the destruction of the sensitive copy, not the erasure of the evidence that a document was reviewed and accepted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The nine subsections below follow an audit wave from the moment the census is loaded to the moment the program is closed and its savings are booked. Parameters, thresholds, and cascade rules are introduced where the business narrative needs them rather than being collected into schema blocks, so a complete implementation of any single operation generally draws on more than one subsection.

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program: Census, Household, and Documentation Policy

Before any audit can be scoped, a `ProgramAdmin` loads the subscriber census. Registering an employee records their `full_name`, a `work_email` that serves as the notification address and must be unique across all employees — a duplicate address is a malformed request and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the enrollment tier they hold, and the employer's own reported monthly cost for that tier in `employer_monthly_premium_cents`, which must be zero or greater. The tier is one of `EMPLOYEE_ONLY`, `EMPLOYEE_SPOUSE`, `EMPLOYEE_CHILDREN`, or `FAMILY`, stored as `coverage_tier`. The employee's `hire_date` is an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form. A newly registered employee starts with `enrolled_dependent_count` at zero; thereafter that field always equals the number of their dependents whose `coverage_status` is `ENROLLED`, recomputed whenever a dependent of theirs is added or changes status.

A dependent joins a household with a declared `relationship_type` — one of `SPOUSE`, `DOMESTIC_PARTNER`, `CHILD_BIOLOGICAL`, `STEPCHILD`, `ADOPTED_CHILD`, `DISABLED_ADULT_CHILD`, or `LEGAL_GUARDIANSHIP` — together with the dependent's `full_name` and `date_of_birth` as an ISO 8601 calendar date. The household reference `employee_id` is taken from the path the enrollment is addressed to, not from the payload. Although the enrollment payload nominally carries `monthly_cost_cents`, the stored value is always the schedule figure for the declared relationship class described in §2.7; a client-supplied figure is never persisted. The dependent's `enrolled_at` is likewise server-controlled: it is the calendar-date portion of the logical clock at the moment of creation, never accepted from the request, and it is the date that a new-dependent audit scope later filters on.

Enrollment is where ongoing **point-of-enrollment verification** attaches, and this is the design that keeps a closed audit from re-accumulating ineligible dependents. When the payload does not name a program explicitly, the service resolves `poe_program_id` by searching for programs that satisfy four conditions at once: `poe_verification_enabled` is true, `plan_year` equals the calendar year of the current logical clock, `status` is one of `AMNESTY_OPEN`, `RESPONSE_WINDOW`, or `ESCALATION`, and the program publishes a documentation rule whose `phase` is `POINT_OF_ENROLLMENT` for exactly the relationship class being enrolled. Surviving candidates are ranked by their `created_at` ascending, and where two share a `created_at` the earlier `amnesty_end_at` wins. If after applying both keys the leading two candidates are still tied on both, the enrollment is refused with `ERR_POE_PROGRAM_AMBIGUOUS` — the service will not pick arbitrarily between two governing programs. If instead the candidate pool is empty, that is not an error at all: the dependent is simply created with `coverage_status` `ENROLLED`, `poe_program_id` null, and `poe_deadline_at` null. A payload that names `poe_program_id` explicitly bypasses the selector entirely and is instead validated for that program's existence and for the presence of a matching point-of-enrollment rule.

When a program is selected or supplied, the dependent does not enroll outright. It is created directly in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` — an `ENROLLED` to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` transition realised at birth — with `poe_deadline_at` set to the current clock instant advanced by the governing rule's `grace_days` whole days, `accepted_proof_type_count` at zero, and `documentation_complete` false. The household's `enrolled_dependent_count` is recomputed either way. Two further fields on the dependent start at their base values and are described where they are used: `recovery_revision` begins at zero and `paid_claims_total_cents` begins at zero, while `coverage_end_date` and `reinstated_at` both begin null.

Documentation policy is declared per relationship class per phase. A rule names its `program_id`, its `relationship_type`, and its `phase` — `AUDIT` for rules that govern the verification audit itself, `POINT_OF_ENROLLMENT` for rules that govern new dependent adds. The triple of program, relationship class, and phase must be unique; a second rule for the same triple is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_RULE_CONFLICT`, as is a rule that carries `grace_days` for the wrong phase — the field is required and must be between 1 and 180 whole days when the phase is `POINT_OF_ENROLLMENT`, and must be null when the phase is `AUDIT`. Each rule lists its `acceptable_proof_types`, which must be non-empty and drawn from the ten recognised document classes: `MARRIAGE_CERTIFICATE`, `JOINT_TAX_RETURN`, `JOINT_ACCOUNT_STATEMENT`, `DOMESTIC_PARTNER_AFFIDAVIT`, `SHARED_RESIDENCE_PROOF`, `BIRTH_CERTIFICATE`, `ADOPTION_DECREE`, `COURT_ORDER`, `DISABILITY_CERTIFICATION`, and `TAX_DEPENDENCY_PROOF`. It sets `min_accepted_documents`, the number of distinct accepted document classes required before the dependent counts as verified, which must be between 1 and 5. `affidavit_allowed` defaults to false and, when true, means a domestic-partnership affidavit is a supported appeal ground for that relationship class. `requires_recency_days` defaults to 365 and must be between 30 and 1095 days; it caps how old a recency-scoped document may be. `auto_terminate_on_lapse` defaults to false and, on a point-of-enrollment rule, authorises the sweep to terminate a dependent whose grace period lapsed with incomplete documentation.

The program itself is created in `DRAFT` with a `program_name` between 1 and 120 characters and a `plan_year` between 2020 and 2100; the pair of plan year and program name must be unique across all programs, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM`. Four cadence parameters may be tuned at creation and each has a default: `amnesty_days` defaults to 21 and must be between 0 and 90; `response_days` defaults to 45 and must be between 7 and 180; `reminder_min_gap_days` defaults to 7 and must be between 1 and 60; `max_reminders` defaults to 3 and must be between 1 and 10. `poe_verification_enabled` defaults to true. A freshly created program has `scope_type` null, `announcement_at`, `amnesty_end_at`, `response_deadline_at`, and `last_savings_variant` all null, `sweep_epoch` at zero, and every counter and every money ledger — `in_scope_case_count`, `in_scope_dependent_count`, `pending_dependent_count`, `verified_dependent_count`, `amnesty_removed_count`, `termination_pending_count`, `terminated_dependent_count`, `responded_case_count`, `non_responsive_case_count`, `provisional_annual_savings_cents`, `audited_annual_savings_cents`, `recovery_ledger_cents`, `recovered_total_cents`, and `refund_due_total_cents` — at zero.

### 3.2. Deciding Scope and Materialising Audit Cases

The scope decision is the audit's single most consequential act, because it freezes exactly who is under audit and which of their dependents must be documented. A `ProgramAdmin` makes it by declaring a `scope_type` of `FULL_CENSUS`, `TARGETED`, or `NEW_DEPENDENT_ONLY` alongside the `expected_program_version` described in §2.6. `TARGETED` additionally requires a non-empty `targeted_employee_ids` list; `NEW_DEPENDENT_ONLY` additionally requires a `new_dependent_since_date`; `FULL_CENSUS` forbids both. A payload whose parameters do not match its declared scope class is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_PARAMETERS_INVALID`.

Candidate selection then runs over the census, and only dependents whose `coverage_status` is exactly `ENROLLED` are ever eligible to be pulled into a new case — a dependent in any other coverage status is invisible to scoping. Under `FULL_CENSUS`, every employee holding at least one enrolled dependent is a candidate. Under `TARGETED`, an employee is a candidate when their `id` appears in `targeted_employee_ids` **and** they hold at least one enrolled dependent. Under `NEW_DEPENDENT_ONLY`, an employee is a candidate when at least one of their enrolled dependents has an `enrolled_at` date on or after `new_dependent_since_date`. Candidates are then processed in a pinned order: `created_at` ascending, and where two employees share the same `created_at`, ascending `work_email` breaks the tie — never the identifier.

For each candidate in that order, one `AuditCase` is created carrying the program reference in `program_id`, the subscriber reference in `employee_id`, a `status` of `SELECTED`, a `priority_score` of zero, and an `in_scope_dependent_ids` array. That array is the frozen, authoritative in-scope set for the case: it holds the identifiers of the employee's enrolled dependents ordered by the dependents' own `created_at` ascending. Under `NEW_DEPENDENT_ONLY` the per-case set is narrowed further to only those enrolled dependents whose `enrolled_at` falls on or after `new_dependent_since_date`, so an employee can be in scope for their newly added stepchild without their spouse of fifteen years being dragged in. The case's `in_scope_dependent_count` is simply the length of that array, and `unverified_dependent_count`, `proof_submission_count`, `reminders_sent_count`, and `communication_sequence_no` all start at zero, with `notified_at`, `last_reminder_at`, `non_responsive_at`, `escalated_at`, and `completed_at` all null.

Two guards can defeat a scope run after the parameters check passes. If candidate selection produces no employee at all, the run is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_EMPTY`: no case is created and the program's `status` is left exactly as it was, whether that was `DRAFT` or `SCOPED`. And if the selected dependent set contains any relationship class for which this program has published no `AUDIT`-phase documentation rule, the run is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_RULE_MISSING` — the audit will not ask an employee for documentation it has not defined.

On success, every selected dependent moves `ENROLLED → PENDING_VERIFICATION`, each affected employee's `enrolled_dependent_count` is recomputed, the program's `scope_type` is recorded, `status` becomes `SCOPED`, and `in_scope_case_count`, `in_scope_dependent_count`, and `pending_dependent_count` are refreshed. Each newly created case has its `priority_score` computed. The program's `version` increments by 1.

A scope run is legal from `DRAFT` and legal again from `SCOPED`; from any other phase it is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_PHASE_INVALID`. Re-running it against a `SCOPED` program is the audit's one sanctioned supersede path, available only while no case has yet been notified, and it is a two-stage cascade. First, every existing case of this program still in `SELECTED` moves `SELECTED → VOIDED`, and because those cases are voided, each of their in-scope dependents moves back `PENDING_VERIFICATION → ENROLLED`. Only then is the new case set materialised exactly as described above. The program stays in `SCOPED`, all counters are recomputed over the surviving population, and `version` increments once. Voided cases are permanently inert: as §2.9 states, they contribute nothing to any program counter or savings figure ever again.

### 3.3. Announcement, the Amnesty Period, and the Reminder Cadence

Publishing the audit announcement is what starts the clock running on every deadline in the program, and it is a single bulk act rather than a per-case one. A `ProgramAdmin` supplies the `channel` the outreach goes out on — `EMAIL`, `MAIL`, or `PORTAL` — and optionally a `template_code` recorded for audit purposes. The program must be in `SCOPED` and at least one of its cases must still be in `SELECTED`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_PHASE_INVALID`.

Before anything is written, the announcement checks that it will not place any employee under two overlapping audits at once. For each case about to be notified, the service looks for another `AuditCase` held by the same employee whose `status` is `NOTIFIED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `NON_RESPONSIVE`, or `ESCALATED`. Each such case belongs to a program with a closed response-window date interval running from the calendar date of that program's `announcement_at` through the calendar date of its `response_deadline_at`. This program's projected window runs from the calendar date of the current clock through that date advanced by `response_days` whole days. If those two closed intervals intersect at all — and intervals that merely touch on a single shared boundary date **do** intersect — the announcement is refused with `ERR_CASE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The check is atomic across the whole batch: if any one case collides, no case is notified and no program field is written.

On success, `announcement_at` is set to the current clock instant, `amnesty_end_at` becomes that instant advanced by `amnesty_days` whole days, `response_deadline_at` becomes that instant advanced by `response_days` whole days, and the program moves `SCOPED → AMNESTY_OPEN`. Every case in `SELECTED` moves `SELECTED → NOTIFIED` with `notified_at` equal to the program's `announcement_at`. Because each of those cases was notified, one `CommunicationEvent` is appended per case with `kind` `ANNOUNCEMENT` and `sequence_no` 1, and each case's `communication_sequence_no` becomes 1. The program's `version` increments by 1.

Every outreach record shares one shape. It names the `case_id` it belongs to, its `kind` — one of `ANNOUNCEMENT`, `REMINDER`, `AMNESTY_CONFIRMATION`, `TERMINATION_NOTICE`, or `RESCISSION_NOTICE` — the `channel` it went out on, its `sequence_no` as a one-based per-case ordinal that always equals the case's `communication_sequence_no` immediately after insertion, the `sent_at` clock instant of dispatch, and the optional `template_code`. The `kind` is determined by the workflow that emitted the record and is never chosen by the caller.

Chasing a silent employee is deliberately rate-limited, because an audit that spams its population loses the legitimacy it needs. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `VerificationAnalyst` may send a reminder against a case, choosing a `channel` and optionally a `template_code`, but only while the case's `status` is `NOTIFIED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. Two independent limits then apply. The **cadence gap** anchors on the case's `last_reminder_at` when one exists, and otherwise on its `notified_at`, so the announcement itself anchors the first reminder and each reminder anchors the next. The elapsed time from the anchor to the current clock instant is converted to whole elapsed days by discarding any partial day — six days and twenty-three hours, fifty-nine minutes, fifty-nine seconds counts as six days, not seven — and the reminder is accepted only when that whole-day count is greater than or equal to the program's `reminder_min_gap_days`. A gap exactly equal to the configured minimum is therefore allowed; anything shorter is refused with `ERR_REMINDER_CADENCE_VIOLATION`. The **volume cap** requires the case's `reminders_sent_count` to be strictly less than the program's `max_reminders`; a case that has already received its full allowance is refused with `ERR_REMINDER_LIMIT_REACHED`.

An accepted reminder increments the case's `reminders_sent_count`, sets `last_reminder_at` to the current clock instant, increments `communication_sequence_no`, and appends one `CommunicationEvent` with `kind` `REMINDER` carrying that new sequence number. It then recomputes the case's `priority_score`.

That score is the audit's triage instrument, and it exists so that the escalation pass processes the worst offenders first. It is a weighted blend of four signals. The **unverified ratio** is the case's `unverified_dependent_count` divided by its `in_scope_dependent_count`, where the divisor is treated as 1 whenever the count is zero so the ratio is always defined; the numerator is the number of members of `in_scope_dependent_ids` whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `TERMINATION_PENDING`. That ratio is weighted at 40. A **sensitive-relationship flag** contributes 25 whenever at least one in-scope dependent has a `relationship_type` of `DOMESTIC_PARTNER`, `STEPCHILD`, or `LEGAL_GUARDIANSHIP`, and contributes nothing otherwise — those three classes are where documentation exceptions cluster. The **reminder ratio** is the case's `reminders_sent_count` divided by the program's `max_reminders`, again with the divisor treated as 1 when it would otherwise be zero, weighted at 20. A **silence flag** contributes 15 when the case's `proof_submission_count` is exactly zero, and nothing otherwise. The four weighted terms are added using real division for the two ratios, the sum is rounded half up to a single integer exactly once at the end, and only then is a ceiling of 100 applied — so a case that would score 104 before capping stores 100. The score is recomputed on every mutation of the case, and again at the very start of the escalation pass before any ordering decision is made.

Throughout the amnesty and response windows, the first response of any kind on a notified case moves it `NOTIFIED → IN_PROGRESS`, which requires the program to be in either `AMNESTY_OPEN` or `RESPONSE_WINDOW`; the case's `proof_submission_count`, `unverified_dependent_count`, and `priority_score` are recomputed, and the program's `responded_case_count` — the number of cases in `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETE`, or `CLOSED` holding at least one proof submission or amnesty removal — is refreshed. A case reaches `COMPLETE` the moment every member of its `in_scope_dependent_ids` has a `coverage_status` in `VERIFIED`, `REINSTATED`, `AMNESTY_REMOVED`, or `TERMINATED`, with `completed_at` set to the current clock instant. That completion can fire from `IN_PROGRESS` in the ordinary case, and it can also fire directly out of `NOTIFIED` when the very first response happens to resolve the case's only unresolved dependent — in which case both the `NOTIFIED → IN_PROGRESS` and the onward move to `COMPLETE` occur inside the same request, and the persisted end state is `COMPLETE`.

### 3.4. Amnesty: Voluntary Removal Without Penalty

The amnesty period is the audit's most cost-effective mechanism: an employee who already knows a dependent is ineligible can say so and walk away clean. Either the `Employee` themselves or a `ProgramAdmin` acting on their behalf records the removal by naming the `dependent_id`, the `case_id` the removal answers, a `reason_code` of `NO_LONGER_ELIGIBLE`, `DIVORCE_FINALIZED`, `AGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `DOCUMENTATION_UNAVAILABLE`, or `OTHER`, and an `attestation_signed` flag that must be exactly true — the employee is attesting that the dependent is ineligible, and a false or absent attestation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Four gates protect the removal. The program must be in `AMNESTY_OPEN`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_PHASE_INVALID`. The owning case must be in `NOTIFIED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The dependent's `coverage_status` must be either `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or already `AMNESTY_REMOVED`, and its identifier must be a member of the case's `in_scope_dependent_ids`; otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`. The already-removed status is deliberately admitted at that gate precisely so that a duplicate removal falls through to the last gate and reports the specific `ERR_AMNESTY_ALREADY_RECORDED` — at most one amnesty removal may ever exist for a given dependent. Separately, the current clock instant must be no later than the program's `amnesty_end_at`; the boundary instant itself is inside the window, and anything past it is refused with `ERR_AMNESTY_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

An accepted removal writes a permanent, immutable record. Its `recorded_at` is the current clock instant. Its `coverage_end_date` is the last calendar day of the month containing that instant — coverage runs out the end of the month, not the day of the attestation. Both `penalty_waived` and `claims_recovery_waived` are recorded as true unconditionally, which is exactly what the amnesty promise means: no penalty is assessed, and no claims recovery may ever be opened against a dependent in `AMNESTY_REMOVED`. Its `audited_savings_contribution_cents` is the dependent's `monthly_cost_cents` multiplied by twelve minus the calendar month number of the removal's `coverage_end_date` — so a removal whose coverage ends on the last day of April, month four, books eight months of saving, while one whose coverage ends in December books none.

The removal cascades. The dependent moves `PENDING_VERIFICATION → AMNESTY_REMOVED` with its own `coverage_end_date` set to the removal's. One `CommunicationEvent` with `kind` `AMNESTY_CONFIRMATION` is appended to the case. The case moves `NOTIFIED → IN_PROGRESS` if this was its first response, its `unverified_dependent_count` and `priority_score` are recomputed, and it moves onward to `COMPLETE` with `completed_at` set when this removal resolved its last unresolved in-scope dependent. At the program level, `amnesty_removed_count` and `pending_dependent_count` are recomputed, `audited_annual_savings_cents` grows by the removal's `audited_savings_contribution_cents`, and `provisional_annual_savings_cents` is recomputed as well.

### 3.5. Documentation Review and the Completeness Test

An employee answers the audit by offering documents. Either the `Employee` or a `VerificationAnalyst` transcribing a mailed packet may lodge a submission, naming the `case_id` it answers, the `dependent_id` it is about, the `proof_type` being offered, an opaque `document_reference` handle for the uploaded image, the `document_date` printed on the document as an ISO 8601 calendar date, and optionally a `redaction_applied` flag asserting that identifiers were masked before upload.

Submissions are accepted only into a live audit. The program must be in `AMNESTY_OPEN` or `RESPONSE_WINDOW`; the case must be in `NOTIFIED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; the dependent's `coverage_status` must be exactly `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and its identifier must appear in the case's `in_scope_dependent_ids`, since a submission for someone outside the frozen scope set is meaningless. Independently, the current clock instant must be no later than the program's `response_deadline_at` — the boundary instant is inside the window — and a submission after it is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Four content rules then govern what may be offered. First, privacy: a submission whose `proof_type` is one of the four sensitive classes listed in §2.10 must carry `redaction_applied` equal to true, and one that does not is refused with `ERR_PRIVACY_REDACTION_REQUIRED`. Second, policy existence: the program must publish an `AUDIT`-phase documentation rule for the dependent's `relationship_type`, and its absence is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_RULE_MISSING`. Third, acceptability: the offered `proof_type` must be a member of that rule's `acceptable_proof_types`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_PROOF_TYPE_NOT_ACCEPTED` — a birth certificate proves nothing about a marriage. Fourth, recency: exactly two document classes are recency-scoped, `JOINT_TAX_RETURN` and `JOINT_ACCOUNT_STATEMENT`, because both are evidence of an ongoing shared financial life rather than of a one-time event. For those two only, the `document_date` must fall on or after the calendar date of the program's `announcement_at` moved backwards by the governing rule's `requires_recency_days` whole days; an older document is refused with `ERR_PROOF_STALE_DOCUMENT`. The remaining eight document classes are never checked for recency at all — a birth certificate does not go stale.

A newly created submission starts with `review_status` `SUBMITTED`. Its `privacy_class` is derived from the offered proof type as described in §2.10. Its `privacy_status` starts at `REDACTED` when the proof type is sensitive and `redaction_applied` was true, and at `NOT_REQUIRED` in every other case. Its `reviewed_by_user_id`, `decision_reason_code`, `decided_at`, and `redacted_document_reference` all start null. The submission increments the case's `proof_submission_count` and, when it is the case's first response, drives the `NOTIFIED → IN_PROGRESS` move; note that it does **not** touch `communication_sequence_no`, because a submission is an inbound document rather than an outbound communication. The case's `priority_score` and the program's `responded_case_count` are recomputed.

A `VerificationAnalyst` — and only a `VerificationAnalyst` — decides a submission, choosing a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `REJECT`, or `REQUEST_REDACTION`, optionally recording a `reason_code` and a free-text `reviewer_note`. Decisions are legal only while the program is in `RESPONSE_WINDOW` or `ESCALATION`, so review naturally begins after the amnesty period has closed and continues through escalation while appeals are pending. The submission's `review_status` must be `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`; a decision against an already-terminal submission is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`. Every decision stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` with the current clock instant, and records the supplied reason in `decision_reason_code`, whose permitted values are `VERIFIED_MATCH`, `ILLEGIBLE`, `EXPIRED_DOCUMENT`, `WRONG_DEPENDENT`, and `INSUFFICIENT_PROOF`. That stored reviewer identity is what the segregation-of-duties rule in §2.1 later consults.

The three decisions diverge as follows. An **accept** moves the review dimension `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, or `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED` for a submission that had been parked, and it is permitted only when the submission's `privacy_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` or `REDACTED`; a submission still sitting at `REDACTION_REQUIRED`, or one already `PURGED`, cannot be accepted and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PRIVACY_HOLD`. A **reject** moves the review dimension `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` and records the reason code; it carries no privacy precondition, since refusing a document does not require the document to have been cleaned up. A **redaction request** leaves the review dimension parked at `UNDER_REVIEW` — moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` — while advancing the privacy dimension to `REDACTION_REQUIRED`; this is the clearest illustration of the two dimensions moving independently, and `reviewed_by_user_id` is stamped here too.

Curing a privacy hold is the employee's job. Either the owning `Employee` or a `VerificationAnalyst` uploads a `redacted_document_reference` — the handle of the re-uploaded masked image — which advances the privacy dimension `REDACTION_REQUIRED → REDACTED`. An upload is legal only when the submission's `privacy_status` is exactly `REDACTION_REQUIRED` and its `review_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`. When an `Employee` performs it, they must own the dependent the submission is about. Crucially, `review_status` is left exactly as it was: the upload cures the privacy obligation and nothing else, and the analyst must still come back and decide the document on its merits.

Whether a dependent is documented is a distinct question from whether any single document was accepted, and it is answered by a **completeness test** re-evaluated after every accept decision and again for point-of-enrollment dependents during the sweep. The test first selects the governing rule: the documentation rule of the program in context whose `relationship_type` matches the dependent's, with `phase` `POINT_OF_ENROLLMENT` when the dependent's `poe_program_id` is set and the program in context is that same program, and `phase` `AUDIT` in every other case. It then gathers the dependent's submissions whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `privacy_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, `REDACTED`, or `PURGED` — a purged sensitive document still counts, because the fact that it was examined and accepted survives the destruction of the copy. Submissions sitting at `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, and submissions that were rejected, are excluded entirely. The dependent's `accepted_proof_type_count` is the number of **distinct** `proof_type` values in that gathered set, so two accepted joint account statements count once and never twice. The dependent's `documentation_complete` becomes true when `accepted_proof_type_count` is greater than or equal to the governing rule's `min_accepted_documents` — exactly the minimum is sufficient.

The moment the test flips to true for a dependent under audit, the dependent moves `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED` with `documentation_complete` set to true. That resolution ripples upward: the owning case recomputes `unverified_dependent_count` and `priority_score`, the program recomputes `verified_dependent_count` and `pending_dependent_count`, and if this was the case's last unresolved in-scope dependent the case moves to `COMPLETE` with `completed_at` set to the current clock instant.

### 3.6. The Deferred-Action Checkpoint

Everything in this audit that happens "when a deadline passes" happens because a `ProgramAdmin` runs the deferred-action checkpoint against the current logical clock. The checkpoint takes no input at all. One invocation executes six steps in a fixed order, and every step whose preconditions are unmet is simply a no-op, which makes the checkpoint safe to invoke repeatedly and safe to invoke early. Because the steps run in order inside one call, a single invocation made after the response deadline will close the amnesty window in the first step and then immediately escalate in the second. The program's `sweep_epoch` and its `version` each increment by exactly 1 on a call in which any step mutated state, and neither moves on a call that changed nothing.

**Step one — closing amnesty.** When the program's `status` is `AMNESTY_OPEN` and the current clock instant is at or past `amnesty_end_at`, the program moves `AMNESTY_OPEN → RESPONSE_WINDOW`. No case and no dependent is touched. The practical effect is that further voluntary removals for this program now fail, since removals require the `AMNESTY_OPEN` phase.

**Step two — audit escalation.** When the program's `status` is `RESPONSE_WINDOW` and the current clock instant is at or past `response_deadline_at`, the program moves `RESPONSE_WINDOW → ESCALATION` and non-responders are escalated. Before any ordering decision, `priority_score` is recomputed for every case whose `status` is `NOTIFIED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Those cases are then processed in descending `priority_score`, and where two cases score equally, ascending `created_at`. Each processed case moves to `NON_RESPONSIVE` with `non_responsive_at` set to the current clock instant — from `NOTIFIED` when nothing was ever heard, and from `IN_PROGRESS` when the employee responded but left at least one in-scope dependent still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`. Because a case became `NON_RESPONSIVE`, each member of its `in_scope_dependent_ids` still sitting at `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, taken in ascending dependent `created_at`, moves `PENDING_VERIFICATION → TERMINATION_PENDING`. Because a dependent entered `TERMINATION_PENDING`, one `TerminationNotice` is created for it in `ISSUED` with `origin` `AUDIT_ESCALATION`, `case_id` naming the owning case, `issued_at` at the current clock instant, `grace_days_applied` recording the fourteen-day grace period, an `effective_date` computed as described in §3.7, a `continuation_election_deadline` sixty days past issuance, `continuation_monthly_premium_cents` at zero, and a `batch_sequence_no` assigned one-based within this sweep batch in exactly the processing order just described — cases by descending priority then ascending creation, dependents within a case by ascending creation. One `CommunicationEvent` with `kind` `TERMINATION_NOTICE` is appended per notice. Because notices were issued for a case, that case then moves `NON_RESPONSIVE → ESCALATED` with `escalated_at` set to the same instant. Finally, `non_responsive_case_count` — the number of cases in `NON_RESPONSIVE` or `ESCALATED` — along with `pending_dependent_count`, `termination_pending_count`, and `provisional_annual_savings_cents` are all recomputed on the program.

**Step three — point-of-enrollment lapse.** This step is about the ongoing verification design rather than the audit wave, and it runs independently of the program's own phase. Every dependent whose `poe_program_id` names this program, whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, whose `poe_deadline_at` is at or before the current clock instant, whose `documentation_complete` is still false, and whose governing point-of-enrollment rule has `auto_terminate_on_lapse` true, taken in ascending `created_at`, moves `PENDING_VERIFICATION → TERMINATION_PENDING` and receives one `TerminationNotice` in `ISSUED` with `origin` `POE_LAPSE`. A lapse notice has no case behind it — its `case_id` is null — and its `effective_date` is computed from the point-of-enrollment branch described in §3.7. A dependent whose governing rule leaves `auto_terminate_on_lapse` false is left alone, however overdue: the rule that opted out of automatic termination is honoured.

**Step four — finalization.** Every `TerminationNotice` still in `ISSUED` whose `effective_date` is strictly earlier than the current clock's calendar date moves `ISSUED → FINAL`, and because its notice went final, its dependent moves `TERMINATION_PENDING → TERMINATED` with `coverage_end_date` set to that notice's `effective_date`. A notice whose effective date is exactly today is not yet final — coverage runs through the end of the effective day. The same step also retires finished cases: a case in `ESCALATED` moves to `CLOSED` when three conditions hold together. Every `TerminationNotice` of that case has a `status` of `FINAL` or `RESCINDED`; no member of its `in_scope_dependent_ids` is still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `TERMINATION_PENDING`; and the appeal window has closed on every one of its notices, meaning the current clock instant is strictly later than each notice's `issued_at` advanced by thirty whole days. That third condition is the important one: while any notice is still inside its thirty-day appeal window the case deliberately stays in `ESCALATED` so an appeal remains filable. The program's `terminated_dependent_count`, `termination_pending_count`, and `audited_annual_savings_cents` are recomputed.

**Step five — reconciliation.** When the program's `status` is `ESCALATION` and every one of its cases has a `status` of `COMPLETE`, `CLOSED`, or `VOIDED`, the program moves `ESCALATION → RECONCILIATION`, and `audited_annual_savings_cents`, `recovery_ledger_cents`, `recovered_total_cents`, and `refund_due_total_cents` are all recomputed. Reconciliation is the phase in which the money is settled but the program is not yet shut.

**Step six — election expiry.** Every `TerminationNotice` whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `FINAL`, whose `continuation_election_status` is still `NOT_ELECTED`, and whose `continuation_election_deadline` is strictly earlier than the current clock instant, moves its `continuation_election_status` to `WINDOW_EXPIRED`. No further election is accepted on such a notice thereafter.

### 3.7. Termination Notices, Effective Dates, and Continuation Coverage Rights

Losing coverage under an eligibility audit is a regulated event, and the notice that effects it must state precisely when coverage ends and what continuation rights follow. Both are computed at issuance from a fourteen-day statutory grace period.

For a notice whose `origin` is `AUDIT_ESCALATION`, the computation begins from a base date: the calendar date of the notice's `issued_at` advanced by fourteen whole days. That base is then floored against the calendar date of the program's `response_deadline_at`, and the later of the two dates becomes the anchor. The floor exists so that an administrator who runs the checkpoint aggressively can never end anyone's coverage before the response window they were promised has actually closed. For a notice whose `origin` is `POE_LAPSE`, the computation is simpler and carries no floor at all: the anchor is the calendar date of the dependent's `poe_deadline_at` advanced by the same fourteen whole days. In both branches, `grace_days_applied` records the fourteen days that were used, and the notice's `effective_date` is the last calendar day of the month containing the anchor — resolved against the real calendar, so an anchor that already falls on the last day of a month yields that same date as the effective date.

Continuation coverage begins the day after coverage ends. The notice's `continuation_start_date` is its `effective_date` advanced by one day and is the inclusive first day of the continuation interval. Its `continuation_end_date` is that start date advanced by eighteen calendar months and is the **exclusive** end of a half-open interval — the last covered day is the day before it, not the date itself. Both dates remain null on a notice until continuation is actually elected. Separately and immediately at issuance, `continuation_election_deadline` is set to the notice's `issued_at` instant advanced by sixty whole days, and `continuation_election_status` starts at `NOT_ELECTED`.

Either the affected `Employee` or a `ProgramAdmin` records the election by supplying an `election` of `ELECT` or `WAIVE`. The notice's `status` must be `ISSUED` or `FINAL` and its `continuation_election_status` must still be `NOT_ELECTED`; a notice that is already rescinded, already elected, already waived, or already expired is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`. The current clock instant must be no later than `continuation_election_deadline` — the boundary instant is inside the window — and a late election is refused with `ERR_CONTINUATION_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Either election stamps `continuation_elected_at` with the current clock instant. A waiver moves `continuation_election_status` from `NOT_ELECTED` to `WAIVED`, leaves `continuation_monthly_premium_cents` at zero, and leaves both continuation dates null. An election moves it from `NOT_ELECTED` to `ELECTED`, materialises the two continuation dates from the formulas above, and prices the premium.

Continuation is priced from a three-component cost table keyed on the dependent's relationship class, and each component is loaded by two percent to cover administration. A `SPOUSE` and a `DOMESTIC_PARTNER` each carry a medical component of 45,125 cents, a dental component of 3,313 cents, and a vision component of 1,275 cents. A `CHILD_BIOLOGICAL`, a `STEPCHILD`, an `ADOPTED_CHILD`, and a `LEGAL_GUARDIANSHIP` dependent each carry 27,450 cents medical, 2,137 cents dental, and 913 cents vision. A `DISABLED_ADULT_CHILD` carries 33,188 cents medical, 2,137 cents dental, and 913 cents vision. **The order of rounding is pinned and is not negotiable:** each of the three components is multiplied by the load factor of 1.02 and rounded half up to a whole cent *individually*, and only then are the three rounded results added together to give `continuation_monthly_premium_cents`. Three rounding steps occur, one per component, and the final addition is exact. Rounding the loaded sum instead of the loaded components — that is, adding the three raw components, applying the load factor once, and rounding once — produces a figure that differs by one cent for spouse and domestic-partner dependents, and must not be used.

A notice's `status` reaches `RESCINDED` only through a granted appeal, described next, at which point `rescinded_at` records the instant and `rescission_reason` records `APPEAL_GRANTED`. `RESCINDED` is the notice lifecycle's terminal state.

### 3.8. Appeals for Documentation Exceptions and the Compensating Cascade

Not every genuine dependent can produce a marriage certificate. Domestic partnerships often have no registrable document at all, custody arrangements are recorded in court orders that a subscriber may not hold a copy of, and administrative mistakes happen. The appeal process is the audit's release valve for exactly those cases, and it is the only mechanism in the system capable of reversing a termination.

Only the affected `Employee` may file an appeal. They name the `case_id` and the `dependent_id`, assert one of four `grounds` — `DOMESTIC_PARTNERSHIP_AFFIDAVIT`, `CUSTODY_ARRANGEMENT`, `DOCUMENT_UNAVAILABLE`, or `ADMINISTRATIVE_ERROR` — and supply a narrative `statement`. Two optional handles support the first two grounds: `affidavit_reference` points at a signed domestic-partnership affidavit, and `custody_order_reference` points at a custody or guardianship order.

Filing is gated four ways beyond ownership. The owning case must be in `ESCALATED` or `NON_RESPONSIVE`, otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The dependent's `coverage_status` must be `TERMINATION_PENDING` or `TERMINATED`, otherwise it is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` — there is nothing to appeal before a notice exists. At most one appeal per dependent may sit in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `REMANDED` at any time, and a second concurrent filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`. And the appeal must be timely: the current clock instant must be no later than the `issued_at` of the dependent's non-rescinded `TerminationNotice` advanced by thirty whole days, with a late filing refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. That thirty-day window is the same one that keeps a case parked in `ESCALATED` during step four of the checkpoint.

The **grounds support** rule is the substantive one, and it is symmetric across the two documented grounds. Asserting `DOMESTIC_PARTNERSHIP_AFFIDAVIT` requires two things together: `affidavit_reference` must be present, and the `AUDIT`-phase documentation rule for the dependent's `relationship_type` must have `affidavit_allowed` set to true — an employer that never sanctioned affidavit evidence for that relationship class cannot have it forced on them by appeal. Asserting `CUSTODY_ARRANGEMENT` likewise requires two things together: `custody_order_reference` must be present, and the dependent's `relationship_type` must be one of `CHILD_BIOLOGICAL`, `STEPCHILD`, `ADOPTED_CHILD`, or `LEGAL_GUARDIANSHIP`, since a custody order is meaningless for a spouse or a domestic partner. A failure of either half of either ground is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_GROUNDS_UNSUPPORTED`. The two remaining grounds, `DOCUMENT_UNAVAILABLE` and `ADMINISTRATIVE_ERROR`, carry no reference requirement and no relationship restriction. A new appeal is created in `SUBMITTED` with `round_number` at 1 and `reviewer_user_id`, `assigned_at`, `decided_at`, `decision_rationale`, and `remand_reason` all null.

An `AppealReviewer` takes the appeal, which moves it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamps `reviewer_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `assigned_at` with the current clock instant. Taking requires the appeal to still be in `SUBMITTED`, and it is where segregation of duties bites: if the acting identifier appears as `reviewed_by_user_id` on any proof submission belonging to this appeal's dependent, the assignment is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The analyst who judged the documents may not also judge the exception.

Only the assigned reviewer may then decide, and only while the appeal is `UNDER_REVIEW`; a decision from anyone else is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_REVIEWER_MISMATCH` and a decision against an appeal in any other status is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`. The reviewer supplies a `decision` of `GRANT`, `DENY`, or `REMAND`, optionally a `decision_rationale` recording their reasoning, and optionally a `remand_reason` stating what further documentation they require. Every decision stamps `decided_at`.

A **denial** moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`. The dependent's `TerminationNotice` proceeds entirely unchanged, and when the case has no other unresolved dependent left it moves to `COMPLETE`.

A **remand** moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → REMANDED` and records `remand_reason`. It is permitted only while `round_number` is strictly less than the two-round cap; a remand that would push the appeal past it is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The notice, the dependent, and the case are all left untouched by a remand. The employee closes the loop by resubmitting — the only path back — which moves the appeal `REMANDED → SUBMITTED`, increments `round_number` by 1, clears `remand_reason`, and clears both `reviewer_user_id` and `assigned_at` so that the appeal must be taken again from scratch, potentially by a different reviewer. Those clearings are exhaustive: a resubmission clears no other field of the appeal, so in particular the `decided_at` stamp left by the remand decision is retained and is overwritten only when the appeal is next decided. Resubmission accepts a fresh `statement` and may supply or replace `affidavit_reference` and `custody_order_reference`. It too requires `round_number` to be strictly below the cap of two rounds, so a remanded appeal may be resubmitted exactly once; a further attempt is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Only the owning `Employee` may resubmit.

A **grant** moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → GRANTED` and triggers the system's largest compensating cascade, which must be applied in the following order. First the dependent is restored, and which restoration applies depends on how far termination had already progressed. When the dependent is in `TERMINATION_PENDING` and its `TerminationNotice` is still in `ISSUED`, the dependent moves `TERMINATION_PENDING → VERIFIED` with `documentation_complete` set to true — the exception is granted in lieu of documentation. When the dependent has already reached `TERMINATED` and its notice has already reached `FINAL`, the dependent moves `TERMINATED → REINSTATED` with `reinstated_at` set to the current clock instant and `coverage_end_date` cleared back to null. Second, every `TerminationNotice` of that dependent in `ISSUED` or `FINAL` moves to `RESCINDED` with `rescinded_at` set and `rescission_reason` recorded as `APPEAL_GRANTED` — both the `ISSUED → RESCINDED` and the `FINAL → RESCINDED` paths exist and behave identically in this respect. Third, one `CommunicationEvent` with `kind` `RESCISSION_NOTICE` is appended to the case. Fourth, because the notice was rescinded, every `ClaimRecovery` of that dependent whose `status` is `OPEN`, `DEMAND_ISSUED`, or `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` moves to `VOIDED`, and each such row's `refund_due_cents` is set equal to its `recovered_cents` — money already collected from a subscriber whose dependent turns out to have been eligible is owed straight back. A voided row that had collected nothing keeps `refund_due_cents` at zero, which is the same rule producing a zero result. Fifth, the case itself re-opens: it moves `ESCALATED → IN_PROGRESS` when at least one member of its `in_scope_dependent_ids` is still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `TERMINATION_PENDING`, and instead moves `ESCALATED → COMPLETE` with `completed_at` set when the restored dependent was the last unresolved one. This is the audit's one genuine cycle: an escalated case can return to an in-progress state and be worked again. Finally, the program recomputes `verified_dependent_count`, `terminated_dependent_count`, `termination_pending_count`, `non_responsive_case_count`, `recovery_ledger_cents`, `recovered_total_cents`, `refund_due_total_cents`, `provisional_annual_savings_cents`, and `audited_annual_savings_cents`.

### 3.9. Claims Recovery, Savings Measurement, and Closing the Program

Terminating coverage stops future spend; it does nothing about the claims the plan already paid on someone who was never eligible. Recovering those is the audit's final and most contentious phase, and it is deliberately handled by a distinct role with a distinct arithmetic.

The raw material is the paid-claims history. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `RecoveryAgent` records a paid claim line against a dependent, supplying the `service_date` on which care was delivered as an ISO 8601 calendar date, the `paid_cents` the plan paid on that line which must be strictly greater than zero, an optional `employee_premium_offset_cents` recording the employee's own premium contribution credited against the line which defaults to zero and may not be negative, the payer's `claim_reference` number, and an optional `provider_type` of `MEDICAL`, `DENTAL`, `VISION`, or `PHARMACY` identifying the benefit component. A paid claim line is immutable evidence once written and carries no `updated_at` and no `version`. Recording one increases the dependent's `paid_claims_total_cents` by the line's `paid_cents`, that field being the exact sum of the dependent's paid lines.

A `RecoveryAgent` opens a recovery by naming the `dependent_id`, the `termination_notice_id` that establishes ineligibility, a `recovery_variant` of `STRICT` or `EQUITABLE`, an `ineligibility_onset_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date, and the `expected_dependent_version` described in §2.6. Three gates apply. The named notice's `status` must be exactly `FINAL`, since an issued-but-not-final notice has not yet ended anyone's coverage; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`. The dependent's `coverage_status` must be exactly `TERMINATED` and no amnesty removal may exist for that dependent; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`. This is where the amnesty promise is enforced structurally rather than by policy alone: an `AMNESTY_REMOVED` dependent can never reach `TERMINATED`, and even a dependent who somehow carried a removal record is excluded, which is what the removal's `claims_recovery_waived` flag documents.

**Pricing the recoverable subtotal.** The calculation reads the dependent's paid claim lines in ascending `service_date`, breaking ties by ascending `created_at`, and confines itself to a closed date window. The window closes at the dependent's `coverage_end_date` when that field is set, and at the notice's `effective_date` when it is not. Where the window opens depends on the variant, and the two variants are the audit program's two negotiating postures. Under `STRICT`, the window opens at the supplied `ineligibility_onset_date` and each qualifying line contributes its full `paid_cents`: this is the gross plan-paid amount reaching all the way back to the date the dependent actually became ineligible, and employee premium contributions are ignored entirely. Under `EQUITABLE`, the window instead opens at the calendar date of the program's `announcement_at` — the date the employer told the population an audit was happening, rather than the date the ineligibility began — and each qualifying line contributes its `paid_cents` reduced by its `employee_premium_offset_cents`, with any line whose offset exceeds its paid amount contributing zero rather than a negative number. That per-line floor of zero is applied **line by line, before summation**, never to the total. Under both variants, a line whose `service_date` falls outside the closed window contributes zero. Both window boundaries are inclusive: a line dated exactly on the opening date, and a line dated exactly on the closing date, both count. Because `EQUITABLE` starts later and nets each line, its subtotal is strictly smaller than the `STRICT` subtotal whenever any pre-announcement line exists or any offset is non-zero. The result is stored as `recoverable_subtotal_cents`.

A new recovery is created in `OPEN` with that subtotal computed, and with `admin_fee_pct`, `admin_fee_cents`, `waiver_credit_cents`, `net_demand_cents`, `recovered_cents`, and `refund_due_cents` all at zero and `demand_issued_at` null. Creation also supersedes: any prior recovery of the same dependent whose `status` is `OPEN`, or whose `status` is `DEMAND_ISSUED` with `recovered_cents` still exactly zero, moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the program's `recovery_ledger_cents` is recomputed so the superseded row no longer counts. A recovery that has already collected money is never superseded. Creation increments the dependent's `recovery_revision` by 1 and the dependent's `version` by 1 — which is exactly why the concurrency guard on this endpoint reads the dependent's version rather than the recovery's.

**Pricing the demand.** Issuing the demand is a separate act, optionally carrying a `waiver_credit_cents` goodwill credit which defaults to zero, may not be negative, and may not exceed the row's `recoverable_subtotal_cents` — an excessive credit is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_EXCEEDS_SUBTOTAL`. The recovery's `status` must be `OPEN`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_STATE_INVALID`. And the subtotal must clear a de minimis floor of 25,000 cents: a subtotal of exactly 25,000 cents passes, and anything below it is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_BELOW_DE_MINIMIS`, because chasing a subscriber for a two-hundred-dollar dental claim costs more in goodwill than it recovers.

The demand arithmetic has a pinned evaluation order, because the administrative fee is a percentage of the demand while the demand includes the fee — a dependency that must be broken in exactly one direction. Step one takes the subtotal already stored on the row. Step two selects the fee rate **from that subtotal**, never from any fee-inclusive figure: a subtotal below 100,000 cents attracts a rate of zero, a subtotal from 100,000 cents up to but not including 500,000 cents attracts five percent, and a subtotal of 500,000 cents or more attracts eight percent. The selected rate is stored in `admin_fee_pct`. Step three multiplies the subtotal by that rate and rounds the product half up to a whole cent exactly once, storing the result in `admin_fee_cents`; this is the single rounding step in the whole computation, and it happens **before** any waiver credit is considered. Step four subtracts last: `net_demand_cents` is the subtotal plus `admin_fee_cents` minus `waiver_credit_cents`. The row then moves `OPEN → DEMAND_ISSUED` with `demand_issued_at` set to the current clock instant, and the program's `recovery_ledger_cents` is recomputed now that this row's `net_demand_cents` counts.

**Collecting.** A payment carries an `amount_cents` and the `received_date` it arrived on. The recovery's `status` must be `DEMAND_ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`, otherwise the payment is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_STATE_INVALID`, and the payment may not overshoot: the sum of the existing `recovered_cents` and the incoming `amount_cents` must be no greater than `net_demand_cents`, with an overshoot refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_DEMAND`. An accepted payment adds `amount_cents` to `recovered_cents`. When the new total is still strictly below `net_demand_cents` the row lands in `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` — reached either from `DEMAND_ISSUED` on a first short payment or from `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` on a subsequent one. When the new total is exactly equal to `net_demand_cents` the row lands in `RECOVERED`, reachable directly from `DEMAND_ISSUED` on a single full payment and from `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` on a closing payment. The program's `recovered_total_cents` is recomputed; `recovery_ledger_cents` is deliberately unchanged by a payment, because a fully recovered row still counts toward the ledger of demands raised.

**The three money ledgers.** All three are computed over the recoveries of the program's in-scope dependents as defined in §2.9. `recovery_ledger_cents` is the sum of `net_demand_cents` across those recoveries whose `status` is `DEMAND_ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`, or `RECOVERED` — so `OPEN`, `WRITTEN_OFF`, `VOIDED`, and `SUPERSEDED` rows are excluded. `recovered_total_cents` is the sum of `recovered_cents` across those recoveries whose `status` is anything other than `VOIDED`. `refund_due_total_cents` is the sum of `refund_due_cents` across all of them, and since `refund_due_cents` equals a row's `recovered_cents` once the row reaches `VOIDED` and is zero otherwise, that total is non-zero only where an appeal has been granted. `VOIDED`, `RECOVERED`, `WRITTEN_OFF`, and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal for a recovery.

**Measuring savings.** A `ProgramAdmin` may ask the program to measure savings at any time, supplying a `variant` of `PROVISIONAL` or `AUDITED`; the requested variant is recorded in `last_savings_variant` and the corresponding figure is written. Both variants sum over the same in-scope population from §2.9, both are exact integer cent arithmetic with no rounding anywhere, and both draw each dependent's `monthly_cost_cents` from the schedule in §2.7. The `PROVISIONAL` variant includes every in-scope dependent whose `coverage_status` is `AMNESTY_REMOVED`, `TERMINATION_PENDING`, or `TERMINATED`, and credits each one a full twelve months of its monthly cost regardless of when coverage actually ended; the result is written to `provisional_annual_savings_cents`. It is the optimistic, forward-looking figure an administrator quotes mid-audit, and it is the figure recomputed automatically on every event before a notice reaches finality. The `AUDITED` variant excludes `TERMINATION_PENDING` dependents entirely — nothing has actually been saved from a dependent still under notice — and prorates each remaining dependent by twelve minus the calendar month number of that dependent's `coverage_end_date`, so a dependent whose coverage ended on the last day of September, month nine, contributes three months of its monthly cost. The result is written to `audited_annual_savings_cents`, and it is the figure recomputed automatically whenever a notice finalises, an appeal is granted, or the program closes. Under both variants a dependent in `REINSTATED`, `VERIFIED`, `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, or `ENROLLED` contributes exactly zero — a reinstated dependent's savings are unwound to nothing, which is what makes the appeal cascade financially honest.

**Closing out.** A `ProgramAdmin` closes the program, which requires its `status` to be `RECONCILIATION` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_PHASE_INVALID`. Two outstanding-work gates then apply: no appeal belonging to any of this program's cases may sit in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `REMANDED`, or the close is refused with `ERR_OPEN_APPEALS_REMAIN`; and no claim recovery of this program's in-scope dependents may sit in `DEMAND_ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`, or the close is refused with `ERR_OPEN_RECOVERIES_REMAIN`. On success the program moves `RECONCILIATION → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and its `version` increments. Every case of the program still in `COMPLETE` moves `COMPLETE → CLOSED`, after which no further proof submission, amnesty removal, or appeal may reference it. Every claim recovery of the program's dependents still in `OPEN` moves `OPEN → WRITTEN_OFF` — a demand that was opened but never priced and issued is abandoned rather than pursued after the fact. Every sensitive accepted proof submission of the program's cases has its `privacy_status` moved to `PURGED` in the retention discharge described in §2.10. And `audited_annual_savings_cents` is recomputed one final time, so the closed program carries the settled figure.
