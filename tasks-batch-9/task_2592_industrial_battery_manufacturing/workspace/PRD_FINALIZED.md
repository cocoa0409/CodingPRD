# Industrial Battery Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Industrial and motive-power battery manufacturing sits at the intersection of three unforgiving regulatory regimes: airborne and blood-lead exposure limits that govern who may stand on the shop floor, environmental air-emission permits that cap how much formation charging a site may perform in a calendar month, and cradle-to-grave hazardous-material tracking that follows every lead-bearing battery from the plate line to the customer and back again as a returned core. Today those three regimes are administered in three disconnected systems, and the seams between them are where the business loses money and regulatory standing: a plant discovers it has over-consumed its monthly emission allowance only after the charging is done, an inspector releases a lot whose acceptance sampling was never completed, or a returned core is weighed in against a design revision that was superseded eighteen months earlier.

This document specifies a single API that makes those seams explicit and enforced. The system models the plant as the compliance envelope, workers as monitored individuals whose exposure history can freeze production, cell designs as reviewed and versioned engineering records, production lots as pipelines that pass through plate production, formation charging, assembly and acceptance testing, and shipped battery units as long-lived field assets carrying two independent obligations — a warranty and a core deposit. Every write is evaluated against the plant's derived compliance posture, and every quantity that can be conserved — lead mass, emission grams, core deposit liability — is conserved arithmetically rather than by convention.

The API is deliberately flat: each collection is a top-level path segment mounted directly under `/`, and relationships are carried by explicit identifier fields in request payloads rather than by deep URL nesting. The only exceptions are the two append-only event logs — exposure samples belonging to a worker and maintenance events belonging to a shipped unit — which are reachable only as sub-resources of their owner. Readers should treat §2 as the binding contract for envelope shape, precision and access control, and §3 as the binding contract for behaviour; §4 and §5 are indices back into that behaviour, not substitutes for it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting in. If either header is absent, empty, or `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the seven roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). There is no anonymous access tier and no default role. Once the headers are accepted, the role is checked against the authorised-role list of the addressed endpoint as published in §4; a role that does not appear there is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlantAdmin`, `DesignEngineer`, `ProductionSupervisor`, `QualityInspector`, `SafetyOfficer`, `FleetEngineer`, and `WarrantyAdministrator`. Their remits are as follows. A `PlantAdmin` is the plant operations administrator: it registers plants, amends permits and tariffs, enrols workers, commissions formation bays and test chambers, and reads every compliance ledger. A `DesignEngineer` authors and reviews cell and plate designs — drafting, editing, submitting and approving them. A `ProductionSupervisor` runs the production pipeline: opening lots, advancing process stages, building assemblies, reworking rejects, scheduling and running formation charging, and shipping finished units. A `QualityInspector` owns acceptance quality: verifying assembly torque and insulation resistance, drawing sampling plans, scheduling and recording capacity and cycle-life tests, and dispositioning lots. A `SafetyOfficer` owns industrial hygiene and environmental compliance: recording exposure samples, clearing removed workers, placing and lifting environmental holds on lots, and aborting unsafe formation runs. A `FleetEngineer` is the motive-power application engineer: cataloguing chargers, sizing forklift battery applications, matching chargers, and accepting or withdrawing the resulting quotes. A `WarrantyAdministrator` is the aftermarket administrator: logging field maintenance and cycle reports, adjudicating warranty claims, and running the core return and recycling programme.

Four of the seven roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `DesignEngineer`, `ProductionSupervisor`, `QualityInspector`, or `SafetyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Worker` row; ownership and segregation checks on any resource that references a `Worker` compare that resource's worker-identifier field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, `submitted_by_worker_id` and `approved_by_worker_id` on a cell design, `last_test_recorded_by_worker_id` on a production lot, and `recorded_by_worker_id` on a capacity test are all populated from, and compared against, the acting `X-User-ID`. If `X-User-ID` for one of those four roles does not resolve to a `Worker` row, the request fails with `ERR_ACTING_WORKER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). If it resolves to a worker whose `removal_status` is `MEDICAL_REMOVAL`, the request fails with `ERR_ACTOR_MEDICALLY_REMOVED` (HTTP 403) — a medically removed employee may perform no operation at all until cleared, regardless of which endpoint is addressed.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `FleetEngineer`, or `WarrantyAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server accepts and may log it, but never resolves it to a `Worker` row and never compares it to any entity's `id`; consequently `ERR_ACTING_WORKER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ACTOR_MEDICALLY_REMOVED` cannot arise on an endpoint whose only authorised roles are opaque ones.

Three operations layer a **job classification** requirement on top of the role check. Approving a cell design requires the acting worker's `job_class` to be `ENGINEER`; recording an exposure sample requires `SAFETY`; releasing a production lot requires `QUALITY`. A worker holding the right role but the wrong `job_class` is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_JOB_CLASS_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The five job classifications a worker may hold are `ENGINEER`, `PRODUCTION`, `QUALITY`, `SAFETY`, and `MAINTENANCE`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum accepted value of 1, and a maximum accepted value of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0 with no upper bound. A supplied `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific list endpoint states a different ordering in §4, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Several collections do pin a different order, and where they do the §3 narrative and the §4 description state it: plants are returned newest first by `created_at` descending with `id` ascending as the tiebreak; exposure samples are returned by `sampled_at` descending then `created_at` ascending; maintenance events by `performed_at` descending then `created_at` ascending; formation runs and capacity tests by `scheduled_start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; shipped units by `shipped_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending. All other collections use `created_at` ascending with `id` ascending as the tiebreak.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is strictly less than `total_count`. Single-resource responses omit all four pagination fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients never supply an `id`. Alongside it, every resource carries a `created_at` timestamp — an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Every other instant stored on a resource is rendered in that same three-fractional-digit form whenever it is returned, whether it was minted by the server or supplied by the client — `updated_at`, a worker's `hire_date` and `last_sample_at`, a sample's `sampled_at`, a run's `actual_start_at` and `actual_end_at`, a lot's `released_at`, a unit's `shipped_at`, a log row's `performed_at`, and the same fields wherever a computed read repeats them inside its `lines`. A client that supplies `2026-09-02T06:00:00Z` therefore reads the stored instant back as `2026-09-02T06:00:00.000Z`; the two spell the same instant and only the rendered form is pinned.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical instant on every accepted mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by 1 on every accepted mutation of that row. Two resources are exceptions because they are strictly append-only event records: an `ExposureSample` and a `MaintenanceLog` each carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on those two — they are never updated after they are written, so there is nothing for either field to express.

The server runs on a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability repositions the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes that instant back; every role may invoke it. Repositioning the clock is not retroactive: no stored row is recomputed when the clock moves. Instead, every window evaluation, exposure look-back, service-month count, warranty age, scheduling comparison and default timestamp reads whatever the logical clock says at the moment the request is processed. This is why the same warranty adjudication can produce different outcomes on two different logical days without any field on the unit having been edited.

### 2.5. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Monetary Units

Rounding throughout the system is **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity rather than per endpoint. Money is always an integer count of whole cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and every monetary rounding is applied half up to a whole cent.

The remaining precisions are as follows. Lead masses in kilograms — the plant's opening, on-hand, in-field and recycled inventories, its closing variance and its cumulative core reconciliation difference, a design's lead content per pack, and a core's declared and received masses — carry three decimal places. Energy in kilowatt-hours carries two decimal places. Band hours, whether scheduled or actual, carry four decimal places. Emission grams carry two decimal places. The emission utilization ratio, the temperature derate factor, the watering compliance ratio and the usage fraction carry four decimal places. Exposure indices, capacity yield percentages, torque and insulation readings expressed as percentages or ratios, and the time-weighted-average intermediate all follow the precisions stated with their calculations in §3. Where a calculation prescribes an explicit rounding order — rounding each component before summing rather than summing before rounding — that order is normative and the alternative order is rejected even when it differs only in the last cent.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Lots and Units

Two write operations in the system can silently destroy evidence a client has not yet seen, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent row's `version`.

A `POST /production-lots/{id}/sampling-plans` request must carry `expected_lot_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProductionLot`. If that value does not equal the lot's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new sampling plan legitimately supersedes the prior one — it advances `sampling_plan_revision` and redefines the evidence window — so a writer holding a stale view of the lot would discard results recorded against a plan it never observed. The check is a stale-version comparison against stored state; it does not depend on requests overlapping in time. The scope of lot version increments is normative: a `ProductionLot`'s `version` advances only on writes addressed to the lot itself (stage advancement, sampling-plan draws, dispositions and environmental holds), on writes that mutate a lot-owned counter (recording a capacity-test result, which moves `completed_test_count`, `failed_test_count` and `passed_cycle_life_count`), and on any system cascade that touches the lot. Creating a child `BatteryAssembly`, recording a verification on one, reworking one, or scheduling a `CapacityTest` do **not** advance the lot's `version` — so a version read at the moment the lot entered `ASSEMBLY` is still current when the first sampling plan is drawn.

A `POST /battery-units/{id}/warranty-claims` request must likewise carry `expected_battery_unit_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `BatteryUnit`. A mismatch against the unit's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BATTERY_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the hazard is that a re-claim on a denied unit supersedes the previous adjudication by clearing `warranty_decision` and `warranty_credit_cents`, while field maintenance and cycle reports also advance the unit's `version`; a stale claimant would therefore adjudicate against cycle and watering figures that no longer exist. Opening a claim itself increments the unit's `version` by 1, so a second claim replaying the same `expected_battery_unit_version` is refused with HTTP 412.

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in the two paragraphs above take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one is an unknown-field schema violation.

### 2.7. The Plant Compliance Posture Gate

Each plant carries a derived `compliance_posture` that gates writes across every one of its children. It is never supplied by a client and never edited directly; it is recomputed by the server immediately after any write that changes the plant's `permit_status`, any worker's `removal_status`, the plant's `emission_reserved_g`, or the plant's `emission_consumed_g`. Its three values are `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, and `FROZEN`, and a newly registered plant with an `ACTIVE` permit and no removals derives `OPEN`.

The recomputation proceeds in a fixed order. First the plant's emission utilization ratio is formed: the sum of `emission_reserved_g` and `emission_consumed_g` is divided by `monthly_lead_emission_permit_g` and the quotient is rounded half up to four decimal places, and that value is stored as `emission_utilization_ratio`. The posture is then chosen by the first matching branch of an ordered cascade. The plant is `FROZEN` if its `permit_status` is `SUSPENDED`, or its `open_medical_removal_count` is two or more, or its utilization ratio is at least 1.0000. Otherwise the plant is `RESTRICTED` if its `open_medical_removal_count` is exactly one, or its utilization ratio is at least 0.8500. Otherwise the plant is `OPEN`. Both ratio breakpoints are inclusive: a ratio of exactly 0.8500 restricts the plant, and a ratio of exactly 1.0000 freezes it.

Where `open_medical_removal_count` comes from is worth stating plainly: it is the number of `Worker` rows belonging to the plant whose `removal_status` is `MEDICAL_REMOVAL`. Where the two emission sums come from is equally load-bearing. `emission_reserved_g` is the sum of `reserved_emission_g` over the formation runs of this plant's lots whose `run_state` is either `SCHEDULED` or `CHARGING`. `emission_consumed_g` is the sum of `consumed_emission_g` over the formation runs of this plant's lots whose `run_state` is `COMPLETED` and whose `actual_end_at` falls inside the current permit month, meaning the UTC calendar month of the logical clock. Aborted runs contribute to neither sum.

Posture does not merely record a condition; entering and leaving each value has consequences. Becoming `RESTRICTED` blocks two things and only two things: no lot may enter `FORMATION`, and no `SCHEDULED` formation run may be started. Runs already in `CHARGING` continue undisturbed and every other operation in the system proceeds normally. Becoming `FROZEN` — whether from `OPEN` or from `RESTRICTED` — additionally triggers the hold cascade described in §2.8. Leaving `FROZEN` for `RESTRICTED` lifts the holds the freeze imposed but leaves permit-driven holds in place; leaving `FROZEN` for `OPEN` lifts every environmental hold on the plant's lots and clears each lot's `hold_reason` to null; leaving `RESTRICTED` for `OPEN` simply records the new posture with no cascade. A posture that is refused as a gate surfaces as `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) when the plant is `FROZEN`, and as `ERR_PLANT_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) when the plant is `RESTRICTED` and the operation is one of the two the restriction blocks.

### 2.8. Cascades, Compensations and Conserved Quantities

Two multi-hop cascades run inside single requests, and both are normative — an implementation that performs only the first hop is incorrect.

**The environmental cascade.** It begins when an exposure sample pushes a worker across the removal threshold. Hop one writes the worker's `exposure_index`, `exposure_index_variant` and `last_sample_at` from the sample. Hop two, because the worker's `removal_status` flipped to `MEDICAL_REMOVAL`, recomputes the employing plant's `open_medical_removal_count` upward by one. Hop three, because that count changed, recomputes the plant's `compliance_posture` — one open removal yields `RESTRICTED`, two or more yield `FROZEN`. Hop four fires only when the posture became `FROZEN`: every `ProductionLot` of that plant whose `process_state` is one of `PLANNED`, `PLATE_PRODUCTION`, `FORMATION`, `ASSEMBLY`, `LOT_TESTING` or `QUARANTINED` moves its `env_hold_status` from `CLEAR` to `HELD` with `hold_reason` set to `PLANT_FROZEN`. Lots already `RELEASED` or `SCRAPPED` are untouched. Hop five follows from hop four: every formation run of those newly held lots whose `run_state` is `CHARGING` moves to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to `ENV_HOLD_CASCADE`, which releases that run's reserved emission grams back to the plant. A cascade abort leaves `actual_end_at` as it stood — null on a run that had not yet been given one — because stamping an end instant on an abort is a side effect of the operator abort endpoint of §3.5 and not of this cascade. The same hops four and five run when a plant's permit is suspended, except that `hold_reason` is written as `PERMIT_SUSPENDED` instead.

The environmental cascade has a matching **compensation** path. When a removed worker is medically cleared, the plant's `open_medical_removal_count` is recomputed downward by one, the posture is re-derived from the new count, and — if the recomputed posture is no longer `FROZEN` — every `ProductionLot` of that plant whose `hold_reason` is `PLANT_FROZEN` moves its `env_hold_status` from `HELD` back to `CLEAR`. Lots held because the permit was suspended stay `HELD`; only reinstating the permit releases those. Aborted formation runs are never resurrected by any hold release — a new run must be scheduled.

**The quarantine cascade.** It begins when a capacity-test result is recorded as `FAIL`, which is hop one and increments the lot's `failed_test_count`. If that increment pushes `failed_test_count` strictly above the lot's `acceptance_number`, hop two moves the lot's `process_state` from `LOT_TESTING` to `QUARANTINED`. Hop three, because the lot became `QUARANTINED`, moves every `BatteryAssembly` of that lot whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` to `REVOKED`. Hop four, because those assemblies were revoked, moves every `CapacityTest` on them whose `test_state` is `SCHEDULED` to `VOIDED`, which releases that test's share of its chamber's power draw. A voided test keeps a null `result` and never contributes to the lot's `completed_test_count`.

Three quantities in the system are **conserved** rather than merely recorded, and each has an invariant that must hold after every write that touches it.

The **emission invariant** governs reservation and consumption. Scheduling a formation run reserves grams; completing it releases the reservation and books consumption in the same write, so the plant's `emission_reserved_g` decreases by that run's `reserved_emission_g` while `emission_consumed_g` increases by its `consumed_emission_g`. Aborting a run — from `SCHEDULED` or from `CHARGING`, by an operator or by the hold cascade — releases the reservation with nothing consumed, returning the reserved-plus-consumed total to its pre-scheduling value. An aborted run keeps its `provisional_charge_cents` on the row as a historical estimate, but that estimate never rolls into the plant's `formation_energy_charge_cents`, and its `final_charge_cents` and `consumed_emission_g` stay 0 while `metered_energy_kwh` stays null.

The **lead mass invariant** governs hazardous material. A plant's `lead_mass_variance_kg` is its `lead_initial_inventory_kg` minus the sum of `lead_inventory_kg`, `lead_in_field_kg` and `lead_recycled_kg`, rounded half up to three decimal places, and it must read exactly 0.000 after every shipment, every core receipt and every core rejection. Shipping a unit moves the design's lead content out of on-hand inventory and into the field; receiving or rejecting a returned core moves the same mass out of the field and into recycled. Lithium designs declare zero lead content and therefore move none of these quantities.

The **core deposit invariant** governs money held on behalf of customers. A plant's `core_deposit_liability_cents` is 12500 cents multiplied by the number of its units whose `core_status` is `DEPOSIT_HELD`, `RETURN_OPEN` or `IN_TRANSIT`. The liability opens when a lead-acid unit ships and closes exactly once — either as a refund when the received core is recycled, or as a forfeit when the core is rejected. In both cases the liability drops by exactly 12500 cents; only the refund path also credits `core_credit_issued_cents` to the customer.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Nested Sub-Resources

Path-templated identifiers are always spelled `{id}` and no endpoint carries two of them. Where an endpoint addresses a sub-resource — a worker's exposure samples, a unit's maintenance events, or the transition surface of a unit's core return — the owner identifier is resolved **before** anything nested is considered. A request naming a worker that does not exist fails with `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) regardless of whether the sample payload would also have been invalid; a request naming a unit that does not exist fails with `ERR_BATTERY_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before any core-return state is inspected. The same left-to-right rule applies to plants, cell designs, production lots, formation bays, test chambers, battery assemblies, formation runs, capacity tests, chargers and fleet sizing requests, each with its own not-found code as catalogued in §5.

Foreign keys carried in request bodies are resolved after every path identifier has resolved. The body keys that participate are `plant_id`, `cell_design_id`, `lot_id`, `bay_id`, `chamber_id`, `assembly_id`, `sizing_request_id` and `supersedes_design_id`; each resolves to the not-found code of its own entity type — a missing plant to `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing design named by either `cell_design_id` or `supersedes_design_id` to `ERR_CELL_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`, a missing lot to `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing bay to `ERR_FORMATION_BAY_NOT_FOUND`, a missing chamber to `ERR_TEST_CHAMBER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing assembly to `ERR_ASSEMBLY_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing sizing request to `ERR_SIZING_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Validation Order and the First-Failure Rule

When several checks would fail on the same request, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the **lowest-numbered tier** below that fails. Text order within a tier does not imply precedence, but the tiers themselves are strictly ordered, and this ordering is observable — a request that is both unauthenticated and semantically nonsensical returns 401, and a request against a frozen plant whose lot is also in the wrong state returns the state error, not the posture error.

**Tier 1 — authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` naming one of the seven declared roles; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.

**Tier 2 — authorisation.** The acting role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorised-role list; otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.

**Tier 3 — payload schema.** Required fields present, no unknown fields, enum members recognised, numeric bounds honoured, and every discriminated field obeying its discriminator; otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`. Applies to every write endpoint. The four discriminator rules are: a `WATERING` maintenance event requires `electrolyte_level_ok` and forbids `cycles_delta`; a `CYCLE_REPORT` event requires `cycles_delta` and forbids `electrolyte_level_ok`; an `AIR` exposure sample requires both `air_lead_ug_per_m3` and `sample_duration_minutes` and forbids `blood_lead_ug_per_dl`; a `BLOOD` sample requires `blood_lead_ug_per_dl` and forbids both air fields. Sharing this tier is the chemistry/plate coherence rule: a `LEAD_ACID` design must use `FLAT_PASTED` or `TUBULAR` plates and declare a lead content strictly greater than 0.000, while a `LITHIUM_LFP` design must use `PRISMATIC_CELL` plates and declare a lead content of exactly 0.000; violations return `ERR_PLATE_TYPE_INCOMPATIBLE` (HTTP 422) and the rule guards both `POST /cell-designs` and `PATCH /cell-designs/{id}`.

**Tier 4 — acting worker exists.** For endpoints whose authorised roles include any entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to a `Worker`; otherwise `ERR_ACTING_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`.

**Tier 5 — acting worker fit for duty.** That worker's `removal_status` is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_MEDICALLY_REMOVED`. Same scope as tier 4.

**Tier 6 — job classification.** Applies to exactly three operations: `POST /cell-designs/{id}/review-actions` when the action is `APPROVE` (requires `ENGINEER`), `POST /workers/{id}/exposure-samples` (requires `SAFETY`), and `POST /production-lots/{id}/disposition` when the action is `RELEASE` (requires `QUALITY`). Otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_JOB_CLASS_MISMATCH`.

**Tier 7 — path identifier existence,** validated left to right, yielding whichever of `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CELL_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORMATION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSEMBLY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAPACITY_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SIZING_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_BATTERY_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` matches the unresolved segment. Formation bays, test chambers and chargers addressed by path id yield `ERR_FORMATION_BAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_CHAMBER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CHARGER_NOT_FOUND` respectively.

**Tier 8 — body foreign-key existence,** as enumerated in §2.9.

**Tier 9 — uniqueness.** `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409) on `POST /plants`, `POST /cell-designs`, `POST /chargers`, `POST /formation-bays`, `POST /test-chambers`, `POST /production-lots`, `POST /battery-assemblies` and `POST /battery-units`. The uniqueness scopes are: a plant's `site_code`, a design's `code`, a charger's `model_code`, an assembly's `serial_number`, a unit's `serial_number` and a unit's `core_manifest_number` are unique across the whole system; a bay's `bay_code`, a chamber's `chamber_code` and a lot's `lot_code` are unique only within their plant. Because the manifest number is globally unique, opening a core return that reuses an existing manifest number also returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

**Tier 10 — optimistic concurrency.** `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` on `POST /production-lots/{id}/sampling-plans` and `ERR_STALE_BATTERY_UNIT_VERSION` on `POST /battery-units/{id}/warranty-claims`, both HTTP 412, exactly as specified in §2.6.

**Tier 11 — state legality.** All HTTP 409. `ERR_DESIGN_STATE_INVALID` guards `PATCH /cell-designs/{id}` and `POST /cell-designs/{id}/review-actions`. `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` guards `POST /production-lots/{id}/advance`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/sampling-plans`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/disposition`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/env-hold`, `POST /formation-runs`, `POST /battery-assemblies`, `POST /capacity-tests` and `POST /battery-units`. `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` guards `POST /formation-runs/{id}/start`, `POST /formation-runs/{id}/complete` and `POST /formation-runs/{id}/abort`. `ERR_ASSEMBLY_AXIS_ALREADY_RECORDED` guards `POST /battery-assemblies/{id}/verifications`. `ERR_ASSEMBLY_STATE_INVALID` guards `POST /battery-assemblies/{id}/rework`. `ERR_CAPACITY_TEST_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_CYCLE_LIFE_SAMPLE_MISSING` guard `POST /capacity-tests/{id}/record-result`. `ERR_WARRANTY_STATE_INVALID` guards both `POST /battery-units/{id}/warranty-claims` and `POST /battery-units/{id}/warranty-adjudication`, while `ERR_WARRANTY_ALREADY_SETTLED` guards only the claim endpoint. `ERR_CORE_STATE_INVALID` guards `POST /battery-units/{id}/core-return` and `POST /battery-units/{id}/core-return/transitions`. `ERR_WORKER_NOT_REMOVED` guards `POST /workers/{id}/medical-clearance`. `ERR_SIZING_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` guards `POST /fleet-sizing-requests/{id}/decision` and `POST /battery-units`.

**Tier 12 — safety, licensing and event timing.** All HTTP 409 except the last, which is 422. `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN` guards `POST /production-lots`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/advance`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/sampling-plans`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/disposition` when the action is `RELEASE`, `POST /formation-runs`, `POST /formation-runs/{id}/start`, `POST /battery-assemblies` and `POST /battery-units`. `ERR_PLANT_RESTRICTED` guards exactly two operations: `POST /production-lots/{id}/advance` when `to_state` is `FORMATION`, and `POST /formation-runs/{id}/start`. `ERR_LOT_ENV_HELD` guards ten operations: `POST /production-lots/{id}/advance`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/sampling-plans`, `POST /production-lots/{id}/disposition` when the action is `RELEASE` or `RETEST`, `POST /formation-runs`, `POST /formation-runs/{id}/start`, `POST /battery-assemblies`, `POST /battery-assemblies/{id}/verifications`, `POST /battery-assemblies/{id}/rework`, `POST /capacity-tests` and `POST /capacity-tests/{id}/record-result`. `ERR_CHEMISTRY_NOT_LICENSED` guards `POST /production-lots` and `POST /production-lots/{id}/advance`. `ERR_CLEARANCE_EVIDENCE_MISSING` guards `POST /workers/{id}/medical-clearance`. `ERR_EVENT_TIME_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422) guards `POST /workers/{id}/exposure-samples` and `POST /battery-units/{id}/maintenance-logs`.

**Tier 13 — segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409) guards `POST /cell-designs/{id}/review-actions` when the action is `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`, and `POST /production-lots/{id}/disposition` when the action is `RELEASE`.

**Tier 14 — cross-entity referential coherence.** All HTTP 409. `ERR_PLANT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` guards `POST /formation-runs`, `POST /capacity-tests` and `POST /battery-units`. `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED` guards `POST /production-lots`. `ERR_ASSEMBLY_NOT_CERTIFIED` guards `POST /capacity-tests`. `ERR_BAY_UNAVAILABLE` guards `POST /formation-runs` and `POST /formation-runs/{id}/start`. `ERR_CHAMBER_UNAVAILABLE` guards `POST /capacity-tests`.

**Tier 15 — windows, capacity and business limits.** `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (422) guards `POST /formation-runs` and `POST /capacity-tests`. `ERR_BAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409) guards `POST /formation-runs`. `ERR_CHAMBER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409) guards `POST /capacity-tests`. `ERR_CONNECTION_COUNT_MISMATCH` (422) guards `POST /battery-assemblies/{id}/verifications`. `ERR_SAMPLING_INCOMPLETE` and `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_EXCEEDED` (both 409) guard `POST /production-lots/{id}/disposition` for the `RELEASE` action, and `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) guards the same endpoint for the `RETEST` action. `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) guards `POST /battery-assemblies/{id}/rework`. `ERR_CORE_MASS_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (422) guards `POST /battery-units/{id}/core-return/transitions` for the `RECEIVE` action, and `ERR_CORE_NOT_APPLICABLE` (409) guards `POST /battery-units/{id}/core-return` and the transition surface. `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CELL_DESIGN` and `ERR_NO_CHARGER_MATCH` (both 422) guard `POST /fleet-sizing-requests`.

**Tier 16 — fiscal and quota.** `ERR_EMISSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409) guards `POST /formation-runs`. `ERR_LEAD_INVENTORY_INSUFFICIENT` (409) guards `POST /battery-units`. `ERR_METERED_ENERGY_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422) guards `POST /formation-runs/{id}/complete`.

Within tier 12 the ordering between the three plant/lot gates is itself fixed for the operations that carry more than one: the plant-level freeze is evaluated before the plant-level restriction, and both are evaluated before the lot-level environmental hold. This matters for the read-only release-readiness preview described in §3.7, which reports gates in exactly that order.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties as a Structural Rule

Two approvals in this system may not be self-approvals, and the rule is enforced by comparing stored worker identifiers to the acting `X-User-ID` rather than by trusting the client. A cell design records `submitted_by_worker_id` when it is submitted for review; the engineer who approves it, and the engineer who requests changes on it, must both be someone else. Requesting changes deliberately **retains** `submitted_by_worker_id` rather than clearing it, so the same person is still barred from approving after the revision loop closes. A production lot records `last_test_recorded_by_worker_id` each time a capacity-test result is recorded inside the lot's current sampling window; the inspector who releases the lot must be someone other than that person. Both violations return `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Plant and Its Environmental Envelope

A `PlantAdmin` opens the system by registering a manufacturing site. The registration payload carries the site's legal `name`, its regulator-issued `site_code` (unique across all plants, four to twelve uppercase alphanumeric characters), the `licensed_chemistries` its environmental permit authorises, the initial `permit_status`, the grams of lead-equivalent air emission the permit allows in a permit month as `monthly_lead_emission_permit_g`, the two energy tariff bands `peak_tariff_cents_per_kwh` and `offpeak_tariff_cents_per_kwh`, and the lead mass already on site as `lead_initial_inventory_kg`. The chemistry list must be a non-empty subset of `LEAD_ACID` and `LITHIUM_LFP` with no duplicates. `permit_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`. The emission allowance and both tariffs must be strictly greater than zero and are carried to two decimal places; the opening lead inventory must be zero or greater and is carried to three decimal places.

Registration seeds a large set of derived quantities, and every one of them starts from a defined value rather than from absence. `emission_reserved_g`, `emission_consumed_g`, `lead_in_field_kg`, `lead_recycled_kg`, `core_deposit_liability_cents` and `formation_energy_charge_cents` all begin at 0. `lead_inventory_kg` begins equal to `lead_initial_inventory_kg`, while `lead_mass_variance_kg` and `core_lead_variance_kg` begin at 0.000. `emission_utilization_ratio` begins at 0.0000, `open_medical_removal_count` at 0, and `compliance_posture` is derived immediately — `OPEN` for an `ACTIVE` permit with no removals.

Two amendment surfaces exist and they behave differently. The first adjusts commercial and permit parameters: any combination of `monthly_lead_emission_permit_g`, `licensed_chemistries`, `peak_tariff_cents_per_kwh` and `offpeak_tariff_cents_per_kwh` may be supplied, every field is optional, but at least one must be present. Because the utilization ratio is recomputed against the new allowance and the posture is re-derived in the same request, **lowering the allowance below the already reserved-plus-consumed total freezes the plant with no production write having occurred at all.** The second surface sets `permit_status` alone. Suspending the permit forces `FROZEN` irrespective of emissions or removals and runs the hold cascade of §2.8 with `hold_reason` written as `PERMIT_SUSPENDED`; reinstating it re-derives the posture from emissions and removals and lifts the system-imposed holds whose `hold_reason` was `PLANT_FROZEN` or `PERMIT_SUSPENDED`. Operator-placed holds survive a permit reinstatement.

Three read-only ledgers make the plant's conserved quantities auditable. The emission ledger returns `monthly_lead_emission_permit_g`, `emission_reserved_g`, `emission_consumed_g`, `emission_utilization_ratio`, `compliance_posture` and `open_medical_removal_count`, plus a `lines` array holding the per-run reservation and consumption entries that sum to those totals. The lead mass balance returns `lead_initial_inventory_kg`, `lead_inventory_kg`, `lead_in_field_kg`, `lead_recycled_kg`, the closing `lead_mass_variance_kg` which must read 0.000, the cumulative `core_lead_variance_kg`, and `core_deposit_liability_cents`. The fleet analysis aggregates the plant's accepted sizing quotes and returns `accepted_request_count`, the summed `recommended_battery_count` and `recommended_charger_count`, the summed `fleet_capital_cents`, and a `lines` array with the per-request contributions. Listing plants supports optional `permit_status` and `compliance_posture` filters and returns newest first.

The four mass quantities are not free-standing counters but recomputations over the plant's shipped units, and they are re-derived on every shipment and on every core receipt or rejection. On-hand lead is the opening inventory minus the summed lead content of every unit ever shipped from this plant, rounded half up to three decimal places. In-field lead is the summed lead content of the plant's units whose `core_status` is `DEPOSIT_HELD`, `RETURN_OPEN` or `IN_TRANSIT`. Recycled lead is the summed lead content of the plant's units whose `core_status` is `RECEIVED`, `RECYCLED` or `REJECTED`. The closing variance is the opening inventory minus the sum of those three partitions and must be 0.000. Separately, `core_lead_variance_kg` is the summed signed difference between each unit's `received_lead_kg` and its design's lead content, over units whose `core_status` is `RECEIVED` or `RECYCLED`, rounded half up to three decimal places — this is a regulatory reconciliation figure and is expected to be non-zero. Units with `core_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE` contribute 0.000 to every term.

### 3.2. Enrolling Workers, Monitoring Exposure and Medical Removal

Employees are enrolled by a `PlantAdmin` with the employing `plant_id`, the employee's `full_name`, their `job_class` — one of `ENGINEER`, `PRODUCTION`, `QUALITY`, `SAFETY` or `MAINTENANCE` — and their `hire_date` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant. A new worker starts with `removal_status` `ACTIVE`, `removal_count` 0, `exposure_index` 0, `exposure_index_variant` `NONE` and `last_sample_at` null. The worker roster may be listed with optional `plant_id`, `job_class` and `removal_status` filters.

Industrial-hygiene sampling is the `SafetyOfficer`'s exclusive province, and it additionally demands that the acting worker's own `job_class` be `SAFETY`. Each sample row records the monitored employee as `worker_id`, resolved from the addressed worker rather than from the payload, and declares its `sample_type` as either `AIR` or `BLOOD`, together with the `sampled_at` instant at which it was taken. An `AIR` sample carries `air_lead_ug_per_m3` (zero or greater, two decimal places) and `sample_duration_minutes` (at least 1 and at most 960), and must not carry the blood field. A `BLOOD` sample carries `blood_lead_ug_per_dl` (zero or greater, one decimal place) and must not carry either air field. The sampling instant must not lie in the logical future and must not precede the worker's `hire_date`; a violation of either bound is `ERR_EVENT_TIME_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Samples are append-only: they are never edited, and the only channel that returns them is the worker's own sample listing, which supports an optional `sample_type` filter and orders results by `sampled_at` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tiebreak.

Each sample recomputes the worker's exposure index, and the modality of the triggering sample selects which of two arithmetically different variants runs. The **90-day air time-weighted-average variant** runs when the triggering sample is `AIR`. Its window is every `AIR` sample of this worker whose `sampled_at` is at or after the logical clock minus 90 days and at or before the logical clock — the lower bound is inclusive, so a sample landing exactly on the 90-day boundary is included. Within that window the time-weighted average is the sum, over samples, of the airborne concentration multiplied by that sample's duration in minutes, divided by the sum of those durations, rounded half up to three decimal places. The index is that average divided by the permissible airborne exposure limit of 50 micrograms per cubic metre, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. The **180-day blood peak variant** runs when the triggering sample is `BLOOD`. Its window is every `BLOOD` sample of this worker whose `sampled_at` is at or after the logical clock minus 180 days, again with an inclusive lower bound. The index is the single highest blood-lead reading in that window divided by the blood-lead limit of 30 micrograms per decilitre, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. Air samples never enter the blood variant and blood samples never enter the air variant, so the two variants read disjoint slices of the same append-only log and will generally disagree for the same worker on the same day: a single very high short air sample is diluted by long clean ones, whereas a single very high blood reading dominates for the whole 180 days.

Whichever variant ran writes its result into the worker's `exposure_index` and records `exposure_index_variant` as `AIR_TWA_90D` or `BLOOD_PEAK_180D`. The sample row itself is stamped with `resulting_worker_index`, `resulting_variant` and the boolean `triggered_removal`, and the worker's `last_sample_at` is set to the latest sampling instant. When the resulting index reaches 100.00 or more, the worker moves `ACTIVE → MEDICAL_REMOVAL`, `removal_count` is incremented by 1, `triggered_removal` is recorded as true on the sample, and the environmental cascade of §2.8 runs through the plant's removal count and posture. The comparison is exact: an index of exactly 100.00 removes the worker and an index of 99.99 does not.

A read-only exposure view exposes both variants side by side without writing anything. It returns `air_twa_90d_index`, `blood_peak_180d_index`, the higher of the two as `governing_index`, the name of the variant that governs as `governing_variant`, a boolean `removal_eligible` indicating whether the governing index has reached the removal threshold, and a `sample_counts` object describing how many samples of each modality fell inside each window.

Returning a removed worker to duty is the `SafetyOfficer`'s decision but it is evidence-gated. The clearance payload carries only an optional `clearance_note`. The worker must currently be in `MEDICAL_REMOVAL`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_WORKER_NOT_REMOVED`. There must exist a `BLOOD` sample for that worker whose `sampled_at` is at or after the logical clock minus 30 days and whose `blood_lead_ug_per_dl` is strictly below the return threshold of 20.0 micrograms per decilitre; absent such evidence the request is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_EVIDENCE_MISSING`. On success the worker moves `MEDICAL_REMOVAL → ACTIVE`, the exposure index is recomputed from the clearing blood sample under the 180-day blood peak variant, and `removal_count` is deliberately **not** decremented — the lifetime removal history is permanent. The plant's `open_medical_removal_count` is decremented, its posture is re-derived, and the compensation path of §2.8 may lift freeze-driven holds on that plant's lots.

### 3.3. Authoring, Reviewing and Superseding Cell Designs

A `DesignEngineer` drafts the engineering record that every downstream lot is built against. The drafting payload carries the catalogue `code` (unique across all designs), the `chemistry` as either `LEAD_ACID` or `LITHIUM_LFP`, the `plate_type` as one of `TUBULAR`, `FLAT_PASTED` or `PRISMATIC_CELL`, the series cell count `cells_per_battery` (at least 1), the per-cell `cell_nominal_voltage_v` (strictly positive, two decimal places), the rated `nominal_capacity_ah` at the six-hour rate (strictly positive, one decimal place), the `rated_cycles` and `warranty_months` (each at least 1), the assembled `pack_weight_kg` (strictly positive, one decimal place), the `lead_content_kg` per battery (three decimal places), the `list_price_cents` (strictly positive), and optionally the `formation_charge_factor`, the `retest_tightening_enabled` flag and a `supersedes_design_id` naming a predecessor design this one replaces. The formation charge factor expresses ampere-hours returned per ampere-hour nominal during formation charging; it defaults to 1.15 and must be at least 1.00 and at most 2.00 with two decimal places. The retest-tightening flag defaults to true.

Chemistry and construction must agree, and this is checked before anything else about the design is considered: a `LEAD_ACID` design must declare `TUBULAR` or `FLAT_PASTED` plates and a lead content strictly greater than 0.000, whereas a `LITHIUM_LFP` design must declare `PRISMATIC_CELL` plates and a lead content of exactly 0.000. Violations surface as `ERR_PLATE_TYPE_INCOMPATIBLE`. The system derives `nominal_pack_voltage_v` at draft time as `cells_per_battery` multiplied by `cell_nominal_voltage_v`, rounded half up to one decimal place. A new design starts with `approval_state` `DRAFT`, `revision` 1, `approved_revision` null, and both `submitted_by_worker_id` and `approved_by_worker_id` null.

Editing an unapproved design is permitted only while `approval_state` is `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; any other state refuses the edit with `ERR_DESIGN_STATE_INVALID`. The editable fields are `nominal_capacity_ah`, `rated_cycles`, `warranty_months`, `pack_weight_kg`, `lead_content_kg`, `formation_charge_factor`, `list_price_cents` and `retest_tightening_enabled`, each optional. Every accepted edit increments `revision` by exactly 1 and must leave the chemistry/plate/lead-content coherence rule satisfied afterwards. A `DRAFT` design edited in place stays `DRAFT` — a self-loop. A `CHANGES_REQUESTED` design edited in place moves `CHANGES_REQUESTED → DRAFT`, and that edit is the only mechanism that reopens it for a further submission; this is what closes the review cycle.

Three review actions drive the approval axis, each supplied as an `action` value alongside an optional `review_comment`. `SUBMIT` requires `DRAFT` and moves the design `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, stamping `submitted_by_worker_id` with the acting worker. `REQUEST_CHANGES` requires `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires that the acting worker is not the submitter, and moves the design `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` without touching `revision`; `submitted_by_worker_id` is retained precisely so the segregation-of-duties bar still applies on the next approval attempt. `APPROVE` requires `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires the acting worker's `job_class` to be `ENGINEER`, and requires that the acting worker is not the submitter; it moves the design `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, copies the current `revision` into `approved_revision`, and stamps `approved_by_worker_id`. Any review action attempted from the wrong state is `ERR_DESIGN_STATE_INVALID`; a self-approval or self-change-request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Approval also retires the past. When the newly approved design names a `supersedes_design_id` and that predecessor is itself `APPROVED`, the predecessor moves `APPROVED → RETIRED` in the same request. A `RETIRED` design is terminal: it leaves the fleet-sizing candidate pool immediately and can no longer back a new production lot. Existing lots are entirely unaffected — each keeps the `design_revision_pinned` it captured at creation, so a supersession never mutates work in flight. Designs may be listed with optional `chemistry`, `approval_state`, `submitted_by_worker_id`, `approved_by_worker_id` and `supersedes_design_id` filters.

### 3.4. Opening a Lot, Advancing Its Stages and Holding It

A `ProductionSupervisor` opens a production lot with the manufacturing `plant_id`, the `cell_design_id` being built, a `lot_code` unique within that plant, and a `quantity` of batteries planned — at least 1 and at most 500. The design must be `APPROVED` at that instant; a lot opened against a `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `RETIRED` design is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED`. The lot copies the design's `chemistry` into its own `chemistry` field and pins `design_revision_pinned` from the design's `approved_revision`, which is what insulates the lot from later editorial revisions. The copied chemistry must appear in the plant's `licensed_chemistries`, otherwise `ERR_CHEMISTRY_NOT_LICENSED`. A new lot starts with `process_state` `PLANNED`, `env_hold_status` `CLEAR`, `hold_reason` null, `retest_count` 0, `sampling_plan_revision` 0, every test counter at 0, `released_at` and `last_test_recorded_by_worker_id` null, `lot_capacity_yield_pct` null, and — importantly — `sample_size` and `acceptance_number` both null, because no sampling plan exists until one is drawn.

Manufacturing progress is a single-step ladder: `PLANNED → PLATE_PRODUCTION → FORMATION → ASSEMBLY`. Each step is requested by naming the target in `to_state`, and `to_state` must be the immediate successor of the current state — skipping a stage is refused with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. The final hop into `LOT_TESTING` is deliberately **not** available through this ladder; it happens only when the acceptance sampling plan is first drawn, as described in §3.7. Entering `PLATE_PRODUCTION` requires the lot to be `PLANNED`, its `env_hold_status` to be `CLEAR`, the plant not to be `FROZEN`, and the lot's chemistry to be licensed at the plant. Entering `FORMATION` requires `PLATE_PRODUCTION`, a `CLEAR` hold, and the stricter posture condition that the plant be `OPEN` — a `RESTRICTED` plant may finish work already in flight but may not begin hazardous formation, and the refusal is `ERR_PLANT_RESTRICTED`. Entering `ASSEMBLY` requires `FORMATION`, a `CLEAR` hold, at least one formation run of the lot in `COMPLETED`, no formation run of the lot still in `SCHEDULED` or `CHARGING`, and a `failed_formation_count` of exactly 0 — a single formation capacity failure blocks the lot from assembly until the batch is re-formed. Once in `ASSEMBLY`, the lot becomes eligible for assembly creation.

Environmental holds are the `SafetyOfficer`'s lever and they are orthogonal to the process ladder. The hold surface accepts an `action` of `HOLD` or `RELEASE_HOLD` plus an optional `hold_reason`. On a `HOLD` the reason must be `OPERATOR_HOLD`; the two system-only reasons `PLANT_FROZEN` and `PERMIT_SUSPENDED` may never be supplied by a client. A hold action that would not change anything — holding an already `HELD` lot, or releasing an already `CLEAR` one — is refused with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. Placing a hold moves `env_hold_status` `CLEAR → HELD` and retains the reason; lifting it moves `HELD → CLEAR` and clears `hold_reason` to null. Because the axis is independent of `process_state`, a lot can legitimately be `ASSEMBLY` and `HELD`, or `LOT_TESTING` and `HELD`, and when the hold lifts the lot resumes at exactly the stage where it stopped. System-imposed holds obey the same axis: a hold arriving from the plant freeze or a permit suspension also aborts every formation run of that lot still in `CHARGING`, with `abort_reason` `ENV_HOLD_CASCADE`, and those aborted runs are never resurrected when the hold lifts. Lots already `RELEASED` or `SCRAPPED` cannot be placed on hold at all.

While a lot is `HELD`, every production, verification, sampling, testing and release step on it is refused with `ERR_LOT_ENV_HELD`. Lots may be listed with optional `plant_id`, `cell_design_id`, `process_state`, `env_hold_status` and `last_test_recorded_by_worker_id` filters.

### 3.5. Formation Charging: Bay Windows, Energy Pricing and the Emission Quota

Formation charging is where the plant spends both money and regulatory headroom, so it is the most heavily instrumented step in the pipeline. A `PlantAdmin` first commissions the physical bays. A bay declares its owning `plant_id`, a `bay_code` unique within that plant, its nameplate rectifier power as `rated_power_kw` (strictly positive, two decimal places), and its mandatory ventilation `cooldown_minutes`, which must be between 0 and 240 inclusive and defaults to 45. A commissioned bay starts with `bay_status` `AVAILABLE`. Bays may later be taken out of service or returned by supplying `bay_status` — the only two values being `AVAILABLE` and `MAINTENANCE` — and their `cooldown_minutes` may be adjusted at the same time. A bay in `MAINTENANCE` accepts no new runs and no run starts, and both refusals are `ERR_BAY_UNAVAILABLE`. Bays may be listed with optional `plant_id` and `bay_status` filters.

Scheduling a run is the `ProductionSupervisor`'s act and it reserves two scarce things at once. The payload names the `lot_id` being formed, the `bay_id` to occupy, and the planned window as `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`. The lot must be in `FORMATION`, its hold must be `CLEAR`, the plant must not be `FROZEN`, and the bay must belong to the same plant as the lot — a bay from another site is `ERR_PLANT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The planned end must be strictly after the planned start, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. The run is created with `run_state` `SCHEDULED`, `actual_start_at` and `actual_end_at` null, `metered_energy_kwh` null, `final_charge_cents` and `consumed_emission_g` at 0, and `charge_factor_used` frozen from the `formation_charge_factor` of the lot's pinned cell design so that later design edits cannot re-price a run already on the books.

**Bay occupancy is single-tenant with a two-sided cooldown buffer.** A requested window collides with an existing run on the same bay whose `run_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHARGING` when both of these hold at once: the requested start is strictly earlier than that existing run's end advanced by the bay's `cooldown_minutes`, and the requested end advanced by the same `cooldown_minutes` is strictly later than that existing run's start. A collision is refused with `ERR_BAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Runs in `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` no longer participate in this scan, which is exactly how a finished or abandoned run frees its bay: `bay_status` itself is never changed by starting, completing or aborting a run — occupancy is expressed purely through this window reservation.

**Provisional pricing runs at scheduling time**, before any energy has flowed. Two candidate energy figures are formed and the smaller is taken. The theoretical figure is the lot's `quantity` multiplied by the design's `cells_per_battery`, multiplied by the design's `nominal_capacity_ah`, multiplied by the design's `cell_nominal_voltage_v`, multiplied by the run's `charge_factor_used`, then divided by 1000 to convert watt-hours to kilowatt-hours. The nameplate figure is the bay's `rated_power_kw` multiplied by the scheduled window's duration in hours, multiplied by a bay utilization factor of 0.85 — rectifiers are never assumed to run flat out. `estimated_energy_kwh` is the smaller of the two, rounded half up to two decimal places.

That energy is then split across two tariff bands. The **peak band** runs from 08:00 inclusive to 20:00 exclusive UTC; every other hour of the day is off-peak. The provisional variant bands the **scheduled** window: `scheduled_peak_hours` is the number of hours of the scheduled window lying inside the peak band and `scheduled_offpeak_hours` the number lying outside it, each rounded half up to four decimal places. A window spanning midnight is split per UTC calendar day before banding. Total hours is the sum of the two band figures. Peak energy is `estimated_energy_kwh` multiplied by peak hours divided by total hours, rounded half up to two decimal places; off-peak energy is `estimated_energy_kwh` minus that peak energy, rounded half up to two decimal places, so the two band energies always re-sum to the total.

**The rounding order of the money is pinned and is not negotiable.** Each band is priced and rounded to whole cents *first*, and only then are the two whole-cent amounts added: the peak energy multiplied by the plant's `peak_tariff_cents_per_kwh` is rounded half up to a whole cent, the off-peak energy multiplied by the plant's `offpeak_tariff_cents_per_kwh` is rounded half up to a whole cent, and the charge is the sum of those two rounded values. The alternative order — adding the two band products and rounding once — is explicitly rejected and yields a different cent value for the same inputs. At scheduling the result lands in `provisional_charge_cents`.

The same energy figure drives the emission reservation. Emission grams are the energy in kilowatt-hours multiplied by the lead-equivalent emission factor for the lot's chemistry — 2.5 grams per kilowatt-hour for `LEAD_ACID` and 0.5 grams per kilowatt-hour for `LITHIUM_LFP` — rounded half up to two decimal places, and at scheduling the result is stored as `reserved_emission_g` and added to the plant's `emission_reserved_g`. Before the reservation is accepted the quota is checked: the plant's `emission_reserved_g` plus its `emission_consumed_g` plus this run's `reserved_emission_g`, divided by `monthly_lead_emission_permit_g`, must be at most 1.0000. This gate compares the quotient exactly as computed and does **not** round it to four decimal places first, unlike the `emission_utilization_ratio` that §2.7 stores on the plant: a projected total of 255.00 grams against a 254.99-gram permit exceeds the quota and is refused, even though rounding that same quotient to four decimal places would read exactly 1.0000. A reservation that lands exactly on 1.0000 is **accepted** and immediately freezes the plant through the posture recompute; only a result strictly above 1.0000 is refused, with `ERR_EMISSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. After a successful reservation the plant's `emission_utilization_ratio` and `compliance_posture` are re-derived in the same request.

Starting a run moves it `SCHEDULED → CHARGING` and stamps `actual_start_at`, which may be supplied explicitly and otherwise defaults to the logical clock. A start requires the run to be `SCHEDULED`, the lot to be in `FORMATION` with a `CLEAR` hold, the bay to be `AVAILABLE`, and — the strictest cross-entity gate in the system — the plant's posture to be `OPEN`. Both `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN` block a start, surfacing as `ERR_PLANT_RESTRICTED` and `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN` respectively. The reservation is not re-booked at start; it simply stays reserved, so no double booking occurs.

Completing a run requires it to be `CHARGING` and carries the operator's `metered_energy_kwh` reading, the post-formation `formation_capacity_pct` expressed as a percentage of the design's nominal capacity, and optionally an explicit `actual_end_at` which otherwise defaults to the logical clock. That end instant must be strictly after `actual_start_at`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. The metered reading must be strictly positive and at most twice `estimated_energy_kwh`; anything else is `ERR_METERED_ENERGY_OUT_OF_RANGE`. On success the run moves `CHARGING → COMPLETED` and is re-priced on the **final metered variant**, which differs from the provisional variant in both of its inputs: the energy is the metered reading rather than the nameplate-capped estimate, and the band split is taken over the *actual* window running from `actual_start_at` up to `actual_end_at`, producing `actual_peak_hours` and `actual_offpeak_hours` at four decimal places. A run that started late is therefore banded differently from how it was quoted, and for identical lot data the two variants generally disagree in both energy and band mix. The proportional allocation and the per-band pinned rounding order are identical to the provisional variant, and the result lands in `final_charge_cents`. Emission grams are recomputed from the metered energy under the same chemistry factor and stored as `consumed_emission_g`.

Completion settles three ledgers in one write: the plant's `emission_reserved_g` decreases by this run's `reserved_emission_g`, its `emission_consumed_g` increases by this run's `consumed_emission_g`, and its `formation_energy_charge_cents` increases by `final_charge_cents`. The utilization ratio and posture are re-derived afterwards, so a completion whose metered energy exceeded the estimate can restrict or freeze the plant on the spot. Completion also validates quality: `capacity_validation_result` becomes `PASS` when `formation_capacity_pct` is at least 92.00 and `FAIL` otherwise, and a `FAIL` increments the lot's `failed_formation_count`, which is what later blocks the lot from entering `ASSEMBLY`.

Aborting a run is available to both a `ProductionSupervisor` and a `SafetyOfficer`, applies to a run in either `SCHEDULED` or `CHARGING`, and requires an `abort_reason` of `OPERATOR_ABORT`, `BAY_FAULT` or `ENV_HOLD_CASCADE`. The run moves to `ABORTED`, `actual_end_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the reservation is released in full with nothing consumed and nothing charged: `final_charge_cents` and `consumed_emission_g` stay 0 and `metered_energy_kwh` stays null. The run keeps its `provisional_charge_cents` as a historical estimate but that figure never rolls into the plant's `formation_energy_charge_cents`. Aborting from `SCHEDULED` and aborting from `CHARGING` are symmetrical in every respect: both release the reservation, both re-derive the plant's utilization ratio and posture, and both free the bay window. Attempting to abort a run already `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` is `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. Runs may be listed with optional `lot_id`, `bay_id` and `run_state` filters, ordered by `scheduled_start_at` ascending.

### 3.6. Assembling Batteries and Verifying Them on Two Independent Axes

Once a lot has reached `ASSEMBLY`, a `ProductionSupervisor` builds physical batteries out of it. Each assembly names its owning `lot_id` — the lot must be in `ASSEMBLY`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` — and carries a `serial_number` that must be unique across all assemblies in the system, with a collision reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The `module_count` is the number of modules seated in the case and must be between 1 and 48 inclusive. The `case_type` is one of `STEEL_TRAY`, `POLY_CASE` or `MODULAR_RACK` and is what selects the fastener torque specification. Two acceptance criteria are frozen onto the assembly at build time so that later engineering changes cannot retroactively re-judge a battery already on the line: `torque_spec_nm`, the torque specification for the chosen `case_type`, recorded to one decimal place, and `ir_min_megaohm`, the minimum insulation resistance for the chemistry of the lot's pinned design, also recorded to one decimal place. Both are supplied on the creation payload and both must be strictly positive. A new assembly initialises `torque_status` and `insulation_status` to `PENDING`, `certification_status` to `PENDING`, `rework_count` to 0, and `measured_torque_nm` and `measured_ir_megaohm` to null. Assemblies cannot be built while the plant is `FROZEN` or while the lot's hold is set.

**Verification is two independent axes joined at the end, not a single linear inspection.** A `QualityInspector` records one axis at a time by naming a `verification_type`, and the two branches carry disjoint payloads.

For `verification_type` `TORQUE` the inspector supplies `measured_torque_nm`, the observed fastener torque, together with `connection_count`, the number of intermodule connections actually torqued. The connection count is a structural cross-check: it must equal exactly twice the assembly's `module_count`, and any other value is refused with `ERR_CONNECTION_COUNT_MISMATCH` — the inspector has either missed connections or worked on the wrong battery, and no verdict is recorded. When the count checks out, `torque_status` becomes `PASS` when the reading falls inside the window from `torque_spec_nm` multiplied by 0.95 up to `torque_spec_nm` multiplied by 1.10, with **both ends inclusive** — a reading exactly on either boundary passes — and `FAIL` for any reading below or above that window. Torque can be too tight as well as too loose, so this is a two-sided window, not a floor. The reading is stamped onto `measured_torque_nm` whichever way the verdict falls.

For `verification_type` `INSULATION_RESISTANCE` the inspector supplies `measured_ir_megaohm`, the observed insulation resistance, together with `test_voltage_v`, the applied test potential. The test voltage must be at least 500 volts; a megohmmeter run at a lower potential does not constitute a valid insulation test and is rejected as a validation failure. `insulation_status` becomes `PASS` when `measured_ir_megaohm` is greater than or equal to `ir_min_megaohm` — equality passes — and `FAIL` when it is below. This axis is a one-sided floor: there is no such thing as too much insulation. The reading is stamped onto `measured_ir_megaohm` either way.

The two axes are recorded in either order and neither depends on the other's verdict; an assembly may sit indefinitely with one axis `PASS` and the other `PENDING`. **Each axis may be recorded only once per rework generation.** A second attempt to record an axis that already holds a `PASS` or `FAIL` verdict is refused with `ERR_ASSEMBLY_AXIS_ALREADY_RECORDED`, regardless of which verdict is already there and regardless of what the new reading would say — inspection results are not overwritten in place. The two branches are symmetrical in this respect: re-recording torque and re-recording insulation resistance are refused identically.

After every recorded verdict `certification_status` is re-derived from the axis pair as a pure join, never set directly. It is `CERTIFIED` only when both `torque_status` and `insulation_status` are `PASS`; it is `REJECTED` when either axis is `FAIL`; and it remains `PENDING` for as long as either axis is still `PENDING`. A `FAIL` on the first axis therefore rejects the assembly immediately, before the second axis has been touched at all. The fourth value, `REVOKED`, is never produced by verification; it is written only by the lot quarantine cascade described in section 2.8, which strips certification from every assembly of a quarantined lot.

**Rework is the escape hatch and it resets a whole generation.** A `ProductionSupervisor` may rework an assembly, optionally attaching a `rework_note`, but only when `certification_status` is `REJECTED` or `REVOKED`. Reworking a `PENDING` assembly whose axes have not both been decided, or a `CERTIFIED` one, is refused with `ERR_ASSEMBLY_STATE_INVALID`. Rework is permitted at most three times per assembly: the operation requires `rework_count` to be less than 3, and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_REACHED`. A successful rework resets `torque_status` and `insulation_status` to `PENDING`, clears `measured_torque_nm` and `measured_ir_megaohm` back to null, resets `certification_status` to `PENDING`, and increments `rework_count` by 1. Because both axes are cleared, both must be re-recorded from scratch — a rework does not preserve the axis that had passed, and the once-per-generation lock is released for both axes together. Rework is refused while the lot's hold is set.

Assemblies may be listed with optional `lot_id`, `torque_status`, `insulation_status` and `certification_status` filters, ordered by `created_at` ascending then `id` ascending, and any single assembly may be read to see both axes and the derived certification.

Both verification and rework are performed while the owning lot is still in `ASSEMBLY`; once acceptance sampling has moved the lot on to `LOT_TESTING`, its assemblies are no longer subject to axis recording or rework except through the quarantine-and-retest loop described below.

### 3.7. Acceptance Sampling, Chamber Scheduling and Lot Disposition

Acceptance testing is the quota-allocation half of the domain: a scarce chamber cap is shared out among concurrent tests, and a statistically drawn sample decides whether a whole lot may ship.

A `PlantAdmin` commissions test chambers, each naming its `plant_id`, a `chamber_code` unique within that plant, and a `power_cap_kw` — the aggregate simultaneous draw the chamber can sustain, strictly positive at two decimal places. A chamber starts `AVAILABLE` and may later be moved between `AVAILABLE` and `MAINTENANCE` through `chamber_status`, with `power_cap_kw` adjustable at the same time. Chambers may be listed with optional `plant_id` and `chamber_status` filters and read individually; a missing chamber is `ERR_TEST_CHAMBER_NOT_FOUND`.

**The sampling plan is drawn, not chosen.** A `QualityInspector` draws the lot's acceptance sampling plan, optionally recording a `plan_reason`, and must supply `expected_lot_version` matching the lot's current `version` as described in section 2.6. The **first** draw requires the lot to be in `ASSEMBLY` and moves it `ASSEMBLY → LOT_TESTING`; a **redraw** requires the lot to already be in `LOT_TESTING` and leaves `process_state` untouched. Any other state is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. The first draw additionally requires at least one assembly of the lot to have reached `CERTIFIED` — there is nothing to sample otherwise. Drawing is refused while the plant is `FROZEN` or the lot's hold is set.

The plan itself is computed from four inputs that interact non-linearly. The base sample size is the ceiling of the square root of the lot's `quantity`. Lithium chemistry adds a fixed offset of 2 to that base, because lithium acceptance sampling is tightened by regulation; lead-acid adds nothing. The result is then clamped from both ends to lie between 3 and 12 inclusive — the floor and the ceiling both genuinely bind, so a lot of 4 batteries still draws 3 samples and a lot of 10 000 draws no more than 12 (or the lithium offset applied before the clamp, which the ceiling then absorbs). From that clamped size an acceptance number is read off a tier table: 0 when the size is 4 or fewer, 1 when it is 8 or fewer, and 2 otherwise. Finally the retest tightening branch is applied: when the lot's `retest_count` is at least 1 **and** the pinned design's `retest_tightening_enabled` flag is true, `sample_size` becomes the smaller of twice the clamped size and 20, and `acceptance_number` becomes the tiered value minus 1 but never below 0. When either condition is absent, `sample_size` and `acceptance_number` are simply the clamped size and its tier value. Everything here is integer arithmetic: the square root is taken to its ceiling and nothing else is rounded. Each draw increments `sampling_plan_revision` by 1 and increments the lot's `version` by 1.

Drawing a plan does **not** clear the acceptance counters. `completed_test_count`, `failed_test_count` and `passed_cycle_life_count` survive a redraw, so a tightened plan is judged against the evidence accumulated since the last disposition rather than against a blank slate. On the very first draw this is immaterial, because a lot arriving from `ASSEMBLY` has those counters at 0, `lot_capacity_yield_pct` at null and `last_test_recorded_by_worker_id` at null already. Counters are zeroed at exactly one place — a `RETEST` disposition — and nowhere else. The derived yield, by contrast, is always scoped to the **current** plan window, so evidence from a superseded plan drops out of the yield figure even though it remains in the counters.

**Scheduling a test allocates chamber power over time.** A `QualityInspector` schedules a test naming the `assembly_id` under test, the `chamber_id` to occupy, the `test_type` (`CAPACITY` or `CYCLE_LIFE`), the window as `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, and the `draw_kw` the test will pull, strictly positive at two decimal places. The owning lot must be in `ASSEMBLY` or `LOT_TESTING` — tests may legitimately be booked before the plan is drawn — and its hold must be clear. The assembly's `certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED`; a `PENDING`, `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` assembly is refused with `ERR_ASSEMBLY_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The chamber must belong to the same plant as the assembly's lot, otherwise `ERR_PLANT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, and its `chamber_status` must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_CHAMBER_UNAVAILABLE`. The end must be strictly after the start, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Unlike a formation bay, a chamber is **not** single-tenant. It admits any number of concurrent tests provided the aggregate draw stays under its cap at every instant. Formally, over every instant covered by the requested window — treated as half-open, including the start instant and excluding the end instant — the sum of `draw_kw` across the `SCHEDULED` tests on that same chamber whose windows overlap that instant, plus the requested `draw_kw`, must be at most the chamber's `power_cap_kw`. A total landing exactly on the cap is **accepted**; only a total strictly above it is refused, with `ERR_CHAMBER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the intervals are half-open, adjacent windows never overlap: a test starting at the precise instant another ends contends with nothing. Only `SCHEDULED` tests occupy the cap — a `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` test releases its draw immediately, which is why the quarantine cascade's voiding of scheduled tests frees chamber capacity as a side effect. A new test initialises `test_state` to `SCHEDULED` with `measured_capacity_ah`, `cycles_completed`, `result` and `yield_pct` all null.

**Recording a result closes the window and can quarantine the lot in the same breath.** A `QualityInspector` records `measured_capacity_ah`, the measured discharge capacity at one decimal place and strictly positive, and for a cycle-life test also `cycles_completed`, a count of at least 0. The discriminator lives on the stored row rather than the payload: `cycles_completed` is required when the test's `test_type` is `CYCLE_LIFE` and must be omitted when it is `CAPACITY`, and either mismatch is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_LIFE_SAMPLE_MISSING`. The test must be `SCHEDULED` and the logical clock must have reached or passed `scheduled_end_at` — a result cannot be filed before the window it describes has closed — and any violation of either is `ERR_CAPACITY_TEST_STATE_INVALID`. The lot must be in `LOT_TESTING` with a clear hold.

The verdict is computed against the **pinned design revision** of the assembly's lot, never against the design's current values. First, `yield_pct` is the measured capacity divided by the pinned design's `nominal_capacity_ah`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places, and is stored on every test regardless of verdict. Then the two branches diverge. A `CAPACITY` test is a single-criterion check: it is a `PASS` when the measured capacity is greater than or equal to the pinned `nominal_capacity_ah` multiplied by 0.95 and **rounded to one decimal place before the comparison**, and a `FAIL` otherwise; a reading exactly equal to that rounded threshold passes. A `CYCLE_LIFE` test is a two-criterion conjunction: it is a `PASS` only when `cycles_completed` is greater than or equal to the ceiling of the pinned design's `rated_cycles` multiplied by 0.90, **and** the measured capacity is greater than or equal to the pinned `nominal_capacity_ah` multiplied by 0.80 and rounded to one decimal place — the end-of-life capacity floor, which is deliberately laxer than the fresh-capacity floor because a battery at the end of its rated cycle count is expected to have faded. Failing either criterion fails the test. Both branches compare with inclusive thresholds, and the intermediate rounding to one decimal place is applied to the threshold, not to the reading.

Recording a result writes several things at once. The test moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, its `result` and `yield_pct` are stamped, and `recorded_by_worker_id` is set to the acting worker and copied onto the lot's `last_test_recorded_by_worker_id`, which is what the release segregation-of-duties rule later reads. The lot's `completed_test_count` increases by 1. A `FAIL` additionally increases `failed_test_count` by 1 — the first hop of the quarantine cascade. A `PASS` on a `CYCLE_LIFE` test additionally increases `passed_cycle_life_count` by 1; a `PASS` on a `CAPACITY` test does not, since the cycle-life counter exists specifically to prove that at least one durability sample was tested. When the incremented `failed_test_count` becomes strictly greater than the lot's `acceptance_number`, the lot moves `LOT_TESTING → QUARANTINED` inside the same request, with the assembly-revocation and test-voiding hops following as described in section 2.8.

Finally the lot's `lot_capacity_yield_pct` is recomputed. The window is the set of tests belonging to assemblies of this lot whose `test_state` is `COMPLETED`, whose `result` is `PASS`, and whose `created_at` is at or after the instant at which the lot's **current** sampling plan was drawn — an inclusive lower bound with no upper bound. Failed tests and voided tests are excluded, and so are tests carried over from a superseded plan revision. When that window is empty the yield is null. Otherwise **the order of operations is pinned**: each test's `yield_pct` was already rounded half up to two decimal places when its result was recorded, and the lot figure is the arithmetic mean of those already-rounded values, rounded half up to two decimal places again. Rounding is therefore applied twice, once per test and once on the mean. The pooled alternative — summing raw measured capacities and dividing by the sample count times nominal capacity — is explicitly **rejected** and produces a different number. The summation runs over the window ordered by `created_at` ascending; addition is commutative but the order is pinned so that a partial-window audit reproduces exactly.

**Disposition is the single decision surface for a lot's fate,** exercised by a `QualityInspector` who names an `action` and may attach a `disposition_note`. Four actions are available and each has a distinct source state.

`RELEASE` moves the lot `LOT_TESTING → RELEASED` and is the most heavily gated operation in the system. It requires the lot to be in `LOT_TESTING` with a clear hold, the plant not to be `FROZEN`, `completed_test_count` to be at least `sample_size`, `passed_cycle_life_count` to be at least 1, and `failed_test_count` to be at most `acceptance_number`. A shortfall on either sampling requirement — too few completed tests, or no passing cycle-life sample at all — is `ERR_SAMPLING_INCOMPLETE`, and a lot whose current sampling window holds zero completed tests fails there. Too many failures is `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_EXCEEDED`; note the asymmetry with the quarantine trigger, which fires on *strictly greater than* the acceptance number, so a lot sitting exactly on its acceptance number is neither quarantined nor blocked. On top of those, the acting worker's `job_class` must be `QUALITY` (`ERR_ACTOR_JOB_CLASS_MISMATCH`) and the acting worker must not be the one recorded in `last_test_recorded_by_worker_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). A successful release stamps `released_at` from the logical clock, recomputes and thereby freezes `lot_capacity_yield_pct` over the current window, and makes the lot's batteries shippable.

`QUARANTINE` moves the lot `LOT_TESTING → QUARANTINED` by inspector judgement rather than by counter arithmetic, and carries the same downstream cascade as an automatic quarantine: certified assemblies are revoked and their scheduled tests voided.

`RETEST` moves the lot `QUARANTINED → LOT_TESTING` and is the only counter-clearing operation in the system. It requires the lot to be `QUARANTINED` with a clear hold and `retest_count` strictly less than 2; a third attempt is `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED`. It increments `retest_count`, zeroes `completed_test_count`, `failed_test_count` and `passed_cycle_life_count` so that the tightened plan is judged on fresh evidence, and then **immediately redraws the plan itself** under the tightened branch, incrementing `sampling_plan_revision` by 1 — the plan is computed after `retest_count` has been incremented, so the very first retest already sees the tightened arithmetic, and no separate draw call is needed afterwards. It also restores the lot's assemblies: every assembly whose `certification_status` is `REVOKED` returns to `PENDING` with both `torque_status` and `insulation_status` reset to `PENDING`, so the whole verification pair must be re-established before new tests can be booked. The quarantine-and-retest loop is the only cycle in the lot lifecycle and it may run at most twice.

`SCRAP` moves the lot `QUARANTINED → SCRAPPED` and is terminal. Every assembly of the lot is set to `REJECTED` and every `SCHEDULED` test on those assemblies is `VOIDED`. Crucially, scrapping does **not** move the plant's lead ledger: lead leaves `lead_inventory_kg` only when a battery ships, so a scrapped lot never entered the mass balance and the lead conservation invariant is unaffected. `RETEST` and `SCRAP` both require the lot to be `QUARANTINED`; `RELEASE` and `QUARANTINE` both require `LOT_TESTING`. Any action attempted from the wrong state is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`.

**The quality summary read explains a refusal before it happens.** Any `PlantAdmin`, `ProductionSupervisor` or `QualityInspector` may read a lot's acceptance position and receives `sample_size`, `acceptance_number`, `sampling_plan_revision`, `completed_test_count`, `failed_test_count`, `passed_cycle_life_count`, `lot_capacity_yield_pct` and `release_blocked_by`. That last field is deliberately **actor-independent**: it reports, as a string, the first of `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LOT_ENV_HELD`, `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN`, `ERR_SAMPLING_INCOMPLETE` and `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_EXCEEDED` that currently holds, evaluated in exactly that order, and the literal string `NONE` when none of them holds. It never reports the two actor-dependent gates, `ERR_ACTOR_JOB_CLASS_MISMATCH` and `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, so a summary of `NONE` means "release would succeed for a suitably qualified inspector who did not record the last test", not "release would succeed for you".

Tests may be listed with optional `assembly_id`, `chamber_id`, `recorded_by_worker_id`, `test_type`, `test_state` and `result` filters, ordered by `scheduled_start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and read individually; a missing test is `ERR_CAPACITY_TEST_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.8. Sizing a Motive-Power Fleet and Matching a Charger

The sizing surface serves the commercial side of the plant: a customer describes a forklift application and the system answers with a specific pack, a specific charger, the fleet counts and the capital cost, all computed in a single request.

A `FleetEngineer` first catalogues chargers. A charger declares a `model_code` unique across the catalogue, the `chemistry_compatibility` it may service (`LEAD_ACID` or `LITHIUM_LFP`), its nominal DC `output_voltage_v` and its `output_amps`, both strictly positive at one decimal place, its `charge_profile` (`FERRO`, `HF_MODULAR` or `LITHIUM_CCCV`), and its `price_cents` as a strictly positive whole-cent integer. A new charger starts `AVAILABLE`. A charger may later be discontinued or returned to the catalogue through `charger_status`, and its `price_cents` revised at the same time; a `DISCONTINUED` charger is invisible to the selector but remains readable and keeps every historical selection that already names it. Chargers may be listed with optional `charge_profile` and `charger_status` filters and read individually; a missing charger is `ERR_CHARGER_NOT_FOUND`.

A sizing request describes the application. It names the quoting site as `plant_id`, a customer-facing `application_name`, the `forklift_class` (`CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III`), the `forklift_count` between 1 and 200 inclusive, the `shift_energy_kwh_per_shift` a single truck consumes in one shift (strictly positive, two decimal places), the `shifts_per_day` between 1 and 3 inclusive, the coldest operating `ambient_temp_c` between −30 and 50 inclusive at one decimal place, the truck system voltage as `required_voltage_v` (strictly positive, one decimal place), the battery compartment weight limit as `compartment_max_kg` (strictly positive, one decimal place), the `charge_window_hours` available between shifts (strictly positive and at most 24, two decimal places), and optionally a `chemistry_preference` of `LEAD_ACID`, `LITHIUM_LFP` or `ANY`, defaulting to `ANY`.

Three scaling factors are derived before any candidate is considered. `daily_energy_kwh` is `shift_energy_kwh_per_shift` multiplied by `shifts_per_day`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `temp_derate_factor` is a cold-weather oversizing multiplier: it is 1.0 plus one hundredth for every degree by which `ambient_temp_c` falls below the reference temperature of 15 degrees, with the shortfall floored at zero so that a warm site is never de-rated below 1.0, and the whole factor capped at 1.25 so that an arctic site is not oversized without limit; the result is rounded half up to four decimal places. `reserve_factor` is 1.15 when `shifts_per_day` is 2 or more and 1.05 when it is 1 — a multi-shift operation needs more headroom than a single-shift one.

**The required capacity depends on the candidate, so it is computed per candidate rather than once.** For a given candidate design, the required ampere-hours are `daily_energy_kwh` multiplied by 1000 to reach watt-hours, divided by `required_voltage_v`, divided by the depth-of-discharge cap for that design's chemistry — 0.80 for `LEAD_ACID`, because deeply discharging a flooded lead-acid pack destroys it, and 0.90 for `LITHIUM_LFP` — then multiplied by `temp_derate_factor` and by `reserve_factor`, with the final figure taken to its ceiling as a whole number of ampere-hours. A lithium candidate therefore needs fewer nameplate ampere-hours than a lead-acid candidate for exactly the same duty.

**The selector's dependency looks circular and its evaluation order is normative.** The pack choice depends on whether a charger can refill it inside the charge window, while charger feasibility depends on the pack's capacity. The order below is the contract, and any implementation that resolves the loop differently will select differently.

First, the eligible design pool is formed. A cell design is eligible when its `approval_state` is `APPROVED` — `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and `RETIRED` designs are all excluded, so a retired design can never be quoted again — and its `chemistry` satisfies the request's `chemistry_preference` (any chemistry passes when the preference is `ANY`), and its `nominal_pack_voltage_v` equals `required_voltage_v` **exactly**, with no tolerance band whatsoever, and its `pack_weight_kg` is less than or equal to `compartment_max_kg`, and its `nominal_capacity_ah` is greater than or equal to that design's own required ampere-hours. The size of this pool is recorded as `evaluated_design_count` on the persisted request, and lets the engineer see how much of the catalogue was in play.

Second, the pool is ordered by `nominal_capacity_ah` ascending, then by `pack_weight_kg` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending. The smallest sufficient pack wins: this is a least-cost-fit policy, not a largest-capacity policy. The tie-break chain **never** falls through to `id`, so two designs created at the same instant with identical capacity and weight are resolved by insertion order and not by identifier ordering.

Third, the ordered pool is walked and the first design that can actually be charged wins. For the design under consideration, the matchable chargers are those whose `charger_status` is `AVAILABLE`, whose `chemistry_compatibility` equals that design's chemistry, whose `output_voltage_v` equals `required_voltage_v` exactly, and whose recharge time fits the window. Recharge time is the design's `nominal_capacity_ah` multiplied by the depth-of-discharge cap for its chemistry — the pack is only refilling the part that was actually used — multiplied by the sizing charge factor for that chemistry, which is 1.15 for `LEAD_ACID` and 1.05 for `LITHIUM_LFP` to account for charge inefficiency, then divided by the charger's `output_amps` and rounded half up to two decimal places. That figure must be less than or equal to `charge_window_hours`; a pair landing exactly on the window is feasible. If at least one charger is feasible, the search stops immediately at this design: `selected_cell_design_id` is set to it, `selected_charger_id` to the cheapest feasible charger ordered by `price_cents` ascending then `output_amps` ascending then `created_at` ascending, `selected_charge_hours` to that pair's recharge hours, and `required_ah` to the required ampere-hours computed for this winning design specifically. Designs later in the order are never examined.

Fourth, the two failure modes are distinguished. When `evaluated_design_count` is zero — no approved design of the preferred chemistry matched the voltage, weight and capacity envelope — the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CELL_DESIGN`. When designs were eligible but not one of them had a feasible charger, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_CHARGER_MATCH`. In both cases **no sizing request row is persisted**; a failed quote leaves no trace, so an engineer cannot list unmatched requests.

On a successful match the fleet analysis follows. `recommended_battery_count` is `forklift_count` for a lithium selection, because a lithium pack is opportunity-charged in the truck, but for a lead-acid selection it is `forklift_count` multiplied by 1 when `shifts_per_day` is exactly 1 and by 2 otherwise — a multi-shift lead-acid fleet needs a second set of packs to swap in. `recommended_charger_count` is `forklift_count` for lithium, one charger per truck, but for lead-acid it is the ceiling of `recommended_battery_count` divided by 2, since two lead-acid batteries share a charger on rotation. `fleet_capital_cents` is `recommended_battery_count` multiplied by the selected design's `list_price_cents`, plus `recommended_charger_count` multiplied by the selected charger's `price_cents`, in whole cents. The request is created with `request_state` `MATCHED`.

The engineer then closes the quote by naming an `action` of `ACCEPT` or `WITHDRAW`, optionally with a `decision_note`. Either action requires the request to be `MATCHED`; a request already `ACCEPTED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_SIZING_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, so neither decision can be reversed and an accepted quote cannot be withdrawn. The decision moves `request_state` to `ACCEPTED` or `WITHDRAWN` respectively and is otherwise inert: the recorded selection and every derived factor are frozen and **never recomputed**, so a later price change, charger discontinuation or design retirement cannot alter a quote that has already been decided. Only an `ACCEPTED` request may be named when a battery is shipped. Sizing requests may be listed with optional `plant_id`, `selected_cell_design_id`, `selected_charger_id` and `request_state` filters, ordered by `created_at` ascending then `id` ascending, and read individually; a missing request is `ERR_SIZING_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.9. Shipping Units, Field Service, Warranty Settlement and Core Return

A shipped battery is a long-lived field asset carrying two entirely independent obligations, and the system tracks them on two parallel axes that never interfere: `warranty_status` and `core_status`. A unit can be `CLAIM_OPEN` and `RETURN_OPEN` at the same time, and neither axis gates the other.

**Shipping moves mass and opens liability.** A `ProductionSupervisor` ships one battery at a time, naming the producing `lot_id`, a `serial_number` unique across all units (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), the receiving `customer_name`, the invoiced `list_price_cents`, optionally a `sizing_request_id` naming the quote the unit fulfils, and optionally an explicit `shipped_at` which otherwise defaults to the logical clock. The lot must be `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, and the plant must not be `FROZEN`. `list_price_cents` is the warranty credit basis; it must be strictly positive when supplied, and when omitted it defaults to the `list_price_cents` of the lot's pinned cell design. A supplied `sizing_request_id` must resolve, must belong to the same plant as the lot — otherwise `ERR_PLANT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` — and must have `request_state` `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_SIZING_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`; a merely `MATCHED` quote is not a purchase order. Finally the plant's `lead_inventory_kg` must be at least the pinned design's `lead_content_kg`, otherwise `ERR_LEAD_INVENTORY_INSUFFICIENT` — the plant cannot ship lead it does not have.

A shipped unit initialises `warranty_status` to `IN_SERVICE`, `cycle_count`, `claim_count`, `watering_events_expected`, `watering_events_ok`, `months_in_service` and `warranty_credit_cents` to 0, `watering_compliance_ratio` to 1.0000, `usage_fraction` to 0.0000, and `warranty_decision`, `last_claim_at`, `core_manifest_number`, `received_lead_kg` and `core_credit_issued_cents` to their null-or-zero defaults. `declared_lead_kg` is copied from the pinned design's `lead_content_kg` at three decimal places, freezing the manifest mass against later design edits. `core_status` initialises to `DEPOSIT_HELD` for a lead-acid unit and to `NOT_APPLICABLE` for a lithium one. Three plant ledgers move in the same write: `lead_inventory_kg` decreases and `lead_in_field_kg` increases by `declared_lead_kg`, keeping the lead mass variance at exactly 0.000, and `core_deposit_liability_cents` increases by the standard core deposit of 12500 cents **for lead-acid units only**. Units may be listed with optional `lot_id`, `sizing_request_id`, `warranty_status` and `core_status` filters, ordered by `shipped_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and read individually; a missing unit is `ERR_BATTERY_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`.

**Field service is an append-only log with a discriminated payload.** A `WarrantyAdministrator` appends events to a unit; each log row records its `battery_unit_id`, the `event_type`, the `performed_at` service instant, and an optional free-text `technician_note`. Four event types exist and they discriminate the payload strictly. `WATERING` requires `electrolyte_level_ok`, a boolean verdict on the electrolyte level, and must not carry `cycles_delta`. `CYCLE_REPORT` requires `cycles_delta`, the cycles accumulated since the previous report, between 1 and 2000 inclusive, and must not carry `electrolyte_level_ok`. `EQUALIZE` and `INSPECTION` carry neither, and supplying either discriminated field with them is a validation failure. `performed_at` must fall within the closed interval from the unit's `shipped_at` to the logical clock — a service event cannot predate the shipment or be filed in the future — and a violation is `ERR_EVENT_TIME_OUT_OF_RANGE`. A `CYCLE_REPORT` increases the unit's `cycle_count` by `cycles_delta` and re-derives `usage_fraction` from the cycle side alone, as `cycle_count` divided by the pinned design's `rated_cycles`. A `WATERING` event whose `electrolyte_level_ok` is true increases `watering_events_ok` by 1; one recording a failed level does not. Every event, whatever its type, recomputes `months_in_service`, `watering_events_expected` and `watering_compliance_ratio` against the logical clock, and the log row is stamped with `resulting_cycle_count` and `resulting_compliance_ratio` so the row carries a snapshot of the unit's position immediately after the event. Maintenance rows are discoverable **only** through the unit's own log listing, which orders them by `performed_at` descending then `created_at` ascending and accepts an optional `event_type` filter; no other read returns them.

**Watering compliance is a chemistry-conditional ratio.** `months_in_service` is the whole number of 30-day service months elapsed from `shipped_at` to the logical clock, taking the floor, so a unit 89 days old has been in service 2 months. For a lithium unit, watering does not apply at all: `watering_events_expected` is 0 and `watering_compliance_ratio` is exactly 1.0000, and the whole branch is skipped. For a lead-acid unit, `watering_events_expected` is `months_in_service` multiplied by one expected watering event per month, and `watering_events_ok` is the count of that unit's `WATERING` log rows whose `electrolyte_level_ok` is true and whose `performed_at` falls inside the closed interval from `shipped_at` to the logical clock; `EQUALIZE`, `INSPECTION` and `CYCLE_REPORT` rows never count, and neither do `WATERING` rows recording a failed level. When the expected count is 0 — a unit less than one service month old — the ratio is 1.0000 by the zero-denominator rule rather than being undefined. Otherwise the ratio is the smaller of 1.0 and `watering_events_ok` divided by `watering_events_expected`, rounded half up to four decimal places, so over-servicing is credited at most fully.

**The warranty evaluation read previews the settlement without touching it.** A `PlantAdmin` or `WarrantyAdministrator` may read a unit's warranty position and receives `usage_fraction`, `watering_compliance_ratio`, `months_in_service`, the pinned design's `warranty_months`, plus `would_decide` naming the outcome an adjudication would reach right now, `would_credit_cents` naming the credit it would issue, and `governing_gate` naming which gate of the cascade is currently decisive. The read is a pure projection: it opens no claim, changes no state, and issues no credit.

**Opening a claim is version-guarded and repeatable.** A `WarrantyAdministrator` opens a claim by supplying `expected_battery_unit_version` matching the unit's current `version`, a `complaint_code` describing the reported symptom, and optionally a `reported_at` instant which otherwise defaults to the logical clock. A claim may be opened from `IN_SERVICE`, the first claim on a fresh unit, or from `CLAIM_DENIED`, a re-claim that supersedes the earlier refusal — this loop back into `CLAIM_OPEN` is the reason the unit axis is concurrency-controlled at all. A unit already `CLAIM_OPEN` cannot open a second claim, which is `ERR_WARRANTY_STATE_INVALID`, and a unit whose `warranty_status` is `CLAIM_HONORED` or `CLAIM_PRORATED` is settled for good and is refused with the more specific `ERR_WARRANTY_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Opening a claim sets `warranty_status` to `CLAIM_OPEN`, increments `claim_count` by 1, sets `last_claim_at` to the supplied `reported_at` or the logical clock, clears `warranty_decision` back to null and `warranty_credit_cents` back to 0, recomputes `months_in_service`, `cycle_count`, `watering_events_expected`, `watering_events_ok` and `watering_compliance_ratio` as of the logical clock, and increments the unit's `version` by 1 — so a second claim replaying the same `expected_battery_unit_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_BATTERY_UNIT_VERSION`.

**Adjudication is a strictly ordered gate cascade and the first gate that fires decides everything.** The administrator supplies an `inspection_finding` of `DEFECT_CONFIRMED` or `NO_DEFECT_FOUND` and an optional `adjudication_note`; the unit must be `CLAIM_OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_WARRANTY_STATE_INVALID`. First `usage_fraction` is recomputed as the **larger** of two independent consumption measures: the cycle fraction, `cycle_count` divided by the pinned design's `rated_cycles`, and the calendar fraction, `months_in_service` divided by the pinned design's `warranty_months`. Whichever ran out first governs, and the result is rounded half up to four decimal places. Then the gates are evaluated in this exact order:

1. When `inspection_finding` is `NO_DEFECT_FOUND`, the decision is `DENIED_NO_DEFECT` with a credit of 0. This gate is first, so a no-defect finding denies the claim even on a brand-new battery with perfect maintenance.
2. Otherwise, when `usage_fraction` is greater than or equal to 1.0000, the decision is `DENIED_USAGE` with a credit of 0. Exactly 1.0000 denies — the warranty is exhausted at the boundary, not just beyond it.
3. Otherwise, when the unit's chemistry is `LEAD_ACID` **and** its `watering_compliance_ratio` is strictly below 0.8000, the decision is `DENIED_MAINTENANCE` with a credit of 0. A ratio of exactly 0.8000 survives this gate. Lithium units never reach this gate at all, since their ratio is fixed at 1.0000.
4. Otherwise, when `usage_fraction` is less than or equal to 0.1000, the decision is `HONORED` and the credit is the full `list_price_cents`. This is the early-failure gate, and 0.1000 exactly is honoured.
5. Otherwise the decision is `PRORATED`. A compliance multiplier is chosen — 1.00 when `watering_compliance_ratio` is greater than or equal to 0.9500, and 0.90 otherwise, which is the middle band that is neither denied nor fully credited — and the credit is `list_price_cents` multiplied by one minus `usage_fraction`, multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half up to a whole cent **once at the end**. No intermediate product is rounded.

The decision is written to `warranty_decision` and the credit to `warranty_credit_cents`, and `warranty_status` becomes `CLAIM_HONORED`, `CLAIM_PRORATED` or `CLAIM_DENIED` accordingly — all three denial reasons collapse onto the single `CLAIM_DENIED` state while `warranty_decision` preserves which gate fired. Every denial sets the credit to 0 exactly.

**The core return runs the physical lead back to the plant.** A `WarrantyAdministrator` opens a return by supplying a `core_manifest_number`, the regulatory manifest the core travels under, which must be globally unique across units — a reused manifest is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The unit's `core_status` must be `DEPOSIT_HELD`, otherwise `ERR_CORE_STATE_INVALID`, its chemistry must be `LEAD_ACID`, otherwise `ERR_CORE_NOT_APPLICABLE` (a lithium unit sits permanently at `NOT_APPLICABLE` and has no core programme), and its `declared_lead_kg` must be strictly positive. Opening a return sets `core_status` to `RETURN_OPEN` and stores the manifest number; the deposit liability is untouched and the unit still counts toward `core_deposit_liability_cents`.

The return is then driven through a linear chain by naming an `action`, with an optional `transition_note` on every step. `SHIP` moves `RETURN_OPEN → IN_TRANSIT` and does nothing else. `RECEIVE` moves `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED` and requires `received_lead_kg`, the mass actually weighed in at three decimal places, which must reconcile against the manifest: the absolute difference between `received_lead_kg` and the pinned design's `lead_content_kg` must be at most 2 percent of that `lead_content_kg`. A variance of exactly 2.00 percent is **accepted**; only a larger one is refused, with `ERR_CORE_MASS_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`. On receipt the weighed mass is stored, the signed difference `received_lead_kg` minus `declared_lead_kg` is rounded to three decimal places and added to the plant's `core_lead_variance_kg` — a running audit figure that may legitimately be negative — and the mass itself moves partitions: `declared_lead_kg` leaves `lead_in_field_kg` and enters `lead_recycled_kg` in the same write, so the lead mass variance stays at exactly 0.000. The deposit is deliberately **not** settled at receipt.

The chain then forks, and the two branches are deliberately symmetrical in their bookkeeping and opposite only in who keeps the money. `RECYCLE` moves `RECEIVED → RECYCLED`, the terminal regulatory disposition, and settles the money only, since the mass already moved at receipt: `core_credit_issued_cents` is set to the 12500-cent deposit and the plant's `core_deposit_liability_cents` is reduced by the same amount, so the liability opened at shipment closes exactly. `REJECT` moves `IN_TRANSIT → REJECTED`, which is the disposition for a core that arrived unusable, and forfeits the deposit: `core_credit_issued_cents` stays 0 and `core_deposit_liability_cents` is still reduced by 12500, because the liability is discharged either way — through a refund on one branch and a forfeit on the other. Because a rejected core never passed through `RECEIVED`, its mass has not yet moved, so `REJECT` also moves `declared_lead_kg` from `lead_in_field_kg` into `lead_recycled_kg`; the rejected core is scrapped on site all the same. No mass ever moves twice: exactly one of the receive path and the reject path runs for any given unit. `SHIP` requires `RETURN_OPEN`, `RECEIVE` and `REJECT` both require `IN_TRANSIT`, and `RECYCLE` requires `RECEIVED`; any action from the wrong state is `ERR_CORE_STATE_INVALID`, and `RECYCLED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal.
