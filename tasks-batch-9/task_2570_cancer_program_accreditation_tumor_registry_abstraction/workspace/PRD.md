# Cancer Program Accreditation & Tumor Registry Abstraction API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Commission on Cancer (CoC) accredited cancer program lives or dies by its registry. Every reportable malignancy diagnosed or treated at the facility must be found on a casefinding list, judged reportable, abstracted to a national standard within a published timeliness window, followed for life, rolled up into quality-measure performance, transmitted to the state central registry, and defended in front of a surveyor. Today that work is coordinated across spreadsheets, abstractor worklists, e-mail threads with the state registry, and a binder of standard-compliance evidence assembled in a panic three weeks before the on-site survey. The consequences of drift are not cosmetic: a registry that falls behind on abstraction silently degrades follow-up rates, misses conference-frequency evidence, and arrives at its survey with deficiencies it cannot answer inside the response window.

This document specifies the API for a system that replaces that patchwork with a single governed workflow. The service owns the whole chain — casefinding source reconciliation, reportability determination, case identification and accession numbering, assignment to credentialed registrars, abstraction with completeness scoring, segregated QA review, re-abstraction audits with abstractor quality and productivity ledgers, lifetime follow-up maintenance, state central registry submission with edit resolution and late-reporting assessments, multidisciplinary conference evidence, quality measure performance with improvement projects, and the accreditation cycle from survey scheduling through deficiency response to the accreditation decision itself.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **the registry has an operating posture** — a single derived gate, computed from the program's accreditation state, its open critical deficiencies, and its abstraction backlog, that can restrict or freeze case assignment, abstraction, and state submission without any operator explicitly closing anything. Second, **duties are segregated**: the registrar who abstracts a case may not pass QA on it or audit it, the coordinator who answers a deficiency may not review that answer, and the surveyor who completes a survey may not render the accreditation decision. Everything else in this specification — the state machines, the derived aggregates, the conserved ledgers — exists to make those two commitments enforceable and auditable rather than aspirational.

All business resources are mounted under `/cancer-registry`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Registry Personas and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven roles recognised by this system, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 401). A syntactically valid caller whose role is not listed for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before anything else in the request is inspected.

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `RegistryManager`, `RegistryAbstractor`, `QaReviewer`, `AccreditationCoordinator`, `StateRegistryLiaison`, and `CoCSurveyor`.

A `PlatformAdmin` operates the registry platform itself: it onboards cancer programs, moves the logical clock, and records assessment payments. It holds no clinical or accreditation authority. A `RegistryManager` runs the registry office — staffing registrars, owning casefinding sources, assigning and releasing cases, opening and transmitting state submissions, and voiding audits. A `RegistryAbstractor` is a certified tumor registrar (or trainee) who abstracts the cases assigned to them. A `QaReviewer` is a senior registrar who passes QA judgement on submitted abstractions and performs re-abstraction audits. An `AccreditationCoordinator` runs the multidisciplinary conference calendar, files deficiency responses, and drives quality-measure improvement projects. A `StateRegistryLiaison` is the state central registry counterpart who returns edit results for a transmitted batch, and holds no authority over cases, abstractions, or accreditation. A `CoCSurveyor` completes the survey, cites deficiencies, reviews responses, and renders the accreditation decision.

Two of these roles are backed by a persisted entity, and the distinction matters for every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `RegistryAbstractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Abstractor`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Abstractor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — concretely, a case's `assigned_abstractor_id` and an abstraction's `abstractor_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `QaReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Abstractor`, which is precisely why it can be compared against — and required to differ from — the `abstractor_id` of the abstraction under review or under audit. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `RegistryManager`, `AccreditationCoordinator`, `StateRegistryLiaison`, or `CoCSurveyor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `decided_by_user_id` on a casefinding entry, or as the surveyor of record on a survey completion — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and Version Counters

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`, which is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix and no domain-flavoured formatting, and a `created_at` timestamp, which is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits. Clients never supply either value.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a program's backlog counts or a registrar's quality scores are recalculated as a downstream consequence of some other operation, that program or registrar has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented like any other write.

`FollowUpContact` is the single exception. A follow-up contact is an immutable event record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation entirely.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Resource collections sit exactly one level below the mount point, and an operation on a specific resource addresses it by a single path identifier: `{programId}` for a cancer program, `{abstractorId}` for a registrar, `{sourceId}` for a casefinding source, `{entryId}` for a casefinding entry, `{caseId}` for a tumor case, `{contactId}` for a follow-up contact, `{abstractionId}` for an abstraction, `{auditId}` for a QA audit, `{sessionId}` for a conference session, `{submissionId}` for a registry submission, `{itemId}` for a submission item, `{deficiencyId}` for a deficiency, and `{measureId}` for a quality measure. Where two identifiers appear in one path — a submission item addressed inside its batch — they are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the error: an unknown `{submissionId}` yields `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` before the `{itemId}` is ever looked up. The per-resource codes are `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ABSTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_MEASURE_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, and they are checked in a fixed declaration order: `program_id` against a cancer program, then `source_id` against a casefinding source, then `case_id` against a tumor case, then `abstractor_id` against a registrar, then `entry_id` against a casefinding entry, then `source_measure_id` against a quality measure. Each unresolved identifier raises that parent's own not-found code; an `auditor_id` that names no registrar raises `ERR_ABSTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, and an `abstraction_id` that names no abstraction raises `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies when creating a registrar, a casefinding source, an abstraction, a QA audit, a conference session, a submission, a deficiency, or a quality measure, and when assigning a case, adding a submission item, or recording a follow-up contact.

Because the topology is flat rather than deeply nested, children are discovered by filtering a collection rather than by walking a nested path. Every inbound foreign key is exposed as an optional filter on the owning collection's list read, alongside the state filters that make a worklist usable: casefinding sources filter by `program_id` and `source_type`; casefinding entries by `source_id`, `program_id`, `created_case_id`, `reportability_state`, and `class_of_case_code`; tumor cases by `program_id`, `entry_id`, `assigned_abstractor_id`, `case_state`, `submission_state`, `timeliness_status`, and `follow_up_status`; follow-up contacts by `case_id` and `outcome`; abstractions by `case_id`, `abstractor_id`, `supersedes_abstraction_id`, and `abstraction_state`; QA audits by `abstraction_id`, `auditor_id`, `audit_state`, and `verdict`; conference sessions by `program_id` and `session_state`; submissions by `program_id` and `submission_state`; submission items by `submission_id`, `case_id`, and `edit_status`; deficiencies by `program_id`, `source_measure_id`, `severity`, and `deficiency_state`; quality measures by `program_id`, `measure_code`, `measurement_year`, `measure_state`, and `performance_state`; registrars by `program_id`, `credential`, and `employment_status`; and programs by `accreditation_state` and `registry_posture`. An unrecognised value for any of these filters is a request-shape failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. The Logical Registry Clock

Timeliness, follow-up ageing, response windows, and audit windows are all measured against a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that a whole accreditation cycle can be exercised deterministically. Before any request touches the server the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. That startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and time only moves forward: a requested instant that is not strictly later than the current logical instant is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422).

Advancing the clock is a reconciliation checkpoint, not merely a variable assignment. Every tumor case has its `timeliness_status` and `days_to_due` recomputed, and its `follow_up_due_date` and `follow_up_status` re-derived. Every casefinding source has its `entries_this_month` and `casefinding_completeness_pct` recomputed for the new calendar month, and its `last_reconciled_through` set to the new clock date — that field is written by the clock checkpoint and by nothing else. Every program has its follow-up rates, conference-compliance flags, backlog counts, and operating posture recomputed. Every registrar has both quality scores and its productivity figures recomputed, because both are windowed against the clock. Finally, the checkpoint sweeps deficiencies: any deficiency still awaiting a first response whose response deadline the new clock date has passed is escalated, with the downstream accreditation consequences described in §3.9. Deficiencies are swept in ascending order of response deadline, and ties are broken by ascending `created_at`. A deficiency whose response was rejected and whose extended deadline has passed is deliberately **not** swept by the checkpoint; instead its next response attempt fails on the closed window.

### 2.7. Registry Posture: The Program-Wide Operating Gate

Each program carries a derived operating posture in `registry_posture`, whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, or `FROZEN`. It is never set by a client. It is recomputed at the end of every request that changes a tumor case's state, changes a deficiency's state, changes the program's accreditation state, records a follow-up contact, or moves the logical clock.

The recomputation first refreshes three counts for the program. `open_backlog_count` is the number of tumor cases of the program whose case state is `IDENTIFIED`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_ABSTRACTION`, `QA_PENDING`, or `REWORK_PENDING`. `overdue_backlog_count` is the subset of that same set whose `timeliness_status` is `LATE`. `open_critical_deficiency_count` is the number of deficiencies of the program whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose state is `CITED`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, or `RESPONSE_REJECTED`. The posture is then decided by four clauses evaluated in exactly this order, the first matching clause winning: if the program's `accreditation_state` is `PROBATION` or `WITHDRAWN`, the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if `open_critical_deficiency_count` is at least 1, the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if `overdue_backlog_count` is at least 2, the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if `open_backlog_count` is at least 4, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. The boundaries are inclusive on the stated side and the ordering is load-bearing: two overdue cases freeze the registry while one does not, four open cases restrict it while three do not, and any freezing condition always masks a restricting one. Follow-up performance deliberately does not feed the posture at all — a poor follow-up rate gates the accreditation decision instead, so posture remains a pure function of accreditation state, critical deficiencies, and backlog.

Posture changes cascade into the state registry submission workflow. Whenever a recompute moves a program from `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` into `FROZEN`, every submission of that program that is in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → HOLD` and has its `pre_hold_state` set to `DRAFT`. Whenever a recompute moves a program out of `FROZEN`, every submission of that program that is in `HOLD` moves `HOLD → DRAFT`, restored to the state recorded in `pre_hold_state` and that field is cleared back to null. Submissions are swept in ascending order of `period_start`, with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. While a program is frozen it accepts no new case assignment, no new abstraction, no new submission item, no transmit, and no retransmit; each such attempt is rejected with `ERR_REGISTRY_FROZEN`. While it is merely restricted, work continues but only registrars holding the `CTR` credential may receive new assignments.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Case and Batch Writes

Two write operations are protected by optimistic concurrency control against their parent resource's `version`, because both are legitimately repeatable against the same parent and would otherwise let a client build on a parent state it has never seen.

Creating an abstraction requires the field `expected_case_version` in the request body. It is not optional. Its value must equal the current `version` of the tumor case named by `case_id`. If another writer has advanced the case's version since the client last read it — a QA verdict, an assignment change, or a prior abstraction on the same case all do so — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Without this check a client holding a stale view of the case could silently attach a second live abstraction to it, since a rework round legitimately supersedes a prior abstraction on the same case.

Adding a case to a state registry submission requires the field `expected_submission_version` in the request body, again non-optional, whose value must equal the current `version` of the submission named in the path. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBMISSION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Each added item changes the batch's `total_case_count` and, once edit results return, the basis of its late-reporting assessment; the version check prevents a client from composing a batch it never inspected.

The version check on both endpoints is evaluated after all existence checks and before any state precondition. A stale `expected_case_version` on a case that is in the wrong state still reports the stale-version error, and a stale `expected_submission_version` on a batch that is not in `DRAFT` still reports the stale-version error. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties and Registrar Eligibility

Four controlled pairs of operations may never be performed by the same `X-User-ID`. The QA reviewer of an abstraction must not be the registrar recorded in that abstraction's `abstractor_id`. The auditor of a re-abstraction audit must not be the `abstractor_id` of the abstraction being audited. The surveyor who reviews a deficiency response must not be the user who filed that response. The surveyor who renders the accreditation decision must not be the user who recorded the survey completion. Any of these four collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409), and those four operations — the QA verdict on an abstraction, the creation of a QA audit, a deficiency response review, and the accreditation decision — are exactly the operations that carry the check.

Registrar eligibility is a separate family of gates. A registrar's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE` to receive a case assignment, to author an abstraction, to act as the QA reviewer of an abstraction, or to be named as the auditor of a QA audit; in all four situations a registrar whose status is `SUSPENDED_FROM_ABSTRACTING`, `LOA`, or `TERMINATED` is rejected with `ERR_ABSTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. A registrar's `certification_expires_on` date must be on or after the logical clock date to receive an assignment or to author an abstraction — those two operations only — and a lapsed credential is rejected with `ERR_ABSTRACTOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. Finally, the registrar being assigned, authoring, reviewing, or auditing must belong to the same program as the case or abstraction in question; a cross-program actor on any of those same four operations (assignment, abstraction creation, QA verdict, audit creation) is rejected with `ERR_ABSTRACTOR_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`.

### 2.10. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Boundary Conventions

Rounding is always **round half up**, and each family of derived numbers has a pinned precision. Percentages that describe registry performance — both follow-up rates, `lost_to_follow_up_pct`, both abstractor quality scores, `casefinding_completeness_pct`, a quality measure's stratified rate and its pooled rate, and each coordinator's attendance rate — are rounded to one decimal place. A registrar's `productivity_rate_per_day` is rounded to two decimal places. A conference session's `prospective_case_ratio` is rounded to three decimal places before it is compared against the prospective-presentation standard.

Money is handled exclusively in integer cents: `assessment_due_cents`, `assessment_paid_cents`, and the payment `amount_cents` are whole numbers of cents, never decimal currency, and the single rounding step in the assessment calculation is a round-half-up on one multiplication (§3.6). Counts, scores, and caps are integers: an abstraction's `completeness_score` is an integer clamped to the range 0 through 100 inclusive, an audit's `weighted_error_score` is an integer point total, and a registrar's capacity cap is an integer produced by rounding half up.

Date arithmetic is whole-day integer arithmetic on calendar dates, and interval arithmetic on instants is at minute resolution. Every threshold in this specification states its own boundary inclusivity explicitly; where a comparison is written as "at least" the boundary value satisfies it, and where it is written as "strictly more than" or "fewer than" it does not.

### 2.11. Order of Validation

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is determined by a fixed precedence rather than by the order in which an implementation happens to check things. The bands, from first-evaluated to last, are: authentication of the two headers; authorization of the role against the endpoint; request shape; existence of path identifiers, then existence of body foreign keys, then membership of ids inside a batched payload; optimistic concurrency; state preconditions; safety and compliance gates; temporal gates; referential and uniqueness constraints; and finally fiscal gates.

Request-shape validation, which yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), covers all of the following: every field the operation declares as required is present; every enum-typed field carries one of its declared values; dates are ISO 8601 calendar dates and timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC instants; counts and cent amounts are non-negative integers; percentages are numbers within 0 through 100 inclusive; and end bounds respect their start bounds, meaning a session's `end_at` must be strictly after its `start_at`, a submission's `period_end` must be on or after its `period_start`, and an audit's `window_end` must be on or after its `window_start`. A conference session held with a prospective case count greater than its presented case count is also a request-shape failure.

Several orderings inside the later bands are deliberate and are asserted by the acceptance tests. A scoring attempt on an audit that has already been voided reports `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_VOIDED` rather than `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_SCORED`, because the voided check is ordered above the open-state check. A frozen registry masks every capacity, referential, and fiscal problem on the same request: an assignment into a frozen registry reports `ERR_REGISTRY_FROZEN` even when the chosen registrar is also over capacity, whose own code `ERR_ABSTRACTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` sits in the later constraint band. Within a submission transmit, the state check comes first and the fiscal check comes last, so a batch that is both out of `DRAFT` and carrying an unpaid assessment reports `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_DRAFT` and never the payment error. Terminal-state lockdown always resolves to the entity's own state error rather than a generic one: a voided tumor case reports `ERR_CASE_NOT_ASSIGNABLE` on assignment and `ERR_CASE_NOT_ABSTRACTABLE` on abstraction creation; a rejected or superseded abstraction reports `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_DRAFT` on a QA submission, `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_IN_QA` on a QA verdict, and `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_REWORKABLE` on a draft reopen; a voided audit reports `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_VOIDED`; a credited or non-credited session reports `ERR_SESSION_NOT_HELD` on a credit decision and `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SCHEDULED` on an attendance record or a hold; an accepted submission reports `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_DRAFT` on item addition or transmit and `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_IN_EDIT_RESOLUTION` on retransmit; a resolved or escalated deficiency reports `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_RESPONDABLE` on a response and `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW` on a response review; and a withdrawn program reports `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` on all three accreditation actions.

### 2.12. Conserved Ledgers and Registry Invariants

Three quantities in this system are conserved, meaning that every forward action which moves them has a compensating action that moves them back by exactly the same amount. They are stated here because they are the fastest way to detect an incomplete cascade.

The first is **open abstraction workload**. For any program, the sum of `assigned_open_case_count` across all its registrars always equals the number of that program's tumor cases which have a non-null `assigned_abstractor_id` and a case state of `ASSIGNED`, `IN_ABSTRACTION`, `QA_PENDING`, or `REWORK_PENDING`. This equality is exact integer equality and it must hold after every assignment, every release, every abstraction creation, every QA verdict, every audit score, every audit void, every staffing change, and every casefinding entry reopen. The compensating pairs are assignment against release, assignment against the void of a case caused by reopening its entry, QA approval against an audit's critical-failure cascade, and that cascade against the void of the audit which applied it.

The second is **audit discrepancy points**. A registrar's `lifetime_discrepancy_points` always equals the sum of `weighted_error_score` over every audit whose audited abstraction was produced by that registrar and whose audit state is `CLOSED_PASS` or `CLOSED_FAIL`. Voiding a scored audit subtracts exactly the points it added, so scoring an audit and then voiding it returns the ledger to its pre-score value. Open and voided audits contribute nothing.

The third is **reportable-case parity**. For any program, the number of its tumor cases whose case state is not `VOIDED` equals the number of its casefinding entries whose `reportability_state` is `REPORTABLE`, and both equal the program's `identified_case_count`. This must hold after every reportability decision and after every entry reopen.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Casefinding Lists, Reportability Rules and Case Identification

Everything in this system hangs off a **cancer program**. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one with a `name`, a `coc_program_id` identifying it to the Commission on Cancer, an `annual_analytic_caseload`, and a `reference_year`, and may optionally supply `timeliness_standard_days` — the number of days after first contact within which an abstract must be completed, which defaults to 180 and may not be set below 30. The `coc_program_id` must be unique across all programs; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COC_PROGRAM_ID`. The `annual_analytic_caseload` may not be negative. A new program opens in `accreditation_state` `ACCREDITED` with `registry_posture` `OPEN`, every derived count at zero, both follow-up rates at 100.0, `lost_to_follow_up_pct` at 0.0, `survey_date` and `survey_year` null, `next_survey_due_year` set to the `reference_year` plus 3, and its `required_sessions_12mo` already derived from the caseload as described in §3.7.

A `RegistryManager` then registers the **casefinding sources** the registry reconciles each month, each with its owning `program_id`, a `source_type` drawn from `PATHOLOGY`, `DISEASE_INDEX`, `RADIATION_ONCOLOGY`, `MEDICAL_ONCOLOGY`, and `AUTOPSY`, and an `expected_monthly_volume` of line items, which may be zero but never negative. A source tracks `entries_this_month`, the number of its entries whose `accession_date` falls inside the logical clock's current calendar month, and reports `casefinding_completeness_pct`: when `expected_monthly_volume` is zero the completeness is reported as 100.0, and otherwise it is the ratio of `entries_this_month` to `expected_monthly_volume`, clamped so it can never exceed one, multiplied by 100 and rounded to one decimal place — so the figure never exceeds 100.0 no matter how many entries arrive. A fresh source starts at 0.0 completeness, or at 100.0 when its expected volume is zero, with `last_reconciled_through` null until the first clock checkpoint writes it.

Line items land on a source as **casefinding entries**, recorded by a `RegistryManager` or a `RegistryAbstractor`. Each entry names the candidate patient's `patient_mrn`, the `accession_date` on which the line item appeared, an ICD-O-3 topography code in `primary_site_code` (for example `C50.9`, `C53.9`, `C71.9`, or `C44.5`), an ICD-O-3 morphology code with behaviour suffix in `histology_code` (for example `8500/3`), a `behavior_code` of `BENIGN_0`, `BORDERLINE_1`, `IN_SITU_2`, or `MALIGNANT_3`, the `diagnosis_date`, the `first_contact_date` at this facility, and two booleans that decide analytic status: `diagnosed_at_facility` and `first_treatment_at_facility`. The registrar may also raise `ambiguous_terminology_flag`, which defaults to false, to mark pathology text that needs judgement, and may include `referral_lag_days` in the payload, though the server always derives that value itself. The `diagnosis_date` may not be later than the logical clock date, and `first_contact_date` may not be earlier than `diagnosis_date`. The combination of `source_id`, `patient_mrn`, `primary_site_code`, and `diagnosis_date` must be unique across all entries; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASEFINDING_ENTRY`. The entry's `program_id` is copied from its source, and the source's `entries_this_month` and `casefinding_completeness_pct` are recomputed on every insert.

At the moment of recording, the system runs two rule engines over the entry and never runs them again. The first derives `referral_lag_days` as the whole number of days from `diagnosis_date` to `first_contact_date` and then fixes `class_of_case_code` by the first matching case of five: an entry both diagnosed at the facility and first-treated at the facility is `CLASS_10`; one diagnosed at the facility but not first-treated there is `CLASS_00`; one not diagnosed at the facility but first-treated there whose referral lag is 120 days or fewer is `CLASS_20`; one not diagnosed at the facility but first-treated there whose referral lag exceeds 120 days is `CLASS_31`; and anything else is `CLASS_40`. The boundary is inclusive — a lag of exactly 120 days is analytic `CLASS_20` while 121 days is non-analytic `CLASS_31`. The derived `analytic_flag` is true when the class of case is `CLASS_00`, `CLASS_10`, or `CLASS_20`, and false for `CLASS_31` and `CLASS_40`.

The second engine suggests reportability, writing `suggested_reportability` (either `REPORTABLE` or `NON_REPORTABLE`) together with exactly one machine reason in `suggested_reason_code`. Six rules are evaluated top to bottom and the first one that matches wins and fixes the reason. If the `diagnosis_date` falls before January 1 of the program's `reference_year`, the suggestion is `NON_REPORTABLE` with reason `BEFORE_REFERENCE_YEAR`. Otherwise, if the behaviour is `MALIGNANT_3` and the `primary_site_code` begins with `C44` and the `histology_code` is one of the three excluded skin morphologies `8070/3`, `8090/3`, or `8091/3`, the suggestion is `NON_REPORTABLE` with reason `SKIN_SQUAMOUS_BASAL_EXCLUDED`. Otherwise, if the behaviour is `IN_SITU_2` and the `primary_site_code` is exactly `C53.9`, the suggestion is `NON_REPORTABLE` with reason `CERVIX_IN_SITU_EXCLUDED`. Otherwise, if the behaviour is either `MALIGNANT_3` or `IN_SITU_2`, the suggestion is `REPORTABLE` with reason `SEER_REPORTABLE_BEHAVIOR`. Otherwise, if the behaviour is `BENIGN_0` or `BORDERLINE_1` and the `primary_site_code` is one of the central-nervous-system topographies `C70.0`, `C70.1`, `C70.9`, `C71.0`, `C71.9`, `C72.0`, or `C72.9`, the suggestion is `REPORTABLE` with reason `CNS_BENIGN_REPORTABLE`. In every remaining case the suggestion is `NON_REPORTABLE` with reason `BENIGN_NON_CNS`.

The engine suggests; a human decides. An entry opens in `reportability_state` `PENDING_REVIEW` with `decision_reason_code`, `override_reason_code`, `decided_by_user_id`, and `created_case_id` all null and `reopen_count` at zero. A `RegistryManager` or `RegistryAbstractor` then records the determination, supplying `decision` — either `REPORTABLE` or `NON_REPORTABLE` — together with a `decision_reason_code`, and an `override_reason_code` where required. The entry must be in `PENDING_REVIEW`, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_PENDING_REVIEW`. When the decision contradicts `suggested_reportability` — a `REPORTABLE` determination on an entry the engine suggested was non-reportable, or a `NON_REPORTABLE` determination on one it suggested was reportable — a non-empty `override_reason_code` is mandatory, and its absence is rejected with `ERR_REPORTABILITY_OVERRIDE_REASON_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A `REPORTABLE` determination additionally requires that the entry's `diagnosis_date` be on or after January 1 of the program's `reference_year`; an earlier diagnosis is rejected with `ERR_DIAGNOSIS_BEFORE_REFERENCE_YEAR` (HTTP 422). Recording such an entry is entirely legitimate — casefinding lists surface pre-reference-year diagnoses all the time, and the engine flags them with the `BEFORE_REFERENCE_YEAR` reason — but the only determination it may ever receive is `NON_REPORTABLE`. A `REPORTABLE` determination is also refused on a program whose accreditation has been withdrawn, with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. Either determination records the `decision_reason_code` and stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`; a `NON_REPORTABLE` determination moves the entry to `PENDING_REVIEW → NON_REPORTABLE`, mints nothing, and leaves `created_case_id` null.

A `REPORTABLE` determination is the origin of the deepest cascade in the system. The entry moves `PENDING_REVIEW → REPORTABLE` and a **tumor case** is minted from it. The new case records `entry_id` pointing back at the entry it came from, the `program_id` of that entry, and copies of the entry's `patient_mrn`, `class_of_case_code`, `analytic_flag`, `diagnosis_date`, and `first_contact_date`. It opens with `case_state` `IDENTIFIED` on the abstraction dimension and `submission_state` `NOT_SUBMITTED` on the state-reporting dimension, with `assigned_abstractor_id` null, `completed_on` null, `vital_status` `UNKNOWN`, and `last_contact_date` null. Its `sequence_number` is one more than the count of non-voided cases already existing for the same `patient_mrn` inside the same program, so a patient's second primary is sequence 2. Its `accession_number` is minted as the four-digit year of `diagnosis_date`, a hyphen, and a five-digit zero-padded sequence that is one more than the count of cases already existing in the same program with the same diagnosis year — the first 2026 case of a program is therefore `2026-00001`. Accession numbers are unique within a program and diagnosis year. The entry's `created_case_id` is set to the new case's `id`. The program's `identified_case_count` is incremented by one, its backlog counts are recomputed, and its posture is recomputed; if that recompute lands on `FROZEN`, every one of the program's submissions in `DRAFT` moves to `HOLD` with `pre_hold_state` recorded as `DRAFT`.

Timeliness is fixed at the same moment and refreshed thereafter. A case's `abstract_due_date` is its `first_contact_date` advanced by the program's `timeliness_standard_days`. While the case is still in the backlog, `days_to_due` is the whole number of days from the logical clock date to `abstract_due_date`, going negative once the due date has passed, and `timeliness_status` follows from it: a negative `days_to_due` is `LATE`, a value of 30 or fewer but not negative is `AT_RISK`, and anything greater than 30 is `ON_TIME`. The boundaries are exact — 30 days to go is `AT_RISK`, 31 days is `ON_TIME`, due today (zero days) is `AT_RISK`, and one day overdue is `LATE`. Once a case reaches `COMPLETED` the status is finalised against the completion date instead of the clock: it is `ON_TIME` when `completed_on` is on or before `abstract_due_date` and `LATE` otherwise, and `days_to_due` becomes the whole number of days from `completed_on` to `abstract_due_date`. A `VOIDED` case is reported as `ON_TIME` with `days_to_due` measured against the clock, so a compensated identification never pollutes the overdue backlog. These figures are recomputed on case creation, on every clock advance, and on the completion transition.

Mistakes in identification are undone by **reopening** the entry, an action reserved to a `RegistryManager` and accompanied by a `reopen_reason`. Reopening is only possible from `REPORTABLE` or `NON_REPORTABLE`, and only while `reopen_count` is still below the cap of one reopen per entry; a `PENDING_REVIEW` entry, an entry that has already been reopened once, or an entry whose minted case has moved beyond `IDENTIFIED` or `ASSIGNED` is rejected with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_REOPENABLE`. Reopening a `NON_REPORTABLE` entry is the simple path, available precisely because no case was ever minted and `created_case_id` is null: the entry returns `NON_REPORTABLE → PENDING_REVIEW`, `reopen_count` increases by one, `decision_reason_code` and `override_reason_code` are cleared to null, and nothing else in the program changes. Reopening a `REPORTABLE` entry is the compensating cascade: the entry returns `REPORTABLE → PENDING_REVIEW` with the same field clearing and counter increment, `decided_by_user_id` is cleared, its minted case is voided (`IDENTIFIED → VOIDED` or `ASSIGNED → VOIDED`), and `created_case_id` returns to null. Because the case was voided, if it had been assigned then that registrar's `assigned_open_case_count` is decremented by one and the case's `assigned_abstractor_id` is cleared to null; because the program's case population changed, `identified_case_count` is decremented by one and both backlog counts are recomputed; because those aggregates changed, the posture is recomputed, and a posture that leaves `FROZEN` restores every submission in `HOLD` to its `pre_hold_state`. Reportable-case parity and open-workload conservation, both stated in §2.12, must hold once the dust settles.

All three resources in this chain are also readable and listable. A casefinding source can be read individually with its reconciliation completeness, and sources can be listed filtered by owning program and `source_type`. A casefinding entry can be read individually, returning both the machine suggestion and the human decision, and entries can be listed as the reverse-reachability index for `source_id`, `program_id`, and `created_case_id`, further filterable by `reportability_state` and `class_of_case_code`. A tumor case can be read individually, returning both of its state dimensions together with its timeliness and follow-up standing, and cases can be listed as the reverse-reachability index for `program_id`, `entry_id`, and `assigned_abstractor_id`, further filterable by `case_state`, `submission_state`, `timeliness_status`, and `follow_up_status`.

### 3.2. Staffing the Registry and Distributing the Backlog

Registrars are created by a `RegistryManager` against a `program_id`, each with a `name`, a `credential` of `CTR`, `NON_CTR`, or `TRAINEE`, a contracted `productivity_target_cases_per_day` that must be greater than zero, and a `certification_expires_on` date written as a plain ISO calendar date. A new registrar starts with `employment_status` `ACTIVE`, with `assigned_open_case_count`, `lifetime_discrepancy_points`, `audit_fail_streak`, and `completed_cases_30d` all at zero, with both `quality_score_rolling_90d` and `quality_score_cumulative` at 100.0, and with `productivity_rate_per_day` at 0.0 — which, since no work has been approved yet, makes the opening `productivity_status` `BELOW` rather than `MEETS`.

Assignment is how a case leaves the unassigned pool: a `RegistryManager` nominates an `abstractor_id` for a case, advancing it `IDENTIFIED → ASSIGNED`. Only a case in `IDENTIFIED` is assignable — an already-assigned, in-abstraction, completed, or voided case is rejected with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`, and a reworked case must be released before it can be re-assigned. The gates that follow are evaluated in a fixed order. A frozen registry refuses the assignment outright with `ERR_REGISTRY_FROZEN`. A merely restricted registry accepts the assignment only if the nominated registrar holds the `CTR` credential; a `NON_CTR` or `TRAINEE` registrar may only take on new work while the posture is `OPEN`, and the refusal is `ERR_RESTRICTED_REQUIRES_CTR`. The registrar must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ABSTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`), must hold a credential that has not lapsed as of the logical clock date (`ERR_ABSTRACTOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`), and must belong to the same program as the case (`ERR_ABSTRACTOR_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`). Last comes capacity.

Capacity is derived, not configured. A registrar's cap is their `productivity_target_cases_per_day` multiplied by ten and rounded half up to a whole number of cases — a registrar contracted at 2.5 cases per working day may hold 25 open cases. Assignment is permitted only while `assigned_open_case_count` is strictly below that cap, so a registrar sitting at exactly the cap is refused the next case with `ERR_ABSTRACTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the frozen check is evaluated well before the capacity check, an over-capacity registrar in a frozen registry produces the freeze error, never the capacity error. A successful assignment writes `assigned_abstractor_id` onto the case, increments the case's `version` by one, increments the registrar's `assigned_open_case_count` by one, and refreshes the case's `timeliness_status` and `days_to_due` against the logical clock.

Release is the exact inverse. A `RegistryManager` may release an assigned case, optionally recording an `unassign_reason`, moving it `ASSIGNED → IDENTIFIED`; a case that is not currently `ASSIGNED` with a non-null `assigned_abstractor_id` is rejected with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ASSIGNED`. Release clears `assigned_abstractor_id` to null, increments the case's `version` by one, and decrements the registrar's `assigned_open_case_count` by one so that the workload ledger of §2.12 balances again.

Staffing changes are the blunt instrument. A `RegistryManager` may set a registrar's `employment_status`, optionally recording an `effective_on` date and a `reason`. Moving a registrar to `TERMINATED`, `SUSPENDED_FROM_ABSTRACTING`, or `LOA` releases their entire open worklist: every tumor case assigned to them whose case state is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_ABSTRACTION`, `QA_PENDING`, or `REWORK_PENDING` is returned to `IDENTIFIED` with `assigned_abstractor_id` cleared to null and its `version` incremented by one, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Because the whole worklist was released, that registrar's `assigned_open_case_count` is set to zero rather than decremented case by case. Any abstraction sitting on a released case is moved to the terminal `REJECTED` state — `DRAFT → REJECTED` for one still being written and `REWORK_REQUIRED → REJECTED` for one awaiting rework — since the registrar who owned it can no longer work it. Finally the program's backlog counts and posture are recomputed, and the open-workload invariant re-holds.

Productivity is measured over a trailing 30-day window that includes both the clock date and the day 29 days before it. A registrar's `completed_cases_30d` is the number of their abstractions in `APPROVED` state whose `approved_at` date falls inside that window; `productivity_rate_per_day` divides that count by the 21 working days assumed in a 30-day period and rounds half up to two decimal places; and `productivity_status` is `MEETS` when the resulting rate is greater than or equal to `productivity_target_cases_per_day` and `BELOW` otherwise, with a rate exactly equal to the target counting as `MEETS`. These three figures are recomputed whenever an abstraction is approved, whenever an audit is voided, and on every clock advance. A registrar can be read individually, which returns both quality-score variants alongside the productivity fields, and registrars can be listed filtered by owning program, `credential`, and `employment_status`.

### 3.3. Abstraction, Completeness Scoring and the QA Rework Loop

A `RegistryAbstractor` opens an **abstraction** against a case by naming its `case_id`, their own `abstractor_id`, and the mandatory `expected_case_version` described in §2.8. The case must be in `ASSIGNED` or `REWORK_PENDING`, otherwise the create is rejected with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ABSTRACTABLE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the case's `assigned_abstractor_id`, and the `abstractor_id` in the payload must equal it too; anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ABSTRACTOR` (HTTP 403). The registrar must still be `ACTIVE`, must hold an unexpired credential as of the logical clock date, and must belong to the case's program, with the same three codes as at assignment time. A successful create places the abstraction in `DRAFT`, sets its `abstractor_id` from the acting user, advances the case `ASSIGNED → IN_ABSTRACTION`, and increments the case's `version` by one — which is exactly why a client that wants to write twice against the same case must re-read the case in between.

The clinical content of the abstract is optional at the API boundary and is scored rather than rejected. The registrar records `stage_source`, which is either `CLINICAL` or `PATHOLOGIC`; the AJCC categories `t_category`, `n_category`, and `m_category` (for example `T2`, `N0`, `M0`); a `stage_group` drawn from `STAGE_0`, `STAGE_I`, `STAGE_II`, `STAGE_III`, `STAGE_IV`, `OCCULT`, and `UNKNOWN`; the `first_treatment_date` on which the first course of treatment began, left null when no treatment was given; a `treatment_modalities` array whose members come from `SURGERY`, `RADIATION`, `CHEMOTHERAPY`, `HORMONE`, `IMMUNOTHERAPY`, and `NO_TREATMENT`, defaulting to an empty array; a `radiation_start_date`; a `diagnostic_confirmation_code`; an `outcome_status` of `ALIVE_NO_EVIDENCE`, `ALIVE_WITH_DISEASE`, `DEAD_OF_DISEASE`, `DEAD_OTHER_CAUSE`, or `UNKNOWN`; and the `last_contact_date` recorded on the abstract. A second abstraction on a case whose prior abstraction has not reached a terminal state must carry `supersedes_abstraction_id`, and its absence is rejected with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). When `supersedes_abstraction_id` is present it must name an abstraction on the same `case_id` whose state is `DRAFT` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`; an abstraction on another case, or one already approved, rejected, or superseded, is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Creating a superseding abstraction against a case in `REWORK_PENDING` advances that case `REWORK_PENDING → IN_ABSTRACTION`, moves the abstraction it names from `REWORK_REQUIRED → SUPERSEDED` (a terminal state), carries the superseded abstraction's `rework_round` forward onto the new one — a fresh abstraction with nothing to supersede starts at round zero — and increments the case's `version` by one.

Completeness scoring runs on creation, on every reopen, and on the `REWORK_REQUIRED → DRAFT` transition, and it never looks beyond the abstraction being written and the case it hangs off. The required-field set starts as `stage_group`, `stage_source`, `first_treatment_date`, `treatment_modalities`, `outcome_status`, and `last_contact_date`, and is then adjusted by three independent inputs. A `PATHOLOGIC` `stage_source` additionally requires `t_category`, `n_category`, and `m_category`. A case whose `class_of_case_code` is `CLASS_00` or `CLASS_10` additionally requires `diagnostic_confirmation_code`. A `treatment_modalities` array containing `RADIATION` additionally requires `radiation_start_date`. Then two subtractions apply: a case whose `analytic_flag` is false drops `outcome_status` and `last_contact_date` from the set, and a `treatment_modalities` array containing `NO_TREATMENT` drops `first_treatment_date`. A field counts as missing when its value is null, an empty string, or an empty array, and `missing_required_fields` is the resulting list of names sorted alphabetically. The score starts at 100, loses 12 points for each missing required field, loses a further 8 points when `stage_group` is `UNKNOWN`, and loses a further 6 points when a `CLINICAL` `stage_source` is recorded on a case whose `class_of_case_code` is `CLASS_10` — a clinical stage on a case the program itself diagnosed and treated. The result is clamped into the range 0 through 100, so a very incomplete abstract bottoms out at zero rather than going negative.

Submission for review moves the abstraction `DRAFT → SUBMITTED_FOR_QA` and the case `IN_ABSTRACTION → QA_PENDING`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, increments the case's `version` by one, and may carry a `submit_note`. Only the abstraction's own `abstractor_id` may submit it, and only from `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_DRAFT`. Submission also demands quality: the `completeness_score` must be at least 85 **and** `missing_required_fields` must be empty, otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_ABSTRACTION_INCOMPLETE`. A score of exactly 85 passes; a score of 84 does not.

The QA verdict is the fork. A `QaReviewer` records a `verdict` of `APPROVED`, `REWORK_REQUIRED`, or `REJECTED`, optionally with a `reviewer_note`, against an abstraction that must be in `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA` (`ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_IN_QA`), and the reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the abstraction's `abstractor_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`). Every verdict increments the case's `version` by one, and every verdict writes `qa_reviewer_user_id` from the acting user. `APPROVED` is the deepest cascade in the system: the abstraction moves `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA → APPROVED` with `approved_at` stamped from the clock; because the abstraction was approved the case moves `QA_PENDING → COMPLETED` with `completed_on` set to the clock date and `timeliness_status` finalised; because the case left the open backlog the registrar's `assigned_open_case_count` is decremented by one and their `completed_cases_30d`, `productivity_rate_per_day`, and `productivity_status` are recomputed; because the backlog shrank, `open_backlog_count`, `overdue_backlog_count`, and `registry_posture` are recomputed; and because the posture was recomputed, a posture that leaves `FROZEN` returns every held batch to its `pre_hold_state`. `REWORK_REQUIRED` is narrower: the abstraction moves `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA → REWORK_REQUIRED` and the case moves `QA_PENDING → IN_ABSTRACTION` with `assigned_abstractor_id` left untouched, because the same registrar is expected to fix their own abstract — no workload, productivity, or posture figure changes. `REJECTED` is the third branch and the most punitive: the abstraction moves `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA → REJECTED`, which is terminal, and the case is returned to the pool as `QA_PENDING → IDENTIFIED` with `assigned_abstractor_id` cleared to null and the registrar's `assigned_open_case_count` decremented by one, so the case must be assigned afresh.

Rework closes the cycle. The registrar who owns a `REWORK_REQUIRED` abstraction may re-enter `DRAFT` on it, supplying revised values for any of `stage_source`, `t_category`, `n_category`, `m_category`, `stage_group`, `first_treatment_date`, `treatment_modalities`, `radiation_start_date`, `diagnostic_confirmation_code`, `outcome_status`, and `last_contact_date`; whatever is supplied is applied, and then `missing_required_fields` and `completeness_score` are recomputed against the revised values. An abstraction not in `REWORK_REQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_REWORKABLE`, a caller other than the owning registrar with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ABSTRACTOR`, and a parent case that is neither `IN_ABSTRACTION` nor `REWORK_PENDING` with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ABSTRACTABLE`. Each reopen increments `rework_round` by one, and reopening is permitted only while `rework_round` is strictly below two — so the first and second reopens succeed and the third is refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, at which point the only ways forward are supersession or rejection. The reopen also moves a `REWORK_PENDING` case back to `IN_ABSTRACTION` and increments its `version` by one. Two further terminal edges exist that no client triggers directly: an audit resolving to a critical failure drags an `APPROVED` abstraction back to `REWORK_REQUIRED` (§3.4), and the staffing release cascade of §3.2 pushes a `DRAFT` or `REWORK_REQUIRED` abstraction to `REJECTED`. Abstractions can be read individually and listed, with the list acting as the reverse-reachability index for `case_id`, `abstractor_id`, `supersedes_abstraction_id`, and `abstraction_state`.

### 3.4. Re-Abstraction Audits, Discrepancy Ledgers and Quality Scores

The program keeps its abstracts honest by re-abstracting a sample of them. A `QaReviewer` or `RegistryManager` opens an audit by naming the `abstraction_id` under review, the `auditor_id` of the registrar who will perform the re-abstraction, an `audit_type` of `ROUTINE_10PCT`, `TARGETED`, or `PRE_SURVEY`, and the inclusive calendar window `window_start` and `window_end` within which the re-abstraction will be done, where `window_end` must not precede `window_start`. Only an abstraction in `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA` or `APPROVED` may be audited; a `DRAFT`, `REWORK_REQUIRED`, `REJECTED`, or `SUPERSEDED` abstraction is refused with `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_AUDITABLE`. The auditor must not be the abstraction's own `abstractor_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`), must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ABSTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`), and must belong to the same program as the abstraction (`ERR_ABSTRACTOR_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`). A new audit opens in `OPEN` with `minor_discrepancy_count`, `major_discrepancy_count`, `critical_discrepancy_count`, and `weighted_error_score` at zero, `verdict` null, and `cascade_applied` false.

Auditor windows are capacity-limited rather than exclusive, and the interval arithmetic here is deliberately different from the conference calendar of §3.7. Two windows belonging to the same auditor overlap when `window_start` of the new window is on or before `window_end` of the existing one and `window_start` of the existing one is on or before `window_end` of the new one — so windows that merely touch at a single shared date do overlap. Overlap is not an error in itself: an auditor may hold up to two mutually overlapping windows, and only the third overlapping window is refused, with `ERR_AUDITOR_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Only the same auditor's non-voided audits count, so voiding one of two overlapping windows immediately makes room for a third.

Scoring is the fork, and it is performed by the auditor themselves. The auditor posts the `audited_field_count` re-abstracted (which must be at least one and forms the denominator of the quality score) together with `minor_discrepancy_count`, `major_discrepancy_count`, and `critical_discrepancy_count`. The `weighted_error_score` is five points per critical discrepancy plus three points per major discrepancy plus one point per minor discrepancy. The `verdict` then follows three ordered rules: any critical discrepancy at all forces `CRITICAL_FAIL` regardless of the total; otherwise a total of two points or fewer is `PASS`; otherwise it is `MINOR_FAIL`. A score of exactly 2 passes and a score of 3 is a `MINOR_FAIL`. An audit already scored is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_SCORED`, and a voided audit is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_VOIDED` — which, as §2.11 records, is checked first, so scoring a voided audit reports the void rather than the prior scoring.

The two closing branches deliberately carry different amounts of consequence. On `PASS` the audit moves `OPEN → CLOSED_PASS`, `scored_at` is stamped from the clock, the audited abstraction's author has their `lifetime_discrepancy_points` increased by the `weighted_error_score`, their `audit_fail_streak` is reset to zero, and both quality scores are recomputed — nothing crosses into case or program state. On `MINOR_FAIL` or `CRITICAL_FAIL` the audit moves `OPEN → CLOSED_FAIL` and the fail branch does strictly more: `lifetime_discrepancy_points` still rises by the same weighted amount, both quality scores are still recomputed, but `audit_fail_streak` is incremented by one instead of cleared. When the verdict is `CRITICAL_FAIL` and the audited abstraction is still `APPROVED`, the audit sets `cascade_applied` to true, drags that abstraction `APPROVED → REWORK_REQUIRED`, and moves its case `COMPLETED → REWORK_PENDING` with `completed_on` cleared to null and the author's `assigned_open_case_count` incremented by one; the program's backlog counts and posture are then recomputed, and a posture that becomes `FROZEN` moves every draft batch to hold. The case's `submission_state` is pointedly not touched — the two dimensions on the row are independent. Separately, and regardless of which failing verdict was reached, an `audit_fail_streak` that reaches three suspends the author: their `employment_status` becomes `SUSPENDED_FROM_ABSTRACTING` and their entire open worklist is released to `IDENTIFIED` with `assigned_open_case_count` set to zero, exactly as in the staffing cascade of §3.2.

Voiding an audit is the compensating action, available to a `RegistryManager` with a mandatory `void_reason`, and it is reachable from all three prior states as `OPEN → VOIDED`, `CLOSED_PASS → VOIDED`, and `CLOSED_FAIL → VOIDED`. Voiding an audit that was never scored, the `OPEN → VOIDED` edge, changes no ledger, because an unscored audit contributed nothing. Voiding a `CLOSED_PASS` audit subtracts its `weighted_error_score` back out of `lifetime_discrepancy_points` and recomputes both quality scores with the voided audit excluded. Voiding a `CLOSED_FAIL` audit does the same subtraction, decrements `audit_fail_streak` by one with a floor of zero (recomputed from the remaining non-voided audits), and — if `cascade_applied` was true and the audited abstraction is still `REWORK_REQUIRED` with no superseding abstraction — unwinds the rework cascade as well: the abstraction returns `REWORK_REQUIRED → APPROVED`, the case returns `REWORK_PENDING → COMPLETED` with `completed_on` restored to the value it held before the audit cascade rather than reset to the current clock date, the author's `assigned_open_case_count` is decremented by one, and the program aggregates and posture are recomputed. Both conserved quantities of §2.12 must land back on their pre-audit values.

Two quality scores are kept on every registrar, both computed from the audits of abstractions *they authored* whose `audit_state` is `CLOSED_PASS` or `CLOSED_FAIL` — open and voided audits are excluded from both — and both recomputed on scoring, on voiding, and on every clock advance. `quality_score_rolling_90d` is the weighted variant: over only those audits whose `scored_at` falls on or after the clock instant less 90 days, it divides the sum of `weighted_error_score` by the sum of `audited_field_count`, multiplies by 100, subtracts the result from 100, rounds half up to one decimal place, and clamps into 0.0 through 100.0; when no audited fields fall in the window the score is 100.0. `quality_score_cumulative` is the unweighted variant reported to the accreditation file: over every closed audit ever, it divides the total count of minor, major, and critical discrepancies — each counted once, with no severity weighting — by the total `audited_field_count`, and applies the same subtraction, rounding, and clamp, again yielding 100.0 on an empty cohort. The consequence is intentional: an old critical-heavy audit keeps dragging the cumulative score long after it has aged out of the rolling window, while a fresh minor-only audit barely moves the cumulative figure but can move the rolling one sharply. Audits can be read individually and listed as the reverse-reachability index for `abstraction_id`, `auditor_id`, `audit_state`, and `verdict`.

### 3.5. Lifetime Follow-Up Maintenance

Accreditation is not satisfied by abstracting a case once; the program must keep track of the patient afterwards. A `RegistryManager` or `RegistryAbstractor` records a follow-up contact against a case with the `contact_date` on which the attempt was made, a `contact_method` of `CLINIC_VISIT`, `LETTER`, `PHONE`, `DEATH_CERTIFICATE`, or `STATE_LINKAGE`, an `outcome` of `CONTACT_MADE`, `NO_RESPONSE`, `DECEASED`, or `MOVED_UNKNOWN`, and the `vital_status_at_contact` observed, which is `ALIVE`, `DEAD`, or `UNKNOWN`. The acting user is stamped onto the contact as `recorded_by_user_id`, which is a recorded identity rather than a foreign key. Only a case in `COMPLETED` may receive follow-up: an unabstracted or reworked case is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_COMPLETED`. The `contact_date` must be neither in the future relative to the logical clock date nor earlier than the case's `diagnosis_date`, and either violation is `ERR_CONTACT_DATE_INVALID`. A contact carries `counts_for_follow_up` true when its `outcome` is `CONTACT_MADE` or `DECEASED` and false for `NO_RESPONSE` or `MOVED_UNKNOWN` — only qualifying contacts advance the case's `last_contact_date`, which is set to the later of its existing value and the new `contact_date` so that back-dated entries cannot walk it backwards. Every contact writes `vital_status` onto the case from `vital_status_at_contact`, then recomputes the case's follow-up standing and the program's follow-up rates and posture. Contacts can be read individually and listed as the reverse-reachability index for `case_id` and `outcome`; as noted in §2.4 they are event rows and carry no `updated_at` or `version`.

A case's own follow-up standing is derived on every contact and on every clock advance. A non-analytic case is `NOT_REQUIRED` with a null `follow_up_due_date`, because non-analytic cases are outside the follow-up obligation entirely. A case whose `vital_status` is `DEAD` is `CLOSED_DECEASED`, also with a null due date — a deceased patient needs no further contact. Otherwise the anchor is `last_contact_date` when a qualifying contact exists and `first_contact_date` otherwise; `follow_up_due_date` is that anchor plus 365 days, and the age of the anchor in whole days against the clock date decides the status: an age of 365 days or fewer is `CURRENT`, an age from 366 through 456 days is `DELINQUENT`, and an age of 457 days or more is `LOST`. The boundaries are exact — 365 is still `CURRENT`, 366 has slipped, 456 is still merely `DELINQUENT`, and 457 is `LOST`.

The program-level follow-up rate exists in two variants that report different numbers from the same case set, and both are stored. Both start from the same base: analytic tumor cases of the program that have reached `COMPLETED`, because a case still sitting in the abstraction backlog is not yet in anybody's follow-up denominator — which is why a brand-new program truthfully reports 100.0. The Commission-facing variant, stored as `follow_up_rate_coc_pct`, restricts the denominator to cases diagnosed within 1826 days of the clock date and drops patients whose `vital_status` is `DEAD` entirely; its numerator is those denominator cases with a non-null `last_contact_date` no more than 456 days old. The state-facing variant, stored as `follow_up_rate_state_pct`, keeps every diagnosis year with no cohort cutoff and keeps dead patients in the denominator, counting a case as followed either when `vital_status` is `DEAD` or when `last_contact_date` is no more than 365 days old. Each is the numerator over the denominator times 100, rounded half up to one decimal place, and an empty denominator reports 100.0. The companion `lost_to_follow_up_pct` is, in the Commission variant, the complement share of that cohort — the denominator count less the numerator count, over the denominator, times 100, rounded the same way, and 0.0 on an empty denominator.

The program's compliance summary is a computed read that recomputes and returns both rates, the conference compliance flags, the backlog counts and the posture without mutating any workflow state. It accepts a `follow_up_variant` parameter that selects only which variant `lost_to_follow_up_pct` is reported from: `coc_five_year`, the default when the parameter is absent, reports the Commission complement described above, while `state_central_all_years` reports 100.0 minus `follow_up_rate_state_pct`. Both rate fields are returned in either case, and an unrecognised value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The 80.0 percent Commission-variant floor that this rate must clear is an accreditation-decision precondition and is described in §3.9.

### 3.6. State Central Registry Batches, Edit Resolution and Late-Reporting Assessments

Completed cases must be reported onward to the state central registry in periodic batches. A `RegistryManager` opens a batch for a closed reporting period by naming the `program_id`, the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end`, and the `deadline_date` by which the batch must be transmitted. Reporting periods are exclusive within a program: a new batch conflicts with any existing batch of the same program whose `period_start` is on or before the new `period_end` and whose `period_end` is on or after the new `period_start` — that is, periods that share even a single calendar day conflict, and the refusal is `ERR_SUBMISSION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Adjacency is fine: a period beginning the day after an existing period ends is accepted. Every batch of the program counts as a blocker regardless of its state, so an already-accepted period is not reusable. A new batch opens in `DRAFT` with `total_case_count`, `accepted_item_count`, `failed_item_count`, `resubmission_round`, `late_case_count`, `assessment_due_cents`, and `assessment_paid_cents` at zero, `relief_pct` at 0.0, `edit_error_codes` empty, and `transmitted_at` null — except that a batch opened while the program's posture is already `FROZEN` is created directly in `HOLD` with `pre_hold_state` set to `DRAFT`, so it never becomes transmittable until the freeze lifts.

Cases are added to a draft batch one at a time by a `RegistryManager` or `RegistryAbstractor`, naming the `case_id` and the mandatory `expected_submission_version` of §2.8. The batch must be in `DRAFT` (`ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_DRAFT`), the case must have reached `COMPLETED` on its abstraction dimension (`ERR_CASE_NOT_COMPLETED`), the case's own `submission_state` must still be `NOT_SUBMITTED` so no case can appear in two batches (`ERR_CASE_ALREADY_IN_SUBMISSION`), and the case must belong to the same program as the batch (`ERR_CASE_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`). The add creates a `SubmissionItem` with `edit_status` `PENDING` and empty `edit_codes`, moves the case `NOT_SUBMITTED → PENDING_SUBMISSION` on its second dimension, increments `total_case_count` by one, and increments the batch's `version` by one — which is what makes the next add require a re-read. Items are readable individually and listable as the reverse-reachability index for `submission_id`, `case_id`, and `edit_status`.

Transmission moves the batch `DRAFT → TRANSMITTED` and every item's case `PENDING_SUBMISSION → SUBMITTED`, stamps `transmitted_at` from the logical clock, increments the batch `version` by one, and may carry a `transmit_note`; items themselves stay `PENDING` because no results have come back yet. Four gates guard it in a fixed order, as recorded in §2.11: the batch must be in `DRAFT`, which is also what a held or already-transmitted batch fails on; the posture must not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_REGISTRY_FROZEN`); the batch must contain at least one item (`ERR_SUBMISSION_EMPTY`); every item's case must still be `COMPLETED`, so a case dragged back into rework by an audit blocks the batch with `ERR_CASE_NOT_COMPLETED`; and last of all, any outstanding assessment blocks it with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_UNPAID`.

Edit results come back from the state as a single batch posted by a `StateRegistryLiaison` as `item_results`, an array whose entries each carry a `case_id`, the returned `edit_status` of `ACCEPTED` or `EDIT_FAILED`, and the `edit_codes` the state raised for that record, written as state edit identifiers such as `IF25` and `IF32`. The batch must be in `TRANSMITTED` (`ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_TRANSMITTED`); every `case_id` in the array must name an item that belongs to this batch, and one that does not is `ERR_SUBMISSION_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); and the array must cover every item on the batch, a partial batch being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each item's `edit_status` and `edit_codes` are stored, `accepted_item_count`, `failed_item_count`, and the sorted distinct union `edit_error_codes` are recomputed, each accepted item's case moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` (terminal for that dimension) and each failed item's case moves `SUBMITTED → EDIT_REJECTED`, and the batch `version` is incremented by one. The batch itself then forks: if every entry was accepted it reaches the terminal `TRANSMITTED → ACCEPTED`; if even one entry failed it goes `TRANSMITTED → EDITS_RETURNED`. Both branches compute the assessment.

The late-reporting assessment has a pinned evaluation order because the relief tier and the assessment total would otherwise be circular. First, `late_case_count` is the number of items on the batch — regardless of their `edit_status` — whose case has a non-null `completed_on` that falls after that case's `abstract_due_date`; the per-record component is 2500 cents for each such case. Second, the batch component is 15000 cents when the calendar date of `transmitted_at` is later than `deadline_date`, and zero otherwise. Third, those two components are added to give the pre-relief subtotal. Fourth, `relief_pct` is selected from that pre-relief subtotal by descending threshold with the first match winning: a subtotal of 100000 cents or more earns 0.30, otherwise 50000 cents or more earns 0.20, otherwise 20000 cents or more earns 0.10, otherwise 0.00. The tier is keyed on the pre-relief subtotal and never on the post-relief amount, and the boundaries are inclusive, so a subtotal of exactly 20000 cents earns ten percent relief. Fifth and last, `assessment_due_cents` is the pre-relief subtotal multiplied by one minus `relief_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — a single rounding step at the end. Computing the assessment never touches `assessment_paid_cents`.

Edit resolution is the cycle. A `RegistryAbstractor` or `RegistryManager` resolves one failed item at a time with a mandatory `resolution_note`, moving that item `EDIT_FAILED → RESOLVED`; an item in any other `edit_status` is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FAILED`. The note is stored, the `edit_codes` are retained for audit rather than cleared, `failed_item_count` is decremented by one, and the case's own dimension deliberately stays `EDIT_REJECTED` until the whole batch goes back out. Retransmission then moves the batch `EDITS_RETURNED → TRANSMITTED`, optionally with a `retransmit_note`, increments `resubmission_round` by one, refreshes `transmitted_at`, increments `version` by one, and returns every `RESOLVED` item to `PENDING` with its case moving `EDIT_REJECTED → SUBMITTED`. Its gates fire in order: the batch must be in `EDITS_RETURNED` (`ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_IN_EDIT_RESOLUTION`), `failed_item_count` must be zero (`ERR_UNRESOLVED_EDITS`), `resubmission_round` must be strictly below two so that the third retransmit is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, the posture must not be `FROZEN`, and finally the assessment must be settled (`ERR_ASSESSMENT_UNPAID`).

Assessments are paid down by a `RegistryManager` or `PlatformAdmin` recording an `amount_cents`, which must be a positive integer and must not exceed the outstanding balance of `assessment_due_cents` less `assessment_paid_cents`; an overpayment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. A payment increases `assessment_paid_cents` and changes nothing else — in particular it never advances `submission_state`, it merely unblocks the transmit or retransmit that the outstanding balance was holding up. Batches are readable individually and listable as the reverse-reachability index for `program_id` and `submission_state`. The two case dimensions remain independent throughout: a case can sit in `REWORK_PENDING` on its abstraction dimension while still reading `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` on its submission dimension, and the audit cascade of §3.4 explicitly leaves the submission dimension alone.

### 3.7. Multidisciplinary Cancer Conference Evidence

The multidisciplinary cancer conference is scheduled on a per-program calendar. An `AccreditationCoordinator` or `RegistryManager` books a session against a `program_id` with a `start_at` instant, an `end_at` instant that must be strictly later than `start_at`, and the `required_disciplines` that must be represented for the session to count, whose members are drawn from `SURGERY`, `MEDICAL_ONCOLOGY`, `RADIATION_ONCOLOGY`, `PATHOLOGY`, and `RADIOLOGY`. A new session opens in `SCHEDULED` with an empty `attendances` array, `cases_presented_count` and `prospective_case_count` at zero, `prospective_case_ratio` at 0.0, `quorum_met` false, and `credited_on` null.

The calendar arithmetic here is deliberately unlike the auditor windows of §3.4 on both axes: the occupied interval is half-open, running from `start_at` inclusive to `end_at` exclusive, and it carries a mandatory 30-minute changeover buffer. A candidate session conflicts with an existing session of the same program when the candidate's `start_at` is strictly earlier than that session's `end_at` plus 30 minutes and that session's `start_at` is strictly earlier than the candidate's `end_at` plus 30 minutes. Three consequences follow from those semantics and are binding. Back-to-back sessions conflict: a candidate starting exactly when an existing session ends is refused, because the changeover buffer has not elapsed. A candidate starting exactly 30 minutes after an existing session ends is accepted, because the comparison against the buffered edge is strict. And sessions belonging to a different program never conflict, because the calendar is per program; within a program, sessions in every state — including `CREDITED` and `NOT_CREDITED` — still occupy their slot. A conflict is `ERR_CONFERENCE_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Attendance is recorded one entry at a time on a session that must still be `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_SCHEDULED`), by an `AccreditationCoordinator` supplying an `attendee_ref` identifying the attendee, their `discipline`, the `attended_minutes` they were present for, and an optional `is_required_coordinator` flag marking the attendees whose personal attendance rate the accreditation standard tracks. The `attendee_ref` must be unique within the session's `attendances` array; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTENDANCE` (HTTP 409). Each call appends an object carrying `attendee_ref`, `discipline`, `is_required_coordinator`, and `attended_minutes` to the array and recomputes `quorum_met`, which stays false while `cases_presented_count` is still zero no matter how many attendees have been recorded.

Holding the session records what was actually presented: an `AccreditationCoordinator` supplies `cases_presented_count` and `prospective_case_count`, moving the session `SCHEDULED → HELD`. The session must be `SCHEDULED`, the logical clock instant must have reached `start_at` — a session cannot be held before it begins, and the refusal is `ERR_SESSION_NOT_YET_STARTED` — and `prospective_case_count` must not exceed `cases_presented_count`, an excess being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Holding stores both counts and recomputes `prospective_case_ratio` and `quorum_met`.

Quorum has three simultaneous conditions. An attendance qualifies only when its `attended_minutes` is at least 30, so an attendee present for exactly 30 minutes qualifies and one present for 29 does not. `prospective_case_ratio` is `prospective_case_count` divided by `cases_presented_count`, rounded to three decimal places, and 0.0 when no cases were presented. Quorum is met when `cases_presented_count` is greater than zero, when every discipline in `required_disciplines` appears among the disciplines of the qualifying attendances, and when the rounded `prospective_case_ratio` is at least 0.80 — a ratio of exactly 0.800 meets the standard. A session with an empty `attendances` array, or with no cases presented, never has quorum.

Crediting the session is the terminal fork. An `AccreditationCoordinator` records a `disposition` of `CREDIT` or `NO_CREDIT`, optionally with a `credit_note`, against a session that must be in `HELD` (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_HELD`). `CREDIT` requires `quorum_met` to be true, refusing otherwise with `ERR_CONFERENCE_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, and moves the session `HELD → CREDITED` with `credited_on` set to the clock date. `NO_CREDIT` moves it `HELD → NOT_CREDITED` with `credited_on` left null, so the session never enters the frequency numerator. Both dispositions recompute the program's conference compliance figures, because the coordinator-attendance denominator excludes non-credited sessions and therefore changes under either outcome.

Conference compliance is measured over the trailing twelve calendar months — from the first day of the month eleven months before the clock's month through the last day of the clock's month, inclusive at both ends — and is also recomputed on every clock advance. A program whose `annual_analytic_caseload` is 500 or more must hold two credited sessions per month, and a smaller program one per month, so `required_sessions_12mo` is that per-month figure multiplied by twelve: 24 sessions for a program at exactly 500 cases, 12 for a smaller one. `credited_sessions_12mo` counts only the program's sessions in `CREDITED` whose `start_at` date falls inside the window — `NOT_CREDITED`, `HELD`, and `SCHEDULED` sessions are excluded from both numerator and denominator — and `conference_frequency_compliant` is true when `credited_sessions_12mo` is at least `required_sessions_12mo`. Coordinator attendance is then checked per person: the distinct `attendee_ref` values flagged `is_required_coordinator` across the attendances of those credited sessions each get an attendance rate, being the number of credited sessions in which that reference appears with `attended_minutes` of at least 30, divided by `credited_sessions_12mo`, times 100, rounded half up to one decimal place. `coordinator_attendance_compliant` is true only when every such rate is at least 50.0 — a rate of exactly 50.0 is compliant — and is false outright when no credited session falls in the window or no coordinator is flagged in any of them. Sessions are readable individually, with their `attendances` array and quorum flag, and listable as the reverse-reachability index for `program_id` and `session_state`.

### 3.8. Quality Measure Performance and Improvement Projects

A `RegistryManager` or `AccreditationCoordinator` registers a Commission quality measure for a program by naming its `measure_code` — the Commission's measure identifier, such as `BCSRT`, `MASTRT`, or `nBx` — the `measurement_year` the measure covers, and the `threshold_pct` the program's rate must reach, which must lie between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive. A new measure opens in `DRAFT` with an empty `strata` array, `included_strata_count` at zero, `performance_rate_pct` and `pooled_rate_pct` unset, and `performance_state` `NOT_EVALUATED`.

Evaluation supplies the numerators and denominators as a `strata` array, each entry an object carrying a `stage_group`, the `eligible_case_count` in that stratum, and the `compliant_case_count` within it. Strata whose `eligible_case_count` is zero are dropped before any arithmetic happens, and `included_strata_count` is the number that survive; if none survive, the evaluation is refused with `ERR_MEASURE_STRATA_EMPTY` and no rate field is written. The rounding order is pinned and load-bearing. Each surviving stratum's rate is `compliant_case_count` divided by `eligible_case_count`, times 100, rounded half up to one decimal place. `performance_rate_pct` is then the unweighted mean of those already-rounded stratum rates, itself rounded half up to one decimal place — so every stratum contributes equally no matter how many eligible cases it holds. Separately, `pooled_rate_pct` sums the `compliant_case_count` values and the `eligible_case_count` values across the surviving strata and divides once, rounding half up to one decimal place. The pooled figure is stored for contrast and is never the accountability rate: for identical inputs, a large low-performing stratum drags the pooled rate below the stratified one, and only the stratified rate is tested against the threshold.

The verdict has three bands, tested against `threshold_pct` with inclusive comparisons: a basis rate at or above `threshold_pct` plus 10.0 is `EXCEEDS`, a basis rate at or above `threshold_pct` is `MEETS`, and anything below is `UNDER_THRESHOLD`. The basis is `performance_rate_pct` on an evaluation and `final_rate_pct` when a project is being closed. A rate exactly equal to `threshold_pct` is `MEETS`, not `UNDER_THRESHOLD`.

Evaluation is also the branch point in the measure's lifecycle. A measure that resolves to `MEETS` or `EXCEEDS` moves `DRAFT → MEASURED`; one that resolves to `UNDER_THRESHOLD` moves `DRAFT → PROJECT_REQUIRED`, because the program now owes an improvement project. Evaluation is accepted from `DRAFT`, `MEASURED`, `PROJECT_CLOSED_ACHIEVED`, and `PROJECT_CLOSED_MISSED` — the last three forming the re-entry edge, `MEASURED → PROJECT_REQUIRED` when the fresh strata come in under the threshold, that lets a measure be re-measured with replacement strata after a project has closed, all rate fields being recomputed and the strata replaced when supplied. Evaluating a measure whose `measure_state` is `PROJECT_REQUIRED` or `PROJECT_ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_MEASURE_STATE_INVALID`, since a project is mid-flight.

The improvement project is driven by an `AccreditationCoordinator` through a single operation carrying an `action` of `OPEN` or `CLOSE`. `OPEN` records a `project_title` and a `target_rate_pct` and moves the measure `PROJECT_REQUIRED → PROJECT_ACTIVE`; a measure not in `PROJECT_REQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_REQUIRED`, and a `target_rate_pct` that is not strictly greater than the measure's current `performance_rate_pct` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, because a project must actually aim higher than where the program already is. `CLOSE` records a `final_rate_pct` against a measure that must be in `PROJECT_ACTIVE` (`ERR_MEASURE_STATE_INVALID`) and forks on it. A `final_rate_pct` at or above `threshold_pct` moves the measure `PROJECT_ACTIVE → PROJECT_CLOSED_ACHIEVED` with `performance_state` recomputed from `final_rate_pct`. A `final_rate_pct` below `threshold_pct` moves it `PROJECT_ACTIVE → PROJECT_CLOSED_MISSED` and reaches across aggregates: because the project missed its threshold, the system auto-cites a deficiency against the program with `standard_code` `STD 7.3`, `severity` `MAJOR`, `cited_on` set to the logical clock date, `source_measure_id` set to this measure's `id`, and `deficiency_state` `CITED`. Because a major deficiency is now open, the program's `registry_posture` is recomputed — a major deficiency on its own does not freeze the registry, only a critical one does, as §2.7 sets out — but the open deficiency does block an `ACCREDIT` decision until it is closed. Measures are readable individually, showing both the stratified accountability rate and the pooled rate, and listable as the reverse-reachability index for `program_id`, `measure_code`, `measurement_year`, `measure_state`, and `performance_state`.

### 3.9. Deficiency Citation, Corrective-Action Response and the Accreditation Cycle

A `CoCSurveyor` cites a deficiency against a program by naming the `standard_code` at issue — a Commission standard identifier such as `STD 6.1` — the `severity`, which is `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, or `CRITICAL`, and the `cited_on` date, which must not be later than the logical clock date, optionally with an `evidence_reference` and, for citations traced to a measure, a `source_measure_id`. A citation opens in `CITED` with `escalation_count` at zero and `response_narrative`, `reviewer_notes`, and (unless supplied) `source_measure_id` null. The response window is derived from severity: a `CRITICAL` deficiency must be answered within 30 days of `cited_on`, a `MAJOR` within 45 days, and a `MINOR` within 60 days, and `response_due_date` is `cited_on` plus that many days. A `CRITICAL` citation immediately recomputes `open_critical_deficiency_count` and forces the posture to `FROZEN`, moving every draft batch to hold; a `MAJOR` or `MINOR` citation does not, though it still counts against an `ACCREDIT` decision.

The program answers through an `AccreditationCoordinator`, who files a mandatory `response_narrative` and an optional `evidence_reference`, moving the deficiency `CITED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` on the first attempt and `RESPONSE_REJECTED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` on any later one. Responses are accepted only from `CITED` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED`; a deficiency already under review, or terminally `RESOLVED` or `ESCALATED`, is refused with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_RESPONDABLE`. The response must be filed while the logical clock date is on or before `response_due_date` — a response filed on exactly the due date is accepted and one filed a day later is refused with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A second response overwrites `response_narrative` and `evidence_reference` rather than appending, and the acting `X-User-ID` is retained on the deficiency so that the segregation-of-duties check on review has something to compare against.

Review is where the deficiency either dies or escalates. A `CoCSurveyor` records an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, optionally with `reviewer_notes`, against a deficiency that must be in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` (`ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`), and the reviewing user must not be the user recorded on the response (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`). `ACCEPTED` moves the deficiency `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESOLVED`, which is terminal, stores `reviewer_notes`, recomputes `open_critical_deficiency_count`, recomputes `registry_posture`, and — because the posture may have just left `FROZEN` — restores every held batch to its `pre_hold_state`. `REJECTED` forks on the escalation cap of two. Below the cap the deficiency moves `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_REJECTED`, `escalation_count` is incremented by one, and `response_due_date` is extended by a further 15 days, giving the program another attempt inside the newly extended window. At the cap — that is, when incrementing `escalation_count` would reach two — the deficiency instead moves `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → ESCALATED`, which is terminal, still incrementing `escalation_count`, and the consequence propagates four links deep: because the deficiency escalated, the program moves `ACCREDITED → PROBATION` if its `accreditation_state` was `ACCREDITED`, `SURVEY_SCHEDULED`, or `DEFICIENCY_RESPONSE`; because the program entered probation, `registry_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN`; and because the posture became frozen, every draft batch of the program moves to `HOLD` with `pre_hold_state` recorded as `DRAFT`. The clock checkpoint of §2.6 reaches the same terminal state by a different route, taking any still-`CITED` deficiency whose `response_due_date` the clock has passed straight through `CITED → ESCALATED` with `escalation_count` incremented by one, and with the identical program and posture cascade — which is precisely why a deficiency sitting in `RESPONSE_REJECTED` past its extended deadline is not swept, since only `CITED` deficiencies are in scope. Deficiencies are readable individually, with their response window and escalation count, and listable as the reverse-reachability index for `program_id`, `source_measure_id`, `severity`, and `deficiency_state`.

The accreditation cycle itself has four states and closes on itself. Scheduling the survey is performed by an `AccreditationCoordinator` supplying a `survey_date`, and is accepted only from `ACCREDITED` or `PROBATION` (`ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`); the date must be at least 60 days after the logical clock date, so a date exactly 60 days out is accepted and anything sooner is refused with `ERR_SURVEY_DATE_TOO_SOON`. The scheduling stores `survey_date` and `survey_year` and moves the program `ACCREDITED → SURVEY_SCHEDULED`.

Recording survey completion is a two-branch fork performed by a `CoCSurveyor` with a `completed_on` date and an optional `surveyor_note`, and it is accepted only from `SURVEY_SCHEDULED`. Both branches set `survey_year` to the logical clock year and `next_survey_due_year` to that year plus three. The branch is decided by counting the program's deficiencies of severity `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` whose `deficiency_state` is `CITED`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, or `RESPONSE_REJECTED` — the open ones. When that count is zero the program returns `SURVEY_SCHEDULED → ACCREDITED` directly and its posture is recomputed. When the count is one or more the program moves `SURVEY_SCHEDULED → DEFICIENCY_RESPONSE` and must clear those deficiencies before any accreditation decision can be recorded; the downstream transitions legal from the two branches are therefore different, which is the point of the fork. Either way the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as the surveyor of record for the segregation check on the decision.

The accreditation decision is rendered by a `CoCSurveyor` as a `decision` of `ACCREDIT`, `PROBATION`, or `WITHDRAW`, optionally with a `decision_note`, and may not be rendered by the same user who completed the survey (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`). It is accepted only from `DEFICIENCY_RESPONSE` or `PROBATION`, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for it as for the other two accreditation actions. `ACCREDIT` carries two compliance gates: the count of open `MAJOR` and `CRITICAL` deficiencies must be zero, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_DEFICIENCIES_BLOCK_ACCREDITATION`, and `follow_up_rate_coc_pct` must be at least 80.0, otherwise `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_RATE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` — a rate of exactly 80.0 clears the bar. A successful `ACCREDIT` moves the program `DEFICIENCY_RESPONSE → ACCREDITED`, or `PROBATION → ACCREDITED` when it was on probation, sets `next_survey_due_year` to `survey_year` plus three, recomputes the posture, and restores every held batch to its `pre_hold_state` as the freeze lifts; the `PROBATION → ACCREDITED` edge is the one that closes the cycle. `PROBATION` moves the program `DEFICIENCY_RESPONSE → PROBATION`, forces the posture to `FROZEN`, and moves every draft batch to `HOLD`. `WITHDRAW` moves the program `DEFICIENCY_RESPONSE → WITHDRAWN`, or `PROBATION → WITHDRAWN` from probation, which is terminal, where the posture stays `FROZEN` permanently and no further reportability decision is accepted — the program is finished, and §2.7 explains why nothing thaws it. Programs are readable individually with every derived compliance aggregate, and listable with filters on `accreditation_state` and `registry_posture`.
