# Nutrient Trading & Watershed Load Allocation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

State water quality programs are required to write a Total Maximum Daily Load (TMDL) for every impaired watershed: a defensible accounting of how much nutrient the water body can absorb, how much each pollutant source contributes today, and how the remaining assimilative capacity is to be divided. That accounting has historically lived in spreadsheets. The consequences of it living there are familiar to every program: wasteload allocations are transcribed by hand into discharge permits, compliance schedules are negotiated without a reproducible derivation, and credits generated by agricultural best management practices are tracked in registries that cannot prove a credit was never sold twice. This service replaces that arrangement with a single system of record covering the whole chain — from source assessment through load allocation, permit limit derivation, credit generation and verification, registry serialization, permittee purchase, and finally back to monitoring data that tells the program whether the watershed actually responded.

The product has two audiences whose incentives pull in opposite directions, and the design is shaped by that tension. Program staff — administrators, modellers and permit writers — need the allocation arithmetic to be conserved to the cent: every pound of loading capacity must be traceable to a margin of safety, a background load, a point-source wasteload allocation, a nonpoint load allocation, or a reserve set-aside, with nothing lost to rounding. Market participants — agricultural producers, third-party verifiers, registry operators and regulated dischargers — need credits to behave like serialized instruments: minted once, encumbered while a trade is open, retired when a trade settles, and voided in full when the practice that generated them turns out not to have been implemented. Both audiences share one non-negotiable requirement: nothing in the trading market may allow a discharger to buy its way out of a load reduction the watershed cannot afford.

Accordingly the system is deliberately cautious. Trading ratios discount every credit for scientific uncertainty, for pollutant equivalence, and for the distance the reduction has to travel downstream before it reaches the buyer's outfall. A fraction of every trade is retired outright into a watershed reserve pool rather than being delivered to anyone. Segregation of duties prevents the person who verified a practice's baseline from verifying its implementation, and prevents the registry operator who certifies a project from having verified it. And when adaptive-management monitoring reports that the watershed is not on track toward its interim reduction target, the program can suspend trading, which unwinds every open and settled trade in the watershed and pushes the affected permits back into shortfall. This document specifies that system: its resources, its state machines, its arithmetic, its rejection ordering, and its HTTP contract.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting under. If either header is absent, is empty once surrounding whitespace has been trimmed, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the seven roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check precedes every other check in the system. The trimmed header values are the ones the server records and compares, so surrounding whitespace never participates in an ownership comparison. Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear on the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called; a role that is not listed is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. Section 4 records the authorized roles for each endpoint.

The seven roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `TmdlAnalyst`, `PermitWriter`, `Permittee`, `Producer`, `PracticeVerifier` and `RegistryAdministrator`. A `ProgramAdministrator` is the state program owner: it registers watersheds, drives the watershed program lifecycle, opens and finalizes adaptive reviews, and holds an emergency power to void credit transactions. A `TmdlAnalyst` is the water quality modeller: it builds assessments, inventories pollutant sources, and executes load allocation runs. A `PermitWriter` drafts permits against point-source wasteload allocations and derives and then finalizes effluent limits; it can neither report compliance nor approve credit trades. A `Permittee` is a regulated point-source discharger that files its own annual report and buys credits against its own permit only. A `Producer` is an agricultural landowner that registers credit-generating practice projects on its own tracts. A `PracticeVerifier` is a third-party field verifier that files baseline, implementation and reverification findings. A `RegistryAdministrator` operates the credit registry: it certifies verified projects into serialized batches, lists and retires batches, and approves transactions, but it may not verify or register the practices it certifies.

Identity semantics differ across these roles and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Producer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Producer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Producer` compare that resource's `producer_id` field to `X-User-ID`, so a producer registering a practice project must submit its own `producer_id` in the body or the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is any of `ProgramAdministrator`, `TmdlAnalyst`, `PermitWriter`, `Permittee`, `PracticeVerifier` or `RegistryAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server does record such opaque identifiers where a workflow demands an audit trail: a permit stores the opaque identifier of the discharger authorised to act on it in `permittee_user_id`, a verification event records the acting verifier in `inspector_user_id`, and a practice project retains `baseline_verifier_user_id` and `implementation_verifier_user_id`. Ownership enforcement for the `Permittee` role therefore compares `X-User-ID` against the target permit's stored `permittee_user_id` value rather than against an entity `id`; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer literal, a `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422; an `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error and yields an empty page. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, and endpoints that are not collection endpoints ignore query parameters altogether. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four collections carry a domain-motivated ordering instead. The credit registry view of batches is ordered by `serial_start` ascending, so the registry reads as a contiguous serial ledger. Verification events are ordered by `observed_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Monitoring samples are ordered by `sample_datetime` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Adaptive reviews are ordered by `period_start_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Because the logical clock does not advance on its own, many rows commonly share one `created_at`; every ordering in this section therefore takes the server's row-creation sequence as its final tie-break, so rows whose declared sort keys are equal appear in the order they were created.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters, all optional and all combinable, applied conjunctively as exact equality against the stored field. A filter whose domain is an enumeration — every `status`, `verdict`, `method`, `reach_class`, `qa_status`, `source_type`, `limit_status`, `compliance_status`, `event_type` and `outcome` filter — rejects a value outside that enumeration with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422 rather than returning an empty page, and the integer filters `vintage_year` and `reporting_period_year` reject a value that is not an integer literal the same way; these filter checks are evaluated before `limit` and `offset`. Filters that carry an identifier or a free-text code are not resolved: one naming no existing row is not an error and simply yields an empty page. Reaches may be filtered by `watershed_id`, by `downstream_reach_id` — which selects the immediate upstream tributaries of one reach — by `reach_class` and by `trading_zone_code`. Assessments accept `watershed_id` and `status`. Source assessments accept `tmdl_assessment_id`, `reach_id`, `allocation_run_id` — which selects exactly the sources a given allocation run currently owns — plus `source_type` and `status`. Allocation runs accept `tmdl_assessment_id`, `status` and `method`; because the ordering is by `created_at` ascending, the last element of an unpaginated result is the newest run on a given assessment. Permits accept `watershed_id`, `source_assessment_id`, `limit_status` and `compliance_status`. Practice projects accept `producer_id`, `reach_id`, `status` and `tract_code`. Verification events accept `practice_project_id`, `event_type` and `outcome`. Credit batches accept `practice_project_id`, `status` and `vintage_year`. Credit transactions accept `credit_batch_id`, `permit_id`, `status` and `reporting_period_year`. Monitoring samples accept `reach_id` and `qa_status`. Adaptive reviews accept `watershed_id`, `status` and `verdict`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Program Clock

Every resource the API creates carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC notation with exactly three fractional-second digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical instant on every mutation and equal to `created_at` at the instant the row is created, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade originating on another resource. Two resource types are append-only records of an observation rather than mutable state, and they are deliberately narrower: a monitoring sample and a verification event each carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field, and neither has an update endpoint; once written they are immutable.

Because compliance schedules, reporting deadlines and review windows are all date-sensitive, the server keeps a logical clock rather than reading the host wall clock. An administrative test-driving capability sets that clock from a supplied `current_utc` instant, and is available to all seven roles; it brings no row into existence and answers HTTP 200 with a `data` object whose sole member is `current_utc`, carrying the submitted value echoed back verbatim. Before the clock has ever been set, its value is 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z. Every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `observed_at`, `approved_at`, `certified_at`, `rescinded_at`, `voided_at` and `finalized_at` value the server writes is read from this clock, and every comparison the specification phrases as "today" or "the current date" means the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC. The two instants a client may itself supply are `current_utc` and `sample_datetime`; each is accepted in ISO 8601 form, and a value carrying an explicit UTC offset is interpreted at that offset while a value carrying no offset at all is read as already being UTC. The instant that results is what the clock and every comparison and ordering then use, but the field is echoed back to the client exactly as the client wrote it rather than rewritten into the canonical form. A date-typed field, by contrast, is accepted only in bare `YYYY-MM-DD` form.

### 2.5. Units, Precision and Rounding Discipline

Loads are carried in pounds per year and are stored to 2 decimal places. Ratios and factors that discount or route a quantity — the watershed's `uncertainty_ratio`, `retirement_ratio` and `equivalence_ratio`, a reach's `delivery_factor`, a source's `controllability_index`, a computed `delivery_ratio` and a batch's `verified_ratio` — are carried to 4 decimal places. Percentages such as `interim_target_pct`, `margin_of_safety_pct`, `required_reduction_pct` and `achieved_reduction_pct` are carried to 2 decimal places, except `margin_of_safety_pct` which is expressed as a fraction of capacity to 4 decimal places. A permit's `design_flow_mgd` is carried to 4 decimal places. Sample concentrations in `measured_conc_mg_l` are carried to 4 decimal places and stream flow in `flow_cfs` to 2 decimal places. Every quantity in this document travels over the wire as a JSON number and never as a string, in either direction; a declared decimal place count therefore constrains how many decimal places a submitted number may carry, not how the number is typed.

Every rounding operation in this system rounds half away from zero — that is, a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds up in magnitude. Where a calculation rounds more than once, the specification of that calculation states each rounding point, and intermediate results are rounded exactly where stated and nowhere else. Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, the allocation arithmetic rounds each individual source's allocation to 2 decimal places before any summation; summing exact quotients and rounding the total once at the end produces a different answer and is incorrect. Second, the geographic delivery ratio is the product of a chain of delivery factors and is rounded to 4 decimal places exactly once, after the entire product has been accumulated — never factor by factor.

Serial numbers are whole pounds. A batch's `issued_lbs`, `serial_start` and `serial_end`, a watershed's `serial_counter`, an assessment's `revision_round`, a run's `included_source_count`, a review's `sample_count` and `voided_transaction_count`, and the year fields `baseline_year`, `vintage_year` and `reporting_period_year` are all integers.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Cross-Watershed Referential Integrity

Path-templated identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the response, which is always HTTP 404 with the not-found code specific to that resource type. The templated identifiers in use are `watershed_id`, `reach_id`, `tmdl_assessment_id`, `source_assessment_id`, `allocation_run_id`, `permit_id`, `producer_id`, `practice_project_id`, `credit_batch_id`, `credit_transaction_id`, `verification_event_id`, `monitoring_sample_id` and `adaptive_review_id`, and their respective failures are `ERR_WATERSHED_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REACH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TMDL_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRACTICE_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ADAPTIVE_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. A monitoring sample fetched by an unknown identifier yields `ERR_MONITORING_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`; a verification event fetched by an unknown identifier yields `ERR_VERIFICATION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, also HTTP 404. Identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved after the path identifiers and after the body's shape has been validated, in the order in which the endpoint's input fields are declared, and they carry the same not-found codes. Routing is decided ahead of every check in this document: a request whose path matches no declared endpoint is answered with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404, and a request to a declared path under a method that path does not declare is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 405, both without the authentication headers being consulted at all. A trailing slash on any path is accepted as equivalent to the same path without it.

A watershed is a closed accounting universe, and rows joined by a single request must belong to one watershed. When a reach is created with a `downstream_reach_id`, that downstream reach must share the new reach's `watershed_id`. When a source assessment is created, its `reach_id` must resolve to a reach in the same watershed as the parent assessment. When a permit is created, its `source_assessment_id` must resolve to a source assessment whose own assessment carries the same `watershed_id` the permit body supplies. When a credit transaction is proposed, the reach that generated the batch and the reach receiving the buyer permit's discharge must sit in the same watershed. Any of these joins spanning two watersheds is rejected with `ERR_WATERSHED_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422.

Four code fields are unique. A watershed's `watershed_code` is unique across every watershed in the system, and a permit's `permit_number` is unique across every permit in the system. A reach's `reach_code` and an assessment's `assessment_code` are each unique within their watershed, so two watersheds may reuse the same reach code without conflict. A collision on any of the four is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` and HTTP 409.

### 2.7. Stale-Parent Rejection on Registry and Allocation Writes

Two write operations in this system commit against a parent row whose contents the client must have observed, and both therefore require the client to declare the parent `version` it last read. These are the only two endpoints that take a version field; no other endpoint accepts one.

Proposing a credit transaction requires the non-optional field `expected_credit_batch_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target credit batch. If that value does not equal the batch's current `version`, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This check exists because partial purchases from one batch are legitimate product behaviour: a second proposal against the same batch is not blocked by any state precondition, so nothing except the stale-version check stops a permittee from committing against a bucket layout that has since changed underneath it.

Creating an allocation run on an assessment requires the non-optional field `expected_tmdl_assessment_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that assessment. If that value does not equal the assessment's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TMDL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This check exists because a new run legitimately supersedes the prior active run and rewrites every source allocation on the assessment; no state precondition blocks the second create, so nothing except the stale-version check stops an analyst from superseding a run they never saw.

### 2.8. The Order in Which Requests Are Rejected

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: the checks below are evaluated in the stated order and the first failing check produces the response. Text order alone would leave this ambiguous, so the ordering is stated explicitly and normatively here, while the substance of each check is developed in the workflow sections that follow.

1. Header presence, non-emptiness after trimming, and role recognition — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears on the called endpoint's authorized-role list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every path-templated identifier resolves, left to right — the resource-specific 404 codes listed in §2.6. Applies to every endpoint with a templated identifier.
4. Request-body shape: every required field present, of the declared type, within the declared enumeration, decimal width and range; unknown fields rejected; `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` not negative — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422. Applies to every endpoint with a request body or query parameters. The internal order of this step is itself fixed: the body is read as JSON irrespective of the request's `Content-Type`, an absent or empty body is treated as an empty object, and a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is rejected; then every field the endpoint does not declare is rejected; then the declared fields are examined in the order the endpoint declares them, each one checked for presence, then type, then enumeration membership or string pattern, then decimal width, then range. An explicit `null` counts as the field being absent, so it is rejected on a required field and takes the declared default on an optional one. A string-typed field that declares no pattern accepts any string value, the empty string included; a boolean-typed or integer-typed field never accepts a value of the other type, so `true` is not a valid integer and `1` is not a valid boolean. An endpoint that declares no request body ignores any body sent to it. Cross-field consistency checks that also report `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a period whose end does not fall strictly after its start, a practice project's `post_load_lbs_yr` that is not strictly below its `pre_load_lbs_yr`, and the rules governing when a verification event's `verified_acres` is required, forbidden or bounded — belong to this step as well, and are evaluated immediately after the per-field checks, ahead of the ownership checks of steps 5 and 6 and ahead of the body-identifier resolution of step 7. Where such a check can only be expressed against a row the request names, as the `verified_acres` comparisons against the project's `enrolled_acres` and current `verified_acres` are, it is evaluated the instant that row resolves and still ahead of every step after 7.
5. A `Permittee` acting on a permit must present the `X-User-ID` recorded in that permit's `permittee_user_id` — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403. Applies to annual reporting and to proposing a credit transaction.
6. A `Producer` registering a practice project must present the `X-User-ID` matching the body's `producer_id` — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403. Applies to practice-project registration.
7. Every identifier referenced in the request body resolves, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields — the resource-specific 404 codes. Applies to reach creation, assessment creation, source-assessment creation, permit creation, practice-project registration, verification-event filing, monitoring-sample creation and credit-transaction proposal.
8. Cross-referenced rows share one watershed — `ERR_WATERSHED_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to reach creation, source-assessment creation, permit creation and credit-transaction proposal.
9. Uniqueness of `watershed_code`, `reach_code`, `assessment_code` and `permit_number` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, 409. Applies to watershed creation, reach creation, assessment creation and permit creation.
10. The declared parent version matches the parent's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_TMDL_VERSION`, 412. Applies to credit-transaction proposal and allocation-run creation.
11. The target row currently sits in a state that declares the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, 409. Applies to every state-transition operation, and includes the requirement that a transaction's batch be `LISTED` and its generating project be `CERTIFIED` or `PENDING_REVERIFICATION` when the transaction is approved.
12. The watershed's `program_status` is `ACTIVE` for every trading operation — `ERR_TRADING_SUSPENDED`, 409. Applies to exactly four operations: certifying a practice project, listing a credit batch, proposing a credit transaction and approving a credit transaction.
13. A baseline verification finding of `PASS` requires the project's `baseline_met` to be true — `ERR_BASELINE_NOT_MET`, 422. Applies to verification-event filing.
14. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 409. Applies to verification-event filing and to project certification.
15. No live practice project on the same tract and practice type occupies an overlapping effective interval — `ERR_PRACTICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, 409. Applies to practice-project registration.
16. A new adaptive review window is clear of every existing window on the watershed — `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409. Applies to adaptive-review creation.
17. The transaction's `reporting_period_year` equals the buyer permit's `reporting_period_year` — `ERR_REPORTING_YEAR_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to credit-transaction proposal.
18. Geographic eligibility — `ERR_ZONE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 422. Applies to practice-project registration, where the reach's `trading_zone_code` must be a member of the watershed's `eligible_zone_codes`, and to credit-transaction proposal, where a downstream path must link the generating reach to the buyer's reach within the depth limit.
19. The governing assessment has status `APPROVED` — `ERR_TMDL_NOT_APPROVED`, 409. Applies to finalizing permit limits and to finalizing an adaptive review.
20. The generating practice project has status `CERTIFIED` — `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CERTIFIED`, 409. Applies to listing a credit batch.
21. The wasteload allocation exists — `ERR_WLA_NOT_ALLOCATED`, 409. Applies to deriving permit limits, where the linked source assessment's `allocated_load_lbs_yr` must not be null, and to the assessment status transition into `APPROVED`, which requires at least one `ACTIVE` allocation run.
22. The permit's `limit_status` is `INTERIM` or `FINAL` — `ERR_LIMITS_NOT_DERIVED`, 409. Applies to annual reporting.
23. No permit whose source assessment carries the run being rescinded has `limit_status` of `INTERIM` or `FINAL` — `ERR_ALLOCATION_RUN_IN_USE`, 409. Applies to allocation-run rescission.
24. No transaction against the batch is still `PROPOSED` — `ERR_BATCH_HAS_OPEN_TRANSACTIONS`, 409. Applies to batch retirement.
25. At least one source assessment on the assessment has status `INVENTORIED` or `ADJUSTED` — `ERR_NO_INCLUDED_SOURCES`, 422. Applies to allocation-run creation.
26. The permit's `source_assessment_id` resolves to a source whose `source_type` is `POINT` — `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_POINT`, 422. Applies to permit creation.
27. The allocable load computed by the allocation run is not below zero — `ERR_ALLOCABLE_LOAD_NEGATIVE`, 422. Applies to allocation-run creation.
28. The requested quantity does not exceed the batch's unencumbered balance — `ERR_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT`, 409. Applies to credit-transaction proposal, where `requested_gross_lbs` may not exceed the batch's `available_lbs`, and to batch retirement, where `retire_lbs` may not exceed `available_lbs`.
29. The buyer permit is in `SHORTFALL` with a `compliance_gap_lbs` above zero — `ERR_NO_COMPLIANCE_GAP`, 409. Applies to credit-transaction proposal and again to credit-transaction approval.
30. The proposed delivered quantity is strictly above zero pounds — `ERR_DELIVERY_BELOW_MINIMUM`, 422. Applies to credit-transaction proposal.

Note the ordering consequence for the trading gate: because the program-suspension check sits at position 12, ahead of every fiscal and geographic check, a proposal into a suspended watershed reports `ERR_TRADING_SUSPENDED` rather than being masked by an insufficient-balance or ineligible-geography finding. Note likewise that the stale-parent check at position 10 precedes the state check at position 11, so a client holding a stale batch version learns that before it learns the batch is in the wrong state.

### 2.9. Load Conservation and Credit Mass Conservation

Two arithmetic identities hold exactly, at all times, and every operation in the system is designed to preserve them.

The first is load conservation on an assessment that has been allocated. The assessment's `loading_capacity_lbs_yr` is exactly equal to the sum of five terms: `margin_of_safety_lbs_yr`, `effective_background_lbs_yr`, `wla_total_lbs_yr`, `la_total_lbs_yr` and `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr`. Because each individual source allocation is rounded to 2 decimal places before summation, the group totals generally do not add up to the pools they were spread from; the reserve term absorbs that residue, which is precisely why the identity holds to the cent and not merely to within a few cents.

The second is credit mass conservation on a batch. A batch's `issued_lbs` is exactly equal to the sum of `available_lbs`, `reserved_lbs`, `retired_lbs` and `invalidated_lbs` at every instant of the batch's life. Pounds move between those four buckets as trades are proposed, settled, voided and as batches are retired or invalidated, but pounds are never created or destroyed after issuance. In particular, a downward reverification never restates `issued_lbs`.

### 2.10. Trading Gates and Program Suspension

A watershed's `program_status` gates the market. It begins at `PROVISIONAL` when the watershed is registered, advances to `ACTIVE` when the watershed's first assessment is approved, can be moved to `ADAPTIVE_HOLD` and back to `ACTIVE` any number of times, and can be moved to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal. The boolean `trading_enabled` is derived and is true if and only if `program_status` equals `ACTIVE`; it is false in `PROVISIONAL`, in `ADAPTIVE_HOLD` and in `TERMINATED`. Four operations require `program_status` to be `ACTIVE` and are rejected with `ERR_TRADING_SUSPENDED` and HTTP 409 otherwise: certifying a practice project, listing a credit batch, proposing a credit transaction, and approving a credit transaction. Note what is deliberately not gated — filing verification events, registering practice projects, retiring or voiding, permit limit derivation and annual reporting all remain available while the market is suspended, because suspending trading must not suspend the regulatory program.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties in Verification and Certification

A credit only means something if the practice behind it was checked by someone with nothing to gain. Two separations are enforced, both reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 409. First, the verifier who filed the passing baseline finding on a project — whose opaque identifier is retained in the project's `baseline_verifier_user_id` — may not file that project's implementation finding; the acting `X-User-ID` on an implementation event must differ from that stored value, and this applies to a failing implementation finding as well as a passing one. Second, the `RegistryAdministrator` who certifies a project must present an `X-User-ID` that differs from both `baseline_verifier_user_id` and `implementation_verifier_user_id` on that project.

### 2.12. Cascade Discipline

Several operations mutate more than one resource. Where a cascade touches a set of rows, that set is processed in `created_at` ascending order, so the observable effect of a cascade is deterministic. Every row a cascade mutates has its own `version` incremented by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, including rows the caller never named — a watershed whose reserve pool moved, a batch whose buckets moved, a permit whose compliance posture was recomputed. Where a single request drives two state axes on one row, as when finalizing a permit's limits also moves that permit's compliance posture, the row's `version` increments once for the whole request, not once per axis. The server also processes requests strictly one at a time, so two cascades never interleave, and every check in §2.8 is evaluated before any row is written: a request that is rejected leaves every row, every counter and the logical clock exactly as they were, and a request that succeeds is fully visible to the next request.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Watershed Program: Reaches, Zones and Monitoring

A `ProgramAdministrator` opens a program by registering a **Watershed**. Registration supplies a human-readable `name`, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the administering program, a `watershed_code` of between 2 and 8 uppercase alphanumeric characters that the credit registry will later use as the middle token of every serial prefix, the regulated nutrient in `pollutant` — either `TOTAL_NITROGEN` or `TOTAL_PHOSPHORUS` — a `baseline_year` naming the calendar year of the load baseline, a `baseline_load_lbs_yr` above zero carrying the watershed-wide annual load at that baseline year to 2 decimal places, and a non-empty `eligible_zone_codes` array of trading-zone strings in which credit-generating practices may be registered.

Four program parameters may be supplied at registration and otherwise take program defaults. `interim_target_pct` is the percentage of the baseline-to-capacity reduction that must have been achieved for an adaptive review to read as on track; it lies between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive, is carried to 2 decimal places, and defaults to 25.00. `uncertainty_ratio` is the trading uncertainty divisor applied to every delivered credit; it is at least 1.0000, is carried to 4 decimal places, and defaults to 2.0000 — that is, by default a credit delivers half of what the practice reduced. `retirement_ratio` is the fraction of every traded gross quantity withheld into the reserve pool instead of being delivered; it lies between 0.0000 and 0.5000 inclusive, is carried to 4 decimal places, and defaults to 0.1000, so by default one pound in ten of every trade is retired outright. `equivalence_ratio` is the pollutant-equivalence multiplier applied both when deriving permit limits from wasteload allocations and when delivering credits; it is strictly above 0.0000, is carried to 4 decimal places, and defaults to 1.0000.

Upon registration the watershed's `program_status` is `PROVISIONAL`, `trading_enabled` is false, `reserve_pool_lbs` is 0.00, `serial_counter` is 0, and both `allocated_wla_total_lbs_yr` and `allocated_la_total_lbs_yr` are 0.00, with `version` at 1. Those last two roll-ups mirror the watershed's most recent allocation: `allocated_wla_total_lbs_yr` carries the `wla_total_lbs_yr` and `allocated_la_total_lbs_yr` the `la_total_lbs_yr` of the last allocation run written on this watershed, each stamped onto the watershed by that run and each set back to 0.00 by a rescission. They are stamped rather than recomputed, so the pair tracks the last run to have been created or rescinded on the watershed: rescinding one assessment's run zeroes both, even when another assessment on the same watershed still carries an `ACTIVE` run of its own, and creating a run on a second assessment overwrites the figures the first assessment's run left behind rather than adding to them. The `reserve_pool_lbs` balance is the program's own holding and accrues from four distinct sources over the program's life: the allocation run's reserve set-aside, the retirement-ratio withholding on settled trades, the fractional pounds dropped when a credit batch is serialized into whole pounds, and explicit batch retirements. It never falls below 0.00 and is carried to 2 decimal places.

The hydrology of the watershed is described by **Reach** rows, which either a `ProgramAdministrator` or a `TmdlAnalyst` may create. A reach declares its `watershed_id`, a `reach_code` unique within that watershed, the `trading_zone_code` of the trading zone it belongs to, a `reach_class` drawn from `HEADWATER`, `MAINSTEM`, `COMPLIANCE_POINT` and `BACKGROUND`, and a `delivery_factor` strictly above 0.0000 and no greater than 1.0000, carried to 4 decimal places, expressing the fraction of a load leaving this reach that arrives at its immediate downstream neighbour. The optional `downstream_reach_id` names that neighbour and defaults to null, which marks the reach as the outlet of its own branch. Following `downstream_reach_id` repeatedly traces an acyclic chain toward the outlet, and that chain is the geographic-eligibility path for trading. The two special classes carry program meaning: `BACKGROUND` reaches supply the monitored background load that the final allocation variant consumes, and `COMPLIANCE_POINT` reaches supply the samples that adaptive-management evaluation consumes.

Water quality observations arrive as **MonitoringSample** rows, which a `TmdlAnalyst` or a `ProgramAdministrator` records. A sample names its `reach_id`, the instant of collection in `sample_datetime`, the measured nutrient concentration in `measured_conc_mg_l` at or above zero to 4 decimal places, the stream flow in `flow_cfs` at or above zero to 2 decimal places, and an optional `qa_status` of `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `PROVISIONAL` that defaults to `ACCEPTED`. On insertion the server computes and stores `computed_load_lbs_yr`, the annualized load the sample implies: multiply the measured concentration by the flow, multiply that product by the concentration-to-pounds-per-day conversion factor of 5.393, multiply again by 365 days in the year, and round the result half-up to 2 decimal places. This field is computed and stored for every sample regardless of `qa_status`; the quality flag never changes the stored value, it only governs which samples the downstream aggregations are allowed to consume — and both aggregations consume `ACCEPTED` samples only.

### 3.2. TMDL Development and the Stakeholder Revision Loop

A `TmdlAnalyst` drafts a **TmdlAssessment** against a watershed. The draft supplies the `watershed_id`, an `assessment_code` unique within that watershed, the total maximum daily load expressed annually as `loading_capacity_lbs_yr` strictly above zero to 2 decimal places, a `margin_of_safety_pct` between 0.0000 and 0.5000 inclusive to 4 decimal places expressing the fraction of capacity withheld as margin of safety, a `background_load_lbs_yr` at or above zero to 2 decimal places carrying the analyst's estimate of natural background load, and the assessment period as `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, both ISO calendar dates in year-month-day form, where the period is a closed interval and `period_end_date` must fall strictly after `period_start_date`. A draft opens in status `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `allocation_variant`, `effective_background_lbs_yr` and `approved_at` all null, `version` at 1, and every allocation roll-up — `margin_of_safety_lbs_yr`, `allocable_load_lbs_yr`, `wla_total_lbs_yr`, `la_total_lbs_yr` and `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr` — at 0.00.

The assessment lifecycle is driven by a status-transition operation that names the desired state in a `target_status` field and may carry an optional `reason`, which is never persisted. The full state set is `DRAFT`, `PUBLIC_COMMENT`, `REVISION`, `APPROVED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED`, with `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` terminal, and `target_status` accepts any one of those six as a well-formed value. A target the current status declares no transition to is therefore a state failure and not a shape failure: naming the cascade-only `SUPERSEDED`, naming a target equal to the current status, or naming any target at all out of `APPROVED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED` — none of which declares an outgoing transition on this operation — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The endpoint's authorized-role list in §4 is the only role check this operation performs, and it admits a `TmdlAnalyst` and a `ProgramAdministrator` alike for every transition: the actor named against each transition below records whose job it is in the program rather than a restriction the server enforces, so either of the two roles may drive any of the seven declared transitions, approval included. Moving `DRAFT → PUBLIC_COMMENT` is a `TmdlAnalyst` action and requires that at least one source assessment on this assessment already sits in status `INVENTORIED` or `ADJUSTED`; publishing an assessment with nothing inventoried is meaningless, and that missing-inventory refusal is itself reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, evaluated inside step 11 of §2.8 immediately after the transition-legality check rather than as the `ERR_NO_INCLUDED_SOURCES` that guards an allocation run. A `ProgramAdministrator` moves `PUBLIC_COMMENT → REVISION` when comments require rework, and that transition increments `revision_round` by 1. A `TmdlAnalyst` moves `REVISION → PUBLIC_COMMENT` to re-publish, which closes the stakeholder loop; `revision_round` is deliberately never reset, so an assessment that cycles repeatedly accumulates an ever-growing revision count. Withdrawal is available to a `ProgramAdministrator` from all three working states — `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, `PUBLIC_COMMENT → WITHDRAWN` and `REVISION → WITHDRAWN` — and each of the three simply increments the assessment's `version` by 1. Every one of these transitions increments `version` by 1.

Approval is the pivotal transition and is reserved to a `ProgramAdministrator`, who moves the assessment `PUBLIC_COMMENT → APPROVED`. Approval requires that at least one allocation run on the assessment currently has status `ACTIVE`; approving an assessment whose capacity has never been allocated is rejected with `ERR_WLA_NOT_ALLOCATED`. On success the server writes exactly three things on the assessment itself — the new `status`, an `approved_at` stamped with the logical clock instant, and a `version` incremented by 1. It does not recopy the active run's totals: the assessment already carries the roll-ups that run stamped onto it when the run was created, and approval leaves every one of those figures, together with `allocation_variant`, `effective_background_lbs_yr` and `revision_round`, exactly as it found them. Approval also has two cross-resource consequences. Any other assessment on the same `watershed_id` that was itself in status `APPROVED` transitions `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, with its own `version` incremented by 1; allocations already written onto source assessment rows by that superseded assessment's runs are deliberately left untouched. And if the watershed's `program_status` is still `PROVISIONAL`, the watershed transitions `PROVISIONAL → ACTIVE`, `trading_enabled` becomes true, and the watershed's `version` increments by 1 — this is the only way a program opens for trading.

### 3.3. Source Inventory, the Allocation Waterfall, and Rescission

Before capacity can be divided it must be attributed. A `TmdlAnalyst` inventories each pollutant source as a **SourceAssessment** naming its `tmdl_assessment_id`, the `reach_id` it discharges to, a `source_name`, a `source_type` of either `POINT` or `NONPOINT`, an `existing_load_lbs_yr` at or above zero to 2 decimal places recording the load currently attributed to the source, and a `controllability_index` between 0.0000 and 1.0000 inclusive to 4 decimal places recording the share of that load which is technically controllable. An optional `status` of `INVENTORIED`, `ADJUSTED` or `EXCLUDED` defaults to `INVENTORIED`. Point sources receive wasteload allocations and are the only sources a discharge permit may be written against; nonpoint sources receive load allocations. Rows in status `INVENTORIED` and `ADJUSTED` enter allocation; rows in status `EXCLUDED` never do, and are omitted both from the weight denominators and from the run's per-source output. On creation `allocated_load_lbs_yr` and `allocation_run_id` are both null. Inventorying is not gated on the parent assessment's status: a source may be added while that assessment sits in any of its six statuses, `APPROVED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` included, and adding one writes nothing on the parent, disturbs no allocation already written, and does not reopen the parent for anything else.

An **AllocationRun** is how a `TmdlAnalyst` divides capacity. Creating a run supplies a `run_label`, a `method` of either `PRO_RATA_BY_EXISTING_LOAD` or `CONTROLLABILITY_WEIGHTED`, and the `expected_tmdl_assessment_version` described in §2.7. A run may not be created unless at least one source on the assessment is in status `INVENTORIED` or `ADJUSTED`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_INCLUDED_SOURCES`. A run's own `status` starts at `ACTIVE`, and `variant` is computed rather than supplied.

The run's arithmetic proceeds as a strict waterfall, and its evaluation order is fixed. **Step one** establishes the background load, and this is where the run's two variants diverge; the resulting value is stored on the run as `effective_background_lbs_yr`. When the parent assessment's status at run time is `DRAFT`, `PUBLIC_COMMENT` or `REVISION` — or, because run creation is not gated on the parent's status at all, the terminal `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED` — the run's `variant` is `PROVISIONAL` and the effective background is the analyst's estimate in the assessment's `background_load_lbs_yr`, used verbatim with no adjustment. `APPROVED` is the only status that selects the other variant: when the parent assessment's status at run time is `APPROVED`, the run's `variant` is `FINAL` and the effective background is the monitored value: take every monitoring sample whose reach belongs to this watershed and has `reach_class` equal to `BACKGROUND`, whose `qa_status` is `ACCEPTED`, and whose `sample_datetime` falls within the assessment period from the start of `period_start_date` — that is, at time 00:00:00.000 UTC on that date — through the last millisecond of `period_end_date` — 23:59:59.999 UTC — inclusive at both ends; take the arithmetic mean of those samples' `computed_load_lbs_yr` values and round it half-up to 2 decimal places. If that sample set is empty, the final variant falls back to the analyst's estimate in `background_load_lbs_yr`. Because a monitored background normally differs from the estimate, the same source rows under the same method yield a different allocable load, different per-source allocations, a different `wla_total_lbs_yr` and a different `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr` under the final variant than under the provisional one.

**Step two** withholds the margin of safety and then the background. The margin of safety is the assessment's `loading_capacity_lbs_yr` multiplied by its `margin_of_safety_pct`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and is stored as `margin_of_safety_lbs_yr`. The allocable load is the loading capacity less that margin of safety less the effective background, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and is stored as `allocable_load_lbs_yr`. If the allocable load comes out strictly below 0.00 the entire run is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCABLE_LOAD_NEGATIVE` and nothing is written; an allocable load of exactly 0.00 is accepted and results in every included source being allocated 0.00.

**Step three** takes the program's cut. The reserve set-aside is 5 percent of the allocable load — the allocable load multiplied by five hundredths — rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and the distributable load is the allocable load less that set-aside, again rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. **Step four** splits the distributable load between the two source families: `point_pool_lbs_yr` is 60 percent of the distributable load — the distributable load multiplied by six tenths — rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and `nonpoint_pool_lbs_yr` is the distributable load less the point pool, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, so the two pools always re-add to the distributable load exactly.

**Step five** spreads each pool across its own sources. The included set is every source row on this assessment whose `status` is `INVENTORIED` or `ADJUSTED`; the point group holds the rows whose `source_type` is `POINT` and the nonpoint group the rows whose `source_type` is `NONPOINT`, and within each group the rows are ordered by `existing_load_lbs_yr` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break and, exactly as in §2.3, the server's row-creation sequence breaking any tie that remains when two included rows share both an existing load and a `created_at`. Each row's weight depends on the run's `method`: under `PRO_RATA_BY_EXISTING_LOAD` a row's weight is simply its `existing_load_lbs_yr`, while under `CONTROLLABILITY_WEIGHTED` it is that row's `existing_load_lbs_yr` multiplied by its `controllability_index`. For each group, sum the weights of its rows to obtain the group denominator, then for each row in the pinned order set `allocated_load_lbs_yr` to the group's pool multiplied by that row's weight and divided by the denominator, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. If a group's weight denominator is zero — every included row weighing nothing — each of that group's rows is allocated 0.00. The rounding order here is pinned and consequential: each source's allocation is rounded to 2 decimal places first and the already-rounded values are then summed. Summing the exact quotients and rounding once at the end is incorrect and can differ by cents. The run's `wla_total_lbs_yr` is the sum of the rounded point allocations and its `la_total_lbs_yr` the sum of the rounded nonpoint allocations. Its `included_source_count` is the number of rows in the included set.

**Step six** balances the books. The run's `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr` is the allocable load less the point total less the nonpoint total, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. It therefore carries both the deliberate 5 percent set-aside and the per-source rounding residue, and it is what makes the load-conservation identity of §2.9 hold exactly. When a group is empty its entire pool is absorbed into this reserve term.

The run also publishes its work as an `allocations` array in the same pinned order — the point rows first, then the nonpoint rows, each group ordered by `existing_load_lbs_yr` descending then `created_at` ascending. Each element is an object carrying the `source_assessment_id` of the row, its `source_type`, its `existing_load_lbs_yr`, the `weight` computed for it under the run's method, and the `allocated_load_lbs_yr` written to it. The published `weight` is rounded half-up to 4 decimal places; that rounding is for publication only, and both the group denominator and each per-source quotient are computed from the exact unrounded weight. Alongside the array, the run writes `allocated_load_lbs_yr` and its own `allocation_run_id` onto every included source assessment row.

**Step seven** re-bases the program. If a prior run on this assessment was `ACTIVE`, that run transitions `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, its `version` increments by 1, and the watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` is first reduced by that superseded run's `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr` so its contribution is withdrawn. The superseded run keeps its own totals and its own `allocations` array as a historical record, and the only source rows the new run rewrites are the rows in its own included set: a source that the earlier run allocated but that has since moved to `EXCLUDED` is deliberately not cleared, keeping the `allocated_load_lbs_yr` and the `allocation_run_id` the earlier run wrote and so remaining attributed to a run that is now `SUPERSEDED`. The watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` is then increased by the new run's `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr`, `allocated_wla_total_lbs_yr` is set to the new `wla_total_lbs_yr`, `allocated_la_total_lbs_yr` to the new `la_total_lbs_yr`, and the watershed's `version` increments by 1. The parent assessment receives the run's roll-ups — `margin_of_safety_lbs_yr`, `allocable_load_lbs_yr`, `wla_total_lbs_yr`, `la_total_lbs_yr`, `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr`, `effective_background_lbs_yr` and `allocation_variant` — and its `version` increments by 1 as well.

Rescission is the compensating action that protects the load-conservation invariant when an allocation turns out to be wrong. Either a `TmdlAnalyst` or a `ProgramAdministrator` may rescind, supplying a `reason`, and only a run in status `ACTIVE` may be rescinded, moving it `ACTIVE → RESCINDED` with `rescinded_at` stamped from the logical clock and `version` incremented by 1. Rescission is blocked once the allocation has been relied upon: if any permit whose `source_assessment_id` points at a source assessment carrying this run's `allocation_run_id` has a `limit_status` of `INTERIM` or `FINAL`, the request is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_RUN_IN_USE`. On success, every source assessment carrying this run's identifier has its `allocated_load_lbs_yr` and its `allocation_run_id` set back to null with its `version` incremented by 1; when no other `ACTIVE` run remains on the assessment, the assessment's `margin_of_safety_lbs_yr`, `allocable_load_lbs_yr`, `wla_total_lbs_yr`, `la_total_lbs_yr` and `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr` are restored to 0.00 while `effective_background_lbs_yr` and `allocation_variant` return to null, and the assessment's `version` increments by 1; and the watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` is decreased by this run's `reserve_allocation_lbs_yr`, restoring the pre-run balance, with `allocated_wla_total_lbs_yr` and `allocated_la_total_lbs_yr` set back to 0.00 and the watershed's `version` incremented by 1.

### 3.4. Permit Issuance and Effluent Limit Derivation

A `PermitWriter` issues a **Permit** against exactly one point source. Issuance supplies the `watershed_id`, the `source_assessment_id` of the source the permit regulates, a `permit_number` unique across the whole system, the `permittee_user_id` holding the opaque identifier of the discharger authorised to report and to buy credits for this permit, a `design_flow_mgd` strictly above zero in million gallons per day to 4 decimal places recorded for the permit record, an `effective_date` as an ISO calendar date giving the inclusive first day the limits take effect and anchoring the compliance schedule, and a `current_annual_load_lbs` at or above zero to 2 decimal places carrying the discharger's most recent annual load. The referenced source assessment must have `source_type` equal to `POINT`; a permit written against a nonpoint source is rejected with `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_POINT`, because nonpoint sources receive load allocations rather than permits. A new permit opens with `limit_status` at `DRAFT_LIMITS`, `compliance_status` at `NOT_REPORTED`, `credit_applied_lbs` at 0.00, `compliance_gap_lbs` at 0.00, `enforcement_flag` false, `version` at 1, and every derived limit field — `wla_lbs_yr`, `final_limit_lbs_yr`, `interim_limit_lbs_yr`, `required_reduction_pct`, `schedule_tier`, `compliance_schedule_end_date`, `applicable_limit_lbs_yr`, `reported_annual_load_lbs` and `reporting_period_year` — null.

A permit carries two entirely independent state axes on the same row. `limit_status` tracks how far the numeric limits have progressed and runs linearly through `DRAFT_LIMITS`, `INTERIM` and the terminal `FINAL`. `compliance_status` tracks the reported-load-versus-limit posture across `NOT_REPORTED`, `IN_COMPLIANCE`, `SHORTFALL`, `RESOLVED` and `VIOLATION`, has no terminal state, and can cycle. Neither axis constrains the other's transition set except where a precondition explicitly says so: a permit can reach `VIOLATION` while its `limit_status` is still `INTERIM`, and it can cycle `SHORTFALL → RESOLVED → SHORTFALL` without `limit_status` moving at all.

Limit derivation is the `PermitWriter` action that advances `limit_status` from `DRAFT_LIMITS` to `INTERIM`, and it takes no request body. It requires that the linked source assessment's `allocated_load_lbs_yr` is not null; deriving limits from an unallocated source is rejected with `ERR_WLA_NOT_ALLOCATED`. The derivation looks circular at first reading, because the compliance-schedule tier grants interim relief and one might suppose the required reduction is measured against the relieved limit. It is not. The evaluation order is pinned as five steps, the tier is selected from the reduction measured against the **final** limit only, and relief is applied afterwards. Deriving the tier from an interim value is incorrect.

Step one captures the allocation and converts it. The permit's `wla_lbs_yr` is set to a copy of the linked source assessment's `allocated_load_lbs_yr` at this instant, and `final_limit_lbs_yr` is that wasteload allocation multiplied by the watershed's `equivalence_ratio`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The equivalence ratio is read once, here, and is not re-read afterwards; a later change to the watershed parameter does not disturb an already-derived permit. Step two measures the burden: if the permit's `current_annual_load_lbs` is zero or below, `required_reduction_pct` is 0.00; otherwise it is the current annual load less the final limit, divided by the current annual load, with the quotient floored at zero so a discharger already below its limit records no required reduction, then multiplied by 100 and rounded half-up to 2 decimal places.

Step three selects the tier from that percentage, with both boundaries inclusive on their upper edge. A required reduction of 20.00 percent or less gives `schedule_tier` of `TIER_A`. A required reduction above 20.00 percent and up to and including 50.00 percent gives `TIER_B`. Anything strictly above 50.00 percent gives `TIER_C`. Step four reads the relief and schedule length off the tier: `TIER_A` grants no relief at all and a schedule of zero years, `TIER_B` grants relief of one quarter and a three-year schedule, and `TIER_C` grants relief of one half and a five-year schedule. Step five turns those into dates and numbers. The `interim_limit_lbs_yr` is the final limit plus the relief fraction applied to the gap between the current annual load and the final limit — that is, the final limit plus the product of the tier's relief fraction and the difference between `current_annual_load_lbs` and `final_limit_lbs_yr` — rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The `compliance_schedule_end_date` is the permit's `effective_date` advanced by the tier's number of calendar years, keeping the same month and day, so a `TIER_A` permit's schedule ends on its own effective date, a `TIER_B` permit's three calendar years later and a `TIER_C` permit's five calendar years later. The single date this cannot reproduce is 29 February when the target year is not a leap year — neither three nor five years after a leap day is itself a leap year — and that case lands on 28 February of the target year instead.

Which limit compliance is actually measured against is held in `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` and recomputed on every mutation that could affect it. While `limit_status` is `DRAFT_LIMITS` it is null. When `limit_status` is `FINAL` it equals `final_limit_lbs_yr`. When `limit_status` is `INTERIM` it equals `interim_limit_lbs_yr` while the logical clock's date is on or before `compliance_schedule_end_date`, and it equals `final_limit_lbs_yr` once the logical clock's date is strictly after `compliance_schedule_end_date` — the schedule expires on its own, and no request has to be filed for that expiry to bind the compliance arithmetic. The stored field is nevertheless a materialised copy rather than a value derived at read time. It is re-derived and rewritten in place exactly when the server has occasion to consult it: when limits are derived and when they are finalized, whenever the permit is reconciled by an annual report, by a credit approval or by any void or cascade, and whenever the permit's outstanding need is evaluated to snapshot `permit_gap_at_proposal_lbs` on a proposal or to cap `applied_lbs` on an approval. That re-derivation on its own never increments the permit's `version` and never counts as a mutation, so a proposal that merely reads the buyer permit's need can leave a refreshed `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` behind on an otherwise untouched permit row. It is re-derived once more whenever a permit row is serialized into a response — by a single fetch, by a page of the permit collection, or as the body a permit write hands back — and that refresh, too, leaves `version` and `updated_at` alone. A plain read therefore never reports a stale figure: once the logical clock has advanced past `compliance_schedule_end_date`, the very next fetch of a permit still in `INTERIM` already reports `final_limit_lbs_yr`, which is exactly the limit every compliance evaluation from that instant on measures against. Derivation increments the permit's `version` by 1.

Finalizing limits is the second `PermitWriter` action, takes no request body, and advances `limit_status` from `INTERIM` to `FINAL`. It requires that the assessment reached from the permit's source assessment has status `APPROVED`; finalizing against an unapproved assessment is rejected with `ERR_TMDL_NOT_APPROVED`. On success `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` is repointed from the interim limit to `final_limit_lbs_yr`. If the permit has already reported — that is, if `reported_annual_load_lbs` is not null — the compliance reconciliation of §3.5 re-runs against the now-tighter applicable limit, and the compliance posture may move as a result. The permit's `version` increments by exactly 1 for the whole request even when both axes move, because finalization is one write.

### 3.5. Annual Reporting and the Two Compliance Axes

A `Permittee` files an annual report against its own permit, supplying `reported_annual_load_lbs` and `reporting_period_year`. The report is refused while limits are still notional: `limit_status` must already be `INTERIM` or `FINAL`, and a report filed against a permit in `DRAFT_LIMITS` is rejected with `ERR_LIMITS_NOT_DERIVED`. A report stores `reported_annual_load_lbs` and `reporting_period_year`, refreshes `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` per §3.4, runs the reconciliation described next, writes `compliance_gap_lbs`, and increments the permit's `version` by 1.

Reconciliation is the single piece of arithmetic that decides the compliance posture, and it runs on six occasions: annual reporting, limit finalization, credit-transaction approval, credit-transaction voiding, and both cascade paths that void transactions in bulk. It computes `compliance_gap_lbs` as the reported annual load less the applicable limit less the credits already applied — that is, `reported_annual_load_lbs` minus `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` minus `credit_applied_lbs` — with the result floored at zero so an over-complying permit shows a gap of exactly 0.00, and the final value rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The `credit_applied_lbs` term is itself the materialised sum of `applied_lbs` over this permit's `APPROVED` credit transactions. From the gap the posture follows. If `reported_annual_load_lbs` is null the posture stays `NOT_REPORTED`. If the gap is exactly 0.00, the posture becomes `IN_COMPLIANCE` when the trigger was an annual report, and `RESOLVED` when the trigger was a credit purchase closing the gap; a limit finalization or a release-void that leaves no gap at all leaves the posture exactly as it stood, and an invalidated-batch void that leaves no gap moves the posture to `VIOLATION` only when it was already `SHORTFALL` and otherwise leaves it alone. If the gap is above 0.00, the posture becomes `VIOLATION` when the trigger was a void caused by an invalidated batch — whatever the posture was beforehand — or when the trigger was an annual report whose logical-clock date falls strictly after `compliance_schedule_end_date`, and `SHORTFALL` in every other case, with one exception running the other way: a permit already in `VIOLATION` is never walked back to `SHORTFALL` by a limit finalization or by a release-void, because only a fresh annual report re-derives the posture from scratch. Once the posture becomes `VIOLATION`, `enforcement_flag` is set true and is never cleared again, even if a later report brings the permit back into compliance.

Read as transitions, the reporting path out of `NOT_REPORTED` has three destinations, all requiring `limit_status` of `INTERIM` or `FINAL`. `NOT_REPORTED → IN_COMPLIANCE` fires when reconciliation yields a gap of exactly 0.00, and the report writes `reported_annual_load_lbs`, `reporting_period_year`, `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` and a `compliance_gap_lbs` of 0.00. `NOT_REPORTED → SHORTFALL` fires when the gap is above 0.00 and the logical clock's date is on or before `compliance_schedule_end_date`, and the report writes the same four fields with the positive gap. `NOT_REPORTED → VIOLATION` fires when the gap is above 0.00 and the logical clock's date is strictly after `compliance_schedule_end_date`; it writes the same four fields, additionally sets `enforcement_flag` to true, and increments `version` by 1 just as the other two do. The symmetry is deliberate: the only thing separating a shortfall from a violation on a first report is whether the compliance schedule has already expired.

Later reports move the posture in both directions. `IN_COMPLIANCE → SHORTFALL` fires on a report whose reconciliation yields a gap above 0.00 while the logical clock's date is on or before `compliance_schedule_end_date`, overwriting `reported_annual_load_lbs`, `reporting_period_year` and `compliance_gap_lbs` and incrementing `version` by 1. `SHORTFALL → IN_COMPLIANCE` fires on a report whose reconciliation yields a gap of exactly 0.00, overwriting the same three fields with a gap of 0.00 and incrementing `version` by 1.

One compliance transition is not driven by a report at all, and it is the single point at which the two axes touch. When limit finalization tightens `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` from the interim limit to the final limit, a permit that was `IN_COMPLIANCE` with a non-null `reported_annual_load_lbs` and whose recomputed gap against the tightened limit comes out above 0.00, with the logical clock's date on or before `compliance_schedule_end_date`, moves `IN_COMPLIANCE → SHORTFALL`. A permit sitting in `RESOLVED` at the moment of finalization moves the same way, `RESOLVED → SHORTFALL`, when the tightened limit reopens a positive gap. The permit's `compliance_gap_lbs` is recomputed against the tightened applicable limit, and the `version` increment is the same single increment the limit transition already performs.

The remaining compliance transitions are driven by the credit market and are specified in §3.8: `SHORTFALL → RESOLVED` when an approved purchase closes the gap, `RESOLVED → SHORTFALL` when previously applied credits are released, `RESOLVED → VIOLATION` when the released credits turn out never to have existed, and `SHORTFALL → VIOLATION` in the same circumstance against a permit that was already short.

### 3.6. Producers, Practice Projects and Field Verification

Credits originate on farmland. A `ProgramAdministrator` or a `RegistryAdministrator` enrols a **Producer** by recording its `legal_name`, the `county_code` of its operation, and a `contact_email` used for verification scheduling. The identifier minted for that row is the `X-User-ID` the producer presents on subsequent calls, which is what makes the ownership check in §2.1 meaningful.

A `Producer` then registers a **PracticeProject**, supplying its own `producer_id`, the `reach_id` the practice discharges to, the `tract_code` of the farm tract, a `practice_type` drawn from `COVER_CROP`, `RIPARIAN_BUFFER`, `NUTRIENT_MANAGEMENT` and `WETLAND_RESTORATION`, an `enrolled_acres` figure strictly above zero to 2 decimal places, a `baseline_met` boolean declaring whether the tract already satisfies the program's regulatory baseline requirement, the modelled annual loads `pre_load_lbs_yr` and `post_load_lbs_yr` at or above zero to 2 decimal places where the post-practice load must be strictly below the pre-practice load, the credit-generation period as `effective_start_date` and `effective_end_date` — ISO calendar dates forming a half-open interval whose end must fall strictly after its start — and the `vintage_year` that will be stamped on the issued batch. Registration is gated on geography: the reach's `trading_zone_code` must appear in the watershed's `eligible_zone_codes`, and a practice outside the eligible zones is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

Registration is also gated on tract occupancy, because the same acres may not generate the same reduction twice over overlapping periods. No other practice project sharing this project's `tract_code` and `practice_type` and sitting in any of the live statuses `REGISTERED`, `BASELINE_VERIFIED`, `IMPLEMENTED`, `CERTIFIED` or `PENDING_REVERIFICATION` may occupy an overlapping half-open interval running from its `effective_start_date` up to but not including its `effective_end_date`. Because the interval is half-open, touching periods do not overlap: a new project whose `effective_start_date` equals an existing project's `effective_end_date` is accepted. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_PRACTICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A revoked project no longer occupies its tract and never blocks a new registration. A registered project opens in status `REGISTERED` with `verified_acres` null, `baseline_verifier_user_id` and `implementation_verifier_user_id` both null, `certified_at` null and `version` at 1.

Field inspections are filed by a `PracticeVerifier` as **VerificationEvent** records, each naming the `practice_project_id`, an `event_type` of `BASELINE`, `IMPLEMENTATION` or `REVERIFICATION`, an `outcome` of `PASS`, `PARTIAL` or `FAIL`, a written `narrative` recording the inspector's finding, and an optional `verified_acres` at or above zero and no greater than the project's `enrolled_acres`, carried to 2 decimal places. The `verified_acres` figure is required for an `IMPLEMENTATION` event with outcome `PASS` and for a `REVERIFICATION` event with outcome `PASS` or `PARTIAL`, and is forbidden on every other combination. The server stamps `inspector_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `observed_at` from the logical clock, records the project's status immediately after the event was applied in `resulting_project_status`, and treats the event as immutable thereafter.

Each combination of `event_type` and `outcome` drives one project transition, and a combination that names no transition out of the project's current status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `BASELINE` event with outcome `PASS` moves the project `REGISTERED → BASELINE_VERIFIED` and requires `baseline_met` to be true — the regulatory baseline requirement cannot be traded around, and an attempt to pass the baseline of a project whose `baseline_met` is false is rejected with `ERR_BASELINE_NOT_MET`. That transition records the acting `X-User-ID` in `baseline_verifier_user_id` and increments the project's `version` by 1. A `BASELINE` event with outcome `FAIL` moves the project `REGISTERED → REVOKED` and increments its `version` by 1; no batches exist at this stage, so no cascade fires.

An `IMPLEMENTATION` event with outcome `PASS` moves the project `BASELINE_VERIFIED → IMPLEMENTED`. It requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the stored `baseline_verifier_user_id`, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` otherwise, and it requires a `verified_acres` value that is present, strictly above zero, and no greater than the project's `enrolled_acres`. On success the project's `verified_acres` is set from the event, `implementation_verifier_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the project's `version` increments by 1. An `IMPLEMENTATION` event with outcome `FAIL` moves the project `BASELINE_VERIFIED → REVOKED` and increments its `version` by 1; it too requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `baseline_verifier_user_id`, so a verifier cannot fail a project it baselined any more than it can pass one.

Reverification keeps a certified practice honest year after year, and its three outcomes diverge sharply. A `REVERIFICATION` event with outcome `PARTIAL` moves the project `CERTIFIED → PENDING_REVERIFICATION` and requires a `verified_acres` value that is present, at or above zero, and strictly below the project's current `verified_acres` — a partial finding means fewer acres were found in place than were certified. Every credit batch of this project whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `LISTED` has its `verified_ratio` set to the event's `verified_acres` divided by the project's `verified_acres`, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places, with that batch's `version` incremented by 1; the project's `version` increments by 1 as well. Crucially, `issued_lbs` is never restated by a partial finding: the reduced ratio only shrinks the settled gross quantity of transactions approved from that point forward. A reverification finding of any outcome leaves the project's own `verified_acres` exactly as the passing implementation finding wrote it — only the batches' `verified_ratio` moves — so the divisor of that ratio is the certified acreage for the whole life of the project. A `REVERIFICATION` event with outcome `PASS` moves the project `PENDING_REVERIFICATION → CERTIFIED` and requires a `verified_acres` value that is present and exactly equal to the project's current `verified_acres`, which is to say equal to that same unrestated certified acreage rather than to whatever a preceding partial finding reported. Every batch of this project whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `LISTED` has its `verified_ratio` restored to 1.0000 with that batch's `version` incremented by 1, and the project's `version` increments by 1. This closes the annual reverification loop, and a project may cycle through `PENDING_REVERIFICATION` any number of times.

A `REVERIFICATION` event with outcome `FAIL` is the gravest finding in the system and revokes the project, moving it `CERTIFIED → REVOKED` or `PENDING_REVERIFICATION → REVOKED`. Both origins run exactly the same four-link cascade, and both increment the project's `version` by 1. The first link is the revocation itself. The second link invalidates the registry: every credit batch of this project whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `LISTED` transitions to `INVALIDATED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with its whole unencumbered and encumbered balance folded into the void — `invalidated_lbs` becomes the sum of its prior `invalidated_lbs`, its `available_lbs` and its `reserved_lbs`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, while `available_lbs` and `reserved_lbs` both become 0.00 and that batch's `version` increments by 1. The third link voids the trades: every credit transaction against those batches whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` transitions to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` of `BATCH_INVALIDATED` and `voided_at` stamped, and for an approved loser the batch's `retired_lbs` decreases by that transaction's `settled_gross_lbs` while `invalidated_lbs` increases by the same amount, so the pounds move into the void rather than back onto the market. The fourth link reconciles the buyers: each affected permit's `credit_applied_lbs` decreases by that transaction's `applied_lbs`, `compliance_gap_lbs` is recomputed, `compliance_status` moves to `VIOLATION` — from `RESOLVED` when the recomputed gap comes out above 0.00, and from `SHORTFALL` unconditionally — `enforcement_flag` becomes true, and the permit's `version` increments by 1. The reasoning is deliberate: a credit that turns out never to have existed is an enforcement matter, not a mere shortfall. Voiding an approved transaction also debits the watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` by that transaction's `retired_to_reserve_lbs` and increments the watershed's `version` by 1.

### 3.7. Certification, Serialization and Registry Operations

Certification is where a verified practice becomes a serialized financial instrument, and it is reserved to a `RegistryAdministrator`. It takes no request body, requires the project to be in status `IMPLEMENTED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from both `baseline_verifier_user_id` and `implementation_verifier_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — and requires the watershed's `program_status` to be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_TRADING_SUSPENDED` otherwise. On success the project moves `IMPLEMENTED → CERTIFIED`, `certified_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the project's `version` increments by 1, and exactly one **CreditBatch** is minted and returned.

The minting arithmetic prorates the modelled reduction by the acres actually found in the field. The batch's `gross_reduction_lbs_yr` is the project's `pre_load_lbs_yr` less its `post_load_lbs_yr`, multiplied by the project's `verified_acres` and divided by its `enrolled_acres`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — note that it is the verified acreage, not the enrolled acreage, that prorates the reduction. The registry then serializes in whole pounds: `issued_lbs` is that gross reduction rounded down to the largest whole number that does not exceed it — a gross reduction that is already whole keeps its full value rather than dropping a pound, and leaves `residual_lbs` at 0.00 — and `residual_lbs` is the gross reduction less that whole number, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The `serial_prefix` is the watershed's `state_code`, a hyphen, the watershed's `watershed_code`, a hyphen, and the project's `vintage_year`. The batch's `serial_start` is one greater than the watershed's current `serial_counter` and its `serial_end` is that counter plus `issued_lbs`, so serial ranges across a watershed are contiguous and never reused; the watershed's `serial_counter` is then advanced to the new `serial_end`, which makes it the high-water mark of every serial number ever minted in that watershed. The batch copies the project's `vintage_year` into its own `vintage_year`, opens in `status` of `ISSUED` with `verified_ratio` of 1.0000, and its four balance buckets open as `available_lbs` equal to `issued_lbs`, `reserved_lbs` 0.00, `retired_lbs` 0.00 and `invalidated_lbs` 0.00. The fractional pounds that whole-pound serialization dropped are not lost to the program: `residual_lbs` is added to the watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs`, and the watershed's `version` increments by 1.

A batch is not tradable the moment it is minted. A `RegistryAdministrator` lists it, moving `ISSUED → LISTED` with no request body and leaving every bucket amount untouched. Listing carries four preconditions, and they are evaluated in the order §2.8 fixes rather than in the order the narrative happens to introduce them: first the batch's own `status` must be `ISSUED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; then the watershed's `program_status` must be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_TRADING_SUSPENDED`; then the generating project's status must still be `CERTIFIED` — a project sitting in `PENDING_REVERIFICATION` cannot have new batches listed and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and only then must the batch's `available_lbs` be strictly above 0.00, failing with `ERR_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT`. A batch whose project has slipped out of `CERTIFIED` inside a watershed that has also been suspended therefore reports `ERR_TRADING_SUSPENDED`, not `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Listing increments the batch's `version` by 1. Only a `LISTED` batch may have a transaction approved against it.

Retirement is how the program permanently withdraws credits from the market, and a `RegistryAdministrator` performs it by supplying a `retire_lbs` quantity and a `reason`. Retirement is available from both `ISSUED` and `LISTED`, and it is refused while any transaction against the batch is still `PROPOSED`, which is reported as `ERR_BATCH_HAS_OPEN_TRANSACTIONS`. The requested `retire_lbs` is at or above 0.00 carried to 2 decimal places and may not exceed the batch's `available_lbs`; an over-large request is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT`. A `retire_lbs` of exactly 0.00 is a well-formed retirement rather than an invalid request: it moves no pounds and adds nothing to the reserve pool, yet it still increments both the batch's and the watershed's `version` by 1, and on a batch whose `available_lbs` was already 0.00 — the batch a sub-one-pound reduction serialized into zero whole pounds — it satisfies the zero-balance condition below and moves that batch to `RETIRED`. On success `retire_lbs` moves out of `available_lbs` and into `retired_lbs`, the same amount is added to the watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` with the watershed's `version` incremented by 1, and the batch's `version` increments by 1. The status change to `RETIRED` fires only when the move leaves `available_lbs` at exactly 0.00; a partial retirement, where `retire_lbs` is less than `available_lbs`, moves the buckets and bumps the version but leaves the batch's `status` unchanged, so a partially retired listed batch remains listed and tradable for its remaining balance.

A batch's registry lifecycle therefore has four states — `ISSUED`, `LISTED`, and the terminal `RETIRED` and `INVALIDATED` — and only one of them is reachable without a registry action: `ISSUED → INVALIDATED` and `LISTED → INVALIDATED` both fire from the revocation cascade of §3.6 and from nowhere else.

### 3.8. Credit Purchase: Proposal, Settlement and Void

A `Permittee` in shortfall buys credits by proposing a **CreditTransaction** against a listed batch. The batch's own `status` is deliberately not a precondition of a proposal — as §2.8 records, the requirement that the batch be `LISTED` binds only at approval — so a proposal against a batch still in `ISSUED` is accepted and simply waits for the registry to list it before it can settle, while a batch that has been fully retired or invalidated is turned away by the unencumbered-balance check rather than by any state check. The generating project's status is likewise unexamined at proposal time. The proposal names the `credit_batch_id` it is buying from, the `permit_id` the credits will be applied to, a `requested_gross_lbs` quantity strictly above zero to 2 decimal places, the `reporting_period_year` the credits are applied to, and the `expected_credit_batch_version` of §2.7. The proposal is subject to the ownership check of §2.1 against the permit's `permittee_user_id`, requires the watershed's `program_status` to be `ACTIVE`, and is refused unless the compliance year lines up: the transaction's `reporting_period_year` must equal the buyer permit's `reporting_period_year`, otherwise `ERR_REPORTING_YEAR_MISMATCH`. It is also refused unless the batch can cover it — `requested_gross_lbs` may not exceed the batch's `available_lbs`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT` — and unless the buyer actually needs credits: the permit's `compliance_status` must be `SHORTFALL` with a `compliance_gap_lbs` strictly above 0.00, otherwise `ERR_NO_COMPLIANCE_GAP`.

Geography is evaluated ahead of both of those fiscal checks — it sits at position 18 of §2.8, before the unencumbered-balance check at position 28 and the compliance-gap check at position 29 — and it is what stops a reduction upstream of nothing from being sold to a discharger it can never reach. The generating reach is the reach of the batch's practice project; the buyer reach is the reach of the permit's source assessment. When those are the same row, the `delivery_ratio` is 1.0000 and `path_reach_count` is 1. Otherwise the server walks downstream: it starts a path list holding the generating reach and a running product of 1, then repeatedly follows the current reach's `downstream_reach_id`. If the current reach has no downstream neighbour, the walk has run off the end of the network and the proposal is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Otherwise the newly reached reach is appended to the path and the running product is multiplied by that newly reached reach's `delivery_factor`; if that reach is the buyer reach the walk stops, and if the path has grown beyond ten reaches without arriving at the buyer reach the proposal is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Note precisely which factors participate: the generating reach's own `delivery_factor` is excluded, and every reach strictly downstream of it up to and including the buyer reach is included. The finished product is rounded half-up to 4 decimal places exactly once, at the end, to give `delivery_ratio`, and `path_reach_count` is the number of reaches on the path counting both ends. Because the walk only ever moves downstream, a buyer sitting upstream of the generator, or on a disconnected branch, is never eligible.

The delivered quantity is then computed from four interacting inputs — the gross quantity, the geographic delivery ratio, and the program's uncertainty and equivalence ratios — under two nested caps. At proposal time the gross quantity is the full `requested_gross_lbs`, and the batch's `verified_ratio` is ignored entirely. The `retired_to_reserve_lbs` withholding is the gross quantity multiplied by the watershed's `retirement_ratio`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The `tradable_lbs` figure is the gross quantity less that withholding, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The delivered quantity is the tradable pounds multiplied by the `delivery_ratio`, multiplied by the watershed's `equivalence_ratio`, then divided by the watershed's `uncertainty_ratio`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places; at proposal time it is stored as `proposed_delivered_lbs`. The permit's outstanding need is computed as the reported annual load less the applicable limit less the credits already applied — `reported_annual_load_lbs` minus `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` minus `credit_applied_lbs` — floored at zero and rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and at proposal time that value is snapshotted onto the transaction as `permit_gap_at_proposal_lbs`, the cap that the eventual applied quantity will be measured against. While the transaction is `PROPOSED`, `applied_lbs`, `settled_gross_lbs` and `settled_delivered_lbs` all remain null. A proposal whose `proposed_delivered_lbs` comes out at exactly 0.00 after the retirement withholding, delivery ratio, equivalence ratio and uncertainty ratio have all been applied buys nothing and is rejected with `ERR_DELIVERY_BELOW_MINIMUM`; only a strictly positive delivered quantity is accepted.

An accepted proposal opens in `status` of `PROPOSED` and encumbers the batch: `requested_gross_lbs` moves out of the batch's `available_lbs` and into its `reserved_lbs`, and the batch's `version` increments by 1. The watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` is deliberately not touched at proposal time even though `retired_to_reserve_lbs` has been computed — a proposed transaction has not yet retired anything.

Approval is a `RegistryAdministrator` action taking no request body, and it settles the trade. It requires the transaction to be `PROPOSED`, the batch's `status` to be `LISTED`, the generating project's status to be `CERTIFIED` or `PENDING_REVERIFICATION`, the watershed's `program_status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_TRADING_SUSPENDED` — and the buyer permit's `compliance_status` to still be `SHORTFALL` with a positive gap, otherwise `ERR_NO_COMPLIANCE_GAP`. Approval re-runs the delivery arithmetic in its settled form. Here the gross quantity is no longer the full request: `settled_gross_lbs` is `requested_gross_lbs` multiplied by the batch's **current** `verified_ratio`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Every subsequent step repeats exactly as at proposal but on that settled gross — `retired_to_reserve_lbs` is recomputed as the settled gross multiplied by the watershed's `retirement_ratio` rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and `settled_delivered_lbs` is the settled gross less that withholding, multiplied by `delivery_ratio` and by `equivalence_ratio`, divided by `uncertainty_ratio`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. That recomputed `retired_to_reserve_lbs` overwrites the figure the proposal stored, but the proposal-time fields `requested_gross_lbs`, `tradable_lbs`, `proposed_delivered_lbs`, `permit_gap_at_proposal_lbs`, `delivery_ratio` and `path_reach_count` are every one of them left as they were; after a downward reverification `tradable_lbs` therefore no longer equals the settled gross less the recomputed withholding, because it records what the proposal asked for rather than what the trade delivered. The consequence of a downward reverification between proposal and approval is therefore explicit: with identical request data, `settled_delivered_lbs` is strictly less than `proposed_delivered_lbs`. Finally `applied_lbs` is the smaller of the settled delivered quantity and the permit's outstanding need, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — and that need is re-read fresh at approval time from the permit's current `reported_annual_load_lbs`, `applicable_limit_lbs_yr` and `credit_applied_lbs`, not taken from `permit_gap_at_proposal_lbs`.

Settlement then moves money in three places. On the batch, `reserved_lbs` decreases by `requested_gross_lbs`, `retired_lbs` increases by `settled_gross_lbs`, `available_lbs` increases by the difference between the requested gross and the settled gross rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — so any pounds the reverification shaved off return to the market rather than vanishing — and the batch's `version` increments by 1. On the watershed, `reserve_pool_lbs` increases by the recomputed `retired_to_reserve_lbs` and `version` increments by 1. On the permit, `credit_applied_lbs` increases by `applied_lbs`, `compliance_gap_lbs` is reconciled per §3.5, `compliance_status` moves `SHORTFALL → RESOLVED` when the recomputed gap comes out at exactly 0.00, and the permit's `version` increments by 1. It is at this point that the purchased credits become an enforceable term of the permit. The transaction moves `PROPOSED → APPROVED` and its own `version` increments by 1.

Voiding is the reverse. A `RegistryAdministrator` or a `ProgramAdministrator` may void explicitly, supplying a `reason`, which sets `void_reason` to `REGISTRY_ACTION`; the watershed suspension and termination cascades set it to `ADAPTIVE_HOLD`; and the project-revocation cascade sets it to `BATCH_INVALIDATED`. In every case `voided_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the transaction's `version` increments by 1. Voiding a `PROPOSED` transaction releases the encumbrance: when `void_reason` is anything other than `BATCH_INVALIDATED`, the batch's `reserved_lbs` decreases by `requested_gross_lbs` and its `available_lbs` increases by the same amount, with the batch's `version` incremented by 1; when `void_reason` is `BATCH_INVALIDATED` the batch's buckets were already folded into `invalidated_lbs` by the batch transition itself, so no further bucket movement occurs. A proposed transaction never credited the reserve pool, so voiding one leaves `reserve_pool_lbs` alone, and it never applied a pound to the buyer permit either, so voiding one leaves that permit entirely untouched: no reconciliation runs against it, its `credit_applied_lbs`, `compliance_gap_lbs` and `compliance_status` all stand, and its `version` does not move. Voiding an `APPROVED` transaction unwinds a settled trade: when `void_reason` is anything other than `BATCH_INVALIDATED`, the batch's `retired_lbs` decreases by `settled_gross_lbs` and `available_lbs` increases by the same amount; when `void_reason` is `BATCH_INVALIDATED`, `retired_lbs` decreases by `settled_gross_lbs` and `invalidated_lbs` increases by the same amount instead. Either way the watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` decreases by the transaction's `retired_to_reserve_lbs` with the watershed's `version` incremented by 1, the batch's `version` increments by 1, and on the buyer permit `credit_applied_lbs` decreases by `applied_lbs`, `compliance_gap_lbs` is recomputed, and `version` increments by 1. The permit's posture then depends on why the void happened: with a `void_reason` other than `BATCH_INVALIDATED` a permit in `RESOLVED` moves `RESOLVED → SHORTFALL` when the recomputed gap comes out above 0.00, whereas with `void_reason` of `BATCH_INVALIDATED` a permit in `RESOLVED` moves `RESOLVED → VIOLATION` when the recomputed gap comes out above 0.00 and a permit already in `SHORTFALL` moves `SHORTFALL → VIOLATION` regardless of the resulting gap, with `enforcement_flag` set true in both violation cases. The `applied_lbs` value itself is not rewritten by a void; it keeps its recorded amount but stops being counted toward `credit_applied_lbs`.

### 3.9. Adaptive Management, Program Evaluation and Program Status

Monitoring exists to answer one question: is the watershed actually responding? A `ProgramAdministrator` opens an **AdaptiveReview** against a watershed by supplying a stakeholder-facing `review_label` and the evaluation window as `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, ISO calendar dates forming a closed interval whose end must fall strictly after its start. Review windows on one watershed may not crowd each other: the new closed window must be separated from every existing review window on that watershed — whether that review is `OPEN` or `FINALIZED` — by at least one clear calendar day on both sides. Concretely, the new window is acceptable only if its start falls more than one day after the existing window's end, or its end falls more than one day before the existing window's start; anything closer, including windows that merely abut, is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A new review opens in `status` of `OPEN` with `sample_count`, `observed_load_lbs_yr`, `achieved_reduction_pct`, `verdict`, `voided_transaction_count` and `finalized_at` all null.

Finalizing a review is a `ProgramAdministrator` action taking no request body; it moves the review `OPEN → FINALIZED`, stamps `finalized_at` from the logical clock, and increments the review's `version` by 1. It requires that the watershed has an assessment in status `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_TMDL_NOT_APPROVED`, and re-finalizing an already finalized review is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The evaluation gathers the qualifying samples strictly: a monitoring sample qualifies when its reach belongs to this review's watershed, that reach's `reach_class` is `COMPLIANCE_POINT`, its `qa_status` is `ACCEPTED`, and its `sample_datetime` falls between the start of `period_start_date` — 00:00:00.000 UTC on that date — and the last millisecond of `period_end_date` — 23:59:59.999 UTC — inclusive at both ends. Samples flagged `PROVISIONAL` or `REJECTED`, and samples taken on `HEADWATER`, `MAINSTEM` or `BACKGROUND` reaches, are excluded and therefore cannot rescue an otherwise empty window. The count of qualifying samples is written to `sample_count`.

If no sample qualifies, the review is honest about it: `observed_load_lbs_yr` and `achieved_reduction_pct` stay null and the `verdict` is `INDETERMINATE`. This empty-set edge is deliberately not treated as failure — an absence of data is not evidence of a failing watershed. Otherwise `observed_load_lbs_yr` is the sum of the qualifying samples' `computed_load_lbs_yr` divided by `sample_count`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The achieved reduction is then measured against the distance the program has to travel: take the watershed's `baseline_load_lbs_yr` less the observed load, divide by the watershed's `baseline_load_lbs_yr` less the `loading_capacity_lbs_yr` of the watershed's approved assessment, multiply by 100 and round half-up to 2 decimal places to give `achieved_reduction_pct`. That divisor is the one degenerate case in the calculation: when the baseline load exactly equals the approved assessment's loading capacity there is no distance to travel and no percentage to express, so `sample_count` and `observed_load_lbs_yr` are still written from the samples, `achieved_reduction_pct` stays null and the `verdict` is `INDETERMINATE` — the same honest outcome an empty sample window produces. The `verdict` is `ON_TRACK` when that achieved percentage is at least the watershed's `interim_target_pct`, and `OFF_TRACK` otherwise. For an `ON_TRACK` or `INDETERMINATE` verdict, `voided_transaction_count` is 0 and the watershed is left untouched.

An `OFF_TRACK` verdict is the adaptive-management trigger and sets off the full suspension cascade. Its first link moves the watershed `ACTIVE → ADAPTIVE_HOLD` — but only when the watershed's `program_status` is `ACTIVE` at that moment — with `trading_enabled` becoming false and the watershed's `version` incremented by 1. The remaining links are exactly the links the watershed suspension itself runs, described below, and they run on every `OFF_TRACK` verdict whatever the watershed's `program_status` happened to be: a review that comes out `OFF_TRACK` on a watershed already sitting in `ADAPTIVE_HOLD`, or in `TERMINATED`, still voids every open and settled trade it finds there even though the first link had no status change left to make. `voided_transaction_count` records how many credit transactions the second link voided.

The watershed lifecycle can also be driven directly. A `ProgramAdministrator` moves a watershed's `program_status` by naming the desired state in `target_status` and optionally a `reason`. Four transitions are available. `ACTIVE → ADAPTIVE_HOLD` suspends the market. `ADAPTIVE_HOLD → ACTIVE` resumes it, setting `trading_enabled` back to true and incrementing the watershed's `version` by 1; this closes the adaptive-management loop, and previously voided transactions are deliberately not resurrected. `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` closes the program permanently, setting `trading_enabled` false and running the same unwinding cascade as a suspension. `ADAPTIVE_HOLD → TERMINATED` also closes the program permanently, but runs no cascade at all — every open and settled trade was already voided when the watershed entered the hold — and merely increments the watershed's `version` by 1. `TERMINATED` is terminal, and `PROVISIONAL → ACTIVE` is not available through this operation at all; it happens only as a consequence of the first assessment approval described in §3.2. The `target_status` field accepts any one of the four program statuses as a well-formed value, `PROVISIONAL` included, so naming a target the watershed's current status declares no transition to — `PROVISIONAL` from anywhere, anything at all from `PROVISIONAL` or from `TERMINATED`, or a target equal to the current status — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and not with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The optional `reason` is not persisted.

The unwinding cascade that both `ACTIVE → ADAPTIVE_HOLD` and `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` run has three further links after the watershed's own state change. Its second link voids the market: every credit transaction whose buyer permit belongs to this watershed and whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` transitions to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` of `ADAPTIVE_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Its third link restores the batches: for a proposed loser the batch's `reserved_lbs` decreases by `requested_gross_lbs` and `available_lbs` increases by the same amount, while for an approved loser the batch's `retired_lbs` decreases by `settled_gross_lbs` and `available_lbs` increases by the same amount, with each touched batch's `version` incremented by 1 and the watershed's `reserve_pool_lbs` debited by each approved loser's `retired_to_reserve_lbs`. Its fourth link reconciles the buyers: each affected permit's `credit_applied_lbs` decreases by that transaction's `applied_lbs`, `compliance_gap_lbs` is recomputed, `compliance_status` moves `RESOLVED → SHORTFALL` when the recomputed gap comes out above 0.00, and the permit's `version` increments by 1. A permit that had been resolved solely by credits therefore falls back into shortfall the moment the program loses confidence in the watershed's trajectory — which is exactly the enforceability the trading programme promises.
