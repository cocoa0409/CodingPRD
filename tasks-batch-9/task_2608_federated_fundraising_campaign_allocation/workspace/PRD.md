# Federated Fundraising Campaign Allocation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A united fundraising organization sits between two populations that never meet directly. On one side are employers whose workforces pledge money each autumn through workplace campaigns — mostly by payroll deduction, sometimes by check, card or gift of stock. On the other side are member agencies who apply, each year, for a share of the community impact fund. Between them sits a promise that is easy to make and hard to keep: every dollar a donor *designates* to a named agency will be honored, and every dollar left *undesignated* will be distributed by volunteer review panels according to community priorities rather than personal preference. This API is the system of record for that promise.

The operational difficulty is that pledged money is not collected money. A payroll pledge signed in October is collected over twenty-six pay periods, and some of it never arrives — employees leave, take unpaid leave, or revoke. The organization must therefore publish two different loss numbers at two different moments: a forward-looking provisional estimate when solicitation closes, derived from the employer's own historical loss experience, and a final actual loss after remittances are recorded, derived from the pledges that genuinely fell short and absorbed where possible against a campaign-level reserve. The two numbers disagree on identical data by design, and when the final realized shrinkage is severe enough the campaign is placed in escrow hold and the employer partnership is suspended.

On the allocation side, the system encodes the governance the organization owes its donors: member agencies must pass financial and governance review before they may receive designations or request funds; volunteer panels score requests under conflict-of-interest and quorum rules; awards from the undesignated pool are reduced by what an agency has already received in donor designations, then topped up for strategic priority alignment; and funded agencies must file outcome reports subject to monitoring site visits, with the panelist who scored a request barred from visiting it. This document specifies every entity, state transition, calculation, validation, endpoint and error code required to implement that system. The service is mounted under `/fundraising`, with one out-of-band administrative clock endpoint described in §4.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identity and audit fields, arithmetic precision, stale-version protection, the logical clock, cascade propagation, path resolution and the order in which checks are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal acts. Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `UnitedFundAdmin`, `CampaignCoordinator`, `FinanceReviewer`, `AgencyRepresentative`, `VolunteerPanelist`, and `AllocationsDirector`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent or is not one of those six strings is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), as is a request that omits `X-User-ID` for any role whose identity is backed by a persisted entity. Once the caller is authenticated, the role is checked against the set of roles the target operation permits; a role outside that set is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). The per-operation role sets are tabulated in §4.

Three of the six roles are backed by persisted entities, which is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `CampaignCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CampaignCoordinator` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references a coordinator compare the resource's coordinator foreign key to `X-User-ID`: the acting coordinator must be seated at the employer that hosts the campaign being written to, and for writes against an existing pledge the pledge's `recorded_by_coordinator_id` must equal `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agency`. Ownership checks compare the target resource's agency foreign key to `X-User-ID`: an allocation request's `agency_id`, or, for an outcome report, the `agency_id` of the allocation request the report belongs to.
- When `X-User-Role` is `VolunteerPanelist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Panelist`. It is stamped onto every score the volunteer files and onto every site visit the volunteer schedules, and for site-visit completion the report's `site_visit_panelist_id` must equal `X-User-ID`.

The remaining three roles carry opaque identities:

- When `X-User-Role` is `UnitedFundAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. `UnitedFundAdmin` is the broadest administrative surface: employer partnerships, agency admission, solicitation closure and the logical clock are exclusive to it.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FinanceReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and never compared to any entity's `id`. Finance staff record remittances, write off uncollectable pledges, and certify agency financial reviews.
- When `X-User-Role` is `AllocationsDirector`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier not compared to any entity's `id`. The director stewards the community impact fund and is the only role that may issue or rescind an award.

Any ownership check that fails is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403). Segregation of duties is enforced on top of role permissions and is described in §2.11.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Sorting and List Filters

List responses are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. Both appear in the `meta` object of the envelope alongside `total_count` (the number of records matching the filter before pagination) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

Unless a specific list states otherwise, records are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Three lists deviate for domain reasons: the campaigns of one employer are returned newest first; the financial reviews of one agency are returned with the highest `fiscal_year` first; and the designation lines of one pledge are returned in ascending `designation_sequence`, superseded and uncollected lines included. The cross-campaign pledge list is ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `donor_reference` ascending.

Filters, all optional, are supplied as query parameters and are additive. The coordinator roster of an employer accepts `status`. The campaign list of an employer accepts `posture` and `campaign_year`. The cross-campaign pledge list accepts `campaign_id`, `recorded_by_coordinator_id`, `giving_method`, `designation_status` and `collection_status`. The designation-line lists — both the per-pledge list and the per-agency list — accept `status`. The agency review list accepts `status` and `fiscal_year`. The panel roster of a cycle accepts `conflict_declared` and `assignment_role`, while the panel seats held by one panelist accept `conflict_declared`. The allocation request list accepts `cycle_id`, `agency_id`, `status` and `priority_area`. Score lists — both per request and per panelist — accept `eligible`. The outcome report list accepts `request_id`, `site_visit_panelist_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Version Semantics

Every resource in this system carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Every resource also carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. The `version` of a freshly created resource starts at 1 and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative — a cascade that flips a child's status, a recomputed aggregate, a stamped timestamp. Whenever `version` increments, `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock instant of the mutation. No entity in this system is exempt: employers, coordinators, campaigns, pledges, designation lines, agencies, agency reviews, allocation cycles, allocation requests, panelists, panel assignments, panel scores and outcome reports all expose all four common properties.

Because aggregates are stored rather than computed on read, the reader should expect a single business action to advance several `version` counters at once. Confirming a pledge, for example, mutates the pledge and re-derives four campaign-level totals, so both the pledge row and the campaign row are mutations in the sense above.

### 2.5. Money, Ratios and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half up. Ratio inputs on a financial review — the program expense ratio, the administrative expense ratio and their audited counterparts — are supplied with at most 4 decimal places and are never rounded before use in a calculation. Scores and score components are carried to 2 decimal places and rounded half up. Basis-point fields are integers: the realized shrinkage rate is rounded half up to zero decimal places. Percentage variance on an outcome report is carried to 2 decimal places, rounded half up, and may be negative.

Rounding *order* is contractual, not incidental, and the calculations in §3 state it explicitly at each step. Two rules recur and are worth stating once here. First, when a calculation rounds a per-line or per-score value and then aggregates, the aggregate is the sum of the already-rounded values and is never re-rounded — summing raw products and rounding the total once at the end produces a different answer and is wrong. Second, when a calculation both clamps and rounds, the pinned order is given step by step, and each rounded intermediate value is what the following step consumes.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Protection on Shared Parents

Two write operations touch a parent whose state the client must have observed before deciding, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on that parent's `version`.

Amending the designation lines of a pledge requires a non-optional `expected_pledge_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Pledge`. If that value does not equal the pledge's current `version`, the amendment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Any earlier amendment, confirmation, remittance or write-off on that pledge has already advanced its `version`, so a client working from an older read is refused rather than allowed to replace a line set it never saw. Nothing in the pledge's own state machine catches this case, because a pledge returns to `CONFIRMED` after each confirmation and a second amendment from `CONFIRMED` is otherwise perfectly legal.

Issuing an award requires a non-optional `expected_allocation_cycle_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AllocationCycle`. If that value does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the award is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Awards draw from a shared pool, so any prior award, rescission, posture change or pool recomputation on that cycle invalidates the balance the director based the decision on. As with the amendment, the cycle's state machine would happily accept the second award on its own merits — only the version check refuses it.

No other operation in this API accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above perform no optimistic concurrency check.

### 2.7. The Logical Clock and Date-Window Conventions

Every timestamp the server stamps, and every window, deadline and buffer rule it evaluates, reads a logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must not fall back to real time. An administrative capability lets a `UnitedFundAdmin` move the clock to any instant by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response reports the new instant as `current_utc`. Moving the clock re-derives outcome-report delinquency for every agency whose reporting deadline has now passed without an accepted report.

Date intervals come in two flavors and the distinction changes answers. A campaign's solicitation window and a cycle's request window are **half-open**: the start date is included and the end date is excluded. A site-visit window is a **closed** interval: both endpoints are included. Comparisons against the logical clock use the clock's date part only.

### 2.8. Cascade Propagation and Aggregate Re-derivation

Three named cascades propagate a single action across the entity graph, and each link is a mutation in the sense of §2.4.

The **write-off cascade** has three links: the pledge itself becomes written off with its uncollected amount fixed; every currently active designation line of that pledge is marked uncollected and its collected designated amount is re-derived to zero; and the year-to-date designated passthrough of every agency that held one of those lines is recomputed with the now-uncollected lines dropped out of the sum.

The **escrow-hold cascade** has two links: a campaign whose realized shrinkage is severe enough enters escrow hold, and that in turn forces its employer to `SUSPENDED` with a stamped reason. Suspension of the employer fans out to every coordinator seated at that employer, flipping each to `INACTIVE`. The fan-out is a consequence of the employer transition, not a further link in the chain.

The **probation cascade** has four links: certification of a review writes the certified figures onto the review; the certified score drives the agency's membership transition; a downgrade to probation or suspended membership pulls every one of that agency's in-flight allocation requests into suspended review, remembering the status each came from; and each affected cycle's suspension counters and ratio are recomputed, with a sufficiently high ratio driving the cycle from open to restricted and blocking awards. Reinstatement reverses links 4, 3 and 2 in the opposite direction, restoring each request to the status it was pulled from and releasing the cycle restriction when the recomputed ratio has fallen back below the threshold.

Stored aggregates are always re-derived from their defining query rather than adjusted incrementally, so a reader can verify any total by re-running its definition. Those definitions live with the workflows that maintain them in §3.

### 2.9. Left-to-Right Path Resolution and Nested Integrity

When a path names more than one resource, existence is validated strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing determines the error. The resolution order across this API is: employer, campaign, pledge, agency, agency review, allocation cycle, allocation request, panelist, outcome report — yielding `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGENCY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_OUTCOME_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` respectively, all with HTTP 404. Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved the same way and raise the same 404 codes: `employer_id`, `campaign_id`, `agency_id`, `cycle_id`, `panelist_id`, `request_id`, and the `agency_id` of each element of a `designations` array.

Existence alone is not integrity. Where a path nests one resource inside another, the child must actually belong to the named parent: certifying a review requires that the review named by `review_id` in the path belong to the agency named earlier in the same path, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_AGENCY_MISMATCH` (409).

### 2.10. Order of Evaluation

Checks are evaluated in a fixed ladder, ascending, and the first failure is the response. The ladder matters because several gates would otherwise mask each other; where two gates could both fire, the earlier one wins.

1. Authentication of the headers, as described in §2.1 (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role permission for the requested operation (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`).
3. Request-shape validation on every write: all required fields present; every value matching its declared type, enum membership and numeric range; money fields carrying at most 2 decimals and ratio fields at most 4 (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400).
4. Existence of every path-segment resource, left to right (§2.9).
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the body — applied to campaign creation, allocation request creation, panel seating, award issuance, pledge recording and designation amendment (§2.9).
6. Ownership for the entity-backed roles (`ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`), applied to pledge recording, pledge confirmation, designation amendment, allocation request creation, allocation request submission, outcome report creation, site-visit completion and outcome report resubmission.
7. Nested path integrity for review certification (`ERR_REVIEW_AGENCY_MISMATCH`).
8. Optimistic concurrency on the named parent, for designation amendment and award issuance (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`, §2.6).
9. Employer status precondition for activation: the employer must be `PROSPECT` (`ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`).
10. Employer must be `ACTIVE` before one of its campaigns may open (`ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_ACTIVE`).
11. Campaign posture must be `OPEN` for pledge recording, pledge confirmation and designation amendment (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_OPEN`).
12. Campaign posture must be exactly the posture the requested campaign transition starts from — `PLANNED` to open, `OPEN` to close solicitation, `SOLICITATION_CLOSED` to reconcile — and must be either `OPEN` or `SOLICITATION_CLOSED` for remittances and write-offs (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_INVALID_STATE`).
13. Pledge designation status must be `DRAFT` or `AMENDED` to confirm, `CONFIRMED` to amend, and either `CONFIRMED` or `AMENDED` for remittances and write-offs (`ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_CONFIRMED`).
14. Pledge collection status must not already be terminal, that is, neither `COLLECTED` nor `WRITTEN_OFF`, for remittances and write-offs (`ERR_PLEDGE_COLLECTION_TERMINAL`).
15. A financial review must still be `PENDING` to be certified (`ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`).
16. Agency membership must not be `SUSPENDED_MEMBERSHIP` for review filing, certification or reinstatement; reinstatement additionally requires membership `PROBATION` with no open review (`ERR_AGENCY_MEMBERSHIP_TERMINAL`).
17. The cycle must not be `FROZEN` — checked on award issuance, panel seating, request submission, score filing and review finalization (`ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`).
18. The cycle must be `OPEN` for request submission and score filing, and a cycle may only be opened from `DRAFT` (`ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`).
19. A `RESTRICTED` cycle blocks award issuance only; scoring and review finalization stay legal while restricted (`ERR_CYCLE_RESTRICTED`).
20. Allocation request status must be one the operation accepts: `DRAFT` to submit; either `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW` to finalize review or to file a score; `PANEL_APPROVED` to award; either `AWARDED` or `REPORTING` to rescind an award; `AWARDED` to file an outcome report (`ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`). Two of those admissions are deliberately permissive so that a later, more specific gate can produce the right error: a still-`SUBMITTED` request passes here and then fails at gate 36, and a `REPORTING` request passes here and then fails at gate 41.
21. Outcome report status must be exactly the status the requested transition starts from: `SUBMITTED` to schedule a site visit, `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED` to complete it, `SITE_VISIT_COMPLETED` to accept or to request a revision, and `REVISION_REQUESTED` to resubmit (`ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`).
22. A panelist who declared a conflict of interest on the cycle may not file a score on any request of that cycle (`ERR_PANELIST_CONFLICT_DECLARED`).
23. Segregation of duties on site-visit scheduling (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
24. The requesting agency must be in `GOOD_STANDING` at submission time (`ERR_AGENCY_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`).
25. The requesting agency must not be carrying an overdue outcome report (`ERR_OUTCOME_REPORT_DELINQUENT`).
26. Every designation target must be an agency eligible to receive designations (`ERR_DESIGNATION_TARGET_INELIGIBLE`).
27. Campaign solicitation windows of one employer must not intersect (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`).
28. Site-visit spacing for the acting panelist (`ERR_SITE_VISIT_BUFFER_CONFLICT`).
29. Site-visit window sanity (`ERR_SITE_VISIT_WINDOW_INVALID`).
30. Request submission must fall inside the cycle's request window (`ERR_REQUEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`).
31. Each designated amount must reach the per-line minimum (`ERR_DESIGNATION_BELOW_MINIMUM`).
32. Designated amounts must not exceed the pledge's gross amount (`ERR_DESIGNATION_EXCEEDS_GROSS`).
33. Donor reference uniqueness within the campaign (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DONOR_REFERENCE`).
34. The acting panelist must hold a seat on the cycle before filing a score or scheduling a site visit (`ERR_PANELIST_NOT_ASSIGNED`).
35. One score per panelist per request, and one seat per panelist per cycle (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_SCORE`).
36. Review finalization requires a non-empty eligible score set (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SCORES`).
37. Review finalization requires the eligible score set to reach quorum once it is non-empty (`ERR_PANEL_QUORUM_NOT_MET`).
38. One live allocation request per agency per cycle (`ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUEST_FOR_CYCLE`).
39. The revision-round ceiling on an outcome report (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`).
40. A cycle may not open without a conflict-free panel seat (`ERR_PANEL_NOT_SEATED`).
41. An award may not be rescinded once any outcome report exists for the request (`ERR_AWARD_ALREADY_REPORTED`).
42. Payroll deduction requires the employer to run payroll (`ERR_PAYROLL_METHOD_UNAVAILABLE`).
43. A remittance may not exceed the pledge's outstanding balance (`ERR_COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`).
44. Reconciliation requires every confirmed or amended pledge to have left `PENDING` collection (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FULLY_REMITTED`).
45. The requested amount may not exceed the cycle's per-agency ceiling multiplied by the request cap multiple, at submission time (`ERR_AWARD_EXCEEDS_AGENCY_CAP`).
46. The assembled, not-yet-clamped award may not exceed the cycle's available pool (`ERR_POOL_INSUFFICIENT`).

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties Across Fund Stewardship

The organization's auditors require that no single person can both certify and disburse. Three separations are enforced structurally. A `FinanceReviewer` certifies agency financial reviews and writes off pledges but may never issue an award. An `AllocationsDirector` issues and rescinds awards but may never certify a financial review. And a `VolunteerPanelist` who filed a score against an allocation request may never conduct the monitoring site visit on that same request's outcome report; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The first two separations are expressed through the role sets in §4; the third is a per-request check evaluated at gate 23 of §2.10, ahead of the panelist's seat check and ahead of the site-visit window rules, so a scorer's attempt to self-monitor surfaces as a duties violation rather than as a scheduling complaint.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Workplace Partnerships and the Coordinator Roster

Everything begins with an employer. A `UnitedFundAdmin` registers a workplace campaign partner by supplying its `legal_name` — between 1 and 200 characters and not blank — and its `employee_count`, which must be at least 1 and serves as the solicitation universe for the campaign. Two further inputs are optional and both carry defaults that matter later. `payroll_periods_per_year` records how many payroll runs the employer executes annually; it defaults to 26, must fall between 0 and 52 inclusive, and a value of 0 declares that this employer cannot host payroll deduction at all. `historical_shrinkage_rate_bps` records the employer's own historical payroll-pledge loss experience in basis points; it defaults to 800, meaning 8 percent, and must fall between 0 and 10000 inclusive. A fifth optional input, `partnership_signed`, defaults to false and records whether the workplace partnership agreement was countersigned at the moment of registration.

Registration computes the partnership's `status` rather than accepting it. An employer registered with `partnership_signed` true is created directly in `ACTIVE`; otherwise it is created in `PROSPECT`. Its `suspended_reason` is null and its `active_campaign_count` starts at 0. Countersigning later is a distinct administrative act available only to a `UnitedFundAdmin`: it advances the partnership `PROSPECT → ACTIVE` and sets `partnership_signed` to true. Because an employer registered as already signed is `ACTIVE` on arrival, a countersignature attempt against it has nothing to advance and is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`.

The third and final partnership status, `SUSPENDED`, is never requested by a client. It is forced by the escrow-hold cascade of §2.8 when one of the employer's campaigns closes out with severe shrinkage, and it stamps `suspended_reason` with `ESCROW_HOLD_SHRINKAGE` — the only value that field ever takes. `SUSPENDED` is terminal: there is no path back to `ACTIVE`. The employer's `active_campaign_count` is always the number of that employer's campaigns whose posture is either `OPEN` or `SOLICITATION_CLOSED`, re-derived whenever a campaign of that employer is created or changes posture.

Campaign coordinators are the employer-side volunteers who actually solicit pledges, and only a `UnitedFundAdmin` may seat one. A coordinator is created against an employer, so the resulting row carries the `employer_id` of the workplace it solicits inside, and it is described by `full_name` (1 to 120 characters) and `email`, which must contain an at-sign and must be unique across every coordinator in the system. The optional `coordinator_role` is either `LEAD` or `DEPARTMENT` and defaults to `DEPARTMENT`; a `LEAD` coordinator may record pledges for any department, while the `DEPARTMENT` value is informational for the purposes of this API and grants no additional capability. Each coordinator's `status` begins at `ACTIVE` and flips to `INACTIVE` only through the escrow-hold fan-out, when the parent employer becomes `SUSPENDED`. The coordinator's `recorded_pledge_count` counts the pledges whose `recorded_by_coordinator_id` is that coordinator and whose designation status is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED` — draft pledges do not count toward a coordinator's tally, and neither do written-off ones as long as their designation status is one of those three. The `id` returned when a coordinator is seated is the value that coordinator supplies as `X-User-ID`.

### 3.2. The Annual Campaign: Solicitation Windows and Posture

A campaign is the annual fundraising drive hosted at one employer. Either a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `CampaignCoordinator` may create one, supplying the `employer_id` of the host, the `campaign_year` label (between 2020 and 2100 inclusive), and the solicitation window as `solicitation_start_date` and `solicitation_end_date`, both valid ISO dates in year-month-day form, with the end strictly later than the start. An optional `loss_reserve_amount` — non-negative, two decimal places, defaulting to 0.00 — establishes the reserve that will later absorb uncollected pledge value. The campaign is created in `PLANNED` posture, with every gross, fee and loss aggregate zeroed and every reconciliation output null: `provisional_estimated_loss`, `provisional_net_expected_revenue`, `reserve_absorbed_amount`, `final_actual_loss`, `final_net_expected_revenue`, `realized_shrinkage_rate_bps`, `solicitation_closed_at` and `reconciled_at` all begin as null.

One employer may not run two overlapping campaigns. The solicitation window is half-open — the start date belongs to the window and the end date does not — and a new campaign's window must not intersect the window of any other campaign of the same employer whose posture is anything other than `ESCROW_HOLD`. Two half-open windows intersect when the new start falls strictly before the existing end **and** the existing start falls strictly before the new end; consequently, a new campaign starting on exactly the date an existing campaign ends does not intersect it and is accepted. An intersection is rejected with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A campaign already in `ESCROW_HOLD` is invisible to this test, so an employer whose prior campaign collapsed into escrow may reuse the same dates.

Opening a campaign moves its posture `PLANNED → OPEN` and is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `CampaignCoordinator`. The transition is legal only from `PLANNED`, and it carries a cross-entity guard: the host employer must be `ACTIVE`. An employer still in `PROSPECT`, or already `SUSPENDED`, blocks the opening with `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Opening re-derives the employer's `active_campaign_count`.

While a campaign is `OPEN`, four money aggregates track what has been confirmed. `confirmed_gross_total` is the sum of `gross_amount` over the campaign's pledges whose designation status is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED`. `designated_gross_total` is the sum of `designated_amount` over active designation lines whose parent pledge belongs to the campaign and sits in one of those same three designation statuses. `undesignated_gross_total` is the sum of `undesignated_amount` over the campaign's pledges in those three statuses, and it is the campaign's contribution to the community impact pool. `designation_fee_total` at campaign level is the sum of each qualifying pledge's own `designation_fee_total`. All four are re-derived whenever a pledge of the campaign is confirmed or its line set is amended.

Closing solicitation advances the posture `OPEN → SOLICITATION_CLOSED` and is exclusive to a `UnitedFundAdmin`. It stamps `solicitation_closed_at` from the logical clock, re-derives `projected_payroll_pledge_total`, and runs the provisional half of the shrinkage estimate described in §3.5. After the close, no new pledge may be recorded, no pledge may be confirmed, and no designation line set may be amended — each attempt is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_OPEN` — while remittances and write-offs remain fully legal, which is precisely how the campaign learns what it actually collected. The two terminal postures, `RECONCILED` and `ESCROW_HOLD`, are reached only through reconciliation, and a campaign transition attempted from a posture the transition does not start from is rejected with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.3. Recording Pledges and Honoring Donor Designations

Inside an `OPEN` campaign, a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `CampaignCoordinator` records a donor's pledge. The payload carries a `donor_reference` — the employer-side donor identifier, 1 to 40 characters, unique within the campaign, and rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DONOR_REFERENCE` when it collides with another pledge of the same campaign — together with a `giving_method` of `PAYROLL_DEDUCTION`, `ONE_TIME_CHECK`, `CREDIT_CARD` or `STOCK_GIFT`, and a `gross_amount` that must be strictly greater than zero with at most two decimal places. When a coordinator records the pledge, `recorded_by_coordinator_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID`; the pledge's `campaign_id` is the campaign it was solicited inside. A new pledge is born with designation status `DRAFT` and collection status `PENDING`, `collected_amount` and `uncollected_amount` at 0.00, `amend_count` at 0 and `write_off_reason_code` null.

Payroll deduction is only available where payroll exists. A pledge whose `giving_method` is `PAYROLL_DEDUCTION` recorded at an employer whose `payroll_periods_per_year` is 0 is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_METHOD_UNAVAILABLE`.

The optional `designations` array is how the donor directs money to named agencies. Each element is an object with exactly two members, `agency_id` and `designated_amount`. Array position is significant: the first element becomes `designation_sequence` 1, the second 2, and so on through N, and that sequence — never any property of a line's `id` — pins the order in which per-line fees are computed. Each line's `pledge_id` is the pledge being split. Three rules gate the array. Every `agency_id` must resolve to an agency whose membership standing is either `GOOD_STANDING` or `PROBATION`; agencies that are `APPLICANT`, `UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW` or `SUSPENDED_MEMBERSHIP` may not receive donor designations, and targeting one is refused with `ERR_DESIGNATION_TARGET_INELIGIBLE`. Every `designated_amount` must be at least 25.00, the minimum designation the organization will process, and a smaller line is refused with `ERR_DESIGNATION_BELOW_MINIMUM`. And the designated amounts must not sum to more than the pledge's `gross_amount`; an excess is refused with `ERR_DESIGNATION_EXCEEDS_GROSS`, while any shortfall is not an error at all — it is the donor's undesignated contribution.

Honoring a designation is not free, and the fee arithmetic is where implementations most often diverge. Lines are processed in ascending `designation_sequence`. For each line, the raw fee is that line's `designated_amount` multiplied by the donor-designation processing rate of 7.25 percent; the line's `processing_fee` is that raw fee rounded half up to two decimal places, or 1.50 — the floor the organization charges to touch any designation at all — whichever is larger. The line's `net_designated_amount` is its `designated_amount` minus its `processing_fee`, and that net figure is what the agency is actually owed. Only after every line has its own rounded fee are the pledge-level totals assembled: `designated_amount_total` is the sum of `designated_amount` over the pledge's active lines; `undesignated_amount` is the pledge's `gross_amount` minus `designated_amount_total`; `designation_fee_total` is the sum of the already-rounded per-line `processing_fee` values; and `net_designated_total` is `designated_amount_total` minus `designation_fee_total`. Totals are never re-rounded after summation.

Consider a 150.00 pledge split into two lines of 25.00 each. Each line's raw fee is 1.8125, which rounds half up to 1.81, and neither line is lifted by the 1.50 floor, so `designation_fee_total` is 3.62 and `undesignated_amount` is 100.00. Summing the two raw amounts first and rounding once — 50.00 at 7.25 percent is 3.625, which would round to 3.63 — produces a different and incorrect total. Per-line rounding before summation is the pinned behaviour.

Recording a pledge leaves it in `DRAFT`, which contributes to nothing. Confirmation is the act that folds it into the campaign's books: a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `CampaignCoordinator` advances the pledge `DRAFT → CONFIRMED` while the campaign is `OPEN`, and the same operation later closes the amendment loop by advancing `AMENDED → CONFIRMED`. Either way, confirmation re-derives the campaign's `confirmed_gross_total`, `designated_gross_total`, `undesignated_gross_total` and `designation_fee_total`, re-derives the recording coordinator's `recorded_pledge_count`, and re-derives `designated_passthrough_ytd` for every agency the pledge designates. A confirmation attempted from a designation status other than `DRAFT` or `AMENDED` — from `CONFIRMED` or from the terminal `LOCKED` — is refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and a confirmation attempted after solicitation has closed is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_OPEN`.

### 3.4. When the Donor Changes Their Mind: Amending a Designation Line Set

Donors revise their designations, and the organization honors revisions until solicitation closes. Amending replaces a pledge's entire active line set at once and is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `CampaignCoordinator`. The operation is legal only from designation status `CONFIRMED` — a `DRAFT` pledge has nothing to revise and a `LOCKED` one is closed, both refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_CONFIRMED` — and only while the campaign is `OPEN`. Alongside a fresh `designations` array of the same element shape, the request must carry `expected_pledge_version` as described in §2.6; a value that does not match the pledge's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 before any line is touched. The same eligibility, minimum-amount and sum-versus-gross rules that governed the original array govern the replacement, and they raise the same three codes.

A successful amendment does five things and then advances the state. Every currently `ACTIVE` designation line of the pledge moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, which drops it out of `designated_amount_total` and out of the receiving agency's `designated_passthrough_ytd` permanently — a superseded line is frozen with `collected_designated_amount` at 0.00 and never re-enters any total. One fresh `ACTIVE` line is created per element of the new array, numbered `designation_sequence` 1 through N in array order. The fee allocation is re-run over the new active set, rewriting `designated_amount_total`, `undesignated_amount`, `designation_fee_total` and `net_designated_total`. The year-to-date designated passthrough is re-derived for every agency that lost a line and every agency that gained one. And `amend_count` — the running count of successful amendments against this pledge — increments by one. Only then does the pledge move `CONFIRMED → AMENDED`, with the campaign's designated, undesignated and fee aggregates re-derived to match.

The pledge may lap this cycle indefinitely while the campaign stays open: `CONFIRMED → AMENDED → CONFIRMED` and around again, each amendment incrementing both `amend_count` and `version`. Note the asymmetry that makes the version guard indispensable: `AMENDED` refuses a second amendment outright, but `CONFIRMED` accepts one, so a client that amended, confirmed, and then amended again from a stale read would otherwise silently overwrite a line set it never observed.

### 3.5. Remittance, Write-Offs and Shrinkage Accounting

Collection runs on an axis entirely independent of designation. A pledge's `collection_status` starts at `PENDING` and evolves through `PARTIAL` to either `COLLECTED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, and it does so without regard to whether the pledge is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED` on the designation axis — a pledge may perfectly well be `PARTIAL` while `AMENDED`. Remittances are recorded by a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `FinanceReviewer`, who supply the `amount` received and `remitted_on`, the date of the remittance. The pledge must be `CONFIRMED` or `AMENDED` on the designation axis, or the remittance is refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_CONFIRMED`; its collection status must not already be terminal, or the remittance is refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_COLLECTION_TERMINAL`; and the parent campaign must be either `OPEN` or `SOLICITATION_CLOSED`, or it is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_INVALID_STATE`. The `amount` may not exceed the pledge's outstanding balance, which is `gross_amount` minus `collected_amount`; a larger remittance is refused with `ERR_COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`.

Which state the pledge lands in is decided by comparing the remittance to that outstanding balance. A remittance strictly smaller than the outstanding balance advances `PENDING → PARTIAL`, or holds a pledge already at `PARTIAL → PARTIAL` for each further instalment. A remittance exactly equal to the outstanding balance closes the pledge, advancing either `PENDING → COLLECTED` or `PARTIAL → COLLECTED`, and in that case `collected_amount` is set to `gross_amount` and `uncollected_amount` to 0.00. In every other case `collected_amount` increases by the remitted `amount` and `uncollected_amount` is re-derived as `gross_amount` minus `collected_amount` — the pledge's live shrinkage exposure.

Each remittance also flows down to the designation lines, because a donor who has paid only half their pledge has only honored half of each designation. Processing the pledge's `ACTIVE` lines in ascending `designation_sequence`, each line's `collected_designated_amount` becomes its `net_designated_amount` multiplied by the pledge's collection ratio — `collected_amount` divided by `gross_amount` — rounded half up to two decimal places. Lines that are `SUPERSEDED` or `UNCOLLECTED` are frozen at 0.00 and never rescaled. Every agency holding an `ACTIVE` line of the pledge then has its `designated_passthrough_ytd` re-derived, that figure being the sum of `collected_designated_amount` over all `ACTIVE` designation lines pointing at that agency, across every campaign. This is the number that later reduces the agency's draw on the undesignated pool, which is why remittance and allocation are two directions of the same ledger.

Some payroll pledges will never be collected. Writing one off is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `FinanceReviewer`, requires a `reason_code` of `TERMINATION`, `LEAVE_OF_ABSENCE`, `DONOR_REVOKED` or `EMPLOYER_DEFAULT`, and is legal from `PENDING` or `PARTIAL` provided the pledge is `CONFIRMED` or `AMENDED` and the campaign is `OPEN` or `SOLICITATION_CLOSED`. The write-off cascade then runs its three links in order. First, the pledge's `collection_status` advances `PENDING → WRITTEN_OFF` or `PARTIAL → WRITTEN_OFF` according to where it stood, `write_off_reason_code` records the supplied reason, and `uncollected_amount` is fixed at `gross_amount` minus `collected_amount`. Second, because of that, every `ACTIVE` designation line of the pledge moves `ACTIVE → UNCOLLECTED` and each such line's `collected_designated_amount` is re-derived to 0.00. Third, because of that, `designated_passthrough_ytd` is re-derived for every agency that held one of those lines, with the newly uncollected lines dropped out of the sum — an agency's apparent designation income therefore falls the moment a donor's pledge is written off, and its future award from the undesignated pool rises correspondingly.

Two loss figures are published at two different moments, from two deliberately different input sets, and they disagree on identical data. The **provisional estimate** is produced when solicitation closes and looks forward using the employer's own history. It first re-derives `projected_payroll_pledge_total` as the sum of `gross_amount` over the campaign's pledges that are simultaneously `PAYROLL_DEDUCTION`, `CONFIRMED` or `AMENDED` on the designation axis, and `PENDING` or `PARTIAL` on the collection axis — that is, payroll pledges still exposed to loss. `provisional_estimated_loss` is then that projected total multiplied by the employer's `historical_shrinkage_rate_bps` divided by 10000, rounded half up to two decimal places; the campaign's own `loss_reserve_amount` is ignored entirely at this stage. `provisional_net_expected_revenue` is `confirmed_gross_total` minus `provisional_estimated_loss`.

The **final actual loss** is produced at reconciliation, ignores the employer's historical rate completely, and walks only the pledges that genuinely fell short: the campaign's pledges whose designation status is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED`, whose collection status is `PARTIAL` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, and whose `uncollected_amount` is strictly greater than zero. A fully `COLLECTED` pledge never enters, and neither does a `PENDING` one — reconciliation cannot run while any confirmed or amended pledge is still `PENDING`. That candidate set is walked in a pinned order that changes the answer: ascending `created_at` first, then descending `gross_amount`, then ascending `donor_reference` as the final tie-break, which is total because `donor_reference` is unique within a campaign. No property of a pledge's `id` is ever consulted.

Absorption against the reserve is all-or-nothing. Starting with the whole `loss_reserve_amount` available, each candidate pledge in that order is tested against the reserve remaining: if the pledge's `uncollected_amount` is less than or equal to the reserve remaining, the whole amount is absorbed, the reserve remaining is reduced by it, and the running absorbed total grows by it; otherwise the pledge is skipped in its entirety — no partial draw is ever taken — and the walk continues, so a later and smaller pledge may still be absorbed after a larger one was skipped. The running absorbed total becomes `reserve_absorbed_amount`. `final_actual_loss` is the sum of `uncollected_amount` across the entire candidate set minus `reserve_absorbed_amount`, and `final_net_expected_revenue` is `confirmed_gross_total` minus `final_actual_loss`. Finally, `realized_shrinkage_rate_bps` expresses the final loss as basis points of confirmed gross: it is `final_actual_loss` divided by `confirmed_gross_total`, multiplied by 10000, rounded half up to zero decimal places — or exactly 0 when `confirmed_gross_total` is zero.

Reconciliation itself is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `FinanceReviewer` and is legal only from `SOLICITATION_CLOSED`. Beyond the posture check, it requires that no pledge of the campaign whose designation status is `CONFIRMED` or `AMENDED` still sits at `PENDING` collection; if one does, reconciliation is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FULLY_REMITTED`. On success the final loss figures are written, every pledge of the campaign whose designation status is `CONFIRMED` or `AMENDED` advances to the terminal locked status, `CONFIRMED → LOCKED` and `AMENDED → LOCKED` respectively — each such pledge being a mutation with its own version increment — and `reconciled_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

The realized shrinkage rate then selects between two forks of the same operation. When `realized_shrinkage_rate_bps` is strictly less than 1500 — that is, under 15 percent of confirmed gross — the campaign lands in the ordinary terminal posture `SOLICITATION_CLOSED → RECONCILED` and nothing further happens. When it is 1500 or more, the boundary value 1500 itself counting as severe, the campaign lands in `SOLICITATION_CLOSED → ESCROW_HOLD` and the escrow-hold cascade fires: the host employer is forced `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `ESCROW_HOLD_SHRINKAGE`, and every coordinator seated at that employer flips `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`. Both forks write the same four final figures and both lock the same pledges; they differ only in the posture they land in and in whether the cascade runs.

### 3.6. Agency Membership Standards and Financial Review

A member agency is admitted by a `UnitedFundAdmin`, who supplies its `legal_name` (1 to 200 characters), its `ein` — exactly nine digits, globally unique across all agencies — and its `primary_priority_area`, one of `BASIC_NEEDS`, `EDUCATION`, `HEALTH` or `FINANCIAL_STABILITY`, naming the community priority its core programs serve. A newly admitted agency stands at `membership_status` `APPLICANT`, with `latest_standing_score` null because no review has been certified, `designated_passthrough_ytd` at 0.00, `open_review_count` and `probation_count` at 0, and `outcome_report_delinquent` false. An `APPLICANT` may not yet receive donor designations and may not request funds.

Standing is earned through financial and governance review. A review packet may be filed by a `UnitedFundAdmin`, a `FinanceReviewer` or the agency's own `AgencyRepresentative`, and carries the `fiscal_year` the statements cover — between 2020 and 2100 inclusive, and unique per agency so that one fiscal year cannot be reviewed twice — plus five self-reported figures: `program_expense_ratio` and `admin_expense_ratio`, each between 0.0000 and 1.0000 with at most four decimal places; `months_operating_reserve`, the agency's unrestricted net assets expressed in months of operating expense, at least 0 and at most 60 with two decimals; `governance_checklist_passed_count`, how many of the ten mandatory governance requirements — independent board, conflict-of-interest policy, audit committee and the rest — the agency satisfies, between 0 and 10 inclusive; and `audit_opinion`, one of `UNQUALIFIED`, `QUALIFIED`, `ADVERSE` or `NONE`. The review is created with `status` `PENDING` and `review_basis` `SELF_REPORTED`; `audited_program_expense_ratio`, `audited_admin_expense_ratio`, `audited_months_operating_reserve` and `certified_score` are null, `standards_met` is null, and `restatement_penalty_applied` is false. Filing also moves the agency into `UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW` — `APPLICANT → UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW` for a first-time applicant, `GOOD_STANDING → UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW` for an annual re-review, and `PROBATION → UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW` for an agency trying to work its way back — and increments `open_review_count`. A re-review is consequential: while the agency sits at `UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW` it is not in `GOOD_STANDING`, so it can neither receive new donor designations nor submit a new allocation request until the re-review is certified. Filing a review against an agency whose membership is the terminal `SUSPENDED_MEMBERSHIP` is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_MEMBERSHIP_TERMINAL`.

The standing score is computed twice from the same shaped formula but two different input sets. The **preliminary** score is computed at filing from the self-reported ratios and written to `preliminary_score`. The **certified** score is computed at certification from the audited ratios and written to `certified_score`. In both variants, four point components are assembled. Program points reward program spending above a 0.65 floor: the program ratio minus 0.65, floored at zero so a ratio at or below 0.65 earns nothing, multiplied by 400, rounded half up to two decimals, and then capped at 40. Reserve points award 5 points per month of operating reserve, rounded half up to two decimals and capped at 25. Governance points award 2.5 points for each satisfied governance requirement, rounded half up to two decimals, which yields a range of 0 to 25 across the ten-item checklist. Audit points are 10 for an `UNQUALIFIED` opinion, 5 for `QUALIFIED`, and 0 for both `ADVERSE` and `NONE` — except in the preliminary variant, which forces audit points to 0 no matter what `audit_opinion` says, because nothing has been audited yet. The four components sum to a raw total.

Two penalties are then subtracted. The administrative penalty is zero when the administrative ratio is at or below 0.25; above that ceiling it is the administrative ratio minus 0.25, multiplied by 200, rounded half up to two decimals. The restatement penalty is a flat 7.5 and applies only in the certified variant, only when the audited program expense ratio came in more than 0.02 below the self-reported one — precisely, when `audited_program_expense_ratio` is strictly less than `program_expense_ratio` minus 0.02 — and in that case `restatement_penalty_applied` is recorded as true. The order of the last three operations is pinned: subtract both penalties from the raw total first, then clamp the result into the range 0 to 100 inclusive, and round half up to two decimals only at the end. `standards_met`, written only by the certified variant, is true when the certified score is at least 70.00.

The divergence between the two variants is not cosmetic. Take an agency reporting a program ratio of 0.7800, an administrative ratio of 0.1900, 4.00 months of reserve, 8 governance items and an `UNQUALIFIED` opinion, whose audit comes back with a program ratio of 0.7400, an administrative ratio of 0.2700 and 3.00 months of reserve. Preliminarily it scores 40 program points (52.00 before the cap), 20.00 reserve points, 20.00 governance points and 0 audit points for a raw 80.00, with no administrative penalty and no restatement penalty, so `preliminary_score` is 80.00 and the agency looks like a comfortable pass. Certified, it scores 36.00 program points, 15.00 reserve points, 20.00 governance points and 10 audit points for a raw 81.00, less a 4.00 administrative penalty and a 7.50 restatement penalty, giving a `certified_score` of 69.50 — which lands the agency on probation.

Certification is performed by a `UnitedFundAdmin` or a `FinanceReviewer`, who supply the three audited figures `audited_program_expense_ratio`, `audited_admin_expense_ratio` and `audited_months_operating_reserve`. The review must still be `PENDING`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`; the review must belong to the agency named earlier in the path, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_AGENCY_MISMATCH`; and the agency must not be `SUSPENDED_MEMBERSHIP`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_MEMBERSHIP_TERMINAL`. On success the audited figures are stored, `review_basis` flips `SELF_REPORTED → AUDITED`, the certified variant runs to write `certified_score`, `restatement_penalty_applied` and `standards_met`, and the review advances `PENDING → CERTIFIED`. That is link 1 of the probation cascade.

Link 2 is the membership fork, selected by the certified score against two inclusive lower bounds. A score of 70.00 or more moves the agency `UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW → GOOD_STANDING`. A score of at least 55.00 but below 70.00 moves it `UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW → PROBATION` and increments `probation_count`. A score below 55.00 moves it `UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW → SUSPENDED_MEMBERSHIP`, which is terminal. In all three cases `latest_standing_score` is written and `open_review_count` is re-derived downward; `latest_standing_score` is always the `certified_score` of that agency's certified reviews taken with the highest `fiscal_year` first and, within a fiscal year, the most recent `created_at` first, and it stays null while no review of the agency is certified. During a re-review the previously certified value is left untouched until the new review certifies. An agency that is still on `PROBATION` when a later review of it is certified follows the same bands from that starting point: a score below 55.00 moves it `PROBATION → SUSPENDED_MEMBERSHIP`, while a score in the probation band holds it at `PROBATION → PROBATION`, increments `probation_count` again, and re-runs links 3 and 4 below.

Link 3 fires on the probation and suspended-membership branches only. Every allocation request belonging to that agency whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW`, and whose cycle posture is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, has its current status copied into `status_before_suspension` and then moves `SUBMITTED → SUSPENDED_REVIEW` or `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW → SUSPENDED_REVIEW` accordingly. Link 4 then re-derives, on each cycle touched, the `suspended_request_count` and the `suspended_request_ratio`, and when that ratio reaches 0.34 the cycle moves `OPEN → RESTRICTED` with `awards_blocked` set to true. The threshold is an inclusive lower bound: a ratio of exactly 0.34 restricts the cycle.

Probation is not permanent. Reinstatement is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or an `AllocationsDirector`, requires a written `justification`, and is legal only from `PROBATION` with `latest_standing_score` at 55.00 or above and `open_review_count` at exactly 0 — a violation of any of those three preconditions is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_MEMBERSHIP_TERMINAL`. Reinstatement moves the agency `PROBATION → GOOD_STANDING` and reverses the cascade exactly: every allocation request of that agency sitting at `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` is restored to its `status_before_suspension` — `SUSPENDED_REVIEW → UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW` where that field holds `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW`, and `SUSPENDED_REVIEW → SUBMITTED` where it holds `SUBMITTED` — and `status_before_suspension` is cleared back to null. Each affected cycle's `suspended_request_count` and `suspended_request_ratio` are then re-derived, and when the recomputed ratio has fallen strictly below 0.34 the cycle moves `RESTRICTED → OPEN` with `awards_blocked` cleared to false. The membership loop `PROBATION → GOOD_STANDING`, re-review, and back to `PROBATION` may therefore be traversed repeatedly, `probation_count` recording each lap.

### 3.7. Allocation Cycles, Volunteer Panels and Composite Scoring

An allocation cycle is the annual distribution of the community impact fund. A `UnitedFundAdmin` or an `AllocationsDirector` creates one with a `cycle_year` between 2020 and 2100 inclusive and unique across all cycles; a `focus_priority_area` — again one of `BASIC_NEEDS`, `EDUCATION`, `HEALTH` or `FINANCIAL_STABILITY` — naming the priority the volunteer panels are told to weight upward this year; an `opening_undesignated_pool` strictly greater than zero with two decimals, being the undesignated money carried over from reconciled campaigns; a `max_award_per_agency` strictly greater than zero with two decimals, the hard per-agency ceiling; and the request window as `request_window_start` and `request_window_end`, valid ISO dates with the end strictly later than the start. The cycle is created in `DRAFT` posture with `available_undesignated_pool` equal to `opening_undesignated_pool`, `awarded_total` at 0.00, `submitted_request_count` and `suspended_request_count` at 0, `suspended_request_ratio` at 0.0000 and `awards_blocked` false.

Volunteers are registered independently of cycles. A `UnitedFundAdmin` or an `AllocationsDirector` records a panelist's `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), `email` (containing an at-sign and globally unique across all panelists) and `expertise_area`, which is the subject-matter lane the volunteer was recruited for and may be `BASIC_NEEDS`, `EDUCATION`, `HEALTH`, `FINANCIAL_STABILITY` or `FINANCE`. Both `submitted_score_count`, the number of scores the volunteer has ever filed, and `site_visit_count`, the number of outcome reports whose site visit the volunteer scheduled, begin at 0. The `id` returned is the value that volunteer supplies as `X-User-ID`.

Seating a volunteer on a cycle creates a panel seat carrying the `cycle_id` of the panel and the `panelist_id` of the volunteer, and is performed by a `UnitedFundAdmin` or an `AllocationsDirector`. Two optional inputs shape the seat. `conflict_declared` defaults to false and records whether the volunteer declared a conflict of interest covering the whole cycle; when true, that volunteer is refused outright when filing any score on any request of the cycle, and their seat does not count toward opening the cycle. `assignment_role` defaults to `SCORER` and may also be `CHAIR`; a `CHAIR` convenes the panel while a `SCORER` only scores, and both may file scores. The seat's `submitted_score_count` counts the scores that volunteer has filed against requests of that particular cycle. A volunteer may hold at most one seat per cycle, and a second seating attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_SCORE`. Seating is refused on a `FROZEN` cycle with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`.

Opening a cycle advances its posture `DRAFT → OPEN`, sets `available_undesignated_pool` to `opening_undesignated_pool`, and is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or an `AllocationsDirector`. It requires at least one seat on the cycle whose `conflict_declared` is false; a cycle with no conflict-free seat cannot open and is refused with `ERR_PANEL_NOT_SEATED`. An opening attempted from any posture other than `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`. Freezing, also available to those two roles, is the deliberate end of a cycle: `OPEN → FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED → FROZEN`, with `awards_blocked` set to true. `FROZEN` is terminal, and it blocks panel seating, request submission, score filing, review finalization and awards alike, each with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`. The middle posture, `RESTRICTED`, is never requested by a client; it arrives through the probation cascade and it blocks awards only — scoring and review finalization remain perfectly legal while a cycle is restricted, and an award attempt against a restricted cycle is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_RESTRICTED`.

Two counters on the cycle drive the restriction. `submitted_request_count` is the number of that cycle's requests that have ever been submitted — every request whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW`, `SUSPENDED_REVIEW`, `PANEL_APPROVED`, `PANEL_DECLINED`, `AWARDED`, `REPORTING` or `CLOSED_SATISFIED`; only a request still in `DRAFT` is excluded. `suspended_request_count` is the number currently at `SUSPENDED_REVIEW`. The `suspended_request_ratio` is the suspended count divided by the submitted count, rounded half up to four decimal places, and exactly 0.0000 when no request of the cycle has ever been submitted.

A member agency's ask is an allocation request. A `UnitedFundAdmin`, the agency's own `AgencyRepresentative`, or an `AllocationsDirector` drafts one against a cycle by supplying the `cycle_id` of the cycle it competes in, the `agency_id` of the requesting agency, a `requested_amount` strictly greater than zero with two decimals, a `priority_area` naming the community priority this particular request serves, and a `narrative_summary` of 1 to 2000 characters that the panel reads before scoring. The request is created in `DRAFT` with `eligible_score_count` at 0 and with `composite_score`, `base_award`, `designation_offset`, `strategic_supplement`, `total_award`, `submitted_at`, `awarded_at`, `outcome_report_due_date` and `status_before_suspension` all null. An agency may hold only one live request per cycle: if it already holds a request on that cycle in any status other than `PANEL_DECLINED`, the draft is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUEST_FOR_CYCLE`, which is what lets a declined agency try again in the same cycle.

Submission moves the request `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or the agency's own `AgencyRepresentative`. Five gates guard it. The cycle must be `OPEN`; `DRAFT` cycles are refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` and `FROZEN` cycles with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`. The agency must be in `GOOD_STANDING` at that moment — an `APPLICANT`, an agency mid-re-review at `UNDER_FINANCIAL_REVIEW`, one on `PROBATION` and one at `SUSPENDED_MEMBERSHIP` are all refused with `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. The agency's `outcome_report_delinquent` flag must be false, or the submission is refused with `ERR_OUTCOME_REPORT_DELINQUENT`. The logical clock's date must fall inside the cycle's half-open request window, at or after `request_window_start` and strictly before `request_window_end`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And the `requested_amount` must not exceed twice the cycle's `max_award_per_agency` — the organization will read an ask of up to double the ceiling but no more — with an excess refused as `ERR_AWARD_EXCEEDS_AGENCY_CAP`. On success, `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the cycle's `submitted_request_count` and `suspended_request_ratio` are re-derived.

Scoring is exclusive to a `VolunteerPanelist`. The volunteer files three integer judgements, each between 0 and 100 inclusive: `priority_alignment_score` for how well the request aligns with community priorities, `outcome_evidence_score` for the strength of the agency's evidence that its program produces outcomes, and `capacity_score` for the organizational capacity to deliver what is proposed. The score row records the `request_id` being scored and takes `panelist_id` from `X-User-ID`. A score may be filed only while the request is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW` and the cycle is `OPEN`. The volunteer must hold a seat on that request's cycle, or the filing is refused with `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_ASSIGNED`; if that seat carries `conflict_declared` true the filing is refused with `ERR_PANELIST_CONFLICT_DECLARED`; and a volunteer may file at most one score per request, a second attempt being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_SCORE`.

Each filed score immediately computes its own `weighted_score`: the priority alignment judgement weighted at 0.40, the outcome evidence judgement at 0.35 and the capacity judgement at 0.25, summed and then rounded half up to two decimal places. That rounding happens per score, before any averaging ever occurs. The score is created with `eligible` true. The first score filed against a request advances it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW` and re-derives `eligible_score_count`; filing also increments the volunteer's `submitted_score_count` and the seat's own `submitted_score_count`.

Closing panel review is performed by a `UnitedFundAdmin` or an `AllocationsDirector`, and is legal while the cycle is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`. The eligible pool is every score row filed against the request whose author holds a seat on that request's cycle with `conflict_declared` false, and its size is written to `eligible_score_count`. An entirely empty pool is its own failure mode, refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SCORES`; this is exactly what a still-`SUBMITTED` request produces, which is why such a request is admitted by the status gate and then rejected here. A non-empty pool of one or two scores fails quorum and is refused with `ERR_PANEL_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, because the panel quorum is three.

Aggregation then branches on the eligible count, not on the scores themselves. When the pool holds five or more scores, a trimmed mean is used: exactly one row holding the highest `weighted_score` and exactly one row holding the lowest are dropped, with ties at either extreme broken by the earliest `created_at` and never by `id`, and each dropped row has its `eligible` flag flipped to false so the trim is visible afterwards. When the pool holds three or four scores, every score counts and a plain mean is used. Either way the base is the sum of the surviving `weighted_score` values divided by the number of survivors, rounded half up to two decimals. The community-priority multiplier is applied next: 1.15 when the request's `priority_area` equals the cycle's `focus_priority_area`, and 1.00 otherwise. The `composite_score` is that base multiplied by the multiplier, capped at 100, and rounded half up to two decimals — cap first, round last. A composite of 72.00 or more advances the request `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW → PANEL_APPROVED`; anything below advances it to the terminal `UNDER_PANEL_REVIEW → PANEL_DECLINED`. Both forks write `composite_score`; the declined fork never touches the cycle's pool.

To make the order concrete: three eligible scores with weighted values of 81.50, 76.50 and 71.50 form a pool of three, so the plain mean applies and the base is 76.50. If the request's priority area matches the cycle's focus, the multiplier of 1.15 gives 87.975, which rounds half up to a `composite_score` of 87.98 — comfortably above 72.00, so the request is approved.

### 3.8. Awarding the Community Impact Fund

Only an `AllocationsDirector` may move money out of the undesignated pool. An award names the `request_id` being funded and carries `expected_allocation_cycle_version` as described in §2.6. The request must be `PANEL_APPROVED`, or the award is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`. The cycle must be `OPEN`: a `FROZEN` cycle is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN` and a `RESTRICTED` one with `ERR_CYCLE_RESTRICTED`. The cycle's `available_undesignated_pool` must be strictly greater than zero, and the assembled award computed below — before its final clamp — must not exceed that available pool; either failure is refused with `ERR_POOL_INSUFFICIENT`. Requests sitting at `PANEL_DECLINED` or `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` never enter the allocation arithmetic at all.

The award is assembled as a seven-step waterfall, and the evaluation order is pinned because the dependency looks circular. Step one applies the hard per-agency ceiling: the eligible request amount is the smaller of the request's `requested_amount` and the cycle's `max_award_per_agency`. Step two lets the panel's judgement scale the ask: the score-scaled figure is the eligible request amount multiplied by the `composite_score` divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimals. Step three bounds that by money actually on hand: `base_award` is the smaller of the score-scaled figure and the cycle's `available_undesignated_pool`.

Step four is the designation-honoring offset, and it is the point where the two halves of this system meet. An agency that donors have already carried through designations should draw less from the undesignated fund, so a rate is applied to `base_award` — and specifically to `base_award`, not to any later figure. The rate is 0.00 when `base_award` is below 50000.00; it is 0.15 when `base_award` is at least 50000.00 and below 150000.00; and it is 0.35 when `base_award` is 150000.00 or more. `designation_offset` is `base_award` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals, and then capped at the agency's `designated_passthrough_ytd` — an agency cannot be offset by more designation income than it actually received.

Step five is the strategic supplement, and its rate is keyed on the net figure that step four just produced, namely `base_award` minus `designation_offset`. That net figure attracts a rate of 0.06 when it is below 50000.00, 0.03 when it is at least 50000.00 and below 120000.00, and 0.01 when it is 120000.00 or more; smaller awards are topped up proportionally more. `strategic_supplement` is the net figure multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals. Step six assembles the unclamped award as the net figure plus the strategic supplement, and this is the figure the pool-sufficiency check tests. Step seven applies the pool clamp last: `total_award` is the smaller of the unclamped award and the cycle's `available_undesignated_pool`. Keying either rate on `total_award`, or clamping before the supplement is computed, yields a different and wrong number.

Worked through: a request for 250000.00 against a cycle whose ceiling is 200000.00 and whose available pool is 500000.00, with a composite score of 87.98 and an agency carrying 92750.00 of designated passthrough, gives 200000.00 at step one; 175960.00 at step two; 175960.00 at step three; a step-four rate of 0.35 producing 61586.00, which stays at 61586.00 because the 92750.00 cap does not bite; a net of 114374.00 at step five attracting the 0.03 rate for a supplement of 3431.22; and a `total_award` of 117805.22, leaving the available pool at 382194.78.

A successful award advances the request `PANEL_APPROVED → AWARDED`, stamps `awarded_at` from the logical clock, and sets `outcome_report_due_date` to the date part of `awarded_at` plus 180 calendar days. It then re-derives the cycle's `awarded_total` as the sum of `total_award` over that cycle's requests whose status is `AWARDED`, `REPORTING` or `CLOSED_SATISFIED`, and `available_undesignated_pool` as `opening_undesignated_pool` minus `awarded_total`. Both the cycle and the request are mutations, so both `version` counters advance. The cycle's invariant holds at all times and with zero drift: `available_undesignated_pool` plus `awarded_total` always equals `opening_undesignated_pool`.

Awards are reversible, but only before the agency reports. Rescission is likewise exclusive to an `AllocationsDirector`, requires a `reason_code`, and moves the request `AWARDED → PANEL_APPROVED`. It is refused with `ERR_AWARD_ALREADY_REPORTED` once any outcome report exists for the request, which is the sole reason a `REPORTING` request is admitted past the status gate at all. A successful rescission sets `base_award`, `designation_offset`, `strategic_supplement`, `total_award`, `awarded_at` and `outcome_report_due_date` all back to null, then re-derives `awarded_total` and `available_undesignated_pool` so that the pool is restored exactly — the same conservation invariant must hold before and after with no residue.

The cycle's overall fund position is available as a computed read that reports, for one cycle, its `cycle_id` and `posture`, the `opening_undesignated_pool`, the `available_undesignated_pool`, the `awarded_total`, a `base_award_total` and a `designation_offset_total` and a `strategic_supplement_total` summing those three components across the cycle's awarded requests, an `awarded_request_count`, plus the `suspended_request_count`, the `suspended_request_ratio` and the `awards_blocked` flag. That flag is true whenever the posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`; it is set by link 4 of the probation cascade and by freezing, and it is cleared only by the reinstatement reversal that returns a cycle `RESTRICTED → OPEN`.

### 3.9. Outcome Reporting, Monitoring Site Visits and Closure

Funding comes with an accounting obligation. A `UnitedFundAdmin` or the funded agency's own `AgencyRepresentative` files an outcome report against an `AWARDED` request, supplying the `reporting_period_end` date closing the reporting period, `persons_served` as a non-negative count of unduplicated individuals, `outcome_metric_name` (1 to 120 characters) naming the measure the agency committed to, `outcome_metric_value` as the non-negative achieved value of that measure, and `funds_expended`, the non-negative award dollars actually spent in the period, at two decimals. The report records the `request_id` it accounts for, is created with `status` `SUBMITTED` and `revision_round_count` at 0, has `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and begins with `site_visit_panelist_id`, `site_visit_window_start`, `site_visit_window_end` and `site_visit_findings` all null. Filing advances the parent request `AWARDED → REPORTING`.

Filing also computes `expenditure_variance_pct`: `funds_expended` minus the request's `total_award`, divided by that `total_award`, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to two decimals — a negative figure when the agency underspent. When the request's `total_award` is null or exactly zero the variance is 0.00 rather than an error.

Delinquency is the enforcement behind the reporting obligation, and it is re-derived whenever a report is filed or accepted, whenever a request is awarded, and whenever the logical clock moves. An agency's `outcome_report_delinquent` flag is true when at least one of its allocation requests is `AWARDED` or `REPORTING`, has a non-null `outcome_report_due_date`, has a due date the logical clock's date is strictly past, and has no outcome report at `ACCEPTED`. A delinquent agency is barred from submitting a new allocation request until it clears.

Monitoring is the volunteer panel's second job, and it is exclusive to a `VolunteerPanelist`. Scheduling a site visit advances the report `SUBMITTED → SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED`, takes `site_visit_panelist_id` from `X-User-ID`, and requires the volunteer to supply `site_visit_window_start` and `site_visit_window_end` as ISO dates. Four checks apply, in the order of §2.10. First, segregation of duties: the acting volunteer must have filed no score at all against the request this report accounts for, and a scorer's attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Second, the volunteer must hold a seat on that request's cycle, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_ASSIGNED`. Third, window sanity: `site_visit_window_start` must be on or after the date part of the report's `submitted_at`, `site_visit_window_end` must be on or after `site_visit_window_start`, and `site_visit_window_end` must be no later than the date part of `submitted_at` plus 90 calendar days; any violation is refused with `ERR_SITE_VISIT_WINDOW_INVALID`. Fourth, spacing: the requested window, which is a closed interval including both endpoints, must be separated by more than 3 calendar days from every other window already held by the same volunteer on a report whose status is `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED`, `SITE_VISIT_COMPLETED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `ACCEPTED`. Two windows collide when the new start falls on or before the existing end plus 3 days **and** the existing start falls on or before the new end plus 3 days; a collision is refused with `ERR_SITE_VISIT_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Scheduling stores the window and increments the volunteer's `site_visit_count`.

Completing the visit advances the report `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED → SITE_VISIT_COMPLETED` and records `site_visit_findings`, the narrative the volunteer brings back. Only the volunteer who scheduled the visit may complete it: `X-User-ID` must equal the report's `site_visit_panelist_id`, and anyone else is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`.

Disposition belongs to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or an `AllocationsDirector`, and there are two ways to dispose of a completed visit. Acceptance advances the report `SITE_VISIT_COMPLETED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and drives the funded request `REPORTING → CLOSED_SATISFIED` while re-deriving the agency's delinquency flag. Requesting a revision instead advances the report `SITE_VISIT_COMPLETED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, carries a `revision_note` explaining what must change, and increments `revision_round_count` by one. The revision loop is capped: once `revision_round_count` has reached 2, a further revision request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Resubmission closes the loop and is available to a `UnitedFundAdmin` or the agency's own `AgencyRepresentative`, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the `agency_id` of the request the report belongs to. It advances the report `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, overwrites `persons_served`, `outcome_metric_value` and `funds_expended` with the corrected figures, re-computes `expenditure_variance_pct` from the new `funds_expended`, and re-stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock. It clears `site_visit_findings`, since the previous visit's narrative no longer describes the corrected report, but it deliberately keeps `site_visit_panelist_id`, `site_visit_window_start` and `site_visit_window_end` so that the historic window remains on the record. The report may therefore lap `SUBMITTED → SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED → SITE_VISIT_COMPLETED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` up to the revision ceiling before it must be accepted.
