# Carpet Mill Fiber Extrusion API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A carpet mill is one continuous material transformation that happens to be run by six different departments who do not naturally trust each other's paperwork. Polymer chip arrives on the receiving dock and is drawn into an extruder; yarn leaves the spinneret at a denier that either matches the specification or does not; the tufter lays that yarn into a backing cloth at a gauge and pile height that must match the style card and the needle bar physically installed on the machine; the dye house either never touches the goods at all — because pigment went into the melt — or takes greige goods and tries to land a color inside a shade band; and the finished rolls are cut against dealer orders where the buyer may have insisted that every yard come out of the same dye lot. Each of those handoffs is where mills lose money today, because each department keeps its own spreadsheet and the arithmetic never reconciles.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record spanning that whole chain, mounted under the `/mill` prefix. The system's job is not merely to store records; it is to hold four things true at once. First, **mass and yardage must conserve** — every kilogram of polymer received sits in exactly one accounting bucket at every instant, and every linear yard of a production run is either on a roll, committed to an allocation, or written off. Second, **compliance must gate commerce** — carpet may not be committed to a buyer unless the style holds an effective flammability certificate, and the technician who ran the test may never be the officer who issues the certificate citing it. Third, **derived numbers must be reproducible** — face weight, delamination strength, CIELAB color difference, per-line pricing and recycled content are all computed by the server from pinned formulas with pinned evaluation orders and pinned rounding, never supplied by the client. Fourth, **credit exposure must never drift** — a cut order that is created and then cancelled must return the buying account to exactly the credit posture it had before.

The intended readers are the engineers building this service. The specification is written from the mill floor inward: the business narrative in §3 defines what each operation means and which conditions guard it, while §2 fixes the cross-cutting contracts — envelope shape, pagination, identity, rounding, stale-read protection, cascade semantics and the order in which requests are admitted. §4 and §5 are indexes back into that narrative, not substitutes for it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent — or present but empty or whitespace-only, which counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The missing `X-User-ID` is reported before the missing `X-User-Role`, and role names are matched case-sensitively. If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `MillPlanner`, `ExtrusionOperator`, `DyeHouseOperator`, `QALabTechnician`, `CertificationOfficer`, `DealerBuyer` and `PlantAdmin`.

- A `MillPlanner` curates the style catalogue, registers tufters, schedules and drives tufting runs, doffs rolls off backed runs, and fulfils cut orders out of roll-good inventory.
- An `ExtrusionOperator` runs the polymer receiving dock, the reclamation line and the spinning extruders.
- A `DyeHouseOperator` formulates piece-dye lots, books and charges vessels, records spectrophotometer readings, and decides approve, re-dye or reject.
- A `QALabTechnician` inspects and dispositions rolls and records wear, stain and flammability tests. This role may never issue the certificates its own tests support.
- A `CertificationOfficer` issues and revokes certificates from lab evidence, and is deliberately segregated from the technician who produced that evidence.
- A `DealerBuyer` is a dealer or builder placing cut orders against its own credit line, scoped strictly to its own account.
- A `PlantAdmin` is the plant superuser and test-harness operator. It may perform every operation available to any of the other six roles — subject to exactly the same segregation-of-duties, state and business rules — and it is the only role permitted to move the logical clock.

Identity binding differs by role, and the distinction matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `DealerBuyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Account` compare the resource's account foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `DealerBuyer` may only read the account whose `account_id` path segment equals its own `X-User-ID`, and may only submit, read or cancel a `CutOrder` whose `account_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. A violation of that scope is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), and the scope check applies to exactly four operations: reading one account, submitting a cut order, reading one cut order, and cancelling a cut order.

When `X-User-Role` is `MillPlanner`, `ExtrusionOperator`, `DyeHouseOperator`, `QALabTechnician`, `CertificationOfficer` or `PlantAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it stamps `tested_by_user_id` on a `ProductTest` from the acting `QALabTechnician`'s `X-User-ID`, and `certified_by_user_id` on a `Certification` from the acting `CertificationOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those two recorded identifiers are the raw material of the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.11.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; concretely it is the literal `req_` followed by sixteen randomly chosen characters drawn from the lowercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three transport-level failures sit outside the §5 catalogue but use this same envelope: a request to a path the API does not route answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (404), a request using a method the path does not admit answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (405), and a request body that is not parseable JSON, or that parses to something other than a JSON object, answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` skips a number of records from the front of the result set; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not parseable as an integer at all. The same 400 answers a filter query parameter whose value lies outside the enumerated set that filter accepts, while a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as if it were absent and narrows nothing. Filters on identifier-valued parameters are matched as exact string equality against the stored field, so an identifier that matches no record simply yields an empty page rather than a 404.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint sorts its results by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts every record matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Record Versioning

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID. Identifiers never carry a type prefix — a roll good's `id` is a bare UUID, not `roll_` followed by a UUID — and clients never supply one. Human-facing codes such as `roll_number`, `run_code` or `order_code` are separate input fields and are never used as the resource identifier.

Alongside `id`, most resources carry three system-managed fields:

- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the record was first persisted. These two audit stamps are the one place the server reads its real wall clock rather than the logical mill clock of §2.10, and they are forced strictly increasing across records, so `created_at` alone is a total order over any set of records and is a sound sole sort key.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the record, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the record. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a fiber batch draws mass and the owning polymer lot's `available_kg`, `reserved_kg` and `status` are recomputed, that polymer lot's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed, even though no client addressed the polymer lot directly. The unit of counting is the request, not the individual write: within one request a record's `version` rises by at most 1 however many recomputation passes revisit it, and a record the request itself created keeps a `version` of 1 however many cascade hops then touch it. A derived-field recomputation that lands on exactly the values the record already held leaves that record's `version` and `updated_at` alone, so a cascade that sweeps a record without changing anything on it is invisible.

`ProductTest` is the single exception. A product test is an immutable laboratory record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `ProductTest` payload entirely, and no operation ever modifies a test once recorded. Every other resource in the system — `Account`, `PolymerLot`, `ReclamationBatch`, `FiberBatch`, `CarpetStyle`, `TuftingMachine`, `DyeVessel`, `ProductionRun`, `DyeLot`, `RollGood`, `Certification`, `CutOrder` and `CutAllocation` — carries the full quartet.

### 2.5. Precision, Rounding and the Prohibition on Intermediate Rounding

Every rounding operation in this system uses the **round-half-up** convention: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. The scale differs by quantity, and each is stated where the quantity is defined, but the summary is:

- Polymer masses in kilograms — `received_kg`, `available_kg`, `reserved_kg`, `consumed_kg`, `scrapped_kg`, `input_kg`, `blend_input_kg`, `extruded_kg`, `depolymerized_kg` — carry 3 decimal places, and no bucket may ever hold a negative figure.
- Linear yardage — `dyed_yards`, `scrapped_yards`, `uncommitted_yards`, `shipped_basis_uncommitted_yards`, `greige_yards`, `initial_yards`, `available_yards`, `requested_yards`, `allocated_yards`, `open_backorder_yards` and an allocation's `yards` — carries 1 decimal place.
- Denier figures `denier_target` and `denier_actual`, machine geometry `min_pile_height_mm`, `max_pile_height_mm`, `needle_bar_width_in`, `pile_height_mm`, `pattern_repeat_mm`, `pile_height_set_mm`, `pattern_repeat_loaded_mm`, `stitch_rate_per_inch`, roll `width_ft`, vessel `capacity_kg`, `intake_lbs`, `contamination_pct`, `melt_flow_index`, `sbr_pct` and `filler_load_pct` carry 1 decimal place.
- Percentages and derived two-place figures — `recycled_content_pct`, `recorded_recycled_content_pct`, `target_face_weight_oz`, `face_weight_oz_per_sy`, `delamination_strength_lbf`, `delta_e` and every CIELAB coordinate — carry 2 decimal places.
- Money is integer cents throughout: `credit_limit_cents`, `credit_used_cents`, `list_price_cents_per_yard`, `reserved_credit_cents`, `line_cents`, `gross_cents`, `freight_cents`, `remnant_credit_cents` and `net_invoice_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the system.
- Oven and integer settings `cure_temp_c`, `cure_seconds`, `changeover_minutes`, `filament_count`, `plies`, `recoat_count` and `redye_count` are plain integers.

Two rules about *when* to round are load-bearing and are repeated in the workflows that depend on them. **Rounding happens once, at the end of a chain.** Where a formula is a product chain or a square root, every intermediate carries full precision and only the final figure is rounded; rounding an intermediate produces a different answer and is incorrect. **Threshold comparisons are made against the rounded figure, not the raw one.** The denier tolerance check, the face-weight tolerance check, the delamination verdict, the shade-band assignment and the recycled-content gate all compare the value as persisted — that is, after rounding — against their threshold. The same discipline applies to numbers arriving from a client: every numeric input is rounded half-up to the scale declared for its field at admission, that rounded value is what the range check of §2.7 rung 4 sees, and that rounded value is what is persisted and echoed back. A `received_kg` of 0.0004 therefore rounds to 0.000 and is rejected as not greater than zero, and a `yards` of 12.34 is admitted, stored and charged as 12.3.

### 2.6. Stale-Read Protection on Dye Lots and Roll Goods

Two write operations in this system supersede a value the caller has already read, and for those two the API requires the caller to prove which version it read. This is optimistic concurrency control, and it is expressed as a mandatory input field rather than a header.

Recording a spectrophotometer reading on a dye lot — the operation that drives a `DyeLot` from `DYEING` to `MEASURED` — requires `expected_dye_lot_version` in the request body. The field is not optional; a request that omits it is malformed, and so is one whose value is not an integer or is an integer below 1 — those are shape failures answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) at rung 4, never with the 412 below. Its value must equal the target `DyeLot`'s current `version`. A second reading on a lot legitimately supersedes the first, overwriting `delta_e` and `shade_band`, so a reading submitted against an outdated version would silently discard a colleague's newer reading and re-band the lot. When the supplied `expected_dye_lot_version` does not equal the lot's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DYE_LOT_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. On success, the lot's `version` increases by 1, which means a caller must re-read before submitting another reading.

Creating a manual allocation line against a named roll — the operation that commits a specific number of yards from one identified `RollGood` to a cut order — requires `expected_roll_good_version` in the request body, likewise non-optional, likewise a 400 at rung 4 when omitted, non-integral or below 1, and likewise carrying the caller's last observed `version` of that `RollGood`. Another allocation, a remnant split, or a QA disposition may have already drawn against the same roll while leaving enough yardage for this request to succeed on its own arithmetic; the stale-version check is the only thing separating a fresh decision from one made against an outdated yardage figure. A mismatch with the roll's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROLL_GOOD_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. A successful manual allocation increases the roll's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint in the system takes a version field. The automatic allocator, the cut, the ship, the cancel, the QA disposition, the tufter setup and every certificate operation are all version-free; supplying a version field to them has no meaning.

### 2.7. Request Admission Order

When a single request could fail several checks, the order in which the server evaluates them is observable and is therefore pinned. Text order in this document is not the specification; the following ladder is. Evaluation stops at the first failing rung.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Account scope.** When the acting role is `DealerBuyer`, `X-User-ID` equals the `Account` the request targets: the order's `account_id` for cut-order paths, and the `account_id` path segment when reading one account. Otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). This rung guards reading one account, submitting a cut order, reading one cut order, and cancelling a cut order. Where the scope target is readable straight off the request the rung genuinely sits here: reading one account compares `X-User-ID` to the `account_id` path segment before that account is looked up, so a `DealerBuyer` naming another buyer's account gets the 403 whether or not that account exists, and submitting a cut order compares `X-User-ID` to the raw `account_id` in the body before rung 4 runs — unless that body field is absent or not a string, in which case there is nothing to compare and the request falls through to the 400 at rung 4. Reading or cancelling one cut order is the exception forced by the data: the scope target is the persisted order's `account_id`, so the order must be resolved first and a `DealerBuyer` naming an unknown order receives `ERR_CUT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` (404) rather than the 403.
4. **Request shape.** Every required field is present, every enum value lies in its declared set, every numeric lies in its declared range, every datetime is ISO 8601 UTC, `limit` lies in the inclusive range 1 through 100 and `offset` is not negative, `scheduled_end_utc` is strictly later than `scheduled_start_utc`, `dye_end_utc` is strictly later than `dye_start_utc`, `blend_input_kg` is greater than zero exactly when `blend_polymer_lot_id` is supplied (supplying one without the other is malformed), and `accounting_basis` is either `MASS_BALANCE` or `PHYSICAL_SEGREGATION` exactly when `cert_type` is `NSF_140`. A certificate's `expires_at_utc` being strictly later than the issuance instant — that is, strictly later than the current logical clock — is also a rung-4 rule. Failures here are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A field supplied with an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: required fields then fail this rung and optional fields take their declared default. Body fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, and required fields are checked in the order they are listed for the operation, so the first missing or malformed one decides the message. One rule deliberately does **not** live on this rung: the `solution_color_code` presence rule is evaluated at rung 16, so that `ERR_DYE_METHOD_COLOR_MISMATCH` remains reachable rather than being masked by a 400.
5. **Path existence, left to right.** Every resource named by a path segment must exist, validated in the order the segments appear in the URL. The first segment that resolves to nothing decides the error: `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLYMER_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECLAMATION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIBER_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STYLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MACHINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VESSEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DYE_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROLL_GOOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CUT_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. A request for a sub-resource under a parent that does not exist reports the parent's not-found error and never looks for the child.
6. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key carried in the request body resolves to an existing row — `account_id`, `style_id`, `machine_id`, `fiber_batch_id`, `polymer_lot_id`, `blend_polymer_lot_id`, `production_run_id`, `vessel_id`, `dye_lot_id`, `roll_good_id`, `source_roll_good_id`, and every element of `source_test_ids`. The applicable 404 from the same catalogue is returned.
7. **Natural-key uniqueness.** The supplied code is not already in use: `account_code`, a polymer lot's `lot_code`, a reclamation batch's `batch_code`, a fiber batch's `batch_code`, `style_code`, `machine_code`, `vessel_code`, `run_code`, a dye lot's `lot_code`, `roll_number` and `order_code`. A collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). This rung applies to every creation endpoint.
8. **State legality.** The target resource is in a status from which the requested transition is declared. The generic failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); where a more specific state error exists it is used instead — `ERR_FIBER_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_BACKED`, `ERR_DYE_LOT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_ROLL_ALREADY_DEPLETED`, `ERR_STYLE_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_STYLE_NOT_ORDERABLE`, `ERR_MACHINE_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_VESSEL_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_REDYE_LIMIT_REACHED` or `ERR_RECOAT_LIMIT_REACHED`, all 409.
9. **Buying-account posture.** A new cut order may only be submitted for an `Account` in status `ACTIVE`; an account in `CREDIT_HOLD` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD` and one in `CLOSED` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`, both 409.
10. **Stale-version check.** The supplied `expected_dye_lot_version` or `expected_roll_good_version` equals the parent's current `version`, as described in §2.6 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_DYE_LOT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ROLL_GOOD_VERSION` (412). This rung guards exactly the dye-lot measurement operation and the manual-allocation-line operation.
11. **Flammability certificate gate.** The ordered style holds a `Certification` whose `cert_type` is `DOC_FF_1_70`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at_utc` is strictly later than the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_FLAMMABILITY_CERT_REQUIRED` (422). This rung guards both the automatic allocation operation and the manual allocation-line operation.
12. **Segregation of duties.** For a certificate issuance, the issuing officer's `X-User-ID` differs from `tested_by_user_id` on every test cited in `source_test_ids` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409).
13. **Evidence sufficiency.** The cited tests support the certificate being issued, and every cited test belongs to the same style — otherwise `ERR_CERT_EVIDENCE_MISSING` (422).
14. **Recycled-content sufficiency.** For an `NSF_140` certificate, the style's recycled content under the requested `accounting_basis` reaches the minimum — otherwise `ERR_RECYCLED_CONTENT_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
15. **Tufter setup coherence.** The five setup checks described in §3.4, evaluated in their own fixed internal order, yielding `ERR_GAUGE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_MACHINE_GAUGE_INCOMPATIBLE`, `ERR_PILE_HEIGHT_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_PILE_HEIGHT_SPEC_DEVIATION` or `ERR_PATTERN_REPEAT_MISMATCH`, all 422. This rung guards the tufter setup operation.
16. **Dyeing-route coherence.** The `solution_color_code` presence rule, the style-to-fiber dye-method coupling, the rule that a dye lot may only be formulated against a piece-dyed style's run, and the rule that a roll doffed off a piece-dyed run carries a dye lot while a roll doffed off a solution-dyed run does not — yielding `ERR_DYE_METHOD_COLOR_MISMATCH`, `ERR_DYE_METHOD_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SOLUTION_DYED_NO_DYE_LOT` or `ERR_DYE_LOT_REQUIRED`, all 422. This rung guards five operations: style creation, fiber-batch creation, production-run creation, dye-lot creation, and doffing a roll off a run.
17. **Extrusion feedstock and yield.** The melt-flow floor, the blend-compatibility rule, the drawn-mass availability rule and the denier release tolerance — yielding `ERR_MELT_FLOW_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_POLYMER_BLEND_INCOMPATIBLE`, `ERR_POLYMER_MASS_UNAVAILABLE` or `ERR_DENIER_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, all 422. This rung guards three operations: fiber-batch creation, the extrude operation, and the fiber-batch disposition operation.
18. **Face-weight tolerance** at greige completion — `ERR_FACE_WEIGHT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (422).
19. **Shade-band disposition legality** on a measured dye lot — `ERR_SHADE_BAND_NOT_APPROVABLE` or `ERR_SHADE_BAND_NOT_REDYEABLE` (422).
20. **Manual-line shade continuity** against the lines already on the order — `ERR_SHADE_BAND_MISMATCH` (422).
21. **Reclaim processability** — `ERR_RECLAIM_CONTAMINATION_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_ROLL_NOT_RECLAIMABLE` (422), on the depolymerization operation.
22. **Machine window exclusivity** at run scheduling — `ERR_MACHINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
23. **Vessel capacity** at dye-lot formulation — `ERR_VESSEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
24. **Booked window opened.** The logical clock has reached `scheduled_start_utc` for a tufter setup, or `dye_start_utc` for charging a lot into its vessel — otherwise `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` or `ERR_DYE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (409).
25. **Style orderability.** A style must be in `ACTIVE` or `RUNOUT` for order creation and for allocation, a `RUNOUT` style refuses `order_type` of `CUSTOM`, and a production run may only be created against a style in `ACTIVE` — yielding `ERR_STYLE_NOT_ORDERABLE`, `ERR_STYLE_RUNOUT_NO_CUSTOM` or `ERR_STYLE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). This rung guards three operations: cut-order creation, automatic allocation, and production-run creation.
26. **Dye-lot availability for allocation.** A `SINGLE_LOT` order may not be closed out of a `DyeLot` in status `EXHAUSTED`, and no allocation may draw from a roll whose dye lot is `REJECTED` — `ERR_DYE_LOT_EXHAUSTED` (409). This rung guards both the automatic and the manual allocation operations.
27. **Manual line feasibility.** The requested `yards` does not exceed the roll's `available_yards` (`ERR_ROLL_YARDAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, 422) and the roll is `PASSED` on quality and `AVAILABLE` on inventory (`ERR_ROLL_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`, 422).
28. **Non-empty candidate set** for automatic allocation — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ROLL_GOODS` (409).
29. **Custom-order full coverage.** For `order_type` of `CUSTOM`, candidate yardage must fully cover `requested_yards` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_YARDAGE` (422). A custom order is never partially allocated and never backordered.
30. **Credit admission** at order creation — `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).

### 2.8. Material Ledgers and Conservation Invariants

Three quantities in this system are ledgers rather than fields, and the server is responsible for keeping each one balanced at all times.

**Polymer mass, in kilograms.** For every `PolymerLot`, the identity `received_kg` equals `available_kg` plus `reserved_kg` plus `consumed_kg` plus `scrapped_kg` must hold at every instant. Mass never appears or disappears; it only moves between the four buckets, and each movement is described with the transition that causes it in §3.2. No bucket may become negative, and each is carried to 3 decimal places. A lot's `status` is a pure function of one bucket: it reads `DEPLETED` whenever `available_kg` is exactly zero and `AVAILABLE` whenever `available_kg` is greater than zero. No operator action sets that status directly — the server recomputes it after every fiber-batch creation, extrusion and disposition, and both directions of the flip are live, so a depleted lot whose mass is returned becomes available again.

**Linear yards of a production run's goods.** For any run, the sum of `available_yards` across its non-scrapped rolls, plus the sum of `yards` across allocations on those rolls in status `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED`, plus the sum of `initial_yards` across its scrapped rolls, must always equal the total `initial_yards` doffed off that run counting only its non-remnant rolls. A remnant split moves residual yardage from parent to child one for one, so remnant children neither add nor remove yardage from the total. Committing yards debits a roll's `available_yards` and credits the allocation's `yards` by the identical figure; cancelling the order releases the allocation and credits exactly that figure back. The forward action and its compensating action must leave the run's total yardage bit-identical, on figures rounded to 1 decimal place.

**Cents of credit exposure on an account.** An `Account`'s `credit_used_cents` must always equal the sum of `reserved_credit_cents` across its orders in status `SUBMITTED`, `ALLOCATED`, `BACKORDERED` or `CUT`, plus the sum of `net_invoice_cents` across its orders in status `SHIPPED`. Cancelled orders contribute nothing and carry a `reserved_credit_cents` of 0 after cancellation. This is a monetary quantity, structurally unlike the linear-yard quantity above: submitting an order debits credit and cancelling it credits the identical amount back, so a submit-then-cancel pair must return both `credit_used_cents` and the account's `status` to the values they held before the submission.

### 2.9. Cascading Reconciliation Across the Mill Graph

The mill is a graph, not a tree. A single client action routinely mutates records the client never named, and those secondary mutations may in turn trigger tertiary ones. Every such reconciliation is immediate and synchronous with the originating request — there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this system — and every record the server touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if a client had modified it.

Three properties of cascades are worth stating up front, because they recur in §3. First, **cascades are transitive**: discontinuing a style cancels custom orders, which releases credit, which can lift an account off credit hold, and all three hops complete inside the one request. Second, **cascades have compensating legs**: whenever the system reserves, commits or holds something, there is a named reverse transition that returns it — a scrapped queued fiber batch returns polymer mass to `available_kg`, an aborted run returns its creel to the pool, a cancelled order returns yardage to its rolls, and a re-issued certificate releases held runs. Third, **derived state is never stale**: a `status`, a `shade_band`, a `replenishment_flag` or an `inventory_status` that the specification defines as a function of other data is recomputed as part of the same request that changed that data, never on read and never on a schedule.

Two entities carry more than one independent status dimension, and confusing them is the most common implementation error in this domain. A `RollGood` carries `qa_status` and `inventory_status` as genuinely orthogonal dimensions: a roll placed on quality hold stays `IN_PROCESS` on the inventory dimension and never becomes `AVAILABLE`, while the inventory dimension moves between `AVAILABLE` and `COMMITTED` without the quality dimension changing at all. A `DyeLot` similarly separates its lifecycle `status` from the two independently persisted uncommitted-yardage figures described in §3.6.

### 2.10. The Logical Mill Clock

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any request moves it, every time-dependent comparison reads that instant, and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `PlantAdmin` — and only a `PlantAdmin` — may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The clock is not required to advance monotonically: any parseable instant is accepted, earlier ones included, and setting it back only means fewer certificates are expired for subsequent comparisons — the lapse sweep below never un-lapses a certificate that has already left `ACTIVE`. The response reports the resulting `current_utc` together with three arrays recording what the move set in motion: `lapsed_certification_ids`, `suspended_style_ids` and `held_production_run_ids`, the first ordered by the certificates' `created_at` and the other two by the order in which the cascade reached them. Instants are stored and echoed as the exact strings they arrived as: `current_utc` repeats the `to_iso8601` text verbatim, and `scheduled_start_utc`, `scheduled_end_utc`, `dye_start_utc`, `dye_end_utc` and `expires_at_utc` all read back byte-for-byte as the client supplied them rather than renormalized to three fractional digits. The two instants the server stamps itself, `tested_at_utc` and `issued_at_utc`, likewise carry the clock's current instant in whatever textual form was last handed to the clock endpoint — `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` exactly, before any move.

Every temporal comparison in the specification resolves against this clock: whether a tufter may be loaded (`scheduled_start_utc` reached), whether a lot may be charged into its vessel (`dye_start_utc` reached), whether a certificate is still effective (`expires_at_utc` strictly later than the clock), and the `tested_at_utc` and `issued_at_utc` stamps the server writes onto tests and certificates.

A certificate is **effective** when its `status` is `ACTIVE` *and* its `expires_at_utc` is strictly later than the current logical clock. That two-part definition is the pivot of the compliance machinery in §3.8: advancing the clock to or past a certificate's `expires_at_utc` moves that certificate to `LAPSED`, and losing the last effective flammability certificate suspends the style, which in turn holds the style's scheduled runs.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties in the Test-and-Certify Chain

The mill's accreditation regime requires that laboratory evidence and the certificate that cites it come from different hands. The system enforces this structurally rather than by policy: a `ProductTest` permanently records the `X-User-ID` of the `QALabTechnician` who ran it in `tested_by_user_id`, and a `Certification` permanently records the `X-User-ID` of the `CertificationOfficer` who issued it in `certified_by_user_id`. At issuance, the officer's own identifier is compared against `tested_by_user_id` on every test named in `source_test_ids`; if it matches even one of them, issuance is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP status 409. Because a `PlantAdmin` may act in either capacity, the same comparison applies to a `PlantAdmin` issuing a certificate that cites a test that same `X-User-ID` recorded.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

This section follows the material through the plant, from the receiving dock to the shipping dock, and then closes the loop back to the dock through the reclamation line. Rules are stated where the material meets them.

### 3.1. The Receiving Dock and the Reclamation Line

Feedstock enters the system as a **polymer lot**. An `ExtrusionOperator` receives one by supplying a `lot_code` that must be unique across all polymer lots, the `polymer_type` being received — one of `NYLON_6`, `NYLON_66`, `PET` or `POLYPROPYLENE` — the dry mass `received_kg` in kilograms, which must be greater than zero and is carried to 3 decimal places, and the `melt_flow_index` in grams per ten minutes, which must be greater than zero and is carried to 1 decimal place. Three further inputs are optional and describe the lot's environmental provenance: `recycled_content_pct`, the declared post-consumer plus post-industrial recycled fraction as a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive at 2 decimal places, defaulting to 0; `segregated`, a boolean defaulting to false that is true only when the lot is physically segregated single-origin reclaim; and `reclaim_source`, one of `VIRGIN`, `POST_CONSUMER` or `POST_INDUSTRIAL`, defaulting to `VIRGIN`. Receiving a lot whose `lot_code` already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

On receipt the four mass buckets are initialized so that the conservation identity of §2.8 holds trivially: `available_kg` is set equal to `received_kg`, while `reserved_kg`, `consumed_kg` and `scrapped_kg` are each set to 0, and the lot's `status` is `AVAILABLE`. Lots can be listed and filtered by `polymer_type`, by `status` and by `reclaim_source`, alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

The plant also runs a post-consumer carpet reclamation program, and this is where the material graph closes on itself. An `ExtrusionOperator` logs an intake as a **reclamation batch**, supplying a `batch_code` unique across reclamation batches, the gross weight `intake_lbs` in pounds (greater than zero, 1 decimal place), the identified `face_fiber_type` of the reclaimed carpet — again one of `NYLON_6`, `NYLON_66`, `PET` or `POLYPROPYLENE` — and the measured non-fiber `contamination_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive at 1 decimal place. Optionally the intake names a `source_roll_good_id`: this is set when the material being reclaimed is one of the mill's own remnants or a returned roll rather than third-party post-consumer carpet, and it is left null otherwise. A newly logged batch has `status` of `RECEIVED`, `depolymerized_kg` of 0, and a null `output_polymer_lot_id`. Batches may be listed filtered by `status`, by `face_fiber_type`, by the roll they reclaimed via `source_roll_good_id`, and by the lot they produced via `output_polymer_lot_id`.

Depolymerizing an intake drives it from `RECEIVED` to `DEPOLYMERIZED` and is guarded by two conditions. Contamination is a gate, not a multiplier: the batch's `contamination_pct` must be at most 12.0 percent, and a dirtier intake is refused with `ERR_RECLAIM_CONTAMINATION_EXCEEDED`. Additionally, when `source_roll_good_id` is not null, that roll must already be finished with — its `inventory_status` must be either `DEPLETED` or `SCRAPPED` — because the mill will not grind up saleable inventory; a roll in any other inventory state is refused with `ERR_ROLL_NOT_RECLAIMABLE`.

The yield calculation converts pounds of dirty carpet into kilograms of reclaimed polymer. Take `intake_lbs`, multiply it by the pound-to-kilogram factor 0.45359237, then multiply by the face-fiber fraction for the batch's `face_fiber_type` — 0.42 for `NYLON_66`, 0.40 for `NYLON_6`, 0.38 for `PET` and 0.35 for `POLYPROPYLENE` — and then multiply by the depolymerization yield of 0.82. The multiplications are performed in that written order at full precision and the result is rounded half-up to 3 decimal places exactly once, at the end; that rounded figure is stored as `depolymerized_kg`.

Depolymerization then mints a brand-new polymer lot, and this is the back-edge that returns reclaimed material to the feedstock inventory. The minted lot takes its `polymer_type` from the batch's `face_fiber_type`, its `received_kg` from the batch's `depolymerized_kg`, a `recycled_content_pct` of exactly 100.00, a `segregated` flag of true, a `reclaim_source` of `POST_CONSUMER`, and a `status` of `AVAILABLE` with the usual bucket initialization. Two fields the operator never supplies are synthesized: the minted lot's `lot_code` is the literal `RECLAIM-` prefixed to the batch's own `batch_code`, and its `melt_flow_index` is set to exactly 12.0, the extrusion floor, so reclaimed polymer is always just barely extrudable. The batch's `output_polymer_lot_id` is set to the new lot's `id`, and the batch's own `status` becomes `DEPOLYMERIZED`, which is terminal; the endpoint's response body is the updated reclamation batch, not the lot it minted, which the caller reaches through `output_polymer_lot_id`. Depolymerizing a batch that is not in `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rung 8, before either processability gate is examined. The combination of `segregated` being true and `recycled_content_pct` being exactly 100.00 is precisely what makes a reclaimed lot count under the stricter of the two recycled-content accounting bases described in §3.8.

### 3.2. Extrusion: Drawing Polymer into Yarn at Denier

A **fiber batch** is one pass through a spinning extruder. An `ExtrusionOperator` queues one by supplying a `batch_code` unique across fiber batches, a `polymer_lot_id` naming the primary polymer stream, the `input_kg` to be drawn from that lot (greater than zero, 3 decimal places), the specified total `denier_target` of the spun yarn bundle (greater than zero, 1 decimal place), the `filament_count` leaving the spinneret (at least 1), and a `dye_method` of either `SOLUTION_DYED` or `PIECE_DYED_READY` recording whether pigment will be metered into the melt or the yarn will be left greige for later piece dyeing. When the batch is solution dyed it must carry a `solution_color_code` naming the pigment metered into the melt; when it is `PIECE_DYED_READY` that field must be null. Supplying a colour code without the solution-dyed method, or omitting it when the method is solution dyed, is refused with `ERR_DYE_METHOD_COLOR_MISMATCH`.

A batch may blend two polymer streams into the same extruder, which is how the mill lifts recycled content without compromising the primary polymer. Supplying `blend_polymer_lot_id` names the second lot, and `blend_input_kg` records the mass metered from it; that mass defaults to 0 and must be strictly greater than 0 exactly when the blend lot is present. Supplying one of the pair without the other is malformed. The two lots must be chemically compatible: the blend lot's `polymer_type` must equal the primary lot's `polymer_type`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_POLYMER_BLEND_INCOMPATIBLE`. Neither draw may exceed what its lot actually has: if `input_kg` exceeds the primary lot's `available_kg`, or `blend_input_kg` exceeds the blend lot's `available_kg`, the request is refused with `ERR_POLYMER_MASS_UNAVAILABLE`.

Queuing a batch reserves rather than consumes. On creation the batch's `status` is `QUEUED`, and for each source lot the drawn mass is subtracted from that lot's `available_kg` and added to its `reserved_kg`, leaving the conservation identity intact. A lot whose `available_kg` reaches exactly zero as a result transitions `AVAILABLE` to `DEPLETED`. The batch's `throughput_g_per_min`, `spin_speed_m_per_min`, `denier_actual` and `extruded_kg` are all null at this point; they are not creation inputs.

Spinning the batch advances it from `QUEUED` to `SPUN` and is the operation that records what the extruder actually did: it carries `throughput_g_per_min`, the extruder mass throughput in grams per minute, and `spin_speed_m_per_min`, the take-up speed in metres per minute, both of which must be greater than zero. Before spinning, the primary lot must be extrudable: its `melt_flow_index` must be at least 12.0, and a lot below that floor is refused with `ERR_MELT_FLOW_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Two figures are computed. The actual denier is back-calculated from what the machine did rather than from what was specified: multiply `throughput_g_per_min` by 9000.0, divide that product by the product of `spin_speed_m_per_min` and `filament_count`, and round the quotient half-up to 1 decimal place; the result is persisted as `denier_actual`. The spun yarn mass is the total drawn mass — `input_kg` plus `blend_input_kg` — multiplied by the spinning yield of 0.965, rounded half-up to 3 decimal places, and persisted as `extruded_kg`. Spinning also moves mass one bucket to the right on each source lot: the drawn mass is subtracted from that lot's `reserved_kg` and added to its `consumed_kg`, after which each lot's `available_kg` and `status` are recomputed.

A spun batch is then dispositioned, with the request carrying a `disposition` of either `RELEASE` or `SCRAP`. Releasing a batch requires it to be `SPUN` and requires the denier to be in tolerance: take the absolute difference between `denier_actual` and `denier_target`, divide it by `denier_target`, and require the result to be at most 0.03 — that is, the actual denier must land within three percent of target. The comparison is made on the persisted `denier_actual` as rounded to 1 decimal place, never on the raw quotient from the spinning calculation. Yarn outside that band is refused with `ERR_DENIER_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` and the batch stays `SPUN`. A successful release sets the batch's `status` to `RELEASED`, which makes it creelable by a production run.

Scrapping is available from two states, and the two behave differently on the ledger — this asymmetry is the heart of the polymer mass conservation rule. Scrapping a batch that is still `QUEUED` returns the mass: for each source lot, the drawn mass is subtracted from `reserved_kg` and added back to `available_kg`, and each lot's `status` is recomputed, so a lot that had gone `DEPLETED` returns to `AVAILABLE` when its `available_kg` becomes greater than zero. Scrapping a batch that has already been `SPUN` charges the mass off instead: for each source lot, the drawn mass is subtracted from `consumed_kg` and added to `scrapped_kg`, and each lot's `available_kg` and `status` are recomputed — but `available_kg` does not change, because spun mass is gone. In both cases the batch's `status` becomes `SCRAPPED`, which is terminal, as is `CONSUMED`.

The remaining two legs of a fiber batch's life are driven by the run that creels it, never by the extrusion operator. When a production run is scheduled against the batch, the batch moves from `RELEASED` to `ALLOCATED`; the run must find the batch in `RELEASED`, and a batch in any other status is refused with `ERR_FIBER_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED`. When that run completes its greige goods, the batch moves from `ALLOCATED` to `CONSUMED`. When that run is instead aborted — whether by an operator or by the style-discontinuation fan-out — the batch moves from `ALLOCATED` back to `RELEASED`, returning the creel to the pool; this is the compensating leg of the run-abort cascade. Batches may be listed filtered by either source lot, using `polymer_lot_id` or `blend_polymer_lot_id`, and by `status` and `dye_method`.

### 3.3. The Style Catalogue: Specification, Activation, Runout and Discontinuation

A **carpet style** is the mill's product specification, and everything downstream is checked against it. A `MillPlanner` drafts one by supplying a `style_code` unique across styles; a `line_type` of either `RUNNING_LINE` for stocked goods or `CUSTOM` for made-to-order; a `gauge_code` naming the needle spacing the style must be tufted at, one of `G_1_8`, `G_1_10` or `G_5_64`; a `pile_construction` of either `CUT` or `LOOP`; the specified finished `pile_height_mm` (greater than zero, 1 decimal place); the `pattern_repeat_mm` that must be loaded on the tufter's pattern controller (greater than zero, 1 decimal place); the `plies` twisted per tuft (at least 1); the specified `target_face_weight_oz` in ounces per square yard (greater than zero, 2 decimal places); a `dye_method` of either `SOLUTION_DYED` or `PIECE_DYED`; and the `list_price_cents_per_yard`, an integer greater than zero, which is the basis of every charge the mill raises against this style. A solution-dyed style must carry a `solution_color_code` and a piece-dyed style must leave it null, with `ERR_DYE_METHOD_COLOR_MISMATCH` the penalty for either error. A new style begins with `status` of `DRAFT`, `open_backorder_yards` of 0 and `replenishment_flag` of `NONE`.

Activation moves the style from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, making it both schedulable and orderable, and it re-checks the colour-code coherence rule at that moment so a draft cannot be activated into an incoherent state; activating a style in any status other than `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rung 8, before that re-check. Only from `ACTIVE` may a run be scheduled against the style — a run scheduled against a style in any other status is refused with `ERR_STYLE_NOT_ACTIVE` — and only a style in `ACTIVE` or `RUNOUT` accepts cut orders, with `ERR_STYLE_NOT_ORDERABLE` refusing orders and allocations against a style in `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` or `DISCONTINUED`.

Between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` runs an unbounded compliance loop that no operator drives directly. When a style loses its last effective flammability certificate — either because a `CertificationOfficer` revokes it or because the logical clock advances past its `expires_at_utc` — and no `Certification` with a `cert_type` of `DOC_FF_1_70`, a `status` of `ACTIVE` and an `expires_at_utc` strictly later than the clock remains for that style, the style transitions `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`. That suspension carries one cascade hop: every production run of the style whose status is `SCHEDULED` transitions `SCHEDULED` to `HELD`, and the machine window each held run occupies stays booked so no other run can take the slot. When a fresh effective `DOC_FF_1_70` certificate is subsequently issued for the style, the style transitions `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, and the mirrored cascade hop releases every run of the style in `HELD` back to `SCHEDULED`. Because certificates may be revoked and re-issued indefinitely, this loop has no bound.

Discontinuation is the planner's announcement that a style is being run out, and it may be triggered from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, optionally carrying a free-text `reason`. In both cases the style's `status` becomes `RUNOUT` and the side-effect chain is identical. `RUNOUT` means no new production run may be created for the style and no cut order with `order_type` of `CUSTOM` may be submitted against it — the latter refused with `ERR_STYLE_RUNOUT_NO_CUSTOM` — while existing stock is allowed to sell through. Three transitive cascade hops then fire, each caused by the one before it. The first hop cancels every cut order of this style whose `order_type` is `CUSTOM` and whose status is `SUBMITTED`, moving each from `SUBMITTED` to `CANCELLED`. The second hop, caused by the first, drives the account credit ledger: each such cancellation decrements the buying account's `credit_used_cents` by that order's `reserved_credit_cents` and then sets the order's own `reserved_credit_cents` to 0. The third hop, caused by the second, re-evaluates account posture: any account whose `credit_used_cents` now sits strictly below its `credit_limit_cents` and whose `status` is `CREDIT_HOLD` transitions `CREDIT_HOLD` to `ACTIVE`. Separately from that transitive chain, discontinuation fans out over production: every production run of the style whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` transitions to `ABORTED`, and each abort returns its creel by moving its fiber batch from `ALLOCATED` back to `RELEASED`. Once the cancellations are in, the style's own `open_backorder_yards` and `replenishment_flag` are recomputed as well, since the cancelled custom orders no longer count toward the backorder pool, and the response body is the discontinued style itself. Discontinuing a style in `DRAFT`, `RUNOUT` or `DISCONTINUED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The final style transition is a system evaluation rather than an operator action. After every cut-order ship and every cut-order cancellation, the server checks each `RUNOUT` style: when no roll good produced by any run of the style has an `inventory_status` of `AVAILABLE` or `COMMITTED`, and no cut order of the style has a status of `SUBMITTED`, `ALLOCATED`, `BACKORDERED` or `CUT`, the style transitions `RUNOUT` to `DISCONTINUED`, which is terminal. Styles may be read individually or listed filtered by `status`, `line_type` and `dye_method`.

Tufters are registered separately from styles but are checked against them. A `MillPlanner` registers a **tufting machine** with a `machine_code` unique across machines, the `native_gauge_code` physically installed on the needle bar (one of `G_1_8`, `G_1_10` or `G_5_64`), the `min_pile_height_mm` and `max_pile_height_mm` bounding the needle stroke (both at 1 decimal place, with the maximum strictly greater than the minimum), the `changeover_minutes` setup buffer that must precede every run on the tufter (a non-negative integer), and the usable `needle_bar_width_in` in inches (greater than zero, 1 decimal place). A machine's `status` is an operator-maintained availability posture of either `IDLE` or `MAINTENANCE`, defaulting to `IDLE`; a tufter in `MAINTENANCE` cannot be loaded, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_MACHINE_UNAVAILABLE`. Machines may be listed filtered by `status` and `native_gauge_code`.

### 3.4. Tufting: Booking the Machine, Loading the Setup and Closing Out Greige

A **production run** binds a style, a tufter and a creel of yarn to a window of time. A `MillPlanner` schedules one by supplying a `run_code` unique across runs, the `style_id` being tufted, the `machine_id` reserved for the run, the `fiber_batch_id` creeled for it, and the nominal `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, where the end must be strictly later than the start. The style must be in `ACTIVE`; the fiber batch must be in `RELEASED` and is moved to `ALLOCATED` by the scheduling itself. Scheduling also enforces the dye-method coupling between the style and the yarn: a style whose `dye_method` is `SOLUTION_DYED` requires a fiber batch whose own `dye_method` is `SOLUTION_DYED` and whose `solution_color_code` equals the style's `solution_color_code`, while a style whose `dye_method` is `PIECE_DYED` requires a fiber batch whose `dye_method` is `PIECE_DYED_READY`. A mismatch on either arm is refused with `ERR_DYE_METHOD_MISMATCH`. A newly scheduled run has a `status` of `SCHEDULED` and a `recoat_count` of 0, and its setup, greige, backing and computed fields are all null.

Tufters are occupied exclusively, and the occupancy is wider than the run itself because the needle bar has to be changed over. A run's occupancy window begins at its `scheduled_start_utc` minus the tufter's `changeover_minutes` and ends at its `scheduled_end_utc`; the start of that window is inclusive and the end is **exclusive**. A new run conflicts when its occupancy window intersects the occupancy window of any existing run on the same `machine_id` whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `TUFTING`, `GREIGE`, `BACKED` or `BACKING_FAILED`; runs in `ABORTED` or `RELEASED` have released their windows and never contend. The buffer is applied identically to the new run and to every existing run when their windows are compared, and instants are compared at minute resolution — each endpoint is truncated to its whole minute, seconds and fractions discarded, before the buffer is subtracted and the intersection tested. Because the end of the window is exclusive, a new run whose effective start — that is, its `scheduled_start_utc` less the changeover buffer — falls exactly on an existing run's `scheduled_end_utc` is admissible, while one minute earlier is a conflict. A conflict is refused with `ERR_MACHINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Loading the setup is the operation that starts tufting, advancing the run from `SCHEDULED` to `TUFTING`, and it carries the four values the operator actually dialled in: `gauge_loaded_code`, the gauge actually installed, again one of `G_1_8`, `G_1_10` or `G_5_64`; `pile_height_set_mm`, the pile height dialled in (greater than zero, 1 decimal place); `pattern_repeat_loaded_mm`, the repeat length loaded into the pattern controller (greater than zero, 1 decimal place); and `stitch_rate_per_inch`, the stitches per inch dialled in (greater than zero, 1 decimal place). The style must still be in `ACTIVE` at this moment — a run that was held because its style was suspended cannot be loaded, and the refusal is `ERR_STYLE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The tufter must be in `IDLE`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_MACHINE_UNAVAILABLE`, and the creeled fiber batch must be in `ALLOCATED`. The logical clock must have reached the run's `scheduled_start_utc`; loading a run whose window has not opened is refused with `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`.

Five setup checks then run, in this fixed order, and the first to fail decides the error. The loaded gauge must equal the style's `gauge_code`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_GAUGE_MISMATCH`. The loaded gauge must also equal the tufter's `native_gauge_code`, because a needle bar cannot be re-gauged on the fly, failing which the refusal is `ERR_MACHINE_GAUGE_INCOMPATIBLE`. The dialled `pile_height_set_mm` must lie within the tufter's inclusive envelope — at least the machine's `min_pile_height_mm` and at most its `max_pile_height_mm` — failing which the refusal is `ERR_PILE_HEIGHT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The dialled pile height must also honour the style specification: the absolute difference between `pile_height_set_mm` and the style's `pile_height_mm` may be at most 0.5 mm, and a larger deviation is refused with `ERR_PILE_HEIGHT_SPEC_DEVIATION`. Finally the loaded `pattern_repeat_loaded_mm` must equal the style's `pattern_repeat_mm` exactly, failing which the refusal is `ERR_PATTERN_REPEAT_MISMATCH`. When all five pass, the four setup values are persisted on the run and the run enters `TUFTING`.

Closing out tufting advances the run from `TUFTING` to `GREIGE` and carries a single input, `greige_yards`, the linear yards of greige goods produced, which must be greater than zero and is carried to 1 decimal place. This is the moment the mill discovers whether it actually built the carpet it specified, because face weight is computed here from six inputs spread across three records, and the computation must be performed in exactly this order.

Begin with needles per inch, looked up from the style's `gauge_code`: 8.0 needles per inch for `G_1_8`, 10.0 for `G_1_10` and 12.8 for `G_5_64`. Multiply that needles-per-inch figure by the run's `stitch_rate_per_inch` and then by 1296, the number of square inches in a square yard, to obtain the number of tufts in a square yard. Next determine the yarn allowance per tuft, which depends on the style's `pile_construction`: 0.0600 inches when the construction is `CUT` and 0.1200 inches when it is `LOOP`. The yarn consumed per tuft, in inches, is twice the run's `pile_height_set_mm` divided by 25.4 millimetres per inch — that is, the pile height converted to inches and doubled because the yarn goes down and comes back up — plus that allowance. Multiply the tufts per square yard by the yarn inches per tuft and divide by 36 to convert inches to yards, giving yarn yards per square yard. Multiply that by the creeled batch's `denier_actual`, then by the style's `plies`, then by 0.00010160 grams per yard per unit of denier, giving grams per square yard. Finally divide by 28.349523 grams per ounce and round half-up to 2 decimal places; that single rounding, applied only at the very end, produces `face_weight_oz_per_sy`. Rounding any intermediate — the tufts figure, the yarn-yards figure or the grams figure — is forbidden and yields the wrong answer. Note that `greige_yards` does not enter this formula at all: face weight is a per-square-yard property, independent of how much of it was made.

The tolerance gate is evaluated on the rounded `face_weight_oz_per_sy`: the absolute difference between it and the style's `target_face_weight_oz` may be at most 1.60 ounces per square yard. Goods outside that band are refused with `ERR_FACE_WEIGHT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` and the run stays in `TUFTING`. On success the run's `status` becomes `GREIGE`, and one cascade fires: the creeled fiber batch transitions `ALLOCATED` to `CONSUMED`.

A run that has not yet started tufting may be aborted, optionally with a `reason`. Abort is permitted from `SCHEDULED` and from `HELD`, and the side effects are identical from either state: the run's `status` becomes `ABORTED`, which is terminal, its machine window is freed for subsequent overlap checks, and its fiber batch transitions `ALLOCATED` back to `RELEASED`. The same transition is also driven by the style-discontinuation fan-out described in §3.3, in which case the actor is the system rather than a planner. Runs may be read individually or listed filtered by `style_id`, `machine_id`, `fiber_batch_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Backing, Latex Formulation, Curing and the Recoat Loop

Greige goods are not saleable carpet until a latex compound has been applied and cured and a secondary backing laminated on. Applying backing carries five inputs, all recorded on the run: `sbr_pct`, the styrene-butadiene latex solids share of the compound, which must lie between 30 and 70 inclusive at 1 decimal place; `filler_load_pct`, the calcium carbonate filler load, between 0 and 60 inclusive at 1 decimal place; `cure_temp_c`, the oven temperature in Celsius as an integer between 100 and 200 inclusive; `cure_seconds`, the oven dwell time as an integer of at least 1; and `secondary_backing_type`, one of `ACTION_BAC`, `WOVEN_POLYPROPYLENE` or `CUSHION`. The run must be in `GREIGE`. All five inputs are persisted whatever the outcome.

Peel strength is then predicted from the formulation and the cure, using only those five payload values and no cross-entity inputs. Start with a base strength: multiply `sbr_pct` by 0.0500 pounds-force per percentage point, and subtract from it `filler_load_pct` multiplied by 0.0200 pounds-force per percentage point — filler is cheap but it weakens the bond. Next compute a cure index as the lesser of 1.00 and the quotient of `cure_seconds` divided by the reference cure time of 120.0 seconds, so that dwelling longer than two minutes earns no further credit while dwelling less is penalised proportionally. Then determine a temperature factor from `cure_temp_c` by taking the first matching band: 1.00 when the temperature is at least 150 degrees, otherwise 0.88 when it is at least 135 degrees, otherwise 0.70. Then take the secondary-backing factor from `secondary_backing_type`: 1.00 for `ACTION_BAC`, 0.95 for `WOVEN_POLYPROPYLENE` and 1.10 for `CUSHION`. Multiply the base strength by the cure index, by the temperature factor and by the backing factor, carrying full precision throughout, and round the product half-up to 2 decimal places exactly once; that figure is `delamination_strength_lbf`.

The verdict follows from that rounded figure compared against the minimum acceptable peel strength of 2.50 pounds-force. When `delamination_strength_lbf` is at least 2.50, the run transitions `GREIGE` to `BACKED`, which makes it eligible for roll-good creation. When it is strictly below 2.50, the run transitions `GREIGE` to `BACKING_FAILED` instead; the formulation inputs and the computed strength are still persisted, so the operator can see exactly which lever was short.

A failed backing may be stripped and re-attempted once. Recoating requires the run to be in `BACKING_FAILED` and requires its `recoat_count` to be strictly less than the recoat limit of 1 — a run that has already been recoated once is refused with `ERR_RECOAT_LIMIT_REACHED`. A permitted recoat increments `recoat_count` by 1, clears `sbr_pct`, `filler_load_pct`, `cure_temp_c`, `cure_seconds`, `secondary_backing_type` and `delamination_strength_lbf` all back to null, and returns the run's `status` to `GREIGE`, from which backing may be applied afresh with a different formulation. `GREIGE` to `BACKING_FAILED` to `GREIGE` is therefore a bounded revision loop: at most one traversal per run.

The last transition out of a run is driven by quality inspection rather than by production. When the first roll doffed off a `BACKED` run passes inspection — that is, when at least one roll good of the run reaches a `qa_status` of `PASSED` — the run transitions `BACKED` to `RELEASED`, which is terminal. `RELEASED` does not close the run to further doffing: additional roll goods may still be created against a run in `RELEASED`.

### 3.6. The Dye House: Vessel Booking, Colour Matching, Shade Banding and Re-dye

Piece-dyed goods pass through the dye house; solution-dyed goods never do, because their colour went into the melt at extrusion. Dye machines are registered as **dye vessels** by a `MillPlanner` or a `DyeHouseOperator`, each with a `vessel_code` unique across vessels, a `vessel_type` of either `BECK` or `JET`, a `capacity_kg` giving the maximum simultaneous goods mass the vessel can hold (greater than zero, 1 decimal place), and a `status` of either `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE`, defaulting to `AVAILABLE`. Vessels may be listed filtered by `status` and `vessel_type`.

A **dye lot** is a booking of a vessel window against a specific run's greige goods. A `DyeHouseOperator` formulates one by supplying a `lot_code` unique across dye lots, the `production_run_id` whose greige goods are being dyed, the `vessel_id` being booked, the target `color_code`, the CIELAB coordinates of the colour standard as `target_lab_l`, `target_lab_a` and `target_lab_b` (each at 2 decimal places), the goods mass `lot_kg` loaded into the vessel (greater than zero, 1 decimal place), the booked window as `dye_start_utc` and `dye_end_utc` where the end must be strictly later than the start, and `dyed_yards`, the linear yards of greige goods entering the lot (greater than zero, 1 decimal place). The named run's style must have a `dye_method` of `PIECE_DYED` — formulating a lot against a solution-dyed style's run is refused with `ERR_DYE_METHOD_MISMATCH` — and the run must have reached backing, meaning its status must be `GREIGE`, `BACKED`, `BACKING_FAILED` or `RELEASED`. A new lot begins with `status` of `FORMULATED`, `redye_count` of 0, `scrapped_yards` of 0, a null `parent_dye_lot_id`, null `measured_lab_l`, `measured_lab_a`, `measured_lab_b`, `delta_e` and `shade_band`, and both uncommitted-yardage figures — `uncommitted_yards` and `shipped_basis_uncommitted_yards` — initialized equal to `dyed_yards`.

Unlike a tufter, a vessel is shared rather than exclusive: several lots may occupy the same window provided the mass loaded at every instant fits inside `capacity_kg`. A lot's occupancy window runs from its `dye_start_utc` to its `dye_end_utc` with **both ends inclusive** — the opposite adjacency rule from the tufter, and a deliberate difference. To test a new booking, the server assembles a set of probe instants consisting of the new lot's `dye_start_utc`, plus the `dye_start_utc` of every existing lot on the same vessel that falls inside the new lot's closed window, plus the `dye_end_utc` of every existing lot on the same vessel that falls inside the new lot's closed window. At each probe instant it computes the load as the new lot's `lot_kg` plus the sum of `lot_kg` over every existing lot on that vessel whose `status` is `FORMULATED` or `DYEING` and whose own closed window contains that instant; lots in `MEASURED`, `APPROVED`, `REDYE_QUEUED`, `EXHAUSTED` or `REJECTED` have left the vessel and never contend. If the load at any probe instant is strictly greater than the vessel's `capacity_kg`, the booking is refused with `ERR_VESSEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a load exactly equal to capacity is admissible. Because both ends are inclusive, a lot whose `dye_start_utc` equals another lot's `dye_end_utc` does contend for capacity. Masses are summed at 1 decimal place and instants compared at minute resolution.

Charging the lot into its booked vessel moves it into `DYEING`, and it may be charged from either `FORMULATED` or `REDYE_QUEUED`. In both cases the vessel must be in `AVAILABLE` — a vessel in `MAINTENANCE` yields `ERR_VESSEL_UNAVAILABLE` — and the logical clock must have reached the lot's `dye_start_utc`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_DYE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. From `FORMULATED` the only effect is that `status` becomes `DYEING`. From `REDYE_QUEUED` the charge additionally increments `redye_count` by 1 and clears `measured_lab_l`, `measured_lab_a`, `measured_lab_b`, `delta_e` and `shade_band` all back to null, so the second bath is measured from scratch; this is the re-dye leg of the revision loop.

Recording the spectrophotometer reading advances the lot from `DYEING` to `MEASURED`. The request carries the three measured CIELAB coordinates `measured_lab_l`, `measured_lab_a` and `measured_lab_b`, each at 2 decimal places, together with the mandatory `expected_dye_lot_version` described in §2.6; a value not equal to the lot's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_DYE_LOT_VERSION` at HTTP 412. The colour difference is the unweighted CIE76 distance: take the difference between `measured_lab_l` and `target_lab_l`, the difference between `measured_lab_a` and `target_lab_a`, and the difference between `measured_lab_b` and `target_lab_b`; square each of the three differences; sum the three squares; take the square root of that sum; and round the result half-up to 2 decimal places exactly once, after the square root. That figure is persisted as `delta_e`.

The shade band is assigned immediately afterwards from the **rounded** `delta_e`, never from the raw square root, by taking the first matching band: `A` when `delta_e` is at most 0.80, otherwise `B` when it is at most 1.50, otherwise `C` when it is at most 2.50, otherwise `OUT`. All three comparisons are inclusive, so a `delta_e` of exactly 0.80 is band `A`, exactly 1.50 is band `B` and exactly 2.50 is band `C`. Recording the measurement also increments the lot's `version` by 1, which means a caller who wishes to re-measure must re-read the lot first.

A measured lot is then dispositioned, with the request carrying a `disposition` of `APPROVE`, `REDYE` or `REJECT`, and the assigned band decides which of the three is legal. `APPROVE` requires the lot to be in `MEASURED` and its `shade_band` to be `A`, `B` or `C`; a lot banded `OUT` cannot be approved and is refused with `ERR_SHADE_BAND_NOT_APPROVABLE`. Approval sets `status` to `APPROVED`, which is what permits roll goods to be doffed against the lot, and recomputes both uncommitted-yardage figures.

`REDYE` requires the lot to be in `MEASURED` and its `shade_band` to be `C` or `OUT` — a lot that already landed in band `A` or `B` has nothing to gain from a second bath and is refused with `ERR_SHADE_BAND_NOT_REDYEABLE` — and requires `redye_count` to be strictly less than the maximum of 2 completed re-dye cycles, failing which the refusal is `ERR_REDYE_LIMIT_REACHED`. Note that band `C` is deliberately legal for both `APPROVE` and `REDYE`: it is the operator's judgement call whether to sell a marginal match or run it again. A permitted re-dye sets `status` to `REDYE_QUEUED` and, when `parent_dye_lot_id` is still null, sets `parent_dye_lot_id` to this lot's own `id`, recording the head of the re-dye chain. `MEASURED` to `REDYE_QUEUED` to `DYEING` to `MEASURED` is thus a genuine revision loop, bounded at two completed cycles.

`REJECT` is always legal from `MEASURED` and equally legal from `APPROVED`, and it is the most consequential operation in the dye house because rejecting a lot after rolls have been doffed and committed unwinds five levels of downstream commitment. Rejection sets the lot's `status` to `REJECTED`, which is terminal, and then fires five cascade hops, each caused by the one before it. The first hop scraps the goods: every roll good whose dye lot is this lot and whose `inventory_status` is not already `SCRAPPED` has its `qa_status` set to `FAILED`, its `inventory_status` set to `SCRAPPED` and its `available_yards` forced to 0, and for each such roll the lot's `scrapped_yards` increases by that roll's scrap charge, defined as the roll's `initial_yards` less the yards already shipped off that roll. Charging `initial_yards` less shipped yards — rather than the roll's `available_yards` — is what makes the physical yardage leave the lot exactly once, because merely reserved yardage would otherwise be undercharged. The second hop, caused by the first, voids commitments: every allocation line whose roll is one of those newly scrapped rolls and whose status is `RESERVED` transitions `RESERVED` to `VOIDED`. The third hop, caused by the second, reopens orders: every cut order referenced by a voided line recomputes its `allocated_yards`, and each such order whose status is `ALLOCATED` and whose `allocated_yards` now falls strictly below its `requested_yards` transitions `ALLOCATED` to `BACKORDERED`. The fourth hop, caused by the third, recomputes each affected style's `open_backorder_yards`. The fifth hop, caused by the fourth, raises the alarm: each such style whose `open_backorder_yards` reaches or exceeds 250 yards sets its `replenishment_flag` to `REPLENISH_REQUIRED`. Rejection finally recomputes both of the lot's uncommitted-yardage figures.

Those two figures are the same quantity measured on two different bases, and both are persisted side by side so the divergence between them is directly observable. Both start from the same base: the lot's `dyed_yards` less its `scrapped_yards`. From that base, `uncommitted_yards` subtracts the sum of `yards` over allocation lines drawn from rolls of this lot whose status is `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED`, so a merely reserved commitment already removes the yardage from the sellable pool. `shipped_basis_uncommitted_yards` subtracts only the sum of `yards` over lines whose status is `SHIPPED`, so reserved and cut-but-unshipped yardage still counts as sellable. In both sums, allocation lines drawn from rolls whose `inventory_status` is `SCRAPPED` are excluded, because that yardage has already been charged to `scrapped_yards`. Each figure is rounded half-up to 1 decimal place. For identical data, `shipped_basis_uncommitted_yards` is always greater than or equal to `uncommitted_yards`, and the gap between them equals exactly the reserved-plus-cut yardage. Both are recomputed on every allocation, cut, ship, cancel and roll-good scrap touching a roll of the lot, and on approval of the lot.

Only the commitment-aware figure decides exhaustion. When a lot in `APPROVED` has an `uncommitted_yards` of zero or less after any such recomputation, it transitions `APPROVED` to `EXHAUSTED`, which is terminal, and from then on the lot can no longer back a `SINGLE_LOT` allocation. The shipped-only figure is a settlement and reporting view; it never drives a transition. Dye lots may be read individually or listed filtered by `production_run_id`, `vessel_id`, `parent_dye_lot_id`, `status` and `shade_band`.

### 3.7. Roll Goods: Doffing, Quality Inspection, and the Two Inventory Dimensions

Finished carpet is doffed off a backed run as a **roll good**. A `MillPlanner` doffs one by supplying a `roll_number` unique across rolls, the roll `width_ft` in feet (greater than zero, 1 decimal place) and the `initial_yards` on the roll at creation (greater than zero, 1 decimal place). The run must be in `BACKED` or `RELEASED`; doffing off a run in any other status is refused with `ERR_RUN_NOT_BACKED`. Whether the roll carries a `dye_lot_id` is determined entirely by the style's route: a roll doffed off a piece-dyed run must name a `dye_lot_id`, and omitting it is refused with `ERR_DYE_LOT_REQUIRED`, while a roll doffed off a solution-dyed run must omit it, and supplying it is refused with `ERR_SOLUTION_DYED_NO_DYE_LOT`.

Doffing snapshots the colour situation onto the roll. A piece-dyed roll copies its `shade_band` from the named dye lot at the moment of creation, whatever that band currently reads and whatever status the lot currently holds — doffing itself never inspects the lot's `status`, so a lot that has not yet been measured hands the roll a null band and a lot banded `OUT` hands it `OUT`. A solution-dyed roll takes a `shade_band` of `SOLUTION`. In practice a roll's band is one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `SOLUTION`, because the lot must reach `APPROVED` before that roll can be released by QA — the `ERR_DYE_LOT_NOT_APPROVED` refusal lives on the QA release, not on the doff — and an `OUT` lot can never be approved; a roll carrying a null or `OUT` band therefore never becomes allocatable. The roll also records the `production_run_id` that produced it, begins with a null `parent_roll_id` and an `is_remnant` flag of false, and has its `available_yards` initialized equal to `initial_yards`. The `is_remnant` flag is derived, not supplied: it is true exactly when `parent_roll_id` is not null.

Every roll carries two orthogonal status dimensions. Its `qa_status` starts at `PENDING` and moves among `PENDING`, `PASSED`, `HELD` and `FAILED`. Its `inventory_status` starts at `IN_PROCESS` and moves among `IN_PROCESS`, `AVAILABLE`, `COMMITTED`, `DEPLETED` and `SCRAPPED`. These dimensions are independent, and the point of separating them is that a roll held for quality review must never appear in inventory.

A `QALabTechnician` inspects the roll and records a `disposition` of `RELEASE`, `HOLD` or `SCRAP`, and every disposition increments the roll's `version` by 1. `RELEASE` is permitted when the roll's `qa_status` is `PENDING` or `HELD`, requires the producing run's status to be `BACKED` or `RELEASED` (otherwise `ERR_RUN_NOT_BACKED`), and, for a roll from a piece-dyed style, requires the roll's dye lot to be in `APPROVED` (otherwise `ERR_DYE_LOT_NOT_APPROVED`). Release sets `qa_status` to `PASSED` and, on the independent inventory dimension, moves `inventory_status` from `IN_PROCESS` to `AVAILABLE`. It also cascades upward: when this is the first roll of the run to reach `PASSED`, the run transitions `BACKED` to `RELEASED`. The preconditions and the effects are identical whether the roll was released out of `PENDING` or out of `HELD`.

`HOLD` is permitted only when the roll's `qa_status` is `PENDING`. It sets `qa_status` to `HELD` and leaves `inventory_status` completely untouched at `IN_PROCESS` — a held roll never becomes `AVAILABLE`, which is exactly the independence the two dimensions are designed to express. A held roll may later be released, rejoining the path above.

`SCRAP` is permitted from a `qa_status` of `PENDING`, `HELD` or `PASSED`, and requires the roll's `inventory_status` not to be `DEPLETED`; a roll that has already been fully cut, or already scrapped, is refused with `ERR_ROLL_ALREADY_DEPLETED`. Scrapping sets `qa_status` to `FAILED` and `inventory_status` to `SCRAPPED`, and forces `available_yards` to 0. When the roll has a `dye_lot_id`, the owning lot's `scrapped_yards` increases by that roll's `initial_yards` less the yards already shipped off the roll — again, using `available_yards` alone would undercharge a roll whose yardage is merely reserved. Scrapping then cascades: every allocation line on this roll whose status is `RESERVED` transitions `RESERVED` to `VOIDED`. The identical `qa_status` and `inventory_status` outcome is reached when the system, rather than a technician, scraps the roll as hop one of the dye-lot rejection cascade in §3.6.

A roll's `available_yards` is a ledger figure, never a client input. It is zero whenever `inventory_status` is `SCRAPPED`; otherwise it is the roll's `initial_yards`, less the sum of `yards` over allocation lines on that roll whose status is `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED`, less any residual this roll has already had split off to remnant children, rounded half-up to 1 decimal place and clamped so it can never be negative. Lines in `RELEASED` or `VOIDED` are excluded from that sum, which is precisely why cancelling an order returns yardage to the roll. The figure is recomputed after every allocation-line creation, cut, ship, release, void, roll scrap and remnant split.

The inventory dimension is likewise recomputed from that ledger after every change, in this order of precedence: `SCRAPPED` is sticky and is set only by a scrap or the rejection cascade; `DEPLETED` is sticky and is set only by the cut that zeroes the roll; otherwise a roll whose `qa_status` is `PASSED` reads `COMMITTED` when its `available_yards` is exactly 0 and at least one line on it is `RESERVED`, and reads `AVAILABLE` when its `available_yards` is greater than 0; and a roll whose `qa_status` is `PENDING` or `HELD` reads `IN_PROCESS`. Both directions of the reservation flip are live: an allocation that drives `available_yards` to exactly zero moves a passed roll `AVAILABLE` to `COMMITTED`, taking it out of the candidate pool, and the release or voiding of lines that returns yardage moves it `COMMITTED` back to `AVAILABLE`. That compensating leg is the reason the yardage conservation invariant of §2.8 survives cancellation. A roll reaches `DEPLETED` — terminal on the inventory dimension — when every line on it is in `CUT`, `SHIPPED`, `RELEASED` or `VOIDED` and its `available_yards` is zero after the cut, counting any residual split off as a remnant child. Rolls may be read individually or listed filtered by `production_run_id`, `dye_lot_id`, `parent_roll_id`, `qa_status`, `inventory_status` and `shade_band`.

### 3.8. Product Testing, Certification and Recycled-Content Accounting

Physical specimens are cut from finished rolls and tested. A `QALabTechnician` records a **product test** by supplying the `style_id` under test, the `roll_good_id` the specimen was cut from, the `test_type` performed, and the raw instrument reading `result_value`, which must be greater than zero and carries 1 decimal place. Three standardized tests are recognized, and `result_value` means something different in each: `WEAR_TARR` records a TARR appearance-retention rating on a scale of 1.0 to 5.0, `STAIN_AATCC175` records an AATCC red-dye stain-resistance rating on a scale of 1.0 to 10.0, and `FLAMMABILITY_PILL` records the char radius in millimetres from the methenamine pill test. The server stamps `tested_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `tested_at_utc` from the logical clock, and, as noted in §2.4, a product test is immutable: it carries `id` and `created_at` but neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

The `verdict` is computed by the server, never supplied, and the direction of the comparison depends on the test — higher is better for two of the three, lower is better for the third. A `WEAR_TARR` test yields `PASS` when its `result_value` is at least 3.0. A `STAIN_AATCC175` test yields `PASS` when its `result_value` is at least 8.0. A `FLAMMABILITY_PILL` test yields `PASS` when its `result_value` is at most 25.4 millimetres of char radius, because on this test the reading is a defect measurement. All three comparisons are inclusive, and any reading that fails its comparison yields `FAIL`. Nothing else influences the verdict: the roll's shade band and the style's status are irrelevant to it. Tests may be read individually or listed filtered by `style_id`, `roll_good_id`, `test_type` and `verdict`.

A **certification** is issued by a `CertificationOfficer` against a style, citing tests as evidence. The request supplies the `style_id` being certified, the `cert_type` being issued — one of `DOC_FF_1_70` for flammability, `NSF_140` for sustainable-carpet assessment, or `GREEN_LABEL_PLUS` for indoor air quality — the array `source_test_ids` naming the product tests cited as evidence, and the `expires_at_utc` instant, which must be strictly later than the issuance instant. An optional `accounting_basis` declares the recycled-content accounting model and takes one of `MASS_BALANCE`, `PHYSICAL_SEGREGATION` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, defaulting to `NOT_APPLICABLE`; it must be either `MASS_BALANCE` or `PHYSICAL_SEGREGATION` exactly when `cert_type` is `NSF_140`, and supplying a basis for a non-`NSF_140` certificate or omitting it for an `NSF_140` certificate is malformed. The server stamps `certified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `issued_at_utc` from the logical clock, and sets `status` to `ACTIVE`.

Issuance runs three substantive checks in the order given in §2.7. The segregation-of-duties check of §2.11 comes first: the officer's own `X-User-ID` must differ from `tested_by_user_id` on every cited test, and a match yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 409. The evidence check comes next, and what counts as sufficient evidence depends on the certificate. A `DOC_FF_1_70` certificate requires at least one `FLAMMABILITY_PILL` test on the style with a `verdict` of `PASS`. A `GREEN_LABEL_PLUS` certificate requires at least one `STAIN_AATCC175` test with a `verdict` of `PASS` and at least one `WEAR_TARR` test with a `verdict` of `PASS`, both on the style. In addition, every cited test's own `style_id` must equal the certificate's `style_id`. A failure of any part of this check yields `ERR_CERT_EVIDENCE_MISSING` at HTTP 422. The `source_test_ids` array must be non-empty for `DOC_FF_1_70` and for `GREEN_LABEL_PLUS`; for `NSF_140` it may legitimately be empty, because that certificate rests on mass accounting rather than on bench tests. The field itself is required in every case, so an `NSF_140` application supplies an explicit empty array — omitting the field altogether is a rung-4 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Within this check the same-style test is examined first, ahead of the type-specific evidence requirement, though both arms answer with the same error.

Recycled content is the third check and applies only to `NSF_140`. It is computed from the polymer that physically went into the style's goods, so the qualifying mass set is assembled by walking the style's production runs whose status is `GREIGE`, `BACKED` or `RELEASED` — runs in `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `TUFTING` or `ABORTED` contribute nothing at all — taking each such run's fiber batch, deduplicating those batches by their `id` so a batch creeled by two runs counts once, and emitting from each batch up to two mass contributions: the primary polymer lot paired with the batch's `input_kg`, and, only when `blend_polymer_lot_id` is set, the blend polymer lot paired with the batch's `blend_input_kg`. The total qualifying mass is the sum of the kilogram figures across those contributions.

The recycled mass credited from that same set is where the two accounting bases diverge, on identical inputs. Under `MASS_BALANCE` — the chain-of-custody model — each contribution credits its kilogram figure multiplied by its lot's `recycled_content_pct` divided by 100, so a 2000 kg contribution from a lot declared at 60.00 percent recycled credits 1200 kg of recycled mass. Under `PHYSICAL_SEGREGATION` — the stricter model — a contribution credits its full kilogram figure only when its lot's `segregated` flag is true **and** its lot's `recycled_content_pct` is exactly 100.00, and credits nothing otherwise; that same 2000 kg blended contribution therefore credits 0 kg. The percentage is then the credited recycled mass multiplied by 100 and divided by the total qualifying mass, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places exactly once; when the total qualifying mass is zero the percentage is 0.00 rather than an error. The gate compares that rounded percentage against the minimum of 25.00 percent: a percentage strictly below it is refused with `ERR_RECYCLED_CONTENT_INSUFFICIENT`. Because the two bases can straddle the threshold on the same data, an `NSF_140` application can pass under one basis and fail under the other. On a successful `NSF_140` issuance the percentage computed under the requested `accounting_basis` is persisted on the certificate as `recorded_recycled_content_pct`; for other certificate types that field stays null.

The same computation is also exposed as a read-only diagnostic on a style, which reports `mass_balance_pct` and `physical_segregation_pct` — the percentage under each basis, each at 2 decimal places — together with `qualifying_kg`, the total qualifying mass rounded half-up to 3 decimal places, and `qualifying_fiber_batch_ids`, the deduplicated list of fiber batches that contributed, ordered by the `created_at` of the run that first creeled each batch. Those four keys are the whole of the diagnostic's `data` object; it is not a persisted resource and carries no `id` or audit fields.

Issuing a certificate has two further consequences. Any prior certificate for the same style with the same `cert_type` and a `status` of `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal — a style holds at most one live certificate per type. And when the style was in `SUSPENDED` and the new certificate is an effective `DOC_FF_1_70`, the style transitions `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` and its `HELD` runs are released back to `SCHEDULED`, as described in §3.3.

Certificates leave `ACTIVE` by three doors. A `CertificationOfficer` may revoke one, optionally supplying a `reason`, which sets `status` to `REVOKED`, terminal; only an `ACTIVE` certificate may be revoked, and revoking one already `LAPSED`, `REVOKED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Advancing the logical clock to or past a certificate's `expires_at_utc` sets `status` to `LAPSED`, terminal, and the clock response lists every certificate so lapsed in `lapsed_certification_ids`. And issuance of a newer certificate of the same type supersedes the older one, as above. Both revocation and lapse carry the same compliance cascade: when the affected style is left with no effective `DOC_FF_1_70` certificate, the style transitions `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` and its `SCHEDULED` runs are held — the clock response reports these in `suspended_style_ids` and `held_production_run_ids` respectively. Certificates may be read individually or listed filtered by `style_id`, `cert_type` and `status`.

### 3.9. Accounts, Cut Orders, Allocation, Cutting and Settlement

Commerce begins with an **account**. A `MillPlanner` opens one with an `account_code` unique across accounts and 3 to 32 characters long, an `account_type` of `DEALER`, `BUILDER` or `COMMERCIAL` describing the commercial channel, and a `credit_limit_cents` giving the maximum outstanding credit in cents, which may not be negative. A new account starts with `credit_used_cents` of 0 and `status` of `ACTIVE`.

An account's `credit_used_cents` is a ledger, computed exactly as stated in §2.8 and never supplied by a client, and its `status` is derived from that ledger: the account reads `CREDIT_HOLD` when `credit_used_cents` is greater than or equal to `credit_limit_cents` and `ACTIVE` when it is strictly below, unless an operator has closed it. Because the comparison is inclusive, an account whose usage lands exactly on its limit is held. Both directions are live and both are recomputed after every cut-order creation, ship and cancellation, including the cancellations produced by the style-discontinuation cascade.

An account may be amended, supplying a new `credit_limit_cents`, a new `status`, or both. Amending the limit re-evaluates the credit posture immediately: raising the limit can flip an account `CREDIT_HOLD` to `ACTIVE`, and lowering it can flip an account `ACTIVE` to `CREDIT_HOLD`. Setting `status` to `CLOSED` is permitted from `ACTIVE` and from `CREDIT_HOLD`, and is terminal, but only when the account holds no cut order in status `SUBMITTED`, `ALLOCATED`, `BACKORDERED` or `CUT`; an attempt to close an account with live orders is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_HAS_OPEN_ORDERS`. `CLOSED` is the only status an amendment can actually impose: a supplied `status` of `ACTIVE` or `CREDIT_HOLD` is a legal enum value and is accepted, but it is then discarded and the posture re-derived from the ledger, because that posture is never a client's to choose. Because `CLOSED` is terminal, an amendment of any kind against an already-closed account is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and that check precedes both the open-orders check and the limit change. Every amendment increments the account's `version` by 1. Accounts may be listed filtered by `account_type` and `status`.

A **cut order** is a request for yardage. It is submitted by a `DealerBuyer` for its own account, or by a `MillPlanner` on a buyer's behalf, supplying an `order_code` unique across orders, the `account_id` being billed, the `style_id` ordered, an `order_type` of either `RUNNING_LINE` for stock draw or `CUSTOM` for a made-to-order run, the `requested_yards` (greater than zero, 1 decimal place), and a `shade_tolerance` of `SINGLE_LOT`, `BAND_MATCH` or `ANY` declaring how much shade continuity the buyer requires across the rolls that fill the order. The style must be in `ACTIVE` or `RUNOUT`, and a `RUNOUT` style refuses an `order_type` of `CUSTOM`. The buying account must be in `ACTIVE`: an account in `CREDIT_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD` and one in `CLOSED` with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`.

Submission reserves credit before it accepts the order. The reservation is the order's `requested_yards` multiplied by the style's `list_price_cents_per_yard`, rounded half-up to whole cents, and stored as `reserved_credit_cents`. The admission gate is evaluated **before** that reservation is written: the account's current `credit_used_cents` plus the new `reserved_credit_cents` must be at most the account's `credit_limit_cents`. The gate is inclusive, so an order that lands the account exactly on its limit is admissible — and, by the posture rule above, immediately puts the account into `CREDIT_HOLD`. An order that would take the account strictly past its limit is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and nothing is written. An admitted order begins with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `allocated_yards` of 0, and `gross_cents`, `freight_cents`, `remnant_credit_cents` and `net_invoice_cents` all 0. Because a `SUBMITTED` order already counts toward the style's unfilled demand, submission also recomputes the ordered style's `open_backorder_yards` and `replenishment_flag` before the order is returned. Orders may be read individually or listed filtered by `account_id`, `style_id`, `status` and `order_type`.

Filling an order means creating **cut allocation** lines, each of which records a `cut_order_id`, the `roll_good_id` the yards are drawn from, the `yards` committed on that line (greater than zero, 1 decimal place), a `shade_band` snapshot copied from the roll at reservation, a `from_remnant` flag copied from the sourced roll's `is_remnant` at reservation, a computed `line_cents`, and a `status` that begins at `RESERVED` and moves among `RESERVED`, `CUT`, `SHIPPED`, `RELEASED` and `VOIDED`. An order's `allocated_yards` is at all times the sum of `yards` over its lines in status `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED`. Lines may be read individually or listed filtered by `cut_order_id`, `roll_good_id` and `status`.

Automatic allocation is the primary path and is driven entirely by a deterministic selector, with no client inputs at all. Before it runs, two compliance gates apply: the style must still be orderable, and the style must hold an effective `DOC_FF_1_70` certificate — one whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at_utc` is strictly later than the logical clock — because carpet may not be committed to a buyer without an effective flammability certificate. A style lacking one is refused with `ERR_FLAMMABILITY_CERT_REQUIRED`. The same certificate gate applies to a manual allocation line.

The selector first builds a candidate set. A roll is a candidate when all of the following hold: the roll's producing run is a run of the order's style; the roll's `qa_status` is `PASSED`; its `inventory_status` is `AVAILABLE`; its `available_yards` is greater than zero; its `shade_band` is not `OUT`; and either the roll has no `dye_lot_id` or that dye lot's `status` is `APPROVED` or `EXHAUSTED`. Rolls whose `inventory_status` is `IN_PROCESS`, `COMMITTED`, `DEPLETED` or `SCRAPPED`, and rolls whose `qa_status` is anything other than `PASSED`, are never candidates. Ineligibility is silent on this automatic path: a roll the selector rejects for any of those reasons is simply left out of the candidate set, and the automatic selector never raises `ERR_DYE_LOT_NOT_APPROVED` — its only lot-status refusal is `ERR_DYE_LOT_EXHAUSTED`, raised when the order's `shade_tolerance` is `SINGLE_LOT` and the lot the selector itself chooses is in `EXHAUSTED`, which is checked before the empty-candidate-set refusal below. The refusals that name a client-supplied roll belong to the manual line of the paragraph on line-by-line filling: there, an allocation that would draw from a roll whose dye lot is not `APPROVED`, `EXHAUSTED` or `REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_DYE_LOT_NOT_APPROVED`, one drawing from a roll whose dye lot is `REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_DYE_LOT_EXHAUSTED`, and a `SINGLE_LOT` order may not be closed out of a lot in `EXHAUSTED`, which carries the same `ERR_DYE_LOT_EXHAUSTED` refusal. A roll carrying no `dye_lot_id` at all skips every one of those lot checks.

The order's `shade_tolerance` then narrows that set. Under `ANY` no further restriction applies. Under `BAND_MATCH` only rolls whose `shade_band` equals a single chosen band survive, where the chosen band is the band that has the greatest total `available_yards` among the pre-shade candidate rolls, with ties broken by band ordinal so that `A` beats `B`, `B` beats `C`, and `C` in turn beats `SOLUTION`, the band solution-dyed goods carry. Under `SINGLE_LOT` only rolls whose `dye_lot_id` equals a single chosen lot survive, where the chosen lot is the dye lot with the greatest `uncommitted_yards` — the commitment-aware figure — among the pre-shade candidate rolls, with ties broken by the earlier `created_at`; when no candidate roll carries a `dye_lot_id` at all there is no lot to choose, and the surviving set is exactly the rolls whose `dye_lot_id` is null, so `SINGLE_LOT` over solution-dyed stock behaves like `ANY`. If the candidate set is empty, either before or after this narrowing, nothing is created and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ROLL_GOODS`.

Surviving candidates are then ranked, and the ranking is the mill's stated inventory policy: burn down remnants first, then work from the smallest roll upward so that large rolls stay whole for large orders. Rank first by `is_remnant` descending, so remnant rolls come before non-remnant rolls; then by `available_yards` ascending; then by `created_at` ascending as the final tie-break. The roll's `id` and any random value are never consulted in the ranking.

The waterfall then draws from candidates in rank order. The remaining need starts at the order's `requested_yards` less its current `allocated_yards`, floored at zero — for a freshly `SUBMITTED` order that is the full `requested_yards`, and for a `BACKORDERED` order being retried it is only the shortfall, which is what lets the retry keep the lines it already holds instead of double-allocating them. While that need is greater than zero, take from the current roll the lesser of the remaining need and that roll's `available_yards`, round it half-up to 1 decimal place, and create one line in `RESERVED` for that many `yards` against that roll, copying the roll's `shade_band` into the line's `shade_band` and the roll's `is_remnant` into the line's `from_remnant`; then reduce the remaining need by the amount taken and move to the next roll.

Three outcomes are possible when the waterfall stops. If the need reached zero, the order's `allocated_yards` now equals its `requested_yards` and the order transitions to `ALLOCATED`, whether it started in `SUBMITTED` or in `BACKORDERED`. If the need is still positive and the order's `order_type` is `CUSTOM`, nothing at all is created and the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_YARDAGE` — a custom order is never partially allocated and never backordered. If the need is still positive and the `order_type` is `RUNNING_LINE`, the partial lines are kept and the order transitions to `BACKORDERED`.

Every successful allocation, partial or complete, reconciles four things. Each touched roll's `available_yards` is recomputed and a roll driven to exactly zero flips `AVAILABLE` to `COMMITTED`. The order's `gross_cents`, `freight_cents`, `remnant_credit_cents` and `net_invoice_cents` are recomputed, first by the per-line charge rule and then by the settlement rule below. Each touched dye lot's two uncommitted-yardage figures are recomputed, and a lot whose commitment-aware figure reaches zero or less flips `APPROVED` to `EXHAUSTED`. And each affected style's backorder posture is recomputed: `open_backorder_yards` is the sum, over that style's orders in status `SUBMITTED` or `BACKORDERED`, of each order's `requested_yards` less its `allocated_yards`, rounded half-up to 1 decimal place — orders in `ALLOCATED`, `CUT`, `SHIPPED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing — and the style's `replenishment_flag` becomes `REPLENISH_REQUIRED` when that figure reaches or exceeds 250 yards and `NONE` when it falls below. A short `RUNNING_LINE` order therefore raises the flag as it backorders, and re-allocating that order back to `ALLOCATED` recomputes `open_backorder_yards` and clears the flag to `NONE` once the figure drops below 250 yards. `ALLOCATED` to `BACKORDERED` to `ALLOCATED` is thus a genuine retry loop.

An order may also be filled line by line. A manual allocation line names one `roll_good_id`, the `yards` to commit from it, and the mandatory `expected_roll_good_version` of §2.6. The requested `yards` may not exceed the roll's `available_yards`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_ROLL_YARDAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, and the named roll must be `PASSED` on quality and `AVAILABLE` on inventory, failing which the refusal is `ERR_ROLL_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`; the yardage test is the earlier of the two, exactly as rung 27 orders them, so a roll that is both over-drawn and unallocatable reports `ERR_ROLL_YARDAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. Shade continuity is enforced against the lines already on the order rather than against a chosen band: under a `shade_tolerance` of `SINGLE_LOT` the roll's `dye_lot_id` must equal the `dye_lot_id` of the roll behind the order's earliest-created line in status `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED`; under `BAND_MATCH` the roll's `shade_band` must equal that line's `shade_band`; under `ANY` no constraint applies. The first line on an order always passes this check, since there is no earlier line to match. A violation is refused with `ERR_SHADE_BAND_MISMATCH`. A successful manual line creates one `RESERVED` allocation, increments the roll's `version` by 1, recomputes the roll's yardage ledger, and recomputes the order's charges and settlement; once `allocated_yards` reaches `requested_yards`, the order transitions from `SUBMITTED` or `BACKORDERED` to `ALLOCATED`.

Charging is per line, and the order of operations is pinned because it is observable in the cents. Process the order's lines in ascending `created_at` order. For each line, take a shade-band price factor from the line's `shade_band`: 1.00 for `A`, 0.97 for `B`, 0.90 for `C`, 1.00 for `SOLUTION`, and 0.00 for `OUT`. Multiply the line's `yards` by the style's `list_price_cents_per_yard`, then by that band factor, and then — when the line's `from_remnant` is true — by 0.85, reflecting a fifteen percent discount for remnant yardage; when `from_remnant` is false, multiply by 1.00 instead. Round that product half-up to whole cents and store it as the line's `line_cents`. Only after every line has been rounded individually is the order's `gross_cents` formed, as the exact integer sum of `line_cents` over the order's lines in status `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED`. Rounding the sum of the unrounded line values instead of summing the rounded line values is incorrect and will differ by whole cents. Lines in `RELEASED` or `VOIDED` are excluded from the sum, and their `line_cents` are frozen at whatever value they last held.

Settlement runs immediately after the line charges on every allocation, manual line and cancellation that changes an order's lines, and its evaluation order is pinned explicitly because the freight tier and the remnant credit would otherwise look circular. Step one is `gross_cents`, from the line charges above. Step two is `freight_cents`, keyed **on `gross_cents`** and never on the net figure: freight is 0 when `gross_cents` is at least 100000 cents, 4500 cents when `gross_cents` is at least 50000 cents, and 8900 cents otherwise. Both tier comparisons are inclusive, so a `gross_cents` of exactly 100000 ships freight-free and one of exactly 50000 pays 4500. Step three is `remnant_credit_cents`, also keyed **on `gross_cents`** and never on gross plus freight: when any line of the order in status `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED` has `from_remnant` true, the credit is `gross_cents` multiplied by 0.03 and rounded half-up to whole cents; otherwise it is 0. Step four is `net_invoice_cents`, which is `gross_cents` plus `freight_cents` less `remnant_credit_cents`. A zero-line guard overrides all of this: when `gross_cents` is 0 because no `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED` line remains — every line having been released or voided — then `freight_cents`, `remnant_credit_cents` and `net_invoice_cents` are all 0, and the freight tier is never applied to a line-less order.

Cutting the order advances it from `ALLOCATED` to `CUT` and requires every *live* line of the order — every line in `RESERVED`, `CUT` or `SHIPPED` — to be in `RESERVED`, refusing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise; lines already `RELEASED` or `VOIDED` are dead and are ignored by both the check and the cut. Each live line transitions `RESERVED` to `CUT`, and each roll sourced by those lines is then resolved, each distinct roll exactly once. A roll is resolved only once nothing on it is still merely reserved: if any line against that roll remains `RESERVED` — a line of some other order, for instance — or the roll is already `SCRAPPED` or `DEPLETED`, the resolution is skipped and no remnant is split. When the residual left on a parent roll after cutting is greater than 0 and strictly less than the remnant threshold of 30.0 yards, the residual is split off as a remnant child roll and the parent is driven to `DEPLETED`. The child copies `production_run_id`, `dye_lot_id`, `width_ft` and `shade_band` from its parent, sets `parent_roll_id` to the parent's `id`, carries `is_remnant` of true, `qa_status` of `PASSED` and `inventory_status` of `AVAILABLE`, takes both `initial_yards` and `available_yards` equal to the residual, and receives a `roll_number` formed from the parent's `roll_number` followed by a hyphen, the letter R, and a counter starting at 1 for the first remnant split off that parent. A residual of at least 30.0 yards mints no child at all: the parent keeps that residual and stays `AVAILABLE`. A residual of exactly zero likewise mints nothing and the parent goes `DEPLETED`.

Shipping advances the order from `CUT` to `SHIPPED`, which is terminal. Every line transitions `CUT` to `SHIPPED`. The credit hold is converted into an invoice: on the buying account, the order's `reserved_credit_cents` hold is replaced by its `net_invoice_cents`, so `credit_used_cents` now counts the shipped order at its invoiced value rather than at its reserved value. Both uncommitted-yardage figures are recomputed for every dye lot touched by the shipment — the shipped-only figure moves for the first time here — and the style is re-evaluated for the `RUNOUT` to `DISCONTINUED` transition of §3.3.

Cancellation is available to a `DealerBuyer` for its own order and to a `MillPlanner` for any order, optionally carrying a `reason`, and it is also driven by the system as hop one of the style-discontinuation cascade for submitted custom orders. It is permitted from `SUBMITTED`, from `ALLOCATED` and from `BACKORDERED`, and the side effects are identical from all three. Every line of the order in `RESERVED` transitions `RESERVED` to `RELEASED`. Each affected roll's `available_yards` is recomputed, and a roll that regains yardage flips `COMMITTED` back to `AVAILABLE` — the compensating leg of the yardage conservation invariant. The account credit ledger is driven in reverse: `credit_used_cents` is decremented by the order's `reserved_credit_cents` and the order's own `reserved_credit_cents` is then zeroed, which is the compensating leg of the credit conservation invariant, so that a submit-then-cancel pair returns both the account's `credit_used_cents` and its `status` to their pre-submission values. An account in `CREDIT_HOLD` whose `credit_used_cents` now falls strictly below its `credit_limit_cents` transitions `CREDIT_HOLD` to `ACTIVE`. The order's `status` becomes `CANCELLED`, which is terminal, and the style is re-evaluated for `RUNOUT` to `DISCONTINUED`. Cancellation also recomputes that style's `open_backorder_yards` and `replenishment_flag`, and it does so whatever the order's prior status was, not only from `BACKORDERED`, because a `SUBMITTED` order counts toward the backorder pool just as a `BACKORDERED` one does and cancelling it must take its unfilled yardage back out.

One asymmetry in the ledger deserves emphasis, because it is easy to get wrong. When the dye-lot rejection cascade of §3.6 voids an order's lines and drives it `ALLOCATED` to `BACKORDERED`, the order recomputes `allocated_yards` and then re-runs the line charges and the settlement — and with every line voided, the zero-line guard drives `gross_cents`, `freight_cents`, `remnant_credit_cents` and `net_invoice_cents` all to 0. But the order's `reserved_credit_cents` and the account's `credit_used_cents` are left completely untouched, because the credit hold follows `requested_yards`, not `allocated_yards`. The buyer still wants the yardage; the mill still holds the credit for it.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is rooted at the `/mill` prefix.
