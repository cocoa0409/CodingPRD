# Continuing Disclosure & Investor Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A municipal bond issuer's finance office carries a promise that outlives the sale of every bond it has ever issued: the continuing-disclosure undertaking. For each outstanding issue the office has contractually committed to publish audited financial statements and operating data on a fixed annual calendar, and to tell the market about a defined list of consequential events within ten business days of their occurrence. Those promises are cheap to make and expensive to keep. They are scattered across dozens of series with different fiscal year ends, different filing windows, and different sets of required tables; they are discharged by an outside dissemination agent whose submissions the office does not directly control; and every promise the office breaks must be confessed in the official statement of the next issue it brings to market, for five years.

This document specifies the service that carries that burden. It maintains the undertaking inventory across outstanding issues together with the content each annual filing must contain and the date it is due; it drives the preparation and attestation of that content through to an assembled annual report; it determines the materiality of listed events and enforces the ten-business-day filing window measured from the occurrence date; it records submission to the central repository along with the repository's confirmation, rejection, and correction cycles; it derives failure-to-file history and the risk posture that follows from it; and it clears the failure-to-file disclosure of every new official statement against a five-year lookback across the whole credit program. Bank loans and direct placements, rating agency actions, and defeasance all fold into the same disclosure machinery.

The service is deliberately built as a compliance record rather than a workflow convenience. Duties are segregated: the officer who attests a piece of content may not declare the annual report assembled, and the reviewer who clears an official statement may not publish it. Derived quantities — completeness scores, risk scores, remediation tiers, obligation counts, agent invoices — are recomputed from their source rows on every trigger rather than incremented in place, because a disclosure record that drifts is worse than no record at all. All business resources are mounted under `/disclosure`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, if `X-User-ID` is empty, or if `X-User-Role` holds a value outside the four roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. When the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among those authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` with HTTP 403. The authorized roles for every endpoint are listed in §4.

Four roles exist, spelled exactly as follows.

A **`FinanceDirector`** is the issuer's finance office principal. This role owns the debt inventory and the undertaking register, signs off on defeasance and rating actions, engages and terminates dissemination agents, drives the server's logical clock, and runs the dated calendar sweep. A `FinanceDirector` cannot prepare or attest filing content, cannot determine materiality, and cannot submit anything to the central repository.

A **`DisclosureOfficer`** is the preparer. This role declares the undertaking's required content, opens and withdraws reporting cycles, records preparation progress on individual content items and attests them, opens listed-event notices and records direct placements, drafts filings and official statements, and closes failure-to-file events against remediation evidence. A `DisclosureOfficer` cannot determine materiality, cannot waive content, cannot review or publish an official statement, and cannot submit to the repository.

A **`ComplianceReviewer`** is the independent compliance function. This role judges the materiality of listed events, waives content the undertaking is not obliged to provide, classifies direct placements against the materiality boundary, and runs and publishes the official-statement failure-to-file review. A `ComplianceReviewer` cannot open or prepare anything, and cannot submit to the repository.

A **`DisseminationAgent`** is the engaged outside agent and the only role permitted to touch the central-repository submission surface: it submits and resubmits filings and carries the repository's confirmation or rejection back into the record. It also acknowledges the filing calendar it is engaged to monitor.

Role identity binds to `X-User-ID` differently depending on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `FinanceDirector`, `DisclosureOfficer`, or `ComplianceReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition says so — as `assembled_by_user_id` and `attested_by_user_id` on cycle and content rows, as `determined_by_user_id` on a listed-event notice, as `created_by_user_id` on a notice the officer opens, and as `reviewed_by_user_id` and `posted_by_user_id` on an official statement — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `DisseminationAgent`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DisseminationEngagement` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DisseminationEngagement` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.

That agent-identity binding is enforced by a dedicated check. When the acting role is `DisseminationAgent`, `X-User-ID` must resolve to a stored `DisseminationEngagement` row and that engagement must be the one the request belongs to. On a submit, the engagement's `undertaking_id` must equal the undertaking that owns the filing's subject — the undertaking of the subject cycle, or the undertaking of the issue named by the subject notice or placement. On a confirm, a reject, or a resubmit, `X-User-ID` must equal the filing's `submitting_engagement_id` exactly, so that only the agent that handed the document over may carry the repository's answer back. On a calendar acknowledgement, `X-User-ID` must equal the engagement named by the `{id}` path segment. All three arms fail with `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ENGAGED` and HTTP 403, and this check runs regardless of the engagement's own status, so a terminated agent is told it is not engaged rather than being allowed to read another engagement's record.

Two further prohibitions are enforced on `X-User-ID` beyond the role gate, and both are same-role prohibitions that no role hierarchy can satisfy. First, preparation is segregated from assembly: the user declaring an annual report assembled must not equal the `attested_by_user_id` of any non-`VOID` content item of that cycle. Second, review is segregated from publication: the user publishing an official statement must not equal that statement's `reviewed_by_user_id`. Both are refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

Read access is uniform. Every single-resource read and every list endpoint under `/disclosure` is available to all four roles; the write surface is partitioned exactly as described above and enumerated in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` is an integer with a minimum of 1, a maximum of 100, and a default of 10 when omitted. `offset` is an integer with a minimum of 0 and a default of 0 when omitted. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 422.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any row remains beyond the returned window.

Rows are returned in ascending `created_at` order, with `id` ascending as the tie-breaker. The same ordering governs every cascade the service performs: when a single request has to touch several rows of one kind, it processes them in ascending `created_at` order.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources also carry three further housekeeping properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, with three fractional digits, at which the row was first written. `updated_at` is the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the row suffers as a cascade of somebody else's request. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, again including cascaded mutations. The `version` field is the value optimistic concurrency compares against, so its discipline is strict: if a request changes any persisted field of a row, that row's `version` advances by one and its `updated_at` is refreshed.

`ContentRequirement` is the single exception. A content requirement is immutable configuration — there is no endpoint that modifies one — and it therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. A `ContentRequirement` has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, every window, and every sweep rule in this service reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until the clock is explicitly set, that instant is what all rules read, and the service must never fall back on real wall-clock time. A `FinanceDirector` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the response echoes `current_utc`. Setting the clock writes no entity row and, by itself, produces no consequences: elapsed-time effects such as delinquency, missed events, and monitoring suspension materialise only when the dated calendar sweep described in §3.11 is invoked.

Every field named `*_at` in this specification — `assembled_at`, `attested_at`, `determined_at`, `submitted_at`, `repository_confirmed_at`, `remediated_at`, `reviewed_at`, `posted_at`, `defeased_at`, and `filed_at` — is stamped from this logical clock, not from wall-clock time.

### 2.6. The Business-Day Calendar

Two of the service's date derivations step over business days rather than calendar days, and both use one shared calendar. A date is a business day when its weekday is neither Saturday nor Sunday and the date is not one of the recognised market holidays. The recognised market holidays are exactly these fourteen dates: 2026-07-03, 2026-09-07, 2026-10-12, 2026-11-11, 2026-11-26, 2026-12-25, 2027-01-01, 2027-01-18, 2027-02-15, 2027-05-31, 2027-07-05, 2027-09-06, 2027-11-25, and 2027-12-24. The calendar reads no entity row, so no entity state can alter it.

Two operations are built on that definition. **Adding business days** to a base date walks forward one calendar day at a time, counting only business days, and stops when the requested count has been reached; the base date itself is never counted, whether or not it is itself a business day, so adding ten business days to a base date yields the tenth business day strictly after it. **Rolling forward to a business day** walks a date forward one calendar day at a time until it lands on a business day, and returns the date unchanged when it is already one. Rolling is always forward — never backward.

### 2.7. Precision and Rounding

All rounding in this service is round-half-up: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Where a quantity has a stated number of decimal places, that count is part of the contract and applies to the persisted value.

`disclosure_risk_score` and `completeness_score_pct` are held to 2 decimal places. `completeness_score_pct` additionally uses an intermediate rounding to 4 decimal places, per-item, before summation — the exact order is specified in §3.3 and is load-bearing. `materiality_ratio_bp` is an integer number of basis points. All monetary amounts — `original_par_amount_cents`, `outstanding_par_amount_cents`, `escrow_deposit_cents`, `principal_amount_cents`, `retainer_cents`, and `invoice_total_cents` — are integer counts of cents, and the only rounding step anywhere in the invoice derivation is a round-half-up to whole cents on the posture surcharge. Day counts (`days_late`, `monitoring_lapse_days`) are whole calendar days floored at zero, and all date comparisons are made in UTC.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints are guarded against acting on a stale view of their parent row. Each declares a non-optional version field in its request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of that parent, and each rejects a mismatch with the parent's current `version`.

- `POST /disclosure/filings` requires `expected_issue_version`, matching the current `version` of the `BondIssue` named by `issue_id`. If another writer has advanced that issue's `version` since the client last read it — most commonly because a correcting filing for the same subject has already been opened, or because a defeasance has landed — the create is rejected with `ERR_ISSUE_VERSION_STALE` and HTTP 412. Drafting a correction is legitimate product behaviour on its own terms; what the check refuses is a client whose view of the issue's disclosure record is out of date.
- `POST /disclosure/bond-issues/{id}/rating-actions` requires `expected_issue_version`, matching the current `version` of the issue named by the `{id}` path segment. A rating action legitimately overwrites `current_rating` and `current_rating_agency`, so a second recording is valid in itself; the check forces the writer to have actually observed the rating its action supersedes. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_ISSUE_VERSION_STALE` and HTTP 412.
- `POST /disclosure/dissemination-engagements` requires `expected_undertaking_version`, matching the current `version` of the `Undertaking` named by `undertaking_id`. A new engagement legitimately supersedes the incumbent, so the create would otherwise succeed; the check forces the writer to have observed the engagement it is about to displace, and to have observed any suspension or termination of the undertaking itself. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_UNDERTAKING_VERSION_STALE` and HTTP 412.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. The version comparison is evaluated before any state precondition on the target row, so a caller acting on stale data always learns that fact first rather than being told about a state conflict it could not have known about.

### 2.9. Path and Foreign-Key Resolution

Path parameters are resolved left to right. Every `{id}` segment must resolve to a stored row of the endpoint's own entity; the first segment that fails yields that entity's own not-found error with HTTP 404. Foreign-key fields carried in a request body are resolved next, in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, and yield the same entity-specific not-found errors. The fields subject to this check are `issue_id`, `undertaking_id`, `content_requirement_id`, `cycle_id`, `material_event_id`, `direct_placement_id`, `submitting_engagement_id`, `supersedes_filing_id`, `remediation_filing_id`, and every element of `disclosed_event_ids`.

The eleven not-found codes are `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUIREMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAILURE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFICIAL_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. All of them carry HTTP 404.

### 2.10. The Validation Ladder

Checks are evaluated in a single strictly ordered ladder, and the **first** rule that fails is the **only** error returned. Where two rules could both fire on the same request, the lower-numbered rule wins. The ordering below is normative; the business meaning of each rule and the workflow it guards are developed in §3.

1. Authentication — `X-User-Role` present and one of the four declared roles, `X-User-ID` present and non-empty → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Authorization — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's role list → `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Path existence — every `{id}` path segment resolves, left to right → the entity-specific not-found error (404). Applies to every endpoint with an `{id}` path parameter.
4. Body foreign-key existence — every foreign-key field in the body resolves, in declared input order → the entity-specific not-found error (404). Applies to every create and update endpoint carrying a foreign key.
5. Request shape — every required input present; every enum value inside its declared set; integers non-negative where declared; dates in ISO 8601 form; `period_start_date` strictly before `period_end_date`; `weight_bp` between 1 and 10000 inclusive; `retainer_cents` at least 0; `disclosed_event_ids` a possibly empty array of distinct ids → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every create and update endpoint.
6. Agent identity — the three arms described in §2.1 → `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ENGAGED` (403). Applies to `POST /disclosure/filings/{id}/submit`, `/confirm`, `/reject`, `/resubmit`, and to `POST /disclosure/dissemination-engagements/{id}/acknowledge-calendar`. This is distinct from rule 2: the role is permitted, the identity is wrong.
7. Optimistic concurrency — the supplied `expected_issue_version` or `expected_undertaking_version` equals the parent's current `version` → `ERR_ISSUE_VERSION_STALE` or `ERR_UNDERTAKING_VERSION_STALE` (412). Applies to `POST /disclosure/filings`, `POST /disclosure/bond-issues/{id}/rating-actions`, and `POST /disclosure/dissemination-engagements`.
8. Own-status transition legality — the target row's own `status` admits the requested transition → `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action endpoint and to `PATCH /disclosure/cycle-content-items/{id}`. This rule inspects the `status` field only and never a derived field such as `disclosure_adequacy`; publishing a statement that is in `COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` but whose disclosure is inadequate therefore passes this rung and is caught at rule 32. This is the highest-priority business check and wins every conflict with the rules below it.
9. Issue gate — the `BondIssue` that owns the target row is `OUTSTANDING` → `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_OUTSTANDING` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/undertakings`, `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles`, `POST /disclosure/material-event-notices`, `POST /disclosure/direct-placements`, `POST /disclosure/filings`, and `POST /disclosure/official-statements/{id}/compliance-review`.
10. Undertaking gate — the `Undertaking` that owns the target row is `ACTIVE` → `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles`, `PATCH /disclosure/cycle-content-items/{id}`, `POST /disclosure/cycle-content-items/{id}/attest`, `/reopen`, `/waive`, `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles/{id}/assemble`, and `POST /disclosure/dissemination-engagements`.
11. Engagement gate — the engagement acting on this request is `ACTIVE` → `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/filings/{id}/submit`, `/resubmit`, `/confirm`, and `/reject`. A `SUSPENDED` engagement may still acknowledge the calendar, which is its cure path, and may still be terminated by a `FinanceDirector`; it may not submit, resubmit, confirm, or reject. A `TERMINATED` engagement may do none of these, and its terminate or acknowledge attempts are caught one rung earlier at rule 8. This rule sits deliberately below rule 8, so confirming an already-`CONFIRMED` filing through a terminated engagement returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, not `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.
12. Segregation of duties, preparation versus assembly → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles/{id}/assemble`.
13. Segregation of duties, review versus publication → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /disclosure/official-statements/{id}/post`.
14. At most one `Undertaking` per `issue_id`, in any status → `ERR_UNDERTAKING_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/undertakings`.
15. At most one `ContentRequirement` per undertaking-and-kind pair → `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/content-requirements`.
16. Reporting-period overlap → `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles`.
17. The undertaking has at least one content requirement → `ERR_NO_CONTENT_REQUIREMENTS` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles`. This runs before rule 18 on purpose: an empty requirement set also fails the weight-closure test, so checking closure first would make this error unreachable.
18. Content-weight closure → `ERR_CONTENT_WEIGHTS_UNBALANCED` (422). Applies to `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles`.
19. Duplicate listed-event notice → `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVENT_NOTICE` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/material-event-notices` and `POST /disclosure/bond-issues/{id}/rating-actions`. Ranked above rules 20 and 21, so a re-submitted stale duplicate is reported as a duplicate rather than as a stale occurrence date.
20. Occurrence date not excessively backdated → `ERR_OCCURRENCE_DATE_STALE` (422). Same two endpoints as rule 19.
21. Occurrence date not in the future → `ERR_OCCURRENCE_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422). Same two endpoints as rule 19.
22. Direct-placement review-window capacity → `ERR_PLACEMENT_REVIEW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/direct-placements`.
23. Review window ordered correctly and principal at least 1 cent → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to `POST /disclosure/direct-placements`.
24. Attestation readiness, in two ordered sub-steps — first `source_document_ref` and `data_as_of_date` both non-null → `ERR_CONTENT_DATA_AS_OF_REQUIRED` (409); then, when the underlying requirement demands an audit opinion, `audit_opinion_type` non-null → `ERR_AUDIT_OPINION_REQUIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/cycle-content-items/{id}/attest`.
25. Assembly readiness — every non-`VOID` content item of the cycle is `READY` or `WAIVED` → `ERR_CONTENT_NOT_READY` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles/{id}/assemble`.
26. Fileable-content floor — at least one non-`VOID` content item of the cycle is `READY` → `ERR_NO_FILEABLE_CONTENT` (409). Same endpoint as rule 25, and the boundary case rule 25 cannot see.
27. Waivability → `ERR_REQUIREMENT_NOT_WAIVABLE` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/cycle-content-items/{id}/waive`.
28. Filing subject exclusivity and type agreement → `ERR_FILING_SUBJECT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /disclosure/filings`.
29. Subject not already filed → `ERR_SUBJECT_ALREADY_FILED` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/filings`.
30. Supersede-target validity → `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/filings`.
31. Remediation evidence → `ERR_REMEDIATION_FILING_INVALID` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/failure-to-file-events/{id}/remediate`.
32. Official-statement disclosure adequacy → `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INADEQUATE` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/official-statements/{id}/post`.
33. Repository confirmation payload and correction payload — `repository_receipt_id` unique across all filings → `ERR_RECEIPT_ID_DUPLICATE` (409) on `POST /disclosure/filings/{id}/confirm`; a correcting draft carrying `supersedes_filing_id` must also carry a non-empty `correction_reason` → `ERR_CORRECTION_REASON_REQUIRED` (422) on `POST /disclosure/filings`.

36. Pagination bounds → `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422). Applies to every list endpoint.
37. A cycle may not be withdrawn while a filing on it is live → `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_LIVE_FILING` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/annual-report-cycles/{id}/withdraw`.
38. An engagement may not be terminated while a filing it submitted is live → `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_HAS_LIVE_FILING` (409). Applies to `POST /disclosure/dissemination-engagements/{id}/terminate`.
39. Editable-cycle gate for content preparation → `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_EDITABLE` (409). Applies to `PATCH /disclosure/cycle-content-items/{id}` and to `POST /disclosure/cycle-content-items/{id}/attest`, `/reopen`, and `/waive`.
40. Branch-specific required fields — a non-empty `waiver_reason` on the waived branch of placement classification → `ERR_WAIVER_REASON_REQUIRED` (422) on `POST /disclosure/direct-placements/{id}/classify`; and a refusal to declare a per-se listed event immaterial → `ERR_PER_SE_EVENT_IS_MATERIAL` (422) on `POST /disclosure/material-event-notices/{id}/determine`.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Recomputation

A single accepted request routinely mutates several rows. Three principles govern that.

First, cascades are **immediate and complete within the request**. There is no deferred or eventual reconciliation: by the time the response is written, every dependent row and every derived field the change affects already holds its new value.

Second, cascades are **ordered**. Where a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, the set is processed in ascending `created_at` order. Where a cascade fans out across hops — an issue terminating an undertaking, the undertaking closing its cycles, the cycles voiding their content items — the hops execute in the order stated in §3, and a hop that mints a new row after a void pass leaves that new row untouched by the pass that preceded it.

Third, derived quantities are **recomputed from source, never adjusted in place**. `open_obligation_count`, `open_material_event_count`, `late_filing_count`, `missed_event_count`, `delinquent_cycle_count`, `content_weight_bp_total`, `completeness_score_pct`, `billable_filing_count`, and `monitoring_lapse_days` are each defined as a full aggregation over their source rows and are re-derived whenever a trigger fires. This is what makes compensating operations exact: withdrawing a cycle created moments earlier returns `open_obligation_count` to precisely its pre-creation value.

Every row a cascade mutates has its own `version` advanced by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been written directly.

### 2.12. Discovery of Cascade-Created Rows

Two entities are never created by a direct call and exist only as the product of a cascade: `CycleContentItem` rows, minted when a reporting cycle is opened, and `FailureToFileEvent` rows, minted when a filing lands late or when the calendar sweep finds a missed obligation. The list endpoints are their only discovery channel, and each list endpoint therefore accepts the filters needed to walk backwards along a foreign key.

Content items are filterable by `cycle_id`, `content_requirement_id`, and `status`. Failure events are filterable by `undertaking_id`, `cycle_id`, `material_event_id`, `remediation_filing_id`, `kind`, `status`, and `severity`. The remaining lists follow the same pattern: bond issues by `issuer_program_code` and `status`; undertakings by `issue_id`, `status`, and `compliance_posture`; content requirements by `undertaking_id` and `kind`; reporting cycles by `undertaking_id`, `filed_filing_id`, `status`, and `fiscal_year_label`; listed-event notices by `issue_id`, `financial_obligation_ref`, `filed_filing_id`, `status`, and `event_type`; filings by `issue_id`, `cycle_id`, `material_event_id`, `direct_placement_id`, `supersedes_filing_id`, `superseded_by_filing_id`, `submitting_engagement_id`, `filing_type`, and `status`; direct placements by `issue_id`, `linked_notice_id`, `status`, and `disclosure_practice`; official statements by `issue_id`, `status`, and `lookback_policy`; and dissemination engagements by `undertaking_id`, `superseded_by_engagement_id`, and `status`. Every one of those filters is optional, and all of them combine with `limit` and `offset`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Debt Inventory: Registering Issues, Recording Ratings, Retiring Debt

Everything in this service hangs off a **`BondIssue`** — one row per series the issuer has sold and still owes on. A `FinanceDirector` registers an issue by supplying an `issuer_program_code`, a `series_designation`, a `security_type`, a `dated_date`, a `final_maturity_date`, and an `original_par_amount_cents`. The `issuer_program_code` is the credit-program grouping code, between 1 and 32 characters of uppercase alphanumerics and hyphens, and it is far more than a label: it defines the blast radius of the official-statement failure-to-file lookback described in §3.9, because a new offering must confess the disclosure failures of every issue that shares its exact code. The `series_designation` is a short label such as `2026A`, between 1 and 16 characters, and is unique within a given `issuer_program_code`. `security_type` records the pledge class and is one of `GENERAL_OBLIGATION`, `REVENUE`, or `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`. Both dates are ISO 8601 calendar dates and `final_maturity_date` must fall strictly after `dated_date`. `original_par_amount_cents` is an integer of at least 1.

A freshly registered issue opens in `status` `OUTSTANDING` with `version` 1. Its `outstanding_par_amount_cents` is initialised to the value of `original_par_amount_cents`; `open_material_event_count` is 0; and `current_rating`, `current_rating_agency`, `defeasance_type`, and `defeased_at` are all null, while `escrow_deposit_cents` starts at 0. The `outstanding_par_amount_cents` figure matters downstream because it is the denominator of the direct-placement materiality ratio and the escrow yardstick for a legal defeasance.

Rating agency actions arrive through their own recording capability, reserved to the `FinanceDirector` and returning HTTP 201 because the substantive product of the call is a new listed-event notice rather than an edit to the issue. The payload carries the `rating_agency` — one of `MOODYS`, `SP`, `FITCH`, or `KBRA` — plus the `prior_rating` symbol, the `new_rating` symbol, the `occurrence_date` of the agency's action, and `expected_issue_version`. A rating change is a per-se listed event, so a single call both records the action and arms the disclosure clock: the service mints a `MaterialEventNotice` with `event_type` `RATING_CHANGE`, `per_se_material` set to true, `status` `DETERMINATION_PENDING`, its `rating_agency`, `prior_rating`, and `new_rating` stamped from the payload, and its `filing_deadline_date` derived from the ten-business-day rule of §3.5. The issue's own `current_rating` and `current_rating_agency` are overwritten with `new_rating` and `rating_agency`, the issue's `version` advances, and `open_material_event_count` on the issue and `open_obligation_count` on the undertaking are both recomputed. The duplicate-notice guard and the two occurrence-date guards of §3.5 apply to this call exactly as they do to a hand-opened notice.

Debt leaves the inventory in one of two ways, and both are terminal. **Defeasance** is a deliberate act by a `FinanceDirector` on an `OUTSTANDING` issue, carrying a `defeasance_type` of either `LEGAL` or `ECONOMIC`, an `escrow_deposit_cents` amount, and the `occurrence_date` of the defeasance. Escrow must actually fund the retirement: for a `LEGAL` defeasance, `escrow_deposit_cents` must be at least the issue's current `outstanding_par_amount_cents`; for an `ECONOMIC` defeasance, `escrow_deposit_cents` must be at least 1 cent. A shortfall is refused with `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT`, and because this is a fiscal check it sits near the bottom of the ladder — a defeasance attempt on an already-`DEFEASED` issue reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` instead. **Maturity** is not an act at all but a discovery: the dated sweep of §3.11 finds an `OUTSTANDING` issue whose `final_maturity_date` has fallen strictly before the logical clock's date and moves it to `MATURED`.

Both endings drive `outstanding_par_amount_cents` to 0, advance the issue's `version`, and fire the same four-hop termination cascade. Hop 1: the `Undertaking` whose `issue_id` names this issue, if its `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, moves to `TERMINATED`, with `termination_cause` set to `DEFEASANCE` for the defeasance path and `MATURITY` for the maturity path. Hop 2: every `AnnualReportCycle` of that undertaking whose `status` is `DRAFTING`, `READY_TO_FILE`, or `DELINQUENT` moves to `CLOSED_NOT_REQUIRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order; where such a cycle was `DELINQUENT`, leaving that status also forces a fresh derivation of `delinquent_cycle_count` and a re-run of the risk score of §3.8. Hop 2b: every `DisseminationEngagement` of that undertaking whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `UNDERTAKING_TERMINATED`, and its `invoice_total_cents` freezes at whatever value it then holds. Hop 3: every `CycleContentItem` of each closed cycle whose `status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `DRAFT`, `READY`, or `WAIVED` moves to `VOID` in ascending `created_at` order, each with `readiness_factor` driven to 0.0 — though an item that had been attested keeps its `attested_by_user_id` for the audit trail — and each affected cycle's `completeness_score_pct` falls to 0.00 because its entire input set has left. Alongside the hops, every `MaterialEventNotice` on the issue whose `status` is `DETERMINATION_PENDING` or `MATERIAL` is driven to `VOID`, again in ascending `created_at` order. The undertaking's `open_obligation_count`, its delinquency counters, and the issue's `open_material_event_count` are all re-derived.

The two endings differ in exactly one respect, and it is a consequential one. Defeasance is itself a listed event, so after the void pass has finished the defeasance path mints one further `MaterialEventNotice` on the issue: `event_type` `DEFEASANCE`, `occurrence_date` taken from the request body, `status` `DETERMINATION_PENDING`, `per_se_material` true, `created_by_user_id` set from `X-User-ID`, and `filing_deadline_date` derived from the ten-business-day rule. Because that notice is minted after the void pass rather than before it, it is never voided by its own trigger, and it is the one notice on a defeased issue that survives in an open status. Maturity mints no notice at all.

### 3.2. The Undertaking Register and the Content It Requires

An **`Undertaking`** is the contractual promise attached to one issue, and it is opened by a `FinanceDirector` against an `OUTSTANDING` issue. The payload names the `issue_id`, the `fiscal_year_end_month_day` as a two-digit month and two-digit day joined by a hyphen — `06-30` for a June fiscal year end, constrained to a real month between 01 and 12 and a day between 01 and 31 — and, optionally, `filing_window_days` and `timeliness_basis`. `filing_window_days` is the number of calendar days after a reporting period ends by which the annual report is due; it accepts any integer from 1 through 365 and defaults to 210 when omitted. `timeliness_basis` is one of `SUBMISSION_BASIS` or `CONFIRMATION_BASIS`, defaults to `SUBMISSION_BASIS`, and is the single most consequential choice on the row: it selects, once and for every reporting cycle the undertaking will ever own, which instant the timeliness verdict of §3.7 measures.

At most one undertaking may exist per `issue_id`, and the guard is absolute rather than merely current: a second undertaking on the same issue is refused with `ERR_UNDERTAKING_ALREADY_EXISTS` even when the incumbent is already `TERMINATED`, because that terminated row still owns the historical failure events the five-year lookback has to find.

A new undertaking opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `compliance_posture` `COMPLIANT`, `remediation_tier` `TIER_0`, `disclosure_risk_score` 0.00, `version` 1, and null `suspended_reason` and `termination_cause`. All four counters — `late_filing_count`, `missed_event_count`, `delinquent_cycle_count`, and `open_obligation_count` — start at 0, and so does `content_weight_bp_total`.

The undertaking carries two state dimensions on one row, and they move on entirely separate tracks. The **engagement dimension**, held in `status`, runs `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` and back, with `TERMINATED` as its only terminal value. A `FinanceDirector` may suspend an `ACTIVE` undertaking — pending a legal opinion, for instance — by supplying a `suspended_reason`, which is stamped on the row; suspension blocks new reporting cycles, all content preparation, assembly, and all new engagements, while filings already in flight continue undisturbed. Reinstating a `SUSPENDED` undertaking takes no payload at all, returns it to `ACTIVE`, and clears `suspended_reason` back to null. Neither suspension nor reinstatement touches the second dimension: `compliance_posture`, `disclosure_risk_score`, `remediation_tier`, and every counter are left exactly as they were. The **posture dimension**, held in `compliance_posture`, runs across `COMPLIANT`, `WATCH`, `IMPAIRED`, and `DELINQUENT`, has no terminal value at all, and is advanced only by the risk-score derivation of §3.8.

Before an undertaking can report anything, a `DisclosureOfficer` must declare what its annual filing has to contain. Each declaration is one **`ContentRequirement`** naming its `undertaking_id`, a `kind` drawn from `AUDITED_FINANCIALS`, `OPERATING_DATA_TABLE`, `BUDGET_SUMMARY`, `DEBT_SERVICE_SCHEDULE`, or `ENTERPRISE_STATISTICS`, a human `label` of 1 to 120 characters for the annual report's table of contents, and a `weight_bp` between 1 and 10000 giving that item's share of the completeness score in basis points. An optional `waivable` flag, false when omitted, records whether a `ComplianceReviewer` is permitted to waive the item on a cycle. One further field, `requires_audit_opinion`, is derived rather than supplied: it is true when and only when `kind` is `AUDITED_FINANCIALS`, and any value a client sends for it is discarded.

Only one requirement may exist per undertaking-and-`kind` pair; a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT`. Each accepted requirement re-derives the undertaking's `content_weight_bp_total` as the sum of `weight_bp` across all of that undertaking's requirements, which is the figure the weight-closure gate of §3.3 tests. Requirements are immutable configuration: nothing in the service modifies one once written, which is why a `ContentRequirement` carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

### 3.3. Reporting Cycles: Due Dates, Snapshots, and Content Preparation

An **`AnnualReportCycle`** is one turn of the annual filing calendar. A `DisclosureOfficer` opens one against an `ACTIVE` undertaking of an `OUTSTANDING` issue, naming the `undertaking_id`, a `fiscal_year_label` of 1 to 16 characters such as `FY2026`, and the reporting period's `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`. The period is a half-open interval: `period_start_date` is inclusive, `period_end_date` is exclusive, and `period_end_date` must fall strictly after `period_start_date`. That half-open reading decides the overlap rule. A new cycle is refused with `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when its start falls strictly before an existing cycle's end **and** that existing cycle's start falls strictly before the new cycle's end, considering only existing cycles of the same undertaking whose `status` is neither `WITHDRAWN` nor `CLOSED_NOT_REQUIRED`. Exact adjacency — a new cycle beginning on the very day an existing cycle's `period_end_date` falls — is therefore permitted, since neither strict inequality holds.

Two content gates guard creation, and they fire in a fixed order. The undertaking must first have at least one `ContentRequirement` row, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_CONTENT_REQUIREMENTS`; there is nothing to snapshot otherwise. Over that non-empty set, `content_weight_bp_total` must then equal exactly 10000 basis points, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTENT_WEIGHTS_UNBALANCED`. The ordering is deliberate: an empty requirement set sums to zero and so fails closure too, which would make the first error unreachable if closure were tested first.

The cycle's `due_date` is derived once, at creation, and never again. Take the cycle's own `period_end_date`, advance it by the owning undertaking's `filing_window_days` in **calendar** days, then roll the result forward — never backward — to the first business day at or after it, using the calendar of §2.6. A later edit to the undertaking's `filing_window_days` does not re-derive an existing cycle's `due_date`; the value read is the one in force at the moment of creation.

Creation also snapshots the content. One **`CycleContentItem`** is minted for each `ContentRequirement` of the undertaking, each carrying the `cycle_id` of the new cycle, the `content_requirement_id` of its source requirement, `status` `NOT_STARTED`, `readiness_factor` 0.0, and a `weight_bp_snapshot` copied from the requirement's `weight_bp` at that instant. That copy is what keeps an open cycle stable: a requirement added to the undertaking tomorrow never retro-reweights an item minted today. The item's `audit_opinion_type`, `data_as_of_date`, `source_document_ref`, `attested_by_user_id`, `attested_at`, and `waiver_reason` all start null. The cycle itself opens in `status` `DRAFTING` with `version` 1, `completeness_score_pct` 0.00, `days_late` 0, and null `assembled_by_user_id`, `assembled_at`, `filed_filing_id`, `filed_at`, `timeliness_basis_applied`, and `withdrawal_reason`. The undertaking's `open_obligation_count` is re-derived, and the new cycle raises it.

Content items are never created directly and never deleted; they are worked. A `DisclosureOfficer` records preparation progress on one item by supplying any combination of `audit_opinion_type`, `data_as_of_date`, and `source_document_ref` — at least one of the three must be present, or the update is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `audit_opinion_type` is one of `UNMODIFIED`, `QUALIFIED`, or `UNAUDITED_INTERIM` and records the opinion carried by the attached financial statements; `data_as_of_date` is the as-of calendar date of the operating data or statements; `source_document_ref` is an opaque reference to the prepared document. Supplied fields overwrite their stored values and omitted fields are left untouched, so partial preparation is a legitimate repeat operation rather than an error: the first such update advances the item from `NOT_STARTED` to `DRAFT`, and every subsequent one is a `DRAFT` to `DRAFT` self-loop. Either way `readiness_factor` becomes 0.5 and the cycle's `completeness_score_pct` is re-derived, which matters even on the self-loop because changing `audit_opinion_type` changes the item's opinion multiplier.

Attesting an item is the preparer's assertion that it is complete and accurate, and it moves a `DRAFT` item to `READY`. Two readiness conditions are checked in order: `source_document_ref` and `data_as_of_date` must both be non-null, failing which the attestation is refused with `ERR_CONTENT_DATA_AS_OF_REQUIRED`; and then, when the item's underlying `ContentRequirement` has `requires_audit_opinion` true, `audit_opinion_type` must be non-null, failing which the attestation is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_OPINION_REQUIRED`. Letting unaudited numbers reach the repository under the banner of audited financials is precisely what the second condition prevents. On success the item records `attested_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `attested_at` from the logical clock, and `readiness_factor` becomes 1.0.

Reopening reverses that. When the underlying data changes, a `DisclosureOfficer` returns a `READY` item to `DRAFT`, which clears `attested_by_user_id` and `attested_at` back to null and drops `readiness_factor` to 0.5. Reopening also has a reach beyond the item: if the owning cycle had already advanced to `READY_TO_FILE`, it is dragged back to `DRAFTING` and its `assembled_by_user_id` and `assembled_at` are cleared. This is the cyclic edge that makes assembly non-monotone — an assembled annual report can be un-assembled by a single reopened item.

Waiving is the compliance function's escape hatch and is reserved to a `ComplianceReviewer`. An item in `NOT_STARTED` or `DRAFT` moves to `WAIVED` on supply of a `waiver_reason`, but only when its underlying `ContentRequirement` has `waivable` true; otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_REQUIREMENT_NOT_WAIVABLE`. A waived item takes `readiness_factor` 1.0 and contributes its **full** weight to completeness.

All four content operations — the progress update, the attestation, the reopen, and the waive — are gated twice over. The owning undertaking must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the owning cycle's `status` must be `DRAFTING`, `READY_TO_FILE`, or `DELINQUENT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_EDITABLE`. A delinquent cycle deliberately stays editable, because curing and reassembling it is the only path back; a cycle that has moved to `FILING_IN_PROGRESS`, or that has reached any terminal status, accepts none of the four.

**Completeness scoring.** Every one of those writes, and every assembly, withdrawal, or closure of the cycle, re-derives `completeness_score_pct` from scratch. The input set is every `CycleContentItem` of the cycle whose `status` is not `VOID`; a `VOID` item is removed from the input set entirely, which is why voiding items can never raise a score. Each item in the set has a readiness factor — 1.0 when its `status` is `READY` or `WAIVED`, 0.5 when `DRAFT`, and 0.0 when `NOT_STARTED` — and an opinion multiplier that reflects the quality of the audit opinion behind it. The opinion multiplier is 1.0 whenever the item's `ContentRequirement` has `requires_audit_opinion` false, regardless of what `audit_opinion_type` holds. Where the requirement does demand an opinion, the multiplier is 1.0 for an `UNMODIFIED` opinion, 0.8 for a `QUALIFIED` one, 0.5 for `UNAUDITED_INTERIM`, and 0.0 when `audit_opinion_type` is still null.

The three arithmetic steps run in this pinned order. First, each item's contribution is its `weight_bp_snapshot` divided by 10000, multiplied by its readiness factor, multiplied by its opinion multiplier, and that product is rounded half-up to **4 decimal places**. Second, those already-rounded contributions are summed exactly, with no further rounding of the sum. Third, the sum is multiplied by 100 and rounded half-up to **2 decimal places** to give `completeness_score_pct`. When the input set is empty — because every item is `VOID`, or because the cycle has no items at all — the sum is zero and the score is 0.00.

The order is not interchangeable, and the alternative of summing unrounded contributions and rounding once at the end is explicitly rejected. Consider a cycle whose four items carry `weight_bp_snapshot` values of 3333, 3333, 3333, and 1, where the three heavy items are in `DRAFT` and none of them carries an audit-opinion penalty, and the light item is still `NOT_STARTED`. Under the pinned order each heavy item contributes 0.1667 after rounding to four places, the sum is 0.5001, and the score is 50.01. Under the rejected order the unrounded sum would be 0.49995 and the score would be 50.00. Implementations must produce 50.01.

### 3.4. Assembly, Withdrawal, and the Segregation of Preparation from Assembly

Declaring the annual report **assembled** is the act that hands it to the dissemination agent, and a `DisclosureOfficer` performs it with no payload at all. It is available from two starting statuses: `DRAFTING`, the ordinary path, and `DELINQUENT`, which is how a cycle that already blew its deadline becomes fileable again. Either way the cycle lands in `READY_TO_FILE`.

Four conditions guard the act, and their relative order is fixed by the ladder of §2.10. The cycle's own `status` is tested first, so assembling a cycle that is `FILING_IN_PROGRESS`, `WITHDRAWN`, `CLOSED_NOT_REQUIRED`, or already filed yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The owning undertaking must then be `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_ACTIVE`. Segregation of duties is checked next and therefore **before** either content check: the `X-User-ID` performing the assembly must differ from the `attested_by_user_id` of every non-`VOID` content item of the cycle, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Only then does content readiness come into play — every non-`VOID` item must be `READY` or `WAIVED`, and any item still `NOT_STARTED` or `DRAFT` yields `ERR_CONTENT_NOT_READY`. Last comes the fileable-content floor: at least one non-`VOID` item must be `READY`, and a cycle whose every remaining item is `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_NO_FILEABLE_CONTENT`. That last check exists because a wholly waived cycle scores `completeness_score_pct` of 100.00 while carrying no document whatsoever; such a cycle belongs in `CLOSED_NOT_REQUIRED`, not in the repository, and the readiness check one rung above cannot see the difference.

A successful assembly stamps `assembled_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `assembled_at` from the logical clock, and re-derives `completeness_score_pct`. When the prior status was `DELINQUENT`, leaving that status also re-derives the undertaking's `delinquent_cycle_count` and re-runs the risk-score pass of §3.8, which can move `compliance_posture` off `DELINQUENT`. What it does not do is erase history: the `FailureToFileEvent` that the sweep already minted against the cycle stays exactly where it is. Leaving `DELINQUENT` clears the live count, not the recorded failure.

**Withdrawal** is the compensating operation for cycle creation, used when a cycle was opened in error. A `DisclosureOfficer` supplies a `withdrawal_reason` and the cycle moves to `WITHDRAWN`. It is available from `DRAFTING` and from `READY_TO_FILE`; attempting it from any other status yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. One further condition applies: no `Filing` referencing this cycle may sit in `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED` status, because the repository already holds that document and withdrawing would drop an obligation the market can see. Such an attempt is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_LIVE_FILING`.

Withdrawal cascades. Every `CycleContentItem` of the cycle whose `status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `DRAFT`, `READY`, or `WAIVED` moves to `VOID` in ascending `created_at` order, each with `readiness_factor` driven to 0.0. With the entire input set gone, `completeness_score_pct` is re-derived to 0.00. And the undertaking's `open_obligation_count` is re-derived from source, which is what makes the compensation exact: withdrawing a cycle created moments earlier must restore `open_obligation_count` to precisely the value it held before the creation.

The third way a cycle can end without being filed is `CLOSED_NOT_REQUIRED`, which is never requested directly. It is reached only as hop 2 of the termination cascade of §3.1, from `DRAFTING`, `READY_TO_FILE`, or `DELINQUENT`, and it voids the cycle's content items and zeroes its `completeness_score_pct` in exactly the same way withdrawal does.

### 3.5. Listed Events, Materiality Determination, and the Ten-Business-Day Window

A **`MaterialEventNotice`** records one of the listed events the issuer has undertaken to disclose. A `DisclosureOfficer` opens one against an outstanding issue by naming the `issue_id`, the `event_type`, and the `occurrence_date`, optionally supplying `financial_obligation_ref`. The `event_type` is one of `PRINCIPAL_PAYMENT_DELINQUENCY`, `RATING_CHANGE`, `DEFEASANCE`, `BOND_CALL`, `UNSCHEDULED_DRAW_ON_RESERVE`, `TRUSTEE_SUBSTITUTION`, `FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION_INCURRENCE`, or `MERGER_OR_SALE`. `financial_obligation_ref` points at a `DirectPlacement` and is set only when the notice discloses an incurred financial obligation; it is null for every other event type. The notice opens in `status` `DETERMINATION_PENDING`, `version` 1, `days_late` 0, and records `created_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`; `determination_note`, `determined_by_user_id`, `determined_at`, `rating_agency`, `prior_rating`, `new_rating`, and `filed_filing_id` all start null.

Two computed fields are written at creation and never revised. `per_se_material` is true if and only if `event_type` is one of the four per-se listed events — `PRINCIPAL_PAYMENT_DELINQUENCY`, `RATING_CHANGE`, `DEFEASANCE`, `BOND_CALL` — and false for the other four. And `filing_deadline_date` is the **tenth business day strictly after** `occurrence_date`, computed with the add-business-days rule of §2.6: the occurrence date itself is never counted whether or not it is a business day, and the result is the tenth business day encountered walking forward. The window is measured from the occurrence date, never from the determination date, so a slow determination consumes the same ten business days.

Three refusals guard creation, in this order. Duplicate suppression comes first: no other `MaterialEventNotice` on the same `issue_id` may share the same `event_type` **and** `occurrence_date` while its `status` is neither `NOT_MATERIAL` nor `VOID`; a collision returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVENT_NOTICE`. Then `occurrence_date` must not lie more than 30 calendar days before the logical clock's date, or the request is refused with `ERR_OCCURRENCE_DATE_STALE` — backdating further would mint a notice whose ten-business-day window had already closed at creation. Then `occurrence_date` must not lie after the logical clock's date, or the request is refused with `ERR_OCCURRENCE_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. The duplicate check deliberately outranks both temporal checks, so re-posting a stale duplicate returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVENT_NOTICE` rather than `ERR_OCCURRENCE_DATE_STALE`. All three refusals apply equally to the rating-action call of §3.1, which mints its notice through the same machinery. Creation also re-derives the issue's `open_material_event_count` and the undertaking's `open_obligation_count`, because a `DETERMINATION_PENDING` notice is an open obligation.

Determination is the `ComplianceReviewer`'s act, and it takes a `determination` of `MATERIAL` or `NOT_MATERIAL` plus an optional `determination_note`. Either way the notice records `determined_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `determined_at` from the logical clock, and either way the source status must be `DETERMINATION_PENDING`. But from there the two branches have strictly disjoint consequences. A `MATERIAL` determination leaves the notice in the open set — `open_material_event_count` and `open_obligation_count` are unchanged, the notice becomes fileable, and it remains exposed to the sweep that can strand it as `MISSED`. A `NOT_MATERIAL` determination is terminal and releases the obligation: the notice leaves the open set, both counters fall, no `filing_deadline_date` enforcement ever applies to it again, the calendar sweep skips it permanently, and any later filing that names it is refused with `ERR_FILING_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`.

A per-se listed event can never be determined immaterial. When `per_se_material` is true, a `NOT_MATERIAL` determination is refused with `ERR_PER_SE_EVENT_IS_MATERIAL`. Per-se events nonetheless still pass through `DETERMINATION_PENDING` — they are not auto-determined at creation, which is exactly why the ten-day clock can run out on an undetermined per-se event.

The timeliness rule for a listed event is always submission-based, with no variant and no configuration. When the filing that carries the notice is confirmed, the notice is `FILED_TIMELY` if the date part of the filing's `submitted_at` is **less than or equal to** `filing_deadline_date` — a submission on the deadline date is timely — and `FILED_LATE` otherwise, with `days_late` set to the whole calendar days from `filing_deadline_date` to that submission date, floored at 0. Either branch stamps `filed_filing_id`, re-derives `open_material_event_count` and `open_obligation_count`, and, when `financial_obligation_ref` is set, moves the referenced `DirectPlacement` from `DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` to `DISCLOSED`.

One asymmetry with the annual report is deliberate and must be implemented exactly. **A late material-event filing mints no `FailureToFileEvent`.** Only the sweep's `MISSED` transition does. A notice that is filed after its ten-business-day window closes lands in `FILED_LATE` and stops there; the undertaking's counters and risk score are untouched by it. The obligation is recorded as failed only when the deadline passes with the notice still undischarged and a sweep observes it.

### 3.6. Bank Loans, Direct Placements, and Disclosure Practice

A **`DirectPlacement`** records a bank loan or direct purchase incurred against an outstanding issue. A `DisclosureOfficer` supplies the `issue_id`, the `lender_name` (1 to 120 characters), the `principal_amount_cents` (at least 1), the `execution_date`, the closed review window bounded by `review_window_start_date` and `review_window_end_date`, and the office's declared `disclosure_practice`, which is one of `MANDATORY_MATERIAL_EVENT`, `VOLUNTARY`, or `NOT_DISCLOSED`. The window must be non-empty in the sense that `review_window_end_date` may not precede `review_window_start_date`, and a violation of that or of the principal floor is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The placement opens at `status` `RECEIVED`, `version` 1, with `linked_notice_id` and `waiver_reason` null.

The review window models the finance office's own capacity to assess obligations, and it is a **closed** interval: `review_window_start_date` and `review_window_end_date` are both inclusive, so two windows that merely touch on a single day do overlap. At most two placements whose `status` is not `DISCLOSED` may cover any single calendar date on the same issue. The check is aggregate rather than pairwise — each existing placement is individually legal, and it is the third one covering some shared date that is refused, with `ERR_PLACEMENT_REVIEW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

`materiality_ratio_bp` is derived once, at creation, as `principal_amount_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by the issue's `outstanding_par_amount_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole basis point. It is frozen thereafter: a later defeasance that zeroes outstanding par never re-derives it, and creation against an issue that is not `OUTSTANDING` is refused before the division can be attempted.

Classification is the `ComplianceReviewer`'s act on a `RECEIVED` placement, and the single call forks into two terminal outcomes with disjoint side effects, decided at the 500-basis-point boundary. The placement becomes `DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` when `materiality_ratio_bp` is **greater than or equal to** 500, or when `disclosure_practice` is `MANDATORY_MATERIAL_EVENT` regardless of the ratio. That branch mints a `MaterialEventNotice` with `event_type` `FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION_INCURRENCE`, `issue_id` copied from the placement, `occurrence_date` set to the placement's `execution_date`, `financial_obligation_ref` pointing back at the placement, `per_se_material` false, and `status` `DETERMINATION_PENDING`, with its own `filing_deadline_date` ten business days after that occurrence date; the new notice's id is stored in `linked_notice_id`, and the issue's `open_material_event_count` and the undertaking's `open_obligation_count` are both re-derived. Otherwise — ratio strictly below 500 basis points **and** `disclosure_practice` not `MANDATORY_MATERIAL_EVENT` — the placement becomes `DISCLOSURE_WAIVED`, which requires a `waiver_reason` in the body and is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_REASON_REQUIRED` without one. The waived branch creates no notice, leaves `linked_notice_id` null, and moves no counter at all.

The boundary is inclusive and must be exact. A principal of 5,000,000 cents against outstanding par of 100,000,000 cents is exactly 500 basis points and classifies as `DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`; a principal of 4,990,000 cents against the same par rounds to 499 basis points and classifies as `DISCLOSURE_WAIVED`.

A `DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` placement reaches `DISCLOSED` only as a cascade of a confirmed filing — either a `VOLUNTARY_FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION` filing naming this placement directly, or a `MATERIAL_EVENT_NOTICE` filing naming the notice stored in `linked_notice_id`. Both `DISCLOSED` and `DISCLOSURE_WAIVED` are terminal.

### 3.7. The Repository Submission Surface

A **`Filing`** is the package the issuer hands to the central repository, and it carries exactly one subject. A `DisclosureOfficer` drafts one by naming the `issue_id`, the `filing_type`, and the `expected_issue_version` that arms the concurrency guard of §2.8, plus exactly one of `cycle_id`, `material_event_id`, or `direct_placement_id`, and optionally `supersedes_filing_id` and `correction_reason`. The `filing_type` is one of `ANNUAL_REPORT`, `MATERIAL_EVENT_NOTICE`, `FAILURE_TO_FILE_NOTICE`, or `VOLUNTARY_FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION`. The draft opens at `status` `DRAFT`, `version` 1, `submission_attempt_count` 0, with `submitted_at`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitting_engagement_id`, `repository_receipt_id`, `repository_confirmed_at`, `rejection_reason`, and `superseded_by_filing_id` all null. Creating the draft moves no subject: the subject advances only when the draft is submitted. Because the draft is guarded on the parent issue, accepting it also increments that `BondIssue`'s `version`.

**Subject exclusivity and type agreement.** Exactly one of the three subject fields may be present, and the one present must agree with `filing_type`: `ANNUAL_REPORT` and `FAILURE_TO_FILE_NOTICE` both take `cycle_id`, `MATERIAL_EVENT_NOTICE` takes `material_event_id`, and `VOLUNTARY_FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION` takes `direct_placement_id`. The `issue_id` in the body must equal the subject's own owning issue. And the subject must be in its fileable state: for `ANNUAL_REPORT` the cycle must be `READY_TO_FILE`; for `FAILURE_TO_FILE_NOTICE` the cycle must be `DELINQUENT`; for `MATERIAL_EVENT_NOTICE` the notice must be `MATERIAL`; for `VOLUNTARY_FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION` the placement must be `DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`. Any breach of any part of this returns `ERR_FILING_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`. Corrections invert the last clause: when `supersedes_filing_id` is present, the fileable-state test is replaced by a **discharged**-state test, because a correction re-files a document the repository already holds — the cycle or notice must be `FILED_TIMELY` or `FILED_LATE`, or the placement must be `DISCLOSED`.

**One live filing per subject.** No other `Filing` may already carry the identical four-field subject tuple of `filing_type`, `cycle_id`, `material_event_id`, and `direct_placement_id` in `status` `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, or `CONFIRMED`, unless this new draft supplies `supersedes_filing_id` naming that exact `CONFIRMED` filing; otherwise the draft is refused with `ERR_SUBJECT_ALREADY_FILED`. Because the tuple includes `filing_type`, a delinquent cycle may legitimately carry both a `FAILURE_TO_FILE_NOTICE` filing and, once reassembled, an `ANNUAL_REPORT` filing. When `supersedes_filing_id` is present the target must be `CONFIRMED`, must reference the same subject id, and must not already be superseded — its `superseded_by_filing_id` must be null — or the draft is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`. A correcting draft must also carry a non-empty `correction_reason`, checked at draft creation and refused with `ERR_CORRECTION_REASON_REQUIRED`.

**Submission.** Only a `DisseminationAgent` submits. The filing must be `DRAFT`; the acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to a `DisseminationEngagement` whose `undertaking_id` is the undertaking of the filing's issue, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ENGAGED`; that engagement's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the submission must not be the fourth attempt on this filing, since at most three submissions and resubmissions are permitted in total and the fourth is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. A successful submission sets `submitted_at` from the logical clock, `submitted_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, `submitting_engagement_id` to the resolved engagement, and raises `submission_attempt_count` by 1. It then advances the subject: an `ANNUAL_REPORT` filing drives its cycle from `READY_TO_FILE` to `FILING_IN_PROGRESS`, and a `MATERIAL_EVENT_NOTICE` filing drives its notice from `MATERIAL` to `FILING_IN_PROGRESS`. A correcting filing leaves the subject's status untouched, since the subject is already discharged.

**Rejection and resubmission.** Recording a repository rejection requires a `rejection_reason` and moves a `SUBMITTED` filing to `REJECTED`; only the engagement in `submitting_engagement_id` may do it, and any other agent is refused with `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ENGAGED`. Rejection is a compensating transition: it returns the cycle from `FILING_IN_PROGRESS` to `READY_TO_FILE`, or the notice from `FILING_IN_PROGRESS` back to `MATERIAL`, and it mints no failure event. It does not raise `billable_filing_count` — a rejected submission is not billable. Resubmission takes a fresh `correction_reason`, requires `status` `REJECTED`, re-resolves an `ACTIVE` engagement for the filing's undertaking, and is subject to the same three-attempt ceiling. It clears `rejection_reason` to null, **overwrites** `submitted_at` with the current clock instant, raises `submission_attempt_count`, re-stamps `submitting_engagement_id`, and drives the subject back into `FILING_IN_PROGRESS`. Because timeliness is judged on the latest `submitted_at` and the earlier rejected attempt is discarded, a rejection loop can convert a filing that would have been timely into a late one.

**Confirmation.** Recording the repository's confirmation takes a `repository_receipt_id`, requires `status` `SUBMITTED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be the `submitting_engagement_id`, and requires the receipt id to be unique across every `Filing` row, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_ID_DUPLICATE`. It sets `repository_confirmed_at` from the logical clock and stamps the receipt. This is the most consequential call in the service, and its cascade runs in a fixed order.

Hop 1 discharges the subject. For an `ANNUAL_REPORT` filing the timeliness assessment of §3.8 runs and moves the cycle from `FILING_IN_PROGRESS` to `FILED_TIMELY` or `FILED_LATE`, writing `filed_at`, `days_late`, `timeliness_basis_applied`, and `filed_filing_id`. For a `MATERIAL_EVENT_NOTICE` filing the notice moves to `FILED_TIMELY` or `FILED_LATE` on the submission-date comparison of §3.5. For a `VOLUNTARY_FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION` filing the placement moves from `DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` to `DISCLOSED`. **Hop 1 is skipped entirely for a correcting filing:** when `supersedes_filing_id` is set, the timeliness branch does not re-run, the subject's `status`, `filed_at`, `days_late`, and `timeliness_basis_applied` are all left exactly as they stood, and no new `FailureToFileEvent` is minted. Hop 2 runs only when hop 1 produced a `FILED_LATE` **cycle**, and creates the `FailureToFileEvent` described in §3.8. Hop 3 re-derives the undertaking's counters and then re-runs the risk-score pass, which may move `compliance_posture`. Hop 4 re-derives the submitting engagement's `billable_filing_count` and then its `invoice_total_cents`, the latter sensitive to the posture hop 3 just wrote. Hop 5 re-runs the official-statement lookback for every `OfficialStatement` in `COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` whose issue shares the failure event's `issuer_program_code`, which can flip `disclosure_adequacy` to `INCOMPLETE`.

Finally, when `supersedes_filing_id` is set, the target filing moves from `CONFIRMED` to `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_filing_id` is stamped with this filing's id, and the subject's `filed_filing_id` is repointed at this filing. Because the target leaves the `CONFIRMED` set at the same moment this filing enters it, the engagement's `billable_filing_count` is unchanged and the invoice total is identical — a correction is never double-billed. The confirmation also re-derives `open_obligation_count` and `open_material_event_count`. `SUPERSEDED` is the only terminal `Filing` status.

### 3.8. Failure to File: Timeliness, Counters, Risk Score, Posture, and Remediation

**Annual-report timeliness has two variants**, selected per undertaking by its persisted `timeliness_basis` and recorded on the cycle as `timeliness_basis_applied` so the verdict is auditable after the fact. Under `SUBMISSION_BASIS`, the default, the basis instant is the filing's `submitted_at` — the moment the agent handed the document to the repository — so a document submitted on the due date is timely even if the repository confirms it days later. Under `CONFIRMATION_BASIS` the basis instant is the filing's `repository_confirmed_at`. In both variants the cycle's `filed_at` is set to the chosen basis instant, the cycle is timely when the date part of that instant is **less than or equal to** `due_date` (an inclusive boundary), and `days_late` is 0 when timely and otherwise the whole calendar days from `due_date` to that basis date. Only the filing being confirmed is read; earlier rejected submissions of the same filing are invisible, because `submitted_at` always holds the latest submission instant. For one and the same filing — identical `submitted_at`, identical `repository_confirmed_at` — `CONFIRMATION_BASIS` can yield `FILED_LATE` where `SUBMISSION_BASIS` yields `FILED_TIMELY`, and therefore mints a failure event, raises `late_filing_count`, and re-runs the risk score, none of which the other variant does.

A **`FailureToFileEvent`** is never created by a direct request. It arises in exactly three ways. A cycle reaching `FILED_LATE` mints one with `kind` `ANNUAL_REPORT_LATE`, `undertaking_id` and `cycle_id` from the cycle, `material_event_id` null, `occurred_at` set to the cycle's `due_date`, `days_late` copied from the cycle's `days_late`, and `status` `OPEN` — but only if no event with that `cycle_id` already exists; if the delinquency sweep already minted one, its `kind` is left unchanged, its `days_late` is refreshed, and its `status` moves from `OPEN` to `REMEDIATION_PENDING`. The delinquency step of the sweep mints one with `kind` `ANNUAL_REPORT_MISSED`, `occurred_at` set to the cycle's `due_date`, `days_late` set to the calendar days from that due date to the sweep's clock date, and `severity` `MAJOR`. The missed-event step of the sweep mints one with `kind` `MATERIAL_EVENT_MISSED`, `material_event_id` set to the notice, `cycle_id` null, `occurred_at` set to the notice's `filing_deadline_date`, and `severity` `MAJOR`. A cycle reaching `FILED_TIMELY` mints nothing, but every event on that cycle still `OPEN` moves to `REMEDIATION_PENDING`.

`severity` is `MAJOR` when `kind` is `ANNUAL_REPORT_MISSED` or `MATERIAL_EVENT_MISSED`, or when `days_late` is **greater than or equal to** 30; it is `MINOR` otherwise. Both missed kinds are therefore `MAJOR` regardless of their `days_late`. For an `ANNUAL_REPORT_LATE` event the boundary is exact: 29 days late is `MINOR`, 30 days late is `MAJOR`. `days_late` and `severity` are frozen at creation; later remediation never rewrites either. `disclosed_in_official_statement_ids` starts as an empty array and is appended to by the official-statement machinery of §3.9. `remediation_filing_id`, `remediation_note`, and `remediated_at` start null.

**The three undertaking counters** are always recomputed from source, never incremented in place. `late_filing_count` is the number of that undertaking's failure events whose `kind` is `ANNUAL_REPORT_LATE` or `ANNUAL_REPORT_MISSED`. `missed_event_count` is the number whose `kind` is `MATERIAL_EVENT_MISSED`. Both are **monotone**: remediating an event does not decrement them, because the historical failure still has to be disclosed. `delinquent_cycle_count` is the number of that undertaking's cycles currently in `status` `DELINQUENT`, and it is **live** — a cycle leaving `DELINQUENT` for `FILED_LATE` or `READY_TO_FILE` decrements it, which is what lets `compliance_posture` recover. These counters, together with `content_weight_bp_total`, are re-derived before the risk-score pass runs in the same request.

**The disclosure risk score** runs whenever any of those three counters is written. Let L be `late_filing_count`, M be `missed_event_count`, D be `delinquent_cycle_count`, and let T be the undertaking's **persisted** `remediation_tier` as it stood before this pass. Step 1 forms an additive base of 12 times L, plus 20 times M, plus 15 times D. Step 2 escalates that base by the tier multiplier for T — 1.00 for `TIER_0`, 1.10 for `TIER_1`, 1.25 for `TIER_2`, 1.40 for `TIER_3` — and rounds the product half-up to 2 decimal places. Step 3 writes `disclosure_risk_score` as the lesser of 100.00 and that rounded product. Step 4 derives the new `remediation_tier` from the score just written, using inclusive lower bounds: `TIER_3` at 75.00 or above, else `TIER_2` at 50.00 or above, else `TIER_1` at 25.00 or above, else `TIER_0`. Step 5 persists both fields. The pass runs **exactly once** per trigger and is never iterated to a fixed point, so `disclosure_risk_score` and `remediation_tier` may legitimately disagree by one escalation step until the next trigger fires.

The distinction between the persisted tier and the freshly derived one is load-bearing. Take a fresh undertaking and let it suffer three successive `MATERIAL_EVENT_MISSED` failures. After the first, the base is 20, the persisted tier is `TIER_0`, the score is 20.00, and the new tier is `TIER_0`. After the second, the base is 40, the persisted tier is still `TIER_0`, the score is 40.00, and the new tier becomes `TIER_1`. After the third, the base is 60, the persisted tier is `TIER_1`, so the score is 66.00 and the new tier becomes `TIER_2`. Reading the tier this pass is about to derive, instead of the one persisted from the previous pass, would give 75.00 and `TIER_3` on the third pass. That reading is wrong.

Step 6 derives `compliance_posture`, and here any delinquent cycle dominates the score bands outright: the posture is `DELINQUENT` whenever D is 1 or more, regardless of score; otherwise `IMPAIRED` at a score of 50.00 or above; otherwise `WATCH` at 25.00 or above; otherwise `COMPLIANT`. Note that `compliance_posture` is entirely independent of the undertaking's own `status` — suspending or reinstating an undertaking never touches the posture, the score, or any counter.

**Remediation** closes a failure event without erasing it. A `DisclosureOfficer` supplies a `remediation_filing_id` and a `remediation_note` against an event in `status` `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PENDING`, and the event moves to `REMEDIATED` with `remediated_at` stamped from the logical clock. The named filing must be `CONFIRMED`, and its subject must belong to the same undertaking as the event; the test has two arms, keyed on the event's own subject. When the event's `cycle_id` is not null, the filing's `cycle_id` must equal it — the delinquent annual report is exactly what still has to be filed. When the event's `cycle_id` is null, meaning a material-event failure whose notice is terminally `MISSED` and can therefore never itself be filed, any `CONFIRMED` filing of the same undertaking is acceptable evidence, including a later annual report or an unrelated event notice. A failure of either arm returns `ERR_REMEDIATION_FILING_INVALID`. Remediation changes no counter, no `disclosure_risk_score`, and no `remediation_tier`; the score does not fall. It does re-run the official-statement lookback for every `OfficialStatement` in `COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` whose issue shares the `issuer_program_code`, because under one lookback variant this event may now drop out of the required set. `REMEDIATED` is terminal.

### 3.9. Official Statements and the Five-Year Failure-to-File Lookback

When the issuer offers a new series, the offering document must describe the issuer's recent continuing-disclosure failures. An **`OfficialStatement`** models that document. A `DisclosureOfficer` drafts one against the new `issue_id`, naming its `dated_date` and optionally a `lookback_policy` (default `STRICT_FIVE_YEAR`) and an initial `disclosed_event_ids` array (default empty). The draft opens at `status` `DRAFT`, `version` 1, `disclosure_adequacy` `UNKNOWN`, `required_disclosure_event_ids` empty, and `required_disclosure_event_count` 0, with `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at`, `posted_by_user_id`, and `posted_at` all null. The lookback does not run at drafting; it runs first at compliance review. `disclosed_event_ids` is the drafter's assertion of what the document actually says, and it may not contain duplicate ids.

**The lookback.** The window's right edge is `dated_date`, exclusive. Its left edge is `dated_date` less five calendar years — the same month and day five years earlier, with 29 February mapping to 28 February in a non-leap year — and that edge is inclusive. The scope is the credit **program**, not the single issue: let the scope program be the `issuer_program_code` of the official statement's own `BondIssue`, and the candidate set is every `FailureToFileEvent` whose undertaking hangs off any `BondIssue` sharing that `issuer_program_code`, the statement's own issue included, whose `occurred_at` is at or after the left edge and strictly before `dated_date`. Failure events in every `status` are candidates — `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_PENDING`, and `REMEDIATED` alike. An event that occurred exactly five years before the dated date is in scope; one that occurred on the dated date is not.

Two variants then filter that candidate set. Under `STRICT_FIVE_YEAR` the required set is the candidate set unchanged: every in-window failure must be described in the document, even ones already cured, which yields the larger required set. Under `REMEDIATED_EXCLUDED` an event drops out when its `status` is `REMEDIATED` **and** the date part of its `remediated_at` is strictly before `dated_date`; a failure remediated on or after the dated date stays in. For the identical failure population `REMEDIATED_EXCLUDED` therefore yields a smaller `required_disclosure_event_count` and can flip `disclosure_adequacy` from `INCOMPLETE` to `ADEQUATE`, which in turn decides whether the document may be posted at all.

`required_disclosure_event_ids` is the surviving set sorted by `occurred_at` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by id. `required_disclosure_event_count` is its length. `disclosure_adequacy` is `ADEQUATE` when every id in `required_disclosure_event_ids` appears in `disclosed_event_ids`, and `INCOMPLETE` otherwise; ids in `disclosed_event_ids` that are not required are ignored rather than treated as errors, so an over-inclusive document is adequate.

**Compliance review** is the `ComplianceReviewer`'s act and is a repeatable self-loop: it is available from `DRAFT` and from `COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`, and it requires the statement's issue to be `OUTSTANDING`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_OUTSTANDING`. Supplied `disclosed_event_ids` and `lookback_policy` overwrite the stored values wholesale, and omitted keys leave them untouched. Any id in `disclosed_event_ids` that does not resolve to a `FailureToFileEvent` is refused with `ERR_FAILURE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`. The call then runs the lookback under the now-current policy, writes all three computed fields, and stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `reviewed_at` from the logical clock — overwriting them on every self-loop, so the reviewer of record is the most recent one. It also stamps this statement's id into `disclosed_in_official_statement_ids` on every failure event that appears in both `required_disclosure_event_ids` and `disclosed_event_ids`. The reviewer widens `disclosed_event_ids` or switches `lookback_policy` and re-reviews until `disclosure_adequacy` is `ADEQUATE`.

**Posting** is available only from `COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` and is guarded twice. The lookback is **re-run at post time**, and posting is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INADEQUATE` unless the freshly recomputed `disclosure_adequacy` is `ADEQUATE` — so a failure event minted between the review and the posting blocks the post even though the stored verdict said `ADEQUATE`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `reviewed_by_user_id`, or the post is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; a `ComplianceReviewer` may not both review and publish the same document, and no role hierarchy can satisfy the prohibition because both acts belong to the same role. A successful post stamps `posted_by_user_id` and `posted_at`, and appends this statement's id to `disclosed_in_official_statement_ids` on every failure event listed in `required_disclosure_event_ids`, in `required_disclosure_event_ids` order. `POSTED` is terminal.

### 3.10. Dissemination Agent Engagements, Calendar Monitoring, and Invoicing

A **`DisseminationEngagement`** is the contract with the firm that carries documents to the repository, and — as §2.1 establishes — the engagement's own `id` is the `X-User-ID` a `DisseminationAgent` presents. A `FinanceDirector` creates one against an `ACTIVE` undertaking of an `OUTSTANDING` issue, naming the `undertaking_id`, the `agent_name` (1 to 120 characters), the `effective_date` and `expiration_date` of the term (the expiration may not precede the effective date), the annual `retainer_cents` (zero or more), the `expected_undertaking_version` that arms the concurrency guard of §2.8, and optionally an initial `last_calendar_ack_date`. The engagement opens at `status` `ACTIVE`, `version` 1, `billable_filing_count` 0, with `superseded_by_engagement_id` and `termination_reason` null. `monitoring_lapse_days` and `invoice_total_cents` are both derived immediately at creation, the latter meaning the minimum annual fee floor binds from the very first moment. Accepting the engagement increments the parent `Undertaking`'s `version`.

Creating a newer engagement supersedes the incumbent rather than colliding with it: every other non-`TERMINATED` engagement on the same undertaking is driven to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` `SUPERSEDED` and `superseded_by_engagement_id` pointing at the new row, and its `invoice_total_cents` freezes at whatever final value it then held.

**Calendar monitoring.** `monitoring_lapse_days` is the whole calendar days from the later-of-nothing baseline — `last_calendar_ack_date` when it is set, and `effective_date` when it is null — to the logical clock's date, floored at 0. It is recomputed on every acknowledgement and on every sweep, and it is computed for engagements in `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` but frozen for `TERMINATED`. Acknowledgement is the agent's own act: it takes a `last_calendar_ack_date`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the engagement's own id (else `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ENGAGED`), and requires that date to be at or after `effective_date` and at or before the logical clock's date (else `ERR_ACK_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`). From `ACTIVE` it is a self-loop that resets the lapse clock; from `SUSPENDED` it is the cure that restores the engagement to `ACTIVE` and re-enables submissions. Either way it stamps the new `last_calendar_ack_date` and refreshes `monitoring_lapse_days`.

Suspension is a sweep-only transition and fires when `monitoring_lapse_days` is **strictly greater than** 45, so a lapse of exactly 45 days is still compliant while 46 days suspends. A `SUSPENDED` engagement can no longer submit or resubmit filings, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The `ACTIVE`-to-`SUSPENDED`-to-`ACTIVE` cycle can repeat without limit.

**Termination** is the `FinanceDirector`'s act, available from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED`, and it takes no payload. It is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_HAS_LIVE_FILING` while any `Filing` whose `submitting_engagement_id` is this engagement still sits in `SUBMITTED`, since only the submitting agent may carry the repository's response back and terminating first would strand the filing. A successful termination sets `termination_reason` to `ISSUER_TERMINATED` and freezes `invoice_total_cents`: the invoice calculation is never re-run for a `TERMINATED` engagement, whatever later happens to filings or posture. `TERMINATED` is terminal, and it is also reached as the cascade of a superseding engagement (`termination_reason` `SUPERSEDED`) and as hop 2b of the undertaking termination cascade of §3.1 (`termination_reason` `UNDERTAKING_TERMINATED`).

**Invoicing.** The invoice is re-derived whenever the engagement's billable count changes — that is, on a filing reaching `CONFIRMED` and on a filing leaving `CONFIRMED` for `SUPERSEDED` — and whenever the owning undertaking's `compliance_posture` is written. It is skipped entirely for a `TERMINATED` engagement.

The first input is the conserved filing count: let F be the number of `Filing` rows whose `submitting_engagement_id` is this engagement and whose `status` is exactly `CONFIRMED`. Filings in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, and `SUPERSEDED` are all excluded, which is precisely why a correction that supersedes its predecessor leaves F unchanged. F is written to `billable_filing_count`.

The second input is a **marginal**, never averaged, tier allocation over F. The first four confirmed filings are billed at 450 dollars each; filings five through nine are billed at 325 dollars each; filings ten and beyond are billed at 250 dollars each. In cents: take the lesser of F and 4 at 45000 cents, plus the lesser of 5 and the excess of F over 4 (floored at zero) at 32500 cents, plus the excess of F over 9 (floored at zero) at 25000 cents. Those filing fees are added to the engagement's `retainer_cents` to give a subtotal.

The third input is a conditional surcharge on the owning undertaking's `compliance_posture`. When the posture is `IMPAIRED` or `DELINQUENT`, the surcharge is 15 percent of the subtotal — computed on the subtotal **before** any cap, rounded half-up to whole cents — and then itself capped at 1,500 dollars (150000 cents). For any other posture the surcharge is zero.

The cap and the floor then apply in this exact nested order: the subtotal plus surcharge is first capped at 12,000 dollars (1200000 cents), and that capped figure is then raised to the minimum annual fee of 3,000 dollars (300000 cents) if it falls below it. The result is `invoice_total_cents`.

Two worked cases pin the order. With F of 6, a `retainer_cents` of 250000, and posture `IMPAIRED`: filing fees are four at 45000 plus two at 32500, which is 245000; the subtotal is 495000; the surcharge is the lesser of 74250 and 150000, which is 74250; the capped figure is the lesser of 569250 and 1200000, which is 569250; and `invoice_total_cents` is the greater of 569250 and 300000, which is 569250. With F of 1, a `retainer_cents` of 250000, and posture `COMPLIANT`: the subtotal is 295000, the surcharge is 0, the capped figure is 295000, and `invoice_total_cents` is 300000 — the floor binds.

### 3.11. The Dated Calendar Sweep

Elapsed time produces no consequence on its own in this service. Setting the logical clock forward changes what deadline comparisons return but writes no row; deadlines materialise only when the ops-run calendar sweep is invoked by a `FinanceDirector`. The sweep takes no payload. Its five steps execute in the pinned order 1 through 5, and within each step candidate rows are iterated `created_at` ascending with `id` ascending as the tie-break.

**Step 1 — maturity.** Every `BondIssue` in `status` `OUTSTANDING` whose `final_maturity_date` is strictly before the logical clock's date moves to `MATURED`, its `outstanding_par_amount_cents` is set to 0, its `matured_at` is stamped from the clock, and it fires the same four-hop termination cascade as defeasance with `termination_cause` `MATURITY`: the undertaking to `TERMINATED`, its non-terminal cycles to `CLOSED_NOT_REQUIRED`, its non-`TERMINATED` engagements to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` `UNDERTAKING_TERMINATED`, and the non-`VOID` content items of those cycles to `VOID`. Every `DETERMINATION_PENDING` or `MATERIAL` notice on the issue is voided. Unlike defeasance, maturity mints no notice of its own.

**Step 2 — delinquency.** Every `AnnualReportCycle` in `status` `DRAFTING` or `READY_TO_FILE` whose `due_date` is strictly before the logical clock's date moves to `DELINQUENT`, with `assembled_by_user_id` and `assembled_at` cleared. Each such cycle mints a `FailureToFileEvent` with `kind` `ANNUAL_REPORT_MISSED`, `occurred_at` set to the cycle's `due_date`, `days_late` set to the calendar days from that due date to the clock date, `severity` `MAJOR`, and `status` `OPEN`. The undertaking's `delinquent_cycle_count` is re-derived and the risk score re-runs, which drives `compliance_posture` to `DELINQUENT`.

The order of steps 1 and 2 is load-bearing. An issue matured in step 1 has already closed its cycles to `CLOSED_NOT_REQUIRED`, so step 2 cannot also mark those same cycles delinquent. Running step 2 first would produce spurious failure events against obligations that had already ceased to exist.

**Step 3 — missed listed events.** Every `MaterialEventNotice` in `status` `MATERIAL` whose `filing_deadline_date` is strictly before the logical clock's date moves to `MISSED`, with `days_late` set to the calendar days from `filing_deadline_date` to the clock date. Each mints a `FailureToFileEvent` with `kind` `MATERIAL_EVENT_MISSED`, `material_event_id` set to the notice, `cycle_id` null, `occurred_at` set to the notice's `filing_deadline_date`, `severity` `MAJOR`, and `status` `OPEN`. A notice still in `DETERMINATION_PENDING` is not swept, and a notice determined `NOT_MATERIAL` is skipped permanently.

**Step 4 — monitoring lapse.** Every `DisseminationEngagement` in `status` `ACTIVE` whose refreshed `monitoring_lapse_days` exceeds 45 moves to `SUSPENDED`.

**Step 5 — recomputation.** For every undertaking touched by steps 1 through 4, the delinquency counters are re-derived, then the disclosure risk score pass runs, then `open_obligation_count` is re-derived, then the dissemination-agent invoice is re-derived, then the derived flags — `open_material_event_count` above all — are re-derived. This ordering matters because the invoice reads the `compliance_posture` the risk pass has just written.

The response is not an entity row. It reports `swept_at`, the logical-clock instant of the sweep, alongside five counts: `matured_issue_count` for step 1, `delinquent_cycle_count` for step 2, `missed_event_count` for step 3, `suspended_engagement_count` for step 4, and `failure_event_created_count` for every `FailureToFileEvent` minted across steps 2 and 3 together. The sweep is idempotent for a fixed clock: a second sweep at the same clock instant finds no candidates and returns all five counts as 0.
