# Athletic Field Turf Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A municipal sports turf management department carries an inventory of playing surfaces that behave nothing alike. A natural grass field needs aeration, overseeding, fertility and irrigation scheduled into the gaps between practices and tournaments; a synthetic field needs grooming, infill redistribution and seam inspection, and its infill depth is a certifiable specification rather than a horticultural judgement. Both need impact attenuation testing against published g-max thresholds, both need striping and goal-anchoring inspections before sanctioned play, and both wear out — which means both eventually need a capital replacement plan, a waste manifest for whatever synthetic material has to leave the site, and a retirement date.

Today those concerns are tracked in disconnected spreadsheets, and the failures they produce are always the same failure: somebody books a tournament on a field that a safety inspector had already condemned, or a certification document goes out to a sanctioning body citing an agronomic programme that was never actually completed. The department therefore wants one service in which a field's **playability posture** and its **certification posture** are not opinions typed in by a coordinator but values the server derives from the evidence on record — open safety remediation, outstanding rest obligations, administrative closures, and the approval state of the certification paperwork. Nothing books, nothing certifies and nothing gets funded except through those derived postures.

This document specifies that service. It is mounted under `/turf`, with one out-of-band test-driving route for the server's logical clock. The reader should treat the derived posture rules in §2 and the workflow narratives in §3 as jointly normative: every gate that blocks an operation is stated in the workflow that the gate guards, and the endpoint and error tables in §4 and §5 deliberately carry no trigger conditions of their own.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the individual making the call, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, or present but empty or consisting only of whitespace, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). Beyond that emptiness test neither value is trimmed: `X-User-Role` is matched against the five declared names exactly as sent, and `X-User-ID` is resolved exactly as sent. If `X-User-Role` is a string that is not one of the five declared role names, the request fails with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401).

The five roles are `FieldOperationsCoordinator`, `TurfAgronomist`, `SafetyInspector`, `CertificationOfficer` and `FacilitiesDirector`. A `FieldOperationsCoordinator` allocates fields to activities and closes out what actually happened on them. A `TurfAgronomist` runs the agronomic and synthetic maintenance programme and offers completed remediation work up for verification. A `SafetyInspector` performs impact-attenuation, goal-anchoring and striping inspections and rules on remediation verifications — but may never submit the remediation work it then judges. A `CertificationOfficer` assembles and decides certification packets for sanctioned competition. A `FacilitiesDirector` owns the field inventory, the staff roster, administrative closures, capital budgets and replacement planning, and is the only role permitted to drive the logical clock.

All five roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldOperationsCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`; the same holds when `X-User-Role` is `TurfAgronomist`, when it is `SafetyInspector`, when it is `CertificationOfficer`, and when it is `FacilitiesDirector`. Ownership and segregation checks on any resource that references a `StaffMember` therefore compare that resource's staff-member reference directly to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the acting identity must resolve to a `StaffMember` row whose `roles` array contains the value in `X-User-Role` and whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`; if it does not — the id is unknown, the person does not hold that role, or the person's `employment_status` is `INACTIVE` — the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The single exemption is `POST /turf/staff-members`, which does not perform this resolution, so that the very first `FacilitiesDirector` row can be bootstrapped into an empty database.

Once the identity resolves, the role must appear in the authorised-roles list for the endpoint being called, as recorded in §4. A role acting outside that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Every `GET` endpoint in this service is readable by all five roles; the write endpoints are partitioned as §4 shows.

A `StaffMember` carries a `full_name` for the inspection and certification paperwork, and its `roles` array may name more than one role — one person can legitimately be dual-hatted. That is precisely why role membership alone never authorises a decision. **Segregation of duties** is a separate, independent control: on the four decision and verification endpoints the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the staff member who submitted the artefact under decision (and, for certification approval, additionally against the inspector who produced the governing safety assessment), and a match is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A person holding both `CertificationOfficer` and `SafetyInspector` therefore passes the role check and is still blocked at the second step. The exact comparisons are set out in §2.10 and again in the workflows that use them.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints; `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned on this page is still strictly less than `total_count`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Two envelope-level failures fall outside the §5 catalogue because they are not business rules: a path matching no route at all is answered `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a path matching a route only under a different method is answered `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405).

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Results on every list endpoint are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching record remains beyond the current page.

Filter parameters are additive: supplying several narrows the result set, and an unrecognised value for an enumerated filter simply matches nothing rather than erroring. Matching is by exact, case-sensitive string comparison against the stored value rendered as a string, with the single exception of a boolean-valued filter such as `planned_for_certification_window`, which accepts `true` or `false` in any letter case and matches nothing for anything else; a row whose value for the filtered attribute is null never matches.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix — a field id and a booking id are indistinguishable by inspection, so path parameters must be resolved against the collection the route names rather than parsed. Clients never supply `id`.

Nine of the ten resource kinds — `Field`, `StaffMember`, `MaintenanceTask`, `SafetyAssessment`, `RemediationOrder`, `UsageBooking`, `CertificationPacket`, `CapitalBudget` and `ReplacementPlan` — carry the full set of common properties: `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording creation; `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. `UsageLedgerEntry` is the exception: because a ledger entry is immutable once appended, it carries only `id` and `created_at`. A ledger entry has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and none should be emitted for it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are real-time audit metadata rather than business instants: the server stamps them from the actual UTC time at which the row was written or last mutated, not from the logical clock described in the next paragraph, which is reserved for the instants this document binds to it explicitly (`effective_at_utc`, `opened_at_utc`, `started_at_utc`, `confirmed_at_utc`, `closed_at_utc` and their kind). A `UsageLedgerEntry`'s `created_at` is stamped the same way. Two rows written in the course of one request therefore carry `created_at` values that do not decrease in write order, and the `created_at` tie-breakers of §2.9 and §3.3 resolve any remaining tie in favour of the order in which the server wrote the rows.

All instants in request bodies and responses are ISO 8601 UTC. Two request inputs are calendar dates rather than instants and are accepted as such: `installed_on_date` and `warranty_expires_on` each take either a bare `YYYY-MM-DD`, which is read as midnight UTC on that day, or a full ISO 8601 instant, and each is stored and echoed back in the response in the form the client supplied; a value of any other shape, or one that is not a string, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every other temporal input — every `*_utc` body field, and `to_iso8601` on the clock route — remains strictly instant-only. Those too are echoed back byte-for-byte in the exact lexical form the client sent, including a trailing non-`Z` UTC offset, which is honoured when the instant is compared but never rewritten; an instant the server stamps from the logical clock is emitted in exactly the form the clock currently holds, and an instant the server computes for itself — `certified_until_utc`, `rest_window_end_utc` — is rendered canonically, carrying three fractional digits only when it has sub-second detail and none at all when it does not. The server does not read the operating system clock for business decisions; it keeps a **logical clock**, referred to throughout this document as the current instant. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. Only a `FacilitiesDirector` may move it, by supplying `to_iso8601`; the new instant must be strictly later than the clock's present value, and a request that would leave it unchanged or move it backwards is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). A successful advance returns the new `current_utc` together with `fields_recomputed`, the count of fields whose derived values changed as a result — that is, the count of fields for which at least one of `age_years`, `warranty_status`, `play_status`, `certification_status`, `certified_until_utc`, `latest_gmax_mean_value`, `cumulative_wear_units`, `usage_hours_ytd`, `rest_debt_hours`, `nitrogen_applied_ytd_lb_per_1000_sq_ft`, `condition_index_agronomic`, `open_mandatory_remediation_count`, `open_advisory_remediation_count`, `open_certification_packet_count`, `open_replacement_plan_count` or `unsafe_cancellation_count` differs from the value it held immediately before the advance. The `data` object of that response carries those two keys alone and is not a resource.

### 2.5. The Derived Play Posture: One Gate for All Field Use

`Field.play_status` is the single most consequential value in the system, and no endpoint writes it directly. It is recomputed from the field's own derived counters whenever anything that feeds them changes — on field registration, on an administrative closure order, on the creation, closure or cancellation of any remediation order, on maintenance-task completion, on booking close-out, on replacement-plan disposal completion, and for every field on every clock advance.

The five postures are `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `CLOSED_ADMIN`, `CLOSED_UNSAFE` and `RETIRED`, and precedence between them is strict with the first match winning. If `retired_at` is not null the posture is `RETIRED`. Otherwise, if `open_mandatory_remediation_count` is greater than zero the posture is `CLOSED_UNSAFE`. Otherwise, if `admin_closure_active` is true the posture is `CLOSED_ADMIN`. Otherwise, if `rest_debt_hours` is strictly greater than 0.00 or `open_advisory_remediation_count` is greater than zero, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Failing all of those, the posture is `OPEN`. Note the strictness of the rest-debt comparison: a debt of exactly 0.00 does **not** restrict the field. Note also the ordering: an unsafe closure outranks an administrative one, so a field carrying an open mandatory remediation order reads `CLOSED_UNSAFE` even if a director has separately flagged it closed.

Posture determines which activity classes may be allocated. An `OPEN` field accepts all four of `PRACTICE`, `GAME`, `TOURNAMENT` and `COMMUNITY`. A `RESTRICTED` field accepts only `PRACTICE` and `COMMUNITY`. A `CLOSED_ADMIN`, `CLOSED_UNSAFE` or `RETIRED` field accepts no activity at all. Two distinct errors express these refusals and they are not interchangeable: a posture of `CLOSED_ADMIN`, `CLOSED_UNSAFE` or `RETIRED` yields `ERR_FIELD_NOT_PLAYABLE` (HTTP 422), whereas an `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` field that simply will not take the requested intensity yields `ERR_FIELD_RESTRICTED_FOR_ACTIVITY` (HTTP 422). Both gates apply at booking creation and again at booking confirmation.

`Field.certification_status` is the second derived posture, and it too is never written directly. Its four values are `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `CERTIFIED` and `EXPIRED`, and it is recomputed after every certification-packet transition and for every field on every clock advance. The evaluation is again first-match-wins over the field's packets, considering only those with status `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` — `DRAFT`, `RETURNED` and `SUPERSEDED` packets are ignored entirely. If `retired_at` is not null the status is `NOT_CERTIFIED`. Otherwise, if an `APPROVED` packet exists on the field and its `certified_until_utc` is strictly later than the current instant, the status is `CERTIFIED`. Otherwise, if any packet on the field is `SUBMITTED`, the status is `PENDING_REVIEW`. Otherwise, if an `APPROVED` packet exists at all, the status is `EXPIRED`. Failing all of those, the status is `NOT_CERTIFIED`. At most one packet on a field is ever `APPROVED`, because approving a newer one supersedes the older. `Field.certified_until_utc` mirrors the approved packet's own `certified_until_utc` while the status is `CERTIFIED` and is null otherwise; the validity comparison is strict, so a certification whose `certified_until_utc` exactly equals the current instant has already lapsed. Because `CERTIFIED` outranks `PENDING_REVIEW` in that ordering, submitting a packet for next season never revokes an in-force certification.

The two postures cycle. A field may run `NOT_CERTIFIED` → `PENDING_REVIEW` → `CERTIFIED` → `EXPIRED` → `PENDING_REVIEW` indefinitely, and `certification_status` has no terminal value. `play_status` has exactly one terminal value, `RETIRED`; every other posture is reachable in both directions.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision, Rounding and Monetary Units

Rounding in this service is always **half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity rather than per caller.

Most derived quantities are carried to two decimal places: `age_years`, `cumulative_wear_units`, `usage_hours_ytd`, `rest_debt_hours`, `nitrogen_applied_ytd_lb_per_1000_sq_ft`, `condition_index_agronomic`, `condition_index_certification`, `documentation_completeness_score`, `wear_load_units`, `rest_requirement_hours`, `rest_credit_hours`, `wear_units_delta`, `running_balance_after`, `replacement_priority_score` and `diversion_ratio` are all two-decimal values rounded half up. Two quantities are carried to one decimal place: `gmax_mean_value`, the mean of an impact test's readings, and each individual reading inside `gmax_readings`. `infill_depth_mm` is likewise a one-decimal measurement, expressed in millimetres and constrained to the range 0.0 through 80.0 inclusive. A fertility application's `nitrogen_lb_per_1000_sq_ft` is a two-decimal rate constrained to the range 0.00 through 6.00 inclusive. Both quantities are stored rounded half up to their own precision. Those two range constraints, and every other numeric range this document states, are ordinary shape checks evaluated with the rest of the body: a value outside the range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, never the pairing error that governs whether the field may be present at all.

Where a formula composes several intermediate terms, the **order of rounding is part of the specification** and is stated explicitly in the workflow that owns the formula. Two conventions appear in this system and they must not be swapped. The wear calculation multiplies its factors at full precision and rounds exactly once, at the very end. The certification completeness score does the opposite: it rounds each weighted component to two decimal places *before* summing them, and then rounds the sum again — summing at full precision and rounding once produces a different value and is wrong here. Likewise the condition index rounds each of its three components to two decimals first and only then applies the weights.

Money is never a floating-point quantity. `total_authorized_amount_cents`, `committed_amount_cents`, `available_amount_cents`, `lock_threshold_amount_cents`, `base_construction_cents`, `disposal_cents`, `pre_contingency_subtotal_cents`, `contingency_cents` and `plan_funding_amount_cents` are all integers denominated in cents. Where a monetary value is derived by multiplying by a rate — the contingency uplift and the budget lock threshold both do this — the product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `contingency_rate` itself is a decimal rate, not a monetary amount.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Field Record

Two creation endpoints hang new obligations off a field whose observable state the client must have read first, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency on `Field.version`.

`POST /turf/certification-packets` requires `expected_field_version` as a non-optional body field carrying the `version` the client last observed on the target `Field`. If that value does not equal the field's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FIELD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because a new packet legitimately supersedes any earlier packet on the same field once approved — nothing in the packet state machine would stop a second create — so the version check is what makes a client working from a stale view of the field fail deterministically instead of silently queueing a redundant packet.

`POST /turf/replacement-plans` carries the same requirement: `expected_field_version` is a non-optional body field, it must equal the referenced field's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FIELD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Multiple draft plans for one field are legitimate product behaviour, so again no state precondition blocks the second create; concurrency control on `Field.version` is what prevents a director from drafting against an outdated view of the field's wear, posture and open-plan count.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. In particular the transition endpoints on maintenance tasks, safety assessments, remediation orders, bookings, packets and plans take no `expected_field_version` and no version of their own resource, and supplying one is an unknown field.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Counter Consistency

A `Field` carries a substantial set of counters that no client ever writes. `open_mandatory_remediation_count` is the number of the field's remediation orders whose `severity` is `MANDATORY` and whose `status` is either `OPEN` or `VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED`. `open_advisory_remediation_count` is the same count for `severity` `ADVISORY`. `open_certification_packet_count` is the number of the field's certification packets whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED`. `open_replacement_plan_count` is the number of the field's replacement plans whose `funding_status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DEFERRED` or `APPROVED`. `unsafe_cancellation_count` is the number of the field's bookings that ended in `CANCELLED_UNSAFE`. `usage_hours_ytd` is the sum of `actual_duration_hours` over the field's bookings whose `status` is either `PLAYED` or `RAINED_OUT` and whose `closed_out_at_utc` falls inside the current calendar year — a rain-out consumed the slot even though the ledger gave its wear straight back, so it counts here, while `CANCELLED_LATE` and `CANCELLED_UNSAFE` do not. `nitrogen_applied_ytd_lb_per_1000_sq_ft` is the sum of `nitrogen_lb_per_1000_sq_ft` over the field's `COMPLETED` maintenance tasks of type `FERTILITY` whose `completed_at_utc` falls inside the current calendar year. All of them default to zero at registration — the integer counters to 0, the decimal accumulators to 0.00.

Because these counters feed the posture calculation, every operation that moves one of them must also recompute the posture in the same request, and the recomputation may itself cascade further. When an operation mutates the field record — a counter moving, a posture changing, a closure being placed or lifted, a retirement landing — the field's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by exactly 1. A single request never bumps `Field.version` more than once, however many derived values it touched. Field registration is not one of those operations: a newly registered field is returned at the initial `version` of 1 described in §2.4, and it is the first mutation *after* registration that takes it to 2. The specific operations that bump it are: an administrative closure or reopening; recording a safety assessment; voiding a safety assessment; accepting a remediation verification; closing out a booking under any of the three outcomes; completing a maintenance task; creating, rejecting or rescinding a replacement plan; completing a plan's disposal; creating, submitting or deciding a certification packet; and a clock advance, for each field whose derived values actually changed.

Cascades in this system are **immediate and synchronous**. By the time a request returns, every dependent counter, posture, index and ledger entry that the request implies has already been written, and a subsequent read reflects it. Where a cascade fans out over several sibling rows, the order in which those rows are visited is specified — unsafe booking cancellations are processed by `window_start_utc` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and deferred replacement plans by `replacement_priority_score` descending and then `created_at` ascending — because the ledger entries and audit notes they produce are ordered artefacts.

### 2.9. Two Conserved Quantities: Field Wear and Committed Capital

The system maintains two invariants that hold after every request and are the sharpest tool available for checking an implementation.

The first is the **wear ledger**. `Field.cumulative_wear_units` must always equal the sum of `wear_units_delta` over every `UsageLedgerEntry` whose `field_id` is that field, rounded half up to two decimal places, with no status filter of any kind — ledger entries are immutable, are never deleted and are never amended. When an effect has to be undone, it is undone by appending a further compensating entry, never by editing history. The clearest case is the rain-out: the compensating `RAIN_OUT_RELIEF` entry is the exact negative counterpart of the `PLAY_WEAR` entry that precedes it, and the pair sums to exactly 0.00. Each entry records `running_balance_after`, the value of `Field.cumulative_wear_units` immediately after that entry was applied; replaying a field's ledger in order of `effective_at_utc` ascending and then `created_at` ascending must reproduce that column exactly. Each entry also carries `effective_at_utc`, the logical-clock instant at which it was appended, and `note`, a short machine-generated audit string naming the transition that produced it.

The second is **committed capital**. For every `CapitalBudget`, `available_amount_cents` plus `committed_amount_cents` must always equal `total_authorized_amount_cents`, and `committed_amount_cents` must always equal the sum of `plan_funding_amount_cents` over the plans on that budget whose `funding_status` is `APPROVED`. Plans in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DEFERRED`, `REJECTED` or `RESCINDED` contribute nothing. An approval followed by a rescission of the same plan therefore leaves all three amounts exactly where they were before the approval.

### 2.10. Request Evaluation Order

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is determined by a fixed evaluation order, not by whichever check the implementation happens to reach first. The order below is normative and is the reason several §3 gates are described as being "masked" by earlier ones.

Authentication and authorisation come first, in four steps: header presence (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`), then role recognition (`ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`), then identity resolution against an `ACTIVE` staff member holding the claimed role (`ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, with staff-member creation exempt), then endpoint authorisation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).

Path-parameter resolution comes next, one collection at a time and before any body is read. An unresolvable id in a field route yields `ERR_FIELD_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — this applies to reading one field and to placing a closure order on it. An unresolvable staff-member route id yields `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable maintenance-task id yields `ERR_MAINTENANCE_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, on both the single read and the transition route. An unresolvable assessment id yields `ERR_SAFETY_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on both the single read and the void route. An unresolvable remediation-order id yields `ERR_REMEDIATION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, on the single read, the verification submission and the verification decision. An unresolvable booking id yields `ERR_USAGE_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, on the single read, the confirmation and the close-out. An unresolvable ledger-entry id yields `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable packet id yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_PACKET_NOT_FOUND`, on the single read, the submission and the decision. An unresolvable budget id yields `ERR_CAPITAL_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND`. An unresolvable plan id yields `ERR_REPLACEMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, on the single read, the submission, the funding decision, the disposal manifest and the disposal completion. All ten of these are HTTP 404.

Segregation of duties is evaluated **immediately after path resolution and before the body is parsed**, so that a malformed payload can never mask a control breach. Four operations carry it. On a remediation verification submission, `X-User-ID` must not equal the `inspector_staff_member_id` of the order's source assessment. On a remediation verification decision, `X-User-ID` must not equal the order's `submitted_by_staff_member_id`. On a certification packet decision, `X-User-ID` must equal neither the packet's `submitted_by_staff_member_id` nor the `inspector_staff_member_id` of the governing assessment. On a replacement-plan funding decision, `X-User-ID` must not equal the plan's `submitted_by_staff_member_id`. Any of these yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A comparison against a null reference — an order whose `submitted_by_staff_member_id` has not been set or has been cleared by a rejection, a packet whose submitter has been cleared by a return — cannot match and passes vacuously, leaving the later state precondition to surface the real complaint.

Only then is the request body examined. Every POST endpoint requires that the body contain each of its required fields, contain no unknown field, use the correct JSON type for each value, and use only declared members for every enumerated value; a breach yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). An absent or empty body is read as the empty object; a body that is not a JSON object, or is not parseable as JSON at all, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The unknown-field sweep runs before the missing-field sweep, so a payload that is both unrecognised and incomplete is reported as unknown. An explicit `null` counts as absence throughout: supplied for a required field it fails as a missing field, and supplied for an optional field it selects that field's documented default. `expected_field_version` must be a JSON integer but carries no range constraint of its own. Pagination arguments on list endpoints are checked at the same stage, yielding `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Body-borne foreign keys are resolved next. A `field_id` in the body that does not resolve yields `ERR_FIELD_NOT_FOUND`, and this applies to maintenance-task creation, safety-assessment recording, booking creation, certification-packet creation, replacement-plan creation, and — for the `predecessor_field_id` reference — field registration. An unresolvable `assigned_staff_member_id`, `inspector_staff_member_id`, `requested_by_staff_member_id`, `submitted_by_staff_member_id` or `decided_by_staff_member_id` yields `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, and this applies to maintenance-task creation, safety-assessment recording, booking creation, remediation verification submission, certification-packet submission, certification-packet decision, replacement-plan submission and replacement-plan funding decision. An unresolvable `governing_assessment_id` or `verification_assessment_id` yields `ERR_SAFETY_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on certification-packet creation and remediation verification submission respectively. An unresolvable `capital_budget_id` yields `ERR_CAPITAL_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND` on replacement-plan creation.

Uniqueness checks follow: a duplicate field `name` among non-retired fields yields `ERR_FIELD_NAME_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409) and a duplicate `fiscal_year` yields `ERR_FISCAL_YEAR_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). Then come the static shape-and-pairing constraints on individual payloads — the role-set rule, the infill-depth rule, the assessment-metric pairing rule, the fertility-rate rule and the surface-class rule — followed by the temporal constraints on windows, assessment instants and the clock, then the predecessor-retirement rule. Each of these is described in the workflow it guards.

Optimistic concurrency on `expected_field_version` is evaluated after those static checks and before any state precondition. Then the retirement guard: an operation naming a field whose `retired_at` is not null is rejected with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED` (HTTP 409), and this guard covers maintenance-task creation, safety-assessment recording, booking creation, certification-packet creation, certification-packet submission, replacement-plan creation, closure orders and the `START` action on a maintenance task. Nothing else consults it: on a field that has since retired, a maintenance task may still be completed or cancelled, an assessment may still be voided, a booking may still be confirmed or closed out, a remediation verification may still be submitted or decided, a packet may still be decided, and a plan may still be submitted, decided, manifested or completed. State preconditions on the target resource come next, then posture and safety gates, then referential business rules, then the two interval-conflict rules and the staff-capacity rule, and finally the fiscal and scoring thresholds — completeness, condition index, priority score and budget availability. Within that final group the ordering is: certification completeness before certification condition index; plan priority score before budget availability.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering Fields, Tracking Lineage and Closing Them Administratively

The inventory is owned by the `FacilitiesDirector`, and registering a surface is the only way a `Field` comes into existence. The director supplies a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters that will appear on certification paperwork, the `surface_type` — either `NATURAL_GRASS` or `SYNTHETIC_TURF` — the `installed_on_date` recording the instant the surface went into service, an `expected_life_years` of between 1 and 30 whole years representing the manufacturer's or agronomist's nominal service life, the playing surface `area_sq_ft` between 1 and 400000 square feet, and a `sanctioned_level` drawn from `NONE`, `LOCAL`, `REGIONAL` and `STATE` describing the highest sanctioning body the field is presented to. Neither date input is constrained against the clock — an `installed_on_date` in the future, or a `warranty_expires_on` already long past, is accepted as given and simply flows through the age and warranty derivations. Two fields that are both still in service may not share a `name`; the comparison is an exact, case-sensitive string match, and the uniqueness test deliberately ignores fields whose `retired_at` is populated, so a replacement surface may inherit the retired one's name, and a collision against a live field is refused with `ERR_FIELD_NAME_DUPLICATE`.

Three inputs are optional and each carries its own rule. `warranty_expires_on` defaults to null, and null means no warranty was ever issued rather than a warranty of unknown duration. `infill_depth_mm` is the measured synthetic infill depth in millimetres: it is **required** when `surface_type` is `SYNTHETIC_TURF` and must be absent or null when `surface_type` is `NATURAL_GRASS`, and either mistake — missing on a synthetic field, or supplied on a grass field — is refused with `ERR_INFILL_DEPTH_REQUIRED`. `predecessor_field_id` defaults to null and points back at the field this one replaced; a null value simply means an original installation. When it is supplied it must name a field that has already been retired, meaning a field whose `retired_at` is not null, and pointing at a field still in service is refused with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_FIELD_NOT_RETIRED`. Because that link is stored on the successor, the reverse direction — "what replaced this retired field?" — is answered by filtering the field list on `predecessor_field_id`, alongside the filters on `surface_type`, `play_status` and `certification_status`.

Registration immediately derives everything time-dependent. `age_years` is the number of whole and fractional days between `installed_on_date` and the current logical instant, divided by 365.25, rounded half up to two decimal places. `warranty_status` follows a three-way rule: it is `NONE` when `warranty_expires_on` is null, `ACTIVE` when the current instant is strictly earlier than `warranty_expires_on`, and `EXPIRED` otherwise — the boundary belongs to expiry, so at exactly `warranty_expires_on` the warranty already reads `EXPIRED`. The agronomic condition index is computed at once, and because a brand-new field has no impact test yet, its `latest_gmax_mean_value` is null and the safety term falls back to the no-data value of 50.00 described in §3.7. `play_status` resolves to `OPEN`, `certification_status` to `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `certified_until_utc` and `admin_closure_reason_code` and `retired_at` to null, `admin_closure_active` to false, every integer counter to 0, and `cumulative_wear_units`, `usage_hours_ytd`, `rest_debt_hours` and `nitrogen_applied_ytd_lb_per_1000_sq_ft` to 0.00.

Administrative closure is the director's blunt instrument for taking a field out of service for reasons that have nothing to do with measured safety — a flooded car park, a funeral, a resurfacing contractor on site. A closure order names an `action` of either `CLOSE_ADMIN` or `REOPEN_ADMIN` together with a `reason_code` of 1 to 200 characters, which is required on both actions even though `REOPEN_ADMIN` discards it. Placing a closure requires that no closure is already in force, so `CLOSE_ADMIN` against a field whose `admin_closure_active` is already true is refused with `ERR_FIELD_ALREADY_CLOSED`; lifting one requires that a closure *is* in force, so `REOPEN_ADMIN` against a field whose `admin_closure_active` is false is refused with `ERR_FIELD_NOT_CLOSED`. Neither action may be layered on top of a safety problem: if the field's `open_mandatory_remediation_count` is anything other than zero, the closure order is refused with `ERR_UNSAFE_CLOSURE_IN_FORCE`, because the unsafe posture is the department's finding and an administrative flag must not be allowed to obscure or appear to resolve it. A retired field takes no closure orders at all and is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED`.

A successful `CLOSE_ADMIN` sets `admin_closure_active` to true, records the supplied `reason_code` in `admin_closure_reason_code`, recomputes the posture and increments `Field.version` by 1. From either `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` the posture moves to `CLOSED_ADMIN`; both source postures behave identically. A successful `REOPEN_ADMIN` sets `admin_closure_active` back to false, clears `admin_closure_reason_code` to null, recomputes the posture and increments `Field.version` by 1 — and where the posture lands depends on what else is outstanding. `CLOSED_ADMIN → OPEN` requires that `rest_debt_hours` is exactly 0.00 and `open_advisory_remediation_count` is zero; if instead `rest_debt_hours` is strictly above 0.00 or an advisory order is still open, the field reopens into `RESTRICTED` rather than `OPEN`, via `CLOSED_ADMIN → RESTRICTED`. Either way, the reopening also requires `open_mandatory_remediation_count` to be zero, which the unsafe-closure guard has already ensured.

One divergence here is deliberate and is the single most commonly mis-implemented rule in this specification. An administrative closure **does not cancel anything**. Bookings already sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` on the field are left exactly as they are; they simply cannot be created or confirmed while the closure is in force, and if the closure is lifted before their windows arrive they proceed normally. This is the opposite of the unsafe closure described in §3.4, which does cancel forward bookings, and the two must not be unified.

### 3.2. The Staff Roster and the Maintenance Programme

A `StaffMember` row is created by the `FacilitiesDirector` with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `roles` array. `roles` must be a non-empty array of distinct values drawn from `FieldOperationsCoordinator`, `TurfAgronomist`, `SafetyInspector`, `CertificationOfficer` and `FacilitiesDirector`; an empty array, a repeated entry, a member that is not a string, or any name outside those five is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE_SET`, whereas a `roles` value that is not a JSON array at all is caught earlier as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `employment_status` is optional and defaults to `ACTIVE`, and a value other than `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; setting it to `INACTIVE` leaves the row readable and referenced by history, but the person can no longer act, as §2.1 describes.

Maintenance work is scheduled either by a `TurfAgronomist` or by a `FieldOperationsCoordinator`. The payload names the `field_id` being worked, the `assigned_staff_member_id` who will occupy the window, a `task_type`, and the inclusive window bounded by `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc`. The end must be strictly after the start; an end at or before the start is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. A task on a retired field is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED`.

`task_type` is where the two surface families diverge. `AERATION`, `OVERSEEDING`, `FERTILITY` and `IRRIGATION` are agronomic operations and may only be scheduled on a field whose `surface_type` is `NATURAL_GRASS`; `GROOMING`, `INFILL_REDISTRIBUTION` and `SEAM_INSPECTION` are synthetic operations and may only be scheduled on a field whose `surface_type` is `SYNTHETIC_TURF`. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_TASK_TYPE_SURFACE_MISMATCH`. The field's `surface_type` is copied into the task's own `surface_class` at creation and then frozen, so the historical record of what class of work was done survives even a later replacement. Only a fertility application carries a rate: `nitrogen_lb_per_1000_sq_ft` is required when `task_type` is `FERTILITY` and must be absent or null for every other type, with either mistake refused as `ERR_FERTILITY_RATE_MISMATCH`. The optional `planned_for_certification_window` flag defaults to false and marks the task as part of the documented agronomic programme that the certification completeness score counts — it has no other effect.

Two scheduling conflicts are checked at creation, and they use deliberately **different interval semantics**. The first protects the person. Counting only tasks assigned to the same `assigned_staff_member_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and treating each task's window as the closed interval running from `scheduled_start_utc` to `scheduled_end_utc` inclusive — so two tasks that merely touch at an endpoint *do* intersect — the request is refused with `ERR_STAFF_TASK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that count already stands at two or more. There is no buffer and a single overlap is perfectly legal; it is the aggregate of two concurrent tasks that is the cap. The second protects the surface. The requested closed maintenance interval must not *strictly* overlap the half-open occupation interval of any booking on the same field whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED`; a strict overlap is refused with `ERR_TASK_CONFLICTS_WITH_BOOKING`. Adjacency with a zero gap is permitted here — grooming that begins the moment a practice ends is fine — which is exactly the opposite of the changeover-buffered rule that governs booking-against-booking conflicts in §3.5.

At creation the task also materialises `recovery_credit_hours`, the rest-hour credit it will grant once completed, from a fixed table keyed on `task_type`: a full-depth `AERATION` is worth 12.00 hours of recovery, an `OVERSEEDING` a full day at 24.00 hours, a `FERTILITY` application 6.00 hours, an `IRRIGATION` cycle 4.00 hours, a `GROOMING` pass 3.00 hours, an `INFILL_REDISTRIBUTION` 6.00 hours, and a `SEAM_INSPECTION` nothing at all at 0.00 hours — inspecting a seam does not rest a surface. The value is frozen on the task at creation, so retuning the table later never rewrites history. `status` initialises to `SCHEDULED`, with `started_at_utc`, `completed_at_utc` and `cancellation_reason` all null.

A task advances through a single transition operation carrying an `action` of `START`, `COMPLETE` or `CANCEL`. `START` is permitted only from `SCHEDULED` and takes the task `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, stamping `started_at_utc` from the logical clock; a task on a field that has already been retired cannot be started and is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED`, which is tested ahead of the status precondition so a retired field masks `ERR_TASK_NOT_TRANSITIONABLE`. `COMPLETE` is permitted only from `IN_PROGRESS` and takes the task `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`; it requires a `completed_at_utc` in the body which must be at or after the task's `started_at_utc` and at or before the current logical instant, and each of those three faults — the field absent, the instant in the future, the instant earlier than `started_at_utc` — is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a transition error, the first two evaluated before the status precondition and the third, which needs `started_at_utc` to exist, after it. `CANCEL` is permitted from either `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, takes the task to `CANCELLED`, and requires a `cancellation_reason` of 1 to 500 characters in the body, whose absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Requesting an action the current status does not permit is refused with `ERR_TASK_NOT_TRANSITIONABLE`.

Completion is where a maintenance task earns its keep. The supplied `completed_at_utc` is recorded, the field's `rest_debt_hours` is recomputed by the rule in §3.7, and if the credit that recomputation is actually able to absorb is strictly greater than 0.00 a ledger entry with `entry_type` `RECOVERY_CREDIT` is appended carrying `wear_units_delta` of 0.00 and `rest_credit_hours` equal to that absorbed credit; when the absorbed credit works out to exactly 0.00 — which is always the case for a `SEAM_INSPECTION`, and is also the case for any task completed against a field with no outstanding rest window — **no entry is appended at all**. When the task's `task_type` is `FERTILITY`, the field's `nitrogen_applied_ytd_lb_per_1000_sq_ft` increases by the task's `nitrogen_lb_per_1000_sq_ft`. The posture is then recomputed — a field whose only reason for being `RESTRICTED` was rest debt can go `RESTRICTED → OPEN` here — and `Field.version` increments by 1.

Cancellation is quieter. It records the `cancellation_reason` and nothing else on the field: the task stops occupying its window for the staff-capacity count, and it drops out of the certification agronomic-programme denominator described in §3.8. Cancelling a task that was already `IN_PROGRESS` grants **no** rest credit whatsoever — half-finished aeration rests nothing — and appends no ledger entry. The maintenance list is filterable by `field_id`, `assigned_staff_member_id`, `task_type`, `status` and `planned_for_certification_window`.

### 3.3. Safety Testing: Impact Attenuation, Goal Anchoring and Striping

Only a `SafetyInspector` may record a `SafetyAssessment`. The payload names the `field_id` tested, the `inspector_staff_member_id` who performed the work, an `assessment_type`, and the `assessed_at_utc` instant at which the test was actually carried out. That instant may be at or before the current logical instant but never after it; a future-dated assessment is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_IN_FUTURE`. Recording an assessment against a retired field is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED`.

The three assessment types split into one instrumented test and two inspector verdicts, and the payload must match. When `assessment_type` is `IMPACT_ATTENUATION`, `gmax_readings` must hold exactly three numeric drop readings, each between 50.0 and 400.0 inclusive and expressed to one decimal place, and `declared_result` must be null. When `assessment_type` is `GOAL_ANCHORING` or `STRIPING_LAYOUT`, the reverse holds: `gmax_readings` must be null and `declared_result` must be present, carrying either `PASS` or `FAIL`. Any violation of that pairing — the wrong number of readings, a `declared_result` on an impact test, readings on a non-instrumented inspection, or a member of `gmax_readings` that is not a number — is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_METRIC_MISMATCH`. Two adjacent faults are instead `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: a `gmax_readings` value that is not a JSON array at all, and a numeric reading falling outside 50.0 through 400.0. Each reading is stored rounded half up to one decimal place, and it is the rounded value that the range test and the mean both see.

For an impact test the server derives the verdict itself. `gmax_mean_value` is the sum of the three supplied readings divided by three, rounded half up to one decimal place; only the readings on this request participate, and earlier assessments are never averaged in. The verdict is then read off two thresholds, both of which are inclusive on the lower side of the band they close: a mean of 165.0 g or less is a `PASS`, a mean above 165.0 g but no more than 200.0 g is an `ADVISORY`, and a mean strictly above 200.0 g is a `FAIL`. A mean of exactly 165.0 is therefore a `PASS` and a mean of exactly 200.0 is an `ADVISORY`. For the two non-instrumented types, `gmax_mean_value` stays null and `result` is copied straight from `declared_result`, which means those assessments are only ever `PASS` or `FAIL` and never `ADVISORY`.

An assessment enters the world with `status` `RECORDED`, `superseded_at_utc` and `void_reason` null. Recording a newer assessment of the same `assessment_type` on the same field — one whose `assessed_at_utc` is at or after the existing one's — takes the earlier assessment `RECORDED → SUPERSEDED`, stamps its `superseded_at_utc` with the current logical instant and increments that earlier assessment's own `version` by 1. Only the forward direction collapses the set: a back-dated recording, one whose `assessed_at_utc` is strictly earlier than the live assessment's, supersedes nothing, so both rows stay `RECORDED` and the field does carry more than one live assessment of that type. `Field.latest_gmax_mean_value` is accordingly not written by the supersession but derived on every recomputation of the field, as the `gmax_mean_value` of the still-`RECORDED` `IMPACT_ATTENUATION` assessment with the greatest `assessed_at_utc`, ties broken by the greatest `created_at` and then by the later write, and null when no such assessment exists — so the field's very first impact test populates it although nothing was superseded, and a back-dated one leaves it alone. The field's `condition_index_agronomic` is recomputed from whatever that leaves. Recording any assessment increments `Field.version` by 1.

Voiding exists for the mis-recorded test — the wrong field, the wrong drop height, a transcription error. Only a `SafetyInspector` may void, and only an assessment whose `status` is still `RECORDED`: an assessment already `SUPERSEDED` or already `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_RECORDED`. Nor may an assessment be pulled out from under live certification paperwork — if any certification packet whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` names this assessment as its `governing_assessment_id`, the void is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_IN_USE`. A permitted void takes the assessment `RECORDED → VOIDED`, records the supplied `void_reason`, which is required and runs 1 to 500 characters, cancels the remediation orders it opened as described in §3.4, decrements whichever field remediation counter those orders were populating, recomputes `play_status`, and increments `Field.version` by 1. When the voided assessment was an `IMPACT_ATTENUATION`, `latest_gmax_mean_value` does not simply go null: it **falls back** to the `gmax_mean_value` of the next most recent still-`RECORDED` impact assessment on the field, chosen by `assessed_at_utc` descending and then `created_at` descending, and becomes null only when no such assessment remains. Either way `condition_index_agronomic` is recomputed from the new value. The assessment list is filterable by `field_id`, `inspector_staff_member_id`, `assessment_type`, `status` and `result`.

### 3.4. Remediation Orders and the Unsafe Closure Cascade

A `RemediationOrder` is never created by a client. It is created by the server, as the head of a five-hop cascade that begins the moment a failing or marginal assessment is recorded. Hop one is the assessment itself and the verdict derived from its readings. Hop two opens the order: a `result` of `FAIL` opens an order with `severity` `MANDATORY`, a `result` of `ADVISORY` opens one with `severity` `ADVISORY`, and a `result` of `PASS` opens nothing. The new order copies the assessment's `field_id` and records the assessment's own id as its `source_assessment_id`; neither is ever client-supplied. Its `status` initialises to `OPEN`, its `opened_at_utc` to the current logical instant, its `rejection_count` to 0, and its `verification_assessment_id`, `submitted_by_staff_member_id`, `remediation_notes` and `closed_at_utc` to null. Hop three increments the matching field counter — `open_mandatory_remediation_count` or `open_advisory_remediation_count` — and recomputes the posture.

The consequence of hop three depends on severity, and this is the whole point of the two-tier scheme. An advisory order pushes the field `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, which permits `PRACTICE` and `COMMUNITY` bookings to continue while barring `GAME` and `TOURNAMENT` — an attempt at either of the barred classes is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RESTRICTED_FOR_ACTIVITY`. A mandatory order pushes the field to `CLOSED_UNSAFE`, from either `OPEN → CLOSED_UNSAFE` or `RESTRICTED → CLOSED_UNSAFE`, and that is where hops four and five fire. Hop four cancels **every** booking on the field whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `window_start_utc` is at or after the current logical instant, taking each `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED_UNSAFE` or `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED_UNSAFE`, and it visits them in order of `window_start_utc` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Bookings already in a terminal status are never touched, and neither are bookings whose windows have already begun. Hop five is the ledger consequence: each cancellation appends a `UsageLedgerEntry` with `entry_type` `UNSAFE_CANCELLATION`, `wear_units_delta` of 0.00 and `rest_requirement_hours` of 0.00 — an unsafe cancellation charges no wear because no play occurred — increments the field's `unsafe_cancellation_count` by 1, and forces a recompute of `rest_debt_hours`. Each cancelled booking's own `version` increments by 1 while its `outcome` stays null — `outcome` is only ever written by a close-out, never by a cascade.

Discharging an order runs through two hands, and the two hands must belong to different people. A `TurfAgronomist` offers a re-test as evidence, naming the `verification_assessment_id`, the `submitted_by_staff_member_id` doing the offering, and free-text `remediation_notes` of 1 to 2000 characters; all three are required. The order must be `OPEN`, and an order in any other status is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OPEN`. The nominated re-test must genuinely be a re-test of the same problem: it must sit on the same field as the order, share the `assessment_type` of the order's source assessment, still be `RECORDED`, and have an `assessed_at_utc` at or after the order's `opened_at_utc`. Any of those failing is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_ASSESSMENT_INVALID`. The acting `X-User-ID` must not be the `inspector_staff_member_id` of the source assessment, as §2.10 sets out. A permitted submission takes the order `OPEN → VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED`, records the three supplied values, and leaves the field's posture entirely alone — the order still counts toward its open-order counter, so a `CLOSED_UNSAFE` field stays closed while its verification is pending.

A `SafetyInspector` then rules on the submission with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and optional `decision_notes` of 1 to 2000 characters, which are validated and then dropped: a `RemediationOrder` carries no decision-notes attribute of its own, so nothing on the record echoes them back. The order must be `VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_AWAITING_DECISION`. The acting `X-User-ID` must not be the order's `submitted_by_staff_member_id`. `ACCEPT` additionally requires that the verification assessment's `result` is `PASS` — an `ADVISORY` or `FAIL` re-test is not evidence of remediation and is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_PASSING`. An accepted verification takes the order `VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED → CLOSED_VERIFIED`, stamps `closed_at_utc` from the logical clock, decrements whichever of `open_mandatory_remediation_count` or `open_advisory_remediation_count` this order was populating by 1, recomputes the posture, and increments `Field.version` by 1. `REJECT` is the cycle in this state machine: it takes the order `VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED → OPEN`, increments `rejection_count` by 1, clears both `verification_assessment_id` and `submitted_by_staff_member_id` back to null so a different agronomist may try again, **retains** `remediation_notes` for audit, and leaves the posture unchanged. The loop `OPEN → VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED → OPEN` may repeat any number of times.

Voiding a source assessment is the other way an order ends. Every order whose `source_assessment_id` is the voided assessment and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED` transitions to `CANCELLED` — `OPEN → CANCELLED` and `VERIFICATION_SUBMITTED → CANCELLED` behave identically — with `closed_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, the matching field counter decremented by 1, and the posture recomputed. `CLOSED_VERIFIED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

When the last mandatory order on a field closes, by verification or by cancellation, the unsafe closure lifts — but where the posture lands depends on what remains. `CLOSED_UNSAFE → OPEN` requires that `admin_closure_active` is false, `rest_debt_hours` is exactly 0.00 and `open_advisory_remediation_count` is zero. If `admin_closure_active` is false but `rest_debt_hours` is strictly above 0.00 or an advisory order is still outstanding, the field lifts only as far as `RESTRICTED`, via `CLOSED_UNSAFE → RESTRICTED`. And in neither case are the bookings that hop four already cancelled reinstated: `CANCELLED_UNSAFE` is terminal, and re-establishing the fixture requires a fresh allocation. The remediation list is filterable by `field_id`, `source_assessment_id`, `verification_assessment_id`, `submitted_by_staff_member_id`, `severity` and `status`, and because these orders are created only by the cascade, that list is their sole discovery channel.

### 3.5. Allocating Play: Requesting and Confirming a Booking

Allocation is the `FieldOperationsCoordinator`'s exclusive province. A request for field time names the `field_id`, the `requested_by_staff_member_id` making the request, an `activity_type` drawn from `PRACTICE`, `GAME`, `TOURNAMENT` and `COMMUNITY`, the occupation window bounded by `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, and an `expected_participant_count` between 1 and 500 which is a forecast headcount used purely for capacity screening and never for wear. The window is **half-open**: it includes `window_start_utc` and excludes `window_end_utc`, and the end must be strictly after the start or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Allocation against a retired field is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED`.

Both posture gates apply at request time. A field whose `play_status` is `CLOSED_ADMIN`, `CLOSED_UNSAFE` or `RETIRED` accepts no allocation and the request is refused with `ERR_FIELD_NOT_PLAYABLE`; a field that is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` but will not take the requested intensity — that is, a `RESTRICTED` field asked for a `GAME` or a `TOURNAMENT` — is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RESTRICTED_FOR_ACTIVITY`.

Two bookings may not collide on the same surface, and the collision test builds in a **changeover buffer of thirty minutes** on each side, applied symmetrically. Considering only bookings on the same field whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` — terminal-status bookings are excluded from the candidate set entirely — the requested window conflicts with an existing one when the requested `window_start_utc` falls strictly before the existing `window_end_utc` extended forward by thirty minutes **and** the existing `window_start_utc` falls strictly before the requested `window_end_utc` extended forward by thirty minutes. Both halves must hold for a conflict. The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: two back-to-back bookings with a gap of zero **do** conflict, because the trailing edge of the earlier one, widened by the buffer, still covers the leading edge of the later; a gap of exactly thirty minutes is accepted. A conflict is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Note how this differs from the maintenance-against-booking rule in §3.2, which has no buffer at all and permits zero-gap adjacency.

A newly created booking has `status` `SCHEDULED`, and `started_with_rest_debt` stays null until confirmation — it is not false, it is genuinely absent, because the field's rest position has not yet been sampled. `outcome`, `actual_duration_hours`, `actual_participant_count`, `weather_condition`, `wear_load_units`, `rest_requirement_hours`, `confirmed_at_utc` and `closed_out_at_utc` are all null as well.

Confirmation carries no payload of its own — the body must be absent or empty, and any key at all in it is an unknown field yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it simply re-runs the gates against the world as it now stands, because a field that was `OPEN` when the fixture was requested may have gone `CLOSED_UNSAFE` since. The booking must be `SCHEDULED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The posture must be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_FIELD_NOT_PLAYABLE`, and when the posture is `RESTRICTED` the `activity_type` must be `PRACTICE` or `COMMUNITY`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RESTRICTED_FOR_ACTIVITY`. A third gate applies only here and not at request time: when the `activity_type` is `TOURNAMENT` **and** the field's `sanctioned_level` is anything other than `NONE`, the field's `certification_status` must be exactly `CERTIFIED`, and a field that is `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `PENDING_REVIEW` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_FIELD_NOT_CERTIFIED`. A tournament on an unsanctioned field — `sanctioned_level` of `NONE` — needs no certification. Finally, confirmation must happen before play starts: the current logical instant must be strictly earlier than `window_start_utc`, and an attempt at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_STARTED`.

A permitted confirmation takes the booking `SCHEDULED → CONFIRMED`, stamps `confirmed_at_utc` from the logical clock, and samples the field's rest position into `started_with_rest_debt`, which is set to true when `rest_debt_hours` is strictly greater than 0.00 and false otherwise. That flag feeds no calculation whatsoever and is retained purely for audit, so that the department can later see how often it played a field it had not properly rested. The booking list is filterable by `field_id`, `requested_by_staff_member_id`, `activity_type` and `status`, which is also how cascade-cancelled fixtures are discovered.

### 3.6. Closing Out a Booking: Three Outcomes, Three Different Ledgers

Every confirmed booking must be closed out by the coordinator, and the close-out payload records what actually happened: an `outcome` of `PLAYED`, `RAINED_OUT` or `CANCELLED_LATE`, the `actual_duration_hours` of play, the `actual_participant_count` observed, and the `weather_condition` — either `DRY` or `WET` — under which the slot ran. All four values are required; `actual_duration_hours` is a two-decimal quantity of 0.00 or more with no upper bound, stored rounded half up to two places, and `actual_participant_count` is an integer of 0 or more, likewise unbounded above. The supplied `outcome` is copied onto the booking's own `outcome` field and is also the booking's new `status`. The booking must be `CONFIRMED`, or the close-out is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and the window must already have elapsed: the current logical instant must be at or after `window_end_utc`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. Each outcome then carries its own consistency requirement — `PLAYED` requires `actual_duration_hours` strictly greater than 0.00, `CANCELLED_LATE` requires `actual_duration_hours` of exactly 0.00, and `RAINED_OUT` requires `weather_condition` to be `WET`. All three of those refusals are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and unusually for a body-shape check they are evaluated *after* both `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED` and `ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`, because they read the booking as well as the payload.

**Wear.** Every outcome computes `wear_load_units`, and the computation is a product of four factors with a capped crowd surcharge nested inside it. Two inputs are chosen by outcome first. The duration used is `actual_duration_hours` for `PLAYED` and for `RAINED_OUT`, but for `CANCELLED_LATE` it is instead the full scheduled window length, measured in hours from `window_start_utc` to `window_end_utc`. The headcount used is `actual_participant_count` for `PLAYED` and for `RAINED_OUT`, but for `CANCELLED_LATE` it is the booking's `expected_participant_count`. From those, the base load is the duration multiplied by an intensity weight keyed on `activity_type`: 1.00 for `PRACTICE`, 1.50 for `GAME`, 2.00 for `TOURNAMENT` and 0.75 for `COMMUNITY`. The crowd factor is 1.00 plus a surcharge, where the surcharge is the smaller of 0.50 and the product of 0.01 and the number of participants in excess of a free allowance of forty — a headcount at or below forty contributes no surcharge at all, and the surcharge stops growing once it reaches 0.50. The surface factor is keyed on the field's `surface_type`: 1.20 for `NATURAL_GRASS`, which bruises, and 0.80 for `SYNTHETIC_TURF`, which does not. The weather factor is 1.35 when `weather_condition` is `WET` and 1.00 when it is `DRY`. The raw load is the base load multiplied by the crowd factor, then by the surface factor, then by the weather factor, all at full precision. For `PLAYED` and `RAINED_OUT`, `wear_load_units` is that raw load rounded half up to two decimal places. For `CANCELLED_LATE`, the raw load is first multiplied by a late-cancellation fraction of 0.50 — a fixture called off after the window has passed still cost the surface something, in stand-by preparation and marking, but only half of what playing it would have — and the result of that multiplication is rounded half up to two decimal places. Rounding happens exactly once, at the very end; the factors are never rounded individually.

**Rest.** For `PLAYED` and `RAINED_OUT` the booking also computes `rest_requirement_hours`, using **marginal** tier rates applied to `wear_load_units` — each tier's rate applies only to the portion of the wear that falls inside that tier, never to the whole amount. The first 6.00 wear units earn 0.50 rest hours each. Wear units above 6.00 and up to 12.00 earn 1.00 rest hour each. Every wear unit above 12.00 earns 1.75 rest hours. Those tier contributions are summed, and then exactly one adjustment applies. If the field's `surface_type` is `SYNTHETIC_TURF`, the summed hours are multiplied by 0.40, because synthetic carpet recovers far faster than living grass. Otherwise — that is, on a `NATURAL_GRASS` field — if `weather_condition` is `WET`, a flat surcharge of 2.00 hours is added, because a saturated root zone needs longer before it can take traffic again. The two adjustments are mutually exclusive by construction: the wet surcharge only ever reaches a natural surface. The result is rounded half up to two decimal places, once, after the multiplier or the surcharge has been applied. For a `CANCELLED_LATE` outcome, `rest_requirement_hours` is 0.00 and no rest window is opened at all.

**Ledger.** The three outcomes then diverge sharply. `CONFIRMED → PLAYED` appends a single `UsageLedgerEntry` with `entry_type` `PLAY_WEAR`, carrying `wear_units_delta` equal to the positive `wear_load_units`, the booking's `rest_requirement_hours`, and a `rest_window_end_utc` equal to the booking's `window_end_utc` advanced by those rest hours. `Field.cumulative_wear_units` is recomputed, `usage_hours_ytd` increases by `actual_duration_hours`, `rest_debt_hours` and `condition_index_agronomic` and `play_status` are all recomputed, and `Field.version` increments by 1.

`CONFIRMED → RAINED_OUT` computes `wear_load_units` and `rest_requirement_hours` exactly as `PLAYED` does and appends exactly the same `PLAY_WEAR` entry — and then immediately appends a second, compensating entry with `entry_type` `RAIN_OUT_RELIEF` whose `wear_units_delta` is the exact negative of that wear, whose `rest_requirement_hours` is 0.00 and whose `rest_credit_hours` is 0.00. Sanctioned rain-out policy does not charge the tenant's wear allocation, so the two entries sum to exactly 0.00 and `Field.cumulative_wear_units` returns to precisely its pre-close-out value. The **rest obligation from the `PLAY_WEAR` entry stands**, however — a wet surface still needs its rest window, whether or not anybody played on it — so `rest_debt_hours` rises even though cumulative wear did not, and the field can go `OPEN → RESTRICTED` on the strength of a fixture that never happened. `usage_hours_ytd` still increases by `actual_duration_hours`, and `Field.version` increments by 1.

`CONFIRMED → CANCELLED_LATE` appends one entry with `entry_type` `LATE_CANCEL_WEAR` carrying the halved wear as its `wear_units_delta`, a `rest_requirement_hours` of 0.00 and a `rest_window_end_utc` of null. `Field.cumulative_wear_units` is recomputed and so are `condition_index_agronomic` and `play_status`, but `usage_hours_ytd` is left **unchanged** — nothing was played — and `Field.version` increments by 1.

Whichever branch ran, `closed_out_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock, and `PLAYED`, `RAINED_OUT`, `CANCELLED_LATE` and the cascade-only `CANCELLED_UNSAFE` are all terminal. Every ledger entry, whatever its `entry_type`, records the `field_id` whose balance it moves, the `booking_id` that produced it when a booking did (null for maintenance-sourced entries), the `maintenance_task_id` that produced it when a task did (null for booking-sourced entries), its `effective_at_utc`, its `running_balance_after`, and a `note` naming the triggering transition. Every entry also always carries `rest_requirement_hours` and `rest_credit_hours` as two-decimal numbers that default to 0.00 rather than to null, plus `rest_window_end_utc`, which is populated only on a `PLAY_WEAR` entry and is null on every other `entry_type`. The seven `note` values the server writes are `BOOKING_CLOSED_OUT_PLAYED`, `BOOKING_CLOSED_OUT_RAINED_OUT`, `BOOKING_CLOSED_OUT_RAINED_OUT_RELIEF`, `BOOKING_CLOSED_OUT_CANCELLED_LATE`, `MAINTENANCE_TASK_COMPLETED_RECOVERY_CREDIT`, `UNSAFE_CLOSURE_CASCADE_FROM_ASSESSMENT` and `FIELD_RETIRED_BY_REPLACEMENT_PLAN`. The ledger list is filterable by `field_id`, `booking_id`, `maintenance_task_id` and `entry_type`, and since entries are only ever appended by side effects, that list is their sole discovery channel.

### 3.7. Rest Debt, Recovery Credit, the Condition Index and the Passage of Time

`Field.rest_debt_hours` is the department's central agronomic obligation, and it is deliberately **not** an accumulator. Overlapping rest windows are merged by taking the maximum, never by summing. The calculation first identifies the governing entry: among the field's ledger entries whose `entry_type` is `PLAY_WEAR`, the one with the greatest `rest_window_end_utc`, breaking ties by the greatest `effective_at_utc` and then the greatest `created_at`. If the field has no `PLAY_WEAR` entry at all, `rest_debt_hours` is 0.00 and there is nothing further to do. Otherwise the outstanding obligation is the number of hours from the current logical instant to that governing entry's `rest_window_end_utc`, floored at 0.00 so an elapsed window contributes nothing, rounded half up to two decimal places. Against that, credit is taken: the sum of `recovery_credit_hours` over the field's maintenance tasks whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `completed_at_utc` is at or after the governing entry's `effective_at_utc`, but capped so it can never exceed the outstanding obligation itself. `rest_debt_hours` is the outstanding obligation minus that capped credit, rounded half up to two decimal places. The ordering matters: the outstanding figure is rounded *before* the credit is capped and subtracted. Recomputation is triggered on every booking close-out, on every maintenance-task completion, on the unsafe and retirement cascades, and for every field on every clock advance.

The cap is also what decides whether a completed task leaves a ledger trace at all. Because the recovery credit a task actually delivers is the amount the cap admits, a task completed against a field with no outstanding rest window delivers 0.00 and appends no `RECOVERY_CREDIT` entry, and a `SEAM_INSPECTION` — whose `recovery_credit_hours` is 0.00 by definition — never appends one under any circumstances.

`Field.condition_index_agronomic` is the department's single headline number for surface health, and it is a weighted blend of three components each of which is rounded to two decimal places **before** the weights are applied. The wear component is 100.00 minus the field's `cumulative_wear_units` divided by 4.00 — four wear units cost one index point — floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimals. The age component is 100.00 minus the ratio of `age_years` to `expected_life_years` expressed as a percentage, likewise floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimals, so a surface exactly at its nominal life scores 0.00 on age and one past it is floored rather than negative. The safety component depends on the latest impact test: when `latest_gmax_mean_value` is null it takes the no-data value of 50.00 exactly, and otherwise it is the distance from the mandatory g-max limit of 200.0 down to the measured mean, divided by the distance from that mandatory limit down to an ideal value of 90.0, expressed as a percentage, clamped to lie between 0.00 and 100.00, and rounded half up to two decimals. The `AGRONOMIC` variant then weights the wear component at 0.50, the age component at 0.20 and the safety component at 0.30, sums them, and rounds half up to two decimal places, applying no cap — an agronomically excellent surface can still score well on this variant with a mediocre g-max reading. The `CERTIFICATION` variant, described in §3.8, uses the same three components with different weights and adds a cap. Recomputation of the `AGRONOMIC` variant is triggered on field registration, on every safety assessment recorded or voided, on booking close-out under `PLAYED` and under `CANCELLED_LATE`, on every clock advance, and whenever a certification packet is submitted.

Time in this system moves only when a `FacilitiesDirector` moves it, by supplying `to_iso8601`. That value must be strictly later than the clock's current reading, and a request that is not is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`. Advancing the clock is not a passive operation: it sweeps every field in the inventory and recomputes everything time-dependent. `age_years` and `warranty_status` are recomputed, so a warranty crossing its `warranty_expires_on` flips `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` — which in turn changes the penalty term in replacement scoring. `rest_debt_hours` is recomputed against the new instant, so rest windows expire and fields can drift `RESTRICTED → OPEN` without anybody touching them. `certification_status` is recomputed, so a field whose `certified_until_utc` has been reached or passed moves `CERTIFIED → EXPIRED` provided no packet on it is currently `SUBMITTED`, and its `TOURNAMENT` fixtures can no longer be confirmed from that moment on. The `AGRONOMIC` condition index and the play posture are recomputed for every field as well. `Field.version` increments by 1 for each field whose derived values actually changed, and the response reports the new `current_utc` together with `fields_recomputed`, the count of fields that changed.

### 3.8. Certifying a Field for Sanctioned Competition

A `CertificationPacket` is the document set a sanctioning body sees, and it is assembled and decided by `CertificationOfficer` staff. Opening one requires the `field_id`, the `governing_assessment_id` naming the impact-attenuation test the packet rests on, a `competition_season_label` of 1 to 40 characters that will be printed on the certificate, and the `expected_field_version` demanded by §2.7. The governing assessment must belong to the same field, must have `assessment_type` `IMPACT_ATTENUATION`, must still be `RECORDED`, and must have a `result` that is not `FAIL` — an `ADVISORY` reading is acceptable evidence at packet-opening time, a `FAIL` is not. Any breach is refused with `ERR_GOVERNING_ASSESSMENT_NOT_CURRENT`, and a retired field is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED`. Creating the packet increments `Field.open_certification_packet_count` by 1 and `Field.version` by 1. The packet starts in `DRAFT` with `documentation_completeness_score` and `condition_index_certification` both null, `submitted_by_staff_member_id`, `decided_by_staff_member_id`, `submitted_at_utc`, `decided_at_utc`, `certified_until_utc` and `decision_notes` all null, and `return_count` at 0.

Submission is permitted from `DRAFT` and from `RETURNED` alike — `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and `RETURNED → SUBMITTED` — and a packet in any other status is refused with `ERR_PACKET_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. The submitting officer's `submitted_by_staff_member_id` is recorded, `submitted_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock, the governing assessment is re-checked against the same currency rule and refused with `ERR_GOVERNING_ASSESSMENT_NOT_CURRENT` if it has since been superseded or voided, and the field must not be retired. Crucially, **both scores are recomputed from scratch at every submission** and never carried over from a previous one, because a resubmission is a fresh assertion about a world that has moved on. `Field.certification_status` is then recomputed to `PENDING_REVIEW` — taking the field `NOT_CERTIFIED → PENDING_REVIEW` or `EXPIRED → PENDING_REVIEW` — unless a still-valid `APPROVED` packet keeps it `CERTIFIED`, in which case the posture stays `CERTIFIED` and `Field.certified_until_utc` is retained untouched until the new packet is actually approved. Submitting next season's paperwork never revokes this season's certificate. `Field.version` increments by 1.

The **documentation completeness score** is the heart of the packet, and it is assembled over a review window of the 180 days ending at `submitted_at_utc`, with **both bounds inclusive**. Six components each produce a ratio between 0.00 and 1.00, each ratio is multiplied by its weight, each weighted product is rounded half up to two decimal places **individually**, the six rounded products are summed, and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places again. Summing at full precision and rounding once is a different number and is not what this specification asks for.

The safety component is worth 30 points and its ratio is 1.00 when the governing assessment is still `RECORDED`, has a `result` of either `PASS` or `ADVISORY`, and has an `assessed_at_utc` inside the window; otherwise 0.00. The agronomic-programme component is worth 20 points: the denominator is the set of maintenance tasks on the field whose `planned_for_certification_window` is true, whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, and whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls inside the window, and the ratio is the proportion of that set whose `status` is `COMPLETED` — except that when the set is empty the ratio is 1.00, not 0.00, because there is nothing to have failed to do. The striping component is worth 15 points and its ratio is 1.00 when the field has a `STRIPING_LAYOUT` assessment that is `RECORDED`, has `result` `PASS`, and was assessed inside the window; otherwise 0.00. The goal-anchoring component is worth 15 points on exactly the same terms with `assessment_type` `GOAL_ANCHORING`. The surface-specification component is worth 10 points and its ratio depends on the surface family: on a `SYNTHETIC_TURF` field it is the field's `infill_depth_mm` divided by a required depth of 51.0 millimetres, capped at 1.00; on a `NATURAL_GRASS` field it is the field's `nitrogen_applied_ytd_lb_per_1000_sq_ft` divided by a required season rate of 3.00 pounds per thousand square feet, again capped at 1.00. The remediation-closure component is worth 10 points: the denominator is the set of remediation orders on the field whose `opened_at_utc` falls inside the window, and the ratio is the proportion of that set whose `status` is `CLOSED_VERIFIED` or `CANCELLED`, with an empty set again scoring 1.00 for vacuous compliance.

Each submission also writes `condition_index_certification`, using the `CERTIFICATION` variant of the condition index. It takes the very same three components as §3.7 — wear, age and safety, each rounded to two decimal places first — but weights the wear component at 0.20, the age component at 0.20 and the safety component at 0.60, sums them and rounds half up to two decimal places, and then applies a cap the agronomic variant does not have: the final value is the **lesser** of that weighted sum and the safety component alone. A field presented for sanctioned competition can never be scored above its measured impact attenuation, however pristine its wear and age figures are.

Deciding a packet requires a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `RETURN`, the `decided_by_staff_member_id`, and optional `decision_notes` of 1 to 2000 characters, recorded as null when omitted. The packet must be `SUBMITTED`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_PACKET_NOT_SUBMITTED`. Segregation of duties bites hardest here and is checked before anything else on the request: the deciding officer may be neither the packet's `submitted_by_staff_member_id` nor the `inspector_staff_member_id` behind the governing assessment. Three further gates then apply to `APPROVE` only, and they are evaluated in this order. First, the field must carry no open mandatory remediation — an `open_mandatory_remediation_count` above zero is refused with `ERR_OPEN_MANDATORY_REMEDIATION`, so that a mid-flight safety failure surfaces as itself rather than being masked by a scoring complaint. Second, `documentation_completeness_score` must be at least 90.00, and anything lower is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. Third, `condition_index_certification` must be at least 55.00, and anything lower is refused with `ERR_CONDITION_INDEX_BELOW_CERTIFICATION_FLOOR`.

An approval takes the packet `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `decided_at_utc` from the logical clock, records `decided_by_staff_member_id` and any `decision_notes`, and sets the packet's own `certified_until_utc` to the current instant advanced by a certification validity period of 365 days. Any other packet on the same field that was `APPROVED` transitions `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, retaining its own `certified_until_utc` for audit but no longer driving the field's, and its own `version` increments by 1. The field itself goes `PENDING_REVIEW → CERTIFIED` — a transition that requires the newly approved packet's `certified_until_utc` to be strictly later than the current instant — `Field.certified_until_utc` is set from that packet, `Field.open_certification_packet_count` decrements by 1, and `Field.version` increments by 1. From this moment `TOURNAMENT` fixtures on the field may be confirmed.

A return takes the packet `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, increments `return_count` by 1, stamps `decided_at_utc` and `decision_notes`, and clears `submitted_by_staff_member_id` back to null so that a *different* officer may resubmit — which is how the segregation control survives the cycle. `SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` may repeat any number of times, and each pass re-evaluates both scores against the state of the world at that moment. The field's posture is then recomputed, and where it lands depends on what else exists: with no other `SUBMITTED` packet on the field and no `APPROVED` packet whose `certified_until_utc` is still ahead of the current instant, the field goes `PENDING_REVIEW → NOT_CERTIFIED` and `Field.certified_until_utc` stays null; but if an `APPROVED` packet with a `certified_until_utc` still ahead of the current instant does exist, the field goes `PENDING_REVIEW → CERTIFIED` instead and `Field.certified_until_utc` is left unchanged, because returning a resubmission must never revoke an in-force certification. `Field.version` increments by 1 either way. `SUPERSEDED` is the packet lifecycle's only terminal status. Packets are listable with filters on `field_id`, `governing_assessment_id`, `submitted_by_staff_member_id`, `decided_by_staff_member_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Capital Budgets, Replacement Planning and Synthetic Disposal

Capital authorisation comes first. A `FacilitiesDirector` authorises a `CapitalBudget` for a `fiscal_year` between 2020 and 2040 with a `total_authorized_amount_cents` between 1 and 99999999999 integer cents. Only one budget may exist per fiscal year, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_FISCAL_YEAR_DUPLICATE`. On creation, `committed_amount_cents` is 0, `available_amount_cents` equals `total_authorized_amount_cents`, `status` is `OPEN`, and `lock_threshold_amount_cents` is set to ninety percent of the authorisation — the total multiplied by 0.90 and rounded half up to a whole number of cents. That threshold is what stops the department from spending the last tenth of a year's capital without a fresh look: the budget's `status` is `LOCKED` whenever `committed_amount_cents` is at or above `lock_threshold_amount_cents` and `OPEN` whenever it is below, and because the comparison is inclusive, a commitment exactly equal to the threshold locks the budget. `available_amount_cents` is always `total_authorized_amount_cents` minus `committed_amount_cents`.

A `ReplacementPlan` names the `field_id` to be replaced, the `capital_budget_id` it draws against, the `target_surface_type` to be installed, the `area_sq_ft` to be replaced between 1 and 400000, a `priority_basis`, and a `planned_install_year` between 2026 and 2040 — plus the `expected_field_version` demanded by §2.7. A retired field takes no new plans and is refused with `ERR_FIELD_RETIRED`.

Pricing runs in a fixed order, and the order is the trap. First, `base_construction_cents` is the plan's `area_sq_ft` multiplied by a unit cost keyed on `target_surface_type`: 22 cents per square foot for `NATURAL_GRASS`, 65 cents per square foot for `SYNTHETIC_TURF`. Second, `disposal_cents` accounts for taking the *existing* surface away: when the existing `Field.surface_type` is `SYNTHETIC_TURF` it is the plan's `area_sq_ft` multiplied by 5 cents per square foot, and when the existing surface is `NATURAL_GRASS` it is 0, because grass is not a waste stream. Third, `pre_contingency_subtotal_cents` is `base_construction_cents` plus `disposal_cents`. Fourth — and this is the pinned step — the `contingency_rate` is selected from that **pre-contingency** subtotal and never from the final total: a subtotal below 2000000 cents takes a rate of 0.10, a subtotal of 2000000 cents or more but below 5000000 cents takes 0.07, and a subtotal of 5000000 cents or more takes 0.05. The upper bounds are strict, so a subtotal of exactly 5000000 cents selects 0.05. Fifth, `contingency_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Finally, `plan_funding_amount_cents` is `pre_contingency_subtotal_cents` plus `contingency_cents`. Reading the tier off the finished total would be circular and is wrong.

`replacement_priority_score` is computed at the same moment, under whichever of two variants the `priority_basis` names. Both variants share a warranty penalty: 8.00 points when the field's `warranty_status` is `EXPIRED`, and 0.00 otherwise. The `REMAINING_LIFE` variant scores how far past its nominal service life the surface has run. The life deficit is the field's `age_years` minus its `expected_life_years`, rounded half up to two decimal places and floored at 0.00; the score is that deficit multiplied by 6.00 points per year, plus an accumulated-usage term equal to the field's `cumulative_wear_units` divided by 25.00 but capped at 20.00 points, plus the warranty penalty, all rounded half up to two decimal places. Under this basis a surface still inside its nominal life scores almost nothing however badly it tests. The `CONDITION_RISK` variant ignores nominal life entirely. It counts the field's remediation orders whose `severity` is `MANDATORY` and whose `opened_at_utc` falls at or after the current instant less a lookback of 365 days — counted regardless of their current `status`, so orders that have since been closed or cancelled still weigh — and the score is 100.00 minus the field's `condition_index_agronomic`, multiplied by 0.80 points per index point, plus 5.00 points per counted mandatory order, plus the warranty penalty, rounded half up to two decimal places. The same field can clear the approval floor under one basis and fail it under the other, which is exactly why the basis is a recorded decision rather than an implementation detail.

A new plan starts with `funding_status` `DRAFT`. Its second, **independent** axis, `disposal_status`, starts at `PENDING` when the *existing* `Field.surface_type` is `SYNTHETIC_TURF` — synthetic infill and carpet must be manifested to a licensed receiver — and at `NOT_APPLICABLE` otherwise. `manifest_reference`, `disposal_facility_name`, `diverted_tonnage`, `total_tonnage`, `diversion_ratio`, `submitted_by_staff_member_id`, `decided_by_staff_member_id`, `submitted_at_utc`, `decided_at_utc`, `completed_at_utc` and `decision_notes` are all null. Creating the plan increments `Field.open_replacement_plan_count` by 1 and `Field.version` by 1.

Submitting a plan records the `submitted_by_staff_member_id`, stamps `submitted_at_utc`, and takes the funding axis `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. The plan must be in `DRAFT`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, and its budget must be `OPEN`, or it is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_LOCKED`. The disposal axis is untouched by submission.

The funding decision carries a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `RESCIND`, the `decided_by_staff_member_id`, and optional `decision_notes` of 1 to 2000 characters, recorded as null when omitted; the decider may never be the plan's `submitted_by_staff_member_id`. `APPROVE` and `REJECT` both require `funding_status` to be `SUBMITTED` and are otherwise refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SUBMITTED`. `APPROVE` additionally requires the budget to be `OPEN`, refusing with `ERR_BUDGET_LOCKED`; then that `replacement_priority_score` is at least 20.00, refusing with `ERR_PLAN_PRIORITY_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; then that `plan_funding_amount_cents` does not exceed the budget's `available_amount_cents`, refusing with `ERR_BUDGET_INSUFFICIENT`.

An approval fires a four-hop cascade. Hop one is the plan itself moving `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, with `decided_at_utc` and `decided_by_staff_member_id` recorded. Hop two increases the budget's `committed_amount_cents` by exactly `plan_funding_amount_cents`, recomputes `available_amount_cents` and increments the budget's own `version` by 1. Hop three tests the lock: when `committed_amount_cents` has reached or passed `lock_threshold_amount_cents`, the budget moves `OPEN → LOCKED`. Hop four is the consequence of that lock — **every other** plan on the same `capital_budget_id` whose `funding_status` is `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → DEFERRED`, visited in order of `replacement_priority_score` descending and then `created_at` ascending, each deferred sibling's own `version` incrementing by 1 — and a deferred plan can no longer be submitted until its budget reopens. An approval leaves `Field.version` and every field-derived value alone, because `open_replacement_plan_count` already counted the plan while it was `DRAFT` and counts it still now that it is `APPROVED`.

`REJECT` takes the plan `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, moves no capital at all and leaves the budget entirely untouched, decrements `Field.open_replacement_plan_count` by 1 and increments `Field.version` by 1. `RESCIND` is the compensating action and requires `funding_status` to be `APPROVED` and `disposal_status` to be anything other than `COMPLETED`; a plan that is not approved, or whose disposal has already completed, is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`. A rescission takes the plan `APPROVED → RESCINDED` and releases exactly `plan_funding_amount_cents` back to the budget, decreasing `committed_amount_cents` by that precise figure, recomputing `available_amount_cents` and incrementing the budget's own `version` by 1 — the one other decision that touches the budget row, since a `REJECT`, moving no capital, leaves the budget's `version` untouched too. If that release drops `committed_amount_cents` back below `lock_threshold_amount_cents`, the budget moves `LOCKED → OPEN`, and every plan on that budget whose `funding_status` is `DEFERRED` moves `DEFERRED → DRAFT`, in `created_at` ascending order and each with its own `version` incrementing by 1, and becomes submittable again. The disposal axis is also withdrawn: a `disposal_status` of `PENDING` moves `PENDING → NOT_APPLICABLE` and a `disposal_status` of `MANIFESTED` moves `MANIFESTED → NOT_APPLICABLE`, in the latter case retaining the recorded manifest values for audit while the obligation itself lapses. `Field.open_replacement_plan_count` decrements by 1 and `Field.version` increments by 1. `REJECTED` and `RESCINDED` are the funding axis's terminal states.

The disposal axis advances on its own and, until the very last step, **never reads the funding axis**. Recording the waste manifest supplies a `manifest_reference` of 1 to 60 characters, the `disposal_facility_name` receiving the material at 1 to 120 characters, the `diverted_tonnage` kept out of landfill and the `total_tonnage` removed, both tonnages being numbers of 0.00 or more that are stored rounded half up to two decimal places. The plan's `disposal_status` must be `PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISPOSAL_NOT_PENDING`. The tonnage must make sense and must clear the department's diversion policy: `total_tonnage` must be strictly greater than 0.00, `diverted_tonnage` must not exceed `total_tonnage`, and `diverted_tonnage` divided by `total_tonnage`, rounded half up to two decimal places, must be at least 0.75 — at least three quarters of the removed material has to be diverted from landfill. Any of those failing is refused with `ERR_DISPOSAL_DIVERSION_BELOW_MINIMUM`. A permitted manifest takes the axis `PENDING → MANIFESTED`, records the four supplied values, and writes `diversion_ratio` as that same rounded quotient. `funding_status` is neither read nor changed.

Completing the disposal is the **single cross-axis guard** in the whole specification, and it is also what retires a field. It requires a `completed_at_utc` at or before the current logical instant — a later one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, tested before the axis guard — and it requires both axes to be in position: `disposal_status` must be `MANIFESTED` and `funding_status` must be `APPROVED`. Either one out of position is refused with `ERR_DISPOSAL_COMPLETION_NOT_PERMITTED`. On success the axis moves `MANIFESTED → COMPLETED`, the supplied `completed_at_utc` is recorded on the plan, and the field is retired: `Field.retired_at` is set to that same `completed_at_utc`, which drives `play_status` to `RETIRED` from whichever posture it held — `OPEN → RETIRED`, `RESTRICTED → RETIRED`, `CLOSED_UNSAFE → RETIRED` and `CLOSED_ADMIN → RETIRED` all behave identically — and drives `certification_status` to `NOT_CERTIFIED`. **Every** non-terminal booking on the field is then cancelled, moving `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED_UNSAFE` and `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED_UNSAFE` with no window filter at all, unlike the unsafe cascade of §3.4 which spares fixtures already under way, but visited in that same order of `window_start_utc` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; each such cancellation appends its own `UNSAFE_CANCELLATION` ledger entry with a `wear_units_delta` of 0.00, increments `unsafe_cancellation_count` and forces a rest-debt recompute. `Field.version` increments by 1. `COMPLETED` and `NOT_APPLICABLE` are the disposal axis's terminal states, and `RETIRED` is terminal for the field. The retired field's `name` is now free for its successor to claim, and the successor records the retired field's id as its `predecessor_field_id`. Plans are listable with filters on `field_id`, `capital_budget_id`, `submitted_by_staff_member_id`, `decided_by_staff_member_id`, `funding_status` and `disposal_status`, which is also how cascade-deferred plans are discovered.
