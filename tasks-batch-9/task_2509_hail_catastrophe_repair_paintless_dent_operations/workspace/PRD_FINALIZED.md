# Hail Catastrophe PDR Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Hail catastrophe work is a chase business. A supercell drops golf-ball ice across three counties on a Tuesday afternoon, and within seventy-two hours every paintless dent repair (PDR) team in the country is deciding whether that swath is worth a fleet of trailers, forty traveling technicians, six weeks of motel rooms, and a municipal permit fight. Get the targeting call right and a single storm funds a quarter. Get it wrong — chase a modelled swath that never materialised, or land in a market three competitors already saturated — and the team burns payroll and housing on a queue of vehicles that never arrives.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that business end to end. It is mounted under `/hail-ops` and it carries three tightly coupled economies on one gating surface. The first is **repair pricing**: every hail-damaged vehicle is estimated twice — once through the carrier's per-dent matrix and once as conventional body labour — and the cheaper feasible variant becomes the approved repair, unless the projected cost plus rental exposure crosses the total-loss floor. The second is **crew payroll**: technicians are sourced across traveling crews, deployed into date intervals with rest buffers and shared housing, and paid out of a labour pool that is a fraction of the approved repair value under one of two mutually exclusive split models. The third is **carrier exposure**: direct-billing arrangements, storm-scoped rate agreements, supplement negotiation rounds, and rental days the carrier will not cover.

All three economies are gated by a single derived value — the **facility posture**. A temporary site whose municipal permits have lapsed, whose staging lot is full, or whose on-site licensed crew has gone home cannot take vehicles in, cannot put them on the floor, and cannot accrue labour. Because posture is recomputed lazily on every read and write that touches a facility or any of its children, the specification below is unusually strict about evaluation order, rounding order, and cascade depth. Implementers should treat those orderings as contractual rather than incidental.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Roles and Access Control on the Catastrophe Team

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming that principal's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the nine declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The nine roles are `CatastropheDirector`, `PermitOfficer`, `CrewCoordinator`, `IntakeAdvisor`, `ShopEstimator`, `PdrTechnician`, `QualityInspector`, `InsurerAdjuster`, and `BillingCoordinator`. Their duties divide as follows. A `CatastropheDirector` owns event targeting, facility stand-up, carrier onboarding, rate agreements, demobilization and closeout. A `PermitOfficer` is the municipal authority that decides permit applications and never files them. A `CrewCoordinator` sources technicians, books housing, and drives deployment status. An `IntakeAdvisor` runs the intake lane — vehicles in, damage matrix recorded, finished vehicles released. A `ShopEstimator` prices the matrix, triages repairability, moves vehicles onto the floor, and negotiates supplements from the shop's side. A `PdrTechnician` logs labour. A `QualityInspector` inspects finished work. An `InsurerAdjuster` is the carrier's representative, authorizing and settling direct bills and reviewing supplements. A `BillingCoordinator` is the shop-side biller who submits released vehicles to the carrier.

Six of the nine roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `CatastropheDirector`, `PermitOfficer`, `CrewCoordinator`, `IntakeAdvisor`, `ShopEstimator`, or `BillingCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record or log it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Three roles are **entity-backed**, and for those the header value is an ownership assertion the server checks. When `X-User-Role` is `PdrTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; a labour ticket may only be opened or submitted when the ticket's `technician_id` equals `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityInspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Technician`, and a quality inspection may only be recorded when its `inspector_technician_id` equals `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `InsurerAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Insurer`; billing actions and supplement reviews are permitted only when the target vehicle's `insurer_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Any of these three mismatches is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection reads accept two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request rather than a clamped one.

Every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so a page boundary is stable as long as no new rows are inserted. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the total number of rows matching the filters before pagination — and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every date, expiry, window and interval rule in this service reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it holds that value until it is explicitly moved; the service never falls back to real time. A `CatastropheDirector` may move the clock through an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a single `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the new `current_utc` value. Moving the clock forward is consequential rather than cosmetic: the next posture recomputation on any facility may expire permits, suspend a commissioned site, and invalidate technician licences.

Where a rule speaks of "the current clock date", it means the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC, with the time-of-day discarded. Date-typed fields such as `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `license_expires_at`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `actual_end_date`, `effective_end_date` and `effective_from` are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Instant-typed fields such as `first_hail_at`, `commissioned_at`, `demobilized_at`, `decided_at`, `activated_at`, `inspected_at`, `rental_opened_at`, `rental_closed_at`, `released_at` and `warranty_expires_at` are full ISO 8601 UTC timestamps taken from the logical clock at the moment the writing transition ran. A server-stamped instant renders the logical clock's value as the clock holds it: a fractional-seconds part — three digits carrying the instant's whole milliseconds — appears only when the clock instant carries sub-second precision, so a clock standing on a whole second stamps `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` rather than `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. The `current_utc` the clock-driving capability answers with uses that same rendering, as does `warranty_expires_at`, whose value is a logical instant offset forward. `first_hail_at` is the one instant-typed field a client supplies rather than the server stamping it, and it is stored and returned exactly as the client wrote it.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Versioning and Audit Fields

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Unlike the instant-typed business fields of §2.4, the audit stamps `created_at` and `updated_at` are not read from the logical clock: they record real elapsed time, always render all three fractional digits, and strictly increase over the life of the process, so no two rows ever share a `created_at` and two rows written while the logical clock stands at one and the same instant still order by which of them was written first. The `created_at` ascending ordering of §2.3 and the `created_at` tie-breaks of §3.5 and §3.7 are therefore total orders that agree with the order rows were inserted.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, refreshed on every mutation from that same audit stamp — so a row that has never been mutated since creation carries an `updated_at` equal to its `created_at` — and a `version` integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that resource — including mutations that only rewrite derived money or counter fields. Storm events, facilities, technicians, housing units, crew deployments, insurers, rate agreements, vehicles, work tickets and supplements all behave this way.

Three resource types are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`: **Permit**, **DamageLine** and **QualityInspection** have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. A permit's status still changes over its life, and a damage line still receives computed pricing when the vehicle is estimated, but neither event surfaces an `updated_at` or a `version` on those rows.

### 2.6. Facility Posture: the Master Gate

Each temporary site exposes a derived `posture` whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. Posture is not stored by an operator; it is recomputed by the server at the start of every request that reads or mutates a facility, or that reads or mutates any of that facility's permits, housing units, crew deployments or vehicles. The recomputation runs in a fixed order.

It begins with **lazy permit expiry**: every permit on the facility that is currently `APPROVED` and whose `valid_to` is strictly earlier than the current clock date is moved to `EXPIRED` before anything else is evaluated. Next, permit coverage is judged. The two **required permit types** are `TEMP_BUSINESS_LICENSE` and `ZONING_USE`; coverage holds only when, for each of those two types, the facility has at least one permit of that `permit_type` whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose window contains the current clock date inclusively at both ends — that is, `valid_from` is no later than the current clock date and `valid_to` is no earlier than it. Then the server counts the distinct technicians who are genuinely on site and legal: a deployment on this facility whose `status` is `ON_SITE`, whose technician's `license_state_code` equals the facility's `state_code`, and whose technician's `license_expires_at` is no earlier than the current clock date. Finally the staging counters are refreshed. `staging_slots_occupied` is the number of vehicles at this facility whose `repair_status` is one of the eight non-terminal repair states `INTAKE`, `ESTIMATED`, `TRIAGED`, `IN_REPAIR`, `QC_PENDING`, `QC_FAILED`, `READY` or `TOTAL_LOSS_PENDING`; `staging_slots_free` is `staging_slot_count` minus `staging_slots_occupied`; and `staging_occupancy_ratio` is `staging_slots_occupied` divided by `staging_slot_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

Posture itself is then selected by the first matching condition in this exact sequence. If the facility's `status` is `DEMOBILIZED`, posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if permit coverage does not hold, posture is `FROZEN` — and if the facility was `COMMISSIONED`, the same recomputation moves it to `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if the facility's `status` is `SETUP`, posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if `staging_slots_free` is zero, posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if the count of on-site licensed technicians is zero, posture is `RESTRICTED`. Only when none of those conditions holds is posture `OPEN`.

Because the recomputation is lazy, the `COMMISSIONED` → `SUSPENDED` and `SUSPENDED` → `COMMISSIONED` transitions fire inside whichever request happened to trigger the recomputation, and each of them increments the facility's `version` by one. Suspension is therefore reversible: a facility that lost coverage returns to `COMMISSIONED` as soon as a replacement permit restores coverage for every required type, and its original `commissioned_at` instant is never rewritten by that return.

### 2.7. Money, Precision and Rounding Order

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in **whole cents**, and every monetary rounding uses **round half up**. There are no two-decimal money fields; a field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer count of cents. Rate and percentage inputs such as `admin_fee_pct`, `supplement_auto_approve_pct` and `flat_split_pct` are fractions between 0 and 1 and preserve the precision they were supplied with.

Two rounding orders are pinned and must not be optimised away. First, **per-panel rounding precedes summation**: each damage line's paintless charge is rounded to whole cents on its own, and only the rounded per-panel values are summed into the vehicle's variant total. Summing unrounded panel values and rounding once at the end produces a different answer and is incorrect. Second, in the billable waterfall the administrative fee is keyed on the repair subtotal while the volume discount is keyed on the gross amount that already contains that fee; the fee is never keyed on the discounted amount, and the chain is never iterated to a fixed point.

The only non-integer derived value in the system is `staging_occupancy_ratio`, which is rounded half up to four decimal places. Counts such as `rental_days_billed`, `bed_nights_committed`, `occupied_beds` and `projected_days_to_clear` are whole numbers, and `projected_days_to_clear` in particular is rounded **up** to the next whole day rather than half up.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Derived Aggregates

Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource. When they do, every affected resource's derived fields are brought up to date within the same request, and every mutated resource's `updated_at` and `version` move accordingly. Cascades are described hop by hop in §3 so that implementers can see how deep each one runs; the deepest are three hops.

A number of fields are pure aggregates that the server maintains rather than accepting from a client, and they are recomputed whenever any contributing row changes. On a facility, `billable_accrued_cents` is the sum of `net_billable_cents` over that facility's vehicles whose `billing_status` is `AUTHORIZED`, `BILLED` or `PAID`, and `absorbed_rental_cents` is the sum of `shop_absorbed_rental_cents` over that facility's vehicles whose `repair_status` is `RELEASED` or `TOWED_OUT`. On a carrier, `outstanding_direct_bill_cents` is the sum of `net_billable_cents` over that carrier's vehicles whose `billing_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `BILLED`, `settled_cents` is the same sum restricted to `billing_status` equal to `PAID`, and `active_rate_agreement_count` is the number of that carrier's rate agreements whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. On a technician, `accrued_pay_cents` is the sum of `payable_cents` over that technician's work tickets whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `approved_ticket_count` is the number of such tickets, and `deployment_state` is `DEPLOYED` when at least one of that technician's crew deployments has `status` of `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SITE` and `AVAILABLE` otherwise. On a vehicle, `approved_supplement_cents` is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over that vehicle's supplements whose `status` is `AUTO_APPROVED` or `APPROVED`. All of these default to zero (and `deployment_state` defaults to `AVAILABLE`) when no contributing row exists.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Guards on Two Write Paths

Exactly two write paths in this system take an optimistic-concurrency input, and each names its own parent.

`POST /hail-ops/rate-agreements/{id}/activate` requires `expected_insurer_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the carrier named by the agreement's `insurer_id`. If that value does not equal the carrier's current `version`, the activation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSURER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because activation legitimately supersedes the carrier's previous active agreement for the same storm event, so a writer holding a stale carrier version is promoting a rate sheet negotiated against a rate landscape that no longer exists.

`POST /hail-ops/supplements` requires `expected_vehicle_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the vehicle named by `vehicle_id`. If that value does not equal the vehicle's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VEHICLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because both the auto-approval threshold and the cumulative supplement cap are computed from the vehicle's `approved_repair_cents` and `approved_supplement_cents`, so a stale view would be gated against numbers that have already moved.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints outside these two paths must not require, and must not honour, an `expected_..._version` input.

### 2.10. Collections, Reverse Lookups and List Filters

Every business resource lives directly under a top-level collection of `/hail-ops`; no business resource is nested inside another's URL path. Peers are joined by explicit identifier fields carried in request bodies — `storm_event_id`, `facility_id`, `technician_id`, `housing_unit_id`, `insurer_id`, `vehicle_id`, `rate_agreement_id` and `supersedes_supplement_id`.

Four read paths are nested, and all four are read-only reverse lookups: the permits of a facility, the housing units of a facility, the throughput summary of a facility, and the damage lines of a vehicle. Path parameters on any nested or single-resource path are validated left to right, so an unresolvable facility identifier surfaces as `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` before any filter on the sub-collection is considered. Unresolvable identifiers report the type-specific code where one exists — `ERR_STORM_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSURER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` — and fall back to `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for permits, housing units, crew deployments, damage lines, work tickets, supplements and quality inspections.

List endpoints accept optional equality filters in addition to `limit` and `offset`. Facilities may be filtered by `storm_event_id` and `status`. The permits of a facility may be filtered by `permit_type` and `status`. Crew deployments may be filtered by `technician_id`, `facility_id`, `housing_unit_id` and `status`. Rate agreements may be filtered by `insurer_id`, `storm_event_id` and `status`. Vehicles may be filtered by `facility_id`, `insurer_id`, `pinned_rate_agreement_id`, `repair_status` and `billing_status` — the two status dimensions are independent filters. A vehicle's damage lines may be filtered by `routed_to`. Work tickets may be filtered by `vehicle_id`, `technician_id` and `status`. Supplements may be filtered by `vehicle_id`, `rate_agreement_id`, `supersedes_supplement_id` and `status`. Quality inspections may be filtered by `vehicle_id`, `inspector_technician_id` and `result`.

### 2.11. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates several rules at once, the response reports the violation with the lowest ordinal in the list below. The ordering is contractual: a mid-flight lifecycle problem must not be masked by a later fiscal check, and a fiscal check must not be reported for a request that was never authorized in the first place.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and role declared: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. **Endpoint role authorization** — the acting role appears on the endpoint's authorized list: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. **Request shape** — required fields present, enum values inside their declared sets, and numeric ranges honoured: `dent_count` at least 1, `acv_cents` at least 1, `staging_slot_count` at least 1, `bed_count` at least 1, `flat_split_pct` greater than 0 and at most 1, `labor_units` at least 0, `requested_amount_cents` at least 1, `start_date` no later than `end_date`, `valid_from` no later than `valid_to`, `defect_panel_codes` empty when `result` is `PASS`, and `vin` exactly 17 characters. Violations yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint.
4. **Path existence** — every identifier in the path resolves, checked left to right: the 404 family above, on every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
5. **Body foreign-key existence** — `storm_event_id`, `facility_id`, `insurer_id`, `technician_id`, `housing_unit_id`, `vehicle_id` and `supersedes_supplement_id` resolve: the 404 family above, on `POST /hail-ops/facilities`, `POST /hail-ops/permits`, `POST /hail-ops/housing-units`, `POST /hail-ops/crew-deployments`, `POST /hail-ops/rate-agreements`, `POST /hail-ops/vehicles`, `POST /hail-ops/damage-lines`, `POST /hail-ops/work-tickets`, `POST /hail-ops/supplements` and `POST /hail-ops/quality-inspections`.
6. **Entity-backed identity** — `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (403), on `POST /hail-ops/work-tickets`, `POST /hail-ops/work-tickets/{id}/submit`, `POST /hail-ops/quality-inspections`, `POST /hail-ops/vehicles/{id}/billing` and `POST /hail-ops/supplements/{id}/review`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Targeting a Storm and Committing to the Deployment

A catastrophe response begins when a `CatastropheDirector` registers a candidate hail event. The registration carries the event's operational `name`, the two-uppercase-letter `region_state_code` of the affected area, the largest reported stone diameter as `max_hail_size_mm` (at least 1), the swath polygon area as `swath_area_km2` (at least 1), a `swath_confidence` of either `MODELED` or `GROUND_TRUTHED`, the `vehicle_population_estimate` inside the swath (zero or more), the `competitor_shop_count` already working the area (zero or more), and the `first_hail_at` instant of first hail fall. The new event lands in `EVALUATING` and its `demobilized_at` is null.

At the moment of registration — and only then — the server computes a `targeting_score` between 0 and 100 from those four swath and market inputs. No other entity participates in the score, and the score is never recomputed afterwards, even if the market changes. The computation runs as follows. A severity weight is chosen from the provenance of the swath data: 2.2 when `swath_confidence` is `GROUND_TRUTHED`, and 1.6 when it is `MODELED`, because ground-truthed hail reports earn more confidence than a model run. The hail diameter is then capped at 60 millimetres — stones larger than that add no further points — and the **severity term** is the severity weight multiplied by that capped diameter, divided by two. The **density term** rewards vehicle population at one point per 5,000 vehicles, with the population itself capped at 250,000 vehicles before the division. A flat **swath bonus** of 8 points is added when `swath_area_km2` is at least 400 square kilometres, and nothing is added below that threshold. A **competition penalty** of 1.5 points is subtracted for each competing team, counting at most 12 competitors. The raw score is the severity term plus the density term plus the swath bonus minus the competition penalty; all four terms are carried in exact decimal arithmetic. That raw value is then rounded half up to an integer and clamped into the range 0 through 100 inclusive — anything negative becomes 0 and anything above 100 becomes 100 — and the result is stored as `targeting_score`.

The director then records a decision, supplying a `decision` value of either `TARGET` or `DECLINE`. Both branches require the event to still be `EVALUATING`; a second decision on an already-decided event is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STORM_EVENT_STATE`. The two branches differ in exactly one respect. `TARGET` is score-gated: the event's `targeting_score` must be at least 60, and a lower score is rejected with `ERR_TARGETING_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR` — the storm is real, but not worth the trailers. `DECLINE` carries no score gate at all and may be recorded at any score. A `TARGET` decision moves `EVALUATING` → `TARGETED`; a `DECLINE` decision moves `EVALUATING` → `DECLINED`, which is terminal. Either way the event's `version` increments by one.

Once at least one site is standing, the director opens the event for production, moving `TARGETED` → `ACTIVE`. Activation requires the event to be `TARGETED` and requires at least one facility of the event to have `status` equal to `COMMISSIONED`; with no commissioned site the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_COMMISSIONED_FACILITY`. Activation increments the event's `version` by one.

### 3.2. Standing Up a Temporary Site: Permits, Commissioning and the Suspension Cycle

With the event `TARGETED` or already `ACTIVE`, a `CatastropheDirector` stands up one or more temporary repair facilities. The creation payload names the parent event through `storm_event_id`, the facility `name`, the two-uppercase-letter `state_code` the lot physically sits in, the street `address`, the `staging_slot_count` of vehicle slots on that lot (at least 1), and the crew `pay_model`, which is either `FLAT` or `POOLED` and permanently selects which pay split the site's crews are paid under. Attempting to stand up a facility against an event that is neither `TARGETED` nor `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STORM_EVENT_STATE`. A new facility begins in `SETUP` with `warranty_counter` at 0, a null `commissioned_at`, and — because a `SETUP` site is never fully open — a posture of `RESTRICTED`.

Permits are filed against the site by the same director, naming the `facility_id`, the `permit_type` — one of `TEMP_BUSINESS_LICENSE`, `ZONING_USE`, `FIRE_OCCUPANCY` or `SIGNAGE` — and the `issuing_jurisdiction`, the municipality or county that will rule on it. A filed permit sits in `PENDING` with a null `valid_from`, a null `valid_to` and a null `decided_at`; the shop does not get to choose its own validity window.

The municipality answers separately. A `PermitOfficer` — and nobody else, since the party that applies for a permit can never be the party that decides it — records a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` on a permit that is still `PENDING`; a decision on any other status is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_ALREADY_DECIDED`. An `APPROVE` decision must supply both `valid_from` and `valid_to`, with `valid_from` no later than `valid_to`, and both dates are persisted on the permit as the officer supplied them; the permit moves `PENDING` → `APPROVED` and `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock. A `DENY` decision needs no dates, moves `PENDING` → `DENIED`, which is terminal, and likewise stamps `decided_at`. Both branches trigger a posture recomputation on the owning facility, and the `APPROVE` branch is the one that can heal a site: a `SUSPENDED` facility whose coverage becomes complete again returns `SUSPENDED` → `COMMISSIONED` inside the very same request.

Commissioning is the director's declaration that the site is legally and practically able to work. It moves the facility `SETUP` → `COMMISSIONED` and demands four things at once. The facility's own `status` must be `SETUP`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE`. The parent event's `status` must be `TARGETED` or `ACTIVE`, or the rejection is `ERR_INVALID_STORM_EVENT_STATE`. For each of the two required permit types, `TEMP_BUSINESS_LICENSE` and `ZONING_USE`, the site must hold a permit that is `APPROVED` and whose window contains the current clock date inclusively at both ends; a missing, denied, expired or out-of-window permit for either type is rejected with `ERR_REQUIRED_PERMIT_MISSING`. And at least one crew deployment on the site must be `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SITE` with a technician whose `license_state_code` equals the facility's `state_code` and whose `license_expires_at` is no earlier than the current clock date; without such a deployment the rejection is `ERR_NO_LICENSED_TECHNICIAN`. On success the facility becomes `COMMISSIONED`, `commissioned_at` is stamped from the logical clock, posture is recomputed, and `version` increments by one.

The interesting part of a facility's life is that commissioning is not permanent. Because posture recomputation runs on the way into every request touching the site or its children, an expiring `TEMP_BUSINESS_LICENSE` moves that permit `APPROVED` → `EXPIRED` and, in the same recomputation, drives the facility `COMMISSIONED` → `SUSPENDED` with posture `FROZEN` and a `version` increment. Nothing may be taken in or worked on at a frozen site. When the municipality later approves a replacement permit covering every required type, the next recomputation drives `SUSPENDED` → `COMMISSIONED`, recomputes posture to `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` as the staging and crew conditions dictate, and increments `version` again — closing the `COMMISSIONED` → `SUSPENDED` → `COMMISSIONED` cycle without ever rewriting `commissioned_at`.

Operations planners also have a computed throughput read on each site. It answers with the site's `staging_slot_count`, `staging_slots_occupied`, `staging_slots_free`, `staging_occupancy_ratio` and current `posture`, together with the queue partition: `open_vehicle_count` is the number of the site's vehicles in the eight non-terminal repair states, `vehicles_in_repair` counts those whose `repair_status` is exactly `IN_REPAIR`, `vehicles_ready` counts those in `READY`, and `released_count` counts those that have left in either terminal state, `RELEASED` or `TOWED_OUT`. It also reports `on_site_technician_count`, the number of distinct technicians whose deployment at the site is `ON_SITE`, and `projected_days_to_clear`. The projection assumes each on-site technician clears one and a half vehicles per day: daily capacity is one and a half multiplied by the on-site technician count, except that the count is treated as at least one so that an unstaffed site still yields a finite projection, and `projected_days_to_clear` is `open_vehicle_count` divided by that daily capacity, rounded **up** to a whole number of days. An empty queue projects zero days. The read closes with the money view — `billable_accrued_cents` and `absorbed_rental_cents` — and, like every facility read, it recomputes posture on the way in and may therefore expire permits or flip the site between `COMMISSIONED` and `SUSPENDED` as a side effect.

### 3.3. Sourcing Traveling Crews, Deployment Intervals and Housing

Crews are the second economy. A `CrewCoordinator` adds a traveling technician to the roster with a `full_name`, the `home_state_code` of their home base, the two-uppercase-letter `license_state_code` of the state whose repair licence they actually hold, the `license_expires_at` date on which that licence lapses inclusively, a `skill_tier` of `APPRENTICE`, `JOURNEYMAN` or `MASTER`, and a `flat_split_pct` strictly greater than 0 and at most 1 representing their contracted share of ticket work value. A fresh technician has `deployment_state` of `AVAILABLE`, `accrued_pay_cents` of 0 and `approved_ticket_count` of 0.

Housing is registered against a site with the `facility_id`, a human `label` such as a motel room name, a `bed_count` of at least 1 available for concurrent occupancy, and the `nightly_rate_cents` cost of the whole unit (zero or more). A housing unit reports two derived numbers. `occupied_beds` is the number of deployments on the unit that are `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SITE` and whose interval covers the current clock date, counting the date from `start_date` inclusive up to but not including the deployment's effective end. `bed_nights_committed` sums, over the unit's deployments that are `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SITE` or `ENDED`, the whole number of days from `start_date` to the deployment's effective end date — so a cancelled booking contributes nothing while an ended one contributes its shortened stay.

A deployment is created by naming the `technician_id`, the `facility_id`, an inclusive `start_date` and an inclusive `end_date` no earlier than the start, and optionally a `housing_unit_id`; when the housing identifier is omitted the technician self-houses and the field stays null. Creation copies the technician's `flat_split_pct` into `pay_split_pct_snapshot`, freezing it on the deployment row for the record — although, as §3.7 makes explicit, the pay computation itself reads the technician's live `flat_split_pct` rather than this snapshot. The new deployment starts `CONFIRMED`, with a null `actual_end_date`; its `effective_end_date` is the planned `end_date` until the deployment ends, after which it is the `actual_end_date`. Every interval and bed-night computation in the system reads `effective_end_date`, never the raw `end_date`. Creating a deployment also refreshes the technician's `deployment_state` and, when housing is named, that unit's occupancy figures.

Two safety rules guard creation, and they are checked in this order. First, the technician must be licensed: `license_expires_at` must be no earlier than the current clock date and `license_state_code` must equal the destination facility's `state_code`, since work performed under an out-of-state licence is unbillable. A violation is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_LICENSE_INVALID`. Second, **deployment exclusivity** protects the traveling crew from being double-booked and from being driven straight from one storm to the next. A rest buffer of one calendar day is added to both ends of every interval before the comparison, so the requested deployment collides with an existing `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SITE` deployment of the same technician whenever the requested `start_date` is no later than one day after that existing deployment's effective end, **and** one day after the requested `end_date` is no earlier than that existing deployment's `start_date`. The practical consequence is that at least one clear calendar day must separate two deployments of the same technician; back-to-back intervals collide. A collision is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DEPLOYMENT_OVERLAP`.

Housing capacity is checked last, and it is an aggregate rather than a pairwise rule. For every calendar date in the half-open span running from the requested `start_date` inclusive up to the requested `end_date` exclusive, the server counts the deployments already on that housing unit that are `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SITE` and whose own half-open span — from their `start_date` inclusive up to their effective end exclusive — contains that date. Adding one bed for the requested deployment, the total on every one of those dates must not exceed the unit's `bed_count`. If any single date would overflow, the whole request is rejected with `ERR_HOUSING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Deployment status is driven by the coordinator through a `to_status` value of `ON_SITE`, `ENDED` or `CANCELLED`, and each target has its own from-state. Moving `CONFIRMED` → `ON_SITE` requires the deployment to be `CONFIRMED`, the destination facility's `status` to be `SETUP` or `COMMISSIONED`, and the technician's `license_expires_at` to be no earlier than the current clock date — an expired licence is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_LICENSE_INVALID`. Arrival increments the deployment's `version` and recomputes the facility's posture, which is how an arriving licensed technician can lift a site out of `RESTRICTED`. Moving `CONFIRMED` → `CANCELLED` requires only that the deployment be `CONFIRMED`; the deployment becomes terminal, its `version` increments, the housing unit's `occupied_beds` and `bed_nights_committed` are recomputed — the cancelled interval drops out of both sums — and the technician's `deployment_state` is refreshed. Moving `ON_SITE` → `ENDED` requires the deployment to be `ON_SITE`; it writes `actual_end_date` from the current clock date, which immediately becomes the `effective_end_date`, increments `version`, recomputes the housing unit's `occupied_beds` and `bed_nights_committed` from the shortened interval, and refreshes the technician's `deployment_state`. Any of these three requested from a status that does not allow it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`.

### 3.4. Carrier Onboarding, Rate Agreements and the Stale-Carrier Guard

The third economy is the carrier relationship. A `CatastropheDirector` registers a carrier with its `name`, its `naic_code` identifier, and the `direct_bill_enabled` feature flag. That flag is consequential rather than informational: when it is false the carrier refuses direct billing and no vehicle of that carrier may ever be authorized. A carrier's `outstanding_direct_bill_cents`, `settled_cents` and `active_rate_agreement_count` all begin at 0.

Rate sheets are storm-scoped. A drafted agreement names its `insurer_id` and `storm_event_id`, the `effective_from` date on which its rates may first be pinned to a vehicle, and then the full pricing surface: four per-dent matrix rates — `matrix_rate_dime_cents`, `matrix_rate_nickel_cents`, `matrix_rate_quarter_cents` and `matrix_rate_half_dollar_cents`, each at least 1 cent — a `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` for body and remove-and-install labour (at least 1 cent), a `panel_cap_cents` ceiling above which a single panel's paintless charge is considered uneconomic (at least 1 cent), an `admin_fee_pct` fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive with its `admin_fee_cap_cents` ceiling (zero or more cents), a `supplement_auto_approve_pct` fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive, and the rental terms `rental_cap_days` and `rental_daily_rate_cents`, both zero or more. A drafted agreement lands in `DRAFT` with a null `activated_at`, and drafting mutates nothing on the carrier — the carrier's `version` is untouched by a draft.

Activation is the mutation that matters, and it is guarded. The director activating a `DRAFT` agreement must supply `expected_insurer_version` carrying the last carrier `version` they observed; a mismatch against the carrier's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSURER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Activation also requires the agreement to still be `DRAFT` and the parent event's `status` to be `TARGETED` or `ACTIVE`, the latter rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STORM_EVENT_STATE`. On success the agreement moves `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE`, `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `version` increments. The cascade then runs two hops: every other agreement sharing the same carrier and the same storm event that was `ACTIVE` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with its own `version` increment, and the carrier's `version` increments by one while its `active_rate_agreement_count` is recomputed. That carrier version bump is precisely what makes a second activation attempt with a now-stale `expected_insurer_version` fail.

Supersession is deliberately toothless towards work already priced. A vehicle that pinned an agreement keeps its `pinned_rate_agreement_id` unchanged when that agreement is superseded, and every rate the vehicle's money chain reads continues to come from the pinned sheet rather than from whichever sheet is currently `ACTIVE`. Frozen rates survive supersession; that is the whole point of pinning.

### 3.5. Vehicle Intake, the Damage Matrix and Dual-Variant Pricing

An `IntakeAdvisor` takes a hail-damaged car into a staging slot. The intake payload names the `facility_id` of the site, the `insurer_id` of the carrier of record, the 17-character `vin`, the `model_year`, `make` and `model`, the `acv_cents` actual cash value of at least 1 cent, the `owner_name` of the registered owner, and the `rental_required` flag saying whether the customer takes a rental while the car waits. Intake requires the site's `status` to be `COMMISSIONED` — a `SETUP`, `SUSPENDED` or `DEMOBILIZED` site is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_COMMISSIONED` — and requires the site's `posture` to be exactly `OPEN`, since a `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` lot must not accept new metal; a posture violation is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Intake also enforces VIN uniqueness within the lot: no other vehicle at the same facility may carry the same `vin` while its `repair_status` is anything other than `RELEASED` or `TOWED_OUT`, and a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN_AT_FACILITY`. A car released last week may legitimately come back under the same VIN.

The new vehicle begins with `repair_status` of `INTAKE` and `billing_status` of `UNBILLED` — two independent dimensions living on one row, the first driven by the shop and the second by the carrier. Its `pinned_rate_agreement_id`, `matrix_variant_total_cents`, `conventional_variant_total_cents`, `triage_disposition`, `warranty_number` and `warranty_expires_at` are all null; `approved_repair_cents`, `approved_supplement_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `gross_billable_cents`, `discount_cents`, `net_billable_cents`, `rework_count`, `rental_days_billed` and `shop_absorbed_rental_cents` are all 0; and `released_at` and `rental_closed_at` are null. When `rental_required` is true, intake stamps `rental_opened_at` from the logical clock, starting the rental exposure clock; when it is false, `rental_opened_at` stays null. Intake also refreshes the facility's staging counters, occupancy ratio and posture — a car that fills the last slot drives the lot straight to `RESTRICTED` and blocks the next intake.

The damage matrix is recorded panel by panel, one row per panel, by the same intake advisor. Each row names its `vehicle_id` and its `panel_code`, one of `HOOD`, `ROOF`, `DECK_LID`, `LEFT_FENDER`, `RIGHT_FENDER`, `LEFT_FRONT_DOOR`, `RIGHT_FRONT_DOOR`, `LEFT_REAR_DOOR`, `RIGHT_REAR_DOOR`, `LEFT_QUARTER` or `RIGHT_QUARTER`. It carries the `dent_count` on that panel (at least 1), the `dent_size_class` of `DIME`, `NICKEL`, `QUARTER` or `HALF_DOLLAR`, and the `aluminum` flag marking a panel whose metal is harder to push. It optionally carries `r_and_i_hours`, the remove-and-install labour hours needed to reach the panel, defaulting to 0, and `oversize_dent_count`, the number of dents simply too large for paintless work, also defaulting to 0 and never negative. Finally, because the shop prices both repair philosophies for every panel, each row carries the conventional inputs as well: `conventional_labor_hours` of body labour and `conventional_paint_materials_cents` of paint and materials, both zero or more. Damage lines may only be added while the vehicle's `repair_status` is `INTAKE` or `ESTIMATED`; later than that the matrix is settled, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VEHICLE_REPAIR_STATE`. The computed fields `pdr_line_cents`, `conventional_line_cents`, `capped` and `routed_to` stay null (with `capped` defaulting to false) until the next estimate runs, and damage lines are immutable once written.

Estimating is a `ShopEstimator` action that advances the vehicle to `ESTIMATED`, and it is re-runnable: the estimator may run it from `INTAKE` or from `ESTIMATED`, so adding a missed panel and re-pricing is a normal move. Any other repair state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VEHICLE_REPAIR_STATE`, and a vehicle with no damage lines at all is rejected with `ERR_NO_DAMAGE_LINES`.

The estimate's first act is to choose the rate sheet. The candidate agreements are those whose `insurer_id` equals the vehicle's `insurer_id`, whose `storm_event_id` equals the storm event of the vehicle's facility, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `effective_from` is no later than the current clock date. Drafted and superseded sheets are not candidates. Candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending — the most recently effective sheet wins — and ties on that date are broken by `created_at` descending, never by identifier. The first row after that ranking is pinned into `pinned_rate_agreement_id`, replacing whatever a previous estimate had pinned, since a re-estimate re-runs the selector. When no candidate exists at all, the estimate is rejected with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATE_AGREEMENT`.

With the sheet pinned, every damage line of the vehicle is priced under both variants, processed in `created_at` ascending order so that per-panel rounding is reproducible. The **paintless variant** works through the carrier's matrix. The per-dent rate is read from the pinned sheet according to the panel's `dent_size_class` — the dime rate for `DIME`, the nickel rate for `NICKEL`, the quarter rate for `QUARTER`, the half-dollar rate for `HALF_DOLLAR`. A dent-count tier multiplier rewards volume: 1.00 when `dent_count` is 15 or fewer, 0.85 when `dent_count` is greater than 15 and at most 50, and 0.70 when `dent_count` is greater than 50. An aluminum multiplier of 1.30 applies when the panel's `aluminum` flag is true and 1.00 when it is false. The panel's raw paintless charge is the per-dent rate multiplied by `dent_count`, multiplied by the tier multiplier, multiplied by the aluminum multiplier, plus `r_and_i_hours` multiplied by the sheet's `labor_rate_cents_per_hour`. That raw charge is rounded half up to whole cents and stored as `pdr_line_cents`. The rounded value is then compared against the sheet's `panel_cap_cents`: if it is strictly greater than the cap, `capped` becomes true and `pdr_line_cents` is reduced to exactly the cap; otherwise `capped` stays false and the rounded value stands. A panel is routed to `CONVENTIONAL` in `routed_to` when it was capped or when its `oversize_dent_count` is greater than zero, and to `PDR` otherwise. The **conventional variant** prices the same panel as `conventional_labor_hours` multiplied by the sheet's `labor_rate_cents_per_hour`, plus `conventional_paint_materials_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents and stored as `conventional_line_cents`; it ignores dent counts, tier multipliers and the aluminum multiplier entirely, and the paintless variant in turn ignores paint materials entirely, which is why identical panel rows produce genuinely different variant totals.

Both variants price every panel, and the vehicle's two totals are then the sums of the rounded per-panel values: `matrix_variant_total_cents` is the sum of every `pdr_line_cents` (post-cap, where a cap applied), and `conventional_variant_total_cents` is the sum of every `conventional_line_cents`. Feasibility follows from the panel-level findings: `pdr_feasible` is true only when no damage line of the vehicle was capped and the total of every line's `oversize_dent_count` across the vehicle is zero. A single capped panel or a single oversize dent anywhere on the car takes the whole vehicle off the paintless path. The estimate closes by increasing the vehicle's `version` by one.

### 3.6. Repairability Triage and the Net-Billable Waterfall

Triage is the shop's recommendation among paintless repair, conventional repair and total loss, and it is a `ShopEstimator` action reachable from `ESTIMATED` or, in the rework case described in §3.7, from `QC_FAILED`. From any other repair state it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VEHICLE_REPAIR_STATE`.

The triage runs on values already stored on the vehicle plus the pinned sheet's rental terms. It first projects the rental exposure the job will carry: when `rental_required` is true, the projection is the pinned sheet's `rental_cap_days` multiplied by its `rental_daily_rate_cents`; when `rental_required` is false, the projection is zero. It then picks a candidate. If `pdr_feasible` is false, the candidate is `CONVENTIONAL` at the vehicle's `conventional_variant_total_cents` — the paintless path is simply unavailable. Otherwise, if `matrix_variant_total_cents` is less than or equal to `conventional_variant_total_cents`, the candidate is `PDR_REPAIR` at the matrix total, ties going to paintless. Otherwise the candidate is `CONVENTIONAL` at the conventional total. Next the total-loss floor is computed as 75 percent of the vehicle's `acv_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The candidate cost plus the projected rental exposure is compared against that floor, and the comparison is **inclusive**: if the sum is greater than or equal to the floor, the vehicle is totalled — `triage_disposition` becomes `TOTAL_LOSS` and `approved_repair_cents` is set to 0 — and a sum exactly equal to the floor totals the car. Otherwise `triage_disposition` becomes the candidate, either `PDR_REPAIR` or `CONVENTIONAL`, and `approved_repair_cents` becomes the candidate cost.

The two outcomes drive different repair states. A disposition of `PDR_REPAIR` or `CONVENTIONAL` moves the vehicle `ESTIMATED` → `TRIAGED` and resets `rework_count` to 0. A disposition of `TOTAL_LOSS` moves the vehicle `ESTIMATED` → `TOTAL_LOSS_PENDING`, leaving `approved_repair_cents` at 0 so that the whole money chain recomputes to zero — an administrative fee on a zero subtotal is zero. Either way the money chain is rewritten and the vehicle's `version` increments by one.

That money chain — the **net-billable waterfall** — is recomputed on exactly three occasions: at triage, when a supplement auto-approves at filing, and when an adjuster approves a supplement. Its six steps run in a pinned order. Step one, the repair subtotal is `approved_repair_cents` plus `approved_supplement_cents`. Step two, `admin_fee_cents` is the pinned sheet's `admin_fee_pct` multiplied by that subtotal, rounded half up to whole cents, and then reduced to the sheet's `admin_fee_cap_cents` whenever the rounded fee would exceed the cap — the fee is whichever of the two is smaller. Step three, `gross_billable_cents` is the subtotal plus the administrative fee. Step four selects a volume discount rate from the gross amount, checking the higher tier first: 4 percent when `gross_billable_cents` is at or above 500,000 cents (5,000 dollars), otherwise 2 percent when it is at or above 250,000 cents (2,500 dollars), and otherwise 0 percent. Both tier comparisons are inclusive at their floor, so a gross of exactly 250,000 cents earns the 2 percent tier. Step five, `discount_cents` is that discount rate multiplied by `gross_billable_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. Step six, `net_billable_cents` is `gross_billable_cents` minus `discount_cents`. Rounding happens only at step two and step five. The rates always come from the vehicle's pinned agreement and never from whichever agreement is currently `ACTIVE`, and the supplement contribution counts only supplements whose `status` is `AUTO_APPROVED` or `APPROVED` — a `DENIED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `COUNTERED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` supplement contributes nothing.

### 3.7. The Repair Floor: Labour Tickets, Quality Inspection, the Rework Loop and Crew Pay

A triaged vehicle reaches the floor when a `ShopEstimator` starts the repair, moving `TRIAGED` → `IN_REPAIR`. Starting a repair is the most heavily gated transition in the system because it commits crew time against carrier money. The vehicle's `repair_status` must be `TRIAGED` or `QC_FAILED`, its `triage_disposition` must be `PDR_REPAIR` or `CONVENTIONAL` — a totalled car never goes onto the floor — its `billing_status` must be `AUTHORIZED`, the site's `posture` must be anything other than `FROZEN`, and the vehicle's `rework_count` must be at most 2. A missing authorization is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VEHICLE_BILLING_STATE`, a frozen site with `ERR_FACILITY_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, an uncommissioned site with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_COMMISSIONED`, and a vehicle that has already failed inspection more than twice with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Success increments the vehicle's `version` by one.

Labour is claimed by the technician who performed it. A `PdrTechnician` opens a ticket naming the `vehicle_id`, their own `technician_id`, the `labor_units` hour-equivalents claimed (zero or more), the `dents_repaired` pushed on that ticket and the `panels_worked` touched (both zero or more). Tickets may only be opened against a vehicle whose `repair_status` is `IN_REPAIR`, at a site whose `status` is `COMMISSIONED` and whose `posture` is not `FROZEN`, by a technician whose licence is valid — `license_expires_at` no earlier than the current clock date and `license_state_code` matching the site's `state_code`, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_LICENSE_INVALID` — and who actually has a deployment with `status` of `ON_SITE` at that vehicle's facility, else `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_DEPLOYED`. Because the role is entity-backed, the ticket's `technician_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. A new ticket lands in `OPEN` with `payable_flat_cents`, `payable_pooled_cents` and `payable_cents` all 0 and `pay_model_applied` null.

Submitting a ticket moves it `OPEN` → `SUBMITTED`. The ticket must be `OPEN`, its `labor_units` must be strictly greater than zero — a zero-labour submission is rejected with `ERR_ZERO_LABOR_UNITS` — and the vehicle must still be `IN_REPAIR`; a ticket in any other state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. Submission increments the ticket's `version`. The submission that leaves **no** `OPEN` ticket on the vehicle, provided at least one ticket on that vehicle is now `SUBMITTED`, additionally drives the vehicle `IN_REPAIR` → `QC_PENDING` with a `version` increment of its own. Handing the car to quality control is therefore not a separate operator action; it is the arithmetic consequence of the last technician putting their pencil down.

Inspection enforces the team's hardest segregation rule: the technician who logged repair labour on a vehicle can never be that vehicle's quality inspector. A `QualityInspector` records an inspection naming the `vehicle_id`, their own `inspector_technician_id` (which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`), a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, and optionally `defect_panel_codes`, an array of panel codes that defaults to empty. The vehicle must be `QC_PENDING`; the inspecting technician must hold **no** work ticket of any status whatsoever on that vehicle, and holding one is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the inspector must be licensed for the site and must have an `ON_SITE` deployment at the vehicle's facility, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_LICENSE_INVALID` or `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_DEPLOYED`; and the site must be `COMMISSIONED`. A `PASS` must arrive with `defect_panel_codes` empty and a `FAIL` must name at least one panel — a non-empty list on a `PASS` and an empty list on a `FAIL` are both rejected as malformed with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every inspection stamps `inspected_at` from the logical clock and records `rework_index`, the vehicle's `rework_count` as it stands after this inspection has been applied.

A `FAIL` moves the vehicle `QC_PENDING` → `QC_FAILED`, increases `rework_count` by one, and writes that new count into the inspection's `rework_index`. A `PASS` moves the vehicle `QC_PENDING` → `READY` and triggers the crew-pay cascade, which runs two hops. Hop one: every work ticket of that vehicle whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED`, which is terminal, receiving `payable_flat_cents`, `payable_pooled_cents`, `payable_cents` and `pay_model_applied` in the process, each with a `version` increment. Hop two, caused by hop one: every technician holding one of those newly approved tickets recomputes `accrued_pay_cents` and `approved_ticket_count`.

The pay computation itself is where the two crew-pay models diverge, and both variants are always computed and stored so that the site's choice is auditable. The ticket set under consideration is exactly those tickets of the vehicle whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` at the instant the passing inspection is recorded, ordered by `created_at` ascending; open tickets cannot exist at that instant, and voided tickets are excluded. The vehicle's labour pool is 55 percent of its `approved_repair_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — note that supplements never enter the labour pool, which is keyed on the approved repair value alone. Total labour units is the sum of `labor_units` across the ticket set.

Under the **flat** model, each technician is paid their own contract percentage and skill tier is irrelevant. A ticket's work value is the labour pool multiplied by that ticket's `labor_units` divided by the total labour units, rounded half up to whole cents; the ticket's `payable_flat_cents` is that work value multiplied by the technician's own `flat_split_pct`, again rounded half up to whole cents. Nothing constrains the flat payouts to sum to any particular figure.

Under the **pooled** model a fixed purse is divided by tier-weighted labour, each technician's `flat_split_pct` is ignored, and the purse is conserved to the cent. The purse is 50 percent of the vehicle's labour pool, rounded half up to whole cents. Each ticket's weight is its `labor_units` multiplied by the tier weight of its technician's `skill_tier`: 0.80 for `APPRENTICE`, 1.00 for `JOURNEYMAN` and 1.25 for `MASTER`, so a master earns one and a quarter times a journeyman for the same hours. Each ticket's exact share is the purse multiplied by that ticket's weight divided by the total weight across the set, and `payable_pooled_cents` is that exact share rounded half up to whole cents. Because per-ticket rounding can drift the total away from the purse, a residual is then computed as the purse minus the sum of the rounded pooled payouts. When the residual is not zero it is applied in full to a single ticket: the one whose exact share had the largest fractional part, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by identifier. That ticket's `payable_pooled_cents` absorbs the residual, whether positive or negative, so that the pooled payouts sum to exactly the purse.

Selection between the two is the site's standing policy rather than a per-ticket choice. Each approved ticket records `pay_model_applied` as a copy of the vehicle's facility `pay_model` at the moment of approval, and `payable_cents` is `payable_flat_cents` when that copy is `FLAT` and `payable_pooled_cents` when it is `POOLED`. Only `payable_cents` feeds a technician's `accrued_pay_cents`.

The rework loop closes back onto the floor. A vehicle in `QC_FAILED` whose `rework_count` is at most 2 may be started again by the estimator, moving `QC_FAILED` → `IN_REPAIR` with the same authorization and posture gates as the first start; tickets already `SUBMITTED` on that vehicle deliberately stay `SUBMITTED`, since labour already claimed is not discarded by a rework. That is the `QC_PENDING` → `QC_FAILED` → `IN_REPAIR` → `QC_PENDING` cycle. But the loop is bounded, and the boundary is a mirror image: once `rework_count` is greater than 2, start-repair is refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and the only way forward is a **re-triage** from `QC_FAILED` — which, symmetrically, is itself refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` whenever `rework_count` is 2 or less. Re-triage declares the paintless path exhausted: it forces `pdr_feasible` to false and re-runs the triage formula with the conventional variant forced as the candidate, rewriting `triage_disposition` and `approved_repair_cents` and resetting `rework_count` to 0 so the vehicle gets a fresh rework budget. Every work ticket of the vehicle whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` is moved to `VOIDED`, which is terminal, with `payable_cents` left at 0 and a `version` increment on each — the labour claimed against a repair philosophy that failed is written off. The money chain is recomputed, and when the vehicle's `billing_status` is `AUTHORIZED`, `BILLED` or `PAID`, the facility's `billable_accrued_cents` and the carrier's `outstanding_direct_bill_cents` absorb the resulting change in `net_billable_cents`. The vehicle lands in `TRIAGED` — or in `TOTAL_LOSS_PENDING` when the forced re-run yields `TOTAL_LOSS` — with a `version` increment.

### 3.8. Supplement Negotiation

Hidden damage surfaces once panels come apart, and the shop claims the difference through a supplement. A `ShopEstimator` files one against a vehicle, naming the `vehicle_id`, a `reason_code` of `HIDDEN_DAMAGE`, `ADDITIONAL_PANEL`, `R_AND_I_ADDITION` or `PARTS_PRICE_CHANGE`, the `requested_amount_cents` claimed (at least 1), the mandatory `expected_vehicle_version`, and optionally a `supersedes_supplement_id` pointing at the earlier filing this one replaces. Filing requires the vehicle's `repair_status` to be one of `TRIAGED`, `IN_REPAIR`, `QC_PENDING`, `QC_FAILED` or `READY` — a car still being estimated or already gone has no supplement surface, and other states are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VEHICLE_REPAIR_STATE`. It requires the vehicle to have a pinned agreement to price against, absent which the rejection is `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATE_AGREEMENT`. And when `supersedes_supplement_id` is supplied, its target must be a supplement whose `status` is `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN`; pointing at a live or already-approved filing is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLEMENT_STATE`. A new supplement copies the vehicle's `pinned_rate_agreement_id` into its own `rate_agreement_id`, starts at `round_index` 1, and increments the vehicle's `version` by one.

Two fiscal gates are evaluated at filing, and the **cap is checked before auto-approval**. The cumulative cap is 40 percent of the vehicle's `approved_repair_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The vehicle's already-approved supplement total — its `approved_supplement_cents`, which counts only supplements whose `status` is `AUTO_APPROVED` or `APPROVED` — plus the amount under test must not exceed that cap, and the comparison is strict, so an amount that lands the running total exactly on the cap passes while one cent more is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_CUMULATIVE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Only once the cap holds is auto-approval considered. The auto-approval threshold is the larger of 15,000 cents (150 dollars) and the pinned sheet's `supplement_auto_approve_pct` multiplied by the vehicle's `approved_repair_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — the floor exists so that trivial supplements clear even on cheap repairs. When the amount under test is less than or equal to that threshold, and only when `round_index` is 1 so that the evaluation happens at creation and nowhere else, the supplement is born `AUTO_APPROVED`, a terminal state: `approved_amount_cents` is set to `requested_amount_cents` and `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Otherwise the supplement is born `UNDER_REVIEW` with `approved_amount_cents` at 0 and `counter_amount_cents` null.

An auto-approval cascades three hops. Hop one: the vehicle's `approved_supplement_cents` grows by the approved amount and the whole net-billable chain is recomputed. Hop two, caused by hop one: when the vehicle's `billing_status` is `AUTHORIZED`, `BILLED` or `PAID`, the facility's `billable_accrued_cents` absorbs the change in `net_billable_cents`. Hop three, caused by hop two: the vehicle's carrier recomputes `outstanding_direct_bill_cents` and `settled_cents` from the same change.

Review belongs to the carrier, and the party that files a supplement can never be the party that reviews it. An `InsurerAdjuster` whose `X-User-ID` equals the vehicle's `insurer_id` records a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `COUNTER` or `DENY` on a supplement that is `UNDER_REVIEW`; any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLEMENT_STATE`, and an adjuster from the wrong carrier is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. `APPROVE` moves `UNDER_REVIEW` → `APPROVED`, terminal, sets `approved_amount_cents` to `requested_amount_cents`, stamps `decided_at`, increments the supplement's `version`, and runs exactly the same three-hop cascade as an auto-approval: vehicle money chain first, then the facility's `billable_accrued_cents` when the vehicle's `billing_status` is `AUTHORIZED`, `BILLED` or `PAID`, then the carrier's `outstanding_direct_bill_cents`. `DENY` moves `UNDER_REVIEW` → `DENIED`, terminal, forces `approved_amount_cents` to 0, stamps `decided_at` and increments `version`; nothing cascades, because a denied supplement never entered the ledger. `COUNTER` moves `UNDER_REVIEW` → `COUNTERED` and is the one branch with its own inputs and limits: it must supply `counter_amount_cents` strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than the current `requested_amount_cents` — a counter at or above the claim is not a counter — and it requires `round_index` to still be less than 3, else `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_ROUND_LIMIT`. The counter amount is persisted, `decided_at` is stamped, and `version` increments.

The shop answers a counter with a revision or a withdrawal, supplying an `action` of `REVISE` or `WITHDRAW`. A `REVISE` requires the supplement to be `COUNTERED`, requires `round_index` to still be less than 3 — three rounds is the negotiation ceiling, and a fourth is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_ROUND_LIMIT` — and requires `revised_amount_cents` to be supplied inside the bracket the two sides have established: no less than `counter_amount_cents` and no greater than the current `requested_amount_cents`. The revised amount must also still satisfy the cumulative cap described above, evaluated with the revised amount as the amount under test, else `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_CUMULATIVE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A successful revision overwrites `requested_amount_cents` with `revised_amount_cents`, increases `round_index` by one, and returns the supplement `COUNTERED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` with a `version` increment — closing the `UNDER_REVIEW` → `COUNTERED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` negotiation cycle. Auto-approval is deliberately **not** re-evaluated on these later rounds: only a round-one filing can auto-approve, however small the revised figure becomes. A `WITHDRAW` is available from either `UNDER_REVIEW` or `COUNTERED`, moves the supplement to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, forces `approved_amount_cents` to 0 and increments `version`.

### 3.9. Carrier Billing, Release and Warranty, Demobilization and Closeout

The billing dimension runs in parallel with the repair dimension and is driven by a single `action` value of `AUTHORIZE`, `VOID_AUTHORIZATION`, `SUBMIT_BILL` or `SETTLE`. The action also decides who may ask: `AUTHORIZE`, `VOID_AUTHORIZATION` and `SETTLE` are the carrier's to make and require an `InsurerAdjuster`, while `SUBMIT_BILL` is the shop's and requires a `BillingCoordinator`. Asking from the wrong side of the table — a coordinator authorizing, or an adjuster submitting a bill — is rejected with `ERR_BILLING_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH`, and an adjuster acting on a vehicle whose `insurer_id` is not their own is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`.

`AUTHORIZE` moves `UNBILLED` → `AUTHORIZED`. It requires the vehicle's `billing_status` to be `UNBILLED`, its `repair_status` to be one of `TRIAGED`, `IN_REPAIR`, `QC_PENDING`, `QC_FAILED`, `READY` or `TOTAL_LOSS_PENDING` — a car still in intake or estimating has no settled number to authorize — and requires the carrier's `direct_bill_enabled` flag to be true, else `ERR_DIRECT_BILL_NOT_ENABLED`. Authorization adds the vehicle's `net_billable_cents` to the facility's `billable_accrued_cents` and to the carrier's `outstanding_direct_bill_cents`, and increments the vehicle's `version`. `VOID_AUTHORIZATION` is its exact compensating action, moving `AUTHORIZED` → `UNBILLED`: it requires the vehicle's `billing_status` to be `AUTHORIZED` and its `repair_status` to be anything other than `RELEASED` or `TOWED_OUT`, and it subtracts the same `net_billable_cents` from the facility's `billable_accrued_cents` and the carrier's `outstanding_direct_bill_cents` before incrementing `version`. `SUBMIT_BILL` moves `AUTHORIZED` → `BILLED` and requires the vehicle's `repair_status` to already be `RELEASED` or `TOWED_OUT` — the shop bills finished work, not work in progress — with a `version` increment and no ledger movement. `SETTLE` moves `BILLED` → `PAID`, requiring only that the vehicle be `BILLED`; it subtracts `net_billable_cents` from the carrier's `outstanding_direct_bill_cents` and adds the same amount to its `settled_cents`, while the facility's `billable_accrued_cents` is left untouched because `PAID` is still inside the set of statuses that figure contributes from. Any billing action attempted from a `billing_status` that does not allow it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VEHICLE_BILLING_STATE`, and one attempted against a disallowed `repair_status` with `ERR_INVALID_VEHICLE_REPAIR_STATE`.

Release is the `IntakeAdvisor`'s closing act on a car, and it has two shapes. A repaired car moves `READY` → `RELEASED`; a totalled car moves `TOTAL_LOSS_PENDING` → `TOWED_OUT`. Both shapes require `billing_status` to be `AUTHORIZED`, both are terminal, both stamp `released_at` from the logical clock, and both increment the vehicle's `version`. They differ on warranty. A released repair mints a warranty: the facility's `warranty_counter` is incremented first, then the vehicle's `warranty_number` is minted as the literal prefix `PDR-` followed by that counter value zero-padded to four digits, and `warranty_expires_at` is set to `released_at` plus 90 days. A towed-out total loss mints nothing — its `warranty_number` stays null and the facility's `warranty_counter` is deliberately **not** incremented, so warranty numbers stay dense across the site's real repairs.

Both shapes also close the rental. When `rental_required` is true, release stamps `rental_closed_at` from the logical clock and computes the rental exposure; when it is false, `rental_days_billed` and `shop_absorbed_rental_cents` are both zero and no rental instant is written. With a rental open, `rental_days_billed` is the whole number of calendar days between the date of `rental_opened_at` and the date of `rental_closed_at`, floored at a minimum of one day so that a same-day turnaround still bills a day; the difference is a calendar-date difference, not an elapsed-hours conversion. Covered days are however many of those days fall within the pinned sheet's `rental_cap_days`, capped at the sheet's figure. Excess days are the billed days minus `rental_cap_days`, floored at zero, and `shop_absorbed_rental_cents` is those excess days multiplied by the sheet's `rental_daily_rate_cents` — the exposure the carrier will not cover and the shop eats. A rental that lands on exactly `rental_cap_days` absorbs nothing. Release finally frees the staging slot: the facility's `staging_slots_occupied`, `staging_slots_free`, `staging_occupancy_ratio` and `absorbed_rental_cents` are all recomputed and its posture is recomputed, which is how releasing one car can lift a full lot from `RESTRICTED` back to `OPEN` and let the next intake through.

Demobilization begins the wind-down. A `CatastropheDirector` moves the event `ACTIVE` → `DEMOBILIZING`, and it is allowed only when every vehicle at every facility of the event has reached a terminal repair state — that is, no vehicle remains in any state other than `RELEASED` or `TOWED_OUT`. A single car still on the lot blocks the whole demobilization with `ERR_EVENT_CLOSEOUT_BLOCKED`. On success the event's `status` becomes `DEMOBILIZING`, `demobilized_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` increments, followed by a three-hop cascade. Hop one: every facility of the event whose `status` is `SETUP`, `COMMISSIONED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `DEMOBILIZED`, terminal, with `posture` forced to `FROZEN` and a `version` increment each. Hop two, caused by hop one: for each facility that just became `DEMOBILIZED`, every crew deployment at that facility whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SITE` is moved to `ENDED`, terminal, with `actual_end_date` written from the current clock date and a `version` increment — the crew goes home whether or not it was formally on site. Hop three, caused by hop two: for each deployment that just ended, its housing unit — where `housing_unit_id` is not null — recomputes `occupied_beds` and `bed_nights_committed` from the newly shortened `effective_end_date`, and its technician recomputes `deployment_state`, which for most of the crew flips `DEPLOYED` back to `AVAILABLE`.

Closeout is the final act. The director moves the event `DEMOBILIZING` → `CLOSED`, terminal, and it requires three things: the event must be `DEMOBILIZING`, every facility of the event must already be `DEMOBILIZED`, and no vehicle at any facility of the event may still have a `billing_status` of `AUTHORIZED` or `BILLED` — every claim must be either settled to `PAID` or never authorized at all. A live facility or an unsettled claim is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_CLOSEOUT_BLOCKED`. On success the event's `version` increments and one last cascade runs: every rate agreement scoped to this event whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, terminal, with a `version` increment, and each affected carrier recomputes its `active_rate_agreement_count`. Vehicles that pinned one of those agreements keep their `pinned_rate_agreement_id` and their frozen rates, so the historical money picture of a closed storm remains reconstructible long after the trailers have left.
