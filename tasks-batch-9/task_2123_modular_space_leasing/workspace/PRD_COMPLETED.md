# Modular Space Leasing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A modular space lessor does not simply rent boxes. It runs a fleet of transportable buildings that spend their lives cycling between a yard, a customer's job site, and a refurbishment shop — and every leg of that cycle is governed by a different authority. A local permitting office decides what the building must be anchored to and how many days ahead the paperwork must be filed. A branch yard decides how many crews it can put on the road on a given calendar day and how many units it can physically park. The commercial desk decides what the customer pays, based on term length, wind exposure, floorplan content and credit standing. When the building comes home, an inspector decides what the customer owes for damage, and a fleet administrator decides whether the unit is worth refurbishing. Today those decisions live in spreadsheets, e-mail threads and the memory of long-tenured branch managers, and the cost of that fragmentation shows up as double-booked units, buildings delivered without an approved permit, ramps that fail an ADA inspection on arrival, and damage chargebacks that cannot be defended.

This document specifies the API that consolidates those decisions into a single system of record. The API governs the full commercial and physical lifecycle of a modular building: catalog definition (building models, floorplan configurations, branch yards and permitting jurisdictions), commercial quoting and reservation, accessory and permit preparation, logistics execution (delivery and installation, mid-term relocation, teardown and return), post-return damage assessment and dispute, final lease settlement, and refurbishment back into the leasable fleet. Every monetary figure the system publishes is derived, never typed in by an operator, and every derivation is reproducible from the inputs recorded on the resources involved.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, a lease carries **two independent status axes** — a physical occupancy axis and a financial billing axis — because in this business a building can be paid for before it ships and can be standing on a site long after the money has been settled; collapsing the two into one status is the single most common modelling mistake in this domain. Second, the system is **deterministic in time**: all date arithmetic reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that lead-time rules, occupancy calendars and settlement windows behave identically on every run.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that apply across the entire API surface. Everything here is contractual: response shape, header handling, precision, ordering of checks, and the concurrency guard.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names the system recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check runs before anything else, including the existence of any resource named in the path.

The five roles are `FleetAdmin`, `BranchOps`, `LeasingAgent`, `DamageAssessor`, and `Customer`. A `FleetAdmin` is the corporate fleet operator: it owns the catalog (branches, jurisdictions, building specs, floorplan configurations and units), renders permit decisions, finalizes damage, authorizes or declines refurbishment, and drives the logical clock. A `BranchOps` account is a yard operations login belonging to exactly one branch; it books and runs logistics jobs and quotes and completes refurbishment work. A `LeasingAgent` staffs the commercial desk: it registers lessees, quotes and reserves leases, attaches accessory packages, files permit applications, and activates, voids or extends leases. A `DamageAssessor` is the independent teardown inspector who opens assessments, appends line items and submits them, and who is never permitted to authorize spend. A `Customer` is the lessee's own self-service login, able to read its own records and to push back on a submitted damage assessment. When a caller's role is not listed among the roles authorized for the operation being invoked, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403.

Roles differ in how `X-User-ID` is interpreted, and the difference matters for ownership enforcement:

- When `X-User-Role` is `BranchOps`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Branch`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Branch` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — specifically `origin_branch_id` on a logistics job and `branch_id` on a refurbishment order.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks compare the `customer_id` of the lease standing behind the addressed resource to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FleetAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it is stored as `approved_by_user_id` on a refurbishment order the administrator authorizes, and it is compared against a damage assessment's `assessor_user_id` for segregation of duties — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `DamageAssessor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `assessor_user_id` on the assessment the inspector opens, but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `LeasingAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server neither persists it on any resource nor compares it to any entity's `id`.

Ownership is enforced on exactly nine operations, and a violation on any of them is answered with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403: booking a logistics job, dispatching a logistics job, completing a logistics job, cancelling a logistics job, quoting a refurbishment order, completing a refurbishment order, disputing a damage assessment, reading a single lease, and reading a single damage assessment. On the seven job- and order-related operations the comparison is the branch comparison described above; on the dispute and on the two single-resource reads the comparison is the customer comparison. Roles whose identity is opaque are never subject to this check.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. A request for a route this API does not define, or for a method that route does not support, is answered in this same envelope with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under the status the routing layer produced — 404 and 405 respectively.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that does not parse as an integer, or that falls outside those bounds, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422 rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is returned sorted by the resource's creation timestamp in ascending order — oldest first. This ordering is also the order in which the system walks records when a calculation is defined as processing a set "in creation order".

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` reflects the size of the filtered set before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when the offset plus the number of records actually returned is still less than `total_count`. Every list filter named in §3 is an exact string match against the field it names, and a filter supplied with an empty value is ignored rather than read as a match on emptiness.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Mutation Bookkeeping

Every top-level resource in this system — branches, jurisdictions, building specs, floorplan configurations, units, customers, leases, accessory packages, permit applications, logistics jobs, damage assessments and refurbishment orders — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits, recorded when the resource was first persisted.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as a downstream consequence of an operation on some other resource.
- **`version`** (integer): a counter that stands at 1 when the resource is first persisted and increments by one on every mutation of the resource thereafter. It is the value the concurrency guard in §2.8 compares against.

Damage line items are the one exception. A `DamageLineItem` is an append-only audit record: it is never edited after it is written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither appears in its representation.

The bookkeeping rule that follows from this is easy to state and easy to get wrong: a resource's `version` and `updated_at` move whenever the server writes to it, and the server writes to more than one resource in most operations described in §3. When completing a logistics job moves a lease's occupancy status and, because of that, moves the unit's status and rewrites the unit's custody branch, all three resources are mutated and all three carry the bookkeeping. Recomputed derived values — a branch's live counters, a customer's exposure figures, a unit's cumulative counters — are mutations of the resource that holds them, with the same consequence. Where §3 specifies the exact chain of consequences for an operation, that chain is normative and the whole chain must be applied.

### 2.5. Money, Distance and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary field in this system is an integer number of **cents**, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is such an integer. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value or a response body. Where this document expresses a constant in dollars for readability, the stored figure is that amount multiplied by one hundred.

Wherever a calculation in §3 says a figure is *rounded*, the rounding is to a whole cent using the **round-half-up** convention: a residue of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, upward. Where a calculation rounds each of several lines and then adds the rounded lines, that evaluation order is binding — summing the unrounded lines and rounding once at the end can differ by a cent and is incorrect.

Three other numeric conventions apply throughout. *Rounding up to a whole number* (used for segment counts, whole crew hours and month counts) always yields the smallest whole number that is not less than the value, so a value that is already whole is left alone. *Rounding down to a whole number* (used for included HVAC tonnage) always yields the largest whole number that is not greater than the value. And a branch's `utilization_pct` is the one non-integer stored figure in the system: it is carried at exactly two decimal places, rounded half up. Linear dimensions in feet and inches, and the floor area derived from them, are exact and are never rounded; comparisons against them are exact comparisons. Request bodies keep the two numeric kinds apart: every `_cents` figure and every counting field — `sections`, `max_occupancy`, `wind_zone`, `permit_lead_days`, `private_offices`, `restrooms`, `landing_count`, `step_count`, `term_months`, `expected_unit_version` and `expected_lease_version` — must arrive as a JSON integer and a fractional value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while the measured quantities — `width_ft`, `length_ft`, `floor_height_in`, `min_ramp_run_ratio`, `partition_linear_ft`, `hvac_tons`, `ramp_run_ft`, `mileage_miles`, `crew_hours`, `labor_hours` and a line item's `quantity` — accept a fractional value.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

All date-sensitive behaviour in this system reads a server-held logical clock rather than the host's wall-clock time. At server start-up the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until it is explicitly moved it continues to read that instant — it must never fall back to real time. A `FleetAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response's `data` carries the single field `current_utc`, which repeats the supplied string verbatim whenever that string contains a `T` and otherwise renders the parsed instant as `yyyy-mm-ddThh:mm:ssZ`.

Everything derived from "today" reads this clock: the `submitted_on` stamp on a permit application, the `decided_on` stamp on a permit decision, the `actual_vacated_date` stamp on a lease whose building has come home, the permit lead-time arithmetic, and the contiguity check on a lease extension. Two date values that both come from the clock are compared as calendar dates in UTC.

### 2.7. Reference Resolution and Nested Retrieval

Identifiers named in a URL path are resolved strictly from left to right, and the first one that does not resolve decides the response. The only nested route in the API is the damage line-item log hanging under an assessment, so in practice this means that a request against the line items of an assessment that does not exist is answered with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404 before anything about the line items is considered.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after path identifiers, and they are resolved **in the order the fields are declared for that operation** — the first unresolvable reference decides the response. Each entity type has its own dedicated 404: `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUILDING_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCESSORY_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOGISTICS_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_REFURB_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. Existence is a question about the reference itself; whether the referenced row is in a usable state is a separate, later question answered by a different code.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Re-Quotes and Re-Assessments

Two creation operations in this system are legitimately repeatable — quoting refurbishment for a returned unit, and opening a damage assessment for a vacated lease — because a second create deliberately retires the earlier record rather than colliding with it. Precisely because a repeat is sanctioned, the system needs a way to tell a deliberate re-quote from a client acting on a record it has not re-read. Optimistic concurrency control on the parent's `version` provides it.

A `POST /refurbishment-orders` request must carry `expected_unit_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Unit`. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, so a client cannot retire a quote whose surrounding unit state it has not observed.

A `POST /damage-assessments` request must carry `expected_lease_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Lease`. If that value does not equal the lease's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, so an inspector cannot supersede an assessment on a lease whose settlement state has moved on since it was read.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Requests to the remaining endpoints neither require nor accept `expected_unit_version` or `expected_lease_version`.

### 2.9. The Screening Ladder

Requests are screened by a fixed ladder of checks evaluated in strictly ascending rank. The **first** check that fails decides the response, its error code is returned, and no lower-ranked check is consulted — this is what makes error responses predictable when a request is wrong in more than one way at once. The full ladder is:

1. **Authentication.** Both context headers present and the role recognised — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The caller's role appears among the roles the endpoint declares — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path resources exist**, resolved left to right — otherwise the matching 404 from §2.7. Applies to every endpoint with an identifier in its path.
4. **Body foreign keys resolve**, in the declared field order — otherwise the matching 404. Applies to the eight creation operations that accept a foreign key: `POST /floorplan-configurations`, `POST /units`, `POST /leases`, `POST /accessory-packages`, `POST /permit-applications`, `POST /logistics-jobs`, `POST /damage-assessments`, and `POST /refurbishment-orders`.
5. **Body shape.** Required fields present and, where a required field is a string, not blank; enum members legal, numeric fields inside their declared ranges, dates parsing as `yyyy-mm-dd`, and the two conditional logistics fields supplied exactly when they are due — `destination_branch_id` present for a teardown-and-return job and absent otherwise, `new_site_address` present for a relocation job and absent otherwise. Failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 422. Applies to every endpoint that accepts a body. A body that is absent or unparseable is read as an empty object, so an operation all of whose body fields are optional succeeds without one while an operation carrying any required field fails at this rank.
6. **Resource ownership**, as defined in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Applies to the nine operations enumerated there.
7. **Source state.** The addressed resource is in the state the requested transition departs from — otherwise the applicable conflict code: `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, `ERR_LEASE_ALREADY_ACTIVATED`, `ERR_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVATED`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_DISPATCHABLE`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_SUBMITTED`, or `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FINALIZED`. Applies to every state-changing endpoint. When a new lease or an extension names a unit, the only unit status that fails at this rank is `REFURBISHING`, which yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`; units in `IN_FLEET`, `RESERVED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `ON_LEASE` or `RETURNED` pass this rank and are judged instead by the occupancy calendar at rank 20.
8. **Stale parent version.** The supplied expected version equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` with HTTP 412. Applies to `POST /refurbishment-orders` and `POST /damage-assessments`.
9. **ADA package compliance** on booking a delivery-and-install job in a jurisdiction that requires a ramp — otherwise `ERR_ADA_PACKAGE_NON_COMPLIANT` with HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /logistics-jobs`.
10. **Permit approval** on dispatching a delivery-and-install job in a jurisdiction that requires a permit — otherwise `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /logistics-jobs/{id}/dispatch`.
11. **Anchoring agreement** between a permit application's `foundation_type` and its jurisdiction's `anchoring_standard` — otherwise `ERR_ANCHORING_STANDARD_MISMATCH` with HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /permit-applications`.
12. **Segregation of duties** between the administrator approving refurbishment and the inspector who authored the assessment — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403. Applies to `POST /refurbishment-orders/{id}/approve`.
13. **Cross-entity link agreement** — otherwise `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /leases`, `POST /permit-applications`, `POST /damage-assessments`, and `POST /refurbishment-orders`.
14. **Configuration published** — otherwise `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_PUBLISHED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /leases` and `POST /leases/{id}/activate`.
15. **Servicing branch operational** — otherwise `ERR_BRANCH_SUSPENDED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /logistics-jobs` and `POST /refurbishment-orders`.
16. **Identifier uniqueness** — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /building-specs`, `POST /jurisdictions`, and `POST /units`.
17. **One accessory package per lease** — otherwise `ERR_ACCESSORY_ALREADY_ATTACHED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /accessory-packages`.
18. **One live successor per lease** — otherwise `ERR_LEASE_ALREADY_EXTENDED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /leases/{id}/extensions`.
19. **Dispute budget remaining** — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_LIMIT_REACHED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /damage-assessments/{id}/dispute`.
20. **Unit occupancy calendar** — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /leases` and `POST /leases/{id}/extensions`.
21. **Branch dispatch capacity for the scheduled day** — otherwise `ERR_BRANCH_DISPATCH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /logistics-jobs`.
22. **Permit lead time** — otherwise `ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION` with HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /permit-applications/{id}/submit`.
23. **Extension contiguity against the clock** — otherwise `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_CONTIGUOUS` with HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /leases/{id}/extensions`.
24. **Receiving yard has room** — otherwise `ERR_YARD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /logistics-jobs/{id}/complete`.
25. **Customer not on credit hold** — otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_ON_CREDIT_HOLD` with HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /leases` and `POST /leases/{id}/extensions`.
26. **Credit headroom at activation** — otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 402. Applies to `POST /leases/{id}/activate`.

The substance of ranks 9 through 26 — what each check actually compares — is developed in the workflow narratives of §3, alongside the business situation that motivates it.

### 2.10. Yard Custody and Fleet Counters

Custody of a physical unit is expressed by `current_branch_id`, and it changes exactly once in the lifecycle: when a teardown-and-return job completes, the unit's custody moves to the receiving yard. Until that moment a unit that is standing on a customer's site still belongs, on paper, to the branch that sent it out.

Each branch publishes three derived counters that must be recomputed from scratch — never incrementally patched — after every write that changes a unit's status or its custody branch. `units_in_yard` is the number of units whose `current_branch_id` is this branch and whose status is one of `IN_FLEET`, `RESERVED`, `RETURNED`, or `REFURBISHING`; those four statuses are collectively the "on the yard" statuses, while `IN_TRANSIT` and `ON_LEASE` are off-yard. `units_available` is the number of units whose `current_branch_id` is this branch and whose status is exactly `IN_FLEET`. And `utilization_pct` measures the share of the branch's assigned fleet that is not immediately leasable: take the total number of units whose `current_branch_id` is this branch, regardless of status; subtract `units_available` from that total; divide the difference by the total; multiply by one hundred; and round the result half up to two decimal places. When the branch has no units assigned at all, `utilization_pct` is 0.00 and no division is attempted. Note that a unit away on lease keeps its custody branch and therefore still counts in the total that drives utilization, while contributing to neither `units_in_yard` nor `units_available`.

The eight unit-status writes that force this recomputation are: reserving an available unit for a new lease, releasing a reserved unit when its lease is voided, sending a reserved unit into transit on dispatch, pulling an in-transit unit back to reserved when a dispatched delivery is aborted, receiving an in-transit unit into the destination yard on teardown completion, moving a returned unit into the shop on refurbishment approval, returning a returned unit to the fleet on refurbishment rejection, and returning a refurbished unit to the fleet on refurbishment completion. Some of these leave one or more counters numerically unchanged — a move from `RETURNED` to `REFURBISHING`, for instance, changes none of the three, because both statuses are on-yard and neither is available — but the recomputation is still performed.

### 2.11. Customer Exposure and Credit Discipline

A customer's `outstanding_balance_cents` is not a stored running total that gets nudged; it is recomputed as the sum, over every lease belonging to that customer, of that lease's `billed_to_date_cents` minus its `credited_cents`, with the leases processed in ascending order of their creation timestamp. No lease is excluded from the sum on the basis of its status: a voided lease contributes exactly zero because its credited amount cancels its billed amount, and that cancellation is the mechanism, not an exclusion rule.

From that balance the system derives `credit_hold`, which is true when `outstanding_balance_cents` is strictly greater than `credit_limit_cents`. Equality is deliberately not a hold — a customer sitting exactly on its limit is still in good standing. Both figures are recomputed together on each of the four billing-axis events: activating a lease, settling a lease, voiding an unbilled lease, and voiding an accruing lease.

One consequence of the hold deserves its own sentence, because it is permanent in a way the balance is not: when a recomputation flips `credit_hold` from false to true as part of lease settlement, the customer's `credit_tier` is demoted to `WATCH`. If the flag was already true, or does not become true, `credit_tier` is left alone. A demotion never re-rates leases that already exist, because each lease priced itself against a snapshot of the tier taken when it was created.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Network: Yards, Authorities and Catalog Models

Before a single building can be quoted, a `FleetAdmin` has to describe the physical and regulatory world the fleet operates in. That begins with **branches** — the depots and yards that hold steel and dispatch crews. A branch is registered with a display `name` and a `region_code` such as "TX-N", together with three operating capacities: `yard_capacity_units`, the hard ceiling on how many units may sit in the yard at one time; `daily_dispatch_capacity`, the number of logistics jobs the branch may hold against any one scheduled calendar day; and `labor_rate_cents_per_hour`, the crew labour rate that later drives both haulage pricing and refurbishment estimates. The first two must each be at least 1, and the labour rate may not be negative. A branch also carries an operational posture in its `status`, which is either `OPERATIONAL` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `OPERATIONAL` when the caller omits it. A newly registered branch begins with `units_in_yard` and `units_available` at 0 and `utilization_pct` at 0.00, and those three counters thereafter behave exactly as §2.10 describes.

Permitting authorities are modelled as **jurisdictions**. Each one is registered with a `code` such as "TX-DAL" that must be unique across all jurisdictions, a human-readable `name`, and the seven rules by which it governs a site. `permit_required` says whether a building may be delivered into this jurisdiction only after an approved permit; it and `ada_ramp_required` below are both required booleans carrying no default. `permit_lead_days` is the minimum number of whole days that must remain between the day an application is filed and the day the lease is due to start; zero is permitted. `anchoring_standard` declares the foundation class the authority mandates and is one of `NONE`, `GROUND_ANCHOR`, `PIER_FOUNDATION`, or `ENGINEERED_SLAB`. `wind_zone` is the wind exposure band, an integer that is 1, 2, 3, or 4, and it feeds directly into rent. `permit_fee_cents` is the authority's flat filing fee before any area-based component, and may not be negative. `ada_ramp_required` says whether the authority insists on a compliant accessible ramp before a building may be delivered. And `min_ramp_run_ratio` is the run-to-rise ratio the authority demands — a value of 12.0 means twelve feet of horizontal ramp run for every one foot of vertical rise — and it must be greater than zero.

Catalog models are **building specs**. A spec is registered with a `model_code` unique across the catalog, its overall `width_ft` and `length_ft` in feet (both strictly positive), and the number of transportable `sections` that make it up, which is between 1 and 4 inclusive — a single-wide is one section, and the section count is a transport attribute that drives the oversize haulage component and nothing else. `floor_height_in` is the finished-floor height above grade in inches, which may be zero or greater and which is the rise an accessible ramp must overcome. Four money fields complete the commercial picture: `base_monthly_rate_cents`, the list monthly rent before any term, wind or credit adjustment; `delivery_base_cents` and `install_base_cents`, the two list charges that seed the mobilization subtotal; and `replacement_value_cents`, the insured replacement value that later caps both the normal-wear allowance and the damage chargeback. None of the four may be negative. `max_occupancy`, the rated occupant count, must be at least 1 and is carried for information only — no calculation reads it. On registration the system derives `floor_area_sqft` as the width in feet multiplied by the length in feet. That product is exact and is not rounded, and the section count never multiplies it.

Three identifiers in this catalog are enforced as unique, and an attempt to reuse any of them is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`: a building spec's `model_code`, a jurisdiction's `code`, and a unit's `serial_number`. A branch's `name` and `region_code` are deliberately not unique — two yards in the same region may legitimately share a name.

### 3.2. Floorplans: Configuring and Publishing

A building spec describes a shell; a **floorplan configuration** describes how that shell is cut up inside, and it is where most of the per-lease price variation originates. A `FleetAdmin` drafts a configuration against a spec by supplying the parent's identifier as `building_spec_id`, a display `name`, and four content figures: `private_offices`, the count of framed private offices; `restrooms`, the count of plumbed restrooms; `partition_linear_ft`, the linear feet of interior partition wall; and `hvac_tons`, the installed HVAC tonnage. None of the four may be negative.

From those four inputs plus the parent spec's floor area the system derives `config_surcharge_cents`, the one-off capital cost of the interior build-out, as the sum of four components. The office component charges $420.00 for each private office. The restroom component charges $1,750.00 for each restroom. The partition component charges $95.00 for each four-foot segment of partition wall, where the segment count is the partition length in feet divided by four and then rounded up to a whole number — a 13-foot partition is therefore charged as four segments. The HVAC component is the interesting one, because a certain amount of tonnage comes free with the building: the included tonnage is half a ton for every complete 400 square feet of the spec's floor area, computed by dividing the floor area by 400, rounding that quotient **down** to a whole number, and halving the result. Any tonnage above the included allowance is excess — and if the installed tonnage is at or below the allowance the excess is zero, never negative. The excess is then charged at $680.00 for each half ton, where the number of chargeable half-tons is the excess tonnage divided by one half and rounded up to a whole number. Every intermediate here is an exact integer number of cents; nothing in this derivation reads lease, jurisdiction or customer data.

A configuration is born in `DRAFT`, recorded in its `status`, and only a `PUBLISHED` configuration may be named by a new lease. Publishing is a `FleetAdmin` act that moves the configuration `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`; attempting it on a configuration that is already `PUBLISHED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Configurations may be listed and narrowed by their parent spec through a `building_spec_id` filter and by publication state through a `status` filter.

### 3.3. Fleet Units and the Custody Loop

A **unit** is one physical building in the fleet. A `FleetAdmin` adds one by supplying its `serial_number`, the `building_spec_id` of the model it is, and the `current_branch_id` of the yard that takes custody of it; an optional `condition_grade` may be supplied, and when it is omitted the unit is graded `A`. The grade is one of `A`, `B`, `C`, or `D`. A newly added unit enters the fleet in status `IN_FLEET`, and the receiving branch's three counters are recomputed immediately.

Four cumulative counters ride along with each unit for its whole service life. `lifetime_relocations` counts the relocation jobs completed against it. `refurb_cycles` counts the refurbishment orders that have reached completion on it. `accumulated_damage_cents` is the running sum of the chargeable damage absorbed by those completed refurbishments. And `latest_quote_estimate_cents` holds the estimated cost of the newest open refurbishment quote on the unit, sitting at 0 whenever no quote is open. All four start at zero. A fifth system-managed field, `installed_config_id`, records which floorplan configuration is physically installed in the unit; it is null until a delivery-and-install job completes, at which point it is written with that lease's configuration — and it is never cleared afterwards, so a unit back in the yard still reports the last floorplan it wore.

The unit's `status` traces a closed loop rather than a one-way lifecycle: `IN_FLEET → RESERVED → IN_TRANSIT → ON_LEASE → RETURNED → REFURBISHING → IN_FLEET`. Reserving happens when a lease is quoted against an available unit. Dispatching a delivery-and-install crew takes the unit `RESERVED → IN_TRANSIT`, and aborting that dispatch before completion puts it back `IN_TRANSIT → RESERVED`. Completing the delivery takes it `IN_TRANSIT → ON_LEASE`. A relocation completed mid-term is the self-loop `ON_LEASE → ON_LEASE`, which leaves the unit where it was. Dispatching the teardown crew takes it `ON_LEASE → IN_TRANSIT`, and completing that teardown lands it `IN_TRANSIT → RETURNED` in the receiving yard. From `RETURNED` the loop forks: authorizing refurbishment takes the unit `RETURNED → REFURBISHING` and completing the work closes the loop at `REFURBISHING → IN_FLEET`, while declining the refurbishment takes the unit straight `RETURNED → IN_FLEET` with no grade change, no increment to `refurb_cycles`, and no damage absorbed. Voiding an un-shipped lease returns a reserved unit `RESERVED → IN_FLEET`. There is no terminal status; a unit that survives long enough visits `IN_FLEET` many times. Every one of those moves is guarded by its departing status: where an operation below is specified as taking a unit from one status to another, the status is rewritten only when the unit actually stands in the departing status, and a unit standing anywhere else keeps the status it has while every other consequence of that operation — the write itself, the `version` increment, any custody rewrite and any counter recomputation — still lands.

Fleet units may be listed across the whole network and narrowed by `current_branch_id`, by `building_spec_id`, by `installed_config_id`, and by `status`, which makes it possible to ask, for example, for every unit in a given yard currently sitting in `REFURBISHING`.

### 3.4. Lessees, Quotes and Unit Reservation

A lessee is registered by a `LeasingAgent` as a **customer**, carrying a legal `name`, a `billing_email` for invoicing, a `credit_tier` that is one of `PLATINUM`, `GOLD`, `STANDARD`, or `WATCH`, and a `credit_limit_cents` ceiling on tolerated exposure that may not be negative. A new customer starts with `outstanding_balance_cents` at 0 and `credit_hold` at false; from then on both are derived rather than set, exactly as §2.11 describes.

Quoting a lease is the commercial heart of the system, and it is a single act that both prices the deal and takes the unit off the market. A `LeasingAgent` supplies the `customer_id` of the lessee, the `unit_id` of the physical building, the `jurisdiction_id` of the permitting authority governing the site, the `floorplan_configuration_id` of the interior build-out, the delivery `site_address`, the `start_date` as the first occupied calendar day in `yyyy-mm-dd` form, and the `term_months` of the contracted term, which must be at least 1. Months in this business are uniform thirty-day months, so the system derives `end_date` by advancing thirty days per contracted month from the start date and then stepping back one day — the lease occupies a closed interval whose last day is the derived end date. A twelve-month lease starting on a given day therefore occupies three hundred and sixty consecutive days ending on the three hundred and fifty-ninth day after it starts.

Three preconditions guard the deal before any money is computed. The configuration named on the lease must be `PUBLISHED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The configuration must have been cut against the same model the unit actually is — the configuration's `building_spec_id` and the unit's `building_spec_id` must agree — or the request is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH`. And the unit must not be in the shop: a unit whose status is `REFURBISHING` has no committed return date and is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, while a unit in any other status is bookable forward and is judged instead by the occupancy calendar below.

That calendar is the real gate on dates. Each lease on a unit that has not been cancelled occupies the closed interval running from its `start_date` to its `end_date`, or, once the lease's occupancy axis has reached `VACATED`, the closed interval running from its `start_date` to its `actual_vacated_date`. Between two consecutive tenancies the yard needs a refurbishment window, so each existing interval is treated as if it were widened by fourteen days at each end. A candidate interval is therefore rejected with `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` whenever its first day falls on or before fourteen days after an existing interval's last day **and** its last day falls on or after fourteen days before that existing interval's first day. One exemption exists, and it applies to exactly one pair at a time: when the candidate is an extension successor whose `extends_lease_id` names the very lease it would collide with, and the candidate's first day is the day immediately after that parent lease's last day, the fourteen-day widening is not applied to that pair — a contiguous handoff on the same building needs no refurbishment window.

A lessee already over its limit may not take on new obligations: if the customer's `credit_hold` is true, the quote is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_ON_CREDIT_HOLD`. When the customer passes, the system snapshots the lessee's current `credit_tier` into the lease's own `pinned_credit_tier`. That snapshot is permanent: a later demotion to `WATCH` never re-rates a lease that already exists.

Rent is then derived into `monthly_base_rate_cents` as the sum of two separately rounded lines. The **shell line** starts from the model's `base_monthly_rate_cents` and applies three multipliers in one product. The first is a term factor rewarding commitment: 1.25 when `term_months` is 5 or fewer, 1.10 when it is between 6 and 11 inclusive, 1.00 when it is between 12 and 23 inclusive, and 0.92 when it is 24 or more. The second is a wind-exposure factor read from the jurisdiction's `wind_zone`: 1.00 in zone 1, 1.15 in zone 2, 1.35 in zone 3, and 1.60 in zone 4. The third is a credit factor equal to one minus the discount earned by the lease's `pinned_credit_tier`, where `PLATINUM` earns a discount of 0.06, `GOLD` earns 0.03, and both `STANDARD` and `WATCH` earn nothing at all. The product of the list rate and those three factors is rounded half up to a whole cent, and that is the shell line. The **build-out line** amortizes the configuration's `config_surcharge_cents` over a thirty-six-month horizon: divide the surcharge by 36 and round half up to a whole cent. The monthly base rate is the shell line plus the build-out line, added **after** each has been rounded on its own; summing the two unrounded figures and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ by a cent.

Getting the building to the site is priced separately into `mobilization_fee_cents`, and its evaluation order resolves what looks like a circular dependency. First the system forms a transport subtotal: the model's `delivery_base_cents` plus its `install_base_cents` plus the anchoring allowance dictated by the jurisdiction's `anchoring_standard`, which contributes nothing for `NONE`, $850.00 for `GROUND_ANCHOR`, $2,400.00 for `PIER_FOUNDATION`, and $6,100.00 for `ENGINEERED_SLAB`. Second — and this is the pinned part — the volume discount tier is selected by looking at **that subtotal alone**, never at the lease's grand total, which already contains the fee: no discount when the subtotal is below $3,000.00, five percent when it is at or above $3,000.00 but below $8,000.00, and nine percent when it is at or above $8,000.00. Third, the discount amount is the subtotal multiplied by the selected percentage, rounded half up to a whole cent. Fourth, the mobilization fee is the subtotal minus that discount. Permit fees are emphatically not part of mobilization; they enter the lease's economics only at final settlement.

Finally the system projects `quoted_total_cents`, the forward-looking value of the deal as signed: the mobilization fee plus the sum of the monthly base rate and the monthly accessory rate, multiplied by the contracted `term_months`. At quote time the lease's `monthly_accessory_rate_cents` is still 0, so the projection covers shell and build-out only; attaching an accessory package re-runs this same projection. The projection assumes the full contracted term is served, assumes no permit fee because no application exists yet, and assumes no damage and no relocation.

A newly quoted lease is `PENDING` on its `occupancy_status` axis and `UNBILLED` on its `billing_status` axis, with `extends_lease_id`, `actual_vacated_date` and `final_settlement_cents` all null, and with `monthly_accessory_rate_cents`, `relocation_surcharge_cents`, `billed_to_date_cents`, `credited_cents` and `assessment_round` all at zero. In the same operation, a unit that is currently `IN_FLEET` and whose custody branch is `OPERATIONAL` is taken `IN_FLEET → RESERVED`; a unit already `RESERVED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `ON_LEASE` or `RETURNED` is being booked forward and keeps the status it has, and a unit that is `IN_FLEET` in a `SUSPENDED` yard likewise keeps `IN_FLEET`, the quote itself never being refused on account of the branch's posture. In every one of those cases the unit's `version` is incremented by one, and the custody branch's `units_available` and `utilization_pct` are recomputed — `units_in_yard` does not move, because `RESERVED` is still a yard status.

Reading a single lease returns both status axes, every money field and the `version` that the concurrency guard needs. Leases may be listed and narrowed by `customer_id`, by `unit_id`, by `jurisdiction_id`, by `floorplan_configuration_id`, by the self-referential `extends_lease_id` (which is how the successor chain on a unit is traced), by `occupancy_status`, and by `billing_status`.

### 3.5. Accessory Packages and ADA Ramp Compliance

Buildings sit on blocks, and people have to get into them. A `LeasingAgent` attaches at most one **accessory package** to a lease by naming the `lease_id` and the `package_type`, which is `STEPS` for a step unit alone, `RAMP_ADA` for an accessible ramp, or `COMBO` for both. Three optional figures describe the kit: `ramp_run_ft`, the horizontal run of the ramp in feet, which is 0 for a steps-only package; `landing_count`, the number of intermediate and top landings supplied; and `step_count`, the number of treads in the step unit. All three default to 0 and none may be negative. A second attempt to attach a package to a lease that already carries one is rejected with `ERR_ACCESSORY_ALREADY_ATTACHED`.

The package's `monthly_accessory_rate_cents` is the sum of three parts: a base rent for the kit type — $125.00 for `STEPS`, $475.00 for `RAMP_ADA`, and $550.00 for `COMBO` — plus a ramp component charging $38.00 for each four-foot segment of ramp run, where the segment count is the run in feet divided by four and rounded up to a whole number, plus a landing component charging $62.00 for each landing supplied. The `step_count` is recorded for the yard's benefit and prices nothing. All three parts are exact whole-cent integers.

Compliance is scored at the same moment into `compliance_status`, which is either `COMPLIANT` or `NON_COMPLIANT`. The system first converts the building's finished-floor height to a rise in feet by dividing the model's `floor_height_in` by twelve, then multiplies that rise by the jurisdiction's `min_ramp_run_ratio` to obtain the run the authority requires. It also computes the number of landings the ramp needs: the supplied `ramp_run_ft` divided by thirty and rounded up to a whole number, one landing per started thirty feet of run. The package is `COMPLIANT` only when all three conditions hold together — the `package_type` is `RAMP_ADA` or `COMBO`, the supplied ramp run is greater than or equal to the required run, and the supplied `landing_count` is greater than or equal to the required landing count. Both comparisons are inclusive: a run that exactly equals the requirement passes, and a landing count that exactly equals the requirement passes. If any of the three fails — including any steps-only package, which can never be accessible — the status is `NON_COMPLIANT`.

Attaching a package has three consequences on the lease. The computed accessory rent is mirrored onto the lease's own `monthly_accessory_rate_cents`; the forward projection described in §3.4 is re-run so that `quoted_total_cents` absorbs the accessory rent across the full contracted term; and the lease's `version` is incremented by one. Packages may be read individually and listed, optionally narrowed to one lease by a `lease_id` filter.

The compliance verdict matters at exactly one later moment, and it is worth flagging here even though it belongs to §3.8: in a jurisdiction whose `ada_ramp_required` is true, a delivery-and-install job may only be booked for a lease that carries an accessory package whose `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT`. A lease with no package at all, or with a `NON_COMPLIANT` one, is refused at booking with `ERR_ADA_PACKAGE_NON_COMPLIANT`.

### 3.6. Permits, Foundations and Lead Time

Where a jurisdiction demands paperwork, a `LeasingAgent` opens a **permit application** against the lease by naming the `lease_id`, the `jurisdiction_id` of the reviewing authority, and the proposed `foundation_type`, which is drawn from the same four-value set as a jurisdiction's anchoring standard: `NONE`, `GROUND_ANCHOR`, `PIER_FOUNDATION`, or `ENGINEERED_SLAB`. Two agreements are enforced at creation. The authority named on the application must be the authority named on the lease — a mismatch between the application's `jurisdiction_id` and the lease's `jurisdiction_id` is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH` — and the proposed foundation must be exactly the class the authority mandates in its `anchoring_standard`, with any deviation refused as `ERR_ANCHORING_STANDARD_MISMATCH`. The authority whose `anchoring_standard` that second comparison reads is the one the application itself names in its `jurisdiction_id`, not the lease's, and the two agreements are screened in the order §2.9 fixes — the anchoring agreement at rank 11, ahead of the link agreement at rank 13 — so an application that names an authority other than the lease's and proposes a foundation that the named authority does not mandate is answered with `ERR_ANCHORING_STANDARD_MISMATCH`.

The filing fee is derived into `permit_fee_cents` as the authority's flat `permit_fee_cents` plus an area component of $15.00 for each hundred-square-foot block of the building's `floor_area_sqft`, where the block count is the floor area divided by one hundred and rounded up to a whole number. A 1,050-square-foot building is therefore charged eleven blocks, not ten and a half.

An application is created in `DRAFT`. Filing it with the authority moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and it may only be filed while the lease's occupancy axis is still `PENDING`, a lease that has left `PENDING` being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The lead-time rule bites here: the number of whole days between the logical clock's calendar date and the lease's `start_date` must be greater than or equal to the jurisdiction's `permit_lead_days`, and an application filed too late for the authority to turn it around is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION`. On a successful filing the system stamps `submitted_on` with the logical clock's calendar date. Only a `DRAFT` application may be filed; filing one that has already left `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and there is no path back into `SUBMITTED` from a decided application.

The authority's answer is recorded by a `FleetAdmin` in a single decision act that carries a `decision` of either `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and an optional `reviewer_note`. Only a `SUBMITTED` application may be decided; an application in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and an omitted note is stored as null. Both outcomes move the application out of `SUBMITTED`, stamp `decided_on` from the logical clock, and store the note. Their downstream consequences diverge sharply. `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` unlocks the delivery dispatch that a permit-requiring jurisdiction would otherwise block, and it makes the application's `permit_fee_cents` a chargeable component of the lease's final settlement. `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` does neither: the fee never enters settlement and dispatch stays blocked. Permit applications may be read individually and listed with `lease_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, and `status` filters.

### 3.7. The Money Axis: Activation, Voiding and Extension

The billing axis moves on its own schedule, and nothing on the occupancy axis pushes it. A lease starts `UNBILLED`; activation by a `LeasingAgent` takes it `UNBILLED → ACCRUING`; final settlement takes it `ACCRUING → SETTLED`; and voiding takes it to `VOIDED` from either `UNBILLED` or `ACCRUING`. `SETTLED` and `VOIDED` are both terminal.

Activation is where the first money is actually charged, and it is guarded four ways. The lease's billing axis must still be `UNBILLED`, and a second attempt on a lease that has already been activated is rejected with `ERR_LEASE_ALREADY_ACTIVATED`. The occupancy axis must still be `PENDING`, and a lease that has left it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; because the billing test is the earlier of the two, a lease whose billing axis has reached `VOIDED` or `SETTLED` is answered with `ERR_LEASE_ALREADY_ACTIVATED` all the same. The floorplan configuration the lease names must still be `PUBLISHED`, or activation is refused with `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_PUBLISHED`. And the customer must have the headroom to absorb the first charge: the customer's current `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the sum of the lease's `mobilization_fee_cents`, `monthly_base_rate_cents` and `monthly_accessory_rate_cents` must be less than or equal to the customer's `credit_limit_cents`, with equality allowed. If that sum would exceed the limit, activation is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 402 — the only 402 in the API, because it is the only place where a request fails purely for want of credit. On success the lease's `billed_to_date_cents` is set to that same sum of mobilization fee, monthly base rate and monthly accessory rate; the customer's `outstanding_balance_cents` and `credit_hold` are recomputed as §2.11 prescribes; and the lease's `version` is incremented. The occupancy axis is deliberately left exactly where it was — activating a lease does not put a building on the ground.

Voiding is the mirror image and the only way out of a deal that has not shipped. A `LeasingAgent` voids a lease, optionally recording a `void_reason`, and the operation moves three things at once. The occupancy axis goes `PENDING → CANCELLED`, which requires that the axis is still `PENDING` and that no logistics job on this lease has reached `DISPATCHED` or `COMPLETED` — once a crew is on the road, the deal is no longer voidable. Either of those two failures is answered with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The billing axis goes `UNBILLED → VOIDED` or `ACCRUING → VOIDED` depending on where it stood, and in both cases the lease's `credited_cents` is set equal to its `billed_to_date_cents`, which is an exact reversal of whatever activation charged; on an un-activated lease both figures are zero and the reversal is a formality. The customer's `outstanding_balance_cents` and `credit_hold` are then recomputed, and because the credit line reads billed minus credited across every lease, the voided lease's net contribution becomes exactly zero. Finally the unit is released `RESERVED → IN_FLEET`, its `version` is incremented, and its custody branch's `units_available` and `utilization_pct` are recomputed — but only when no **other** lease on that same unit still sits at `PENDING` occupancy, because a surviving forward booking keeps the unit reserved. A unit that is not `RESERVED` when the void lands is not written at all, so neither its own bookkeeping nor its branch's counters move. The lease's own `version` is incremented in every case.

Extending a lease creates a **successor** rather than editing the original, which is what makes the rent re-rate honestly and what keeps the occupancy calendar auditable. A `LeasingAgent` supplies only the successor's `term_months`; everything else is derived. The successor names the same customer, unit, jurisdiction, floorplan configuration and site address as its parent, records the parent's identifier in its own `extends_lease_id`, and takes as its `start_date` the day immediately after the parent's `end_date`, from which its own `end_date` follows by the usual thirty-days-per-month rule. The successor's `mobilization_fee_cents` is 0 and the four-step mobilization derivation is skipped entirely — the building is already standing on the site, so there is nothing to mobilize. Its `monthly_base_rate_cents` is recomputed from scratch using the successor's own `term_months` — which is precisely the point of an extension, since a longer commitment earns a better term factor — while the credit factor is taken from the parent's `pinned_credit_tier` rather than from the customer's present standing — the successor's own `pinned_credit_tier` carries that same inherited value. The successor's forward projection is formed the same way as any other lease's, so its `quoted_total_cents` is the sum of its monthly base rate and its `monthly_accessory_rate_cents` multiplied by its own `term_months`, with a mobilization fee of zero contributing nothing. Because the building is already installed, the successor is born with `occupancy_status` at `INSTALLED` and `billing_status` at `UNBILLED`, so it still has to be activated in its own right before anything is charged. Like any other new lease it is born with `actual_vacated_date` and `final_settlement_cents` null and with `monthly_accessory_rate_cents`, `relocation_surcharge_cents`, `billed_to_date_cents`, `credited_cents` and `assessment_round` all at zero.

Four checks guard an extension, and neither the parent's own occupancy axis nor its billing axis is among them. A lease may carry at most one successor whose occupancy axis is not `CANCELLED`; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_LEASE_ALREADY_EXTENDED`. The unit may not be in the shop, or the request fails with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The derived successor start day must not fall before the logical clock's calendar date — a handoff cannot be back-dated onto a term that has already run out — and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`. And the occupancy calendar of §3.4 is applied with its one exemption: the successor is allowed to butt up directly against its own parent without the fourteen-day refurbishment window, but it is still checked in full against every other lease on that unit and fails with `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` if it collides with one. A customer on `credit_hold` may not extend either, and is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_ON_CREDIT_HOLD`. On success the parent lease's `version` is incremented by one and the unit's `version` is incremented by one.

The last billing move, `ACCRUING → SETTLED`, is never requested directly. It is cascaded by the finalization of a damage assessment, and it requires the lease's occupancy axis to have reached `VACATED`, while the billing axis standing at `ACCRUING` gates only the cascade itself — a finalization against a lease whose billing axis is anywhere else still succeeds and simply settles nothing. §3.9 describes it in full.

### 3.8. Logistics: Booking, Dispatch, Delivery, Relocation and Return

Every physical move of a building is a **logistics job**, booked by the `BranchOps` account of the branch supplying the crew. A job is created against a `lease_id` with a `job_type` of `DELIVERY_INSTALL`, `TEARDOWN_RETURN`, or `RELOCATION`; an `origin_branch_id` naming the branch that supplies the crew and holds the dispatch slot; a `scheduled_date` in `yyyy-mm-dd` form for the day the crew is booked; `mileage_miles`, the one-way haul distance, which may not be negative; and `crew_hours`, the booked crew time, which may not be negative either. Two further fields are conditional on the job type and are policed as part of body validation: `destination_branch_id`, the receiving yard, must be supplied for a `TEARDOWN_RETURN` job and must be omitted for any other type; `new_site_address`, the address the building is moving to, must be supplied for a `RELOCATION` job and must be omitted for any other type. The job's `unit_id` is never client-supplied — it is copied from the lease at creation. A new job is born `SCHEDULED`.

Haulage is priced into `transport_cost_cents` from three separately handled components. The **mileage line** is banded on the marginal principle: the first 50 miles are included and cost nothing; the next 100 miles — that is, the portion of the haul lying above 50 miles and up to a ceiling of 150 miles — are charged at $9.50 per mile; and every mile beyond 150 is charged at $14.50 per mile. A haul of exactly 150 miles therefore produces one hundred chargeable band-two miles and zero band-three miles. The two banded amounts are added and the total is rounded half up to a whole cent. The **crew line** takes the booked `crew_hours`, rounds them **up** to a whole number of hours, multiplies by the origin branch's `labor_rate_cents_per_hour`, and applies a difficulty factor that depends on the job type — 1.0 for `DELIVERY_INSTALL`, 0.75 for `TEARDOWN_RETURN`, and 1.5 for `RELOCATION`, since re-siting an occupied building is the most delicate of the three — with the product rounded half up to a whole cent. The **oversize line** charges $780.00 for every transportable section beyond the first, so a single-wide adds nothing and a four-section building adds three times that amount; the section count comes from the model of the unit the job is moving. The transport cost is the sum of the three, added after the mileage and crew lines have each been rounded on their own.

Three checks guard the booking itself. The origin branch must be `OPERATIONAL`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_BRANCH_SUSPENDED`. If the job is a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` into a jurisdiction whose `ada_ramp_required` is true, the lease must carry an accessory package scored `COMPLIANT`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_ADA_PACKAGE_NON_COMPLIANT`. And the branch must have a dispatch slot free on the requested day: the number of jobs whose `origin_branch_id` is that branch, whose `scheduled_date` is exactly the requested calendar date, and whose status is either `SCHEDULED` or `DISPATCHED` must be strictly less than the branch's `daily_dispatch_capacity`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_BRANCH_DISPATCH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The window is the single calendar date and nothing else — no adjacency, no buffer, no neighbouring days. Jobs that have been cancelled or completed do not consume a slot, which is why cancelling a job frees its day up again.

Dispatching sends the crew and moves the job `SCHEDULED → DISPATCHED`, but the preconditions and the consequences differ by type. For a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` job, the lease's billing axis must be `ACCRUING` — an unactivated lease is refused with `ERR_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVATED` — and, where the jurisdiction's `permit_required` is true, there must be a permit application on that lease sitting at `APPROVED`, failing which the dispatch is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`; a jurisdiction that does not require a permit imposes no such condition. On success the unit moves `RESERVED → IN_TRANSIT` under the guard of §3.3, its `version` is incremented, and its custody branch's `units_in_yard`, `units_available` and `utilization_pct` are all recomputed, with the in-yard count dropping by one as the building leaves the fence. For a `TEARDOWN_RETURN` job, the lease's occupancy axis must be `INSTALLED`, failing which the dispatch is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the unit moves `ON_LEASE → IN_TRANSIT`, its `version` is incremented, and no branch counter moves at all, because both statuses are off-yard. For a `RELOCATION` job, the lease's occupancy axis must likewise be `INSTALLED`, and the unit's status deliberately stays `ON_LEASE` — a relocation never re-enters a yard. That `INSTALLED` requirement is enforced with the same `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and on success the unit is not written at all, so its `version` does not move either. Dispatching a job that is not `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_JOB_NOT_DISPATCHABLE`, while a job addressed for completion while it is still `SCHEDULED` — or from any state other than `DISPATCHED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Completing a job moves it `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED` and is where the heaviest cascades in the system fire. Beyond the job's own state, completion also reads the lease: a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` requires the lease's occupancy axis to be `PENDING`, while a `TEARDOWN_RETURN` or a `RELOCATION` requires it to be `INSTALLED`, and a lease standing anywhere else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Completing a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` job takes the unit `IN_TRANSIT → ON_LEASE`, writes the lease's `floorplan_configuration_id` into the unit's `installed_config_id`, and increments the unit's `version`; in the same operation the lease's occupancy axis moves `PENDING → INSTALLED` and its `version` is incremented, while its billing axis is left untouched. Completing a `TEARDOWN_RETURN` job first requires room in the receiving yard: the destination branch's `units_in_yard` must be strictly less than its `yard_capacity_units`, or the completion is refused with `ERR_YARD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and the crew has nowhere to put the building. When there is room, the lease's occupancy axis moves `INSTALLED → VACATED` with `actual_vacated_date` stamped from the logical clock's calendar date and its `version` incremented; because the lease vacated, the unit moves `IN_TRANSIT → RETURNED` with its `current_branch_id` rewritten to the job's `destination_branch_id` and its `version` incremented; and because the unit re-entered a yard status at a new branch, the destination branch's `units_in_yard`, `units_available` and `utilization_pct` are all recomputed. Custody moved, so the recomputation spans both yards: once the destination branch has been restated, the branch that held the unit's custody immediately before the move — which is the unit's own former `current_branch_id`, not necessarily the job's `origin_branch_id` — has its own three counters recomputed from scratch as well, so that the yard the building departed stops counting a unit that is no longer assigned to it. The one exception is the single-yard case where that former custody branch is the destination branch itself: there the one recomputation already performed stands and the branch is not restated a second time. The lease's billing axis is not touched here — settlement waits for the damage inspection. Completing a `RELOCATION` job requires the lease to be `INSTALLED` and drives the self-loop `ON_LEASE → ON_LEASE`, leaving the unit exactly where it was: the unit's `lifetime_relocations` is incremented by one, the lease's `site_address` is rewritten to the job's `new_site_address`, and the lease's `relocation_surcharge_cents` is increased by eighty-five percent of that job's `transport_cost_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent — the lessor absorbs the remaining fifteen percent. Both the unit's `version` and the lease's `version` are incremented, and no branch counter moves, because the building never re-enters a yard.

Aborting is possible from two places, optionally carrying a `cancellation_reason`. A job still `SCHEDULED` may be cancelled outright, moving `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and releasing the branch's dispatch slot on its `scheduled_date`. A job already `DISPATCHED` may be cancelled only when it is a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` — the crew turns the load around before it is set — moving `DISPATCHED → CANCELLED`, taking the unit back `IN_TRANSIT → RESERVED`, incrementing the unit's `version`, and recomputing the custody branch's counters so that `units_in_yard` rises by one, exactly reversing what dispatch did; the dispatch slot is released here as well. Cancelling from any other combination of state and type is refused with `ERR_JOB_NOT_DISPATCHABLE`. Jobs may be read individually and listed with filters on `lease_id`, `unit_id`, `origin_branch_id`, `destination_branch_id`, `status`, and `scheduled_date`.

### 3.9. Teardown Assessment, Final Settlement and Refurbishment Between Leases

When a building comes home, an independent `DamageAssessor` opens a **damage assessment** against it. The inspector supplies the `lease_id` of the tenancy that produced the damage, the `unit_id` of the returned building, the `logistics_job_id` of the move that brought it back, and `expected_lease_version` as described in §2.8. The job reference is checked hard: it must name a job that has reached `COMPLETED`, whose type is `TEARDOWN_RETURN`, whose own `lease_id` equals the assessment's lease, and whose unit is the unit being assessed. Any disagreement in that triple is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH`. On creation the system stamps `assessor_user_id` with the inspector's `X-User-ID` — the handle that later blocks that same operator from authorizing the resulting spend — and copies the unit's present `condition_grade` into the assessment's `condition_grade_before`, freezing the "as returned" grade before any repair is contemplated.

Re-assessment is a sanctioned path rather than an error. Creating a new assessment for a lease retires every existing assessment on that lease that is still open, taking it `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` or `DISPUTED → SUPERSEDED` according to where it stood, increments the lease's `assessment_round` by one so the number of rounds ever opened is auditable, and increments the lease's `version` by one. `SUPERSEDED` and `FINALIZED` are the two terminal positions for an assessment. A fresh assessment begins at `DRAFT` with `dispute_rounds` at 0, with `gross_damage_cents`, `wear_allowance_cents` and `chargeable_cents` all at 0, and with `condition_grade_after` and `dispute_reason` both null.

Findings are recorded as **line items** appended to the assessment's log, one at a time, while the assessment is `DRAFT` or `DISPUTED`; appending to an assessment in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Each line names a `component` — `FLOOR`, `WALL`, `HVAC`, `ROOF`, `DOOR`, or `ELECTRICAL` — a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, or `SEVERE`, a `quantity` multiplier that must be strictly greater than zero, and a `wear_and_tear` flag marking the line as ordinary deterioration rather than abuse. Each line's own `repair_cost_cents` is derived immediately as the component's base cost multiplied by the severity factor multiplied by the quantity, rounded half up to a whole cent. The base costs are $320.00 for a floor, $180.00 for a wall, $1,450.00 for HVAC, $960.00 for a roof, $410.00 for a door, and $730.00 for electrical work. The severity factors are 0.35 for `MINOR`, 1.00 for `MODERATE`, and 2.25 for `SEVERE`. The `damage_assessment_id` under which a line was filed travels with it, and because these lines are an append-only audit record they carry only an `id` and a `created_at`, as §2.4 sets out. The log of one assessment may be listed on its own.

Submitting the assessment moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and re-submitting after a dispute moves it `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`; in both cases the system recomputes `gross_damage_cents` from the current line-item set as the sum of every line's `repair_cost_cents`, walked in ascending order of the lines' creation timestamps. Wear-and-tear lines are fully included in this provisional figure and no cap of any kind is applied — this is the inspector's unmitigated view of the damage. An assessment with no lines yields zero. Because lines may be appended while the assessment is `DISPUTED`, the re-submission genuinely re-prices rather than merely re-stating; the `dispute_reason` the lessee recorded is left standing through it rather than cleared. Submitting an assessment that is in neither `DRAFT` nor `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and so is voiding a lease whose occupancy axis has already left `PENDING`.

The lessee gets a say. A `Customer` whose `X-User-ID` matches the `customer_id` of the lease behind the assessment may push a `SUBMITTED` assessment back with a mandatory `dispute_reason`, moving it `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` and incrementing `dispute_rounds` by one; an assessment that is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and because source state is screened at rank 7 that refusal precedes the dispute-budget test. The lessee gets two such rounds and no more: a dispute attempted when `dispute_rounds` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_LIMIT_REACHED`. The cycle `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → SUBMITTED` may therefore run twice before the assessment can only go forward.

Finalization moves the assessment `SUBMITTED → FINALIZED`. It is a `FleetAdmin` act, permitted only on a `SUBMITTED` assessment — anything else is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_SUBMITTED` — and only once the lease's occupancy axis has reached `VACATED`, a lease short of that being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The administrator supplies the `condition_grade_after` that the building is judged to carry as returned, and the system computes the net position in four steps. First it totals the repair cost of the line items whose `wear_and_tear` flag is true. Second it caps that total by a tenancy-length allowance: count the days the building was actually occupied, from the lease's `start_date` through its `actual_vacated_date` with both ends included; divide that day count by thirty and round the quotient up to a whole number of months; multiply the model's `replacement_value_cents` by one quarter of one percent per month of that count; and round the result half up to a whole cent. The `wear_allowance_cents` recorded on the assessment is the smaller of the wear-line total and that cap. Third it subtracts the allowance from `gross_damage_cents`, with the result held at zero if the allowance exceeds the gross. Fourth it clips the remainder at forty percent of the model's `replacement_value_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and stores the smaller of the two as `chargeable_cents`. For an identical set of line items the finalized figure is therefore never greater than the provisional one, and an assessment with no lines finalizes at zero across the board.

Finalization is also the settlement trigger, and the cascade runs to the customer record. Because the chargeable amount has become final, the lease's billing axis moves `ACCRUING → SETTLED` and its `final_settlement_cents` is computed as the actual, chronological accounting of the tenancy — as distinct from the forward projection made at quote time. That accounting starts from the lease's combined monthly rent, the sum of `monthly_base_rate_cents` and `monthly_accessory_rate_cents`. The billable month count is the actual occupied day count — from `start_date` through `actual_vacated_date`, both ends included — divided by thirty and rounded up to a whole number, and the rent component is the combined monthly rent multiplied by that count. An adjustment then reconciles the actual tenancy against the contract: when the billable month count is **fewer** than the contracted `term_months`, an early-termination charge equal to thirty-five percent of the combined monthly rent for each unserved month is added, rounded half up to a whole cent; when the billable month count is **greater** than `term_months`, a holdover charge equal to twenty-five percent of the combined monthly rent for each excess month is added, likewise rounded half up; and when the two counts are equal the adjustment is zero. To that are added the lease's `mobilization_fee_cents`; the `permit_fee_cents` of the permit application on this lease if one reached `APPROVED` — the first such application in creation order, if more than one did — and nothing if none did; the lease's accumulated `relocation_surcharge_cents`; and the `chargeable_cents` of the finalized assessment on this lease. The sum of mobilization fee, permit fee, rent, adjustment, relocation surcharge and damage chargeback is the final settlement. The lease's `billed_to_date_cents` is then set equal to `final_settlement_cents`; because that changed, the customer's `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed; because the balance changed, `credit_hold` is recomputed; and if and only if the hold flipped from false to true, the customer's `credit_tier` is demoted to `WATCH`. Both the lease's `version` and the customer's `version` are incremented.

With the damage settled, the yard decides what to do with the building. A `BranchOps` account quotes a **refurbishment order** by naming the `unit_id`, the `branch_id` of the yard that will do the work — which must be the branch the caller is acting as — the `damage_assessment_id` the quote is based on, a `scope` of `COSMETIC`, `STANDARD`, or `FULL`, the estimated shop `labor_hours`, the estimated `parts_cost_cents` before any scope adjustment, the `target_condition_grade` the building is expected to carry afterwards, and `expected_unit_version` as described in §2.8. Neither `labor_hours` nor `parts_cost_cents` may be negative. The assessment named must have reached `FINALIZED`, or the quote is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FINALIZED`, and it must be an assessment of the very unit being refurbished, or the quote is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH`. The servicing branch must be `OPERATIONAL`, or the quote is refused with `ERR_BRANCH_SUSPENDED`.

The order's `estimated_cost_cents` is the sum of three separately rounded lines. The labour line takes the estimated `labor_hours`, multiplies by the servicing branch's `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` and by a scope factor of 0.45 for `COSMETIC`, 1.00 for `STANDARD`, or 1.85 for `FULL`, and rounds half up to a whole cent. The parts line multiplies the estimated `parts_cost_cents` by the same scope factor and rounds half up. The recovery line takes sixty percent of the assessment's `chargeable_cents` and rounds half up — the shop expects to recover most, but not all, of what the lessee was charged. The three rounded lines are then added.

Quoting has three consequences and one deliberate non-consequence. Any refurbishment order already sitting at `QUOTED` on that unit moves `QUOTED → SUPERSEDED`; the unit's `latest_quote_estimate_cents` is set to the **new** order's `estimated_cost_cents`; and the unit's `version` is incremented by one. The unit's `status` is deliberately not changed: the building stays `RETURNED` until someone with spending authority approves the work.

That authority is a `FleetAdmin`, and it is fenced off by segregation of duties. Approval requires the order to be `QUOTED` — approving or declining an order that has already left `QUOTED`, such as rejecting one that is already `APPROVED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — the underlying assessment to be `FINALIZED`, failing which the approval is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FINALIZED`, and the unit to be `RETURNED`, a unit standing anywhere else being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; it further requires that the approving administrator's `X-User-ID` differs from the `assessor_user_id` recorded on that assessment, and an operator who tries to authorize spend against damage they themselves documented is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On approval the order moves `QUOTED → APPROVED`, the approver's identifier is stored as `approved_by_user_id`, the unit moves `RETURNED → REFURBISHING`, and the unit's `version` is incremented. The counters recomputed on principle — even though none of the three moves — are those of the unit's own custody branch, which is not necessarily the order's servicing `branch_id`: `RETURNED` and `REFURBISHING` are both yard statuses and both non-available.

Declining is the divergent branch of the same fork, and it does markedly less. Rejection requires the order to be `QUOTED` and the unit to be `RETURNED` — either state failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and it carries a mandatory `rejection_reason`. The order moves `QUOTED → REJECTED`, the reason is stored, the unit goes straight `RETURNED → IN_FLEET` and becomes leasable again as-is, the unit's `latest_quote_estimate_cents` is reset to 0, the unit's `version` is incremented, and the `units_available` and `utilization_pct` of the unit's custody branch are recomputed. Nothing else moves: there is no grade change, no increment to `refurb_cycles`, and no damage absorbed into `accumulated_damage_cents`.

Completing the work closes the loop. The `BranchOps` account of the servicing branch reports the `actual_cost_cents` actually spent on an order that stands at `APPROVED`; that figure is a required non-negative integer, and an order in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The order moves `APPROVED → COMPLETED` and the actual cost is stored. The unit moves `REFURBISHING → IN_FLEET`; its `condition_grade` is rewritten to the order's `target_condition_grade`; its `refurb_cycles` is incremented by one; its `accumulated_damage_cents` is increased by the `chargeable_cents` of the assessment behind the order, which is how a building's lifetime damage burden is tracked across tenancies; its `latest_quote_estimate_cents` is reset to 0; and its `version` is incremented. Because the unit re-entered `IN_FLEET`, the `units_available` of the unit's custody branch rises by one, and because that count changed, `utilization_pct` is recomputed.

Assessments may be read individually — subject to the ownership rule of §2.1 for a lessee reading its own — and listed with filters on `unit_id`, `lease_id`, `logistics_job_id`, and `status`. Refurbishment orders may be read individually and listed with filters on `unit_id`, `branch_id`, `damage_assessment_id`, and `status`. Finally, an administrative test-driving capability moves the server's logical clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and reports the clock's new position as `current_utc`; it is described in §2.6 and exists so that lead-time, occupancy and settlement behaviour can be exercised deterministically.
