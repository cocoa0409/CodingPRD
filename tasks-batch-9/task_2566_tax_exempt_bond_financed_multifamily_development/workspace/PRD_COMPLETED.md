# Tax-Exempt Bond Multifamily API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Affordable rental housing financed with tax-exempt private-activity bonds sits at the intersection of three separate rulebooks that almost never agree on timing. A state housing finance agency must ration a finite annual volume cap among competing sponsors; an indenture trustee must move construction money without letting undrawn proceeds bleed value through negative arbitrage; and the federal housing credit only attaches if at least half of a development's aggregate basis turns out to have been financed with those bonds — a fact that is provisionally estimated at bond closing and then re-decided years later against an audited cost certification. Today this coordination happens in spreadsheets and email threads, and the failure modes are expensive: a cap award silently double-spent because two officers priced their decisions against the same stale pool balance, a bond sized to the penny that fails once the denomination round-up pushes par above the award, a credit strip syndicated twice because nobody recomputed the undelivered remainder.

This document specifies the service that replaces those spreadsheets. It is a single system of record spanning the whole arc of a bond-financed deal: volume cap request and award out of a program-year pool, land-use restriction agreement drafting and county recording, bond sizing and closing, construction-period draws with a hard negative-arbitrage allowance, the recording of stabilized operating months, conversion from construction to permanent financing behind debt-service-coverage and occupancy tests, final cost certification, computation and issuance of the housing credit allocation, delivery of that credit to equity investors, and then decades of annual compliance certifications and physical inspections under the extended-use commitment.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **every derived number is derived, never asserted**: pool availability, applicable fractions, restricted rent ceilings, par amounts, arbitrage accruals, qualified basis and credit totals are all recomputed by the server from stored inputs, and the pinned evaluation order of each calculation is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Second, **the agency's decisions are serialized against what the decision-maker actually saw**: a cap award, a construction draw and a credit delivery each carry the version of the parent record the actor read, and a request built on a superseded view is refused rather than silently re-priced.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this service is a top-level, peer resource. There is no owning URL prefix and no nested resource path: a draw request is addressed at `/draw-requests/{id}`, never underneath its bond issue. Parent linkage always travels in the request body as an explicit foreign-key field, which means that when a request names several parents, all of them are resolved from the payload.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication and authorization are header-driven. Every request carries `X-User-Role`, and every request whose role is backed by a stored entity — or whose endpoint records the acting user's identity on a row — additionally carries `X-User-ID`. A missing or blank header of either kind is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the service so much as looks up the entity named in the path. A well-formed but unauthorized caller receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403); a caller acting on somebody else's resource receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Developer`, `Investor`, `HfaAllocationOfficer`, `HfaComplianceOfficer`, `BondTrustee`, and `SystemOperator`. Any other `X-User-Role` value — an unknown word, or one of the six mis-cased — appears in no endpoint's authorized-role list and is therefore refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, not with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A `Developer` is the bond-financed affordable-housing sponsor. It files developments, designates units, requests volume cap, drafts the land-use restriction agreement, sizes the bond issue and submits draws against it, records operating months, certifies costs, delivers credit to its syndication investors, and files the annual owner compliance certification. When `X-User-Role` is `Developer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Developer` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Developer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a developer-scoped write must resolve to a `Project` whose `developer_id` equals `X-User-ID`, whether that project is named directly in the payload or reached indirectly through a bond issue, a draw request, a unit restriction, an agreement, a certification, an allocation or a delivery.

An `Investor` is the tax-credit investor or syndicator that accepts delivered credit strips; it can accept a delivery but can neither create nor rescind one. When `X-User-Role` is `Investor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Investor` entity; an `InvestorDelivery` may be acted on only when its `investor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

The three agency-side and trustee-side roles are operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `HfaAllocationOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it (as `decided_by_user_id` on a decided `VolumeCapAllocation`, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a reviewed `CostCertification`, and as `issued_by_user_id` on a created `CreditAllocation`) and compares those recorded values to each other for segregation of duties, but never compares them to any entity's `id`. The `HfaAllocationOfficer` opens program-year cap pools, registers sponsors and investors, awards or denies cap requests, reviews cost certifications, and computes and issues the final credit allocation. When `X-User-Role` is `HfaComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, recorded where a transition writes it (as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a reviewed `ComplianceReport`) and never matched against an entity `id`; this role records and amends land-use restriction agreements, sanctions sponsors, reviews annual owner certifications, and schedules and closes physical inspections. When `X-User-Role` is `BondTrustee`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that the server may record but never compares to any entity's `id`; the trustee closes the issue, approves, funds and voids construction draws, converts to permanent financing and executes mandatory redemption, and touches nothing else. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and not compared to any entity's `id`; this identity owns the logical clock and holds no business permission on any domain endpoint.

The `HfaAllocationOfficer`, `HfaComplianceOfficer` and `BondTrustee` roles are exempt from the ownership rule entirely — they are agency and indenture actors and may read and act across sponsors. No role may perform another role's exclusive operations: the compliance officer cannot award cap, review a cost certification or move bond money; the trustee cannot designate units, award cap, record covenants or allocate credit; the sponsor cannot decide its own cap request, record its own covenant or accept its own credit delivery. Section 4 lists the authorized roles for each endpoint.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty further characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the host's real time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it does not move when a `SystemOperator` sets `current_utc`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. Both are echoed in `meta`, alongside `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). A `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied blank or as anything that is not a base-ten integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the parameters are parsed only after the endpoint's role check, so an unauthorized caller sending a bad `limit` still receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

Unless a specific list is documented otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — this is the default for developments, cap allocations, bond issues, agreements, cost certifications, credit allocations and credit deliveries. Four lists deliberately depart from that default because their domain order is the order the arithmetic uses: construction draws come back ordered by `period_start` ascending with ties broken by `draw_number` ascending (the same ordering the negative-arbitrage series walks); unit designations come back ordered by `unit_number` ascending, compared as the plain strings they are rather than numerically, so `"10"` precedes `"2"`; annual compliance reports come back ordered by `reporting_year` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; and physical inspections come back ordered by `window_start` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. Credit deliveries are ordered by `created_at` ascending, which is precisely the order in which the delivery waterfall fills. Every one of these orderings is total, because whenever the stated keys tie — two rows sharing a `created_at` millisecond, two draws of one issue sharing both a `period_start` and a `draw_number`, two units of different developments sharing a `unit_number` — the tie is broken by the server's internal row-creation sequence, so the earlier-created row sorts first. That sequence is bookkeeping only and appears in no response field.

Filters combine conjunctively. The available narrowing parameters are `developer_id` and `development_status` on developments; `project_id`, `cap_pool_id` and `status` on cap allocations; `project_id`, `volume_cap_allocation_id` and `status` on bond issues; `bond_issue_id` and `status` on draws; `project_id`, `designation` and `status` on unit designations; `project_id` and `status` on agreements, on cost certifications and on inspections; `project_id`, `cost_certification_id` and `status` on credit allocations; `credit_allocation_id`, `investor_id` and `status` on credit deliveries; and `project_id`, `reporting_year` and `status` on compliance reports. A filter is matched by comparing the supplied text against the decimal rendering of the row's stored value, so `reporting_year=2030` matches the integer 2030, and a row whose filtered field is null never matches any supplied value. No filter value is validated in its own right: an unknown, unparseable or blank one — `status=nonsense`, `reporting_year=abc`, `project_id=` — is not a malformed request but simply matches no row, and the endpoint answers 200 with an empty `data` array and a `total_count` of zero. Query parameters other than these narrowing names and the two pagination parameters are ignored.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Identity and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, a `created_at` timestamp, an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. Timestamps are ISO 8601 in UTC with exactly three fractional digits. `created_at` and `updated_at` are the two fields stamped from the host's real time rather than from the logical clock, so they order rows by the real sequence of requests and stand still when a `SystemOperator` moves `current_utc`; every other instant a transition writes onto a row comes from the logical clock. `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation was requested directly or arrived as a cascade from a sibling entity; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. All fourteen resource types in this domain — sponsors, investors, cap pools, developments, cap allocations, bond issues, draw requests, unit designations, regulatory agreements, cost certifications, credit allocations, credit deliveries, compliance reports and physical inspections — carry the full quartet; none of them is timestamp-only.

Path identifiers are validated left to right, and there is exactly one of them per path in this domain: the `{id}` segment. When it fails to resolve, the response is that entity's own 404 rather than a generic one, so that a client can tell which record is missing. Foreign keys named in a request body are resolved in the declaration order in which their entities are introduced in this document, so a payload carrying two bad references surfaces the earlier entity's 404.

No business rule in this service reads the host's wall clock — the host's real time is consulted only for the `created_at` and `updated_at` bookkeeping stamps of the paragraph above and for the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`, neither of which any rule evaluates. A single **logical UTC clock** governs every deadline, expiry, window, period and interval rule described below. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it changes only when a `SystemOperator` sets it, supplying the new instant as `current_utc`. Advancing the clock evaluates every time-gated transition in the domain in a single pass, in the order described in §3.9. The clock is also the source of every timestamp the server stamps onto a row as part of a transition — a recording date, an acceptance instant, a placed-in-service date.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers in **cents**, and every field name that ends in `_cents` obeys that convention. All ratios, yields and percentages that end in `_bps` are integers in **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points is one hundred percent. There are no floating-point amounts anywhere in the API surface.

Two rounding modes appear, and which one applies is never left to the implementer's discretion. **Round half up to whole cents** applies to the cost-of-issuance allowance, to each negative-arbitrage period accrual, to the annual credit, and to the recapture exposure. **Floor** (truncate toward zero) applies to every basis-point ratio in the domain — the unit fraction, the floor-space fraction, the applicable fraction, the set-aside ratio, both fifty-percent-test ratios, the achieved set-aside ratio, the debt-service coverage ratio, the monthly occupancy ratios, the price per credit dollar and the negative-arbitrage allowance — and to each intermediate division step of the restricted rent ceiling and the per-unit qualified basis. One calculation rounds **up**: the bond denomination step, which raises unrounded par to the next whole authorized denomination and never lowers it. Where a formula pins the order of rounding relative to summation, that order is part of the specification and is stated with the formula.

Cap-pool arithmetic, undrawn-proceeds arithmetic and credit-delivery arithmetic are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding at all.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Pool, Issue and Allocation Parents

Three writes in this domain are priced against a balance held on a *parent* record, and all three therefore require the client to declare which revision of that parent it read. In each case the declared value must equal the parent's current `version` at the moment the request is processed. These version fields are not optional and have no default: omitting one is a malformed request.

An award-or-deny decision on a cap allocation — `POST /volume-cap-allocations/{id}/decision` — must carry `expected_cap_pool_version` matching the current `version` of the `CapPool` the allocation draws from. The officer's decision is admissible only against the pool ceiling the officer observed; if another allocation of the same pool has since been awarded, denied, returned, revised, expired or released, the pool's `version` has advanced and the observed `awardable_cents` is stale. Such a request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAP_POOL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The read that supplies this value is the single-pool fetch.

Creating a construction draw — `POST /draw-requests` — must carry `expected_bond_issue_version` matching the current `version` of the target `BondIssue`. A draw is sized against the undrawn balance the sponsor observed; if another draw has since been funded or voided, the issue's `version` has advanced and the undrawn balance the new draw would consume has moved. Such a request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOND_ISSUE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The read that supplies this value is the single-bond-issue fetch.

Creating a credit delivery — `POST /investor-deliveries` — must carry `expected_credit_allocation_version` matching the current `version` of the target `CreditAllocation`. A delivery is sized against the undelivered credit the syndicator observed; if another delivery or a rescission has since moved `undelivered_credit_cents`, the allocation's `version` has advanced and the waterfall clamp described in §3.8 would silently deliver a different amount than the syndication agreement priced. Such a request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_ALLOCATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The read that supplies this value is the single-credit-allocation fetch.

No other endpoint in this domain takes a version field of any kind. In particular, the cap-allocation revise and return operations, the bond issuance, conversion and redemption operations, the draw decision and funding operations, the delivery acceptance and rescission operations and all of the compliance operations are unguarded by optimistic concurrency and must not be given one.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

A request that violates several rules must surface exactly one error, and which one is chosen by a fixed global priority ladder — never by the order in which fields appear in the payload, and never by which check happens to be cheapest. Every request walks the ladder from the top and returns the first violated rule's error. The bands, in order, are: authentication and authorization; payload well-formedness; existence of the path entity; existence of referenced foreign keys; optimistic-concurrency version match; entity-state rules; temporal business rules; fiscal business rules; safety business rules; and finally structural constraint rules.

Within that skeleton the individual rules rank as follows.

1. **Caller is authenticated.** Every endpoint requires `X-User-Role`; every endpoint whose role is entity-backed (`Developer`, `Investor`) or that records an acting user also requires `X-User-ID`. The acting-user endpoints are exactly the five that write an operator id onto a row — `POST /volume-cap-allocations/{id}/decision`, `POST /cost-certifications/{id}/review`, `POST /credit-allocations`, `POST /credit-allocations/{id}/issue` and `POST /compliance-reports/{id}/review`; every other endpoint reached by an operator role, the trustee's bond and draw transitions, the sponsor sanction endpoint and the clock endpoint among them, is satisfied by `X-User-Role` alone and does not care whether `X-User-ID` was sent. The requirement is decided by the role actually supplied rather than by the endpoint's authorized list, so a `Developer` or `Investor` header with no `X-User-ID` fails here even on an endpoint that role may not call. Missing or blank means `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, evaluated before the path entity is even looked up.
2. **Role is permitted for the endpoint.** The `X-User-Role` value must appear in that endpoint's authorized-role list, and this is checked before existence — so an `HfaComplianceOfficer` deciding a cap allocation whose id does not exist receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, not a 404.
3. **Caller owns the resource.** For a `Developer` caller the target must resolve to a `Project` whose `developer_id` equals `X-User-ID`; for an `Investor` caller the target `InvestorDelivery`'s `investor_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. On the two registry reads the comparison is against the fetched row itself rather than against a project: a `Developer` calling `GET /developers/{id}` must name its own sponsor row and an `Investor` calling `GET /investors/{id}` must name its own investor row. When the row the ownership test would examine does not exist at all, the test is skipped and rule 5's 404 is returned instead. Violation is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This scope covers the developer-scoped and investor-scoped single-resource endpoints only; the agency and trustee roles are exempt, and no list endpoint is ownership-scoped — a `Developer` or `Investor` listing a collection sees every sponsor's and every investor's rows unless it narrows the list with an explicit filter.
4. **Request body is well formed.** All required inputs present and non-null; every enum value inside its declared set; every `_cents` and `_bps` field an integer that is not negative; every count-of-units field an integer of at least 1 where the entity requires at least one; a JSON boolean and a numeric string are never accepted where an integer is declared; every datetime an ISO-8601 string carrying a full calendar date *and* a time of day and terminated by a `Z`. A bare calendar date such as `2027-03-01` is therefore malformed even in a field this document calls a date, as is any numeric UTC offset standing in place of the `Z`; a fractional-seconds part is optional and may run one to nine digits, and a lowercase `t` separator or `z` terminator is accepted. Unknown keys are ignored rather than rejected, and an optional field present but explicitly `null` counts as absent, taking whatever default the field documents. `period_end` must be strictly greater than `period_start`, and `window_end` must be greater than or equal to `window_start`. Violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). This rule guards every create and every modify endpoint.
5. **Path entity exists.** The `{id}` in the path must resolve to a stored row of that endpoint's own entity, otherwise that entity's specific 404 is returned: `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVELOPER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAP_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOND_ISSUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRAW_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_RESTRICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REG_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COST_CERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
6. **Referenced foreign keys exist.** Every foreign key in the request body must resolve, returning the referenced entity's own 404. When one body carries more than one unresolvable reference, they are resolved in the entity declaration order used in §3 — so a bond-issue creation naming both a bad `project_id` and a bad `volume_cap_allocation_id` returns `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards the creation of developments, cap allocations, bond issues, draw requests, unit designations, regulatory agreements, cost certifications, credit allocations, credit deliveries, compliance reports and physical inspections.
7. **Expected parent version matches.** On the three endpoints listed in §2.6 — `POST /volume-cap-allocations/{id}/decision`, `POST /draw-requests` and `POST /investor-deliveries` — the supplied version must equal the parent's current `version`. This is checked *after* existence but *before* any state or business rule, so a stale version wins over a wrong-state parent: a client that pins an old pool version *and* targets an already-awarded allocation gets `ERR_STALE_CAP_POOL_VERSION`, not the state error.
8. **Owning sponsor is active.** The owning `Developer`'s `status` must be `ACTIVE` for any project-advancing write: filing a development, requesting cap, sizing a bond issue, closing that issue, submitting a draw, and submitting a cost certification. A `SUSPENDED` or `DEBARRED` sponsor is blocked with `ERR_DEVELOPER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Read endpoints are unaffected. The precise endpoint set is `POST /projects`, `POST /volume-cap-allocations`, `POST /bond-issues`, `POST /bond-issues/{id}/issue`, `POST /draw-requests` and `POST /cost-certifications/{id}/submit`, plus the award branch of `POST /volume-cap-allocations/{id}/decision`, which is the one place where the standing test sits *after* the subject's own state rule rather than before it — so a suspended sponsor's already-decided request reports `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID` — and a denial there is never blocked by standing at all.
9. **Subject entity is in a state that admits the trigger.** The state tables in §3 govern: the subject's current state must have an outgoing transition for the trigger being invoked. Each entity maps to its own 409 — `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BOND_ISSUE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DRAW_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_UNIT_RESTRICTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REG_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_COST_CERT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CREDIT_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DELIVERY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. The sponsor sanction machine owns no dedicated state error, so a sanction action with no outgoing transition from the sponsor's current `status` — a suspension against an already `SUSPENDED` sponsor, or any action at all against the terminal `DEBARRED` status — is reported as `ERR_DEVELOPER_NOT_ACTIVE`. There is no idempotent replay anywhere in this domain: repeating a trigger that already fired hits this rule.
10. **Referenced award has not expired.** Sizing a bond issue and closing it both require the referenced `VolumeCapAllocation` to be `AWARDED` with its `expiry_date` strictly after the current logical clock. An `expiry_date` exactly equal to the clock instant counts as expired, because the deadline is exclusive. Violation is `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXPIRED`, and the rule guards both `POST /bond-issues` and `POST /bond-issues/{id}/issue`.
11. **Regulatory agreement is recorded before issuance.** Bonds may not close until the development has a `RegulatoryAgreement` whose `status` is `RECORDED`. `DRAFT` does not satisfy the rule, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` does not satisfy it, and `RELEASED` does not either. Violation on `POST /bond-issues/{id}/issue` is `ERR_REG_AGREEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`.
12. **Development is placed in service before a credit allocation.** Creating a credit allocation requires the development's `development_status` to be either `PLACED_IN_SERVICE` or `STABILIZED`; a development still `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` cannot receive a final allocation even when its cost certification has been accepted. Violation on `POST /credit-allocations` is `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_PLACED_IN_SERVICE`. The same requirement and the same error guard a second endpoint, `POST /cost-certifications/{id}/submit`, where the certification may not go to the agency until its development has reached `PLACED_IN_SERVICE` or `STABILIZED`; there the test sits *after* the certification's own state rule, so a certification that has already left `DRAFT` and `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` on a development still `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` reports `ERR_COST_CERT_STATE_INVALID` rather than this error.
13. **Cost certification is accepted before a credit allocation.** The referenced `CostCertification`'s `status` must be `ACCEPTED`; a `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` certification is refused here with `ERR_COST_CERT_NOT_ACCEPTED` even though it exists and belongs to the development.
14. **Cost certification belongs to the named development.** The referenced certification's `project_id` must equal the request's `project_id`; a certification belonging to a sibling development of the same sponsor is refused with `ERR_COST_CERT_PROJECT_MISMATCH`.
15. **Final fifty-percent test must have passed before credit is deliverable.** Issuing the allocation certificate requires the development's `automatic_credit_triggered` to be true at that instant. Violation on `POST /credit-allocations/{id}/issue` is `ERR_FIFTY_PERCENT_TEST_FAILED`.
16. **Segregation of duties between cap award and credit issuance.** The `X-User-ID` issuing the allocation certificate must differ from the `decided_by_user_id` recorded on the development's awarded cap allocation, and must also differ from the `reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on the referenced cost certification. Violation on `POST /credit-allocations/{id}/issue` is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
17. **Draw periods do not overlap** — `ERR_DRAW_PERIOD_OVERLAP` on `POST /draw-requests`; see §3.6.
18. **Inspection windows respect the buffer** — `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` on `POST /physical-inspections`; see §3.9.
19. **Inspection falls inside the compliance period** — `ERR_INSPECTION_OUTSIDE_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD` (HTTP 422) on `POST /physical-inspections`; see §3.9.
20. **Compliance report year is unique and in period** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLIANCE_REPORT` on `POST /compliance-reports`; see §3.9.
21. **Pool has enough awardable cap** — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VOLUME_CAP` (HTTP 422) on `POST /volume-cap-allocations/{id}/decision`; see §3.3.
22. **Par amount is within the cap award** — `ERR_PAR_EXCEEDS_CAP_AWARD` (HTTP 422) on `POST /bond-issues`; see §3.5.
23. **Draw does not exceed undrawn proceeds** — `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_UNDRAWN` (HTTP 422). This rule guards three endpoints, not one: `POST /draw-requests`, `POST /draw-requests/{id}/decision` and `POST /draw-requests/{id}/fund`; see §3.6.
24. **Negative-arbitrage allowance is not exceeded** — `ERR_NEGATIVE_ARBITRAGE_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) on `POST /draw-requests/{id}/fund`; see §3.6.
25. **Fifty-percent test at issuance** — `ERR_FIFTY_PERCENT_TEST_FAILED` (HTTP 422) on `POST /bond-issues/{id}/issue`; see §3.5.
26. **Conversion requires coverage and stabilization** — on `POST /bond-issues/{id}/convert`, insufficient operating history is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_OPERATING_HISTORY` and is checked before either of the two ratio tests; a coverage shortfall is `ERR_DSCR_BELOW_MINIMUM` and an occupancy shortfall is `ERR_OCCUPANCY_BELOW_STABILIZATION`, with the coverage failure reported first when both hold. See §3.7.
27. **Delivery has credit left to deliver** — `ERR_NO_UNDELIVERED_CREDIT` (HTTP 422) on `POST /investor-deliveries`; see §3.8.
28. **Set-aside election is satisfied before recording** — `ERR_SET_ASIDE_NOT_SATISFIED` (HTTP 422). This safety rule guards two endpoints: `POST /regulatory-agreements/{id}/record` and `POST /bond-issues/{id}/issue`. It is deliberately checked *before* the structural term-length and designation-count rules, because it protects the tenants the recorded covenant is meant to bind.
29. **Every designated unit is accounted for before recording** — `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) on `POST /regulatory-agreements/{id}/record`; see §3.4.
30. **Agreement election matches the development's election** — `ERR_SET_ASIDE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) on both `POST /regulatory-agreements` and `POST /regulatory-agreements/{id}/amend`.
31. **Extended-use term is long enough** — `ERR_EXTENDED_USE_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422) on both `POST /regulatory-agreements` and `POST /regulatory-agreements/{id}/amend`.
32. **Unit number is unique within the development** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER` (HTTP 409) on `POST /unit-restrictions`, and at the same rank but evaluated immediately after the uniqueness test, `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_EXCEEDS_TOTAL` (HTTP 422) when the designation would push the row count past the development's total unit count.
33. **AMI band is allowed for the designation and the election** — `ERR_AMI_PERCENT_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422) on both `POST /unit-restrictions` and `PATCH /unit-restrictions/{id}`.
34. **Operating month index is unique and contiguous** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATING_MONTH` (HTTP 409) on `POST /bond-issues/{id}/operating-months`.
35. **One live allocation per development** — `ERR_ALLOCATION_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409) on both `POST /volume-cap-allocations` and `POST /credit-allocations`.
36. **Compliance report counts are coherent** — `ERR_REPORT_COUNTS_INCOHERENT` (HTTP 422) on `POST /compliance-reports`.

### 2.8. Two Independent Status Axes and the Cascade Discipline

A development is not on one timeline but two. Its `development_status` tracks financing and construction and runs `PLANNING` → `FINANCED` → `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` → `PLACED_IN_SERVICE` → `STABILIZED`, with `WITHDRAWN` as its only terminal state. Its `compliance_status` tracks restriction and runs `UNRESTRICTED` → `RESTRICTED` → `EXTENDED_USE` → `RELEASED`, with `NONCOMPLIANT` reachable from and returnable to `RESTRICTED`; `RELEASED` is terminal. The two axes are genuinely independent: neither one gates a transition of the other, and a development can perfectly well be `RESTRICTED` while still `PLANNING`, because the covenant must be recorded before the bonds can close.

Cascades in this domain are deep and ordered, and each level's own effects are part of the specification. When an operation mutates more than one row, every affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — including rows the caller never named. The deepest chains are three levels long: a noncompliance finding moves the development's compliance axis, which moves its credit allocation, which flags every accepted delivery of that allocation; the clearing of a later finding reverses all three; conversion of the bonds moves the development's development axis, which stamps the recorded agreement's end dates, which activates every proposed unit designation. Section 3 states each chain link by link.

Compensating actions exist for the two money-moving creates, and they are required to restore the invariants exactly rather than approximately. Voiding a funded draw restores the undrawn balance and recomputes the entire negative-arbitrage series over the surviving funded draws, and rescinding a delivery returns its credit to the undelivered remainder.

### 2.9. Conservation Invariants

Three ledgers in this system must balance after every mutation, including after every compensating action, and the tests of correctness are stated as equalities rather than as tolerances. All three are derived at read time from the live child rows they summarise rather than kept as stored counters — every single-resource fetch and every list entry recomputes them from scratch — which is what makes the equalities hold even for a parent row whose own `version` did not move on the mutation that changed a child, and why "re-deriving the pool" in the narratives below is observable as a bump to the pool's `updated_at` and `version` rather than as an arithmetic adjustment of a stored figure. The same read-time derivation governs the sponsor's `active_project_count`, a development's four designation counters, its `unit_fraction_bps`, `floor_space_fraction_bps` and `applicable_fraction_bps`, its `set_aside_ratio_bps` and `set_aside_satisfied`, and a unit's `imputed_household_size`. Every other figure any response carries — including an investor's `total_credit_accepted_cents`, a unit's `restricted_rent_cents`, a delivery's `price_per_credit_dollar_bps`, an allocation's `qualified_basis_cents`, `annual_credit_cents`, `total_credit_cents` and snapshotted `applicable_fraction_bps`, an issue's `negative_arbitrage_cents` and `dscr_bps`, and a report's `achieved_set_aside_bps` and `shortfall_bps` — is read back exactly as it was stamped onto the row.

For a program-year cap pool, the total cap always equals the sum of the available, reserved and committed amounts. `total_cap_cents` is `base_cap_cents` plus `carryforward_cents`; `reserved_cents` is the sum of `requested_amount_cents` over the pool's allocations that are still `PENDING`; `committed_cents` is the sum of `awarded_amount_cents` over the pool's allocations that are `AWARDED` or `COMMITTED`; `available_cents` is the total cap less the reserved amount less the committed amount, and it is allowed to go negative when pending requests oversubscribe the pool; and `awardable_cents` is the total cap less the committed amount only. Allocations that are `DENIED`, `DEFERRED`, `RETURNED`, `EXPIRED` or `RELEASED` contribute to neither aggregate. The derived `pool_status` is `EXHAUSTED` when `awardable_cents` is less than or equal to zero; otherwise `OVERSUBSCRIBED` when `available_cents` is less than zero; otherwise `OPEN`. Every allocation create, decision, revision, return, expiry and release re-derives all six of those figures from scratch.

For a bond issue, `par_amount_cents` always equals `cumulative_funded_cents` plus `undrawn_cents`, where `cumulative_funded_cents` is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over the issue's draws whose `status` is `FUNDED`. Approved-but-unfunded draws do not reduce the undrawn balance, which is why several of them can together exceed it and the excess is caught at funding time.

For a credit allocation, `total_credit_cents` always equals `delivered_credit_cents` plus `undelivered_credit_cents`, where `delivered_credit_cents` is the sum of `delivered_credit_cents` over the allocation's deliveries whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `RECAPTURE_FLAGGED`. A `RESCINDED` delivery contributes nothing.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Sponsor Standing

The agency deliberately splits authority so that no single officer can both ration the public resource and hand out the federal benefit. The allocation officer who decided a development's cap award is recorded on that allocation as `decided_by_user_id`, and the officer who accepted its cost certification is recorded as `reviewed_by_user_id`; the officer who creates the final credit allocation is recorded as `issued_by_user_id`. At the moment the allocation certificate is issued, the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against both recorded reviewers and must differ from each of them.

Sponsor standing is the other global gate. A `Developer` row carries a `status` of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `DEBARRED`, together with a `sanction_reason` that is null while the sponsor has never been sanctioned, and an `active_project_count` that counts the sponsor's developments whose `development_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`. Sanctions never rewrite history — existing allocations and issues keep their states — but they do freeze forward motion, and while a sponsor is not `ACTIVE` no new cap award may be made for its developments either.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The nine subsections below follow a deal from the day the agency opens its program-year pool to the day the extended-use covenant is released, decades later. Each subsection carries the fields, thresholds and cascades that belong to that stage of the narrative; a complete implementation of any single operation generally has to draw on more than one of them.

### 3.1. Program Onboarding: Sponsors, Investors and the Program-Year Cap Pool

Before any deal exists, an `HfaAllocationOfficer` populates the three registries the rest of the system references. Registering a development sponsor takes the sponsor's legal `name`, its federal employer identification number as `tax_id`, and a notice address for agency correspondence as `contact_email`. The `tax_id` is expected to be unique across all sponsors, but that expectation is administrative rather than enforced: no error in §5 reports a collision, and a second sponsor registered under an already-used `tax_id` is created normally. All three fields must be non-blank strings. A newly registered sponsor opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, with `sanction_reason` null, and with `active_project_count` at zero — that counter is derived, not supplied, and reflects how many of the sponsor's developments are in any `development_status` other than `WITHDRAWN`. It is derived at read time rather than stored, so a later change to one of the sponsor's developments — including a move to `WITHDRAWN` under the redemption cascade of §3.5 — changes what the counter reports without itself mutating the sponsor row; the sponsor's `updated_at` and `version` move only when a sanction action fires or when the sponsor files a new development.

Registering a tax-credit investor takes the investor's legal `name`, its `tax_id` (also expected to be unique across all investors, and unenforced in the same way) and an `investor_type` describing the channel through which credit will be taken, which is one of `SYNDICATOR`, `DIRECT_CORPORATE` or `FUND`. An investor opens with `total_credit_accepted_cents` at zero; thereafter that figure is a stored running total on the investor row, moved by exactly two events: accepting a delivery raises it by that delivery's `delivered_credit_cents`, and rescinding a delivery that had already reached `ACCEPTED` lowers it by the same amount. No other transition adjusts it — in particular, a delivery moved out of `ACCEPTED` into `RECAPTURE_FLAGGED` by the recapture cascade of §3.9, or returned from `RECAPTURE_FLAGGED` to `ACCEPTED` by the cure, leaves the figure untouched, so credit once accepted stays on the investor's ledger while it is flagged.

Opening a program-year private-activity volume cap pool takes a `program_year`, an integer of at least 1 which is expected to be unique across all pools though the service does not enforce it either, so a second pool opened for a year that already has one is created normally, and a `base_cap_cents` giving the statutory per-capita cap for that year, which must be greater than zero. An optional `carryforward_cents` records unused cap rolled forward from prior years; it defaults to zero and may not be negative. On creation the pool's ledger is solved for the first time: `total_cap_cents` becomes the base cap plus the carryforward, `reserved_cents` and `committed_cents` are both zero, `available_cents` and `awardable_cents` both equal the total cap, and `pool_status` opens at `OPEN`. The complete definition of these six derived figures and the conservation equality they must satisfy is in §2.9.

Sponsor sanctions are the `HfaComplianceOfficer`'s tool and are driven by an `action` field taking one of three values with a mandatory accompanying `reason`. A `SUSPEND` action moves an `ACTIVE` sponsor to `SUSPENDED` and records the supplied text as `sanction_reason`; existing allocations and issues keep their states, but while the sponsor sits in `SUSPENDED` no new cap award decision may be made for any of its developments. A `REINSTATE` action moves a `SUSPENDED` sponsor back to `ACTIVE` and clears `sanction_reason` to null; this `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE` cycle is unbounded and may be repeated indefinitely. A `DEBAR` action is available from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, records `sanction_reason`, and lands the sponsor in `DEBARRED`, which is terminal — no further sanction action of any kind may follow it. Debarment additionally reaches into the sponsor's live pipeline: every development of that sponsor whose `development_status` is `PLANNING`, `FINANCED` or `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` has `financing_stalled` set to true. Each sanction action increments the sponsor's `version` by 1. A sanction action with no outgoing transition from the sponsor's current standing — a suspension of an already-`SUSPENDED` sponsor, or anything at all aimed at a `DEBARRED` one — is refused with `ERR_DEVELOPER_NOT_ACTIVE`, because the sponsor machine has no state error of its own.

### 3.2. Filing a Development and Designating Its Units

A `Developer` files a development by supplying its own `developer_id` — which must equal `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, reported ahead of both the payload checks and the sponsor 404 whenever the supplied value is a string at all, so naming another sponsor's id, or a string id matching no sponsor whatever, is an ownership failure rather than a `ERR_DEVELOPER_NOT_FOUND` — the project trade `name`, the `total_units` count of residential units restricted and market alike, the `total_floor_space_sqft` giving aggregate residential floor space, the `land_cost_cents` and the `depreciable_basis_cents` as filed. Both `total_units` and `total_floor_space_sqft` must be greater than zero; `land_cost_cents` may be zero but not negative; `depreciable_basis_cents` must be greater than zero. Land cost is part of aggregate basis but is never part of eligible basis, a distinction that matters twice later. The seventh input is the `set_aside_election`, the income and rent restriction election that governs the whole development and takes one of three values: `MIN_20_50`, `MIN_40_60` or `AVG_INCOME`. On creation the server solves `aggregate_basis_cents` as the land cost plus the filed depreciable basis — this is the denominator of the provisional fifty-percent test — sets both status axes to their initial values `PLANNING` and `UNRESTRICTED`, raises the sponsor's `active_project_count` by 1 and increments the sponsor's `version` by 1. A sponsor that is not `ACTIVE` cannot file a development at all.

Unit-by-unit designation is where nearly all of the restriction arithmetic originates. Designating a unit takes the owning `project_id`, a `unit_number` label, the `bedroom_count` (zero or more, since a studio is a legitimate restricted unit), the unit's `floor_space_sqft` (greater than zero), a `designation` of `RESTRICTED`, `MARKET` or `EMPLOYEE_UNIT`, the monthly `utility_allowance_cents` deducted from the gross rent ceiling (zero or more), and the achievable unrestricted monthly rent as `market_rent_cents` (greater than zero). A restricted unit additionally elects an area-median-income band as `ami_percent`. Every unit opens in `status` `PROPOSED`. Designation is gated on neither status axis nor on the sponsor's standing: a unit may be added at any point in the development's life, and one designated after the development has already stabilized still opens `PROPOSED` and is never retroactively activated, though it counts toward the fractions and counters below immediately. A `unit_number` is unique within its development across every unit status, `RELEASED` ones included, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER`; separately, the number of designated units may never exceed the development's `total_units`, and an attempt to designate one more than that is `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_EXCEEDS_TOTAL`, evaluated immediately after the uniqueness test.

The `ami_percent` rules are strict in three directions at once. The field is required when the designation is `RESTRICTED` and must be omitted otherwise; when supplied it must be one of 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 or 80; and a band above 60 is permitted only when the development's `set_aside_election` is `AVG_INCOME`. Any of those three failures is `ERR_AMI_PERCENT_NOT_ALLOWED`, on both initial designation and later redesignation. For a unit that is not `RESTRICTED`, `restricted_rent_cents` stays null.

Redesignation is allowed while a unit is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` and takes a new `designation`, optionally restating `ami_percent`, `utility_allowance_cents` or `market_rent_cents`. The `designation` is required on every patch even when it merely repeats the unit's current one, `bedroom_count` and `floor_space_sqft` cannot be restated here at all, and any of the three optional fields the payload omits keeps its stored value. Because only an `ami_percent` actually present in the payload can offend the omission rule, a patch that designates `RESTRICTED` without restating the band carries the unit's existing band forward, and a patch to `MARKET` or `EMPLOYEE_UNIT` that says nothing about the band simply clears the stored one to null instead of failing. Patching a unit that has already reached `RELEASED` is `ERR_UNIT_RESTRICTION_STATE_INVALID`, and that state check precedes the `ami_percent` rules. Because every derived development figure is re-derived from scratch on each designation change rather than incrementally adjusted, redesignating a unit and then reverting it restores the prior applicable fraction exactly.

Five counters on the development are re-derived on every designation and every redesignation: `restricted_unit_count`, `market_unit_count` and `employee_unit_count` count the development's units carrying each designation; `designated_unit_count` counts all of its units regardless of designation; and each such re-derivation increments the development's `version` by 1.

Two fractions are then re-derived from the same set. The **unit fraction**, stored as `unit_fraction_bps`, is the number of the development's `RESTRICTED` units multiplied by 10,000, divided by `total_units`, with the quotient floored to a whole basis point. The **floor-space fraction**, stored as `floor_space_fraction_bps`, is the summed `floor_space_sqft` of the development's `RESTRICTED` units multiplied by 10,000, divided by `total_floor_space_sqft`, again floored. Units in every status count toward both numerators — `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` and `RELEASED` alike — while `MARKET` and `EMPLOYEE_UNIT` units are excluded from both numerators but never from the two denominators. The `applicable_fraction_bps` is the smaller of the two fractions. With zero restricted units both fractions are zero and the applicable fraction is zero — not null, and not an error.

The **set-aside compliance ratio** is derived from the same population but selects it by AMI band, and the selection depends on the election. Under `MIN_20_50` the qualifying units are the restricted units whose `ami_percent` is at most 50, and the ratio must reach 2,000 basis points — twenty percent of total units. Under `MIN_40_60` the qualifying units are the restricted units whose `ami_percent` is at most 60, and the ratio must reach 4,000 basis points — forty percent of total units. Under `AVG_INCOME` the qualifying units are the restricted units whose `ami_percent` is at most 80, the ratio must likewise reach 4,000 basis points, and one additional ceiling applies. In all three cases `set_aside_ratio_bps` is the count of qualifying units multiplied by 10,000, divided by `total_units`, floored to a whole basis point.

The extra `AVG_INCOME` ceiling works on the average elected band rather than on the count: the summed `ami_percent` of the qualifying units is multiplied by 100 and divided by the number of qualifying units, floored, and that average — expressed in basis points, so a set averaging sixty percent of area median income reads as 6,000 — may not exceed 6,000 basis points. When the qualifying set is empty the average reads as zero, but an empty set can never satisfy an `AVG_INCOME` election because the election additionally requires at least one qualifying unit. Putting the pieces together, `set_aside_satisfied` becomes true when the set-aside ratio reaches its election's threshold and, for `AVG_INCOME` only, the qualifying set is non-empty and its average band does not exceed 6,000 basis points; under the two minimum-set-aside elections the average plays no part at all. Both boundaries are inclusive: a ratio exactly equal to the threshold satisfies the election, and an average exactly equal to 6,000 basis points satisfies it too.

The **restricted rent ceiling** is solved for each `RESTRICTED` unit — on designation, on a redesignation into `RESTRICTED`, and again when the unit is activated at stabilization — and four inputs interact non-linearly to produce it. First, the unit's `imputed_household_size` is the larger of 1 and one more than `bedroom_count`, so a studio imputes a one-person household and a three-bedroom imputes four. That figure is reported on every unit's response, `MARKET` and `EMPLOYEE_UNIT` ones included, even though only a `RESTRICTED` unit ever prices a ceiling against it. Second, the election imposes a band cap of its own: 50 under `MIN_20_50`, 60 under `MIN_40_60`, and 80 under `AVG_INCOME`. The **effective band** is the smaller of the unit's own `ami_percent` and that election cap, so a unit that elected a 70-percent band in a `MIN_40_60` development charges against 60. Third, one hundred percent of area median annual income is a fixed schedule by imputed household size, in cents: 6,300,000 for a household of one, 7,200,000 for two, 8,100,000 for three, 9,000,000 for four, 9,720,000 for five and 10,440,000 for six. The schedule stops at six and is not extrapolated beyond it: a unit of five or more bedrooms, whose imputed household size would run to seven or higher, is clamped down onto the six-person row and charges against 10,440,000 cents. The unit's annual income figure is that schedule value for its imputed household size multiplied by the effective band, divided by 100, floored to whole cents.

From there the nesting order is pinned and must be reproduced exactly. Thirty percent of that annual income figure is taken — multiplied by 30, divided by 100 — and **floored to whole cents first**; only then is the result divided by 12 and **floored again** to produce the maximum monthly gross rent. The `utility_allowance_cents` is subtracted from that AMI-derived gross ceiling and from nothing else — it is never subtracted from market rent. The smaller of the utility-adjusted AMI ceiling and the unit's `market_rent_cents` is taken. Finally, and last of all, a program rent floor of 25,000 cents is applied by taking the larger of that floor and the value just computed, which means the floor can legitimately raise the answer above both caps. The result is stored as `restricted_rent_cents`.

### 3.3. Volume Cap: Request, Award, Deferral and Recycling

A `Developer` requests private-activity volume cap by naming the `project_id` the cap is for, the `cap_pool_id` of the program-year pool it should come from, the `requested_amount_cents` the sponsor asks the agency to reserve (greater than zero), and the `requested_closing_date` on which the sponsor intends to close the bond issue. No rule constrains that date: it may lie before the current clock instant, it need not agree with the `closing_date` the trustee eventually supplies, and no calculation ever reads it again. The pool's own `pool_status` is not a gate either — a request may be filed against a pool that is already `OVERSUBSCRIBED` or even `EXHAUSTED`, and only the award decision is capped — and when both of the request's foreign keys fail to resolve it is the pool's `ERR_CAP_POOL_NOT_FOUND` that is reported, the cap pool being the earlier-declared of the two entities. The request opens in `PENDING` with `decision_round` at 1, and it immediately reserves its requested amount against the pool: the pool's `reserved_cents` rises by that amount, `available_cents` falls by the same amount and may go negative, `pool_status` may therefore flip to `OVERSUBSCRIBED`, and the pool's `version` increments by 1. A development may hold at most one cap allocation in `PENDING`, `AWARDED` or `COMMITTED` at any one time, and a second live request is `ERR_ALLOCATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`. That test is made only here, at creation: the revision described below does not repeat it, so a development whose earlier request was denied, a second request then created and awarded, can revise the denied one back to `PENDING` and end up holding two live allocations. A sponsor that is not `ACTIVE` cannot request cap at all.

An `HfaAllocationOfficer` decides a pending request with a `decision` field of `AWARD` or `DENY`. An award requires `awarded_amount_cents` and an `expiry_date` after which an unused award lapses — accepted exactly as supplied, with no requirement that it be later than the current clock instant, so an award may legitimately be stamped already lapsed, in which case no bond issue can be sized against it; a denial requires `denial_reason`. Both branches require `expected_cap_pool_version`, and both refuse a request whose current status is anything other than `PENDING` with `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`. An award additionally requires the owning sponsor's standing to be `ACTIVE` — a suspended or debarred sponsor's request cannot be awarded — and requires the awarded amount to be no greater than both the requested amount and the pool's `awardable_cents` as computed *before* this allocation leaves `PENDING`. An awarded amount above the awardable ceiling is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VOLUME_CAP`; equality is explicitly allowed and drives the pool straight to `EXHAUSTED`. A pool that is merely `OVERSUBSCRIBED` by pending requests does not block an award.

Awarding a request moves it `PENDING` → `AWARDED`, stamps `awarded_amount_cents`, `expiry_date` and `decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and re-derives the pool: `reserved_cents` drops by this allocation's `requested_amount_cents`, `committed_cents` rises by the `awarded_amount_cents`, `available_cents` and `awardable_cents` follow from those two, `pool_status` is re-derived, and the pool's `version` increments by 1. Denial moves the request `PENDING` → `DENIED`, stamps `denial_reason` and `decided_by_user_id`, and re-derives the pool with `reserved_cents` dropping by the requested amount and no corresponding rise in `committed_cents`; the pool's `version` still increments by 1.

Exhaustion is what makes an award interesting, because it cascades. If the re-derivation leaves the pool at `pool_status` `EXHAUSTED`, then every *other* allocation of the same pool that is still `PENDING` and whose `requested_amount_cents` exceeds the new `awardable_cents` is moved `PENDING` → `DEFERRED`. Each of those deferrals in turn increments its own development's `volume_cap_deferral_count` by 1 and sets that development's `financing_stalled` to true; and because the pool counts a request in `reserved_cents` only while it is `PENDING`, the pool's reserved figure drops by each deferred request's requested amount as well. A pending request whose requested amount is within the new awardable ceiling is left alone.

Three states — `DEFERRED`, `DENIED` and `RETURNED` — are all re-fileable. Revision is the sponsor's move and supplies a fresh `requested_amount_cents` and `requested_closing_date`. From any of those three states the allocation returns to `PENDING`, `decision_round` increments by 1, and `awarded_amount_cents`, `expiry_date`, `denial_reason`, `return_reason` and `decided_by_user_id` are all cleared to null. Coming back from `DEFERRED` additionally sets the development's `financing_stalled` to false. In every case the pool is re-derived so that `reserved_cents` rises by the **new** requested amount, and the pool's `version` increments by 1. The `PENDING` → `DENIED` → `PENDING` path is the negotiation loop with the agency and may repeat; `decision_round` records how many rounds have occurred.

Returning an award is the sponsor's way of giving cap back before it has been committed to a bond issue, and it takes a `return_reason`. It is permitted only from `AWARDED`, and only when no bond issue references the allocation with a `status` of `ISSUED`, `CONVERTED` or `MANDATORY_REDEEMED`. The return records the reason, clears `awarded_amount_cents` and `expiry_date` to null, and re-derives the pool: `committed_cents` falls by the returned award, `awardable_cents` and `available_cents` rise, `pool_status` is re-derived and the pool's `version` increments by 1. And because cap has just come back, the reverse cascade fires: if the re-derivation leaves `pool_status` at anything other than `EXHAUSTED`, every `DEFERRED` allocation of that pool returns to `PENDING`, and each of those reinstatements sets its own development's `financing_stalled` back to false.

Two more transitions out of `AWARDED` are driven by machinery rather than by a person. An award whose `expiry_date` the logical clock passes strictly moves `AWARDED` → `EXPIRED`, and the pool is re-derived so that `committed_cents` drops by the lapsed `awarded_amount_cents` and `pool_status` is re-derived; `EXPIRED` is terminal. Alternatively, when the development's bonds close, the award moves `AWARDED` → `COMMITTED` — the pool keeps counting the award in `committed_cents`, and the point of the state is that `COMMITTED` protects the award from clock expiry. Later, if the issue is mandatorily redeemed, the allocation moves `COMMITTED` → `RELEASED`, which is terminal: `committed_cents` falls by the `awarded_amount_cents`, `awardable_cents` rises, `pool_status` is re-derived, the pool's `version` increments by 1, and — exactly as on a return — if the pool is left at anything other than `EXHAUSTED`, every `DEFERRED` allocation of that pool returns to `PENDING` with each affected development's `financing_stalled` cleared to false.

### 3.4. Recording and Amending the Land-Use Restriction Agreement

The land-use restriction agreement is the instrument that actually binds the development, and it must exist and be recorded before the bonds can close. A `Developer` drafts it by naming the `project_id`, restating the `set_aside_election` the agreement will record, the `compliance_period_years` giving the qualified project period, the `extended_use_years` giving the total extended-use commitment, and the `recording_county` — the county register of deeds where the instrument will be filed. The agreement opens in `DRAFT` with `instrument_number` and `recording_date` null, `amendment_count` at zero, `amendment_reason` null, and both `compliance_period_end_date` and `extended_use_end_date` null. Nothing limits a development to a single agreement: no uniqueness rule and no state rule stops a second one from being drafted alongside an existing one, whatever state that one is in. Where more than one of a development's agreements has reached `RECORDED`, every rule that speaks of the development's recorded agreement — the issuance precondition of §3.5, the end-date stamping of §3.7 and the release cascade below — reads the earliest-created one.

Two structural rules govern the terms, and both are enforced at drafting and again at every amendment. The agreement's `set_aside_election` must equal the development's own election; a mismatch is `ERR_SET_ASIDE_MISMATCH`. And the term lengths must satisfy the statutory minimum: `compliance_period_years` must be at least 15, and `extended_use_years` must be at least 15 years longer than the compliance period — that is, at least the compliance period plus 15. Equality passes in both tests; anything shorter is `ERR_EXTENDED_USE_TOO_SHORT`. Neither year count carries a well-formedness floor of its own beyond being an integer, so a zero or negative term is not a malformed request but simply a term that is too short, and it too reports `ERR_EXTENDED_USE_TOO_SHORT` rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Recording is the `HfaComplianceOfficer`'s act and supplies the county's `instrument_number`. It is available from `DRAFT` and, after an amendment, from `AMENDMENT_PENDING`; attempting it from `RECORDED` or `RELEASED` is `ERR_REG_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Two substantive gates stand in front of it. The development's `set_aside_satisfied` must be true, evaluated under the *agreement's* set-aside election — and this is checked first, before the designation-count rule, because the covenant exists to protect tenants and a covenant recorded over a development that does not actually meet its election protects nobody; failure is `ERR_SET_ASIDE_NOT_SATISFIED`. Then the development's `designated_unit_count` must equal its `total_units`, so that every unit in the building carries a designation and no unrestricted unit can hide outside the covenant; under-designation is `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`.

A first recording moves the agreement `DRAFT` → `RECORDED`, stamps the supplied `instrument_number` and stamps `recording_date` from the logical clock. It also moves the development's compliance axis `UNRESTRICTED` → `RESTRICTED` and increments the development's `version` by 1 — but only while that axis still reads `UNRESTRICTED`; a first recording over a development whose axis has already moved on, a second agreement's being the obvious case, leaves the axis, the development's `updated_at` and its `version` all untouched. What it deliberately does *not* do is activate anything: the development's unit designations stay in `PROPOSED`, and `compliance_period_end_date`, `extended_use_end_date` and the development's mirrored `compliance_start_date`, `compliance_end_date` and `extended_use_end_date` all remain null. Those dates are unknowable at recording because they are measured from conversion, and they are stamped when the development reaches `STABILIZED` as described in §3.7.

Amending a recorded agreement is also the compliance officer's act. It requires a `amendment_reason` and optionally restates `compliance_period_years`, `extended_use_years` and `set_aside_election`. It is available only from `RECORDED`, and a restated `extended_use_years`, when supplied, must still be at least the `compliance_period_years` plus 15 — each restated value standing in for the stored one it replaces, so an amendment that restates neither term simply re-tests the stored pair. A restated `set_aside_election` must equal the development's own election exactly as at drafting, and a different value is `ERR_SET_ASIDE_MISMATCH`, checked before the term-length rule; an amendment can therefore never move the agreement onto an election the development does not itself carry. The amendment moves the agreement `RECORDED` → `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, stamps the reason, and applies the requested changes to the agreement's own fields. No amendment ever rewrites the development's own `set_aside_election`, and the development's `set_aside_ratio_bps` and `set_aside_satisfied` are always derived from the development's election rather than from any agreement's, so an amendment leaves both figures exactly as they were. Crucially the amendment is not yet in force: the development's `compliance_status` stays exactly where it was, and `amendment_count` is *not* incremented yet.

Re-recording is what completes the cycle. Recording an agreement that sits in `AMENDMENT_PENDING` moves it `AMENDMENT_PENDING` → `RECORDED`, overwrites `instrument_number` with the newly supplied value and `recording_date` with the current logical-clock instant, increments `amendment_count` by 1 — so the counter records completed amend-then-record cycles rather than opened amendments — and clears `amendment_reason` back to null. The same set-aside-satisfaction gate applies, now evaluated under the *amended* election. If the development has already reached stabilization and therefore already carries a `compliance_start_date`, both `compliance_period_end_date` and `extended_use_end_date` are re-derived from the amended term lengths — measured from that unchanged compliance start date — on the agreement and on the development's two mirrored copies alike, and the development's `version` increments by 1; a development not yet stabilized has no dates to refresh and is not touched at all. The `RECORDED` → `AMENDMENT_PENDING` → `RECORDED` cycle may repeat any number of times.

The agreement's fourth transition is not a human act at all. When the development's compliance axis reaches `RELEASED` at the end of the extended-use period, the recorded agreement moves `RECORDED` → `RELEASED`, which is terminal, and that release in turn moves every one of the development's `ACTIVE` unit designations to `RELEASED`, clearing each `restricted_rent_cents` back to null.

### 3.5. Sizing and Closing the Bond Issue

Sizing the issue is the sponsor's act and reads only two cost figures. The payload names the `project_id` and the `volume_cap_allocation_id` of the award that authorizes the par amount, and supplies `eligible_cost_cents` — bond-eligible development costs before capitalized interest, greater than zero — `capitalized_interest_cents` for construction-period interest funded from proceeds, which may be zero, and the two arbitrage yields: `bond_yield_bps`, the arbitrage yield on the bonds, greater than zero, and `investment_yield_bps`, the yield earned on undrawn proceeds sitting in the project fund, which may be zero. No draw request and no cost-certification figure participates in the sizing; the two cost inputs on this issue are the whole input set, and the solved result is frozen at creation and never re-solved afterwards. The named award is not required to belong to the named development: no mismatch error pairs the two, so an issue may be sized against a sibling development's award, or against another sponsor's, and is created normally. Nor is a development limited to one issue — further issues may be sized in `DRAFT` against the same award — though only the first of them can ever close, because closing moves that award to `COMMITTED` and every later attempt then fails the live-award test with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXPIRED`.

The sizing arithmetic has an apparent circularity, and the way out of it is pinned. The cost-of-issuance allowance is a percentage of par, and par includes the allowance — but the allowance is computed on **provisional par**, which excludes it, and is never re-solved against the final answer. Provisional par is the eligible cost plus the capitalized interest. The `issuance_allowance_cents` is two percent of provisional par, rounded half up to whole cents. Unrounded par is provisional par plus that allowance. Then `par_amount_cents` is unrounded par raised to the next whole authorized denomination of 500,000 cents — five thousand dollars — by dividing unrounded par by 500,000, rounding that quotient **up** to a whole number, and multiplying back by 500,000; this step never lowers par. Finally `negative_arbitrage_allowance_cents` is 200 basis points of the final `par_amount_cents` — two percent of par — with the quotient floored to whole cents.

Because the denomination step rounds par up, a deal that fits its award before rounding can fail after it, and that is exactly what the next check catches: the solved `par_amount_cents` must be no greater than the referenced allocation's `awarded_amount_cents`, and an overshoot is `ERR_PAR_EXCEEDS_CAP_AWARD`. The referenced allocation must also be `AWARDED` with its `expiry_date` strictly after the current logical clock, and an award already lapsed at the clock instant is `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXPIRED`. The sponsor's standing must be `ACTIVE`. On success the issue opens in `DRAFT` with `closing_date`, `conversion_date`, `redemption_date`, `dscr_bps`, `dscr_basis` and `min_occupancy_bps` all null, `operating_months` an empty list, `cumulative_funded_cents` at zero, `negative_arbitrage_cents` at zero and `undrawn_cents` equal to the full `par_amount_cents`.

Closing the issue belongs to the `BondTrustee`, who supplies the `closing_date`. Four preconditions guard the transition beyond the sponsor's standing being `ACTIVE`: the issue must be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is `ERR_BOND_ISSUE_STATE_INVALID`; the development must have a regulatory agreement in `RECORDED`, and neither `DRAFT` nor `AMENDMENT_PENDING` nor `RELEASED` will do, or the attempt is `ERR_REG_AGREEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`; the referenced allocation must still be `AWARDED` with the solved par no greater than its awarded amount and with the supplied `closing_date` no later than its `expiry_date`; and the development's `set_aside_satisfied` must still be true, or the attempt is `ERR_SET_ASIDE_NOT_SATISFIED`. The supplied `closing_date` is compared against the award's `expiry_date` and against nothing else: the logical clock does not constrain it, so a closing date already in the past, or one before the issue was even sized, is accepted.

Closing then runs the **provisional variant of the fifty-percent aggregate basis test**, which is the gate on the automatic housing credit. The ratio is the issue's `par_amount_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by the development's `aggregate_basis_cents` — the land cost plus the depreciable basis as filed at creation — with the quotient floored to a whole basis point. The test passes when that ratio is at least 5,000 basis points; the passing side is inclusive, so exactly 5,000 basis points triggers the credit. A ratio below 5,000 basis points refuses the closing outright with `ERR_FIFTY_PERCENT_TEST_FAILED`, on the reasoning that a bond issue that cannot trigger the automatic credit would strand the development.

A successful closing therefore does five things at once. It moves the issue `DRAFT` → `ISSUED` and stamps `closing_date`. It writes the computed ratio to the development's `provisional_fifty_percent_ratio_bps` and sets `automatic_credit_triggered` to whether that ratio reached 5,000 basis points. It moves the development's development axis `PLANNING` → `FINANCED`. It moves the referenced cap allocation `AWARDED` → `COMMITTED`, which re-derives the pool's `reserved_cents`, `committed_cents`, `available_cents`, `awardable_cents` and `pool_status`. And it increments both the development's `version` and the issue's `version` by 1. Only the axis move is conditional: a closing whose development is not in `PLANNING` leaves that axis exactly where it stands, while still rewriting `provisional_fifty_percent_ratio_bps` and `automatic_credit_triggered` and still incrementing the development's `version`.

Mandatory redemption is the trustee's unwind and the branch sibling of conversion, with a deliberately disjoint set of effects: where conversion starts the compliance clock, redemption dismantles the financing. It takes a `redemption_date` and a `reason`, and is available only from `ISSUED`. It stamps `redemption_date` and moves the issue `ISSUED` → `MANDATORY_REDEEMED`, which is terminal. Every draw request of that issue sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` is moved to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` recorded as `bond issue redeemed`. The development moves to `WITHDRAWN` — from `FINANCED`, from `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` or from `PLACED_IN_SERVICE`, with the identical effect set in each case — its `automatic_credit_triggered` is set back to false and its `provisional_fifty_percent_ratio_bps` is cleared to null. The referenced allocation moves `COMMITTED` → `RELEASED`, the pool is re-derived so that `committed_cents` falls by the `awarded_amount_cents`, and deferred allocations of that pool may consequently be reinstated exactly as described in §3.3. Both the development's `version` and the pool's `version` increment by 1.

### 3.6. Construction Draws and Negative Arbitrage Management

Construction proceeds leave the project fund one draw at a time, and each draw covers a half-open funding period. A sponsor submits one by naming the `bond_issue_id`, a sponsor-assigned `draw_number` greater than zero — expected to be unique within the issue, but unenforced: no error in §5 reports a repeat, so a duplicate number is accepted, and the number's only real work is as the tie-break in the orderings described below — the inclusive `period_start` and the exclusive `period_end` of the construction period, the `requested_amount_cents` greater than zero, and `expected_bond_issue_version`. The draw opens in `SUBMITTED` with `approved_amount_cents`, `funding_date`, `rejection_reason` and `void_reason` all null and `negative_arbitrage_accrual_cents` at zero, and the issue's `version` increments by 1.

Periods may not collide. A new draw's half-open interval, running from its `period_start` inclusive to its `period_end` exclusive, must not strictly overlap the half-open interval of any existing draw of the same issue whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `FUNDED`; a collision is `ERR_DRAW_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Touching endpoints are explicitly fine — a draw that starts exactly when another one ends is accepted — and draws that have reached `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` are ignored entirely, so their intervals become reusable. The requested amount must also fit inside the issue's `undrawn_cents` at that moment, and an overshoot is `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_UNDRAWN`. A sponsor that is not `ACTIVE` cannot submit a draw.

The trustee decides a submitted draw with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. Approval requires `approved_amount_cents`, which may legitimately be less than the sponsor requested but may exceed neither the `requested_amount_cents` nor the issue's `undrawn_cents` — the same `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_UNDRAWN` guards this endpoint too. Rejection requires a `rejection_reason`. Both branches require the draw to be in `SUBMITTED` and the issue to be in `ISSUED`, in that order: the draw's own state is tested first, so a draw that has left `SUBMITTED` reports `ERR_DRAW_STATE_INVALID` even when the issue has also left `ISSUED`, as a `FUNDED` draw on a mandatorily redeemed issue has. The same precedence holds at funding and at voiding. Approval moves the draw `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` and stamps the approved amount; rejection moves it `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED`, which is terminal, stamps the reason and frees the draw's funding period so a later draw may occupy the interval. A rejected draw leaves the issue's `undrawn_cents` completely untouched.

Funding is where the money actually moves and where the arbitrage discipline bites. The trustee supplies a `funding_date`; the draw must be in `APPROVED` and the issue in `ISSUED`. That date is recorded and nothing more: it enters no calculation, it need not fall inside the draw's own funding period, and it is not compared against the logical clock. Because undrawn proceeds earn less than the bonds cost, holding them is a real loss, and the indenture caps that loss. The **negative-arbitrage series** is computed over the issue's `FUNDED` draws as a chronological walk. First the `spread_bps` is the larger of zero and the issue's `bond_yield_bps` less its `investment_yield_bps`. Then the funded draws are ordered by `period_start` ascending with ties broken by `draw_number` ascending — never by `id` and never by `created_at`. Walking that list with a running total of proceeds already drawn, each draw's accrual is computed from the undrawn balance *before* that draw, which is the issue's `par_amount_cents` less the running total: that undrawn balance is multiplied by the spread in basis points, divided by 10,000, multiplied by the number of whole days in the draw's half-open period from `period_start` up to but not including `period_end`, and divided by a 360-day year. Each period's accrual is **rounded half up to whole cents and stored on that draw as `negative_arbitrage_accrual_cents` before it enters the sum**; only then is it added to the total and the draw's `approved_amount_cents` added to the running total. The issue's `negative_arbitrage_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-period accruals. This ordering is pinned: summing unrounded accruals and rounding once at the end is a different and wrong answer, and it can differ by a cent per period.

A funding attempt projects that series forward to include the draw being funded, and refuses when the projected `negative_arbitrage_cents` would exceed the issue's `negative_arbitrage_allowance_cents`. Equality is allowed; only a strict overshoot is `ERR_NEGATIVE_ARBITRAGE_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. The approved amount must also still fit the issue's `undrawn_cents` at funding time — several approved-but-unfunded draws can together exceed the undrawn balance precisely because approval does not consume it, and the excess is caught here as `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_UNDRAWN`.

A successful funding moves the draw `APPROVED` → `FUNDED` and stamps `funding_date`. The issue's `cumulative_funded_cents` rises by the approved amount and `undrawn_cents` falls by the same amount, so that par continues to equal funded plus undrawn. The whole negative-arbitrage series is re-run over the funded draws, rewriting the issue's `negative_arbitrage_cents` and each funded draw's `negative_arbitrage_accrual_cents`, and the issue's `version` increments by 1. Wherever that series is re-run — at a funding and at a void alike — a funded draw whose recomputed accrual is identical to the figure already stored on it is left entirely alone, its `updated_at` and `version` unmoved; only the draws whose accrual actually changes are re-stamped. If this is the first funded draw on the issue — that is, the issue's `cumulative_funded_cents` was zero immediately before — then the development also moves `FINANCED` → `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, with no other field change on the development beyond that status, its `updated_at` and its `version`.

Voiding is the compensating action for funding, used when proceeds are returned, and it takes a `void_reason`. The draw must be in `FUNDED` and the issue in `ISSUED`. The void moves the draw `FUNDED` → `VOIDED`, which is terminal, stamps the reason, clears `funding_date` back to null and zeroes the draw's `negative_arbitrage_accrual_cents`. The issue's `cumulative_funded_cents` falls by the approved amount and `undrawn_cents` rises by the same amount. Then the entire negative-arbitrage series is recomputed over the **surviving** funded draws only: a voided draw leaves the input set altogether rather than having its accrual subtracted, which means every later draw's accrual is recomputed against restored — and therefore larger — undrawn balances. The issue's `version` increments by 1, and afterwards par must once again equal `cumulative_funded_cents` plus `undrawn_cents`.

### 3.7. Placement in Service, Conversion and Stabilization

Once the building starts operating, the sponsor reports its performance one month at a time onto the issued bond. Each report supplies a `month_index`, the month's net operating income as `noi_cents`, its `debt_service_cents`, and the count of `occupied_units`. The series is append-only, is held on the issue as `operating_months` ordered by `month_index` ascending, and must stay contiguous from 1: the supplied `month_index` must be exactly one more than the number of months already recorded. A repeat of an index already present, or an index that is not exactly one past the last, is `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATING_MONTH`. Each recorded month increments the issue's `version` by 1. The three figures are bounded only by being non-negative integers: `noi_cents` and `debt_service_cents` may each be zero, and nothing caps `occupied_units` at the development's `total_units`, so a month may report more occupied units than the development has and its occupancy ratio then reads above 10,000 basis points.

The very first recorded month is a milestone. When the issue's `operating_months` was empty immediately beforehand, recording that month moves the development `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` → `PLACED_IN_SERVICE` and stamps `placed_in_service_date` from the logical clock at the moment the month is recorded. Later months change no development state. The milestone is conditional on the axis actually reading `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`: on a development still in `FINANCED` because no draw has yet been funded, that first month is recorded normally but the development is left untouched, and because the milestone belongs to the first month alone no later month will stamp `placed_in_service_date` either.

Conversion from construction financing to permanent financing is the trustee's act and the moment the deal is judged. The request supplies a `conversion_date` and a `dscr_basis` naming which coverage test to run, either `T3` or `T12`. The issue must be in `ISSUED`. The `conversion_date` is taken exactly as supplied and is compared against nothing — not the logical clock, not the closing date, not the operating months already recorded — even though it becomes the development's `compliance_start_date` verbatim and every downstream compliance date is measured from it. The **debt service coverage ratio** is then computed over a window of the recorded operating months: the months are taken in `month_index` ascending order and the window is the last entries of that ordering by `month_index`. Under `T3` the window is the three highest month indices and the annualization factor is 4 — a trailing quarter, annualized — so a strong lease-up quarter can pass even when the trailing year would fail. Under `T12` the window is the twelve highest month indices and the annualization factor is 1 — the trailing year, actual — so weak early months drag both the coverage ratio and the occupancy floor down. Entries outside the selected window are ignored entirely.

Within the window, annualized net operating income is the summed `noi_cents` of the window's months multiplied by the annualization factor, and annualized debt service is the summed `debt_service_cents` of the same months multiplied by the same factor. The `dscr_bps` is annualized net operating income multiplied by 10,000, divided by annualized debt service, floored to a whole basis point. Because `debt_service_cents` is allowed to be zero in every month, that divisor can itself be zero; the quotient is then defined to be 0 rather than infinite, which means a window carrying no debt service at all always *fails* the coverage threshold below however large its net operating income. The `min_occupancy_bps` is computed per month and then minimized: for each month in the window, the month's `occupied_units` multiplied by 10,000 divided by the development's `total_units`, floored to a whole basis point, and the smallest of those monthly figures is taken.

Three refusals guard the conversion, in this order. If fewer operating months have been recorded than the requested window needs — fewer than three for `T3`, fewer than twelve for `T12` — the attempt is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_OPERATING_HISTORY`, and this is checked before either ratio. Then coverage: `dscr_bps` must be at least 11,500 basis points, which is 115 percent of debt service, and a shortfall is `ERR_DSCR_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Then stabilization: `min_occupancy_bps` must be at least 9,000 basis points, which is ninety percent physical occupancy in the weakest month of the window, and a shortfall is `ERR_OCCUPANCY_BELOW_STABILIZATION`. Both thresholds are inclusive — exactly 11,500 basis points of coverage and exactly 9,000 basis points of occupancy pass — and when both fail, the coverage failure is the one reported.

A successful conversion stamps `conversion_date`, the chosen `dscr_basis`, the computed `dscr_bps` and `min_occupancy_bps` onto the issue, and moves it `ISSUED` → `CONVERTED`, which is terminal. From there a three-level cascade unrolls — but only when the development's axis actually reads `PLACED_IN_SERVICE`; a conversion on a development that never reached it converts the issue alone and leaves the development, its agreement and its unit designations entirely untouched. The development moves `PLACED_IN_SERVICE` → `STABILIZED` and its `compliance_start_date` is stamped equal to the issue's `conversion_date`. That development transition in turn causes the development's `RECORDED` regulatory agreement to stamp its own two end dates — `compliance_period_end_date` becomes the compliance start date plus the agreement's `compliance_period_years`, and `extended_use_end_date` becomes the compliance start date plus its `extended_use_years` — and those two values are mirrored onto the development as `compliance_end_date` and `extended_use_end_date` respectively. Both additions are calendar-year additions: they advance the year and keep the compliance start date's month, day and time of day untouched, with exactly one adjustment — a start date of 29 February whose target year is not a leap year clamps its day down to 28 February. And because the server computes these two instants rather than echoing a client string, both are emitted in the canonical three-fractional-digit form of §2.4 even when the `conversion_date` that seeded `compliance_start_date` verbatim was supplied with a different number of fractional digits or with a lowercase `t` or `z`. The identical addition and the identical leap-year clamp produce the re-derived end dates of §3.4. Unit activation is the third level, but it does not in fact depend on that stamping having happened: whether or not a `RECORDED` agreement is found — and none is found when the development's agreement happens to sit in `AMENDMENT_PENDING` at the instant of conversion, in which case all four end-date fields simply stay null while `compliance_start_date` is stamped anyway — every one of the development's `PROPOSED` unit designations moves to `ACTIVE` and `restricted_rent_cents` is refreshed on the `RESTRICTED` ones under the ceiling rules of §3.2, while `MARKET` and `EMPLOYEE_UNIT` units keep `restricted_rent_cents` null. The development's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Cost Certification, Final Allocation and Credit Delivery

The audited cost certification is where the fifty-percent test gets its second and binding answer. A sponsor drafts one by naming the `project_id` and supplying the audited `certified_land_cost_cents` (zero or more), the audited `certified_depreciable_basis_cents` (greater than zero), the audited `certified_eligible_basis_cents` (greater than zero and no greater than the certified depreciable basis, since eligible basis is a subset of depreciable basis and never includes land), and the certifying public accountant as `preparer_name`. At drafting that ceiling is an ordinary payload check in the ordinary position, so a request that both names an unresolvable `project_id` and certifies eligible basis above depreciable basis reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`. The server solves `certified_aggregate_basis_cents` as the certified land cost plus the certified depreciable basis. The certification opens in `DRAFT` with `submission_round` at zero, `deficiency_notes` null, `reviewed_by_user_id` null and `accepted_at` null.

Submitting the certification for agency review is available from `DRAFT` and from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, requires the development's `development_status` to be `PLACED_IN_SERVICE` or `STABILIZED`, and requires the sponsor's standing to be `ACTIVE`. The submission may optionally restate any of `certified_land_cost_cents`, `certified_depreciable_basis_cents` or `certified_eligible_basis_cents`; when any of them is restated, `certified_aggregate_basis_cents` is recomputed. Each submission increments `submission_round` by 1 and clears `deficiency_notes` to null — which is why a certification that was returned once and resubmitted reads `submission_round` 2. The eligible-basis ceiling is re-tested on every submission with each restated value standing in for the stored one it replaces, so an omitted figure is tested at its stored value and a restatement that would push eligible basis above depreciable basis is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; that comparison ranks with the payload checks but is evaluated after the certification's own 404 and before the sponsor-standing and state rules. `reviewed_by_user_id` is deliberately *not* cleared by a resubmission, so the officer who returned the certification stays recorded on it until the next review overwrites the field.

Review belongs to the `HfaAllocationOfficer` and takes a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` with optional `deficiency_notes`; either branch requires the certification to be in `SUBMITTED`. A return moves it `SUBMITTED` → `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, stamps the supplied `deficiency_notes` and stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and leaves the development's `final_fifty_percent_ratio_bps` untouched; the `SUBMITTED` → `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` → `SUBMITTED` loop is the audit revision cycle and `submission_round` records how many passes occurred. An acceptance moves it `SUBMITTED` → `ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock and stamps `reviewed_by_user_id`. Acceptance writes `deficiency_notes` from the payload exactly as a return does, so notes supplied alongside an acceptance are recorded on the accepted certification, and an acceptance that omits the field clears whatever text an earlier return had left there.

Acceptance also runs the **final variant of the fifty-percent aggregate basis test**, and this variant reads a different numerator and a different denominator than the provisional one did over the same development. Its numerator is the summed `approved_amount_cents` of the `FUNDED` draws of **every** bond issue the development holds — not merely the one that closed, and with no regard to what state each issue now sits in, so the funded draws of an issue that was later mandatorily redeemed still count, and the funded draws of two sibling issues add together. Draws that reached `VOIDED` or `REJECTED`, and draws still merely `APPROVED` or `SUBMITTED`, are all excluded, so proceeds that were authorized but never funded do not count. Its denominator is this certification's `certified_aggregate_basis_cents`. The ratio is the numerator multiplied by 10,000, divided by the denominator, floored to a whole basis point, and it is written to the development's `final_fifty_percent_ratio_bps`. The development's `automatic_credit_triggered` is then set to whether that final ratio is at least 5,000 basis points, **overwriting** whatever the provisional variant concluded: a development whose provisional test passed can lose its credit trigger here, and one whose provisional test barely passed can be confirmed. The development's `version` increments by 1.

Computing the final credit allocation is the allocation officer's act. The payload names the `project_id`, the `cost_certification_id` of the accepted certification the allocation is computed from, the applicable credit percentage as `credit_percentage_bps` (greater than zero and no greater than 1,000 basis points, that is ten percent), and the `placed_in_service_date` certified on the allocation. Four gates apply in order: the development must be `PLACED_IN_SERVICE` or `STABILIZED`, or the attempt is `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_PLACED_IN_SERVICE`; the referenced certification's `status` must be `ACCEPTED`, or `ERR_COST_CERT_NOT_ACCEPTED`; that certification's own `project_id` must equal the request's `project_id`, or `ERR_COST_CERT_PROJECT_MISMATCH`; and the development may hold at most one credit allocation whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ISSUED` or `RECAPTURE_PENDING`, a second being `ERR_ALLOCATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`.

The **qualified basis** is then solved unit by unit, and the rounding order is pinned. The development's `RESTRICTED` unit designations are taken in `unit_number` ascending order — any status counts, and the order is irrelevant to the sum but fixed for reproducibility. For each such unit, the certification's `certified_eligible_basis_cents` is multiplied by that unit's `floor_space_sqft`, divided by the development's `total_floor_space_sqft`, and **floored to whole cents for that unit**; `qualified_basis_cents` is the sum of those already-floored per-unit figures. Summing the floor areas first and performing a single division instead is a different and wrong answer. Note that the eligible basis used here is always the accepted certification's audited figure, never the development's filed `depreciable_basis_cents`. The allocation also snapshots the development's current `applicable_fraction_bps` onto itself. The `annual_credit_cents` is the qualified basis multiplied by `credit_percentage_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents. The `total_credit_cents` is the annual credit multiplied by the ten-year credit period. At creation `delivered_credit_cents` is zero and `undelivered_credit_cents` equals the total credit, `recapture_amount_cents` is zero, `form_8609_number` is null, the creating officer's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `issued_by_user_id`, and the allocation opens in `PENDING`.

Issuing the allocation certificate makes the credit deliverable and takes a `form_8609_number`. The allocation must be in `PENDING`; the development's `automatic_credit_triggered` must be true at that instant, so a development whose provisional test passed but whose final test fell below 5,000 basis points is refused with `ERR_FIFTY_PERCENT_TEST_FAILED`; and the segregation-of-duties comparison of §2.10 must hold. That comparison reads exactly two recorded identities, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from both. The certification side is the `reviewed_by_user_id` of the very certification this allocation was computed from. The cap-award side is the `decided_by_user_id` of the earliest-created cap allocation of the development that carries a recorded decider *and* whose status is `AWARDED`, `COMMITTED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` — so an officer whose only decision on the development was a denial, a deferral or an award the sponsor has since returned is not blocked, and where a development has more than one once-awarded allocation only the earliest-created one's officer is. Issuance moves the allocation `PENDING` → `ISSUED`, stamps `form_8609_number`, stamps `issued_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and increments the allocation's `version` by 1.

Delivery to investors runs as a waterfall in creation order. The target allocation must be in a state that admits one, and exactly two do: `ISSUED` and `RECAPTURE_PENDING` — credit may therefore still be syndicated while a recapture is pending against the allocation — whereas an allocation still in `PENDING` or already terminated at `RECAPTURED` refuses the create with `ERR_CREDIT_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`, tested after the version guard of §2.6 and before the remainder test below. A sponsor creates one by naming the `credit_allocation_id` and the `investor_id` taking delivery, the `requested_credit_cents` the syndication agreement asks for (greater than zero), the `capital_contribution_cents` of equity paid for the credit (greater than zero), and `expected_credit_allocation_version`. The undelivered remainder is computed as the allocation's `total_credit_cents` less the summed `delivered_credit_cents` of its deliveries whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `RECAPTURE_FLAGGED`; deliveries that reached `RESCINDED` are excluded and their credit is back in the pool. When that remainder is already zero the create is refused with `ERR_NO_UNDELIVERED_CREDIT`. A positive but insufficient remainder is emphatically **not** an error: `delivered_credit_cents` is the smaller of the requested credit and the remainder, so an over-ask is clamped down rather than rejected, and the last delivery that fits absorbs whatever is left. The `price_per_credit_dollar_bps` is then the `capital_contribution_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by the clamped `delivered_credit_cents`, floored — which is precisely why a stale allocation version is refused rather than re-priced. The delivery opens in `DELIVERED` with `accepted_at` and `rescind_reason` null; the allocation's `delivered_credit_cents` rises and `undelivered_credit_cents` falls by the delivered amount, and the allocation's `version` increments by 1.

The investor's own move is acceptance, and it is confined to deliveries whose `investor_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`. Acceptance is available only from `DELIVERED`; it moves the delivery `DELIVERED` → `ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock, raises the investor's `total_credit_accepted_cents` by the delivered amount and increments the investor's `version` by 1. Accepting a delivery that has already been rescinded, or accepting twice, is `ERR_DELIVERY_STATE_INVALID`.

Rescission is the sponsor's compensating action and takes a `rescind_reason`. It is available from `DELIVERED` and from `ACCEPTED`, and both branches behave identically toward the allocation: the delivery moves to `RESCINDED`, which is terminal, the reason is stamped, the allocation's `delivered_credit_cents` falls and its `undelivered_credit_cents` rises by the delivered amount, and the allocation's `version` increments by 1 — so that total credit once again equals delivered plus undelivered. The one asymmetry is the investor ledger: rescinding a delivery that had reached `ACCEPTED` also lowers that investor's `total_credit_accepted_cents` by the delivered amount, whereas rescinding one still in `DELIVERED` leaves the investor ledger alone, because it was never counted there.

### 3.9. Ongoing Compliance, Recapture, Inspections and the Passage of Time

For as long as the covenant runs, the sponsor files one owner compliance certification per calendar year. The filing names the `project_id`, the `reporting_year` covered, the `restricted_units_occupied` by income-qualified households at year end (zero or more), the `total_units_occupied` across all unit types at year end, and the `average_ami_percent` across occupied restricted units. Coherence is checked as a chain: `restricted_units_occupied` must not exceed `total_units_occupied`, which must not exceed the development's `total_units`, and `average_ami_percent` must lie between 1 and 80 inclusive; any breach is `ERR_REPORT_COUNTS_INCOHERENT`, with one exception at the bottom end — a *negative* `average_ami_percent` is caught a band earlier as a malformed payload and reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while zero and any value above 80 reach the coherence rule and report `ERR_REPORT_COUNTS_INCOHERENT`. At most one report may exist for a given development and `reporting_year` in any status, and the year must fall inside the calendar years spanned by the development's `compliance_start_date` through its `extended_use_end_date` inclusive; a duplicate year or an out-of-span year is `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLIANCE_REPORT`. A development that has not yet stabilized carries neither of those two dates and therefore has no span at all to test a year against; it reports that same `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLIANCE_REPORT` for any `reporting_year` whatever, so the earliest a development can file its first annual report is after the conversion of §3.7 has stamped its compliance dates.

Two figures are derived at filing. The `achieved_set_aside_bps` is the `restricted_units_occupied` multiplied by 10,000, divided by the development's `total_units`, floored to a whole basis point. The `shortfall_bps` is the larger of zero and the required threshold less the achieved ratio, where the required threshold is 2,000 basis points when the development's `set_aside_election` is `MIN_20_50` and 4,000 basis points under either of the other two elections. The report opens in `SUBMITTED` with `finding_notes` and `reviewed_by_user_id` null.

The compliance officer reviews the report with a `finding` of `CLEARED` or `NONCOMPLIANCE` and optional `finding_notes`, and the report must be in `SUBMITTED` — reviewing twice is `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. Which branch is admissible is decided by the computed shortfall rather than by the reviewer's preference: `CLEARED` requires `shortfall_bps` to be exactly zero, and `NONCOMPLIANCE` requires `shortfall_bps` to be greater than zero. Either branch stamps `finding_notes` and `reviewed_by_user_id`, and both are terminal for the report: a cleared report lands in the status `CLEARED`, a noncompliance finding in the status `NONCOMPLIANCE_FOUND`.

A `NONCOMPLIANCE` finding cascades three levels deep. The report moves `SUBMITTED` → `NONCOMPLIANCE_FOUND`, and the development's compliance axis moves `RESTRICTED` → `NONCOMPLIANT`. That development transition moves every credit allocation of the development sitting in `ISSUED` to `RECAPTURE_PENDING` and stamps its `recapture_amount_cents`: the **recapture exposure** is the allocation's `annual_credit_cents` multiplied by the `shortfall_bps` of the single report that triggered the finding, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents — earlier reports do not accumulate into it. That allocation transition in turn moves every `ACCEPTED` delivery of the allocation to `RECAPTURE_FLAGGED`; a flagged delivery keeps counting toward the allocation's `delivered_credit_cents` while flagged. The development's `version` and the allocation's `version` each increment by 1 when the cascade fires.

The cure runs the identical chain backwards. A later report that clears — one whose `reporting_year` is strictly greater than the highest `reporting_year` among *all* of the development's reports that have reached `NONCOMPLIANCE_FOUND`, which is the comparison used even where the finding that actually moved the axis happened to cover an earlier year than some other finding — moves the development `NONCOMPLIANT` → `RESTRICTED`, which returns every `RECAPTURE_PENDING` credit allocation of the development to `ISSUED` with `recapture_amount_cents` reset to zero, which in turn returns every `RECAPTURE_FLAGGED` delivery of that allocation to `ACCEPTED` with no field change beyond its status, `updated_at` and `version`. This `RESTRICTED` → `NONCOMPLIANT` → `RESTRICTED` cure cycle may recur, at most once per `reporting_year`.

What ends the cure is a second consecutive finding. When a `NONCOMPLIANCE` finding arrives while the development's credit allocation is already in `RECAPTURE_PENDING`, that allocation moves `RECAPTURE_PENDING` → `RECAPTURED`, which is terminal: the `recapture_amount_cents` already stamped is retained as the final recaptured amount — it is emphatically not re-stamped against the second report's `shortfall_bps` — and no further delivery may be created against the allocation. That branch then stops: the development's compliance axis is already `NONCOMPLIANT` and is left there, and the allocation's deliveries keep the `RECAPTURE_FLAGGED` status the first finding gave them rather than being visited a second time.

Physical inspections are scheduled by the compliance officer, who names the `project_id`, the inclusive `window_start`, the inclusive `window_end` and the assigned `inspector_name`. Inspection windows are **closed** intervals, and unlike draw periods, adjacency is a conflict here. A new window, expanded by fourteen days on each side, must not intersect the unexpanded closed window of any existing inspection of the same development whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; a window that has lapsed into `MISSED` likewise continues to reserve the same fourteen-day buffer against an overlapping replacement. Two separated windows therefore need a real gap of more than fourteen days between them, and a violation is `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The window must also lie wholly inside the compliance period: `window_start` at or after the development's `compliance_start_date` and `window_end` at or before its `extended_use_end_date`, both bounds inclusive; otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_OUTSIDE_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD`. A development that has not yet stabilized carries neither bound and so has no period any window could lie inside: every attempt to schedule an inspection against it reports that same `ERR_INSPECTION_OUTSIDE_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD`, though only after the buffer test above, which a development holding no inspections yet passes vacuously. The inspection opens in `SCHEDULED` with `completed_date`, `score`, `deficiency_count` and `next_inspection_due_date` all null.

Recording a result requires the inspection to be in `SCHEDULED` — completing one that is already `COMPLETED` or has lapsed to `MISSED` is `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID` — and supplies the `completed_date`, the `score` out of 100 and the `deficiency_count`. The completed date must fall inside the scheduled window, at or after `window_start` and at or before `window_end`; a date outside it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, but tested after the inspection's own state rule rather than with the payload checks, so completing an inspection that has already lapsed to `MISSED` with an out-of-window date still reports `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`. Completion moves the inspection `SCHEDULED` → `COMPLETED`, which is terminal, stamps all three supplied values, and sets `next_inspection_due_date` to the `completed_date` plus 1,095 days — a three-year inspection cycle.

Finally, several transitions in this domain are driven purely by time, and the only way time moves is a `SystemOperator` setting the logical clock to a new `current_utc`. A single clock advance evaluates every time-gated transition in one pass. The new value need not be later than the current one: a `current_utc` that moves the clock backwards, or that repeats the instant it already holds, is accepted, runs the same single pass and reverses nothing, because every transition below is triggered by a deadline that now lies strictly in the past — so a backward move simply finds nothing to do, and no error reports a non-monotonic clock. Every cap allocation still in `AWARDED` whose `expiry_date` now lies strictly in the past moves `AWARDED` → `EXPIRED`, with the pool re-derivation described in §3.3. Every inspection still in `SCHEDULED` whose `window_end` now lies strictly in the past moves `SCHEDULED` → `MISSED`; a missed inspection's `next_inspection_due_date` stays null. And the compliance axis rolls over: a development in `RESTRICTED` whose `compliance_end_date` is not null and which the new clock value has passed strictly moves `RESTRICTED` → `EXTENDED_USE`, with no unit status change at all, because the restrictions simply continue under the extended-use commitment. A development in `EXTENDED_USE` whose `extended_use_end_date` is not null and which the new clock value has passed strictly moves `EXTENDED_USE` → `RELEASED`, and on that second step the cascade of §3.4 fires: the development's `RECORDED` regulatory agreement moves to `RELEASED`, and that release moves every `ACTIVE` unit designation of the development to `RELEASED` with its `restricted_rent_cents` cleared to null. Those two rollover steps are evaluated one after the other within the same pass and the second reads the status the first has just written, so a single large clock advance that passes both end dates at once carries a development the whole way from `RESTRICTED` through `EXTENDED_USE` to `RELEASED` in one call — firing the release cascade and incrementing the development's `version` twice.
