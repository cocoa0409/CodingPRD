# Private Fleet Backhaul Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A retail private fleet is the most expensive logistics asset a chain owns and the least forgiving to schedule. Every week the merchandising organisation hands transportation a pile of store replenishment requirements, each with a case count, a temperature class and a receiving window that a store crew of finite size must be able to absorb across a finite number of dock doors. Transportation must turn that demand into tractor-and-trailer trips staffed by drivers whose legal driving hours, mandated rest between duty periods and home-domicile assignment all constrain what is even feasible — and it must do so while the shop is holding equipment for preventive maintenance and the safety department is pulling drivers off the board for coaching. The Private Fleet Backhaul Operations API is the system of record for that entire negotiation.

The commercial case for a private fleet, however, is not made on the outbound leg. It is made on the way home. Vendor pickups and third-party brokered freight turn empty miles into revenue miles, and the decision to take a backhaul is a contribution-margin decision: linehaul revenue less the incremental variable cost of the miles, the cases handled and any reefer surcharge, less a brokerage commission whose rate itself moves with the margin tier. The system prices every offer, enforces a contribution-margin floor that only a second operator can waive, and credits the accepted margin back against the domicile that ran the trip — so that when a transportation manager compares fleet cost per case and cost per mile against a dedicated-carriage quote, the comparison includes the backhaul benefit rather than ignoring it.

Driver economics sit on the same Trip row. Pay is a four-component structure — mileage, stop and drop-hook activity, hourly detention beyond plan, and a backhaul bonus — floored by a daily guarantee, and it is computed twice: once as a planning accrual when the schedule is committed, and once for real at close-out on the driver's actual minutes. The pay plan that governs a trip is pinned at plan time, so a later rate amendment never silently re-prices work already scheduled. Around all of this the API also runs the human side of a fleet: seniority-ranked route bidding, telematics penalty points that accumulate into a coaching hold, and a domicile balance index that decides whether one terminal may borrow a driver from another. This document specifies the complete contract.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API surface, which is mounted under `/fleet`. Where §3 describes what a business operation means, this section fixes the mechanics that every operation shares: who is calling, what a response looks like, how numbers are rounded, and in what order the server voices its objections.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it is acting. If `X-User-Role` is missing, empty, or carries a value outside the six roles below, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before any path parameter is resolved, so a request with a bad role against a completely fictional resource id still returns 401 rather than a 404. Once the role is recognised, it must appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called; if it does not, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and again this happens before existence is tested, so a role with no business at an endpoint learns nothing about whether the addressed row exists.

The six recognised roles are `FleetPlanner`, `TransportManager`, `Driver`, `PayrollAnalyst`, `SafetyManager`, and `MaintenanceCoordinator`.

A `FleetPlanner` builds the schedule: it registers stores, domiciles and replenishment orders, opens trips, appends and removes stops, commits the plan, and sources backhaul offers. A `TransportManager` owns execution and the commercial decisions: store suspension, driver hiring and termination, duty-log corrections, equipment and driver assignment, dispatch, backhaul accept/reject and the expiration sweep, the route board, and the cost benchmark. A `PayrollAnalyst` owns pay structure and money: pay-plan creation and amendment, pay-plan assignment, statement settlement and reversal, and trip re-accrual. A `SafetyManager` owns telematics and coaching and is the only role that may charge or retire penalty points. A `MaintenanceCoordinator` owns the equipment roster and the shop schedule and may never assign equipment to a trip — it can only make equipment available or unavailable through the shop lifecycle. A `Driver` executes the work: it bids on route postings and withdraws its own bids, checks in and out of its own stops, closes out its own trip, and may open a settlement dispute on its own pay.

When `X-User-Role` is `Driver`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Driver` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Driver` compare that resource's driver reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: a `Driver` closing out a trip, arriving at a stop or completing a stop must be the trip's own driver, a `Driver` disputing a settlement must be the disputed trip's driver, and a `Driver` withdrawing a bid must be the bid's own driver. Any of these mismatches is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When a bid is placed, the bidder's `driver_id` is taken from `X-User-ID` and never from the request body. A `TransportManager` sharing those trip and stop operations is not scope-restricted and may act on any trip in the fleet.

When `X-User-Role` is `FleetPlanner`, `TransportManager`, `PayrollAnalyst`, `SafetyManager`, or `MaintenanceCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `created_by_user_id` on a trip opened by a `FleetPlanner`, as `created_by_user_id` on a backhaul offer sourced by a `FleetPlanner`, as `created_by_user_id` on a route posting opened by a `TransportManager`, and as `coached_by_user_id` on a telematics event retired by a `SafetyManager` — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. Those recorded operator ids exist for audit and for the two segregation-of-duties comparisons described in §2.11; they are not foreign keys.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100 is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). `offset` controls how many rows are skipped; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0.

Collection results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless the endpoint's own description states otherwise — the one exception in this API is the listing of a trip's stops, which is ordered by ascending `sequence` because a trip's stop set is inherently ordered. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true whenever rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Row Versions

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, recorded at insertion and never changed thereafter.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. A mutation includes a state transition, a field overwrite, and a cascade-driven change originating in another aggregate: when the completion of a trip advances a tractor's odometer, that is a mutation of the tractor row and the tractor's `version` and `updated_at` both move. Reads never move a version.

`TelematicsEvent` is the single exception. It is an append-only record, and it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Its one mutable business field is `coached`, and the coaching flip does not create version semantics on the event; the version movement that a coaching session causes lands on the affected `Driver` row instead.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this system is a server-held logical clock, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the machine's real time. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it, echoing back `current_utc`; every role may call it and no entity is mutated by the call. Only subsequent time-dependent evaluations shift.

Four decisions read the logical clock: whether a backhaul offer's `offer_expires_at` has passed, whether a route posting's `bid_close_at` has been reached, the `coached_at` instant stamped on a coached telematics event, and the maintenance window that the preventive-maintenance projection pins when a trip completes. Nothing else consults it. In particular, none of the dock-hours, delivery-window, rest-gap or maintenance-conflict geometry checks compare against "now"; they compare the supplied instants against each other.

### 2.6. Precision: Cents, Minutes, Miles and Ratios

All money in this API is an integer count of cents. Wherever a monetary quantity is derived from a multiplication or a division, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent, and — this matters for the pay engine — each component is rounded on its own before any components are summed. Never round a subtotal that was itself assembled from unrounded intermediates.

All ratios — the domicile balance index, the pre-commission margin ratio and the reported contribution-margin ratio — are rounded half up to four decimal places.

All durations are whole minutes. A stop's service time is rounded up to a whole minute at that stop, before the trip's service total is formed by summation; rounding the trip's exact fractional total once at the end is a different and incorrect answer. Miles are integers throughout and are never rounded because they are never divided in the aggregation path. Instant comparisons are exact to the second.

### 2.7. Order of Objection: Validation Precedence

A single fleet request can be wrong in a dozen ways at once, and which complaint the server voices is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Checks are evaluated in strict ascending precedence order. The **first** violated check short-circuits the request: no later check runs, no side effect is applied anywhere in the graph, and no row's `version` moves. The sixteen precedence levels, from highest priority to lowest, are:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` must be present and must be one of the six declared roles. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation.** The supplied role must appear in the target endpoint's authorised-role list. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. This deliberately outranks existence, so a forbidden role probing a random UUID receives 403 and never 404.
3. **Segregation of duties.** Applies to exactly two operations: accepting a backhaul offer when the request carries `margin_exception_approved` as true, and awarding a route posting. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Because the comparison needs the target row, the path entity's own existence check runs first in practice; the rule sits at this precedence because it outranks every check on the entity's own state. On acceptance the check is conditional — without a margin exception, an offer's own creator may accept it.
4. **Existence of the addressed entity.** The row named by the final path parameter must exist. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter. The code is per entity: `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOMICILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAY_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAILER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIP_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Existence of body references.** Every foreign key supplied in a request body must resolve, using the same per-entity codes as level 4. This guards driver creation, tractor creation, trailer creation, replenishment-order creation, trip creation, stop append, trip assignment, backhaul linkage, pay-plan assignment, backhaul-offer creation, work-order creation, route-posting creation, bid placement, and telematics-event creation — fourteen operations in all. When more than one reference is unresolvable, the reported code follows the order in which the body fields are declared for that operation in §4's companion narrative in §3.
6. **Ownership through the path.** A nested resource must belong to the parent named in its path, and a body reference must belong to the addressed aggregate. Three concrete forms: a stop's trip reference must equal the trip named in the path when appending or acting on a stop; a replenishment order referenced by a new stop must not already be attached to a different trip; and a bid being withdrawn must belong to the posting it claims. This guards stop append, stop arrival, stop completion, and bid withdrawal — four operations. Violation: `ERR_PARENT_MISMATCH`, which returns HTTP 403 because the row exists but is not addressable through this path.
7. **Request shape.** Required body fields must be present and non-null; enum fields must carry a declared value; integers declared non-negative must be zero or greater; instants must parse as RFC 3339 UTC; a window's start must be strictly earlier than its end; and `limit` must lie within 1 through 100. Applies to every endpoint that has a request body or query parameters. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two conventions here: unknown body keys are ignored rather than rejected, and any field the system computes or owns itself is silently ignored when a client supplies it.
8. **The addressed entity's own state.** The row's current state must admit the requested transition. This guards twenty-nine operations: trip plan, dispatch, complete, cancel, stop append, assignment, backhaul linkage, re-accrual and settlement dispute; stop arrival and stop completion; offer submit-for-review, revise, accept, reject and withdraw; work-order defer, reschedule, start, complete and cancel; statement settle and reverse; posting award and cancel; bid withdrawal; replenishment-order cancel; driver termination; and telematics-event coaching. The general code is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, with more specific codes where §3 names them. This level outranks every capacity and constraint check at level 15 — deferring a work order that is *already* `DEFERRED` and whose deferral count has reached its ceiling reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, never `ERR_DEFERRAL_LIMIT`.
9. **Stale parent version.** Where an operation declares a stale-version guard (§2.8), the supplied expected version must equal the parent row's current `version`. This guards exactly three operations: backhaul linkage, bid placement, and pay-plan assignment. Violation: the guard's own code — `ERR_STALE_TRIP_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_POSTING_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_DRIVER_VERSION`. It runs after the entity's own state check and before every cross-entity check, so a stale version on an otherwise doomed request still returns 412.
10. **Cross-entity state.** A referenced row in a different aggregate must be in an admissible state: an assigned tractor or trailer must be `AVAILABLE` and a tractor must not be under a preventive-maintenance hold; an assigned or dispatched driver must be `ACTIVE`; a store referenced by a new replenishment order must be `ACTIVE`; a linked backhaul offer must not be `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` — a `PROPOSED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` offer may be pre-linked and simply credits nothing until it is accepted — and must not already be linked to a different trip; a bid's posting must be `OPEN`; a coached event's driver must not be `TERMINATED`; and a settling statement's trip must be `COMPLETED`. This guards seven operations: trip assignment, trip plan, backhaul linkage, replenishment-order creation, bid placement, event coaching, and statement settlement. Codes: `ERR_ASSET_NOT_AVAILABLE`, `ERR_TRACTOR_PM_HOLD`, `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_STORE_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_POSTING_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_ACCRUED`.
11. **Driver eligibility.** A driver under a coaching hold, or whose safety score sits below the bidding threshold of 80, may neither be assigned to a trip nor place a bid; and a driver's domicile must match the trip's domicile unless the cross-domicile borrow gate permits the loan. This guards two operations: trip assignment and bid placement. Within the level the codes are evaluated in this exact order — coaching hold first (`ERR_DRIVER_COACHING_HOLD`), then safety score (`ERR_DRIVER_SAFETY_INELIGIBLE`), then domicile (`ERR_DOMICILE_MISMATCH`).
12. **Hours of service.** The trip's projected drive, duty and rolling-weekly minutes must all stay inside their ceilings for the driver in question. This guards three operations: trip assignment, trip plan, and trip dispatch. Codes: `ERR_HOS_DRIVE_LIMIT`, `ERR_HOS_DUTY_LIMIT`, `ERR_HOS_WEEKLY_LIMIT`. This level outranks the rest-gap check at level 14: a driver who both blows the daily duty ceiling and has only ten hours' worth of shortfall in rest since a previous trip reports the hours-of-service code, not the rest-gap code.
13. **Structural completeness of the aggregate.** A `DELIVERY` stop must carry both a store reference and a replenishment-order reference while every other stop type must carry neither; a trip being planned must have at least one stop and stop sequence numbers that run contiguously from 1; a trip being dispatched must have a driver, a tractor and a trailer; a trip being completed must have every stop in a terminal stop state; and a trip being re-accrued must still have its original pay-plan pin resolvable. This guards five operations: stop append, trip plan, trip dispatch, trip complete, and re-accrual. Violation: `ERR_REFERENTIAL_INCOMPLETE`, except where a more specific code exists. Because structural completeness sits below the state check at level 8, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_DRAFT` always beats `ERR_TRIP_HAS_NO_STOPS` and `ERR_TRIP_NOT_STAFFED`.
14. **Interval geometry.** Stop occupations must fit store dock hours and the order's requested window; a store's door count must not be over-subscribed; an assigned asset must not collide with a buffered shop window; a driver must have the minimum rest between consecutive trips; and stop instants must not run backwards across sequence numbers. This guards five operations: trip plan, stop append, trip assignment, work-order creation, and work-order reschedule. Codes: `ERR_DOCK_HOURS_VIOLATION`, `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_VIOLATION`, `ERR_DOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_ASSET_MAINTENANCE_CONFLICT`, `ERR_DRIVER_REST_GAP`, `ERR_STOP_SEQUENCE_DISORDER`. Within the level the evaluation order is fixed: sequence ordering, then dock hours, then window fit, then dock capacity, then maintenance conflict, then rest gap. This level loses to hours of service at level 12.
15. **Capacity and uniqueness.** The trip's peak case load must not exceed the assigned trailer's case capacity, where equality passes; a refrigerated replenishment order requires a reefer trailer; the eight business codes — store, domicile, plan, driver, tractor unit, trailer, trip and route — must each be globally unique; a driver may hold only one submitted bid per posting; a work order's deferral count must not exceed two; and a backhaul offer may be referenced by at most one trip. This guards ten operations: store creation, domicile creation, pay-plan creation, driver creation, tractor creation, trailer creation, trip assignment, backhaul linkage, bid placement, and work-order deferral. Codes: `ERR_TRAILER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_TEMPERATURE_MODE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID`, `ERR_DEFERRAL_LIMIT`, `ERR_BACKHAUL_ALREADY_LINKED`. This level loses to the state check at level 8.
16. **Money floors and ceilings.** An accepted offer's reported contribution-margin ratio must be at or above the floor unless a margin exception is approved; a statement being settled must have a gross amount greater than zero; and a benchmark window must contain volume. This guards three operations: offer acceptance, statement settlement, and the domicile cost benchmark. Codes: `ERR_MARGIN_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_ZERO_PAY_SETTLEMENT`, `ERR_BENCHMARK_NO_VOLUME`. Money objections are reported last of all — every structural, temporal and capacity complaint surfaces before them.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Shared Parents

Three write operations reach into a row that other writers are also moving, and each carries a mandatory expected-version field. These fields are not optional and there is no default: omitting one is a malformed request under level 7. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and supplying one elsewhere is ignored.

Linking a backhaul offer to a trip requires `expected_trip_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Trip`. If it does not equal the trip's current `version`, the link is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRIP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because linking legitimately supersedes whatever offer the trip already carried — the displaced offer is unlinked and, if it had been accepted, returned to the market — so a writer reasoning from an outdated read of the trip would silently discard a link it never saw.

Placing a bid on a route posting requires `expected_posting_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `RoutePosting`. A mismatch with the posting's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POSTING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every accepted bid raises the posting's bid count and version, and an award or a cancellation closes the board outright, so the posting version is the bidder's proof that the board they reasoned about — its bid count, its status and its close time — is still the board they are bidding into.

Assigning a pay plan to a driver requires `expected_driver_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Driver`. A mismatch with the driver's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DRIVER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A pay-plan assignment supersedes the driver's current plan, and without the guard a writer working from an outdated read would overwrite a plan change it never observed.

### 2.9. Interval Geometry Conventions

Three different resources in this domain reserve time, and each uses a deliberately different geometry. Confusing them is the most common way to get the schedule wrong, so the conventions are stated once here and referenced from the workflows that apply them.

**Dock occupation is half-open.** A delivery stop occupies its store's dock from `scheduled_arrival_at` inclusive up to `scheduled_departure_at` exclusive. Two stops whose windows merely touch — one departing at precisely the instant the next arrives — do **not** collide. Overlap is measured strictly: an existing stop overlaps the candidate when the existing arrival is strictly earlier than the candidate's departure **and** the existing departure is strictly later than the candidate's arrival.

**Shop windows are closed, and trip occupations are buffered.** A maintenance work order occupies the shop from `window_start_at` through `window_end_at`, both edges inclusive. A trip's equipment occupation runs from two hours before `planned_start_at` through two hours after `planned_end_at`, also both edges inclusive. Because both edges are inclusive, merely touching intervals **do** collide: a shop window starting at exactly the instant two hours after a trip's planned end is rejected, while one starting a single minute later is accepted. This is the opposite adjacency rule from the dock, on a different resource, and both are intentional.

**Driver duty periods are separated by a gap, not merely disjoint.** Two trips of the same driver must not only avoid overlapping, they must be separated by a minimum rest period measured between the earlier trip's planned end and the later trip's planned start.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities and Cascade Propagation

Several fields in this system are ledgers: they hold a running total that must always equal the sum of the rows currently inside a stated filter, and every operation that moves a row in or out of that filter must move the ledger by the same amount in the same request. Seven such quantities exist.

`Store.open_scheduled_cases` equals the sum of `cases` over that store's replenishment orders currently in `SCHEDULED` status. `Store.delivered_cases_total` accumulates the cases of every order of that store that has ever reached `DELIVERED` and never decreases. `Domicile.accrued_pay_cents` equals the sum of `gross_cents` over the pay statements of that domicile's drivers whose own status is `ACCRUED` or `SETTLED`. `Driver.ytd_gross_cents` is the same sum restricted to one driver. `Driver.open_penalty_points` equals the sum of `penalty_points` over that driver's telematics events not yet coached. `Domicile.assigned_driver_count` counts that domicile's drivers whose status is `ACTIVE`. `RoutePosting.bid_count` counts that posting's bids whose status is `SUBMITTED`.

Cascades in this system are chains, not single steps, and each link in a chain is a real mutation of a real row with its own `version` and `updated_at` movement. Where a workflow in §3 says that a trip's completion delivers its orders, the chain runs: the stop's order moves from `SCHEDULED` to `DELIVERED`, which lowers that store's open-scheduled ledger and raises its delivered-lifetime ledger. Where a cancellation skips a trip's stops, the chain runs: the stop moves to `SKIPPED`, which pushes any order the stop carried from `SCHEDULED` back to `UNSCHEDULED`, which lowers the store's open-scheduled ledger. A cascade never stops halfway. If the operation is rejected at any precedence level, none of the links fire at all.

### 2.11. Path Addressing, Filtered Collections and Segregation of Duties

The entity graph in this API is flat: every relationship is an explicit reference field on the child row, and no resource is *only* reachable through a parent path. Path parameters are nonetheless validated left to right. When a stop is appended under a trip, the trip named by `trip_id` is resolved first and a missing trip yields `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` before the body is even shape-checked; when a stop is deleted from a trip, `trip_id` resolves first and `trip_stop_id` second, and a stop that exists but belongs to a different trip is an ownership failure rather than a not-found. The remaining path parameters in this API are `store_id`, `domicile_id`, `pay_plan_id`, `driver_id`, `tractor_id`, `trailer_id`, `order_id`, `trip_stop_id`, `offer_id`, `work_order_id`, `statement_id`, `posting_id`, `bid_id`, and `event_id`.

Because the graph is flat, reverse reachability is provided by filters on the peer collections rather than by nested paths. A trip's stops are discoverable both as a sub-resource of the trip and through the stop collection filtered by `trip_id`, `store_id` or `replenishment_order_id`. Pay statements — which no client ever creates directly, since they are born from trip close-out and re-accrual — are discoverable through the statement collection filtered by `driver_id`, `trip_id`, `pay_plan_id`, `status` or `variant`. Auto-generated preventive-maintenance work orders are discoverable through the work-order collection filtered by `auto_generated`, alongside `tractor_id`, `trailer_id`, `asset_kind`, `service_type` and `status`. Trips are filterable on each of their six references — `domicile_id`, `driver_id`, `tractor_id`, `trailer_id`, `backhaul_offer_id` and `pinned_pay_plan_id` — plus both status dimensions, `plan_status` and `settlement_status`. Drivers are filterable by `domicile_id`, `pay_plan_id`, `status` and `coaching_hold`; tractors by `domicile_id`, `status` and `pm_hold`; trailers by `domicile_id`, `trailer_type` and `status`; stores by `region_code` and `status`; domiciles by `region_code`; replenishment orders by `store_id`, `status` and `temperature_mode`; backhaul offers by `dest_store_id`, `origin_kind`, `origin_region_code` and `status`; route postings by `domicile_id`, `awarded_driver_id` and `status`; route bids by `route_posting_id`, `driver_id` and `status`; telematics events by `driver_id`, `trip_id`, `event_type` and `coached`; and trip stops additionally by `stop_type` and `status`.

Two decisions in this domain may not be made by a single person, and because operator identifiers are role-agnostic — the same operator id may legitimately arrive under different role headers — the check compares identifiers, not roles. Accepting a backhaul offer *with a margin exception* requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the offer's recorded `created_by_user_id`; awarding a route posting requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the posting's recorded `created_by_user_id`. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Accepting an offer that clears the margin floor on its own merits carries no such restriction.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Network of Record: Stores, Domiciles and Pay Plans

Before a single trip can be built, three reference populations must exist. A `FleetPlanner` registers each receiving location as a store, supplying a `store_code` of one to sixteen characters that must be unique across every store in the system — a collision is reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — together with a `region_code` naming the operating region the location sits in (the fixtures in use exercise `MIDWEST`, `SOUTHEAST` and `WEST`, though the field is free text and the backhaul origin matching simply compares strings). Three operational capacities complete the record. `dock_count`, at least 1, is the number of receiving doors and therefore the aggregate ceiling on how many delivery stops may sit on the store's dock at overlapping times. `cases_per_hour_unload_rate`, at least 1, is how fast the store's crew works and is the divisor of the delivery service-minute rule. `dock_open_minute` and `dock_close_minute` express receiving hours as minutes of the UTC day, where zero is midnight and 1439 is the final minute; the open minute must be zero or greater and strictly less than the close minute, and the close minute must not exceed 1439. A newly registered store starts with `status` of `ACTIVE` and both of its case ledgers, `open_scheduled_cases` and `delivered_cases_total`, at zero.

A store's operating posture is a two-state affair. A `TransportManager` may flip `status` between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` by supplying the target value in the `status` field. Suspension is deliberately toothless towards work already committed: no cascade fires, trips already planned keep every stop they had, and the only consequence is that a *new* plan commitment touching a `SUSPENDED` store is refused with `ERR_STORE_SUSPENDED`. Reinstatement is the same operation in the other direction, and each flip advances the store's `version`.

Domiciles are the terminals that own drivers, equipment and cost. A domicile is registered with a `domicile_code`, unique across all domiciles and one to sixteen characters long, a `region_code`, and a `tractor_capacity` of at least 1 that records physical parking capacity for reporting only and gates nothing. `driver_headcount_target`, at least 1, is the budgeted headcount and is the denominator of the domicile's balance index. Four money rates complete the record, all of them zero or greater: `fixed_ownership_cents_per_day` and `overhead_cents_per_day` are the equipment-ownership and terminal-overhead charges per calendar day, which enter the cost benchmark under one variant only, while `dedicated_rate_cents_per_mile` and `dedicated_rate_cents_per_case` are the rates the alternative dedicated carrier has quoted. A new domicile starts with `assigned_driver_count` at zero, `balance_index` at 0.0 and `accrued_pay_cents` at zero.

The **balance index** is the domicile's staffing health: the count of that domicile's `ACTIVE` drivers divided by its `driver_headcount_target`, rounded half up to four decimal places. Terminated drivers leave the numerator immediately. The index has exactly one job, and it is described in §3.5: it decides whether one terminal may lend a driver to another.

Pay structure lives in pay plans, which only a `PayrollAnalyst` may author. A plan carries a `plan_code` unique across all plans and one to sixteen characters long, and six rate fields. `mileage_cents_per_mile` prices the trip's miles. `stop_cents_per_stop` prices each paid delivery or pickup stop, and `drop_hook_cents` prices each drop-off stop on top of the per-stop rate structure. `hourly_cents_per_minute` prices detention minutes beyond plan plus grace. `minimum_daily_cents` is the daily guarantee floor that makeup pay tops a trip up to. `backhaul_bonus_pct` is the fraction of the mileage-plus-activity base paid as a bonus when the trip carries an accepted backhaul, and it must be between 0 and 0.50 inclusive. The first five are counts of cents and must be zero or greater.

Plans are amended rather than versioned by the client: a `PayrollAnalyst` may supply any subset of `mileage_cents_per_mile`, `stop_cents_per_stop`, `drop_hook_cents`, `hourly_cents_per_minute`, `minimum_daily_cents` and `backhaul_bonus_pct`, and omitted rates are left alone. The amendment raises the plan's `version` and does **not** recompute a single already-pinned trip or an already-issued pay statement. That is the whole point of the pin described in §3.5 — a rate change is forward-looking, and yesterday's settled work stays settled at yesterday's rates.

### 3.2. Fleet Assets: Tractors, Trailers and the Shop Schedule

The equipment roster belongs to the `MaintenanceCoordinator`. A tractor is added with a `unit_code` unique across all tractors and one to sixteen characters long, a `domicile_id` naming its home terminal, an `odometer_miles` reading of zero or more, a `pm_interval_miles` of at least 1 expressing how often it needs preventive maintenance, and a `pm_last_service_odometer` reading between zero and the current odometer inclusive. Two derived fields follow immediately: `miles_since_pm` is the current odometer reading less the last-service reading, and `pm_hold` is set at creation when `miles_since_pm` has already reached or passed `pm_interval_miles`. A tractor's `status` starts at `AVAILABLE` and moves among `AVAILABLE`, `ASSIGNED` and `IN_SHOP`.

A coordinator may correct a tractor's `odometer_miles`, `pm_interval_miles` or `pm_last_service_odometer`, supplying any subset. Both `miles_since_pm` and `pm_hold` are re-derived from the corrected values and the tractor's `version` advances — but no work order is ever created by a correction. Preventive maintenance is projected in exactly one place, at trip close-out (§3.7), and nowhere else.

Trailers are simpler. A trailer carries a `trailer_code` unique across all trailers and one to sixteen characters long, a `domicile_id`, a `trailer_type` of either `DRY_VAN` or `REEFER`, a `capacity_cases` of at least 1, and an `inspection_due_at` instant that records the next DOT inspection for reporting only and gates nothing. A trailer's `status` starts at `AVAILABLE` and moves among `AVAILABLE`, `ASSIGNED` and `IN_SHOP` exactly as a tractor's does. Neither tractors nor trailers are ever borrowed across domiciles — that flexibility belongs to drivers alone.

Shop time is booked as a work order. Because the reference is polymorphic, `asset_kind` selects which asset field must be populated: `TRACTOR` requires `tractor_id` and forbids `trailer_id`, and `TRAILER` requires `trailer_id` and forbids `tractor_id`. Disagreement between `asset_kind` and which field was supplied is a malformed request. `service_type` is one of `PM_A`, `PM_B`, `INSPECTION` or `REPAIR`, where the two `PM_` classes are the preventive ones the system itself generates. `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` bound the shop window, the end strictly after the start, and — per §2.9 — both edges are inclusive. A manually booked order starts with `status` of `SCHEDULED`, `deferral_count` at zero and `auto_generated` false. The booking is refused with `ERR_ASSET_MAINTENANCE_CONFLICT` when the proposed window touches or overlaps the buffered occupation of any trip that already references the same asset and sits in `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; trips in `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` reserve nothing at all.

The shop lifecycle then runs on five transitions. Deferral takes a work order `SCHEDULED → DEFERRED`, raising `deferral_count` by 1, and is allowed only while `deferral_count` is below 2 — a third attempt is `ERR_DEFERRAL_LIMIT`. Crucially, a deferral does **not** release the asset's `pm_hold` for an auto-generated preventive order: the hold survives the postponement, which is why a tractor cannot be pushed back into service by rescheduling its PM out of the way. Rescheduling takes an order `DEFERRED → SCHEDULED` and overwrites `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` with the supplied pair, re-running the same buffered-closed conflict check against every committed trip of the asset in the reverse direction; a collision is again `ERR_ASSET_MAINTENANCE_CONFLICT`. Starting the work takes the order `SCHEDULED → IN_SHOP` and drives the asset itself `AVAILABLE → IN_SHOP`; if the asset is not `AVAILABLE` at that moment the request fails with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Completion takes the order `IN_SHOP → COMPLETED` and returns the asset `IN_SHOP → AVAILABLE`; a coordinator may record the `performed_odometer_miles` observed in the bay, and when the completed work is a `PM_A` or `PM_B` on a tractor the PM baseline resets — `pm_last_service_odometer` becomes the tractor's current `odometer_miles`, `miles_since_pm` becomes zero, and `pm_hold` clears to false. Cancellation takes an order `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and is permitted only when `auto_generated` is false; an auto-generated PM order can never be cancelled, only deferred at most twice and then performed, and an attempt to cancel one is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Deferral, cancellation and the reject-style actions elsewhere in this API all accept an optional free-text `reason_code` that is recorded and never interpreted.

### 3.3. Drivers: Hiring, Duty Counters, Seniority and Termination

Hiring is a `TransportManager` act. A driver is created with a `driver_code` unique across all drivers and one to sixteen characters long, a `domicile_id` naming the home terminal, a `pay_plan_id` naming the plan the driver is currently on, and a `seniority_date` in year-month-day form where an earlier date means more senior. The new row starts `ACTIVE` with `hos_status` of `AVAILABLE` — the other posture being `ON_DUTY`, which the driver holds between dispatch and close-out — and with all three duty counters at zero: `drive_minutes_used_today`, `duty_minutes_used_today` and `weekly_on_duty_minutes`. Safety fields start clean as well: `open_penalty_points` at zero, `safety_score` at 100, `coaching_hold` false. `ytd_gross_cents` starts at zero. The hire immediately raises the domicile's `assigned_driver_count` by 1 and recomputes its `balance_index`.

Pay-plan changes are a `PayrollAnalyst` act and are guarded by the driver's row version. The request names the new `pay_plan_id` and carries `expected_driver_version`; on success the driver's `pay_plan_id` is replaced and the driver's `version` advances. What does *not* happen is any change to a trip that has already pinned a plan — a superseded plan continues to govern every trip that snapshotted it.

Two operator surfaces exist for duty counters. A rest-period record takes a `reset_kind` of either `DAILY`, which zeroes `drive_minutes_used_today` and `duty_minutes_used_today`, or `WEEKLY` — the thirty-four-hour restart — which zeroes those two and `weekly_on_duty_minutes` as well; either way `hos_status` returns to `AVAILABLE` and the driver's `version` advances. Separately, a duty-log correction accepts any subset of `drive_minutes_used_today`, `duty_minutes_used_today` and `weekly_on_duty_minutes`, overwrites exactly the counters supplied, leaves the omitted ones alone, re-derives `hos_status`, and advances the `version`.

Termination is one-way. A `TransportManager` terminates a driver, optionally recording a `termination_reason`, moving `status` `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`. `TERMINATED` is terminal, so a second termination is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and so is a termination attempted while any trip references the driver in `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — a driver cannot be walked out from under committed work. The status change then cascades twice. The domicile's `assigned_driver_count` falls by 1 and its `balance_index` is recomputed. And every one of the driver's route bids still in `SUBMITTED` moves to `PASSED_OVER`, with each such bid lowering its posting's `bid_count` by 1. Postings the driver had already won keep their `AWARDED` status and their `awarded_driver_id`: retention history is preserved on purpose rather than rewritten.

### 3.4. From Replenishment Demand to a Draft Trip

Demand enters the system as a replenishment order raised by a `FleetPlanner`. The order names its `store_id`, a `cases` count of at least 1 that drives service minutes, trailer load and cost per case, and a `weight_lbs` of zero or greater that is carried for reporting only. `requested_window_start` and `requested_window_end` bound the merchandising window inside which the trailer must arrive and be released, the end strictly after the start. `temperature_mode` is either `DRY` or `REFRIGERATED`, and a refrigerated order can only be carried on a reefer trailer. `priority` runs from 1 for the most urgent to 5 for the least and defaults to 3; it is reporting only and gates nothing. A new order's `status` is `UNSCHEDULED`, and because the store's open-scheduled ledger tracks only orders in `SCHEDULED` status, a freshly raised order is deliberately *not* yet inside `open_scheduled_cases`. Raising an order against a `SUSPENDED` store is refused with `ERR_STORE_SUSPENDED`.

An order that has not yet been planned onto a trip may be cancelled, optionally with a `reason_code`, moving `UNSCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. Any other starting status — `SCHEDULED`, `DELIVERED` or already `CANCELLED` — is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. The store's `open_scheduled_cases` does not move, because an `UNSCHEDULED` order was never inside the ledger's filter to begin with.

A trip begins as a `DRAFT` shell: a `FleetPlanner` supplies a `trip_code` unique across all trips and one to sixteen characters long, the `domicile_id` that owns the trip and will absorb its cost, and a `planned_start_at` instant marking when the tractor leaves the terminal. The new row records the planner's operator identifier in `created_by_user_id`, sets `plan_status` to `DRAFT` and `settlement_status` to `PENDING`, puts `stop_count` at zero and `planned_end_at` at null, and zeroes every mileage, minute, case and money aggregate.

Stops are then appended one at a time, and only while the trip is still `DRAFT` — an append against any later plan status is `ERR_TRIP_NOT_DRAFT`. Each append supplies a `sequence` that must be exactly one greater than the highest sequence the trip already holds, so the first stop is 1 and the numbering stays contiguous; any other value is `ERR_STOP_SEQUENCE_DISORDER`. `stop_type` is one of `DELIVERY`, `VENDOR_PICKUP`, `THIRD_PARTY_PICKUP`, `DROP_OFF` or `FUEL`. A `DELIVERY` stop must carry both `store_id` and `replenishment_order_id`; every other stop type must carry neither, and either shape violation is `ERR_REFERENTIAL_INCOMPLETE`. A referenced order that is already attached to a different trip is not addressable here and is refused with `ERR_PARENT_MISMATCH`. The stop's dock occupation is given by `scheduled_arrival_at` and `scheduled_departure_at`, the departure strictly after the arrival, and the arrival must not be earlier than the previous stop's `scheduled_departure_at` — a stop that would run backwards in time relative to its predecessor is also `ERR_STOP_SEQUENCE_DISORDER`. Three segment facts describe the leg *into* the stop: `drive_minutes_from_prev` and `miles_from_prev`, both zero or greater, measured from the previous stop or from the domicile for sequence 1, and `segment_loaded`, a flag saying whether that leg was run loaded. Finally `cases` records how many cases are dropped at a delivery or drop-off, or picked up at a vendor or third-party pickup; it defaults to zero and must be zero or greater. Every new stop starts with `status` of `PLANNED`, and the four stop states are `PLANNED`, `ARRIVED`, `COMPLETED` and `SKIPPED`.

Each stop's own **service minutes** are computed at append time from its type. For a `DELIVERY`, take the stop's `cases`, multiply by 60, divide by the store's `cases_per_hour_unload_rate`, round the result **up** to a whole minute, and then add a fixed fifteen minutes of setup and paperwork at the dock. For a `VENDOR_PICKUP` or a `THIRD_PARTY_PICKUP`, `service_minutes` is a flat forty-five minutes. For a `DROP_OFF` it is a flat twenty minutes, and for a `FUEL` stop a flat twenty-five minutes. The rounding order here is part of the contract and is stated again because it is easy to get wrong: the rounding up happens *at each stop*, and the whole-minute results are then added together. Summing the exact fractional unload times across the trip and rounding the total once produces a different — and wrong — answer.

Every append re-derives the trip's aggregates by walking the trip's stops in ascending `sequence` order. Because sequence numbers are unique per trip and contiguous from 1, that walk order is total and no tie-break is ever needed. `stop_count` is the number of stops of the trip regardless of their status. `planned_end_at` is the `scheduled_departure_at` of the highest-sequence stop, and it is null while the trip has no stops. `total_miles` sums `miles_from_prev` across all stops; `loaded_miles` sums `miles_from_prev` across only those stops whose `segment_loaded` is true; and `empty_miles` is total miles less loaded miles. `planned_drive_minutes` sums `drive_minutes_from_prev` across all stops. `planned_service_minutes` sums the per-stop `service_minutes` already rounded up individually. `planned_break_minutes` is thirty minutes whenever `planned_drive_minutes` is strictly greater than eight hours of driving, and zero otherwise — the trigger sits at eight hours, so a trip with exactly eight hours of driving gets no mandatory break. `planned_duty_minutes` is the sum of planned drive minutes, planned service minutes and planned break minutes. `planned_delivery_cases` sums `cases` across stops whose type is `DELIVERY`. All of these aggregates include stops in every status — a `SKIPPED` stop keeps contributing to the planned totals; only `delivered_cases`, set at close-out, filters on stop status.

The trailer's binding constraint is not the trip's total case volume but its **peak in-transit load**, and `peak_case_load` captures it. The trailer leaves the domicile already loaded with every case the trip will deliver, so start the running load at the sum of `cases` across all `DELIVERY` stops and record that starting figure as the peak so far. Then walk the stops in ascending sequence: at a `DELIVERY` subtract that stop's `cases` from the running load; at a `VENDOR_PICKUP` or `THIRD_PARTY_PICKUP` add that stop's `cases`; at a `DROP_OFF` subtract that stop's `cases`; at a `FUEL` stop leave the load unchanged. After each stop, if the running load is higher than the peak recorded so far, the peak becomes the running load. `peak_case_load` is the highest running load seen anywhere on that walk.

A planner may also remove a stop from a `DRAFT` trip. The surviving stops' `sequence` values are compacted so that the numbering remains contiguous from 1, every trip aggregate above is recomputed on the reduced stop set, and the trip's `version` advances.

### 3.5. Crewing and Committing: Assignment Gates and the Plan Gate

Crewing a trip is a `TransportManager` operation that names a `driver_id`, a `tractor_id` and a `trailer_id` together. It is also a *re*-assignment surface: calling it again with different equipment releases whatever tractor and trailer the trip previously held back to `AVAILABLE` before claiming the new pair. On success the tractor and the trailer each move `AVAILABLE → ASSIGNED`, the trip records all three references, and the trip's `version` advances.

Nine gates stand between the request and that outcome, and they fire in the precedence order of §2.7. **Asset availability** comes first among the cross-entity checks: a tractor or trailer that is not `AVAILABLE` is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and a tractor whose `pm_hold` is true is refused with `ERR_TRACTOR_PM_HOLD`. A `TERMINATED` driver is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE`. **Driver eligibility** follows, in its own fixed internal order: a driver whose `coaching_hold` is true is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_COACHING_HOLD`; failing that, a driver whose `safety_score` is below 80 is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_SAFETY_INELIGIBLE`; failing that, the domicile comparison runs.

Equipment domicile is absolute: a tractor's or a trailer's `domicile_id` must equal the trip's `domicile_id` outright, and any difference is `ERR_DOMICILE_MISMATCH`. Drivers, by contrast, may be borrowed — but only when both terminals agree to it arithmetically. When the driver's `domicile_id` differs from the trip's, read the balance index of the *source* domicile (the driver's home) and of the *target* domicile (the trip's owner). The loan is permitted only when the source's `balance_index` is at or above 1.5000 — a terminal sitting exactly at one-and-a-half times its headcount target may lend — **and** the target's `balance_index` is strictly below 1.0000, so a terminal already at exactly its target may not borrow. If either condition fails, the assignment is `ERR_DOMICILE_MISMATCH`.

**Hours-of-service feasibility** is next, and it is evaluated against the candidate driver's counters as they stand at the instant the request is processed. Add the trip's `planned_drive_minutes` to the driver's `drive_minutes_used_today` to get the projected drive figure; add the trip's `planned_duty_minutes` to the driver's `duty_minutes_used_today` to get the projected daily duty figure; add the same trip duty figure to the driver's `weekly_on_duty_minutes` to get the projected rolling-weekly figure. The three comparisons then run in a pinned order — drive, then duty, then weekly — so a trip that busts two of the three reports the first one in that order. A projected drive figure of more than eleven hours is `ERR_HOS_DRIVE_LIMIT`; a projected daily duty figure of more than fourteen hours is `ERR_HOS_DUTY_LIMIT`; a projected rolling-weekly figure of more than seventy hours is `ERR_HOS_WEEKLY_LIMIT`. All three comparisons are strict: a projection landing exactly on a ceiling is feasible. Note the asymmetry that `planned_duty_minutes` already contains `planned_break_minutes`, so the mandatory thirty-minute break consumes daily duty and rolling-weekly duty while never counting against the drive ceiling.

**Rest between duty periods** is a separate geometry, checked for the candidate driver against every *other* trip that already references them and sits in `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; trips in `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` impose no rest requirement. For each such trip, if the candidate trip's `planned_start_at` is at or after the other trip's `planned_end_at`, the gap is the number of minutes between them; if instead the other trip's `planned_start_at` is at or after the candidate trip's `planned_end_at`, the gap is the number of minutes in that direction; and if neither holds — the two duty periods overlap outright — the gap is zero. A gap of fewer than ten hours is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_REST_GAP`. The comparison is strict, so a gap of exactly ten hours is legal and one minute less is not.

**Maintenance conflict** applies the buffered-closed geometry of §2.9 to each of the two assets: the trip's occupation runs from two hours before its `planned_start_at` through two hours after its `planned_end_at`, both edges inclusive, and it is compared against every work order of that same asset whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `DEFERRED` or `IN_SHOP`; `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` orders are ignored entirely. A touch counts as a collision and yields `ERR_ASSET_MAINTENANCE_CONFLICT`. A trip with no stops has a null `planned_end_at` and therefore can never be assigned at all, so the interval is always well defined by the time this check runs. Finally the **capacity and temperature** gates: the trip's `peak_case_load` must not exceed the assigned trailer's `capacity_cases`, where equality passes and any excess is `ERR_TRAILER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; and if any `DELIVERY` stop's order has a `temperature_mode` of `REFRIGERATED`, the assigned trailer's `trailer_type` must be `REEFER`, else `ERR_TEMPERATURE_MODE_MISMATCH`.

Committing the schedule is a `FleetPlanner` act that takes the trip `DRAFT → PLANNED`, and it is where the temporal contract with the stores is finally enforced. The trip must still be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_TRIP_NOT_DRAFT`. It must be fully crewed — a driver, a tractor and a trailer all present — otherwise `ERR_TRIP_NOT_STAFFED`. It must carry at least one `DELIVERY` stop, otherwise `ERR_TRIP_HAS_NO_STOPS`. Every `DELIVERY` stop's store must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_STORE_SUSPENDED`, and every `DELIVERY` stop's order must still be `UNSCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`.

Then, once per `DELIVERY` stop, three temporal gates run in a fixed order. First **dock hours**: convert the stop's `scheduled_arrival_at` and `scheduled_departure_at` to minutes of the UTC day by taking the hour times sixty plus the minute. The stop is refused with `ERR_DOCK_HOURS_VIOLATION` if the arrival and the departure do not fall on the same UTC calendar date, or if the arrival minute is earlier than the store's `dock_open_minute`, or if the departure minute is later than the store's `dock_close_minute`. Second **merchandising window fit**, with both edges inclusive against the order: the stop is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_VIOLATION` if `scheduled_arrival_at` is earlier than the order's `requested_window_start` or `scheduled_departure_at` is later than the order's `requested_window_end`. A stop that violates both of these reports the dock-hours code, because dock hours are checked first. Third **dock capacity**, which is an aggregate constraint rather than a pairwise exclusion: count the other delivery stops at the same store whose own status is `PLANNED` or `ARRIVED`, whose owning trip is in `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and whose half-open occupation strictly overlaps the candidate stop's — that is, whose arrival is strictly before the candidate's departure and whose departure is strictly after the candidate's arrival. Stops of `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` trips, and `SKIPPED` stops, are excluded from that count. If the number of such existing occupants plus the candidate itself would exceed the store's `dock_count`, the commitment is refused with `ERR_DOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; equivalently, reject as soon as the already-committed overlapping stops have reached the door count. Overlap up to the door count is entirely legal — that is what having several doors means.

The plan gate closes with the trailer capacity check on `peak_case_load` and a fresh hours-of-service evaluation against the driver's counters at that instant, using the same three ceilings and the same pinned drive-duty-weekly order as the assignment gate. When everything passes, five things happen. The trip's aggregates and its `peak_case_load` are recomputed one last time before the gates are judged. The **pay-plan pin** is taken: `pinned_pay_plan_id` becomes the assigned driver's current `pay_plan_id`, and `pinned_pay_plan_version` becomes that plan's `version` as observed at this instant. `planned_pay_cents` is set from the planning-accrual pay variant of §3.8. Every `DELIVERY` stop's order moves `UNSCHEDULED → SCHEDULED`, and each order that turned `SCHEDULED` raises its store's `open_scheduled_cases` by that order's `cases` — a two-link chain in which the ledger movement is caused by the order transition, not by the commitment call itself. And the trip's `version` advances.

### 3.6. The Backhaul Market: Margin, Commission and Linkage

Sourcing backhaul is a `FleetPlanner` responsibility. An offer is registered with an `origin_kind` of either `VENDOR_PICKUP`, for freight collected at a supplier, or `THIRD_PARTY`, for brokered freight; a `shipper_name` recorded for reporting only; an `origin_region_code` naming where the freight is collected; and a `dest_store_id` naming the store the freight is delivered to. Its economics are `revenue_cents`, the linehaul revenue offered, which must be at least 1 because it is the denominator of every margin ratio; `cases`, at least 1, which both drives the handling cost and adds to the trip's in-transit load; `weight_lbs`, zero or greater, for reporting only; `deadhead_miles`, zero or greater, being the empty miles needed to reach the pickup; and `loaded_miles`, zero or greater, from pickup to destination. `temperature_mode` is `DRY` or `REFRIGERATED`, and refrigerated freight both attracts a surcharge and requires a reefer trailer on the linked trip. `pickup_window_start` and `pickup_window_end` bound the shipper's pickup offer, the end strictly after the start, and `offer_expires_at` is the instant after which the expiration sweep retires the offer. A new offer's `status` is `PROPOSED`, its `review_round` is zero, its `margin_exception_approved` flag is false, its `created_by_user_id` records the sourcing planner's operator identifier, and all six of its computed margin fields sit at zero — they are not computed until the offer enters review.

Moving an offer `PROPOSED → UNDER_REVIEW` is what triggers pricing for the first time. The offer must be in `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, and the logical clock must still be strictly before `offer_expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

The **contribution-margin calculation** then runs, and its step order is part of the contract because the commission rate is keyed on a margin tier while the commission is itself a component of the margin. The loop is broken deliberately by keying the tier on the *pre-commission* figure. Step one, the incremental miles are `deadhead_miles` plus `loaded_miles`. Step two, the linehaul variable cost is those incremental miles multiplied by a fleet running cost of 185 cents per mile, rounded half up to whole cents, and stored as the linehaul component. Step three, the handling cost is the offer's `cases` multiplied by 9 cents per case, rounded half up. Step four, the reefer surcharge applies only when `temperature_mode` is `REFRIGERATED`, and it is charged on the loaded miles alone — `loaded_miles` multiplied by 22 cents per mile, rounded half up — while a `DRY` offer carries no surcharge at all. Step five, `variable_cost_cents` is the sum of the linehaul component, the handling component and the reefer component. Step six, `gross_margin_cents` is `revenue_cents` less `variable_cost_cents`. Step seven produces the **tier key**: `tier_margin_pct` is `gross_margin_cents` divided by `revenue_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places. Step eight selects the commission rate from that tier key and from nothing else: when `tier_margin_pct` is at or above 20 percent the brokerage rate is 6 percent of revenue, and otherwise it is 3 percent of revenue. Step nine computes `commission_cents` as `revenue_cents` multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half up. Step ten sets `contribution_margin_cents` to `gross_margin_cents` less `commission_cents`. Step eleven reports `margin_pct` as `contribution_margin_cents` divided by `revenue_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

Two things about that sequence deserve emphasis. `margin_pct` is the *reported* ratio and never re-enters the tier decision of step eight — keying the commission on the post-commission ratio changes the persisted `contribution_margin_cents` for any offer whose pre-commission ratio lands near the tier boundary, and is simply wrong. And no trip, trailer or store data enters the margin at all; the trailer only gates acceptance. All six computed fields — `variable_cost_cents`, `gross_margin_cents`, `tier_margin_pct`, `commission_cents`, `contribution_margin_cents` and `margin_pct` — are written by this calculation.

Negotiation is a bounded revision loop. Sending an offer back to the shipper takes it `UNDER_REVIEW → PROPOSED`, and it is permitted only while `review_round` is below 3; a fourth attempt is `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT`, and an offer that is not `UNDER_REVIEW` is `ERR_OFFER_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. The revision may supply any subset of `revenue_cents`, `cases`, `deadhead_miles` and `loaded_miles`; supplied values overwrite, omitted values are left alone. `review_round` then rises by 1, the full contribution-margin calculation re-runs on the revised economics, and — because the offer is by definition no longer accepted — any trip that links this offer has its `backhaul_credit_cents` reset to zero.

Acceptance belongs to the `TransportManager` and carries the most conditions of any offer action. The offer must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. Exactly one trip must already link the offer, and that trip's `plan_status` must be `PLANNED` or `DISPATCHED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. When the offer's `temperature_mode` is `REFRIGERATED`, the linked trip's trailer must have a `trailer_type` of `REEFER`, otherwise `ERR_TEMPERATURE_MODE_MISMATCH`. The offer's `margin_pct` must be at or above the 8 percent contribution-margin floor; below the floor the acceptance is refused with `ERR_MARGIN_BELOW_FLOOR` unless the request carries `margin_exception_approved` as true. And when a margin exception *is* claimed, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the offer's `created_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — one operator may not both source a thin load and waive the floor on it. On success the offer's `margin_exception_approved` is set to the supplied flag, the offer moves `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED`, and the linked trip's `backhaul_credit_cents` becomes the offer's `contribution_margin_cents` with the trip's `version` advancing alongside the offer's.

Three ways exist to take an offer off the market. Rejection, a `TransportManager` act with an optional `reason_code`, takes an `UNDER_REVIEW` offer to `REJECTED` and, when a trip links it, clears that trip's `backhaul_offer_id` to null and its `backhaul_credit_cents` to zero. Withdrawal, a `FleetPlanner` act with an optional `reason_code`, takes a `PROPOSED` offer to `WITHDRAWN` and is permitted only while **no** trip links the offer; a linked offer cannot be withdrawn, and the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, as is a withdrawal from any status other than `PROPOSED`. Expiry is a bulk sweep run by a `TransportManager`: every offer in `PROPOSED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` whose `offer_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, and each swept offer is unlinked from its trip with that trip's `backhaul_offer_id` cleared to null and its `backhaul_credit_cents` zeroed. Offers not yet due are skipped, not failed, and the sweep reports what it did as an `expired_count`, an `expired_offer_ids` list and an `unlinked_trip_ids` list. `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

Linking an offer to a trip is a separate `TransportManager` operation naming a `backhaul_offer_id` and carrying the `expected_trip_version` guard of §2.8. It is permitted while the trip's `plan_status` is `DRAFT`, `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; a `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` trip refuses the link with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A `PROPOSED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` offer may be pre-linked and simply credits nothing until it is accepted, but a `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` offer can never be linked, and the attempt is `ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACCEPTED`. An offer already linked to a *different* trip is `ERR_BACKHAUL_ALREADY_LINKED`; the trip's own current link is never a conflict, because re-linking is designed to supersede it. On success, any offer previously linked to this trip is unlinked and, if it had been `ACCEPTED`, returned to `PROPOSED`; the trip's `backhaul_offer_id` becomes the new offer; the trip's `backhaul_credit_cents` becomes the new offer's `contribution_margin_cents` when that offer is already `ACCEPTED` and zero otherwise; and the trip's `version` advances.

The market also has a compensating direction. When a trip that links an `ACCEPTED` offer is cancelled, the offer returns `ACCEPTED → PROPOSED` rather than dying with the trip: its `margin_exception_approved` flag resets to false, its `review_round` is left exactly where it was, and its computed margin fields are untouched. The offer re-enters the market at the same point in its negotiation history, which is the whole intent — a cancelled trip is the fleet's problem, not the shipper's.

### 3.7. On the Road: Dispatch, Arrival, Close-Out and Cancellation

A trip carries **two independent status dimensions on the same row**. `plan_status` is the operations dimension and runs `DRAFT`, `PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, with `CANCELLED` as a second terminal outcome. `settlement_status` is the payroll dimension and runs `PENDING`, `ACCRUED`, `SETTLED`, `DISPUTED`, `ADJUSTED`, cycling back to `ACCRUED`. The operations dimension reaches a terminal state while the payroll dimension keeps moving on the very same row, and no transition on one dimension reads the other — with one exception: `PENDING → ACCRUED` on the payroll dimension is fired by the `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` side-effect chain on the operations dimension.

Dispatch is a `TransportManager` act taking the trip `PLANNED → DISPATCHED`. Any other starting `plan_status` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The driver must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE`, and must not be under a `coaching_hold`, otherwise `ERR_DRIVER_COACHING_HOLD` — the hold can have landed in the window between commitment and release. The tractor must still be `ASSIGNED` and its `pm_hold` must still be false, otherwise `ERR_TRACTOR_PM_HOLD`. Hours of service are re-evaluated at this moment against the driver's counters as they now stand, using the same three ceilings and the same drive-duty-weekly order. On success the driver's `hos_status` moves `AVAILABLE → ON_DUTY` and the trip's `version` advances.

Execution then runs stop by stop. Checking in at a stop — a `Driver` acting on their own trip or a `TransportManager` acting on any — takes the stop `PLANNED → ARRIVED` and may record an `actual_arrival_at` instant. The owning trip must be in `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and the stop itself must be in `PLANNED`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A `Driver` whose `X-User-ID` is not the trip's driver is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Stops must be worked in order: every stop of the same trip with a lower `sequence` must already be `COMPLETED` or `SKIPPED`, otherwise the check-in is refused with `ERR_STOP_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT`. The **first** arrival on a trip that is still `DISPATCHED` bubbles the parent upward — the trip moves `DISPATCHED → IN_PROGRESS` as a consequence of the child stop's transition, and the stop's own `version` advances.

Checking out of a stop takes it `ARRIVED → COMPLETED` and may record an `actual_departure_at` instant. It may also correct reality: when `cases_handled` is supplied, the stop's `cases` is overwritten with that value and the stop's `service_minutes` is recomputed from the new case count under the same per-stop rule of §3.4; when `cases_handled` is omitted, both are left alone. Because the case count feeds four trip aggregates, `planned_service_minutes`, `planned_duty_minutes`, `planned_delivery_cases` and `peak_case_load` are all re-derived on the parent trip. A stop that is not `ARRIVED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and a `Driver` acting outside their own trip is again `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Close-out is where the largest cascade in this domain fires. A `Driver` closing their own trip, or a `TransportManager` closing any trip in the fleet, supplies `actual_drive_minutes` and `actual_duty_minutes` — the minutes the day really took. The trip must be `IN_PROGRESS` and every one of its stops must already be in a terminal stop status, that is `COMPLETED` or `SKIPPED`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and a `Driver` closing someone else's trip is `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On success the trip moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and the following happens, all in one request:

- `delivered_cases` is set to the sum of `cases` over the trip's `DELIVERY` stops whose own status is `COMPLETED`.
- Each `COMPLETED` `DELIVERY` stop's replenishment order moves `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED`, and each order that reaches `DELIVERED` lowers its store's `open_scheduled_cases` by that order's `cases` while raising the store's `delivered_cases_total` by the same amount. The ledger movement is caused by the order transition, which is in turn caused by the trip transition.
- The driver's counters absorb the day: `drive_minutes_used_today` rises by `actual_drive_minutes`, and both `duty_minutes_used_today` and `weekly_on_duty_minutes` rise by `actual_duty_minutes`. The driver's `hos_status` returns to `AVAILABLE`.
- The tractor's `odometer_miles` rises by the trip's `total_miles`, and its `miles_since_pm` is then recomputed as the new `odometer_miles` less `pm_last_service_odometer` — the second link caused by the first.
- The **preventive-maintenance projection** runs on that recomputed figure. When `miles_since_pm` has reached or passed the tractor's `pm_interval_miles` — the boundary is inclusive, so a tractor landing exactly on its interval is due — a work order is created for that tractor with `asset_kind` of `TRACTOR`, `auto_generated` true, `status` of `SCHEDULED` and `deferral_count` at zero. Its `service_type` is `PM_B` when `miles_since_pm` has reached or passed twice the interval and `PM_A` otherwise. Its window is pinned to clock-independent boundaries: `window_start_at` is the next UTC midnight after the logical clock's current instant, and `window_end_at` is eight hours after that start. The creation of that order then sets the tractor's `pm_hold` to true — a fourth link, caused by the third. At most one auto-generated PM order exists per tractor at a time: when `pm_hold` is already true the projection creates nothing.
- The tractor and the trailer both move `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE`.
- The payroll dimension moves `PENDING → ACCRUED`, which is described in §3.8.
- The trip's `version` advances.

Cancellation is available from three plan statuses and its cascade differs by starting point, so both shapes are stated in full. From `DRAFT`, cancellation takes the trip `DRAFT → CANCELLED`: every stop in `PLANNED` or `ARRIVED` moves to `SKIPPED`; the tractor and trailer, if non-null, return to `AVAILABLE`; and the trip's `driver_id`, `tractor_id` and `trailer_id` are all cleared to null. No replenishment order is ever `SCHEDULED` while a trip is `DRAFT`, so the order and store-ledger links do **not** fire in this shape. From `PLANNED`, cancellation takes the trip `PLANNED → CANCELLED` with a longer chain: every stop in `PLANNED` or `ARRIVED` moves to `SKIPPED`; each stop that turned `SKIPPED` and carries a `replenishment_order_id` whose order is `SCHEDULED` drives that order `SCHEDULED → UNSCHEDULED`; each order that turned `UNSCHEDULED` lowers its store's `open_scheduled_cases` by its `cases`; the tractor and trailer both move `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE`; the driver's `hos_status` returns to `AVAILABLE`; all three equipment and driver references are cleared to null; a linked `ACCEPTED` offer returns to `PROPOSED` as described in §3.6 while a linked `UNDER_REVIEW` offer stays `UNDER_REVIEW`, and in either case the trip's `backhaul_offer_id` is cleared to null and its `backhaul_credit_cents` to zero; and `pinned_pay_plan_id`, `pinned_pay_plan_version` and `planned_pay_cents` are cleared to null, null and zero respectively. From `DISPATCHED`, cancellation runs that identical `PLANNED` cascade — stops skipped, orders un-scheduled, store ledgers lowered, assets released, offer returned, pin cleared — plus the driver's `hos_status` moving `ON_DUTY → AVAILABLE`. Every shape advances the trip's `version`, and every shape accepts an optional `reason_code`. Cancellation is a `FleetPlanner` or `TransportManager` act; a `COMPLETED` or already-`CANCELLED` trip cannot be cancelled again.

### 3.8. Driver Pay, Settlement and Cost Benchmarking

Driver pay is computed by one engine with **two variants**, and which variant runs depends entirely on where in the lifecycle the computation is asked for. Both variants read the same pay plan: once a trip has been committed, that is the plan named by `pinned_pay_plan_id` at the version recorded in `pinned_pay_plan_version`, and never the driver's current plan; while a trip is still `DRAFT` and has therefore pinned nothing, the plan is the one the driver currently holds through their `pay_plan_id`. The engine has a pinned evaluation order — mileage, then activity, then hourly, then bonus, then the subtotal, then the guarantee — and each component is rounded half up to whole cents on its own line *before* the summation, so the guarantee is compared against the rounded subtotal and never against an unrounded intermediate.

The **mileage component** is the trip's `total_miles` multiplied by the plan's `mileage_cents_per_mile`, rounded half up.

The **activity component** depends on the variant, because the two variants disagree about which stops count. Under `FINAL_SETTLEMENT`, a paid stop is one whose status is `COMPLETED` and whose `stop_type` is `DELIVERY`, `VENDOR_PICKUP` or `THIRD_PARTY_PICKUP`, and a drop-hook stop is one whose status is `COMPLETED` and whose `stop_type` is `DROP_OFF`. Under `PLANNED_ACCRUAL`, a paid stop is one whose status is `PLANNED`, `ARRIVED` or `COMPLETED` with those same three stop types, and a drop-hook stop is one in those same three statuses with `stop_type` of `DROP_OFF`. `FUEL` stops are never paid activity under either variant. The activity component is then the number of paid stops multiplied by the plan's `stop_cents_per_stop`, plus the number of drop-hook stops multiplied by the plan's `drop_hook_cents`.

The **hourly component** prices detention. Take the duty basis — under `FINAL_SETTLEMENT` that is the trip's `actual_duty_minutes`, and under `PLANNED_ACCRUAL` it is the trip's `planned_duty_minutes` — subtract the trip's `planned_duty_minutes`, and subtract a further thirty minutes of grace. If the result is negative, the detention is zero. The hourly component is those detention minutes multiplied by the plan's `hourly_cents_per_minute`. Note the structural consequence: under `PLANNED_ACCRUAL` the duty basis *is* the planned duty figure, so detention is always zero and the hourly component is always zero.

The **backhaul bonus** applies only when the trip's `backhaul_offer_id` is non-null **and** that offer's status is `ACCEPTED`. In that case the bonus is the sum of the mileage component and the activity component, multiplied by the plan's `backhaul_bonus_pct`, rounded half up; otherwise the bonus is zero. The bonus base deliberately excludes the hourly component — detention is not bonus-bearing.

The **subtotal** is the sum of the mileage, activity, hourly and bonus components. The **guarantee makeup** is the plan's `minimum_daily_cents` less that subtotal when the plan floor is higher, and zero when the subtotal already clears the floor. The **gross** is the subtotal plus the guarantee makeup.

`PLANNED_ACCRUAL` runs when the schedule is committed and writes the result to the trip's `planned_pay_cents`. Because it counts every non-skipped stop as paid and cannot produce detention, it yields an amount at or below the final settlement whenever a trip ran long, and above it whenever stops were skipped. `FINAL_SETTLEMENT` runs at close-out and at re-accrual, writes the trip's `driver_pay_cents`, and is the only variant ever persisted on a pay statement. Either variant may also be evaluated on demand, without persisting anything, by naming it in a `variant` parameter on the trip's pay preview; that read returns the `variant` it evaluated, the `pay_plan_id` and `pay_plan_version` it used, and the full breakdown as `mileage_cents`, `activity_cents`, `hourly_cents`, `bonus_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `guarantee_makeup_cents` and `gross_cents`. It is the channel that makes the divergence between the two variants observable.

When close-out advances the payroll dimension `PENDING → ACCRUED`, three things happen. The trip's `driver_pay_cents` is set from the `FINAL_SETTLEMENT` variant. The trip's `backhaul_credit_cents` is set to the linked offer's `contribution_margin_cents` when that offer's status is `ACCEPTED`, and to zero otherwise. And a pay statement is created: its `driver_id` copied from the trip's driver, its `trip_id` naming the settled trip, its `pay_plan_id` and `pay_plan_version` copied from the trip's pin rather than from anything current, its `variant` set to `FINAL_SETTLEMENT` — the only value that field ever holds — its component fields `mileage_cents`, `activity_cents`, `hourly_cents`, `bonus_cents` and `guarantee_makeup_cents` taken from the calculation, its `gross_cents` being the sum of those five, its `status` at `ACCRUED`, and its `settlement_reference` null until it is paid. The created statement then raises the driver's domicile `accrued_pay_cents` by `gross_cents` and the driver's own `ytd_gross_cents` by `gross_cents` — a second link caused by the statement's creation. The pin is what makes this stable: a later pay-plan amendment moves that plan's `version` but never re-prices this statement.

Settlement is a `PayrollAnalyst` act that records a `settlement_reference` and takes the statement `ACCRUED → SETTLED`, and in the same request takes the trip's payroll dimension `ACCRUED → SETTLED`. A statement that is not `ACCRUED` is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_ACCRUED`, and a statement whose trip's payroll dimension is not `ACCRUED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A statement whose `gross_cents` is zero cannot be paid at all and is refused with `ERR_ZERO_PAY_SETTLEMENT`. The domicile's `accrued_pay_cents` does **not** move on settlement, because both `ACCRUED` and `SETTLED` sit inside that ledger's status filter.

Disputes are opened by the driver being paid, or by a `PayrollAnalyst` acting on the driver's behalf, on a trip whose payroll dimension is `ACCRUED` or `SETTLED`; an optional `dispute_note` may be recorded. The dimension moves to `DISPUTED` — from `ACCRUED` or from `SETTLED`, both are valid origins — no monetary field changes at all, and the trip's `version` advances. `plan_status` is untouched, which is exactly the point of running two dimensions on one row. Any other payroll status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and a `Driver` disputing a trip that is not theirs is `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Reversal is the money unwind, a `PayrollAnalyst` act with an optional `reason_code`, and it is valid from either `ACCRUED` or `SETTLED`; both origins produce an identical cascade. The statement moves to `REVERSED`, which is terminal for that statement. That reversal then lowers the domicile's `accrued_pay_cents` by the statement's `gross_cents` and lowers the driver's `ytd_gross_cents` by the same amount. And it drives the trip's payroll dimension to `ADJUSTED` while zeroing the trip's `driver_pay_cents`. A statement in any other status is `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_ACCRUED`.

Re-accrual closes the cycle. A `PayrollAnalyst` supplies corrected `actual_drive_minutes` and `actual_duty_minutes` for a trip whose payroll dimension is `DISPUTED` or `ADJUSTED` — any other origin is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — whose `plan_status` is `COMPLETED`, and none of whose statements is still in `ACCRUED` or `SETTLED`. The trip's `actual_drive_minutes` and `actual_duty_minutes` are overwritten with the corrected values, `driver_pay_cents` is recomputed under `FINAL_SETTLEMENT` on those corrected minutes and on the **original** pinned pay plan version, a *new* statement is created in `ACCRUED` status raising both the domicile `accrued_pay_cents` and the driver `ytd_gross_cents` by its `gross_cents`, the payroll dimension returns to `ACCRUED`, and the trip's `version` advances. The pin is deliberately not refreshed, so a superseded pay plan still governs the corrected settlement. If the pinned plan row no longer resolves at all, the re-accrual is refused with `ERR_REFERENTIAL_INCOMPLETE`. Because the payroll dimension has no terminal state, a trip whose operations dimension reached `COMPLETED` long ago can keep cycling `ACCRUED → SETTLED → DISPUTED → ADJUSTED → ACCRUED` indefinitely.

The **cost benchmark** is the read that answers whether running the fleet beats buying dedicated carriage. A `TransportManager` or `PayrollAnalyst` names a `variant` and a `window_start` and `window_end` pair, and nothing is persisted. Three inclusion filters are all mandatory: a trip enters the benchmark only if its `domicile_id` is the domicile being examined, its `plan_status` is `COMPLETED`, its `settlement_status` is `ACCRUED` or `SETTLED`, and its `planned_start_at` falls at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end` — a half-open window. Trips in `ADJUSTED` settlement are excluded on purpose, so a reversal removes a trip from the benchmark entirely until it is re-accrued. Included trips are accumulated in `created_at` ascending order for determinism, and their count is reported as `trip_count`.

From that set: `total_miles` is the sum of the included trips' `total_miles`. `total_cases` is the sum of `cases` over those trips' stops whose status is `COMPLETED` and whose `stop_type` is `DELIVERY`. `driver_pay_cents` is the sum of `gross_cents` over those trips' pay statements whose own status is `ACCRUED` or `SETTLED`, so reversed statements contribute nothing. `fuel_cents` is `total_miles` multiplied by 62 cents per mile, rounded half up. `maintenance_cents` is `total_miles` multiplied by 19 cents per mile, rounded half up. `backhaul_credit_cents` is the sum of `contribution_margin_cents` over the offers linked to those trips whose offer status is `ACCEPTED`. `variable_cost_cents` is driver pay plus fuel plus maintenance, **less** the backhaul credit — the backhaul earns its keep by reducing the cost base.

`window_days` is the number of whole 24-hour days between `window_start` and `window_end`, taking the floor and never reporting fewer than 1. `fixed_cost_cents` is `window_days` multiplied by the sum of the domicile's `fixed_ownership_cents_per_day` and `overhead_cents_per_day`. Under the `FULLY_ALLOCATED` variant — the comparison to make against a dedicated-carriage quote, since a carrier's quote embeds its own equipment and overhead — `total_cost_cents` is `variable_cost_cents` plus `fixed_cost_cents`. Under the `INCREMENTAL_VARIABLE` variant — the comparison to make when deciding whether to take one more load on equipment already owned — `total_cost_cents` is `variable_cost_cents` alone, with all fixed and overhead cost excluded. For any window with a positive fixed rate the fully-allocated variant therefore reports strictly higher per-unit costs and a strictly lower advantage than the incremental variant.

Before either division is attempted, the zero-volume guard runs: if `total_cases` is zero or `total_miles` is zero, the read fails with `ERR_BENCHMARK_NO_VOLUME`. Otherwise `cost_per_case_cents` is `total_cost_cents` divided by `total_cases`, rounded half up, and `cost_per_mile_cents` is `total_cost_cents` divided by `total_miles`, rounded half up — the only two divisions in the whole benchmark. `dedicated_equivalent_cents_per_case` is simply the domicile's `dedicated_rate_cents_per_case`, and `advantage_cents_per_case` is that dedicated rate less the fleet's own `cost_per_case_cents`, so a positive advantage means the private fleet is beating dedicated carriage on cases. The response echoes the `variant`, `window_start`, `window_end` and `window_days` it evaluated alongside every figure above, and the domicile's `dedicated_rate_cents_per_mile` remains available on the domicile record for the analyst's per-mile comparison.

### 3.9. Safety Telematics, Coaching Holds and the Route Bid Board

Onboard telematics feeds a stream of exception events into the system, each recorded by a `SafetyManager` against a `driver_id`. An event may name a `trip_id`, but that field is nullable: hard braking in a yard between trips is still a coachable event, and it is recorded with no trip at all. The `event_type` is one of `HARD_BRAKE`, `SPEEDING`, `FOLLOWING_DISTANCE` or `SEATBELT`, the `severity` is an integer from 1 to 3 inclusive, and `occurred_at` records when the exception happened — a field used for reporting and ordering only, never as a filter on the score.

Each event carries `penalty_points`, computed once at creation as a per-type base multiplied by the `severity`. The bases are 2 for `HARD_BRAKE`, 3 for `SPEEDING`, 2 for `FOLLOWING_DISTANCE` and 4 for `SEATBELT`, so a severity-3 seatbelt exception is worth twelve points while a severity-1 hard brake is worth two. Those points are fixed at creation and are never recomputed afterwards, whatever else changes.

An event also tracks its own coaching state through `coached`, which starts false, together with `coached_at` and `coached_by_user_id`, which start null. That flag is the event's whole lifecycle: an event is in the `NOT_COACHED` condition while `coached` is false and in the `COACHED` condition once it is true, and `COACHED` is terminal — there is no un-coaching. From those three fields the driver's standing is derived. `open_penalty_points` is the sum of `penalty_points` across every one of that driver's events whose `coached` flag is still false — with no date cutoff and no restriction to any trip, so a yard event with a null trip counts exactly as much as an on-road one. `safety_score` is one hundred less those open points, floored at zero so it never goes negative. `coaching_hold` is true exactly when `safety_score` is strictly below 70, which is to say when open points exceed thirty.

Creating an event recomputes all three driver fields immediately, and if that recomputation flips `coaching_hold` from false to true, a cascade fires at once against everything the driver is currently holding. Every trip of that driver whose `plan_status` is `DRAFT` or `PLANNED` is stripped: its `driver_id`, `tractor_id` and `trailer_id` are cleared to null, its `pinned_pay_plan_id` and `pinned_pay_plan_version` are cleared to null, its `planned_pay_cents` is reset to zero, and any trip that had reached `PLANNED` is pushed backwards to `DRAFT` — the one transition in the operations dimension that ever runs in reverse. The released tractor and trailer each move `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE`. Every `DELIVERY` stop on those trips releases its replenishment order `SCHEDULED → UNSCHEDULED`, each release lowering that store's `open_scheduled_cases` by the order's `cases`, and the stops themselves go to `SKIPPED`. Each affected trip's `version` advances. In parallel, every one of the driver's route bids still in `SUBMITTED` is moved to `PASSED_OVER`, and each such bid lowers its posting's `bid_count` by one.

Coaching is the counterweight. A `SafetyManager` coaches a single event, optionally recording a `coaching_note`. The event must not already be coached — a second attempt is `ERR_ALREADY_COACHED` — and the driver must not be `TERMINATED`, which is `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Coaching sets the event's `coached` to true, stamps `coached_at` from the logical clock and `coached_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, lowers the driver's `open_penalty_points` by that event's `penalty_points`, recomputes `safety_score`, and clears `coaching_hold` when the recomputed score reaches 70 or above. The driver's `version` advances. The asymmetry is deliberate and permanent: coaching restores points and can lift the hold, but it does not restore the trips that were stripped or the bids that were passed over. Those must be rebuilt by hand.

Open route positions are published as postings by a `TransportManager`. A posting carries a `route_code` unique across the whole system, a `domicile_id` that fixes which drivers are eligible to bid — only drivers domiciled there — a `weekly_miles` and `weekly_stops` pair used for reporting only, a `start_effective_date` in year-month-day form, and a `bid_close_at` instant after which bidding is judged. Its `status` runs `OPEN`, then either `AWARDED` or `VACANT` or `CANCELLED`; `awarded_driver_id` starts null; `bid_count` starts at zero; and `created_by_user_id` records who published it, because that identity is later checked against whoever awards it.

A `Driver` bids on a posting by naming a `route_posting_id`. The bidder is always taken from the authenticated `X-User-ID` and never from the request body, so no driver can bid on another's behalf. Because a posting is a contended parent, a bid must also carry `expected_posting_version`, and a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_POSTING_VERSION`. The posting must be `OPEN` or the bid is `ERR_POSTING_NOT_OPEN`. A driver may hold only one `SUBMITTED` bid per posting; a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID`, though a driver whose earlier bid was withdrawn or passed over may bid again. The eligibility gates apply at bid time as well as at award time: a driver who is not `ACTIVE` is `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE`, a driver under a hold is `ERR_DRIVER_COACHING_HOLD`, a driver whose `safety_score` is below the award threshold is `ERR_DRIVER_SAFETY_INELIGIBLE`, and a driver domiciled elsewhere is `ERR_DOMICILE_MISMATCH`. Each accepted bid records `seniority_date_snapshot` and `safety_score_snapshot`, copies of the driver's `seniority_date` and `safety_score` as they stood at bid time, kept for audit only. Its `status` starts at `SUBMITTED` and may become `AWARDED`, `PASSED_OVER` or `WITHDRAWN`. Creation raises the posting's `bid_count` by one and its `version` by one. A driver may withdraw their own bid — anyone else's is `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — which moves it `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`, lowers the posting's `bid_count` by one, and advances the posting's `version`.

Awarding is a `TransportManager` act with three preconditions. The posting must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_POSTING_NOT_OPEN`. The logical clock must have reached `bid_close_at`, and an award attempted while bidding is still open is `ERR_BID_WINDOW_OPEN`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the posting's `created_by_user_id`; the manager who published a route may not award it, which is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The award itself runs a selection over the posting's bids. The candidate pool is every bid of this posting whose status is `SUBMITTED` and whose driver, read **live** at award time, is `ACTIVE`, has `coaching_hold` false, has a `safety_score` of 80 or above, and has a `domicile_id` equal to the posting's `domicile_id`. The live driver row governs: the `safety_score_snapshot` and `seniority_date_snapshot` stored on the bid are audit records and are never consulted by the selection, so a driver whose score has decayed since bidding is dropped and a driver whose score has recovered is admitted. Candidates are then ranked by `seniority_date` ascending, the earlier date being the more senior claim. Ties on seniority are broken by `safety_score` descending, and any remaining tie by the bid's own `created_at` ascending, first bid winning. The row `id` is never used as a tie-break. The winning bid moves `SUBMITTED → AWARDED`, every other `SUBMITTED` bid moves to `PASSED_OVER`, the posting's `awarded_driver_id` is set to the winner's driver, the posting moves `OPEN → AWARDED`, its `bid_count` is set outright to zero, and its `version` advances.

An empty candidate pool is not an error. If the posting drew no bids at all, or if every bid it drew is ineligible on the live driver's standing, the posting moves `OPEN → VACANT` and the request succeeds with HTTP 200. `awarded_driver_id` stays null, every `SUBMITTED` bid is moved to `PASSED_OVER` anyway so no bid is left dangling, and `bid_count` is zeroed. A vacant posting is the recorded outcome of a real award attempt, distinct from a posting that was never judged. Cancelling a posting is the other terminal path: an `OPEN` posting moves to `CANCELLED` with an optional `reason_code`, every `SUBMITTED` bid is moved to `WITHDRAWN` rather than `PASSED_OVER` because no judging occurred, and `bid_count` is zeroed. Note the three different ways `bid_count` moves: individual bid creation and withdrawal adjust it by one, the coaching-hold cascade and driver termination lower it by one per bid they release, and awarding or cancelling a posting zeroes it outright.
