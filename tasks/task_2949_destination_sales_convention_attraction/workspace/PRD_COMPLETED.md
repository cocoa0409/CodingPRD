# Destination Sales & Convention Attraction API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Winning a citywide convention is not a sale that one organization can close alone. A convention and visitors bureau must simultaneously persuade a meeting planner that the destination is worth their delegates, persuade the convention center to hold exhibit space on dates that are still two or three years out, and persuade a dozen competing hotels to pledge rooms at a rate the planner will accept — all before a single contract exists. Today that coordination lives in spreadsheets, e-mail threads and a legacy CRM whose room-night counters no one fully trusts. The bureau's board, which funds the effort out of lodging-tax receipts, cannot answer the only question it actually cares about: how many room nights did the incentive money buy, and what did each of those room nights cost?

This document specifies the Destination Sales & Convention Attraction service, a single backend of record for the bureau's entire attraction pipeline. It carries a citywide convention lead from first qualification through date-and-space hold coordination with the convention center, room-block commitment assembly across the hotel community with rate ceilings and cutoff alignment, bid package preparation with incentives and community support letters, economic impact estimation that justifies every dollar of incentive spend, definite-booking conversion and lost-business analysis by reason, on-site servicing across housing, transportation and volunteer workstreams, and finally board reporting on room-night production against the lodging-tax fund that paid for it.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every temporal rule reads a server-controlled logical clock**, never wall-clock time, so that lead-time gates, hold expiries, challenge deadlines and reporting windows are all reproducible. Second, **derived figures are never trusted from the client**: coverage percentages, incentive caps, admin fees, attrition damages and board rollups are all recomputed by the server from primary records, and the fund's spendable balance is a conserved quantity that must reconcile after every commit, decommit and disbursement.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every endpoint of this service is mounted at the root path — there is no versioning prefix or product namespace in front of the collection names.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based and there is no session, cookie or token exchange. Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. Header values are compared with surrounding whitespace trimmed. If either header is absent, present but empty or holding only whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the eight roles enumerated below — the role name is matched exactly, so its casing must be identical — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check precedes every other check in the system, including request-schema validation and path resolution; only the transport-level dispatch of the request comes earlier, so a path matching no declared route answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404 and a declared path called with an undeclared method answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405, in both cases without the headers being inspected at all.

The eight roles are `BureauAdmin`, `SalesManager`, `HotelPartner`, `CenterScheduler`, `MeetingPlanner`, `IncentiveApprover`, `ServicesManager` and `BoardAnalyst`. Each endpoint in Section 4 lists the roles authorized to call it; a well-authenticated caller whose role is not on that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

Two of the eight roles are **entity-backed**, meaning their `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary operator string but the identifier of a persisted row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `HotelPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Hotel`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Hotel` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: a hotel partner may answer, withdraw from, or report nightly pickup against a room block commitment only when that commitment's `hotel_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `MeetingPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `MeetingPlanner`. Ownership checks compare the resource's planner foreign key to `X-User-ID`: a planner may answer a bid package only when the `planner_id` of the opportunity behind that bid package equals the caller's `X-User-ID`.

Ownership violations return `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403. The acting `X-User-ID` is itself never resolved to a row, so a hotel partner or planner whose header names an identifier no live row carries simply fails the comparison with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` rather than producing a 404. This ownership check guards exactly four operations: the hotel's offer on a commitment, the hotel's withdrawal from a commitment, the hotel's nightly pickup reporting on a commitment, and the planner's response to a submitted bid package. No other operation performs an ownership comparison.

The remaining six roles — `BureauAdmin`, `SalesManager`, `CenterScheduler`, `IncentiveApprover`, `ServicesManager` and `BoardAnalyst` — carry an **opaque** identity. For each of them, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example as `prepared_by_user_id` and `incentive_requested_by_user_id` on a bid package when a `SalesManager` acts, or as `decided_by_user_id` on an incentive decision when an `IncentiveApprover` acts) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded operator identifiers are nevertheless compared *to each other* — see the segregation-of-duties principle in Section 2.11.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty further characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the host's wall clock rather than from the logical clock of Section 2.4, and it is the only instant anywhere in the system that is.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated by offset. The `limit` query parameter defaults to 10 when omitted, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100, and the `offset` query parameter defaults to 0 with a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400 rather than being silently clamped, and so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer literal at all — a decimal such as `2.5`, a non-numeric word, or an empty value.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest row appears first and a listing that pages forward walks the collection in the order it was recorded. Because the logical clock of Section 2.4 only moves when a client moves it, rows very commonly share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were recorded, and never by `id`. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` set to true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Filters offered by a list endpoint are combined conjunctively and are applied before pagination, so `total_count` always reflects the filtered set. A filter whose value names an enum — `status`, `tier`, `engagement_status`, `participation_status`, `hall_status`, `sales_stage`, `hold_posture`, `hold_rank`, `letter_type`, `variant`, `category`, `decision` and `posture` — is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400 when the supplied value is not a declared member of that enum, and `fiscal_year` is rejected the same way when it is not an integer; a filter naming an identifier that matches no row is by contrast perfectly legal and simply yields an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than a 404. Query parameters an endpoint does not offer are ignored.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the system is identified by a server-minted UUID exposed as `id`. Identifiers carry no type prefix and clients never supply them on creation. Most resources also carry three further common properties: `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant the row was recorded, rendered with exactly three fractional digits; `updated_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, rendered the same way; and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of that row. Both `created_at` and `updated_at` are read from the logical clock described below and never from the host's wall clock, so two rows recorded without an intervening clock move carry byte-identical timestamps, and a mutation made without moving the clock leaves `updated_at` equal to `created_at` while still advancing `version`.

Five resource kinds are deliberately append-only records of a single event and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — for these, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response payload entirely: support letters, incentive decisions, economic impact estimates, nightly pickup records, and lodging tax receipts. Every other resource kind — meeting planners, hotels, center halls, opportunities, space holds, room block commitments, bid packages, incentive funds, servicing orders and board reports — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

All temporal reasoning in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and before any client sets it the server must read that default rather than falling back to the real time of day. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to an explicit UTC instant supplied as `to_iso8601`, and answers with the resulting instant under the key `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity; it simply changes the instant against which lead-time gates, hold expiries, challenge deadlines, response deadlines and reporting-window filters are subsequently evaluated. Every field described below as "stamped from the clock" — `last_touch_at`, `definite_at`, `expires_at`, `challenge_deadline`, `submitted_at`, `received_at`, `computed_at`, `published_at` — takes its value from this logical clock.

Two distinct temporal shapes appear in request bodies and must not be confused. **Dates** — `arrival_date`, `departure_date`, `cutoff_date`, `night_date`, `period_start`, `period_end` — are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component. **Instants** — `move_in_start`, `move_out_end`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `response_due_at`, `received_at`, `to_iso8601` — are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with a trailing `Z`. The fractional-second part of an inbound instant is optional and may carry anywhere from one to nine digits, which the server retains to microsecond precision while always rendering the value back with exactly three fractional digits; a numeric UTC offset such as `+00:00` written in place of the literal `Z`, a bare date with no time part, or any other shape is a schema violation. Where a date is compared against the clock, the date is interpreted at `00:00:00Z` on that day.

### 2.5. Money, Rounding and Aggregate Precision

Every monetary field in this system is a decimal amount with at most two fraction digits, and every monetary computation rounds to two decimal places using round-half-up. A request body carrying a money value with more than two fraction digits is a schema violation. Money travels as a JSON number in both directions: in a request body a quoted numeral such as `"250.00"`, a boolean, or any other non-numeric JSON type in a money field is a schema violation, and in a response a monetary field is likewise a JSON number rather than a quoted string, so an amount whose cents are zero renders as `100.0` rather than as `"100.00"`.

Three rounding disciplines matter enough to state globally, because getting them backwards produces answers that are wrong by cents:

1. **Component-level rounding precedes summation.** Where a total is defined as the sum of named components — the three spend components of an economic impact estimate, or the per-commitment damages of an attrition settlement — each component is rounded to two decimals *first* and the rounded components are then added. Summing raw products and rounding once at the end is incorrect.
2. **Selection bands are evaluated on the input they name, never on the output.** Where a rate or fee band is chosen by comparing a value against thresholds, the band is selected from the value the rule names and is never re-derived after the fee has been added.
3. **Counts use floor, scores are integers, and person counts round half-up.** Where a formula divides to produce a whole number of room nights it takes the floor; relationship scores are integers with no decimal component; and the one place where a person count is derived from a room count rounds half-up to a whole person.

Percentages and ratios that the server derives — `block_coverage_pct` above all, and `tax_payback_ratio` with it — are stored and returned with two decimal places, as JSON numbers on exactly the same rendering rule as money. Whole-day and whole-room quantities — `event_nights`, `cutoff_days_before_arrival`, `citywide_cutoff_days_before_arrival`, `cutoff_alignment_spread_days`, `committed_peak_rooms`, `rooms_peak_committed` — are integers.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write operations in this system create a child row whose correctness depends on a parent whose state other actors are also moving. In all three cases a strictly later request would legitimately succeed — a second bid package may properly supersede a declined predecessor, a second hotel invitation is a normal act of block assembly, and a second incentive decision against the same fund is routine committee work — so the system cannot distinguish a good request from a stale one by inspecting the target's state alone. The only sanctioned guard is an explicit version check against the parent, supplied by the client as a required, non-optional field in the request body:

- **Creating a bid package** (`POST /bid-packages`) requires `expected_opportunity_version` carrying the `version` of the target `Opportunity` that the client last observed. Every block invitation, commit, withdrawal and sales-stage change bumps that opportunity's `version`, so a client preparing a bid from a stale read would price the package against a citywide block that no longer exists. If `expected_opportunity_version` does not equal the opportunity's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OPPORTUNITY_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- **Inviting a hotel into the citywide block** (`POST /room-block-commitments`) likewise requires `expected_opportunity_version` matching the target opportunity's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OPPORTUNITY_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Assembling a block from a stale coverage view is precisely what this check prevents.
- **Recording an incentive decision** (`POST /bid-packages/{id}/incentive-decisions`) requires `expected_fund_version` carrying the `version` of the named `IncentiveFund` that the approver last observed. Decisions on other bid packages, posted lodging-tax receipts and every decommit all bump the fund's `version`, so a decision quoting an outdated fund version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` and HTTP 412 rather than charged against a balance that has already moved.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. An `expected_opportunity_version` or `expected_fund_version` key is not part of the input schema of any operation other than the three named above.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and the Order in Which Rules Are Evaluated

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and resolution completes before any body field is examined. For a nested path such as the pickup-record collection under a commitment, the commitment identifier is resolved first; only once it resolves does the server look at the body. Each resource kind has its own dedicated not-found code, all carrying HTTP 404: `ERR_PLANNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPACE_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICING_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BOARD_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. The same codes are reused for foreign keys supplied in a request body: `planner_id`, `supersedes_opportunity_id`, `opportunity_id`, `center_hall_id`, `hotel_id`, `bid_package_id`, `fund_id`, `challenging_space_hold_id` and `room_block_commitment_id` must all resolve to live rows, and a dangling reference returns the not-found code of the referenced kind.

When a single request violates several rules at once, the answer is deterministic: the rule with the lowest position in the following ladder wins, and the request carries that rule's error code. The ladder is global — it is not re-ordered per endpoint — and every rule below applies only to the operations named next to it.

1. Both authentication headers present, non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming a declared role — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401, on every endpoint.
2. The acting role authorized for the called endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403, on every endpoint.
3. Entity-backed ownership of the target resource — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403, on the commitment offer, commitment withdrawal, pickup-record creation and planner-response operations only.
4. Request-body schema conformance — `ERR_VALIDATION`, 400, on every endpoint. This covers required fields being present, enum values being declared members, dates being `YYYY-MM-DD`, instants being ISO-8601 UTC with a trailing `Z`, money having at most two fraction digits and being strictly positive where the field demands it, integers falling inside their declared ranges, `arrival_date` being strictly earlier than `departure_date`, `move_in_start` strictly earlier than `move_out_end`, `window_start` strictly earlier than `window_end`, `period_start` strictly earlier than `period_end`, and pagination `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive with `offset` at least 0. Four conventions of this rung are worth stating because the whole surface shares them. A field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as though it had been omitted, so a required field explicitly set to null fails as a missing field while an optional one falls back to its documented default. Body keys the operation does not name are ignored rather than rejected, so an unrecognised field is never itself a violation. Integer fields must be JSON integer literals, which makes `5.0`, `"5"` and `true` all violations in a field declared as a count. And a request body that is absent, empty or unparseable is treated as an empty object — its required fields then simply report as missing — whereas a body that parses to something other than a JSON object, an array or a bare string for instance, is itself a violation.
5. Every path identifier resolving, left to right — the matching 404 code, on every path containing an identifier segment. Because path resolution completes before any body field or query parameter is examined, as stated at the head of this section, an endpoint carrying an identifier segment reaches this rung ahead of rungs 3 and 4; the effective order on such an endpoint is authentication, role authorization, path resolution, ownership, body-schema conformance, the body's foreign keys, and then everything below. So a hotel partner naming a commitment that does not exist is answered `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and a nested collection listed under a parent that does not exist answers the parent's 404 even when `limit` is out of range as well.
6. Every foreign key in the body resolving — the matching 404 code, on every creation that carries a foreign key.
7. `expected_opportunity_version` equal to the target opportunity's `version` — `ERR_STALE_OPPORTUNITY_VERSION`, 412, on bid-package creation and room-block-commitment creation.
8. `expected_fund_version` equal to the target fund's `version` — `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION`, 412, on incentive-decision creation.
9. The opportunity's `sales_stage` being a declared from-state of the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_OPPORTUNITY_STAGE`, 409, on qualify, lose, space-hold creation, room-block-commitment creation, commitment commit, bid-package creation and servicing-order creation. Two further operations also consult the owning lead's stage and answer the same code, but deliberately out of ladder order — after their own target's state gate rather than at this rung: a commitment withdrawal refuses a lead that has already reached `SERVICED` only once the commitment itself is in a permitted from-status, and a bid submission refuses a lead that is no longer `QUALIFIED` only once the package itself is `APPROVED`.
10. The space hold's `status` being a declared from-state — `ERR_INVALID_HOLD_STATE`, 409, on hold challenge and hold release.
11. The commitment's `status` being a declared from-state — `ERR_INVALID_COMMITMENT_STATE`, 409, on offer, commit, withdraw and pickup-record creation.
12. The bid package's `status` being a declared from-state — `ERR_INVALID_BID_STATE`, 409, on support-letter attachment, incentive request, incentive decision, submission and planner response.
13. The servicing order's `status` being a declared from-state — `ERR_INVALID_SERVICING_STATE`, 409, on schedule, deliver and cancel.
14. The board report still being in `DRAFT` — `ERR_BOARD_REPORT_PUBLISHED`, 409, on report publication.
15. Segregation of duties on the deciding officer — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403, on incentive-decision creation.
16. The hall's `hall_status` being `AVAILABLE` — `ERR_HALL_UNAVAILABLE`, 409, on space-hold creation.
17. The hotel's `participation_status` being `PARTICIPATING` — `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_PARTICIPATING`, 409, on room-block-commitment creation, offer and commit.
18. The minimum lead time before arrival — `ERR_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT`, 422, on qualify.
19. The target hold not having lapsed — `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`, 409, on hold challenge and hold release.
20. The planner answering before the response deadline — `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, 422, on planner response.
21. No confirmed hold conflicting with the candidate's buffered window — `ERR_HALL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409, on space-hold creation.
22. Fewer than the permitted number of live holds already conflicting — `ERR_HALL_HOLD_LIMIT_REACHED`, 409, on space-hold creation.
23. The pickup night lying inside the block's occupied nights and not already reported — `ERR_PICKUP_NIGHT_OUT_OF_RANGE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_PICKUP_NIGHT`, 422, on pickup-record creation.
24. The challenge being well-formed — `ERR_CHALLENGE_TARGET_INVALID`, 422, on hold challenge.
25. The hall being large enough for the event — `ERR_HALL_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT`, 422, on space-hold creation.
26. No duplicate live commitment for the same opportunity and hotel — `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMMITMENT`, 409, on room-block-commitment creation.
27. The offered rate within the ceiling and the offered rooms within the property's inventory — `ERR_RATE_CEILING_EXCEEDED`, 422, on offer and commit.
28. The hotel's per-night committed aggregate staying inside its inventory — `ERR_HOTEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 422, on commit.
29. An active space hold existing at submission time — `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_SPACE_HOLD`, 422, on submission.
30. Enough distinct support-letter types — `ERR_SUPPORT_LETTERS_INSUFFICIENT`, 422, on submission.
31. Citywide coverage at or above the floor and cutoff spread inside tolerance — `ERR_BLOCK_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` or `ERR_CUTOFF_MISALIGNED`, 422, on submission.
32. At most one live bid package per opportunity — `ERR_BID_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`, 409, on bid-package creation.
33. The requested estimate variant being available — `ERR_ESTIMATE_VARIANT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, 422, on economic-impact-estimate creation.
34. The fund not being exhausted — `ERR_FUND_EXHAUSTED`, 409, on incentive-decision creation.
35. The approved amount within the recomputed cap — `ERR_INCENTIVE_EXCEEDS_CAP`, 422, on incentive-decision creation.
36. The total charge within the fund's uncommitted balance — `ERR_FUND_INSUFFICIENT`, 422, on incentive-decision creation.
37. The tax payback ratio at or above the floor — `ERR_TAX_PAYBACK_BELOW_FLOOR`, 422, on incentive-decision creation.

The practical consequence worth internalising: authorization always beats everything below it, the existence of the path's own targets beats both ownership and schema validation, schema validation beats the existence of the foreign keys carried in the body, existence always beats concurrency, concurrency always beats state, state always beats business safety, and the fiscal gates on an incentive decision are the very last things checked — so an approver who is also the preparer learns about the segregation-of-duties violation before ever learning whether the fund could have afforded the charge.

### 2.8. The Two Independent Dimensions of an Opportunity

A citywide lead advances along two state dimensions at once, recorded on the same `Opportunity` row and evolving independently of each other.

`sales_stage` is the pipeline dimension. It begins at `LEAD` and moves through `QUALIFIED`, `BIDDING`, `DEFINITE` and `SERVICED`, or leaves the pipeline at `LOST`. `SERVICED` and `LOST` are terminal: no transition leaves either of them.

`hold_posture` is the space dimension. It begins at `NONE` and takes the values `TENTATIVE`, `CHALLENGED`, `CONFIRMED` and `RELEASED`. It has no terminal state — a released posture can return to `TENTATIVE` when replacement space is held. Crucially, `hold_posture` is driven **only** by the side effects of space holds; qualifying a lead, inviting a hotel, committing a block or requesting an incentive never touch it. Conversely, placing or releasing a hold never moves `sales_stage` except in the one cascade explicitly described in Section 3.4, where an opportunity in `BIDDING` that loses its last active hold falls to `LOST`.

Because both dimensions live on one row, a mutation of either bumps the same `version` counter and refreshes the same `updated_at`. An opportunity in `sales_stage` `QUALIFIED` with `hold_posture` `CHALLENGED` is an entirely ordinary state, and so is `DEFINITE` with `CONFIRMED`, or `LOST` with `RELEASED`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived Aggregates

No derived figure in this system is ever supplied by a client, and no derived figure is ever left stale. Whenever a primary record changes, every aggregate downstream of it is recomputed in the same request, before the response is written. This applies to the room and coverage aggregates on hotels and opportunities, the confirmed-hold counter on halls, the pickup and attrition figures on commitments and opportunities, the relationship scores and tier on planners, and the balances and posture on incentive funds.

Cascades are described in the workflow sections as numbered hops, and the numbering is meaningful: hop 1 is the operation the client asked for, hop 2 is the effect that operation has on a neighbouring entity, hop 3 is the effect *that* change has in turn, and so on. Every hop that mutates a versioned row bumps that row's `version` by one and refreshes its `updated_at` — including rows the client never named. A single commitment withdrawal can therefore touch the commitment, its hotel, its opportunity, a submitted bid package and an incentive fund, and each of those five rows will show an advanced `version`. Where several hops of one request land on the *same* row, though, that row does not accumulate one increment per hop: the workflow section that owns the operation states the increment it leaves on the row it is about — one for the whole of a loss, one for the whole of an acceptance, one for a hold creation, one for a withdrawal — and the hops inside that operation share that single increment.

Where a hop is conditional, the condition is stated with its threshold and the hop simply does not fire when the condition is unmet. A cascade that does not fire leaves no trace: no counter moves, no version is bumped on the untouched row. Where a recomputation is the only thing a hop does to a neighbouring row — the property's committed aggregates, the planner's counters, scores and tier, the lead's attrition settlement — a recomputation that lands on the value the row already carried is exactly such a non-event: it bumps no `version` and refreshes no `updated_at`.

### 2.10. The Conserved Incentive Fund Identity

The bureau's incentive money comes from lodging tax and is tracked on an `IncentiveFund` row per fiscal year. Four money fields on that row satisfy a conservation identity that must hold after every single operation touching the fund:

`budget_amount` equals `uncommitted_amount` plus `committed_amount` plus `disbursed_amount`.

`budget_amount` is the fund's total appropriation and grows by the amount of every lodging-tax receipt posted to the fund; `lodging_tax_receipts_total` separately accumulates the same receipt amounts so the appropriation's origin remains auditable. `committed_amount` is the sum of the total charge of every live fund charge. A charge becomes live when an `APPROVED` or `REDUCED` decision creates it, and it leaves the committed pool exactly once and never twice — decommitted when the bid it funds leaves the committed set, whether through a planner-requested revision, a coverage drop that returns the bid to `DRAFT`, or the lead being lost; or disbursed when the funded opportunity reaches `SERVICED`. In the ordinary case that is precisely the set of bid packages in status `APPROVED`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` whose opportunity has not yet reached `SERVICED`; the one exception is a bid left standing in `SUBMITTED` by a lead that was lost for want of space, whose charge has already been decommitted even though the package's own `status` never moved. `disbursed_amount` is the sum of the total charges that have moved out of the committed pool because their funded opportunity reached `SERVICED`. `uncommitted_amount` is the residual: `budget_amount` less `committed_amount` less `disbursed_amount`.

The fund's `posture` is derived from that residual and is recomputed after any change to `committed_amount`, `disbursed_amount` or `budget_amount`. When `uncommitted_amount` is exactly zero the posture is `EXHAUSTED`. When it is strictly greater than zero but strictly less than ten percent of `budget_amount`, the posture is `CONSTRAINED`; the boundary is strict, so a residual exactly equal to ten percent of the budget leaves the posture `OPEN`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Because the residual moves both up and down, the posture oscillates freely: `OPEN` to `CONSTRAINED`, `CONSTRAINED` to `EXHAUSTED`, `EXHAUSTED` back to `CONSTRAINED` or straight back to `OPEN` when a decommit or a fresh receipt restores at least ten percent of the budget, and `OPEN` directly to `EXHAUSTED` when one charge consumes the entire remaining balance. Every one of these posture recomputations bumps the fund's `version` by one. An `EXHAUSTED` fund refuses every new charge whatever its size; a `CONSTRAINED` fund still approves, but only up to what remains uncommitted.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties on Incentive Spend

Because incentive money is public lodging-tax revenue, the officer who assembles a bid or asks for the money may never be the officer who grants it. Two operator identifiers are captured on each bid package for this purpose: `prepared_by_user_id` records the `X-User-ID` of the sales manager who opened the package, and `incentive_requested_by_user_id` records the `X-User-ID` of the sales manager who requested the incentive on the current round. When an incentive approver decides the request, the decision records that approver's `X-User-ID` as `decided_by_user_id`, and that value must differ from **both** captured identifiers. If it equals either one, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403 — and, per the ladder in Section 2.7, that refusal happens before any cap, balance or payback check is performed. Note that a revision round clears `incentive_requested_by_user_id`, so the sales manager who requested the previous round's incentive is free to request the next one, but whoever ends up recorded there is barred from deciding it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The nine subsections below follow the working life of a citywide convention lead, from the inventory the bureau must register before any selling can happen through to the board report that justifies the money spent. Rules are placed where the business narrative puts them, which means the complete specification of a single operation is frequently assembled from two or three subsections: the rooms a hotel offers are described with block assembly, but the rate ceiling they must respect was set when the lead was created, and the coverage percentage they move is only consumed at submission time.

### 3.1. Registering the Bureau's Inventory and Managing Planner Relationships

Nothing can be sold until the bureau has told the system what it has to sell. A `BureauAdmin` registers each **meeting planner** — the organization on the other side of the negotiation — with a legal `org_name` between 1 and 120 characters and a `contact_email` between 5 and 120 characters that must contain an `@`. Because the bureau is migrating off a legacy CRM, two optional counters may be supplied at registration: `legacy_definite_count`, the number of conventions this planner had already booked before the system existed, and `legacy_definite_room_nights`, the room nights those conventions produced. Both default to 0 and neither may be negative. A freshly registered planner starts with `engagement_status` `PROSPECT` and a `last_touch_at` of null, and its derived figures are then computed once from the migrated counters alone, exactly as the recomputation rule below would compute them for a planner with no opportunities: `definite_count`, `lost_count` and `relationship_score_rolling_24m` therefore come out 0 whatever was migrated — the rolling scope excludes the legacy counters entirely — while `lifetime_definite_room_nights` comes out equal to the supplied `legacy_definite_room_nights` rather than 0, and `relationship_score_lifetime` and `tier` are whatever the migrated definite count and room nights earn, `tier` starting at `TIER_C` unless they earn better. That creation-time computation is part of creating the row, not a mutation of it, so the planner is returned at `version` 1. Planner `engagement_status` flips from `PROSPECT` to `ENGAGED` the first time any lead of theirs is qualified, and never flips back.

Three derived counters track the planner's raw production. `definite_count` is the number of that planner's opportunities currently in `sales_stage` `DEFINITE` or `SERVICED`. `lost_count` is the number currently in `LOST`. `lifetime_definite_room_nights` is `legacy_definite_room_nights` plus the sum of `definite_room_nights` across that same definite-or-serviced set. All three are re-derived, and both relationship scores and the `tier` recomputed, whenever one of the planner's opportunities enters `DEFINITE`, `SERVICED` or `LOST`. That recomputation advances the planner's own `version` and `updated_at` only when it actually moves one of the three counters, one of the two scores or the `tier`: a lead crossing from `DEFINITE` to `SERVICED` leaves all six figures exactly where they were, and because nothing else in the servicing cascade touches the planner, the planner's row comes out of it completely untouched.

The **relationship score** itself is a weighted integer computed identically in two scopes. Within a scope, the raw score is twelve points for each definite convention in scope, plus one point for every full 500 room nights in scope with that room-night term capped at 40 points, minus five points for each lost lead in scope. The room-night term takes the floor of the room-night total divided by 500 before the 40-point cap is applied, and the whole raw score is then clamped into the range 0 to 100 inclusive — a planner with more losses than wins scores 0 rather than a negative number, and an enormous producer scores 100 rather than more. The result is an integer with no decimal component.

What differs between the two scopes is which rows count. In the **LIFETIME** scope, stored in `relationship_score_lifetime`, the definite count is `legacy_definite_count` plus every opportunity of that planner in `DEFINITE` or `SERVICED` regardless of age; the room-night total is `legacy_definite_room_nights` plus the `definite_room_nights` of that same set; and the lost count is every opportunity of that planner in `LOST`, again with no time limit. In the **ROLLING_24M** scope, stored in `relationship_score_rolling_24m`, the migrated legacy counters are excluded entirely, only opportunities whose `definite_at` falls at or after the instant 730 days (two years) before the current logical clock are counted as definite — the lower bound is inclusive — and only opportunities whose `updated_at` falls at or after that same instant are counted as lost. Identical stored data therefore yields two different numbers, which is the point: the board wants recent production, the incentive committee wants the whole relationship. Only the LIFETIME variant derives the planner's `tier`: `TIER_A` when `relationship_score_lifetime` is at least 60, `TIER_B` when it is at least 30, and `TIER_C` otherwise. The rolling score never influences `tier`, and therefore never influences the incentive rate that `tier` selects. Planner listings may be filtered by `tier` and by `engagement_status`.

A `BureauAdmin` also registers each **hotel** in the citywide package with a property `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `committable_rooms` figure of at least 1 giving the maximum rooms that property may hold for citywide blocks on any single night, and a `walk_distance_blocks` count of walking blocks from the convention center that is itself optional, defaults to 0 when omitted, and must not be negative. A property's `participation_status` may be supplied at registration and defaults to `PARTICIPATING`; the only other value is `SUSPENDED`. Suspension and reinstatement are a later administrative act that names the new `participation_status` and replaces the stored one outright: existing committed blocks are left completely untouched, and because the act is an explicit client write rather than a recomputation it advances the property's `version` by one every time it is performed, including when the status supplied is the one the row already carried. While a property is `SUSPENDED` it may neither be invited into a new block, nor answer an invitation, nor have an offer committed, each of those three attempts failing with `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_PARTICIPATING`. Three aggregates on the property start at 0 and are maintained by the system: `committed_peak_rooms`, `committed_room_nights` and `pickup_room_nights`. Hotel listings may be filtered by `participation_status`.

Exhibit space is registered as **center halls**, which either a `BureauAdmin` or a `CenterScheduler` may create. A hall carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `exhibit_sqft` of rentable exhibit square footage of at least 1, a `capacity_persons` of at least 1 giving the maximum persons the hall may host, and a `turnaround_hours` figure of at least 0 that defaults to 48 and expresses how long the hall needs between one event's move-out and the next event's move-in. A hall's `hall_status` defaults to `AVAILABLE`; a hall set to `UNDER_RENOVATION` rejects every new hold whatever its free window or spare capacity, with `ERR_HALL_UNAVAILABLE`. The hall's `confirmed_hold_count` starts at 0 and tracks how many holds on that hall currently sit in status `CONFIRMED`. Hall listings may be filtered by `hall_status`.

Finally, the money. A `BureauAdmin` opens one **incentive fund** per bureau `fiscal_year`, which must be 2020 or later, seeded with a strictly positive `budget_amount` from the lodging-tax appropriation. At creation `committed_amount`, `disbursed_amount` and `lodging_tax_receipts_total` are all `0.00`, `uncommitted_amount` equals `budget_amount`, and `posture` is `OPEN`. Throughout the fiscal year the treasury remits lodging tax, and each remittance is credited to the fund as an immutable **lodging tax receipt** carrying the `fund_id` it is credited to, a strictly positive `amount` with at most two fraction digits, and a `received_at` instant recording when the treasury recognized the money. Each receipt row also exposes its own `id` and a `created_at` stamped from the logical clock; because a receipt is an event and never changes, it carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Posting a receipt raises both `lodging_tax_receipts_total` and `budget_amount` by the receipt `amount`, recomputes `uncommitted_amount` from the conservation identity, re-derives `posture`, and bumps the fund's `version` by one — which is exactly why an approver's `expected_fund_version` can go stale without any other approver having acted. Fund listings may be filtered by `fiscal_year` and by `posture`, and the decisions charged against a fund are readable as a sub-collection filterable by `decision`. The receipts credited to a fund are likewise readable as a sub-collection, but that one offers no filter of its own beyond pagination.

### 3.2. Opening and Qualifying a Citywide Lead

A `SalesManager` opens a citywide convention lead by naming the `planner_id` whose organization is holding the convention and the `event_name` as it will be marketed, between 1 and 160 characters. The dates come as a pair: `arrival_date` is the first occupied night of the citywide room block and `departure_date` is the day the delegates leave. The block occupies the half-open range from `arrival_date` up to but **not including** `departure_date`, so the departure night is not an occupied night, and the server derives `event_nights` as the whole number of days from `arrival_date` to `departure_date`. A three-night convention arriving on the 10th therefore departs on the 13th. `arrival_date` must be strictly earlier than `departure_date`.

The planner's own claims about the event arrive as four figures the bureau will later be held to. `peak_rooms_requested`, at least 1, is the citywide peak-night room count the planner wants; it is the denominator of the coverage percentage that gates bid submission. `total_room_nights_requested`, at least 1, is the citywide room-night total; it drives both the provisional impact valuation and the incentive band ladder. `attendance_estimate`, at least 1, is the delegate headcount; it drives the provisional valuation and is checked against a hall's `capacity_persons` before any space is held. `exhibit_sqft_requested`, at least 0, is the exhibit square footage the event needs. Alongside them the lead carries a `rate_ceiling_amount`, a strictly positive currency amount with two decimals that no hotel may exceed in its nightly rate for this block. When a lead is a re-bid of an earlier lead the bureau lost, `supersedes_opportunity_id` names that earlier opportunity; it defaults to null, and the only thing the server asks of it is that it resolve to a live opportunity row — the superseded lead's own `sales_stage` is never inspected, so naming a lead that is still `QUALIFIED`, or indeed already `DEFINITE`, is accepted exactly as naming a `LOST` one is. Opportunity listings may be filtered by it so the bureau can trace a re-bid chain, just as they may be filtered by `planner_id`, `sales_stage` and `hold_posture`.

A new lead is born in `sales_stage` `LEAD` with `hold_posture` `NONE`, and every derived aggregate on it starts at zero: `committed_peak_rooms`, `block_coverage_pct`, `citywide_cutoff_days_before_arrival`, `cutoff_alignment_spread_days`, `definite_room_nights` and `attrition_settlement_amount` are all 0, while `definite_at` and `lost_reason` are null. Creating the lead also refreshes the owning planner's `last_touch_at` from the logical clock, and that refresh is an unconditional write rather than a recomputation, so it advances the planner's own `version` by one and refreshes its `updated_at` on every lead created — even though nothing else on the planner moves and the planner keeps its `PROSPECT` engagement until a lead of theirs is actually qualified.

**Qualification** is the gate between an inbound enquiry and real bureau effort, and a `SalesManager` performs it. It moves `sales_stage` from `LEAD` to `QUALIFIED`, and it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OPPORTUNITY_STAGE` if the lead is in any other stage — including a lead that is already `QUALIFIED`. The one substantive gate is lead time: `arrival_date`, interpreted at `00:00:00Z`, must be at least 180 days (roughly six months) after the current logical clock. A lead whose arrival is nearer than that cannot be worked, and qualification fails with `ERR_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT` and HTTP 422. A successful qualification produces three side effects beyond the stage change: the owning planner's `engagement_status` is set to `ENGAGED`, that planner's `last_touch_at` is refreshed from the logical clock, and the opportunity's `version` is incremented by one. Those two writes to the planner share a single increment of the planner's own `version`, and that increment is unconditional: a second and third lead of the same planner being qualified each advance the planner's `version` by one again even though `engagement_status` already read `ENGAGED` and the clock has not moved since the last touch. Qualification does not recompute the planner's counters, scores or `tier` — only a lead entering `DEFINITE`, `SERVICED` or `LOST` does that. Qualification does not touch `hold_posture`, which remains `NONE` until space is actually held.

### 3.3. Holding Dates and Space at the Convention Center

Space is the scarcest thing the destination sells, and the convention center will not give it away for free. A `CenterScheduler` places a **date-and-space hold** on behalf of a lead by naming the `opportunity_id` the hold protects, the `center_hall_id` being held, and the occupation window as two instants: `move_in_start`, the first instant the hall is occupied for move-in, and `move_out_end`, the instant the hall is released after move-out and thus the exclusive end of the occupation. `move_in_start` must be strictly earlier than `move_out_end`. Holds may only be placed for a lead in `sales_stage` `QUALIFIED` or `BIDDING`; anything else, `LEAD` included, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OPPORTUNITY_STAGE`.

Two suitability gates follow. The hall must be `AVAILABLE`, as described in Section 3.1. And the hall must be big enough for the event on both dimensions: the lead's `attendance_estimate` must not exceed the hall's `capacity_persons`, and its `exhibit_sqft_requested` must not exceed the hall's `exhibit_sqft`. A failure of either dimension is reported as `ERR_HALL_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT` with HTTP 422.

The interesting gate is the window. Two events cannot occupy the same hall back to back, because the hall needs its `turnaround_hours` to strike one show and set the next. The system therefore compares **buffered** windows using a single-sided buffer, applied to the earlier edge of each hold. Let the buffer be the hall's own `turnaround_hours`. Two holds on the same hall conflict when, and only when, both of the following hold simultaneously: the first hold's `move_in_start` pulled back by the buffer is strictly earlier than the second hold's `move_out_end`, **and** the second hold's `move_in_start` pulled back by the buffer is strictly earlier than the first hold's `move_out_end`. Stated in the scheduler's own terms: two holds on one hall must be separated by at least the buffer between one hold's `move_out_end` and the next hold's `move_in_start`. Because both comparisons are strict, exact adjacency at the buffer edge is permitted — a candidate whose `move_in_start` falls exactly at an existing hold's `move_out_end` plus the buffer does not conflict with it. Instants are compared at the full precision retained on parse — microseconds, not the three fractional digits they are rendered with. A hall with the default 48-hour turnaround therefore accepts a move-in exactly two days after the previous show's move-out, and rejects one an instant earlier.

The set of holds that can block a candidate is every hold on the same hall in status `HELD`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED` whose buffered window conflicts with the candidate's. Holds in `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` never block anything. Three outcomes follow from that blocking set, evaluated in this order. If any blocking hold is `CONFIRMED`, the space is already sold and the request is refused with `ERR_HALL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Otherwise, if the blocking set contains two or more holds, the hall has already sold both of the options it offers on that window and the request is refused with `ERR_HALL_HOLD_LIMIT_REACHED` — the center sells a first option and a second option on any buffered window and nothing beyond. Otherwise the hold is created in status `HELD`, with `hold_rank` set to `FIRST` when the blocking set was empty and `SECOND` when it contained exactly one hold.

Rank determines the hold's life expectancy. A `FIRST`-rank hold has its `expires_at` stamped at 90 days after the current logical clock — a full quarter for the bureau to convert the lead. A `SECOND`-rank hold does not get its own clock: it **copies** the `expires_at` of the single `FIRST` hold that blocked it, so the second option can never outlive the first option it is queued behind. Neither rank sets `challenge_deadline`, which stays null until a challenge is actually raised, and `release_reason` and `challenged_by_space_hold_id` likewise stay null.

Rank also determines what the hold does to the lead's space posture. Creating a `FIRST`-rank hold moves the owning opportunity's `hold_posture` to `TENTATIVE` — from `NONE` for a first-ever hold, or back up from `RELEASED` when the bureau re-holds space for a lead still in `QUALIFIED` or `BIDDING` after an earlier hold went away. Creating a `SECOND`-rank hold leaves `hold_posture` exactly as it was; being somebody's backup is not a tentative booking. Either way the opportunity's `version` is incremented by one — the `SECOND`-rank case included, where the increment is the only mark the creation leaves on the lead's row. The hall being held is by contrast not touched at all by a hold creation: neither its `confirmed_hold_count`, which counts only holds in `CONFIRMED`, nor its `version` moves, however many options the window has now sold. Holds are readable individually and as a list filterable by `opportunity_id`, `center_hall_id`, `challenged_by_space_hold_id`, `status` and `hold_rank`.

### 3.4. Challenges, Releases, Lapses and the Promotion Ladder

A second option is worth little unless its holder can force the issue, so the convention center's most consequential lever is the **challenge**. A `CenterScheduler` challenges the `FIRST`-rank hold identified in the path by naming, as `challenging_space_hold_id`, the `SECOND`-rank hold acting on its behalf. The challenge is well-formed only when all four of these are true: the named challenger is a hold in status `HELD` with `hold_rank` `SECOND`; the challenged hold is in status `HELD` with `hold_rank` `FIRST` on the same `center_hall_id`; the two holds' buffered windows conflict by the rule in Section 3.3; and the two holds belong to different opportunities. Any of those failing yields `ERR_CHALLENGE_TARGET_INVALID` with HTTP 422. A hold that already has a live challenge — that is, whose `challenge_deadline` is not null — cannot be challenged again. A challenged hold must also still be alive: if the current logical clock has passed its `expires_at`, the challenge is refused with `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`, and a hold that is no longer `HELD` at all is refused with `ERR_INVALID_HOLD_STATE`.

A successful challenge moves the challenged hold from `HELD` to `CHALLENGED`, stamps its `challenge_deadline` at 14 days after the current logical clock, and records the challenger's identifier in the challenged hold's `challenged_by_space_hold_id`. The acting `SECOND`-rank hold itself does not change state — it stays `HELD`; the whole state change lands on the hold that was challenged. As a second-order effect, the challenged hold's opportunity moves `hold_posture` from `TENTATIVE` to `CHALLENGED`, which is the bureau's signal that this lead now has a fortnight to go definite or lose the space. That hop is strictly conditional on the posture already reading `TENTATIVE`: when it does, the lead's `version` advances by one along with the posture, and when it reads anything else — a lead sitting at `CONFIRMED` or `RELEASED` because of what its other holds have done — the hop does not fire and the lead's row comes out of the challenge entirely untouched. The hall's `version` is incremented by one. The operation answers with the challenged hold, the one the path names, and never with the challenger that acted on its behalf.

**Releasing** a hold is the scheduler's voluntary act, permitted on a hold in status `HELD` or `CHALLENGED` and refused with `ERR_INVALID_HOLD_STATE` on anything else. A `CONFIRMED` hold in particular is never released this way; its only exit is the servicing bubble-up described in Section 3.9. A release that targets a hold whose `expires_at` has already passed is refused with `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`. A successful release sets the hold's status to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `SCHEDULER_RELEASE`, and then sets off a four-hop cascade that is the single most intricate piece of choreography in the system:

- **Hop 2 — promotion.** Because a `FIRST` hold left the active set, the promotion rule described below picks at most one waiting `SECOND` hold on the same hall and raises its `hold_rank` to `FIRST`, re-stamping its `expires_at` at 30 days after the current logical clock. The promoted hold gets a fresh month, not the remainder of the old hold's quarter.
- **Hop 3 — the promoted lead's posture.** Because that hold became a live `FIRST` hold, its opportunity's `hold_posture` becomes `TENTATIVE`.
- **Hop 2 prime — the releasing lead's posture.** Because the released hold left the active set, the releasing opportunity's `hold_posture` becomes `RELEASED`, but only once no hold of that opportunity remains in status `HELD` or `CHALLENGED`. A lead holding space in two halls does not lose its posture when one of them is given back.
- **Hop 3 prime — the releasing lead may be lost.** If the releasing opportunity was in `sales_stage` `BIDDING` when it lost its last active hold, it cannot honour a bid it has no space for: its `sales_stage` becomes `LOST` with `lost_reason` `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, which withdraws its room block commitments and decommits its fund charge exactly as described in Section 3.8. A lead in `QUALIFIED` is not lost — it simply has to re-hold space.

The hall's `version` is incremented by one on the release.

The **promotion rule** deserves its own statement because three different events invoke it: a hold moving from `HELD` to `RELEASED`, a hold moving from `CHALLENGED` to `RELEASED`, and a hold moving from `HELD` to `EXPIRED`. Candidates are exactly those holds on the same hall as the departing hold that are in status `HELD`, carry `hold_rank` `SECOND`, and whose buffered window conflicts with the departing hold's. Nothing in `CHALLENGED`, `CONFIRMED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` is ever promoted. The departing hold's own rank is immaterial to this: the rule runs whenever any hold leaves the active set through one of those three transitions, so giving back a `SECOND`-rank option can itself promote another `SECOND`-rank hold whose window conflicted with it. If there are no candidates, no promotion happens at all. Otherwise the winner is chosen by sorting the candidates by the `peak_rooms_requested` of their opportunity in descending order, breaking ties by the hold's own `created_at` in ascending order, and taking the first — the biggest piece of business wins, and among equals the one that queued earliest wins. The resource identifier is never a tiebreaker. The winner's `hold_rank` becomes `FIRST`, its `expires_at` is re-stamped at 30 days after the current logical clock, and its opportunity's `hold_posture` becomes `TENTATIVE` — a posture write that advances the promoted lead's `version` only when the posture actually changes, so a lead already sitting at `TENTATIVE` because it holds live space elsewhere is left untouched by its own promotion. Exactly one hold is promoted per vacated window.

Time itself is applied by an operations checkpoint that either a `BureauAdmin` or a `CenterScheduler` may fire, and that processes candidate holds in order of `expires_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tiebreaker. Two things happen in that run. Every hold in status `HELD` whose `expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock moves to `EXPIRED` with `release_reason` `HOLD_EXPIRED` — the boundary is inclusive, so a hold whose `expires_at` equals the clock exactly does expire in that run. Every hold in status `CHALLENGED` whose `challenge_deadline` is strictly before the current logical clock moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `CHALLENGE_LAPSED` — here the boundary is strict, so a hold whose deadline equals the clock exactly survives that run. Both outcomes run the same promotion and posture cascade as a voluntary release, which means a lapsed challenge hands the space to the challenger that forced it, and both bump the affected hall's `version` — one bump per departing hold, so a single run that ends two holds on the same hall advances that hall's `version` twice. The checkpoint's own answer is a summary object rather than an entity: `expired_count` for holds that expired, `lapsed_challenge_count` for challenges that lapsed, `promoted_count` for holds promoted, and `promoted_space_hold_ids` listing the identifiers of the promoted holds in the order the promotions happened, which is the same `expires_at` ascending order the run walks. The posture of every affected opportunity is recomputed as part of the run, and a lead in `BIDDING` that loses its last active hold to expiry or lapse falls to `LOST` with `lost_reason` `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE` just as it would on a voluntary release.

Conversely, a lead reaching `DEFINITE` **confirms** its space. Its `FIRST`-rank hold, in status `HELD` or `CHALLENGED`, moves to `CONFIRMED` — and when the lead holds two such holds, in two different halls, exactly one of them is confirmed, the earliest-created of the two, the other being left exactly as it stands; both `challenge_deadline` and `challenged_by_space_hold_id` are cleared to null, and the hall's `confirmed_hold_count` is incremented and its `version` bumped. When the hold being confirmed was `CHALLENGED`, confirmation is only legitimate while the current logical clock is at or before its `challenge_deadline` — a lead that lets the fortnight run out cannot rescue the space by going definite afterwards. A lapsed deadline is not itself an error, though: the confirmation hop simply does not happen, so the hold stays `CHALLENGED`, the hall's counter and `version` do not move, no sibling is superseded, and the lead's `hold_posture` stays `CHALLENGED` — while the acceptance that asked for the confirmation still stands and the lead still reaches `DEFINITE`. Confirmation then supersedes the competition: every other hold on the same hall in status `HELD` or `CHALLENGED` whose buffered window conflicts with the newly confirmed one moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_CONFIRMED`, and each of those superseded leads has its `hold_posture` set to `RELEASED` once none of its own holds remains in `HELD` or `CHALLENGED`. Superseding costs the hall no further `version` bump — the single increment that accompanied `confirmed_hold_count` covers the whole confirmation, however many siblings it sweeps away — and a superseded hold does not itself invoke the promotion rule, because the window it was queued for has just been sold rather than vacated. That posture change carries no precondition on the posture the superseded lead was previously in — `NONE` included, which is where a lead whose only hold was a `SECOND`-rank option has been sitting all along. When that lead's posture already reads `RELEASED`, the hop is a no-op that moves nothing and bumps nothing. A superseded sibling is ordinarily a `SECOND`-rank hold, though a hold promoted to `FIRST` on a window that a later confirmation covers can be swept away too; and whichever rank it carried, losing space this way never loses the lead. The `BIDDING`-to-`LOST` fall of the promotion-and-posture cascade is deliberately suppressed here: a superseded lead in `BIDDING` stays in `BIDDING`, with its bid still standing on the planner's desk, and merely takes the `RELEASED` posture — it must re-hold space rather than be written off, because the competitor's confirmation is news about one hall, not about the city's ability to house the event.

### 3.5. Assembling the Citywide Room Block

A citywide convention is not housed by one hotel, and the bureau's hardest commercial task is persuading a dozen competing properties to pledge rooms at a rate ceiling somebody else negotiated. Each property's participation is tracked as one **room block commitment**, which is created by a `SalesManager` as an invitation naming the `opportunity_id` whose block is being assembled, the `hotel_id` of the property being invited, and `rooms_peak_invited`, at least 1, being the peak-night rooms the bureau is asking that property for. The invitation is guarded on the opportunity's `version` per Section 2.6. It requires the lead to be in `QUALIFIED` or `BIDDING`, the property to be `PARTICIPATING`, and — importantly — no existing commitment for that same pair of opportunity and hotel to be live in status `INVITED`, `OFFERED` or `COMMITTED`. A second invitation to a property that is already in the block is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMMITMENT` and HTTP 409; a property whose earlier commitment ended in `WITHDRAWN` or `RELEASED` may be invited afresh. A new commitment starts in status `INVITED` with `offer_round` 0, `rooms_peak_offered`, `rate_offered_amount`, `cutoff_days_before_arrival`, `cutoff_date` and `withdraw_reason` all null, and `rooms_peak_committed`, `committed_room_nights`, `picked_up_room_nights`, `attrition_shortfall_nights` and `attrition_damages_amount` all 0. Creating it increments the opportunity's `version` by one. Commitments are readable individually and as a list filterable by `opportunity_id`, `hotel_id` and `status`.

The property answers through its `HotelPartner`, and this is the only place in the system where a hotel's revenue manager sets commercial terms. An **offer** carries three fields, all three of them required: `rooms_peak_offered`, the peak-night rooms the property will actually hold, which must be at least 0 — a property answering with 0 rooms is making a real offer, not declining one; `rate_offered_amount`, the nightly rate, which must be strictly positive; and `cutoff_days_before_arrival`, at least 0, being how many days before arrival the property will release unsold block rooms back to general inventory. Two ceilings bind the offer, and both produce `ERR_RATE_CEILING_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 422: the `rate_offered_amount` may not exceed the lead's `rate_ceiling_amount`, and `rooms_peak_offered` may not exceed the property's own `committable_rooms`. Offering also requires the property to still be `PARTICIPATING`. A successful offer stores the three fields, derives `cutoff_date` as the lead's `arrival_date` less `cutoff_days_before_arrival` days, and increments `offer_round` by one. From status `INVITED` the offer moves the commitment to `OFFERED`; from `OFFERED` it is a **self-loop** that leaves the status at `OFFERED`, overwrites the three offer fields and increments `offer_round` again. A property may re-offer as many times as it likes for as long as the bureau has not committed the block, and `cutoff_date` is recomputed on every one of those offers. Any other starting status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMMITMENT_STATE`. An offer's blast radius is exactly one row: it advances the commitment's own `version` and nothing else, leaving both the property's aggregates and the lead's coverage figures — and therefore both of those rows' `version` counters — completely untouched, because nothing is committed yet.

Committing is the bureau's decision, taken by a `SalesManager` on a commitment in status `OFFERED`, with the lead still in `QUALIFIED` or `BIDDING` and the property still `PARTICIPATING`, and with the same rate-ceiling check re-applied at commit time. Beyond that lies the per-night inventory check, which exists because one property is simultaneously in several citywide blocks. Consider the nights the lead occupies — every date from `arrival_date` up to but not including `departure_date`. For each of those nights, the property's already-occupied load is the sum of `rooms_peak_committed` across every commitment of that same hotel in status `COMMITTED` whose own opportunity occupies that same night. If that load plus the `rooms_peak_offered` now being committed would come out **strictly greater** than the property's `committable_rooms` on even one of those nights, the commit is refused with `ERR_HOTEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422; landing exactly on `committable_rooms` is allowed. A useful consequence of the half-open night convention: two conventions whose windows merely touch, one's `departure_date` equalling the other's `arrival_date`, share no occupied night and therefore never contend for the same room.

A successful commit freezes `rooms_peak_committed` at the value of `rooms_peak_offered` and sets `committed_room_nights` to `rooms_peak_committed` multiplied by the lead's `event_nights`. The property's aggregates are then re-derived: `committed_peak_rooms` on the hotel is the **maximum** over every night of that night's occupied load — an aggregate over dates, never a sum across them — while `committed_room_nights` on the hotel is the plain sum of `committed_room_nights` across all of that property's commitments in status `COMMITTED`. That re-derivation advances the property's `version` only when one of those two figures actually comes out different from the value the row already carried. Finally the lead's citywide figures are re-derived from its commitments in status `COMMITTED` only, with commitments in `INVITED`, `OFFERED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `RELEASED` all excluded: `committed_peak_rooms` on the opportunity is the sum of `rooms_peak_committed`; `block_coverage_pct` is that committed peak multiplied by 100 and divided by `peak_rooms_requested`, rounded half-up to two decimals; `citywide_cutoff_days_before_arrival` is the **largest** `cutoff_days_before_arrival` among the committed properties, which is the citywide cutoff the bid will advertise, or 0 when nothing is committed; and `cutoff_alignment_spread_days` is the largest such value minus the smallest, or 0 when fewer than two commitments are committed. The commit increments the opportunity's `version` by one.

Withdrawal is the property's exit, taken by its `HotelPartner` with a `withdraw_reason`, and permitted only while the lead has not reached `SERVICED` — a lead that has reached it refuses the withdrawal with `ERR_INVALID_OPPORTUNITY_STAGE`. The `withdraw_reason` field's declared vocabulary is exactly the three reasons a property may itself give — `PROPERTY_SOLD_OUT`, `RATE_DISPUTE` and `INVITE_DECLINED` — so the two reasons the system writes on a commitment it ends of its own accord, `OPPORTUNITY_LOST` and `EVENT_SERVICED`, are not acceptable client input and are rejected at the schema rung like any other undeclared enum value. Within that vocabulary the operation has three distinct arms and they are deliberately asymmetric. From `INVITED`, the only reason a property may give is `INVITE_DECLINED`; no room counter anywhere moves, because `rooms_peak_committed` was already 0, and this short arm produces none of the downstream cascade — the property's `committed_peak_rooms` is not even touched. From `OFFERED`, the reason must be `PROPERTY_SOLD_OUT` or `RATE_DISPUTE`; again no room counter moves because `rooms_peak_committed` was still 0, and the lead's `block_coverage_pct` is left unchanged. From `COMMITTED`, the reason must likewise be `PROPERTY_SOLD_OUT` or `RATE_DISPUTE`, and the consequences run four hops deep:

- **Hop 2.** Because rooms have left the block, `rooms_peak_committed` and `committed_room_nights` on the commitment are both reset to 0, the property's `committed_peak_rooms` and `committed_room_nights` are recomputed, and the lead's `committed_peak_rooms`, `block_coverage_pct` and `cutoff_alignment_spread_days` are recomputed from the commitments that remain committed.
- **Hop 3.** If that recomputation drops `block_coverage_pct` below 85.00 while a bid package of this lead is in status `SUBMITTED`, that bid package returns to `DRAFT` with its `round_number` incremented by one, its `submitted_at` and `response_due_at` cleared, and the lead's `sales_stage` moves back from `BIDDING` to `QUALIFIED`. The bureau cannot leave a bid on a planner's desk that promises rooms the city no longer has. Unlike the revision cycle of Section 3.8, this fallback leaves the finished round's incentive apparatus standing on the package: `incentive_request_amount`, `incentive_cap_amount`, `approved_incentive_amount`, `admin_fee_amount`, `total_fund_charge_amount`, `tax_payback_ratio` and `incentive_requested_by_user_id` all keep the values the decision wrote, even though the charge behind them has left the committed pool. Only the first bid package of the lead found in `SUBMITTED`, oldest first, is treated this way; and if the coverage recomputation happens to leave `block_coverage_pct` at 85.00 or above, or no package of the lead is in `SUBMITTED`, hops 3 and 4 simply do not happen.
- **Hop 4.** Because that bid left `SUBMITTED`, its live fund charge is decommitted: the fund's `committed_amount` falls by the bid's `total_fund_charge_amount`, `uncommitted_amount` rises by the same amount, and `posture` is re-derived.

Every arm increments the opportunity's `version` by one, and every arm is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMMITMENT_STATE` if the commitment is not in one of the three permitted from-statuses. Because which reason an arm permits is a function of the from-status, that per-arm reason check cannot be evaluated at the schema rung with the rest of the body: the three gates run in the order commitment status, then the lead not having reached `SERVICED`, then the reason being permitted for the arm the status selected. A reason outside the arm's permitted set is nevertheless a schema violation and not a state violation — it is answered with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400 — so a `HotelPartner` withdrawing from a `COMMITTED` commitment with `INVITE_DECLINED` gets a 400, while the same call against a commitment already `WITHDRAWN` gets the 409 first.

There is a fourth way a commitment ends, and the system rather than a person drives it: when the owning lead reaches `SERVICED` or `LOST`, every commitment of that lead still in `COMMITTED` moves to `RELEASED` with `withdraw_reason` `EVENT_SERVICED` or `OPPORTUNITY_LOST` respectively, the property's `committed_peak_rooms` and `committed_room_nights` are recomputed, and the commitment's `attrition_shortfall_nights` and `attrition_damages_amount` are frozen at whatever the settlement rule computes at that moment. The released commitment keeps its own `rooms_peak_committed` and `committed_room_nights` exactly as they stand — this arm does not zero them the way hop 2 of a withdrawal does; they simply stop counting towards the property's and the lead's aggregates because those aggregates read status `COMMITTED` only. The two triggers then part company over the rest of the block. A **lost** lead also ends every commitment still sitting in `INVITED` or `OFFERED`: each moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` `OPPORTUNITY_LOST`, and because such a commitment was never contributing rooms, ending it recomputes no property aggregate of its own — the lead's citywide figures are re-derived once for the cascade as a whole, as Section 3.8 describes, not once per commitment. A lead reaching **`SERVICED`** ends only the `COMMITTED` commitments and leaves anything still in `INVITED` or `OFFERED` standing exactly where it is.

After the convention has actually happened, each property reports what it really housed. A `HotelPartner` files one **pickup record** per night against a commitment in status `COMMITTED` or `RELEASED`, carrying the `night_date` being reported, the `rooms_picked_up` on that night (which may legitimately be 0), and the `rate_charged_amount`, a strictly positive nightly rate with two decimals which need not equal the rate that was offered. The `night_date` must fall inside the block's occupied nights — at or after `arrival_date` and strictly before `departure_date`, the departure night itself included in the rejection — or the filing is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_NIGHT_OUT_OF_RANGE`; and only one pickup record may exist per commitment and night, a repeat being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PICKUP_NIGHT`, both with HTTP 422. The server derives `room_night_revenue_amount` as `rooms_picked_up` multiplied by `rate_charged_amount`, rounded half-up to two decimals, then raises the commitment's `picked_up_room_nights` and the property's `pickup_room_nights` by the rooms reported, and recomputes the commitment's `attrition_shortfall_nights` and `attrition_damages_amount` per Section 3.9. The three rows the filing touches advance on their own terms: the commitment's `version` advances on every accepted filing, whatever the recomputation produces; the property's `pickup_room_nights` and `version` move only when the rooms reported are greater than 0, so a night filed with `rooms_picked_up` of 0 leaves the property's row entirely untouched; and the lead's `version` advances only when the recomputed `attrition_settlement_amount` of Section 3.9 actually comes out different from the figure the lead already carried. Nothing here consults the lead's `sales_stage`: the commitment's own status is the whole gate, so a night may still be filed against a `RELEASED` commitment of a lead that has already reached `SERVICED`. A pickup record is an immutable event: it exposes `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and no `version`.

### 3.6. Valuing the Convention: Economic Impact Estimates

Incentive money is public money, and the bureau may not pledge it without an arithmetic case. That case is an **economic impact estimate**, which a `BoardAnalyst` or a `SalesManager` may compute for an `opportunity_id` in one of two `variant` values: `PROVISIONAL`, the forward-looking case built from what the planner claims, and `FINAL`, the retrospective case built from what the city actually observed. Each estimate is an immutable snapshot carrying `id`, `created_at` and a `computed_at` stamped from the logical clock, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Computing one is a pure reading of the state it summarises: it writes nothing back to the lead, the planner, the commitments or the halls it reads, so no other row's `version` moves on an estimate's account. Estimates of one lead are readable as a sub-collection, newest last, filterable by `variant`.

Both variants share the same three-component structure, and in both the components are each rounded half-up to two decimals **before** they are added together. The operation itself imposes no gate on the lead's `sales_stage` in either variant: the only availability rule anywhere in this section is the `FINAL`-variant pickup rule stated below, so a `PROVISIONAL` snapshot may be computed against a lead in any stage at all, `LEAD` and `LOST` included, and it is simply read for what it is — an arithmetic valuation of the numbers the lead currently carries. The `PROVISIONAL` variant, which is meaningful in practice from `sales_stage` `QUALIFIED` onward and reads nothing but input fields on the lead itself, works as follows. Its `room_nights_basis` is the lead's `total_room_nights_requested`. Its `lodging_spend_amount` is that basis multiplied by the lead's `rate_ceiling_amount`, rounded to two decimals. Its `delegate_spend_amount` is the lead's `attendance_estimate` multiplied by `event_nights` and then by a delegate daily spend of 285.00 dollars per person per day, rounded to two decimals. Its `exhibitor_spend_amount` is `exhibit_sqft_requested` multiplied by an exhibitor spend of 4.75 dollars per square foot, rounded to two decimals.

The `FINAL` variant replaces every claim with an observation. Its `room_nights_basis` is the sum of `rooms_picked_up` across the eligible pickup records — those belonging to commitments of this lead in status `COMMITTED` or `RELEASED`, summed in order of `night_date` ascending with the pickup record's `created_at` ascending as tiebreaker. Pickup rows under a `WITHDRAWN` commitment are excluded entirely. Its `lodging_spend_amount` is not the basis times a single rate but the sum, over those same eligible pickup records, of each record's `rooms_picked_up` multiplied by its own `rate_charged_amount` rounded to two decimals — a per-night rounded product, then summed. Its delegate figure is derived from rooms rather than from the planner's headcount: the actual room nights are multiplied by an occupancy factor of 1.6 persons per room and rounded half-up to a whole number of delegate person-nights, and `delegate_spend_amount` is that whole number multiplied by the same 285.00 dollars per person per day, rounded to two decimals. Its `exhibitor_spend_amount` is based on space actually confirmed: the total `exhibit_sqft` of the halls held by this lead's space holds in status `CONFIRMED` is capped at the lead's `exhibit_sqft_requested` — whichever of the two is smaller — and that capped square footage is multiplied by 4.75 dollars per square foot, rounded to two decimals. Where the lead holds several `CONFIRMED` halls their `exhibit_sqft` are added before the cap is applied; and because the figure reads the holds' *current* status, a `FINAL` estimate computed after the lead has reached `SERVICED` — which releases the confirmed hold per Section 3.9 — finds no confirmed hall and reports an `exhibitor_spend_amount` of 0.00. The moment at which a `FINAL` estimate is computed is therefore part of what it means. Because a `FINAL` estimate is meaningless before any pickup has been reported, requesting one while the lead has no pickup record on any commitment in `COMMITTED` or `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_ESTIMATE_VARIANT_NOT_AVAILABLE` and HTTP 422.

The tail is shared by both variants. `direct_spend_amount` is the sum of the three already-rounded components: `lodging_spend_amount` plus `delegate_spend_amount` plus `exhibitor_spend_amount`. `total_impact_amount` is `direct_spend_amount` multiplied by an indirect-effects multiplier of 1.65 — the ratio economists use to account for the money delegates' dollars earn as they circulate through the local economy — rounded half-up to two decimals. And `lodging_tax_yield_amount`, the single most consequential number in this document, is `lodging_spend_amount` multiplied by the city's lodging tax rate of 0.15, rounded half-up to two decimals; it is the figure that justifies every dollar of incentive spend, and the numerator of the payback ratio in Section 3.7.

One `PROVISIONAL` estimate is also produced automatically, without anybody asking for it, as a side effect of a bid package's incentive request. Where a later rule refers to "the newest `PROVISIONAL` estimate" of a lead, it means the one with the highest `created_at`, with ties broken in favour of the `PROVISIONAL` variant — and, among several `PROVISIONAL` snapshots sharing that same instant, in favour of the one recorded most recently. Because every estimate is stamped from the logical clock, that last tiebreaker is the usual case rather than an edge case.

### 3.7. Preparing the Bid: Community Support, Incentive Requests and Fund Decisions

A **bid package** is the document the bureau puts in front of the planner, and a `SalesManager` opens one against a lead in `sales_stage` `QUALIFIED` by naming its `opportunity_id` and carrying the opportunity's `expected_opportunity_version` per Section 2.6. Only one package per lead may be live at a time: the create is refused with `ERR_BID_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED` and HTTP 409 while any package of that lead is still in status `DRAFT`, `INCENTIVE_PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `SUBMITTED`. A package whose life ended in `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED` does not block a new one, which is exactly why the create is version-guarded — a new package legitimately supersedes a declined predecessor, so the server cannot tell a deliberate fresh start from a stale client's duplicate without the version check. The new package is born in status `DRAFT` with `round_number` 1 and `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and it increments the lead's `version` by one. Everything else on it starts empty: `incentive_requested_by_user_id`, `incentive_request_amount`, `incentive_cap_amount`, `approved_incentive_amount`, `admin_fee_amount`, `total_fund_charge_amount`, `tax_payback_ratio`, `submitted_at`, `response_due_at`, `planner_response` and `decline_reason` are all null, `service_commitment_codes` is an empty array, and `support_letter_type_count` is 0. Packages are readable individually and as a list filterable by `opportunity_id` and `status`.

Community backing is assembled as **support letters**, which a `SalesManager` attaches to a package while it is still in `DRAFT` — an attachment attempted in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BID_STATE`. Each letter names the `bid_package_id` it backs, the `organization_name` that signed it in 1 to 120 characters, and a `letter_type` identifying which community constituency it speaks for, one of `MAYOR`, `CHAMBER`, `UNIVERSITY`, `INDUSTRY_PARTNER` or `CONVENTION_CENTER`. Its `received_at` is stamped from the logical clock; being an immutable event record, a letter carries `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and no `version`. What the submission gate actually counts is breadth, not volume: after each attachment the package's `support_letter_type_count` is recomputed as the number of **distinct** `letter_type` values attached to it, so four letters from four chambers of commerce still count as one. Each accepted attachment advances the package's own `version` by one whether or not the recount actually moved `support_letter_type_count` — the count is recomputed as part of an operation that writes to the package, not as a bare recomputation on a neighbouring row — while the lead behind the package is not touched at all. Letters of a package are readable as a sub-collection filterable by `letter_type`.

Asking for money is a separate act, performed by a `SalesManager` on a package in `DRAFT`. The package's own status is the entire gate here: the lead's `sales_stage` is deliberately not consulted, so an incentive may be requested from a `DRAFT` package whatever the lead behind it now reads, and only the package being in some status other than `DRAFT` is refused, with `ERR_INVALID_BID_STATE`. The request carries `incentive_request_amount`, which must be strictly greater than zero, and `service_commitment_codes`, the array of bureau service pledges attached to the bid — codes such as `HOUSING_BUREAU`, `SHUTTLE_NETWORK`, `VOLUNTEER_CORPS` and `WELCOME_SIGNAGE`. That vocabulary is genuinely open: the field is validated only as an array of non-empty strings, the four names above being examples rather than a closed enum, and whatever the caller sends is stored and echoed back verbatim. An empty array is acceptable. Both are stored, `incentive_requested_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and the package moves from `DRAFT` to `INCENTIVE_PENDING`. Two computations happen on the way: a `PROVISIONAL` economic impact estimate is created for the lead per Section 3.6, and from that fresh snapshot the package's `incentive_cap_amount` is computed. The cap is deliberately **not** enforced at this point — a sales manager may ask for more than the ceiling allows, and the request will reach the approver and be refused there. That is a negotiating position, not a bug.

The **incentive cap** is a four-step computation and the order of its steps is pinned. First, a marginal band ladder is walked over the lead's `total_room_nights_requested`: the first 5,000 requested room nights are worth 12.00 dollars each, the next 10,000 room nights — those from the 5,001st through the 15,000th — are worth 8.00 dollars each, and every room night beyond the 15,000th is worth 5.00 dollars, with the three band contributions added and the total rounded half-up to two decimals to give the band ceiling. Second, a lodging-tax yield ceiling is computed from the newest `PROVISIONAL` estimate's `lodging_tax_yield_amount` multiplied by a rate keyed on the planner's `tier`: 0.35 for `TIER_A`, 0.25 for `TIER_B` and 0.15 for `TIER_C`, rounded half-up to two decimals. Recall from Section 3.1 that `tier` derives from `relationship_score_lifetime` alone, so the rolling score never moves this rate. Third, the smaller of those two ceilings is taken and multiplied by a coverage factor drawn from the lead's current `block_coverage_pct`: the factor is 1.00 when coverage is at least 95.00 percent, 0.90 when coverage is at least 85.00 percent but below 95.00, and 0.75 otherwise — and the multiplication is applied to the already-rounded smaller ceiling, with the product itself then rounded half-up to two decimals. Fourth and last, the result is clamped down to the named fund's `uncommitted_amount`, so the cap can never exceed money the fund actually still has. That final clamp is skipped when the cap is computed at request time, because no fund has been named yet; it applies when the cap is recomputed at decision time against the fund the approver names.

The decision itself belongs to an `IncentiveApprover` acting on a package in status `INCENTIVE_PENDING`, and it is the most heavily gated operation in the system. The request names the `fund_id` to be charged, the `decision` — one of `APPROVED`, `REDUCED` or `REJECTED` — the `approved_amount` of cash to be pledged, a written `rationale` of 1 to 500 characters recorded for board audit, and `expected_fund_version` per Section 2.6. The server snapshots the package's `incentive_request_amount` onto the decision as `requested_amount` and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `decided_by_user_id`. The verdict constrains the amount, and all four constraints are schema violations answered with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400: `approved_amount` must equal `requested_amount` when the `decision` is `APPROVED`, must be strictly greater than zero and strictly less than `requested_amount` when the `decision` is `REDUCED`, and must be exactly 0 when the `decision` is `REJECTED`. The two of them that stand on their own — 0 for a rejection, strictly positive for a reduction — are checked at the schema rung with the rest of the body, ahead of the fund even being resolved. The two that compare `approved_amount` against `requested_amount` cannot be, because `requested_amount` is read off the package: they are evaluated late, after the fund's version guard, after the package's `INCENTIVE_PENDING` state gate and after the segregation-of-duties check, and only then, still ahead of every fiscal gate. So an approver who is also the preparer, quoting an amount that does not match the request, learns about the segregation-of-duties violation rather than about the amount. Like the other event records, a decision exposes `id` and `created_at` and carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`; and the operation answers with the decision record itself, at HTTP 201, rather than with the bid package it decides. Decisions are readable as a sub-collection of the package, filterable by `decision`.

Segregation of duties, described in Section 2.11, is checked before any money question is asked. After it, and in the order set out in Section 2.7, four fiscal gates apply to an `APPROVED` or `REDUCED` verdict. The named fund must not be in `posture` `EXHAUSTED`, or the decision fails with `ERR_FUND_EXHAUSTED` and HTTP 409. The `approved_amount` must not exceed the incentive cap **recomputed at decision time** — not the value stored at request time — or the decision fails with `ERR_INCENTIVE_EXCEEDS_CAP`. The resulting `total_charge_amount` must not exceed the fund's `uncommitted_amount`, or the decision fails with `ERR_FUND_INSUFFICIENT`. And the `tax_payback_ratio` must be at least 3.00 — the city insists on three dollars of projected lodging tax for every dollar of gross charge — or the decision fails with `ERR_TAX_PAYBACK_BELOW_FLOOR`. The last three all carry HTTP 422.

The charge is composed as follows, and the evaluation order here is pinned as tightly as the cap's. Take the gross figure, which is the decision's `approved_amount`. Select the administrative fee rate from **that gross figure only**: 0.05 when the gross is below 100,000.00, 0.04 when the gross is at or above 100,000.00 but below 250,000.00, and 0.03 when the gross is at or above 250,000.00. The `admin_fee_amount` is the gross multiplied by that rate, rounded half-up to two decimals, and `total_charge_amount` is the gross plus that fee. The fee band is never re-derived from `total_charge_amount`, even when adding the fee pushes the total across a band boundary: a gross of 96,000.00 attracts a fee of 4,800.00 for a total of 100,800.00, and the fee stays 4,800.00 — it is emphatically not recomputed as 3,840.00. Finally `tax_payback_ratio` is the newest `PROVISIONAL` estimate's `lodging_tax_yield_amount` divided by `total_charge_amount`, rounded half-up to two decimals.

An `APPROVED` or `REDUCED` verdict that clears all four gates stores `admin_fee_amount`, `total_charge_amount` and `tax_payback_ratio` on the decision, mirrors them onto the package as `approved_incentive_amount` (the gross), `admin_fee_amount`, `total_fund_charge_amount` and `tax_payback_ratio`, and moves the package from `INCENTIVE_PENDING` to `APPROVED`. The fund is then charged: `committed_amount` rises by `total_charge_amount`, `uncommitted_amount` falls by the same amount, `posture` is re-derived per Section 2.10, and the fund's `version` is incremented by one.

A `REJECTED` verdict is a **compensating revert** rather than a charge. The package returns from `INCENTIVE_PENDING` to `DRAFT`, its `incentive_request_amount` is cleared to null, and `approved_incentive_amount`, `admin_fee_amount`, `total_fund_charge_amount` and `tax_payback_ratio` all remain null because no charge was ever created. `incentive_cap_amount` keeps the ceiling the request computed, and — unlike the revision round of Section 3.8 — `incentive_requested_by_user_id` is left standing too, so the officer whose request was just rejected remains recorded on the package and remains barred by Section 2.11 from deciding the replacement request they may now go on to make. No fund balance moves — but the fund's `version` is still incremented by one. Critically, a rejection does **not** consume a negotiation round: `round_number` is left exactly where it was, so the sales manager may immediately request a smaller incentive on the same round. Only a planner-requested revision, described next, advances `round_number`.

### 3.8. Submission, the Planner's Verdict, the Revision Cycle and Lost Business

Putting the bid in front of the planner is a `SalesManager`'s act on a package in status `APPROVED`, and it is where the bureau's promises are finally tested against reality. The only field it carries is `response_due_at`, the instant after which the planner may no longer answer, which must be strictly later than the current logical clock. Five gates guard the submission. The package's own status is checked first, at its rung in Section 2.7; the lead's stage is checked immediately after it and therefore ahead of the four business gates, out of the ladder order its rung would otherwise imply. The lead must still be in `sales_stage` `QUALIFIED`, or the submission fails with `ERR_INVALID_OPPORTUNITY_STAGE`. The lead must hold at least one space hold in status `HELD`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, or the submission fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_SPACE_HOLD` — in practice this is the `FIRST`-rank hold that will be confirmed if the planner accepts. The package's `support_letter_type_count` must be at least 2, or the submission fails with `ERR_SUPPORT_LETTERS_INSUFFICIENT`; two letters of the same constituency do not satisfy the community-support requirement. The lead's `block_coverage_pct` must be at least 85.00, or the submission fails with `ERR_BLOCK_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` — the bureau does not promise a citywide block it has assembled less than 85 percent of. And the lead's `cutoff_alignment_spread_days` must be at most 2, or the submission fails with `ERR_CUTOFF_MISALIGNED`; a block whose properties release unsold rooms more than two days apart cannot be advertised with a single citywide cutoff. All four of those business gates return HTTP 422.

A successful submission stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, stores `response_due_at`, and moves the package from `APPROVED` to `SUBMITTED`. Two hops follow: because the bid became `SUBMITTED`, the lead's `sales_stage` moves from `QUALIFIED` to `BIDDING` and its `version` is incremented by one; and because the lead was touched, the owning planner's `last_touch_at` is refreshed from the logical clock, an unconditional write that advances the planner's own `version` by one on every submission even when the clock has not moved since the previous touch. Nothing else on the planner moves: submission recomputes no counter, no score and no `tier`.

The verdict belongs to the `MeetingPlanner` whose `X-User-ID` matches the lead's `planner_id`, acting on a package in status `SUBMITTED`. The request carries `response`, one of `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and an optional `decline_reason` used when the verdict is a decline. `decline_reason` is optional in the strong sense: a decline that omits it is accepted and stores `decline_reason` as null, and a `decline_reason` supplied alongside an `ACCEPTED` or a `REVISION_REQUESTED` verdict is validated as a declared member of the enum and then simply discarded — only the decline arm ever stores it. The answer must arrive while the current logical clock is at or before `response_due_at`; a late answer is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED` and HTTP 422. Whatever the verdict, it is stored on the package as `planner_response`, and whatever the verdict, the owning planner's `last_touch_at` is refreshed from the logical clock — qualification, submission and the planner's own answer are the three events that count as a touch on the relationship. However many hops of this one operation land on the planner's row — the touch itself, plus the score-and-tier recomputation that an acceptance or a lost cascade fires — the planner's `version` comes out of the whole operation exactly one higher than it went in, and its `updated_at` reads the current clock. That single increment is unconditional even when the recomputation moved nothing.

**Acceptance** is the moment the destination wins, and it cascades four hops deep. Hop 1 moves the package from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`. Hop 2: because the bid was accepted, the lead's `sales_stage` moves from `BIDDING` to `DEFINITE`, its `definite_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `definite_room_nights` is frozen at `committed_peak_rooms` multiplied by `event_nights` — frozen meaning that later commitment churn does not move it. Hop 3: because the lead became `DEFINITE`, its `FIRST`-rank space hold in status `HELD` or `CHALLENGED` is confirmed exactly as described at the end of Section 3.4, and the lead's `hold_posture` becomes `CONFIRMED` — arriving there from `TENTATIVE` when the hold was unchallenged, or from `CHALLENGED` when it was, in which case the confirmation is only legitimate while the clock is at or before that hold's `challenge_deadline`. Hop 4: because that hold became `CONFIRMED`, every other hold on the same hall in status `HELD` or `CHALLENGED` whose buffered window conflicts is released with `release_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_CONFIRMED`, and each superseded lead's `hold_posture` becomes `RELEASED` once none of its own holds remains active. Running in parallel with hops 3 and 4 is the planner-side recomputation: because the lead became `DEFINITE`, the owning planner's `definite_count` and `lifetime_definite_room_nights` are re-derived and then `relationship_score_lifetime`, `relationship_score_rolling_24m` and `tier` are all recomputed per Section 3.1. The lead's `version` is incremented by one.

**A revision request** is the cyclic heart of the negotiation, and its behaviour forks on `round_number`. While `round_number` is below 3, the package returns from `SUBMITTED` to `DRAFT` with `round_number` incremented by one; `submitted_at` and `response_due_at` are cleared to null; and the entire incentive apparatus of the finished round is wiped — `incentive_request_amount`, `approved_incentive_amount`, `admin_fee_amount`, `total_fund_charge_amount`, `tax_payback_ratio` and `incentive_requested_by_user_id` are all cleared to null. Because the bid left the committed set, the fund's `committed_amount` falls by the previous `total_fund_charge_amount`, `uncommitted_amount` rises by the same amount and `posture` is re-derived, preserving the conservation identity of Section 2.10 across the decommit. And because the bid returned to preparation, the lead's `sales_stage` moves back from `BIDDING` to `QUALIFIED` and the lead's `version` is incremented by one. `incentive_cap_amount` is not among the wiped fields — it keeps the ceiling the finished round computed until a fresh request overwrites it. The bureau may then re-request an incentive, re-approve it and re-submit — the `QUALIFIED` to `BIDDING` to `QUALIFIED` loop may run up to three rounds in total. When, however, a revision is requested while `round_number` already equals 3, the ladder terminates instead of looping: the package moves from `SUBMITTED` to `DECLINED` with `decline_reason` `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, and the lead moves from `BIDDING` to `LOST` with `lost_reason` `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` plus the full lost cascade described below.

**A decline** stores the planner's `decline_reason` — one of `RATE_TOO_HIGH`, `INSUFFICIENT_INCENTIVE`, `LOST_TO_COMPETITOR` or `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — moves the package from `SUBMITTED` to `DECLINED`, and moves the lead from `BIDDING` to `LOST` with `lost_reason` `PLANNER_DECLINED_BID`, again with the full lost cascade.

Losing business is also something a `SalesManager` can do deliberately, on a lead in `sales_stage` `QUALIFIED` or `BIDDING`, by supplying a `lost_reason` that must be a declared member of `RATE_TOO_HIGH`, `DATES_UNAVAILABLE`, `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, `LOST_TO_COMPETITOR`, `PLANNER_DECLINED_BID`, `INSUFFICIENT_INCENTIVE` or `REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. The stage moves to `LOST`, the reason is stored, and the lead's `version` is incremented by one. When the lead was in `BIDDING`, the live package — whatever its status among `DRAFT`, `INCENTIVE_PENDING`, `APPROVED` and `SUBMITTED` — is moved to `DECLINED` with its `decline_reason` mapped from the lead's `lost_reason`: `RATE_TOO_HIGH`, `INSUFFICIENT_INCENTIVE` and `LOST_TO_COMPETITOR` map through unchanged, and every other reason maps to `LOST_TO_COMPETITOR`.

Whichever route reaches it, **the lost cascade is one fixed set of consequences**, and it is identical whether the lead was in `QUALIFIED` or `BIDDING`, whether a person or the system triggered it. Every space hold of the lead in status `HELD` or `CHALLENGED` is released with `release_reason` `OPPORTUNITY_LOST`, which runs the promotion rule of Section 3.4 and can hand a `FIRST` rank to a competitor's waiting second option. Every room block commitment of the lead in status `INVITED`, `OFFERED` or `COMMITTED` is withdrawn with `withdraw_reason` `OPPORTUNITY_LOST`, which lowers the affected properties' `committed_peak_rooms` and `committed_room_nights`. Because that leaves the lead with no commitment in `COMMITTED` at all, the lead's own citywide block figures are then re-derived from the committed set exactly as Section 3.5 defines them, so `committed_peak_rooms`, `citywide_cutoff_days_before_arrival` and `cutoff_alignment_spread_days` all land on 0 and `block_coverage_pct` on 0.00; this is a recomputation only and does not itself fire the coverage-drop hop of Section 3.5, so no bid package of the lost lead is returned to `DRAFT` by it. Any live fund charge is decommitted: the fund's `committed_amount` falls by the package's `total_fund_charge_amount`, `uncommitted_amount` rises by the same amount, and `posture` is re-derived. Every servicing order of the lead still in `REQUESTED` is cancelled. Because the planner's `lost_count` has changed, `relationship_score_lifetime`, `relationship_score_rolling_24m` and `tier` are recomputed. And the lead's `hold_posture` becomes `RELEASED` once no non-terminal hold of its own remains. However many of those hops land on the lead's own row, the loss as a whole advances the lead's `version` by exactly one — with one arithmetically visible exception: when the loss was itself provoked by the lead's last active hold leaving the active set, the posture hop of Section 3.4 has already advanced the row once before the cascade starts, so such a lead comes out of the pair of operations two versions ahead. And the cascade does not touch a bid package's own `status`: a package is moved to `DECLINED` only where the operation that reached the cascade says so — the planner's decline, the exhausted revision ladder, or a deliberate loss recorded while the lead was in `BIDDING`. A lead lost for want of space therefore leaves its package standing in `SUBMITTED`, with nothing but the fund charge decommitted out from under it.

### 3.9. Servicing the Event, Settling Attrition and Reporting to the Board

Once a convention is definite, the bureau's job changes from selling to delivering. A `ServicesManager` opens one **servicing order** per workstream against a lead in `sales_stage` `DEFINITE` — attempting one before the lead is definite is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OPPORTUNITY_STAGE`. The order names its `opportunity_id`, its `category`, which is one of `HOUSING`, `TRANSPORTATION` or `VOLUNTEER`, the `requested_units` of housing-bureau seats, shuttle vehicles or volunteer shifts needed, which must be at least 1, and the servicing window as `window_start` and its exclusive end `window_end`, with `window_start` strictly earlier than `window_end`. Nothing ties that window to the event it services: it is not required to fall inside, overlap, or even come anywhere near the lead's `arrival_date` and `departure_date`, and no gate compares the two. The order is created in status `REQUESTED` with `assigned_units` and `delivered_units` both 0. Servicing orders of a lead are readable as a sub-collection filterable by `category` and `status`.

Scheduling the order records `assigned_units`, the units actually put on the calendar, which must be at least 1 and at most `requested_units`, and moves the order from `REQUESTED` to `SCHEDULED`. Delivery records `delivered_units`, the units actually delivered on site, which may be as low as 0 and at most `assigned_units`, and moves the order from `SCHEDULED` to `DELIVERED`. Cancellation is available from `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED`, moves the order to `CANCELLED`, and leaves `assigned_units` and `delivered_units` exactly as they stand — a cancelled order contributes nothing to servicing coverage. A `DELIVERED` order can never be cancelled, and every one of these three operations attempted from the wrong status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SERVICING_STATE` and HTTP 409.

Two things about these three operations are easy to over-specify. The lead's `sales_stage` is a gate on opening a servicing order and on nothing else: scheduling, delivering and cancelling all consult the order's own status alone and never the lead's stage, so an order left standing on a lead the bureau afterwards lost may still be scheduled, delivered or cancelled. And the two upper bounds on the unit counts — `assigned_units` at most `requested_units`, `delivered_units` at most `assigned_units` — are schema violations answered with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400, not state violations; because each reads a figure off the order row, each is evaluated after that operation's `ERR_INVALID_SERVICING_STATE` gate rather than at the schema rung, so a delivery of 99 units against an order that is still `REQUESTED` is answered 409 and not 400.

The event's completion is not declared by anybody; it **bubbles up**. Whenever a delivery or a cancellation leaves no servicing order of the lead in status `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED`, and at least one order of that lead is in `DELIVERED`, the lead's `sales_stage` moves from `DEFINITE` to `SERVICED` and its `version` is incremented by one. The "at least one delivered" condition is what makes the bubble-up conditional rather than automatic: because a delivered order can never be cancelled, a lead whose every order was cancelled has nothing delivered and therefore never bubbles up — it stays `DEFINITE` indefinitely. Reaching `SERVICED` fires four consequences at once. The lead's `attrition_settlement_amount` is recomputed across every commitment as described below — settled at the moment of servicing rather than sealed for ever, since a night filed afterwards against one of the released commitments will move it again. Every room block commitment of the lead in status `COMMITTED` is released with `withdraw_reason` `EVENT_SERVICED`, freeing the properties' `committed_peak_rooms` and `committed_room_nights`. The lead's fund charge moves from committed to disbursed — the fund's `committed_amount` falls by the package's `total_fund_charge_amount` while `disbursed_amount` rises by the same amount, leaving `budget_amount` and `uncommitted_amount` untouched so the conservation identity of Section 2.10 still holds, after which `posture` is re-derived. And the lead's `CONFIRMED` space hold is released with `release_reason` `EVENT_SERVICED` and the hall's `confirmed_hold_count` is decremented — the hall is handed back because the show is over. Note carefully that the lead's `hold_posture` **stays** `CONFIRMED` through all of this: that dimension records the historical fact that citywide space was confirmed for the event, and servicing never rewinds it. All four consequences together advance the lead's `version` by exactly one, the single increment that accompanies the move to `SERVICED`; each released hold advances its hall's `version` once; and handing a hall back never promotes a waiting second option, because the show the space was held for has already happened.

**Attrition** is the money the bureau gets back when a convention fails to fill the block the city held for it, and it is computed per property before being totalled. Only commitments in status `COMMITTED` or `RELEASED` are ever assessed; whenever a commitment in `INVITED`, `OFFERED` or `WITHDRAWN` is assessed it is assessed at 0 shortfall nights and 0.00 damages, and a commitment with no pickup record at all is skipped equally, its figures left at 0 rather than computed — no shortfall is charged before any pickup has been reported, which is why a lead's `attrition_settlement_amount` remains 0.00 until the first pickup record exists. For each participating commitment, the allowance is 80 percent of its `committed_room_nights`, taking the floor to a whole number of nights; the shortfall, exposed as `attrition_shortfall_nights`, is that allowance less the commitment's `picked_up_room_nights`, or 0 when pickup met or beat the allowance; and that commitment's `attrition_damages_amount` is the shortfall nights multiplied by the commitment's `rate_offered_amount` and then by a damage rate of 0.75 — the city recovers three quarters of the contracted rate on rooms it held and nobody used — rounded half-up to two decimals. The rounding order is pinned: each commitment's damages are rounded to two decimals **first**, and the rounded per-commitment figures are then added together in order of the property's `name` ascending with the commitment's `created_at` ascending as tiebreaker, to give the lead's `attrition_settlement_amount`. Summing raw products and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ by cents. The total is assembled from the `attrition_damages_amount` each commitment of the lead is *carrying* at that moment, and no commitment is re-assessed on the way: every commitment of the lead is added in, its stored figure taken as it stands, so a commitment that was assessed while it was `COMMITTED` and only later withdrawn keeps contributing its last-assessed damages to the lead's total for good — withdrawal is not one of the events that revises an assessment. The events that do revise a commitment's assessment are exactly two: a pickup record filed against it, and its release by a lead reaching `SERVICED` or `LOST`. The lead's `attrition_settlement_amount` is therefore recomputed on every pickup record filed, and again when the lead reaches `SERVICED`; because a night may still be filed against a `RELEASED` commitment afterwards, it may move again after that.

The last act belongs to the `BoardAnalyst`, who answers to the body that appropriates the lodging tax. A **board report** is created over a reporting period given as `period_start`, the inclusive first date, and `period_end`, the exclusive last date, with `period_start` strictly earlier than `period_end`. The window is half-open and interpreted at midnight UTC on each boundary: a row counts when its governing instant falls at or after `period_start` at `00:00:00.000Z` and strictly before `period_end` at `00:00:00.000Z`. Every figure is computed once, at creation, and the report is born in status `DRAFT`.

Which instant governs depends on the figure, and getting the pairing wrong is the classic way to produce a report the board cannot reconcile. Room-night production is keyed on `definite_at`: `definite_opportunity_count` is the number of opportunities in `sales_stage` `DEFINITE` or `SERVICED` whose `definite_at` falls in the window, and `definite_room_nights` is the sum of `definite_room_nights` over that same set. Lost business is keyed on `updated_at`: `lost_opportunity_count` is the number of opportunities in `sales_stage` `LOST` whose `updated_at` falls in the window, and `lost_room_nights_by_reason` is a map from each `lost_reason` to the summed `total_room_nights_requested` of the in-window lost opportunities carrying that reason, with any reason whose total would be zero omitted from the map entirely rather than reported as 0. Incentive spend is keyed on the decision's own `created_at`: `incentive_spend_committed_amount` is the sum of `total_charge_amount` across incentive decisions created inside the window whose `decision` was `APPROVED` or `REDUCED`, so rejections contribute nothing. Recognized revenue is keyed on the receipt's `received_at`: `lodging_tax_recognized_amount` is the sum of the `amount` of every lodging tax receipt whose `received_at` falls in the window. Attrition recovery is keyed on `updated_at` again: `attrition_recovered_amount` is the sum of `attrition_settlement_amount` over opportunities in `sales_stage` `SERVICED` whose `updated_at` falls in the window. And the headline efficiency figure, `cost_per_definite_room_night`, is `incentive_spend_committed_amount` divided by `definite_room_nights`, rounded half-up to two decimals — except when `definite_room_nights` is 0, in which case it is reported as null rather than as 0.00. Rows falling outside the window are excluded even when their opportunity is otherwise in scope. Where a figure is keyed on `updated_at`, that is the row's live last-modified stamp and not a record of when the lead was lost or serviced: anything that touches the lead afterwards — a pickup filed against a released commitment, for instance — moves it out of the period it was originally reported in and into a later one, which is why a board report is a snapshot computed once at creation rather than a live query. The three figures keyed on it are read from each lead independently, so one lead can perfectly well be counted as lost in one report and, after a later touch, in the next one too.

Publishing a report stamps `published_at` from the logical clock and moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED`. A published report is frozen forever: its aggregates are never recomputed no matter how the underlying data later moves, and a second publication attempt is refused with `ERR_BOARD_REPORT_PUBLISHED` and HTTP 409. Board reports are readable individually and as a list filterable by `status`.
