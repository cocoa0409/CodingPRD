# Livestock Mandatory Price Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Livestock market news only works if the firms that dominate slaughter are compelled to report what they actually paid. This service is the system of record for that compulsion: it decides which packers, packer-processors and importers cross the statutory slaughter-volume thresholds and must therefore register as reporting entities, it collects their transaction detail window by window, it aggregates those filings into published weighted-average prices, volumes and price ranges, and it verifies the filings against the plants' own books after the fact.

Three tensions drive the design. The first is between **publication and confidentiality** — a market report is worthless if it is not released, and unlawful if a reader can back out an individual reporter's prices from it, so every aggregate must survive a mechanical set of publication guidelines before it becomes public. The second is between **speed and correctability** — daily and weekly deadlines mean filings arrive fast and imperfect, so the platform supports a bounded correction and resubmission loop, a self-superseding filing ledger, and a re-aggregation cascade that automatically pulls a published report back off the wire when the data underneath it moves. The third is between **enforcement and fairness** — lateness and inaccuracy carry escalating monetary penalties and delinquency points that can suspend or revoke a registration, so the penalty pipeline is deliberately split across separate pairs of hands and every multiplier that produced an assessed amount is retained on the record.

Every temporal rule in this document — submission lateness, correction cutoffs, publication release times, comment periods and notice periods — is evaluated against a **logical clock** that the platform owns, never against the host machine's wall clock. That single decision makes the whole compliance calendar reproducible: an integration test can advance the program from a Tuesday deadline to a Friday audit in one call.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All paths in this document are absolute; the service exposes no common URL prefix. Every request, without exception, is authenticated, shape-checked and authorized before any business rule runs.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present and well formed but the presented role is not one of the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is checked before authorization, and both are checked before request shape, path resolution and every business rule.

The six roles are `ReporterAgent`, `ProgramAdmin`, `MarketAnalyst`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `PenaltyOfficer` and `PublicUser`.

- A `ReporterAgent` is a packing firm acting for itself: it starts filings, appends transaction and wholesale product lines, files and refiles them, comments on proposed methodology changes, and appeals its own threshold determinations. When `X-User-Role` is `ReporterAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ReportingEntity`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ReportingEntity` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for filing operations the comparand is the owning firm of the filing's plant — the `reporting_entity_id` of the `Plant` named by the submission's `plant_id` — and for an appeal it is the `reporting_entity_id` of the registration itself. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FOREIGN_REPORTING_ENTITY` (HTTP 403).
- A `ProgramAdmin` administers the program: reporting entities, plants, registrations and their determinations, reporting windows, the methodology-change docket and the logical clock. A `ProgramAdmin` may also act on a reporter's behalf anywhere along the filing chain and may waive a penalty.
- A `MarketAnalyst` validates filings and owns the publication pipeline: accepting or rejecting filed submissions, creating report shells, aggregating, applying the publication guidelines and releasing reports. A `MarketAnalyst` never audits and never assesses money.
- A `ComplianceAuditor` verifies accepted filings against plant records and may reopen a filing for correction. A `ComplianceAuditor` never assesses money.
- A `PenaltyOfficer` assesses penalties, records payment and waives assessments.
- A `PublicUser` reads published market news and files public comments on proposed methodology changes.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `MarketAnalyst`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `PenaltyOfficer` or `PublicUser`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `opened_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on an `AuditCase`, or as `assessed_by_user_id` on a `PenaltyAssessment`, where those transitions write it — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two access rules go beyond a plain role list. **Segregation of duties** governs the audit-to-penalty handoff: the `X-User-ID` finalizing an audit case must differ from the `opened_by_user_id` recorded when that case was opened, and the `X-User-ID` assessing a penalty must differ from the `finalized_by_user_id` of the audit case that penalty references. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). **Public disclosure scoping** governs market news: when `X-User-Role` is `PublicUser`, a `MarketReport` is readable only while its `publication_state` is `PUBLISHED`; a report that is unpublished, withheld or withdrawn is invisible to the public and both the single-report read and the report list reject the attempt with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 403). Both of these rules are evaluated after the role-list check and before the optimistic-concurrency check, with segregation of duties ranking ahead of public disclosure scoping.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as a boolean indicating whether records remain beyond the current page. All the filter parameters described alongside the individual workflows in §3 compose with pagination and with each other.

### 2.4. The Program's Logical Clock

The compliance calendar is driven by a server-owned logical clock rather than by real time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must never fall back to wall-clock time, not on the first request and not after a restart. A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock to any instant by supplying `current_utc`, and the response echoes back the instant now in force. Setting the clock mutates no entity: nothing is recomputed, no state advances, no version is bumped. What changes is the answer every subsequent temporal evaluation gives — submission lateness, the correction cutoff, the earliest publication instant, window locking, comment-period closure and the two methodology notice periods all read this clock, and so do the `submitted_at`, `accepted_at`, `settled_at`, `closed_at`, `computed_at`, `published_at`, `received_at`, `effective_from` and `industry_notice_issued_at` stamps that operations write.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Immutable Line Items

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Clients never supply either.

Most resources — reporting entities, plants, registrations, reporting windows, submissions, market reports, audit cases, penalty assessments and methodology changes — additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and a `version` integer. `version` starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly or arrived as a cascade from somewhere else in the program; `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock on the same occasions.

Three record types are append-only ledger lines and are deliberately leaner: a `TransactionRecord`, a `WholesaleLot` and a `PublicComment` carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` and no `version`, because nothing ever updates them — once a transaction line, a wholesale product line or a public comment is written, it is immutable. Corrections to filed data are made by superseding the whole filing, never by editing a line.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Ledger-Bearing Parents

Two write operations depend on a running total held on a parent record, and both therefore demand that the client prove it read that parent recently.

Starting a filing — the `POST /submissions` operation — requires `expected_window_version`, a non-optional field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ReportingWindow`. A filing decision depends on the window's collected state — `accepted_submission_count`, `total_head_reported`, and whether the new filing has to supersede an already-accepted one — so if the window's `version` has advanced past the supplied value, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WINDOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than being filed against an out-of-date view. The window read is the natural place to obtain the current value.

Assessing a penalty — the `POST /penalty-assessments` operation — requires `expected_reporting_entity_version`, a non-optional field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ReportingEntity`. The assessed amount and the point escalation are both functions of the firm's `delinquency_points` and ledger balances as of assessment time, and another assessment stacking under the recidivism rule will have moved them; if the entity's `version` no longer equals the supplied value, the assessment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENTITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reporting-entity read is the natural place to obtain the current value.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Every other write is accepted on the strength of its own state preconditions.

### 2.7. Precision: Two Deliberately Opposite Rounding Orders

All monetary and rate figures carry 2 decimal places and are rounded half up unless this document says otherwise. Two places in the program round the *same* underlying transaction data in *opposite orders*, and the difference is intentional.

**Round then sum** governs what a reporter books. Each transaction line's value and each wholesale lot's value is rounded half up to 2 decimals *at the line*, and the submission's booked totals are the sums of those already-rounded line figures. Two transaction lines each worth 2427.1750 dollars therefore book as 2427.18 plus 2427.18, that is 4854.36 — not the 4854.35 that summing at full precision and rounding once would give. The booked figure 4854.36 is what an audit compares against the plant's records.

**Sum then round once** governs what the program publishes. A market report's weighted average price accumulates every per-item weight and every per-item weight-times-price product at full precision, divides once, and rounds half up to 2 decimals exactly once at the end. Rounding each submission's own weighted average first and then combining those rounded averages is explicitly not the program methodology and yields a different cent value.

Beyond these two, `measured_head`, `total_head`, `total_head_reported`, `reported_head`, `reported_load_count`, `lateness_minutes`, `days_late`, `points` and all `_cents` figures are integers; `volume_factor` alone carries 3 decimals; percentage rates (`materiality_pct`, `head_discrepancy_rate_pct`, `value_discrepancy_rate_pct`, `max_reporter_share_pct`, `max_single_reporter_share_pct`) carry 2 decimals rounded half up. All money moves in whole cents, so no amount field ever holds a fraction of a cent.

### 2.8. Path and Foreign-Key Resolution

Path segments resolve strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error. A reporting-entity segment that does not resolve yields `ERR_REPORTING_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, a plant segment `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, a registration segment `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, a reporting-window segment `ERR_REPORTING_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, a submission segment `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, a market-report segment `ERR_MARKET_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, an audit-case segment `ERR_AUDIT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, a penalty-assessment segment `ERR_PENALTY_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` and a methodology-change segment `ERR_METHODOLOGY_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND`. All are HTTP 404. The two sub-collection paths in the program — a submission's line items and a methodology change's comments — resolve the parent first, so a bad parent id never surfaces as a child-level error.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after every path segment, in declaration order: `reporting_entity_id` yields `ERR_REPORTING_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, `plant_id` yields `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `reporting_window_id` yields `ERR_REPORTING_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `supersedes_submission_id` and `submission_id` both yield `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, and `audit_case_id` yields `ERR_AUDIT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`. This body-level resolution applies to the six creation operations that carry foreign keys: creating a plant, a registration, a submission, a market report, an audit case and a penalty assessment.

### 2.9. The Order of Rejection

A request that violates several rules at once must fail with a single, predictable error. The platform therefore evaluates checks in one fixed global precedence, and the first violated check decides the response. Reading from highest precedence downward, the bands are:

1. **Authentication**, then **authorization** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, then `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
2. **Request shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). This covers a missing required input; an out-of-range value for any of the enumerated inputs `entity_type`, `species`, `species_scope`, `purchase_type`, `product_class`, `sale_type`, `decision`, `violation_type`, `stance`, `basis_variant`, `weighting_variant`, `purchase_type_filter`, `category`, `report_class` and `frequency`; a `head_count`, `load_count` or `total_pounds` that is not strictly positive; an `avg_net_price_per_cwt`, `avg_carcass_weight_cwt` or `price_per_cwt` that is not strictly positive; a negative `verified_head` or `verified_value_dollars`; an `annual_head_history` whose keys are not four-digit year strings or whose values are not non-negative integers; a `period_start` that is not strictly earlier than `period_end`; a deadline triple that is out of order, meaning `submission_deadline_utc` must be no later than `correction_deadline_utc`, which in turn must be no later than `publication_release_utc`; and a `limit` outside 1 through 100 or a negative `offset`.
3. **Path existence**, then **body foreign-key existence** — the HTTP 404 family of §2.8, path segments before body keys.
4. **Identity-scoped and disclosure-scoped access** — `ERR_FOREIGN_REPORTING_ENTITY`, then `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, then `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PUBLISHED`.
5. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_WINDOW_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_ENTITY_VERSION`.
6. **State preconditions** — in order: `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_EDITABLE`, `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_METHODOLOGY_STATE`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_REVIEWED`, `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_PENALTY_ALREADY_SETTLED`.
7. **Confidentiality and eligibility safeguards** — in order: `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_SUPPRESSION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DATA`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_WHOLESALE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.
8. **Temporal rules** — in order: `ERR_CORRECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_PUBLICATION_BEFORE_RELEASE_TIME`, `ERR_WINDOW_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`, `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`, `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_TOO_SOON`, `ERR_FORWARD_DELIVERY_INTERVAL_INVALID`.
9. **Referential agreement** — in order: `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_PLANT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_SPECIES_CLASS_MISMATCH`, `ERR_LINE_ITEM_CLASS_MISMATCH`, `ERR_AUDIT_CASE_NOT_DISCREPANT`.
10. **Structural constraints** — in order: `ERR_WINDOW_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION_YEAR`, `ERR_MISSING_PURCHASE_TYPE_ELEMENT`, `ERR_FORBIDDEN_PURCHASE_TYPE_ELEMENT`, `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_INCOMPLETE_SLAUGHTER_HISTORY`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PLANTS`, `ERR_EMPTY_SUBMISSION`, `ERR_ENTITY_EXEMPT`, `ERR_PENDING_SUBMISSIONS_EXIST`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_LOCKED`, `ERR_NO_PUBLIC_COMMENTS`.
11. **Fiscal and capacity rules** — in order: `ERR_FORWARD_COMMITMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_OUTSTANDING_PENALTY_BLOCKS_ACTIVATION`, `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

Three of these orderings are counter-intuitive enough to be worth calling out, because a naive implementation would get them backwards.

- **Editability outranks the purchase-type element rules.** A `FORMULA` transaction line with no `formula_base_reference` posted to a submission that is no longer editable returns `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_EDITABLE`, not `ERR_MISSING_PURCHASE_TYPE_ELEMENT`.
- **Suppression outranks the release-time rule.** A withheld report that someone tries to publish before its release instant returns `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_SUPPRESSION_REQUIRED`, not `ERR_PUBLICATION_BEFORE_RELEASE_TIME`.
- **The supersede-target agreement rule outranks the duplicate-filing rule.** A filing that names a supersede target belonging to another plant, on a plant that already holds an open filing for the window, returns `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_PLANT_MISMATCH`, not `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION`.

One vacuous-truth interaction also matters: because the slaughter-history completeness check ranks ahead of the eligible-plant check, a firm with no eligible plant at all passes the history check trivially (there is no plant to be missing a year) and then fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PLANTS`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Re-Aggregation

The program is immediately consistent: a request that changes data also, in the same request, drives every consequence of that change to completion before responding. Two cascade chains cross five entities each and are worth stating once here, because they recur in several workflows below.

The **data-movement chain** runs from a filing to the public wire. Whenever any submission of a window enters or leaves `ACCEPTED` status, the window's collected ledger is recomputed, and every `MarketReport` of that window whose `aggregation_state` is `COMPUTED` is forced to `STALE`. Going stale is never quiet: it forces the report's `disclosure_state` back to `UNREVIEWED` from either `CLEARED` or `WITHHELD` and clears `suppression_reason`, and if the report's `publication_state` was `PUBLISHED` it becomes `WITHDRAWN` while `published_at` is retained for the record. When withdrawal leaves no report of that window still `PUBLISHED`, the window itself falls back from `PUBLISHED` to `LOCKED`. A stale report cannot be reviewed or published again until it is recomputed.

The **enforcement chain** runs from money to filings. An assessment that pushes a firm's delinquency total into the top tier suspends or revokes that firm's governing registration, and a registration entering `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` voids every submission of that firm's plants that is still in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` status. Voiding changes no window ledger, because neither a `DRAFT` nor a `SUBMITTED` filing was ever counted in one.

Every entity touched by a cascade has its own `version` incremented and its own `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. A single audit finalization can therefore bump the versions of an audit case, a submission, a window and several market reports.

### 2.11. Ledger Conservation and Full Recomputation

Two running ledgers in this program are always recomputed from scratch over the current rows rather than adjusted by deltas, so that no drift can accumulate across correction and settlement cycles.

The **window ledger** holds `accepted_submission_count`, the number of submissions of that window currently in `ACCEPTED` status, and `total_head_reported`, the sum of `reported_head` over exactly those submissions. Every write of the window ledger also increments the window's `version` by 1 — which is precisely why starting a filing needs a fresh window version. Because a superseded predecessor drops out of `ACCEPTED` in the very same request that accepts its successor, head is never double counted.

The **firm penalty ledger** holds five figures on the `ReportingEntity`, recomputed over all of that firm's assessments with no time filter: `assessed_penalty_cents` is the sum of `assessed_amount_cents` across every assessment regardless of settlement state; `paid_penalty_cents` sums those whose `status` is `PAID`; `waived_penalty_cents` sums those whose `status` is `WAIVED`; `outstanding_penalty_cents` sums those whose `status` is still `ASSESSED`; and `delinquency_points` sums the `points` of assessments whose `status` is either `ASSESSED` or `PAID`, so a waived assessment contributes no points. Every write of the firm penalty ledger increments the entity's `version` by 1. The conservation invariant holds at every observable instant: `assessed_penalty_cents` equals `paid_penalty_cents` plus `waived_penalty_cents` plus `outstanding_penalty_cents`, and all four are integers no smaller than zero, as is `delinquency_points`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrolling Firms and Their Establishments

A `ProgramAdmin` brings a firm into the program by registering it with its `legal_name` — between 1 and 200 characters and not blank — its `entity_type`, which is `PACKER`, `PACKER_PROCESSOR` or `IMPORTER`, and its `species_scope`, which is `CATTLE`, `SWINE` or `LAMB`. The species scope is not decorative: it selects the head threshold the firm will be measured against, and it constrains which plants may hang off the firm. A newly registered firm starts with `delinquency_points` at 0 and all four penalty ledger figures — `assessed_penalty_cents`, `paid_penalty_cents`, `waived_penalty_cents` and `outstanding_penalty_cents` — at 0, and its `version` at 1. Firms may be listed and narrowed by `entity_type` or by `species_scope`.

Each federally inspected establishment is recorded as a plant hanging off one firm, created with a payload naming the owning `reporting_entity_id` and carrying a `plant_code` that must be unique among that firm's plants. A plant's `species` records what it slaughters and must equal the owning firm's `species_scope`. The heart of a plant record is its `annual_head_history`, an object keyed by four-digit year strings whose values are non-negative integer head counts — a firm might report a history in which the key for 2021 maps to 120000 head and the key for 2025 maps to 132000. Three further figures are optional at creation: `boxed_beef_volume_lbs_prior_year`, the prior year's boxed beef and wholesale product volume in pounds, which may not be negative; `forward_commitment_cap_head`, the largest number of head the plant may hold under overlapping forward-contract delivery commitments on any single calendar day, which also may not be negative; and `status`, which is `ACTIVE`, `IDLE` or `CLOSED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`.

From the wholesale volume the platform derives `wholesale_reporting_required`, a boolean that is true exactly when `boxed_beef_volume_lbs_prior_year` reaches one thousand short tons — that is, 2,000,000 pounds — or more. That flag is the gate on wholesale product reporting: only a plant whose flag is true may attach wholesale product lines to a filing. A plant's operating status, slaughter history, wholesale volume and forward commitment cap may all be amended later by a `ProgramAdmin`, each of `status`, `annual_head_history`, `boxed_beef_volume_lbs_prior_year` and `forward_commitment_cap_head` being individually optional in the amendment payload; any amendment recomputes `wholesale_reporting_required` from the value then in force and increments the plant's `version` by 1. Plants may be listed and narrowed by `reporting_entity_id`, by `species` or by `status`.

### 3.2. Threshold Determination, Appeal and Activation

Whether a firm must report at all is settled by a determination file, opened by a `ProgramAdmin` against one `reporting_entity_id` and one `determination_year` between 2000 and 2100. Only one determination file may be live per firm and year: if a record for that pair already exists in any status other than `REVOKED`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION_YEAR`. The optional `basis_variant` chooses the measurement basis and defaults to `FIVE_YEAR_AVERAGE`, the other option being `PRIOR_YEAR_ACTUAL`. A new file opens in `DRAFT` with `appeal_count` at 0, no `registration_number`, no `effective_from` and no `determination_outcome`.

Running the determination advances the record from `DRAFT` to `DETERMINED`, and it is where the slaughter-volume arithmetic happens. Only plants of the firm whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `IDLE` participate — a `CLOSED` plant never contributes a single head. If no plant of the firm is `ACTIVE` or `IDLE`, the determination fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PLANTS`. The lookback span is the five calendar years strictly before `determination_year`, namely the year five before it, the year four before it, the year three before it, the year two before it and the year immediately before it. Under the `FIVE_YEAR_AVERAGE` basis, every participating plant's `annual_head_history` must contain all five of those years; under the `PRIOR_YEAR_ACTUAL` basis, only the year immediately before `determination_year` must be present. A missing year on any participating plant fails the determination with `ERR_INCOMPLETE_SLAUGHTER_HISTORY`.

The two bases then diverge, and the divergence is the whole point of allowing an appeal. Under `FIVE_YEAR_AVERAGE`, the platform pools every participating plant's head count for every one of the five lookback years into a single total, divides that one pooled total by five, and discards any fractional remainder — `measured_head` is the floor of the pooled total divided by five. The division and the flooring happen once, after pooling; never once per plant and never once per year. Under `PRIOR_YEAR_ACTUAL`, `measured_head` is simply the sum, across participating plants, of the head count recorded for the year immediately before `determination_year`. A firm that grew sharply in its most recent year can therefore measure below threshold on the averaged basis and above threshold on the prior-year basis, from byte-identical plant data.

The threshold itself is copied onto the record as `threshold_head` at determination time, read from the species schedule by the firm's `species_scope`: 125,000 head for `CATTLE`, 100,000 head for `SWINE` and 35,000 head for `LAMB`. The `determination_outcome` is `MANDATORY` when `measured_head` is greater than or equal to `threshold_head`, and `EXEMPT` otherwise; it remains null for as long as the record sits in `DRAFT`. The determination runs under whatever `basis_variant` the record already carries.

A firm that disputes its determination may contest it. Appealing advances the record from `DETERMINED` to `UNDER_APPEAL` and requires an `appeal_reason` in the payload; only the firm itself may appeal, so a `ReporterAgent` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the record's `reporting_entity_id` is turned away with `ERR_FOREIGN_REPORTING_ENTITY`. Each appeal increments `appeal_count` by 1, and the ceiling is two: an appeal filed when `appeal_count` has already reached 2 is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. A `ProgramAdmin` closes the loop by redetermining, which carries `UNDER_APPEAL` back to `DETERMINED`. Redetermination demands an explicit `basis_variant`, overwrites the stored basis with it, and re-runs the whole determination under the same eligibility and history rules — so a firm that has since closed all its plants fails redetermination with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PLANTS`, and a basis switch to `FIVE_YEAR_AVERAGE` over plants holding only the immediately preceding year fails with `ERR_INCOMPLETE_SLAUGHTER_HISTORY` — so `measured_head`, `threshold_head` and `determination_outcome` may all be rewritten, and `determination_outcome` may legitimately flip between `MANDATORY` and `EXEMPT` for identical plant data. The cycle `DETERMINED → UNDER_APPEAL → DETERMINED` may therefore run at most twice.

Activation is what actually obliges the firm's plants to file, and it carries the record from `DETERMINED` to `ACTIVE`. Two gates guard it. A determination whose outcome is `EXEMPT` has no reporting obligation and cannot be activated: the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_EXEMPT`. And a firm that still owes money cannot be activated: if the firm's `outstanding_penalty_cents` is greater than 0, activation is rejected with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_PENALTY_BLOCKS_ACTIVATION`. On success the platform mints the `registration_number` in the form `MPR-` followed by the `determination_year`, a hyphen, and a sequence number zero-padded to four digits — `MPR-2026-0001` for the first registration of the 2026 program year. The sequence number is one greater than the count of registrations that share the same `determination_year` and already hold a non-null `registration_number`, counted immediately before this assignment with those records ordered by `created_at` ascending. Activation also sets `effective_from` to the logical clock and clears `suspended_reason`.

A `ProgramAdmin` may suspend an active registration for cause, moving it from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`; the request carries `suspended_reason`, and the only value this operation accepts is `ADMIN_ACTION`, the other possible reason `DELINQUENCY_TIER_BREACH` being reserved for the enforcement chain. Suspension immediately voids the firm's in-flight work: every submission belonging to any of the firm's plants whose status is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` transitions to `VOIDED`. No window ledger moves, because a `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` filing was never counted in one. A registration in `REVOKED` status is terminal and accepts no action at all; any lifecycle action requested from a status that does not permit it — determining anything other than a `DRAFT`, appealing anything other than a `DETERMINED` record, redetermining anything other than an `UNDER_APPEAL` record, activating anything other than a `DETERMINED` record, suspending anything other than an `ACTIVE` record — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`. Determination files may be listed and narrowed by `reporting_entity_id`, `determination_year`, `status` or `determination_outcome`.

### 3.3. Opening and Closing a Trade Window

Collection happens inside reporting windows, each opened by a `ProgramAdmin` for one `species`, one `report_class` and one `frequency`. The `report_class` separates live-animal reporting from wholesale product reporting and is `LIVE_ANIMAL`, `BOXED_BEEF` or `WHOLESALE_CUTS`; the `frequency` is `DAILY` or `WEEKLY`. The covered trade period runs from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive — window intervals are half-open, so a window ending at midnight and a window starting at that same midnight cover disjoint trade. Two windows sharing the same `species`, `report_class` and `frequency` may not have intersecting half-open intervals; an overlapping proposal is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, while exact adjacency, where the new window's `period_start` equals an existing window's `period_end`, is explicitly allowed.

Three instants govern the window's calendar. `submission_deadline_utc` is the instant after which a filing counts as late — note that lateness is measured, never blocked, so a filing arriving a week after the deadline is still accepted into the process and merely carries its lateness on its face. `correction_deadline_utc` is the last instant at which a reporter-side correction may be opened. `publication_release_utc` is the earliest instant at which a market report for this window may be released. The three must be supplied in non-decreasing order, as §2.9 describes.

Three further parameters are the confidentiality guidelines that will later decide whether an aggregate may be published, and all three are optional at creation. `min_reporter_count` is the minimum number of distinct reporting firms that must stand behind an aggregate and defaults to 3, with 1 as its floor. `max_single_reporter_share_pct` is the largest share of the window's volume any single firm may hold, expressed in percent with 2 decimals, defaulting to 70.00 and constrained to between 0.01 and 100.00 inclusive. `min_reported_head` is the minimum aggregate volume — head for a `LIVE_ANIMAL` window, pounds for a `BOXED_BEEF` or `WHOLESALE_CUTS` window — and defaults to 2000, with 0 as its floor. A new window opens in `OPEN` status with `accepted_submission_count` and `total_head_reported` both at 0.

A window stops collecting when a `ProgramAdmin` locks it, taking it from `OPEN` to `LOCKED`. Locking requires that the trade period be over: the logical clock must be at or after `period_end`, and locking exactly at `period_end` is allowed, while locking strictly earlier is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. Locking also requires that no filing be left hanging: if any submission of the window is still in `SUBMITTED` or `RESUBMITTED` status, awaiting a reviewer's decision, the lock is refused with `ERR_PENDING_SUBMISSIONS_EXIST`. A successful lock increments the window's `version` by 1. Once locked, no new filing may be started for the window and no existing filing may be filed against it — both attempts return `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`.

Beyond `LOCKED`, the window follows its reports. It moves to `PUBLISHED` when a market report of that window is released, and back to `LOCKED` when withdrawal leaves no report of that window still published — the cycle `LOCKED → PUBLISHED → LOCKED` may repeat as often as the data underneath moves. Windows may be listed and narrowed by `species`, `report_class`, `frequency` or `status`.

### 3.4. Building a Filing: Purchase Types and Required Data Elements

A filing begins as a draft against one plant and one open window. The creating payload names the `plant_id` and the `reporting_window_id`, carries the `expected_window_version` described in §2.6, and may optionally name a `supersedes_submission_id` when the filing is intended to replace a report the program has already accepted. The new record opens in `DRAFT` with every booked total — `reported_head`, `reported_cwt`, `reported_value_dollars`, `reported_pounds` and `reported_load_count` — at 0, `resubmission_count` at 0, and no `correction_reason` or `review_note`. Creating a filing increments the window's `version` by 1.

Four agreement rules guard the creation. The plant's `species` must equal the window's `species`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_SPECIES_CLASS_MISMATCH`. A supersede target must belong to the same `plant_id` and the same `reporting_window_id` as the new filing, or the creation fails with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_PLANT_MISMATCH`; and it must be in `ACCEPTED` status, or the creation fails with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Finally, a plant may hold only one live filing per window: the creation is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION` when another filing for the same plant and window is in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_CORRECTION` or `RESUBMITTED` status, and also when another is in `ACCEPTED` status and the new filing does not name it as its supersede target. As §2.9 records, the supersede-agreement rule is evaluated before the duplicate rule. Ownership is also scoped here and on every line-item append: a `ReporterAgent` may start a filing, append a transaction line or append a wholesale lot only for a plant whose owning `reporting_entity_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch on any of those three operations is rejected with `ERR_FOREIGN_REPORTING_ENTITY`.

Live-animal detail is appended one transaction line at a time. Each line records its `purchase_type` — `NEGOTIATED`, `NEGOTIATED_GRID`, `FORMULA`, `FORWARD_CONTRACT` or `PACKER_OWNED` — together with `head_count`, which must be at least 1, `avg_net_price_per_cwt`, the average net price per hundredweight, and `avg_carcass_weight_cwt`, the average carcass weight per head in hundredweight. Both prices and weights are strictly positive and carry exactly 2 decimals. **Required data elements vary by purchase type**: a `FORMULA` line must carry `formula_base_reference`, naming the base the formula references; a `NEGOTIATED_GRID` line must carry `grid_premium_discount_per_cwt`; and a `FORWARD_CONTRACT` line must carry both `forward_delivery_start` and `forward_delivery_end`. A missing required element is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_PURCHASE_TYPE_ELEMENT`. The mirror rule is equally strict: a `NEGOTIATED` or `PACKER_OWNED` line must carry none of `formula_base_reference`, `grid_premium_discount_per_cwt`, `forward_delivery_start` or `forward_delivery_end`, and a `FORMULA` line must not carry `grid_premium_discount_per_cwt`; a forbidden element present on a line is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_PURCHASE_TYPE_ELEMENT`. A forward-contract delivery span is a **closed** interval on both ends, so `forward_delivery_start` must be no later than `forward_delivery_end`; an inverted span is rejected with `ERR_FORWARD_DELIVERY_INTERVAL_INVALID`.

Forward contracts also consume physical capacity, and the platform enforces the plant's daily commitment cap whenever a `FORWARD_CONTRACT` line is appended. The candidate set is every `FORWARD_CONTRACT` transaction line of the **same plant** — across all of that plant's filings, not just this one — whose owning filing is in a non-terminal status, meaning `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `UNDER_CORRECTION` or `RESUBMITTED`; a filing that ended in `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED` releases its commitments entirely. The platform then probes each distinct `forward_delivery_start` day found among those candidates and the line being appended, because the running commitment total can only step up on a day where some interval begins. For each probe day it sums `head_count` over every candidate line, and the new line, whose `forward_delivery_start` is on or before that day and whose `forward_delivery_end` is on or after it. The largest of those daily sums is the plant's peak commitment, and if it exceeds `forward_commitment_cap_head` the append is rejected with `ERR_FORWARD_COMMITMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Note that this is an aggregate-capacity rule, not a mutual-exclusion rule: two forward contracts may share delivery days freely as long as their combined head on every shared day stays within the cap, and a peak exactly equal to the cap is permitted. Contrast the window-calendar rule of §3.3, which uses half-open intervals and forbids any intersection at all.

Wholesale product detail is appended as lots, and it is a distinct reporting stream from the live-animal categories. Each lot records its `product_class` — `BOXED_BEEF_CHOICE_CUTOUT`, `BOXED_BEEF_SELECT_CUTOUT`, `GROUND_BEEF` or `TRIMMINGS` — its `sale_type`, which is `NEGOTIATED`, `FORMULA_NET` or `DELIVERED`, a `load_count` of at least 1, a strictly positive `total_pounds` with 2 decimals, and a strictly positive `price_per_cwt` with 2 decimals. A lot may be appended only when the filing plant's `wholesale_reporting_required` is true; otherwise the append is refused with `ERR_WHOLESALE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.

Two structural rules apply to both kinds of line. Lines may be appended only while the filing is in `DRAFT` or `UNDER_CORRECTION` status; an append against any other status is rejected with `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_EDITABLE`, and as §2.9 stresses, that rejection takes precedence over the purchase-type element rules. And the line's kind must match the window's class: transaction lines belong only to `LIVE_ANIMAL` windows and wholesale lots only to `BOXED_BEEF` or `WHOLESALE_CUTS` windows, with a mismatch rejected as `ERR_LINE_ITEM_CLASS_MISMATCH`.

Each appended line values itself immediately and updates the filing's booked totals under the round-then-sum discipline of §2.7. A transaction line's `line_value_dollars` is its `head_count` multiplied by its `avg_carcass_weight_cwt` multiplied by its `avg_net_price_per_cwt`, that single product rounded half up to 2 decimals at the line. A wholesale lot's `lot_value_dollars` is its `total_pounds` divided by 100 and then multiplied by its `price_per_cwt`, rounded half up to 2 decimals at the lot. From those, the filing's `reported_head` is the sum of `head_count` over its transaction lines, counting every purchase type including `PACKER_OWNED`; its `reported_cwt` is the sum, over the same lines, of each line's `head_count` multiplied by its `avg_carcass_weight_cwt` with that per-line product rounded half up to 2 decimals before it is added in; its `reported_value_dollars` is the sum of `line_value_dollars` over its transaction lines plus the sum of `lot_value_dollars` over its wholesale lots, each addend already rounded; its `reported_pounds` is the sum of `total_pounds` over its wholesale lots; and its `reported_load_count` is the sum of `load_count` over those same lots. Line items may be listed back per filing — transaction lines narrowable by `purchase_type`, wholesale lots narrowable by `product_class` — and both listings are read-only views of immutable records.

### 3.5. Filing, Review, Correction and Supersession

Filing a draft advances it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, and it is the moment the program's obligations are tested. Only the owning firm may file — a `ReporterAgent` whose `X-User-ID` does not match the `reporting_entity_id` of the filing plant's owner is turned away with `ERR_FOREIGN_REPORTING_ENTITY`, though a `ProgramAdmin` may file administratively on a reporter's behalf. The window must still be `OPEN`, or the attempt returns `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The firm must be under an active obligation: it needs a determination file whose `determination_year` equals the calendar year of the window's `period_start`, whose `determination_outcome` is `MANDATORY` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a missing, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` registration blocks the filing with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, even for a draft that was created while the registration was still good. And the filing must not be empty: a `LIVE_ANIMAL` window requires at least one transaction line, a `BOXED_BEEF` or `WHOLESALE_CUTS` window at least one wholesale lot, failing which the attempt returns `ERR_EMPTY_SUBMISSION`.

**Lateness is computed, not judged.** Filing sets `submitted_at` to the logical clock and then measures the gap to the window's `submission_deadline_utc`: the platform takes the elapsed time from the deadline to the submission instant, converts it to whole minutes discarding any partial minute, and floors the result at zero, so a filing at or before the deadline yields a `lateness_minutes` of 0. Exactly at the deadline the filing is on time. `is_late` is true only when `lateness_minutes` is greater than 0, and `days_late` is `lateness_minutes` divided by 1440 — the number of minutes in a 24-hour day — rounded **up** to the next whole number, so anything from one minute to a full day late counts as one day late and 1441 minutes counts as two. Those three figures feed the late-filing penalty tier directly.

Review is the reviewer's decision on a filed report, taken by a `MarketAnalyst` or a `ProgramAdmin` with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `review_note`. Acceptance is available from either `SUBMITTED` or `RESUBMITTED` and drives the filing to `ACCEPTED`: it records `accepted_at` at the logical clock and stores the `review_note`; when the filing names a `supersedes_submission_id` the target must still be in `ACCEPTED` status at this instant, failing which the acceptance is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_NOT_ACCEPTED`, and on success that target moves `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED`; the window ledger is then recomputed so that `accepted_submission_count` and `total_head_reported` reflect exactly the set of currently accepted filings, with the predecessor dropping out in the same request its successor enters, so its head is counted exactly once; and finally every market report of that window sitting in `COMPUTED` aggregation is forced to `STALE`, which as §2.10 sets out drives disclosure back to `UNREVIEWED`, withdraws any published report and can drop the window from `PUBLISHED` back to `LOCKED`. Rejection is likewise available from either `SUBMITTED` or `RESUBMITTED` and drives the filing to `REJECTED`: it records the `review_note` and does nothing else — no `accepted_at`, no ledger movement and no report invalidation, because a rejected filing was never counted in the first place. `REJECTED` is terminal.

Corrections run in a bounded loop. A `ComplianceAuditor` or a `ProgramAdmin` reopens an accepted filing by supplying a `correction_reason`, which moves it `ACCEPTED → UNDER_CORRECTION`. Two gates apply to this reporter-facing route: the filing's `resubmission_count` must be below 2, or the request is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`, and the logical clock must be at or before the window's `correction_deadline_utc`, or it is refused with `ERR_CORRECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` — at the deadline instant exactly, reopening is still allowed. Reopening stores the `correction_reason`, recomputes the window ledger so this filing's head leaves it, and forces every `COMPUTED` market report of the window to `STALE`. While `UNDER_CORRECTION` the filing is editable again and fresh lines may be appended.

The owning firm then refiles, moving `UNDER_CORRECTION → RESUBMITTED`; the same ownership check applies, so a foreign `ReporterAgent` is rejected with `ERR_FOREIGN_REPORTING_ENTITY`, the filing must still carry at least one line item of the window's class or the refiling returns `ERR_EMPTY_SUBMISSION`, and the firm's registration must still be an `ACTIVE` `MANDATORY` one or the refiling returns `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Refiling increments `resubmission_count` by 1, resets `submitted_at` to the logical clock, and re-measures lateness against the window's original `submission_deadline_utc` — a corrected filing does not get a fresh deadline, so `lateness_minutes`, `is_late` and `days_late` are all overwritten with figures measured from the same fixed instant. A reviewer then accepts or rejects the refiling exactly as before; acceptance closes the cycle `ACCEPTED → UNDER_CORRECTION → RESUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and brings the head back into the window ledger, while rejection leaves the head out. Because the ceiling is checked at reopening, a filing may traverse that cycle at most twice.

Two terminal states arrive from outside the filing's own lifecycle. A filing is driven to `SUPERSEDED` when another filing naming it as its target is accepted. A filing in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` is driven to `VOIDED` when its firm's registration transitions to `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`, whether that suspension was an administrative action or the consequence of the enforcement chain; neither transition moves a window ledger. `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal and accept no further action. Any lifecycle action requested from a status that does not permit it — filing anything but a `DRAFT`, reviewing anything but a `SUBMITTED` or `RESUBMITTED` filing, reopening anything but an `ACCEPTED` filing, refiling anything but an `UNDER_CORRECTION` filing, or any action at all on a terminal filing — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. Filings may be listed and narrowed by `plant_id`, by `reporting_window_id`, by `supersedes_submission_id`, by `status` or by `is_late`.

### 3.6. Aggregation, the Publication Guidelines and Release

A market report is created as an empty shell over one window, naming its `reporting_window_id`, its published `category` and its `weighting_variant`, with an optional `purchase_type_filter` that defaults to `ALL_PRICED` and may otherwise be `NEGOTIATED_ONLY`. The `category` is `LIVE_STEER_HEIFER`, `LIVE_COW_BULL`, `BOXED_BEEF_CUTOUT` or `WHOLESALE_PORK_CUTS`, and it must be compatible with the window it summarizes: `LIVE_STEER_HEIFER` and `LIVE_COW_BULL` both require a `CATTLE` window of class `LIVE_ANIMAL`, `BOXED_BEEF_CUTOUT` requires a `CATTLE` window of class `BOXED_BEEF`, and `WHOLESALE_PORK_CUTS` requires a `SWINE` window of class `WHOLESALE_CUTS`. An incompatible pairing is rejected with `ERR_SPECIES_CLASS_MISMATCH`, both when the shell is created and again when aggregation is attempted.

**A market report carries three independent state dimensions, not one.** Its `aggregation_state` tracks whether the arithmetic is current and is `PENDING`, `COMPUTED` or `STALE`, starting at `PENDING` (the aggregation dimension's initial designation `AGG_PENDING`, with `AGG_COMPUTED` and `AGG_STALE` naming its other two positions). Its `disclosure_state` tracks the confidentiality review and is `UNREVIEWED`, `CLEARED` or `WITHHELD`, starting at `UNREVIEWED` (designated `DISC_UNREVIEWED`, `DISC_CLEARED` and `DISC_WITHHELD`). Its `publication_state` tracks release and is `UNPUBLISHED`, `PUBLISHED` or `WITHDRAWN`, starting at `UNPUBLISHED` (designated `PUB_UNPUBLISHED`, `PUB_PUBLISHED` and `PUB_WITHDRAWN`). The three advance on their own axes, and the reason they exist separately is that recomputation must be able to invalidate a confidentiality clearance and a publication at once without losing track of either.

Aggregating drives `aggregation_state` from `PENDING` to `COMPUTED`, and equally from `STALE` to `COMPUTED` when data has moved underneath a report that was already current. Either way the window must actually have data: if not one submission of the window is in `ACCEPTED` status, the attempt is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DATA`. The eligible set is exactly the window's submissions in `ACCEPTED` status — filings in `SUBMITTED`, `RESUBMITTED`, `UNDER_CORRECTION`, `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED` status are all excluded — and `source_submission_count` records how many were folded in.

For a `LIVE_ANIMAL` window the items are every transaction line of those eligible filings. `total_head` is the sum of `head_count` across all of them, `PACKER_OWNED` lines included, and `total_pounds` is null. The **priced** subset, however, excludes `PACKER_OWNED` entirely — a packer-owned transfer is a volume, not a price — and when `purchase_type_filter` is `NEGOTIATED_ONLY` the priced subset narrows further to lines whose `purchase_type` is `NEGOTIATED` or `NEGOTIATED_GRID`. Each priced line's price is its `avg_net_price_per_cwt`. Its weight depends on the variant: under `HEAD_WEIGHTED` the weight is the line's `head_count`, so a lot of light carcasses pulls the average down exactly as hard as an equal count of heavy ones; under `WEIGHT_WEIGHTED` the weight is the line's `head_count` multiplied by its `avg_carcass_weight_cwt`, left unrounded, so heavier carcasses dominate. Identical transaction data therefore publishes two different averages depending on the variant, which is precisely why a methodology change to the variant is a rulemaking act.

For a `BOXED_BEEF` or `WHOLESALE_CUTS` window the items are every wholesale lot of those eligible filings, and every lot is priced — there is no packer-owned exclusion on the wholesale side. `total_head` is null and `total_pounds` is the sum of `total_pounds` across the lots. Each lot's price is its `price_per_cwt`; under `HEAD_WEIGHTED` its weight is its `load_count`, and under `WEIGHT_WEIGHTED` its weight is its `total_pounds`.

Reporter concentration is measured on the volume metric, which is `total_head` for a `LIVE_ANIMAL` window and `total_pounds` otherwise. `reporter_count` is the number of **distinct** owning reporting firms across the eligible filings, resolved through each filing's plant. The platform also totals the volume metric separately per owning firm; `max_reporter_share_pct` is then 100 multiplied by the largest single firm's volume, divided by the window's volume metric total, rounded half up to 2 decimals — or exactly 0.00 when that total is null or zero.

Price statistics come last and follow the sum-then-round-once discipline of §2.7. When the priced subset is empty — a live-animal window carrying nothing but `PACKER_OWNED` lines, or a `NEGOTIATED_ONLY` report over a window with no negotiated trade — then `weighted_average_price_per_cwt`, `price_low_per_cwt` and `price_high_per_cwt` are all null, while the volume figures and `reporter_count` stand. Otherwise the platform accumulates, at full precision, the sum over priced items of each item's weight multiplied by its price, and separately the sum of those same weights; divides the first total by the second; and rounds that single quotient half up to 2 decimals exactly once. `price_low_per_cwt` is the smallest price among priced items and `price_high_per_cwt` the largest, both taken as reported without further rounding. Aggregation finishes by stamping `computed_at` with the logical clock, and — because a recomputed aggregate must be reviewed afresh — by forcing `disclosure_state` back to `UNREVIEWED` and clearing `suppression_reason`.

**The publication guidelines are what prevent a reader from identifying an individual reporter**, and they are applied in a disclosure review that moves `disclosure_state` out of `UNREVIEWED` to either `CLEARED` or `WITHHELD`. The review is available only once the arithmetic is current: a report whose `aggregation_state` is `PENDING` or `STALE` is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED`. Four guidelines are then evaluated in this exact order, and the **first** failure wins and is recorded in `suppression_reason`:

1. If `weighted_average_price_per_cwt` is null, because the priced set was empty, the reason is `NO_ELIGIBLE_DATA`.
2. Otherwise, if `reporter_count` is strictly less than the window's `min_reporter_count`, the reason is `INSUFFICIENT_REPORTERS`. A count exactly equal to the minimum passes.
3. Otherwise, if `max_reporter_share_pct` is strictly greater than the window's `max_single_reporter_share_pct`, the reason is `DOMINANT_REPORTER`. A share exactly equal to the ceiling passes.
4. Otherwise, if the volume metric — `total_head` for a `LIVE_ANIMAL` window, `total_pounds` otherwise — is strictly less than the window's `min_reported_head`, the reason is `INSUFFICIENT_VOLUME`. A volume exactly equal to the minimum passes.

When no guideline fails, `suppression_reason` stays null, `disclosure_state` becomes `CLEARED`, and every price and volume figure survives intact. When one fails, `disclosure_state` becomes `WITHHELD` and the report is masked: `weighted_average_price_per_cwt`, `price_low_per_cwt`, `price_high_per_cwt`, `total_head` and `total_pounds` are all set to null, while `reporter_count` and `max_reporter_share_pct` are deliberately **retained** so the suppression decision remains auditable.

Releasing a report moves `publication_state` from `UNPUBLISHED` to `PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` with the logical clock, and carries the report's window from `LOCKED` to `PUBLISHED`. Six preconditions guard release, and they are evaluated in the global order of §2.9: the aggregation must be `COMPUTED`, or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED`; the disclosure review must have happened, so a `disclosure_state` still at `UNREVIEWED` yields `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_REVIEWED`; the report must not already have been released, so a `publication_state` of `PUBLISHED` or `WITHDRAWN` yields `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED` — publication is deliberately not idempotent, and a withdrawn report must be recomputed and re-reviewed before it can go out again; a `WITHHELD` report may never be released, yielding `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_SUPPRESSION_REQUIRED`; the logical clock must have reached the window's `publication_release_utc`, with release exactly at that instant allowed and strictly earlier yielding `ERR_PUBLICATION_BEFORE_RELEASE_TIME`; and the window must be `LOCKED` or already `PUBLISHED`, since an `OPEN` window is still collecting, with an open window yielding `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_LOCKED`.

Withdrawal is never requested; it is always a consequence. When a report's `aggregation_state` falls from `COMPUTED` to `STALE` while its `publication_state` is `PUBLISHED`, the report moves `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`, keeps its `published_at` for the record, and — if no other report of that window is still `PUBLISHED` — drops the window from `PUBLISHED` back to `LOCKED`. Two things trigger that staleness: any submission of the window entering or leaving `ACCEPTED` status, and the adoption of a methodology change that re-weights the report's category. Reports may be listed and narrowed by `reporting_window_id`, `category`, `weighting_variant`, `publication_state` or `disclosure_state`; a `PublicUser` sees only reports whose `publication_state` is `PUBLISHED`, as §2.1 sets out.

### 3.7. Verifying Filings Against Plant Records

Compliance verification is the program's check that what a firm reported matches what its own books say. A `ComplianceAuditor` opens a case against one accepted filing by naming its `submission_id`, optionally setting a per-case `materiality_pct` in percent with 2 decimals — constrained to between 0.01 and 100.00 inclusive and defaulting to 2.00 — and the platform records the opening auditor's `X-User-ID` as `opened_by_user_id`. A new case sits in `OPEN` with no `finding`, no rates and no `closed_at`. Beginning fieldwork moves the case `OPEN → FIELDWORK` and changes nothing else; it exists so that a case under active examination is distinguishable from a queued one.

Finalizing is where the case is settled, and it demands the figures read out of the plant's records: `verified_head`, a non-negative head count, and `verified_value_dollars`, a non-negative transaction value with 2 decimals. **Segregation of duties bites here**: the finalizing `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `opened_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The platform then compares the verified figures against the filing's *booked* totals — `reported_head` and `reported_value_dollars`, computed under the round-then-sum order of §2.7, which is why that rounding order is pinned — and the verified figure is always the denominator.

`head_discrepancy_rate_pct` is 0.00 when `verified_head` and `reported_head` are both zero; it is 100.00 when `verified_head` is zero but `reported_head` is not, since a nonzero report against nothing verified is a total deviation; and otherwise it is 100 multiplied by the absolute difference between `verified_head` and `reported_head`, divided by `verified_head`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. `value_discrepancy_rate_pct` follows the same three-way rule with `verified_value_dollars` and `reported_value_dollars` in place of the head figures. Each rate is material only when it is **strictly greater** than the case's `materiality_pct`; a rate exactly equal to the threshold is immaterial. The `finding` follows from the pair: `MATERIAL_BOTH` when both rates are material, `MATERIAL_HEAD` when only the head rate is, `MATERIAL_VALUE` when only the value rate is, and `IMMATERIAL` when neither is.

The finding forks the case, and the two branches have deliberately disjoint consequences.

On an **immaterial** result the case moves `FIELDWORK → RECONCILED`. The platform records `finalized_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, both discrepancy rates, and a `finding` of `IMMATERIAL`. Nothing else moves: the audited filing is left in `ACCEPTED`, the window ledger is untouched, and no market report is invalidated.

On a **material** result the case moves `FIELDWORK → DISCREPANCY`, and the same three fields are written — `finalized_by_user_id`, both rates, and the material `finding` — but the enforcement consequences then run to completion in the same request. The audited filing is driven `ACCEPTED → UNDER_CORRECTION` with its `correction_reason` set to the literal prefix `AUDIT_` followed by the finding, so a head-only discrepancy records `AUDIT_MATERIAL_HEAD`, a value-only discrepancy `AUDIT_MATERIAL_VALUE` and a both-ways discrepancy `AUDIT_MATERIAL_BOTH`. This regulatory reopening **bypasses** the window's `correction_deadline_utc` entirely, because the trigger is a compliance finding rather than a voluntary correction; the reopened filing may therefore sit `UNDER_CORRECTION` long after a reporter-initiated correction would have been refused. The window ledger is then recomputed with this filing's head removed, every market report of the window sitting in `COMPUTED` is forced to `STALE`, any such report that was `PUBLISHED` is `WITHDRAWN`, and a window left with no published report drops from `PUBLISHED` back to `LOCKED`.

Both branches join at closure: a case in `RECONCILED` or `DISCREPANCY` is closed by a `ComplianceAuditor`, moving it to `CLOSED` and stamping `closed_at` with the logical clock. Closing a discrepant case does not extinguish the violation — a penalty may still be assessed against a case that has already been closed. `CLOSED` is terminal, and any action requested from a status that does not permit it — beginning fieldwork on anything but an `OPEN` case, finalizing anything but a `FIELDWORK` case, closing anything but a `RECONCILED` or `DISCREPANCY` case, or anything at all on a `CLOSED` case — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`. Cases may be listed and narrowed by `submission_id`, by `status` or by `finding`.

### 3.8. Penalties, Delinquency Points and Settlement

A `PenaltyOfficer` assesses a penalty against a firm by naming the `reporting_entity_id`, the `violation_type` and the `expected_reporting_entity_version` of §2.6, together with whichever substantiating reference the violation class requires. **The substantiation rules are exact.** An `INACCURATE_DATA` assessment must name an `audit_case_id` referencing a case whose `status` is `DISCREPANCY` or `CLOSED` and whose `finding` is `MATERIAL_HEAD`, `MATERIAL_VALUE` or `MATERIAL_BOTH`. A `LATE_FILING` assessment must name a `submission_id` referencing a filing whose `is_late` is true. A `NON_REGISTRATION` assessment requires neither reference. A violation that fails its substantiation rule is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_CASE_NOT_DISCREPANT`. Segregation of duties applies again: the assessing `X-User-ID` must differ from the `finalized_by_user_id` of the referenced audit case, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the platform records `assessed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and the assessment opens in `ASSESSED` status.

**The order in which the assessment is computed is pinned, because the amount depends on the tier, the tier depends on the point total, and the point total depends on this very assessment. An assessment never sees the consequences of itself.** The platform first reads the firm's `delinquency_points` as they stand *before* this row, and classifies them into `delinquency_tier_at_assessment`: `TIER_0` for a total of 9 points or fewer, `TIER_1` for 10 through 19 points, and `TIER_2` for 20 points or more — the tier floors are inclusive, so exactly 10 points is `TIER_1` and exactly 20 points is `TIER_2`. It then reads the governing registration's status, also as it stands before this row. Only then does it compute the money, and only after the money does it book the points.

The amount is a chain of multipliers over a base. `base_amount_cents` is selected by `violation_type`: 250,000 cents — twenty-five hundred dollars — for `LATE_FILING`, 500,000 cents for `INACCURATE_DATA`, and 1,000,000 cents, ten thousand dollars, for `NON_REGISTRATION`. For a `LATE_FILING` violation the platform copies `days_late` from the referenced filing; for every other violation class `days_late` is 0. The `day_tier_multiplier` is 1.00 for any violation that is not `LATE_FILING`, and for a late filing it is 1.00 when `days_late` is 1 or 2, 1.50 when `days_late` is between 3 and 7 inclusive, 2.25 when `days_late` is 8 or more, and 1.00 when `days_late` is 0. The `repeat_index` is the count of the firm's earlier assessments that share this `violation_type` and whose `status` is `ASSESSED` or `PAID`, counted immediately before this row is written — a waived assessment is invisible to recidivism, which is one of the substantive consequences of a waiver. From it comes the `repeat_multiplier`: 1.00 at a `repeat_index` of 0, 1.50 at 1, 2.00 at 2, and 3.00 at 3 or more.

Two further factors come from the firm's registration. The **governing registration** is the firm's determination file whose `determination_year` equals the calendar year of the logical clock and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; if more than one qualifies, the one with the greatest `created_at` governs, and if none qualifies then `volume_factor` is 1.000 and `suspension_surcharge` is 1.00. Otherwise `volume_factor` is 1 plus the governing registration's `measured_head` divided by one million, with that uplift capped at 0.500 so no firm can be charged more than one and a half times the base on volume grounds, the whole factor carried to 3 decimals rounded half up. And `suspension_surcharge` is 1.25 when the governing registration's `status` read before this assessment was `SUSPENDED`, and 1.00 otherwise.

The raw amount is `base_amount_cents` multiplied by `day_tier_multiplier`, by `repeat_multiplier`, by `volume_factor` and by `suspension_surcharge`. That raw figure is then rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and **only after rounding** is it clamped: `assessed_amount_cents` is the smaller of 2,000,000 cents — a twenty-thousand-dollar per-assessment cap — and the rounded raw amount. Rounding before clamping and clamping before rounding are not equivalent at the cap boundary, and the program's order is round first.

Points are booked last. `points` is 2 for `LATE_FILING`, 5 for `INACCURATE_DATA` and 8 for `NON_REGISTRATION`, and that figure is **doubled** when `delinquency_tier_at_assessment` is `TIER_2` — a firm already in the top tier accrues points twice as fast. The firm's `delinquency_points` becomes its pre-assessment total plus this row's `points`, and the firm's five penalty ledger figures are recomputed per §2.11, incrementing the firm's `version` by 1.

If the post-assessment total lands in `TIER_2`, the enforcement chain fires against the governing registration. A governing registration in `ACTIVE` status moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `DELINQUENCY_TIER_BREACH` — this happens only on the crossing, that is when the tier before the assessment was not `TIER_2` and the tier after it is. A governing registration already in `SUSPENDED` status moves `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` instead, and `suspended_reason` is cleared to null because the registration is no longer merely suspended. Either transition then voids the firm's in-flight filings: every submission of the firm's plants in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` status becomes `VOIDED`, with no window ledger movement.

Settlement closes an assessment one of two ways, and both require the assessment to still be in `ASSESSED` status — `PAID` and `WAIVED` are terminal, and any attempt to settle an already-settled row is rejected with `ERR_PENALTY_ALREADY_SETTLED`. **Payment must be in full.** The payer supplies `paid_amount_cents`, which must equal the assessment's `assessed_amount_cents` exactly; a partial or excess figure is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. A successful payment moves the row `ASSESSED → PAID`, stamps `settled_at` with the logical clock, and shifts the amount from `outstanding_penalty_cents` into `paid_penalty_cents` on the firm while `delinquency_points` stay exactly where they were — paying a fine does not erase the violation that earned it. The firm's `version` increments by 1.

A waiver, available to a `PenaltyOfficer` or a `ProgramAdmin`, moves the row `ASSESSED → WAIVED`, records the supplied `waiver_reason`, stamps `settled_at`, and shifts the amount from `outstanding_penalty_cents` into `waived_penalty_cents`. Unlike payment, a waiver **also reverses the points**: the firm's `delinquency_points` are reduced by exactly this row's `points`, the precise figure booked when the row was assessed. The firm's `version` increments by 1. And a waiver can undo a suspension — but only a suspension it caused. When the firm's governing registration is `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` equal to `DELINQUENCY_TIER_BREACH`, and the post-waiver point total no longer classifies as `TIER_2`, that registration is reinstated `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspended_reason` cleared to null, while `registration_number` and `effective_from` are left exactly as they were. A registration suspended by `ADMIN_ACTION` is never reinstated by a waiver, and a `REVOKED` registration is never reinstated at all. Assessments may be listed and narrowed by `reporting_entity_id`, `audit_case_id`, `submission_id`, `violation_type` or `status`, and each record retains its full multiplier breakdown — `base_amount_cents`, `days_late`, `day_tier_multiplier`, `repeat_index`, `repeat_multiplier`, `volume_factor`, `suspension_surcharge`, `assessed_amount_cents`, `points` and `delinquency_tier_at_assessment` — so any assessed figure can be reconstructed after the fact.

### 3.9. Methodology Change, Public Comment and Restatement

Changing how the program weights a published category is a rulemaking act, not a configuration tweak, and the platform models it as a public docket. A `ProgramAdmin` proposes a change with a `change_code`, the public docket code, which must be unique across all methodology changes; an `affected_category`, naming which published `category` the change touches; a `proposed_weighting_variant`, the weighting that category switches to on adoption, either `HEAD_WEIGHTED` or `WEIGHT_WEIGHTED`; a `comment_period_end`, the instant the public comment period closes; and an `effective_from`, the instant the new methodology takes effect. Two notice periods are enforced at proposal time, each of 30 days: `comment_period_end` must be at least 30 days after the logical clock at proposal, failing which the proposal is rejected with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`, and `effective_from` must be at least 30 days after `comment_period_end` so that the industry receives genuine notice, failing which the proposal is rejected with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_TOO_SOON`. A new docket opens in `COMMENT_OPEN` with `comment_count` and `restated_report_count` both at 0 and no `industry_notice_issued_at`.

Anyone may comment while the docket is open — a `PublicUser`, a `ReporterAgent` or a `ProgramAdmin`. A comment records the `methodology_change_id` of the docket it was filed against, a `commenter_reference` between 1 and 120 characters identifying the commenter publicly, a `comment_text` body between 1 and 4000 characters, and a `stance` of `SUPPORT`, `OPPOSE` or `NEUTRAL`. The platform stamps `received_at` from the logical clock and increments the docket's `comment_count` by 1. Comments are immutable once filed and may be listed back per docket, narrowable by `stance`.

Closing the comment period moves the docket `COMMENT_OPEN → COMMENT_CLOSED`, and requires that the logical clock have reached `comment_period_end`; closing exactly at that instant is allowed, while closing strictly earlier is rejected with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`.

Adoption moves the docket `COMMENT_CLOSED → ADOPTED` and is the point at which the industry notification issues and existing market news is restated. **A change may not be adopted over silence**: if `comment_count` is 0, adoption is rejected with `ERR_NO_PUBLIC_COMMENTS`. On adoption the platform stamps `industry_notice_issued_at` with the logical clock, then walks every market report whose `category` equals the docket's `affected_category` and whose `publication_state` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`, replacing each one's `weighting_variant` with the docket's `proposed_weighting_variant`. Each such report that was sitting in `COMPUTED` aggregation is forced to `STALE`, which as always drives its `disclosure_state` back to `UNREVIEWED`, clears its `suppression_reason`, withdraws it if it was `PUBLISHED`, and drops its window from `PUBLISHED` back to `LOCKED` once no published report of that window remains. `restated_report_count` is set to the number of reports whose `weighting_variant` was rewritten. `ADOPTED` is terminal; commenting on, closing or adopting a docket from a status that does not permit it — commenting on or closing anything but a `COMMENT_OPEN` docket, adopting anything but a `COMMENT_CLOSED` one, or any action at all on an `ADOPTED` one — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_METHODOLOGY_STATE`. Dockets may be listed and narrowed by `status` or by `affected_category`.
