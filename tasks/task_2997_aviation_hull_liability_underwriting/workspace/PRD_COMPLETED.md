# Aviation Hull & Liability Underwriting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Aviation hull and liability is written one airframe and one pilot at a time. An underwriter does not price "an aircraft"; they price a specific registered airframe, flown for a declared purpose, by a named pilot whose logged hours and recurrent training are recorded in the slip as warranties, inside a declared territory, for a defined period. When any one of those four inputs moves — the pilot's audited logbook comes back lower than the broker declared, the operator changes the aircraft from private to instructional use, the territory extends, the agreed value is revised mid-term — the premium moves with it, and the movement has to be recorded as money on a ledger that balances. This service is the system of record for that discipline.

The problems it exists to solve are the ones that make aviation accounts expensive to administer by hand. Pilot warranties are asserted by brokers and only later verified against logbooks, so the same schedule can legitimately carry one rate before the audit and a different rate after it. Hulls are insured on an agreed-value basis, but a constructive total loss can be settled either at the agreed value or on market-value (depreciated) terms depending on whether the pilot warranty survived — two different arithmetic paths over the same loss. War, hijacking and terrorism perils are not written on the hull slip at all; they are placed into a separate market with a finite aggregate line that must not be oversubscribed. Fleet accounts attach newly acquired aircraft automatically and only report them later, so a declaration submitted late attracts a loading. Each of these is a place where a manual process silently loses money or silently over-commits capacity.

This document specifies the complete external contract: the resources, their status dimensions, the arithmetic of every rate and settlement, the validation ladder, the role model, and the error vocabulary. Every endpoint described here is mounted under the `/aviation` prefix. The specification is written for an engineer building the service from scratch, and it is deliberately exhaustive about arithmetic and evaluation order, because in insurance a figure that is one cent out is a figure that is wrong.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Underwriting Authority and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the authority under which they act. If either header is absent, empty, or contains only whitespace, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the five recognised roles, the request is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles the target operation admits, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `Underwriter`, `Broker`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `OperatorUser` and `Auditor`, spelled exactly as shown. The comparison against that list is exact and case-sensitive, and no surrounding whitespace is trimmed before it is made: a header whose value is a correctly spelled role padded with spaces is non-blank, and so clears the first rule, but matches no role and is therefore refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.

An `Underwriter` owns pricing and the insurer's balance sheet. Binding policies, verifying pilot logbook hours, revising agreed values in place, recording pilot-warranty findings, quoting, referring, binding and voiding endorsements, opening war-risk markets, placing and cancelling war risk, and accepting or rejecting fleet declarations are all reserved to this role and to no other. A `Broker` places business on behalf of insureds: it may register operators, airframes and pilots, open draft policies, attach schedules, submit endorsements and fleet declarations, and notify losses — but a `Broker` can never price, bind or settle anything. A `ClaimsAdjuster` owns the claim file end to end: recording investigations, appending damage assessments, taking the coverage decision, disposing of salvage and executing settlement are reserved to this role. An `OperatorUser` is the insured itself; it may notify a loss and read its own policy tree and nothing else. An `Auditor` has read-only oversight of the entire book — the premium ledger, reserves, warranty findings and war-risk placements — and holds no write permission whatsoever beyond repositioning the logical clock.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters. When `X-User-Role` is `Underwriter`, `Broker`, `ClaimsAdjuster` or `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `quoted_by_user_id` on an endorsement when an underwriter produces a quote, or as `coverage_decided_by_user_id` on a claim when an adjuster records the coverage decision — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `OperatorUser`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Operator`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Operator` compare the resource's `operator_id` to `X-User-ID`. An `OperatorUser` reaching a policy, an aircraft schedule, an endorsement, a claim, a war-risk placement, a fleet declaration or a premium-ledger row that belongs — directly or transitively through the policy — to a different operator is refused with `ERR_OPERATOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), on reads as well as on writes. That refusal governs requests that name one specific resource of those kinds, whether through an `{id}` in the path or through a foreign key in the request body. On claim notification — the only write of this sort an `OperatorUser` may itself perform, and the only one carrying two such foreign keys — the check is made on the `policy_id` alone: naming the acting operator's own `policy_id` together with a `schedule_id` that hangs off some other operator's policy is not a scope violation but the cross-aggregate coherence failure `ERR_SCHEDULE_POLICY_MISMATCH` of rank 33. Collection reads work the other way round: rather than refusing, every list endpoint of §2.3 is silently narrowed to the acting operator's own tree before its filters are applied, so an `OperatorUser` sees only its own rows and the `total_count` in `meta` counts only those. Because narrowing precedes filtering, a filter naming a `policy_id` — or any other value — outside the acting operator's tree is not a scope violation but simply selects nothing: the answer is HTTP 200 with an empty `data` array and a `total_count` of 0. The single exception to the scope check as a whole is the logical-clock endpoint, which carries no operator context and is therefore exempt from it.

Two further identity rules enforce segregation of duties, and both compare the header identity rather than the role. The user id that settles a claim must differ from the `coverage_decided_by_user_id` recorded on that claim, and the user id that binds an endorsement must differ from the `quoted_by_user_id` recorded on that endorsement. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Two different `Underwriter` users are therefore required to move an endorsement from quotation to binding, and two different `ClaimsAdjuster` users are required to move a claim from coverage decision to payment.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`. It is exactly 24 characters long: the literal prefix `req_` followed by twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, and from no other alphabet. A fresh identifier is minted for every single response, error responses included.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the real wall-clock instant at which the response was produced, and it is the one value in this contract that is deliberately **not** taken from the logical clock of §2.5; it therefore keeps moving while the logical clock stands still, and it carries exactly three fractional-second digits, never more and never fewer.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Four framework-level identifiers sit outside the business catalogue of §5 and are returned in this same envelope: `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when the request path matches no endpoint this document declares, `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405) when a declared path is addressed with a method it does not support, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) when the framework itself rejects the request before any endpoint logic runs, and `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500) for an unexpected server fault. Path and method resolution happen before the authentication ladder of §2.11, so the 404 and the 405 are returned even when the request carries no authentication headers at all.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filtering

Every collection read is paginated with two query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Either parameter supplied with an empty value — `?limit=` — is treated exactly as though it had been omitted and takes its default. Either parameter supplied with a value that is not a base-ten integer, or with an integer outside its permitted range, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` (HTTP 422); there is no silent clamping to the nearest legal figure. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error but simply an empty page: HTTP 200, an empty `data` array, the true `total_count`, and `has_more` false. Collection reads are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection of this document states a different order for a particular collection; because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock of §2.5, which never advances of its own accord, rows written by different requests routinely share an instant, and every such tie is broken by the order in which the rows were written, earliest first, so the order of any page is total and deterministic. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

Filters are additive: when several are supplied on the same request, only rows matching all of them are returned. The policy collection filters on `operator_id`, `status` and `policy_type`. The aircraft-schedule collection filters on `policy_id`, `aircraft_id`, `named_pilot_id`, `reported_declaration_id`, `coverage_status` and `warranty_status`. The endorsement collection filters on `policy_id`, `target_schedule_id`, `aircraft_id`, `named_pilot_id`, `status` and `endorsement_type`. The war-risk placement collection filters on `policy_id`, `market_id`, `perils_scope` and `status`. The claim collection filters on `policy_id`, `schedule_id`, `adjustment_status`, `salvage_status` and `loss_type`. The damage-assessment collection filters on `claim_id` and `verdict`. The premium-ledger collection filters on `policy_id`, `endorsement_id` and `txn_type`. The fleet-declaration collection filters on `policy_id`, `status` and `late_flag`. Every filter just listed except `late_flag` is a plain string equality test applied to the named field, and a value that matches nothing is never an error: a filter naming an identifier no row carries, or a value that is not a member of the field's enumeration at all, yields HTTP 200 with an empty `data` array and a `total_count` of 0 rather than a validation failure. A filter supplied with an empty value behaves the same way, matching only rows whose field is the empty string and therefore matching nothing. `late_flag` is the one boolean filter and is the one exception: it accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any mixture of case, treats an empty value as absent, and refuses any other value with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`. Query-string parameters other than `limit`, `offset` and the filters listed above for the collection being read are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recording the instant the row was written, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from real time. Timestamps are ISO 8601 in UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits, and an instant held with finer precision than a millisecond is emitted truncated to three digits rather than rounded to them. Every field this document defines for a resource appears on every read of that resource: a field that has no value is emitted explicitly as JSON `null` rather than omitted from the object, and no internal bookkeeping beyond the fields named here is ever exposed.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that row and equal to that row's `created_at` until the first such mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. The unit of increment is the request rather than the individual field write: where a single request writes the same row more than once — a warranty finding that also drives that schedule's coverage dimension, or a cascade that reaches a row an earlier hop of the same request had already written — that row's `version` advances by exactly 1 in total, not once per hop, while its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock on each of them. `version` is the token the optimistic concurrency rules of §2.10 compare against, and any cascade described in this document that mutates a resource also advances that resource's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`.

Two resources are immutable once written and therefore carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`: `DamageAssessment` and `PremiumTransaction`. Both expose only `id` and `created_at` alongside their own attributes. There is no operation anywhere in this API that alters an existing damage assessment or an existing ledger row; corrections are made by appending new rows, never by editing old ones.

### 2.5. The Logical Underwriting Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a single logical UTC clock, and every deadline, expiry test, depreciation reference year, training-recurrency horizon and late-reporting test in this specification is evaluated against that clock. Before any request repositions it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. This startup default is mandatory: the server must never fall back to real time.

A test-driving capability repositions the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and is open to all five roles. The instant supplied may lie earlier than the clock's present reading as freely as later — the clock is repositioned, not advanced, and a backwards move is accepted without complaint — and the capability answers HTTP 200 with an empty JSON object as its `data`, since it transitions no resource. Repositioning the clock has exactly one eager consequence, described in §3.3: a single policy-expiry sweep. That sweep runs in one direction only, so an expiry it has already recorded is never undone by a later backwards reposition. Nothing else is recomputed at that moment. Derived read-model figures such as an airframe's `current_depreciated_value_cents` and a declaration's `late_flag` pick the new instant up the next time they are evaluated, which means a value read before a clock advance and the same value read afterwards can legitimately differ without any write having occurred.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are integer counts of cents, and every field carrying money ends in `_cents`. There are no decimal or floating-point money values anywhere in the contract.

Rates, factors and ratios are expressed in parts per million — one millionth — and every such field ends in `_ppm`. A factor of 1,000,000 parts per million means "unchanged"; 1,150,000 means a fifteen per cent uplift; 900,000 means a ten per cent discount. Expressing multipliers this way keeps every intermediate step in integer arithmetic.

Rounding is never left to the implementation's discretion. Unless a particular calculation in §3 states otherwise, every division discards the remainder — the result is the largest whole number that does not exceed the exact quotient. For the non-negative quantities that dominate this contract that is the same as truncating the fraction away; where a quotient is negative, it is a rounding downwards, away from zero, and not a truncation towards it. Three families of figures deliberately round the other way, upwards to the next whole cent, and each is named explicitly where it is defined: the premium delta of an endorsement, the per-line pro-rata charge and late loading of a fleet declaration, and the territory step of the war-risk premium. Where a calculation performs several divisions in sequence, the rounding is applied after **each** division, not once at the end; multiplying several factors together first and rounding once produces a different figure for many inputs and is not the specified behaviour. Where a calculation applies a floor and a ceiling to the same figure, both bounds are inclusive. One degenerate case is pinned rather than left to fail: where a division's denominator is zero — as a policy's `term_days` is whenever its `effective_from` and its `effective_to` fall on the same calendar date, which the strict-ordering rule of §3.3 does not prevent — the quotient is taken to be 0, in both the rounding-down and the rounding-up families, and no error is raised.

### 2.7. Resource Addressing and Resolution Order

The resource model is deliberately flat. Every collection sits directly beneath the `/aviation` prefix as a peer, and no resource is nested inside another resource's URL. A path that differs from a declared path only by a trailing slash addresses the same endpoint. Parent context always travels in the request body or the query string: a schedule names its `policy_id` in the body, a claim names its `policy_id` and `schedule_id` in the body, a declaration names its `policy_id`, a placement names its `policy_id` and `market_id`, and a damage assessment names its `claim_id`.

Resolution proceeds strictly left to right and outside in. An `{id}` appearing in the path is resolved first, against the collection the endpoint belongs to; if it matches no row, the request fails with that collection's own not-found code — `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PILOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_FOUND` — each carrying HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved next, in the body-field order the operation declares, which is always: `operator_id`, then `policy_id`, then `aircraft_id`, then `named_pilot_id`, then `market_id`, then `schedule_id`, then `target_schedule_id`, then `claim_id`. The first unresolvable reference in that order decides the error, so a request naming both a non-existent policy and a non-existent aircraft reports `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_FOUND`. Because this resolution outranks the JSON-type rule of §2.11 rank 7, a foreign key that is present but is not a JSON string at all — a number, a boolean, an array or an object supplied where an identifier belongs — cannot resolve and is reported with that collection's own not-found code and HTTP 404, not as a type failure; only a foreign key that is absent or explicitly null falls through to the rank-7 presence failure and HTTP 400. Beyond mere existence, two cross-aggregate coherence rules apply: a claim's `schedule_id` must name a schedule belonging to the claim's `policy_id`, and an endorsement's `target_schedule_id` must name a schedule belonging to the endorsement's `policy_id`. A reference that exists but belongs to the wrong policy is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_POLICY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

### 2.8. Parallel Status Dimensions and Cascade Propagation

Two resources in this domain carry two independent status dimensions on the same row, and the dimensions advance separately. An `AircraftSchedule` carries `coverage_status` (whether the hull is on risk) alongside `warranty_status` (whether the pilot warranty is satisfied). A `Claim` carries `adjustment_status` (how far the claim file has progressed) alongside `salvage_status` (what has happened to the wreck). Neither pair is a single combined state; a schedule can be on risk with an unproven warranty, and salvage can be disposed of before or after the claim is settled.

The dimensions are not, however, unrelated. Movement in one dimension can drive movement in the other, and that movement can propagate further. The longest such chain in this system runs four hops from a single request: an underwriting finding of breach moves a schedule's `warranty_status` to `BREACHED` (hop one); because the warranty is breached, the same schedule's `coverage_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` (hop two); because cover is suspended, every open claim on that schedule moves to `COVERAGE_REFERRED` (hop three); and because those claims are no longer reserved, the owning policy's `outstanding_reserve_cents` falls (hop four). Settlement drives a comparable chain: the settlement figures are written onto the claim (hop one), a total-loss basis moves the schedule to `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` (hop two), the hull leaves the sum-insured set and a return of unearned premium is appended to the ledger (hop three), and the policy's aggregated figures are re-derived (hop four).

Every cascade in this specification is applied immediately, inside the same request, before the response is produced. There is no deferred or eventual settling of derived figures: when a request returns 200 or 201, every aggregate this document defines already reflects it. Where a cascade touches several rows of the same collection, the rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, so that ledger appends and version increments occur in a deterministic sequence.

### 2.9. The Append-Only Premium Ledger

All movement of premium is recorded as rows in a single append-only ledger. A ledger row names the `policy_id` whose premium it moves, optionally the `endorsement_id` that produced it, a `txn_type` describing its origin, a signed `amount_cents`, an `effective_date` from which the amount is earned, a `basis` of either `ANNUAL` for a full-term charge or `PRO_RATA` for a time-apportioned charge or return, and a human-readable `memo` recording the origin in prose (for example, "bind of endorsement" followed by the endorsement's identifier). There is no operation that creates a ledger row directly; rows are stamped only by the transitions that earn or return premium, and `policy_id` is always supplied by that transition rather than by a client.

`txn_type` takes one of seven values: `INITIAL_PREMIUM` for the charge written when a policy is bound, `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM` for the delta charged when an endorsement is bound, `RETURN_PREMIUM` for a reversal, `RERATE_ADJUSTMENT` for the annual difference produced by an in-place re-rating, `DECLARATION_PREMIUM` for the priced lines of an accepted fleet declaration, `WAR_RISK_PREMIUM` for a war-risk placement, and `SETTLEMENT_RETURN` for the unearned hull premium returned when a total loss takes a hull off risk. `endorsement_id` is populated only on `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM` and `RETURN_PREMIUM` rows arising from an endorsement; it is null on `INITIAL_PREMIUM`, `RERATE_ADJUSTMENT`, `DECLARATION_PREMIUM`, `WAR_RISK_PREMIUM` and `SETTLEMENT_RETURN` rows. An `amount_cents` of zero is never written: where a calculation yields a delta of zero, the ledger append is skipped entirely rather than recorded as a zero-value row. Returns are recorded as negative amounts.

A policy's `written_premium_cents` is, at every instant, the plain signed sum of `amount_cents` over every ledger row whose `policy_id` is that policy. No status filter applies, because the ledger is append-only and nothing in it is ever retracted. This identity is the system's central money invariant, and it holds after every single request. It has a corollary the implementation must honour exactly: for every forward action that charges premium there is a compensating action that returns it, and after the compensating action the policy's `written_premium_cents` equals its value before the forward action, to the cent. Two such pairs are pinned by this specification — binding an endorsement against voiding that endorsement, and creating a war-risk placement against cancelling that placement.

Two further policy aggregates are derived in the same immediate fashion. `outstanding_reserve_cents` is the sum of `reserve_cents` over the claims on the policy whose `adjustment_status` is `RESERVED`, and no other claims. `paid_hull_cents` is the sum of `settlement_amount_cents` over the claims on the policy whose `adjustment_status` is `SETTLED` and whose `loss_type` is either `HULL_DAMAGE` or `TOTAL_LOSS`. Both are integer cent figures and neither is rounded, because the quantities being summed are already integral.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency on Value Revisions and War Placements

Two operations in this API require the client to prove that it is acting on the version of the parent record it last observed. Both are operations where the request is a perfectly well-formed product action whose only defect is that the figure it was sized against has moved.

Revising the agreed value of an aircraft schedule in place requires a non-optional `expected_schedule_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `AircraftSchedule`. A direct agreed-value revision legitimately supersedes the schedule's prior agreed value, so a second revision is a sound operation in itself; it is rejected only because the schedule's `version` has advanced since the client read it — another revision, a re-rating cascade or a warranty finding has already been recorded — which means the new value was priced against a stale agreed value. If `expected_schedule_version` does not equal the schedule's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCHEDULE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is mutated. The token is compared at exactly the point rank 19 of §2.11 places it, which is neither first nor last: the authentication and scope ladder, the path resolution, the field presence and type rules, the declared constraints and the rank-14 liveness gate on the target schedule are all evaluated **before** it, so a revision naming a `REMOVED` schedule reports `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE` even when its token is also stale; everything from rank 20 downwards — the warranty, over-insurance and hull-aggregate rules — is evaluated **after** it, so a stale token is reported in preference to any of those.

Creating a war-risk placement requires a non-optional `expected_policy_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Policy` being placed. A new placement supersedes any earlier placement for the same period, so creating one is legitimate in itself; it is rejected only when the policy's `version` has advanced since the client read it, because the hull exposure the line was sized against — the policy's `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` — is then stale. If `expected_policy_version` does not equal the policy's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is mutated. As with the schedule token, the comparison sits precisely at rank 19: the rank-12 policy-state gate is evaluated before it, so a placement against an `EXPIRED` policy reports `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND` even when its token is also stale, while the window, perils and market-capacity tests of §3.7 are all evaluated after it.

No other operation in this API accepts a version field. Endorsement binding, policy binding, warranty findings, claim actions, declaration acceptance and placement cancellation all act on the current state of their target without an expected-version token.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When a single request would fail more than one rule, the outcome is not free choice: the rules form a strict ladder and the highest-ranked failing rule decides the response. The ladder is given below in rank order, from 1 (evaluated first) to 43 (evaluated last). The detailed trigger conditions are developed in §3 alongside the workflows they guard; what follows fixes only their relative precedence, the code each carries, and the operations each covers.

1. Both authentication headers present and non-blank on every request — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles — `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, 401.
3. The caller's role is admitted by the target operation — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403.
4. An `OperatorUser` acts only within its own operator's tree, reached directly — the operator row itself, or a policy naming it — or transitively through a schedule, endorsement, claim, damage assessment, placement, declaration or ledger row — `ERR_OPERATOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403. Applies to every business operation that names one specific resource of those kinds; collection reads are narrowed rather than refused, as §2.1 sets out; the logical-clock capability is exempt.
5. The `{id}` in the path resolves in the endpoint's own collection — the matching not-found code from §2.7, 404. Applies to every operation whose path carries an `{id}`.
6. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, in the declared body-field order — the matching not-found code, 404. Applies to every creating operation that carries foreign keys in its body.
7. Every input field that has no default is present, and every present field carries its declared JSON type: integers are JSON numbers with no fractional part, instants are RFC-3339 UTC strings ending in `Z`, and booleans are JSON booleans — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Applies to every write operation. Four points of detail are pinned here because they are otherwise open to reading either way. A JSON `null` supplied for a field is indistinguishable from omitting that field: for a field with no default it is this rank-7 failure, and for a field with a default it selects that default. A JSON boolean is never an acceptable integer, though a JSON number whose fractional part is zero — `5.0` — is. An acceptable instant is `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss`, optionally followed by a decimal point and one or more fractional-second digits, and then a terminating `Z`; the `T` and the `Z` may each be given in upper or lower case, fractional digits beyond the sixth are discarded, and every other shape is a rank-7 failure — including a numeric UTC offset such as `+00:00`, a bare calendar date with no time part, and a calendar-impossible date such as a thirteenth month. And a body that is absent or empty is read as an empty JSON object, so the request fails on the first field it does not supply, whereas a body that is not syntactically valid JSON, or that is valid JSON but is not an object, is itself a rank-7 failure; fields present in the body that the operation does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.
8. Every supplied value satisfies its declared constraint: enum membership, string length and character set, integer sign and range, and the ordering rules that `effective_from` precede `effective_to`, that `period_from` be at or before `period_to`, and that `coverage_from` precede `coverage_to` — `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`, 422. Applies to every write operation, and on a collection read to the `limit`, `offset` and boolean-filter query parameters of §2.3, which carry this same code and are checked before the collection is narrowed to the acting operator's tree or filtered.
9. Conditional field presence for discriminated payloads — `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to operator registration, endorsement submission, and the recording of a pilot-warranty finding, where a `finding` of `BREACH` requires a `breach_reason`. On the last of those three the check is displaced downwards and is evaluated after the rank-14 liveness gate rather than before it, so a finding of `BREACH` with no `breach_reason` against a `REMOVED` or `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` schedule reports `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`; on the other two of the three the rank as stated governs. Three further operations carry this same code with the same downward displacement: the coverage decision of §3.9, where a `decision` of `COVERED` requires `reserve_cents` and a `decision` of `DENIED` requires `denial_reason`, evaluated after the rank-15 claim-state gate; salvage disposition, where an action of `SELL` requires `salvage_proceeds_cents`, evaluated after the rank-18 reachability gate; and fleet-declaration acceptance, where a `decision` of `REJECT` requires a `rejection_reason`, evaluated after the rank-17 declaration-state gate.
10. `registration_mark` is unique across all airframes — `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`, 409. Applies to aircraft registration.
11. The policy is `DRAFT` — `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT`, 409. Applies to policy binding and to attaching a schedule whose `acquisition_source` is `SCHEDULED`.
12. The policy is `BOUND` — `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`, 409. Applies to endorsement submission, war-placement creation, claim notification, fleet-declaration creation, and attaching a schedule whose `acquisition_source` is `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION`. War-placement creation is the one relaxation: a `DRAFT` policy is also accepted there. This rank additionally guards endorsement quotation, endorsement binding and the acceptance branch of a fleet-declaration decision, on each of which it is displaced below that operation's own state gate, as ranks 13 and 17 record. An `EXPIRED` policy refuses every write this rank guards; the writes it does not guard — endorsement voiding, declaration rejection, and every action on a claim file from investigation through to settlement — remain available on a policy that has since expired.
13. The endorsement is in the state its action requires — `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, 409. Applies to endorsement quotation, referral, binding and voiding. On quotation and binding it is displaced above the rank-12 policy-state gate, so an endorsement in the wrong state on a policy that is no longer `BOUND` reports `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`; on voiding it carries a second condition as well, the bar on voiding a `BOUND` endorsement that has already attached, which §3.6 develops.
14. The target aircraft schedule is live, meaning its `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` — `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`, 409. Applies to value revision, warranty findings, endorsement binding, claim notification and the coverage decision. It also guards the recording of an investigation, where the test is narrowed to `REMOVED` alone — a `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` schedule does not block that one operation — and where it is displaced below the rank-15 claim-state gate. On the coverage decision it is displaced below that same rank-15 gate and below the displaced rank-9 conditional-field check as well, and there it bites on a decision of `COVERED` only: a decision of `DENIED` consults the schedule's liveness not at all.
15. The claim is in the state its action requires — `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, 409. Applies to recording an investigation, appending a damage assessment, the coverage decision, settlement and salvage disposition, where on that last operation it governs the `RETAIN` and `TENDER` actions only. It also guards the binding of a `REMOVE_AIRCRAFT` endorsement whose target schedule carries an open claim, where it is displaced to the very foot of the ladder and is evaluated after rank 40.
16. The war-risk placement is `BOUND` — `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_BOUND`, 409. Applies to placement cancellation.
17. The fleet declaration is `SUBMITTED` — `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`, 409. Applies to declaration acceptance, where it is displaced above the rank-12 policy-state gate, so a declaration that has already been decided reports `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_SUBMITTED` rather than `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND` even where its policy has since expired.
18. The requested salvage action is reachable from the claim's current `salvage_status` — `ERR_SALVAGE_INVALID_STATE`, 409. Applies to salvage disposition.
19. The optimistic-concurrency token matches the parent's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SCHEDULE_VERSION`, 412. Applies to war-placement creation and value revision, as set out in §2.10.
20. The named pilot's medical certificate is unexpired at the schedule's start of cover — `ERR_PILOT_MEDICAL_EXPIRED`, 422. Applies to schedule attachment and to binding an `ADD_AIRCRAFT` endorsement.
21. The schedule's `warranty_status` is not `BREACHED` — `ERR_PILOT_WARRANTY_BREACHED`, 422. Applies to value revision, endorsement binding against that schedule, and claim settlement.
22. The use class is permitted by the policy and plausible for the operator — `ERR_USE_CLASS_NOT_PERMITTED`, 422. Applies to schedule attachment and to submitting `ADD_AIRCRAFT` and `USE_CLASS_CHANGE` endorsements.
23. War, hijacking and terrorism exposure is placed in the separate market — `ERR_WAR_RISK_NOT_PLACED`, 422. Applies to notifying a `WAR_PERIL` loss, to claim settlement, to binding a `TERRITORY_EXTENSION` endorsement whose `territory_code` is `WAR_ZONE_A`, to binding a policy whose `war_risk_required` is true, and — in its mirror-image sense, where the offence is withdrawing a placement the book still needs rather than lacking one — to cancelling a placement that is holding up a bound war-zone extension, as §3.7 sets out. On policy binding alone it is displaced downwards and is evaluated after rank 43, so a draft policy that is both unplaced and empty reports `ERR_POLICY_HAS_NO_SCHEDULES`.
24. Cover is not confirmed while cooperation is being withheld — `ERR_INVESTIGATION_COOPERATION_MISSING`, 422. Applies to a coverage decision of `COVERED`.
25. Segregation of duties on the acting `X-User-ID` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. Applies to claim settlement and endorsement binding.
26. The schedule's coverage window lies inside the policy term — `ERR_SCHEDULE_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_POLICY`, 422. Applies to schedule attachment.
27. The endorsement's `effective_date` lies inside the policy term — `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`, 422. Applies to endorsement submission, and to endorsement binding, where the same code reports an `effective_date` the logical clock has already reached; on binding it is evaluated after rank 25 and before rank 28.
28. One airframe carries at most one live schedule at a time — `ERR_AIRCRAFT_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`, 409. Applies to schedule attachment and to binding an `ADD_AIRCRAFT` endorsement.
29. The placement window lies inside the policy term and clears other placements of the same policy — `ERR_PLACEMENT_WINDOW_INVALID`, 422. Applies to war-placement creation.
30. The loss date is covered by the named schedule and is not in the future — `ERR_LOSS_DATE_NOT_COVERED`, 422. Applies to claim notification.
31. The declaration period lies inside the policy term and does not overlap another live declaration — `ERR_DECLARATION_PERIOD_INVALID`, 422. Applies to fleet-declaration creation.
32. `assessed_at` lies at or after the loss date and not after the current logical clock — `ERR_ASSESSED_AT_OUT_OF_RANGE`, 422. Applies to appending a damage assessment.
33. Cross-aggregate coherence of `schedule_id` and `target_schedule_id` against the named policy — `ERR_SCHEDULE_POLICY_MISMATCH`, 409. Applies to claim notification and endorsement submission.
34. Automatic acquisition is enabled on the policy — `ERR_AUTO_ACQUISITION_DISABLED`, 422. Applies to attaching an `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION` schedule and to fleet-declaration creation.
35. The requested perils scope is offered by the target market — `ERR_PERILS_NOT_OFFERED`, 422. Applies to war-placement creation.
36. The three-round quotation ceiling — `ERR_QUOTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, 422. Applies to endorsement quotation and to endorsement referral.
37. The requested settlement basis matches the claim's evidence — `ERR_SETTLEMENT_BASIS_NOT_PERMITTED`, 422. Applies to claim settlement.
38. Salvage has been dealt with before a total loss is paid — `ERR_SALVAGE_DISPOSITION_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to claim settlement.
39. Agreed value does not over-insure the airframe — `ERR_OVER_INSURED_VALUE`, 422. Applies to schedule attachment, value revision, and submitting `ADD_AIRCRAFT` and `VALUE_REVISION` endorsements.
40. The policy's hull sum insured does not exceed its hull aggregate — `ERR_HULL_AGGREGATE_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to schedule attachment, value revision, endorsement binding and claim settlement.
41. The war line fits the market's aggregate over the overlapping window — `ERR_MARKET_LINE_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to war-placement creation.
42. A claim reserve does not exceed the exposure it protects — `ERR_RESERVE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`, 422. Applies to the coverage decision.
43. A policy has something to insure before it is bound — `ERR_POLICY_HAS_NO_SCHEDULES`, 409. Applies to policy binding, and ranks below the fiscal rules because none of those fiscal rules is checked at bind at all: a policy that is not `DRAFT` fails at rank 11 first, and the only rule this one genuinely competes with is the war-risk rule of rank 23, which at bind is evaluated after it rather than before it.

Two precedence consequences are worth stating outright, because they are easy to get backwards. Because rank 19 sits above every business-safety rule, a stale concurrency token is reported even when the request would also have failed a fiscal check — the token is checked and the request abandoned before any rule ranked below it is evaluated, though, as §2.10 records, the state gates ranked above it still bite first. And because rank 25 sits below the state and warranty rules but above the temporal and fiscal ones, an attempt by the coverage-deciding user to settle a claim reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` rather than a settlement-basis or aggregate failure, but reports `ERR_PILOT_WARRANTY_BREACHED` if the hull's warranty is also breached.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Risk Register: Operators, Airframes and Pilots

Before anything can be priced, three independent registers must be populated, and each is maintained by an `Underwriter` or a `Broker`.

An insured is registered as an **Operator**. Registration takes the operator's `legal_name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters and must not be blank once surrounding whitespace is trimmed; an `operator_type` describing its operating character, drawn from `PRIVATE_OWNER`, `BUSINESS_OPERATOR`, `FLIGHT_SCHOOL`, `AIR_CARRIER` and `FLYING_CLUB`; and a `home_base_icao` giving the ICAO code of its home aerodrome, which must be exactly four characters and may contain only uppercase letters A through Z. An `aoc_number` — the Air Operator Certificate reference — is optional in general and defaults to null, but it is mandatory whenever `operator_type` is `AIR_CARRIER`; supplying it when the operator is not an air carrier is permitted, and when it is supplied it must be between 4 and 16 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen. An operator registered as an `AIR_CARRIER` without an `aoc_number` is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. Each operator also exposes a `policy_count`, initialised to 0 at registration and thereafter always equal to the number of policies naming this operator whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `BOUND`; policies that have reached `EXPIRED` drop out of the count. An operator is itself one of the resources the operator-scope rule of §2.1 governs, reached directly rather than through a policy: an `OperatorUser` reading a single operator whose `id` is not its own `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_OPERATOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Airframes and pilots, by deliberate contrast, belong to the book at large rather than to any one insured — the same airframe can be scheduled by successive operators over its life — so a single-airframe read and a single-pilot read carry no operator context and are never scoped, and any role admitted to them sees any row.

An airframe is registered as an **Aircraft** and is identified by its `registration_mark` — the nationality and registration mark painted on the tail. Marks are between 3 and 8 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, and are globally unique across the whole book: a second registration of a mark that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`. The character-set test on this field, and equally the one on `aoc_number` above, is anchored strictly at the start but tolerates a single newline at the very end, so a value that is otherwise well formed and still inside its length bounds is accepted with one trailing newline attached and is then stored, echoed and compared for uniqueness exactly as supplied; a second trailing newline, or any other stray character at either end, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` as usual. The `home_base_icao` code cannot reach that leniency, because its exactly-four-character length rule is tested first and a trailing newline puts it at five. Registration also records the `aircraft_class`, one of `SINGLE_PISTON`, `MULTI_PISTON`, `TURBOPROP`, `JET` or `HELICOPTER`; the `manufacture_year`, which must be no earlier than 1950 and no later than 2026 and from which the depreciation clock runs; the `hull_new_value_cents`, the new-replacement value that forms the depreciation base and must be greater than zero; the `seat_count`, between 1 and 500 inclusive, counting crew as well as passengers, which is what caps the per-seat exposure structures described in §3.6; and the `max_takeoff_weight_kg`, which must be greater than zero.

Every read of an airframe reports a `current_depreciated_value_cents`, and it is re-derived on each read rather than stored once. The derivation is a straight-line decay with a hard residual floor. Take the reference calendar year, subtract the `manufacture_year` to obtain the airframe's age in whole years, and treat a negative result as zero. Each year of age erodes 40,000 parts per million of the new value — four per cent a year — so subtract the age in years multiplied by 40,000 from 1,000,000 to obtain a residual proportion, and then raise that residual to 400,000 parts per million if it has fallen below it. Forty per cent of new value is the floor: an airframe fifteen years old and an airframe forty years old both retain forty per cent. Finally, multiply `hull_new_value_cents` by the residual proportion, divide by 1,000,000, and discard the fraction. For the value reported on a read, the reference year is the calendar year of the current logical clock. This reported figure is a read model and nothing else: settlement never reads it, because settlement re-derives depreciation against the calendar year of the loss, which differs from the clock year whenever the loss fell in an earlier year.

A pilot is registered as a **Pilot** carrying the figures the broker declares. Registration records the `pilot_name`, between 1 and 120 characters; the `license_type`, being the highest licence held and drawn from `STUDENT`, `PPL`, `CPL` and `ATPL`; the `total_logged_hours` and `hours_on_type`, both of which must be zero or greater and where the hours on type may not exceed the total logged hours; the `last_training_completed_at` instant of the most recent completed recurrent training event; and the `medical_expires_at` instant at which the pilot's medical certificate lapses. An `instrument_rated` flag is optional and defaults to false. All three audit fields — `verified_total_hours`, `verified_hours_on_type` and `verified_at` — start as null, and remain null until a logbook audit is recorded as described in §3.2.

### 3.2. Pilot Qualification Scoring and the Logbook Audit

Every pilot carries a qualification score out of 1,000 points and a rating band derived from it, and both exist in two parallel versions: one computed from the figures the broker declared, and one computed from the figures a logbook audit confirmed. At registration only the declared version exists: `qualification_score_logged` and `rating_band_logged` are populated, while `qualification_score_verified` and `rating_band_verified` stay null.

The scoring formula is identical for both versions; only which pair of hour figures feeds it differs. There are four positive components and one penalty. The **hours component** multiplies the basis total hours by 400, divides by 2,000, discards the fraction, and then caps the result at 400 points — so the component saturates at 2,000 hours and no further total hours earn anything. The **type component** multiplies the basis hours on type by 250, divides by 200, discards the fraction, and caps the result at 250 points, saturating at 200 hours on type. The **rating component** contributes 150 points when `instrument_rated` is true and nothing at all when it is false. The **licence component** contributes according to `license_type`: nothing for `STUDENT`, 80 points for `PPL`, 150 points for `CPL`, and 200 points for `ATPL`. The **recurrency penalty** deducts 200 points when the pilot's recurrent training has lapsed, and nothing otherwise: training is lapsed when the number of whole days from the calendar date of `last_training_completed_at` to the calendar date of the current logical clock is greater than 365. A pilot exactly 365 days out of training is not penalised; a pilot 366 days out is.

The four positive components are added together and the recurrency penalty subtracted from that sum. The result is then confined to the range 0 to 1,000: a negative sum becomes 0 and a sum above 1,000 becomes 1,000. From the confined score the band follows, and every threshold is inclusive: a score of at least 750 is `BAND_A`, otherwise a score of at least 550 is `BAND_B`, otherwise a score of at least 350 is `BAND_C`, and anything lower is `BAND_D`. Because the recurrency penalty is evaluated against the clock at the moment of computation rather than frozen at registration, the same untouched pilot record legitimately scores 200 points lower once the clock has been advanced past their recurrency horizon.

An `Underwriter` records a logbook audit by supplying the audited `verified_total_hours` and `verified_hours_on_type`. Both are mandatory and both must be zero or greater; and unlike the declared pair at registration, the audited pair carries **no** ordering constraint between them, so an audit whose `verified_hours_on_type` exceeds its `verified_total_hours` is accepted rather than refused. Recording an audit is repeatable: a pilot who has already been audited may be audited again, and the later audit simply overwrites all three audit fields and repeats the whole fan-out described below, rather than being refused as a duplicate. This is a creating operation and answers with HTTP 201, returning the updated pilot. It stamps `verified_at` with the current logical clock, and it computes the verified version of the score using the audited figures, writing `qualification_score_verified` and `rating_band_verified`. Because audited hours are routinely lower than declared hours, the two versions frequently place the same pilot in different bands, and that is the entire point of the mechanism: the rate the insurer charges should reflect the hours it can prove.

Recording the audit is not a passive act. It reaches out across the book to every aircraft schedule that names this pilot and whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY`, processes them in `created_at` ascending order, and for each one flips `warranty_basis` from `LOGGED_HOURS` to `VERIFIED_HOURS`, then re-derives that schedule's `rated_pilot_band`, `rate_ppm` and `hull_premium_cents` from the audited band. Schedules that have reached `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` or `REMOVED` are never re-rated. For each affected schedule the annual difference between the new `hull_premium_cents` and the old one is taken, and when that difference is not zero **and** the schedule's policy has `status` `BOUND`, a `RERATE_ADJUSTMENT` ledger row is appended with a `basis` of `ANNUAL`, a null `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` of the current logical clock, and an `amount_cents` equal to that difference. Schedules on a `DRAFT` policy are re-rated but write no ledger row, because their premium has not yet been written; a difference of zero writes no row either. Note carefully that this adjustment posts the full annual difference of two already-rounded premiums and is **not** apportioned over the unexpired term — pro-rating is what the endorsement route in §3.6 does instead.

The audit also re-tests the pilot warranty on each of those schedules, which can move `warranty_status` and, through it, `coverage_status`. That mechanism is described in §3.5.

### 3.3. Opening, Binding and Expiring a Policy

A **Policy** is the contract of insurance. Either an `Underwriter` or a `Broker` may open one, and it always opens in `DRAFT`. Opening a policy names the insured through `operator_id` and fixes the commercial terms that every hull on the policy will be rated against: a `policy_type` of either `SINGLE_AIRCRAFT` or `FLEET`; the period, given as an `effective_from` inception instant and an `effective_to` expiry instant which must be strictly later; a `territorial_limit_code` of `DOMESTIC`, `CONTINENTAL` or `WORLDWIDE`; a `base_hull_rate_ppm` between 1 and 100,000 inclusive, expressing the base annual hull rate as a proportion of agreed value; a `hull_deductible_ppm` between 0 and 200,000 inclusive, expressing the hull deductible as a proportion of the settlement gross; a `liability_limit_cents` greater than zero, being the combined single limit for third-party liability; and a `hull_aggregate_limit_cents` greater than zero, being the ceiling on hull exposure across the whole policy.

Four inputs are optional at opening. `permitted_use_classes` is the set of use classifications the policy is allowed to schedule; it defaults to all four — `PRIVATE`, `BUSINESS`, `INSTRUCTIONAL` and `COMMERCIAL`, emitted in exactly that order when the default is taken — and when supplied must be a non-empty subset of those four. It behaves as a set only for the membership test of §3.4; as a stored and echoed value it is a verbatim copy of the array the client supplied. Its element order is preserved rather than canonicalised, and a repeated element is preserved rather than collapsed, so a policy opened with `["COMMERCIAL", "PRIVATE", "PRIVATE"]` reads back with those three entries in that order on every subsequent fetch. The two failure modes are graded differently: a `permitted_use_classes` that is present but is not a JSON array at all is a rank-7 type failure carrying `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400, whereas an array that is empty, or that contains any element which is not one of the four declared use classes, is a rank-8 constraint failure carrying `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` and HTTP 422 — there is no dedicated identifier for either case. `automatic_acquisition_enabled` defaults to false and governs whether newly acquired aircraft may attach automatically pending a declaration. `acquisition_reporting_days` defaults to 30 and must lie between 1 and 180 inclusive; it is the number of days after a declaration period closes within which the declaration must be submitted before the late loading of §3.8 bites. `war_risk_required` defaults to false and records whether a separate war placement is a condition of the policy.

Two figures are stamped by the server at opening. `policy_number` is a sequential human reference formed from the prefix `AVN-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded counter, allocated in policy creation order by `created_at` ascending and starting at `AVN-000001`. `term_days` is the pro-rata denominator used by every apportioned charge on the policy, and it is the number of whole days between the calendar date of `effective_from` and the calendar date of `effective_to`. Opening a policy also increments the owning operator's `policy_count`, and the five derived money figures — `total_hull_sum_insured_cents`, `written_premium_cents`, `outstanding_reserve_cents`, `paid_hull_cents` and `live_schedule_count` — all begin at 0. The two liability-extension flags, `premises_liability_extension` and `product_liability_extension`, are likewise stamped by the server rather than supplied: both begin `false` on every newly opened policy and are moved only by binding the corresponding endorsement of §3.6. They are not inputs to policy opening, so a body that names either of them has that field ignored, exactly as any other undeclared field is.

Binding is reserved to an `Underwriter` and takes the policy from `DRAFT` to `BOUND`. Three preconditions govern it, and they are evaluated in the order in which they are set out below, which is worth stating because that order is not the order their ranks in §2.11 would suggest. The policy must currently be in `DRAFT`, and a policy that has already reached `BOUND` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT`. At least one aircraft schedule on the policy must be sitting in `coverage_status` `PENDING`, because a policy with nothing to insure cannot be bound; a bind attempt against an empty policy is refused with `ERR_POLICY_HAS_NO_SCHEDULES`. And if `war_risk_required` is true, a war-risk placement on this policy must exist in `BOUND` status whose closed coverage window contains the policy's `effective_from`; without it the bind is refused with `ERR_WAR_RISK_NOT_PLACED`. Because this last test is evaluated only once the schedule test has passed, a draft policy that carries no `PENDING` schedule **and** lacks its required placement reports `ERR_POLICY_HAS_NO_SCHEDULES` rather than `ERR_WAR_RISK_NOT_PLACED`, notwithstanding that the war-risk rule ranks higher in the ladder of §2.11.

Binding does four things in sequence. First, every aircraft schedule on the policy whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING` **and** whose `acquisition_source` is `SCHEDULED` advances `PENDING → ACTIVE`; where such a schedule's `warranty_status` is already `BREACHED`, the system transition `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` follows immediately in the same request. Schedules whose `acquisition_source` is `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION` deliberately remain in `PENDING`: they are covered, and they count toward the policy's sum insured, but they are activated and charged only when their reporting declaration is accepted. Second, one `INITIAL_PREMIUM` ledger row is appended with a `basis` of `ANNUAL`, a null `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` equal to the policy's `effective_from`, and an `amount_cents` equal to the sum of `hull_premium_cents` over the schedules just activated; schedules are processed in `created_at` ascending order. That single row is subject to the zero-amount rule of §2.9 like any other: where the sum comes out at zero — because every `PENDING` schedule on the policy is an `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION` one and nothing was activated, or because the activated hulls are small enough that each rounds to a premium of zero — no `INITIAL_PREMIUM` row is written at all, and the bind still succeeds. A bind therefore appends either exactly one ledger row or none, never more. Third, `written_premium_cents`, `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` and `live_schedule_count` are re-derived. Fourth, the policy's `version` advances by one. The response carries the bound policy with HTTP 200, not 201, because no new resource was created.

Expiry is not a request anyone makes; it is a consequence of time passing. Whenever the logical clock is repositioned, a single expiry sweep runs immediately afterwards: every policy currently in `BOUND` whose `effective_to` is at or before the new instant advances `BOUND → EXPIRED`, and the sweep visits candidate policies in `created_at` ascending order. Expiring a policy moves every schedule on it whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` to `REMOVED`, re-derives `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` and `live_schedule_count` — both of which necessarily fall to 0 — and advances the policy's `version` by one. No premium is returned on expiry: the term is fully earned. `EXPIRED` is terminal, and no operation returns a policy to `BOUND`. Every write guarded by rank 11 or rank 12 of §2.11 is refused on an expired policy, and a value revision or a warranty finding against one fails too — not on the policy's own state, which neither operation tests, but at rank 14, because expiry has already carried every schedule on the policy to `REMOVED`. Only the writes that apply no policy-state gate at all, enumerated at rank 12 of §2.11, still succeed on a policy that has expired.

Three derived figures on a policy repay a second look, because they are read from the response of a policy fetch and are easy to compute from the wrong population. `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` is the sum of `agreed_value_cents` over the schedules on the policy whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY`; schedules that have reached `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` or `REMOVED` are excluded. `live_schedule_count` counts exactly that same population. `written_premium_cents` is the signed sum of the whole ledger for the policy, as set out in §2.9.

### 3.4. Scheduling a Hull: Warranty Minima, Rating and the Annual Premium

An **AircraftSchedule** is the marriage of a policy, an airframe, a named pilot and a use classification, and it is where nearly all of this system's arithmetic lives. Either an `Underwriter` or a `Broker` may attach one. Attachment supplies `policy_id`, `aircraft_id` and `named_pilot_id`; a `use_class` of `PRIVATE`, `BUSINESS`, `INSTRUCTIONAL` or `COMMERCIAL`; an `agreed_value_cents` greater than zero, which is the sum the insurer agrees to pay for a total loss; and the hull's own coverage window as `effective_from` and `effective_to`. An `acquisition_source` may be supplied and defaults to `SCHEDULED`; the alternative is `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION`, described in §3.8.

The route into the schedule differs by acquisition source, and so does the policy state each requires. A `SCHEDULED` attachment is part of the original underwriting and requires the policy to be in `DRAFT`; attempting one against a bound or expired policy is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT`. An `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION` attachment represents an aircraft bought mid-term under the fleet clause and requires the policy to be in `BOUND`; attempting one against a draft or expired policy is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`, and attempting one against a policy whose `automatic_acquisition_enabled` is false is refused with `ERR_AUTO_ACQUISITION_DISABLED`. Either way the schedule is born with `coverage_status` `PENDING` and `warranty_status` `PENDING_EVIDENCE`, the latter recording that evidence of the pilot warranty has not yet been sighted.

Six gates stand between the request and the new schedule, and they are worth taking one at a time; the order they are set out in below is expository, and the order they are actually evaluated in is the one fixed by their ranks in §2.11 — medical, then use class, then window, then overlap, then over-insurance, then hull aggregate. The hull's window must sit inside the policy's term, meaning the policy's `effective_from` must be at or before the schedule's `effective_from`, the schedule's `effective_from` must be strictly earlier than its `effective_to`, and the schedule's `effective_to` must be at or before the policy's `effective_to`; a window that escapes the term is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_POLICY`. The named pilot's medical must still be current when cover starts, meaning `medical_expires_at` must be at or after the schedule's `effective_from`; a lapsed medical blocks attachment outright with `ERR_PILOT_MEDICAL_EXPIRED` and is not treated as a warranty matter. The `use_class` must appear in the policy's `permitted_use_classes` and must be plausible for the operator: `INSTRUCTIONAL` requires the operator's `operator_type` to be `FLIGHT_SCHOOL` or `FLYING_CLUB`, and `COMMERCIAL` requires `operator_type` to be `AIR_CARRIER` with a non-null `aoc_number`. Either failure yields `ERR_USE_CLASS_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The fourth gate is the aircraft-level overlap rule, and it spans the whole book rather than one policy: one airframe carries at most one live schedule at a time, whichever policy that schedule sits on. Two schedules for the same `aircraft_id` conflict when their windows, treated as half-open intervals that include the start instant and exclude the end instant, intersect. Windows that merely touch — where one schedule's `effective_to` equals another's `effective_from` — are adjacent rather than overlapping, and are allowed. Schedules that have reached `REMOVED` or `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` never conflict with anything. A genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_AIRCRAFT_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`.

The fifth and sixth gates are fiscal. Agreed value may not exceed 1,200,000 parts per million — one hundred and twenty per cent — of the airframe's depreciated value, where that depreciated value is derived exactly as in §3.1 but with the reference year taken as the calendar year of the schedule's `effective_from`: take that depreciated value, multiply by 1,200,000, divide by 1,000,000, discard the fraction, and require `agreed_value_cents` to be no greater than the result. Over-insurance is refused with `ERR_OVER_INSURED_VALUE`. Separately, the policy's hull sum insured may not outgrow its aggregate: the sum of `agreed_value_cents` over the policy's live schedules, counting the one being created or revised, must be no greater than `hull_aggregate_limit_cents`, and a breach is refused with `ERR_HULL_AGGREGATE_EXCEEDED`.

Once the gates are cleared, the schedule's warranty terms are frozen from its use class. The `warranted_min_total_hours` is taken from a table indexed by `use_class`: 100 hours for `PRIVATE`, 400 for `BUSINESS`, 500 for `INSTRUCTIONAL` and 1,500 for `COMMERCIAL`. The `warranted_min_hours_on_type` comes from a parallel table: 10 hours for `PRIVATE`, 25 for `BUSINESS`, 50 for `INSTRUCTIONAL` and 100 for `COMMERCIAL`. A third table records whether an instrument rating is warranted at all: it is not required for `PRIVATE` or `INSTRUCTIONAL` operations, and it is required for `BUSINESS` and `COMMERCIAL` operations. These three tables are precisely what the hours-verification warranty test of §3.5 compares audited figures against, and both hour comparisons there are inclusive — a pilot with exactly the warranted minimum satisfies the warranty. The minima are frozen from the use class in force at the moment of computation and are recomputed only when the use class itself changes; they never move because the pilot's hours moved.

Alongside the minima the schedule records a `warranty_basis`, which decides which set of the pilot's figures rates this hull. It is `VERIFIED_HOURS` when the named pilot's `verified_at` is non-null at the moment the schedule's premium is computed, and `LOGGED_HOURS` otherwise, which is also its default. The schedule then adopts the pilot's band under that basis as its own `rated_pilot_band`: `rating_band_verified` when the basis is `VERIFIED_HOURS`, and `rating_band_logged` when the basis is `LOGGED_HOURS`.

**The four-factor rate chain.** The hull's `rate_ppm` is built from the policy's `base_hull_rate_ppm` by three successive multiplications, and the order is pinned. First apply the use-class factor: multiply the base rate by 1,000,000 for `PRIVATE`, 1,150,000 for `BUSINESS`, 1,400,000 for `INSTRUCTIONAL` or 1,750,000 for `COMMERCIAL`, divide by 1,000,000, and discard the fraction. Second apply the territory factor to that result: multiply by 1,000,000 for `DOMESTIC`, 1,080,000 for `CONTINENTAL` or 1,250,000 for `WORLDWIDE`, divide by 1,000,000, and discard the fraction. Third apply the pilot-band factor to that result: multiply by 900,000 for `BAND_A`, 1,000,000 for `BAND_B`, 1,250,000 for `BAND_C` or 1,600,000 for `BAND_D`, divide by 1,000,000, and discard the fraction. Finally clamp the figure into the underwriting corridor: raise it to 8,000 parts per million if it has fallen below that, and lower it to 60,000 parts per million if it has risen above that. Both corridor bounds are inclusive. Multiplying the three factors together before applying them, or rounding only once at the end, produces a different rate for many inputs and is not the specified behaviour: the fraction must be discarded after each of the three multiplications, in the stated order.

**The annual hull premium.** The gross annual premium is `agreed_value_cents` multiplied by `rate_ppm`, divided by 1,000,000, with the fraction discarded. A fleet credit may then reduce it. The credit applies only when the policy's `policy_type` is `FLEET` **and** the number of live schedules on that policy — those whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY`, counting the schedule currently being computed — has reached at least five. A `SINGLE_AIRCRAFT` policy never earns the credit however many hulls it carries. Where the credit applies, it is five per cent: multiply the gross by 50,000, divide by 1,000,000, discard the fraction, and subtract that separately-rounded credit from the gross to obtain `hull_premium_cents`. Where it does not apply, `hull_premium_cents` equals the gross.

The credit is evaluated afresh at each premium-computation event and is never applied retroactively. A policy's first four hulls are priced without the credit; the fifth and later hulls are priced with it; and the earlier four are not re-rated when the fleet later crosses the threshold. Two structurally identical hulls on the same policy therefore legitimately carry different premiums, and that is correct behaviour rather than a defect. Attaching a schedule closes by updating the policy's `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` and `live_schedule_count` and advancing the schedule's `version`. That closing list is exhaustive so far as the policy is concerned. Both of those policy figures are recomputed from the policy's live schedules whenever the policy is read, as §3.3 sets out, so re-deriving them writes nothing to the policy row and does not engage the cascade rule of §2.4: the policy's own `version` and `updated_at` do not move when a schedule is attached. More generally, a policy's `version` advances only in those transitions where this document says in terms that it does.

**Revising an agreed value in place.** An `Underwriter` may revise a live schedule's agreed value directly, without an endorsement, by supplying a `new_agreed_value_cents` together with the `expected_schedule_version` token of §2.10. The schedule must be live — `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` — and a `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` or `REMOVED` schedule is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`. The schedule's warranty must not be `BREACHED`; a breached warranty suspends cover and blocks the revision with `ERR_PILOT_WARRANTY_BREACHED`. The new value is subject to the same over-insurance ceiling and the same policy hull aggregate as an original attachment, yielding `ERR_OVER_INSURED_VALUE` or `ERR_HULL_AGGREGATE_EXCEEDED` respectively. "The same ceiling" is meant strictly, including its reference year: the depreciated value the ceiling is built on is re-derived against the calendar year of the schedule's own stored `effective_from`, exactly as at attachment, and **not** against the calendar year of the current logical clock. A revision made in a later calendar year than the one the hull attached in therefore faces the very same ceiling it faced originally, and the ceiling never erodes merely because time has passed. For the hull aggregate, the prospective total is the sum of `agreed_value_cents` over the policy's live schedules with this schedule's own old value removed and the new value put in its place. The revision replaces `agreed_value_cents`, then re-derives `rate_ppm` and `hull_premium_cents` — leaving `rated_pilot_band` and `warranty_basis` exactly as they were, because nothing about the pilot has changed — and takes the annual difference between the new and old `hull_premium_cents`. When that difference is not zero and the policy's `status` is `BOUND`, a `RERATE_ADJUSTMENT` ledger row is appended with a `basis` of `ANNUAL`, a null `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` of the current logical clock and an `amount_cents` equal to the difference; a zero difference or a draft policy writes no row. The policy's `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` and `written_premium_cents` are re-derived, the schedule's `version` advances, and the answer is the updated schedule with HTTP 200.

### 3.5. The Pilot Warranty: Evidence, Breach, Suspension and Reinstatement

The pilot warranty is the promise, recorded in the slip, that the hull will be flown by a pilot who meets the hours and training standards of its use class. It lives in its own status dimension, `warranty_status`, which is entirely separate from whether the hull is on risk. A schedule starts at `PENDING_EVIDENCE` and can reach `COMPLIANT`, `BREACHED` or `WAIVED`. The first three of those are not terminal: a warranty can be proven, later found breached, and later proven again, and the dimension keeps moving between them for as long as the hull is live. `WAIVED` is the one terminal value of this dimension — no finding and no logbook audit ever moves a schedule out of it, as the transition rules below make explicit — so a waiver is the insurer's final word on that hull's warranty.

Only an `Underwriter` may move this dimension, and there is exactly one way to do it: recording a finding against the schedule. The request carries a `finding` of `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED`, `BREACH` or `WAIVER`, and — for a breach only — a `breach_reason`. The schedule must be live; a `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` or `REMOVED` schedule accepts no finding and is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`. The answer is the updated schedule with HTTP 200.

A finding of `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED` records that the underwriter has sighted the logbook evidence. It is accepted from `PENDING_EVIDENCE` and from `BREACHED`, moving `warranty_status` to `COMPLIANT` in both cases and clearing `warranty_breach_reason` back to null; the schedule's `version` advances. A finding of `BREACH` records that the warranty has failed. It is accepted from `PENDING_EVIDENCE` and from `COMPLIANT`, requires `breach_reason` to be present, moves `warranty_status` to `BREACHED`, and stores the supplied reason into `warranty_breach_reason`. A finding of `WAIVER` records that the insurer has chosen to stand behind a hull whose warranty failed. It is accepted only from `BREACHED`, moves `warranty_status` to `WAIVED`, and advances the schedule's `version`. A waiver restores cover but it does **not** restore the agreed-value basis: a claim on a `WAIVED` schedule can only ever be settled on depreciated, market-value terms, as §3.9 sets out. Each of those three findings names the `warranty_status` values it is accepted from, and a finding recorded against a schedule in any other warranty state is neither an error nor a transition: it is a silent no-op. The request answers HTTP 200 with the schedule exactly as it stood, no dimension moves, `warranty_breach_reason` is untouched, the schedule's `version` does not advance and no cascade runs — so a second `BREACH` against an already-`BREACHED` schedule, an `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED` against an already-`COMPLIANT` one, and a `WAIVER` against anything other than a `BREACHED` one all return the unchanged row rather than a 409. A `breach_reason` supplied alongside a finding of `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED` or `WAIVER` is simply ignored, and is required only for `BREACH`. One asymmetry in the clearing of `warranty_breach_reason` is deliberate and easy to get wrong: a finding of `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED` clears it to null unconditionally, whereas a finding of `WAIVER` clears it **only** when that waiver also lifts a suspension — that is, only when the schedule's `coverage_status` was `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY`. Waiving a `BREACHED` schedule whose cover was never suspended, because it was still `PENDING` and had not yet been activated, leaves the recorded breach reason standing on the row beside its new `WAIVED` status.

The consequences of a breach reach further than the warranty dimension, and this is the four-hop cascade of §2.8 in concrete terms. When `warranty_status` becomes `BREACHED` and the schedule's `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE`, the coverage dimension immediately follows with `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` in the same request. Suspension in turn reaches every claim on that schedule whose `adjustment_status` is `NOTIFIED`, `INVESTIGATING` or `RESERVED`, moving each to `COVERAGE_REFERRED` and processing them in `created_at` ascending order. Each such claim keeps its `reserve_cents` verbatim — the cascade never destroys the adjuster's figure, which stays on the record for audit — but because the owning policy's `outstanding_reserve_cents` counts only claims whose `adjustment_status` is `RESERVED`, that policy figure falls by exactly the referred reserves. The schedule's `version` advances on the suspension.

Reinstatement runs the other way. When a finding of `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED` or `WAIVER` is recorded on a schedule whose `coverage_status` is `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY`, and that finding moves `warranty_status` to `COMPLIANT` or `WAIVED`, the coverage dimension follows with `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY → ACTIVE`, `warranty_breach_reason` is cleared to null, and the schedule's `version` advances. Reinstatement does not, however, retroactively re-open the claims that the suspension referred: those claims stay in `COVERAGE_REFERRED` until an adjuster records a coverage decision on each of them. The referral is a permanent fact about the claim file, not a temporary marker.

The warranty is also tested automatically, without any finding being recorded, whenever a logbook audit is registered against a pilot. For each live schedule naming that pilot — those whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` — the audited figures are compared against the schedule's frozen minima. The warranty passes when `verified_total_hours` is at least `warranted_min_total_hours`, **and** `verified_hours_on_type` is at least `warranted_min_hours_on_type`, **and** either the schedule's use class does not warrant an instrument rating or the pilot's `instrument_rated` is true. A schedule that was at `PENDING_EVIDENCE` and passes moves to `COMPLIANT`, and its `warranty_basis` becomes `VERIFIED_HOURS` with the re-rating described in §3.2. `PENDING_EVIDENCE` is the only state a pass moves: a schedule already at `COMPLIANT` stays there, and — this is the consequential one — a schedule at `BREACHED` or `WAIVED` whose audited figures now clear all three tests is **not** returned to `COMPLIANT` by the audit and its suspended cover is **not** reinstated. Only an underwriter's explicit finding of `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED` or `WAIVER` can lift a breach, so an audit is capable of suspending a hull but never of un-suspending one. A schedule that fails at least one of those three tests moves to `BREACHED` — from `PENDING_EVIDENCE` or from `COMPLIANT` alike, and from those two states only, so a failing audit likewise leaves an already-`BREACHED` schedule and a `WAIVED` schedule exactly where they stand and runs no cascade against them — has its `warranty_breach_reason` set to the fixed string `VERIFIED_HOURS_BELOW_WARRANTY`, and drags `coverage_status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` with the full claim cascade behind it. An audit can therefore simultaneously reduce a premium, suspend a hull and refer that hull's open claims, all inside one request.

### 3.6. Mid-Term Endorsements: Negotiation, Binding and Reversal

Everything that changes about a bound policy mid-term, apart from a direct agreed-value revision, happens through an **Endorsement**. An endorsement is a discriminated request: its `endorsement_type` selects which optional payload fields are required and which premium branch prices it. The ten types are `ADD_AIRCRAFT`, `REMOVE_AIRCRAFT`, `VALUE_REVISION`, `USE_CLASS_CHANGE`, `TERRITORY_EXTENSION`, `PREMISES_LIABILITY`, `PRODUCT_LIABILITY`, `NON_OWNED_EXPOSURE`, `RENTER_EXPOSURE` and `FLYING_CLUB_EXPOSURE`.

Either an `Underwriter` or a `Broker` may submit one. Submission always carries `policy_id`, `endorsement_type` and an `effective_date`, and the policy must be in `BOUND` — a draft or expired policy is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`. The `effective_date` is the instant the endorsement attaches and the start of the pro-rata numerator; it must fall inside the policy term treated as a half-open interval that includes `effective_from` and excludes `effective_to`, so an endorsement effective exactly at expiry is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`. A new endorsement is born in `SUBMITTED` with `quote_round` at 0.

The discriminated payload rules are strict in both directions, and a violation in either direction yields `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. A `target_schedule_id` is mandatory for `VALUE_REVISION`, `USE_CLASS_CHANGE` and `REMOVE_AIRCRAFT`, and must be null for every other type — including `ADD_AIRCRAFT`, where the server stamps it at bind time with the identifier of the schedule it creates. `ADD_AIRCRAFT` requires all four of `aircraft_id`, `named_pilot_id`, `new_agreed_value_cents` and `new_use_class`. `VALUE_REVISION` requires `new_agreed_value_cents`, which must be greater than zero when present. `USE_CLASS_CHANGE` requires `new_use_class`, which when present must be one of the four use classifications. `TERRITORY_EXTENSION` requires `territory_code`, which when present must be `CONTINENTAL`, `WORLDWIDE` or `WAR_ZONE_A`. The three exposure types — `NON_OWNED_EXPOSURE`, `RENTER_EXPOSURE` and `FLYING_CLUB_EXPOSURE` — each require both an `exposure_member_count`, being the number of covered renters, club members or non-owned pilots and lying between 1 and 500 inclusive when present, and a `per_seat_limit_cents` greater than zero, being the per-seat liability limit of the structure. Conversely each of `aircraft_id`, `named_pilot_id`, `new_agreed_value_cents`, `new_use_class`, `territory_code`, `exposure_member_count` and `per_seat_limit_cents` must be null on every type that does not require it, so a `territory_code` supplied on an `ADD_AIRCRAFT`, or an `exposure_member_count` supplied on a `VALUE_REVISION`, is refused with the same code. Where a `target_schedule_id` is supplied it must name a schedule belonging to the endorsement's own policy, or the submission is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_POLICY_MISMATCH`. Where the type is `ADD_AIRCRAFT` or `USE_CLASS_CHANGE`, the use class being introduced is tested against the policy's `permitted_use_classes` and the operator's character exactly as in §3.4, yielding `ERR_USE_CLASS_NOT_PERMITTED`; and where the type is `ADD_AIRCRAFT` or `VALUE_REVISION`, the value being introduced is tested against the over-insurance ceiling, yielding `ERR_OVER_INSURED_VALUE`; the reference year of that ceiling is taken exactly as §3.4 takes it, being the calendar year of the endorsement's own `effective_date` for `ADD_AIRCRAFT`, since that instant becomes the new schedule's `effective_from`, and the calendar year of the target schedule's stored `effective_from` for `VALUE_REVISION`.

**The negotiation cycle.** An endorsement is priced by an `Underwriter`, and pricing moves it `SUBMITTED → QUOTED`. Quotation requires the endorsement to be in `SUBMITTED` and the policy to be in `BOUND`; an endorsement in the wrong state is refused with `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and the endorsement's own state is tested before the policy's, so a request that offends both reports `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`. Each quotation increments `quote_round` by one, writes the computed delta into `quoted_premium_delta_cents`, and records the acting `X-User-ID` into `quoted_by_user_id`. A quotation leaves `referral_reason` exactly as it found it: the reason recorded by an earlier referral is never cleared, and remains readable on the re-quoted, bound or voided endorsement.

If the broker will not accept the figure, an `Underwriter` records a referral, which cycles the endorsement `QUOTED → SUBMITTED` for re-pricing. Referral requires the endorsement to be in `QUOTED` and carries a mandatory `referral_reason` recording why the quote was pushed back, which must be present as a string but carries no length or non-blank constraint of its own. It clears `quoted_premium_delta_cents` back to null and `quoted_by_user_id` back to null, but it deliberately **retains** `quote_round`, so the negotiation ceiling still bites. That ceiling is three rounds: the third quote is final. Once `quote_round` has reached 3, a fourth quotation attempt is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and the endorsement must be bound or voided; the referral back-edge is likewise refused at that point, because the endorsement can no longer be re-priced.

**The premium delta.** Quotation computes a signed delta from two shared quantities: the policy's `term_days` as the denominator, and an unexpired count as the numerator, being the number of whole days from the calendar date of the endorsement's `effective_date` to the calendar date of the policy's `effective_to`. Exactly one branch then runs, selected by `endorsement_type`.

For `ADD_AIRCRAFT`, the annual hull premium of the hypothetical new schedule is computed exactly as in §3.4 — using the supplied `aircraft_id`, `named_pilot_id`, `new_use_class` and `new_agreed_value_cents`, with a coverage window running from the endorsement's `effective_date` to the policy's `effective_to` — and the delta is that annual premium multiplied by the unexpired days, divided by `term_days`, rounded **up** to the next whole cent. For `VALUE_REVISION`, the target schedule's annual premium is recomputed with its `agreed_value_cents` replaced by `new_agreed_value_cents`, the target's existing `hull_premium_cents` is subtracted from that recomputed annual figure, and the difference is multiplied by the unexpired days, divided by `term_days`, and rounded up. For `USE_CLASS_CHANGE`, the same shape applies with the target's `use_class` replaced by `new_use_class`. Both of those recomputations reuse the target schedule's stored `rated_pilot_band` and `warranty_basis` unchanged, because nothing about the pilot has changed, and both count the target itself among the live schedules that decide the fleet credit of §3.4. Where the difference they produce is negative — a downward revision, or a move to a cheaper use class — rounding up keeps its strict arithmetic sense of a ceiling, towards positive infinity, so a negative delta is rounded towards zero rather than away from it. For `TERRITORY_EXTENSION`, a flat annual charge is apportioned: 180,000 cents for `CONTINENTAL`, 420,000 cents for `WORLDWIDE` and 950,000 cents for `WAR_ZONE_A`, in each case multiplied by the unexpired days, divided by `term_days`, and rounded up. For `PREMISES_LIABILITY` the flat annual charge is 240,000 cents and for `PRODUCT_LIABILITY` it is 360,000 cents, each apportioned and rounded up the same way. For the three exposure structures, a base annual figure is built first: multiply `exposure_member_count` by `per_seat_limit_cents`, multiply that product by the rate for the type — 900 parts per million for `NON_OWNED_EXPOSURE`, 1,400 for `RENTER_EXPOSURE` and 1,100 for `FLYING_CLUB_EXPOSURE` — divide by 1,000,000 and discard the fraction; then apportion that base over the unexpired days against `term_days` and round up.

`REMOVE_AIRCRAFT` is the one branch that returns money, and its rounding is nested in a pinned order. Take the target schedule's `hull_premium_cents`, multiply by the unexpired days, divide by `term_days`, and discard the fraction: that is the unearned premium. Then apply the short-rate return factor of 900,000 parts per million — ninety per cent, the insurer retaining ten per cent as a short-rate penalty — to that already-rounded figure by multiplying by 900,000, dividing by 1,000,000 and discarding the fraction again. The delta is the negation of that twice-rounded result. Applying the ninety per cent factor to the un-rounded pro-rata amount, or rounding only once, gives a different figure and is not the specified behaviour.

**Binding.** Binding an endorsement is reserved to an `Underwriter` and moves it `QUOTED → BOUND`. The endorsement must be in `QUOTED`, the policy must be in `BOUND`, the endorsement's `effective_date` must be at or after the current logical clock so that no endorsement is back-dated — a clock that has already reached that instant refuses the bind with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`, the same code the submission-time term test carries — and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `quoted_by_user_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Where the endorsement targets a schedule, that schedule must be live or the bind is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`, and its warranty must not be `BREACHED` or the bind is refused with `ERR_PILOT_WARRANTY_BREACHED`. Where the type is `ADD_AIRCRAFT`, the named pilot's medical must be current at the new schedule's start of cover, yielding `ERR_PILOT_MEDICAL_EXPIRED` otherwise, and the new hull must clear the airframe overlap rule, yielding `ERR_AIRCRAFT_COVERAGE_OVERLAP` otherwise. Where the type is `TERRITORY_EXTENSION` with `territory_code` `WAR_ZONE_A`, a war-risk placement on this policy must exist in `BOUND` status whose closed coverage window contains the endorsement's `effective_date`, or the bind is refused with `ERR_WAR_RISK_NOT_PLACED`. Any bind that would push the policy's live hull sum insured past `hull_aggregate_limit_cents` is refused with `ERR_HULL_AGGREGATE_EXCEEDED`. That test is made on `ADD_AIRCRAFT` and `VALUE_REVISION` binds alone, those being the only two types that move the sum insured: for `ADD_AIRCRAFT` the prospective total is the policy's current live sum plus `new_agreed_value_cents`, and for `VALUE_REVISION` it is that same sum with the target's own current `agreed_value_cents` taken out and `new_agreed_value_cents` put in its place. Nothing at bind re-tests the use class or the over-insurance ceiling; both were settled at submission and are not revisited.

Binding freezes `premium_delta_cents` from `quoted_premium_delta_cents` — the figure charged is the figure last quoted, not a fresh computation — and appends one `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM` ledger row with a `basis` of `PRO_RATA`, the endorsement's own identifier in `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` equal to the endorsement's `effective_date`, and an `amount_cents` equal to `premium_delta_cents`. That append is skipped entirely, and only, when the delta is zero. The structural effect then follows, one per type. `ADD_AIRCRAFT` creates a new aircraft schedule directly in `coverage_status` `ACTIVE` with `acquisition_source` `SCHEDULED`, and stamps the endorsement's `target_schedule_id` with the new schedule's identifier. That schedule is born with `warranty_status` `PENDING_EVIDENCE`, a null `reported_declaration_id` and a null `warranty_breach_reason`; its `effective_from` is the endorsement's `effective_date` and its `effective_to` is the policy's `effective_to`; and its `warranty_basis`, `warranted_min_total_hours`, `warranted_min_hours_on_type`, `rated_pilot_band`, `rate_ppm` and `hull_premium_cents` are all computed afresh at bind exactly as in §3.4, from the state prevailing at bind rather than from the state that produced the quote — quotation freezes the ledger amount and nothing else. `REMOVE_AIRCRAFT` moves the target schedule to `REMOVED`, which is permitted only when no claim on that schedule is in `NOTIFIED`, `INVESTIGATING`, `COVERAGE_REFERRED` or `RESERVED` — an open claim refuses the bind with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, and that test is the last gate of all, evaluated after every other rule this subsection imposes; the target may be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` when it is removed. `VALUE_REVISION` replaces the target's `agreed_value_cents` with `new_agreed_value_cents` and re-rates it. `USE_CLASS_CHANGE` replaces the target's `use_class` with `new_use_class`, recomputes its `warranted_min_total_hours` and `warranted_min_hours_on_type` from the new class, and re-rates it. Both re-ratings recompute `rate_ppm` and `hull_premium_cents` exactly as in §3.4 while leaving the target's `rated_pilot_band` and `warranty_basis` untouched, and neither appends a `RERATE_ADJUSTMENT` row: the single `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM` row above is the whole ledger effect of a bind. Nor does a `USE_CLASS_CHANGE` re-evaluate `warranty_status` against the minima it has just moved, so a schedule that was `COMPLIANT` under the old minima remains `COMPLIANT` under higher new ones until a fresh warranty finding of §3.5 says otherwise. `TERRITORY_EXTENSION` rewrites the policy's `territorial_limit_code` to the supplied `territory_code` when that code is `CONTINENTAL` or `WORLDWIDE`; a `WAR_ZONE_A` extension leaves `territorial_limit_code` untouched, because war zone cover is carried by the separate placement rather than by the hull slip's territorial limit. Rewriting `territorial_limit_code` re-rates nothing: the schedules already on the policy keep the `rate_ppm` and `hull_premium_cents` they were priced with, and the new territory factor bears only on premiums computed after the change. `PREMISES_LIABILITY` sets the policy's `premises_liability_extension` to true and `PRODUCT_LIABILITY` sets its `product_liability_extension` to true; both flags default to false and are moved only by these endorsements. The three exposure types have no structural effect beyond their premium. Binding closes by re-deriving `written_premium_cents`, `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` and `live_schedule_count` on the policy and advancing the policy's `version` by one.

**Voiding.** Voiding is reserved to an `Underwriter`, requires a `void_reason` — present as a string, with no length or non-blank constraint of its own — and reaches `VOID` — which is terminal — from `SUBMITTED`, from `QUOTED` and from `BOUND`. A second void of the same endorsement is refused with `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and voiding applies no policy-state gate whatever, so an endorsement sitting on a policy that has since expired may still be voided. Voiding from `SUBMITTED` or `QUOTED` simply records the reason and advances the endorsement's `version`; a void from `QUOTED` retains `quoted_premium_delta_cents` for audit rather than clearing it. Voiding a `BOUND` endorsement is the compensating action of §2.9 and is permitted only while the current logical clock is strictly earlier than the endorsement's `effective_date`, so that cover never incepted; a clock that has reached or passed that instant refuses the void with `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. It appends one `RETURN_PREMIUM` ledger row with a `basis` of `PRO_RATA`, the endorsement's identifier in `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` of the current logical clock, and an `amount_cents` that is the exact negation of `premium_delta_cents` — no short-rate penalty applies, precisely because the endorsement never attached. That append is skipped entirely, and only, when the frozen delta was zero, and `premium_delta_cents` is itself retained on the endorsement for audit rather than cleared. Every structural effect is then undone: a schedule created by an `ADD_AIRCRAFT` bind moves to `REMOVED`, a schedule removed by a `REMOVE_AIRCRAFT` bind returns from `REMOVED` to `ACTIVE` and is then immediately re-suspended to `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` under the cascade of §2.8 where its `warranty_status` is `BREACHED`, a revised `agreed_value_cents` or `use_class` is restored to its pre-bind value together with any `warranted_min_total_hours` and `warranted_min_hours_on_type` a `USE_CLASS_CHANGE` moved, a `territorial_limit_code` rewritten by a `TERRITORY_EXTENSION` bind is put back to the code the policy carried before that bind, and a `premises_liability_extension` or `product_liability_extension` flag returns to the value it carried immediately before that bind rather than unconditionally to false. The restored schedule's `rate_ppm` and `hull_premium_cents` are written back verbatim from the figures captured at bind rather than re-derived, so an intervening change in the policy's live-schedule count cannot alter the premium that is restored. The policy's aggregates are re-derived and its `version` advances, and `written_premium_cents` returns to exactly its pre-bind value.

### 3.7. War, Hijacking and Terrorism: Placement in a Separate Market

War, hijacking and terrorism perils are never carried on the hull slip. They are ceded to a separate market with a finite appetite, and the system models both sides of that arrangement.

A **WarRiskMarket** is opened by an `Underwriter` and represents a market's standing appetite. It carries a `market_name` between 1 and 80 characters that must not be blank once surrounding whitespace is trimmed and that is unique across all markets, an `aggregate_line_cents` greater than zero being the largest total hull line the market will carry at any single instant, and a `perils_offered` recording the widest scope it writes. That name collision carries no dedicated identifier of its own — there is no market analogue of `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION` — and is reported as an ordinary rank-8 constraint failure with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` and HTTP 422; the comparison is exact and case-sensitive, so two names differing only in case are two distinct markets. `perils_offered` is a single scalar enumeration value naming one scope, never an array of scopes: the market's appetite is expressed as the widest single scope it will write, and every narrower scope is admitted by implication rather than by separate enumeration. Scope is ordered: `WAR_ONLY` is narrower than `WAR_HIJACK`, which is narrower than `WAR_HIJACK_TERRORISM`. A market's `committed_line_cents` starts at 0 and is always the sum of `war_hull_line_cents` over the placements on that market whose `status` is `BOUND`, with cancelled placements excluded; its `available_line_cents` starts equal to `aggregate_line_cents` and is always `aggregate_line_cents` less `committed_line_cents`. Both are re-derived when the market is read.

A **WarRiskPlacement** cedes a policy's war exposure into one such market, and creating one is reserved to an `Underwriter`. It names the `policy_id` and the `market_id`, a `war_hull_line_cents` greater than zero being the hull line ceded, a `perils_scope` being the perils actually placed, and the placement period as `coverage_from` and a strictly later `coverage_to`. It also carries the `expected_policy_version` token of §2.10. A placement is born in `status` `BOUND`.

Placement is the one write in this system that a `DRAFT` policy accepts, and the reason is chronological: the war slip is arranged in the separate market **before** inception, and the policy bind itself demands that slip whenever `war_risk_required` is true. A placement against an `EXPIRED` policy is still refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`, as every write guarded by rank 12 of §2.11 is.

Three admission tests govern a placement. The requested `perils_scope` must be no wider than the market's `perils_offered`, and a request for a wider scope is refused with `ERR_PERILS_NOT_OFFERED`. The placement window must lie inside the policy's term, meaning the policy's `effective_from` must be at or before `coverage_from` and `coverage_to` must be at or before the policy's `effective_to` — both endpoints inclusive, so a placement running the exact length of the policy term is admissible — and its interval must not touch or overlap the interval of any other `BOUND` placement of the same policy carrying an overlapping perils scope; either failure is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_WINDOW_INVALID`. The scope qualifier in that second test never narrows it in practice, and it is worth being explicit about why: the three scopes are nested rather than disjoint, so every one of them contains the war peril and any two of them consequently overlap. The same-policy test therefore reduces to the window comparison alone — a `WAR_ONLY` placement conflicts with an overlapping `WAR_HIJACK_TERRORISM` placement on the same policy just as surely as with another `WAR_ONLY` one — while `CANCELLED` placements are disregarded entirely and never conflict with anything. And the line must fit the market's appetite.

That last test uses closed-interval arithmetic, which is a deliberate contrast with the half-open arithmetic of aircraft coverage windows. Two placements overlap when the existing placement's `coverage_from` is at or before the requested `coverage_to` **and** the requested `coverage_from` is at or before the existing `coverage_to`. Under that definition two placements that merely touch at an endpoint **do** overlap. Gather every placement on the target market whose `status` is `BOUND` and whose closed interval overlaps the requested one, sum their `war_hull_line_cents` to obtain the peak already committed over the requested window, and admit the placement when that peak plus the requested `war_hull_line_cents` is no greater than the market's `aggregate_line_cents`. The test is inclusive, so filling the line to its last cent succeeds; exceeding it by one cent is refused with `ERR_MARKET_LINE_EXCEEDED`.

The war premium is a function of the ceded line alone, and its two steps round in opposite directions. Multiply `war_hull_line_cents` by the perils rate — 1,500 parts per million for `WAR_ONLY`, 2,400 for `WAR_HIJACK` and 3,600 for `WAR_HIJACK_TERRORISM` — divide by 1,000,000 and discard the fraction. Then multiply that base by the policy's territory factor — 1,000,000 for `DOMESTIC`, 1,080,000 for `CONTINENTAL`, 1,250,000 for `WORLDWIDE` — divide by 1,000,000 and round **up** to the next whole cent. The result is the placement's `premium_cents`. This ordering resolves an apparent circularity between the two premium ledgers: the war premium never depends on the hull premiums, it is added to `written_premium_cents` after any hull fleet credit has already been applied, and it never counts toward the live-schedule test that governs the fleet credit. Creating a placement appends one `WAR_RISK_PREMIUM` ledger row — carrying a `basis` of `ANNUAL`, because the war premium is a full-term charge for the window placed and is not apportioned, a null `endorsement_id`, and an `effective_date` equal to the placement's own `coverage_from` rather than the current logical clock — updates the market's `committed_line_cents` and `available_line_cents`, re-derives the policy's `written_premium_cents` and advances the policy's `version`. Where the two rounding steps deliver a premium of zero, which a very small ceded line can, the zero-amount rule of §2.9 applies and no ledger row is written; the placement itself is still created and still commits the market's line.

Cancellation is the compensating action for the market's capacity and is reserved to an `Underwriter`. It requires a `cancellation_reason`, which is null while the placement is `BOUND`, and moves the placement `BOUND → CANCELLED`, which is terminal; a second cancellation is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_BOUND`. Cancellation is blocked while the placement is holding up a war-zone extension: if any `BOUND` `TERRITORY_EXTENSION` endorsement on the same policy carrying `territory_code` `WAR_ZONE_A` has an `effective_date` inside the placement's closed coverage window, the placement cannot be withdrawn and the attempt is refused with `ERR_WAR_RISK_NOT_PLACED` (HTTP 422) — the same identifier the forward direction of this rule uses, since the offence is the same one of leaving war exposure unplaced. That test is evaluated only after the placement has been confirmed to be `BOUND`, so a second cancellation of an already-`CANCELLED` placement reports `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_BOUND` even where a war-zone extension also stands against it. Cancelling releases the market line exactly — `committed_line_cents` falls by this placement's `war_hull_line_cents` and `available_line_cents` returns to its precise pre-placement figure — appends one `RETURN_PREMIUM` ledger row with a `basis` of `PRO_RATA`, a null `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` of the current logical clock rather than of either end of the coverage window, and an `amount_cents` that is the exact negation of `premium_cents`, re-derives the policy's `written_premium_cents` and advances the policy's `version`. Despite the `PRO_RATA` basis the amount is the whole premium reversed and is never apportioned over the elapsed part of the window; and where `premium_cents` is itself zero the negation is zero too, so §2.9 suppresses the row and the money invariant holds trivially.

The placement also gates claims. A loss notified with `loss_type` `WAR_PERIL` requires a `BOUND` placement on the same policy whose closed coverage interval contains the claim's `loss_date`; without one, both notification and any later settlement of that claim are refused with `ERR_WAR_RISK_NOT_PLACED`.

### 3.8. Fleet Accounts: Automatic Acquisition and Reporting Declarations

A fleet operator buying an aircraft mid-term cannot wait for underwriting before flying it. Where a policy has `automatic_acquisition_enabled` set to true, a new hull may be attached with `acquisition_source` `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION` against the `BOUND` policy, and it sits in `coverage_status` `PENDING`: it is covered and counts toward `total_hull_sum_insured_cents`, but it has not been activated and no premium has been charged for it. A `reported_declaration_id` stays null until a declaration reports it.

A **FleetDeclaration** is the periodic report that regularises those hulls. Either an `Underwriter` or a `Broker` may create one against a `BOUND` policy whose `automatic_acquisition_enabled` is true; on a policy where it is false, creation is refused with `ERR_AUTO_ACQUISITION_DISABLED`. Creation supplies only the `policy_id` and the reporting period as `period_from` and `period_to`, both of which are **inclusive** bounds: `period_from` must be at or after the policy's `effective_from`, `period_to` must be strictly later than `period_from` and at or before the policy's `effective_to`, and the period must not overlap the period of any other declaration on the same policy that is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. Any of those failures is refused with `ERR_DECLARATION_PERIOD_INVALID`. Because the ordering rule at rank 8 of §2.11 requires only that `period_from` be at or before `period_to`, a `period_to` equal to `period_from` clears that rank and fails here instead, with `ERR_DECLARATION_PERIOD_INVALID` rather than `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`; and because both bounds are inclusive, the overlap test is inclusive at both ends too, so two periods that merely touch at an endpoint do overlap and the second is refused. A declaration is born in `SUBMITTED`.

Creation prices the declaration once and freezes the figures; nothing is recomputed later. The reportable population is gathered first: aircraft schedules on the declaration's policy whose `acquisition_source` is `AUTOMATIC_ACQUISITION`, whose `reported_declaration_id` is still null, whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING`, and whose `effective_from` falls at or after `period_from` and at or before `period_to`. Schedules attached as `SCHEDULED`, schedules already reported by an earlier declaration, and schedules whose cover started outside the period are all excluded. The population is ordered by `effective_from` ascending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and their identifiers are recorded in that order into `reported_schedule_ids`; `declared_count` is the size of the population. An empty population is perfectly legal and yields a `declared_count` of 0, a `declared_premium_cents` of 0 and a `late_loading_cents` of 0; its `late_flag` nonetheless still records the clock test of the paragraph after next, so an empty declaration filed after the deadline is flagged late while carrying a `late_loading_cents` of 0.

Pricing is round-per-line-then-sum, and that order is pinned. For each reportable schedule in turn, take its `hull_premium_cents`, multiply by the number of whole days from the calendar date of that schedule's own `effective_from` to the calendar date of the policy's `effective_to`, divide by the policy's `term_days`, and round **up** to the next whole cent; then add those individually-rounded lines together. Summing the exact fractional line values and rounding once at the end under-charges by the accumulated per-line fractions and is not the specified behaviour.

The late-reporting test then runs against the clock. `late_flag` becomes true when the number of whole days from the calendar date of `period_to` to the calendar date of the current logical clock is greater than the policy's `acquisition_reporting_days`; a declaration submitted exactly on the deadline is not late. Where `late_flag` is true, `late_loading_cents` is the line total multiplied by 150,000 parts per million — a fifteen per cent loading — divided by 1,000,000 and rounded **up**; where it is false, `late_loading_cents` is 0. The `declared_premium_cents` is the line total plus `late_loading_cents`. Creating a declaration mutates no schedule and writes no ledger row: it is purely a priced proposal.

Acceptance is reserved to an `Underwriter` and carries a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, plus a `rejection_reason` that is mandatory when the decision is `REJECT`. The declaration must be in `SUBMITTED`; `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal and a second decision is refused with `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`. That state test is taken before the conditional-presence test on `rejection_reason` and before the policy-state gate of the acceptance branch, so a second decision on a declaration already `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` reports `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_SUBMITTED` whatever else the request omits. A rejection records the `rejection_reason` — required as a string when the decision is `REJECT` but otherwise unconstrained, with no length or non-blank rule of its own — activates no schedule, writes no ledger row, applies no policy-state gate at all so that it still succeeds on a policy that has since expired, and advances the declaration's `version` while leaving the policy's own `version` untouched.

Acceptance requires the policy to still be in `BOUND`. It moves every schedule whose identifier appears in `reported_schedule_ids` from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` and stamps each one's `reported_declaration_id` with the declaration's identifier; where such a schedule's `warranty_status` is already `BREACHED`, the system transition `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` follows immediately in the same request. The stamp is unconditional but the activation is not: a listed schedule that has left `PENDING` between pricing and acceptance is still stamped with the declaration's identifier and is then left in whatever `coverage_status` it now holds, with no error and no second transition, and a listed identifier naming no surviving row is passed over in silence. It then appends one `DECLARATION_PREMIUM` ledger row with a `basis` of `PRO_RATA`, a null `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` equal to the declaration's own `period_from` rather than the clock, and an `amount_cents` equal to `declared_premium_cents` — an append that is skipped entirely when `declared_count` is 0. Finally the policy's `written_premium_cents` and `live_schedule_count` are re-derived and the policy's `version` advances by one.

### 3.9. The Claim File: Notification, Investigation, Assessment and Coverage

A **Claim** is notified against a specific scheduled hull rather than against a policy in the abstract, and a `Broker`, an `OperatorUser` or a `ClaimsAdjuster` may notify one. Notification supplies the `policy_id`, the `schedule_id` of the hull involved, the `loss_date`, a `loss_type` and a `loss_description` of between 1 and 500 characters narrating the occurrence, which must not be blank once surrounding whitespace is trimmed. An `investigator_reference` may be supplied at notification and otherwise defaults to null.

The policy must be in `BOUND` and the named schedule must be live — `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` — with a `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` or `REMOVED` schedule refused as `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`. The `schedule_id` must belong to the `policy_id`, or the notification is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_POLICY_MISMATCH`; that coherence test sits at rank 33 and is therefore taken after the loss-date conditions of rank 30, so a loss date outside the schedule's own window is reported even where the schedule belongs to some other policy. The `loss_date` must fall inside the schedule's coverage window treated as a half-open interval that includes `effective_from` and excludes `effective_to`, and must not lie in the future relative to the current logical clock; either failure is refused with `ERR_LOSS_DATE_NOT_COVERED`. The seven recognised values of `loss_type` are `HULL_DAMAGE`, `TOTAL_LOSS`, `WAR_PERIL`, `LIABILITY_BODILY`, `LIABILITY_PROPERTY`, `PREMISES` and `PRODUCT`, and a notification of `WAR_PERIL` additionally requires the war placement described in §3.7.

A new claim starts with `adjustment_status` `NOTIFIED`, `salvage_status` `NONE`, `cooperation_status` `PENDING`, `reserve_cents` 0, `assessment_count` 0 and `ctl_flag` false. Its `ctl_ratio_ppm`, `settlement_basis`, `settlement_amount_cents` and `deductible_applied_cents` are all null until the relevant step populates them, and `salvage_proceeds_cents` starts at 0.

The adjustment dimension is owned entirely by the `ClaimsAdjuster` role, and `SETTLED` and `DENIED` are its terminal states: a claim that has reached either refuses every further action **on that dimension** with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, meaning a further investigation record, a further assessment, a second coverage decision and a settlement. The salvage dimension is not bound by it. The actions `SELL` and `ABANDON` carry no `adjustment_status` condition whatever, so they remain available on a claim already `SETTLED` or `DENIED` — which is exactly what makes the post-settlement sale described later in this section possible.

**Investigation and cooperation.** An adjuster records accident-investigation activity by supplying a `cooperation_status` of either `COOPERATING` or `NON_COOPERATION` together with an `investigator_reference` naming the investigation file, which is mandatory here — unlike at notification, where it is optional — and overwrites whatever value notification stored. The claim must be in `NOTIFIED` or `INVESTIGATING`, and that test is taken first, before the schedule is examined at all. The schedule must then not have reached `REMOVED`, and that is the whole of the liveness rule at this step: only `REMOVED` is refused, with `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`, and a schedule sitting in `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` does not stop an investigation record being taken even though it would stop a notification. The submitted value is stored as `cooperation_status`, the reference is stored, and where the claim was in `NOTIFIED` the adjustment dimension moves: `NOTIFIED → INVESTIGATING` when the insured cooperated, and `NOTIFIED → COVERAGE_REFERRED` when it did not. That asymmetry is deliberate — failure to cooperate with an accident investigation is a coverage question rather than an adjustment step, so the claim skips `INVESTIGATING` altogether and goes straight to referral. The claim's `version` advances either way.

**Damage assessment and the constructive total loss test.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` appends a **DamageAssessment** to a claim, and assessments are immutable: once written, one is never altered or withdrawn. An assessment supplies the `claim_id`, a `repair_cost_estimate_cents` of zero or more being the surveyor's estimate, an `assessor_reference` of between 1 and 80 characters naming the surveyor issuing it, which must not be blank once surrounding whitespace is trimmed, and an `assessed_at` instant recording when the survey took place. The claim must be in `NOTIFIED`, `INVESTIGATING` or `RESERVED`. The `assessed_at` instant must be at or after the claim's `loss_date` and must not be after the current logical clock; either failure is refused with `ERR_ASSESSED_AT_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

Each assessment carries its own verdict. Its `ctl_ratio_ppm` is the repair-to-value ratio: multiply `repair_cost_estimate_cents` by 1,000,000, divide by the linked schedule's `agreed_value_cents`, and discard the fraction. Its `verdict` is `CONSTRUCTIVE_TOTAL_LOSS` when that ratio has reached at least 750,000 parts per million — seventy-five per cent of agreed value — and `REPAIRABLE` otherwise. The threshold is inclusive: a ratio of exactly 750,000 is a constructive total loss and 749,999 is not.

The claim then re-derives its own view over **all** assessments on it, with no status filter, because none of them can be superseded. One assessment is selected: the one with the greatest `assessed_at`; where two share that instant, the one with the greater `repair_cost_estimate_cents`; where they still tie, the one with the earlier `created_at`. The claim's `ctl_ratio_ppm` becomes the selected assessment's ratio, its `ctl_flag` becomes true when that ratio has reached at least 750,000 parts per million and false otherwise, and its `assessment_count` becomes the number of assessments on the claim. Where `ctl_flag` becomes true and the claim's `loss_type` is `HULL_DAMAGE`, the loss type is promoted to `TOTAL_LOSS`. That promotion is one-way: a later, lower assessment can pull `ctl_flag` back to false, but it never demotes `loss_type` back to `HULL_DAMAGE`. Appending an assessment advances the claim's `version`; the assessment row itself, having no version, is simply created with HTTP 201.

**The coverage decision.** An adjuster takes the coverage decision by supplying a `decision` of either `COVERED` or `DENIED`, with a `reserve_cents` that is mandatory for `COVERED` and a `denial_reason` that is mandatory for `DENIED`. The claim must be in `INVESTIGATING` or `COVERAGE_REFERRED`; from either of those two states both decisions are reachable, and a claim in `NOTIFIED`, `RESERVED`, `SETTLED` or `DENIED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. That claim-state test is taken before either conditional-presence test, so a decision aimed at a claim in the wrong state reports `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` even when the field its own decision makes mandatory has also been omitted. The `denial_reason` is otherwise an unconstrained string: it must be present and of string type for a denial, but carries no length or non-blank rule of its own. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded into `coverage_decided_by_user_id` on both branches.

A decision of `COVERED` moves the claim to `RESERVED` and carries three further conditions, which are evaluated in the order the precedence ladder of §2.11 gives them — schedule liveness at rank 14, then cooperation at rank 24, then the reserve ceiling at rank 42 — rather than in the order in which they are narrated here. The claim's `cooperation_status` must not be `NON_COOPERATION`: cover cannot be confirmed while the insured withholds cooperation with the accident investigation, and only a recorded investigation submitted as `COOPERATING` can produce the value the rule demands, so an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_COOPERATION_MISSING`. This is also the reason no settled claim can ever sit behind a non-cooperating investigation: the gate bites at the reserve step upstream of settlement, and settlement itself performs no cooperation re-check. The schedule must be live, meaning `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_WARRANTY` — a hull whose warranty is breached and whose cover is therefore suspended may still be **reserved**, though as the next subsection shows it may never be **settled** — while a `REMOVED` or `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` schedule is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_LIVE`. And the reserve must not exceed the exposure it protects: `reserve_cents` must be no greater than the schedule's `agreed_value_cents` for a hull loss — a `loss_type` of `HULL_DAMAGE`, `TOTAL_LOSS` or `WAR_PERIL` — and no greater than the policy's `liability_limit_cents` for a liability loss, meaning any of `LIABILITY_BODILY`, `LIABILITY_PROPERTY`, `PREMISES` and `PRODUCT`, with a breach refused as `ERR_RESERVE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`. The ceiling is chosen on `loss_type` as it stands when the decision is taken, so a `HULL_DAMAGE` claim already promoted to `TOTAL_LOSS` by the assessment above is measured against agreed value. `WAR_PERIL` counts as a hull loss for this ceiling and for this ceiling alone: as §2.9 states, `paid_hull_cents` gathers only settled `HULL_DAMAGE` and `TOTAL_LOSS` claims, so a settled war-peril loss never consumes the hull aggregate tested at settlement even though its reserve was capped at the hull's agreed value. On success the reserve is stored and the policy's `outstanding_reserve_cents` is re-derived.

A decision of `DENIED` moves the claim to `DENIED`, which is terminal. It carries none of the three conditions of the covered branch: the schedule need not be live, a `cooperation_status` of `NON_COOPERATION` does not stand in its way, and no ceiling is consulted, so a claim may always be denied from either permitted state. It stores the mandatory `denial_reason`, sets `reserve_cents` to 0, records the deciding user id, re-derives the policy's `outstanding_reserve_cents` and advances the claim's `version`.

**Salvage disposition.** The salvage dimension advances independently of the adjustment dimension: it is legal to dispose of salvage before or after settlement, subject only to the per-step conditions below. A `ClaimsAdjuster` drives it by supplying a `salvage_action`, with a `salvage_proceeds_cents` that is mandatory when the action is a sale. `salvage_status` starts at `NONE` and a disposition that is not reachable from the current value is refused with `ERR_SALVAGE_INVALID_STATE`.

From `NONE`, an action of `RETAIN` moves the claim to `RETAINED_BY_INSURED` and an action of `TENDER` moves it to `TENDERED`; both require the claim's `adjustment_status` to be `INVESTIGATING`, `COVERAGE_REFERRED` or `RESERVED` — a test taken before reachability, so a `RETAIN` or `TENDER` aimed at a settled or denied claim reports `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_SALVAGE_INVALID_STATE` — and tendering additionally requires the claim's `ctl_flag` to be true, since only a constructive total loss produces a wreck worth tendering. Reachability is tested before that `ctl_flag` condition, so a `TENDER` from a `salvage_status` other than `NONE` is refused for the transition rather than for any want of a total loss. From `TENDERED`, an action of `SELL` moves the claim to `SOLD` and requires a `salvage_proceeds_cents` that is present and greater than zero, which is stored on the claim; an action of `ABANDON` moves it to `ABANDONED`. Neither `SELL` nor `ABANDON` carries any `adjustment_status` condition at all — that omission is deliberate and is what makes the post-settlement sale below possible — and reachability is tested before the sale's missing-proceeds condition, so a `SELL` attempted from `NONE` reports `ERR_SALVAGE_INVALID_STATE` even when no proceeds were supplied either. A `salvage_proceeds_cents` sent with any action other than `SELL` is ignored rather than refused, though it must still satisfy the minimum of 1 that governs the field whenever it is present, and the claim's stored `salvage_proceeds_cents` remains 0. `RETAINED_BY_INSURED`, `SOLD` and `ABANDONED` are all terminal, so a second disposition on the same claim is refused. Every disposition advances the claim's `version`. Timing matters for money: selling salvage **before** settlement credits the proceeds against the settlement, whereas selling **after** settlement does not retroactively adjust `settlement_amount_cents`.

### 3.10. Settlement: Deductibles, Salvage Credit and the Three Bases

Settlement is the last act on a claim, is reserved to a `ClaimsAdjuster`, and moves the adjustment dimension `RESERVED → SETTLED`. The only input is a `settlement_basis` of `AGREED_VALUE`, `DEPRECIATED_VALUE` or `PARTIAL_REPAIR`. The claim must be in `RESERVED`; any other adjustment state is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`.

Six further conditions guard the payment, and they are evaluated in the order the precedence ladder of §2.11 gives them — the warranty breach at rank 21, the war placement at rank 23, segregation of duties at rank 25, basis permission at rank 37, the salvage disposition at rank 38 and the hull aggregate at rank 40 — rather than in the order they are narrated here. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `coverage_decided_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the adjuster who confirmed cover may not be the adjuster who releases the money. The schedule's `warranty_status` must not be `BREACHED`: a suspended hull is reservable but not payable, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_PILOT_WARRANTY_BREACHED` until a waiver or a verification finding lifts the breach. The requested basis must match the claim's evidence, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_BASIS_NOT_PERMITTED`: `AGREED_VALUE` requires the claim's `ctl_flag` to be true **and** the schedule's `warranty_status` to be exactly `COMPLIANT`; `DEPRECIATED_VALUE` requires `ctl_flag` to be true; `PARTIAL_REPAIR` requires `ctl_flag` to be false; and every basis requires `assessment_count` to be at least 1, so a claim with no assessment cannot be settled on any basis. Those are the only inputs the basis test consults — `ctl_flag`, `assessment_count` and, for `AGREED_VALUE`, `warranty_status`. None of the three bases is restricted by `loss_type`, so a liability loss carrying an assessment settles on whichever basis its `ctl_flag` admits exactly as a hull loss would. Where the basis is a total-loss basis — `AGREED_VALUE` or `DEPRECIATED_VALUE` — the claim's `salvage_status` must no longer be `NONE`, because a total loss cannot be paid until the wreck has been dealt with; an attempt is refused with `ERR_SALVAGE_DISPOSITION_REQUIRED`. And the payment must fit the policy's hull aggregate: the policy's `paid_hull_cents` plus the net settlement about to be paid must be no greater than `hull_aggregate_limit_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_HULL_AGGREGATE_EXCEEDED`. The figure added is the net settlement of the paragraphs below — after the deductible, the salvage credit and the zero floor — and never the gross, and the running total is exactly the `paid_hull_cents` of §2.9, which gathers only settled `HULL_DAMAGE` and `TOTAL_LOSS` claims, so a previously settled `WAR_PERIL` loss is absent from it. The test is applied to every settlement, on all three bases and whatever the `loss_type`, not to total losses alone. A claim whose `loss_type` is `WAR_PERIL` also requires the war placement of §3.7 to be in place, failing which settlement is refused with `ERR_WAR_RISK_NOT_PLACED`.

The three bases differ only in the gross figure they start from, and this is the pricing engine's second dual-variant calculation. On the `AGREED_VALUE` basis the gross is simply the schedule's `agreed_value_cents`: the agreed sum is paid without any proof of market value, which is the whole commercial point of agreed-value hull cover. On the `DEPRECIATED_VALUE` basis the gross is the lower of the schedule's `agreed_value_cents` and the airframe's depreciated value — market-value terms, so the identical loss on the identical schedule settles for materially less. That depreciated value is derived exactly as in §3.1, but the reference year is the calendar year of the claim's `loss_date`, **not** the calendar year of the current clock and never the airframe's reported `current_depreciated_value_cents`. A loss that fell in an earlier calendar year than the clock therefore settles on a higher depreciated value than the airframe currently reports, and reading the stored reporting field instead of re-deriving from the loss year is a defect. On the `PARTIAL_REPAIR` basis the gross is the `repair_cost_estimate_cents` of the assessment selected by the rule in §3.9.

From the gross, the net is built in a pinned order. The deductible is computed on the **gross** figure first: multiply the gross by the policy's `hull_deductible_ppm`, divide by 1,000,000, discard the fraction, and then raise the result to 250,000 cents if it fell below that minimum. The percentage deductible is a floor of 250,000 cents, so a small loss carries the flat minimum and a large one carries the percentage. The salvage credit is next: it equals the claim's `salvage_proceeds_cents` when and only when the claim's `salvage_status` is `SOLD`, and is zero in every other salvage state. Then subtract both the deductible and the salvage credit from the gross, and if the result is negative treat the settlement as zero. That floor bears on `settlement_amount_cents` alone: `deductible_applied_cents` still records the deductible exactly as computed, so a claim whose deductible and salvage credit together exceed the gross is stored with a `settlement_amount_cents` of 0 beside a `deductible_applied_cents` that may be larger than the gross itself. The order is not interchangeable: deducting the salvage credit first and then applying the percentage deductible to the reduced figure yields a different number and is not the specified behaviour. Because the deductible is a proportion of the gross, the deductible on a depreciated-value settlement is smaller than the deductible on the agreed-value settlement of the very same loss.

Settlement writes `settlement_basis`, `deductible_applied_cents` — the deductible actually applied, after the 250,000-cent minimum has been considered — and `settlement_amount_cents`, and it zeroes `reserve_cents`. On a total-loss basis, the linked schedule then moves to `TOTAL_LOSS_SETTLED` from whatever `coverage_status` it holds at that moment — `ACTIVE` ordinarily, but `PENDING` for an automatically acquired hull that no declaration has yet regularised — with no further condition placed on that status, which requires the claim's `ctl_flag` to be true and takes the hull permanently off risk. Because the hull has left the sum-insured population, the policy's `total_hull_sum_insured_cents`, `live_schedule_count` and `paid_hull_cents` are re-derived and one `SETTLEMENT_RETURN` ledger row is appended with a `basis` of `PRO_RATA`, a null `endorsement_id`, an `effective_date` equal to the claim's own `loss_date` rather than the current clock, and an `amount_cents` that is the negation of the following: the schedule's `hull_premium_cents` multiplied by the number of whole days from the calendar date of `loss_date` to the calendar date of the policy's `effective_to`, divided by the policy's `term_days`, with the fraction discarded. Note the deliberate contrast with the `REMOVE_AIRCRAFT` endorsement branch of §3.6: that branch additionally applies the ninety-per-cent short-rate factor, whereas this return does not — a total loss is not a mid-term cancellation and carries no short-rate penalty. The schedule's `version` advances on the transition.

A `PARTIAL_REPAIR` settlement leaves the schedule in `ACTIVE`. No `SETTLEMENT_RETURN` row is written, and `total_hull_sum_insured_cents` does not move, because the hull remains on risk for the balance of the term. In every case, settlement re-derives the policy's `outstanding_reserve_cents` and `paid_hull_cents` and advances the claim's `version` — but never the policy's own `version`, which no settlement moves on any basis, because re-deriving those aggregates writes nothing to the policy row; this is the general rule of §3.4 applied here — and answers with the updated claim and HTTP 200.
