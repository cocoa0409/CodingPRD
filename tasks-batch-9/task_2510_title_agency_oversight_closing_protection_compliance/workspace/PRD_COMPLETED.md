# Title Agency Oversight API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A title insurance underwriter does not write most of its own business. It delegates — to independent title agencies that hold the underwriter's paper, sign closing protection letters in its name, hold other people's money in escrow trust accounts, and remit the underwriter's share of premium months after the closing has funded. Every one of those delegations is a place where the underwriter can lose money without ever having made a decision. An agency whose errors-and-omissions policy quietly expired keeps binding liability. An agency whose trust account is short by fifty thousand dollars keeps taking deposits. An agency that never reports a policy keeps the underwriter's premium. By the time a claim arrives, the agency file is the only record of what the underwriter agreed to, and it is usually incomplete.

This service is the underwriter's agency oversight system of record. It carries the full appointment lifecycle — licensing, errors-and-omissions coverage, financial review, appointment, suspension, reinstatement and termination — and it carries the four independent exposure ledgers that determine whether an agency may keep writing: outstanding closing protection exposure, unremitted premium, escrow three-way reconciliation variance, and a weighted defalcation risk score. Those ledgers are not reports. They are gates. A single adverse escrow audit closure recomputes the agency's defalcation score, drives its escrow standing to `DEFALCATION_RISK`, freezes its oversight posture, restricts its trust accounts, declines every letter it has in flight, and opens a surprise follow-up audit — all inside one request.

The design goal is that no oversight decision is reversible by accident and no ledger drifts. Money is held in integer cents. Conserved quantities are restored exactly when the operation that moved them is compensated — voiding an issued letter returns the exposure ledger to its precise pre-issuance value, and reopening a settled remittance statement returns the unremitted-premium ledger to its precise pre-settlement value. Decisions that must not be made by one person are enforced against a persisted actor identifier rather than against a role name, so an auditor cannot close their own audit and the counsel who lifted an authority limit cannot later adjudicate the claim that limit produced.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Section 2 is the contract layer. Everything here holds on every endpoint unless a later section names an explicit exception.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role` naming the actor's role. There are exactly five roles, spelled as follows: `AgencyPrincipal`, `LicensingAnalyst`, `EscrowAuditor`, `UnderwritingCounsel`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. If either header is absent — or if `X-User-ID` is present but empty or entirely whitespace — or if `X-User-Role` carries any string other than those five, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The role comparison is exact and case-sensitive: the header value is neither trimmed nor case-folded before it is matched against the five, so a spelling carrying surrounding whitespace or different capitalisation is simply not one of them. `X-User-ID` is otherwise taken verbatim, surrounding whitespace included, and every identifier comparison this document calls for is byte-for-byte string equality. If both headers are well formed but the role is not among the roles authorized for the addressed endpoint in Section 4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Endpoint authorization is coarse; transition authorization is finer. Several endpoints accept two or more roles because they serve two or more decisions, and in those cases the role must additionally be permitted for the *specific* decision requested. A role that clears the endpoint gate but fails the transition gate is rejected with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The complete set of transition-level restrictions is: only a `ComplianceOfficer` may terminate an agency, and correspondingly only a `LicensingAnalyst` may appoint, suspend or reinstate one; only an `UnderwritingCounsel` may move a `REFERRED` closing protection letter to `ISSUED` or to `DECLINED`, while a `REQUESTED` letter is decided by a `ComplianceOfficer`; only an `UnderwritingCounsel` may resolve a policy file that is sitting in `PENDING_REFERRAL`; only an `AgencyPrincipal` may move a `PENDING` policy file to issuance; only an `AgencyPrincipal` may submit a `DRAFT` remittance statement, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may reconcile a `SUBMITTED` statement or settle a `RECONCILED` one, while a reopen to `DRAFT` out of `RECONCILED` or `SETTLED` is narrowed by no transition rule at all and may be made either by a `ComplianceOfficer` or by the agency's own `AgencyPrincipal`; only an `EscrowAuditor` may submit an escrow audit, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may review one or require remediation on one, while a closure — clean or adverse — is likewise narrowed by no transition rule and may be made by either an `EscrowAuditor` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, SoD-1 rather than a role name being what guards it; only an `AgencyPrincipal` may submit finding remediation, and only an `EscrowAuditor` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may clear or escalate a finding; only an `UnderwritingCounsel` may adjudicate a claim out of `INVESTIGATING`, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may resolve a claim that is already in `RECOURSE_DEMANDED`.

Role identity resolves differently for the agency than for the underwriter's own staff. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyPrincipal`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agency`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Agency` compare the resource's `agency_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). For resources that do not carry `agency_id` directly — a remittance line, an escrow balance snapshot, an audit finding remediation — the comparison is made against the `agency_id` of the parent statement, account or audit. The ownership rule guards exactly ten write operations: `POST /agencies/{id}/financial-review`, `POST /closing-protection-letters`, `POST /policy-files`, `POST /policy-files/{id}/issuance-state`, `POST /remittance-statements`, `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/lines`, `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/state`, `POST /escrow-accounts`, `POST /escrow-accounts/{id}/balance-snapshot`, and `POST /audit-findings/{id}/state`.

When `X-User-Role` is `LicensingAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `EscrowAuditor`, `UnderwritingCounsel` and `ComplianceOfficer`: each is opaque. The server does, however, *record* those opaque identifiers where a transition nominates them, and two of those recordings are later compared against a fresh `X-User-ID` as segregation-of-duties anchors (Section 2.9). Specifically, opening an escrow audit stores the actor's `X-User-ID` as `conducted_by_user_id`; closing one stores it as `closed_by_user_id`; approving or declining a policy-file referral stores it as `referral_decided_by_user_id`; deciding a closing protection letter or adjudicating a claim out of investigation stores it as `decided_by_user_id`.

Reads are open. Every list and detail endpoint in Section 4 admits all five roles, and no read is ownership-scoped — an `AgencyPrincipal` may read another agency's records. Every ownership rule in this document applies to writes only.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`. It is exactly `req_` followed by twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`, generated afresh for every response including error responses.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the host's real wall clock and never from the logical clock of Section 2.4, so a clock-setting call does not move it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field, serialised after the request's mutations have been finalised, so the `version` and `updated_at` it carries are the post-mutation values.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and are absent altogether — not present-and-null — from single-object success responses and from error responses.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0; there is no upper bound on `offset`. A `limit` that is not an integer, or an integer below 1 or above 100, and an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, are both rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than silently clamped. Both parameters arrive as query-string text and are accepted only when that text, once surrounding whitespace is stripped, is an optionally signed run of decimal digits; an empty value, a decimal spelling such as `10.0`, or any other non-integer form is rejected the same way.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending on every list endpoint, and because `created_at` is minted strictly increasing (Section 2.4) no two rows ever share one — the ordering is a total order with no ties to break. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error: the response carries an empty `data` array, the full `total_count`, and `has_more` false. Filter query parameters other than `limit` and `offset` are matched by exact equality against the stored field value, so a filter carrying a value outside the field's vocabulary is not rejected — it simply matches no rows. The typed filters are the exception: `premium_mismatch` and `complex_title_flag` must each spell `true` or `false` — matched case-insensitively and with surrounding whitespace stripped — and `closing_date_from` and `closing_date_to` must be `YYYY-MM-DD`, a malformed one of those giving `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system — `Agency`, `StateProgram`, `AgencyAppointment`, `ClosingProtectionLetter`, `PolicyFile`, `RemittanceStatement`, `RemittanceLine`, `EscrowAccount`, `EscrowAudit`, `AuditFinding` and `AgencyClaim` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted random version-4 UUID in canonical lowercase hyphenated 36-character form, with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, minted from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock described below, and minted strictly increasing so that no two rows and no two mutations ever share an instant. `created_at` is fixed at creation, and `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations applied by a cascade rather than by a direct request. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 for each *request* that changes the row: the request is the unit of mutation, so a row that one request and its cascades touch several times still advances exactly one version and receives exactly one fresh `updated_at`, and every row a single request changes receives the identical `updated_at` value. A row whose fields end a request exactly as they began it receives neither a version increment nor a fresh `updated_at` — with one deliberate exception: wherever this document says a transition increments `Agency.version` by 1, that agency row advances even when no other field of it moved. Rejected requests advance nothing at all; a request that returns any error of Section 5 leaves every row exactly as it was, so no partially applied mutation and no version increment survives a rejection. No entity in this system is exempt: all eleven carry `updated_at` and `version`.

No date or deadline in this system is read from the host. All date and deadline arithmetic, and every business timestamp a transition stamps onto a row — `issued_at`, `submitted_at`, `reconciled_at`, `settled_at` and `closed_at` — read a logical clock; only the row-metadata timestamps `created_at` and `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` come from the host wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` — a fixed server startup default, never a fallback to real wall-clock time. A `ComplianceOfficer` may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability — the instant is required and must parse as an ISO 8601 datetime, a value bearing a `Z` or a numeric UTC offset being converted to UTC and one bearing no offset at all being read as already UTC, with no bound whatsoever on how far forward or backward it may move or how often — and every subsequent evaluation that depends on "today" uses the new value: errors-and-omissions expiry, license expiry, policy issuance elapsed days, the remittance filing grace period, and the rolling 365-day window of the defalcation score. Where a rule compares a `YYYY-MM-DD` date field against "today", it compares against the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC.

### 2.5. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary field in this system is an integer count of cents, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is such an integer. There are no floating-point money fields and no currency conversion. Percentages and factors are the only non-integer inputs, and they exist purely to be multiplied against cents.

Wherever a rule multiplies cents by a factor, the product is computed as an exact rational and then rounded **half up** to a whole number of cents — a fractional remainder of exactly one half always rounds away from zero, never to the nearest even value. Banker's rounding and floating-point truncation both produce wrong answers here and are forbidden. Two rules take a floor rather than a half-up round, and both say so explicitly: the four coverage caps of a closing protection letter are each floored before the smallest is chosen (Section 3.3).

Order of operations is part of the contract. When a total is built from individual rows that each carry their own percentage — as remittance premium splits do, because the split percentage belongs to the state program of each individual policy — the rounding happens once per row and the totals are sums of the already-rounded row values. Rounding a statement-level product is forbidden: with three lines of 33,333 cents each at an agency retention fraction of 0.800, the correct per-line-then-sum result is a retention of 26,666 cents per line (79,998 cents in total) and an underwriter share of 6,667 cents per line (20,001 cents in total), whereas summing first and rounding once would yield 79,999 and 20,000. A single-line illustration of the half-up direction: a reported premium of 12,012 cents at a retention fraction of 0.875 gives the exact product 10,510.5, which becomes 10,511 cents of retention — banker's rounding would give 10,510 — leaving 1,501 cents of underwriter share.

Day arithmetic is whole-day arithmetic on calendar dates, with no partial days and no time-of-day component. A percentage input, `premium_split_agency_pct`, is accepted as any JSON number — integer or fractional, and both endpoints of the interval admissible — in the closed interval from 0 through 1, and a value outside that interval is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a boolean, which is not a number. Callers are expected to express it with at most three decimal places, but that expectation is not enforced: the interval is the only validation, and a value of finer precision is accepted and multiplied exactly as supplied.

### 2.6. Oversight Posture: The Single Gate on New Coverage

An agency's `oversight_posture` is the one derived value that decides whether the agency may put new liability on the underwriter's books. It takes one of three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — and it is recomputed, in full, immediately after any change to any of its six inputs: `appointment_status`, `financial_review_status`, `escrow_standing`, `open_critical_finding_count`, `unreported_policy_count` and `unremitted_premium_cents`. It is never partially updated and never stale.

The posture is `FROZEN` when either of two conditions holds: the agency's `appointment_status` is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, or its `escrow_standing` is `DEFALCATION_RISK`. Otherwise, the posture is `RESTRICTED` when any one of six conditions holds: `appointment_status` is `PENDING_REVIEW`; `financial_review_status` is anything other than `ACCEPTED`; `escrow_standing` is `WATCH`; `open_critical_finding_count` is at least 1; `unreported_policy_count` is at least 3; or `unremitted_premium_cents` is strictly greater than five hundred thousand dollars, that is, greater than 50,000,000 cents. If none of the freeze conditions and none of the restrict conditions hold, the posture is `OPEN`. A freshly registered agency sits at `PENDING_REVIEW` with a `financial_review_status` of `NOT_SUBMITTED`, so its posture is `RESTRICTED` from the moment of creation.

A `FROZEN` posture blocks new coverage outright: requesting a closing protection letter, issuing one, and issuing a policy file are all rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). A `RESTRICTED` posture does not block — it degrades. It halves the closing protection coverage cap and it forces every letter request into counsel referral, as Section 3.3 describes.

One condition deliberately does *not* affect the posture: an expired errors-and-omissions policy. Even when the agency's effective `eo_expires_on` lies in the past, the posture is computed from the six inputs above and nothing else. Lapsed coverage is enforced as its own separate check, at a lower precedence, precisely so that `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_LAPSED` remains reachable instead of being masked by the higher-precedence frozen-posture rejection.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Ledgers

Three named cascades run in this system, and each of them mutates several entities inside a single request. CASCADE-A is fired by the adverse closure of an escrow audit. CASCADE-B is fired by the termination of an agency. CASCADE-C is fired by the settlement of a remittance statement. Each is described in full at the workflow that fires it, but three rules hold across all of them.

First, ordering. Whenever a cascade hop mutates a set of sibling rows — appointments withdrawn, letters declined, escrow accounts restricted — the rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order, so the resulting audit trail is deterministic.

Second, the derived-value chain runs to completion. A cascade hop that changes a counter must then recompute every value derived from that counter, and if that recomputation changes the agency's `oversight_posture`, the posture's own downstream effects fire in turn. An `Agency` row touched anywhere in a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1 for the request, and any other row the cascade mutates has its own `updated_at` and `version` advanced the same way.

Third, conservation. Two agency-level ledgers are conserved quantities, meaning that the compensating action of an operation restores the ledger to the exact value it held before that operation, to the cent. `cpl_outstanding_exposure_cents` is the sum of `approved_coverage_cents` across the agency's closing protection letters whose `status` is `ISSUED`; issuing a letter raises it by that letter's approved coverage, and voiding an issued letter zeroes that letter's `approved_coverage_cents` and removes it from the issued set, returning the ledger to its exact pre-issuance value. `unremitted_premium_cents` is the sum of `gross_premium_cents` across the agency's policy files whose `issuance_status` is `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED` and whose `remittance_status` is `UNREPORTED`, `REPORTED` or `DISPUTED`; settling a statement removes its reported policies from that set, and reopening the settled statement puts them back, returning the ledger to its exact pre-settlement value. Both ledgers are always recomputed from the underlying rows rather than adjusted by a delta.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Agency Oversight File

Three creation endpoints stamp a routing or baseline decision onto a new row using the agency's oversight profile as it stood when the client last read it. All three are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency against `Agency.version`, and on all three the field `expected_agency_version` is a required, non-optional input. At the shape tier it is validated only as a JSON integer and carries no minimum, so a zero or negative value is well formed there and is simply carried into the comparison, which it then loses.

A `POST /closing-protection-letters` request must carry `expected_agency_version` matching the target agency's current `version`. If the agency's version has advanced since the client last observed it, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGENCY_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the letter's routing decision between `REQUESTED` and `REFERRED` is derived from `oversight_posture` and `underwriting_authority_limit_cents` as of that observation, and a version that has since moved means the request was routed against an agency profile that no longer holds.

A `POST /policy-files` request must likewise carry `expected_agency_version` matching the agency's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGENCY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the referral routing stamped onto the new file is derived from `underwriting_authority_limit_cents` at that version.

A `POST /remittance-statements` request must also carry `expected_agency_version` matching the agency's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGENCY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new statement legitimately supersedes the agency's prior remittance baseline in `last_remittance_period_end`, so a creation carrying a version that has since advanced is refused as a stale-baseline write rather than accepted on top of a period the client never saw.

No other endpoint takes a version field. State-changing endpoints, line creation, balance snapshots, finding creation and claim creation carry no `expected_agency_version` and perform no version comparison.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this system may not be made by the person who set them up, and both are enforced by comparing the incoming `X-User-ID` against an identifier persisted on an earlier transition — not against a role name, which the same operator could hold on both occasions. Both comparisons are exact string equality against the persisted value, and both pass vacuously when that persisted anchor is null: a claim on a policy file that never went through counsel referral carries no `referral_decided_by_user_id` for the acting identifier to collide with, and an audit opened by the freeze cascade of Section 3.7 carries the literal `SYSTEM` as its `conducted_by_user_id`, which no real actor's `X-User-ID` will equal.

**SoD-1 — audit closure.** The `X-User-ID` closing an escrow audit, whether to `CLOSED_CLEAN` or to `CLOSED_ADVERSE`, must differ from the `conducted_by_user_id` recorded when that audit was opened. If they are equal the close is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

**SoD-2 — claim adjudication.** The `X-User-ID` adjudicating a claim out of `INVESTIGATING`, whether to `RECOURSE_DEMANDED` or to `ABSORBED`, must differ from the `referral_decided_by_user_id` recorded on the claim's linked policy file. The counsel who lifted the authority limit on a file may not later decide whether the agency is on the hook for the loss that file produced. Equality is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.10. Path and Body Reference Resolution

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and each segment produces its own not-found error before anything to its right is examined: an unresolvable agency segment gives `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, a state program segment `ERR_STATE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, an appointment segment `ERR_AGENCY_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a closing protection letter segment `ERR_CPL_NOT_FOUND`, a policy file segment `ERR_POLICY_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, a remittance statement segment `ERR_REMITTANCE_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, an escrow account segment `ERR_ESCROW_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, an escrow audit segment `ERR_ESCROW_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, an audit finding segment `ERR_AUDIT_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, and an agency claim segment `ERR_AGENCY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. All ten are HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved the same way, in a fixed declaration order rather than in the order the client happened to serialize them: `agency_id` first, then `state_program_id`, then `escrow_account_id`, then `policy_file_id`, then `cpl_id`, then `follow_up_of_audit_id`, then `transferee_agency_id`. Each must reference an existing row, and the first one that does not decides the error — `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND` for `agency_id` or `transferee_agency_id`, `ERR_STATE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESCROW_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CPL_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_ESCROW_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` for `follow_up_of_audit_id`, each HTTP 404. This body-reference resolution applies to agency appointment creation, letter requests, policy file creation, remittance statement creation, remittance line creation, escrow account creation, escrow audit creation, claim creation, and the agency appointment decision (which may carry `transferee_agency_id`).

Every state-transition request names the state it is asking for. Each such request carries a `to_state` field holding the target value for the dimension that endpoint governs — the letter, issuance, transfer, statement, audit, finding and claim dimensions each have their own — and any target that is not a declared transition out of the resource's current state is rejected as described in Section 2.11. The agency appointment decision is the one exception: it names an action in `decision` rather than a target state, because a single decision word drives two dimensions at once. Requests that supply a `to_state` value outside the dimension's declared vocabulary are malformed rather than merely ill-timed and are refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule would reject a request, the outcome is fully determined: rules are evaluated in the following precedence order, and the first failure is returned. Nothing later in the ladder is evaluated once an earlier tier has rejected.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Endpoint role.** The acting role is listed for the endpoint, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Request shape.** Every required body field present, of the declared type, within its declared enum domain for enum fields, non-negative for `_cents` and `_days` integers, and ISO-8601 for date and datetime fields. Unknown fields are ignored, and any computed or system-owned field a client tries to supply is ignored rather than rejected. A field supplied as JSON `null` counts as absent: a required field spelled `null` fails as missing, while an optional field spelled `null` takes its declared default rather than being stored as null. Integer fields admit only JSON integers — a boolean is not an integer, and neither is a number carrying a fractional part, not even one such as `10.0` whose fraction is zero. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, while a body that is present but is not a JSON object at all fails this tier. Failures give `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), except that a termination decision missing its `termination_reason` gives `ERR_TERMINATION_REASON_REQUIRED` (HTTP 400) — an out-of-domain `termination_reason` is merely malformed and gives `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. List endpoints validate `limit` and `offset` in this same tier, and ahead of tier 4, so a bad page window on a nested list endpoint outranks an unresolvable path segment.
4. **Path existence.** Each path segment resolves, per Section 2.10.
5. **Body reference existence.** Each body foreign key resolves, in the declaration order of Section 2.10.
6. **Ownership.** An `AgencyPrincipal` acts only on its own agency's resources, else `ERR_AGENCY_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.
7. **Transition role.** The acting role is permitted for the specific requested transition, else `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
8. **Segregation of duties.** SoD-1 and SoD-2 per Section 2.9, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_agency_version` matches the agency's current `version` on the three guarded creates, else `ERR_STALE_AGENCY_VERSION`.
10. **State.** Within this tier the terminal check runs first: the addressed dimension is not already sitting in one of its terminal values (`ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, HTTP 409), and only then is the requested transition checked to exist from the resource's current state on that dimension (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`, HTTP 409). That order is load-bearing, because no transition is declared out of a terminal value: a request against a terminal resource therefore always yields `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` and never `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, whatever in-vocabulary `to_state` it names. After those two come every other state-shaped precondition in this tier — frozen posture, closed escrow account, missing balance snapshot, empty statement, finding-population requirements, an issued policy blocking a void, and the escrow-standing bar on reinstatement.
11. **Business safety.** Effective errors-and-omissions coverage, financial-review acceptance, license expiry and the financial minimums.
12. **Business referential.** Cross-entity relationship rules: active appointment in state, state permission and scope for letters, letter issuance before policy issuance, line-to-policy eligibility and period membership, claim-to-policy consistency, and transferee eligibility.
13. **Business constraint and temporal.** Uniqueness rules and interval rules: duplicate tax identifier, state code, appointment, escrow account reference, policy number or statement line; overlapping remittance periods; audit spacing and surprise-audit exclusivity.
14. **Business fiscal.** Amount ceilings: the state coverage cap on a requested letter, the underwriting authority limit at issuance, the referral approval ceiling, the settlement amount identity, and the recovery ceiling.

Two precedence pairings are load-bearing and worth stating outright, because in each case the naive reading of the endpoint would produce the other error.

**P1.** On a closing protection letter request, the frozen-posture check sits in tier 10 while the state coverage-cap check sits in tier 14. An agency that is both `FROZEN` and asking for coverage above the state's `cpl_coverage_cap_cents` therefore receives `ERR_AGENCY_POSTURE_FROZEN`, not `ERR_CPL_COVERAGE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

**P2.** On a policy file issuance, the lapsed errors-and-omissions check sits in tier 11 while the active-appointment check sits in tier 12. An agency whose coverage has expired *and* whose appointment in the state is no longer `ACTIVE` therefore receives `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_LAPSED`, not `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPOINTMENT_IN_STATE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding an Agency: Registration, Financial Review and the Appointment Decision

Registration is deliberately cheap. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `LicensingAnalyst` records the agency's `legal_name`, its federal `tax_id`, and the `underwriting_authority_limit_cents` — the per-file liability the agency will be permitted to bind without referring the title to underwriting counsel. The authority limit must be zero or a positive number of cents; a negative value is a shape failure. The `tax_id` must be unique across all agencies ever registered, and a reused identifier is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAX_ID` (HTTP 409). A newly created agency lands in a fully defined but fully restricted state: `appointment_status` is `PENDING_REVIEW`, `financial_review_status` is `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `escrow_standing` is `COMPLIANT`, `defalcation_score` is 0, and `oversight_posture` is consequently `RESTRICTED`. Every counter and ledger starts at zero — `open_critical_finding_count`, `unreported_policy_count`, `cpl_outstanding_exposure_cents`, `unremitted_premium_cents` and `pending_transfer_count` are all 0 — while `last_remittance_period_end`, `termination_reason` and `transferee_agency_id` all start null. The four effective financial figures also start empty: `eo_policy_limit_cents`, `net_worth_cents` and `fidelity_bond_cents` are 0 and `eo_expires_on` is null, and the four staged figures start exactly the same way — `submitted_eo_policy_limit_cents`, `submitted_net_worth_cents` and `submitted_fidelity_bond_cents` at 0 with `submitted_eo_expires_on` null.

Financial review runs on a staging-then-mirror pattern, and the distinction matters because coverage decisions read the *effective* figures, never the claimed ones. When an agency submits its financial package — supplying `submitted_eo_policy_limit_cents`, `submitted_eo_expires_on`, `submitted_net_worth_cents` and `submitted_fidelity_bond_cents`, all four of which are required, the three cents figures as non-negative integers and the expiry as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date — those four values are persisted onto the staging fields and the effective `eo_policy_limit_cents`, `eo_expires_on`, `net_worth_cents` and `fidelity_bond_cents` are left exactly as they were. Only the agency itself may submit, so `X-User-ID` must equal the agency's own identifier, and a terminated agency may not submit at all: when `appointment_status` is already `TERMINATED` the submission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The submission drives `financial_review_status` to `SUBMITTED` from whatever origin that dimension is sitting in — `NOT_SUBMITTED` for a first filing, `DEFICIENT` for a re-filing after a rejection, `ACCEPTED` for the annual renewal, and `SUBMITTED` itself, because the terminated check is the submission's only state precondition, so a fresh filing lodged over one still awaiting decision is accepted and simply overwrites the staged figures — after which `oversight_posture` is recomputed and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. Because a status of `SUBMITTED` is not `ACCEPTED`, the recomputation drives the posture to at least `RESTRICTED`. That is intentional on the renewal path: the previously accepted effective figures remain in force and still satisfy the coverage checks, but the agency's coverage cap is halved and its letter requests are forced into referral for as long as the renewal sits unaccepted.

A `LicensingAnalyst` disposes of the submission by supplying a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `DEFICIENT`, optionally with a free-text `note` that must be a string when supplied but is not persisted on any row. Either decision demands, in the state tier, that the agency's `financial_review_status` be exactly `SUBMITTED`: there is nothing to dispose of otherwise, so a decision against a review sitting in `NOT_SUBMITTED`, in `DEFICIENT` or already in `ACCEPTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Acceptance is where the two hard financial minimums bite, and both read the staged values rather than the effective ones. The staged errors-and-omissions limit in `submitted_eo_policy_limit_cents` must be at least two hundred fifty thousand dollars — 25,000,000 cents — and anything below that is rejected with `ERR_EO_LIMIT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). The staged audited net worth in `submitted_net_worth_cents` must be at least fifty thousand dollars — 5,000,000 cents — and anything below that is rejected with `ERR_NET_WORTH_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). The two minimums are evaluated in that order, so an agency failing both receives `ERR_EO_LIMIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. There is no minimum on the fidelity bond. When both minimums are satisfied, `financial_review_status` becomes `ACCEPTED` and all four staged values are mirrored onto the effective fields: `eo_policy_limit_cents`, `eo_expires_on`, `net_worth_cents` and `fidelity_bond_cents` each take the corresponding `submitted_` value. The posture is then recomputed and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. A `DEFICIENT` decision carries no preconditions beyond that `SUBMITTED` origin: it simply moves `financial_review_status` to `DEFICIENT`, leaves all four effective figures untouched, recomputes the posture — `DEFICIENT` is likewise not `ACCEPTED`, so the posture stays at `RESTRICTED` or worse — and increments `Agency.version` by 1.

The appointment dimension is separate from the financial dimension and advances on its own decisions. A `LicensingAnalyst` supplies a `decision` of `APPOINT`, `SUSPEND` or `REINSTATE`, optionally with a `reason` that must be a string when supplied but is not persisted on any row; a `ComplianceOfficer` supplies `TERMINATE`, with `termination_reason` and optionally `transferee_agency_id`. A `transferee_agency_id` is resolved for its existence on whichever decision carries it, per Section 2.10, but it is tested for eligibility and persisted only on `TERMINATE`; on the other three decisions it is resolved and then ignored. A `termination_reason` supplied alongside a decision other than `TERMINATE` is ignored without being validated. Appointing an agency moves `appointment_status` from `PENDING_REVIEW` to `APPOINTED` and demands two things of the agency's current record: its `financial_review_status` must be `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_FINANCIAL_REVIEW_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 422), and it must already hold at least one appointment in some state whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPOINTMENT_IN_STATE` (HTTP 422). On success the posture is recomputed and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1 — an agency that is `APPOINTED` with an accepted review, a `COMPLIANT` standing, no open critical findings, fewer than three unreported policies and unremitted premium at or below five hundred thousand dollars finally reaches `OPEN`.

Suspension is the blunt instrument. Moving `APPOINTED` to `SUSPENDED` requires nothing beyond the decision itself, and its effects fan out: because `SUSPENDED` is one of the two freeze conditions, `oversight_posture` becomes `FROZEN`, and because the recomputation moved the posture into `FROZEN` from a value that was not already `FROZEN`, every escrow account belonging to the agency whose `status` is `OPEN` moves to `RESTRICTED` — the account side effect is keyed to that entry into `FROZEN`, so suspending an agency that was already `FROZEN` on the other freeze condition restricts nothing further. `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. Reinstatement — `SUSPENDED` back to `APPOINTED` — is the guarded direction, and it is guarded by escrow risk rather than by licensing: the agency's `escrow_standing` must not be `DEFALCATION_RISK` and its `open_critical_finding_count` must be exactly 0. Either condition failing produces `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_BLOCKED_BY_ESCROW_STANDING` (HTTP 409). When reinstatement succeeds, the posture is recomputed; because it has left `FROZEN`, every escrow account of the agency whose `status` is `RESTRICTED` moves back to `OPEN`, and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. The suspend-and-reinstate cycle may run any number of times. `TERMINATED` is the only terminal value of the appointment dimension, and a further decision against a terminated agency is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409); termination itself is described in Section 3.9.

Agencies can be listed and filtered by `appointment_status`, `financial_review_status`, `oversight_posture` and `escrow_standing`, and — reversing the agency self-reference — by `transferee_agency_id`, which returns the agencies designated to receive a terminated agency's files.

### 3.2. State Programs, Appointments and License Verification

A state program is the rule book for one jurisdiction, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may write one. It is keyed by a two-letter `state_code`, unique across all state programs, with a reuse refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_CODE` (HTTP 409). The two-letter shape is a naming convention and not a validation: `state_code` is required and must be a JSON string, but it is stored exactly as supplied with no length check, no alphabet check and no case folding, and the uniqueness test is byte-for-byte string equality — so two spellings differing only in case are two distinct programs. The program declares whether closing protection letters may be written at all through the required boolean `cpl_permitted`; the statutory ceiling on letter coverage before the scope factor is applied, in `cpl_coverage_cap_cents`; the set of party combinations the state allows, in `cpl_scope_allowed`, which must be a non-empty array drawn from the three permitted values `FULL_SETTLEMENT`, `LENDER_ONLY` and `SELLER_PROCEEDS`; the agency's retention fraction of gross premium in `premium_split_agency_pct`, from which the underwriter's factor is derived as one minus that fraction; the number of whole days after a closing within which a policy may still be issued on time, in `policy_issuance_deadline_days`, which must be at least 1; the escrow reconciliation break the underwriter will tolerate before raising a finding, in `three_way_tolerance_cents`; and the per-file liability above which counsel referral is mandatory no matter how much authority the agency holds, in `counsel_referral_threshold_cents`. All three cents fields and the deadline must be non-negative, and an empty or out-of-domain `cpl_scope_allowed`, like an out-of-range `premium_split_agency_pct`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Appointment is the junction between an agency and a state: one row per pair, created by a `LicensingAnalyst` with the `agency_id`, the `state_program_id`, and the license the agency claims to hold — `license_number` together with `license_expires_on`. All four of those inputs are required. The new row lands in `PENDING_LICENSE_VERIFICATION` with `verified_license_number`, `verified_expires_on` and `appointed_on` all null, and creation imposes no precondition on the agency itself — neither an accepted financial review nor any particular `appointment_status` is demanded, and the claimed `license_expires_on` is not compared against the clock until verification, so a licence already expired on its face may still be recorded. Only one live appointment may exist per pair, so a creation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPOINTMENT` (HTTP 409) whenever an appointment for the same agency and the same state program already exists with a `status` of `PENDING_LICENSE_VERIFICATION`, `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`. A `WITHDRAWN` predecessor does not block re-appointment.

Verification is the licensing desk's own act, not the agency's claim. A `LicensingAnalyst` supplies a `decision` of `VERIFY` or `LAPSE`, and on a `VERIFY` supplies the values confirmed against the state registry as `verified_license_number` and `verified_expires_on`. Both of those two are optional at the shape tier, and omitting one has a different consequence from omitting the other: a `VERIFY` carrying no `verified_expires_on` at all is a verification against no unexpired registry date and is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`, not with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a `VERIFY` that omits `verified_license_number` succeeds and leaves whatever `verified_license_number` the row already carried. On a `LAPSE` both are ignored entirely. Verifying moves the appointment from `PENDING_LICENSE_VERIFICATION` to `ACTIVE`, and it demands two things: the confirmed `verified_expires_on` must fall strictly after the logical clock's current date — a license expiring today is already expired for this purpose — else `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422); and the owning agency's `financial_review_status` must be `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_FINANCIAL_REVIEW_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 422). The expiry is tested first, so an appointment failing both returns `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`. On success the confirmed expiry is persisted, the confirmed licence number is persisted whenever one was supplied, and `appointed_on` is set to the logical clock's current date if and only if it is currently null, so it records the first activation and is never overwritten by a later renewal.

Lapsing runs the other way: `ACTIVE` to `LAPSED`, with no preconditions whatsoever. The consequence is felt elsewhere — with no `ACTIVE` appointment for that agency-and-state pair, every subsequent letter request, letter issuance, policy file creation and policy issuance touching that pair fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPOINTMENT_IN_STATE`. Renewal reverses it: `LAPSED` back to `ACTIVE` under exactly the same two preconditions as the first verification — an unexpired confirmed expiry and an accepted financial review — and on success `verified_expires_on` is overwritten with the renewed value, `verified_license_number` is overwritten only when the renewal supplies one, and `appointed_on`, already set, stands. This lapse-and-renew cycle may repeat indefinitely. `WITHDRAWN` is the appointment dimension's only terminal value and is never reached by a client request; it is written only by the termination cascade of Section 3.9. Appointments may be listed and filtered by `agency_id`, `state_program_id` and `status`.

### 3.3. Closing Protection Letters: Routing, Coverage Caps, Issuance and Void

A closing protection letter is the underwriter's direct undertaking to the parties at a closing table, so it is requested by the agency and decided by the underwriter. The agency supplies the `agency_id` it is acting for, the `state_program_id` whose rules will govern the letter, its own `transaction_ref` for the closing, the `coverage_scope` naming which parties are protected, the `requested_coverage_cents` — which must be strictly greater than zero — the scheduled `closing_date`, the `lender_name` being protected, and, when the title is one the agency believes needs counsel eyes, `complex_title_flag`, which defaults to false when omitted. The request also carries `expected_agency_version` as described in Section 2.8.

Before any routing happens, the request must survive four business gates in the precedence order of Section 2.11. The agency's `oversight_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, else `ERR_AGENCY_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Its effective errors-and-omissions coverage must be live: `eo_expires_on` must be non-null and must fall on or after the logical clock's current date, else `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_LAPSED`. An appointment with `status` `ACTIVE` must exist for the agency-and-state pair, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPOINTMENT_IN_STATE`. The state must permit letters at all — `cpl_permitted` must be true, else `ERR_CPL_NOT_PERMITTED_IN_STATE` — and the requested `coverage_scope` must be one of the members of that state's `cpl_scope_allowed` array, else `ERR_CPL_SCOPE_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422). Last of all, in the fiscal tier, `requested_coverage_cents` must not exceed the state's `cpl_coverage_cap_cents`, else `ERR_CPL_COVERAGE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). That last check is the one outranked by the frozen-posture check, per pairing P1.

Routing then decides where the new letter lands. Four referral conditions are evaluated in a fixed order and evaluation stops at the first one that matches; `referral_reason` is set to that condition's label. First, if `complex_title_flag` is true, the reason is `COMPLEX_TITLE`. Second, if `requested_coverage_cents` is strictly greater than the state's `counsel_referral_threshold_cents`, the reason is `STATE_REFERRAL_THRESHOLD`. Third, if `requested_coverage_cents` is strictly greater than the agency's `underwriting_authority_limit_cents`, the reason is `AUTHORITY_LIMIT`. Fourth, if the agency's `oversight_posture` is `RESTRICTED`, the reason is `RESTRICTED_POSTURE`. If any condition matched, the letter is created with `status` `REFERRED` and that `referral_reason`; if none matched, it is created with `status` `REQUESTED` and a null `referral_reason`. In either case `approved_coverage_cents` starts at 0, `issued_at`, `decided_by_user_id` and `void_reason` all start null, and the request itself is recorded against the agency's oversight file by incrementing `Agency.version` by 1.

Issuance is a single decision reachable from either branch, and the branch decides who may make it. A `REQUESTED` letter is released by a `ComplianceOfficer`; a `REFERRED` letter may be released only by an `UnderwritingCounsel`, and a `ComplianceOfficer` attempting it is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Both branches carry the identical four preconditions, evaluated in the tier order of Section 2.11: `oversight_posture` not `FROZEN`, else `ERR_AGENCY_POSTURE_FROZEN`; non-null `eo_expires_on` on or after the clock's current date, else `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_LAPSED`; an `ACTIVE` appointment for the pair, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPOINTMENT_IN_STATE`; and `cpl_permitted` true, else `ERR_CPL_NOT_PERMITTED_IN_STATE` — the last two share tier 12, and within it the appointment check runs ahead of the state-permission check. The `coverage_scope` is not re-tested against the state's `cpl_scope_allowed` at issuance, and neither is `requested_coverage_cents` against `cpl_coverage_cap_cents`: both were settled at request time, and the cap now binds only through the coverage computation. Both branches carry the identical side effects: `approved_coverage_cents` is computed as described below, `issued_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, `decided_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, the agency's `cpl_outstanding_exposure_cents` is recomputed and thereby rises by the newly approved coverage, and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. The only difference on the referred branch is that the letter's `referral_reason` is retained on the row for audit rather than cleared. An optional `rationale` may accompany the decision; it must be a string when supplied but is not persisted on the row.

Approved coverage is the smallest of four independent caps, each computed to whole cents before the comparison. The scope factor is 1.00 when `coverage_scope` is `FULL_SETTLEMENT`, 0.80 when it is `LENDER_ONLY`, and 0.60 when it is `SELLER_PROCEEDS`. The **state cap** is the state's `cpl_coverage_cap_cents` multiplied by that scope factor, with any fraction of a cent discarded downward. The **posture cap** equals the state cap when the agency's `oversight_posture` is `OPEN`, and equals half the state cap, again floored, when the posture is `RESTRICTED`; a `FROZEN` posture never reaches this computation because the frozen-posture rejection fires first. The **errors-and-omissions sublimit** is half the agency's effective `eo_policy_limit_cents`, floored. The fourth cap is simply the `requested_coverage_cents` itself. `approved_coverage_cents` is the minimum of those four. Consider a request for 90,000,000 cents with a `LENDER_ONLY` scope against a state cap of 100,000,000 cents: the state cap becomes 80,000,000. With an `OPEN` posture and an effective errors-and-omissions limit of 200,000,000 cents the sublimit is 100,000,000 and the state cap binds at 80,000,000. Change only the posture to `RESTRICTED` and the posture cap becomes 40,000,000, which binds. Return the posture to `OPEN` but drop the errors-and-omissions limit to 100,000,000 and the sublimit becomes 50,000,000, which binds instead.

Declining is the mirror decision and follows the same role split — a `ComplianceOfficer` declines a `REQUESTED` letter, an `UnderwritingCounsel` declines a `REFERRED` one, and a role crossing that line is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Neither branch carries any further precondition. On both, `decided_by_user_id` is set to the acting identifier, `approved_coverage_cents` remains 0, and the agency's `cpl_outstanding_exposure_cents` is unchanged, since a declined letter was never in the issued set. Because no field of the agency row moves, a decline — unlike an issuance or a void — advances neither `Agency.version` nor the agency's `updated_at`. Letters can also reach `DECLINED` without any client request at all — the freeze cascade of Section 3.7, the termination cascade of Section 3.9 and the punitive tail of a defalcation recourse demand in Section 3.8 all decline letters in flight, and in those cases `decided_by_user_id` is recorded as the literal string `SYSTEM`. The list endpoint is the discovery channel for letters declined that way.

Voiding is the compensating action for issuance, available to a `ComplianceOfficer` on a letter whose `status` is `ISSUED`. It is barred while the letter is actually backing paper: if any policy file whose `cpl_id` references this letter has an `issuance_status` of `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED`, the void is refused with `ERR_CPL_HAS_ISSUED_POLICY` (HTTP 409). Otherwise the supplied `void_reason` is persisted — the field is optional, and a void that omits it simply leaves the letter's `void_reason` null rather than being refused — the letter's `approved_coverage_cents` is reset to 0, the agency's `cpl_outstanding_exposure_cents` is recomputed — which, because the letter has left the issued set and its approved coverage is now zero, restores the ledger to precisely the value it held before the letter was issued — and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. `DECLINED` and `VOIDED` are the two terminal values of the letter dimension; a further transition against either is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and because no transition-level role restriction is declared out of a terminal value, either of the endpoint's two roles receives that same rejection rather than `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Letters may be listed and filtered by `agency_id`, `state_program_id`, `status` and `complex_title_flag`, the last of which is a typed boolean filter per Section 2.3.

### 3.4. Policy Files: Referral Routing, Authority Limits and Issuance Timeliness

A policy file carries three status dimensions on one row, advanced by three different actors, and they are genuinely independent. `issuance_status` is the underwriting dimension and is driven by the agency and by counsel. `remittance_status` is the accounting dimension and is advanced *only* by remittance statement transitions, never by a direct request against the file. `transfer_status` is the wind-down dimension and only ever moves after the owning agency has been terminated. A file may perfectly well be `LATE_ISSUED`, `DISPUTED` and `PENDING_TRANSFER` at the same time.

Opening a file is the agency's act. It supplies the `agency_id`, the `state_program_id` whose deadline and premium split will govern the file, optionally the `cpl_id` of a closing protection letter covering the same closing, its own `order_number`, the actual `closing_date`, the `liability_amount_cents` it wants to bind and the `gross_premium_cents` it collected — both of which must be strictly greater than zero — optionally `complex_title_flag`, and `expected_agency_version`. Creation requires an appointment with `status` `ACTIVE` for the agency-and-state pair, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPOINTMENT_IN_STATE`. A supplied `cpl_id` is checked for existence only: creation tests neither that the letter belongs to the same agency nor that it names the same `state_program_id`, and the letter's own `status` is not consulted until issuance.

Referral routing is stamped at creation and is not revisited. A referral is required when any of three conditions holds: `complex_title_flag` is true, or `liability_amount_cents` is strictly greater than the agency's `underwriting_authority_limit_cents`, or `liability_amount_cents` is strictly greater than the state's `counsel_referral_threshold_cents`. All three comparisons are strict, so a liability exactly equal to the agency's authority limit is *within* authority. When a referral is required the file lands in `PENDING_REFERRAL` with `approved_liability_cents` of 0; when none is required it lands in `PENDING` with `approved_liability_cents` equal to the full `liability_amount_cents`. In both cases `remittance_status` starts at `UNREPORTED`, `transfer_status` starts at `NOT_REQUIRED`, `policy_number`, `issued_at`, `issuance_elapsed_days`, `referral_decided_by_user_id` and `transferee_agency_id` all start null, and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1.

Only an `UnderwritingCounsel` may resolve a file sitting in `PENDING_REFERRAL`, and there are exactly two ways out. Approving the referral moves the file from `PENDING_REFERRAL` to `PENDING` and requires an `approved_liability_cents` in the body that is present and strictly greater than zero, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that does not exceed the file's own `liability_amount_cents`, else `ERR_APPROVED_LIABILITY_ABOVE_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). The approved figure is persisted and `referral_decided_by_user_id` is set to the counsel's `X-User-ID` — this is the anchor that SoD-2 will read much later, when someone tries to adjudicate a claim on this same file. Declining the referral moves the file from `PENDING_REFERRAL` to `ISSUANCE_DECLINED` with no preconditions at all; `referral_decided_by_user_id` is still recorded, and `approved_liability_cents` stays at 0. An optional `rationale` may accompany either decision; it must be a string when supplied but is not persisted on the row. Neither referral decision touches the agency row at all: no counter, no ledger and no `Agency.version` moves.

Issuance belongs to the agency and only to the agency: a `PENDING` file is moved to issuance by its own `AgencyPrincipal`, and any other role attempting it is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Seven conditions guard it, and they surface in the precedence order of Section 2.11. A `policy_number` must be present in the body, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The agency's `oversight_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, else `ERR_AGENCY_POSTURE_FROZEN`. The effective `eo_expires_on` must be non-null and on or after the clock's current date, else `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_LAPSED`. An appointment with `status` `ACTIVE` must exist for the pair, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPOINTMENT_IN_STATE` — and when both the coverage and the appointment have failed, pairing P2 dictates that the coverage error is the one returned. When the file carries a non-null `cpl_id`, that letter's `status` must be `ISSUED`, else `ERR_CPL_NOT_ISSUED` (HTTP 422). The supplied `policy_number` must not already be in use among that same agency's policy files whose `issuance_status` is `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_NUMBER` (HTTP 409); numbers on pending, declined or voided files do not reserve anything. Finally, in the fiscal tier, either the file's `approved_liability_cents` must not exceed the agency's `underwriting_authority_limit_cents`, or the file must carry a non-null `referral_decided_by_user_id` — a counsel referral decision lifts the authority limit — and failing both gives `ERR_UNDERWRITING_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Timeliness then decides which of two states the issuance lands in, from the same request and the same requested target. The file's `issuance_elapsed_days` is the signed number of whole days from its `closing_date` to the calendar date of the logical clock at issuance, so a `closing_date` later than that date yields a negative count rather than an error. When that elapsed count is less than or equal to the state's `policy_issuance_deadline_days` the file becomes `ISSUED` — a negative count therefore lands comfortably on `ISSUED` — and when it is strictly greater the file becomes `LATE_ISSUED`. The boundary is inclusive on the timely side. With a `policy_issuance_deadline_days` of 30 and a logical clock reading 2026-08-01, a `closing_date` of 2026-07-02 gives 30 elapsed days and lands on `ISSUED`, while a `closing_date` of 2026-07-01 gives 31 and lands on `LATE_ISSUED`. Either way `issued_at` is stamped with the clock instant, the agency's `unremitted_premium_cents` is recomputed and thereby rises by this file's `gross_premium_cents`, and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. The only declared target out of `PENDING` is `ISSUED`: `LATE_ISSUED` is a landing the server chooses, never a target a client may name, so a request naming it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `ISSUANCE_DECLINED` and `VOIDED` are the terminal values of the issuance dimension, and `VOIDED` is only ever reached by the termination cascade. `ISSUED` and `LATE_ISSUED` are not themselves terminal even though no transition leaves them, so a second issuance attempt against a file already sitting in either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and since no transition-level role restriction is declared out of them, the rejection is the same for either of the endpoint's two roles once the ownership tier is satisfied.

Files may be listed and filtered on all three dimensions independently — by `issuance_status`, `remittance_status` and `transfer_status` — as well as by `agency_id`, `state_program_id`, `cpl_id`, `transferee_agency_id`, and by a closing-date window whose bounds are `closing_date_from` and `closing_date_to`. The same list endpoint is the discovery channel for files the termination cascade moved to `VOIDED` or to `PENDING_TRANSFER`.

### 3.5. Remittance: Reporting Premium, Reconciliation, Settlement and Reopen

Remittance is the accounting half of the relationship and it runs on closed calendar periods. An agency opens a statement for a period by supplying its `agency_id`, the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end` dates, and `expected_agency_version`. The period must be well formed — a `period_start` after `period_end` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it must not overlap any statement the agency already holds, in any `status` whatsoever. Two closed intervals overlap when the new `period_start` is on or before the existing `period_end` and the existing `period_start` is on or before the new `period_end`; an overlap is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A period that begins exactly one day after an existing period ends is therefore accepted. The new statement lands in `DRAFT` with every money field and counter at 0 — `reported_gross_premium_cents`, `underwriter_share_cents`, `agency_retention_cents`, `expected_gross_premium_cents`, `variance_cents`, `unreported_policy_count`, `unreported_premium_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `settled_amount_cents` and `reopen_count` all zero — with `submitted_at`, `reconciled_at`, `settled_at` and `reopen_reason` all null. Creation also advances the agency's remittance baseline: `last_remittance_period_end` holds the greatest `period_end` among the agency's statements, so it takes the new `period_end` whenever that date is later than the value already there, and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1.

Each policy is reported on its own line. The agency supplies the `policy_file_id` and the `reported_gross_premium_cents` it claims to have collected, which must be strictly greater than zero, and the line records the `remittance_statement_id` of the statement it belongs to. Three referential rules guard the line. The referenced policy must belong to the same agency as the statement and must have an `issuance_status` of `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED`, else `ERR_LINE_POLICY_NOT_ISSUED` (HTTP 422) — a pending, declined or voided file cannot be remitted, and neither can another agency's file. The policy's `closing_date` must fall inside the statement's period, inclusive of both bounds, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). And no existing line of this same statement may already reference that `policy_file_id`, else `ERR_POLICY_ALREADY_ON_STATEMENT` (HTTP 409). The statement's own `status` is not a precondition of line creation: a line may be added to a statement that is already `SUBMITTED`, `RECONCILED` or even `SETTLED`, and the statement's split totals are refreshed the moment it lands. Line creation touches no field of the agency, so it advances neither `Agency.version` nor the agency's `updated_at`.

The premium split is computed on the line, at line creation, using the split percentage of the state program attached to *that line's policy* — not the statement's, because one statement may report policies written in several states. The line's `agency_retention_cents` is the reported premium multiplied by that state program's `premium_split_agency_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent; the line's `underwriter_share_cents` is then the reported premium minus that retention. Deriving the underwriter share directly from the complementary factor is not the same computation and is not what this system does. Each line also carries `premium_mismatch`, which is true exactly when the line's `reported_gross_premium_cents` differs from the referenced policy's `gross_premium_cents` and false otherwise. Statement totals are then sums of the already-rounded line values, per the order-of-operations rule in Section 2.5: the statement's `reported_gross_premium_cents` is the sum of the line reported premiums, its `agency_retention_cents` is the sum of the line retentions, and its `underwriter_share_cents` is the sum of the line underwriter shares. Those three totals are recomputed from the statement's current lines at every line creation, again at submission and again at reconciliation, so a line added after submission is inside the totals that the reconciliation variance and the settlement amount identity later read. Lines can be listed for a statement, filtered by `premium_mismatch`, and separately listed for a single policy to see every statement that ever reported it.

Submission is the agency's act and moves the statement from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`. It requires at least one line — an empty statement is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_HAS_NO_LINES` (HTTP 409) — and it requires that the agency's `appointment_status` not be `TERMINATED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant and the statement's three split totals are refreshed from the current lines.

Reconciliation belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer` and is where unreported policies are caught. The **expected set** for the statement is every policy file of the statement's agency whose `issuance_status` is `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED` and whose `closing_date` falls between `period_start` and `period_end` inclusive. From that set, `expected_gross_premium_cents` is the sum of `gross_premium_cents` across the expected set, and `variance_cents` is the expected total minus the statement's reported total. The **unreported set** is those expected-set policies that no line of this statement references; `unreported_policy_count` is its size and `unreported_premium_cents` is the sum of `gross_premium_cents` across it. Two penalties then combine. A late-filing fee of two hundred fifty dollars — 25,000 cents — applies when the calendar date of `submitted_at` falls strictly after the fifteenth day following `period_end`; a filing landing exactly on that fifteenth day carries no fee, because the comparison is strict. A penalty equal to one tenth of `unreported_premium_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, applies on top. `late_fee_cents` is the sum of the filing fee and the penalty, and `reconciled_at` is stamped with the clock instant.

Consider a period running 2026-07-01 through 2026-07-31 in which the agency issued four policies: three of 33,333 cents each, all reported on lines, and one of 500,000 cents with no line at all, with the statement submitted at 2026-08-20T00:00:00Z. The expected total is 599,999 cents against a reported total of 99,999, giving a variance of 500,000. The unreported count is 1 and the unreported premium is 500,000. Because 2026-08-20 falls after 2026-08-15, the filing fee is 25,000 cents; the penalty is one tenth of 500,000, or 50,000 cents; and `late_fee_cents` is 75,000. Where every expected policy does appear on a line, `unreported_policy_count` and `unreported_premium_cents` are both 0 and the penalty component is 0 as well.

Reconciliation also advances the reported policies' accounting dimension, and the direction depends on the line. Every referenced policy whose line has `premium_mismatch` false moves from `UNREPORTED` to `REPORTED`, which requires the file to be `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED`. Every referenced policy whose line has `premium_mismatch` true moves from `UNREPORTED` to `DISPUTED` instead, and a disputed policy's `gross_premium_cents` deliberately stays inside the agency's `unremitted_premium_cents` — the underwriter has not agreed the number, so the money is not treated as accounted for. The agency's `unreported_policy_count` is then mirrored from the agency's most recently reconciled statement — the statements still sitting in `RECONCILED` taken in `reconciled_at` order with creation order breaking a tie, which is this statement itself unless the logical clock has been rewound below another reconciled statement's stamp — the agency's `oversight_posture` is recomputed — three or more unreported policies is one of the restrict conditions — and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1.

Settlement is CASCADE-C. A `ComplianceOfficer` moves the statement from `RECONCILED` to `SETTLED` by supplying `settled_amount_cents`, which must be present, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and which must equal the statement's `underwriter_share_cents` plus its `late_fee_cents` exactly, else `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Hop 1 sets the statement to `SETTLED`, stamps `settled_at` with the clock instant and persists the settled amount. Hop 2 fires because the statement became `SETTLED`: every referenced policy whose `remittance_status` is `REPORTED` moves to `REMITTED`, while `DISPUTED` policies are left exactly where they are. Hop 3 fires because those files became `REMITTED`: the agency's `unremitted_premium_cents` is recomputed and consequently falls. Hop 4 fires because the agency's `unremitted_premium_cents` and `unreported_policy_count` have changed: `oversight_posture` is recomputed. Hop 5 fires when that recomputation leaves `FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED` and lands on `OPEN`: every escrow account of the agency whose `status` is `RESTRICTED` moves back to `OPEN`. `Agency.version` is incremented by 1 for the request.

Reopening is CASCADE-C's compensating action, and it is available from two places. Both require a `reopen_reason` in the body, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, both persist that reason, and both increment `reopen_count` by 1. Neither is narrowed by role — either a `ComplianceOfficer` or the agency's own `AgencyPrincipal` may reopen — and neither clears `settled_amount_cents`, which stands at the amount last settled as the record of what was paid. From `SETTLED` back to `DRAFT`: `settled_at`, `reconciled_at` and `submitted_at` are all reset to null; `expected_gross_premium_cents`, `variance_cents`, `unreported_policy_count`, `unreported_premium_cents` and `late_fee_cents` are all reset to 0; every referenced policy whose `remittance_status` is `REMITTED` moves back to `REPORTED` and every one that is `DISPUTED` moves back to `UNREPORTED`; the agency's `unremitted_premium_cents` is recomputed, restoring it to exactly its pre-settlement value; the agency's `unreported_policy_count` is recomputed from that agency's most recently reconciled statement, becoming 0 when no statement of the agency remains reconciled; `oversight_posture` is recomputed; and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. From `RECONCILED` back to `DRAFT`: every referenced policy whose `remittance_status` is `REPORTED` or `DISPUTED` moves to `UNREPORTED`; the same five reconciliation outputs are reset to 0, while `submitted_at` and `reconciled_at` are deliberately left standing, so this path returns the statement to `DRAFT` still carrying the timestamps of the pass it is redoing; the agency's `unreported_policy_count` and `oversight_posture` are recomputed; and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. The whole loop — `SETTLED` to `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` to `RECONCILED` to `SETTLED` — may run repeatedly, which is why `reopen_count` exists. The statement dimension has no terminal state. Statements may be listed and filtered by `agency_id` and `status`.

### 3.6. Escrow Trust Accounts and the Three-Way Reconciliation Audit

An escrow trust account is the agency's own record, registered by the `AgencyPrincipal` with the `agency_id`, the depository `bank_name` and a masked `account_ref`. The reference must be unique among that agency's accounts whose `status` is not `CLOSED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ESCROW_ACCOUNT_REF` (HTTP 409); a reference freed by closure may be reused. A new account opens with `status` `OPEN`, every balance at 0 and `snapshot_as_of` null. Registration is not gated by the agency's oversight posture: an account registered while the agency is `RESTRICTED` or even `FROZEN` still opens `OPEN`, and the registration moves no agency field and no `Agency.version`.

Balances arrive as snapshots rather than as a transaction feed. A snapshot carries the trust ledger's `book_balance_cents`, the bank statement's `bank_balance_cents`, the `outstanding_items_cents` covering outstanding checks and deposits in transit, the `trial_balance_cents` summing the individual open file ledgers, the `unidentified_funds_cents` not yet attributed to any file, the `aged_unidentified_over_90_cents` portion of those funds that has been held more than ninety days, and the `snapshot_as_of` date the figures are drawn to. Posting a snapshot derives `adjusted_bank_balance_cents` as `bank_balance_cents` minus `outstanding_items_cents` and persists it on the account. All seven inputs are required and the six cents figures must be non-negative integers, but no relationship among them is enforced: an `aged_unidentified_over_90_cents` larger than `unidentified_funds_cents`, or an `outstanding_items_cents` larger than the bank balance and hence a negative adjusted balance, is accepted and simply flows into the variance arithmetic. The call answers **HTTP 200 OK** with the updated escrow account row rather than with a snapshot resource of its own, and it moves no agency field. A later snapshot simply overwrites the previous one; only the latest set of balances is retained. Snapshots are refused against an account whose `status` is `CLOSED`, with `ERR_ESCROW_ACCOUNT_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), but they are perfectly acceptable while the account is `RESTRICTED` — restriction gates coverage, not bookkeeping. The account's three states are `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `CLOSED`; they are written only by cascades that follow the owning agency's posture, never by a direct request, and `CLOSED` is terminal.

Audits are opened by an `EscrowAuditor` or a `ComplianceOfficer` against a specific account, carrying the `agency_id`, the `escrow_account_id`, the `audit_type` — either `SCHEDULED` or `SURPRISE` — the reconciliation cut-off in `as_of_date`, and optionally `follow_up_of_audit_id` pointing at the predecessor audit whose findings this one re-tests. Those references are resolved for existence only: the supplied `agency_id` is never tested against the account's own owning agency, and `follow_up_of_audit_id` is not required to name an audit of the same account or the same agency. The target account must not be `CLOSED`, else `ERR_ESCROW_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. Scheduled audits must be spaced: the absolute gap between the new `as_of_date` and the `as_of_date` of every existing `SCHEDULED` audit of the same account must be at least ninety days, with a gap of exactly ninety days accepted, and a tighter gap refused with `ERR_AUDIT_SPACING_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Surprise audits are instead exclusive: no other `SURPRISE` audit of the same account may be sitting in a non-terminal `status` — that is, in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` — else `ERR_SURPRISE_AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409).

The new audit opens in `DRAFT` with `conducted_by_user_id` set to the opening actor's `X-User-ID` — the SoD-1 anchor — with `risk_tier` `LOW`, with `open_finding_count` and `critical_finding_count` at 0, with `variance_method`, `submitted_at`, `closed_at` and `closed_by_user_id` null, and with `reconciliation_variance_cents` and `unidentified_funds_reviewed_cents` at 0. Opening an audit is also one of the two triggers that recompute the agency's `defalcation_score`, and it uses the lifetime variant described in Section 3.7; the score is recomputed first, then `escrow_standing`, then `oversight_posture`, and `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. Should that rescore carry the standing to `DEFALCATION_RISK`, the posture becomes `FROZEN` and the agency's `OPEN` escrow accounts are restricted on that entry into `FROZEN` — but nothing further: opening an audit never declines a letter and never spawns a successor audit, both of which belong to CASCADE-A alone.

Submission is the auditor's act and is reachable twice — from `DRAFT` on the first pass and from `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` on every subsequent pass. Both require the linked account to have a non-null `snapshot_as_of`, else `ERR_BALANCE_SNAPSHOT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409), and both require the linked account's `status` not to be `CLOSED`, else `ERR_ESCROW_ACCOUNT_CLOSED` — the snapshot check runs ahead of the closed-account check, so an account that is both closed and never snapshotted yields `ERR_BALANCE_SNAPSHOT_REQUIRED`. Both compute the reconciliation variance from the account's current snapshot, capture `unidentified_funds_reviewed_cents` from the account's `unidentified_funds_cents` at that moment, and refresh `submitted_at` to the current clock instant. Because a re-submission reads the account's *current* snapshot, an agency that posts fresh balances between review loops will see a different `reconciliation_variance_cents` on the second pass than on the first.

The variance itself has two variants, chosen by `audit_type`, and `variance_method` is persisted on the audit as the variant's name. A `SURPRISE` audit uses `STRICT_THREE_WAY`: `reconciliation_variance_cents` is the largest of three absolute differences — the adjusted bank balance against the book balance, the adjusted bank balance against the trial balance, and the book balance against the trial balance — so a book-to-trial break is caught even when the bank leg happens to agree with the trial balance. A `SCHEDULED` audit uses `BANK_TO_TRIAL`: `reconciliation_variance_cents` is the single absolute difference between the adjusted bank balance and the trial balance, and a book-to-trial break invisible in that one pair goes unreported. In both variants the adjusted bank balance is `bank_balance_cents` minus `outstanding_items_cents`. Take a snapshot with a book balance of 10,000,000, a bank balance of 10,120,000, outstanding items of 100,000 and a trial balance of 10,015,000: the adjusted bank balance is 10,020,000, so the strict variant yields the largest of 20,000, 5,000 and 15,000, which is 20,000, while the scheduled variant yields 5,000.

Submission also raises findings automatically, and there are exactly two auto-raised conditions. When `reconciliation_variance_cents` is strictly greater than the reconciliation tolerance that governs the agency — the `three_way_tolerance_cents` of the state program attached to the agency's oldest appointment whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, taking the earliest `created_at` where several are active, and falling back to a tolerance of 0 for an agency holding no `ACTIVE` appointment at all, so that any non-zero variance breaches it — a `THREE_WAY_VARIANCE` finding is created with its `exposure_cents` equal to that variance. When the account's `aged_unidentified_over_90_cents` is strictly greater than zero, an `UNIDENTIFIED_FUNDS_AGED` finding is created with its `exposure_cents` equal to that aged amount. On a re-submission out of `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, an auto-finding is created only for a condition that does not already have a finding on this audit whose `status` is `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, so the review loop does not accumulate duplicates. The two conditions are evaluated in the order just given, so where both fire on one submission the `THREE_WAY_VARIANCE` finding carries the earlier `created_at`, and each auto-raised finding carries a server-composed `narrative` describing the breach rather than a null one. After the findings are settled, the audit's `risk_tier`, `open_finding_count` and `critical_finding_count` are recomputed, then the agency's `open_critical_finding_count`, then the agency's `oversight_posture`; a submission forces no `Agency.version` bump of its own, so the agency row advances only if that recomputation actually moved one of its fields.

Review is the compliance side of the loop, and an optional `note` may accompany any transition on the audit state endpoint — submissions and closures included; it must be a string when supplied but it is never persisted on the audit row. Moving `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` carries no preconditions and no side effects: nothing but the audit's `status` changes, so no audit counter is refreshed, no agency field moves and `Agency.version` stands still. Moving `UNDER_REVIEW` to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` requires that at least one finding of the audit have a `status` of `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_NO_OPEN_FINDING` (HTTP 409); it too changes nothing beyond the `status`. From `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` the auditor re-submits, and the `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` → `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` cycle may repeat as many times as the remediation takes.

Closure ends the audit, is available only from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and is barred to the auditor who opened it by SoD-1. A clean closure — `UNDER_REVIEW` to `CLOSED_CLEAN` — additionally requires that *every* finding of the audit have a `status` of `CLEARED`, else `ERR_UNREMEDIATED_FINDING_PRESENT` (HTTP 409) — an audit that raised no findings at all satisfies that requirement vacuously and closes clean. An adverse closure — `UNDER_REVIEW` to `CLOSED_ADVERSE` — instead requires that at least one finding have a `status` of `ESCALATED`, else `ERR_NO_ESCALATED_FINDING` (HTTP 409). Both closures set `closed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` and `closed_at` to the clock instant, both recompute the agency's `defalcation_score` under the rolling-window variant of Section 3.7 followed by `escrow_standing` and `oversight_posture`, and both increment `Agency.version` by 1. A *clean* closure whose rolling-window rescore nonetheless reaches 60 therefore drives the standing to `DEFALCATION_RISK` and the posture to `FROZEN` and restricts the agency's `OPEN` escrow accounts on that entry into `FROZEN`, but it declines no letter and creates no successor audit. The adverse closure then fires the full freeze cascade described in Section 3.7. `CLOSED_CLEAN` and `CLOSED_ADVERSE` are the two terminal values of the audit dimension. Audits may be listed and filtered by `agency_id`, `escrow_account_id`, `audit_type`, `status`, and — reversing the audit self-reference — by `follow_up_of_audit_id`, which is also the discovery channel for the successor audit the freeze cascade creates.

### 3.7. Findings, Remediation, Risk Tiers and the Defalcation Score

Findings are raised by an `EscrowAuditor` against an audit that is still open for writing: the parent audit's `status` must be `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, and a finding written against an audit still sitting in `DRAFT`, or against one already closed, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — a draft audit has not yet been performed, and a closed audit's findings are frozen. A finding records the `escrow_audit_id` of its parent, a `code` drawn from `THREE_WAY_VARIANCE`, `UNIDENTIFIED_FUNDS_AGED`, `NEGATIVE_FILE_BALANCE`, `MISSING_RECONCILIATION` and `COMMINGLING`, the trust dollars at risk in `exposure_cents` — which must be zero or positive — and the auditor's `narrative`.

Severity is derived, never supplied. The `base_severity` comes from the code, and only two codes consult the exposure. A `THREE_WAY_VARIANCE` finding is `LOW` when `exposure_cents` is at most one thousand dollars (100,000 cents), `MEDIUM` when it is above that but at most ten thousand dollars (1,000,000 cents), `HIGH` when it is above that but at most fifty thousand dollars (5,000,000 cents), and `CRITICAL` when it exceeds fifty thousand dollars. An `UNIDENTIFIED_FUNDS_AGED` finding is `MEDIUM` when `exposure_cents` is at most five thousand dollars (500,000 cents) and `HIGH` when it exceeds that. The remaining three codes are fixed regardless of exposure: `NEGATIVE_FILE_BALANCE` is always `HIGH`, `MISSING_RECONCILIATION` is always `MEDIUM`, and `COMMINGLING` is always `CRITICAL`.

Repetition then escalates. The flag `is_repeat` is true when at least one other finding exists carrying the same `code` whose own parent audit covers the same `escrow_account_id`, has a different identifier from this finding's parent audit, and has a strictly earlier `created_at` than this finding's parent audit. That other finding's own `status` is irrelevant to the test: a predecessor long since `CLEARED` still makes the new finding a repeat. When `is_repeat` is false, `severity` equals `base_severity`. When it is true, `severity` is one rung higher on the ladder running `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `CRITICAL`, with `CRITICAL` as the ceiling that does not escalate further. A `THREE_WAY_VARIANCE` finding with an `exposure_cents` of 100,000 on a first audit of an account therefore has a base of `LOW`, no repeat, and a `severity` of `LOW`; the same code with the same exposure on a later audit of the same account has a base of `LOW`, a repeat, and a `severity` of `MEDIUM`.

A new finding opens with `status` `OPEN`, a null `remediation_note`, a `cleared_amount_cents` of 0 and a null `disposition_note`. Its creation then drives three levels of recomputation. On the parent audit, `open_finding_count` is the number of findings whose `status` is `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`; `critical_finding_count` is the number whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is anything other than `CLEARED`; and `risk_tier` is `CRITICAL` when any finding has a `severity` of `CRITICAL` and a `status` other than `CLEARED`, otherwise `HIGH` when any finding has a `severity` of `HIGH` and a `status` other than `CLEARED`, otherwise `MEDIUM` when `open_finding_count` is at least 1, and otherwise `LOW` — which covers both an audit with no findings and an audit whose every finding is cleared. On the agency, `open_critical_finding_count` is the number of findings across *all* of that agency's audits whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`. The agency's `oversight_posture` is then recomputed, since one open critical finding is enough to restrict it; the `defalcation_score` is not touched, a finding being neither of the score's two triggers. Neither a finding's creation nor its later disposition forces an `Agency.version` bump, so the agency row advances only where that recomputation actually moved one of its fields — as a `CRITICAL` finding does through `open_critical_finding_count`, and a `LOW` one does not.

The agency answers a finding by submitting remediation, moving it from `OPEN` to `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`. Only the `AgencyPrincipal` of the audited agency may do so — `X-User-ID` must equal the `agency_id` of the parent audit — and a `remediation_note` must be present in the body, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The parent audit must be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The narrative is persisted along with `cleared_amount_cents`, the trust dollars the agency says it restored — an optional figure which must be a non-negative integer when supplied and, when omitted, leaves the stored figure exactly as it stands, which on a first pass is the 0 the finding opened with; a `disposition_note` sent on a remediation is accepted by the shape check and then ignored. The parent audit's three counters are recomputed, but `open_finding_count` is deliberately *unchanged*: a finding under remediation still counts as open. Nothing above the audit moves — a remediation recomputes no agency field, no `oversight_posture` and no `Agency.version`, and it restricts no escrow account.

Disposition is the reviewer's act, available to either an `EscrowAuditor` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, and it has two outcomes with a shared `disposition_note` that is optional and overwrites the stored note only when it is actually supplied, an omitted note leaving whatever was there before; a `cleared_amount_cents` sent on a disposition is ignored. Neither outcome tests the parent audit's `status`, so a finding that an adverse closure left standing in `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` may still be cleared or escalated after that audit has closed. Clearing moves the finding from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` to `CLEARED`, then recomputes the parent audit's `open_finding_count`, `critical_finding_count` and `risk_tier`, then the agency's `open_critical_finding_count`, then the agency's `oversight_posture`. Escalating moves the finding to `ESCALATED` and is reachable from two origins with identical handling — from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` when the agency's answer was inadequate, and directly from `OPEN` when the auditor skips remediation entirely. Either way the parent audit's `open_finding_count`, `critical_finding_count` and `risk_tier` are recomputed, the agency's `open_critical_finding_count` is recomputed, and because that count changed the agency's `oversight_posture` is recomputed as well; escrow accounts of the agency whose `status` is `OPEN` move to `RESTRICTED` only in the case where the recomputation carries the posture into `FROZEN` from some other value — not merely onto `RESTRICTED`, and not where the agency was already `FROZEN` before the disposition, whose `OPEN` accounts are therefore left exactly as they were. `CLEARED` and `ESCALATED` are the two terminal values of the finding dimension. Findings may be listed for an audit and filtered by `code`, `severity` and `status`, and that list is the only discovery channel for auto-raised findings.

The agency's `defalcation_score` is a weighted count, capped, of the things that historically precede a defalcation. Four in-scope counts feed it: the number of findings whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`, the number whose `severity` is `HIGH`, the number of audits whose `status` is `CLOSED_ADVERSE`, and the number of claims whose `cause_code` is `ESCROW_DEFALCATION` and whose `status` is anything other than `INVESTIGATING`. The raw score is twenty-five points for each critical finding, twelve for each high finding, eight for each adverse closure and fifteen for each defalcation claim, all summed; `defalcation_score` is that raw total capped at 100. From the score, `escrow_standing` follows directly: `DEFALCATION_RISK` when the score is at least 60, otherwise `WATCH` when it is at least 30, and otherwise `COMPLIANT`.

What counts as "in scope" depends on which trigger fired the recomputation, and the two variants disagree on purpose. When an escrow audit closure recomputes the score — either `CLOSED_CLEAN` or `CLOSED_ADVERSE` — the rolling-window variant applies: in scope are only rows dated on or after the logical clock's current date minus 365 days, where a finding is dated by its parent audit's `as_of_date`, an audit by its own `as_of_date`, and a claim by its `notice_date`. Under this variant a finding's `status` is ignored entirely, so a finding that has already been cleared still scores as long as it falls inside the window. When an actor-initiated audit creation recomputes the score — of either `audit_type` — the lifetime variant applies instead: there is no date window at all and the agency's entire history is in scope, but findings whose `status` is `CLEARED` are excluded. An old uncleared critical finding therefore scores under the lifetime variant and not under the rolling window, while a recently cleared finding scores under the rolling window and not under the lifetime variant. The successor audit that the freeze cascade creates automatically does *not* recompute the score at all. To make the divergence concrete: an agency with no critical findings, one high finding, one adverse closure and no defalcation claims in the rolling window scores twelve plus eight, or 20, and stands `COMPLIANT`; the same agency assessed under the lifetime variant, where one critical finding, one high finding and two adverse closures are in scope, scores twenty-five plus twelve plus sixteen, or 53, and stands `WATCH`.

An adverse audit closure fires CASCADE-A, the freeze cascade, in five hops. Hop 1 sets the audit's `status` to `CLOSED_ADVERSE`, records `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_at`, and recomputes the agency's `defalcation_score` under the rolling-window variant. Hop 2 fires because that recomputed score reached at least 60: the agency's `escrow_standing` becomes `DEFALCATION_RISK`. Hop 3 fires because the standing became `DEFALCATION_RISK`, which is one of the two freeze conditions: `oversight_posture` becomes `FROZEN`. Hop 4 fires because the posture became `FROZEN`: every escrow account of the agency whose `status` is `OPEN` moves to `RESTRICTED`, and every closing protection letter of the agency whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `REFERRED` moves to `DECLINED`, both sets processed in ascending `created_at` order and the letters carrying `SYSTEM` as their `decided_by_user_id`. Hop 5 also fires because the posture became `FROZEN`: a successor audit is created automatically against the same `escrow_account_id`, with `audit_type` `SURPRISE`, `status` `DRAFT`, an `as_of_date` equal to the logical clock's current date, a `follow_up_of_audit_id` pointing at the audit just closed, and `SYSTEM` as its `conducted_by_user_id`. That system-created audit does not itself recompute the score. Hops 4 and 5 are conditioned on the recomputed posture *being* `FROZEN` and not on its having only just arrived there, so an agency that was already frozen before the closure landed still has its remaining `OPEN` accounts restricted, its letters in flight declined and a successor audit created. Conversely, when the rolling-window rescore leaves the score below 60 and no other freeze condition holds — a suspended or terminated appointment being the only other one — the posture is not `FROZEN`, hops 2 through 5 do not run at all, and the adverse closure moves nothing beyond the audit row and the agency's ordinary recomputed fields. `Agency.version` is incremented by 1 for the whole request whichever way that goes.

### 3.8. Claims Arising from Agency Conduct: Recourse and Indemnity

When the underwriter pays a loss it traces the loss back to the agency whose conduct produced it. A claim is recorded by a `ComplianceOfficer` or an `UnderwritingCounsel` and carries the `agency_id` whose conduct is at issue, the `policy_file_id` the loss arose under, optionally the `cpl_id` of the letter the claimant invoked, a `cause_code` of `AGENCY_ERROR`, `SEARCH_DEFECT`, `ESCROW_DEFALCATION` or `UNDERWRITING_BREACH`, the gross `loss_amount_cents` paid or reserved — which must be strictly greater than zero — and the `notice_date` the claim came in. One referential rule ties the three together: the referenced policy's `agency_id` must equal the claim's `agency_id`, that policy's `issuance_status` must be `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED`, and when a `cpl_id` is supplied it must equal that policy's own `cpl_id`. Any of the three failing is `ERR_CLAIM_POLICY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — another agency's file, an unissued file, or a letter that does not belong to the file. The `notice_date` is a bare calendar date and is never compared with anything — not with the policy's `closing_date`, not with the statement periods, and not with the logical clock, so a notice dated years before the closing or well into the future is recorded as given. The claim opens in `INVESTIGATING` with a null `recourse_tier`, a null `decided_by_user_id`, and `indemnity_offset_cents`, `recourse_amount_cents`, `net_underwriter_exposure_cents` and `recovered_amount_cents` all 0. Recording a claim moves nothing on the agency: a claim still sitting in `INVESTIGATING` is outside the defalcation score's claim count, so no rescore is performed, no derived agency field is refreshed, and neither `Agency.version` nor the agency's `updated_at` advances.

Adjudication out of `INVESTIGATING` belongs to the `UnderwritingCounsel` alone, and it is barred to the individual counsel who cleared the referral on that very file: SoD-2 requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the linked policy's `referral_decided_by_user_id`, and both adjudication branches require the agency to carry errors-and-omissions coverage with capacity in it, refusing with `ERR_EO_COVERAGE_LAPSED` when `eo_policy_limit_cents` is not strictly greater than zero. That single test on the effective limit is the whole of the check: unlike the coverage gates of Sections 3.3 and 3.4, this one never consults `eo_expires_on`, so an agency whose policy has expired but whose limit is positive may still be adjudicated against, and the check exists here chiefly because the waterfall divides by that limit. It is evaluated after the state guard, so an adjudication attempted out of an already-terminal claim is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` even where the coverage is also zero.

Demanding recourse — `INVESTIGATING` to `RECOURSE_DEMANDED` — runs a four-step waterfall in which every step consumes the output of the one before it. **Step 1 pins the tier from the gross loss.** The gross ratio is `loss_amount_cents` divided by the agency's `eo_policy_limit_cents`, evaluated as an exact rational; `recourse_tier` is `TIER_1` when that ratio is at most 0.25, `TIER_2` when it is above 0.25 but at most 0.60, and `TIER_3` otherwise. **Step 2 derives the indemnity from the pinned tier.** The agency's participation share is one half for `TIER_1`, three quarters for `TIER_2` and the whole loss for `TIER_3`; `indemnity_offset_cents` is the gross loss multiplied by that participation, rounded half up to a whole cent, then capped at the agency's `eo_policy_limit_cents` — the E&O policy cannot pay more than its own limit. **Step 3 scales the demand by culpability.** The cause factor is six tenths for `AGENCY_ERROR`, four tenths for `SEARCH_DEFECT`, eight tenths for `UNDERWRITING_BREACH` and the whole amount for `ESCROW_DEFALCATION`; `recourse_amount_cents` is `indemnity_offset_cents` multiplied by that factor, rounded half up to a whole cent independently of the rounding in step 2. **Step 4 states the exposure.** `net_underwriter_exposure_cents` is `loss_amount_cents` minus `indemnity_offset_cents`. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded in `decided_by_user_id`.

The pinning in step 1 is deliberate and must not be revisited once the later steps have run. With an `eo_policy_limit_cents` of 100,000,000, a `loss_amount_cents` of 40,000,000 and a `cause_code` of `AGENCY_ERROR`, the gross ratio is 0.40, which pins `TIER_2`; the indemnity is three quarters of 40,000,000, or 30,000,000; the recourse is six tenths of that, or 18,000,000; and the exposure is 40,000,000 minus 30,000,000, or 10,000,000. Re-deriving the tier from that 10,000,000 exposure would give a ratio of 0.10 and hence `TIER_1`, producing an indemnity of 20,000,000, a recourse of 12,000,000 and an exposure of 20,000,000 — all three wrong. The tier boundary itself is inclusive at the top. With the same 100,000,000 limit and a `cause_code` of `SEARCH_DEFECT`, a loss of 25,000,000 gives a ratio of exactly 0.25, which is `TIER_1`: the indemnity is 12,500,000, the recourse is four tenths of that or 5,000,000, and the exposure is 12,500,000. A loss of 25,000,001 puts the ratio just over 0.25, which is `TIER_2`: three quarters of 25,000,001 is 18,750,000.75, which rounds half up to 18,750,001; four tenths of that is 7,500,000.4, which rounds to 7,500,000; and the exposure is 25,000,001 minus 18,750,001, or 6,250,000.

A recourse demand on a defalcation carries a punitive tail. When the claim's `cause_code` is `ESCROW_DEFALCATION`, the demand additionally drives the agency's `escrow_standing` to `DEFALCATION_RISK`; because the standing changed, `oversight_posture` becomes `FROZEN`; and because the posture is now `FROZEN`, every escrow account of the agency whose `status` is `OPEN` moves to `RESTRICTED` and every closing protection letter of the agency whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `REFERRED` moves to `DECLINED`, both sets processed in ascending `created_at` order and the letters carrying `SYSTEM` as their `decided_by_user_id` exactly as in the two cascades. Those two side effects are conditioned on the posture *being* `FROZEN` rather than on entry into it, so an agency already frozen when the demand lands still has its remaining `OPEN` accounts restricted and its letters in flight declined. The `defalcation_score` itself is deliberately *not* recomputed here: the standing is set directly, so an agency may sit at `DEFALCATION_RISK` above a score still below 60 until the next audit creation or audit closure rescores it and re-derives the standing from that score — which may then drop the standing back to `WATCH` or `COMPLIANT` and release the posture. `Agency.version` is incremented by 1. A claim on any of the other three causes leaves the standing, the score, the posture, the accounts and the letters untouched, and because no agency field moves it advances no `Agency.version` either.

Absorbing the loss — `INVESTIGATING` to `ABSORBED` — is the divergent sibling: the underwriter takes the loss and demands nothing. `indemnity_offset_cents` is set to 0 and `recourse_amount_cents` is set to 0, `net_underwriter_exposure_cents` is the full `loss_amount_cents`, and `decided_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. `recourse_tier` is nonetheless computed and stored exactly as step 1 describes, so the file records what the underwriter could have demanded. Absorption changes no `escrow_standing`, no `defalcation_score`, no `oversight_posture`, no account and no letter, whatever the `cause_code` may be — an absorbed defalcation claim carries no punitive tail — and it forces no `Agency.version` bump.

Resolving a demand belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer` and has two outcomes. Recovery — `RECOURSE_DEMANDED` to `RECOVERED` — requires `recovered_amount_cents` in the body as a non-negative integer, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that figure may not exceed the claim's `recourse_amount_cents`, else `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_RECOURSE` (HTTP 422); a recovery equal to the full demand is accepted, and so is a recovery of 0. The amount is persisted and `net_underwriter_exposure_cents` is recomputed as `loss_amount_cents` minus `indemnity_offset_cents` minus `recovered_amount_cents`. Write-off — `RECOURSE_DEMANDED` to `WRITTEN_OFF` — has no preconditions at all; `recovered_amount_cents` stays 0 and `net_underwriter_exposure_cents` is recomputed as `loss_amount_cents` minus `indemnity_offset_cents`, which leaves it at the value the demand already produced. Neither resolution rewrites `decided_by_user_id`, which stands at the counsel who made the demand rather than at the officer who resolved it, and neither forces an `Agency.version` bump. `ABSORBED`, `RECOVERED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are the three terminal values of the claim dimension. Any of the four transitions on the state endpoint may carry an optional `note`; it must be a string when supplied, but it is not persisted on the claim row. Claims may be listed and filtered by `agency_id`, `policy_file_id`, `cpl_id`, `status` and `cause_code`.

### 3.9. Termination and the Transfer of In-Force Files

Termination is the end of the relationship and it is CASCADE-B, the widest cascade in the system. It is reachable from `APPOINTED` and from `SUSPENDED`, both running an identical side-effect chain, and it is reserved to the `ComplianceOfficer`: a `LicensingAnalyst` attempting it is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. A `termination_reason` of `FOR_CAUSE`, `VOLUNTARY` or `NON_RENEWAL` must be present in the body, else `ERR_TERMINATION_REASON_REQUIRED` (HTTP 400). A `transferee_agency_id` is optional, but when one is supplied it must resolve to an existing agency — a missing reference is `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND` — that agency's `appointment_status` must be `APPOINTED`, its `oversight_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, and it must not be the terminated agency itself, any of which failing is `ERR_TRANSFEREE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). Books of business cannot be handed to a suspended, terminated, frozen or self-referencing recipient.

The cascade then runs in five hops plus two further consequences of the first. Hop 1 sets `appointment_status` to `TERMINATED` and persists `termination_reason` and `transferee_agency_id`. Hop 2 fires because the appointment status became `TERMINATED`, which is one of the two freeze conditions: `oversight_posture` becomes `FROZEN`. Hop 3 also fires from hop 1: every appointment of the agency whose `status` is `PENDING_LICENSE_VERIFICATION`, `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Hop 4 fires because those appointments were withdrawn: every closing protection letter of the agency whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `REFERRED` and whose `state_program_id` matches a withdrawn appointment's `state_program_id` moves to `DECLINED` with `decided_by_user_id` set to `SYSTEM`, again in ascending `created_at` order. Hop 5 fires because those letters were declined: every policy file whose `cpl_id` is one of them and whose `issuance_status` is `PENDING` or `PENDING_REFERRAL` moves to `VOIDED`. Also caused by hop 1, and independently of the letter chain: every policy file of the agency whose `issuance_status` is `ISSUED` or `LATE_ISSUED` and whose `transfer_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` moves to `PENDING_TRANSFER` with `transferee_agency_id` copied from the agency's `transferee_agency_id`, after which the agency's `pending_transfer_count` is recomputed. Also caused by hop 1 through the posture change: every escrow account of the agency whose `status` is `OPEN` moves to `CLOSED`, which is terminal — a terminated agency's trust accounts do not come back with a later reinstatement, because there is no reinstatement out of `TERMINATED`.

In-force files therefore leave the terminated agency in `PENDING_TRANSFER`, and moving them the rest of the way is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s work. Completing a transfer — `PENDING_TRANSFER` to `TRANSFERRED` — requires the file's own `transferee_agency_id` to be non-null and that transferee agency to have an `appointment_status` of `APPOINTED` and an `oversight_posture` other than `FROZEN`, either failing being `ERR_TRANSFEREE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; the eligibility is therefore re-tested at the moment of transfer, not merely at termination, so a transferee that has itself been frozen in the interim blocks the move. On success the terminated agency's `pending_transfer_count` is recomputed. Parking a file — `PENDING_TRANSFER` to `TRANSFER_EXCEPTION` — has no preconditions whatsoever, accepts an optional `note` that must be a string when supplied but is not persisted on the row, and leaves `pending_transfer_count` unchanged, because an exception is still a file awaiting transfer and so remains inside the count. Retrying from there — `TRANSFER_EXCEPTION` to `TRANSFERRED` — carries exactly the same preconditions as the direct completion and likewise recomputes `pending_transfer_count`. The count is at all times the number of the agency's policy files whose `transfer_status` is `PENDING_TRANSFER` or `TRANSFER_EXCEPTION`. `TRANSFERRED` is the single terminal value of the transfer dimension, and the exception-retry loop may run as many times as it takes to find an eligible recipient. Files in each transfer state are discoverable by filtering the policy file list on `transfer_status`, and the agency list can be filtered by `transferee_agency_id` to find every agency designated to receive a terminated agency's book.
