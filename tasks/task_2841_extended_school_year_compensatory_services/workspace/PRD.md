# Extended School Year & Compensatory Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every spring, district special-education teams face the same two questions in quick succession. First: which students will lose so much skill over the summer break that a shortened summer session is legally required rather than merely nice to have? Second: when the district itself fails to deliver a service it promised — a bus that never arrived, a therapist who was absent, a day the buildings closed for heat — how many minutes does the district now owe the child, and how will those minutes actually be delivered before the obligation expires? Today both questions are answered in spreadsheets that nobody can reconcile, and the answers surface for the first time in a due-process hearing, where the district cannot show its arithmetic.

This document specifies the Extended School Year & Compensatory Services API, mounted under `/esy`. The service is, at its heart, a **minute ledger with a negotiation layer bolted on top**. Minutes are promised by an IEP, prorated onto a shortened summer calendar, delivered (or missed) day by day, converted into a compensatory-education debt when the district is at fault, negotiated with the parent until both sides agree on a number, and finally paid down through makeup sessions that draw real dollars from real federal and local budget codes. Every one of those steps is a place where a number can silently drift, so the service treats derived minute totals and derived dollar totals as **recomputed aggregates**, never as incrementally patched counters.

Three constraints shape almost every rule that follows. The service is a system of record for a legal process, so its arithmetic must be reproducible from stored inputs alone — hence pinned minute plans, pinned probe arrays, and an explicit evaluation order for every formula. It is a fiscal system subject to federal supplement-not-supplant restrictions, so charges walk a fixed funding waterfall and a compensatory charge against a federal grant is capped. And it is a system operating under procedural-safeguard law, so an open complaint freezes service reductions and dispute arbitration outright, and no single employee may both produce and approve the same determination or the same award proposal.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Acting Users and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — including reads and including the test-driving clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). No endpoint is anonymous.

Six roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `CaseManager`, `ProgramCoordinator`, `SpedDirector`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RelatedServiceProvider`, and `ParentGuardian`. If `X-User-Role` holds any string outside this set of six, or holds a valid role that is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being addressed, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The authoritative role-to-endpoint mapping is the table in §4.

The four staff roles carry **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `CaseManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `analyzed_by_user_id` on an eligibility determination, or as `authored_by_user_id` on a compensatory award) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `ProgramCoordinator`, for `ComplianceOfficer`, and for `SpedDirector` — whose identifier is recorded as `finalized_by_user_id` when a determination is finalized and as `resolved_by_user_id` when a disputed award is arbitrated.

Two roles are entity-backed, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `RelatedServiceProvider`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare that resource's `provider_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ParentGuardian`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Student` compare that resource's `student_id` to `X-User-ID`.

The guardian ownership check guards exactly six operations: reading one student, listing that student's eligibility determinations, reading one eligibility determination, submitting a parent response to a determination, reading one compensatory award, and submitting a parent response to an award. In all six, a `ParentGuardian` whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the addressed record's `student_id` (or, for the student read, the addressed `Student`'s own `id`) is rejected with `ERR_NOT_STUDENT_GUARDIAN` (HTTP 403). The provider ownership check guards exactly two operations: logging the outcome of a makeup session, and creating a service log. In both, a `RelatedServiceProvider` whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the `provider_id` of the addressed makeup session, or of the assignment covering the service log's commitment, is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROVIDER` (HTTP 403).

Separately from role membership, two approval operations enforce **segregation of duties**. The `SpedDirector` who finalizes an eligibility determination must not be the user recorded in `analyzed_by_user_id`, and the `SpedDirector` who resolves a compensatory dispute must not be the user recorded in `authored_by_user_id`. Either collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

For paginated responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for that request, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` — true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific list states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four lists deviate because their domain has a more useful natural order: service logs are ordered by `service_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; transportation runs are ordered by `service_date` ascending, then `window_start_time` ascending, then `created_at` ascending; makeup sessions are ordered by `session_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; and a program's fund allocations are returned in funding-priority order — the two federal grants first in the order `IDEA_611_FEDERAL` then `IDEA_619_FEDERAL`, then `LOCAL_GENERAL` — with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break inside one funding stream, which is deliberately the same order the charge waterfall of §2.9 walks.

Where a list endpoint offers filters, the filters combine conjunctively: a request supplying both `program_id` and `status` returns only records matching both. The filterable lists are the service-commitment list (`program_id`, `student_id`, `transportation_run_id`, `status`), the service-log list (`commitment_id`, `provider_id`, `service_date`, `outcome`), the compensatory-award list (`student_id`, `source_program_id`, `negotiation_status`), and the makeup-session list (`award_id`, `provider_id`, `supersedes_session_id`, `status`).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the Version Counter

Every resource this service persists carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID; it carries no type prefix and clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant the resource was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the resource's most recent mutation, in the same format; at creation it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly or produced as a side effect of an operation against some other resource. There are no exceptions in this domain: all twelve resource types — `Student`, `EligibilityDetermination`, `EsyProgram`, `Provider`, `ServiceCommitment`, `ProviderAssignment`, `TransportationRun`, `ServiceLog`, `CompensatoryAward`, `MakeupSession`, `FundAllocation`, and `DueProcessRecord` — carry all four properties.

The `version` counter is not decoration. It is the observable evidence that a cascade actually fired, and it is the value the stale-version checks of §2.8 compare against. Whenever a rule in §3 says that some secondary resource's derived totals were recomputed, that resource's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed at the same time.

Newly created resources are returned with HTTP 201. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every state-transition operation and every list or single-resource read, return HTTP 200. In both cases the response body's `data` field holds the affected resource in the shape described above, unless the endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Deadlines, expiries and lapse evaluations in this domain are numerous, so the service does not read wall-clock time. Instead it maintains a **logical clock** whose value before any client sets it is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. The server must never fall back to real system time, not at startup and not between requests.

An administrative test-driving capability, available to all six roles, sets the logical clock to an explicit ISO 8601 instant supplied as `current_utc`. Setting the clock has no immediate effect on stored records; instead it changes the answer every subsequent deadline comparison gives. In particular, the compensatory-award delivery lapse of §3.8 is evaluated lazily — the transition is applied at the start of any request whose path or body references the award, which includes simply reading that award.

Where a rule compares a stored calendar date (`session_date`, `delivery_deadline_date`, `credential_expires_on`, `service_date`) against "today", the comparison uses the calendar date portion of the logical clock in UTC. Where a rule stores an instant (`resolved_at`), it stores the full logical-clock instant in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits.

### 2.6. Path Resolution for Nested Reads

Three read families are nested: reads scoped to one student (that student's eligibility determinations, that student's due-process records), reads scoped to one program (its fund allocations, its transportation runs), and reads scoped to one provider (that provider's assignments). One further nested read hangs off a commitment (its provider assignments) and one off an award (its documentation packet). These nestings are reverse-reachability conveniences, not ownership trees: every write endpoint addresses its own collection directly and carries its parents as identifier fields in the request body.

Path parameters are validated **left to right**, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error. A request naming a student that does not exist fails with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND` before anything else in the path or body is examined. The remaining path-level not-found codes are `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND` for `determination_id`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` for `program_id`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND` for `provider_id`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `commitment_id`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `assignment_id`, `ERR_SERVICE_LOG_NOT_FOUND` for `log_id`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND` for `award_id`, `ERR_MAKEUP_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` for `session_id`, `ERR_FUND_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` for `allocation_id`, and `ERR_DUE_PROCESS_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` for `record_id`. All ten answer HTTP 404.

Identifiers supplied in a request **body** are resolved the same way and with the same codes, once the path has resolved cleanly. A body carrying `student_id`, `program_id`, `source_program_id`, `provider_id`, `commitment_id`, `award_id`, `transportation_run_id` or `supersedes_session_id` that points at nothing yields the matching not-found code for the referenced kind of record — `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` covers both `program_id` and `source_program_id`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND` covers `transportation_run_id`, and `ERR_MAKEUP_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` covers `supersedes_session_id`.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence: The Order in Which Checks Run

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates more than one rule, and a due-process file is only defensible if the district's system always reports the *same* violation for the same facts. The service therefore evaluates its checks in one fixed global order and returns the first violation it finds. The order below is normative; a check numbered lower always masks a check numbered higher.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the six declared roles and that role is authorized for the addressed endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. A `ParentGuardian` caller owns the addressed record — otherwise `ERR_NOT_STUDENT_GUARDIAN`. Applies to the six guardian-visible operations enumerated in §2.1.
4. A `RelatedServiceProvider` caller is the provider on the addressed record — otherwise `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROVIDER`. Applies to makeup-session outcome logging and to service-log creation.
5. Every identifier in the request path resolves, checked left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code from §2.6. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
6. Every identifier in the request body resolves — otherwise the matching not-found code. Applies to every write endpoint carrying an identifier in its body.
7. The request body is structurally sound: all required inputs present, every enum value inside its declared set, integers non-negative, dates written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, times written as `HH:MM` on a 24-hour clock, any end value strictly after its matching start value, and delivered minutes never exceeding the scheduled or planned minutes they settle — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every write endpoint.
8. `limit` and `offset` are within their declared ranges — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Applies to every list endpoint.
9. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists, including the cross-entity state guards — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Applies to every state-transition endpoint and every child-creation endpoint. The cross-entity guards folded into this check are: a commitment may only be activated while its program is `OPEN`; a makeup session may only be booked against an award whose negotiation dimension reads `AGREED` and whose delivery dimension is not `LAPSED`; and an allocation may only be closed while its encumbered balance is zero.
10. Where the endpoint takes a parent-version field, that field equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_AWARD_VERSION` as §2.8 describes. Applies to provider-assignment creation and makeup-session creation.
11. The student is not under stay-put protection — otherwise `ERR_STAY_PUT_BLOCKED`. Applies to exactly three operations: suspending a service commitment, terminating a service commitment, and arbitrating a disputed or mediated compensatory award.
12. The provider's `credential_expires_on` falls on or after the last date the work covers — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`. Applies to provider-assignment creation (where the last covered date is `effective_to`) and to makeup-session creation (where it is `session_date`).
13. The provider's `status` reads `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Applies to provider-assignment creation and makeup-session creation.
14. The provider's `role_type` is permitted for the commitment's `service_type` — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_ROLE_MISMATCH`. Applies to provider-assignment creation only.
15. The approving user differs from the user who produced the artifact — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to determination finalization and to award dispute resolution.
16. No `ACTIVE` assignment of the same provider collides with the requested weekly window — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_TIME_CONFLICT`. Applies to provider-assignment creation only.
17. No `SCHEDULED` run of the same `route_code` on the same `service_date` collides with the requested window — otherwise `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Applies to transportation-run creation only.
18. Consecutive non-overlapping runs of the same route on the same date leave the required turnaround gap — otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_TURNAROUND_VIOLATION`. Applies to transportation-run creation only.
19. The makeup session's `session_date` lies inside the award's delivery window — otherwise `ERR_MAKEUP_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. Applies to makeup-session creation only.
20. The supplied `service_date` is an instructional date of the program — otherwise `ERR_NOT_INSTRUCTIONAL_DATE`. Applies to three operations: transportation-run creation, service-log creation, and day cancellation.
21. The commitment's student is ESY-eligible — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ESY_ELIGIBLE`. Applies to service-commitment creation only.
22. The program calendar yields at least one instructional day — otherwise `ERR_NO_INSTRUCTIONAL_DAYS`. Applies to service-commitment creation only.
23. The plan's heaviest single-week daily load fits under the program's cap — otherwise `ERR_DAILY_MINUTES_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Applies to service-commitment creation only.
24. The determination carries enough probe domains to analyse — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PROBE_DATA`. Applies to determination analysis only.
25. The computed award reaches the minimum awardable size — otherwise `ERR_NO_COMPENSABLE_MINUTES`. Applies to compensatory-award creation only.
26. The offered or arbitrated minute figure lies inside the permitted proposal band — otherwise `ERR_PROPOSAL_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Applies to award proposal and to award dispute resolution.
27. Booking the new session would not overbook the award — otherwise `ERR_OVERBOOKED_MAKEUP_MINUTES`. Applies to makeup-session creation only.
28. No `VERIFIED` service log already covers the same commitment and the same `service_date` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERVICE_LOG`. Applies to service-log creation only.
29. The `program_id` and `budget_code` pair is unique — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUDGET_CODE`. Applies to fund-allocation creation only.
30. No non-terminal award already exists for the same student and source program — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_AWARD`. Applies to compensatory-award creation only.
31. The provider's aggregate weekly load stays within contract — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Applies to provider-assignment creation only.
32. The referenced transportation run has a free seat — otherwise `ERR_RUN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Applies to service-commitment creation only.
33. At least one fund allocation of the program is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_FUND_FROZEN`. Applies to provider-assignment creation and to makeup-session outcome logging.
34. Some `OPEN` allocation, taken in priority order, can absorb the base cost — otherwise `ERR_NO_FUND_WITH_CAPACITY`. Applies to provider-assignment creation and to makeup-session outcome logging.
35. The allocation selected on base cost can also absorb the indirect cost — otherwise `ERR_FUND_INDIRECT_OVERAGE`. Applies to provider-assignment creation and to makeup-session outcome logging.
36. A compensatory charge against a federal grant stays under the federal compensatory cap — otherwise `ERR_SUPPLANT_RESTRICTION`. Applies to makeup-session outcome logging only.

Three collisions are worth stating outright because they are easy to get backwards. A release request aimed at an assignment that is already `RELEASED`, whose program's only allocation happens to be `FROZEN`, violates both the state check and the fund-frozen check; the state check is earlier, so `ERR_INVALID_STATE` is returned. An expired speech-language pathologist assigned to a `SPECIALIZED_INSTRUCTION` commitment violates both the credential check and the role-match check; the credential check is earlier, so `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED` is returned. And a makeup session booked both past the award's delivery deadline and for more minutes than remain owed violates both the deadline check and the overbooking check; the deadline check is earlier, so `ERR_MAKEUP_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` is returned.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Staffing and Makeup Writes

Two write operations in this domain can legitimately be repeated, and that is exactly what makes them dangerous. Both therefore require the caller to prove its view of the parent record is current, by echoing the parent's `version` back to the server.

Creating a provider assignment — `POST /esy/provider-assignments` — requires the non-optional field `expected_commitment_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ServiceCommitment`. If that value does not match the commitment's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a second assignment on the same service line legitimately supersedes the incumbent one and silently releases the incumbent's fund encumbrance; before the server does that, the writer must demonstrate that its picture of the commitment — its staffing roster, its `PLANNED` or `ASSIGNED` status, its pinned minute plan — reflects the commitment as it stands and not as it stood before some other writer moved it.

Creating a makeup session — `POST /esy/makeup-sessions` — requires the non-optional field `expected_award_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `CompensatoryAward`. A mismatch against the award's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AWARD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Nothing in the award's state machine stops a scheduler from booking additional minutes, so this check is the only thing preventing a scheduler from booking against an obsolete `remaining_owed_minutes`, an obsolete `delivery_deadline_date`, or an `agreed_minutes` figure a director has since revised.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a parent-version field, and none may be made to require one. Where an operation mutates a resource without a version echo, the last writer's change simply stands.

### 2.9. Money in Cents and the Fund Charge Waterfall

All money in this service is held as an integer number of cents; there are no fractional currency amounts anywhere in a request or a response. A provider's `rate_cents_per_minute` is a fully loaded cost per service minute in cents, and every derived amount — `base_cost_cents`, `indirect_cost_cents`, `total_charge_cents`, `charge_cents`, `authorized_amount_cents`, `encumbered_amount_cents`, `expended_amount_cents`, `compensatory_expended_cents`, `available_cents` — is likewise an integer count of cents.

Two operations spend money: creating a provider assignment (which **encumbers** a budget code) and logging a makeup session as delivered (which **expends** one). Both price the work through the identical five-step waterfall, and the five steps run in exactly this order with no step ever revisited.

**Step one — direct cost.** The chargeable minute count depends on which operation is charging. For a provider assignment it is `covered_total_minutes`, derived from the requested `covered_weekly_minutes` exactly as §3.5 sets out. For a makeup session it is the `minutes_delivered` credited by the outcome log. `base_cost_cents` is the provider's `rate_cents_per_minute` multiplied by that chargeable minute count.

**Step two — bucket selection, on base cost only.** The candidate set is every `FundAllocation` belonging to the charged program whose `status` reads `OPEN`; `FROZEN` and `CLOSED` allocations never enter the candidate set. Candidates are ordered by funding priority — `IDEA_611_FEDERAL` first, `IDEA_619_FEDERAL` second, `LOCAL_GENERAL` third — with `created_at` ascending as the only tie-break among allocations sharing a `fund_source`. An allocation's `id` is never a decision input. The selected allocation is the **first** candidate in that order whose `available_cents` is greater than or equal to `base_cost_cents`. If the program has fund allocations but not one of them is `OPEN`, the request fails with `ERR_FUND_FROZEN`; if `OPEN` allocations exist but none of them has enough `available_cents` to cover `base_cost_cents`, the request fails with `ERR_NO_FUND_WITH_CAPACITY`.

**Step three — indirect cost, at the selected source's rate.** `indirect_cost_cents` is `base_cost_cents` multiplied by the indirect-cost rate of the *selected* allocation's `fund_source`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Both federal streams carry an eight-percent indirect rate; `LOCAL_GENERAL` carries fourteen and a half percent. The rate is applied to `base_cost_cents` only and is never compounded onto a previously computed indirect amount. `total_charge_cents` is `base_cost_cents` plus `indirect_cost_cents`.

**Step four — overage check, with no re-selection.** If `total_charge_cents` is greater than the selected allocation's `available_cents`, the request fails with `ERR_FUND_INDIRECT_OVERAGE`. Step two is not re-run: the request fails even in the case where a lower-priority `OPEN` allocation could comfortably have absorbed the whole of `total_charge_cents`. Selection is base-only and final, and this is deliberate — the district's chart of accounts, not the arithmetic, decides which grant pays.

**Step five — posting.** A provider assignment adds `total_charge_cents` to the selected allocation's `encumbered_amount_cents` and records the selection on the assignment as `charged_fund_source` and `charged_budget_code`. A delivered makeup session adds `total_charge_cents` to both `expended_amount_cents` and `compensatory_expended_cents`, and records the selection on the session as `charged_fund_source` and `charged_budget_code`, with the session's own `charge_cents` holding that same total. For makeup sessions **only**, one further test runs before the posting: when the selected allocation's `fund_source` is either `IDEA_611_FEDERAL` or `IDEA_619_FEDERAL`, and the allocation's existing `compensatory_expended_cents` plus `total_charge_cents` would exceed one quarter of its `authorized_amount_cents` (that ceiling being the authorized amount multiplied by twenty-five hundredths, rounded half up to a whole cent), the request is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLANT_RESTRICTION`. Federal special-education money must supplement local effort rather than replace it, and an uncapped compensatory draw is the classic way that principle gets violated.

At all times, and after every posting or reversal, an allocation's `available_cents` equals its `authorized_amount_cents` less its `encumbered_amount_cents` less its `expended_amount_cents`. That identity is the invariant the whole fiscal layer conserves: whenever an encumbrance is released or an expenditure voided, exactly the amount originally posted comes back, so `available_cents` returns to the value it held before.

### 2.10. Minute Arithmetic and Rounding Discipline

Service minutes are integers everywhere they are stored. Wherever a division produces a fraction, the result is rounded **half up** — a remainder of exactly one half rounds away from zero, to the larger whole number — and the rounded value, never the raw quotient, is what subsequent steps consume. Three places deviate from plain integer half-up rounding and each is called out where it lives: a per-probe regression percentage is carried to one decimal place (also half up), a projected recoupment day count is rounded **up** to the next whole day, and a severity-scaled award is rounded to the nearest fifteen-minute unit. Seat counts, probe counts, round counts and log counts are plain integers with no rounding involved.

Two rounding subtleties are load-bearing and are stated here so that no implementation reaches them by accident. First, when the summer minute plan prorates weekly minutes across partial calendar weeks, each week's figure is rounded to a whole minute **before** the weeks are summed; rounding the exact per-week quotients only once at the end is a different and incorrect answer. Second, every comparison in the regression analysis is made against the rounded one-decimal-place percentage, not against the unrounded quotient that produced it, so a raw value of 24.96 percent is compared as 25.0 and therefore clears an at-least-25.0 threshold.

### 2.11. Ledger Recomputation and Cascade Discipline

Nine fields in this domain are derived minute or money totals rather than stored inputs, and the service maintains them by **full recomputation over the current row set**, never by applying an incremental delta. Whenever a service log is created or voided, the affected commitment's `delivered_minutes` and `compensable_owed_minutes` are recomputed from scratch across that commitment's `VERIFIED` logs. Whenever a commitment ledger moves, or an award's negotiated or delivered figures move, or a due-process record changes status, the affected student's `pending_award_minutes`, `outstanding_owed_minutes`, `delivered_makeup_minutes_total`, `esy_service_alert` and `stay_put_active` are recomputed from scratch. Whenever a makeup session is created, superseded, or has its outcome logged, the award's `delivered_minutes`, `scheduled_minutes` and `remaining_owed_minutes` are recomputed from scratch. This is why voiding a log or a delivered session is a clean reversal: the voided row simply leaves the input set and every total returns to the value it held before.

Full recomputation also means that changes propagate **upward** through the chain program → service log → service commitment → student, and every resource the chain touches has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed. Downward cascades exist too — closing a program moves its commitments, releasing their assignments and their encumbrances — and they are equally version-bearing. Whether a cascade fires can be conditional on the touched row's current state; §3 states, for each cascade, exactly which rows participate and which are skipped.

One derived value is deliberately excluded from recomputation. An award's `forfeited_minutes` accumulates from the parent-no-show forfeiture rule and **persists**: it is never rebuilt from the session set, so a later recomputation of the award's delivery ledger leaves it untouched.

Finally, three commitment-level figures are **pinned at creation** and never recomputed: `planned_total_minutes`, `weekly_minutes_committed` and `daily_minutes`. When the program calendar later shrinks — a blackout added, a day cancelled — those three values stand unchanged, and the resulting shortfall surfaces instead as owed minutes on the commitment's `compensable_owed_minutes`. Only the program's own `instructional_days_count` and `instructional_week_count` move when the calendar changes.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Student Records and the Running Minute Ledger

A `CaseManager` registers each student the district might serve over the summer. Registration takes the district's `student_number` as printed on the IEP cover sheet — required, unique across all students, and between 1 and 16 characters — along with the legal `first_name` and `last_name` (each required and between 1 and 40 characters), the `grade_band` that will later pick the summer staffing ratio (one of `PRE_K`, `K_5`, `MS_6_8`, `HS_9_12`), the `primary_disability` naming the IDEA category (one of `AUTISM`, `INTELLECTUAL_DISABILITY`, `SPECIFIC_LEARNING_DISABILITY`, `SPEECH_LANGUAGE_IMPAIRMENT`, `EMOTIONAL_DISTURBANCE`, `MULTIPLE_DISABILITIES`), the `iep_weekly_minutes_total` carrying the school-year IEP's whole weekly special-education minute figure (required, between 1 and 3000), and the `home_school_code` naming the attendance-area school (required, 1 to 12 characters). An optional `transportation_required` flag records whether the IEP marks transportation as a related service; when omitted it is stored as false.

Everything else on a student record is derived. At registration `esy_eligibility_status` reads `NOT_DETERMINED` — the other possible values being `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE` and `PENDING_DATA` — and it is written by exactly one event in the whole system: the finalization of an eligibility determination. Nothing else may touch it. Alongside it, four minute totals and two flags start at zero or false and are maintained by full recomputation as described in §2.11.

`pending_award_minutes` is the total compensable shortfall the district has accumulated but not yet turned into an award: it sums `compensable_owed_minutes` across every service commitment belonging to this student whose `status` is anything other than `TERMINATED`. The moment that total reaches sixty minutes — a full hour of undelivered, district-fault service — the boolean `esy_service_alert` flips to true, flagging the case for compensatory review; below sixty minutes it reads false. `outstanding_owed_minutes` is the debt the district has actually acknowledged: for each compensatory award of this student whose `negotiation_status` is either `AGREED` or `CLOSED`, take the award's `agreed_minutes` less its `delivered_minutes` less its `forfeited_minutes`, treat any negative result as zero, and sum those figures. Awards sitting in `DRAFT`, `PARENT_REVIEW`, `DISPUTED`, `MEDIATION` or `DECLINED` contribute nothing at all. `delivered_makeup_minutes_total` sums `delivered_minutes` across **every** award of this student regardless of negotiation state.

The last derived flag is `stay_put_active`, and it is the one with teeth. It reads true exactly when at least one due-process record naming this student has `status` `OPEN` and a `record_type` of either `COMPLAINT` or `DUE_PROCESS_HEARING`; an `OPEN` record of type `MEDIATION_AGREEMENT` does **not** raise it. §3.9 describes what the flag blocks.

A single student read returns all six derived values alongside the registered inputs, and it is available to every staff role and — restricted to their own child — to a `ParentGuardian`.

### 3.2. ESY Eligibility: Regression, Recoupment, and Parent Agreement

Eligibility for extended-school-year service turns on a question of evidence: does this child lose skills over a break (**regression**) and take unreasonably long to win them back (**recoupment**)? A determination's `status` takes one of five values — `DRAFT`, `PARENT_REVIEW`, `PARENT_AGREED`, `FINALIZED` and `WITHDRAWN` — of which the last two are terminal. A `CaseManager` opens a determination in `DRAFT` for one `student_id` and one `school_year` written as `YYYY-YYYY` (for example `2026-2027`), choosing an `analysis_variant` of either `PROBE_BASED` or `TEAM_JUDGMENT` and attaching the evidence itself as `skill_probes`.

`skill_probes` is an ordered array of between 1 and 8 probe objects, and **array order is the pinned evaluation order** — index zero is evaluated first, and the embedded probes' position in the array, not any timestamp, decides sequence. Each probe carries a `skill_domain` (one of `READING_FLUENCY`, `MATH_COMPUTATION`, `EXPRESSIVE_LANGUAGE`, `SELF_REGULATION`, `ADAPTIVE_SKILLS`), a `pre_break_mastery_pct` and a `post_break_mastery_pct` each between 0 and 100, a `recoupment_days_observed` between 0 and 120, and a `trials_count` between 0 and 50. Every probe's `pre_break_mastery_pct` must be greater than zero — a probe with no pre-break baseline cannot produce a regression percentage — and a request violating that is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Analysing a determination advances it from `DRAFT` to `PARENT_REVIEW` and is available only to a `CaseManager`. The request may optionally supply a replacement `skill_probes` array, which is precisely how the team responds after a parent objection. Two preconditions guard the run: the determination must currently be in `DRAFT`, and it must carry at least two probe entries — fewer than two probe domains is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PROBE_DATA`.

The analysis itself reads only the target determination's own `skill_probes` array. Probes are never borrowed from another determination, not even from another determination of the same student in the same `school_year`. For each probe in array order, the regression percentage is the probe's `pre_break_mastery_pct` less its `post_break_mastery_pct`, multiplied by one hundred, divided by its `pre_break_mastery_pct`, and rounded half up to one decimal place; a probe whose `post_break_mastery_pct` is greater than or equal to its `pre_break_mastery_pct` yields zero point zero. `max_regression_pct` is the largest of those per-probe percentages, likewise carried to one decimal place.

What happens next depends on the variant, and the two variants differ in three ways at once, which is why identical probe data can flip the recommendation.

Under `PROBE_BASED` — the district collected genuine post-break probe data — each probe's recoupment day count *is* its `recoupment_days_observed`, taken as observed. A probe **qualifies** when its regression percentage is at least twenty-five point zero **and** its recoupment day count is at least twenty days; both comparisons are inclusive. Data sufficiency under this variant additionally demands trial evidence: `data_sufficiency` reads `SUFFICIENT` only when the array holds at least two probes **and** every single probe has a `trials_count` of at least three; otherwise it reads `INSUFFICIENT`.

Under `TEAM_JUDGMENT` — no post-break data exists and the IEP team projects recoupment from the magnitude of the regression — each probe's observed `recoupment_days_observed` is discarded entirely, and its recoupment day count is instead **projected** as the probe's regression percentage divided by one point two percentage points per day, rounded **up** to the next whole day. A probe qualifies when its regression percentage is strictly greater than twenty-five point zero **and** its projected recoupment day count is strictly greater than twenty days; both comparisons are exclusive. Data sufficiency under this variant ignores `trials_count` altogether: `data_sufficiency` reads `SUFFICIENT` whenever the array holds at least two probes, and `INSUFFICIENT` otherwise.

Whichever variant ran, `max_recoupment_days` records the largest recoupment day count the variant considered — the largest observed figure under `PROBE_BASED`, the largest projected figure under `TEAM_JUDGMENT` — as a whole number of days, and `criteria_met_count` records how many probe domains satisfied **both** tests under that variant.

The `eligibility_recommendation` then follows a strictly ordered three-way decision. If `data_sufficiency` came out `INSUFFICIENT`, the recommendation is `PENDING_DATA` and nothing further is considered. Otherwise, if `max_regression_pct` is at least forty-five point zero, the recommendation is `ELIGIBLE` — this is the severe-regression override, and it deliberately bypasses the qualifying-domain count, because a child losing nearly half a skill needs no corroborating breadth. Otherwise, if `criteria_met_count` is at least two, the recommendation is `ELIGIBLE`. Failing all three, the recommendation is `INELIGIBLE`. Analysis also writes `analyzed_by_user_id` with the calling `CaseManager`'s `X-User-ID`, and increments the determination's `version`.

The parent now responds, and only a `ParentGuardian` whose `X-User-ID` matches the determination's `student_id` may do so — anyone else draws `ERR_NOT_STUDENT_GUARDIAN`. The response carries a `response` value of `AGREE`, `OBJECT` or `WITHDRAW`, plus an optional `parent_comment` of up to 500 characters recording the family's words; all three branches require the determination to be in `PARENT_REVIEW` and all three write the supplied `parent_comment` and increment `version`. `AGREE` advances `PARENT_REVIEW → PARENT_AGREED`. `WITHDRAW` moves `PARENT_REVIEW → WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and — importantly — leaves the student's `esy_eligibility_status` completely untouched. `OBJECT` sends the determination back with `PARENT_REVIEW → DRAFT`, increases `objection_round_count` by one, and **clears `analyzed_by_user_id` to null**, so that the next analysis run re-establishes authorship from scratch; the team may then re-analyse, optionally after amending `skill_probes`, and push the determination to `PARENT_REVIEW` again. That objection loop may run any number of times, each round adding one to `objection_round_count`.

Finalization is a `SpedDirector` act and completes `PARENT_AGREED → FINALIZED`, which is terminal. It requires the determination to be in `PARENT_AGREED`, and it requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `analyzed_by_user_id`; a director trying to rubber-stamp their own analysis is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the director's identifier is stored as `finalized_by_user_id`, and the cross-entity write that the rest of the system waits for happens: the determination's `eligibility_recommendation` is copied onto the student's `esy_eligibility_status`, the student's `version` increases by one and its `updated_at` is refreshed, and the determination's own `version` increases by one as well.

### 3.3. The Shortened Summer Calendar and Its Transportation Routes

Summer sessions are short, irregular, and interrupted. A `ProgramCoordinator` creates each session as a program in `PLANNING`, giving it a `name` of 1 to 80 characters (typically something like "Summer 2027 ESY - Elementary"), the `school_year` it belongs to in `YYYY-YYYY` form, a `session_start_date` and a `session_end_date` written as `YYYY-MM-DD` with the end never earlier than the start, both inclusive. `instructional_weekdays` lists between one and five distinct weekday names drawn from `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU` and `FRI` — a four-day summer week is normal — and `daily_minutes_cap` fixes, between 1 and 480, the most service minutes any single student may be scheduled to receive on one instructional day. An optional `blackout_dates` array names planned non-instructional dates inside the session window, such as a holiday or a staff-development day; each entry must be an `YYYY-MM-DD` date falling within the session window, and when the array is omitted it is stored empty.

From those inputs the program's **instructional-day calendar** is derived, and it is the spine of everything downstream. Walk the inclusive range of dates from `session_start_date` through `session_end_date`; keep a date when its weekday name appears in `instructional_weekdays`, when it does not appear in `blackout_dates`, and when it does not appear in `cancelled_dates`. `instructional_days_count` is how many dates survive. Group the surviving dates into ISO 8601 weeks — Monday-anchored year-and-week buckets — and take them in ascending week order; `instructional_week_count` is how many distinct such weeks contain at least one surviving date. A partial first or last week counts as a whole week for this purpose, which is exactly why prorating matters later.

`cancelled_dates` is not a client input. It starts empty and is appended to only by the day-cancellation operation of §3.6, at which point both derived counts are re-derived. A single program read returns both counts alongside the inputs.

The program lifecycle runs `PLANNING → STAFFING_LOCKED → OPEN → CLOSED`, driven by a `ProgramCoordinator` supplying a `target_status`. Locking staffing (`PLANNING → STAFFING_LOCKED`) requires the program to be in `PLANNING` and requires at least one of its service commitments to have reached `ASSIGNED`; a program with no staffed commitment at all cannot lock, and the attempt returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Once locked, no further service commitments may be created against that program — a create aimed at a program outside `PLANNING` is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Opening the session (`STAFFING_LOCKED → OPEN`) requires the program to be in `STAFFING_LOCKED` and requires that **every** commitment of the program still sitting in either `PLANNED` or `ASSIGNED` has in fact reached `ASSIGNED`, meaning it holds at least one `ACTIVE` provider assignment; a single unstaffed `PLANNED` commitment blocks the opening with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Each of these transitions increments the program's `version`.

Closing a program is where the interesting cascade lives, and it takes two different shapes depending on where the program is when it closes. Closing from `OPEN` (`OPEN → CLOSED`) moves every commitment of that program whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to `COMPLETED`, while commitments still in `PLANNED` or `ASSIGNED` — never having run at all — move instead to `TERMINATED`; this conditional split is the whole point of the cascade. Each commitment that leaves the set `PLANNED`, `ASSIGNED`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` gives up its transportation seat, so every affected run's `seats_committed` is recomputed. Every `ACTIVE` provider assignment held by those commitments moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `PROGRAM_CLOSED`, and each release returns that assignment's `total_charge_cents` to its allocation by subtracting it from `encumbered_amount_cents`. The program's `version` increments. Closing from `PLANNING` (`PLANNING → CLOSED`) is the abandoned-session case: every commitment of the program moves to `TERMINATED`, and the program's `version` increments. `CLOSED` is terminal in both cases.

Transportation is planned run by run, not route by route. A `ProgramCoordinator` schedules a run against a program whose `status` is `PLANNING`, `STAFFING_LOCKED` or `OPEN`, supplying the `route_code` identifying the route or vehicle (1 to 12 characters), the `service_date` the run operates on, a `window_start_time` and `window_end_time` written as 24-hour `HH:MM` with the end strictly later than the start, and a `vehicle_capacity_seats` between 1 and 40 reflecting the wheelchair-adjusted seat count of the vehicle actually assigned. The `service_date` must be an instructional date of that program — right weekday, not blacked out, not already cancelled, inside the session window — and a date failing any of those tests is rejected with `ERR_NOT_INSTRUCTIONAL_DATE`.

A run's occupied window is a **closed** interval: both `window_start_time` and `window_end_time` are inside it. Two `SCHEDULED` runs sharing a `route_code` and a `service_date` therefore collide whenever the new run's start time is at or before the existing run's end time and the existing run's start time is at or before the new run's end time. A touching endpoint is a conflict here — a run finishing at 07:45 and another starting at 07:45 on the same route and date do collide — and the collision is reported as `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Note carefully that this is the opposite convention from the provider windows of §3.5, and the difference is intentional: a bus physically cannot be in two places at the changeover minute.

Runs that clear the overlap test face a second, gentler test. For two non-overlapping runs of the same `route_code` on the same `service_date`, measure the gap in minutes from the earlier run's `window_end_time` to the later run's `window_start_time`; if that gap is greater than zero but smaller than fifteen minutes, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_TURNAROUND_VIOLATION`. A quarter of an hour is the district's standing turnaround allowance for a driver to unload, re-route and reload.

Seat occupancy is derived rather than booked. A run's `seats_committed` counts the service commitments naming that run in their `transportation_run_id` whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ASSIGNED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; commitments that have reached `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` have released their seat and no longer count. When a commitment attempts to occupy a seat, the check is that the run's current `seats_committed` plus one does not exceed its `vehicle_capacity_seats`; a full bus is reported as `ERR_RUN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A run's own `status` starts at `SCHEDULED` and has exactly one exit. When a day cancellation removes the run's `service_date` from the calendar, every `SCHEDULED` run of that program on that date moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, its `version` increments, and its `seats_committed` is frozen at whatever value it then held; the commitments that referenced it keep their `transportation_run_id` pointing at the cancelled run, deliberately, so that the audit record still shows who was supposed to be on which bus. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

### 3.4. Service Commitments and the Pinned Minute Plan

A **service commitment** is the district's promise that one student will receive one service line inside one summer program. Either a `CaseManager` or a `ProgramCoordinator` may create one, naming the `program_id`, the `student_id`, the `service_type` (`SPECIALIZED_INSTRUCTION`, `SPEECH`, `OT`, `PT` or `COUNSELING`), the `iep_weekly_minutes` for that line taken from the school-year IEP (between 1 and 1500), and the `staffing_ratio` the IEP team agreed for the summer (`ONE_TO_ONE`, `ONE_TO_THREE`, `ONE_TO_SIX` or `GROUP_TEN`). An optional `transportation_run_id` seats the student on a bus run at the same time. Two hard gates guard creation: the named program must be in `PLANNING`, and the named student's `esy_eligibility_status` must read `ELIGIBLE`. A student whose eligibility reads `NOT_DETERMINED`, `INELIGIBLE` or `PENDING_DATA` cannot be committed, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ESY_ELIGIBLE`.

Creation runs the prorated minute plan against the program calendar **as it reads at that instant**, and the resulting three figures are pinned there permanently. The plan proceeds in four steps.

The first step builds the program's instructional-day calendar exactly as §3.3 describes and groups the surviving dates into ascending ISO weeks. If the calendar yields no instructional day at all, the create fails with `ERR_NO_INSTRUCTIONAL_DAYS`.

The second step prorates the weekly IEP minutes onto each calendar week, and rounds **per week, before summing**. Let the nominal weekday count be the number of entries in the program's `instructional_weekdays` — the length of a full summer week. For each ISO week in ascending order, that week's minute figure is the request's `iep_weekly_minutes` multiplied by the count of instructional dates actually falling in that week, divided by the nominal weekday count, rounded half up to a whole minute. `planned_total_minutes` is the sum of those per-week figures taken in week order. Rounding each week first and then adding is normative and is not the same answer as adding the exact per-week quotients and rounding once at the end; a four-weekday program running Monday through Thursday over a session whose last week holds only two instructional dates will show it.

The third step averages the pinned total back down. `weekly_minutes_committed` is `planned_total_minutes` divided by the number of ISO weeks, rounded half up to a whole minute. `daily_minutes` is `weekly_minutes_committed` divided by the nominal weekday count, again rounded half up to a whole minute; this daily figure is what a system-generated day-cancellation log will later claim as scheduled minutes.

The fourth step enforces the program's daily load ceiling, and it deliberately uses the **heaviest single week rather than the average**. For each ISO week, divide that week's rounded minute figure by the count of instructional dates in that week; take the largest such quotient across all weeks. If that maximum daily load is greater than the program's `daily_minutes_cap`, the create fails with `ERR_DAILY_MINUTES_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A short final week concentrating a full week's minutes into two days is exactly the case this catches.

When a `transportation_run_id` is supplied, the seat check of §3.3 runs and the run's `seats_committed` rises. A commitment's own ledger fields, `delivered_minutes` and `compensable_owed_minutes`, both start at zero and are maintained by full recomputation from that commitment's service logs, as §3.6 describes.

Commitments follow a six-state lifecycle beginning at `PLANNED`. The step out of `PLANNED` is not a client operation at all: the moment the commitment's first provider assignment reaches `ACTIVE`, the system advances `PLANNED → ASSIGNED` and increments the commitment's `version` by one. That version bump is worth noticing, because it is precisely the bump that makes a second assignment create carrying the now-stale `expected_commitment_version` fail with `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION`.

From there a `ProgramCoordinator` drives the commitment with a `target_status`. Activation (`ASSIGNED → ACTIVE`) requires the commitment to be in `ASSIGNED` and additionally requires its program's `status` to read `OPEN`; a commitment cannot go live before its session does, and the mismatch returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Once `ACTIVE`, the commitment becomes eligible to receive system-generated day-cancellation service logs. Suspension (`ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`) may be driven by either a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, requires the commitment to be `ACTIVE`, and requires the student's `stay_put_active` to be false — otherwise `ERR_STAY_PUT_BLOCKED`. A suspended commitment stops receiving day-cancellation logs but **keeps its transportation seat**, so the run's `seats_committed` does not change. Reactivation (`SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`) is a `ProgramCoordinator` act, requires the commitment to be `SUSPENDED`, and requires the program to be `OPEN`; the same status operation drives both directions, closing the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` cycle, and each direction increments the commitment's `version`.

Completion (`ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, and equally from `SUSPENDED`) is never requested directly; it is the system's response to the program closing, as §3.3 sets out, and it releases the transportation seat so the run's `seats_committed` is recomputed. `COMPLETED` is terminal.

Termination is the destructive exit, available to a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` from any of `PLANNED`, `ASSIGNED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and blocked with `ERR_STAY_PUT_BLOCKED` while the student's `stay_put_active` is true. It unwinds three things in one movement. Every `ACTIVE` provider assignment on the commitment moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `COMMITMENT_TERMINATED`. Each released assignment then subtracts exactly its own `total_charge_cents` from the `encumbered_amount_cents` of the allocation identified by its `charged_fund_source` and `charged_budget_code`, so that allocation's `available_cents` rises by precisely the same amount. Each released assignment also subtracts its `covered_weekly_minutes` from its provider's `assigned_weekly_minutes`. The commitment releases its transportation seat, so `seats_committed` is recomputed, and the commitment's `version` increments. `TERMINATED` is terminal, and a terminated commitment drops out of the student's `pending_award_minutes` sum entirely.

A filterable list of commitments is available to all four staff roles, and a single commitment read returns the pinned minute plan alongside the live ledger fields.

### 3.5. Provider Roster, Budget Codes, and Staffing Assignments

Before anyone can be staffed, two registries must exist. A `ProgramCoordinator` registers each provider with a `full_name` of 1 to 60 characters, a credentialed `role_type` (`SPED_TEACHER`, `SLP`, `OT`, `PT`, `COUNSELOR` or `PARAEDUCATOR`), a `credential_expires_on` date naming the last day the credential is valid inclusive, a `rate_cents_per_minute` between 1 and 2000 giving the fully loaded cost of a service minute, and a `max_weekly_minutes` between 1 and 3000 fixing the contractual weekly service-minute ceiling. A provider's `status` starts at `ACTIVE` and may later read `ON_LEAVE` or `INACTIVE`; a coordinator may update `status`, `credential_expires_on` or `max_weekly_minutes` at any time, each field independently optional on the update. The derived `assigned_weekly_minutes` starts at zero and always equals the sum of `covered_weekly_minutes` across that provider's `ACTIVE` assignments.

Separately, a `SpedDirector` authorizes the money. Each fund allocation names the `program_id` it serves, the `fund_source` it draws on (`IDEA_611_FEDERAL`, `IDEA_619_FEDERAL` or `LOCAL_GENERAL`), the district `budget_code` charges will be coded to (1 to 24 characters), and the board-authorized `authorized_amount_cents` ceiling between 1 and 100,000,000. The pair of `program_id` and `budget_code` must be unique across all allocations, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUDGET_CODE`; that uniqueness is what lets an auditor resolve a single allocation from the `charged_fund_source` and `charged_budget_code` recorded on a charge. An allocation's `encumbered_amount_cents`, `expended_amount_cents` and `compensatory_expended_cents` all start at zero, and `available_cents` is derived as described in §2.9.

Allocations cycle. A director may move one `OPEN → FROZEN`, which removes it from the waterfall's candidate filter, and back `FROZEN → OPEN`, which returns it; either direction increments the allocation's `version`. Closing an allocation is permitted from either `OPEN` or `FROZEN`, but only when its `encumbered_amount_cents` has fallen to exactly zero — an allocation still holding an encumbrance cannot be closed, and the attempt returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

Staffing an assignment is the busiest write in the service. A `ProgramCoordinator` names the `commitment_id` and the `provider_id`, a `weekday` from `MON` through `FRI`, a `start_time` and `end_time` in 24-hour `HH:MM` with the end strictly after the start, an `effective_from` and `effective_to` bracketing the dates the recurring block applies (both inclusive, the end no earlier than the start, both inside the program's session window), the `covered_weekly_minutes` between 1 and 3000 that this assignment covers, and the `expected_commitment_version` demanded by §2.8. The target commitment must currently be in `PLANNED` or `ASSIGNED`, and the chosen `weekday` must be a member of the program's `instructional_weekdays` — staffing a Friday block on a Monday-through-Thursday program is meaningless.

The provider must survive four separate suitability tests, applied in the order §2.7 fixes. The provider's `credential_expires_on` must fall on or after the assignment's `effective_to`, or the request draws `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`. The provider's `status` must read `ACTIVE`, or the request draws `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The provider's `role_type` must be permitted for the commitment's `service_type`: `SPECIALIZED_INSTRUCTION` accepts a `SPED_TEACHER` or a `PARAEDUCATOR`, `SPEECH` accepts only an `SLP`, `OT` accepts only an `OT`, `PT` accepts only a `PT`, and `COUNSELING` accepts only a `COUNSELOR`; anything else draws `ERR_PROVIDER_ROLE_MISMATCH`. And the provider must be free at the requested time.

Provider windows are **half-open**: the occupied interval runs from `start_time` up to but not including `end_time`. Two `ACTIVE` assignments of the same provider on the same `weekday` therefore collide only when the new block's start is strictly before the existing block's end **and** the existing block's start is strictly before the new block's end — so a 09:00-to-10:00 block and a 10:00-to-11:00 block do not collide, because touching endpoints are fine for a therapist walking between rooms. A collision additionally requires the two date ranges to intersect, tested inclusively: the new `effective_from` at or before the existing `effective_to`, and the existing `effective_from` at or before the new `effective_to`. Only when both the time windows and the date ranges intersect is the request rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_TIME_CONFLICT`. Assignments in `RELEASED` or `SUPERSEDED` neither block a new booking nor consume capacity.

Contractual load is checked in aggregate rather than per booking. Sum `covered_weekly_minutes` across all `ACTIVE` assignments the provider already holds, add the requested `covered_weekly_minutes`, and if that total is greater than the provider's `max_weekly_minutes`, reject with `ERR_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success, the provider's `assigned_weekly_minutes` is set to the recomputed sum.

Pricing then runs the five-step waterfall of §2.9 in encumbering mode. `covered_total_minutes` is the requested `covered_weekly_minutes` multiplied by the number of distinct ISO weeks in the inclusive range from `effective_from` to `effective_to` that contain at least one instructional date of the program. `base_cost_cents` is the provider's `rate_cents_per_minute` multiplied by `covered_total_minutes`; `indirect_cost_cents` applies the selected allocation's indirect rate to that base and rounds half up; `total_charge_cents` sums the two; and that exact total is added to the selected allocation's `encumbered_amount_cents` and recorded on the assignment as `charged_fund_source` and `charged_budget_code`.

Two further consequences follow a successful create. If the commitment was in `PLANNED`, it advances to `ASSIGNED`. And if an `ACTIVE` assignment already existed on the same commitment for the same service line, that incumbent moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` carrying `release_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ASSIGNMENT`, and the same three compensating effects a release performs are applied to it: its `total_charge_cents` leaves its allocation's `encumbered_amount_cents`, its `covered_weekly_minutes` leaves its provider's `assigned_weekly_minutes`, and its own `version` increments. This legitimate supersession is the whole reason the stale-version echo exists.

Releasing an assignment outright is a `ProgramCoordinator` act requiring a `release_reason` in the request and requiring the assignment to be `ACTIVE`. The release moves it `ACTIVE → RELEASED` and reverses its footprint exactly: `total_charge_cents` is subtracted from the `encumbered_amount_cents` of the allocation identified by `charged_fund_source` and `charged_budget_code`, so `available_cents` returns to its pre-assignment value; `covered_weekly_minutes` is subtracted from the provider's `assigned_weekly_minutes`; and if the release leaves no `ACTIVE` assignment on a commitment whose `status` reads `ASSIGNED`, the commitment reverts to `PLANNED`. Both the assignment's `version` and the commitment's `version` increment. `RELEASED` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal.

### 3.6. Running the Session: Service Logs, Day Cancellations, and Voids

What actually happened on a given day is recorded as a **service log**, one per commitment per date. Either the `RelatedServiceProvider` who delivered the service or the `CaseManager` overseeing the case may record it, naming the `commitment_id`, the `service_date` the session was scheduled for, the `outcome`, the `minutes_scheduled` the student was supposed to receive that day (between 1 and 480), and the `minutes_delivered` actually provided (between 0 and 480, never greater than `minutes_scheduled`). An optional `provider_id` records who delivered it, and an optional `miss_reason_code` records why it went wrong.

The `outcome` is one of five values, and only two of them create district liability. `DELIVERED` means the session ran in full; `PARTIAL` means it ran short; `MISSED_DISTRICT` means the district failed to provide it; `MISSED_PARENT` means the family cancelled; and `MISSED_STUDENT_ABSENT` means the child was absent. Only `MISSED_DISTRICT` and `PARTIAL` place minutes on the district's tab. The outcome constrains the minute fields tightly: `minutes_delivered` must equal `minutes_scheduled` exactly when the outcome is `DELIVERED`, and must be exactly zero for any of the three outcomes beginning with `MISSED_`. A `miss_reason_code` is required whenever the outcome is anything other than `DELIVERED`, and must be one of `WEATHER`, `STAFF_ABSENCE`, `TRANSPORT_FAILURE`, `PARENT_CANCELLED`, `STUDENT_ILL` or `OTHER`. Violations of any of these shape rules answer `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two further gates apply. The `service_date` must be an instructional date of the commitment's program, or the log is refused with `ERR_NOT_INSTRUCTIONAL_DATE`. And no `VERIFIED` log may already exist for that same combination of `commitment_id` and `service_date` — a second log for a day already documented is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERVICE_LOG`. A log's `status` begins at `VERIFIED`; only `VERIFIED` rows enter the minute ledgers and the compensatory-award input set. Its `generated_by` records provenance as `PROVIDER`, `CASE_MANAGER` or `SYSTEM_DAY_CANCELLATION`, defaulting to `PROVIDER`.

Creating a `VERIFIED` log recomputes the owning commitment's ledger in full: `delivered_minutes` becomes the sum of `minutes_delivered` across that commitment's `VERIFIED` logs, and `compensable_owed_minutes` becomes the sum, across that commitment's `VERIFIED` logs whose outcome is `MISSED_DISTRICT` or `PARTIAL`, of `minutes_scheduled` less `minutes_delivered`. That change then recomputes the student's ledger as §3.1 describes, potentially flipping `esy_service_alert` to true when `pending_award_minutes` reaches a full hour.

**Day cancellation** is the operation that turns a closed building into a documented debt. A `ProgramCoordinator` cancels one instructional day of an `OPEN` program, supplying the `service_date` to remove and a `cancellation_reason`. The program must be `OPEN`, and the date must currently be an instructional date of that program — a date already blacked out, already cancelled, on the wrong weekday, or outside the session window is refused with `ERR_NOT_INSTRUCTIONAL_DATE`. This is a self-transition: the program stays `OPEN` and its `version` increments.

The cancellation appends the date to `cancelled_dates` and re-derives `instructional_days_count` and `instructional_week_count`. It then drives a four-link cascade. First, for every commitment of that program whose `status` reads exactly `ACTIVE`, a service log is created for that `service_date` with outcome `MISSED_DISTRICT`, `minutes_scheduled` set to the commitment's pinned `daily_minutes`, `minutes_delivered` of zero, `miss_reason_code` set to the request's `cancellation_reason`, `status` `VERIFIED`, `generated_by` `SYSTEM_DAY_CANCELLATION`, and `provider_id` left null. Commitments sitting in `PLANNED`, `ASSIGNED`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` are **skipped** — this conditional filter is the crux of the cascade, and a suspended commitment accrues nothing. Second, each new log recomputes its commitment's ledger, raising `compensable_owed_minutes` by that log's `minutes_scheduled` and incrementing the commitment's `version` by one. Third, each commitment whose `compensable_owed_minutes` moved recomputes its student's ledger, raising `pending_award_minutes` and incrementing the student's `version` by one. Fourth, when the recomputed `pending_award_minutes` reaches sixty minutes or more, that student's `esy_service_alert` flips to true.

Two things happen alongside the cascade without being part of it. Every transportation run of that program whose `service_date` equals the cancelled date and whose `status` reads `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`, as §3.3 describes. And the pinned figures `planned_total_minutes`, `weekly_minutes_committed` and `daily_minutes` on existing commitments are pointedly **not** recomputed — the lost day shows up as owed minutes, not as a quietly reduced plan. These logs are only discoverable through the filterable service-log list; nothing else announces them.

Voiding a log is a `ComplianceOfficer` power and the cleanest compensation in the system. It requires a `void_reason`, requires the log to be `VERIFIED`, and moves it `VERIFIED → VOIDED`, which is terminal. The voided row simply leaves every input set: the commitment's ledger is recomputed, so `delivered_minutes` and `compensable_owed_minutes` return to their pre-log values, and the student's ledger is recomputed, so `pending_award_minutes` and `esy_service_alert` return to theirs. The log's `version`, the commitment's `version` and the student's `version` all increment by one.

### 3.7. Compensatory Award Calculation and the Negotiation Cycle

When the district has accumulated a documented deficit, a `CaseManager` opens a **compensatory award** against one `student_id` and one `source_program_id`, choosing a `calculation_variant` of `MINUTE_FOR_MINUTE` or `QUALITATIVE_BENEFIT`. Only one live award may exist per student and source program: if a non-terminal award already covers that pair, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AWARD`. A new award begins with `negotiation_status` `DRAFT` and `delivery_status` `NOT_STARTED` — two independent state dimensions on the same record, evolving separately, neither one gating the other except where explicitly stated below.

Creation computes the owed minutes immediately, in four steps.

The first step collects the district-fault deficit. The input set is every service log whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, whose commitment belongs to the award's `student_id`, whose commitment belongs to the award's `source_program_id`, and whose `outcome` is either `MISSED_DISTRICT` or `PARTIAL`. The district's missed minutes are the sum, across that set, of `minutes_scheduled` less `minutes_delivered`. `verified_missed_log_count` records how many logs entered the set. `excluded_log_count` records how many logs of the same student and the same program were passed over — those with `status` `VOIDED`, and those whose outcome was `DELIVERED`, `MISSED_PARENT` or `MISSED_STUDENT_ABSENT`. Parent cancellations and childhood illness are not the district's debt, and the excluded count exists so that an investigator can see the district did not quietly discard them.

The second step reads severity from the eligibility record. Take the student's `EligibilityDetermination` rows whose `status` is `FINALIZED`, order them by `created_at` ascending, and read `max_regression_pct` from the last of them — the most recent finalized determination. `severity_factor` is one point zero zero when that percentage is below twenty-five point zero, one point two five when it is at least twenty-five point zero but below forty-five point zero, and one point five zero when it is at least forty-five point zero. When the student has no finalized determination at all, `severity_factor` is one point zero zero.

The third step turns that into raw minutes, and this is where the two variants part company. Under `MINUTE_FOR_MINUTE` — the district's default remedy of one owed minute per missed minute — the raw figure is the district's missed minutes exactly. `severity_factor` is still computed and still stored on the award for the audit record, but it is **not applied**, and no fifteen-minute rounding is applied either. Under `QUALITATIVE_BENEFIT` — the remedy scaled by the regression severity documented at eligibility — the district's missed minutes are multiplied by `severity_factor`, and that product is then rounded to the nearest quarter-hour: divide the product by fifteen, add one half, take the whole-number part below the result, and multiply back by fifteen. A remainder of exactly half a unit therefore rounds up, and the rounding can move the result off the raw product in either direction. Identical logs consequently yield a strictly larger award under `QUALITATIVE_BENEFIT` whenever `severity_factor` exceeds one point zero zero.

The fourth step clamps and floors. `computed_minutes` is the smaller of the rounded raw figure and the annual ceiling of 3600 minutes — sixty hours in a school year. If `computed_minutes` then falls below thirty minutes, there is no award worth making and the create is refused with `ERR_NO_COMPENSABLE_MINUTES`.

Negotiation runs on the first state dimension. A `CaseManager` proposes an amount, which advances `DRAFT → PARENT_REVIEW` on the first offer and `DISPUTED → PARENT_REVIEW` on any revised offer. Both directions behave identically: the award's `computed_minutes`, `severity_factor`, `verified_missed_log_count` and `excluded_log_count` are **recomputed first**, from the log set as it now stands, and only then is the offer range-checked. The offered `proposed_minutes` must fall between `computed_minutes` at the low end and one and one-half times `computed_minutes` at the high end, the upper bound taken as the whole-number part below that product, both endpoints inclusive; anything outside that band is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_OUT_OF_RANGE`. A district may be generous, up to half again the computed figure, but never stingier than its own arithmetic. On success `proposed_minutes` is stored, `authored_by_user_id` records the proposing case manager's `X-User-ID`, and the award's `version` increments.

The parent then responds — only a `ParentGuardian` whose `X-User-ID` equals the award's `student_id`, on pain of `ERR_NOT_STUDENT_GUARDIAN` — with a `response` of `AGREE`, `DISPUTE` or `DECLINE`, and every branch requires the award to be in `PARENT_REVIEW`.

`AGREE` completes `PARENT_REVIEW → AGREED`: `agreed_minutes` is set to `proposed_minutes`, `delivery_deadline_date` is set to the calendar date of the current logical clock plus one hundred and eighty days, the student's `outstanding_owed_minutes` is recomputed and the student's `version` increments by one, and the award's `version` increments.

`DECLINE` completes `PARENT_REVIEW → DECLINED`, which is terminal: `agreed_minutes` stays null and no minutes ever enter the student's `outstanding_owed_minutes`. The award's `version` increments.

`DISPUTE` is the branch with the threshold, and the *same request body* produces two different outcomes depending on history. Each dispute increases `dispute_round_count` by one and increments the award's `version`. If that increased count is still below two, the award moves `PARENT_REVIEW → DISPUTED`, from which a `CaseManager` may propose a revised amount and return it to `PARENT_REVIEW` — the negotiation cycle `PARENT_REVIEW → DISPUTED → PARENT_REVIEW` may be walked as many times as the threshold allows. Once the increased count reaches two, the same dispute escalates straight past `DISPUTED` to `PARENT_REVIEW → MEDIATION` instead. Two rounds of haggling is the district's limit before a neutral must be involved.

Arbitration closes the argument, and it belongs to the `SpedDirector`. It is permitted from either `DISPUTED` or `MEDIATION`, and both source states behave identically: `DISPUTED → AGREED` and `MEDIATION → AGREED` share one set of effects. Three preconditions guard it. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `authored_by_user_id`, or the request draws `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the director who wrote the offer may not also be the neutral who imposes it. The submitted `arbitrated_minutes` must fall inside the same band as a proposal, from `computed_minutes` up to the whole-number part below one and one-half times `computed_minutes`, inclusive at both ends, or the request draws `ERR_PROPOSAL_OUT_OF_RANGE`. And the student's `stay_put_active` must be false, or the request draws `ERR_STAY_PUT_BLOCKED`. On success `agreed_minutes` is set to `arbitrated_minutes`, `resolved_by_user_id` records the arbitrating director, `delivery_deadline_date` is set to the current logical clock's calendar date plus one hundred and eighty days, the student's `outstanding_owed_minutes` is recomputed with the student's `version` incremented by one, and the award's `version` increments. An award arbitrated out of `MEDIATION` is recorded as an arbitrated resolution in the trail attached to `resolved_by_user_id`.

The award finally closes without anyone asking. When the delivery dimension reaches either `FULFILLED` or `LAPSED`, the system moves the negotiation dimension `AGREED → CLOSED`, increments the award's `version`, and recomputes the student's `outstanding_owed_minutes` — noting that a `CLOSED` award still contributes its `remaining_owed_minutes`, which is zero for a fulfilled award and greater than zero for a lapsed one. A `CLOSED` award accepts no new makeup sessions, and an attempt to book one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `DECLINED` and `CLOSED` are the negotiation dimension's terminal states.

### 3.8. Makeup Delivery, Forfeiture, and the Delivery Dimension

Agreed minutes are worthless until someone actually sits down with the child, so the award's second state dimension tracks delivery entirely independently of the negotiation. Either a `CaseManager` or a `ProgramCoordinator` books a **makeup session** against an award, naming the `award_id`, the `provider_id` who will deliver the minutes, the `session_date` the session is booked for, a `start_time` and `end_time` in 24-hour `HH:MM` with the end strictly after the start, the `minutes_planned` the session is to deliver (between 1 and 480), and the `expected_award_version` required by §2.8. An optional `supersedes_session_id` names a currently `SCHEDULED` session that this booking replaces. The session's occupied interval is half-open on its `session_date`, running from `start_time` up to but not including `end_time`.

Booking is gated on all sides. The award's `negotiation_status` must read `AGREED` — a draft, disputed, mediated, declined or closed award accepts no bookings, and the attempt returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as does any booking against an award whose `delivery_status` has reached `LAPSED`. The named provider's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`) and their `credential_expires_on` must fall on or after the `session_date` (`ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`). The `session_date` itself must fall on or after the calendar date of the current logical clock and on or before the award's `delivery_deadline_date`; a booking in the past or past the deadline is refused with `ERR_MAKEUP_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`.

The overbooking guard is the last check, and it reads the award's delivery ledger. That ledger is recomputed in full on every session create, supersession or outcome log: `delivered_minutes` is the sum of `minutes_delivered` across the award's sessions whose `status` is `DELIVERED`; `scheduled_minutes` is the sum of `minutes_planned` across the award's sessions whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`; and `remaining_owed_minutes` is `agreed_minutes` (treated as zero while still null) less `delivered_minutes` less `forfeited_minutes`, with any negative result clamped to zero. Sessions in `SUPERSEDED`, `VOIDED`, `NO_SHOW_PARENT` or `CANCELLED_DISTRICT` contribute to neither sum. To book, `scheduled_minutes` plus the new session's `minutes_planned` must not exceed `remaining_owed_minutes`; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_OVERBOOKED_MAKEUP_MINUTES`. When the booking carries a `supersedes_session_id`, the comparison is made **after** the superseded session's minutes have left `scheduled_minutes`, so a like-for-like replacement always fits.

A successful booking with `supersedes_session_id` moves the named session `SCHEDULED → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal, removes its `minutes_planned` from the award's `scheduled_minutes`, and increments that session's `version`. And the first session ever created for an award advances the delivery dimension `NOT_STARTED → SCHEDULING` — permitted only while `negotiation_status` reads `AGREED` — recomputing `scheduled_minutes` and incrementing the award's `version`.

Outcomes are logged one session at a time, and the three provider-reported branches fork out of `SCHEDULED` with deliberately different consequences. All three require the session to be `SCHEDULED` and require the acting `RelatedServiceProvider`'s `X-User-ID` to equal the session's `provider_id`, failing which the request answers `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROVIDER`. Each accepts an optional `outcome_note` recorded against the session.

Logging a session as delivered advances `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED` and additionally requires a `minutes_delivered` figure between 1 and the session's `minutes_planned` inclusive (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` otherwise), and requires the award's `negotiation_status` to read either `AGREED` or `CLOSED` with a `delivery_status` other than `LAPSED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise). Its effects are the fullest of the three. The `minutes_delivered` are credited, and the session is priced through the waterfall of §2.9 in expending mode: `charge_cents`, `charged_fund_source` and `charged_budget_code` are written on the session, and the selected allocation's `expended_amount_cents` and `compensatory_expended_cents` both rise by that charge — subject first to the federal compensatory cap, which answers `ERR_SUPPLANT_RESTRICTION`. Three cascade links then fire. The award's `delivered_minutes`, `scheduled_minutes` and `remaining_owed_minutes` are recomputed and the delivery dimension advances. The student's ledger is recomputed, lowering `outstanding_owed_minutes` and raising `delivered_makeup_minutes_total`, with the student's `version` incremented by one. And finally, when the recomputed `outstanding_owed_minutes` has reached exactly zero, every due-process record of that student whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `record_type` is `MEDIATION_AGREEMENT` moves to `RESOLVED`, carrying `resolution_note` `OWED_MINUTES_SATISFIED` and `resolved_at` set to the current logical clock instant. The session's own `version` increments.

Logging a parent no-show advances `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW_PARENT`, which is terminal. No minutes are credited and no fund is charged at all. The award's `parent_no_show_count` — which counts exactly the award's sessions sitting in `NO_SHOW_PARENT` — increases by one, and both the session's `version` and the award's `version` increment. Here the forfeiture threshold bites: once the recomputed `parent_no_show_count` reaches two, this session's `minutes_planned` is added to the award's `forfeited_minutes`, which lowers `remaining_owed_minutes` and in turn lowers the student's `outstanding_owed_minutes`. The district's obligation is not open-ended when the family repeatedly does not attend; and as §2.11 notes, `forfeited_minutes` persists and is never rebuilt by a later ledger recomputation.

Logging a district cancellation advances `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED_DISTRICT`, also terminal, and is the mirror image of a no-show. No minutes are credited and no fund is charged, but the minutes **return to the schedulable pool**: the session's `minutes_planned` leaves `scheduled_minutes` while `remaining_owed_minutes` stays exactly where it was, so the same minutes can be rebooked. In compensation for the district's own failure, the award's `delivery_deadline_date` is extended by a further thirty days. Both the session's `version` and the award's `version` increment.

Voiding a delivered session is a `ComplianceOfficer` act, requires the session to be `DELIVERED`, and moves it `DELIVERED → VOIDED`, which is terminal. It reverses the delivery precisely: the session's `minutes_delivered` leaves the award's `delivered_minutes`, so `remaining_owed_minutes` and the student's `outstanding_owed_minutes` return to their pre-delivery values; and the allocation's `expended_amount_cents` and `compensatory_expended_cents` both drop by the session's `charge_cents`, so `available_cents` returns to its pre-delivery value. The session's `version`, the award's `version` and the student's `version` all increment by one.

The delivery dimension itself moves only as a consequence of these outcomes and of the clock. It begins at `NOT_STARTED`, reaches `SCHEDULING` when the award's first session is booked, and advances `SCHEDULING → IN_PROGRESS` when the award's first session reaches `DELIVERED` while the resulting `remaining_owed_minutes` is still greater than zero. From either `SCHEDULING` or `IN_PROGRESS`, an outcome that brings `remaining_owed_minutes` to exactly zero advances the dimension to `FULFILLED`, which in turn triggers the negotiation dimension's `AGREED → CLOSED` move. Every one of these system transitions increments the award's `version`.

Two paths out of the ordinary progression matter. First, the cycle: voiding a `DELIVERED` session against a `FULFILLED` award moves the delivery dimension `FULFILLED → IN_PROGRESS` whenever the resulting `remaining_owed_minutes` is greater than zero, and drags the negotiation dimension back with it from `CLOSED` to `AGREED`. The debt is reopened, and the conservation of owed minutes is visible on both dimensions at once. Second, the lapse: from either `SCHEDULING` or `IN_PROGRESS`, when `remaining_owed_minutes` is greater than zero and the calendar date of the current logical clock is strictly later than the award's `delivery_deadline_date`, the delivery dimension moves to `LAPSED`, which is terminal, and that too triggers the negotiation dimension's `AGREED → CLOSED`. This evaluation is lazy: it is applied at the start of any request whose path or body references the award, which includes merely reading the award. Sessions already `SCHEDULED` against a lapsed award stay `SCHEDULED`, but no new session may be booked, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_MAKEUP_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`.

### 3.9. Due Process, Stay-Put Protection, and the Documentation Packet

Procedural safeguards are the reason this service exists in a database rather than a spreadsheet. A `ComplianceOfficer` files each record against a `student_id`, giving the `record_type`, the `filed_on` date the filing was received, and an `allegation_summary` of 1 to 500 characters narrating the allegation or, for a mediated settlement, the terms agreed. `record_type` is one of `COMPLAINT`, `DUE_PROCESS_HEARING` or `MEDIATION_AGREEMENT`, and the distinction is legally consequential: a `COMPLAINT` and a `DUE_PROCESS_HEARING` assert **stay-put** protection the moment they are filed, while a `MEDIATION_AGREEMENT` does not. Each record's `status` starts at `OPEN`, and its own `stay_put_active` flag reads true exactly when its `status` is `OPEN` and its `record_type` is `COMPLAINT` or `DUE_PROCESS_HEARING`. Filing recomputes the student's `stay_put_active` and increments the student's `version`.

Stay-put freezes exactly three operations, and it freezes them hard. While a student's `stay_put_active` reads true, that student's service commitments may not be suspended and may not be terminated, and any compensatory award of that student may not be arbitrated by a director. All three attempts answer `ERR_STAY_PUT_BLOCKED`. Nothing else is blocked: services continue to be logged, days continue to be cancellable, proposals continue to be made and parents continue to be able to agree, dispute or decline. The protection is against the district *reducing* the child's services or unilaterally *imposing* a remedy amount while a filing is live.

A `ComplianceOfficer` closes a record by supplying a `status` of either `RESOLVED` or `DISMISSED`, with an optional `resolution_note`; both branches require the record to be `OPEN` and both are terminal. Resolving a record moves it `OPEN → RESOLVED`, sets its `resolved_at` to the current logical-clock instant, and drops its own `stay_put_active` to false; the student's `stay_put_active` is then recomputed and the student's `version` increments, which is what unblocks commitment suspension, commitment termination and award arbitration again. Dismissing a record moves it `OPEN → DISMISSED` with the identical downstream effect: `stay_put_active` becomes false, the student's flag is recomputed, the student's `version` increments, and the record's `version` increments. There is also a system route into `RESOLVED`, described in §3.8: when a delivery brings a student's `outstanding_owed_minutes` to zero, that student's `OPEN` `MEDIATION_AGREEMENT` records resolve automatically with `resolution_note` `OWED_MINUTES_SATISFIED`.

For an investigator or a hearing officer, the service offers a computed **documentation packet** for one award, readable by a `SpedDirector` or a `ComplianceOfficer` only. Rather than the stored award record, the packet returns a flat purpose-built summary: `award_id` and `student_id` identifying the case; `calculation_variant` and `severity_factor` showing which remedy formula ran and at what scale; `computed_minutes`, `proposed_minutes` and `agreed_minutes` tracing the arithmetic through the negotiation; `delivered_minutes`, `scheduled_minutes`, `forfeited_minutes` and `remaining_owed_minutes` showing where the debt stands; `verified_missed_log_count` and `excluded_log_count` proving which service logs were counted and how many were set aside; `dispute_round_count` and `parent_no_show_count` recording the negotiation and attendance history; `negotiation_status`, `delivery_status` and `delivery_deadline_date` giving both state dimensions and the clock they run against; and `open_due_process_record_count`, the number of due-process records of that student still sitting in `OPEN`. The student's own due-process records are listed separately, and that list is restricted to the `CaseManager`, the `SpedDirector` and the `ComplianceOfficer` — a `ParentGuardian` cannot read it.
