# Grain Merchandising & Basis Trading API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A commercial grain desk lives in two markets at once. On the cash side it posts bids at country elevators, strikes purchase contracts with farmers for delivery months out, takes physical grain across a scale, and grades every load for moisture, test weight, foreign material and damage. On the board side it sells or buys futures against that cash length, posts initial margin with its clearing broker, meets variation-margin calls when the market moves against it, and rolls positions forward when delivery slips. The two sides are the same business decision viewed from different ledgers, and a merchandising desk that lets them drift apart discovers the gap only when the clearing house calls.

This document specifies a REST API for that desk. It covers bid posting by delivery period and location; the four purchase-contract types the trade uses (cash, hedge-to-arrive, basis and deferred pricing) and the machinery for fixing their floating legs; futures hedging with mark-to-market, margin issuance and forced liquidation; delivery application against contracts with a quality discount schedule applied at intake; contract rolls into later board months; freight and rail car acquisition with exclusive fleet occupancy; position reporting against published risk limits; and the termination paths — mutual cancellation and seller default — with the damages arithmetic each one carries.

Three design commitments run through everything that follows. First, the desk's credit picture is a single derived quantity: every hedge lot opened, every margin call issued or cured, and every contract settled, cancelled, defaulted or rolled recomputes the desk's utilisation of its clearing credit line, and that utilisation gates whether the desk may write new business at all. Second, values computed at intake are frozen: a graded load's shrink, discounts and settlement value are computed once against the schedule in force at that moment and never re-derived, even after the schedule is superseded. Third, every rejection has exactly one identity — the checks a request must survive are strictly ordered, and the first one that fails is the one the caller hears about.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Desk Roles and Request Identity

Every request to this API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the operator making the call, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the operation being attempted, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `DeskAdmin`, `Merchandiser`, `FuturesTrader`, `RiskOfficer`, `GrainInspector` and `FreightCoordinator`, and the split between them is a deliberate segregation of duties rather than a convenience. A `DeskAdmin` is a reference-data operator: it stands up trading desks, registers country elevators, drafts and activates quality discount schedules, and drives the server's logical clock. It never touches a bid, a contract, a hedge position or a delivery ticket. A `Merchandiser` posts bids, strikes and rolls purchase contracts, and applies graded loads to contracts — but it may not cancel a contract it created itself, and it has no access to the futures book. A `FuturesTrader` owns the board: opening positions, adding lots, marking them to market and liquidating them. A `RiskOfficer` polices exposure it cannot itself create — it publishes risk limits, wires variation margin to cure open calls, and terminates contracts by cancellation or by declaring default — but it may not post a bid, strike a contract or open a hedge. A `GrainInspector` grades inbound loads and disposes of them by rejection or by reversal, but never applies a graded ticket to a contract: application is the `Merchandiser`'s act. A `FreightCoordinator` acquires rail car sets and books, confirms, substitutes and cancels their movements, and does nothing else. Two write operations are deliberately shared rather than exclusive: placing a pricing order on either leg of a contract is open to both a `Merchandiser` and a `FuturesTrader`. Every read endpoint is open to all six roles.

Identity in this system is deliberately thin. When `X-User-Role` is `DeskAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. The same is true of `Merchandiser`, `FuturesTrader`, `RiskOfficer`, `GrainInspector` and `FreightCoordinator` — for all six roles `X-User-ID` is opaque. The server does record it in three places where the scaffolded workflow demands an actor of record: `created_by_user_id` on a purchase contract captures the `X-User-ID` of the `Merchandiser` that struck it, `graded_by_user_id` on a delivery ticket captures the `X-User-ID` of the `GrainInspector` that graded it, and `placed_by_user_id` on a pricing order captures the `X-User-ID` of whoever placed the order. Only one of those recorded values is ever compared against a caller: the contract's `created_by_user_id`, at cancellation time, where a match means the same person is cancelling what they wrote and the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). No role identifier is otherwise used as an ownership key.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside these bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first — and that ordering is stable regardless of any filters supplied alongside the pagination parameters. For list responses, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Row Versions

Every resource this API mints carries a server-generated `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id`; they never parse one either. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits, recording the logical-clock instant at which the row was created.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. A mutation here means any change the server writes to the row, including changes driven by a cascade from a different entity: when settling a contract lifts hedge lots off a position, that position's `version` advances and its `updated_at` refreshes just as if it had been written directly.

One entity is deliberately exempt. A pricing order is an immutable event row — once placed it is never edited — and so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all; neither key appears in its representation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this system is a server-held logical clock, never the host's wall clock. At startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. A `DeskAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability, whose response reports the new `current_utc` together with `expired_bid_ids`, the list of bid identifiers that the move retired.

Advancing the clock has exactly one automatic consequence: any bid whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the new clock reading transitions to `EXPIRED`, and its id appears in `expired_bid_ids`. Nothing else in the system moves on a clock tick — margin call deadlines, delivery grace periods and bid activation windows are all evaluated at the moment a request touches them, by comparing the request-time clock reading against the stored instant.

All date-only fields (`delivery_period_start`, `delivery_period_end`, `delivery_start`, `delivery_end`) are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and denote closed, inclusive intervals. All instant fields (`expires_at`, `effective_from`, `issued_at`, `cure_deadline_at`, `placement_at`, `release_at`, `occupancy_end_at`) are ISO 8601 UTC datetimes. A `futures_month` is a board month written as `YYYY-MM`. Anything that fails to parse in its declared shape is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. Money, Bushels and Rounding Discipline

Prices in this domain are quoted in **cents per bushel** and may legitimately be negative — a basis of minus thirty cents under the board is an ordinary posting at an interior elevator. Money is carried in **US dollars**. Bushel quantities are decimal numbers; futures lots are whole integers.

Rounding is always half-up — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of places depends on the quantity:

- Every US dollar amount is carried to 2 decimal places: `credit_line_usd`, `margin_requirement_usd`, `open_margin_call_usd`, `available_credit_usd`, `posted_margin_usd`, `unrealized_pnl_usd`, `realized_pnl_usd`, `realized_hedge_pnl_usd`, `call_amount_usd`, `cured_amount_usd`, `payment_usd`, `discount_total_usd`, `gross_contract_value_usd`, `settlement_value_usd`, `settled_value_usd` and `damages_usd`.
- `credit_utilization` and `hedge_coverage_ratio` are ratios carried to 4 decimal places.
- `avg_entry_price_cents` is carried to 4 decimal places, as are the two contract leg prices `futures_price_cents` and `basis_cents` whenever they are derived as a volume-weighted average.
- `net_bushels` is carried to 2 decimal places.
- `hedge_lots_assigned` and `required_hedge_lots` are whole lot counts obtained by rounding half-up, so a bushel quantity landing exactly on a half lot rounds up.

Two rounding *orders* matter enough to state separately, because computing them in the natural-looking order produces answers that differ by a cent. Quality discounts are extended and rounded **per factor** and only then summed — never summed as rates and rounded once at the end. And the merchandising service fee tier is chosen from the **gross** contract value of a load, computed before the fee is subtracted; it is never re-derived from the post-fee value even when the post-fee value would fall in a different tier. Both rules are spelled out in full where the grading workflow is described.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Desk and Fleet Writes

Two create operations reach across an aggregate boundary and would silently destroy work if the client's picture of the parent were out of date. Both are guarded by optimistic concurrency on the parent row's `version`.

The first is `POST /grain/risk-limits`. Its request body must carry `expected_desk_version` — a required field, never optional — holding the `version` of the target `TradingDesk` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the desk's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DESK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because publishing a limit legitimately supersedes the prior `ACTIVE` limit for the same desk and commodity, so a writer holding a stale view of the desk would retire a limit it never read. A successful create increments the desk's `version` by 1, which by construction invalidates every other client's stale expectation.

The second is `POST /grain/freight-bookings`. Its request body must carry `expected_fleet_version` — likewise required — holding the `version` of the target `RailFleet` as the client last observed it. A value that does not equal the fleet's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FLEET_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the guard matters because a non-intersecting booking is otherwise accepted on its own merits, so the version check is the only signal available that the occupancy picture the client reasoned about is no longer current. A successful create increments the fleet's `version` by 1, and so does a successful substitution onto a different fleet.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two carry no `expected_desk_version`, no `expected_fleet_version`, and no analogue of either.

### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder

Every request is screened by an ordered sequence of checks, and **the first check that fails decides the response**. This ordering is normative, not a description of a convenient implementation: when two conditions are simultaneously violated, the response must name the earlier one. The ladder, in ascending priority order, is:

1. Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears on the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every id embedded in the URL path resolves to a stored row, checked left to right. The code names the missing entity: `ERR_TRADING_DESK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCOUNT_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RISK_LIMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RAIL_FLEET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICING_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_TICKET_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FREIGHT_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path id.
4. Every foreign-key id in the request body resolves to a stored row, checked in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input list, yielding `ERR_TRADING_DESK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RAIL_FLEET_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FREIGHT_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint whose body carries a foreign key.
5. Required fields are present, enum values are members of their declared sets, numbers respect their declared ranges, dates parse as `YYYY-MM-DD` and datetimes as ISO 8601 UTC — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
6. `expected_desk_version` equals the current desk `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_DESK_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /grain/risk-limits`.
7. `expected_fleet_version` equals the current fleet `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_FLEET_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /grain/freight-bookings`.
8. The target row's own lifecycle state permits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/bids/{bidId}/activate`, `POST /grain/bids/{bidId}/suspend`, `POST /grain/bids/{bidId}/withdraw`, `POST /grain/discount-schedules/{scheduleId}/activate`, `POST /grain/freight-bookings/{bookingId}/confirm`, `POST /grain/freight-bookings/{bookingId}/substitute`, `POST /grain/freight-bookings/{bookingId}/cancel`, `POST /grain/margin-calls/{marginCallId}/cure`, `POST /grain/hedge-positions/{positionId}/add-lots`, `POST /grain/hedge-positions/{positionId}/mark-to-market` and `POST /grain/hedge-positions/{positionId}/liquidate`.
9. The referenced purchase contract's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/pricing-orders`, `POST /grain/delivery-tickets`, `POST /grain/delivery-tickets/{ticketId}/apply`, `POST /grain/delivery-tickets/{ticketId}/reverse`, `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/cancel`, `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/declare-default`, `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll` and `POST /grain/freight-bookings`.
10. The delivery ticket is in the state its action requires — `GRADED` for apply and for reject, `APPLIED` for reverse — otherwise `ERR_TICKET_NOT_GRADED` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/delivery-tickets/{ticketId}/apply`, `POST /grain/delivery-tickets/{ticketId}/reject` and `POST /grain/delivery-tickets/{ticketId}/reverse`.
11. The referenced bid's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_BID_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts`.
12. The referenced hedge position's `status` is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts`, `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll`, and `POST /grain/pricing-orders` when the named component is `FUTURES`.
13. When the delivery ticket carries a `freight_booking_id`, that booking's `status` is `CONFIRMED` — otherwise `ERR_FREIGHT_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/delivery-tickets/{ticketId}/apply`.
14. The hedge position agrees with the bid on desk, commodity and board month — otherwise `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts` and `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll`.
15. The delivery ticket's `location_id` equals the contract's `location_id` — otherwise `ERR_LOCATION_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/delivery-tickets`.
16. The contract's elevator has `rail_served` true and the fleet is homed at that elevator — otherwise `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_RAIL_SERVED` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/freight-bookings` and `POST /grain/freight-bookings/{bookingId}/substitute`.
17. The requested delivery window sits inside the bid's posted delivery period on both ends — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_INVALID` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts` and `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll`.
18. The logical clock is strictly before the bid's `expires_at` — otherwise `ERR_BID_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/bids/{bidId}/activate`.
19. The logical clock is at or before the call's `cure_deadline_at` — otherwise `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/margin-calls/{marginCallId}/cure`.
20. The delivery grace period has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_GRACE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/declare-default`.
21. The contract's `pricing_locked` flag is false — otherwise `ERR_PRICING_LOCKED` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/pricing-orders` and `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll`. **This check precedes the desk-posture gate below**, so a pricing order aimed at a locked contract on a frozen desk reports the contract-level lock rather than the desk-level freeze.
22. The owning desk's `posture` is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_DESK_FROZEN` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts`, `POST /grain/pricing-orders` and `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll`.
23. When the requested `contract_type` is `DEFERRED`, the owning desk's `posture` is not `RESTRICTED` — otherwise `ERR_DESK_RESTRICTED` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts`.
24. The desk's projected unpriced exposure stays within the active risk limit — otherwise `ERR_RISK_LIMIT_BREACH` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts`.
25. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the contract's `created_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/cancel`.
26. No other bid in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status occupies the same market slot — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/bids/{bidId}/activate`.
27. The requested fleet occupancy interval does not intersect an existing reservation on the same fleet — otherwise `ERR_FLEET_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/freight-bookings` and `POST /grain/freight-bookings/{bookingId}/substitute`.
28. Elevator intake capacity over overlapping delivery windows is not exceeded — otherwise `ERR_LOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts` and `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll`.
29. A discount schedule with `status` `ACTIVE` exists for the contract's commodity — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_DISCOUNT_SCHEDULE` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/delivery-tickets`.
30. Under the market-loss damages variant, at least one bid satisfies the local eligibility filter — otherwise `ERR_NO_MARKET_BID` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/cancel` when `damages_method` is `MARKET_LOSS`, and to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/declare-default`.
31. Under the replacement-cost damages variant, at least one bid satisfies the desk-wide eligibility filter — otherwise `ERR_NO_REPLACEMENT_BID` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/cancel` when `damages_method` is `REPLACEMENT_COST`.
32. `cars_requested` does not exceed the fleet's `car_count` — otherwise `ERR_CARS_EXCEED_FLEET` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/freight-bookings` and `POST /grain/freight-bookings/{bookingId}/substitute`.
33. The hedge position has enough uncommitted lots for the contract — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_HEDGE_LOTS` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts` and `POST /grain/purchase-contracts/{contractId}/roll`.
34. The bid's remaining bushel allowance covers the new contract — otherwise `ERR_BID_MAX_BUSHELS_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/purchase-contracts`.
35. The named component still has floating bushels, is permitted for the contract type, and the order does not exceed what remains floating — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_PRICED` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/pricing-orders`.
36. The ticket's `net_bushels` fits inside the contract's undelivered balance — otherwise `ERR_BUSHELS_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/delivery-tickets/{ticketId}/apply`.
37. `payment_usd` is at least the call's `call_amount_usd` — otherwise `ERR_CURE_AMOUNT_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /grain/margin-calls/{marginCallId}/cure`.

The exact business meaning of ranks 8 through 37 — what "agrees with the bid" means, how intake capacity is aggregated, how the grace period is measured — is developed in the workflow narratives of Section 3. What this ladder fixes is only the order in which the questions are asked.

### 2.9. Credit Posture: the Desk's Single Derived Ledger

A `TradingDesk` is registered with a `desk_code` unique across all desks, a human-readable `name`, and a `credit_line_usd` greater than zero representing the total clearing credit the desk may consume. Everything else on the desk row is derived, recomputed from scratch after any mutation that changes a hedge position's open lots or status, creates or resolves a margin call, or settles, cancels, defaults or rolls a purchase contract. A newly registered desk starts with `posture` `NORMAL` and every derived aggregate at zero.

The derivation runs as follows. `margin_requirement_usd` is the sum, over the desk's hedge positions whose `status` is either `OPEN` or `MARGIN_CALL`, of that position's open lot count multiplied by the initial margin rate for its commodity — 1,650.00 dollars per lot for `CORN`, 3,300.00 dollars per lot for `SOYBEANS`, and 2,200.00 dollars per lot for `HRW_WHEAT` — with the total rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. `open_margin_call_usd` is the sum of `call_amount_usd` over the margin calls whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose position belongs to this desk, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. `available_credit_usd` is the credit line less the margin requirement less the open margin calls, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. `credit_utilization` is the margin requirement plus the open margin calls, divided by the credit line, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places.

`posture` follows directly from that ratio, and both breakpoints are inclusive: the desk is `FROZEN` when `credit_utilization` is at or above 1.0000, `RESTRICTED` when it is at or above 0.8000 but below 1.0000, and `NORMAL` otherwise. A desk sitting at exactly 0.8000 is `RESTRICTED`; a desk sitting at exactly 1.0000 is `FROZEN`.

Four further aggregates ride along on the same recomputation. `committed_hedge_lots` is the sum of committed lots over the same `OPEN`-or-`MARGIN_CALL` position set, and it is the conserved quantity behind the contract create-and-cancel invariant: striking a contract raises it and cancelling that contract must return it to exactly where it was. `open_bushel_commitment` is the sum, over the desk's purchase contracts whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, of contracted bushels less delivered bushels, and it is the conserved quantity behind the delivery apply-and-reverse invariant. `unpriced_bushels` on the desk is the sum of each `ACTIVE` contract's own unpriced bushel figure. `realized_hedge_pnl_usd` is the running sum of realised profit and loss over **all** of the desk's hedge positions regardless of status, so a liquidated position keeps contributing to it forever.

Posture is not a label; it is a gate. While a desk is `FROZEN`, no new purchase contract may be struck against it, no pricing order may be placed on its contracts, and no contract of its may be rolled — all three are refused with `ERR_DESK_FROZEN`. While a desk is `RESTRICTED`, it retains all of those capabilities except one: a purchase contract of type `DEFERRED`, which fixes neither price leg and therefore carries the most open exposure, is refused with `ERR_DESK_RESTRICTED`. Freezing additionally reaches into the contracts themselves, as the next subsection describes.

### 2.10. Cascades, Locks and Compensating Reversals

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row, and the secondary effects are as much part of the contract as the primary one. Two cascades run four hops deep and are worth stating end to end.

The first begins on the futures book. Marking a position to market at a price that drops account equity below maintenance margin (hop 1) creates a margin call row in `OPEN` status; because that call now exists, the desk's `open_margin_call_usd` rises by the call amount and `available_credit_usd` and `credit_utilization` are recomputed (hop 2); because utilisation has crossed a breakpoint, `posture` is rewritten (hop 3); and if the new posture is `FROZEN`, then `pricing_locked` is set to true on every purchase contract of that desk whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `pricing_status` is not yet `FULLY_PRICED` (hop 4). The `pricing_locked` flag is what makes the freeze bite at the contract level: while it is true, no pricing order may be placed against that contract and the contract may not be rolled, both refused with `ERR_PRICING_LOCKED`. Curing the call runs the cascade in reverse, and when `posture` leaves `FROZEN`, `pricing_locked` is cleared back to false on every `ACTIVE` contract of that desk.

The second begins at the scale. Applying a graded load raises the contract's delivered bushels and settled value (hop 1); if that completes delivery on a fully priced contract the contract's lifecycle status advances to `SETTLED` (hop 2); settlement lifts the contract's hedge lots off its position, booking realised profit or loss and releasing posted margin (hop 3); the desk's margin requirement, committed lots, bushel commitment, unpriced bushels, available credit, utilisation, realised hedge profit and posture are all recomputed (hop 4); and if that recomputation takes posture out of `FROZEN`, `pricing_locked` clears on the desk's `ACTIVE` contracts (hop 5).

Reversals compensate rather than delete. Reversing an applied ticket subtracts exactly the bushels and value it added, restoring the contract's `delivered_bushels`, `settled_value_usd` and `delivery_status` and the desk's `open_bushel_commitment` to their pre-apply readings, while leaving every computed grading field on the ticket itself untouched. Cancelling or defaulting a contract releases the hedge lots it had committed, so the position's committed lot count and the desk's `committed_hedge_lots` return to their pre-contract values, and the elevator's `committed_intake_bushels` sheds the contract's bushels.

Nested reads follow the same left-to-right discipline the rejection ladder describes: where a path carries an identifier, that identifier is resolved before anything in the body is examined, and a request naming a missing row is answered with that entity's not-found code rather than with a validation error about the payload.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Desk: Elevators, Rail Fleets, Discount Schedules and Risk Limits

Before any grain trades, a `DeskAdmin` registers the merchandising desk itself, supplying a `desk_code` that must be unique across every desk in the system, a `name`, and the `credit_line_usd` the clearing broker has extended. That credit line must be strictly greater than zero. The desk comes into existence with `posture` `NORMAL` and every derived figure — `margin_requirement_usd`, `open_margin_call_usd`, `credit_utilization`, `committed_hedge_lots`, `open_bushel_commitment`, `unpriced_bushels`, `realized_hedge_pnl_usd` — at zero, and `available_credit_usd` equal to the full credit line.

Country elevators hang off the desk. Registering one, again a `DeskAdmin` act, takes the owning `desk_id`, a `location_code` unique across all elevators, a `name`, and an `intake_capacity_bushels` greater than zero. The optional `rail_served` flag says whether the elevator has a rail spur; it defaults to false when omitted, and an elevator without a spur can never host a rail booking. A fresh elevator's `committed_intake_bushels` — the running total of bushels on `ACTIVE` purchase contracts written at that elevator — starts at zero.

Rail car sets are the `FreightCoordinator`'s to register, each with the `home_location_id` of the elevator it is stationed at, a `fleet_code` unique across all fleets, and a `car_count` greater than zero. The car count is a hard ceiling: no booking may ever request more cars than the set physically contains.

Quality discount schedules are the desk's grading rulebook, one live schedule per commodity. A `DeskAdmin` drafts one by naming its `commodity_code` — one of `CORN`, `SOYBEANS` or `HRW_WHEAT` — an `effective_from` instant at or after which it may be put into force, and a non-empty array of `tiers`. Each tier row is an object carrying a `factor` drawn from `MOISTURE`, `TEST_WEIGHT`, `FOREIGN_MATERIAL` and `DAMAGE`; a `direction` of either `ABOVE` or `BELOW`, saying whether the penalty applies to readings above the threshold or below it; a `threshold_value` marking where the penalty begins; a `unit_step` giving the granularity of the penalty; and a `rate_cents_per_bushel_per_step` giving the cents-per-bushel charge for each step. At most one tier row may name any given factor. Two optional numbers round out the draft: `moisture_standard_pct`, the moisture percentage at or below which no shrink and no moisture discount apply, which defaults to 15.0 when omitted; and `shrink_factor_pct_per_point`, the shrink percentage taken per whole point of moisture above that standard, which defaults to 1.18. A drafted schedule sits in `DRAFT` status and is invisible to grading.

Putting a schedule into force is a separate act. Activation requires that the schedule still be in `DRAFT` and that the logical clock have reached or passed its `effective_from`; a schedule activated too early, or a schedule that has already been activated or superseded, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the schedule moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, and every other schedule for the same `commodity_code` that was sitting in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` in the same operation. Superseding is not retroactive: delivery tickets already pinned to the old schedule keep their `discount_schedule_id` and every amount computed from it, unchanged and unrecomputed, forever.

Risk limits are the `RiskOfficer`'s instrument, and exactly one is live at a time for each pairing of desk and commodity. Publishing one names the `desk_id`, the `commodity_code`, a `max_unpriced_bushels` ceiling of at least zero on how much unpriced exposure the desk may carry in that commodity, a `max_net_short_lots` ceiling of at least zero on the absolute size of the desk's net short futures position in that commodity, and the `expected_desk_version` guard described in Section 2.7. Publication moves any prior limit for the same desk and commodity from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, leaves the incoming row as the single `ACTIVE` limit for that pairing, and increments the desk's `version` by one.

### 3.2. Posting Bids and the Bid Lifecycle

A bid is the desk's public offer to buy a commodity at one elevator over one delivery window, quoted as a basis against a board month. A `Merchandiser` drafts it with the `location_id` of the elevator, a `commodity_code`, the `futures_month` the basis is quoted against, the `delivery_period_start` and `delivery_period_end` dates bounding the window — the end date may not precede the start date, and the window is inclusive of both — a `basis_cents` figure that may be positive or negative, a `max_bushels` allowance greater than zero capping how much the desk will contract against this posting in aggregate, and an `expires_at` instant. The draft is created in `DRAFT` status with `contracted_bushels` at zero.

Posting the bid to the market moves it to `ACTIVE`, and this is the transition that guards the desk's market slot. A bid may be activated from `DRAFT` or re-activated from `SUSPENDED`, but in either case two conditions must hold. First, no other bid whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` may already occupy the same market slot, where the slot is the combination of `location_id`, `commodity_code`, `futures_month` and `delivery_period_start`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID`. Second, the logical clock must be strictly before the bid's `expires_at` — a bid whose expiry the clock has already reached or passed is refused with `ERR_BID_EXPIRED`, even when it is still nominally sitting in `DRAFT`. Once `ACTIVE`, the bid becomes eligible in two distinct capacities: as the posting a new purchase contract may be struck against, and as a price source for damages arithmetic when some other contract is terminated.

Bids can be pulled and put back. Suspending an `ACTIVE` bid moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, which takes it off the board without destroying it; contract creation against a suspended bid is refused with `ERR_BID_NOT_ACTIVE`. Re-activation moves it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, subject to the same duplicate-slot and expiry checks as a first activation, and this `ACTIVE ↔ SUSPENDED` loop may be traversed any number of times. Withdrawal is the permanent exit: a bid in either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` may be withdrawn, landing in `WITHDRAWN`, after which it is neither a contract source nor an eligible damages price source. Expiry is the other terminal state and it is never requested directly — advancing the logical clock to or past `expires_at` moves any `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` bid to `EXPIRED` and reports its id back to the caller who moved the clock.

`contracted_bushels` tracks how much of the bid's allowance has been consumed. It is the sum of contracted bushels over every purchase contract referencing this bid whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SETTLED` or `ROLLED`. Contracts that ended in `CANCELLED` or `DEFAULTED` are deliberately excluded, which means a cancellation returns the bid's allowance to the pool while a roll does not — the roll's successor is a new contract that consumes allowance of its own.

### 3.3. The Futures Book: Positions, Marks, Calls and Liquidation

A `FuturesTrader` opens a board position for one `desk_id`, one `commodity_code` and one `futures_month`, declaring a `side` of `SHORT` — selling futures against cash length, the ordinary hedge for a desk that has bought grain — or `LONG`, an initial `lots_open` count greater than zero, and an `avg_entry_price_cents` fill price. Each futures lot represents 5,000 bushels. At creation the position posts initial margin: `posted_margin_usd` becomes the open lot count multiplied by the per-lot initial margin for the commodity — 1,650.00 dollars for `CORN`, 3,300.00 for `SOYBEANS`, 2,200.00 for `HRW_WHEAT` — and `last_settlement_price_cents` is seeded to the entry price so that an unmarked position shows no unrealised profit or loss. The desk's margin requirement, available credit, utilisation and posture are recomputed immediately.

Growing a position is a re-averaging operation. Adding lots takes a `lots` count and a `fill_price_cents`, and rewrites `avg_entry_price_cents` as the volume-weighted blend of the old and new fills: the prior average multiplied by the prior open lots, plus the fill price multiplied by the lots being added, all divided by the sum of the prior open lots and the added lots, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places. `lots_open` then becomes the prior open lots plus the added lots, and `posted_margin_usd` is recomputed as the new open lot count multiplied by the commodity's per-lot initial margin, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Lots may only be added to a position that is `OPEN`; a position sitting in `MARGIN_CALL` or `LIQUIDATED` refuses the request with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Marking to market is the daily discipline. The trader supplies a `settlement_price_cents` and the server recomputes the position's exposure. Direction is carried by a sign that is negative one when `side` is `SHORT` and positive one when `side` is `LONG`. `unrealized_pnl_usd` becomes that sign multiplied by the difference between the supplied settlement price and `avg_entry_price_cents`, multiplied by `lots_open`, multiplied by 5,000 bushels per lot, divided by 100 to convert cents into dollars, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Account equity is `posted_margin_usd` plus that unrealised figure, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The maintenance requirement is `lots_open` multiplied by the per-lot maintenance margin for the commodity — 1,500.00 dollars for `CORN`, 3,000.00 for `SOYBEANS`, 2,000.00 for `HRW_WHEAT` — rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The position is breached only when account equity is **strictly less** than that maintenance requirement; equity exactly equal to maintenance does not call. Whatever the outcome, `last_settlement_price_cents` is updated to the supplied price.

When equity holds at or above maintenance the position stays where it is — an `OPEN → OPEN` self-loop that changes only the mark. When equity falls below maintenance, the position moves `OPEN → MARGIN_CALL` and a call row is raised against it. The call's `call_amount_usd` is the amount needed to restore the position to full initial margin: the initial requirement — `lots_open` multiplied by the commodity's per-lot initial margin, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — less the account equity just computed, floored at zero so a call is never negative, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Its `issued_at` is the logical clock reading at that moment and its `cure_deadline_at` is exactly 24 hours later. From there the four-hop cascade of Section 2.10 runs: the desk's open call total rises, utilisation and available credit are recomputed, posture is rewritten, and a posture of `FROZEN` locks pricing on the desk's unfinished contracts.

Curing a call is the `RiskOfficer`'s act and it takes a single `payment_usd` figure. The call must still be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the logical clock must be at or before `cure_deadline_at`, with a cure landing on exactly that instant still accepted and anything strictly later refused with `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_EXPIRED`; and the payment must be at least the full `call_amount_usd`, with anything smaller refused as `ERR_CURE_AMOUNT_INSUFFICIENT` — partial cures are not a concept here. On success the call moves `OPEN → CURED` with `cured_amount_usd` set to the payment actually made, the payment is added to the position's `posted_margin_usd`, and the position moves `MARGIN_CALL → OPEN`. The desk's open call total, available credit, utilisation and posture are recomputed, and if posture leaves `FROZEN` the `pricing_locked` flag is cleared on every `ACTIVE` contract of that desk. A single position may travel `OPEN → MARGIN_CALL → OPEN` any number of times over its life.

Liquidation closes a position at a supplied `settlement_price_cents`, and it has two distinct entry points with deliberately different preconditions. The voluntary path runs from `OPEN` and requires that the position carry no committed lots at all — `lots_committed` must be zero, meaning no `ACTIVE` purchase contract is still relying on it — and a position with live commitments is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The forced path runs from `MARGIN_CALL` and carries **no** committed-lots precondition whatsoever; what it requires instead is that the position's open call be past its cure deadline, that is, `cure_deadline_at` strictly earlier than the current logical clock. On the forced path the outstanding call moves `OPEN → DEFAULTED` as part of the same operation.

Both paths book the same arithmetic. Realised profit and loss increases by the side sign multiplied by the difference between the supplied settlement price and `avg_entry_price_cents`, multiplied by `lots_open`, multiplied by 5,000, divided by 100, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and that amount is added to the position's `realized_pnl_usd`. `posted_margin_usd` and `unrealized_pnl_usd` are both zeroed and the position lands in `LIQUIDATED`. Because the desk's margin requirement and `committed_hedge_lots` both count only positions whose status is `OPEN` or `MARGIN_CALL`, a forced liquidation drops the position out of both aggregates even though `ACTIVE` contracts pointing at it keep their assigned lot counts on their own rows. The desk's margin requirement, open call total, committed lots, available credit, utilisation, realised hedge profit and posture are all recomputed on either path. `lots_available` on any position is simply its open lots less its committed lots.

### 3.4. Striking a Purchase Contract

Striking a contract is the moment cash and board meet, and it is a `Merchandiser` act. The payload names the `bid_id` of the posting being hit, the `hedge_position_id` of the board position that will carry the contract's hedge, a `contract_type`, the `bushels` being bought, the `seller_name` of the producer or counterparty, and the `delivery_start` and `delivery_end` dates of the agreed window. Two further fields are conditional: `futures_price_cents` is **required** when `contract_type` is `CASH` or `HTA` and **must be omitted** when it is `BASIS` or `DEFERRED`, and `settlement_basis` is optional, choosing between `SHRINK_FIRST` and `GROSS_GRADE` and defaulting to `SHRINK_FIRST`.

The four contract types differ only in which price legs are nailed down at signing. `CASH` fixes both: the futures leg from the supplied `futures_price_cents` and the basis leg copied from the bid's `basis_cents`. `HTA` — hedge-to-arrive — fixes the futures leg from the supplied price and leaves the basis floating. `BASIS` copies the bid's basis into the contract and leaves the futures leg floating. `DEFERRED` fixes neither and is therefore the type that carries the most naked exposure. Whichever legs are fixed at signing count as fully priced for their whole bushel quantity: a `CASH` contract is born with both `priced_futures_bushels` and `priced_basis_bushels` equal to its `bushels` and a `pricing_status` of `FULLY_PRICED`; an `HTA` contract is born with `priced_futures_bushels` equal to its bushels and `priced_basis_bushels` at zero; a `BASIS` contract is the mirror image; and a `DEFERRED` contract is born with both at zero and a `pricing_status` of `UNPRICED`.

Several fields are copied rather than supplied. The contract's `desk_id` and `location_id` are taken from the bid's elevator, and its `commodity_code` and `futures_month` are taken from the bid. `created_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. `rolled_from_contract_id` is null on a directly struck contract, `roll_adjustment_cents` starts at zero, `damages_usd` starts at zero, `termination_reason` is null, `delivered_bushels` and `settled_value_usd` start at zero, `pricing_locked` starts false, `delivery_status` starts at `OPEN`, and the lifecycle `status` starts at `ACTIVE`. The `gross_unit_price_cents` a contract trades at is the sum of three components — `futures_price_cents`, `basis_cents` and `roll_adjustment_cents` — and it remains null for as long as either of the first two is unset. `unpriced_bushels` on the contract is its `bushels` less the smaller of `priced_futures_bushels` and `priced_basis_bushels`, so a contract is only free of unpriced exposure once **both** legs are covered.

Ten conditions gate the strike, and the rejection ladder of Section 2.8 decides which one is reported when several fail at once. The bid must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_BID_NOT_ACTIVE` answers. The nominated hedge position must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_OPEN` answers. The position must agree with the bid on all three of desk, commodity and board month — the position's `desk_id` equal to the desk owning the bid's elevator, its `commodity_code` equal to the bid's, and its `futures_month` equal to the bid's — with any disagreement reported as `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_MISMATCH`. The requested delivery window must sit wholly inside the bid's posted period: the bid's `delivery_period_start` must be at or before `delivery_start`, `delivery_start` must be at or before `delivery_end`, and `delivery_end` must be at or before the bid's `delivery_period_end`. Containment is on the closed interval, so a window whose endpoints coincide exactly with the bid's is acceptable; anything else is `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Then come the desk gates. A desk whose `posture` is `FROZEN` cannot write new business at all and answers `ERR_DESK_FROZEN`. A desk whose `posture` is `RESTRICTED` can still write `CASH`, `HTA` and `BASIS` contracts but refuses a `DEFERRED` one with `ERR_DESK_RESTRICTED`. Next the risk limit: if an `ACTIVE` risk limit exists for this desk and commodity, the desk's current `unpriced_bushels` plus the unpriced bushels the new contract would itself carry must not be **strictly greater** than `max_unpriced_bushels`. A projected total landing exactly on the ceiling is accepted; only a total above it is refused, with `ERR_RISK_LIMIT_BREACH`. When no `ACTIVE` limit exists for that desk-and-commodity pairing, the check is skipped entirely rather than treated as a limit of zero.

Elevator capacity is next. The contracts already booked at the same elevator whose closed delivery date interval overlaps the new contract's closed interval are summed by contracted bushels — counting only contracts whose `status` is `ACTIVE` — and adding the new contract's bushels must not push that sum past the elevator's `intake_capacity_bushels`. Overlap here is generous: two windows that merely touch at a single date, one ending on the day the other begins, **do** count as overlapping, because the same pit is receiving both loads. Breaching capacity is `ERR_LOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Two fiscal checks close the ladder. `hedge_lots_assigned` is the contract's `bushels` divided by 5,000 bushels per lot, rounded half-up to a whole number of lots — so 12,500 bushels, which is two and a half lots, reserves three. The nominated position's open lots less its committed lots must be at least that many, or the strike is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_HEDGE_LOTS`; the committed figure counts only contracts on that same position whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Finally, the bid's `contracted_bushels` plus the new contract's `bushels` must not exceed the bid's `max_bushels`, or `ERR_BID_MAX_BUSHELS_EXCEEDED` answers.

A successful strike commits the assigned lots on the hedge position and recomputes, in one pass, the bid's `contracted_bushels`, the elevator's `committed_intake_bushels`, and the desk's `committed_hedge_lots`, `open_bushel_commitment` and `unpriced_bushels`.

### 3.5. Fixing the Floating Legs

A contract with a floating leg is priced incrementally through pricing orders, and this is the one write operation two different roles share: both a `Merchandiser` and a `FuturesTrader` may place an order, on either component. Each order names the `contract_id` it applies to, a `component` of `FUTURES` or `BASIS`, the `bushels` it prices — strictly greater than zero — and the `price_cents` at which that quantity is fixed. The server stamps `placed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Pricing orders are immutable event rows: they carry an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else in the way of common properties, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Which components a contract will accept follows from its type, and this is the substance of `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_PRICED`. A `CASH` contract has both legs fixed at signing and accepts no pricing orders at all. An `HTA` contract accepts `BASIS` orders only, its futures leg having been fixed at signing. A `BASIS` contract accepts `FUTURES` orders only. A `DEFERRED` contract accepts both. Beyond the type rule, the named component must still have floating bushels left, and the order's `bushels` must not exceed the bushels still floating on that component. Any of those three failures — wrong component for the type, component already fully fixed, or an over-large order — is reported as `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_PRICED`.

Three further conditions guard the operation. The contract must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` answers. When the component is `FUTURES`, the contract's hedge position must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_OPEN` answers; a `BASIS` order carries no such requirement because it does not touch the board. And the contract's `pricing_locked` flag must be false, or `ERR_PRICING_LOCKED` answers — a check that, as Section 2.8 fixes, is evaluated **before** the desk-posture gate, so a locked contract on a frozen desk reports the lock rather than `ERR_DESK_FROZEN`.

Pricing is cumulative and is always recomputed from the whole order history rather than blended incrementally. On each accepted order the server gathers every pricing order on that contract for that same component, including the one just placed, in `created_at` ascending order. `priced_futures_bushels` (for a `FUTURES` order) or `priced_basis_bushels` (for a `BASIS` order) becomes the sum of those orders' bushels. The leg price — `futures_price_cents` or `basis_cents` respectively — becomes the sum, over those orders, of each order's price multiplied by its bushels, divided by the total priced bushels for the component, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places.

Everything downstream then falls out. `unpriced_bushels` on the contract becomes its `bushels` less the smaller of `priced_futures_bushels` and `priced_basis_bushels`. `pricing_status` becomes `FULLY_PRICED` when both priced figures are at least the contract's bushels, `UNPRICED` when both are still zero, and `PARTIALLY_PRICED` in every other case. `gross_unit_price_cents` is re-derived as the futures leg plus the basis leg plus `roll_adjustment_cents`, and the desk's `unpriced_bushels` aggregate is recomputed. One further consequence deserves emphasis: when a pricing order is what finally makes a contract `FULLY_PRICED`, and that contract's `delivery_status` is already `COMPLETE` because its bushels were delivered before they were priced, the contract's lifecycle status advances `ACTIVE → SETTLED` in the same request, dragging the hedge-lift, desk-recompute and pricing-unlock cascade hops behind it exactly as a final delivery would.

### 3.6. Rail Car Acquisition and Fleet Occupancy

Moving grain by rail means holding a set of cars for a window, and a `FreightCoordinator` reserves one by naming the `fleet_id`, the `contract_id` the cars will serve, a `cars_requested` count greater than zero, a `placement_at` instant when the cars are spotted at the elevator, a `release_at` instant strictly after placement when they are released, and the `expected_fleet_version` guard of Section 2.7. The booking is created in `REQUESTED` status with `substitution_count` at zero, and the reserving fleet's `version` increments by one.

Occupancy is not the placement window alone. The cars are unavailable to anyone else until they have been turned around, so the booking's `occupancy_end_at` is computed as `release_at` plus a 48-hour turnaround buffer, and the interval the booking occupies runs from `placement_at` up to but **not including** `occupancy_end_at`. Two bookings on the same fleet conflict when their half-open intervals intersect; intervals that merely touch — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — do not conflict. Only bookings whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` block; completed and cancelled bookings release their hold. A conflicting reservation is refused with `ERR_FLEET_UNAVAILABLE`.

Two referential conditions bound the reservation as well. The contract must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` answers. The contract's elevator must have `rail_served` set true and the fleet must be homed at that same elevator, meaning the fleet's `home_location_id` equals the contract's `location_id`; either failure is `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_RAIL_SERVED`. And `cars_requested` may not exceed the fleet's `car_count`, or `ERR_CARS_EXCEED_FLEET` answers.

A still-`REQUESTED` booking can be moved onto different iron without losing its place in the schedule. Substitution names a `new_fleet_id` and leaves the booking in `REQUESTED` — a self-loop rather than a state change. The replacement fleet must be homed at the booking's contract elevator, must have a `car_count` at least as large as the booking's `cars_requested`, and must have no `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` booking whose interval intersects this booking's own placement-to-occupancy-end interval. The three failure codes are the same ones the original reservation uses: `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_RAIL_SERVED`, `ERR_CARS_EXCEED_FLEET` and `ERR_FLEET_UNAVAILABLE`. On success the booking's `fleet_id` becomes the new fleet, `substitution_count` rises by one, and the **new** fleet's `version` increments by one. A booking that has already been confirmed can no longer be substituted and answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Confirming a booking moves it `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`, and that is the state a booking must be in before any delivery ticket carrying its id may be applied. Cancellation is available from either `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED`, takes a `cancellation_reason` that is stored on the booking, lands the booking in `CANCELLED` and frees the fleet's occupancy interval for other reservations. The remaining transition is never requested directly: a `CONFIRMED` booking moves to `COMPLETED` automatically at the moment a delivery ticket carrying its id is applied to a contract, which likewise frees the occupancy interval.

### 3.7. Grading an Inbound Load at the Scale

When a truck or a string of cars reaches the pit, a `GrainInspector` grades the load. The payload names the `contract_id` the load is meant for, the `location_id` of the elevator that took it in, the `gross_bushels` the scale weight converts to before any shrink — strictly greater than zero — and four measured quality readings: `moisture_pct` and `foreign_material_pct` and `damage_pct`, each at least zero, and `test_weight_lbs` in pounds per bushel, strictly greater than zero. An optional `freight_booking_id` is supplied when the load moved by rail and is left out for a truck delivery, in which case it is null.

Three conditions gate grading. The contract must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` answers. The ticket's `location_id` must equal the contract's own `location_id` — grain delivered to the wrong elevator is not this contract's grain — with a mismatch reported as `ERR_LOCATION_MISMATCH`. And there must be a discount schedule in `ACTIVE` status for the contract's commodity; when none exists, grading is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_DISCOUNT_SCHEDULE`. That single `ACTIVE` schedule is the only one ever consulted — a `DRAFT` schedule and a `SUPERSEDED` schedule are both invisible here — and its id is written onto the ticket as `discount_schedule_id`, pinning the grading rules for the life of the ticket even after a newer schedule supersedes it.

Grading computes everything at once and freezes the result. The acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `graded_by_user_id`, the ticket lands in `GRADED` status, and **nothing else in the system moves**: no contract field, no hedge position, no desk aggregate and no freight booking changes at grading time. All of that waits for application.

**Step one — shrink and net bushels.** The contract's `settlement_basis` selects between two settlement variants that differ in how moisture is charged for, and the choice is made once, on the contract, and applies to every load delivered against it. Under `SHRINK_FIRST`, the excess moisture is the measured `moisture_pct` less the schedule's `moisture_standard_pct`, floored at zero so a dry load has no excess; `shrink_pct` is that excess multiplied by the schedule's `shrink_factor_pct_per_point`; and `net_bushels` is `gross_bushels` multiplied by one minus one-hundredth of `shrink_pct`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Under `GROSS_GRADE`, no bushels are removed at all: `shrink_pct` is zero and `net_bushels` is simply `gross_bushels` rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Note that the moisture standard is a ceiling that is itself penalty-free — a load reading exactly the standard has zero excess points and therefore zero shrink; only a reading strictly above it shrinks.

**Step two — quality discounts.** Four factors are evaluated, always in this exact order and never any other: `MOISTURE` first, then `TEST_WEIGHT`, then `FOREIGN_MATERIAL`, then `DAMAGE`. Each factor reads its measured value from the ticket — `MOISTURE` from `moisture_pct`, `TEST_WEIGHT` from `test_weight_lbs`, `FOREIGN_MATERIAL` from `foreign_material_pct`, `DAMAGE` from `damage_pct` — and looks up the schedule's tier row bearing that `factor`. A factor with no tier row on the schedule contributes a rate of zero, and therefore an amount of exactly 0.00.

The two settlement variants diverge sharply on moisture here, and this is the heart of the dual-variant design. Under `SHRINK_FIRST` the `MOISTURE` factor's rate is forced to zero, because the shrink taken in step one has already extracted the moisture penalty and charging the tier as well would be charging twice. Under `GROSS_GRADE` no shrink was taken, so the `MOISTURE` tier is evaluated normally and becomes a cents-per-bushel drying charge. The other three factors are evaluated identically under both variants; what differs is only that under `SHRINK_FIRST` they extend on the shrunken bushels while under `GROSS_GRADE` they extend on the full gross bushels, since in that variant the two quantities are the same number.

For each evaluated factor, the number of penalty steps depends on the tier's `direction`. When `direction` is `ABOVE`, the shortfall is the measured value less the tier's `threshold_value`, floored at zero; when `direction` is `BELOW`, it is the tier's `threshold_value` less the measured value, floored at zero. That shortfall is divided by the tier's `unit_step` and **rounded up to the next whole step**, so any part of a step counts as a whole one. The factor's rate is the step count multiplied by `rate_cents_per_bushel_per_step`.

The rounding order is pinned and it matters. Each factor's dollar amount is computed on its own — `net_bushels` multiplied by that factor's rate, divided by 100 to turn cents into dollars, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — and only then are the four rounded per-factor amounts added together to give `discount_total_usd`. Summing the four rates first and rounding the product once at the end is explicitly **not** the rule, and on real numbers the two orders can differ by a cent.

**Step three — service fee and settlement value.** These four figures depend on the contract having a fixed price. When the contract's `gross_unit_price_cents` is null — because at least one leg is still floating — then `gross_contract_value_usd`, `service_fee_cents_per_bushel`, `net_unit_price_cents` and `settlement_value_usd` are all null on the ticket, and the load is still perfectly gradeable and appliable; it simply carries no money with it. When the contract does have a gross unit price, the computation runs in a fixed four-step order whose apparent circularity is deliberate.

First, `gross_contract_value_usd` is `net_bushels` multiplied by the contract's `gross_unit_price_cents`, divided by 100, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Second, the merchandising service fee tier is selected **from that gross figure**: a gross value below 50,000.00 dollars draws a fee of 3.0 cents per bushel, a gross value below 250,000.00 dollars draws 2.0 cents per bushel, and anything at or above 250,000.00 dollars draws 1.0 cent per bushel — both breakpoints being exclusive upper bounds, so a value landing exactly on 50,000.00 falls into the second band and one landing exactly on 250,000.00 falls into the third. The chosen figure is stored as `service_fee_cents_per_bushel`. Crucially, the tier is never re-derived from the post-fee value: a load whose gross value clears a breakpoint but whose net value would fall back below it keeps the tier the gross value selected. Third, `net_unit_price_cents` is the contract's `gross_unit_price_cents` less `service_fee_cents_per_bushel`. Fourth, `settlement_value_usd` is `net_bushels` multiplied by `net_unit_price_cents`, divided by 100, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and then reduced by the whole of `discount_total_usd` — the discount total, already rounded per factor, is subtracted from the rounded net extension rather than folded into it before rounding.

Only the grading ticket's own `net_bushels` and its contract's `gross_unit_price_cents` participate; no other ticket already sitting against the same contract influences the arithmetic.

### 3.8. Applying, Rejecting and Reversing Loads

A graded ticket sits in limbo until someone decides what to do with it, and the three dispositions belong to two different roles. Application — the act that actually credits the contract — is the `Merchandiser`'s. Rejection and reversal are the `GrainInspector`'s.

Applying a ticket moves it `GRADED → APPLIED` and requires four things. The ticket must currently be `GRADED`, or `ERR_TICKET_NOT_GRADED` answers. The contract must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` answers. The ticket's `net_bushels` must fit inside what the contract still has coming — that is, at most the contract's `bushels` less its `delivered_bushels` — with an over-large load refused as `ERR_BUSHELS_EXCEEDED`. And when the ticket carries a `freight_booking_id`, the booking it names must be in `CONFIRMED` status, or `ERR_FREIGHT_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED` answers; a booking still merely `REQUESTED`, or one already `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED`, blocks the application.

On success the contract's `delivered_bushels` grows by the ticket's `net_bushels` and its `settled_value_usd` grows by the ticket's `settlement_value_usd`, where a null settlement value is treated as zero. `delivery_status` is then re-derived: `OPEN` while nothing has been delivered, `PARTIALLY_DELIVERED` once some bushels have arrived but the contract's full quantity has not, and `COMPLETE` once delivered bushels reach or exceed the contracted quantity. The desk's `open_bushel_commitment` is recomputed, and a linked freight booking transitions `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`, releasing its rail cars' occupancy interval.

Settlement is the consequence, not a separate request. A contract whose `delivery_status` has reached `COMPLETE` while its `pricing_status` is `FULLY_PRICED` transitions `ACTIVE → SETTLED` on the spot, and that transition carries the deep cascade. The hedge lots the contract had reserved are lifted from its position: `lots_open` on the position decreases by the contract's `hedge_lots_assigned`, the position's committed lot count is recomputed, its `realized_pnl_usd` grows by the side sign multiplied by the difference between the position's `last_settlement_price_cents` and its `avg_entry_price_cents`, multiplied by the lifted lot count, multiplied by 5,000 bushels per lot, divided by 100, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places; and its `posted_margin_usd` decreases by the lifted lot count multiplied by the commodity's per-lot initial margin. Because open lots and posted margin both moved, the desk's `margin_requirement_usd`, `committed_hedge_lots`, `open_bushel_commitment`, `unpriced_bushels`, `available_credit_usd`, `credit_utilization`, `realized_hedge_pnl_usd` and `posture` are all recomputed; and if that recomputation pulls the desk out of `FROZEN`, `pricing_locked` is set back to false on every `ACTIVE` contract of the desk. The same `ACTIVE → SETTLED` transition is reachable from the other direction, when the final pricing order arrives after delivery is already complete, as Section 3.5 describes.

Rejecting a load is the simplest disposition and the least consequential. It requires only that the ticket be `GRADED`, refusing anything else with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_GRADED`, takes a `rejection_reason` which is stored on the ticket as `disposition_reason`, and moves the ticket `GRADED → REJECTED`. No contract field, no hedge position, no desk aggregate and no freight booking changes — the load is simply turned away.

Reversal undoes an application that should not have happened. It requires the ticket to be `APPLIED`, refusing anything else — including a merely `GRADED` ticket — with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_GRADED`, and it requires the contract to still be `ACTIVE`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A `reversal_reason` is captured into the ticket's `disposition_reason` and the ticket lands in `REVERSED`. The compensation is exact and symmetric: the contract's `delivered_bushels` shrinks by the ticket's `net_bushels`, its `settled_value_usd` shrinks by the ticket's `settlement_value_usd` (again treating null as zero), `delivery_status` is re-derived from the reduced figure, and the desk's `open_bushel_commitment` returns to precisely the value it held before the application. What is *not* undone is the grading itself: `shrink_pct`, `net_bushels`, `discount_total_usd`, `gross_contract_value_usd`, `service_fee_cents_per_bushel`, `net_unit_price_cents`, `settlement_value_usd` and `discount_schedule_id` all remain on the reversed ticket exactly as they were computed at the scale.

### 3.9. Rolling Forward, Terminating, and Reading the Position

**Rolling.** When delivery slips past the board month a contract was hedged in, a `Merchandiser` rolls the undelivered balance forward. The request names a `new_hedge_position_id` in the later month, a `new_delivery_start` and `new_delivery_end` for the successor's window, and a `roll_spread_cents` figure capturing the market spread between the two board months. The contract being rolled must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` answers; its delivery axis must not already have reached `COMPLETE`, since a fully delivered contract has no balance to carry; and its `pricing_locked` flag must be false, or `ERR_PRICING_LOCKED` answers — checked, as always, ahead of the desk-posture gate, which itself refuses a `FROZEN` desk with `ERR_DESK_FROZEN`. The nominated position must be `OPEN` (`ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_OPEN`), must sit on the same desk and the same commodity as the original (`ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_MISMATCH` otherwise — on a roll the successor takes its board month from the new position, so only desk and commodity need to agree), and must have at least as many uncommitted lots as the successor will require (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_HEDGE_LOTS`). The successor's window is checked for containment inside the bid's posted delivery period exactly as an original strike is (`ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_INVALID`), and the elevator's intake capacity is re-tested against the successor's overlapping window (`ERR_LOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`).

The roll retires the original and mints a successor in one request. The original moves `ACTIVE → ROLLED` and releases its hedge lots. The successor is created carrying `rolled_from_contract_id` pointing back at the original — the one self-referential link in the model — with `bushels` set to the original's `bushels` less its `delivered_bushels`, `futures_month` taken from the new position, and `roll_adjustment_cents` set to the supplied `roll_spread_cents` less a roll service fee of 2.0 cents per bushel that the desk charges for handling the roll. Contract type, `settlement_basis`, `bid_id`, `seller_name` and `basis_cents` all carry across unchanged, and the successor's own `hedge_lots_assigned` is computed from its reduced bushel quantity and committed on the new position. The desk's `committed_hedge_lots`, `open_bushel_commitment` and `unpriced_bushels` are recomputed across both rows.

**Terminating.** Two terminal paths end a live contract early, and both belong to the `RiskOfficer`. Mutual cancellation takes a `damages_method` of either `MARKET_LOSS` or `REPLACEMENT_COST` together with a `cancellation_reason`, requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, and — this is the segregation of duties the role split exists for — requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the contract's `created_by_user_id`; a `RiskOfficer` cancelling a contract they themselves struck is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Declaring seller default takes a `default_reason`, requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, requires its delivery axis not to have reached `COMPLETE`, and requires that the delivery grace period have run out: the logical clock must be strictly after the last millisecond of the `delivery_end` date — that is, `23:59:59.999Z` on that date — plus a further 10 days. A declaration made any earlier is refused with `ERR_GRACE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. Default always assesses damages under `MARKET_LOSS`; it accepts no choice of method.

Both paths write the reason text into the contract's `termination_reason` — `cancellation_reason` on the cancel path, `default_reason` on the default path — store the computed damages in `damages_usd`, and land the contract in `CANCELLED` or `DEFAULTED` respectively. Both release the contract's hedge lots back to its position and recompute the elevator's `committed_intake_bushels` alongside the desk's `committed_hedge_lots`, `open_bushel_commitment` and `unpriced_bushels`, restoring the conserved quantities to their pre-contract readings.

**Damages arithmetic.** A contract that was never fully priced owes nothing on termination: when `pricing_status` is anything other than `FULLY_PRICED`, `damages_usd` is exactly 0.00 and **no bid lookup is performed at all** — meaning neither `ERR_NO_MARKET_BID` nor `ERR_NO_REPLACEMENT_BID` can arise for such a contract. For a `FULLY_PRICED` contract the calculation is a forward-looking replacement of the bushels the desk will not receive. The undelivered quantity is the contract's `bushels` less its `delivered_bushels`. The futures reference is the `last_settlement_price_cents` carried on the contract's hedge position. A reference basis is drawn from the eligible bid pool, in a way that differs by variant. The market unit price is the futures reference plus that reference basis. Damages are then the amount by which the market unit price exceeds the contract's `gross_unit_price_cents`, floored at zero so a market that has fallen produces exactly 0.00 rather than a credit to the seller, multiplied by the undelivered bushels, divided by 100, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — plus the variant's administrative fee, added after that rounding.

The two variants differ only in which bids are eligible, how they are ranked, and what fee rides along. Under `MARKET_LOSS` — the choice on cancel, and the only behaviour on default — the reference basis comes from the **local** pool: bids whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `location_id` equals the contract's own `location_id`, whose `commodity_code` matches the contract's, whose `delivery_period_start` is at or before the contract's `delivery_end`, and whose `delivery_period_end` is at or after that same `delivery_end`. The pool is ranked by `created_at` descending, so the most recently posted local bid wins, with `basis_cents` descending as the tie-break. An empty pool refuses the whole request with `ERR_NO_MARKET_BID`. The administrative fee under this variant is 0.00.

Under `REPLACEMENT_COST` — available only on cancel — the reference basis comes from the **desk-wide** pool: bids whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `commodity_code` matches the contract's, posted at any elevator belonging to the contract's own desk, and satisfying the same delivery-window containment, namely `delivery_period_start` at or before the contract's `delivery_end` and `delivery_period_end` at or after it. This pool is ranked by `basis_cents` descending — the dearest basis the desk would have to pay to replace the bushels — with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. An empty pool refuses the request with `ERR_NO_REPLACEMENT_BID`. The administrative fee under this variant is 250.00 dollars, added to the rounded damages figure. In both variants only `ACTIVE` bids are eligible: a `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` bid is never a price source.

**Position reporting.** The desk's risk picture is available as a computed read, open to a `DeskAdmin`, a `Merchandiser`, a `FuturesTrader` or a `RiskOfficer`, and optionally narrowed to a single commodity by supplying a `commodity_code` query parameter. The report's scope is the desk's purchase contracts whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, narrowed to the requested commodity when one is given; settled, cancelled, defaulted and rolled contracts are all outside the scope, as are liquidated hedge positions. It returns `desk_id` and the `commodity_code` it was narrowed to, together with eight figures. `cash_long_bushels` is the sum over the scoped contracts of contracted bushels less delivered bushels. `unpriced_bushels` is the sum of the scoped contracts' own unpriced quantities. `net_futures_lots` is the sum, over the desk's hedge positions whose `status` is `OPEN` or `MARGIN_CALL` and narrowed the same way, of the position's side sign multiplied by its `lots_open` — so short lots enter negative and long lots positive. `required_hedge_lots` is `cash_long_bushels` divided by 5,000 bushels per lot, rounded half-up to a whole number. `hedge_coverage_ratio` is zero when no lots are required — an empty book yields zeros and never a division by zero — and otherwise the absolute value of `net_futures_lots` divided by `required_hedge_lots`, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places. `margin_requirement_usd`, `open_margin_call_usd`, `credit_utilization` and `posture` are read straight off the desk.

The last figure, `risk_limit_status`, compares the report against the published limit. When the report has not been narrowed to a single commodity, or when no `ACTIVE` risk limit exists for the desk-and-commodity pairing, it reads `NO_LIMIT`. Otherwise it reads `BREACH` when the scoped `unpriced_bushels` strictly exceeds the limit's `max_unpriced_bushels`, or when the absolute size of the net **short** position — the magnitude of `net_futures_lots` when that figure is negative, and zero when it is not — strictly exceeds `max_net_short_lots`; and `WITHIN` when neither ceiling is breached.

**Reading the books.** Every collection in the system is listable and filterable, and the filters cover the foreign keys each entity declares. Elevators may be narrowed by `desk_id`; rail fleets by `home_location_id`; discount schedules by `commodity_code` and `status`; risk limits by `desk_id`, `commodity_code` and `status`; bids by `location_id`, `commodity_code` and `status`; hedge positions by `desk_id`, `commodity_code` and `status`; margin calls by `hedge_position_id` and `status`; pricing orders by `contract_id` and `component`; purchase contracts by `bid_id`, `desk_id`, `location_id`, `hedge_position_id`, `rolled_from_contract_id` and `status`; delivery tickets by `contract_id`, `location_id`, `freight_booking_id`, `discount_schedule_id` and `status`; and freight bookings by `fleet_id`, `contract_id` and `status`. Every one of these lists honours the `limit` and `offset` pagination of Section 2.3 and the `created_at` ascending ordering, and every one of them is readable by all six roles.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every collection in this API is a peer resource mounted directly under `/grain`; parentage travels in request bodies and query filters, never in nested URL segments. Operations that bring a new resource into existence answer **201 Created** — registering a desk, an elevator, a rail fleet, a discount schedule or a risk limit, drafting a bid, opening a hedge position, striking a purchase contract, rolling one (the successor is a new resource), placing a pricing order, grading a delivery ticket, and reserving a freight booking. Every other successful operation — reads, lifecycle transitions, cures, cancellations and the clock capability — answers **200 OK**, returning the affected resource in the `data` field.
