# Wine Grape Contract & Weigh Tag Settlement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every autumn a winery's grower-relations desk lives or dies by paperwork that arrives faster than anyone can reconcile it. A single day of harvest produces dozens of truckloads, each one weighed twice at the crush pad, graded in the receipt lab, priced against a contract signed months earlier, and then charged against a tonnage cap that nobody can see in real time. When the vintage ends, the winery must convert that pile of weigh tags into a settlement statement that survives audit — and into an installment payment schedule that satisfies the statutory producer lien protecting the grower's claim on the fruit. Today that reconciliation happens in spreadsheets, and the two most expensive mistakes are always the same: fruit accepted after the contract cap was already full, and a price adjustment computed in the wrong order.

This document specifies the Wine Grape Contract & Weigh Tag Settlement API, the system of record for the entire grower relationship: contract structure and negotiation, sugar/acid/rot/MOG quality specifications with their bonus, penalty and rejection provisions, harvest sampling and pick-call notification, crush-pad delivery appointments, gross/tare/net weight capture, quality grading at receipt, tonnage-cap enforcement with over-delivery acceptance or rejection, settlement with installments and lien holdback, and multi-year term renewal with evergreen and termination provisions. All grower-relations resources are mounted under the `/grower-relations` prefix; two additional administrative endpoints live under `/_clock` and exist so the harvest calendar can be driven deterministically.

Two design commitments shape the whole specification. First, **the winery never guesses at money**: every price adjustment, every tonnage slice and every installment amount is derived by an explicitly ordered calculation whose intermediate rounding points are part of the contract, because the same inputs evaluated in a different order produce a different — and legally indefensible — number. Second, **the crush pad enforces segregation of duties**: the operator who weighs a load may never be the person who grades it, and every reader of a load's state must prove they have observed the version they are acting on. The remainder of this document defines the global contract in §2, the domain workflows in §3, the endpoint surface in §4, and the error catalogue in §5.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere in the API: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and audit fields, the logical clock, arithmetic discipline, stale-version protection, and the order in which rules are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — without exception, including the clock endpoints — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal acts. If either header is absent or blank, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` and HTTP 401. Six roles exist, and the spelling is exact: `GrowerRelationsManager`, `Grower`, `HarvestCoordinator`, `ScaleOperator`, `QualityLabTech`, and `AccountsPayableClerk`. If `X-User-Role` is not one of those six strings, or is one of them but is not listed among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The `GrowerRelationsManager` is the winery-side contract owner: it registers producers and blocks, drafts contracts, puts them on the table, withdraws offers, closes the vintage settlement, renews the term and serves termination notices. It is the only role permitted to touch a contract's commercial terms. The `HarvestCoordinator` runs the harvest desk — booking sampling windows, recording field readings, issuing and revising pick calls, and managing crush-pad slots. The `ScaleOperator` staffs the scale house and captures the inbound and outbound weight passes; it deliberately cannot grade. The `QualityLabTech` records the receipt grading that drives price adjustment, load rejection and tonnage allocation; it cannot capture weights, and it cannot grade a load it captured itself. The `AccountsPayableClerk` pays the installment schedule and reconciles the statutory producer-lien holdback, and may not alter contract terms, weights or gradings. The `Grower` is the counterparty: it counters and accepts offers, acknowledges pick calls, books delivery slots and disputes gradings.

Identity binding differs between the producer role and the five winery roles. When `X-User-Role` is `Grower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Grower` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Grower` compare the resource's producer reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a contract action is permitted only when the contract's `grower_id` equals `X-User-ID`; a block read is permitted only when the block's `grower_id` equals `X-User-ID`; and a grade dispute is permitted only when the producer reached from the weigh tag through its appointment, that appointment's pick call, and that pick call's contract equals `X-User-ID`. Any attempt by a producer to act on another producer's rows is refused with `ERR_NOT_CONTRACT_GROWER` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `GrowerRelationsManager`, `HarvestCoordinator`, `ScaleOperator`, `QualityLabTech` or `AccountsPayableClerk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition demands it — as `captured_by_user_id` on a weigh tag when a `ScaleOperator` opens the tag, and as `graded_by_user_id` on a grading row when a grader records one — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

The recorded operator identity is what makes segregation of duties enforceable. When a grading is recorded, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `captured_by_user_id` stored on the weigh tag being graded; if the same person weighed and now attempts to grade the load, the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_SAME_USER_GRADING` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is negative or not an integer, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400 — note the deliberate distinction from the 422 used for general request-shape problems.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the installment collection, which is ordered by `installment_no` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, so that the first element of a contract-filtered page is always that contract's `installment_no` 1.

Every list response repeats `limit` and `offset` in the `meta` object alongside `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity and the Audit Trail

Every resource in the system is addressed by an `id` that the server mints: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, same format, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Growers, vineyard blocks, quality specifications, grape contracts, contract-block allocations, crush-pad lanes, sampling crews, sampling visits, pick calls, delivery appointments, weigh tags, settlement lines and payment installments all behave this way. **Grading rows are the single exception.** A grading is an append-only lab record: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is never modified after it is written — a correction is expressed by writing a newer grading and stamping `superseded_at` on the older one, not by editing anything.

Recomputation counts as mutation. When a load is graded and the cascade recomputes a contract's delivered tonnage, that contract's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if a client had written to it directly. The same holds for a lane whose committed tonnage was recomputed because an appointment elsewhere was cancelled, and for a producer whose reliability scores were recomputed because a pick call expired.

### 2.5. Flat Addressing and Left-to-Right Path Validation

The resource topology is deliberately flat: every entity is a peer addressed at `/grower-relations/<collection>/{id}`, and no resource is nested inside another's URL. Parentage travels in request bodies as identifier fields, and children are discovered through filtered collection reads — an allocation collection filtered by contract, a weigh-tag collection filtered by appointment, a settlement-line collection filtered by contract or by weigh tag. Settlement lines and payment installments are created only as cascade side effects and are never posted directly, so their filtered collection reads are the only channel through which a client can discover them.

Where a path carries an identifier, that identifier is resolved before any body field is examined, and where more than one identifier appears they are resolved left to right, the first unresolvable one deciding the error. The catalogue provides a distinct not-found identifier per resource family: `ERR_GROWER_NOT_FOUND` for `growerId` and for a `grower_id` in a body, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND` for `blockId` and `block_id`, `ERR_QUALITY_SPEC_NOT_FOUND` for `specId` and `quality_spec_id`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` for `contractId` and `contract_id`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` for `allocationId`, `ERR_LANE_NOT_FOUND` for `laneId` and `lane_id`, `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND` for `crewId` and `crew_id`, `ERR_SAMPLING_VISIT_NOT_FOUND` for `visitId`, `ERR_PICK_CALL_NOT_FOUND` for `pickCallId` and `pick_call_id`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `appointmentId` and `appointment_id`, `ERR_WEIGH_TAG_NOT_FOUND` for `tagId` and `weigh_tag_id`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_LINE_NOT_FOUND` for `lineId`, and `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `installmentId`. All of these carry HTTP 404. Foreign-key identifiers arriving in a request body are resolved after the path identifiers, in the order in which the fields are described for the target resource in §3. The single grading read is addressed by `gradeId`; a grading identifier that does not resolve is likewise refused as 404 Not Found.

### 2.6. The Logical Harvest Clock

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. All temporal reasoning — notice periods, acknowledgement deadlines, arrival windows, dispute windows, installment due dates, renewal windows and termination effectiveness — consults a server-held **logical clock**, and every timestamp the server stamps (`issued_at`, `sampled_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `settlement_closed_at`, `voided_at`, `superseded_at`, `lien_released_at` and the rest) is taken from it. Before any client moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time.

Instants are serialised in two distinct shapes, and the difference is observable in every response body. `created_at`, `updated_at` and `api_request_timestamp` always carry three fractional digits, exactly as §2.2 and §2.4 describe. **Every other instant the API emits** carries the clock's own shape instead: the fractional part is omitted entirely when the instant has no sub-second component, and exactly three fractional digits appear when it does. That second shape covers the clock-stamped fields just listed, the instants derived from them by adding whole hours or days (`ack_deadline_at`, `dispute_deadline_at`, `renewal_window_opens_at`, `renewal_window_closes_at`, `due_at`), the client-supplied instants echoed back on a resource (`window_start`, `window_end`, `target_pick_at`, `termination_effective_at`), and the `current_utc` returned by both clock endpoints. Because the clock starts on a whole second and is normally moved to one, a row written while the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` reports `created_at` as `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` and every one of its other instants as `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

Two administrative endpoints expose it, and all six roles may call both. `GET /_clock/current_utc` returns the current instant as `{ "current_utc": ... }`. `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it, returning the same shape. Moving the clock is not a passive act: it fires every time-driven transition in the system, and it fires them in a fixed order so that the outcome is deterministic. First, pick calls whose acknowledgement deadline has passed expire. Second, delivery appointments whose window has ended without a single weigh tag are marked as no-shows. Third, payment installments past their due date with money still owing fall delinquent. Fourth, contracts whose termination notice has become effective terminate. Fifth and last, the renewal dimension is evaluated: contracts whose renewal window has just opened move into it, and contracts already sitting in an open window whose closing instant has passed either roll evergreen or stop renewing. Any producer touched by those transitions has both reliability scores recomputed as part of the same call.

The "current vintage" everywhere in this document means the UTC calendar year of the logical clock's current instant.

### 2.7. Tonnage, Money and Rounding Discipline

Three numeric domains coexist and each has its own discipline.

**Tonnage** is carried to exactly 3 decimal places and rounded half up. This applies to derived caps (`contract_cap_tons`, `hard_cap_tons`, `block_cap_tons`, `block_hard_cap_tons`), to the converted net weight (`net_tons`), to every tonnage slice on a weigh tag, to the tonnage on a settlement line, and to every delivered-tonnage rollup. Whenever a load's tonnage is divided into slices, the slices are guaranteed to sum to exactly the load's `net_tons` — no ton is ever created or lost by rounding.

**Money** is always a whole number of cents, and rounding is half up at each step the calculation declares. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a response body, and no monetary value is ever transported as a string. Where a pipeline declares an intermediate rounding — most notably the adjusted per-ton price, which is fixed to whole cents *before* it multiplies tonnage — that intermediate rounding is part of the contract, not an implementation liberty: collapsing two declared rounding steps into one produces a different and incorrect figure.

**Percentages and readings** carry 1 decimal place and round half up, except juice pH, which carries 2. Because a stepped bonus/penalty band computed on one-decimal readings is exquisitely sensitive to binary floating point, all threshold comparisons and step counts in the quality-adjustment calculation are performed on integer tenths: each one-decimal value is multiplied by ten and rounded half up to a whole number before it is compared or subtracted. Reliability scores carry 1 decimal place. Whole-pound weights and whole-cent amounts are integers.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection at the Scale House

Two write operations require the client to prove it has observed the current state of a parent row before it writes a child. Both use the same mechanism: a mandatory field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent, compared against that parent's current `version`, with a mismatch refused. These fields are **not optional** on the two endpoints named below, and **no other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind.**

A crush-pad slot legitimately receives several tags — one truck per block — so a second tag against the same appointment is a valid product operation rather than a state violation. That is exactly why the guard is needed: what the client must prove it has seen is how much of the slot's planned tonnage is still uncaptured. A `POST /grower-relations/weigh-tags` request must therefore carry `expected_appointment_version` matching the current `version` of the `DeliveryAppointment` named by `appointment_id`. If the appointment's version has advanced since the client last read it, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

Similarly, a re-grade after a dispute legitimately supersedes the prior grading, so a second grading against the same tag is a valid product operation. A `POST /grower-relations/weigh-tags/{tagId}/grades` request must carry `expected_weigh_tag_version` matching the target weigh tag's current `version`. If the tag's version has advanced since the client last read it — its net weight settled, its disposition moved, another grading superseded — the grading is refused with `ERR_STALE_WEIGH_TAG_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the grader's view of the load is out of date.

### 2.9. The Order in Which Rules Are Evaluated

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error comes back, and which one is fully determined. The doctrine for this domain, highest authority first, is: authentication, then existence, then request shape, then stale-version protection, then state legality, then producer compliance and fruit safety, then scheduling constraints, then temporal envelopes, then referential integrity, and finally fiscal capacity. The reasoning is domain-specific: a suspended producer or a load of immature fruit is a compliance question that outranks how the crush pad happens to be booked, and a booking that lands outside the pick window is meaningless before anyone asks whether the lane has tonnage left — so temporal outranks fiscal.

Concretely, the forty-two rules are evaluated in this relative order.

Ranks 1 through 4 are authentication and authorisation, in this order: (1) both auth headers present and non-blank, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER`, on every endpoint; (2) the declared role is recognised and is authorised for this endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, on every endpoint; (3) a `Grower` acts only on its own rows, else `ERR_NOT_CONTRACT_GROWER`, on every endpoint a `Grower` is permitted to call; (4) the grader is not the operator who weighed the load, else `ERR_SOD_SAME_USER_GRADING`, on the grading create only.

Ranks 5 and 6 are existence: (5) every identifier in the URL path resolves, checked left to right; (6) every foreign-key identifier in the request body resolves, checked in the field order described for the target resource. Both use the family-specific not-found identifiers of §2.5 with HTTP 404, and both outrank every shape check — a body naming a non-existent lane is a 404, not a 422.

Ranks 7 and 8 are request shape: (7) required fields present, JSON types correct, enum values inside their declared sets, numeric ranges and decimal-place limits respected, ISO-8601 instants parseable, and any window's `window_end` strictly after its `window_start` — else `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 422, on every endpoint; (8) pagination bounds, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` with HTTP 400, on every list endpoint.

Ranks 9 through 11 are structural shape rules that need context beyond a single field: (9) the basis-conditional tonnage fields, else `ERR_BASIS_FIELD_MISMATCH` with HTTP 422, on contract create and on allocation create; (10) the installment schedule template's internal consistency, else `ERR_INVALID_INSTALLMENT_PLAN` with HTTP 422, on contract create; (11) uniqueness of the human-readable codes, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` with HTTP 409, on the creates for producers, vineyard blocks, quality specifications, grape contracts, crush-pad lanes, sampling crews and contract-block allocations.

Ranks 12 and 13 are the stale-version guards of §2.8: (12) `expected_appointment_version` on weigh-tag create, else `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION`; (13) `expected_weigh_tag_version` on grading create, else `ERR_STALE_WEIGH_TAG_VERSION`. Both are HTTP 412, and both are checked before any state legality question — a client holding a stale version learns that first.

Ranks 20 through 30 are state legality, in this order: (20) the contract's lifecycle permits the requested transition or child write, else `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`; (21) the pick call's status permits the requested action, else `ERR_PICK_CALL_STATE_INVALID`; (22) the appointment is open for receiving or cancellation, else `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_OPEN`; (23) the lane is open, else `ERR_LANE_CLOSED`; (24) the weigh tag's weight-capture dimension permits the action, else `ERR_TAG_WEIGH_STATE_INVALID`; (25) the weigh tag's disposition dimension permits the action, else `ERR_TAG_NOT_DISPUTABLE`; (26) the installment is payable, else `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_PAYABLE`; (27) the cycle-termination counters, else `ERR_MAX_OFFER_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_MAX_PICK_CALL_REVISIONS_EXCEEDED`; (28) the renewal window is open and the producer's lifetime reliability clears the renewal bar, else `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` or `ERR_RELIABILITY_BELOW_RENEWAL_THRESHOLD`; (29) at least one block allocation exists before a contract can be accepted, else `ERR_NO_ALLOCATIONS`; (30) at least one provisional settlement line exists before the vintage can be closed, else `ERR_NO_PROVISIONAL_LINES`. All eleven carry HTTP 409.

Ranks 31 and 32 are producer compliance and fruit safety: (31) the producer reached from the target row is not suspended, else `ERR_GROWER_SUSPENDED` with HTTP 403; (32) the called block is demonstrably pick-ready, else `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_PICK_READY` with HTTP 422.

Ranks 33 and 34 are scheduling capacity in time: (33) lane exclusivity including turnaround buffer, else `ERR_LANE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; (34) sampling-crew saturation across overlapping windows, else `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_OVERLAP_LIMIT`. Both are HTTP 409.

Ranks 35 through 40 are the temporal envelopes: (35) the appointment window sits inside the pick call's delivery envelope, else `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OUTSIDE_PICK_WINDOW` with HTTP 422; (36) the truck presents inside the arrival envelope, else `ERR_OUTSIDE_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW` with HTTP 409; (37) sufficient pick notice, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PICK_NOTICE` with HTTP 422; (38) the acknowledgement window is still open, else `ERR_ACK_WINDOW_CLOSED` with HTTP 409; (39) the dispute window is still open, else `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` with HTTP 409; (40) the termination notice period is long enough, else `ERR_TERMINATION_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT` with HTTP 422.

Ranks 41 through 43 are referential integrity between rows that already exist: (41) the block is allocated to the contract, else `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_ALLOCATED` with HTTP 409; (42) the sampling crew covers the producer's region, else `ERR_CREW_REGION_MISMATCH` with HTTP 422; (43) the block belongs to the contract's producer and is not retired, else `ERR_BLOCK_GROWER_MISMATCH` with HTTP 409.

Ranks 44 through 48 are fiscal and physical capacity, evaluated last: (44) the lane's daily tonnage, else `ERR_LANE_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; (45) the lane's single-load ceiling, else `ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_LANE_MAX`; (46) tare not exceeding gross, else `ERR_TARE_EXCEEDS_GROSS` with HTTP 422; (47) no overpayment of an installment, else `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`; (48) no outstanding producer lien when a termination notice is served, else `ERR_LIEN_OUTSTANDING`. Apart from rank 46, all carry HTTP 409.

Three collisions are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. When a delivery appointment is booked for a suspended producer into a lane window that also conflicts with an existing booking, the answer is `ERR_GROWER_SUSPENDED`, not `ERR_LANE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` — compliance outranks scheduling. When the same booking both falls outside the pick call's delivery envelope and would breach the lane's daily tonnage, the answer is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OUTSIDE_PICK_WINDOW` — the temporal envelope outranks fiscal capacity. And when a truck presents against an appointment that is neither scheduled nor arrived *and* does so outside the arrival envelope, the answer is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_OPEN` — state legality outranks the temporal envelope.

### 2.10. Cascade Integrity

The system is immediately consistent: there are no queues and no deferred reconciliations. When one write changes a fact that another row's derived fields depend on, those rows are updated inside the same request, and each such update is a mutation in the sense of §2.4 — `updated_at` refreshed, `version` incremented. A grading, for example, does not merely write a grading row: it re-slices the load's tonnage, may create settlement lines, recomputes the contract's delivered and rejected tonnage, re-derives the contract's cap posture, may suspend the contract's pick calls, may cancel those pick calls' scheduled appointments, recomputes the affected lanes' committed tonnage and appointment counts, recomputes the block's and the allocation's delivered tonnage, may advance the pick call to fulfilled, may close the appointment, and may recompute the producer's reliability scores. Every one of those steps is specified in §3, and every one of them is mandatory.

Cascades are also reversible where the domain says they should be. A grade dispute voids the provisional settlement lines it produced, and because those lines no longer count, the tonnage they consumed returns to the contract's available capacity, the contract's cap posture is re-derived, and pick calls suspended by that cap come back to life. Derived rollups are always recomputed from the surviving rows rather than adjusted by deltas, so a reversal restores exactly the state that preceded the event.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Reference Book: Producers, Blocks and Quality Specifications

Before a single ton can be contracted, the grower-relations desk builds three reference tables, all of them owned by the `GrowerRelationsManager`.

A **producer** is registered with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters that may not be blank, a `license_number` carrying the state producer licence identifier as 4 to 32 characters and unique across all producers, and a `region_code` of 2 to 16 characters drawn only from uppercase letters and the hyphen. The `region_code` is not decoration: it is what a sampling crew's coverage is matched against later. An optional `status` may be supplied at registration and defaults to `ACTIVE`; the only other permitted value is `SUSPENDED`. Registration also initialises the producer's derived standing: `rejected_load_count_vintage`, `expired_pick_call_count_vintage`, `no_show_appointment_count_vintage` and `prior_vintage_penalty_units` all start at 0, `outstanding_lien_balance_cents` starts at 0, and both `reliability_score_vintage` and `reliability_score_lifetime` start at 100.0. A duplicate `license_number` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Standing can be revoked and restored. Suspension moves `status` to `SUSPENDED` and accepts an optional free-text `reason` that the server does not interpret; reinstatement moves `status` back to `ACTIVE` and takes no body at all. Both are `GrowerRelationsManager`-only, and both are modifications of an existing row rather than creations. A `SUSPENDED` producer is frozen out of six operations, and the refusal is always `ERR_GROWER_SUSPENDED` with HTTP 403: putting a contract on the table, accepting a contract, issuing a pick call, booking a delivery appointment, capturing a weigh tag, and paying an installment. Reads are never blocked by suspension. Producers can be browsed as a collection with the optional filters `region_code` and `status`, and a single producer read returns both reliability scores and the outstanding lien balance.

A **vineyard block** is the physical unit everything else references. It is created with a `grower_id` naming the producer who farms it, a `block_code` that is the grower-scoped label printed on the weigh tag and must be unique within that producer, a `varietal` of 3 to 40 characters drawn only from uppercase letters and the underscore, the planted `acres` as a positive number with at most 3 decimal places, an `expected_yield_tons_per_acre` agronomic estimate likewise positive with at most 3 decimals, and an `ava_code` of 2 to 24 uppercase letters and hyphens recording the American Viticultural Area for appellation reporting. An optional `status` defaults to `ACTIVE`, the alternative being `RETIRED`. Reusing a `block_code` within the same producer is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Three fields on a block are derived and never supplied: `latest_sampling_brix` and `latest_sampling_rot_pct` mirror the most recent completed field sample, and `delivered_net_tons_vintage` accumulates the block's accepted tonnage for the current vintage. Blocks are listed with the optional filters `grower_id`, `status` and `varietal`, and a single block read exposes the sampling readings and the vintage tonnage.

The **quality specification** is the price-and-rejection rulebook, and it is the one reference table a `QualityLabTech` may author alongside the `GrowerRelationsManager`. Five values are mandatory: a `spec_code` of 3 to 32 characters unique across specifications, `min_brix_accept` (the minimum graded sugar at receipt, below which the load is rejected on quality), `brix_bonus_start` (which must be strictly greater than `min_brix_accept`), `brix_penalty_start` (which must be at least `min_brix_accept` and at most `brix_bonus_start`), and `rot_rejection_pct` (which must be strictly greater than `rot_free_pct`). Everything else is optional and defaulted: `min_brix_pick_call`, the minimum sampled sugar that lets a pick call be issued, defaults to 21.0; `brix_step`, the sugar increment that constitutes one bonus or penalty step, defaults to 0.5; `brix_bonus_pct_per_step` defaults to 1.5 and `brix_penalty_pct_per_step` to 2.0 percentage points; the titratable-acidity band runs from `ta_min_gl`, defaulting to 5.5 grams per litre, to `ta_max_gl`, defaulting to 9.0 and required to be strictly greater than `ta_min_gl`, with `acid_out_of_spec_penalty_pct` defaulting to a flat 3.0 percentage points outside that band; `rot_free_pct`, the rot percentage tolerated at no cost, defaults to 2.0 and `rot_penalty_pct_per_point` to 1.5 percentage points per whole point above it; `mog_max_pct`, the tolerated material-other-than-grapes percentage, defaults to 1.0 with a flat `mog_penalty_pct` of 2.5 percentage points beyond it; and the two ceilings, `max_total_bonus_pct` defaulting to 8.0 and `max_total_penalty_pct` defaulting to 20.0, bound the combined adjustment. All sugar, acid, rot and MOG values carry at most 1 decimal place; `min_brix_pick_call`, `min_brix_accept`, `brix_bonus_start`, `brix_penalty_start`, `brix_step` and `ta_min_gl` must all be strictly positive, while the percentage-point figures need only be non-negative, and `max_total_penalty_pct` may not exceed 100. A duplicate `spec_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Specifications are listed with an optional `spec_code` filter.

### 3.2. Negotiating the Grape Contract

A contract begins life as a draft authored by the `GrowerRelationsManager`. It names its counterparty through `grower_id` and its rulebook through `quality_spec_id`, carries a winery-facing `contract_code` of 3 to 32 characters that must be unique across contracts, and fixes `base_price_per_ton_cents` as a positive whole number of cents per net ton before any quality adjustment. Two further mandatory choices define the commercial shape. `contract_basis` is one of `TONNAGE`, `ACREAGE` or `BLOCK`, and it decides which tonnage field the request must carry: a `TONNAGE` contract requires `committed_tons` and must not carry `contracted_yield_tons_per_acre`; an `ACREAGE` contract requires `contracted_yield_tons_per_acre` and must not carry `committed_tons`; a `BLOCK` contract must carry neither. Supplying or omitting either field inconsistently with the declared basis is refused with `ERR_BASIS_FIELD_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422. Both tonnage fields, when present, are positive numbers with at most 3 decimals. `over_delivery_policy` is one of `ACCEPT_AT_CONTRACT`, `ACCEPT_AT_SPOT` or `REJECT` and governs what happens to fruit delivered above the soft cap. The term is bounded by `term_start_vintage` and `term_end_vintage`, whole calendar years between 2000 and 2100 with the end no earlier than the start.

The remaining terms are optional with defaults that encode the winery's house standard. `spot_price_pct`, the percentage of the base price paid on over-delivery tonnage under an `ACCEPT_AT_SPOT` policy, defaults to 70.0 and must be greater than 0 and at most 100. `over_delivery_tolerance_pct`, the percentage by which the soft cap may be exceeded before fruit is refused outright, defaults to 10.0 and lies between 0 and 100 inclusive. `settlement_basis` selects which final-settlement algorithm runs at close and defaults to `LOAD_BY_LOAD`, the alternative being `WEIGHTED_BLEND`. `provisional_payment_pct`, the share of each settlement line advanced provisionally at grading time, defaults to 60.0 and must be greater than 0 and at most 100. `lien_holdback_pct`, the share of the settlement total withheld pending statutory producer-lien release, defaults to 5.0 and may not exceed 50. `min_pick_notice_hours` defaults to 48 whole hours and may not be negative; `pick_call_ack_window_hours`, the time the producer has to acknowledge a call, defaults to 24 whole hours and must be positive. `evergreen_flag` defaults to false; when true, the term rolls forward automatically once the renewal window closes without an explicit renewal or termination notice, and `evergreen_term_years` — defaulting to 1 and constrained to the range 1 through 5 — is how many years each roll adds. `termination_notice_days` defaults to 90, must be at least 1, and does double duty: it is both the minimum notice a termination requires and the width of the renewal window.

`installment_plan` is mandatory and is the payment schedule template. It is an ordered array of 1 to 6 elements, each element carrying an integer `installment_no`, a number `pct_of_settlement`, an integer `due_offset_days` and a boolean `lien_protected`. The `installment_no` values must be exactly 1 through *n* with no gaps and no repeats, every `pct_of_settlement` must be strictly greater than 0, and the `pct_of_settlement` values must sum to exactly 100.0. A plan with zero or more than six elements, gapped or repeated numbering, a non-positive percentage, or percentages that miss 100.0 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSTALLMENT_PLAN` and HTTP 422. A duplicate `contract_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A freshly drafted contract sits in `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT` with `renewal_status` `NOT_DUE`, `offer_round` 0 and `tonnage_status` `OPEN`.

Contracts carry **two independent state dimensions**. `lifecycle_status` moves through `DRAFT`, `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED`, `ACTIVE`, `SETTLED`, `CLOSED`, `TERMINATION_PENDING`, `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which `CLOSED`, `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal. `renewal_status` moves independently through `NOT_DUE`, `RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN`, `RENEWED`, `EVERGREEN_ROLLED` and `NOT_RENEWING`, of which `NOT_RENEWING` is terminal. The two never constrain each other's vocabulary: a contract can be `ACTIVE` and `RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN` at once, and a renewal never changes the lifecycle dimension.

Putting a contract on the table is a `GrowerRelationsManager` action that drives `DRAFT → OFFERED`, and — because the negotiation is a genuine cycle — also `COUNTERED → OFFERED`. It accepts an optional `base_price_per_ton_cents`, and when that value is supplied it replaces the stored price. Every such transition increments `offer_round` by 1, and it is permitted only while `offer_round` is strictly less than 3. The fourth attempt is therefore refused with `ERR_MAX_OFFER_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409, which is what makes the `OFFERED → COUNTERED → OFFERED` loop terminate. Attempting the transition from any lifecycle state other than `DRAFT` or `COUNTERED` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`.

The producer answers. Countering drives `OFFERED → COUNTERED`, is permitted only to the `Grower` whose identifier matches the contract's `grower_id`, and requires a `counter_base_price_per_ton_cents` in the body, which is stored on the contract as `countered_price_per_ton_cents`. That field is null until the first counter occurs. Alternatively the winery can pull the offer, driving `OFFERED → WITHDRAWN`; withdrawal takes no body, and it also drives the renewal dimension straight to `NOT_RENEWING`, because a contract that never existed will never renew.

Acceptance is the pivot of the whole system. It is a `Grower` action on the contract's own producer, it drives `OFFERED → ACTIVE`, it takes no request body, and it requires two preconditions beyond the lifecycle state: at least one block allocation must already exist for the contract, else `ERR_NO_ALLOCATIONS`, and the accepting producer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_GROWER_SUSPENDED`. Acceptance then derives everything the harvest will need. `cap_scope` is set to `PER_BLOCK` when `contract_basis` is `BLOCK` and to `PER_CONTRACT` otherwise. The tonnage caps are computed as described below and `tonnage_status` is set to `OPEN`. The renewal window is fixed: `renewal_window_closes_at` becomes midnight UTC on the first of November of the year given by `term_end_vintage` (the year, then `-11-01T00:00:00Z`), and `renewal_window_opens_at` becomes that closing instant less `termination_notice_days` days. And the payment schedule is materialised: one installment row is created for each element of `installment_plan`, copying `installment_no`, `pct_of_settlement`, `due_offset_days` and `lien_protected` verbatim, each starting in status `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` with `planned_amount_cents` 0 and `paid_amount_cents` 0.

**Deriving the tonnage caps.** The soft cap `contract_cap_tons` depends entirely on the basis. On a `TONNAGE` contract it is simply `committed_tons`, and `cap_scope` is `PER_CONTRACT`. On an `ACREAGE` contract the allocated acreage of every block allocation of the contract is summed, that total is multiplied by `contracted_yield_tons_per_acre`, and the product is rounded half up to 3 decimals; `cap_scope` is again `PER_CONTRACT`. On a `BLOCK` contract each block carries its own cap: for every allocation, `block_cap_tons` becomes the block's `acres` multiplied by its `expected_yield_tons_per_acre`, rounded half up to 3 decimals, and `block_hard_cap_tons` becomes that block cap multiplied by one plus `over_delivery_tolerance_pct` divided by 100, again rounded half up to 3 decimals; the contract-level `contract_cap_tons` is the sum of the per-block caps, rounded half up to 3 decimals, and `cap_scope` is `PER_BLOCK`. In every case, `hard_cap_tons` is `contract_cap_tons` multiplied by one plus `over_delivery_tolerance_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to 3 decimals. On `PER_CONTRACT` contracts the per-allocation `block_cap_tons` and `block_hard_cap_tons` remain null.

**Committing blocks to the contract.** A block allocation is authored by the `GrowerRelationsManager` and carries a `contract_id`, a `block_id`, and — only on an `ACREAGE`-basis contract — `allocated_acres`, a positive number with at most 3 decimals that may not exceed the block's own `acres`. On a `TONNAGE` or `BLOCK` contract, `allocated_acres` must be absent; supplying it there, or omitting it on an `ACREAGE` contract, is the same `ERR_BASIS_FIELD_MISMATCH`. The same block may be committed to a given contract only once: a repeated contract-and-block pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The block must belong to the contract's producer and must not be retired — a block whose `grower_id` differs from the contract's `grower_id`, or whose `status` is `RETIRED`, is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_GROWER_MISMATCH` and HTTP 409. Allocations may be created while the contract is `DRAFT`, `OFFERED` or `ACTIVE`; anything else is `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. At creation time `block_cap_tons` and `block_hard_cap_tons` are null and stay null until the contract is accepted. The allocation also carries a derived `delivered_net_tons`, which accumulates the tonnage settled against this contract from this block. Allocations are listed with the optional filters `contract_id` and `block_id`, which is how either side of the many-to-many between contracts and blocks is walked.

No write of any kind is accepted against a contract whose `lifecycle_status` has reached `CLOSED`, `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN`; every such attempt is `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. Contracts are listed with the optional filters `grower_id`, `quality_spec_id`, `lifecycle_status`, `renewal_status` and `tonnage_status`.

### 3.3. Sampling the Fruit and Establishing Maturity

Field sampling is what converts an agronomic hunch into a defensible decision to pick. The crews that do it are registered by either the `GrowerRelationsManager` or the `HarvestCoordinator` with a `crew_code` of 1 to 16 characters unique across crews, a `region_code` of 2 to 16 uppercase letters and hyphens describing the territory they cover, and an optional `max_concurrent_visits` ceiling defaulting to 2 and required to be at least 1. A crew also exposes a derived `scheduled_visit_count`, the number of its visits that are either still scheduled or already completed. Crews are listed with an optional `region_code` filter; a duplicate `crew_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Booking a sampling window is exclusively a `HarvestCoordinator` action. The request names the `contract_id` whose specification the reading will be judged against, the `block_id` to be sampled, the `crew_id` that will be occupied, and the field window as an inclusive `window_start` and an exclusive `window_end`, the latter strictly after the former. The contract must be in `DRAFT`, `OFFERED` or `ACTIVE`; anything else is `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. The block must be allocated to that contract — the contract-and-block pair must already have an allocation row — or the booking is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_ALLOCATED` and HTTP 409. The crew must cover the right territory: its `region_code` must equal the `region_code` of the producer that farms the sampled block, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CREW_REGION_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422. Finally the crew must not be saturated: counting only that crew's visits that are still in status `SCHEDULED` and whose own half-open window overlaps the requested half-open window, the count must be strictly less than the crew's `max_concurrent_visits`, otherwise `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_OVERLAP_LIMIT` with HTTP 409. Because both windows are treated as half-open, a visit ending exactly at the requested `window_start` does not overlap it. A successful booking creates the visit in status `SCHEDULED` and recomputes the crew's `scheduled_visit_count`.

Recording the readings is likewise a `HarvestCoordinator` action and drives the visit `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. Four measurements are mandatory: `measured_brix`, the sugar in degrees Brix, greater than 0 and at most 40 with at most 1 decimal; `measured_ta_gl`, titratable acidity in grams per litre, greater than 0 and at most 20 with at most 1 decimal; `measured_ph`, the juice pH, greater than 0 and at most 5 with at most 2 decimals; and `measured_rot_pct`, the bunch-rot percentage, at least 0 and at most 100 with at most 1 decimal. All four are null while the visit is merely `SCHEDULED`. Recording stamps `sampled_at` from the logical clock and derives `maturity_flag`: `PICK_READY` when `measured_brix` is greater than or equal to the contract specification's `min_brix_pick_call`, and `IMMATURE` otherwise. `maturity_flag` is also null while the visit is `SCHEDULED`. The sampled block is then refreshed: `latest_sampling_brix` and `latest_sampling_rot_pct` take the `measured_brix` and `measured_rot_pct` of that block's completed visit with the greatest `sampled_at`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`, and both are null when the block has no completed visit at all. Because the logical clock readily stamps two visits with an identical `created_at`, a tie that survives that far is broken in favour of the row written first: two visits of the same block recorded at the same logical instant leave the block mirroring whichever of them was booked earlier, whatever their readings say.

A scheduled visit can also end unrecorded. When a termination notice is served on the visit's contract, driving that contract from `ACTIVE` to `TERMINATION_PENDING`, every one of its still-`SCHEDULED` visits transitions `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, and the crew's `scheduled_visit_count` is recomputed for each. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal. Visits are listed with the optional filters `contract_id`, `block_id`, `crew_id`, `status` and `maturity_flag`.

### 3.4. The Pick Call: Notification, Acknowledgement and Revision

A pick call is the winery telling the producer to bring fruit, and it is issued by the `HarvestCoordinator` naming a `contract_id`, a `block_id`, a `target_pick_at` instant at which the winery wants the fruit picked, and an `estimated_tons` figure — positive, at most 3 decimals — that travels in the notification as the winery's tonnage expectation. The contract must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`; the producer must not be suspended, else `ERR_GROWER_SUSPENDED`; the block must belong to the contract's producer and must not be `RETIRED`, else `ERR_BLOCK_GROWER_MISMATCH`; and the block must be allocated to the contract, else `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_ALLOCATED`.

Two gates then decide whether the call may go out at all. The **maturity gate** comes first, before scheduling is considered in any way: the called block must have at least one sampling visit for that same contract in status `COMPLETED` with `maturity_flag` `PICK_READY`, and that visit's `measured_rot_pct` must be strictly below the contract specification's `rot_rejection_pct`. Calling immature or rotten fruit to the crush pad is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_PICK_READY` and HTTP 422. The **notice gate** follows: `target_pick_at` must be at least the contract's `min_pick_notice_hours` after the logical clock's current instant, and a shorter horizon is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PICK_NOTICE` and HTTP 422.

Issuance stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock and derives two figures from it. `notice_hours` is the number of whole hours between `issued_at` and `target_pick_at` — the elapsed seconds between the two instants divided by 3600 and rounded down to a whole number. `ack_deadline_at` is `issued_at` plus the contract's `pick_call_ack_window_hours` hours. The call starts in status `ISSUED` with `revision_count` 0 and `acknowledged_at` null.

The producer whose identifier matches the contract's `grower_id` acknowledges the call, driving `ISSUED → ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamping `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock. Acknowledgement requires that the logical clock be **strictly before** `ack_deadline_at`; at or after that instant it is refused with `ERR_ACK_WINDOW_CLOSED` and HTTP 409. Note the deliberately narrow gap this creates: automatic expiry needs the clock to be strictly *past* `ack_deadline_at`, so at the deadline instant exactly the call is still `ISSUED` and yet acknowledgement is already refused.

Plans change, and the `HarvestCoordinator` may re-issue an acknowledged call, driving `ACKNOWLEDGED → ISSUED` — a genuine cycle, because a revised call must be acknowledged again. The revision carries a mandatory `revised_target_pick_at` which replaces `target_pick_at`, and an optional `revised_estimated_tons` which replaces `estimated_tons` when supplied. It is permitted only while `revision_count` is strictly less than 2, so the third revision is refused with `ERR_MAX_PICK_CALL_REVISIONS_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409, terminating the cycle; and only while the call is `ACKNOWLEDGED`, any other status being `ERR_PICK_CALL_STATE_INVALID`. The recomputed notice must still satisfy the contract's `min_pick_notice_hours`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PICK_NOTICE`. On success `revision_count` increments by 1, `acknowledged_at` is cleared, and `issued_at`, `notice_hours` and `ack_deadline_at` are all recomputed from the logical clock. Because the call has left `ACKNOWLEDGED`, every delivery appointment of that call still in status `SCHEDULED` transitions to `CANCELLED`; because those appointments became cancelled, the `committed_tons` and `scheduled_appointment_count` of each affected lane are recomputed; and the call's own `appointed_tons` is recomputed.

Three further ways out of the live states exist, all of them driven by the system rather than by a client. When the logical clock advances strictly past `ack_deadline_at` while the call is still `ISSUED`, it transitions `ISSUED → EXPIRED`, and that expiry counts against the producer: `expired_pick_call_count_vintage`, `prior_vintage_penalty_units` and both reliability scores are recomputed. When a graded load pushes the contract's cap posture out of `OPEN`, calls in `ISSUED` transition to `SUSPENDED_CAP` and calls in `ACKNOWLEDGED` do the same — with one ordering caveat that matters: the fulfilment check on an acknowledged call is evaluated first, so a call that has just reached `FULFILLED` is never suspended. Suspending an acknowledged call also cancels its `SCHEDULED` appointments and recomputes each affected lane's `committed_tons` and `scheduled_appointment_count`; suspending an `ISSUED` call has no appointments to cancel. Cap suspension is reversible: when a grade dispute returns the contract's cap posture to `OPEN`, every `SUSPENDED_CAP` call transitions back to `ISSUED` with `issued_at`, `notice_hours` and `ack_deadline_at` recomputed from the logical clock and `acknowledged_at` cleared — a second genuine cycle, since the call revisits `ISSUED` without any new call being created.

Fulfilment closes a call happily: when an accepted or partially accepted load brings the call's derived `delivered_net_tons` to at least its `estimated_tons`, an acknowledged call transitions `ACKNOWLEDGED → FULFILLED` and `fulfillment_pct` is recomputed. Withdrawal closes it unhappily: when the contract leaves `ACTIVE` through a termination notice, calls in `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` transition to `WITHDRAWN`, and an acknowledged call's `SCHEDULED` appointments are cancelled with each affected lane's `committed_tons` recomputed; when the contract leaves `ACTIVE` through settlement close, calls in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` **or** `SUSPENDED_CAP` all transition to `WITHDRAWN`, again cancelling any scheduled appointments of an acknowledged call and recomputing lane load. `FULFILLED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the three terminal statuses.

Three derived figures ride on every call. `appointed_tons` is the sum of `planned_tons` over the call's appointments in status `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED`, to 3 decimals. `delivered_net_tons` is the sum of `net_tons` over weigh tags whose appointment belongs to this call, whose weight capture has reached `NET_FINAL` and whose disposition is `ACCEPTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, to 3 decimals. `fulfillment_pct` is `delivered_net_tons` divided by `estimated_tons`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 1 decimal. Calls are listed with the optional filters `contract_id`, `block_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Booking the Crush Pad

Receiving capacity is modelled as lanes, opened by either the `GrowerRelationsManager` or the `HarvestCoordinator`. A lane is created with a `lane_code` of 1 to 16 characters unique across lanes and a `daily_capacity_tons` figure — positive, at most 3 decimals — that bounds how much planned tonnage the lane may hold for a single UTC calendar date. Three optional settings follow: `turnaround_buffer_minutes`, the whole minutes of cleanout that must separate two appointment windows on the lane, defaulting to 30 and not allowed to be negative; `max_load_tons`, the largest single appointment the lane will accept, defaulting to 40.0 and required to be positive with at most 3 decimals; and `status`, defaulting to `OPEN` with `CLOSED` as the alternative. Two derived figures round out the row: `committed_tons`, the sum of `planned_tons` over the lane's appointments in status `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED` to 3 decimals, and `scheduled_appointment_count`, the count of those same appointments. Lanes are listed with an optional `status` filter, and a duplicate `lane_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Booking a slot is one of only two operations the producer shares with the winery: both the `HarvestCoordinator` and the `Grower` may reserve a delivery appointment. The request carries the `pick_call_id` of the acknowledged call being served, the `lane_id` of the lane being reserved, the reservation as an inclusive `window_start` and exclusive `window_end` with the end strictly after the start, and `planned_tons`, the tonnage the producer intends to deliver into the slot, positive with at most 3 decimals. The pick call must be in status `ACKNOWLEDGED` — a call that is merely `ISSUED`, or has been suspended, expired, fulfilled or withdrawn, yields `ERR_PICK_CALL_STATE_INVALID` — and the lane's `status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_LANE_CLOSED`. A suspended producer may not be booked at all, and that check outranks every scheduling question on this endpoint.

Four capacity and envelope rules then apply, and their relative order is exactly as follows. **Lane exclusivity** is checked first: the requested half-open window must not overlap the buffered window of any other appointment on the same lane in status `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED`, where the buffered window of an existing appointment runs from its `window_start` less the lane's `turnaround_buffer_minutes` to its `window_end` plus the same buffer. Windows that merely touch once the buffer has been applied are accepted; a genuine overlap is `ERR_LANE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` with HTTP 409. Next the **delivery envelope**: the appointment's `window_start` must be at or after the pick call's `target_pick_at`, and no later than 72 hours after that same `target_pick_at` — three days of grace, no more. Outside that band the booking is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OUTSIDE_PICK_WINDOW` and HTTP 422. Then the lane's **daily tonnage**: summing `planned_tons` over the appointments on the same lane in status `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED` whose `window_start` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the requested `window_start`, and adding the requested `planned_tons`, the total may not exceed the lane's `daily_capacity_tons`; equality is accepted, and a breach is `ERR_LANE_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Last, the **single-load ceiling**: `planned_tons` may not exceed the lane's `max_load_tons`, else `ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_LANE_MAX`. Because the envelope check outranks the tonnage check, a booking that is both mis-timed and over the daily capacity reports the timing problem.

A successful booking creates the appointment in status `SCHEDULED` and recomputes both the lane's `committed_tons` and `scheduled_appointment_count` and the pick call's `appointed_tons`. Its own derived figures start empty: `captured_net_tons`, the sum of `net_tons` over the appointment's weigh tags whose weight capture has reached `NET_FINAL`, to 3 decimals; and `weigh_tag_count`, the number of weigh tags presented against it.

An appointment's lifecycle has five states, of which `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` are terminal. The `HarvestCoordinator` may release a slot, driving `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with an optional `reason` in the body; cancellation is permitted only from `SCHEDULED`, any other status being `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_OPEN`, and it recomputes the lane's `committed_tons` and `scheduled_appointment_count` and the pick call's `appointed_tons`. The first weigh tag presented against a scheduled appointment drives `SCHEDULED → ARRIVED` as the tag's `weigh_tag_count` moves from 0 to 1. Once every weigh tag of an arrived appointment has reached `NET_FINAL` and holds a disposition other than `PENDING` or `DISPUTED`, the appointment transitions `ARRIVED → CLOSED`, recomputing its `captured_net_tons` and the lane's `committed_tons` and `scheduled_appointment_count`. And if the logical clock advances to or past `window_end` while the appointment is still `SCHEDULED` with `weigh_tag_count` at 0, it transitions `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`; that recomputes the lane's two derived figures and the pick call's `appointed_tons`, and it counts against the producer, whose `no_show_appointment_count_vintage`, `prior_vintage_penalty_units` and both reliability scores are recomputed. Appointments are listed with the optional filters `pick_call_id`, `lane_id` and `status`.

### 3.6. The Scale House: Gross, Tare and Net

Weight capture belongs to the `ScaleOperator` alone, and it happens in two passes with the truck's visit sandwiched between them. The inbound pass opens a weigh tag naming the `appointment_id` the truck presented in, the `block_id` the fruit on this tag came from, the loaded `gross_weight_lbs` as a whole number of pounds greater than 0 and at most 200000, and the mandatory `expected_appointment_version` described in §2.8. Because one appointment legitimately receives several tags — one per block on the truck — the block on the tag is checked independently: it must be allocated to the contract the appointment resolves to, else `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_ALLOCATED`.

Three gates guard the pass. The appointment must be open for receiving, which means its status is `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED`; anything else is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_OPEN`. The producer must not be suspended. And the truck must present inside the **arrival envelope**: the logical clock must be at or after the appointment's `window_start` and strictly before its `window_end` plus 60 minutes of late-arrival grace — one hour, no more. Outside that band the tag is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW` and HTTP 409. Since the appointment-state check outranks the envelope check, a truck arriving late at an already-cancelled slot is told the slot is not open.

A new tag records `captured_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, starts its weight-capture dimension at `GROSS_CAPTURED` and its receipt-decision dimension at `PENDING`, and leaves `tare_weight_lbs`, `net_weight_lbs` and `net_tons` all null. The appointment's `weigh_tag_count` is recomputed and the appointment itself is mutated, so its `version` advances — which is precisely why the next tag against the same slot must carry the newer `expected_appointment_version`. A tag also carries `grade_count`, starting at 0, `active_adjustment_pct`, null until a grading exists, four tonnage slices — `contract_slice_tons`, `over_slice_tons`, `over_rejected_tons` and `excess_rejected_tons` — all starting at 0.000, and `dispute_deadline_at`, null until the first grading.

The outbound pass supplies `tare_weight_lbs`, the empty truck weight as a whole number of pounds at least 0 and at most 200000, and drives the weight dimension `GROSS_CAPTURED → NET_FINAL`. It is permitted only while the tag is still `GROSS_CAPTURED`; a second tare attempt is `ERR_TAG_WEIGH_STATE_INVALID` with HTTP 409. The tare may not exceed the gross: a `tare_weight_lbs` greater than `gross_weight_lbs` is refused with `ERR_TARE_EXCEEDS_GROSS` and HTTP 422, while **equality is explicitly legal** — it records the case where the whole load was condemned in the field and dumped before the outbound pass. The conversion is then trivial and exact: `net_weight_lbs` is `gross_weight_lbs` less `tare_weight_lbs`, a whole number of pounds; and `net_tons` is `net_weight_lbs` divided by 2000 pounds per ton, rounded half up to 3 decimals. An equal tare therefore yields `net_weight_lbs` 0 and `net_tons` 0.000. No other row participates in this conversion. The tare also recomputes the appointment's `captured_net_tons`, and it leaves the receipt-decision dimension untouched at `PENDING`. `NET_FINAL` is the terminal state of the weight dimension.

Tags are read individually — exposing both dimensions, the three weights and the four tonnage slices — and listed with the optional filters `appointment_id`, `block_id`, `weigh_status` and `disposition`.

### 3.7. Grading at Receipt: Price Adjustment and Load Rejection

Grading is the single richest operation in the system, and it belongs to the `QualityLabTech`. It records a lab reading, prices the load, decides how much of it the contract will take, and fans out through half the data model. The request carries five readings and one guard: `brix`, the sugar at receipt, greater than 0 and at most 40 with at most 1 decimal; `ta_gl`, the titratable acidity in grams per litre, greater than 0 and at most 20 with at most 1 decimal; `ph`, the juice pH recorded for the lab file, greater than 0 and at most 5 with at most 2 decimals; `rot_pct`, the bunch rot at receipt, at least 0 and at most 100 with at most 1 decimal; `mog_pct`, the material other than grapes, likewise at least 0 and at most 100 with at most 1 decimal; and the mandatory `expected_weigh_tag_version` of §2.8. The acting `X-User-ID` is stored on the new grading row as `graded_by_user_id`, and it must differ from the tag's `captured_by_user_id` — the segregation-of-duties rule that outranks every state question on this endpoint. The tag's weight dimension must already be `NET_FINAL`, else `ERR_TAG_WEIGH_STATE_INVALID`; and its disposition must be either `PENDING` (a first grading) or `DISPUTED` (a re-grading after a dispute), any already-decided disposition being `ERR_TAG_NOT_DISPUTABLE`. A grading is created with HTTP 201 and the created grading row is what comes back — a grading is append-only, and no update or delete for it exists anywhere in the API.

**Step one: the quality adjustment.** Four independently gated components are computed over the same readings, against the quality specification of the contract the tag resolves to, then summed, then clamped exactly once. Because every reading and threshold carries at most 1 decimal place, all comparisons and step counts are performed on integer tenths: each value is multiplied by ten and rounded half up to a whole number first, which keeps the step arithmetic exact.

The **sugar component**, `brix_component_pct`, is a two-sided stepped band. When the tenths of `brix` exceed the tenths of `brix_bonus_start`, the number of complete bonus steps is the difference between those two tenths values divided by the tenths of `brix_step`, rounded down to a whole number, and `brix_component_pct` is that step count multiplied by `brix_bonus_pct_per_step`, as a positive quantity. When instead the tenths of `brix` fall below the tenths of `brix_penalty_start`, the number of complete penalty steps is the difference between the tenths of `brix_penalty_start` and the tenths of `brix`, divided by the tenths of `brix_step` and rounded down to a whole number, and `brix_component_pct` is that step count multiplied by `brix_penalty_pct_per_step`, as a negative quantity. Between the two thresholds — from `brix_penalty_start` up to and including `brix_bonus_start` — lies a dead band in which `brix_component_pct` is 0.0.

The **acid component**, `acid_component_pct`, is a flat two-sided penalty. When the tenths of `ta_gl` fall below the tenths of `ta_min_gl`, or rise above the tenths of `ta_max_gl`, `acid_component_pct` is the negative of `acid_out_of_spec_penalty_pct`; inside the band, inclusive of both endpoints, it is 0.0.

The **rot component**, `rot_component_pct`, is charged per *whole* point above the free allowance, and the asymmetry is deliberate. When the tenths of `rot_pct` exceed the tenths of `rot_free_pct`, the number of whole points charged is the difference between those two tenths values divided by ten and rounded down to a whole number, and `rot_component_pct` is that count multiplied by `rot_penalty_pct_per_point`, as a negative quantity; at or below the allowance it is 0.0. A reading of 5.5 against a `rot_free_pct` of 2.0 therefore charges 3 whole points, not 3.5 — only completed points are billed.

The **MOG component**, `mog_component_pct`, is flat: when the tenths of `mog_pct` exceed the tenths of `mog_max_pct` it is the negative of `mog_penalty_pct`, and otherwise 0.0.

`raw_adjustment_pct` is the sum of those four components, and it is **persisted unclamped** because one of the two settlement variants blends pre-clamp values. `adjustment_pct` is `raw_adjustment_pct` brought inside the specification's ceiling and floor: it is the lesser of `raw_adjustment_pct` and `max_total_bonus_pct`, and then the greater of that result and the negative of `max_total_penalty_pct`. The clamp is applied exactly once, here, and never again per load. Every component and both totals carry 1 decimal place rounded half up; step counts are exact integers.

`recommended_disposition` is decided from the raw readings, not from the adjustment: it is `REJECT_QUALITY` when the tenths of `brix` fall below the tenths of `min_brix_accept`, or when the tenths of `rot_pct` reach or exceed the tenths of `rot_rejection_pct`; otherwise it is `ACCEPT`. Only the active grading of a tag feeds anything downstream — superseded gradings never contribute to any figure.

**Step two: the tonnage waterfall.** When and only when `recommended_disposition` is `ACCEPT`, the load's `net_tons` is poured through three buckets, strictly in order, each bucket filling completely before the next receives anything. Which caps the waterfall consumes depends on the contract's `cap_scope`. On a `PER_CONTRACT` contract the soft cap is `contract_cap_tons` and the hard cap is `hard_cap_tons`; the soft consumption so far is `delivered_contract_tons`, and the total consumption so far is `delivered_contract_tons` plus `delivered_over_tons`. On a `PER_BLOCK` contract the caps come from the allocation matching the contract and the tag's `block_id`: the soft cap is that allocation's `block_cap_tons` and the hard cap its `block_hard_cap_tons`; the soft consumption is the tonnage on that allocation's settlement lines in the contract bucket, and the total consumption is the tonnage on all of that allocation's settlement lines across both buckets. In either case, consumption counts only lines whose status is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` — voided lines are excluded, which is exactly what returns capacity to a contract after a dispute. Loads consume capacity in ascending order of their grading's `created_at`, ties broken by the weigh tag's own `created_at` ascending; allocation order is never derived from any identifier.

The remaining soft capacity is the soft cap less the soft consumption, floored at 0.000, and the remaining hard capacity is the hard cap less the total consumption, likewise floored at 0.000. Bucket one, contract tonnage, takes `contract_slice_tons` as the lesser of `net_tons` and the remaining soft capacity; the remainder of the load is what is left of `net_tons` after that slice. Bucket two is the over-delivery tolerance band, whose capacity is the remaining hard capacity less `contract_slice_tons`, floored at 0.000; the band slice is the lesser of the remainder and that band capacity. If the contract's `over_delivery_policy` is `REJECT`, then `over_slice_tons` is 0.000 and the whole band slice becomes `over_rejected_tons` — tonnage that fitted inside the tolerance band but that the contract refuses. Under either `ACCEPT_AT_CONTRACT` or `ACCEPT_AT_SPOT`, the band slice becomes `over_slice_tons` and `over_rejected_tons` is 0.000. Bucket three is everything above the hard cap: `excess_rejected_tons` is the remainder less the band slice, and it is always refused regardless of policy. The four slices always sum to exactly `net_tons`, each to 3 decimals rounded half up.

**Step three: the disposition.** The waterfall decides which of three accepting or refusing outcomes the tag lands on. If `contract_slice_tons` and `over_slice_tons` are both 0.000, nothing was taken and the tag moves `PENDING → REJECTED_OVER_CAP`. Otherwise, if either `over_rejected_tons` or `excess_rejected_tons` is greater than 0.000, part of the load was taken and part refused, and the tag moves `PENDING → PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`. Otherwise the whole load was taken and the tag moves `PENDING → ACCEPTED`. And when `recommended_disposition` was `REJECT_QUALITY` in the first place, the waterfall does not run at all: the tag moves `PENDING → REJECTED_QUALITY`, all four slices stay 0.000, and no settlement line is created.

**Step four: pricing the accepted slices.** One settlement line is created for each non-zero accepted bucket, the contract bucket before the over-delivery bucket, each starting in status `PROVISIONAL` and each carrying the tag's `weigh_tag_id`, the `contract_id` it settles against, its `bucket` (`CONTRACT` or `OVER_DELIVERY`) and its `tons`. Pricing is a nested pipeline in which the inner rounding feeds the outer step, and that structure is part of the contract. First, which adjustment and which base price apply. On a `CONTRACT` line, `adjustment_pct_applied` is the grading's `adjustment_pct` verbatim, bonuses included, and the base is the contract's `base_price_per_ton_cents`. On an `OVER_DELIVERY` line, bonuses are suppressed: `adjustment_pct_applied` is the lesser of the grading's `adjustment_pct` and 0.0, so over-cap fruit never earns a quality premium yet still absorbs every penalty; and the base is `base_price_per_ton_cents` multiplied by `spot_price_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents when `over_delivery_policy` is `ACCEPT_AT_SPOT`, or `base_price_per_ton_cents` unchanged when the policy is `ACCEPT_AT_CONTRACT`. Second — the **inner** rounding — `price_per_ton_cents` is the base multiplied by one plus `adjustment_pct_applied` divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Third — the **outer** rounding — `gross_amount_cents` is that whole-cent per-ton price multiplied by the line's `tons`, rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, `provisional_amount_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` multiplied by the contract's `provisional_payment_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. Collapsing the second and third steps into a single rounding of base times adjustment times tons produces a different figure whenever the adjusted per-ton price is not already a whole cent, and is wrong. A line's `final_amount_cents` stays null while its status is `PROVISIONAL`, and its `voided_at` stays null unless a dispute voids it.

**Step five: the cascade.** Recording a grading also writes the grading row itself, stamping `superseded_at` on any earlier grading of the same tag, incrementing the tag's `grade_count` by 1 — superseded rows still count — and setting `active_adjustment_pct` to the new grading's `adjustment_pct`. `dispute_deadline_at` is stamped only when it is still null, at the first grading instant plus 72 hours, and a re-grade never re-stamps it. From there the effects fan out. Because settlement lines were created, the contract's `delivered_contract_tons`, `delivered_over_tons` and `provisional_settlement_cents` are recomputed — the first two being the running sums of `tons` on the contract's lines in each bucket with status `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, the third the sum of `provisional_amount_cents` over its `PROVISIONAL` lines. The contract's `rejected_tons` is recomputed as the running sum of `over_rejected_tons` plus `excess_rejected_tons` plus the full `net_tons` of every tag whose disposition is `REJECTED_QUALITY` or `REJECTED_OVER_CAP`, across the tags that resolve to this contract. Because delivered tonnage moved, `tonnage_status` is re-derived: `OPEN` while `delivered_contract_tons` is below `contract_cap_tons`, `CAP_REACHED` once `delivered_contract_tons` reaches or exceeds `contract_cap_tons`, and `HARD_CAP_REACHED` once `delivered_contract_tons` plus `delivered_over_tons` reaches or exceeds `hard_cap_tons`. If the posture leaves `OPEN`, every pick call of the contract in `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` is suspended to `SUSPENDED_CAP`, the `SCHEDULED` appointments of those suspended calls are cancelled, and each affected lane's `committed_tons` and `scheduled_appointment_count` are recomputed. The allocation's `delivered_net_tons` and the block's `delivered_net_tons_vintage` are recomputed. An acknowledged pick call whose `delivered_net_tons` has reached its `estimated_tons` advances to `FULFILLED` — and that check runs before the suspension check, so a call that has just been fulfilled is not also suspended. If this was the last open tag of its appointment, the appointment transitions `ARRIVED → CLOSED`. And on a `REJECTED_QUALITY` or `REJECTED_OVER_CAP` outcome, the producer's `rejected_load_count_vintage`, `prior_vintage_penalty_units` and both reliability scores are recomputed.

Gradings are read individually — exposing the four components, the pre-clamp `raw_adjustment_pct`, the clamped `adjustment_pct`, the `recommended_disposition` and `superseded_at` — and listed with the optional filters `weigh_tag_id` and `recommended_disposition`, which is how a load's full grading history including superseded rows is walked.

### 3.8. Contesting a Grading

A producer who believes the lab got it wrong has 72 hours from the first grading of the load to say so. Raising a dispute is a `Grower` action, permitted only to the producer reached from the tag through its appointment, that appointment's pick call and that call's contract, and it accepts an optional `reason`. Four conditions must hold. The tag's disposition must be `ACCEPTED`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED_QUALITY`; a tag that is still `PENDING`, that was refused as `REJECTED_OVER_CAP`, or that is already `DISPUTED` cannot be contested and yields `ERR_TAG_NOT_DISPUTABLE` with HTTP 409. The logical clock must be at or before the tag's `dispute_deadline_at`, later attempts being `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` with HTTP 409 — and since that deadline was stamped at the *first* grading and is never extended by a re-grade, a load that has been graded twice still has only the original window. The contract must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`.

A dispute unwinds the grading's financial consequences completely. The active grading receives `superseded_at` from the logical clock. Because the active grading was superseded, every settlement line of that tag in status `PROVISIONAL` transitions to `VOID` with `voided_at` stamped from the logical clock — `FINAL` and `VOID` are both terminal for a line, and a voided line is never restored. Because those lines were voided, the contract's `delivered_contract_tons`, `delivered_over_tons`, `provisional_settlement_cents` and `rejected_tons` are all recomputed and `tonnage_status` is re-derived; the allocation's and the block's delivered tonnage follow. Because the cap posture may have returned to `OPEN`, every pick call of the contract sitting in `SUSPENDED_CAP` transitions back to `ISSUED` with its notice figures re-stamped. The tag's `contract_slice_tons`, `over_slice_tons`, `over_rejected_tons` and `excess_rejected_tons` all reset to 0.000, `active_adjustment_pct` is cleared, and the disposition moves to `DISPUTED`, where it waits for a re-grade. A tag whose rejection was undone this way has left the rejected set, so the producer's `rejected_load_count_vintage` and both reliability scores reflect its departure.

Re-grading a disputed tag runs exactly the same pipeline as a first grading — the same four components, the same single clamp, the same waterfall against the contract's *current* remaining capacity, the same pricing and the same cascade — and lands on whichever disposition that pipeline dictates. The two edges worth naming explicitly are `DISPUTED → ACCEPTED`, when the reading accepts and the waterfall leaves both `over_rejected_tons` and `excess_rejected_tons` at 0.000, and `DISPUTED → REJECTED_QUALITY`, when the new reading's `recommended_disposition` is `REJECT_QUALITY`. This dispute-then-re-grade path is a genuine cycle: the receipt-decision dimension returns to a decided state without any new weigh tag being created, which is also why the disposition dimension has no terminal state at all. The re-grade increments `grade_count` again and stamps `superseded_at` on the grading the dispute already superseded only if it has not been stamped — in practice the dispute has already stamped it, and the newly written grading becomes the sole row with a null `superseded_at`.

### 3.9. Closing the Vintage Settlement

When the last truck has been weighed and graded, the `GrowerRelationsManager` closes the vintage, driving the contract `ACTIVE → SETTLED`. The close takes no request body and demands a quiet crush pad. The contract must be `ACTIVE`. No weigh tag reachable through the contract may still sit in `GROSS_CAPTURED` — an untared load on the pad blocks the close with `ERR_TAG_WEIGH_STATE_INVALID`. No weigh tag reachable through the contract may sit in `PENDING` or `DISPUTED` — an ungraded or contested load blocks the close with `ERR_TAG_NOT_DISPUTABLE`. And at least one settlement line of the contract must be in status `PROVISIONAL`, else there is nothing to settle and the close is refused with `ERR_NO_PROVISIONAL_LINES`.

**Finalising the lines.** Only lines in status `PROVISIONAL` participate, taken in ascending `created_at` order; `VOID` lines are ignored and never restored. Which arithmetic runs is decided by the contract's `settlement_basis`, and the two variants return genuinely different totals for byte-identical load data.

Under `LOAD_BY_LOAD`, every line keeps the price it was struck at: each line's `final_amount_cents` becomes its own `gross_amount_cents`, and the settlement total is the sum of those amounts. Each load's clamp was already applied individually at grading time, and nothing is re-priced.

Under `WEIGHTED_BLEND`, only the `CONTRACT` bucket is re-priced, at one tonnage-weighted blended quality adjustment computed from the **pre-clamp** `raw_adjustment_pct` values. Take the contract-bucket lines; the blend tonnage is the sum of their `tons`. The blended raw adjustment is the sum, over those lines, of each line's `tons` multiplied by the `raw_adjustment_pct` of that line's grading, divided by the blend tonnage, rounded half up to 2 decimals. That blended raw figure is then clamped **once** — the lesser of it and the specification's `max_total_bonus_pct`, then the greater of that and the negative of `max_total_penalty_pct`. The blended price is the contract's `base_price_per_ton_cents` multiplied by one plus the clamped blend divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. The bucket total is that blended price multiplied by the blend tonnage, rounded half up to whole cents. Each contract-bucket line, taken in ascending `created_at` order, then receives a `final_amount_cents` of the bucket total multiplied by its own `tons` divided by the blend tonnage, rounded half up to whole cents; the residual, being the bucket total less the sum of those per-line amounts, is added to the **last** line by `created_at` ascending so that no cent is created or destroyed. `OVER_DELIVERY` lines are not blended at all: each keeps a `final_amount_cents` equal to its own `gross_amount_cents`, because spot tonnage was never part of the contract price. The settlement total is the bucket total plus the sum of the over-delivery gross amounts. The reason the two variants diverge is worth understanding: blending pre-clamp adjustments lets a load whose own bonus was clipped by `max_total_bonus_pct` pull the blend above the effect of that clip.

**Deriving the net payable.** Immediately after the lines are finalised, a strictly sequential pipeline runs, and its order is observable in the output. Step one takes the settled gross, that is, the settlement total just computed, and records it on the contract as `final_settlement_cents`. Step two looks up a prompt-payment tier **on that pre-credit total**: below 5,000,000 cents the rate is 0.0 percent; from 5,000,000 up to but not including 10,000,000 cents it is 0.5 percent; from 10,000,000 up to but not including 15,000,000 cents it is 1.0 percent; and at or above 15,000,000 cents it is 1.5 percent. Step three computes `prompt_pay_credit_cents` as the settlement total multiplied by that rate divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. Step four computes the statutory producer-lien holdback, `lien_holdback_cents`, **also keyed on the pre-credit total**: the settlement total multiplied by the contract's `lien_holdback_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. Step five computes `net_payable_cents` as the settlement total plus `prompt_pay_credit_cents` less `lien_holdback_cents`. The ordering is load-bearing: looking the tier up on the credited or the held-back subtotal moves a contract sitting on a tier boundary into a neighbouring tier, and applying the holdback percentage to the credited subtotal changes the holdback itself.

**Distributing across the installments.** The contract's installments, all of them still in `PENDING_SETTLEMENT`, are taken in ascending `installment_no` — an order materialised from the `installment_plan` array and never derived from any identifier. Each receives a `planned_amount_cents` of `net_payable_cents` multiplied by its own `pct_of_settlement` divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents, and a `due_at` of `settlement_closed_at` plus its own `due_offset_days` days. Because percentages that each round independently need not sum to the whole, the residual — `net_payable_cents` less the sum of the planned amounts — is absorbed in full by the installment with the **highest** `installment_no`, added to its `planned_amount_cents`; the residual may be negative, and it is absorbed exactly rather than rounded. After every close, the sum of the installments' `planned_amount_cents` equals `net_payable_cents` exactly.

The close also stamps `settlement_closed_at` from the logical clock, flips every `PROVISIONAL` line to `FINAL`, transitions every installment `PENDING_SETTLEMENT → SCHEDULED`, recomputes the producer's `outstanding_lien_balance_cents`, and withdraws the harvest: every pick call of the contract in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `SUSPENDED_CAP` transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, the `SCHEDULED` appointments of those calls are cancelled, and each affected lane's committed load is recomputed. Until a close happens, `final_settlement_cents`, `prompt_pay_credit_cents`, `lien_holdback_cents` and `net_payable_cents` are all 0, `settlement_closed_at` is null, and every installment's `planned_amount_cents` and `due_at` are 0 and null respectively.

A dedicated computed read reconciles tonnage against money for a contract in one payload, available to the `GrowerRelationsManager`, the `AccountsPayableClerk` and the owning `Grower`. It returns `contract_cap_tons`, `hard_cap_tons`, `cap_scope`, `delivered_contract_tons`, `delivered_over_tons`, `rejected_tons`, `tonnage_status`, `settlement_basis`, `provisional_settlement_cents`, `final_settlement_cents`, `prompt_pay_credit_cents`, `lien_holdback_cents`, `net_payable_cents`, `installment_planned_total_cents` (the sum of `planned_amount_cents` across the contract's installments), `installment_paid_total_cents` (the sum of their `paid_amount_cents`), and `outstanding_lien_balance_cents` for the producer.

### 3.10. Paying the Grower and Releasing the Lien

Payments are recorded by the `AccountsPayableClerk` against one installment at a time, carrying a single `amount_cents`. The installment must be payable — its status must be `SCHEDULED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `DELINQUENT`; an installment still in `PENDING_SETTLEMENT`, or already `PAID`, yields `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_PAYABLE` with HTTP 409. The producer must not be suspended. And the payment may not exceed what is still owed: `amount_cents` must be at most `planned_amount_cents` less `paid_amount_cents`, because an overpayment is refused outright rather than spilled into the next installment — the refusal is `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` with HTTP 409.

Each accepted payment increases `paid_amount_cents` by `amount_cents`. When the payment leaves a balance still owing — that is, when `amount_cents` is strictly less than the remaining balance — the installment lands in `PARTIALLY_PAID`. When the payment settles the remaining balance exactly, the installment reaches `PAID`, and that same edge is available directly from `SCHEDULED` or `DELINQUENT` when a single payment clears the whole remaining balance in one call. `PAID` is terminal. Every payment against a `lien_protected` installment recomputes the producer's `outstanding_lien_balance_cents`, which is the sum, over every installment of every contract of that producer whose `lien_protected` flag is true and whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `DELINQUENT`, of `planned_amount_cents` less `paid_amount_cents`.

Two cascades ride on the final payment. When the last outstanding `lien_protected` installment of a contract reaches `PAID`, the contract's `lien_released_at` is stamped from the logical clock — and it is stamped only when still null. When every installment of the contract has reached `PAID`, the contract itself transitions `SETTLED → CLOSED`, which is terminal; `lien_released_at` is stamped on that transition too if it is still null.

An installment that is not paid on time is not forgiven. When the logical clock advances strictly past an installment's `due_at` while its `paid_amount_cents` is still below its `planned_amount_cents`, the installment transitions to `DELINQUENT` — reachable from both `SCHEDULED` and `PARTIALLY_PAID` — and remains payable from there. Installments are read individually, exposing `installment_no`, `pct_of_settlement`, `due_offset_days`, `lien_protected`, `planned_amount_cents`, `paid_amount_cents`, `due_at` and `status`, and listed with the optional filters `contract_id`, `status` and `lien_protected`. Because installments are created only as a cascade of contract acceptance, that filtered list is their only discovery channel, and it is ordered by `installment_no` ascending then `created_at` ascending.

### 3.11. Multi-Year Renewal, the Evergreen Roll and Termination Notice

A contract's renewal dimension moves independently of its lifecycle. It starts at `NOT_DUE` on creation and, while the lifecycle stays `ACTIVE`, cycles across terms: `NOT_DUE → RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN`, and equally `RENEWED → RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN` and `EVERGREEN_ROLLED → RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN` once the window has been recomputed for the new term. All three of those edges are fired by the logical clock, not by a client: they require the lifecycle to be `ACTIVE` and the clock to have moved into the half-open interval that begins at `renewal_window_opens_at` and ends strictly before `renewal_window_closes_at`. Each fires a contract version increment. The only terminal renewal state is `NOT_RENEWING`.

Renewing inside the open window is a `GrowerRelationsManager` action carrying a mandatory `renewal_term_years` and an optional `renewed_base_price_per_ton_cents`; it drives `RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN → RENEWED`. Four conditions must hold: the lifecycle is `ACTIVE`; `renewal_status` is `RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN`; the clock is still strictly before `renewal_window_closes_at`; and the producer's **lifetime-scoped** reliability score is at least 80.0. A renewal attempted from any other renewal state, or after the window has closed, is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`; a renewal refused on reliability grounds is `ERR_RELIABILITY_BELOW_RENEWAL_THRESHOLD`. Because the gate reads the lifetime score and not the vintage score, a producer who has had a clean current vintage but a poor history can still be refused. A successful renewal increases `term_end_vintage` by `renewal_term_years`, replaces `base_price_per_ton_cents` with `renewed_base_price_per_ton_cents` when that optional input was supplied and leaves it untouched otherwise, and recomputes the renewal window from the new `term_end_vintage` — `renewal_window_closes_at` becomes midnight UTC on the first of November of that year and `renewal_window_opens_at` becomes that instant less `termination_notice_days` days.

If nobody renews, the clock decides. When the clock reaches or passes `renewal_window_closes_at` on an `ACTIVE` contract whose `renewal_status` is `RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN`, the `evergreen_flag` chooses between two outcomes. With the flag true, the term rolls itself forward: `RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN → EVERGREEN_ROLLED`, `term_end_vintage` increases by `evergreen_term_years`, and the window is recomputed from the new `term_end_vintage` so the cycle can repeat. With the flag false, `RENEWAL_WINDOW_OPEN → NOT_RENEWING`, and the renewal dimension stops there for good.

Serving notice of termination is a `GrowerRelationsManager` action carrying `effective_at` and an optional `reason`; it drives the lifecycle `ACTIVE → TERMINATION_PENDING`. The notice must be long enough — `effective_at` must be at or after the logical clock plus `termination_notice_days` days, else `ERR_TERMINATION_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT` with HTTP 422 — and the producer's statutory lien must already be clear: `outstanding_lien_balance_cents` must be exactly 0, else `ERR_LIEN_OUTSTANDING` with HTTP 409. That balance is producer-wide, so an unpaid lien-protected installment on a *different* contract of the same producer blocks the termination of this one. The notice stores `termination_effective_at` from the `effective_at` input, sets `renewal_status` to `NOT_RENEWING`, and clears the harvest: every pick call of the contract in `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, each withdrawn call's `SCHEDULED` appointments transition to `CANCELLED`, each affected lane's `committed_tons` and `scheduled_appointment_count` are recomputed, and every `SCHEDULED` sampling visit of the contract transitions to `CANCELLED` with its crew's `scheduled_visit_count` recomputed. When the clock later reaches or passes `termination_effective_at`, the contract transitions `TERMINATION_PENDING → TERMINATED`, which is terminal. `WITHDRAWN`, `TERMINATED` and `CLOSED` are the three terminal lifecycle states; a contract that never terminates instead ends at `CLOSED` after the last installment is paid.

Contracts are read individually and listed with the optional filters `grower_id`, `quality_spec_id`, `lifecycle_status`, `renewal_status` and `tonnage_status`, newest first by `created_at` descending.

### 3.12. Scoring Producer Reliability

Every producer carries two reliability scores, both on a 0-to-100 scale at one decimal place, both floored at 0.0 and both derived — never written directly. They start at 100.0 with every penalty counter at 0, and they are recomputed on any transition that produces a rejected load, an expired pick call or a no-show appointment, as well as on any clock move that fires those transitions.

Three kinds of misbehaviour carry weights. A rejected load costs 8 units, an expired pick call costs 5 units, and a no-show appointment costs 3 units. The current-vintage penalty is 8 times `rejected_load_count_vintage`, plus 5 times `expired_pick_call_count_vintage`, plus 3 times `no_show_appointment_count_vintage`, where the vintage is the UTC calendar year of the logical clock. The counters are populated from weigh tags whose disposition is `REJECTED_QUALITY` or `REJECTED_OVER_CAP`, pick calls in `EXPIRED` and appointments in `NO_SHOW`, all reached through the producer's contracts and bucketed by each row's own `created_at` UTC calendar year — a tag whose rejection was undone by a dispute now sits in `DISPUTED` and stops counting. `prior_vintage_penalty_units` is the same weighted sum computed over every UTC calendar year strictly before the current vintage.

The two scores then read the same stored rows differently. `reliability_score_vintage` is 100 less the current-vintage penalty, floored at 0.0, ignoring prior vintages entirely. `reliability_score_lifetime` is 100 less the current-vintage penalty less the prior-vintage penalty halved, floored at 0.0 — and the halved prior term is rounded half up to a **whole unit before** it is subtracted, so an odd prior penalty of 5 units costs 3 rather than 2.5. With a current penalty of 8 units and a prior penalty of 5 units the two scores read 92.0 and 89.0 from identical rows; with a prior penalty of 13 units they read 92.0 and 85.0. No endpoint refuses an action on the vintage-scoped score; the only reliability gate in the system is the renewal gate, and it reads the lifetime-scoped score.

Producers are read individually — exposing both scores, all three vintage counters, `prior_vintage_penalty_units` and `outstanding_lien_balance_cents` — and listed with the optional filters `status` and `region_code`, newest first.
