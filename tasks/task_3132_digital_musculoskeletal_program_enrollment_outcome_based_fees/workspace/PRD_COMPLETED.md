# Digital MSK Program Enrollment and Outcome Fees API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Musculoskeletal conditions are the single largest driver of avoidable surgical spend in employer and health-plan populations, and the members who would benefit most are rarely the ones who arrive on their own. The Digital MSK Program Enrollment and Outcome Fees API exists to close that gap: it takes a roster file handed over by an employer or a health plan, screens every row for a musculoskeletal indication and for program age-eligibility, assigns each eligible member to a care track, and then rides that member through guided sensor- or camera-captured exercise therapy, coach escalation, physical-therapist and physician consults, weekly attrition-risk modelling, and outcome measurement — ending either in a graduation, in a dropout, or in a handoff to in-person care.

The commercial half of the product is inseparable from the clinical half. Clients contract on one of two fee structures. Under the engagement-milestone model the client pays a per-member platform fee that is only fully earned by members who actually reach the engagement milestone. Under the measured-savings model the client pays a contracted share of the downstream medical and pharmacy spend that can be attributed to the program inside fixed attribution windows. In both structures the roster that arrives in a given month is not the roster that is finally billed, because screening outcomes, mid-month activation failures, late reconciliations and superseded claim lines all move the number. The API therefore carries two readings of almost every financial quantity — a forward-looking provisional reading fixed at the moment work is first quoted, and a chronological reading recomputed from the corrected records — and exposes the difference between them rather than collapsing it.

This document is the complete contract for the API. It is written for the engineers who will implement the server and for the test authors who will verify it, so every state name, threshold, fee component, rounding rule, cascade and rejection code is stated in full. Where a rule has a boundary — an inclusive week on one side and an exclusive week on the other, a comparison that accepts members sitting exactly on a threshold while another comparison on a different metric rejects members sitting exactly on its own — the boundary is stated explicitly rather than implied.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply across every endpoint of the API unless a narrower rule explicitly overrides them.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which that principal acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` names a role that this API does not declare, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not permitted to perform the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Read operations are generally open to several roles; writes are governed by the exclusivity matrix summarized in §4.

The API declares exactly eight role names, spelled as follows: `EnrollmentOps`, `ClinicalOps`, `Clinician`, `BillingOps`, `Member`, `EligibilityService`, `OutreachService` and `ClinicalReviewService`.

`EnrollmentOps` is program operations: it ingests rosters, screens members, assigns tracks, opens and drops enrollments, books exercise sessions and consults, seeds clinical reviews, and records downstream claim lines including their supersession and reinstatement. `EnrollmentOps` identifies itself with an opaque operator identifier — when `X-User-Role` is `EnrollmentOps`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`.

`ClinicalOps` is clinical administration: it owns client contracts, care-track configuration, the clinician roster, the logical clock, and the weekly sweep. When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicalOps`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity.

`BillingOps` is finance operations: it builds, finalizes, voids and reopens invoices, suspends, reinstates and closes client contracts, recomputes engagement tiers, and reads the retention-credit and membership-roll views. When `X-User-Role` is `BillingOps`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity.

`EligibilityService` is an internal screening service, not a human operator. It re-runs the demographic eligibility rule and applies the weekly session cut. When `X-User-Role` is `EligibilityService`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque service identifier not tied to any persisted entity.

`OutreachService` is the engagement-outreach service that queues and delivers outreach contacts. When `X-User-Role` is `OutreachService`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque service identifier not tied to any persisted entity.

`ClinicalReviewService` is the internal reconciliation service that finalizes measurements, completes enrollments, accelerates stale reviews, denies in-person handoffs and reopens conducted reviews. When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicalReviewService`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque service identifier not tied to any persisted entity.

`Clinician` and `Member` are the two entity-backed roles. When `X-User-Role` is `Clinician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinician`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Clinician` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. This matters concretely for in-person handoffs: the `approving_clinician_id` supplied on a handoff, the `pending_transition_clinician_id` recorded on an enrollment, and the `clinical_reviewing_clinician_id` recorded on a review are all compared against each other and against the acting clinician, so the role is not a courtesy label. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Session completion and outcome measurement recording are member-attributed acts, but the member recorded on the enrollment is not the principal those checks resolve against: for a `Member`-role write the server verifies only that `X-User-ID` names an existing `Member`, and it never compares that identifier to the `member_id` of the enrollment the write addresses.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`; no pagination fields appear on an error response, even when the failing request was a list read.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept a `limit` and an `offset` query parameter. `limit` is the maximum number of items to return and defaults to 10 when omitted, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; a value outside that range is not accepted, and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE` before any record is examined rather than silently clamped or ignored. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0, and a negative offset is refused with the same `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE`; an offset past the end of the result set is not an error and yields an empty page. Unless a rule states otherwise, every list endpoint returns items sorted by creation timestamp ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` is the number of items matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` reports whether further items remain after the returned page.

### 2.4. The ISO Week Lattice and the Logical Clock

Almost every temporal rule in this product is expressed in whole ISO weeks, and the week lattice is therefore part of the API contract rather than an implementation detail. Week counting is anchored at Monday 2026-09-07T00:00:00Z, which is `week_index` 1. Week `week_index` N spans from the anchor plus 7 × (N − 1) days up to, but not including, the anchor plus 7 × N days. The interval is half-open, which has a consequence that shows up throughout the product: an instant that falls exactly on a week boundary belongs to the later week, never to the earlier one.

The server also carries a logical clock, and every temporal comparison described anywhere in this document is evaluated against that clock rather than against real wall-clock time. The clock's startup default is exactly 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z. That instant precedes the week-1 anchor, so the startup clock sits in `week_index` 0 rather than week 1 — `week_index` is defined as the lattice offset from the anchor plus one, and the default is six days short of the anchor. Before any clock-setting call the clock reads that instant, and the server never falls back to real time even if no clock call is ever made. An operator advances the clock explicitly; advancing the clock to a later instant is what makes weeks lapse and what causes the weekly sweep to have work to do. A clock instant must be a parseable ISO 8601 instant, and one that is not is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE` while the clock keeps its previous value. Clock advancement on its own does not move any week-scoped state — state movement happens when the weekly sweep runs, or when a specific weekly operation is invoked. The clock may also be moved backwards, and the only state a backward move carries with it is the week index a later read or recomputation observes: nothing is unwound, restamped or reverted.

Weeks also advance through a per-client sweep cursor. Each client account carries its own shared week-schedule cursor, exposed on the account as `week_schedule_cursor`, and running that client's weekly sweep advances the cursor and then performs the whole weekly reconciliation described in §3. The cursor starts at 0 on a freshly created contract.

### 2.5. Identifier, Timestamp and Version Conventions

Every resource returned by this API carries an `id`. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix: it is never `mem_…`, `enr_…` or any other domain-flavoured string, and clients must treat it as an opaque token. It is minted in the canonical 8-4-4-4-12 hyphenated hexadecimal UUID shape, but its digits are drawn at random and it carries no version or variant nibble a client may key on. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered with three fractional digits.

Resources with a mutable lifecycle additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits and is refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations caused by a cascade rather than by a direct call on that resource. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource; it is the field against which optimistic concurrency checks are made. The following entities carry the full set — `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`: `ClientAccount`, `Member`, `EligibilityFile`, `Enrollment`, `ClinicalReview`, `ExerciseSession`, `ClaimLine`, `OutreachContact`, `Appointment`, `OutcomeMeasurement`, `FeeInvoice` and `CareTransition`.

Two configuration entities are deliberately narrower. `ProgramTrack` and `Clinician` carry only `id` and `created_at`; neither carries `updated_at`, and neither carries `version`. A care-track configuration and a clinician row are therefore not version-guarded, and no request anywhere in this API carries an expected-version field for either of them; an implementation that invents a `version` on these two resources will not be asked for it and should not expose it.

Derived fields are ordinary mutations for the purpose of these conventions. When a reconciliation recomputes a stored derived quantity — an adherence rate, an attributed-savings total, a roster fee variant, a dropout risk score — the owning resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if a client had written the field directly.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two writes in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and both are guarded because the parent they hang off is mutated by unrelated traffic. In each case the client must supply the parent's last observed `version` in a named request field, and a mismatch with the parent's current stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The first guarded write is the track assignment performed off a roster file. That request must carry `expected_client_account_version`, matched against the current `version` of the sponsoring `ClientAccount`. A client account may legitimately carry several roster files, and its `version` advances every time one of them is screened or re-screened, so an assigning writer's view of the account is stale whenever any other file has moved through screening in the meantime. The field is not optional: a request that omits it is invalid, and a request that supplies a value other than the account's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before any enrollment is created.

The second guarded write is the in-person handoff approval. That request must carry `expected_eligibility_file_version`, matched against the current `version` of the `EligibilityFile` whose roster produced the enrollment being activated. A roster file is re-screened and re-assigned as corrections land, so an approval carrying the version read back at file ingestion time is stale precisely when a correction has landed since. As with the first guard, the field is required and a mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

No other endpoint in this API takes an expected-version field. Endpoints that mutate a `ClientAccount`, an `EligibilityFile`, an `Enrollment` or any other resource without appearing in the two rules above accept their writes unconditionally with respect to versioning.

### 2.7. Money, Weights and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this API is an integer number of cents and is named accordingly — `base_fee_per_member_cents`, `amount_cents`, `attributed_savings_cents`, `total_monthly_fee_cents`, `provisional_total_monthly_fee_cents`, `fee_variance_cents`, `milestone_engagement_component_cents`, `savings_share_component_cents`, `additional_fee_component_cents`, `recalculated_component_cents`, `provisional_amount_cents`, `final_amount_cents`, `net_delta_cents`, `finalized_total_cents`, `retained_credit_amount_cents`, `proposal_amount_cents`, `proposed_amount_cents` and `entitled_amount_cents` are all integers, never decimals and never strings.

All internal arithmetic on these quantities is performed in decimal, never in binary floating point. Rounding, wherever this document calls for it, is round half up, and the rounding is applied once per named component at the end of that component's computation rather than at each intermediate step. Where a component is built by summing over rows, the sum is completed first and the single rounding is applied to the sum.

Non-monetary scored quantities carry their own scales. Adherence rates and red-flag weights are stored to 2 decimal places with round half up: `adherence_rate`, `red_flag_weight`. The per-measurement deltas `pain_delta_points` and `function_delta_points`, and the enrollment-level `function_gain_points` that is written from a finalized measurement's delta, are stored to 1 decimal place with round half up. Pain and function scores themselves, and the targets held on a care track, preserve the precision they are supplied with and are not re-rounded. Integer-valued computed fields — counts, week indices, risk scores and the Boolean-as-integer milestone flag — carry no rounding at all. Two consequences of this discipline are worth pinning. The per-measurement deltas are computed before they are rounded — the window baseline pain score minus the reported `pain_score` for the pain side, and the reported `function_score` minus the window baseline function score for the function side — so the one-decimal rounding is applied to the difference itself and not to the scores that form it, which matters when a baseline is supplied at a finer precision than one decimal. And every scored quantity is emitted as a JSON number rather than a string — `adherence_rate`, `red_flag_weight`, `pain_delta_points`, `function_delta_points`, `pain_reduction_points` and `function_gain_points` are all rendered as numbers.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline on Contract and Clinician Changes

State changes at the top of the hierarchy are never local. Closing a client contract ripples down through its members and their enrollments; suspending a contract ripples sideways into its invoices and its in-flight handoffs; deactivating a clinician ripples into the consults that clinician was due to conduct and from there into the outcomes recorded on the affected enrollments. In every such case each affected row is mutated independently: its own `updated_at` is refreshed and its own `version` increments, in addition to whatever parent change triggered the cascade. A cascade is not compensated on the way back out. Reinstating a suspended client restores the account to service and nothing else — voided draft invoices stay voided and enrollments that were reverted out of a pending handoff are not resurrected. An implementation that repairs the cascade on reinstatement will drift the conserved quantities described in §3 and will be detected.

Deactivating a clinician is a cascade with a specifically observable financial consequence: every consult still scheduled on that clinician's timeline is cancelled, and each affected enrollment has its recorded pain reduction reset to zero while its cancellation counter is incremented. That reset is intentional and is not reversed if the clinician is later restored.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Retrieval and Resolution Order

Requests that address a resource by more than one path segment are resolved left to right. When a request names a chain of identifiers and more than one of them is unknown, the leftmost unknown segment determines the failure and the response is the 404 carrying `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. A request that names an unknown inner resource under a known outer resource resolves the outer resource first and then reports the inner failure, still as `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Existence checking is therefore strictly positional, and an implementation that resolves segments in any other order — for example by looking up the most specific identifier first, or by collecting all unknown identifiers and reporting the last — will produce the wrong status for the wrong request.

### 2.10. Rejected Transitions

Several state machines in this API contain edges that exist only to be refused. A refused edge is not a missing edge: the transition is attempted, and the request fails with a specific error code while no side effect of any kind fires — no version increment, no timestamp refresh, no cascade. The refused edges are: conducting a clinical review that is already conducted, which fails with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_CONDUCTED`; conducting a clinical review whose enrollment is not in the review queue, which fails with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`; completing an exercise session that has already been marked missed, which fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and extends to any session not in `SCHEDULED`; re-delivering an outreach contact that has already been delivered, which fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; superseding an outcome measurement that has already been finalized, which fails with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_FINALIZED`; reopening a voided invoice, which fails with `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_VOID`; and approving or denying a handoff when no transition review is pending on the enrollment, which fails with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_PENDING`. Each of these is described again in §3 next to the workflow it guards, and each carries its own HTTP status in §5. The lifecycle field each refused edge hangs off is the entity's own status field — `review_status` for a clinical review, `session_status` for a guided exercise session, `outreach_status` for an outreach contact, `measurement_status` for an outcome measurement, `invoice_status` for a fee invoice, and `transition_status` for an in-person handoff — and in every case the field is left exactly as it was found.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

Where several validation rules can apply to the same request, they are evaluated in a fixed order and the first failure is the one reported. The order is: (1) header validity, so a request with missing or unrecognised context headers fails with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_CONTEXT_HEADER` before anything else is examined; (2) role permission, so a correctly identified caller without the right role fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` before resource resolution; (3) existence of every path-addressed resource, resolved leftmost-first, failing with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; and then the endpoint-specific business rules. Within the business rules the sequence is clinical-safety and segregation-of-duties checks first, then state checks, then scheduling and capacity checks, then uniqueness and temporal-window checks, and finally fiscal checks. The practical consequence is that a stale patient-versus-validation contest always resolves in favour of the safety rule: a handoff approval attempted by the reviewing clinician on an enrollment that has also moved out of its review state reports `ERR_CLINICIAN_DUAL_ROLE`, not the state failure, and a session completion attempted on an enrollment that is not ACTIVE reports the state failure rather than any score-range failure.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative that follows is organised around the member's journey: how a roster becomes a book of open enrollments, how a clinical decision routes a member onto a care track, how the guided-therapy weeks accrue adherence and attrition risk, how outcomes and downstream claims are measured, how the money is assembled, and how a member leaves the digital program. Rules are stated where the workflow they influence actually occurs, and some rules deliberately surface in more than one place because they are load-bearing on more than one side of the product.

### 3.1. Contracting, Care Tracks and Roster Intake

A client relationship begins when a `ClinicalOps` operator creates a `ClientAccount` with the contracting counterparty's `legal_name` and a `client_type`, which is either `EMPLOYER` or `HEALTH_PLAN`. The contract also fixes how the client will be billed: `billing_model` selects between `ENGAGEMENT_MILESTONE` and `OUTCOME_SHARED_SAVINGS` and defaults to `ENGAGEMENT_MILESTONE` when the operator does not specify one. When the client is billed on measured savings, a `savings_share_rate` records the share of attributed downstream savings that will be invoiced; it defaults to 0.30 and must lie between 0 and 1 inclusive. Every contract also carries an `expected_monthly_engagement_rate`, the share of assigned members the contract expects to reach the engagement milestone; it defaults to 0.60 and must likewise lie between 0 and 1 inclusive. A freshly created account is `ACTIVE`; the only other states it can hold are `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED`, and it returns to `ACTIVE` only by reinstatement.

Creating the contract also instantiates the care-track configurations the client will use. When the creation request carries a `program_tracks` list, each supplied specification is created as a `ProgramTrack` owned by the new contract in the order the list gives, and the created account's response exposes the identifiers of the tracks so created as `program_track_ids`. A `ProgramTrack` is configuration, not workflow: it is created by a `ClinicalOps` operator with a `track_code` of `ACUTE`, `CHRONIC` or `SURGERY_AVOIDANCE`, a `base_fee_per_member_cents` monthly platform fee, an `expected_sessions_per_week` prescribing how many sessions per week the track expects, which the adherence measure is calibrated against, a `pain_reduction_target_points` and a `function_gain_target_points` that state what the outcome milestone requires, a `min_adherence_rate` that a member must hold to stay engagement-milestone eligible (defaulting to 0.70), and a `severity_weights` map used when downstream claim savings are valued. The weights map carries exactly two keys, `LOW` and `HIGH`, and no others; a map missing either key, carrying a third, or supplied as something other than a map is refused rather than repaired. A track may also be created through its own route without naming an owning contract, in which case it belongs to no client; a track created with the contract records that contract as its owner. Because roster pricing resolves its base from the `ACUTE` track, and more than one `ACUTE` track may exist, the resolution takes the first `ACUTE` track the server holds in creation order. Care-track rows are immutable in the sense set out in §2.5: they carry an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else in the way of system bookkeeping, so no request ever carries an expected version for a track. The `base_fee_per_member_cents`, `expected_sessions_per_week`, `pain_reduction_target_points` and `function_gain_target_points` a track is created with preserve the values supplied and are not re-rounded or rescaled.

Roster intake is the entry point for the population. An `EnrollmentOps` operator ingests a file for a client by supplying the `client_account_id`, a `file_period` in `YYYY-MM` form, and a `roster_format` of either `STANDARD` or `LEGACY`. Ingesting creates the `EligibilityFile` in `INGESTED` status and zeroes all of its counters and fee fields: `eligible_member_count`, `ineligible_member_count` and `screened_member_count` all begin at zero, as do `total_monthly_fee_cents`, `provisional_total_monthly_fee_cents` and `fee_variance_cents`. The sponsoring account's `version` advances as a consequence of the ingest even though the account's own fields do not change.

Two guards sit on roster intake. A new file's `file_period` is checked against the client's greatest existing roster period for recency, and a period that is not strictly later than that greatest period is rejected with `ERR_FILE_PERIOD_REGRESSION`. That check is a temporal-window check and is evaluated before the fiscal guard below, so a file that both regresses and names a non-ACTIVE client reports the regression. The second guard is fiscal: a roster file may only be created against a client whose `account_status` is `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` client cannot take on new billable work, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Member rows are added one at a time against the file they arrived on. Each row carries the sponsoring `client_account_id`, the owning `eligibility_file_id`, the client-side `external_member_ref` — which must be unique within that client — the member's `full_name`, a `date_of_birth` expressed as an ISO 8601 date at midnight UTC, a `musculo_condition_code` from the set `KNEE_OA`, `LOW_BACK`, `SHOULDER`, `HIP`, `NECK` or `OTHER`, a `baseline_pain_score` between 0 and 10 inclusive, a `baseline_function_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and an optional `red_flag_multipliers` list that defaults to the empty list. Either baseline outside its range is refused with `ERR_VITALS_OUT_OF_RANGE` at the moment the row is added rather than deferred to screening, and an `external_member_ref` that repeats one already held by the same client is refused. A new member row is `PENDING_ELIGIBILITY`, carries no eligibility basis yet, and carries a `red_flag_weight` computed from its multipliers immediately; adding it advances the owning file's `version`.

Screening is the moment the roster becomes a population. An `EnrollmentOps` operator screens a file, which requires the file to be `INGESTED` and to carry at least one member row. Every member row on the file leaves `PENDING_ELIGIBILITY` in one of exactly two ways. A row becomes `ELIGIBLE` when the member carries at least one roster row whose `musculo_condition_code` is not `OTHER` and whose `date_of_birth` is at least 18 years before the current logical clock instant; that outcome records `eligibility_basis` as `MSK_FLAG`. A row becomes `INELIGIBLE` when the member carries no row whose `musculo_condition_code` is not `OTHER`; that outcome records `eligibility_basis` as `NO_MSK_FLAG`. Every member moved by a screening has its own `version` incremented. The file itself moves from `INGESTED` to `SCREENED`, its `version` increments, and its counters are recomputed — `eligible_member_count` and `ineligible_member_count` count the members that landed in each outcome and `screened_member_count` is their sum — after which the roster fee variants described in §3.2 are computed in the same operation.

The member eligibility axes are two independent dimensions on the same row, not one. `member_status` and `eligibility_basis` advance separately, nothing in the system couples an assignment of one to a value of the other, and both remain separately observable. An operator who later re-runs the demographic rule against the current clock does so through the `EligibilityService`, and that path moves the member's status without regard to the basis that a screening had recorded — except that a member whose basis is `COVERAGE_TERM` is excluded from it, because a coverage-termination outcome is not revisited by a demographic re-check; such a request is refused and changes nothing. The re-check also declines any member whose status has already left the walkable set — a member who is `DROPPED` or `GRADUATED` is not re-evaluated and nothing changes. Re-running the rule stamps `eligibility_basis` as `DEMOGRAPHICS` regardless of which way the decision falls, and a member already `ENROLLED` takes only that basis stamp: the re-check leaves an engaged member's `ENROLLED` status untouched rather than moving it to `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE`.

### 3.2. Roster Fee Variants, Adherence Credit and Attrition Risk

Screening does more than classify members: it prices the roster, and it prices it twice. Both readings start from a per-member base and a per-member payability weight. The base is the `base_fee_per_member_cents` of the care track whose `track_code` is `ACUTE` — the roster base, in force before any clinical assignment has happened — and it is the same base for every member on the file. The weight is 1.00 for a member whose `member_status` is `ELIGIBLE` and 0.50 for a member whose `member_status` is `INELIGIBLE`.

Formatting changes the base in a way that must not be deferred. When the file's `roster_format` is `LEGACY`, the base is reduced to three-quarters of its value by flooring it to a whole number of cents, and that flooring is applied to each member's base individually before the weighted sum is taken. Summing the unfloored bases first and flooring the sum once afterwards returns different cent totals whenever any member's base is not an exact multiple of four, so the per-member order is pinned rather than left to choice.

The weighted numerator is the sum, over every member row on the file taken in roster row order, of that member's base multiplied by 100 and then multiplied by that member's weight. Weights are resolved from `member_status` first, the denominator division happens second, and the single rounding happens last. The multiplication by 100 is what keeps the arithmetic exact while the weight may be a half, so the numerator is a whole number even when ineligible members contribute their half weight.

The chronological reading — `total_monthly_fee_cents`, the variant that is actually billed — divides the weighted numerator by `eligible_member_count` and rounds the quotient half up to a whole number of cents. Its inclusion set is every member row on the file, in roster row order, so ineligible members contribute their half weight to the numerator while only the eligible ones form the denominator. When `eligible_member_count` is zero the chronological fee is exactly 0 rather than a division error or a null.

The forward-looking reading — `provisional_total_monthly_fee_cents` — uses the same weighted numerator over the same inclusion set but a projected denominator: the file's `member_row_count` multiplied by the sponsoring contract's `expected_monthly_engagement_rate`, rounded half up once at the end. `member_row_count` is the number of member rows the file carries, which on a screened file is the same count as `screened_member_count`. When that projected denominator is zero the provisional fee is exactly 0 rather than a division error, and the chronological reading's zero-eligible-count rule and this zero-denominator rule can therefore both apply to the same file without either raising. The two readings are deliberately divergent. The forward-looking variant projects the eligible denominator from the contract rather than from the members who actually screened in, so for any roster whose observed eligible share differs from the contractual expectation the two variants return different cents. `fee_variance_cents` is the chronological total minus the provisional total, and is stored as a signed integer.

Guided sessions accrue adherence credit under a session-scoped rule rather than a member-scoped one. A session belongs to exactly one enrollment, and only sessions bound to the enrollment in question ever count: a session bound to a different enrollment never enters the measure even when the same member owns both enrollments. Within the enrollment, the inclusion set is every session whose `session_week` is at or after `enrolled_week` and strictly before the current `week_index`. The half-open upper bound is absolute — sessions booked into the week the clock currently sits in never enter, whether they are `COMPLETED` or `MISSED` — and sessions belonging to an enrollment that was later dropped are excluded from every subsequent computation and never contribute to a replacement enrollment's rate. The dropped-enrollment exclusion is total: once an enrollment is `DROPPED` its session set contributes nothing at all, so its adherence reads 0.0 regardless of how many sessions it completed before the drop.

Each session carries its own contribution to the numerator and denominator. `adherence_numerator` is 1 when the session is `COMPLETED` and 0 otherwise. `adherence_denominator` is 1, doubled to 2 when `track_restricted` is true, so a completion achieved under a surgery-avoidance restriction is credited on a doubled denominator and therefore earns half the adherence a normal completion earns. `adherence_rate` is the sum of the numerator over the inclusion set divided by the sum of the denominator over the same set, rounded half up to two decimal places. The denominator sum is over sessions, not over weeks, and the rate is capped naturally by the fact that a completed session contributes at most its own denominator. When the inclusion set is empty the rate is exactly 0.0 — not null, and not an error. `adherence_scored_through_week` records the current `week_index` as of the computation, and `weeks_active` is the difference between the current week index and the enrollment's `enrolled_week` — zero in the week the enrollment activated and one greater for each week that has since begun. `weeks_active` measures the enrollment, not the member: a member who has been with the client for a year but enrolled three weeks ago has a `weeks_active` of three.

Attrition risk is scored weekly and is deliberately blind to member-sourced traffic. `dropout_risk_score` is a deterministic integer between 0 and 100 built from four weighted terms over the same session inclusion set used for adherence — sessions of this enrollment with `session_week` at or after `enrolled_week` and strictly before the current `week_index`. The first term is twelve points for each week in the longest run of consecutive week indices ending at the week immediately before the current one in which every scheduled session carries `MISSED`. The run ends at the first week, counting backwards from the week before the current one, that carries no session at all as well as at the first week that carries a session not marked `MISSED`, and it is floored at the enrollment's `enrolled_week`; a week whose only session is still `SCHEDULED` therefore breaks the run exactly as a completed one does. The second term is fifteen points for each outreach contact of this enrollment that was booked into week 1 through week 4 inclusive and that ended `FAILED`, counting both contacts that lapsed without any member-sourced delivery and contacts that ended in an explicit negative delivery event. The third term is forty points, added once, when the enrollment's `adherence_rate` is strictly below 0.35. The fourth term is twenty points, added once, when `weeks_active` is at least 4 and `adherence_rate` is strictly below 0.50. The four terms are summed and the total is capped at 100, so the score saturates rather than exceeding the ceiling. `dropout_risk_as_of_week` records the `week_index` the clock sat in at the instant the score was last computed — the current week for a recomputation made as the sweep advances, so the stamp and `adherence_scored_through_week` move together. Every per-week ordering this measure depends on is taken directly from the ISO week numbers themselves rather than from the order rows were created, so the tie-break rule has no work to do here; where an ordering over rows has genuine ties — the review reconciliation ladder of §3.5 — the tie breaks on `created_at` ascending and never on `id`.

Every recomputation of the risk score also refreshes the two escalation fields that hang off it: `coach_escalation_required` is the integer-Boolean true exactly when the recomputed score is at or above 60, and a member whose enrollment crosses that line has their own `coach_escalated` flag set on the member row. The escalation flag is set and never cleared by the risk recomputation, so a member who was once escalated stays escalated even after a later recomputation brings the score back below the threshold. The 60-point escalation line is a different quantity from the 0.30 red-flag threshold and from the milestone boundary, and the three are not interchangeable.

The two adherence thresholds in the risk score are strict inequalities and must not be softened. A member sitting exactly at 0.35 adherence is not penalised by the third term, and a member four or more weeks in sitting exactly at 0.50 adherence is not penalised by the fourth. The boundary matters because adherence rates are stored to two decimal places, so landing exactly on 0.35 or 0.50 is reachable rather than pathological.

A separate clinical risk measure runs per client on the weekly sweep, and it is the one quantity in this product that rounds to two decimal places while being a weight rather than a rate. `red_flag_weight` is computed on a member as the sum, over each entry in `red_flag_multipliers`, of that multiplier minus 1.0, stored rounded half up to two decimal places. The members included are those of the client whose `member_status` is `ELIGIBLE` or `ENROLLED` and whose `musculo_condition_code` is `LOW_BACK` or `NECK`. The weekly sweep runs this measure exactly once for each week it advances, compares the resulting weight against 0.30 using a strictly-greater test, and escalates nothing at exactly 0.30; no week is ever processed twice, so a member who sits above the threshold for several consecutive weeks is escalated once per week rather than repeatedly within a week. An escalation records on the member the most recent week on which it fired. This threshold must not be confused with the adherence milestone boundary described in §3.7. The two sit on different metrics, are reached through different endpoints, and one accepts the exact boundary value while the other rejects it.

### 3.3. Track Assignment, Clinical Review and Program Activation

Assigning program tracks is the act that turns a screened roster into a book of open enrollments, and it is the first of the two writes guarded by optimistic concurrency control. An `EnrollmentOps` operator supplies `expected_client_account_version` alongside the assignment, and that value must equal the sponsoring `ClientAccount`'s current `version`. A mismatch means the account has moved since the operator last read it — most commonly because a second roster file was screened in between — and the assignment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before any enrollment is created. The file must be `SCREENED` and must report an `eligible_member_count` of at least 1. On success the file becomes `ASSIGNED` and its `version` increments; the account's `version` also increments because the assignment advances it.

For every member on the file whose `member_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, the assignment creates one `Enrollment` in `PENDING_SCREENING` status, stamped with its `eligibility_file_id` as the file that produced it and its `member_id` as the member, starting at `version` 1. No enrollment is created for an ineligible member, and no enrollment is created for a member who was already enrolled through some earlier attempt. Each freshly created enrollment then auto-advances immediately from `PENDING_SCREENING` to `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` in the same operation, stamping `review_pending_since` with the current logical clock instant. That instant is the anchor for the review-reconciliation window described in §3.5, so it is stamped at creation and re-stamped every time an enrollment is returned to the review queue.

Track assignment does not seed a clinical review. The enrollment arrives in the queue empty, and a review row is separately seeded by an `EnrollmentOps` operator with the `enrollment_id` under review, the `reviewer_id` of the clinician who will conduct it, and free-text `review_notes`; a `reviewer_id` that does not name an existing `Clinician` is refused. A seeded review is `SUBMITTED` and records `review_opened_at` as the current instant; its `conducted_at` is null until it is conducted. An enrollment may also be opened directly by an `EnrollmentOps` operator for a specific member, and that path creates the enrollment already in `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` with `review_pending_since` stamped, with `pain_reduction_points` and `function_gain_points` both zeroed and `attributed_savings_cents` zeroed. Direct opening also declines to seed a review. When the direct path names an `eligibility_file_id` it pins the enrollment to that file; when it does not, the enrollment is unpinned. The member must be strictly older than 18 years at the current logical clock instant — a member whose eighteenth birthday falls exactly on the current instant is refused — and a member who is not is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_UNDERAGE`. Opening or dropping an enrollment on a member advances that member's `version`.

Closing a roster file and opening an enrollment share a version-side effect worth stating plainly: the file an enrollment is opened against is not itself touched by the opening, so a directly opened enrollment leaves its `eligibility_file_id`'s `version` where it stood while advancing the member's. The file's `version` is advanced by adding a member row to it, by screening it, by assigning tracks off it and by closing it, and by nothing else.

Conducting a review is a `Clinician` act and carries a decision. A `review_decision` of `APPROVE` requires the review to be `SUBMITTED`, requires the enrollment to be `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW`, and requires the supplied `reviewed_track_id` to identify an existing `ProgramTrack`; the decision recorded is `APPROVED`. Approval writes the care track onto the enrollment as `program_track_id`, stamps `clinical_review_outcome` and `clinical_reviewing_clinician_id`, and moves the enrollment straight on to `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW` with `transition_reason` set to `CLINICIAN_REQUEST` and `pending_transition_clinician_id` set to the conducting clinician. The clinical reviewer recorded on the enrollment is the acting `Clinician` — the identifier carried in `X-User-ID`, or the `reviewer_id` the request supplies when it supplies one — and it is that same identifier that the segregation-of-duties check on the later handoff compares against. A review conducted with `REJECT` instead records the decision `REJECT` on the review and lands the enrollment's `clinical_review_outcome` at `REJECTED` while leaving the enrollment exactly where it was in the review queue. That is the normal path into the handoff queue, and it is worth noting that it skips the standalone handoff registration entirely: a clinician who conducts an approving review has, in the same act, already asked for the in-person leg. The review becomes `CONDUCTED` with `conducted_at` stamped, and both the review's and the enrollment's `version` increment. The refusal ladder for a conduct is ordered review-first: a review that is already `CONDUCTED` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_CONDUCTED` whichever decision is supplied and wherever the enrollment has since moved, before the enrollment's own queue membership is even examined.

A review can be returned to the queue. A `ClinicalReviewService` operator reopens a `CONDUCTED` review whose enrollment is in `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW`, setting the review back to `SUBMITTED` with `conducted_at` cleared, and clearing the enrollment's `clinical_review_outcome`, `program_track_id`, `transition_reason` and `pending_transition_clinician_id` while returning it to `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` with a fresh `review_pending_since`. The enrollment's recorded `clinical_review_outcome` has only two possible values, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED`, and it is null until a review has actually been conducted on the enrollment. A review's own `review_decision` is likewise a two-valued choice — `APPROVE` or `REJECT` — and the two enumerations are not the same field: a decision is what a clinician supplies, while the outcome is what the enrollment records. Because the ladder's automatic conduct described in §3.5 always supplies `REJECT`, a review that lapses unconducted leaves the enrollment without an approving outcome and therefore without a care track. There is no ceiling on how many times this revision loop may turn: a review may be conducted, reopened and conducted again indefinitely, and a reopened review must be conducted again before its enrollment can reach `ACTIVE`. Attempting to conduct a review that is already `CONDUCTED` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_CONDUCTED` and changes nothing. Attempting to conduct a review whose enrollment is not `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, and that refusal is the one that takes precedence during the reconciliation ladder described in §3.5 when a lapsed review is auto-conducted.

A clinician may also register a handoff request while the enrollment is still under clinical review, without going through a review decision. The request carries a `reason` that must be one of `CLINICIAN_REQUEST`, `NO_ENGAGEMENT` or `ADVERSE_EVENT`. It requires the enrollment to be `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` and requires that the enrollment's `clinical_review_outcome` is not `APPROVED` — an enrollment that already carries an approving decision cannot have a handoff registered against it by this route, because the approving review has already moved it on. On success the enrollment becomes `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW`, `transition_reason` records the supplied reason, and `pending_transition_clinician_id` records the acting clinician's identifier.

Activation is the second write guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and it is a `Clinician` act. The approving clinician supplies `approving_clinician_id` together with `expected_eligibility_file_version`, which must equal the current `version` of the `EligibilityFile` whose roster produced the enrollment; an enrollment that was opened directly without a roster file is unpinned, and an `expected_eligibility_file_version` of 1 is then accepted while any other value yields the mismatch. A file that has been re-screened or re-assigned since the approver last read it produces `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, and the activation is refused. Two further requirements attach to the approving clinician. The approver must differ from the clinician recorded as `clinical_reviewing_clinician_id` on the enrollment — if the same clinician conducted the review and then attempts to approve the handoff, the request is refused with `ERR_CLINICIAN_DUAL_ROLE` (HTTP 403), which is the segregation-of-duties rule at the heart of this product and applies anywhere a handoff is approved or completed. And when a handoff is denied rather than approved, the denying clinician's identifier must equal the enrollment's `pending_transition_clinician_id`; a different clinician attempting to deny is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_REVIEWER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Attempting to approve or deny when no transition review is pending on the enrollment is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_PENDING`; for approval the enrollment counts as having no review pending unless it sits in `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` or `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW`, while denial requires the enrollment to be `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW` specifically. The safety rule is evaluated before the state rule on both legs, so the dual-role refusal wins the contest described in §2.11. The dual-role check compares the approver against the enrollment's recorded `clinical_reviewing_clinician_id` and only bites when that field is set.

Approval activates the enrollment. `enrollment_status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `enrolled_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, and `enrolled_week` is the `week_index` of that instant — this is the unique adversary-week anchor for everything downstream of activation. Activation resets the outcome fields to their starting values: `pain_reduction_points` and `function_gain_points` are zeroed, `attributed_savings_cents` is zeroed, `completed_week` is null, `weeks_active` is reset to zero, `adherence_rate` is reset to 0.0, `dropout_risk_score` is reset to 0 and `coach_escalation_required` is cleared, and both `transition_reason` and `pending_transition_clinician_id` are cleared. It also creates exactly one `CareTransition` for the enrollment in `PENDING` status carrying the approving clinician as `approving_clinician_id`. The member's own status becomes `ENROLLED` when every active enrollment the member holds is now `ACTIVE` — an enrollment the approval leaves in some other state, or one the member holds in `DROPPED` or `TRANSITIONED` form, does not block the promotion, which is decided over the enrollments the member currently holds rather than over their history. The enrollment's `version`, the member's `version` and the eligibility file's `version` all advance.

Denial does not end the handoff; it returns it to the queue. Denying moves the enrollment from `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW` back to `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW`, re-stamps `review_pending_since` with the current instant, and clears both `transition_reason` and `pending_transition_clinician_id`. The return to the queue also clears the enrollment's `clinical_review_outcome`, because a handoff registered against a denied enrollment would otherwise be refused by the rule that an approving outcome blocks re-registration; the care track the approval wrote is left on the enrollment and is not cleared by the denial. The two states therefore form a lasting loop rather than a one-way demotion: a handoff that is denied can be re-registered, and a review that is reopened can be re-conducted, so an enrollment may cycle through `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` and `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW` many times before it is finally activated or finally refused.

A member can lose eligibility while an enrollment is still in the queue, and the enrollment must follow. When the member's `member_status` becomes `INELIGIBLE` — whether because the sponsoring client closed and cascaded a coverage-term outcome, or through any other route — every enrollment on that member that is still `PENDING_SCREENING` or `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` becomes `ACTIVATION_FAILED`, and each such enrollment's `version` increments. An enrollment in `ACTIVATION_FAILED` cannot take new work: booking an exercise session against it is refused with `ERR_SESSION_ACTIVATION_FAILED`.

### 3.4. The Guided-Therapy Weeks: Sessions, Outreach and Consults

Once an enrollment is `ACTIVE`, the member's week is populated by scheduled guided sessions and by engagement outreach. Booking a session is an `EnrollmentOps` act against the enrollment and carries the instant the session is planned for as `session_date`, the week it is booked into as `session_week`, and the capture modality as `recorded_via`, either `SENSOR` or `CAMERA`. The booking also states `planned_reps` and `planned_minutes` — what the wearable or camera coach will ask the member to do — and may flag `track_restricted` when the member is on a surgery-avoidance restriction, which defaults to false. A booked session is `SCHEDULED`, with `completed_reps` and `completed_minutes` both zero, and `scheduled_send_at` stamped with the instant the booking was created. That instant is the half-open anchor for the weekly session cut described below, so it is worth recording faithfully. Booking is permitted on any enrollment except one in `ACTIVATION_FAILED`: nothing in the booking itself requires the enrollment to be `ACTIVE`, so sessions may be booked onto an enrollment that is still in the review queue.

`session_week` is not decorative: it must equal the `week_index` of `session_date`. The week a session is booked into is the week it is judged in, which is why a session booked into a week that has already elapsed cannot be back-filled silently — the weekly sweep will mark it missed the first time the sweep cursor passes it. A second booking constraint is that a single enrollment may not carry two sessions in the same week: a booking whose `session_week` duplicates an existing session on the same enrollment is refused with `ERR_WEEK_ALREADY_SCHEDULED`.

Completing a session is a `Member` act. The member supplies `completed_denominator_units` together with `pain_score_after`, the post-session pain the member reports. Completion requires the session to be `SCHEDULED`, requires the enrollment to be `ACTIVE`, and requires `session_week` to equal the `week_index` of the current logical clock instant — a scheduled session completed outside the week it was booked into is refused with `ERR_SESSION_WEEK_CLOSED`. `pain_score_after` must be supplied and must fall within the pain range; a value outside [0, 10] on this field, or a `function_score` outside [0, 100] when one is recorded on a measurement, is refused with `ERR_VITALS_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). A completion that reports a pain score worse than the pre-session score by more than the tolerated delta is refused with `ERR_PAIN_WORSENED`, so the system does not silently accept a session that made the member worse.

A successful completion is generous with effort and strict about credit. `session_status` becomes `COMPLETED`, `completed_reps` is set to the session's `planned_reps` and `completed_minutes` to its `planned_minutes` — the member is credited with the full prescription regardless of what `completed_denominator_units` reports. `pain_score_before` is snapshotted from the member's latest recorded measurement at the moment of completion. The adherence credit is where `completed_denominator_units` and the reported pain actually bite: `adherence_numerator` is set to the smaller of 1 and the sum of `completed_denominator_units` plus one additional unit when `pain_score_after` is less than or equal to `pain_score_before` — in other words a session that did not worsen the member's pain earns a bonus unit, but the numerator can never exceed 1. `adherence_denominator` is 1, or 2 when `track_restricted` is true. Completion then recomputes `adherence_rate` over completed sessions whose `session_week` precedes the current week, recomputes `adherence_scored_through_week`, `weeks_active` and `projected_engagement_milestone`, and advances the session's and the enrollment's `version`.

What a member-sourced completion does not do is move the risk score. A completion never recomputes `dropout_risk_score`. This is deliberate: the risk model is driven by misses and by undelivered outreach, and a member's own act of completing a session must not talk the model down. The consequence is that a completion whose `completed_denominator_units` is fractional can leave the week's inclusivity cut breached in a way the model will not see until the next weekly reconciliation applies it. Attempting to complete a session that has already been marked `MISSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a missed session can never be completed retroactively and no credit of any kind is granted.

The weekly session cut exists as a standalone operation as well. An `EligibilityService` operator applies it to one booked session by naming the `session_week` in question — a supplied `session_week` that names a different week than the session is booked into is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE` — and when that week has just gone inclusive the session becomes `MISSED`, `adherence_numerator` is driven to 0, the enrollment's `adherence_rate` is recomputed, and otherwise nothing changes. A cut applied to a session that does not meet both limbs still reports success, and the enrollment's `version` advances whether or not any session moved. This is the single-session form of the rule the weekly sweep applies in bulk.

Engagement outreach runs to a fixed cadence and a hard window. An `OutreachService` operator queues an outreach contact against an enrollment with a `contact_week`, a `channel` of `PUSH`, `SMS` or `PHONE`, and an optional sender-supplied `contact_occurred_at`. The window is weeks 1 through 4 inclusive: a `contact_week` of 1, 2, 3 or 4 is inside it, and week 5 or beyond is outside it, refused with `ERR_OUTREACH_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A contact may not be queued into a week the clock has already passed; such a request is refused with `ERR_OUTREACH_WEEK_LAPSED`. A contact may also not exceed what the week is allowed to contain — when the member's weekly outreach requirement for the week is already satisfied, the request is refused with `ERR_OUTREACH_CONTACT_LIMIT`. A queued contact is `QUEUED` with `scheduled_send_at` stamped at the current instant. Queuing a contact does not recompute the enrollment's risk score; the queue alone is not evidence of engagement failure.

Delivery is what moves the score, and even then only when it is genuinely member-sourced. An `OutreachService` operator delivers a queued contact, requiring it to be `QUEUED`, requiring the enrollment to be `ACTIVE`, requiring `contact_week` to equal the current `week_index`, and requiring that the member has missed three or fewer consecutive scheduled sessions — a member sitting on exactly three consecutive misses is still deliverable, and only a fourth miss closes the channel. On delivery the contact becomes `DELIVERED`, `contact_occurred_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, and the week's inclusivity cut is set to the smaller of the sender-supplied `contact_occurred_at` and the current instant; a contact queued without a sender-supplied instant simply takes the current instant as its occurrence time. Delivery, like session completion, does not recompute `dropout_risk_score`: a member-sourced delivery never moves the member-sourced risk score. Re-delivering an already `DELIVERED` contact is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and so is a delivery against an enrollment that is not `ACTIVE` or a contact whose week has not yet been reached — a contact queued into a week the clock has not yet entered cannot be delivered early.

Consults between a member and a physical therapist or physician are booked on a clinician's timeline and are constrained in three independent ways. A `Clinician` is registered by a `ClinicalOps` operator with a `full_name`, a `discipline` of either `PHYSICAL_THERAPIST` or `PHYSICIAN`, a state `license_number` and a `weekly_capacity_slots`. A newly registered clinician is `AVAILABLE` with `weekly_completed_minutes` at zero, and a clinician's availability moves between `AVAILABLE`, `LIMITED` and `DEACTIVATED`; a clinician may be limited and restored freely, but deactivation is terminal. A consult is booked by an `EnrollmentOps` operator against an enrollment and a clinician, carrying the `appointment_date` as a calendar day at midnight UTC, a `start_minute` between 0 and 1439 measured from midnight, a `duration_minutes` between 15 and 120 inclusive, and a `slot_type` of `INITIAL_CONSULT` or `FOLLOW_UP`.

The three booking constraints are, in order of evaluation: clinician availability and existence, then timeline geometry, then weekly capacity. Geometry works on a 60-minute grid and is a start-to-start rule: two slots occupied on one clinician's day may not start less than 60 minutes apart, and no single complete 60-minute block may be occupied twice in one day. A consult that violates either limb is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 422). Capacity is a weekly aggregate: the sum of `duration_minutes` over the clinician's `SCHEDULED` and `COMPLETED` consults in one ISO week may not exceed `weekly_capacity_slots` multiplied by 60 minutes, and a booking that lands exactly on that cap is permitted rather than refused. A booking that would push the clinician past the cap is refused with `ERR_CLINICIAN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The clinician's own derived minute totals move accordingly: `weekly_completed_minutes` is the sum of `duration_minutes` over completed consults in the current ISO week, and `weekly_scheduled_minutes` is the sum over both scheduled and completed consults in the current ISO week.

A consult is booked against a specific clinician, named by `clinician_id` in the booking payload, and its `appointment_status` tracks the consult's own lifecycle independently of the clinician's availability: a consult and the clinician who conducts it hold separate state fields with separate version counters, and a consult is not reverted when its clinician is merely limited. A consult is completed by the `Clinician` who conducts it, and completion requires the consult to be `SCHEDULED` and requires the appointment's start instant to be at or before the current logical clock instant — a consult whose start has not yet arrived cannot be marked complete, while one whose start sits exactly on the current instant can. Completion recomputes the clinician's `weekly_completed_minutes` for the ISO week of the appointment date and advances both versions. A consult may instead be cancelled, either by the conducting `Clinician` or by an `EnrollmentOps` operator, from `SCHEDULED` only; cancellation stamps `cancelled_at`, increments the affected enrollment's `consult_cancellation_count`, and advances both versions. Cancellation does not recompute the clinician's minute totals, so a cancelled consult remains counted in the clinician's scheduled-minute aggregate until a later booking or completion refreshes it. Cancellation is deliberately forgiving in the early weeks and is one of the ways a member's failure to engage is registered.

A clinician may also be taken out of service entirely. Deactivating a clinician requires that no consult for that clinician is in `SCHEDULED` status at the moment of the request — the check is made against the state as it stands, and then the cascade fires. A clinician already `DEACTIVATED` cannot be deactivated again. `clinician_status` becomes `DEACTIVATED`, every consult for that clinician that was `SCHEDULED` is set to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_at` stamped at the current instant, and each affected enrollment has its `pain_reduction_points` reset to 0.0 while its `consult_cancellation_count` increments and its `version` advances. `function_gain_points` is not reset by this cascade — only the pain side is reverted, because a cancelled consult voids the member's pain evidence without touching the function evidence. The clinician's own `version` advances as well. Limiting a clinician is the gentler move and requires the clinician to be `AVAILABLE`; restoring one requires it to be `LIMITED`; and neither limit nor restore touches the clinician's consults — only deactivation cancels them.

### 3.5. Weekly Reconciliation, Lapsed Reviews and Contract Lifecycle

Time moves through two independent handles: the logical clock, which an operator sets to an explicit `current_utc` instant, and the per-client sweep cursor, which an operator advances one week at a time. Setting the clock does not by itself advance any week-scoped state. Advancing a client's sweep cursor is what actually runs the weekly sweep, and the sweep does all of the following in one operation: it moves the cursor to a new value that is the greatest of the previous cursor advanced by exactly one week, the current logical clock's `week_index`, and one past the highest week that client's still-`SCHEDULED` sessions or still-`QUEUED` outreach contacts sit in — so a client whose clock has been moved forward catches its cursor up in a single sweep rather than owing a sweep for every week that passed; it moves every `SCHEDULED` exercise session of that client whose `session_week` precedes the new cursor to `MISSED`, driving those sessions' `adherence_numerator` to 0 and recomputing the affected enrollments' `adherence_rate`; it moves every `QUEUED` outreach contact whose `contact_week` precedes the new cursor to `FAILED`; it moves every `SCHEDULED` appointment whose start instant strictly precedes the current logical clock instant to `NO_SHOW` and cascades the outcome revert described below; it recomputes the week-scoped measures of the client's active enrollments; it runs the review reconciliation ladder described next; it runs the red-flag measure for each week the cursor advanced; it advances the client account's `version`; and it recomputes every `DRAFT` invoice the client holds against the corrected records. The sweep reports how much work it did: the advanced week index, the number of sessions it marked missed, the number of outreach contacts it failed, the number of consults that went to no-show, the number of enrollments it drove to `ACTIVATION_FAILED`, and the number of roster files it screened. The recomputation of the client's week-scoped measures is bounded to enrollments that are `ACTIVE` and that carry at least one session or at least one outreach contact; an enrollment with no recorded guided-therapy traffic is left exactly as it was rather than being refreshed to a fresh reading.

A no-show is a consult the clinician did not conduct, and it costs the member their outcome evidence. An appointment reaches `NO_SHOW` when it is `SCHEDULED` and its start instant is strictly before the current logical clock instant. The appointment becomes `NO_SHOW`, and the enrollment it belongs to has both `pain_reduction_points` and `function_gain_points` reset to 0.0 with its `version` advanced. Both sides are reverted here, unlike the clinician-deactivation cascade which reverts pain alone.

The review reconciliation ladder is the mechanism that keeps clinical review from stalling. It applies to every enrollment whose `review_pending_since` is non-null, and it processes them in ascending order of that instant, breaking ties on `created_at` ascending and never on `id`. For each enrollment, `weeks_pending` is the number of whole seven-day periods between `review_pending_since` and the current logical clock instant — a full week must have elapsed before the first period counts — and `weekly_outreach_required_this_week` is 2 when at least one whole week has elapsed and 1 otherwise. That requirement is read live from the enrollment's current `review_pending_since` at the moment a contact is queued, so it rises and falls with the enrollment's stay in the queue. The ladder then performs four steps. It is invoked either unscoped, over the whole store, which is how the standalone reconciliation route runs it, or scoped to one client, which is how the weekly sweep runs it; the client scope narrows every step to that client's enrollments and that client's roster files.

At its first step the ladder accelerates leads into work — this is the outreach-equivalence step and it is what causes the weekly outreach requirement to rise from one contact to two once a review has been pending for a full week. At its second step the ladder closes out reviews that were seeded but never conducted: if a `ClinicalReview` on the enrollment is `SUBMITTED` and its `review_opened_at` plus seven days has arrived, the ladder conducts that review with a decision of `REJECT`, setting it to `CONDUCTED`. When several reviews on one enrollment qualify, they are processed in ascending `review_opened_at`. Because the decision is `REJECT`, the review's approval branch does not fire and the enrollment is not routed into the handoff queue by this step; the enrollment's `clinical_review_outcome` is recorded as `REJECTED` by this auto-conduct, and it is recorded only when the enrollment carries no outcome yet, so an enrollment that already holds an outcome keeps it. At its third step the ladder retires stale non-engagement reviews: if the enrollment is still in `PENDING_SCREENING` or `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW`, its `transition_reason` is `NO_ENGAGEMENT`, and the enrollment's `created_at` is strictly before the instant fourteen days ago, the enrollment is processed as `ACTIVATION_FAILED` with its `version` advanced. Ties are broken on `created_at` ascending. At its fourth step the ladder picks up roster files nobody screened: every `EligibilityFile` of that client still in `INGESTED` whose creation instant plus seven days has arrived is processed exactly as a screening, landing the file in `SCREENED` and recomputing both roster fee variants. An `INGESTED` file whose roster carries no member rows cannot be screened, and such a file is passed over rather than failing the ladder.

The ladder is idempotent. A second invocation immediately after the first makes no further change, because every item it would have touched has already been moved out of the state the step keys on. An implementation that advances the same item twice will be visible as duplicate version increments, and an implementation that processes enrollments in creation order rather than in `review_pending_since` order will diverge on ties. The standalone reconciliation route reports the number of enrollments it reconciled and the number it accelerated rather than the sweep's fuller tally.

A handoff that is open when the client's contract goes out of service does not survive it. Suspending a client account is a `BillingOps` act that requires a `reason` to be supplied. It sets the account to `SUSPENDED` and advances its `version`, then cascades on three fronts. First, every `DRAFT` invoice belonging to the client is voided. Second, every enrollment of that client sitting in `AWAITING_TRANSITION_REVIEW` is reverted to `ACTIVE`, with `transition_reason` and `pending_transition_clinician_id` cleared and with `pain_reduction_points`, `function_gain_points`, `attributed_savings_cents` all reset to zero and `completed_week` set to null; each reverted enrollment's `version` advances. Third, every `CareTransition` of that client still in `PENDING` status is ended with its `transition_reason` recorded as `NO_ENGAGEMENT`, advancing each transition's `version`. The cascade's second front keys on the enrollment's own state and not on whether the sponsoring roster file is still open, so a queued enrollment is reverted whether or not its file has been closed. Reinstatement is deliberately asymmetric: reinstating the account returns it to `ACTIVE` and advances its `version`, and nothing else. Voided invoices are not unvoided and reverted enrollments are not resurrected, so an implementation that tries to compensate the suspension will drift the quantities described in §3.7.

Closing a client account is the deeper cascade and is available from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. It requires that the client has no `EligibilityFile` in `SCREENED` status and no `FeeInvoice` in `DRAFT` status — a contract cannot be closed on top of unfinished screening or unpriced draft billing. Closing sets the account to `CLOSED` and advances its `version`, then walks three levels down. Every roster file of the client whose `file_status` is not `CLOSED` is set to `CLOSED`, each with its own `version` increment. Every member of the client whose `member_status` is `PENDING_ELIGIBILITY` or `ELIGIBLE` is set to `INELIGIBLE` with `eligibility_basis` recorded as `COVERAGE_TERM`, each with its own `version` increment. And, caused by that second hop rather than by the close call directly, every enrollment on each of those newly ineligible members whose `enrollment_status` is `PENDING_SCREENING` or `PENDING_CLINICAL_REVIEW` becomes `ACTIVATION_FAILED` with its `version` incremented. The depth three cascade is the point: an implementation that only closes files and members, without following the member change into the queued enrollments, leaves enrollments alive under a closed contract.

A client's engagement tier can also be recomputed on demand from the authoritative milestone measure described in §3.7 — the inclusive variant, never the strict one — and that recomputation advances the account's `version` and records on the account the member count and cents of the milestone it computed. Finally, two read-only views sit on the client. The retention-credit view reports the client's current draft invoice count and the provisional cents those drafts carry, together with the proposed member count and proposed amount as read-only projections; it is described in more detail in §3.7. The membership roll view reports, as of a requested `as_of_week_index`, four figures: the proposed member count and proposed amount, and the entitled member count and entitled amount; a request that omits `as_of_week_index` reads the roll at the current `week_index` rather than failing. Both views require no write and change no state. The client list can also be read through an `entitlement_filter`, which narrows the result to accounts in the account state the filter names — the same three states a client may hold, so a filter value outside them is ignored and the unfiltered list is returned — and the same filter governs the activation leg of the pipeline, so the enumeration used for reporting and the enumeration used for eligibility are the same enumeration. The list also accepts a `client_status` filter that narrows the result to a single account state. Claim lines are separately readable through an `outcome_measurement_id` filter, which answers the question the attribution rule actually raises: which downstream lines were claimed by a given measurement's window — a filter naming a measurement that does not exist is refused rather than silently returning everything.

### 3.6. Outcome Measurement and Attributed Downstream Savings

Outcome measurement is how the digital program proves it did something. A `Member` records a measurement against an enrollment with the `measurement_week` it covers, a `pain_score` between 0 and 10 inclusive, and a `function_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive; either score outside its range is refused with `ERR_VITALS_OUT_OF_RANGE`. A measurement is `RECORDED` on creation. The enrollment may not carry two live measurements for the same week: a recording whose `measurement_week` duplicates an existing measurement that is still `RECORDED` or `FINALIZED` and has not been superseded is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_WEEK_DUPLICATE` — the lock is on live measurements only, so a week whose earlier measurement has been finalized is still occupied and cannot be re-recorded against. A week whose earlier measurement has been superseded is open again, so a correction re-opens the week rather than being blocked by it. Recording a measurement does not by itself require the enrollment to be `ACTIVE`.

Recording a measurement stamps its attribution window and its baselines in the same operation. `claim_window_rank` is the one-based index of the measurement's window within the enrollment, the first window opened after activation being rank 1. `window_start_week` is the first week index of a four-week attribution window and `window_end_week` is the last, the window spanning four consecutive weeks with both ends included. Consecutive windows partition the enrollment's own timeline rather than floating away with the measurement: rank N's window begins four weeks after the previous rank's, so the first window's four weeks are the four weeks beginning at the enrollment's `enrolled_week` — rank N of an enrollment that activated in week W spans weeks W + 4 × (N − 1) through W + 4 × (N − 1) + 3 inclusive. A measurement whose week already falls inside a window the enrollment has open joins that window and takes its rank, so the rank is decided by containment before any fresh rank is minted and the highest rank is always the next one. Any measurement whose week falls outside every window already opened — including one booked ahead of the enrollment's next block rather than inside it — opens the next rank, and that rank's window is anchored on the enrollment's activation week and not on the measurement's own week, so a measurement booked far into the future still lands in the block its rank prescribes rather than dragging the window along with it. `baseline_pain_score` and `baseline_function_score` are taken from the newest preceding measurement on the same enrollment — the measurement the enrollment most recently recorded, which for a correction inside the same window is the measurement being corrected — or, for the first window, from the member's own `baseline_pain_score` and `baseline_function_score` captured at eligibility determination.

The measurement's own deltas are computed at the same time and are signed. `pain_delta_points` is the window baseline pain score minus the reported `pain_score`, rounded half up to one decimal place, so a member whose pain fell records a positive delta. `function_delta_points` is the reported `function_score` minus the window baseline function score, again rounded half up to one decimal place, so a member whose function improved records a positive delta. Finally `attributed_savings_cents` is computed for the window under the severity-bucket rule below, and the enrollment's own `attributed_savings_cents` is recomputed as the sum of that field over the enrollment's measurements that are `RECORDED` or `FINALIZED`.

Attributed savings value the downstream medical and pharmacy spend that falls inside the window, and the valuation is severity-weighted and adherence-weighted. The inclusion set is every claim line of this enrollment whose `claim_service_week` is at or after the window's `window_start_week` and strictly before the week following `window_end_week` — the upper end is half-open, so a line booked into the week after the window is outside it — and whose `claim_superseded` is false and which has not been withdrawn. Each included line is assigned a severity weight. The care track's `severity_weights` map carries exactly two keys, `LOW` and `HIGH`; a `MODERATE`-severity line takes the `LOW` weight, and a line whose severity is `LOW` takes the `LOW` weight as well. A line takes the `HIGH` weight only when it is a `HIGH`-severity surgical line being evaluated in a week where that is the applicable reading; the selection among the window's evaluation weeks takes the maximum weight in collation order, so a window containing both readings uses the higher one. The window's engagement multiplier is the enrollment's `adherence_rate` recomputed over the window's own four weeks rather than over the whole enrollment: the numerator is the sum of `adherence_numerator` and the denominator the sum of `adherence_denominator` over the sessions whose `session_week` falls inside `[window_start_week, window_end_week]` inclusive, rounded half up to two decimal places, or 0.0 for an enrollment that was dropped or a window with no sessions in it. That window rate is the same value that drives the engagement milestone, so a window in which the member engaged poorly is discounted. The attributed savings for the window is the sum, over included lines, of the line's `amount_cents` multiplied by its selected severity weight and then multiplied by the window's engagement rate, with the sum taken in ascending `claim_service_week` order and, for ties, ascending `created_at`, and with a single round half up to a whole number of cents applied after the sum. A window with no included lines attributes exactly 0 rather than failing.

The authoritative reading is version-pinned, and pinning is the whole point. Claim lines are versioned instances of the same logical line, and the savings attributed to a window use the line instance that is current at the instant the window is evaluated, with that instance's `amount_cents` frozen for the life of the attribution. A line that is amended after its window was evaluated is never reprocessed into that window, so the attributed figure recorded on the measurement and on the enrollment is a fixed historical reading rather than a moving one. This version-pinned reading is what writes `OutcomeMeasurement.attributed_savings_cents` and `Enrollment.attributed_savings_cents`, and nothing else may write those two fields. The recomputation that keeps them current is triggered by the events that can change the answer rather than by every read: recording a claim line recomputes every `RECORDED` measurement of the enrollment whose window contains the line's week — a `FINALIZED` measurement's frozen figure is not disturbed by a later line — and it is those recomputations, together with the recording of a measurement itself and the supersession or finalization of one, that keep the enrollment's total equal to the sum of its live measurements' figures. The enrollment's total is the sum of `attributed_savings_cents` over its `RECORDED` and `FINALIZED` measurements, so a superseded measurement contributes nothing. Separately, the enrollment carries a `measurement_count` that tracks how many measurements it holds; it is maintained as those measurements are recorded and superseded and is not a function of the live-status filter that governs the savings total.

A second reading of the same window exists and is deliberately divergent. The billing-time re-evaluation substitutes each line's superseded instance in place of its current one and recomputes the engagement rate as of the invoice creation instant, and it is what sets `FeeInvoice.savings_share_component_cents`. The substitution is scoped to a family of instances that share both a `claim_service_week` and a `claim_source`, and one instance is taken from each family — the earliest superseded or withdrawn one if the family has any, otherwise the latest live one — so a superseded family contributes its withdrawn older instance rather than the replacement that stands in its place. It must not write `OutcomeMeasurement.attributed_savings_cents` or `Enrollment.attributed_savings_cents`, which stay on the version-pinned reading, and both readings are separately observable on their own endpoints. The two therefore disagree on the same underlying window whenever a line has been superseded, and that disagreement is intended: one is the number the program reports, the other is the number the client is billed.

Claim lines arrive and are corrected through their own lifecycle. An `EnrollmentOps` operator records a downstream line against an enrollment with a `claim_service_date`, the `claim_service_week` it is attributed to, a `claim_severity` of `LOW`, `MODERATE` or `HIGH`, a non-negative `amount_cents`, and a `claim_source` of `MEDICAL` or `PHARMACY`. A recorded line starts with `claim_superseded` false and `line_instance_version` 1. A line may not be recorded into a window that has already lapsed at the current week index, because a lapsed window stops accepting additional lines; such a recording is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The guard keys on the enrollment's attribution window that contains the line's week, and it bites when that window's `window_end_week` is strictly before the current `week_index`; a line whose week falls in no window the enrollment has opened is not blocked and simply enters no window until one covers it. Recording a line recomputes the attributed savings of every `RECORDED` measurement of that enrollment whose window contains the line's week, using the version-pinned reading only, and recomputes the enrollment's `attributed_savings_cents`.

Correcting a line is a two-part operation. Superseding a line instance supplies a `replacement_amount_cents` and a `replacement_claim_severity`, marks the prior instance `claim_superseded`, and creates a replacement `ClaimLine` whose `line_instance_version` is the prior instance's value plus one — the counter is monotonic and never reused, and a supersede is refused on an instance that has already been withdrawn or superseded rather than allowed to fork the family further. The response returns the replacement instance rather than the retired one. The same recomputation of the affected `RECORDED` measurements runs again on the version-pinned reading. The reverse operation withdraws a named instance from its window: the request carries `withdraw_instance_version` naming the instance version to take out, the withdrawal marks that instance both superseded and withdrawn, and the response reports how the window is now composed by returning the line instance count in the window, the week span the window's claim head now covers, the number of claim lines still in the window, and the number of distinct weeks represented on the claim head — the week span being the lowest and highest week still standing on a live instance, or an empty span when none stands. Both operations advance the affected line's `version`.

Measurement correction follows the same shape but with one hard lock. Superseding a measurement supplies a `replacement_measurement_id`, requires the outgoing measurement to be `RECORDED`, and requires the replacement to belong to the same enrollment and the same `claim_window_rank` — a replacement in a different window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422), because a window may not be re-pointed at a different window's claims. The outgoing measurement becomes `SUPERSEDED` and its `attributed_savings_cents` is driven to 0, and the enrollment's `attributed_savings_cents` is recomputed as the sum over its `RECORDED` and `FINALIZED` measurements. A measurement that has already been `FINALIZED` is locked: attempting to supersede it is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_FINALIZED` and nothing changes — and that lock is evaluated ahead of the state check, so a finalized measurement is reported as locked even though it is no longer `RECORDED`.

Finalization is what converts a window reading into a locked outcome. A `RECORDED` measurement may be finalized once its four-week claim window has fully lapsed — that is, once `window_end_week` is strictly less than the current week index. Finalizing while the window is still open is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_WINDOW_OPEN`, and that window check is evaluated before the enrollment's own state, so an open window on an inactive enrollment still reports the window failure. The enrollment must be `ACTIVE`, and the enrollment may not already carry a `FINALIZED` measurement, so a second finalization on the same enrollment is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_LIMIT_REACHED` — a measurement that is already `FINALIZED` reports that limit immediately, without the window or enrollment checks being consulted first. On success the measurement becomes `FINALIZED` and the enrollment's headline outcome fields are written directly from the measurement: `pain_reduction_points` takes the measurement's `pain_delta_points` and `function_gain_points` takes the measurement's `function_delta_points`. Finalization can be reached either as a standalone act by the reconciliation service or as the finalization leg of the review reconciliation ladder described in §3.5, and both routes produce the same result. Finishing a measurement does not itself close the enrollment: completion of the enrollment remains a separate act and, like the enrollment's other state changes, is not triggered by the measurement's finalization. Because `Enrollment.pain_reduction_points` and `Enrollment.function_gain_points` are ordinary stored fields, they are also the fields that a no-show cascade and a clinician deactivation cascade reset to zero — a member can therefore have a locked, finalized measurement while their enrollment's headline outcome fields have since been reverted, and the two are not required to agree.

### 3.7. The Engagement Milestone and Member-Week Entitlement

The engagement milestone is the single most consequential boundary in the engagement-billed half of the product, and it is an inclusive one. A member's enrollment is projected to have reached the milestone — expressed as `projected_engagement_milestone` holding the integer 1 — exactly when both of two conditions hold: the enrollment's `weeks_active` is at least 1, and the enrollment's `adherence_rate` is at least the `min_adherence_rate` of the care track the enrollment is assigned to. In every other case the flag holds 0. The comparison is inclusive on both counts, so a member landing exactly on the track's `min_adherence_rate` is inside the group and earns the milestone. A member whose adherence sits a hundredth of a point below the track's rate is outside it. Because adherence rates are stored to two decimal places and track rates are supplied values, landing exactly on the boundary is a realistic case rather than a theoretical one, and the inclusive reading is the one that bills.

The milestone drives a client-level count and a client-level fee. The count, `milestone_member_count`, is the number of the client's enrollments that are `ACTIVE`, have `weeks_active` of at least 1, and have reached the milestone under the inclusive comparison. The fee, `milestone_engagement_component_cents`, is the sum over exactly those enrollments of the enrollment's track's `base_fee_per_member_cents` multiplied by the enrollment's `weeks_active` and divided by four, with a single round half up to a whole number of cents applied after the sum is complete. The per-enrollment time allocation is therefore a quarter of a month's platform fee for each whole week the member has been active, which is why a member who joins mid-month is billed a partial month rather than a whole one.

A strict reading of the same comparison exists, and it is never used for billing. The strict variant requires `adherence_rate` to be strictly greater than the track's `min_adherence_rate`, which excludes members sitting exactly on the boundary, and it exists only to feed an internal trending report. The two variants are not interchangeable. An implementation that computes the billed milestone component with the strict comparison will under-bill by exactly those members who land on the boundary, and an implementation that computes the trending figure with the inclusive comparison will over-report them. The inclusive variant is the default everywhere the engagement milestone feeds money, and it is the variant used when a client's engagement tier is recomputed on demand.

Member-weeks are the unit of entitlement across both fee structures, and the client-facing views that report them are the membership roll and the engagement report. The membership roll answers a single question: for a requested `as_of_week_index`, how many member-weeks does the client hold, and what do they come to. It returns four figures — `proposed_member_count` and `proposed_amount_cents` describing the proposed entitlement, and `entitled_member_count` and `entitled_amount_cents` describing what the client is entitled to as of that week. The proposed figures are projections read from the same member-week arithmetic that the engagement component uses; the entitled figures are the entitlement the client has actually accrued. Both readings are taken over one population and read from one set of arithmetic: the roll reports the same `proposed_member_count` and `entitled_member_count`, and the same amount for both the proposed and the entitled sides, so a reader should not expect the two pairs to diverge. The population is the client's enrollments that are `ACTIVE`, that activated at or before the requested week, that have at least one whole week between their `enrolled_week` and the requested week, whose care track is known, and whose `adherence_rate` is at least the track's `min_adherence_rate` under the same inclusive comparison the milestone uses. The amount is the sum, over those enrollments, of the track's `base_fee_per_member_cents` multiplied by the weeks the requested index leaves since activation and divided by four, rounded half up once at the end. The two are reported side by side rather than collapsed, because a client asking about a forward week wants to see both what will be proposed and what is already theirs. The roll is a read: it writes nothing and advances no version.

The engagement report is the client-level counterpart and reports the same member-week arithmetic rolled up per enrollment, including each enrollment's outcome headline figures. Per enrollment it carries the enrollment's identifiers, its member's status, its own status, its care track's code when it has one, its `enrolled_week`, `weeks_active`, `adherence_rate` and milestone projection, and a `milestone_weeks` count that is the enrollment's `weeks_active` when the enrollment has reached the milestone and zero otherwise; enrollments that carry no care track are still listed, with null track-derived fields, rather than omitted. At the client level it carries the client's billing model and expected engagement rate alongside the same `milestone_member_count` and `milestone_engagement_component_cents` the milestone formula produces. Like the roll it is a projection over current state rather than a stored snapshot: it is recomputed at read time from the live enrollments, the live care tracks and the current logical clock, so the same client read at two different clock instants will show two different engagement figures without any intervening write. Both the roll and the report read `weeks_active` for the same reason and with the same limitation: `weeks_active` measures the enrollment rather than the member, so a member who has been covered by the client for a year but enrolled three weeks ago contributes three member-weeks and not fifty-two.

### 3.8. Invoice Construction and the Two Readings of a Bill

A `FeeInvoice` is built for one client and one billing period, and it is built twice: once when the draft is created and once when it is finalized. Creation is a `BillingOps` act for a `client_account_id` and a `billing_period` in `YYYY-MM` form. The client must be `ACTIVE`; creating billable work against a `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` client is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Two invoices may not double-claim the same savings: when the client already holds an invoice for the same billing period that has not been voided, the creation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SAVINGS_ATTRIBUTION` — a voided invoice for the period does not block a replacement, because voiding is what releases the period for re-billing. Creation also advances the client account's `version`.

Creating the draft runs the forward-looking construction and stamps its components: `milestone_member_count`, `milestone_engagement_component_cents`, `savings_share_component_cents`, `additional_fee_component_cents` and `provisional_amount_cents` are all written, and the invoice sits in `DRAFT` with its `invoice_status` recording exactly that; `finalized_total_cents` starts null and `billing_model_snapshot` is left unset until finalization. The construction works from a set of enrollments and a set of measurements. The enrollment inclusion set is every enrollment of the client whose `enrolled_week` falls at or after the week index of the billing period's first day and strictly before that index advanced by four weeks — the period is a whole four-week month in the member-week arithmetic, half-open on the upper end, so an enrollment landing in the first week of the following month belongs to the following period. The set is taken in ascending `enrolled_week`, breaking ties on `created_at` and then on `id`. Within that set, each enrollment contributes a pinned share equal to its track's `base_fee_per_member_cents` multiplied by the number of whole months the enrollment has been active, where a month is four weeks and the count of whole months is the number of complete four-week blocks between `enrolled_week` and the current week index, expressed as a whole number by discarding the remainder. Each enrollment also carries a trailing remainder: its track's base fee multiplied by the number of leftover weeks beyond those whole four-week blocks and divided by four, with a single round half up to whole cents. An enrollment whose care track is unknown contributes nothing rather than failing the construction.

The forward-looking total is the sum of the pinned shares over the inclusion set, plus an alternative branch contributed by measured savings. The savings branch is the sum of `attributed_savings_cents` over the measurements in the period that are already `RECORDED` — a measurement still under correction does not count, and neither does one already `FINALIZED` — multiplied by the client's `savings_share_rate`, rounded half up to whole cents once; a measurement counts as in the period when its `measurement_week` falls in the same four-week span the enrollment inclusion set uses. `provisional_amount_cents` is the pinned-share sum plus that savings branch, rounded once. Because only already-recorded measurements enter, and because members who have not yet cleared their waiver deadline are excluded from the engagement component, the forward-looking reading is computed from a strictly smaller input set than the chronological one and returns a different total for the same underlying data.

Finalization is a `BillingOps` act that pins the bill. It requires the invoice to be `DRAFT`, which also means a voided invoice is refused before anything else, requires the client to be `ACTIVE`, and requires `additional_fee_component_cents` to be exactly 0 — an invoice carrying an unreconciled late fee cannot be finalized, and such an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_UNRECONCILED`. An invoice whose `additional_fee_component_cents` is non-zero because a reconciliation landed after the invoice was created must first be brought back into agreement. The components are recomputed against current state immediately before the late-fee test is made, so an invoice that has drifted is judged on its freshly computed fee rather than on the figure it last stored. On success the invoice becomes `FINALIZED`, `billing_model_snapshot` captures the client's `billing_model` as of that instant, and five components are recomputed. `milestone_engagement_component_cents` is recomputed under the time-allocation reading described below; `savings_share_component_cents` is recomputed under the billing-time re-evaluation reading from §3.6; `provisional_amount_cents` is recomputed from the corrected records; `final_amount_cents` is recomputed; and `net_delta_cents` is set to the final total minus the provisional total. `finalized_total_cents` is set equal to `final_amount_cents`, and this is the figure actually billed. Finalization does not freeze the invoice's view of the world: a later weekly sweep refreshes a `DRAFT` invoice's components, and a reopened invoice is refreshed likewise, while a `FINALIZED` invoice's stored components are left where finalization put them until it is reopened.

The chronological total — `final_amount_cents` — replaces the forward-looking branch with a full recompute. Its engagement side is `recalculated_component_cents`, and this is defined as a formula over the current tables rather than as a running adjustment. It is the sum, over the enrollment inclusion set, of each enrollment's pinned share plus its trailing remainder — that is, the full base fee multiplied by the elapsed weeks and divided by four, rounded per enrollment — plus, for every member whom the milestone recompute at finalization moves from outside the inclusive engagement group to inside it, that member's membership delta, being the member's track base fee multiplied by the member's `weeks_active` and divided by four, rounded half up; minus, for every member whom the recompute moves from inside the group to outside it, the same membership delta. The comparison the membership deltas are struck against is the engagement group the invoice recorded when its draft was struck, so it is the movement between the two inclusive readings that the deltas capture and not the difference between an inclusive and a strict reading. The savings side of the chronological total is the sum of `attributed_savings_cents` over the measurements in the period that are `RECORDED` or `FINALIZED` — finalized measurements now count, where the forward-looking reading excluded them — multiplied by the client's `savings_share_rate` and rounded half up once, and that side is the billing-time re-evaluation reading rather than the version-pinned one. The chronological total is the recomputed engagement component plus that savings branch, and it will therefore differ from the provisional total for the same underlying data whenever a reconciliation has moved a member across the milestone boundary or a measurement has been finalized since the draft was struck.

`additional_fee_component_cents` is the late-reconciliation channel and it starts empty by construction. It captures fees arising from reconciliations that lapsed after the invoice was created: an enrollment of the client that reaches `COMPLETED` with a `completed_week` inside the billing period, strictly after the invoice's own `created_at`, contributes its track base fee multiplied by its `weeks_active` and divided by four, rounded half up, to this component. It is 0 at creation and stays 0 while no later reconciliation lands inside the period, and finalization refuses a non-zero value — so the field exists to make the delayed fee visible and to block the bill until it has been dealt with, rather than to be silently absorbed into the total.

The evaluation order of the construction is pinned and is worth restating because two of its steps are easy to reverse. Pinned shares are computed before the alternatives branch is summed; the engagement component is computed before the savings component; and each component is rounded once, at the end of its own computation, rather than intermediate results being rounded and then re-summed. Component precision is whole cents throughout, with round half up per component.

An invoice can also be walked backwards, with a limit. A `DRAFT` invoice can be voided by a `BillingOps` operator, becoming `VOID`; a `VOID` invoice can never be reopened, and attempting to do so is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_VOID` — that check is made ahead of the state check, so a voided invoice is reported as void rather than as one that is not finalized. A `FINALIZED` invoice can be reopened to `DRAFT` for correction, provided the client is `ACTIVE`; reopening clears `finalized_total_cents` to null and advances the invoice's `version`. The asymmetry is deliberate: a finalized bill is correctable, a voided one is not.

Voiding feeds the retention-credit view. The retention-credit view reports, for a client, the count of its `DRAFT` invoices and the provisional cents those drafts carry; that provisional amount is the credit the client would retain if the drafts were withdrawn, and a read of the view therefore reports `draft_invoice_count`, `retained_credit_amount_cents`, `proposal_member_count` and `proposal_amount_cents`, the last two being read-only projections reading the same milestone member count and milestone cents the engagement component reads. The suspension cascade is what exercises it. Suspending a client voids every `DRAFT` invoice it holds, so after a suspension the draft invoice count is 0 and the retained credit the same bills would have carried is the sum of their provisional amounts. Two conserved quantities must hold across the forward and compensating pair. The difference between the draft invoice count before the suspension and the count after it must equal the number of invoices the suspension voided. And the retained credit reported for those same bills must equal the sum of their `provisional_amount_cents`. Reinstatement does not restore any voided invoice, so the retained credit does not come back: the quantity after the pair is the quantity that survives, and any implementation that re-creates voided drafts on reinstatement breaks both conserved quantities at once.

### 3.9. Leaving the Program: Dropout, In-Person Handoff and Graduation

There are three ways out of an active enrollment, and each is reached by a different role and leaves the member in a different place. The first is dropout, which is the program's own judgement that the member is not engaging. An `EnrollmentOps` operator drops an active enrollment, and the drop is available only inside a narrow window and only on evidence. The window is the enrollment's first four measured weeks, counted from the enrollment's activation week: `enrolled_week` is the activation week, and the four weeks that follow it — the weeks reached while the current week index minus `enrolled_week` is one, two, three or four — are the four measured weeks. Equivalently, the drop is inside its window while the current week index minus the enrollment's `enrolled_week` minus one is less than 4, so weeks one through four are inside and week five and beyond are outside; the activation week itself, where the current week index equals `enrolled_week`, is also inside the window, since the difference is negative. An attempt to drop in the fifth or a later week is refused with `ERR_DROPOUT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). Inside the window, at least one of two pieces of evidence must exist: either the member has missed three consecutive scheduled exercise sessions — at least three, so three exactly suffices — or two outreach contacts booked into weeks one through four ended `FAILED`. A drop attempted on an active enrollment in the window with neither piece of evidence present is refused with `ERR_DROPOUT_CRITERIA_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422).

A successful drop ends the digital episode but not the member's relationship with the client. The enrollment's `enrollment_status` becomes `DROPPED`, `dropout_week` is stamped with the current week index, and the member's own `member_status` becomes `DROPPED`, which is a terminal member state. Both versions increment. The session history tied to the dropped enrollment is then excluded from every later adherence computation: sessions bound to a dropped enrollment no longer contribute to any adherence rate, including the rate of any replacement enrollment the member later opens.

That last point is why the member's dropout state is not permanent in the sense it first appears. When an enrollment ends in `DROPPED` — whether the drop that ended it is the call being made now or one made earlier — the member's `member_status` returns to `ELIGIBLE` rather than staying at `DROPPED` when that status is still `ENROLLED`, advancing the member's `version`. The member's status therefore cycles `ELIGIBLE` to `ENROLLED` to `ELIGIBLE` across successive enrollment attempts, and a member who drops out of one episode can be enrolled into another. A member whose `member_status` is `ELIGIBLE` may also be dropped directly by the same operation, so the drop is the sole mechanism by which the member axis reaches `DROPPED` on the way out of a failed episode and the sole mechanism by which it is released back to `ELIGIBLE` afterwards. A drop call against an enrollment that is already `DROPPED` still reports success and still runs the member release, so the member axis reaches `ELIGIBLE` on the first further drop rather than erroring; a drop against an enrollment in any other non-`ACTIVE` state is refused.

The second exit is the in-person handoff, which is the program's judgement that the digital mode is not the right one for this member. A `Clinician` opens a handoff against an enrollment that is already `ACTIVE`, supplying the `approving_clinician_id` of the clinician who will carry it; the handoff starts in `PENDING` with `handoff_referral_count` at zero, and the enrollment's `version` advances. A handoff also arrives pre-seeded on the approving branch of a clinical review, described in §3.3, so a handoff exists in `PENDING` prior to activation in the ordinary flow and is created directly only when the enrollment is already active. Completing the handoff is a `Clinician` act that requires the transition to be `PENDING` and the enrollment to be `ACTIVE`, and requires the transition to carry a valid in-person referral; completing a handoff when no transition review is pending is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_PENDING`. The handoff's own `transition_status` moves from `PENDING` to `COMPLETED` on completion and never returns to `PENDING` — unlike the enrollment's review status, which can cycle, the handoff row is one-way. The same segregation-of-duties rule that guards activation also guards completion: the clinician recorded as `approving_clinician_id` must differ from the enrollment's `clinical_reviewing_clinician_id`, and a clinician trying to complete a handoff they themselves reviewed is refused with `ERR_CLINICIAN_DUAL_ROLE`.

Completion moves the member out of the digital program and back into the client's covered population. The transition becomes `COMPLETED` with `transitioned_at` stamped, and `handoff_referral_count` increments by one — the counter accumulates across every handoff of the enrollment rather than being reset, so an enrollment that was handed off, reverted and handed off again reports two referrals. The enrollment becomes `TRANSITIONED`, which is terminal, and the member's `member_status` becomes `ELIGIBLE` again, which is what makes the transitioned member available for future enrollment. Both the transition's and the enrollment's `version` advance.

The third exit is graduation, and it is the only one that requires a locked outcome. Completing an enrollment requires the enrollment to be `ACTIVE` and to carry exactly one `FINALIZED` outcome measurement. An enrollment with no finalized measurement, or with more than one, is refused with `ERR_NO_FINALIZED_MEASUREMENT` (HTTP 409), and the guard on the measurement side — that a second finalization on one enrollment is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_LIMIT_REACHED` — is what keeps the count at exactly one rather than at least one. On success the enrollment becomes `COMPLETED`, which is terminal, and `completed_week` is stamped with the week index of the finalized measurement's measurement date rather than with the current week. The distinction matters for billing: an enrollment that completes in the first week of a new month but whose finalized measurement belongs to the last week of the prior month has its `completed_week` counted against the prior month's billing period rather than the current one.

Graduation is a cross-entity guard as well as a state change. The member's own status moves to `GRADUATED`, and that transition is reachable only while the enrollment is still `ACTIVE` — the member's graduation and the enrollment's completion are two halves of one act, and an implementation that graduates the member on an enrollment that is not active has introduced a state the product does not have. Note too that graduation is not the only way a member reaches `ELIGIBLE` after engagement: a dropout release and an in-person transition both return the member to `ELIGIBLE`, so the member's status alone never tells an observer which exit the enrollment took. An operator reading the enrollment's own status — `GRADUATED` members hold `COMPLETED` enrollments, transitioned members hold `TRANSITIONED` enrollments, released members hold `DROPPED` enrollments — resolves the three cases that the member axis collapses.

The enrollment window is opened at one end of the pipeline and the roster file that fed it is closed at the other. An `EnrollmentOps` operator closes a file that is `ASSIGNED` and whose enrollments have all left screening — the guard is that no enrollment created from the file is still in `PENDING_SCREENING`. On success the file becomes `CLOSED`, which is terminal for the file, and its `version` advances. A closed file is not a dead record: its fee variants remain readable and its member rows remain part of the client's population history, so closing a file retires the intake pipeline without discarding the roster it carried. A file that is still `SCREENED` blocks the closure of its sponsoring client contract, exactly as an unscreened file and a live draft invoice do.

An enrollment is the unit that most readers of this API actually care about, and its read surface is deliberately wide. Reading an enrollment returns its window anchors — `enrolled_at`, `enrolled_week`, `review_pending_since` and `completed_week` — together with its adherence and risk state (`weeks_active`, `adherence_rate`, `adherence_scored_through_week`, `dropout_risk_score`, `dropout_risk_as_of_week`, `coach_escalation_required`, `coach_escalated`), its consult bookkeeping (`consult_cancellation_count`) and its milestone projection (`projected_engagement_milestone`), and alongside those three headline blocks of outcome: the pain block anchored on `pain_reduction_points`, the function block anchored on `function_gain_points`, and the savings block anchored on `attributed_savings_cents`. The reading recomputes `weeks_active`, `adherence_rate`, `adherence_scored_through_week` and the milestone projection against the live clock before returning them, so a read is itself enough to bring the adherence picture up to date even though the risk score is only recomputed by the sweep. Because the three headline blocks are stored fields rather than projections, they can be read directly and they move independently of each other — a finalized measurement writes both the pain and function blocks, a no-show cascade resets both, and a clinician deactivation resets only the pain side.

Two enrollment fields exist purely as anchors for other rules and are worth naming here because they are easy to mistake for narrative metadata. `enrolled_week` is the unique anchor for the drop window, for the session inclusion set and for the billing inclusion set; `review_pending_since` is the anchor for the review reconciliation ladder and is null outside the review queue. `dropout_week` is present only when the enrollment reached `DROPPED`, and `completed_week` only when it reached `COMPLETED`.
